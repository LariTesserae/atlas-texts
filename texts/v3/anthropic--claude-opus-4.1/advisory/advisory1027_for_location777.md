## Preparation Guide for the Singing Cistern

### Critical Warnings

The Singing Cistern is one of the most physically hostile environments you can visit without entering an actively deadly zone. The acoustic pressure alone can cause permanent physiological damage within minutes of unprotected exposure. This is not a casual destination—treat it with the same respect you would give to a deep ocean dive or high-altitude mountaineering.

### Essential Equipment

**Acoustic Protection (Mandatory)**
- Military-grade electronic hearing protection with active noise cancellation is insufficient. You need *passive* protection first—custom-molded earplugs rated for at least 40dB reduction, worn under industrial earmuffs with additional acoustic dampening foam
- Bring backup earplugs sealed in hard cases—the vibrations will gradually degrade foam and soft materials
- Consider bone conduction dampeners—adhesive pads for your skull behind the ears and at the temples. The sound here bypasses your ears entirely

**Respiratory Protection**
- Full-face respirator with P100 filters minimum—the spores and pollen are not toxic per se, but they accumulate in your lungs and continue to vibrate, causing a condition locals call "singer's lung" 
- Bring multiple replacement filters—they will clog quickly
- Include a backup emergency oxygen mask with 20-30 minutes of supply

**Clothing and Body Protection**
- Wear thick, non-synthetic fabrics in multiple layers. Natural fibers dampen vibration better than synthetics which can actually amplify certain frequencies
- Heavy leather boots with thick rubber soles—you need mass between you and the resonating floor
- Leather or thick canvas gloves—never touch the wire-vines with bare skin. They're at a constant temperature of roughly 45°C (113°F) from friction heating
- Avoid any loose items, jewelry, or dangling straps—they will whip violently in the standing waves

**Navigation and Timing Equipment**
- Mechanical compass only—electronic navigation fails in the electromagnetic fields generated by the vibrating metallic vines
- Wind-up mechanical watch—digital devices malfunction or shut down
- Bring surveyor's chalk or spray paint to mark your path—it's easy to become disoriented

### Physical Preparation

**Conditioning Your Body**
Start at least two weeks before your visit:
- Expose yourself to gradually increasing low-frequency sounds (use a subwoofer, start at 30 minutes daily)
- Practice breathing exercises—you'll need to consciously control your breathing against the pressure waves
- Strengthen your core muscles—the acoustic pressure will constantly push against your diaphragm
- Practice moving and working while dizzy—your inner ear will be compromised the entire time

**Medical Considerations**
- Have your ears examined by a specialist before traveling
- If you have any dental fillings, especially older amalgam ones, have them checked—the vibrations can crack weak dental work
- Take anti-nausea medication 30 minutes before entering
- Bring electrolyte supplements—you will sweat profusely from the heat and stress

### Timing Your Visit

**Never Enter During:**
- The dawn resonance (roughly 0500-0700 local time) when the sound intensifies by approximately 30%
- Following earthquakes or construction work in the city above—this can trigger dangerous harmonics
- Local religious festivals—the drumming and music above can create destructive interference patterns

**Optimal Windows:**
- Mid-afternoon when the city above is quietest
- Limit initial exposure to 15 minutes maximum
- Plan multiple short visits rather than one extended stay

### Accessing the Cistern

**Finding the Entrance:**
- Hire a local guide familiar with the maintenance tunnels—attempting to navigate the forgotten shafts alone is foolish
- The most stable entrance is through the old Tanners' Quarter basement, third building from the mosque
- Avoid the southern entrances—they pass too close to still-active sewer lines
- Bring rope and climbing gear—many passages require vertical descent

**Payment and Permissions:**
- The "tool-keeper" (the person maintaining the tuning instruments) must be contacted through intermediaries
- Standard payment is three bronze ingots or equivalent trade goods—never offer modern currency
- Bring an additional offering for the cistern itself—locally-made musical instruments are preferred
- Some travelers report success with recordings of music from your home universe, played on mechanical devices

### Inside the Cistern

**Movement Protocol:**
- Move slowly and deliberately—sudden movements create pressure waves that can knock you off balance
- Follow the carved channels on the floor when possible—they're somewhat shielded from the worst resonances
- Never lean against the walls—the vibrations will transfer directly to your skeleton
- If you must rest, sit in the "quiet nodes" where the pale insects hover—but only briefly, as these zones shift

**Interacting with the Environment:**
- Do not attempt to harvest the wire-vines—they're integrated into the acoustic system, and removing them can cause dangerous feedback
- The bioluminescent fungi can be observed closely but never touched—they release defensive spores when disturbed
- You may examine the tuning tools but DO NOT adjust the bronze plates or move the positioned stones—this could destabilize the entire acoustic environment
- Photography requires special equipment—standard cameras will produce only blur from the constant vibration

**Emergency Procedures:**
- If you experience sudden vertigo or bleeding from the ears, evacuate immediately
- Loss of vision indicates dangerous pressure on your optic nerves—lie flat and crawl toward the exit
- If the sound suddenly stops, leave immediately—this precedes a catastrophic acoustic event
- The pale insects fleeing upward means structural danger—follow them

### Cultural Sensitivity

The locals above know something exists below but actively avoid discussing it. Asking direct questions about the cistern marks you as either dangerous or cursed. Instead:
- Speak of "visiting the old waterworks for historical research"
- Never mention the sound while in the city
- If you encounter the tool-keeper, bow but don't attempt conversation—they're usually deaf from long exposure
- Leave the space as you found it—this includes not disturbing the patterns in the scattered spore accumulations

### Recovery

After leaving the cistern:
- Expect 2-3 days of tinnitus and mild vertigo
- Your hearing will be hypersensitive for up to a week—have quiet accommodations arranged
- Drink significant amounts of water to help flush accumulated spores from your system
- Some travelers report persistent dreams of being inside massive musical instruments—this is normal and fades

### What to Bring Back

- Acoustic recordings require specialized equipment but are highly valued by certain collectors
- Small samples of the crystallized sap (if already naturally shed) have unique properties
- Sketches or paintings of the vine growth patterns—photography rarely captures their true structure
- Most valuable: careful notes on the tuning modifications and their effects—someone is trying to communicate through this space

### Final Warnings

The Singing Cistern changes those who spend time within it. Some travelers report enhanced hearing that never fully returns to baseline. Others claim they can feel earthquakes days before they occur. A few insist they can hear the cistern's song from anywhere in the city, calling them back. Whether these effects are physiological or psychological remains debated.

Remember: this is not just an unusual ecosystem or acoustic phenomenon. The deliberate modifications, the apparent cultivation of the sound-eating plants, and the encoding patterns in their growth suggest intelligence at work—though whether human, natural, or something else entirely remains unknown. Approach with respect, caution, and the understanding that you're entering something that may be as much organism as location.

The sound never stops. Once you've heard it—truly heard it with your bones and blood—a part of it travels with you. Consider carefully if you're prepared for that before descending.
