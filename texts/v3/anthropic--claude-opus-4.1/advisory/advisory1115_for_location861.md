## Preparation Guide for the Electric Valley

### Initial Warnings and Mental Preparation

Before anything else, understand that this place will unsettle you in ways you cannot fully anticipate. The valley operates on biological and electrical principles that diverge from baseline reality in subtle but profound ways. You will not be in immediate danger if properly prepared, but the psychological impact of prolonged exposure to this environment should not be underestimated. The oppressive silence, the wrongness of the vegetation, and the temporal distortions can induce a specific type of valley-sickness in unprepared travelers. Spend time meditating on environments that exist between categories—neither fully natural nor artificial, neither alive nor dead. This mental flexibility will serve you well.

### Essential Equipment

**Electrical Protection**: The entire valley is suffused with low-level electrical current. Bring rubber-soled boots with thick insulation—military surplus electromagnetic pulse protection boots work well. Pack rubber gloves rated for electrical work. Your clothing should contain no metal fasteners, zippers, or buttons. Use ceramic or plastic alternatives. The current isn't typically strong enough to harm humans directly, but metal objects can accumulate charge over time, delivering painful shocks or interfering with the local ecology in unpredictable ways.

**Moisture Management**: This is critical. The valley's ecosystem revolves around water in ways that will affect you constantly. Bring completely waterproof outer layers—not water-resistant, but truly waterproof. The morning dew is not ordinary condensation; it carries trace electrical charge and unknown biological compounds from the adapted vegetation. You'll need wicking base layers underneath to manage your own perspiration, as the humidity can be oppressive despite the cool air. Pack multiple changes of socks—foot rot develops quickly in this environment due to the unique fungal ecosystem that thrives in the electrically-active moisture.

**Breathing Protection**: While the air isn't toxic, you should bring N95 masks or better. The plants release spores and pollens that, while not immediately harmful, can colonize human respiratory systems temporarily. Travelers report tasting copper for weeks afterward, and some develop a persistent cough that produces bioluminescent phlegm—harmless but disturbing.

**Navigation Tools**: Do not rely on magnetic compasses or GPS devices. The electromagnetic interference will render them useless or actively misleading. Instead, bring a sextant or learn celestial navigation. Mark your path with biodegradable ribbons (the valley will digest anything else within days). The temporal distortions mean you cannot rely on consistent sunrise/sunset times for navigation. A mechanical watch is essential—digital devices fail quickly here.

### Sustenance and Water

**Never drink the valley's water**, no matter how pure it appears. The circular lake at the valley's heart is obviously off-limits, but even morning dew and the clear fluid that sweeps from the tree-things contains heavy metals and bioelectric compounds that accumulate in human tissues. Bring all water in glass or ceramic containers—plastic degrades rapidly in the valley's unique chemistry. Plan for 1.5 times normal water consumption due to the dehydrating effects of the electrical atmosphere.

Food should be in sealed metal containers (kept insulated in rubber bags to prevent charging). The valley's plants, while fascinating, are not edible by baseline humans. The star-shaped succulents concentrate copper and other metals to toxic levels. The moss, while it looks soft and inviting, will cause severe gastrointestinal distress if ingested. Some travelers report that the hexagonal trees produce fruit-like growths, but these are actually specialized electrical storage organs that can deliver dangerous shocks if handled.

### Shelter Considerations

Do not sleep directly on the ground. The moss network is more active at night, and extended contact can lead to electrical burns or, more disturbingly, temporary integration into the valley's electrical network—travelers report experiencing the sensory input of the entire moss network, feeling every droplet of moisture, every trace of current. While not harmful, the experience is overwhelming and can trigger lasting psychological effects.

Bring a hammock with rubber-coated tree straps, or a raised platform tent with non-conductive poles. Set up camp away from the old industrial structures—they still cycle through power loads at unpredictable intervals, and the electromagnetic pulses can cause severe headaches, temporary blindness, or corrupted memories (specifically, you may lose the ability to remember certain numbers or lose your sense of cardinal directions for days).

### Timing Your Visit

The valley is most stable during the three weeks following the spring equinox and autumn equinox. During these periods, the electrical activity reaches a temporary equilibrium. Avoid visiting during electrical storms, even if they don't appear to touch down—the entire valley becomes a receiver for atmospheric electricity, and the plants' behavior becomes erratic. They grow visibly during these storms, sometimes trapping travelers who shelter in the wrong places.

Never stay for more than seven consecutive days. The temporal distortions have a cumulative effect. After a week, travelers report losing hours or gaining them, finding their watches off by irregular amounts despite mechanical consistency. Some report aging effects—finding new gray hairs or healed cuts reopening. These effects reverse once you leave, but the transition can be disorienting.

### Specific Hazards and Phenomena

**The Moss Network**: The bioluminescent moss is essentially a living circuit board. Walking on it is generally safe during daylight, but avoid it during the blue-light period just after dawn and before dusk—this is when it's most electrically active. If you must traverse moss-covered areas during these times, move quickly and avoid stopping. The moss can sense stationary objects and will begin growing over your boots within minutes.

**The Hexagonal Trees**: Do not shelter beneath these, despite their inviting appearance. The purple-dark atmosphere under their canopies contains concentrated electrical fields that interfere with human nervous systems. Brief exposure causes tingling and mild euphoria. Extended exposure (more than 20 minutes) can cause temporary paralysis or synaesthetic episodes where you taste colors and see sounds. The clear fluid they sweat is mildly acidic and will degrade synthetic fabrics and irritate exposed skin.

**The Transformer Stations**: These still hold residual charge and should be avoided, especially during humid nights when the static moths congregate. Their St. Elmo's fire displays, while beautiful, can trigger seizures in sensitive individuals. The moths themselves are harmless but disorienting—they're attracted to the electrical activity of human nervous systems and will cluster around your head, creating a localized static field that makes your hair stand on end and can cause mild hallucinations.

**The Lake**: Under no circumstances should you touch the antifreeze-colored water. It's not water in any conventional sense but a complex suspension of biological conductors and industrial runoff that has reached its own equilibrium. The glow from the machinery below is hypnotic—avoid looking directly at it for more than a few seconds. Some travelers report the machinery appearing to move or reconfigure when viewed peripherally, though this may be an optical illusion caused by the water's unusual refractive properties.

### Biological Encounters

The pale salamanders are harmless but should not be handled—their skin secretes a mild neurotoxin that causes temporary numbness. The bioluminescent bacteria in their organs can transfer to human skin, causing you to glow faintly for several days after contact.

If you encounter the deer-like entity, do not approach or make sudden movements. Its nature is unclear—it may be a heavily adapted normal animal, or something else entirely. It seems to perceive electrical fields rather than light, and will not react to visual stimuli but will flee if you generate static electricity (by rubbing fabric, for instance). Its antlers can conduct and focus electrical current from the atmosphere. One traveler reported seeing it during a storm, its antlers glowing with coronal discharge, standing perfectly still as lightning played around it without ever striking directly.

### Data Collection and Documentation

Cameras and recording devices must be heavily shielded in Faraday bags when not in use. Even then, expect corruption of digital media. Film cameras work better, but the local electromagnetic fields will fog film if you're not careful. Many travelers resort to sketching and written notes in pencil (pens leak or stop working due to pressure variations).

If you're collecting samples, use glass or ceramic containers only. The vegetation seems to recognize and react to attempts at collection—moss samples will dissociate into component cells within hours of removal from the valley, and the tree fluid evaporates instantly when removed from the vicinity of its source. The only samples that remain stable are from the non-biological components—fragments of dissolved concrete impregnated with root acids, or water from the storage organs of the plants (though these should be handled with extreme care).

### Psychological Preparation and Aftercare

The valley will change your perception of the boundary between living and non-living systems. Many travelers report a period of adjustment after returning where normal plants seem "dead" or "sleeping" by comparison. Some develop a sensitivity to electrical fields that persists for months. A few report dreams of being part of a vast network, feeling the flow of current and moisture through impossible body configurations.

Keep a detailed journal during your visit, but be prepared for gaps. The temporal distortions affect memory formation. You may find entries in your own handwriting that you don't remember making, or lose entire hours of experience only to have them return in dreams weeks later.

Have a support system ready for your return. The transition back to baseline reality can be jarring. Some travelers report a profound sense of loss, as if they've been disconnected from something essential. Others feel relief but struggle to articulate what they've experienced. The valley exists in a liminal space that human language hasn't evolved to describe.

### Final Preparations

Before entering the valley, leave detailed information about your planned route and return date with someone in baseline reality. The temporal effects mean you cannot rely on your own sense of how much time has passed. Set multiple mechanical alarms to remind you when it's time to leave.

Bring an offering for the valley—not in any supernatural sense, but as a psychological anchor. Some travelers bring old electronics to leave at the industrial sites, where they'll be slowly digested and incorporated. Others bring pure water from their home reality to pour at the valley's edge. This ritual of exchange seems to help with both entering and leaving the valley's influence.

Most importantly, go with clear intentions but flexible expectations. The valley doesn't conform to normal ecological or physical rules. You're not visiting a place so much as entering a process—a slow conversation between the industrial past and a biological future that humans were never meant to witness. Respect this, and the valley will tolerate your presence. Try to control or fully understand it, and you may find yourself becoming another component in its vast, quiet, electrical dreams.

Remember: you are visiting a place where the boundary between organism and environment has dissolved, where machinery dreams and plants compute. Your safe return depends not on conquering this environment but on passing through it without becoming entangled in its patterns. The valley is patient. It has been converting the industrial into the biological for decades, perhaps a century. It can wait much longer than you can.

Stay humble, stay insulated, and above all, don't stay too long.
