# Preparation Guide for the Suspended Ocean

## Critical Physics Anomalies to Understand

Before anything else, you need to internalize that the normal rules don't apply here. The gravitational inversion means "up" and "down" are local suggestions rather than universal constants. Your inner ear will lie to you constantly. The pressure that should exist at 40+ kilometers of depth simply *doesn't*, but your body might still expect it. Some travelers report phantom pressure symptoms - prepare mentally for sensations that have no basis in the actual environment.

## Essential Equipment

**Acoustic Protection System**: Not optional. The sound here isn't just loud - it's omnipresent and multidimensional. You need something that can filter frequencies selectively while still allowing you to hear important environmental cues. Consider a system with:
- Active noise cancellation for the factory drums (roughly 0.5-20 Hz range)
- Selective filtering for the creature communication bands (ultrasonic, but perceived as pressure)
- Emergency full-dampening mode (the harmonic alignments can apparently cause disorientation or worse)
- Bone conduction compensation (the sound will reach you through your skeleton regardless of ear protection)

**Modified Diving Apparatus**: Standard equipment won't work. You need:
- Pressure suit configured for *null* pressure but still maintaining body integrity
- Heating elements focused on core temperature (the cold is described as "persistent" and "seeping" - this suggests it bypasses normal thermal dynamics)
- Propulsion system that works in perfectly still water (no currents to push against)
- Emergency phase-shift capability for entering the dry pockets inside factories

**Navigation Tools**:
- Dead reckoning system (no magnetic fields, no GPS, no stellar navigation)
- Acoustic mapping device calibrated to use the factory rhythms as position markers
- Timer isolated from local temporal effects (bring multiple, compare regularly)
- Range finder that works through liquid medium across vast distances

## Biological Hazards

The creature-rivers are described as "learning" - this suggests intelligence or at least adaptive behavior. Prepare:
- Full body coverage with no gaps larger than microscopic (they emerge through microscopic cracks)
- Bio-neutral external surface (don't give them anything to react to)
- Emergency dispersal system - sonic, chemical, or thermal depending on what intelligence suggests
- Sample containers if you're scientifically inclined, but assume anything you collect might be actively observing you back

## Psychological Preparation

**Temporal Dissonance**: "Motion occurs but progress doesn't" - this will mess with your head. Bring:
- External memory devices to record that things *have* happened even if they feel like they haven't
- Pre-planned activity schedules to force a sense of progression
- Meditation or grounding techniques for when causality feels broken

**Spatial Vertigo**: You'll be swimming through water above a frozen ocean that shouldn't be floating, beneath water droplets the size of cities. Your brain will struggle to create a coherent map. Practice beforehand with:
- Sensory deprivation exercises
- Inverted gravity simulators if you have access
- Meditation in architecturally impossible spaces

**Acoustic Overload**: Even with protection, the sound will be overwhelming. The description mentions it has "texture" and becomes "another medium to move through." Prepare by:
- Training with industrial noise for extended periods
- Learning to navigate by sound alone
- Developing tolerance for subsonic frequencies that you feel rather than hear

## Practical Considerations

**Entry Strategy**: The description doesn't specify how you get in, but with water extending 40km up and ending at a boundary where "gravity gives up," you'll likely need:
- A vehicle capable of transition through physics boundaries
- Backup systems for when your primary equipment stops making sense
- A tethered probe to test the boundary before crossing

**Shelter**: The factory platforms with their dry pockets might be your only refuge. But they're still running automated processes. You'll need:
- Industrial override codes or hacking equipment
- Understanding of centuries-old machinery that's been running without maintenance
- Ability to exist in "air that won't mix with water" (this suggests another physics anomaly)

**Duration Planning**: With time moving strangely, plan for:
- Supplies that last indefinitely (you might subjectively experience weeks in what registers as minutes, or vice versa)
- Multiple redundant methods of tracking objective time
- Pre-agreed extraction signals that don't rely on synchronized timeframes

## Warning Signs to Abort Mission

- If the creature-rivers begin forming persistent patterns around you
- If factory harmonics begin inducing physiological responses
- If you find yourself unable to remember entering the water
- If the industrial rhythms stop (this might indicate a catastrophic change in the broken physics)
- If you observe the ice shelf beginning to crack or shift

## Final Notes

This environment seems trapped in the aftermath of an industrial catastrophe that broke fundamental laws of physics. The beings that built this are dead, but their machines continue. The new life that's emerged seems alien, possibly not even from this dimension originally. You're entering a grave that refuses to acknowledge it's dead, populated by things that shouldn't exist, in a medium that defies every law you understand.

Bring wonder, bring caution, and bring a way home that doesn't depend on local physics working correctly. The beauty of city-sized water droplets orbiting nothing might be worth the journey, but don't forget you're traveling through the aftermath of an apocalypse that froze an ocean in place and inverted gravity itself.

Most importantly: trust nothing, verify everything, and maintain a clear path back to physics you understand.
