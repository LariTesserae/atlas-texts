# Preparation Guide for the Ossuary Plateau

## Critical Warnings

Before anything else, understand this: the Ossuary Plateau is not hostile, but it is *incompatible* with linear human cognition. Many travelers return physically unharmed but temporally confused, sometimes forgetting they've already come back, sometimes remembering visits they haven't made yet. Keep a journal that you update *before* departure with your reasons for going. You'll need to remember why you thought this was important.

## Temporal Stabilization

Your first priority is maintaining causal coherence. The Plateau exists in what xenophysicists call a "temporal eddy"—time moves, but not forward. Think of it like standing in an ocean wave that's constantly breaking but never advances or retreats.

**Essential Equipment:**
- **Chronometer Anchor**: Not a timepiece, but a device that maintains a fixed reference to your origin timeline. Military surplus units from the Verdanian Conflicts work well—they were designed for areas of temporal distortion. Wear it against your skin; the bioelectric field helps it maintain lock.
- **Sequential Memory Aids**: Bring a series of numbered photographs of yourself, taken at different ages. When you feel temporal drift beginning, look at them in order. This helps your consciousness remember its proper sequence.
- **Causal Loop Breakers**: Small ritual actions you perform every hour—tapping a specific rhythm, reciting a particular phrase. If you notice yourself completing the action before starting it, stop immediately and wait for linearization.

## Respiratory Adaptation

The "thin air" of the Plateau isn't actually lacking oxygen—it's lacking *coherence*. The molecules are there but don't always remember to be breathable when you need them.

**Two-Week Pre-Arrival Protocol:**
1. Practice pranayama breathing exercises, particularly breath retention
2. Spend time at conventional high altitudes if possible
3. Most importantly: practice breathing *deliberately*. The automatic breathing reflex becomes unreliable on the Plateau. You need to consciously remind your lungs to function.

**Bring supplemental oxygen**, but know it's only partially effective. The real solution is training your body to extract what it needs from probability rather than certainty.

## Navigation and Movement

The honeycomb passages beneath the surface are not optional—you'll need to use them. The creatures (locals call them "static ferrets" though they're neither static nor ferrets) use these passages constantly, and while they're not aggressive, being caught in a flowing mass of them is psychologically devastating. Travelers report feeling their sense of self dissolving into the collective movement.

**Navigation Strategy:**
- Never enter the passages during what you perceive as "dawn" or "dusk" (though these don't technically exist there). The creatures' movement intensifies during conceptual transitions.
- Carry sound makers that work on mechanical principles—bells, clickers, whistles. Electronics fail, but mechanical vibrations sometimes work, giving you a bubble of space.
- Map the cairns first. They're your only reliable landmarks, even if their positions occasionally shift.
- The bone surface can suddenly become permeable. Test each step with a walking pole made from something that was never alive—synthetic materials or refined metals.

## Psychological Preparation

The silence will break you if you're not ready. It's not quiet—it's anti-sound, an aggressive absence that makes your heartbeat feel like an intrusion. Your brain will start manufacturing phantom noises after about three hours.

**Mental Conditioning:**
- Spend a month practicing meditation in increasingly quiet environments
- Learn to be comfortable with your internal sounds—heartbeat, breathing, digestion
- Bring a small music player with bone-conduction speakers. The direct vibration bypasses the anti-sound field
- Record yourself reading something meaningful—poetry, philosophy, recipes, anything. Your own voice from linear time can anchor you when the silence becomes overwhelming

## Dealing with the Entities

The "static ferrets" are not hostile, but they're not exactly alive either. They exist in a state between states. Touching one is reportedly like touching television static made solid—unpleasant but not harmful unless you're prone to seizures.

**Interaction Protocol:**
- Never follow one into a passage
- If one phases through you (this will happen), don't panic. The sensation of temporary non-existence passes.
- They're attracted to electrical fields. Keep electronics sealed when not in use.
- If you see yourself interacting with one in the future/past, try to fulfill that action when the time comes/came. Breaking these loops causes local reality instabilities.

## The Cairns

These are the most important features for human visitors. Each cairn is a stabilization point where causality works more normally. The three-second tones they emit can be used for navigation and temporal orientation.

**Cairn Protocol:**
- Never dismantle or alter a cairn, no matter how urgent the need seems
- The symbols carved on them should not be copied or photographed directly. Sketch them with deliberate inaccuracy if you must record them.
- When a cairn "sings," count the seconds. If it lasts more or less than three seconds, you're experiencing temporal drift.
- Sleep near cairns when possible, but never touching them. 3-5 meters is optimal distance.

## Essential Supplies

**Physical Needs:**
- Water: Bring twice what you think you need. The dry bone-ground desiccates everything.
- Food: High-calorie, low-moisture. The Plateau seems to accelerate metabolic processes.
- Shelter: A simple tent with no electronics. The more complex the shelter, the more likely it is to exist in multiple states simultaneously.
- Clothing: Layers you can add/remove quickly. Temperature is stable but your perception of it won't be.

**Cognitive Aids:**
- A physical journal and multiple writing implements
- Colored strings or ribbons to mark your path—the bone-ground doesn't hold footprints
- A mirror—seeing your reflection helps maintain self-coherence
- Playing cards or dice—generating randomness helps break the Plateau's deterministic loops

**Emergency Equipment:**
- Flares (chemical only, no electronics)
- A blade made from meteoric iron (terrestrial metals become unreliable)
- Salt—creates temporary zones of normalcy when scattered in circles
- A photograph of someone who loves you—emotional anchors are surprisingly effective against temporal dissolution

## Duration and Timing

Never stay longer than seven days on your first visit. The human psyche needs linear time to maintain cohesion, and after a week, visitors report difficulty distinguishing between memories, experiences, and possibilities.

The "best" time to visit is during your local autumn, though seasons don't exist on the Plateau. Somehow, arriving when your origin point is in transition makes adaptation easier.

## Return Protocol

Leaving is harder than arriving. The Plateau doesn't hold you, but you might hold yourself. The sensation that you've always been there, are still there, will always be there, can be overwhelming.

**Exit Strategy:**
1. Choose your departure point before arrival and mark it clearly
2. For three days before leaving, perform a specific ritual each morning—counting backwards from 100, naming everyone you know, listing your reasons for leaving
3. Walk, don't run, to your exit point
4. Don't look back after you've decided to leave
5. The transition will feel like waking from a dream you can't quite remember having

## Post-Visit Integration

You will need time to readjust to linear causality. Common experiences include:
- Knowing what someone will say before they've said it (usually fades after a week)
- Seeing multiple probable versions of objects (temporary)
- Difficulty with sequential tasks like reading or cooking (practice helps)
- Dreams of the static ferrets (harmless but unsettling)
- Compulsion to build small stacks of objects (generally therapeutic)

Keep your journal from the Plateau separate from your regular life for at least a month. Reading it too soon can trigger disorientation. When you do read it, do so with someone else present—they can confirm which timeline you're in if you become confused.

## Final Considerations

The Ossuary Plateau changes people, but not in ways that are entirely negative. Many travelers report enhanced intuition, a more flexible relationship with time, and a profound understanding that reality is more negotiable than previously believed.

Go with clear intentions but flexible expectations. The Plateau will show you things that don't exist yet, or anymore, or at all. Your job is not to understand but to witness and return.

Remember: the copper taste fades, but the forgetting is voluntary. You can choose to remember everything, but most travelers find it kinder to let some memories remain in the maybe-state where they're most comfortable.

The Plateau is patient. It has already waited for you forever. It can wait a little longer while you prepare.
