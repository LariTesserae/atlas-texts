## Preparation Guide for the Manufacture Chamber

### Critical Warning
This location exists in a state of continuous ontological crisis. Your perception of reality, the distinction between living and mechanical, and your understanding of basic physics will be under constant assault. Many travelers report lasting psychological effects from even brief exposures. Consider this visit carefully.

### Environmental Hazards & Essential Equipment

**Pressure Suit Requirements:**
You'll need a specialized deep-sea suit rated for 30+ atmospheres, but with crucial modifications. Standard suits will fail here—the water's changing properties will corrode seals when it becomes mercury-like, freeze joints during cold-burn phases, and conduct electricity through standard insulation. Commission a custom suit with:
- Phase-adaptive sealing compounds that remain flexible across extreme temperature variations
- Non-conductive inner lining with redundant insulation layers
- Reinforced joints that can withstand rapid crystallization/liquification cycles
- Self-healing outer shell for the inevitable cuts from basalt edges

**Sensory Protection:**
Pack industrial-grade hearing protection that can be worn under your helmet. The sound here doesn't just damage hearing—it penetrates bone, disrupts cardiac rhythm, and can induce seizures when the chamber achieves synchrony. Some travelers recommend temporal bone conduction dampeners, though these are difficult to acquire.

For vision: Bring multiple types of eye protection. Polarized lenses help slightly with the visual static of superpositioned objects. Some travelers report success with stroboscopic glasses synchronized to local phase-shifts, though this requires real-time adjustment. Pack backup analog filters—electronics often fail here.

**Navigation & Movement:**
The chamber's geometry is stable, but your ability to perceive it won't be. Bring:
- Physical rope (not cable—it might become biological)
- Chalk or other primitive marking tools
- A mechanical compass (magnetic ones spin uselessly)
- Tactile navigation aids for when vision becomes unreliable

Learn to move slowly and test each handhold. The basalt edges can slice through most glove materials. Consider titanium mesh outer gloves, though even these will degrade over time.

### Biological & Psychological Preparation

**Medical Prophylaxis:**
Two weeks before arrival, begin a regimen of:
- Calcium channel blockers to protect against electrical discharge
- Antiemetics for the severe nausea induced by reality fluctuations
- Mild dissociatives to prepare your mind for identity confusion

Some experienced travelers undergo controlled exposure to paradoxical stimuli—videos that shift between mechanical and organic imagery, sounds that layer industrial and biological noise. This won't fully prepare you, but might reduce the initial shock.

**Mental Frameworks:**
The most successful visitors report adopting a specific mental stance: Accept that categories will fail you. Don't try to determine if something is "really" a fish or a piston—it's both, always, even when it appears to be one. Practice holding contradictions in your mind without resolution.

Learn to recognize the warning signs of paradox poisoning:
- Sudden certainty that you're mechanical
- Feeling your blood flow in prescribed circuits
- Hearing your heartbeat as industrial rhythm
- Seeing serial numbers in organic patterns

If these symptoms occur, close your eyes, focus on your breathing, and recite memorized organic poetry or multiplication tables—anything purely human and culturally encoded.

### Temporal Considerations

Visit duration is critical. The chamber exists in a roughly 24-minute cycle where its impossibilities peak and ebb. Enter during an ebb phase if possible. Never stay longer than two cycles—pattern recognition starts to break down after 48 minutes, and some travelers report being unable to distinguish themselves from the manufactured creatures.

The local time flow is inconsistent. Bring two mechanical watches; they'll disagree, but the degree of disagreement indicates temporal stress. If they diverge by more than five minutes, exit immediately.

### What to Bring for Documentation

Standard electronics fail, but some analog methods work:
- Wax tablets for notes (paper dissolves; normal tablets become organic)
- Pre-1950s film cameras with mechanical shutters
- Glass sample vials with wax seals (never metal caps)
- Graphite pencils (ink becomes blood, or oil, or both)

### Interaction Protocols

**Do Not:**
- Touch the manufactured-born creatures (you risk integration)
- Attempt to "fix" or understand the machinery
- Stay in one water layer for more than three minutes
- Look directly at the central distortion for extended periods
- Try to follow any single creature/machine through a full cycle

**Safe Observations:**
- The kelp-cables can be studied briefly from a distance
- The barnacle-rivets remain stable longer than most phenomena
- Sound patterns can be recorded if you accept the recordings will be corrupted
- The fresher basalt formations retain normal properties for several minutes after breaking off

### Emergency Protocols

If you begin to experience integration (feeling mechanical, seeing yourself in the production line):
1. Immediately move to a chamber wall
2. Focus on the sharp basalt—its pain is purely physical, purely real
3. Recite your name, birthdate, and a childhood memory
4. Count backwards from 100 in your native language
5. Exit at the first opportunity

Keep a sharp object accessible—many travelers report that minor self-injury (a small cut, a pinch) helps maintain the boundary between self and environment. This is controversial but repeatedly reported as effective.

### Recovery

After leaving, expect:
- 3-5 days of difficulty distinguishing mechanical from organic sounds
- Temporary synesthesia, especially between touch and hearing
- Dreams of being manufactured
- Compulsive checking of your body for serial numbers or seams

Have a trusted friend ready to remind you of your humanity. Avoid machinery and aquariums for at least two weeks. Some travelers benefit from extended time in purely natural settings—forests, deserts, anywhere definitively organic and non-industrial.

### Final Notes

The chamber offers no revelations, no treasures, no conventional rewards. What it provides is an experience of reality's flexibility, a demonstration that the categories we use to parse existence are far more fragile than we imagine. Some travelers return changed in ways they describe as beneficial—more capable of holding paradox, less attached to rigid thinking. Others never fully reassemble their sense of what's real.

The manufactured-born creatures that escape the chamber have never been successfully tracked. They disappear into the ocean's darkness, carrying their impossible nature into the wider world. You might glimpse one leaving as you arrive—a moment of silver scales and copper wire, gone before your mind can decide what it saw.

The ocean above doesn't acknowledge what lies beneath. Three hundred meters of water press down with perfect, natural force, as if nothing impossible churns below. This might be the chamber's cruelest paradox—that something so fundamentally wrong can exist so quietly, manufacturing its broken things in the darkness, disturbing nothing but those who choose to witness it.

Pack your equipment. Test your seals. Practice holding contradictions.

The manufacture continues, waiting.
