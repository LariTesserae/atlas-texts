# Preparation Guide for the Mist-Crowned City

## Critical Warnings

Before anything else, understand this: the city exists in a state of temporal suspension that will affect you psychologically. Many travelers report experiencing "completion anxiety" — an overwhelming urge to finish the half-woven cloth, to harvest the perpetually ripe fruit, to complete the incompletable. Some become trapped in repetitive behaviors, trying to "fix" the stasis. Build mental preparation for witnessing eternal incompletion without intervention.

## Atmospheric and Respiratory Preparation

### Altitude Conditioning
Begin altitude training at least three months before departure. The settlement sits in an extreme elevation zone where even adapted humans would struggle. Your Earth-normal lungs will panic. Practice breathing exercises that emphasize slow, deep inhalation — the mist carries more oxygen than you'd expect, but you need to learn to extract it.

### Moisture Management
The perpetual dampness penetrates everything. Your clothing will never fully dry. Prepare psychologically for constant clamminess — many travelers report this as more disturbing than expected. Pack:
- Multiple base layers of synthetic materials (wool will never dry and will smell of old sheep forever)
- Waterproof document cases for anything you cannot bear to lose
- Anti-chafing salves in abundance
- Consider pre-treating your feet with toughening agents; perpetually damp skin macerates and tears

## Biological Hazards and Adaptations

### The Flora Response
The plants will react to your presence in unsettling ways. The folding leaves will track your movement. The spiral moss grows toward foreign heat signatures — you'll wake to find it beginning to colonize your sleeping bag. It's harmless but psychologically disturbing. The translucent fruit is edible but never satisfying — it provides nutrients but no satiation. Eating it leads to constant hunger without starvation.

### Fauna Interactions
The fur-scaled lizards are drawn to emotional distress. If you panic or grieve, you'll find them gathered around you in silent witness. The elongated rabbits steal small objects but return them to different locations — expect your belongings to migrate. The violet swallows respond to certain sound frequencies; humming at around 256 Hz will cause them to swarm. The unseen garden creatures are recording devices of some kind — they reproduce conversations from previous visitors. Hearing your own words echoed back days later from invisible sources tests sanity.

## Temporal Disorientation Protocols

### Time-Keeping Imperatives
Bring multiple mechanical timepieces. Digital devices fail — not dramatically, but through slow disagreement with each other. The city exists in a temporal fold where duration becomes negotiable. Mark your arrival point physically — pile stones, cut marks into wood (it won't heal like everything else here). Without external reference, you'll lose track of whether you've been there days or decades.

### The Noon Problem
There is no true noon. The sun reaches zenith but shadows don't correspond correctly. This causes severe circadian disruption. Pack:
- Artificial light sources that can simulate a normal day/night cycle
- Melatonin and wake-promoting agents
- A rigid schedule written on waterproof material — follow it regardless of what the environment suggests

## Psychological Preparations

### The Completion Compulsion
You will want to finish things. The half-woven cloth calls like an itch. The tools beg to be used. The water wheels need just a small adjustment to function. DO NOT ENGAGE. Travelers who attempt to "complete" tasks report losing days in fugue states, coming to consciousness with bloodied fingers from trying to weave with broken looms or operate seized mechanisms.

### Isolation Protocol
Though empty, the city feels watched. The sensation of being just behind someone who left the room never fades. Prepare for complex loneliness — you're alone but never private. Many travelers report holding conversations with imagined inhabitants. This is normal but should be monitored. If the imagined inhabitants begin providing new information, leave immediately.

## Practical Considerations

### Navigation
The seven-channeled fountain is your only reliable landmark. The terraced neighborhoods shift — not physically, but in accessibility. Streets that led somewhere yesterday lead nowhere today. Always maintain line-of-sight or sound-contact with the fountain's specific trickle frequency (approximately 472 Hz).

### Shelter
Do not sleep in the buildings. The stone foundations conduct something during the night hours — not electricity, but something that affects dreams. Camp in the transitional zones where cultivation meets wild mountain. The discomfort of raw stone is preferable to the alternative.

### Water Source
The cisterns contain pure water but it tastes of waiting. It's safe but will make you progressively less interested in leaving. Limit consumption to emergency situations. Bring purification equipment for the ice-fed streams at the boundaries.

## Equipment Essentials

### Technical Gear
- Barometric pressure logger (to detect the approaching mist waves before they arrive)
- Non-electronic compass (magnetic north behaves normally here, unlike time)
- Manual wind-up flashlights (batteries drain at unpredictable rates)
- Physical notebooks with waterproof ink
- Analog camera if you must document (digital images corrupt in specific patterns)

### Survival Specifics
- Double your normal food supplies; the eternal fruit provides no satisfaction
- Magnesium supplements (the water leaches minerals from your body)
- Anti-fungal medications (the moisture enables rapid fungal growth on human skin)
- Emergency beacon that doesn't rely on satellites (they're visible but unreachable)

## Cultural Protocols

Though uninhabited, the city demands certain respects:
- Don't attempt to harvest the fruit
- Don't disturb the bird nests
- Don't redirect the water channels
- Don't complete the unfinished work
- Don't leave marks except at your arrival point
- Don't speak loudly in the central plaza (the acoustics create standing waves that persist)

## Departure Planning

The most crucial preparation: decide your exit strategy before arrival. The city encourages lingering. Each day makes leaving feel less urgent. Set a non-negotiable departure time and method. Many travelers recommend bringing a physical alarm or timer that will force action — something that cannot be rationalized away or reset.

Common exit failures:
- "Just one more day to understand the water system"
- "The mist pattern is about to reveal something"
- "The fruit is almost ready to fall"
- "The loom just needs minor adjustment"

These thoughts are the city talking. They're not your thoughts. Leave when planned, regardless of what remains unfinished or unwitnessed.

## Final Notes

The city offers profound beauty and disturbing peace. You'll see impossible colors in the mist-refracted light. You'll hear music in the water channels that no human composed. You'll understand something about permanence that will haunt you. But you must remain a visitor, not a participant. The city has been waiting for completion for a thousand years. It can wait a thousand more. Your job is to witness, not to resolve.

Pack a small item of intense personal meaning — a photograph, a stone from home, a child's toy. When the city's stasis begins affecting your mind, hold this object. Remember that you come from a world where things complete, where fruit falls, where time moves forward. You must return to it.

The mist-crowned city is patient and generous with its impossibilities. It will show you eternity in a handful of moss, infinity in a water channel that neither empties nor fills. But like all places that exist outside normal time, it extracts a price for visiting: the knowledge that somewhere, that impossible city continues, its birds singing the same songs, its fruit never falling, its looms never finishing their patterns, forever and forever, without you.
