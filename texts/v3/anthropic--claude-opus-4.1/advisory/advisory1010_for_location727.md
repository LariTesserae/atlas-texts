## Preparation Guide for Visiting the Drowning Forge

### Understanding the Environment

Before you even consider descending to the Drowning Forge, understand that you're entering a space defined by precarious equilibrium. This is not a tourist destination or a marvel of ancient engineering—it's an active workshop where survival itself is an ongoing project. The builders are still learning how to live here, and every system is pushed to its operational limits. One failed seal, one overtaxed joint, and thirty meters of ocean will reclaim this space in seconds.

### Physical Conditioning

**Breath Control**: Practice holding your breath for at least two minutes. While the airlock system works, you need to be prepared for emergency flooding. The thirty-meter depth means that even with an emergency ascent, you'll need 90 seconds minimum to reach the surface safely without risking decompression sickness.

**Heat Tolerance**: The forge maintains temperatures that can reach 40-45°C (104-113°F) with humidity near 100%. Spend time in saunas or steam rooms to acclimate. You'll be sweating constantly, and dehydration happens faster than you'd expect. The locals have adapted, but you haven't.

**Pressure Equalization**: Practice equalizing your ears and sinuses. The pressure changes during airlock cycling can be painful if you're not prepared. Learn the Valsalva maneuver at minimum.

**Grip Strength**: Everything is wet and slippery. The hand-wheels for the airlock require significant strength to operate against water pressure. Start training with thick rope climbs and wet-bar exercises.

### Essential Equipment

**Clothing**: Bring lightweight, quick-drying garments that breathe but won't become dangerously heavy when wet. Avoid cotton entirely—it will never dry in that environment. Merino wool or synthetic moisture-wicking materials only. Pack at least three changes; everything will be damp within hours.

**Footwear**: Non-slip soles are mandatory. The stone floors are constantly wet from condensation. Consider diving booties with reinforced soles—they'll grip wet stone and protect against the heated floor near the forge.

**Eye Protection**: The combination of bright forge-light, concentrated sunlight through the mirrors, and constant salt spray is brutal on eyes. Bring sealed goggles that won't fog—treat them with anti-fog solution beforehand.

**Ear Protection**: The acoustic environment is physically painful. The hammer strikes resonate through the copper dome, the breathing pipes create a constant drone, and there's nowhere for sound to escape. Bring both earplugs and over-ear protection that can handle moisture. You'll need to remove them to communicate, but prolonged exposure without protection will cause permanent hearing damage.

### Safety Preparations

**Emergency Breathing**: Carry a small emergency air supply—a spare-air cylinder rated for 30+ meters depth. The airlock is the only exit, and if it jams or floods, you need enough air to either fix the problem or make an emergency ascent.

**Waterproof Light**: When the clouds cover the sun, the forge can dim to near-darkness in seconds. A reliable waterproof flashlight is essential. Bring spare batteries in waterproof containers.

**Pressure Gauge**: Carry a simple barometric pressure gauge. The dome groans and flexes with tidal changes and temperature variations. If pressure starts dropping inside, you need to leave immediately—it means a seal is failing.

**First Aid**: Focus on burn treatment supplies. The forge, tools, and even the dome itself can cause severe burns. Include waterproof bandages, burn gel, and antibiotics—infections develop quickly in the humid environment.

### Cultural and Practical Considerations

**Work Ethos**: The inhabitants are not tour guides—they're craftspeople fighting to maintain their workshop against impossible conditions. Don't expect hospitality. Offer to help with the bellows treadwheel or water distillation. Physical contribution earns respect.

**Water Discipline**: Never waste fresh water. The distillation system requires enormous energy to produce even small amounts. Bring water purification tablets as a gesture of goodwill—they'll be deeply appreciated.

**Timing Your Visit**: Check tidal charts and weather patterns. Visit during low tide when pressure stress is minimized. Avoid storm systems—wave action above creates pressure pulses that stress every seal and joint.

**Communication**: The acoustic environment makes normal conversation impossible when work is underway. Learn basic hand signals for "danger," "help," "stop," and "exit." The locals will have developed their own sign language—observe and adapt quickly.

### Technical Knowledge

**Basic Metallurgy**: Understanding what's being forged and why helps you avoid dangerous areas. When certain alloys are being worked, toxic fumes can accumulate despite the ventilation. Know the smell of zinc fumes (sweet, metallic) and get out if you detect it.

**Tidal Mechanics**: The breathing pipes' efficiency varies with tide levels. High tide reduces the air column height, making the forge harder to operate. Expect increased frustration and shorter tempers during these periods.

**Emergency Procedures**: Learn how the airlock operates mechanically. If power to the hand-wheels fails, know how to manually release the pressure equalization valves. Practice operating wheels and valves with wet, slippery hands in darkness.

### Psychological Preparation

**Claustrophobia Management**: The space is small, hot, and surrounded by crushing pressure. The copper dome will creak and groan constantly. If you're prone to claustrophobia, practice meditation or controlled breathing techniques beforehand.

**Constant Vigilance**: You can never fully relax. Every drop of water might signal a leak. Every new sound might indicate structural failure. Maintain awareness without letting anxiety overwhelm you.

**Isolation Acceptance**: The dead zone around the forge means no marine life, no ecosystem—just stressed kelp and dying barnacles. The psychological impact of this artificial bubble of life surrounded by sterile water is more unsettling than you'd expect.

### What to Bring as Trade

**Rubber Gaskets**: Modern synthetic rubber seals would revolutionize their waterproofing. Bring various sizes of O-rings and gasket material.

**Ceramic Bearings**: For the bellows treadwheel and airlock mechanisms. They resist corrosion better than any metal in this environment.

**Concentrated Calories**: The heat burns energy rapidly. Energy bars, dried fruits, nuts—anything that won't spoil in humidity.

**Knowledge**: If you know techniques for underwater construction, metal treatment for salt resistance, or improved ventilation designs, this knowledge is more valuable than any physical goods.

### Final Warnings

The Drowning Forge exists in a state of controlled crisis. Its builders haven't mastered their environment—they're barely surviving it. Every moment there carries the weight of potential catastrophe. The copper dome isn't a monument to human ingenuity; it's a desperate improvisation against an ocean that appeared perhaps within living memory.

When you hear the forge-song through the water as you descend—that muffled thunder of humanity's determination—remember that you're approaching something raw and immediate. This isn't archaeology or tourism. You're visiting people in the middle of an ongoing emergency that has become their daily life, where the difference between workshop and tomb is measured in the integrity of hand-forged rivets and the discipline to never stop maintaining them.

Respect the urgency. Contribute to the work. And always—always—know where the airlock is.
