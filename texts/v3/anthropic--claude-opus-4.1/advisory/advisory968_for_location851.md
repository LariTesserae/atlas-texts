# Preparation Guide for the Breathing City

## Initial Mental Preparation

Before you even begin packing, understand this: you are not visiting a place built *for* humans or even *by* humans in any sense you understand. This city operates on biological logic that will feel wrong to every instinct you possess. Your body will rebel against it in subtle ways—not through danger, but through a deep evolutionary discomfort with an environment that doesn't acknowledge your kind of life as primary.

Spend at least two weeks before departure practicing meditation focused on accepting discomfort without panic. The city will make you feel emotions that aren't yours, and distinguishing between your own feelings and the city's chemical communication will be crucial for maintaining psychological stability.

## Biological Compatibility Modifications

### Essential Pre-treatments

**Respiratory Adaptation**: Schedule appointments with a biomodification specialist at least 6 weeks before travel. You'll need:
- Nasal filter implants that can screen out psychoactive spores while allowing normal breathing
- Lung coating treatment to prevent bacterial film colonization in your airways
- Enhanced liver processing to handle the constant low-level exposure to alien pheromones

**Sensory Dampening**: The city's spore-language will attempt to communicate with your nervous system, causing symptoms ranging from mild confusion to temporal lobe seizures in unprotected humans. Options include:
- Temporary neural dampening patches (replace every 12 hours)
- A modified antihistamine regimen that blocks certain neurotransmitter receptors
- For longer stays: minor surgical adjustment to your blood-brain barrier permeability

### Recommended Enhancements

**Digestive Preparation**: Nothing in the city is toxic per se, but your gut microbiome will be overwhelmed. Start a course of competitive bacterial supplements one month prior—specifically engineered strains that can coexist with but not be overrun by the city's organisms.

**Skin Barrier Upgrade**: The bacterial films will try to colonize you. While not harmful, the sensation of calculation happening on your skin is deeply disturbing. Apply medical-grade silicone barrier cream every morning, focusing on areas where you sweat.

## Equipment and Clothing

### Absolute Essentials

**Breathing Apparatus**: Not for oxygen—the city produces plenty—but for psychological comfort. Bring a small rebreather that you can use when the sensation of breathing the city's exhalations becomes overwhelming. Many travelers report panic attacks from the feeling that the air has been "pre-breathed."

**Non-Porous Clothing**: Everything fabric will be colonized within hours. Wear only:
- Synthetic sealed-weave garments that can be ultrasonic cleaned
- Rubber or composite footwear with no exposed stitching
- A hat with a non-organic surface (the spores particularly enjoy hair)

**Illumination**: The perpetual twilight will affect your circadian rhythms within days. Bring:
- Full-spectrum LED panels that can simulate proper daylight
- UV sanitizing wands (not for safety, but to create small "dead zones" where you can psychologically rest from the omnipresent life)
- Chemical light sticks as backup (electronics may behave unpredictably)

### Navigation and Communication

**Chemical Translators**: Handheld devices that can provide rough interpretations of pheromone messages. Warning: these are only about 30% accurate and often translate emotions rather than concepts.

**Inert Markers**: Bring physical tokens made of materials the city cannot digest—specific ceramics, certain metal alloys. You'll need these to mark locations, as the city's constant growth makes traditional navigation impossible.

**Sealed Journal**: Paper will be consumed. Bring a tablet with excellent sealing, or better yet, a traditional slate and chalk.

## Psychological Supplies

**Anchor Objects**: Items from home that the city cannot alter—a sealed photograph, a piece of polished stone, a metal coin. You'll need these when the alienness becomes overwhelming, which it will, usually around day 3.

**Mood Stabilizers**: Even with neural protection, you'll experience emotional bleed-through. Bring pharmaceutical mood regulation, but avoid anything that affects serotonin—it interferes with necessary threat detection.

**Exit Strategy**: Have a clear, practiced mental protocol for when you need to leave immediately. The city's influence is subtle and cumulative. Many visitors don't realize they're being psychologically incorporated until they find themselves reluctant to leave.

## Practical Considerations

### Accommodation

There are no hotels. The city doesn't understand the concept of temporary occupancy. You'll need to:
- Bring a sealed shelter pod that can resist colonization for at least two weeks
- Set it up in one of the "sparse zones" between buildings
- Move it every 3-4 days before the city decides to grow around it
- Never sleep directly on any surface that's part of the city

### Water and Food

**Water**: The city's water is technically potable but contains information. Drinking it means participating in the city's communication network at a level your consciousness can't process but your subconscious will try to. Bring:
- Minimum 2 liters per day in sealed containers
- Purification tablets that work on biological contaminants
- Avoid the rain entirely—it's how the city feeds

**Food**: You can eat the glowing cyan produce, but be prepared for:
- Mild phosphorescence in your waste for weeks afterward
- Temporary synesthesia (taste triggering colors, sounds having flavors)
- Dreams that feel like memories from perspectives that aren't human

Better to bring:
- Sealed rations for your entire stay
- Extra calories—your body will burn more trying to maintain homeostasis
- Salt tablets (you'll sweat more than you expect from the stress)

### Time Management

The city's fifteen-minute pulse cycle will interfere with your time perception. Bring multiple timekeeping devices:
- Mechanical watch (less affected by the biological fields)
- Chemical timer strips that change color at set intervals
- Pre-recorded audio cues to remind you of the actual passage of time

Plan to accomplish only 1/3 of what you'd normally expect in a day. The cognitive load of existing in this space is exhausting.

## Social/Cultural Navigation

### The Inhabitants

You won't see them, but they'll be aware of you. The post-human entities that tend this city exist partially in the bacterial films, partially in the building-organisms themselves. Communication attempts should be:
- Chemical rather than verbal (scent markers, pheromone simulators)
- Patient—responses might take hours or days
- Non-sequential—they don't experience time linearly

Never try to enter the nursery towers or the waste processing gardens. These are considered analogous to internal organs—entering would be like crawling inside someone's stomach.

### Behavioral Guidelines

**Movement**: Move slowly and predictably. Sudden movements trigger defensive responses from the buildings—usually just increased spore production, but occasionally rapid growth that can trap you.

**Waste**: The city will try to process anything you discard. Only leave items you're comfortable having dissolved and incorporated. This includes:
- No batteries or electronics
- No medications
- Absolutely no genetic material you're not willing to have analyzed and possibly replicated

**Documentation**: Photography seems to agitate the photosynthetic membranes. Sketch instead, or use recording devices that work in infrared or ultraviolet.

## Warning Signs to Leave Immediately

- You start understanding the spore-language without your translator
- Your skin develops a faint luminescence that persists after washing
- You feel homesick for the city when you're in your sealed shelter
- Your dreams include perspectives from inside the buildings
- You can predict the membrane pulse cycles
- The translucent geckos stop avoiding you
- You realize you haven't checked your timepiece in over a day
- Your emotional responses to the pheromone zones intensify rather than diminish

## Recovery and Decontamination

After leaving, expect:
- 2-3 weeks of respiratory adjustment as your lungs clear the bacterial films
- Temporary depression as your nervous system readjusts to the absence of chemical communication
- Hypervigilance around ordinary plants and buildings
- Possible beneficial effects: enhanced immune function, improved air quality sensitivity, occasional moments of inexplicable peace that might be residual city-song

Schedule a full medical examination within 48 hours of return. The city doesn't mean harm, but it also doesn't understand that you're meant to be separate from it.

## Final Thoughts

This is not adventure tourism. The breathing city represents a form of life so divergent from baseline human experience that simply existing within it is a form of translation between incompatible realities. You will not conquer this place or master it. At best, you'll achieve a temporary, uneasy coexistence.

Go with humility. Go with preparation. Go with the understanding that you're visiting not just another place but another way of being alive.

And whatever else you do, don't go alone. You'll need someone to remind you what human feels like when the city starts making too much sense.
