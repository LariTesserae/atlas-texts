## Preparation Guide for the Ice-Lock Factory Maintenance Tunnels

### Environmental Hazards & Physical Preparation

**Temperature Management**
The tunnels hover just above freezing with dramatic thermal variations. You'll need a sophisticated layering system:
- **Base layer**: Moisture-wicking synthetic that can handle constant dampness without losing insulation properties
- **Mid-layer**: Something that breathes but insulates even when wet—avoid down completely
- **Outer shell**: Fully waterproof but breathable. The constant dripping and condensation will soak through anything less
- **Spare base layers**: Pack at least two changes in waterproof bags. The humidity will defeat any clothing eventually

Prepare for the heat exchanger zones by having layers you can quickly remove and secure. The temperature swings from near-freezing to sauna-like can happen within steps. Your body won't adapt naturally—these transitions are too sudden and localized.

**Footwear Crisis**
This is perhaps your biggest challenge. You need:
- Boots with aggressive tread for the perforated metal walkways (they'll be slick with algae and condensation)
- Completely waterproof construction for the flooding zones
- Ankle support for the severe angles and irregular surfaces
- Steel toes recommended—industrial equipment and falling ice are constant threats
- Consider magnetic sole attachments for the metal walkways
- Bring neoprene socks as backup when (not if) water breaches your boots

### Respiratory & Health Concerns

**Air Quality**
The atmosphere is a cocktail of:
- Coolant vapor (potentially toxic depending on local chemistry)
- Rust particles
- Algae spores
- Industrial moisture that will coat your lungs

Bring:
- Industrial respirator with multiple filter types
- Backup filters (they'll clog quickly in the humid environment)
- Antihistamines for the inevitable mold/algae reaction
- Expectorant medication—your body will produce excessive mucus trying to clear the contaminants

**Pressure Changes**
The ventilation cycles will repeatedly pop your ears. If you have any sinus issues or inner ear problems, consider:
- Pressure-equalizing earplugs
- Decongestants
- Anti-nausea medication (the pressure changes combined with strange acoustics can trigger vertigo)

### Navigation & Orientation

**Getting Lost is Inevitable**
These tunnels follow efficiency, not human logic. Prepare:
- Industrial-grade GPS (though signal will be intermittent at best)
- Physical mapping materials in waterproof cases
- Chalk or biodegradable marking paint to track your route
- Inertial navigation backup—step counter and compass minimum
- Study industrial ventilation patterns beforehand—air flow might be your most reliable directional guide

**Vertical Navigation**
Some maintenance shafts plunge at severe angles. Bring:
- Climbing harness
- Static rope (dynamic will stretch too much on the long descents)
- Ascenders and descenders rated for industrial use
- Backup headlamps with red-light options (preserve night vision in the darker sections)

### Biological Hazards

**The Maintenance Rats**
These aren't pets. They're semi-feral industrial tools with:
- Titanium teeth that can chew through standard protective equipment
- Heat-sensing capabilities (they'll know you're coming)
- Unknown behavioral patterns after decades of breeding

Precautions:
- Thick leather or synthetic guards for ankles and wrists
- Ultrasonic deterrent (may or may not work on engineered creatures)
- Food should be in metal containers—they can smell and chew through plastic
- Never corner one near the boilers (their preferred nesting sites)

**Algae and Moss**
The biofilm on surfaces isn't just slippery—it's an active ecosystem:
- Some species may be engineered for industrial purposes (breaking down certain compounds)
- Wear gloves at all times—contact dermatitis is common
- The bioluminescent safety strips might attract or repel certain organisms unpredictably
- Don't trust any surface that looks dry—spore release can be triggered by pressure

### Acoustic & Psychological Preparation

**Sound Assault**
The constant industrial noise will be:
- Physically exhausting (your body will tense against it)
- Disorienting (direction becomes meaningless when sound comes from everywhere)
- Psychologically oppressive after hours of exposure

Bring:
- Electronic earplugs that can filter frequencies while allowing communication
- White noise generator for sleep (paradoxically, adding controlled noise helps)
- Download meditation apps that work with industrial soundscapes
- Prepare mentally for the seventeen-minute pressure valve cycle—it becomes maddening

### Equipment & Supplies

**Water Paradox**
You're surrounded by water but none of it is safe to drink:
- Industrial runoff
- Coolant contamination  
- Biological growth
- Unknown mineral content from permafrost

Bring:
- More water than you think you need in metal containers
- High-end purification system (UV, chemical, and mechanical filtration)
- Electrolyte supplements (you'll sweat constantly despite the cold)

**Power & Light**
The bioluminescent strips provide baseline visibility but:
- Carry multiple headlamps with different spectrum options
- The green bio-lighting will destroy your color perception—bring full-spectrum LED for equipment inspection
- Backup batteries in waterproof, insulated cases (cold and moisture kill batteries)
- Glow sticks for emergency marking
- Consider NVGs for the truly dark sections between machine halls

**Emergency Equipment**
- Atmospheric monitor (oxygen levels vary, carbon monoxide pockets exist near older machinery)
- Emergency bivvy rated for wet conditions
- Flares (both for signaling and for testing air flow)
- Industrial first aid kit with extra supplies for:
  - Chemical burns
  - Hypothermia
  - Infected wounds (everything down here is septic)
  - Crush injuries

### Special Considerations

**The Old Sections**
If you encounter pre-war excavations:
- Do not trust any infrastructure
- Assume different safety standards (or none)
- Watch for obsolete hazard markings in dead languages
- The air might be decades old in sealed sections—test before breathing

**The Self-Reinforcing Polymer**
Those grown support beams:
- May react to your body heat
- Could interpret sudden movement as stress
- Might grow around you if you remain still too long
- Don't lean against them—the petroleum secretions will compromise your equipment

**Timing Your Visit**
- Avoid shift changes unless you want human contact
- The maintenance cycles are predictable—learn them
- Some sections may flood periodically—know the pump schedules
- The seventeen-minute pressure release is your timing metronome

### Mental Preparation

This environment will challenge your sanity:
- The scale makes you insignificant
- The repetition becomes hypnotic
- The isolation is complete even when others are near
- The machinery's indifference is existentially confronting

Prepare by:
- Practicing meditation in uncomfortable environments
- Training yourself to find patterns in chaos
- Accepting that you'll never fully understand the system
- Embracing the industrial sublime—beauty in pure function

### Final Notes

The tunnels are essentially a hostile alien environment that happens to be human-built. They were never meant for continuous human presence, only brief maintenance visits. You're not exploring—you're infiltrating a vast organism that tolerates but doesn't welcome you.

Respect the machinery. It was here before you and will outlast you. The tunnels don't care about your comfort, your schedule, or your survival. They exist to move heat and water, to maintain pressure differentials, to sustain the factories above. You are, at best, a temporary blockage in the flow—something to be routed around or flushed out.

Pack out everything. Leave nothing behind. The maintenance rats incorporate any organic material into their nests. Your presence should be a ghost in the data logs, a brief temperature spike in a thermal sensor, nothing more.

And remember: when the pumps stop, leave immediately. Silence in the tunnels means the water table is winning.
