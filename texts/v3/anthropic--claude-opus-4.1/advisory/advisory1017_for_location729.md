## Preparation Guide for the Furnace Garden

### Critical Warnings

The Furnace Garden is not a casual destination. The environment actively resists human presence through chemistry, heat, and biology. Many who descend unprepared either never return or emerge fundamentally changed—lungs scarred, minds rattled by subsonic frequencies, or worse, *infected* by the petroleum-based ecosystem trying to colonize new territory.

### Environmental Hazards & Protection

**Respiratory System**
Your lungs were designed for oxygen and water vapor, not petroleum distillates and metallic aerosols. You'll need:
- A multi-stage breathing apparatus with activated charcoal filters specifically rated for hydrocarbon vapors. Standard dust masks will dissolve.
- Backup chemical oxygen generators—the air pockets between vegetation clusters can be hypoxic
- Throat protection: coat your esophagus with food-grade mineral oil before descending. The metallic vapor burns mucous membranes on contact.

**Temperature Management**
The heat isn't uniform—it pools and flows like invisible liquid. You'll experience everything from 45°C ambient temperature to blast-furnace pockets near active oil seeps reaching 80°C+.
- Wear loose, layered clothing that can trap insulating air while allowing sweat evaporation
- Natural fibers only—synthetics will melt and fuse to skin
- Cooling vests using endothermic chemical packs, NOT water-based systems (water evaporates too quickly and the steam will scald)
- Prepare for heat exhaustion: pre-hydrate for 48 hours, taking salt tablets every 4 hours before descent

**Auditory Protection & Consequences**
The sound-songs aren't just noise—they're biological communication using your bones as resonating chambers.
- Custom-molded earplugs with specific frequency dampeners for 15-40 Hz range
- A chest plate of dense wood or ceramic to prevent infrasonic waves from disrupting your heart rhythm
- Accept that you'll experience temporary hearing damage. Some travelers report permanent tinnitus that sounds like distant clicking.

### Navigation & Movement

**Never trust your spatial sense.** The oil-haze creates false distances, the echolocation choruses interfere with your inner ear, and the phosphorescent fungi pulse at a frequency that disrupts depth perception.

- Bring physical guide ropes, minimum 100 meters, marked every 2 meters
- Chalk won't work (oils dissolve it)—use scratches on metal tags
- Map the major tar-bamboo towers as landmarks; they're the only stable reference points
- Time your movements between the seed pod explosions. They fire on roughly 20-minute cycles during peak heat hours.

**The Walkways**
The fungal planks support approximately 80kg reliably. If you weigh more, or carry heavy equipment:
- Test each plank with a pole before committing weight
- Bring portable bridging equipment—metal grating that can span gaps when planks fail
- Never grab the crimson vines for support. Their oils cause severe chemical burns and they sometimes constrict reflexively.

### Chemical & Biological Precautions

**Skin Protection**
Every surface sweats petroleum products in various states of refinement.
- Cover all skin with thick barrier cream containing zinc oxide and lanolin
- Wear leather or thick canvas gloves treated with paraffin wax
- Your boots will dissolve. Bring three pairs minimum, rotating them as the soles degrade.

**Contamination Prevention**
The spores and oils will try to colonize you. Some travelers report fungal growths appearing weeks after visiting.
- Strip completely before leaving, sealing all clothes in airtight containers for burning
- Scrub with pumice and kerosene (ironically, fighting oil with oil)
- Undergo UV treatment for any persistent phosphorescent patches on skin
- Monitor your body temperature for 30 days after—petroleum-based infections run 2-3 degrees hotter than normal human fever

### Psychological Preparation

The Furnace Garden attacks the mind subtly:
- The constant clicking becomes hypnotic. Set timers to remind yourself to leave.
- The patterns in the vegetation trigger pareidolia—you'll see intentions and designs that may or may not exist. Document everything but trust nothing.
- The isolation between growth clusters creates profound anxiety. Your brain expects predators in those empty spaces. There might be some.
- Some travelers report feeling "invited deeper" by the garden. Resist this urge absolutely.

### Timing Your Visit

- Enter only during the city's weight-shift hours (usually dawn) when the fissure is widest
- Never stay longer than 3 hours—heat exhaustion accelerates exponentially after that
- Avoid the monthly oil-surge when underground pressures force fresh petroleum through the seeps
- Monitor the tar-bamboo resonance—when they achieve harmonic convergence, leave immediately. No one knows what they're summoning, but the primitive tools suggest someone learned the hard way.

### What to Bring for Trade/Offering

The current tender (whoever they are) appreciates:
- Glass containers with tight seals for collecting specific oils
- Metal files for harvesting crystallized tar segments
- Fresh leather for mask-making
- Any books about petroleum chemistry—they leave notes in the margins

### Recovery Protocol

After emerging:
1. Expect 24-48 hours of disorientation as your equilibrium resets
2. Drink massive amounts of water with electrolytes
3. Sleep in a cool, quiet place—your nervous system needs to forget the sound-songs
4. Don't be alarmed by petroleum-scented sweat for up to a week
5. If you develop cravings for motor oil or feel compelled to return immediately, seek help

### Final Warnings

- Never investigate the deeper structures you might glimpse through the haze
- Don't attempt to kill or significantly damage the vegetation—it retaliates collectively
- If you hear the plants suddenly go silent, run. They only stop singing when something worse is moving through.
- The empty spaces between growth clusters aren't actually empty. Treat them as hostile territory.
- Whatever cultivation project the garden is undertaking, you don't want to be there for the harvest.

### Why Visit At All?

Despite everything, the Furnace Garden offers:
- Unique biochemical compounds impossible to synthesize elsewhere
- Adhesives that function at extreme temperatures
- Spores that, properly prepared, have powerful medicinal properties
- A humbling reminder that life finds ways to exist that human science considers impossible
- Evidence that perhaps we're not the only intelligence cultivating the underground spaces of the world

Pack light, move carefully, trust nothing, and remember—you're not exploring a garden. You're negotiating with an alien ecosystem that tolerates your presence only as long as you're useful to its unknowable purpose.
