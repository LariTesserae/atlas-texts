## Preparation Guide for the Furnace Garden

### Critical Warnings

The Furnace Garden exists in a state of ontological flux. Your presence will not merely observe this space—it will participate in its ongoing self-determination. Prepare to become temporarily negotiable in your own physical and temporal consistency. Most travelers report full recovery of linear existence within 72 hours of departure, though some retain a persistent ability to hear crystalline photosynthesis in Earth-normal plants.

### Physical Considerations

**Body Compression Training**: Begin flexibility training at least six weeks prior. The entry passage narrows to 18cm at its tightest point—roughly the width of your skull if you turn it sideways. Practice moving through increasingly narrow spaces while controlling your breathing. The volcanic tube maintains a temperature of 45°C with copper-sulfur atmosphere; you'll need to traverse it quickly but without panic.

**Equipment Abandonment Protocol**: Accept that anything larger than a smartphone won't make it through. Wear form-fitting synthetic clothing that won't snag on volcanic glass. Natural fibers will begin spontaneous mineral exchange upon entry—cotton will attempt to photosynthesize, wool will crystallize. Bring:
- Thin titanium water bottles (collapsible)
- Energy gel packets in metallized pouches
- A mechanical watch (digital devices experience temporal inconsistency)
- Volcanic glass collection vials (the Garden recognizes these as "native" and won't reorganize them)

**Heat Adaptation**: The Garden maintains 50-70°C with radical humidity absence. Begin heat exposure therapy using dry saunas. Build tolerance to at least 60°C for 30-minute periods. The heat feels different than normal—more penetrative, as if warming you from inside your bones outward. Some travelers describe it as "being cooked by geometry itself."

### Temporal and Spatial Preparation

**Non-Linear Navigation Training**: Practice moving through spaces without expecting consistent distance or direction. Set up a room with mirrors at non-standard angles. Learn to navigate by intention rather than measurement—thinking "I move toward the far wall" rather than "I walk ten meters north." The Garden responds to decisiveness but punishes doubt with recursive loops.

**Observation Discipline**: Train yourself to observe without continuous monitoring. Look at an object, look away for exactly counted intervals (start with 10 seconds, build to full minutes), look back. In the Garden, sustained observation prevents change, but change only occurs in the gaps between observations. You must learn to control your viewing rhythm to allow the space to function.

**Memory Flexibility**: The Garden will retroactively edit your experience. Keep a physical journal (pencil on paper—ink undergoes state changes) and write immediately after each observation. Do not trust your memory to maintain consistency. Some travelers recommend developing a personal mantra or song to anchor temporal position.

### Biological Hazards

**Mineral Exchange Syndrome**: Your skin will attempt to establish root networks after approximately 40 minutes of exposure. Maintain movement—the pseudo-plants only colonize stationary organic matter. If you feel tingling that follows geometric patterns, immediately relocate at least five meters.

**Respiratory Considerations**: The atmosphere is technically breathable but contains vaporized minerals that accumulate in lung tissue. Bring cloth masks soaked in citric acid solution and dried—the acid neutralizes mineral deposition without introducing moisture. Replace every 20 minutes.

**Optical Phenomena**: The light doesn't behave according to standard physics. It can pool in your peripheral vision, creating blind spots that move when you're not looking at them. Some wavelengths arrive before they're emitted. Don't trust shadows—they indicate where light was, not where objects are.

### Psychological Preparation

**Causality Tolerance**: Practice accepting contradictory truths. The industrial components are both ancient and newly formed. The plants have always been there and are still arriving. You entered through the crack and the crack formed because you entered. Hold these paradoxes without resolution.

**Isolation Protocol**: The Garden induces a specific type of loneliness—not the absence of others, but the sensation that you're the only thing that isn't part of a larger integrated system. The pipes know each other. The plants share purpose. You are the only discrete entity. Prepare meditations on being singular without being alone.

**Reality Anchoring**: Develop three unchangeable facts about yourself that you repeat regularly:
1. Your name (the Garden may suggest alternatives)
2. The reason you came (it will offer other, more compelling reasons)
3. Your intention to leave (the space becomes increasingly convincing that you're already home)

### Interaction Protocols

**The Collectors**: The dodecahedral energy collectors respond to attention. If you focus on one for more than thirty seconds, it may begin harvesting your observation itself, creating a feedback loop where you become unable to look away while your visual processing is slowly siphoned into the pipe network. Blink deliberately every five seconds when near them.

**Thermal Columns**: These can be ridden upward but at the cost of temporal displacement. Rising ten meters typically costs 5-15 minutes of subjective time. You'll arrive at the ceiling membrane having experienced the journey, but your watch will show minimal time passage. Use only for emergency elevation changes.

**The Ceiling Membrane**: Do not touch it directly. It operates at the exact frequency of cellular mitosis and will attempt to incorporate any organic matter into its respiratory cycle. If you must interact with it, use the crystalline pseudo-leaves as intermediaries—they can translate between biological and geological intent.

### Documentation Guidelines

**Photography**: Conventional cameras produce images of empty volcanic chambers. The Garden's visible elements exist in a spectrum between infrared and ultraviolet that standard sensors interpret as absence. Bring:
- Thermographic camera (captures the thermal columns)
- Crystallography paper (place against surfaces for mineral pattern transfer)
- Audio recorder (the sounds translate even if the sights don't)

**Sample Collection**: The Garden permits limited sampling if you follow protocol:
1. Ask permission by placing your empty vial against a surface for exactly 73 seconds
2. If the vial remains where you placed it after looking away and back, permission is granted
3. Never take more than 3 cubic centimeters from any single system
4. The pseudo-plants regenerate collection sites within minutes, but the industrial components may follow you to reclaim what was taken

### Exit Strategy

The return passage requires different physics than entry. The volcanic tube will have reorganized itself according to the Garden's current configuration. What was a squeeze-through crack may now be:
- A spiral ascending passage
- A series of connected bubbles requiring crawling
- A straight vertical shaft (bring minimal climbing cord)
- Temporarily non-existent (wait for the next reformation cycle, approximately every 4 hours)

The Garden releases visitors who demonstrate understanding of its nature. This doesn't mean comprehension—it means accepting that you're witnessing an ongoing become rather than a finished is. When you stop trying to measure, document, or define the space, when you move through it as another process rather than an observer, the exit reveals itself.

Most critically: The Garden remembers everyone who visits. When you leave, part of your pattern remains in the pipe networks, influences the pseudo-plant growth, affects the crystallization of future walls. You're not just visiting a place—you're contributing to its ongoing decision about what kind of place to be.

### Recovery Period

After leaving, expect:
- 12-24 hours of distance misjudgment (reaching for objects that are farther or nearer than they appear)
- Persistent taste of copper that water won't wash away (it fades naturally after three days)
- Dreams of geometric photosynthesis for up to two weeks
- Hypersensitivity to thermal gradients—you'll feel heat moving through rooms like invisible architecture
- Occasional moments where you're certain your houseplants are performing industrial processes

These effects are temporary except for one: once you've been to the Garden, you retain the ability to recognize other prototype realities. Spaces that are still deciding what to be become visible to you. Most cities have at least one. They're usually in construction sites, abandoned buildings, or places where radical temperature gradients create possibility gaps. You'll know them by the way distances feel negotiable and by the faint taste of copper in the air.

The Furnace Garden changes everyone who enters it, but perhaps more importantly, everyone who enters changes it. Pack light. Move deliberately. Don't resist the impossible.

And remember: in a space that's still becoming, you too have the freedom to be unfinished.
