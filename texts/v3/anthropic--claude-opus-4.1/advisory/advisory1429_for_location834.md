## Preparation Guide for the Clicking Galleries

### Critical Warning
This is an extreme environment requiring extensive preparation. The Clicking Galleries represent a unique hazard profile combining industrial dangers, biological threats, and severe psychological stressors. Do not attempt this journey without complete preparation.

### Environmental Protection

**Respiratory System**
- Industrial-grade respirator with copper particulate filters (minimum P100/HEPA rating)
- Backup filter cartridges (bring 3x expected need - the metallic dust accumulation is severe)
- Supplemental oxygen system recommended - the air is technically breathable but the copper taste/content may cause metal fume fever with prolonged exposure
- Consider a full-face mask to protect mucous membranes from the metallic atmosphere

**Hearing Protection**
- Military-grade electronic earmuffs with minimum NRR 30dB rating
- Secondary foam earplugs underneath (double protection mandatory)
- The clicking reaches 90-110 dB constantly with peaks up to 130 dB during mass feeding events
- Bring communication headsets that can function over the noise floor
- Consider bone conduction units as backup

**Body Protection**
- Full-body puncture-resistant suit (the beetles don't attack humans but crushing/stepping incidents are inevitable)
- Kevlar or thick leather minimum, preferably with hard shell segments at joints
- The beetles' mandibles can bore through stone - assume they can penetrate standard clothing if accidentally provoked
- Sealed seams essential to prevent beetle intrusion
- Magnetic/metallic materials may attract unwanted attention from the beetles

### Navigation & Mobility

**Lighting**
- Do NOT rely on the phosphorescence for navigation - it creates dangerous optical illusions
- Powerful headlamp with red filter option (white light may affect beetle behavior unpredictably)
- Backup flashlights (minimum 3)
- Chemical light sticks as emergency markers
- UV light to check for beetle trail patterns (they fluoresce differently than the walls)

**Navigation Tools**
- Traditional compass will not function (magnetic interference from metal deposits)
- Gyroscopic compass or INS (Inertial Navigation System) required
- Physical marking system - chalk won't work on the walls, bring steel cable and pitons
- Detailed 3D mapping equipment if available
- Always maintain physical line back to entrance when possible
- The overlapping levels make vertical position tracking critical

**Movement Equipment**
- Reinforced boots with steel shanks and toe guards
- Crampon attachments for navigating beetle-covered surfaces
- Collapsible ladder for vertical passages
- Rope system rated for the corrosive environment
- Knee and elbow pads (you'll need to crawl through beetle masses)

### Biological Hazards

**Beetle Management**
- Ultrasonic deterrent devices (effectiveness uncertain but worth trying)
- Physical barriers - bring expandable mesh screens to create beetle-free rest zones
- Heavy work gloves with metal mesh lining for handling/moving beetles if necessary
- First aid kit specialized for puncture wounds and crushing injuries
- Antihistamines in case of allergic reaction to beetle secretions

**Contamination Prevention**
- Full decontamination protocol before leaving - the beetles may attach eggs or larvae
- Inspection mirror for checking suit integrity
- Sealed sample containers if collecting specimens
- Do not allow beetles to taste your equipment - they may attempt to incorporate it

### Psychological Preparation

**Mental Health**
- This environment induces severe claustrophobia, sensory overload, and dissociation
- Practice meditation techniques for managing overwhelming stimuli
- Bring anxiolytics if prone to panic attacks
- Maximum recommended exposure: 4 hours initial visit, 8 hours for experienced travelers
- The eternal unchanging nature of the environment can trigger existential distress
- Have a clear extraction plan with defined abort criteria

**Documentation**
- Cameras must be sealed and shock-resistant
- Audio recording equipment will be overwhelmed - bring directional microphones
- Written notes in waterproof notebook (the steam from factory nodes)
- Sample collection supplies for beetles and mineral specimens

### Survival Supplies

**Sustenance**
- Water in metal containers (plastic may degrade from the copper-rich environment)
- Minimum 2 liters per 4-hour period (the heat and dry air are dehydrating)
- High-calorie food bars (you'll burn energy from the stress and difficult movement)
- Electrolyte supplements to counter mineral imbalance from the environment

**Emergency Equipment**
- Satellite emergency beacon won't work underground - bring hardline communication if possible
- Flare gun for signaling in large chambers
- Emergency bivvy sack rated for industrial environments
- Chemical heat packs (the warmth is oppressive but hypothermia is still possible if injured)
- Tourniquets and hemostatic agents for severe beetle-related injuries

### Special Considerations

**Factory Nodes**
- Do not interfere with the machinery - the beetles may respond aggressively to disruption
- Steam vents can cause severe burns - approach carefully
- The fog reduces visibility to near zero - rope team protocols mandatory
- Metal components in the fog may be hot enough to burn through gloves

**Timing Your Visit**
- There's no day/night cycle but beetle activity has subtle patterns
- Every 37 hours there's a 20-minute reduction in clicking (feeding pause) - easier navigation
- Avoid the monthly "reorganization" when beetles redistribute between chambers
- Monitor for signs of new bore-hole creation (increased dust, rhythmic vibrations)

### Exit Protocol
1. Begin exit with 40% reserves remaining (energy, supplies, mental stamina)
2. Follow your marked path exactly - new passages may have opened
3. Check thoroughly for attached beetles before ascending
4. Decompress psychologically before returning to normal environments
5. Medical examination recommended - check for copper toxicity, hearing damage

### Final Warning
The Clicking Galleries represent a unique form of industrial-biological purgatory. The environment is neither actively hostile nor benign - it simply exists in its eternal cycle, indifferent to human presence. Many travelers report lasting psychological effects from exposure to the endless purposeless activity. The beetle collective's behavior patterns may begin to seem meaningful after prolonged exposure - this is a dangerous delusion. Maintain objectivity and remember: you are visiting a dead system that happens to still be running. Do not search for meaning in the patterns. There is none.
