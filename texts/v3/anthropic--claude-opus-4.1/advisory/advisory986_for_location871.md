## Preparation Guide for the Foundry Wells

### Physical Conditioning & Health

**Heat Acclimatization** (Begin 2-3 weeks before)
Start taking progressively longer hot baths or sauna sessions daily. The Foundry Wells maintain a crushing, wet heat that will overwhelm an unprepared body. Work up to spending 45 minutes in steam room conditions. Practice breathing exercises in humid air—the thickness there can trigger panic in the unprepared. Your body needs to learn to sweat efficiently and your mind needs to accept the suffocating quality of the air.

**Tetanus & Prophylactic Medicine**
Update all vaccinations, particularly tetanus. Begin a preventive course of antibiotics—the combination of rust, rat populations, and open water in warm conditions creates perfect bacterial breeding grounds. Pack antifungals; spores will find any scratch or weakness in your skin. Consider rabies pre-exposure prophylaxis given the massive rodent population.

**Respiratory Preparation**
The air quality will be abysmal—metal particles, mold spores, steam carrying mineral deposits. Practice breathing through industrial respirators for extended periods. Your mask will fog constantly; learn to navigate with severely limited vision. Bring multiple filters; they'll clog faster than you expect.

### Equipment & Clothing

**Base Layer Philosophy**
Forget cotton entirely. You need synthetic moisture-wicking materials that won't become sodden dead weight. Bring three complete changes minimum—you'll soak through one set within an hour. The challenge isn't staying dry (impossible) but managing the wetness. Quick-dry hiking pants, not shorts—the rats are bold and metal edges are everywhere.

**Footwear is Everything**
Steel-toed boots with aggressive treading, absolutely waterproof up to mid-calf. The grating is always slick, always warm, sometimes hot enough to damage regular soles. Bring neoprene socks as backup—your feet will be wet regardless, but these prevent the worst of the maceration. Test your boots with oil on wet metal beforehand; that's the surface you'll encounter constantly.

**Critical Gear List:**
- Industrial work gloves, multiple pairs (everything you touch is hot, sharp, or contaminated)
- Headlamp with backup (hands-free essential), plus spare batteries in waterproof cases
- Knee pads—you'll need to crawl through maintenance passages
- Carabiners and 30 feet of static rope (navigation between levels isn't always obvious)
- Waterproof chalk for marking your path—GPS won't work, and the sameness of the chambers is disorienting
- Emergency whistle on lanyard (the ambient noise swallows voices)
- Thermometer to check pipe temperatures before touching
- Small crowbar (for stuck hatches and rat deterrent)

### Environmental Navigation

**Understanding the Rat Rivers**
The rats move in predictable patterns that you must learn to read. They avoid certain temperatures and follow thermal gradients. Watch for their highways—worn paths along pipes and ledges. Never block these routes; they'll swarm over you rather than around. Bring a ultrasonic deterrent but don't rely on it—these rats are partially deaf from generations in the machine noise.

**Water Wisdom**
Never trust standing water, no matter how clear. The thermal runoff pools contain industrial chemicals and biological hazards. Bring more water than you think you need—3 liters minimum. The heat will dehydrate you faster than desert conditions because you can't escape it. Add electrolyte supplements; you'll sweat out salts alarmingly fast.

**Reading the Machines**
Learn to identify live steam pipes (paint blistered off, constant vibration, clean of rat droppings) versus dead ones (rust patterns, rats nesting inside, cool to approach). The analog gauges still function—red zones mean active pressure, stay clear. Some valves turn easily because someone maintains them; these are landmarks for navigation.

### Psychological Preparation

**Accepting the Oppression**
The environment attacks every sense simultaneously. Practice meditation in uncomfortable conditions—the mental challenge exceeds the physical. The constant rat movement in peripheral vision triggers deep evolutionary alarms. You must override the urge to flee. The sounds never stop; bring earplugs but know they only dampen, never silence.

**Time Distortion Management**
Wear a watch with timer functions. The sameness of amber emergency lighting, the constant sounds, the lack of natural light markers—time becomes elastic. Set alarms every 30 minutes to maintain temporal awareness. The environment encourages a dangerous dreamlike state where hours pass unnoticed.

### Practical Protocols

**Entry and Exit Strategy**
Never enter without informing someone topside. The maintenance crews work irregular schedules; you could be trapped by locked hatches. Learn at least two exit routes—elevators fail, passages flood. Mark your entry point obsessively; the return journey is harder than the descent.

**What to Expect, Hour by Hour:**
- First 15 minutes: Overwhelming sensory assault, urge to retreat
- 15-45 minutes: Acclimatization, sweat soaking through everything
- 45-90 minutes: Dangerous comfort as your body adapts (peak accident risk)
- 90+ minutes: Heat exhaustion symptoms begin—monitor yourself carefully

**Emergency Scenarios to Drill:**
- Rat swarm response: Back against wall, make yourself large, move slowly toward exit
- Steam burst: Drop immediately, crawl below steam level
- Equipment failure in darkness: Can you navigate 100 feet by touch alone?
- Heat exhaustion: Recognition symptoms, self-treatment protocol

### Cultural Sensitivity

Remember: This is someone's workplace. The skeleton maintenance crew has unwritten rules about which areas are theirs. Look for recent footprints in the grime, fresh cigarette butts, tools left deliberately as markers. Respect these boundaries. They know you're there—tourists aren't unprecedented, just unwelcome. A small offering (good cigarettes, fresh coffee in a thermos left at a station) shows respect.

### Final Preparations

The night before, sleep in a hot room. Eat a salt-heavy meal. Hydrate aggressively. Set your gear out to dry after testing it in the shower. Accept that you'll return exhausted, filthy, and changed. The Foundry Wells aren't hostile—they're indifferent, which is worse. You're entering a place where humans are barely tolerated guests in a world we created but no longer control.

Most importantly: Know your exit criteria. Decide beforehand what would make you leave—specific temperature, exhaustion level, equipment failure. The environment erodes judgment. When you hit your predetermined limit, leave immediately. The Wells are patient. They've been becoming for decades. They'll outlast your curiosity.
