# Preparation Guide for the Dissolved Boundary Realm

## Critical Pre-Departure Preparations

### Temporal Anchoring
Before entering this realm, you'll need to establish a strong temporal signature. Visit a clockmaker and have them create a mechanical timepiece specifically for you - not just purchased, but commissioned and built while you wait. Watch every gear being placed. This creates a temporal weight that will help you maintain linear experience in the time-pools. Wear this watch on a chain around your neck, never on your wrist where the lattice might notice it too easily.

Spend the three days before departure practicing "temporal breathing" - consciously experiencing each moment as it passes, naming the seconds aloud: "This is now. This is now. This is still now." This builds resistance to temporal pooling.

### Cognitive Preparation
The lattice responds to observation and thought-frequency. You'll need to train your mind to think "sideways" - practice holding contradictory thoughts simultaneously without resolving them. Spend hours looking at optical illusions, especially those that flip between two states. Learn to see both states at once without choosing. The Necker cube is your friend. So is Schrödinger's cat, but as a meditation, not a thought experiment.

Install mental "circuit breakers" through meditation - predetermined thought-patterns you can default to when reality becomes too fluid. Simple counting sequences work well, but count in prime numbers only. The irregularity keeps you conscious while the mathematics provides structure.

### Physical Modifications
Your inner ear will betray you here. Three weeks before departure, begin sleeping with your head at different angles each night - 15 degrees, 30 degrees, 45 degrees off horizontal. This prepares your vestibular system for gravity that doesn't point consistently downward.

Have a piercing made in your left earlobe exactly 17 days before departure (to match the seventeen-second rain inversions). Insert a small weight made of osmium - the densest stable element. This creates a gravity reference point your body can trust when the droplets rise.

## Equipment and Supplies

### Modified Breathing Apparatus
Standard oxygen won't help you here - you need equipment that can process "probability atmosphere." Commission a breathing mask with filters made from sheets of mica interlayed with strips of mirror. The mica allows possibility to pass through while the mirrors reflect certainty back at you. Breathe in through your nose, out through your mouth, never the reverse.

Carry backup filters made from pressed butterfly wings - specifically Morpho butterflies, whose wings create color through structure, not pigment. These can process the spaces between photons when the plants' dark photosynthesis affects local oxygen levels.

### Protective Clothing
Wear nothing that has a single, defined color. Everything should be iridescent, color-shifting, or patterned with interference patterns. This prevents the fog from "writing" on you - if it can't determine your surface, it can't inscribe itself.

Your boots need soles made of two materials: aerogel (for walking on the moss without triggering feeding responses) and a thin layer of gallium (which melts at body temperature, creating a constantly shifting interface with the ground). The combination keeps you from leaving tracks that exist in multiple timeframes simultaneously.

Bring a cloak made of soap bubble film stabilized with glycerin - yes, this is possible with the right equipment. It should be renewed every four hours. The film allows you to pass through sound-waves without being compressed, and its natural iridescence confuses the lattice's probability sensors.

### Navigation Tools
Forget compasses - magnetic north is a democracy here, not a dictatorship. Instead, bring a set of tuning forks in frequencies that correspond to prime numbers between 100Hz and 1000Hz. The lattice can't fully resonate with prime frequencies, creating "dead zones" you can use for orientation.

Carry spools of spider silk - actual spider silk, not synthetic. Anchor one end at your entry point and unspool as you go. The silk exists in few enough dimensions that it won't get tangled in the non-Euclidean curves. Mark every 100 meters with a drop of your own saliva - biological markers maintain consistency even when time pools.

### Documentation Equipment
Standard cameras will only capture what isn't there. Bring a pinhole camera made from a box of lead-lined wood, using photographic plates coated with silver halide mixed with powdered moldavite (meteoric glass). Exposure times should be exactly 17 seconds to sync with the inversion columns.

For notes, use paper made from mushroom mycelium and write with ink made from your own blood mixed with iron gall. This combination remains stable across probability states. Write everything three times - once forward, once backward, once in a spiral from the center outward. At least one version will remain readable.

## Behavioral Protocols

### Movement Patterns
Never walk in straight lines. The moss interprets linear movement as aggression or hunger. Instead, move in gentle arcs, like a knight on a chessboard but with curves instead of angles. Every seventh step, pause and step backward once - this confuses the tracking of never-formed creatures and prevents their possibilities from converging on you.

When you encounter rising droplets, don't dodge them. Let them pass through your space. Attempting to avoid them creates probability wake that can destabilize local reality. If one trembles near you, hum at 432Hz - this frequency calms the equations.

### Interaction with Phenomena
When the moss begins feeding (you'll feel it in your teeth first), immediately sit down and place both palms flat on the ground. Hum deep in your throat - not a melody, just a constant drone. This identifies you as "geology" rather than "prey." The feeding will pass over you.

If you must cross a time-pool, throw a handful of salt mixed with iron filings into it first. This temporarily crystallizes the temporal flow, giving you about 30 seconds to cross before it liquifies again. Don't look at your reflection - it might be from tomorrow, and seeing your future self in this place creates paradoxes that attract unwanted attention from the lattice.

When sound-waves approach, turn perpendicular to their direction and breathe out slowly as they pass. This creates a pressure null that prevents compression. If waves are converging on your position, quickly trace a Möbius strip in the air with your finger - the topology confuses the intersection and causes the inversion column to form elsewhere.

### Mental Hygiene
Every hour, perform a "reality check": Count your fingers (you should have ten). Recite your name backward. Remember the taste of vanilla. These anchor points prevent consciousness drift.

If you start to see the lattice too clearly, immediately close your eyes and think about soup. Any soup. The mundanity disrupts the mathematical resonance. Don't think about the concept of soup - think about specific soup, with chunks of vegetables and steam rising.

Never try to understand the five-dimensional ferns. Your brain will attempt to process them and might succeed - this is not desirable. If you find yourself beginning to comprehend their geometry, immediately recite nursery rhymes while hopping on one foot. The cognitive dissonance breaks the understanding before it solidifies.

## Emergency Procedures

If the mercury sky begins dripping more frequently than one drop per minute, you're approaching a reality fracture. Back away slowly while humming ascending musical scales. Don't complete the scales - stop one note before resolution.

If you see through to the "somewhere else," don't look directly at it. Observe it only in your peripheral vision while thinking aggressively about taxes or dental appointments - maximum mundane thoughts to reinforce your reality anchor.

Should you accidentally trigger a feeding frenzy in the moss (the burgundy will darken to almost black), immediately strip to your undergarments and roll in the fog. The moisture will mask your biological signature. Remain still for at least twenty minutes after the feeding sounds stop.

If your reflection in a time-pool speaks to you, do not respond verbally. Instead, tap your teeth together in Morse code. Your future/past self will understand but the temporal paradox won't fully form.

## Duration and Exit Strategy

Stay no longer than 17 hours (matching the rain inversions). Your entry point will remain stable for exactly this duration. Mark it with a circle of that osmium from your earring - scrape a few grains off and arrange them in a perfect circle. The density differential creates a beacon you can sense even through the fog.

Begin your return journey when you notice the fog starting to form letters spontaneously - this means it's begun to recognize you as a conscious observer. Once the fog knows you're there, the environment becomes increasingly responsive to your thoughts, creating a feedback loop that can trap you in probability states.

Exit backward through your entry point, eyes closed, reciting prime numbers. This prevents you from observing your own departure, which could create a quantum lock.

## Post-Visit Recovery

Expect three days of temporal jet lag - you'll experience moments out of sequence. Eat meals in reverse order (dessert, main, appetizer) to help your chronology resettle.

Your depth perception will be compromised for a week. Don't drive. Don't pour hot liquids. Have someone else cut your food.

You may continue to see movement in your peripheral vision for up to a month. This is residual possibility-creature observation. It's harmless but disconcerting. Wearing glasses with side-shields helps.

The taste of copper and ozone will persist for exactly 17 days. Eating foods high in zinc helps neutralize this.

## Final Critical Warning

The moss remembers visitors. If you return, it will recognize you, and recognition changes the rules. Second visits require entirely different preparation. The place learns, adapts, and evolves its impossibilities specifically for each consciousness that passes through.

Pack light. Think sideways. Breathe carefully. And whatever you do, don't try to take any of the moss with you - it exists in too many dimensions to survive in worlds with conventional physics, and its death creates probability cavitation that can collapse local spacetime.

Remember: You're not visiting a place. You're visiting a state of being where place is negotiable.
