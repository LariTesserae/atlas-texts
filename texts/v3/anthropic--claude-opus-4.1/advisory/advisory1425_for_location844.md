# Preparation Guide for the Nascent Peaks

## Critical Warning
This is not a tourist destination. The Nascent Peaks represent one of the most fundamentally unstable environments a human traveler can encounter. The location exists in a state of constant ontological flux—the very rules that govern reality are still forming. Prepare for an experience that will challenge your understanding of what it means to exist in a physical space.

## Physical Preparation

### Environmental Suit Requirements
You will need a fully autonomous environmental suit—not merely for protection, but as a stable reality anchor. Your suit must:
- Maintain its own localized physics field (minimum 2-meter radius)
- Include redundant life support that doesn't rely on external chemistry
- Feature adaptive structural reinforcement capable of withstanding sudden phase transitions in surrounding matter
- Incorporate emergency solidification protocols for when the ground beneath you decides to become liquid or gaseous

The suit's weight should be variable-compensated, as local gravity may fluctuate or simply forget to exist momentarily. Include magnetic anchoring systems—they won't always work, but when metallic plasma currents are nearby, they may be your only means of maintaining position.

### Vision and Navigation
Standard vision will be intermittently useless. Equip yourself with:
- Vibration-sensing displays that can construct spatial maps from sound/tremor data
- Thermal imaging (though "heat" here doesn't follow conventional thermodynamics)
- Particle-detection overlays to track the metallic plasma currents
- Dead-reckoning navigation systems (GPS is meaningless where geography reshapes itself)
- Physical markers—bring several hundred small, heavy beacons you can drop. Even if the landscape changes, their relative positions might help you maintain some sense of direction

During the profound darkness periods, do not rely on external light sources. They will not merely fail to illuminate—the darkness here actively unmakes light. Your suit should have internal heads-up displays that project information directly to your retinas.

### Breathing and Metabolism
Pack oxygen for at least three times your planned duration. The thin atmosphere isn't merely low in oxygen—it may not contain any recognizable gases at all. Your suit must be completely sealed. Bring chemical CO2 scrubbers rather than relying on any system that might need to exchange with the external environment.

Food and water must be completely self-contained. Do not attempt to process anything from this environment—the chemistry here operates on principles that could be toxic, inert, or simply incompatible with biological processes.

## Psychological Preparation

### Mental Conditioning
Spend at least two weeks before departure practicing existence in unstable environments. VR simulations, while inadequate, can help prepare you for:
- Surfaces that change texture and state while you're touching them
- The absence of consistent directional orientation
- Visual phenomena that your brain cannot properly process
- The deep wrongness of being in a place where physics hasn't decided what it wants to be

### Cognitive Anchoring Techniques
Develop a strong internal narrative before you arrive. The human mind needs consistency, and this place offers none. Create mental anchors:
- Memorize poems or songs you can recite to maintain temporal awareness
- Practice counting exercises to maintain numerical consistency when the external world won't
- Prepare a series of simple, verifiable facts about yourself you can review when reality feels negotiable

### Isolation Protocols
You will be profoundly alone here. This isn't merely solitude—it's existence in a place that doesn't yet understand what life is. The patterns that might be proto-life will not provide companionship. They may, in fact, be disturbing to observe as they attempt and fail to achieve coherence. Prepare for a loneliness that goes beyond the mere absence of other humans.

## Temporal and Durational Concerns

### Mission Duration
Do not plan to stay longer than 72 hours on your first visit. The human psyche needs regular contact with stable physics. Longer exposure to the Nascent Peaks' uncertainty can lead to:
- Temporal displacement anxiety (losing sense of causality)
- Existential dissolution (forgetting the boundaries of self)
- Physics dysphoria (expecting unstable rules when you return to normal space)

### Time-Keeping
Bring multiple redundant time-keeping devices, all internally powered and synchronized before departure. Local time may not flow consistently. You might experience:
- Moments that stretch or compress
- Causality loops where effects precede causes
- Temporal dead zones where duration becomes meaningless

Mark time actively—make recordings every hour (by your suit's clock) describing what you observe. This creates a temporal anchor and helps maintain psychological continuity.

## Movement and Exploration Strategy

### Locomotion
Walking will be unreliable. The ground may:
- Suddenly liquify
- Grow upward, carrying you with it
- Phase-shift to a different state of matter
- Simply cease to exist momentarily

Prepare for multiple movement modes:
- Standard walking (when possible)
- Climbing gear for sudden vertical transitions
- Controlled falling techniques for when gravity shifts
- Magnetic grapples for anchoring to metal structures
- Emergency jetpack for when the ground becomes completely unreliable

Never move quickly. Every step should be tested. Develop a rhythm: probe, test, commit, reassess.

### Safe Zones
The geometric anomalies—the spheres, the proto-towers—often represent slightly more stable regions. While not truly stable, they seem to resist the constant flux slightly better than raw landscape. Use them as temporary refuges, but never trust them completely. The sphere hovering seven meters up might suddenly decide that seven is wrong and adjust without warning.

### Recording and Documentation
Document everything, but prepare for your recordings to be strange upon review. The camera may capture things your eyes didn't see, or miss things that seemed vivid in person. Multiple recording devices using different methods (visual, thermal, vibration, electromagnetic) will give you the best chance of creating a coherent record.

## Specific Hazards and Responses

### The Growing Stone
When you feel the mountain beginning to grow beneath you:
1. Immediately activate magnetic anchors if near metal
2. Prepare for rapid altitude change
3. Monitor suit pressure carefully
4. Be ready to leap to adjacent formations
5. Never resist the growth—move with it until you can safely separate

### Plasma Current Encounters
The metallic plasma streams are not necessarily harmful but are completely alien. If one approaches:
1. Do not touch, even in your suit
2. Observe its movement patterns—they may be trying to communicate
3. Back away slowly—sudden movements might trigger unpredictable responses
4. Document everything—these might be the closest things to "life" here

### Aurora Events
When the purple-green auroras form:
1. Use this brief light to rapidly survey your surroundings
2. Update your mental map
3. Look for changes since the last illumination
4. Prepare for the profound darkness that follows
5. Never stare directly at them for extended periods—they may affect your visual cortex in unexpected ways

### The Darkness Periods
When absolute darkness comes:
1. Stop moving unless absolutely necessary
2. Switch to vibration-based navigation only
3. Focus on internal suit displays
4. Maintain verbal contact with your recording devices (hearing your own voice helps maintain sanity)
5. Count slowly to maintain temporal awareness
6. Remember: this darkness is not empty—it actively unmakes light

## Emergency Procedures

### Reality Anchor Failure
If your suit's physics field begins failing:
1. Immediately begin return journey
2. Do not remove any part of your suit, regardless of discomfort
3. Move toward the most stable-seeming structure
4. Prepare emergency beacon (though rescue is unlikely)
5. Document final observations for future travelers

### Psychological Break
If you begin to feel your sense of self dissolving:
1. Recite your prepared anchor phrases
2. Review your personal facts
3. Look at photos or videos from stable reality
4. Begin immediate evacuation
5. Do not attempt to "understand" or "merge with" the environment

### Landscape Catastrophic Shift
If the entire visible area begins rapid phase transition:
1. Activate emergency jetpack
2. Gain altitude immediately
3. Look for any stable structures
4. If none visible, attempt to ride out the change in mid-air
5. Prepare for hard landing when things stabilize

## Return Protocols

### Pre-Departure Checklist
Before leaving the Nascent Peaks:
1. Ensure all recording devices are secured and sealed
2. Check suit integrity multiple times
3. Document your exact status and observations
4. Prepare for transition back to stable physics
5. Take final readings of local conditions

### Decompression Requirements
After returning, you will need:
- Minimum 48 hours in a controlled, stable environment
- Medical examination for unusual cellular changes
- Psychological evaluation for reality dysphoria
- Careful review of all recordings with trained analysts
- Gradual reintroduction to normal physical activities

Do not be alarmed if, for several days after return, you:
- Expect surfaces to change when you touch them
- Feel suspicious of apparently solid objects
- Experience dreams of growing mountains
- Hear phantom sounds of stone being born

## Final Considerations

The Nascent Peaks offer a chance to witness reality in formation, to observe a universe still deciding its rules. This is profoundly valuable for understanding the nature of existence itself. But remember: you are not equipped to survive in undefined physics. You are a tourist in a place that doesn't yet know what tourists are, in a landscape that hasn't decided if landscapes should be solid.

Respect the instability. Document what you can. And always, always remember that your continued existence depends on the small bubble of stable reality you carry with you. The Nascent Peaks are not hostile—they simply haven't learned what it means to host life yet.

Travel with humility, observe with wonder, and leave before the wrongness of it all begins to feel right. The peaks will still be growing when you're gone, still becoming whatever they're meant to become, still writing the rules of their own existence in stone that can't decide what stone should be.
