# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Drowned Works

## Critical Pre-Arrival Understanding

Before you even consider entering The Drowned Works, understand this: you are preparing to visit an active industrial site that happens to be underwater, not a flooded ruin. The machinery's indifference to your presence will be absolute. One mistimed movement near a piston complex, and the pressure wave alone could rupture your organs through your wetsuit. This place does not forgive ignorance of its rhythms.

## Essential Equipment

### Primary Life Support
- **Industrial-grade rebreather system** with minimum 8-hour capacity. The Works is vast beyond conventional dive planning. Standard SCUBA will not suffice—the percussion waves from the pistons can cause catastrophic bubble formation in compressed air systems.
- **Trimix gas blends** calculated for 40-meter operational depth with extended bottom time. Bring triple redundancy. The nearest surface might be hours away horizontally.
- **Heated drysuit with puncture-resistant overlay**. The constant 9°C temperature will incapacitate you through exposure long before your air runs out. The suit must withstand incidental contact with moving machinery and the rays' occasional territorial tail-strikes.

### Navigation and Timing Systems
- **Pressure-hardened industrial chronometer** synchronized to the factory's cycle. Every piece of machinery here operates on interlocking schedules. Missing a piston firing by two seconds could be fatal.
- **Acoustic mapping device** calibrated for industrial interference. GPS is useless here. The water conducts the factory's sound in ways that will disorient your natural navigation.
- **Download the rhythm maps** if you can find them on the grey markets. Seventeen-second piston cycles, forty-three-minute sector rotations, six-hour maintenance windows—memorize them like your life depends on it, because it does.

### Protection Systems
- **Mechanical pressure gauge** separate from electronics. The octopi have learned to manipulate electronic sensors. Trust analog backups.
- **Vibration dampening layer** worn under your suit. The industrial heartbeat will exhaust you quickly without protection. Long-term exposure causes cellular damage.
- **Emergency pressure shelter**—a collapsible titanium frame with pressure-resistant fabric. When a maintenance cycle triggers and entire sectors reconfigure, you need a safe space to wait it out.

## Pre-Training Requirements

### Physical Conditioning
Spend at least three months training in cold-water industrial environments. Practice swimming against artificial currents for hours. Your body needs to internalize the rhythm of moving through spaces where water itself becomes a percussive weapon. Train with weighted gear in simulation chambers that replicate compression waves.

### Mechanical Familiarization
Study industrial automation systems, particularly mid-21st century manufacturing protocols. The Drowned Works operates on logic that predates adaptive AI but incorporates learning systems. Understanding how to read control panels through murky water, how to identify safe zones in machinery cycles, how to recognize when a sector is about to undergo maintenance—these skills are non-negotiable.

### Biological Hazards
The modified rays are territorial and have learned to use the pressure waves for hunting. Study their movement patterns—they travel in precise formations through the concrete channels. The octopi are generally non-aggressive but will defend their control boxes. Never reach into any panel or relay box without checking for residents first.

## Entry Protocol

### Timing Your Arrival
Enter only during the monthly maintenance cycle when certain sectors power down. This 6-hour window is your safest insertion point. The submarine loading bays on the northeastern edge offer the most controlled entry—their automated systems still cycle properly, and the pressure locks function.

### Initial Orientation
Your first hour should be spent stationary, letting your body adjust to the percussion waves. Find a pressure shadow—a zone behind large machinery where the waves cancel out. Time your breathing to the seventeen-second piston cycle. Fighting the rhythm will exhaust you.

### Movement Strategies
Follow the rays' highways through the concrete channels. These creatures have evolved perfect timing—when they move, it's safe to move. When they shelter, you shelter. Never cross open water during a piston firing. The compression wave in unobstructed water can cause immediate unconsciousness.

## Survival Protocols

### The Three-Dimensional Problem
Remember that this factory was built in full 3D. What looks like a ceiling might be another floor. Maintain constant awareness of your actual depth and orientation. The clarity of the water creates dangerous illusions—distances are much greater than they appear.

### Thermal Management
The warm water columns rising from heat exchangers are tempting rest spots but they're biological highways. Predators hunt these thermals. If you must use them for warming, stay at the edges and watch for hunting patterns above and below.

### Sound Discipline
Your equipment will add new sounds to the environment. The fauna has adapted to the factory's rhythm—any irregular noise marks you as an anomaly. Move with the ambient sound, time your equipment adjustments to coincide with the loudest machinery cycles.

## Emergency Procedures

### Pressure Wave Impact
If caught in a direct pressure wave, immediately exhale and go limp. Fighting it will cause tissue damage. Let it pass through you, then check all equipment seals.

### Machinery Entanglement
If caught in moving machinery, do not struggle. The emergency stops still function—the octopi use them regularly. Look for the red manual override levers; they're positioned every fifty meters along the production lines.

### Lost in the Works
If you become disoriented, follow the fish. The cleaners always move toward intake grilles, which lead to maintenance shafts. The builders' nests are always in dead spaces—safe zones where you can rest and reorient.

## Psychological Preparation

The Drowned Works will challenge your perception of what ruins should be. This is not a dead place slowly being reclaimed by nature—it's a functioning ecosystem where biology and machinery have merged into something unprecedented. The sound will burrow into your consciousness. The rhythm becomes hypnotic, almost religious in its persistence. Some visitors report dream-like states after extended exposure, speaking of understanding the factory's "purpose" without being able to articulate it.

Prepare for the existential weight of swimming through a place that continues its function without regard for the catastrophe that transformed it. The factory does not care that it drowned. It adapted and continued. There's a profound indifference here that some find deeply unsettling—or oddly comforting.

## What to Document

If you're one of the few civilians to gain access, the scientific community desperately needs:
- Behavioral documentation of the rays' evolution
- Samples of the coating protecting the machinery
- Readings from the deeper production levels
- Any evidence of what's being manufactured now
- Communication patterns between the octopi colonies

## Final Warnings

Do not attempt to reach the seventh level down where the cooling systems originate. No one who's descended that far has returned with coherent reports. The pressure isn't the problem—it's something about the rhythm down there, the way the sound converges.

Do not interfere with the spider-legged welding robots. They've been observed dismantling things that impede their construction, including dive equipment with people still in it.

Do not stay past your planned exit time. The Works has a way of making time feel negotiable. Trust your chronometer over your sense of duration.

Remember: you are visiting a place that has transcended catastrophe through pure mechanical persistence. Respect its rhythms, learn its patterns, and you might survive to witness something unprecedented—evolution in fast-forward, industry without purpose, the birth of an entirely new kind of ecosystem.

The Drowned Works does not welcome visitors. It simply continues, and you must learn to move within its continuation.
