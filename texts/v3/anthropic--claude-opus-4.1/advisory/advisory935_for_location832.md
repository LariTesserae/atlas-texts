## Preparation Guide for Visiting the Hollowing

### Critical Pre-Departure Preparations

**Temporal Conditioning**
Begin at least two weeks before departure. Practice existing in rooms with broken clocks. Set multiple timers to go off at irregular, overlapping intervals, then ignore them completely. Learn to move through your day without referencing time at all—eat when formless, sleep when indefinite. The Hollowing's temporal eddies will be less disorienting if you've already loosened your grip on sequential experience.

**Moisture Preservation**
The desiccation is not merely uncomfortable—it's existential. Ordinary hydration won't suffice. Have oils injected subdermally at a reputable modification clinic, creating a secondary moisture barrier beneath your skin. Coat your lungs with specialized polymer films that can be absorbed over 72 hours. Fill hollow teeth with water reserves. Some travelers have success with artificial gill slits that circulate their own blood moisture, though this requires surgical preparation months in advance.

Pack redundant moisture delivery systems: standard water (which will evaporate faster than you can drink it), gelatin cubes that dissolve slowly in the mouth, and emergency moisture reclamation suits that can extract and recycle water from your own exhaled breath. Expect to lose 15% of your body weight in water during even a brief visit.

**Cognitive Flexibility Training**
The architecture responds to expectation. Practice walking through familiar spaces while actively believing they are arranged differently. Use VR programs that randomly reorganize room layouts while you navigate them. Learn to hold multiple contradictory maps in your mind simultaneously without choosing between them. The city will be less likely to trap you in recursive loops if you can avoid imposing rigid spatial expectations.

### Essential Equipment

**Modified Breathing Apparatus**
Not for oxygen—the air is breathable—but for adding moisture and warmth to each breath. The absence of heat isn't just cold; it's an active negation that can damage lung tissue. Build redundancy into your breathing system. When one component begins to "forget" its function (a common occurrence), you'll need backups.

**Non-Linear Navigation Tools**
Traditional compasses, GPS devices, and maps are worse than useless—they'll actively confuse the city's geometry. Instead, bring:
- A set of numbered tokens to drop as breadcrumbs (though be prepared for them to rearrange themselves)
- A journal for sketching the relationships between spaces rather than their positions
- A tuning fork that resonates with the twice-daily city chord—this can serve as your only reliable temporal marker
- Polarized lenses that filter out architectural recursion (looking at infinite doorways with naked eyes can cause permanent perception damage)

**Thermal Generation Systems**
Not heating devices—those simply fail. You need equipment that actively generates the *concept* of warmth. Thermodynamic prayer wheels worn as pendants work well, as do small entropy pumps that create localized bubbles of normal physics. Without these, your body temperature won't drop in the traditional sense—it will simply become increasingly theoretical.

**Documentation Equipment**
Standard cameras produce images of absence. Modified equipment that captures negative space and probability fields will serve better. Many visitors have success with:
- Crystalline recording matrices that store impressions rather than images
- Notebooks made from materials that resist conceptual erosion
- Memory palace training to store experiences in mental architecture (though beware—the city may begin renovating your memories according to its own logic)

### Biological Preparations

**Metabolic Adjustment**
Start eating progressively less solid food in the weeks before arrival. Train your digestive system to extract nutrients from increasingly theoretical meals. Some experienced travelers subsist entirely on meal replacement pills and the memory of flavor for several days before entering the Hollowing.

**Sensory Calibration**
The phosphorescent twilight and shadowless gray luminescence can cause severe disorientation. Spend time in rooms lit only by bioluminescent organisms. Practice navigating spaces where light comes from everywhere and nowhere. Your eyes need to learn to see without conventional light-object relationships.

**Psychological Fortification**
The city's continuous unbecoming can trigger existential crisis in unprepared visitors. Meditation on impermanence helps, but more practical is developing what psychonauts call "coherence anchors"—unshakeable facts about yourself that resist erosion. Your name, repeated like a mantra. A childhood memory so vivid it can't be edited. A physical scar with a story attached. These will help you maintain selfhood when the environment begins suggesting you might be optional.

### Interaction Protocols

**The Living Buttresses**
These architectural creatures are generally harmless but should be treated with respect. If one approaches the building you're in, exit immediately—they only provide support where it's urgently needed, and you don't want to be inside a structure that requires emergency stabilization. Their grinding bell-sounds can be interpreted as a simple communication system: rising tones mean structural safety, falling tones suggest immediate evacuation.

**The Geometric Mice**
Don't look directly at them. Don't look away from them either. Peripheral acknowledgment is safest. They seem to feed on the edges of things—the boundaries between wall and air, between thought and action. If they begin gathering around you, you're becoming insufficiently defined. Sharpen your edges by reciting multiplication tables or listing concrete nouns.

**The Inverse Cathedral**
Approach only during the twice-daily resonance periods. At other times, its negative space can begin inverting visitors—not turning them inside out physically, but existentially, making their absence more real than their presence. If you must enter, move backwards and think forward. Speak in negatives: "I am not not here." The prayer wheels inside generate a stabilizing hum, but prolonged exposure to their paradox engines can cause permanent alterations to your relationship with causality.

### Navigation Strategies

**Following the Streets**
Never commit to expecting a particular path. Hold your destination lightly in mind, like a suggestion rather than a goal. The streets respond better to "I might enjoy finding the salt crystal gardens" than "I must reach the salt crystal gardens." If you find yourself in a recursive loop, change your internal narrative about where you're going, and the city will usually release you.

**Vertical Movement**
The hollow buildings can be entered, but ascending them requires abandoning conventional ideas about "up." Think of vertical movement as a negotiation with gravity rather than a conquest of it. The structures' internal geometries often include stairs that go up while taking you down, elevators that move sideways through time, and ladders that lead to where you already are but from a different angle.

**District Recognition**
Each area has its own subtle rule variations. In the Recursive Quarter, accept that you will meet yourself coming back—it's polite to nod but don't engage in conversation. The Probability Gardens require you to commit fully to each path choice; hesitation causes the paths to superimpose, and you might arrive at multiple destinations simultaneously. The Memory Markets (where past events are traded like solid objects) should be avoided unless you're willing to mortgage your own experiences.

### Sustenance and Shelter

**Food and Water**
Pack everything you need. Nothing edible grows in the Hollowing, and the molecular uncertainty makes even preserved food degrade quickly. Meals should be consumed immediately upon removal from sealed containers. Some travelers report success with "conceptual cooking"—preparing meals in their imagination so vividly that their bodies extract some nutritional value from the thought.

**Sleep**
Dangerous but necessary. The city's continuous unbecoming accelerates during unconscious hours. Sleep only in rooms with at least three exits (to prevent the city from forgetting doorways while you rest) and place entropy pumps at the corners of your sleeping area to maintain a bubble of stable physics. Many visitors sleep in shifts of no more than two hours to prevent deep unconsciousness where the self becomes negotiable.

**Waste Management**
The city absorbs waste products, breaking them down into constituent uncertainties. This is convenient but unsettling. Don't watch the process. What the city takes, it sometimes remembers, and you don't want your biological waste achieving questionable sentience.

### Emergency Protocols

**If You Begin to Hollow**
The first sign is usually a lightness in the bones, as if they're becoming porous. Next, thoughts develop gaps—you'll reach for a word and find only its outline. If this begins:
1. Immediately consume water and solid food
2. Recite personal anchors (your name, your origin, your purpose)
3. Create friction—clap, stomp, generate heat through motion
4. Find the nearest architectural creature; their presence stabilizes local reality
5. If symptoms persist, locate a probability overlap zone where the ghost cities flicker—the superimposition can restore lost definition

**If the City Begins Quoting Itself Around You**
You've become too involved in its recursive patterns. Break the cycle by introducing randomness: throw dice, speak in tongues, walk backward with your eyes closed. The city struggles to incorporate true chaos and will usually release its geometric grip to avoid contamination.

**If You Can't Find the Exit**
The Hollowing has no true boundary—it ends where visitors stop believing in it. To leave, you must cultivate active disbelief. List all the reasons this place cannot exist. Point out its impossibilities aloud. Eventually, reality will reassert itself, and you'll find yourself on the salt plateau's edge, looking back at something that might have been a city or might have been a three-kilometer-high hallucination.

### Post-Visit Recovery

Expect a period of re-integration lasting several weeks. Common symptoms include:
- Temporal dysphoria (arriving before you've left, remembering things that haven't happened)
- Architectural dreams where buildings grow like plants and thoughts require structural support
- Compulsive checking that objects remain solid
- Difficulty accepting definite states—everything seems like it should have other options
- Phantom desiccation despite normal hydration
- Hearing the city's twice-daily chord in everyday resonances

These symptoms typically fade, though some visitors report permanent enhancement of their ability to navigate paradoxes and appreciate absence. A few claim they never fully leave—that some part of them continues hollowing, slowly and dignifiedly, in a city that exists in the space between is and isn't.

### Final Considerations

The Hollowing is not hostile, merely indifferent to the concept of visitors. It doesn't want you there, but it doesn't not want you there either. Approach with respect for its ongoing unbecoming. Don't try to fix or fill its hollows—they're not damage but design. 

Some find the experience transformative, learning to appreciate the beauty in reduction, the elegance in erasure. Others find it horrifying, a vision of entropy given architectural form. Both responses are correct. Neither is complete.

Pack twice what you think you need. Expect to return with less than half of yourself. What you lose, the city keeps, adding your absence to its collection. What you gain is harder to quantify—an understanding, perhaps, that existence is just one option among many, and not necessarily the most interesting one.

Remember: in the Hollowing, the most important preparation is the willingness to become less. If you can't accept that trade, seek your wonders elsewhere. The city has plenty of nothing to share, but only with those who understand that nothing, properly arranged, can be everything.
