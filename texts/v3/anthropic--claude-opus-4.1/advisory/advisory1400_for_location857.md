# Preparation Guide for the Furnace Wells

## Critical Environmental Hazards

The Furnace Wells will actively try to cook you alive. This isn't hyperbole—the ambient temperature likely ranges from 110°F (43°C) in the "cooler" entry areas to potentially 140°F+ (60°C+) near the hexagonal boiler convergence. Combined with near-100% humidity from the constant steam cycles, you're looking at wet-bulb temperatures that can kill an unprotected human in under an hour.

### Essential Thermal Protection

**Primary heat management:** Acquire a personal cooling vest system—the type used by foundry workers or military personnel in desert operations. The phase-change cooling packs won't last more than 2-3 hours in this environment, so bring at least three full sets of replacement packs in an insulated carrier. 

**Clothing:** Lightweight, moisture-wicking technical fabrics only. Cotton will become a sodden death trap. Full-length pants and long sleeves paradoxically help by preventing direct steam burns. Industrial heat-resistant coveralls (aluminum-lined if possible) over your base layer. The reflective surface will help with radiant heat from the boilers.

**Breathing apparatus:** The air will be thick, humid, and potentially oxygen-depleted in dead zones. Bring a half-face respirator with combination cartridges rated for organic vapors and particulates. The rust, mold spores, and possible chemical off-gassing from degrading industrial materials pose real threats. Consider a small emergency oxygen canister.

## Navigation and Movement Safety

### The Vertical Maze Problem

Seven levels of catwalks with no guaranteed structural integrity. Your preparation needs to account for:

**Testing equipment:** A telescoping pole (minimum 8 feet) with a hook end for testing catwalk stability before committing your weight. Every step on metal grating should be considered suspect.

**Redundant anchor points:** Professional-grade climbing harness with at least 50 feet of static rope and multiple carabiners. You'll want to maintain three points of contact at all times, with the fourth being your safety line anchored to the most solid-looking structural element you can identify.

**Marking your path:** The polyrhythmic machinery and identical-looking pipe junctions will destroy your sense of direction within minutes. Bring high-temperature chalk (welding soapstone) to mark your route. Standard chalk will sweat off surfaces immediately. Place markers at eye level and foot level—the steam columns can obscure vision unpredictably.

### The Steam Events

The "unknowable schedules" of steam releases suggest a complex but non-random pattern you won't have time to decode. 

**Early warning system:** A small infrared thermometer will let you spot temperature spikes in pipes before steam release. Watch for subtle vibrations in valve assemblies—they'll likely shudder before venting.

**Emergency shelter:** A compact heat-reflective emergency blanket that can create a temporary shield. When you hear the pre-release hiss, you'll have perhaps 2-3 seconds to crouch and tent the blanket over yourself.

## Timing Your Descent

### The Maintenance Ghost

Someone—or something—maintains this place. They work unseen, which means they either:
- Operate on a schedule you can avoid
- Have ways of moving through the space you can't perceive
- Are actively avoiding contact

**Optimal timing:** Enter during what would have been the original factories' peak operation hours (historically 10 AM - 2 PM). The machinery seems to "remember" its purpose—it may run most predictably during its traditional work schedule.

**Leave evidence of peaceful intent:** Place a small offering at the entrance—perhaps modern machine oil or fresh brass fittings. If there is a maintenance presence, showing respect for their work might prevent interference.

## Specific Equipment Checklist

### Vision and Documentation
- **Headlamp:** LED with red-light option (less disruptive to whatever dark-adapted organisms exist here). Bring three. The humidity will kill electronics.
- **Backup illumination:** Chemical light sticks—the heat will make them burn faster but brighter.
- **Camera:** Waterproof/ruggedized only. Keep it in a sealed bag with desiccant packets between uses.

### Water and Hydration
- **Paradox warning:** You're surrounded by water but none of it is safe to drink. The condensation contains decades of industrial contamination.
- **Bring:** Minimum 4 liters in an insulated hydration system. Add electrolyte supplements—you'll sweat them out faster than you can replace them.
- **Emergency treatment:** Water purification tablets won't help with heavy metals, but bring them anyway for biological contaminants.

### Tools and Instruments
- **Multitool:** Must include pipe wrench functionality. You may need to turn valves to create temporary safe passages.
- **Pressure gauge:** Portable manometer to check differential pressure across spaces—sudden drops might indicate structural failures or dangerous void spaces.
- **Magnetic patches:** Strong neodymium magnets for creating temporary anchor points on iron structures.
- **Lubricant spray:** For valves that need turning. WD-40 won't survive the heat—bring high-temperature industrial lubricant.

## Psychological Preparation

### The Rhythm Trap

The polyrhythmic mechanical breathing will begin to affect your psychological state within 30 minutes. The human brain desperately tries to find patterns in the unsynchronized rhythms, leading to a type of temporal disorientation unique to industrial spaces.

**Countermeasure:** Bring noise-canceling earphones with pre-loaded white noise or natural sounds. Take "silence breaks" every 20 minutes to reset your auditory processing.

### The Purpose Void

The machinery running without product or purpose creates a deep existential unease. Your brain will struggle with the wrongness of perpetual motion without outcome.

**Mental anchor:** Maintain a clear objective for your visit. Write it on waterproof paper and review it regularly. The space wants to make you forget why you came.

## Exit Strategy

### The Heat Exhaustion Timeline

Even with all precautions, you have a maximum safe exploration window of 3-4 hours. Set multiple alarms. Heat exhaustion symptoms in this environment:
- Stage 1 (30 minutes): Profuse sweating, mild disorientation
- Stage 2 (1 hour): Decreased sweating (dangerous sign), confusion about location
- Stage 3 (2 hours): Potential hallucinations, poor decision-making
- Stage 4 (3+ hours): Risk of heat stroke, collapse

### The Ascent Problem

Climbing back up the ladder will take three times longer than descending. Your strength will be depleted, the rungs will be slippery with condensation from your own body, and the heat rises. Budget for this. Consider pre-placing a rope pulley system at the entrance for emergency extraction.

### Emergency Abort Signals

If the machinery suddenly synchronizes—if all the polyrhythms suddenly align into a single beat—leave immediately. This has never been documented but the possibility exists that the machines could achieve dangerous resonance.

If the red glow from the welded hexagonal center begins to pulse faster, abort. You don't want to be underground if whatever powers this place decides to fully wake.

## Final Preparations

### What to Leave Behind
- Anything electronic you're not willing to lose
- Synthetic materials that might melt
- Any open wounds (cover everything with waterproof bandages)
- Your assumptions about industrial logic

### What to Bring That Isn't Equipment
- Respect for the maintenance ghost
- Acceptance that you'll never understand the true purpose
- Willingness to leave when the space tells you to
- A companion (never explore alone) who has read this same preparation guide

### The Most Important Rule

The Furnace Wells operate on their own logic—industrial but not quite, purposeful but not quite, abandoned but not quite. Don't try to fix anything. Don't try to stop anything. You are a temporary observer in a process that started before you arrived and will continue after you leave. 

The machines remember what they were built to do, even if they can no longer remember why. You're entering their memory, not their present. Respect that, and you might survive the experience with just mild heat exhaustion and dreams of endless mechanical breathing for a few weeks after.

The city above has forgotten this place exists. When you return to the surface, you might find that you wish you could forget it too. But the sound of dripping condensation will follow you for months, and you'll find yourself checking the temperature of ladder rungs before climbing, forever after.
