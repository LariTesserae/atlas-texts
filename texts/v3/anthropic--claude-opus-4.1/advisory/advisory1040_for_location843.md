## Preparation Guide for the Iron Lung District

### Physical Preparations

**Clothing and Protection:**
- Bring multiple changes of moisture-wicking, quick-dry clothing. Nothing you wear will stay dry here. Synthetic fabrics or treated wool are essential—cotton will become waterlogged and never dry.
- Waterproof boots with excellent grip are mandatory. The root-walkways are perpetually slick, and a fall could send you into the canal system or through gaps in the flooring.
- Consider bringing knee-high gaiters to protect against the aggressive kudzu variants. They grow quickly enough that standing still for too long might result in vine entanglement.
- Pack a lightweight, breathable rain jacket—not for rain, but for the constant dripping and occasional steam vents.
- Bring work gloves. Many surfaces are either scalding from steam pipes or sticky with sap.

**Respiratory Considerations:**
- The air is thick, humid, and laden with both industrial and organic particles. Bring a respirator rated for both particulates and organic vapors.
- If you have any sensitivity to mold or mildew, pack antihistamines. The spore count here is extraordinary.
- Your lungs will need time to adjust to breathing what feels like soup. Plan for reduced physical capacity for the first few days.

### Navigation and Safety

**Getting Around:**
- Obtain or sketch a three-dimensional map before entering. The district operates on multiple vertical levels, and what appears to be a dead end might have ladder-roots or a steam-lift to another floor.
- Mark your path with something biodegradable but distinctive—chalk won't work in this moisture. Consider bringing colored string that you can tie to the omnipresent vines.
- Never trust a surface that looks solid without testing it first. Living wood can be deceptively rotten, and metal corrodes quickly here.
- Learn to recognize the warning signs of imminent steam venting: a low whistle, increased dripping, or flowers closing rapidly.

**Timing Your Visit:**
- Industrial processes run on strict schedules. Certain walkways may be impassable during production hours when tree-machines flex under load.
- Morning fog makes navigation nearly impossible for newcomers. Plan to arrive mid-day and secure lodging before evening.
- The kudzu harvesting occurs seasonally but unpredictably. During harvest, entire sections may be temporarily inaccessible as cutting crews work.

### Cultural and Social Preparation

**Understanding the Residents:**
- The people who live in the apartment-trees have adapted to constant moisture and mechanical noise. They may seem to whisper, but they're actually speaking at normal volume—your ears need time to filter the background noise.
- Residents navigate by sound and touch as much as sight. Don't be offended if someone reaches out to touch you while passing—it's a safety measure on narrow walkways.
- Time is measured differently here. "After the third venting" or "when the roots finish their cycle" are more meaningful than clock time.

**Economic Interactions:**
- The district operates on a hybrid economy. Bring both conventional currency and items for trade—particularly anything that resists moisture (sealed containers, synthetic materials, rust-proof tools).
- Labor is often communal. Offering to help with simple tasks like vine-clearing or condensate collection can earn you goodwill and information.

### Health and Hygiene

**Preventing Infection:**
- Everything here is alive and growing. Small cuts can become seriously infected within hours. Bring a comprehensive first aid kit with strong antimicrobials.
- Fungal infections are endemic. Pack antifungal powder for your feet and any areas prone to chafing.
- The water, while constantly cycling, is not safe to drink without treatment. Bring purification tablets or a reliable filter.

**Managing Moisture:**
- Accept that you will be damp. Fighting it leads to misery. Instead, establish a routine for drying critical items each evening.
- Electronic devices must be sealed in waterproof cases with desiccant packets.
- Paper is nearly useless here. Bring waterproof notebooks or plan to use digital recording methods.

### Psychological Preparation

**Sensory Adjustment:**
- The constant noise can be overwhelming initially—mechanical groaning, steam hissing, water dripping. Earplugs for sleeping are essential, but don't block your hearing during the day as sound is crucial for navigation and safety.
- The smell is intense and layered. Some find it nauseating at first—a mixture of rotting vegetation, hot metal, and chemical processes. It will become background within a few days.
- Claustrophobia is common in the deeper levels where roots and pipes create tunnel-like passages. Know your limits.

**Temporal Disorientation:**
- With limited natural light and constant activity, circadian rhythms become confused. Bring a watch and maintain strict sleep schedules.
- The slow growth of the district (an inch per year) contrasts sharply with the rapid growth of the kudzu and the constant industrial processing. This temporal paradox can be psychologically unsettling.

### What to Bring for Extended Stays

**Essential Supplies:**
- Moisture-absorbing packets (lots of them)
- Rope or cable rated for your body weight
- Portable camp stove (the local fuel is alcohol-based and readily available)
- Water purification system
- Headlamp with extra batteries (sealed)
- Multi-tool with rust-resistant coating
- Tarp or waterproof shelter material
- Anti-corrosion spray for any metal items

**Comfort Items:**
- Quick-dry towels (multiple)
- Waterproof bags in various sizes
- Clothesline and clips
- Antifungal soap
- Sealed snacks (local food is safe but has a distinct fermented-plant base that takes adjustment)

### Understanding the Ecosystem

Before you visit, understand that you're entering a living machine. The district doesn't just use biological and mechanical components—it has evolved into something between the two. Respect both aspects:

- Don't damage vines or roots without understanding their function. What looks like overgrowth might be a crucial gasket or seal.
- Steam vents and industrial processes have biological rhythms. Forcing or blocking them can cause system-wide disruptions.
- The entire district is essentially one organism. Harm to one section can cascade throughout the system.

### Emergency Protocols

**Know These Signs:**
- Rapid vine growth: indicates a nutrient line has burst
- Flowers opening out of sequence: pressure system malfunction imminent
- Brown or yellowing leaves: toxic leak in that area
- Unusual quiet: major system shutdown, evacuate immediately
- Root systems retracting: structural instability

**Emergency Contacts:**
- The district maintains bio-mechanical emergency responders who know how to navigate both the living and industrial crises
- Keep emergency whistle frequencies handy—different patterns mean different emergencies
- Learn the location of the nearest dry zones (usually maintenance shafts) where you can wait out a crisis

### Final Preparations

This is not a passive environment to observe—it requires active participation to navigate safely. You will leave with your clothes stained green-black, your skin perpetually damp, and your conception of the boundary between natural and artificial permanently altered. The district enters you as much as you enter it, its spores and scents and sounds becoming part of your own biological memory.

Prepare to be changed. The iron lungs will teach you a different way of breathing, and you'll find yourself listening for the rhythm of steam and growth long after you've left. Some travelers report that they can never quite scrub the smell of iron and chlorophyll from their skin, that in humid weather they still hear the groaning of tree-machines, that they dream of roots and pistons intertwined.

This is not a warning—it's a promise. The district doesn't just process plant matter. It processes everyone who enters, breaking them down and rebuilding them with a new understanding of what industry can be when it learns to breathe.
