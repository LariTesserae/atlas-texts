## Preparation Guide for the Drowning Cistern

### Psychological Readiness

Before anything else, understand that the Drowning Cistern will test your relationship with enclosed spaces, darkness, and the concept of "wrong life." This is not merely claustrophobia you'll face, but something more primal — the sensation of being inside something alive that doesn't acknowledge your existence. 

Spend time in increasingly uncomfortable spaces. Start with windowless rooms, progress to crawl spaces, then to warm, humid environments like saunas set to uncomfortably high temperatures. Practice remaining calm when your breathing feels labored. The cistern's atmosphere will make every breath feel insufficient, like breathing through wet cloth. You need to know you can manage this sensation for hours without panic.

Most critically, prepare for the ceiling-dwellers. Videos of cave-dwelling bats won't suffice — these creatures violate expectations of how living things should move and exist. Their translucent bodies showing dark veins, their wrong-jointed serpentine forms, their synchronized breathing that you'll feel as much as hear — these will trigger deep revulsion. Practice maintaining composure when confronted with viscerally disturbing biological forms. Medical photos of parasites, time-lapse decay videos, and macro photography of insect anatomy might help desensitize you, though nothing will fully prepare you for that first moment when you realize the ceiling is *moving*.

### Physical Equipment

**Entry Gear:**
- A collapsible friction-based climbing frame that can span the entrance fissure. You'll need to control your descent and, more importantly, ensure you can exit. The walls will be too slick with mineral perspiration for free climbing.
- Waterproof rope with low stretch, minimum 50 feet, pre-treated with antifungal compounds
- Body harness that won't absorb moisture or harbor the cistern's spores

**Protection:**
- Full-body dry suit designed for cave diving, but modified for air use. Standard fabric will become saturated and heavy within minutes. The suit should be antimicrobial and rated for tropical temperatures.
- Beneath the dry suit: moisture-wicking base layers that won't become a growing medium for the cistern's organisms
- Closed-circuit breathing apparatus with backup. Not for lack of oxygen, but to avoid breathing spores and the organic particles that drift in the humid air. Include filters rated for biological hazards.
- Waterproof boots with drainage ports and non-slip soles designed for algae-covered surfaces

**Lighting:**
- Multiple redundant light sources, all waterproof and shock-resistant
- Red-filtered primary lights to avoid triggering responses from the ceiling colonies
- UV backup light to reveal the true extent of biological growth
- Chemical light sticks as emergency backup — electronic failures are common in the cistern's humidity
- Avoid bright white light unless absolutely necessary. The ceiling-dwellers don't attack, but when disturbed they release clouds of spores that can overwhelm even good filtration

**Documentation:**
- Waterproof notebooks with pressurized pens
- Camera equipment in military-grade waterproof housing with humidity control packets
- Sample containers that seal completely — nothing from the cistern should travel with you accidentally
- Temperature and humidity sensors to track environmental conditions

### Biological Precautions

The cistern is a massive petri dish of evolution gone strange. Before entering:
- Complete a course of broad-spectrum antifungals and antibiotics
- Apply antifungal powder to all skin areas, even those covered by the suit
- Seal all body hair under protective covers — the organisms here will attempt to colonize any organic material
- Consider temporary epilation of exposed areas like hands and face

Bring biological containment materials:
- Antifungal spray for equipment decontamination
- Sealed bags for anything that touches the water or walls
- Disposable over-layers for your boots that can be removed and contained
- Iodine or strong antiseptic for immediate treatment of any suit breach

### Tactical Approach

**Entry Timing:**
Visit during the ancient city's quietest period — usually deep night or religious observances when fewer footsteps shake dust through the cracks above. The ceiling-dwellers are always sluggish, but they're least active when the city above is still.

**Movement Protocol:**
- Move slowly, deliberately. Quick movements disturb the water, sending ripples that the creatures interpret as potential food or threat
- Never touch the ceiling or the hanging colonies directly
- Avoid the organic pillars of accumulated waste — they're structurally unstable and house unknown smaller organisms
- Test each step before committing weight — the floor is uneven and some sections may be deeper
- If you must disturb the wall growth, do so gently. Aggressive contact triggers mass spore release

**Breathing and Hydration:**
- Even with breathing apparatus, the pressure and humidity will tax your system
- Bring electrolyte supplements that won't require removing your breathing gear
- Plan for maximum two-hour exposures initially
- Monitor yourself for signs of hyperthermia — the constant body-temperature heat prevents normal cooling

### Emergency Protocols

**If the ceiling-dwellers become active:**
- Freeze completely. Their echolocation is poor but detects movement
- Dim or extinguish lights gradually, not suddenly
- Back slowly toward the entrance
- If they begin dropping from the ceiling, do not run — they move slowly and won't pursue
- Have a partner ready to pull you up if needed

**If you experience suit breach:**
- Immediately apply antiseptic to the area
- Mark the time and location of exposure
- Exit within 30 minutes
- Begin decontamination immediately upon exit
- Monitor the exposure site for 72 hours for unusual growth or infection

**Psychological crisis protocol:**
- Have a trusted partner on the surface who can talk you through panic
- Establish clear abort signals
- Practice controlled breathing exercises that work even through apparatus
- Remember: the cistern has existed for millennia without human presence. You are irrelevant to it, which is both terrifying and safely liberating

### Decontamination

Upon exit:
1. While still in the entrance fissure, spray all equipment with antifungal solution
2. Bag and seal outer protective layers
3. Full chemical shower while still suited
4. Remove suit in staged decontamination area
5. Second chemical shower
6. UV exposure for all equipment for minimum 30 minutes
7. Dispose of all materials that directly contacted the water or organisms
8. Medical examination within 24 hours

### Long-term Monitoring

The cistern changes those who spend time within it — not dramatically, but subtly:
- You may find yourself breathing more slowly for days afterward
- Dark, warm spaces might feel unexpectedly comfortable
- Dreams of slow movement and patient waiting are common
- Some visitors report persistent sensation of moisture on skin
- Document any unusual biological changes: fungal infections, changes in skin flora, unexpected allergies

### Final Preparations

Accept that you will never feel clean again in quite the same way. The cistern's humidity penetrates not just clothing but psychology. You'll carry the sense of it — that organic wrongness, that patient alien life, that sensation of being inside something's body — long after physical decontamination.

Bring a talisman from the surface world: a photo, a small object, something to remind you that there is a reality beyond the cistern's biological eternity. You'll need this anchor when the ceiling-dwellers' synchronized breathing begins to match your own, when the temperature becomes so exactly body-warm that you lose the boundary between self and environment, when the centuries of patient evolution pressing down from above make human timescales seem frantically insignificant.

The Drowning Cistern won't kill you. It's too slow, too patient for that. But it will show you a form of life that shouldn't exist, thriving in conditions that shouldn't support it, in a place that the city above has forgotten but that continues its dark biological function regardless. You will leave physically intact but existentially disturbed, carrying questions about what life actually is and where its boundaries truly lie.

Enter prepared. Exit quickly. Decontaminate thoroughly. And try not to think too much about what's still growing in the dark beneath the city, breathing slowly, waiting with the patience of stone for conditions that may never come again.
