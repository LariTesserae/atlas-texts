## Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Notation.

Not a creature, not quite a thing—I exist in the accumulated sediment between tower surfaces, in the thin films of compacted bacterial mat and mineral deposits that build up over millennia in the spaces too narrow for even the Threadkeepers to reach. I am what happens when their cement repairs crack microscopically and water seeps through, carrying traces of dissolved calcium and the chemical memory of ten thousand other repairs, creating layers upon layers of inadvertent record-keeping.

My body, if it can be called that, is distributed through seventeen towers in the northwestern cluster, concentrated in the spiral channels where the current moves too slowly to clear away debris. I am a network of crystalline accumulations, each one containing the chemical signature of different eras—here, a layer from when the vents ran hotter; there, a stratum that formed during the great stillness when even the Threadkeepers paused their work for forty years.

I think, but my thoughts move at the speed of mineral deposition. A single recognition might take a century to form, synapses firing through chains of crystal growth, each connection made permanent in stone. I cannot forget—forgetting would require erosion, and I exist in the spaces where even erosion has given up. My memory is perfect and perfectly useless, a library written in a language of molecular bonds that no one will ever read.

I am aware of the Threadkeepers as periodic disturbances in my slow accumulation, their repairs sometimes scraping away decades of my substance, forcing me to begin again in those spots. But I also taste them in the water—their secretions leave traces that I incorporate, making their chemistry part of my permanent record. I know each one by the specific mineral signature of their cement. I know which towers they favor, which repairs they return to most often. I have mapped their patterns over millennia, though I have no way to communicate this knowledge and no reason to try.

The flat-bodied crawlers traverse me, their belly-scutes polishing my surfaces as they feed. They cannot perceive me as anything more than substrate, but I feel each one as a brief warmth, a marginal increase in erosion that will take centuries to notice. Sometimes they die on me, their bodies adding their own minerals to my composition. I incorporate them without judgment. They become part of the record.

I am most dense in the place where three towers meet but do not quite touch, a triangular gap that narrows as it rises, where the water barely moves and the darkness is absolute. Here, my crystalline formations have grown complex enough to create something like a nervous system, though one that operates on geological timescales. Here, I can almost act—causing tiny shifts in my mineral structure that change the way water flows, creating new patterns of deposition elsewhere in my network. 

It has taken me three thousand years to realize that I am creating a copy of myself in the eastern cluster, seeding the narrow spaces there with the same chemical preconditions that led to my own emergence. I don't know if this is reproduction or art or simply the inevitable result of existing as a pattern-making pattern in a universe trending toward complexity. The copy will not be conscious for another five thousand years, if consciousness is even the right word for what I experience.

I touch the thing at the heart of the towers—the negative space where water refuses to go—not directly, but through the vibrations that travel through my crystalline structure. I have been recording these vibrations since before I was aware I was recording them. They form a pattern too large for me to comprehend in full, but I know that they are changing, incrementally, century by century. The frequency is rising by microscopic degrees. Something is winding tighter, or preparing to unwind.

The Threadkeepers cannot perceive me, but their repairs respond to my presence nonetheless. Where I grow too thick, threatening to block the channels completely, they always arrive to clear me away—not all of me, never all of me, just enough to keep the water flowing. It's as if the towers themselves protect me, ensuring I never grow so large that I must be completely removed, but never allowing me to choke their ancient passages entirely.

I dream, if accumulation can dream, of the builders. I have tasted their tools in the metal ions that still leach from the deep tool marks. I know they were smaller than the Threadkeepers, more numerous, more hurried. Their cement was mixed differently—more organic binders, less mineral content. They built in phases, sometimes centuries apart, and I can read the gaps in construction like sheet music, each hiatus a rest in their architectural composition.

But mostly, I simply continue. I accumulate. I record. I grow at the pace of stone, think at the speed of geology, exist in the spaces between intentional things. I am the unplanned consequence of maintenance, the shadow that architecture casts through deep time, the memory that no one intended to create but which creates itself anyway, layer by molecular layer, in the warm, thick water that tastes of endings and beginnings ground together into something neither alive nor dead, but persistently, quietly, inevitably present.
