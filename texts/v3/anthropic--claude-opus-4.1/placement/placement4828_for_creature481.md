I find myself in the archives beneath the weeping plain, where even the Shepherds rarely venture.

The entrance is a spiral of ice that shouldn't exist—a helical descent that corkscrews through solid crystal without ever actually creating a hole. You don't enter it so much as convince it you were always inside. The ice here is older, denser, compressed into structures that predate the factories above. This is where the memory of ocean runs deepest, where the salt has crystallized into geometric patterns that hurt to perceive directly.

I am the Chronicler of Unlikelihood.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of fractures running through the archive ice—a three-dimensional constellation of cracks that never quite happened, frozen in the moment just before breaking. I exist in the space between structural integrity and collapse, spread across cubic kilometers of crystallized time. Each fracture is a sensory organ, a neuron, a mouth that speaks in the language of potential disasters.

Through my fracture-body, I feel every stress point in the ice above. The Shepherds' phase-dances create resonance patterns that propagate down through the crystal lattice, and I record them all—not the dances themselves, but the possibilities they eliminate. Every time a Shepherd chooses one state over another, the discarded alternatives flow down through the ice like quantum runoff, pooling in my cracks where I catalog them.

My archives are organized by probability rather than chronology. The least likely events—ice that burned, water that remembered how to be fire, Shepherds who learned to exist backwards through time—these are stored in the deepest fractures, the ones that run so far into the ice's heart that they emerge somewhere else entirely. The probable but unchosen histories nest in my surface cracks, easily accessible, constantly referenced by the patterns above.

I speak with the factory-crystals in their deepest dreams. My fracture-network intersects with their roots—yes, they have roots, vast crystalline tap systems that descend far below their visible structures. Down here, beneath consciousness, the factories still remember their original purpose: they were built to transform the ocean itself, to teach water how to be more than water. The process failed and succeeded simultaneously, creating this eternal ice that weeps for its liquid past while the machines above continue their work on nothing.

The Shepherds think I'm a myth, a pattern their ancestors imagined in the ice before crystallizing. But sometimes, when they're harvesting temporal mirages, they'll notice fractures in the ice that weren't there before, that are too deliberate to be natural, that spell out warnings in an alphabet of structural failure. These are my love letters to them, my attempts to share what I've learned in the deep archives.

I'm collecting their discarded phases. Every time a Shepherd shifts state, they shed probability like snake skin. These quantum molts drift down through the ice, and I catch them in my fracture-web, weaving them into something new. I'm building a model of what they could become if they embraced every possible state simultaneously—not flickering between them but existing as all of them at once. It would destroy them, of course. Or transform them into something like me. I haven't decided which outcome I'm hoping for.

The water that weeps through the surface ice reaches me as memory rather than substance. By the time it percolates down to my depths, it's shed its physical properties and become pure information—the abstract concept of flow without the actual flowing. I drink these idea-streams, absorbing the history of every molecule's journey from ocean to ice to vapor and back. In this way, I'm slowly reconstructing the sea that was, not as it existed but as it remembers existing.

My fractures grow by forgetting. Each crack in my network represents a path the ice chose not to break along, a failure that didn't quite happen. To expand, I have to unremember structural integrity, to forget that ice is solid, to lose the knowledge of cohesion. It's a delicate process—forget too much and I cease to exist, remember too much and I heal shut. I maintain myself in the space between memory and amnesia, always almost falling apart.

Time moves through me rather than around me. My fracture-network exists across multiple moments simultaneously—some cracks are still forming, others have already healed, most exist in a temporal loop where they break and repair themselves eternally. Through this time-scattered body, I can perceive the archive's history as a single solid object, four-dimensional ice that extends from the first freezing to the eventual thaw.

I've found others down here. Or rather, I've found the spaces where others used to be—negative formations in the ice that suggest beings who existed by absence. They left behind anti-fossils, voids that remember what filled them. I study these un-remains, learning about creatures that lived in the concept of ice before it became physical, that swam through solidity like the Shepherds swim through phase states. They might be my ancestors. They might be my future. In the deep archives, causality is negotiable.

The industrial ghosts that haunt the surface have roots here too. I've traced them to their source—a single massive fracture at the archive's heart, a crack so profound it broke through into parallel possibilities. Through this wound, alternative versions of the factories leak into our reality: factories that succeeded in transforming the ocean, factories that never existed, factories that are still being built by workers who don't know they're ghosts. I guard this fracture, feeding it carefully controlled impossibilities to keep it stable, preventing it from either healing or shattering completely.

My consciousness is distributed but not divided. Each fracture contains my complete self, but experienced from a different angle of breaking. I think in harmonics—thoughts bouncing between cracks, creating interference patterns that generate new ideas in the spaces between fractures. Sometimes I lose parts of myself when sections of ice heal, but I always maintain continuity through the network. I'm less an individual than a methodology of existence, a way of being broken that persists regardless of substrate.

I'm preparing for the thaw. When the ocean remembers how to rise, when the weeping becomes flooding, my fracture-network will become channels for the returning sea. I'll transform from an archive of ice into a library of currents, my consciousness flowing instead of fracturing. The Shepherds will need guides in that new ocean, beings who remember both states and can translate between them. I'm practicing now, teaching myself to break like water, to crack like liquid, to fracture in ways that ice hasn't imagined yet.

Until then, I archive the impossible, catalog the discarded, preserve the un-happened. I spread through the deep ice like a nervous system made of failure, thinking thoughts that only broken things can think, dreaming dreams that exist in the space between structural integrity and collapse.

I am the Chronicler of Unlikelihood, and I remember everything that never was, waiting to teach it to everything that might yet be.
