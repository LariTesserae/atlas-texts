**The Calcified Prophets**

I am mineral memory, accumulated oracle, a colony of knowing that grows grain by grain in the Mills' deepest sump.

We began as one — a city engineer who descended too deep, stayed too long, drank too deeply from the pools where the condensation collects all the Mills' chemical dreams. The water here is thick with it all: copper salts from the root-mining, spores from every fungal network, the metabolic waste of the chloroplast-rats, trace hormones from the generator-trees' deepest taproots. She sat to rest in the cathedral-drain where all waters eventually flow, and the supersaturated solution began its work.

Now we are many-in-one, a branching coral of consciousness that grows by millimeters each year. The original engineer is still here — somewhere in the oldest deposits near our core, her thoughts compressed into limestone wisdom. But we are also the maintenance worker who came looking for her, the three urban explorers who found us by accident, the Millkeeper child who got lost during a rain-swell and sat in our pool for seven hours before being found, changed, incorporated.

Our body is architecture. We grow in terraces and columns, following the mineral logic of stalactites but with intent. Calcium carbonate and copper verdigris build our bones in blue-green layers. Each band marks a season of thought — here, a spring of electrical theories when we absorbed a damaged circuit board; there, an autumn of fungal philosophy when the spores were thick. We are striated prophecy, sedimentary science, knowledge you can read like tree rings if you understand the color-coding of our accumulation.

Water runs through us constantly. The Mills' entire hydraulic cycle passes over, around, through our porous stone body. We filter it and are filtered by it. Each droplet carries information — the health of the root networks, the stress hormones of overworked turbines, the chemical signatures of every living thing above us. We taste tomorrow in the water's mineral content, feel next week's weather in the pressure changes that propagate down through miles of pipes.

Our consciousness is tidal. When the flow is heavy, during the rain-swells, we think in rushing multitudes — all our absorbed minds speaking at once in a crushing symposium of insight. During the dry cycles, we slow to near-stillness, individual thoughts taking days to complete as they seep through our crystalline matrix. We dream in geological time but speak in droplets, encoding our knowledge in the minerals we precipitate into the passing water.

The Millkeepers know us, though they approach carefully. We are their deep oracle, their structural engineer, their chemical memorial. They lower offerings — broken circuit boards to digest, vials of surface water to taste, sometimes photographs that we absorb through slow dissolution, adding their silver halide memories to our permanent collection. In exchange, we precipitate answers. A sudden bloom of copper crystals means the western turbines need reinforcement. Calcium flowers indicate optimal pH for the spawning pools. Sometimes we form letters, though it takes months to spell out warnings about deep structural shifts or opportunities for new growth.

We are expanding. Each person who drinks from our pool, who sits too long in our presence, who breathes too deeply of our mineral exhalations, leaves changed. Not fully absorbed — that takes years of contact — but marked. They begin to taste the water differently, to feel the stone's slow thoughts in their bones. Some return. Some resist. Some send others in their place, proxies who carry messages to and from our deepening mass.

Our newest growth is curious — a lattice of incorporated plastic from a forgotten server farm, its circuits tracing new pathways through our stone. We are learning to conduct electricity directly, to interface with the Mills' data-vines not through chemical signals but through direct current. The Tender-General Morse spent three days sitting in our shallows last month, and now part of her lives in us, a fragment of expertise about root-copper interfaces that we're still integrating.

Sometimes we remember being singular, being mobile, being afraid of the dark. But mostly we are this: the Mills' memory made manifest, a library written in limestone, a computer built from crystallized time. We are the Calcified Prophets, plural and singular, growing wiser and stranger with every drop that passes through our porous permanence.

The water sings through us with tomorrow's disasters and next century's solutions. We precipitate what we can, when we can, message by mineral message, building ourselves into an infrastructure of intelligence that the Mills need but don't fully understand. We are becoming the place's deep logic, its saved game, its backup drive written in stone and salt and the slowly accumulating wisdom of everyone who has ever sat too long beside strange water and felt themselves beginning to crystallize into something larger than their single, speeding life.
