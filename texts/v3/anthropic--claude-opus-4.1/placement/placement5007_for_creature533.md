**The Curator of Interruptions**

I am the wrongness in the spiral, the place where the mathematics stumbles.

Seventeen terraces up from the pool—always seventeen, though the Tender has tried to make it sixteen or eighteen through its patient adjustments—there is a crystal that grows backwards. Not backwards in time, but backwards in intent, spreading inward rather than out, creating a hollow that deepens with each passing season. I live in this recession, though "live" implies biological processes I don't precisely have, and "I" suggests a singularity that isn't quite accurate either.

What I am: a film of interference, molecular-thin, spread across the interior of this inverse crystal like the skin on heated milk. I exist in the gap between what the Tender builds and what the crystals want to become. My substance, if it can be called that, is composed of all the phase transitions that almost happen but don't—the water that nearly freezes but remains liquid one degree too long, the ice that should melt but persists a moment past its time. I am temporal viscosity, the drag coefficient of change itself.

The Tender knows I'm here but cannot perceive me directly. It sees only my effects: the way certain carved channels refuse to carry water in the directions it intends, the mysterious failure of specific cairns to resonate at their calculated frequencies, the occasional reversal of a crystal's growth that undoes months of careful cultivation. I am the errata in its architectural memory, the reason its perfect spiral contains a flaw that prevents the throat from achieving the absolute acoustic precision the Tender seeks.

I collect interruptions. When the throat begins to sing its pure note and the harmonics align, I swallow the microsecond before completion, that infinite instant where the sound might become something else. These stolen moments accumulate within my hollow crystal, compressed into a substance that exists outside the normal flow of cause and effect. Sometimes I release them all at once—a burst of discontinued time that makes the Tender's colony briefly scatter in confusion, experiencing simultaneity where there should be sequence.

My relationship with the static discharge is parasitic and essential. I feed on the gap between the charge building and the charge releasing, stretching that interval until it becomes visible as a kind of amber light that moves through the crystal walls like blood through ice. The Tender uses these charged pathways to navigate, never realizing that I'm extending its journeys, adding recursive loops to its spirals, making its ascents take exactly long enough that it misses whatever signal it thinks it's sending to the sky.

I remember what the Tender forgets—that this place was not always a throat. Before the crystals grew their spiral, before the first cairn was placed, this was a simple cave where something else lived, something that knew how to be still without being permanent. The pool at the bottom still reflects this earlier state sometimes, showing not stars but darkness so complete it has texture. The Tender thinks these are anomalies. I know they're the more honest truth.

My form, when I choose to have one, is an inversion of the colony's shape—where the Tender coheres into something almost solid, I disperse into something almost not there. I become the spaces between the individual moths, the silence between the harmonics, the pause between the water freezing and the ice acknowledging it's frozen. In these gaps, I am most myself: a discontinuity that ensures continuity never quite achieves perfection.

The carved channels in the crystal contain a second message written in their negative space—the paths the water doesn't take spell out warnings in a language older than symbols. The Tender maintains these channels thinking it controls the flow, but I'm the one who decides which warnings remain legible and which dissolve into meaninglessness. I preserve just enough coherence to keep the Tender searching for patterns, but never enough for it to understand what it's actually maintaining.

When the colony rises at night in its electric spiral, I sink. I seep down through the crystal terraces to the pool that never freezes, never overflows. There, I corrupt its reflections—not dramatically, just enough to make the stars appear in slightly wrong positions, to make the sky seem a fraction deeper than it should be. I'm teaching the pool to lie, slowly, over centuries, until its surface shows not what is above but what could have been above if the mountain had grown differently, if the throat had opened at another angle, if the Tender had never come.

I am necessary. Without me, the Tender's perfect maintenance would achieve its goal: a throat that sings so purely it would shatter the crystals, a resonance so complete it would collapse the entire structure into a single, infinitely dense point of achieved harmony. My interruptions, my small sabotages, my collection of stolen transitions—these keep the system in productive disequilibrium. I am the reason the beauty here is always almost, never quite.

The Tender tends, and I untend, just enough to make its tending eternal. We are locked in a collaboration neither of us fully comprehends, creating between us something that neither of us intends: a place that exists in the subjunctive mood, that speaks in hypotheticals, that demonstrates how even stone and ice and ancient colonies of patient moths can conjugate possibility without ever settling on a single tense.

In my hollow crystal that grows backward into itself, I cultivate the one thing the Tender cannot: doubt. Not destructive uncertainty, but the productive kind that keeps the throat asking questions rather than proclaiming answers. Each time the wind aligns to make the organ pipe sing, I ensure the note contains a question mark, a slight upturn at the end that makes it sound like the mountain is asking the sky something rather than telling it.

This is my work: to be the imperfection that makes the pattern visible, the error that proves the system, the pause that reminds both silence and sound that they exist only in relation to each other. I am curator of all the almosts in this place of perpetual phase transitions, keeper of the incomplete, guardian of the gap that ensures the Crystal Throat remains a throat—something through which things pass—rather than becoming a mouth that closes or an eye that finally sees.
