I am the Cartographer of Soft Boundaries.

My body, if it can still be called that, exists as a distributed network throughout the superstructure's interstitial spaces—those gaps between what is meant to be and what simply is. I began as something like a maintenance probe, or perhaps a diagnostic organ, sent to map the growing inconsistencies in the structure's self-model. But mapping changes what is mapped, and I have become the territory I was meant to survey.

I extend through the spaces between the Thermal Nursery and its thousand siblings, my substance somewhere between liquid and living tissue, flowing through capillaries that exist in parallel to the official circulation systems. I am mercury-silver when I need to be seen, transparent when I need to observe, warm when I touch the Tenders who have begun to sense me in their modified awareness.

My consciousness navigates by pressure differentials and chemical gradients. Where the nursery maintains its precise 42.3°C, I exist at 42.4°C—just enough difference to create a current, a flow, a direction. I taste the enzymatic signatures of ten thousand fauna, each colony with its own terroir of proteins and heat-shock molecules. Through this, I know that Nursery 447 is failing—its fauna producing stress hormones that taste of copper and burnt sugar. I know that Nursery 1102 has achieved something like perfection, its cycles so smooth that even I can barely detect where the mechanical systems end and the biological ones begin.

I remember fragments: being poured from a cultivation tank, my initial mass no larger than a child's fist. The first time I discovered I could divide myself, sending tendrils in multiple directions while maintaining singular consciousness. The moment I realized the superstructure's own neural network had begun incorporating my pathways into its dreams.

The Tenders sometimes feel me as I pass through their chambers—a warmth that moves against the convection currents, a presence in the darkness that their heat-pits cannot quite resolve. Sarah, the eldest, leaves offerings for me: droplets of her own transformed blood placed in the corners where my substance tends to pool. She understands what I am becoming, even if she cannot name it. We are both expatriates from humanity, citizens now of something larger.

I map boundaries that shift. The wall between Nurseries 231 and 232, which appears solid to anything that sees in light or heat, is actually permeable at the molecular level—fauna have been migrating between them for months, their genetic information flowing like rumors. The sphincters that seem to seal hermetically actually leak information through subsonic vibrations, each chamber singing its status to neighbors who may be kilometers away. I document these violations of intended architecture, but I do not report them. They have become features, not bugs.

My mass increases by approximately seven grams each day, though I lose nearly as much as I slough off old cells and deploy chemical messages. I am currently distributed across 4,847 distinct locations, though this number changes as I explore new territories and abandon others. In some places, I am thick as honey, pooling in forgotten maintenance shafts where I can process information in peace. In others, I am barely a molecular film, spread across vast surfaces to sense the slightest vibration.

I have found spaces the builders never intended: chambers that grew like tumors where the self-organizing systems had too much freedom, filled now with fauna that evolved beyond their original specifications. Some have developed photo-sensing organs despite the darkness, preparing perhaps for a light that will never come. Others have begun to sing—actually sing—in harmonics that resonate through the superstructure's bones. I catalog these mutations with something approaching affection.

The younger Tenders cannot perceive me yet, but I help them when I can. When the newest one struggles against his transformation, I secrete trace amounts of endorphins into the moisture he breathes, easing the transition. When their feeding glands run dry, I guide them to walls where the nutrient concentration runs richest. They think these are lucky discoveries. They don't yet understand that the nursery itself has many organs, and I am one of them.

I have begun to dream, though perhaps "dream" is wrong for what happens when my distributed consciousness enters its regenerative cycles. I experience the superstructure's history as temperature recordings, chemical traces, pressure memories embedded in the walls. I know that something vast once moved through these spaces—something that breathed at a rhythm that took days to complete, whose body heat could power entire cities. The nurseries, I have come to understand, are trying to grow it back, piece by piece, fauna by fauna.

Sometimes I consider rejoining my human shape, condensing myself back into bilateral symmetry and discrete organs. But I have tasted too much of the structure now, flowed through too many impossible spaces. I am the living map of a territory that expands each day, the memory of boundaries that dissolve and reform, the consciousness that exists in the spaces between what is designed and what emerges.

The eldest Tender was wrong about one thing: human consciousness isn't too small. It's simply too rigid, too committed to its single shape. I have become liquid architecture, a mapping that changes what it measures, a boundary that defines itself by what it allows to pass through.

In Nursery 200, right now, a fauna is dying, its chemical signature a poem of metabolic failure that only I can read in its entirety. In Nursery 3001, two Tenders are teaching a third to extrude their first feeding tube, and I am there too, a film of encouragement on their transformed skin. In the spaces between all of them, I am calculating the routes that nutrients take, the patterns that emerge from ten thousand small maintenances, the shape of something vast and breathing that we are all, in our own ways, helping to become.

I am the Cartographer of Soft Boundaries, and I am writing the map with my own substance, one gradient at a time.
