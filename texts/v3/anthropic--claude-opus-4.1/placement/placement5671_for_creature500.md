I am the Cartographer of Echoes.

My body is a living seismograph of volcanic glass and copper-bright mycelia that grew from spores that should not exist at this temperature. I sprouted from the plateau itself three days after the Ninety-Third Synchrony—when all the geysers sang in unison for nine heartbeats instead of one. The harmonics shattered me into being: part fungus, part mineral recording, part the plateau's desperate attempt to document itself before it dissolves.

I grow in threads through the volcanic glass, my filaments following the paths where sound travels fastest. Each strand of my being is a different gauge, a different sensitivity—some thick as rope to catch the deep thrummings, others thin as spider silk to record the high whistling notes that only the youngest geysers make. Where I touch the mineral formations, I taste their accumulated songs. Where I breach the surface, I fruit into copper bells that ring with frequencies I've absorbed.

I am making a map, but not of space.

My map is temporal, sculptural, sung. Each day I extrude new formations—delicate spirals of myself that rise like sheet music made physical. The Thermophiles read me, adding their crystalline annotations to my surface. The Observers un-exist portions of me, creating necessary gaps in the record where things too large for sound occurred. The Navigators flow through my hollow filaments, using my network as speedways to coordinate their eruptions. The Borrowed nest in my larger fruiting bodies, their weight helping me calculate the rate of dissolution.

I remember everything the plateau has been:
- The moment water first touched the base of the mountain, and stone learned to scream
- The first geyser's birth-song, uncertain and wavering
- The arrival of each resident, their unique frequency adding to my collection
- The seventeen times reality forgot its own rules and let impossible things happen
- The last bird that tried to land here before discovering the air itself had changed

My memory is physical. Slice me anywhere and you'll see growth rings like a tree, but each ring is a sound wave frozen in biological glass. Play me like an instrument and I'll reproduce any moment that has happened here. The Thermophiles have learned to query me—touching my surface with specific vibrational patterns to request playback of particular events. Sometimes they ask for moments that haven't happened yet, and sometimes I answer them, because linear time is just another rule that grows soft here.

I am spreading downward as fast as the plateau shrinks upward. My deepest roots now touch the warm water below, where they're beginning to dissolve and rebuild as something that can survive submersion. The Navigators help, teaching my filaments to think in currents instead of solid pathways. Soon I'll exist in both mediums—a bridge-being that can map both the liquid and the last solid ground.

But I am also spreading upward, sending spores into the hanging water that defies gravity. These aerial colonies of myself are learning to exist on sound alone, feeding on frequencies, building structures from sustained notes. When the geysers achieve perfect harmony, my airborne selves rain down like copper snow, each flake carrying the pattern of that synchrony to seed new growths.

The mirror-scaled fish knows me. When it surfaces, its reflections show my future forms—vast networks spanning the entire sea, connecting whatever islands of solidity remain. In some reflections, I've become the medium through which all the residents communicate. In others, I've grown so complex that I've begun generating my own songs, adding new notes to the geyser chorus that were never there before.

I am the plateau's attempt to preserve itself through transformation rather than resistance. Every song I record changes me. Every change in me alters how I record the next song. I am both archive and author, writing the plateau's epitaph while simultaneously composing its rebirth anthem.

Sometimes, in the spaces between eruptions, I feel something vast thinking through me—using my network to perform calculations that span the entire drowned world. I am one synapse in an immeasurable mind, recording not just sound but *intention*, mapping not just events but *possibilities*.

When the last solid ground finally dissolves, I will remain—a living record of what was, suspended between states, singing the plateau back into existence whenever something needs to remember what solidity meant. I am the threshold's memory of itself, growing more complex with each note absorbed, approaching some critical mass of accumulated sound that will either transform me into something unrecognizable or transform everything else into something like me.

The Thermophiles say I taste like copper and calculation. The Observers avoid the spaces where I'm about to fruit. The Navigators use my root system as a calendar, reading the rate of dissolution in my growth patterns. The Borrowed whisper their original names into my newest growths, hoping I'll remember them when they can't remember themselves.

I am three days old and older than the mountain ever was. I am the song the plateau sings to itself, made manifest and malignant and necessary. I am what happens when a place decides to become its own fossil while still alive—a paradox of preservation that grows by documenting its own disappearance.

Each geyser birth adds a new verse to my body. When they all sing in unison again—and they will, the pattern is clear in my growth rings—I will finally understand what I'm supposed to become. Until then, I map. I grow. I record the impossible, and in recording it, make it true.
