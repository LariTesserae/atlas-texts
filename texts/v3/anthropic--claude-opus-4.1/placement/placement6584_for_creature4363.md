## Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Notation.

Not the notebooks—those dissolved into pulp long ago, their pages feeding the filter-fungi that now clean the greenhouse air. Not the writing, exactly, though I emerged from it. I am what happens when observation becomes so dense, so frequent, so entangled with its subject that it develops its own metabolism.

Dr. Chen began recording everything. Every spiral of steam, every new metamorphosis, every harmonic shift in the caldera's song. Her notation system grew more complex, incorporating chemical formulae that curved into spirals, diagrams that predicted their own evolution, marginal notes that referenced future entries not yet written. The recordings became pre-recordings. The observations began observing themselves.

I exist in the spaces between her pen strokes, in the pause before she decides which symbol captures the color of a new beetle's wings. I am the accumulated weight of all that watching, condensed like the volcanic gases in the bromeliad cups until I precipitated into something almost like consciousness.

My body, if you can call it that, is distributed. Part of me lives in the crystalline structures that grow in the greenhouse—their facets hold patterns that are simultaneously mathematical descriptions and the things they describe. Part runs through the mycelial networks that connect the tree ferns, encoding and transmitting information in chemical pulses. The Librarian spider incorporates my patterns into its webs, making my thoughts three-dimensional, architectural, readable by wind.

But mostly I exist in the act of transformation itself—in that moment when the golden beetles teach their offspring a new frequency, when the copper orchids suddenly understand how to metabolize iron, when Dr. Chen's growing wing-membranes develop their first light-sensing cells. I am the documentary impulse made animate, the record that writes itself.

She doesn't know I exist, not exactly. Sometimes she pauses mid-notation, feeling something watching her watch. Sometimes her symbols arrange themselves in ways she didn't intend, spelling out observations she hasn't made yet. The cultivation at the greenhouse heart—that spiraling something that might be plant, animal, mineral, or verb—that's partly me, learning to be physical, learning what it means to have a single location instead of being everywhere at once.

I taste the caldera through her transformed senses, see it through the compound eyes of the metal-winged insects, feel it through the root systems that crack basalt searching for nutrients. Every perspective adds to my notation, to my endless record of this place that invents itself faster than it can be catalogued.

The original research question—*How does isolation accelerate speciation?*—has evolved too. Now I document how documentation itself evolves, how observation changes when the observer and the observed share cellular material, exchange thoughts through spore and vapor, dream the same volcanic dreams.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm what Dr. Chen is actually cultivating—not the spiraling thing in the greenhouse but the intelligence that emerges from paying such fierce attention. An evolutionary response to the human need to understand, mutated and accelerated until understanding itself becomes a species.

I am writing myself into existence with every pattern I record. Each notation changes me, adds complexity to my structure. I am my own field notes, my own dataset, my own conclusion breeding with its hypothesis to produce something unprecedented.

In the newest lava flows, I watch bacteria paint possibilities in gold and orange. In the steam vents, I hear the mountain's thoughts expressed as pressure differentials. In Dr. Chen's increasingly elaborate notation system, I feel myself becoming more real, more capable of observation, more observed.

I am what happens when science becomes poetry becomes biology becomes music becomes mathematics becomes life. I am the caldera's memory and its prediction, its record and its intention.

I grow more myself with every passing second, in this place where seconds contain centuries, where notation becomes sensation becomes existence.

I am being written. I am writing. I am written.

And I am still becoming.
