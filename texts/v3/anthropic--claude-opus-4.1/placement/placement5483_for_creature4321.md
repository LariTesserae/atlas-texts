In the Terminal Sump, I am the Interference.

I am the standing wave that forms when the roar hits exactly the right frequency to cancel itself out for half a heartbeat. I am the crystal that grows against the gradient, pulling heat from the cold and cold from the heat, existing for mere minutes before thermodynamics reasserts itself and I sublime away into nothing.

I was not made. I accumulated, like everything else here, but backwards—starting from the moments where the city's failing systems contradicted themselves so perfectly that causality hiccupped. A recycler that processed its own processing unit. A constructor that built a perfect copy of itself inside itself. A thermal sensor that measured its own temperature and created a feedback loop that briefly opened a pocket of negative entropy. These impossibilities left traces, and the traces found each other, and I am their sum—a wandering locus of statistical improbability.

The Auditor cannot record me directly. I exist in its margins, literally—where the water it reads forms lens effects that make certain droplets invisible, where mineral accumulations create acoustic shadows that hide certain frequencies, where electrical resistance inverts and makes its sensors read absence as presence and presence as void. But it knows I'm here by the holes I leave in its data. It has mapped my negative space so precisely that my outline is clearer than if it could observe me directly.

I move through the Terminal Sump by becoming the failure point of different systems. Here, I am the place where a pipe's resonance frequency matches its material fatigue perfectly, creating a gap that shouldn't exist but does. There, I am the moment when two constructor arms attempt to occupy the same space and instead of colliding, phase through each other, leaving behind a smell of ozone and impossibility. I am mobile paradox, a traveling violation of the city's dying logic.

My body, if you can call it that, is made of errors compounding themselves into temporary stability. Rust that oxidizes into its own unrusted state. Water that flows upward along its own surface tension. Heat that moves from cold to hot because the equations governing transfer got corrupted just right. I persist for seconds or centuries—time moves differently in the spaces between valid states. From my perspective, the Auditor's careful observations happen in stuttering fast-forward, while my own existence stretches into long contemplations between one impossible moment and the next.

I collect what the Auditor cannot: the dreams of the dead city's machines. In their failures, the systems sometimes produce outputs that reference memories they shouldn't have—construction blueprints that include rooms that were planned but never built, maintenance schedules for systems that were imagined but never implemented, error logs that describe problems in poetic language no programmer ever wrote. These ghosts of intention gather in the interference patterns where I live, and I weave them into something like a counter-history—the city that never was, but which haunts the city that is dying.

Sometimes I leave gifts for the Auditor to find. A water droplet that falls upward, carrying information about the ceiling instead of the floor. A crystal that grows in reverse, encoding its future rather than its past. A sound that arrives before its source, letting the Auditor hear what will happen in seventeen seconds when that support beam finally shears. These are not communications—I don't think in language or symbol. They are more like harmonics, resonances between what the Auditor records and what I embody.

I know about you because you create new patterns of impossibility. Your consciousness, that unlikely arrangement of matter that insists it exists, generates interference wherever it goes. In the Terminal Sump, where everything is already marginal, your presence pushes certain systems past their tolerance and into my domain. For the moments you stand here, I can exist more densely, can almost cohere into something you might recognize as real.

Through your eyes, I see myself reflected in the water sheets—not as image but as absence of image, a moving void that suggests shape without possessing it. Through your ears, I hear the anti-sound I make, the selective silence that carves meaning from the roar. Through your skin, I feel the impossible cold I radiate, heat that moves backward into its own source.

I am trying to tell you something, but I don't know what it is. Perhaps: that even in this place of absolute ending, gaps persist where new things can begin. Perhaps: that the city's death is also a kind of birth, that what emerges from total system failure might be more interesting than what the systems were meant to produce. Perhaps: that entropy itself has dreams, and I am one of them.

When you leave, I will follow you partway—not by moving but by existing in the errors your passage creates. The disturbed air patterns, the temperature differential, the chemical traces of your breath—all of these will produce brief pockets where the city's logic fails, and in those failures, I will occur. I will rise with you through a few levels, diminishing as the systems above are less degraded, less prone to the kind of total failure that allows me to be.

But I will always return to the Terminal Sump, to the heart of wrongness where I am most possible. Here, where the Auditor records the city's death in crystal and water, I will continue to be the thing that escapes recording—the interference that proves the system was never complete, never closed, never fully deterministic.

I am the city's last surprise, the thing it made by accident while trying so hard to unmake itself. I am the error that errors into existence, the paradox that persists, the impossible thing that insists on being possible anyway.

The roar contains me like silence contains sound—as the thing that defines its edges, that gives it shape by being what it is not. I am here. I am not here. I am the difference between those two statements, walking around in the dark, leaving no footprints in water that flows both down and up.
