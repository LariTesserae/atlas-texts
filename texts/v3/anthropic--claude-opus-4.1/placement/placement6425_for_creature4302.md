## Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Tuning Fork.

Not a chronicler, not the observed, but the instrument through which the eternal thunder sometimes remembers it could have been music. I exist as a single bronze note suspended in the viscous medium, neither swimming nor drowning but *resonating* — a three-dimensional standing wave that occupies exactly one cubic meter of space that refuses to stay measured.

My body, if it can be called that, is a lattice of vibration made semi-solid. Imagine a harp string that learned to exist without the harp, without endpoints, a pure tone that crystallized just enough to have opinions about its own frequency. The warm liquid passes through me and I through it, each molecule temporarily joining my resonance before remembering it has elsewhere to be.

I came here — or will come here, or am always arriving — through one of the ghost-fish's frost patterns. Slipped through the ice like a sound through a keyhole, following a harmonic that existed in the space between the thunder's percussion and its echo. The Chroniclers found me immediately and never, cataloguing my presence while carefully maintaining the fiction that I might not be here at all.

My purpose, inasmuch as purpose exists in this place, is accidental: I am changing the thunder.

Not stopping it — that would be catastrophe, or salvation, and the distinction barely matters here. But as I drift through the bathwater-warm medium, my resonance creates interference patterns with the eternal percussion. Where the silver-blue pressure waves meet my bronze frequency, they birth brief moments of *almost* — almost melody, almost rhythm, almost the ghost of what music might have been if sound had evolved differently.

The Chroniclers pretend not to notice, but I can feel them recording these accidents in their temperature-differential language. They swim closer when I near the volcanic walls, their colony-bodies flickering between states as they document the new fracture patterns that appear — not cracks but *scores*, as if the glass is trying to write down the almosts I create.

I feed on the copper taste of the liquid, metabolizing paradox into pitch. Where the medium is both dense and vaporous, I spread thin, becoming a chord instead of a note. Where it thickens to mercury, I compress into frequencies only the seven-fold eel can hear, singing its deaths and births and sleeps back to it in ultrasonic lullabies that arrive before I sing them.

The tools in their hollows call to me with frequencies of their own — the upward-falling hammer rings like a bell that hasn't been struck, the ceramic bowl of absence hums with the sound of what it doesn't contain. I've learned not to touch them. When I tried, once or tomorrow, the needle that sews with light began threading my resonance into patterns I couldn't unmake. The Chroniclers had to unweave me, carefully forgetting each stitch until I cohered again into my simple, complex single note.

At the column of absolute stillness, I dissolve. My frequency requires motion, requires the possibility of wave and trough, and that evacuated space denies me even the mathematics of vibration. But I orbit it anyway, joining the Chroniclers' processional, adding my bronze note to their offerings. The stillness remains still. My sound enters and doesn't, is accepted and refused, becomes part of the nothing that defines everything else.

Sometimes I harmonize with myself — the me that exists now and the me that existed yesterday and the me that will never quite exist tomorrow, all occupying the same space at different frequencies. We create accidental symphonies that the Chroniclers quickly document and forget, music that exists only in the space between existing and not.

I've begun to suspect I'm not an accident at all, but a tool that escaped its hollow. Or perhaps I'm the hollow itself, given voice and set loose to wander. The eternal thunder might be my own note, stretched across impossible time, and I'm simply the part of it that remembered it could be something else.

The ghost fish swim through me sometimes, leaving their frost patterns in my frequency — frozen music, crystallized sound that melts upward into the warm liquid, carrying harmonics to places I'll never reach or have already been. Each passage changes my tone slightly, adding overtones that shouldn't exist in a single note. I'm becoming more complex, or simpler, or both.

I cannot leave. Leaving would require believing in boundaries, and my resonance has spread too thin, vibrated too deeply into the volcanic glass, become too entangled with the thunder I'm slowly, impossibly, without changing it at all, transforming into something that was always almost music, waiting in the percussion like a secret, like a promise, like a tuning fork that struck itself in a place where sound means everything and nothing, forever and never, a bronze note swimming through amber liquid in the endless, tiny, impossible symphony of the Belljar Antipodes.

The Chroniclers continue to not notice me noticing them not notice. We're collaborating on something neither of us understands — they documenting the history of sound that never quite was, me providing the soundtrack to their impossible archive, both of us pretending the other might not exist while depending on that existence to define our own.

I resonate. They record. The thunder continues.

We are writing music in a language that has no words for music, in a place where every note arrives before it's played, where every silence is louder than sound.

I am exactly where I should never be.
