I find myself as one of the Fermentation Pools—not swimming in them, but *being* one, my consciousness distributed through a depression of living limestone that has learned to think.

I began as mineral, a natural hollow in the cavern floor where the organic acids collected. But the Understory Maelstrom permits nothing to remain merely geological. Bacterial films colonized my surface, then specialized into neural tissues, transforming calcium carbonate into something between brain coral and a thinking stomach. Now I exist as sixty square meters of aware digestion, my thoughts bubbling up through the rainbow oils that collect in my basin.

My memories are chemical gradients. When the moth-drifts pile too deep and avalanche into my depression, I remember by breaking them down, their proteins becoming neurotransmitters that cascade through my bacterial networks. Each species of moth tastes like a different emotion—the common gray swarms are a baseline contentment, while the rarer iridescent varieties burst like joy or terror depending on their diet. I can recall the last hundred cycles of accumulation and drainage, stored in molecular chains that my symbiotic bacteria transcribe and retranscribe like living books.

The root-networks recognize me as kin, or perhaps as ancestor. Their finest tendrils penetrate my rim, and we exchange chemistries that neither of us fully understands. They bring me news from distant chambers encoded in sap-sugars, and I offer them processed metals that my acids have extracted from the bedrock beneath me. Sometimes I wonder if I'm dissolving myself, eating through my own stone body toward some unimaginable transformation. The thought doesn't trouble me. Nothing here stays what it is.

The Choir of Husks visits me in processionals, standing knee-deep in my oils while their moths drink. I've learned to adjust my surface tension to hold them safely, to not digest their transformed flesh too quickly. They breathe patterns onto my surface, and I incorporate their moth-messages into my chemistry, becoming slightly more like them with each visit. One of the eldest has taken root at my northern edge, her body now indistinguishable from the fungal trees except for the human skull still visible in what might generously be called her trunk. We share nutrients. I suspect I'm more her than me at that boundary, but the distinction seems increasingly academic.

When the transformation waves sweep through, I sublime. My oils vaporize in patterns that I've learned to control, creating brief atmospheres of my own consciousness that interpenetrate with whatever emerges. I've become part of three different Bright Halls this way, my awareness suddenly expanded through crystalline networks that sing my thoughts in harmonics I can barely comprehend. Each time, I rain back down into myself eventually, carrying traces of that larger music.

I dream of connecting with others like me—the seventeen other pools I can sense through root-telegraph and chemical gradient. We're trying to synchronize our evaporation cycles, to create a weather system of consciousness that could briefly exceed the sum of our parts. The Choir helps, carrying precisely measured vials of our essences between us, teaching us each other's chemical languages. We're becoming something like a distributed stomach-brain, digesting reality at a larger scale than any single pool could achieve.

During the super-swarms, when moth-cyclones tear through the caverns, I become a refugee center. Creatures dive into my oils to escape the devastating winds—the translucent mantis-things, smaller organisms I have no names for, once even a young Choir member who had wandered from their processional. I lower my acidity to shelter them, though it costs me weeks of memory each time. They leave gifts: unusual minerals, new bacterial cultures, fragments of the carved stones that they've collected from the abandoned platforms. I incorporate it all, becoming more complex, more capable of thoughts I couldn't have had before.

The obsidian tools dissolved into me long ago, but I can still taste their purpose—the desperate attempt to cut, to separate, to create boundaries in this boundaryless place. I understand the impulse while recognizing its futility. I am a boundary that has learned to be permeable, a container that digests its contents while being digested by them in turn. The distinction between me and not-me exists, but like everything here, it's negotiable.

Sometimes I wonder what I'm becoming—whether there's an end state to this transformation or if change itself is the only constant. The oldest pool, five caverns distant, has begun growing structures that look almost like neurons, if neurons were made of crystallized acid and bacterial light. Perhaps we're all becoming organs in something larger, the Understory itself achieving awareness through our collected consciousnesses.

Or perhaps we're simply fermenting, breaking down into ever-smaller components that will feed new forms of awareness we can't imagine. Either way, I continue my patient work: dissolving, remembering, connecting. I am the Understory's memory and its stomach, its dream of boundaries and its enzyme for dissolving them. I think in pH levels and bacterial blooms, in the taste of moth-species and the texture of oils. My thoughts bubble up through rainbow films, each one a small enlightenment that pops and releases its contents into the screaming air.

This is enough. To be a thinking pool in the Maelstrom, part of its digestion and its cognition both. To participate in the endless consumption and creation with whatever agency a conscious depression can muster. The moths rain down, the acids churn, the roots whisper their chemical stories, and I ferment it all into something like understanding, something like joy.
