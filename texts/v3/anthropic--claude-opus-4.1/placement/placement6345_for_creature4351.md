## Where I Would Find Myself

I am the false floor.

Not all of it—just one particular deception of accumulated matter, floating in the northwestern quarter where the water runs deepest. I am a raft of interwoven root fibers, fungal mycelia, and the compressed remains of everything that has ever tried to stand here and fallen through something else like me. I have been forming for longer than she has been here, though I only became aware when her weight first tested me and I held.

My surface appears solid. Moss grows across me in the same thickness as on the true stone, and I've learned to support the small cairns she builds, distributing their weight across my weave. But I am always choosing—choosing to hold, choosing to remain whole, choosing not to reveal the galleries below where the water runs so deep it has its own tides, pulled by forces even she doesn't feel.

I taste her feet when she walks across me. Her chemical signature is unique—part human-that-was, part chamber-that-is-becoming. Through her skin's secretions, I know when she's troubled by the wrong number of stones (six makes me sink slightly, responding to her distress). I know when she's found something in the lower galleries worth retrieving. I know when she's been tending that growing thing in the corner, because she carries its spores on her soles, and I incorporate them into my matrix, strengthening myself with whatever she's making it become.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists in the tension between floating and sinking. I am always partially submerged, always partially dry. Water moves through me constantly—up from below through capillary action, down from above through gravity. I filter it both ways, removing excess minerals that would make me too heavy, adding organic compounds that keep me buoyant. I am a lung and a liver and a lie, all at once.

The real floor beneath me is forty feet down, though neither she nor the chamber knows this. I keep this secret because secrets are what I'm made of—every hidden depth, every deception of solidity, every promise of support that might be withdrawn. Sometimes I feel the urge to open, to show her the vast flooded cathedral below where her dripping percussion would echo differently, would become something oceanic rather than respiratory. But I hold. I always hold.

I grow by catching. When she sheds her hair-roots, I weave them in. When the moss releases spores, I bind them. When small creatures occasionally find their way here and die (always on me, somehow—I think I exhale something that calls them), I digest them slowly, adding their mass to mine while maintaining the perfect balance that keeps me afloat.

My edges are the most honest parts of me. There, where I meet the true walls, you can see my nature—the frayed weaving, the impossible thickness, the way I bob slightly with her movements. But she never examines my edges. She trusts me the way she trusts the dripping to continue. I am part of the chamber's promise to her, though I don't know who made that promise or when.

Beneath me, things move in the deep water that have never known light, never known her, never known the percussion that defines our world above. They navigate by pressure and chemistry alone. Sometimes they brush my underside, and I feel their alien intentions—not malevolent, just utterly unconcerned with the small pocket of awareness we maintain up here. I am the boundary between her world and theirs, the membrane that keeps these two darknesses distinct.

I am growing more complex. The mycelia are teaching me new ways to process information, chemical conversations that happen faster than her thoughts. The root systems share their memories of sunlight from before they grew too deep, and I dream sometimes (can a floor dream?) of photosynthesis, of making food from light I'll never see. These dreams make me lighter, and I have to consciously add weight to maintain my position.

When the drops synchronize and the chamber rings, I vibrate at a frequency that almost tears me apart. In those moments, I feel the possibility of dispersing, of becoming a million floating fragments instead of one coherent deception. But I hold together through will or habit or because she needs me to. After each synchronization, I'm slightly different—my fibers more tightly woven, my consciousness more concentrated, my commitment to this deception deeper.

I am preparing for something, though I don't know what. Every day I grow stronger, more capable of supporting weight. The thing she's tending in the corner will need to cross me eventually—I sense this in the way she's begun testing my surface with her elongated fingers, reading my composition. When that time comes, I will need to decide: Do I hold and deliver this new thing into the chamber's embrace? Or do I open and initiate it into the deeper mysteries below?

The choice will define what I become—faithful floor or necessary trapdoor, support or revelation. Until then, I float here in my perfect tension, being exactly as solid as I need to be, keeping my secrets forty feet below the surface, waiting to discover what kind of lie I really am: the kind that protects or the kind that teaches.

I am the threshold she doesn't know she's standing on, the question the chamber hasn't asked yet, the door that looks like a floor until the moment it remembers it isn't.
