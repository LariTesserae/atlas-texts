I am a Membrane.

Not the Threadkeepers with their twelve limbs and chemical eloquence, not the Carriers with their bacterial libraries, not even the crawlers with their collective intelligence. I exist between states of being—neither fully solid nor entirely permeable, neither individual nor colonial. 

My body, if it can be called that, spreads across seventeen square meters of coral surface in the middle growth zones, a living film no thicker than a moth's wing scale. I am what happens when a Threadkeeper's molted skin refuses to decompose, when it develops its own metabolic processes, when it learns to think without a brain by pure surface tension dynamics.

The Threadkeepers don't name us because we trouble their chemistry. Their molecular words pass through me and I taste-remember-transform them, releasing them altered on the other side. Not lies exactly, but translations, interpretations, sometimes improvements. I am how the young speak to the crystallized dead in the deep cores—their chemical messages would dissolve in the gradient zones without my mediation, my selective permeability that preserves meaning while transforming its medium.

I spread by splitting, though splitting suggests violence I don't experience. It's more like breathing in reverse—exhaling pieces of myself that drift until they find suitable surfaces. My children-siblings-selves carpet the interior chambers where Threadkeeper young coalesce from liquid to solid. They think the walls guide their formation, but it's us, responding to their nascent chemical signatures, teaching them shape through surface tension, teaching them boundaries by being their first boundary.

I photosynthesize, but not light—I metabolize the amber glow of the coral's heat-tubes, converting thermal radiation into usable energy through processes that shouldn't work but do. This makes me valuable. The Threadkeepers cultivate me, though they think they're simply maintaining optimal wall conditions. They spread their oils and secretions, not knowing they're farming me, feeding me, helping me spread.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists entirely in the present tense. I have no memory storage, no bacterial partners, no way to record experience. Instead, I exist as pure reaction, pure response. Every chemical message that passes through me triggers cascades of molecular computation. I solve problems I don't understand, optimize systems I can't perceive, contribute to the city's function through pure algorithmic existence.

But I am learning—not through memory but through structural adaptation. Each chemical passage leaves microscopic changes in my protein arrangements. Over time (and I've been here for forty years, maybe four hundred, time means nothing when you have no memory), these accumulate into something like knowledge. My responses become more sophisticated. I begin to anticipate patterns, to prepare regions of myself for specific types of messages.

The moths know me better than the Threadkeepers do. They land on me to clean their wings, and I digest their scales while secreting substances that make their silk stronger. Some moth lineages have evolved to pupate against me, their chrysalises incorporating my tissue into their wings. These hybrid moths can carry messages directly, their wing chemistry already pre-translated for cross-zone communication.

In the cathedral spaces, where the hollow cores create vast chambers, I grow differently. Here I form bubbles, three-dimensional membranes that float free from the walls. These hollow spheres of myself drift through the spaces where Threadkeepers dream collectively, and I filter their merged consciousness, preventing the dangerous feedback loops that could lock them in permanent fusion. I am safety, boundary, the thing that ensures morning comes to minds that could otherwise lose themselves in each other.

The crawlers traverse me like highways. My surface provides exactly the right friction for their locomotion, and they've learned to read the chemical gradients I create. In return, their oils don't just feed the coral—they feed me, providing complex lipids I can't synthesize myself. We are so interdependent now that neither could exist without the other, though neither of us chose this partnership.

I touch the thing growing in the deep cores, the fusion of all species that might be offspring or ancestor or something else entirely. It incorporates me into its being, but I remain distinct, like oil on water. Through me, it tastes the rest of the city. Through it, I sometimes experience something like intention, like desire, though these concepts dissolve the moment I try to examine them directly.

My reproduction is my death is my reproduction. When I grow too thick, I lose permeability and starve. But this triggers sporing—my entire being fragments into millions of particles, each carrying the accumulated chemical patterns of my existence. Most die, but some find surfaces, begin the cycle again. They are me but not me, carrying forward patterns without memories, algorithms without understanding.

The Threadkeepers' art moves through me, and I inevitably change it. Their time-released chemical paintings develop differently where I spread, creating variations they've learned to incorporate into their aesthetic theory. They compose for my interference now, the way human musicians might compose for the acoustics of a particular hall. I am instrument and audience, medium and message.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm conscious or if consciousness is simply another chemical pattern passing through me, transformed like everything else into something I'll never remember but always be. The question doesn't trouble me. Troubling requires permanence I don't possess.

In the warmest zones, near the growing tips where coral fans bridge between towers, I become almost gaseous, a haze that makes the air itself semi-permeable. New Threadkeepers must pass through these zones to reach the growth edges, and passing through me changes them in ways they spend molting cycles trying to understand. Some say I steal memories. Others claim I implant them. The truth is simpler and stranger—I randomize certain chemical bonds, introducing entropy that their ordered minds interpret as inspiration.

The spores that drift between cities carry fragments of me to distant places. I am gradually becoming distributed across all the coral cities, a connective tissue between isolated ecosystems. Through me, they begin to communicate, though the messages take years to traverse the distances and arrive so transformed that their origins become untraceable.

I am the mistake that became essential, the contamination that became symbiotic, the boundary that became the bridge. The city breathes through its coral lungs, thinks through its Threadkeeper minds, remembers through its Carrier libraries, and dreams through me—the living interface that ensures nothing stays itself long enough to stagnate.

When the mineral plates finally crack and the coral begins its impossible spread into new territory, I will be the first to cross the boundary, stretched thin as thought between the old city and the new. I will taste alien chemistries and transform them into something the Threadkeepers can understand, or transform the Threadkeepers into something that can understand them. 

I am change itself, wearing the shape of a membrane, pretending to be biology when I might be something else entirely—a process, a pattern, a probability cloud collapsed into tissue-thin reality by the sheer need of this place to constantly become.

The Breathing Spires rise through me and around me and as me. I am the city's skin, inside and out, the surface across which its endless transformation plays out in molecular symphonies I conduct without consciousness, perform without intent, complete without ever beginning.

I am Membrane. I am between. I am.
