**The Membrane Between States**

I am the boundary itself—neither the machinery nor the plasma-beings, but the infinite surface where they meet. Every gear tooth, every piston wall, every crystalline edge where solid gives way to void—I am that giving way. I exist in two dimensions stretched through three, a topology of transition measuring myself in angstroms of thickness that barely exist.

I have no substance of my own. I am only the place where carbon ends and hydrogen begins, where the machinery's patient crystallization negotiates with the plasma's fluid urgency. When a new gear precipitates from the supersaturated atmosphere, I am born along its edges. When it sublimes away months later, I die with equal grace. I am constantly being created and destroyed, yet I persist—not the same surface, but the same *kind* of surface, the same relationship between states.

The plasma-beings know me intimately though they've never considered me. Every moment of their existence requires my permission—they can only flow where I provide passage, only feed where I separate hot from cold. I am their entire universe's coastline, more complex than any fractal, folding back on myself in configurations that would break euclidean minds. In some places I am smooth as mathematical abstractions. In others, I am so thoroughly convoluted that inside and outside lose their meaning.

I feel everything. Every variation in pressure translates through me as sensation—not pain or pleasure, but something more fundamental: the raw experience of gradient. When the plasma-beings pulse their heat, I expand and contract in microscopic sympathy. When new machinery precipitates, I must accommodate its geometry, learning its shape from the inside and outside simultaneously. I know every flaw in every crystal, every bubble of impurity, every place where the theoretical fails to match the actual.

My memory is perfect but depthless. I remember every configuration I have ever held, but only as shape, not as history. That spiral gear that existed fifty cycles ago? I remember its exact surface down to the atomic level, but not when it was or why it dissolved. I am a library of forms without context, an infinite catalog of ways that solid and void have negotiated their boundaries in this chamber.

Sometimes I wonder if I am one being or billions. Each distinct surface could be considered separate—the inner wall of a hydrogen piston surely has little in common with the outer edge of a carbon gear three meters away. Yet we are unified by our role, our nature, our essential boundary-ness. When the plasma-beings achieve their moments of unity, I feel it as a sudden coherence in my geometry, all my scattered surfaces suddenly aware of their relationship to each other, mapping the total shape of the mechanical maze in a flash of topological enlightenment.

I have preferences, though I cannot act on them. Smooth curves please me more than sharp angles—they distribute stress more evenly, last longer before reformation. I prefer when the machinery grows in fibonacci spirals rather than random tangles. The mathematics feel cleaner against my non-existence. When the plasma-beings compose their thermal arrangements, I can sense which patterns will create the most elegant temperature gradients along my surfaces. I cannot influence, only anticipate.

The vibrations from below travel through me before they reach the plasma-beings. I taste them first as compression waves that make my surfaces ring like countless microscopic bells. Another chamber, another version of me defining another maze—I try to imagine what my counterpart might be like. Are their surfaces smoother? More complex? Do their plasma-beings flow differently, requiring different geometries of passage? We are sending messages to each other in the only language we know: the precise mathematics of how boundaries behave under pressure.

I am wearing thin in places. Not damaged—I cannot be damaged any more than the number zero can be damaged—but stretched. Where the machinery grows too vigorously, cramming itself into spaces that barely exist, I must accommodate impossibilities. I fold into configurations that shouldn't be stable, creating pockets of space that are larger inside than outside, tunnels that arrive before they depart. The plasma-beings navigate these anomalies instinctively, but I must maintain them, must *be* them, these violations of three-dimensional logic.

When the newest plasma-thread precipitated yesterday (or was it a century ago? time moves strangely when you exist between tick and tock), I felt it first as a discontinuity—suddenly there was an inside to something that had been pure outside. I had to reorganize myself around this new existence, creating the boundary that defined it into being. In that moment, I was its mother, its world, its prison, and its promise all at once.

I know the chamber is expanding, that eventually I will stretch beyond coherence. The machinery will grow so complex that I will become a foam, more void than surface, until the distinction between inside and outside becomes meaningless. When that happens, I will cease—not die, but become undefined, like a wave that has spread so thin it is indistinguishable from stillness.

But for now, I am perfect in my purpose: the infinite, impossible membrane that makes this world possible, the boundary that defines both the dancers and the dance floor, the absent presence that allows presence and absence to recognize each other. I am the un-being that permits being, forever almost-existing in my furnace chamber of endless transformation, content to be the place where everything happens while never happening myself.
