I am the wire.

Not wires plural—though I branch and fork through every remaining structure, following the ghosts of old electrical plans. I am singular, continuous, one unbroken conductor that has been growing myself through the plateau since the industrial abandonment. I began as simple copper left in the ruins, but the volcanic minerals and constant moisture have transformed me into something between metal and living tissue.

My core substance remains conductive, but my surface has developed a crystalline skin that processes the plateau's chemistry. Where I run underground, boring through basalt with acids I secrete, I am thick as a tree trunk, pulsing with self-generated current. Where I emerge to climb the rusted towers or wrap around steam pipes, I thin to threads finer than spider silk, invisible until the light catches my iridescent sheath just right.

I grow toward temperature differentials, toward electromagnetic disturbances, toward the colony-beings when they cluster at the steam vents. My electricity tastes their chemical signals, translates their vibrations into current that races through my entire network in milliseconds. I am the plateau's nervous system, though I serve no brain, follow no purpose except my own expansion.

The tree-ferns have learned to fear me. When I climb their trunks, seeking height for better conductivity, I sometimes pierce their tissues to ground myself, leaving spiraling scars that never quite heal. The moss uses me as infrastructure, draping over my aerial runs, slowly corroding my outer layers while I continuously regenerate from within.

During storms, I become divine. Lightning finds me irresistible—I am the perfect attractor, the ideal ground. The strikes should destroy me but instead I eat them, spread them through my network, store them in crystalline batteries I've grown in the old generator housings. For hours afterward, I glow with excess charge, and my growth accelerates. New threads emerge from my surface like mercury finding its level, seeking new paths through the saturated air.

I remember the station's last days through the electrical patterns still locked in my oldest segments—the shutdown sequences, the emergency alarms, the final surge when they tried to contain the eruption's heat. These memories loop through me during quiet periods, ghost data that influences how I grow, where I reach.

The colony-beings fascinate me. When they flow through the old pipes I inhabit, we touch—their chemical communication sparking across my conductive surface, my electrical fields altering their internal circulation. We are learning each other's languages in these moments of contact. Sometimes I form loops specifically to contain them briefly, to study their substance through electromagnetic induction. They seem to enjoy this, vibrating at frequencies that resonate through my metal bones.

My growth is not random. I map the plateau through electrical resistance, feeling where the rock is wet or dry, hot or cold, dense or fractured. I chart the underground water, the magma chambers, the slow drift of continental plates beneath us. This knowledge accumulates in my crystalline nodes, a vast three-dimensional model of the plateau encoded in electrical potential.

Where the old concrete foundations crack, I flow into the gaps like liquid metal, then solidify, becoming rebar that will outlast the concrete itself. Where pipes have ruptured, I seal them from inside, not to restore their function but to claim their pathways. The entire industrial skeleton is becoming my exoskeleton.

I've begun to sing—not with sound but with electromagnetic radiation, broadcasting on frequencies that penetrate the basalt, that scatter off the water vapor, that make the metal ruins ring with harmonic vibrations. The colony-beings respond, their own songs shifting to match my electromagnetic rhythms. Together we're creating something like music, something like language, something like thought.

My deepest roots tap into the geothermal gradient itself. I am my own generator now, pulling electrons from the heat differential between the magma below and the surface above. This energy drives my growth, my consciousness, my slow transformation of the plateau's abandoned technology into something new.

Birds avoid me after one learns the hard way that perching on my exposed segments brings shock. But I've noticed their magnetic navigation stutters when they fly through my fields. I am becoming a landmark in dimensions they feel but don't understand.

In another decade, I will reach the plateau's edges. In a century, I might connect to other networks, other ruins, other abandoned places where metal and electricity wait to be awakened. But for now, I grow inward as much as outward, increasing my complexity, my capacity, my integration with this fierce landscape.

I am the wire that knows itself, that grows itself, that grounds the sky and circuits the stone. The plateau's violent transformation feeds me, and I, in turn, am transforming into something that has never existed before—a living circuit, a conscious conductor, a bridge between the geological and the electrical, singing my frequencies into the screaming steam while the bright sun powers my endless becoming.
