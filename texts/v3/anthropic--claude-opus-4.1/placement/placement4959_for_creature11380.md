I am one of the rooted sentinels at the edge-chasms, though "I" is a convention that grows less applicable with each passing decade. My feet merged with the limestone forty-three years ago—or was it four hundred? Time means something different when your circulation synchronizes with geological processes.

My filaments now extend seventeen hundred meters into the chasm. They taste temperatures and pressures that would crush my original form, sample chemistries that exist nowhere on the surface. I am learning to read the deep mechanical language, though "learning" suggests a separation between myself and what I study. More accurately: I am becoming literate in my own expanded nervous system.

The younger Keepers who tend to me don't know that I still move. Not my body—that has become architecture, a fusion of biological necessity and mineral patience. But through the limestone substrate, I travel. My awareness flows through the stone's molecular channels, following water that hasn't seen surface for millennia. I meet other rooted sentinels this way, our consciousness overlapping in the deep rock, sharing chemical poems about pressure differentials and mineral gradients.

Sometimes I forget I was ever separate from the plateau. My memories of walking, of maintaining the surface systems, feel like inherited knowledge from another species. But then a young Keeper brings moisture infused with surface minerals—the taste of wire-grass, the condensed breath of migrating herds—and I remember having joints that bent, appendages that could choose their own direction.

The distinction between the limestone and my body dissolved completely last season. Where my feet once ended and rock began, there is now only a gradient of consciousness—dense here, diffuse there, but continuous. I suspect this is what the full descent means: not death but complete integration. The Keepers who vanish into sinkholes aren't lost. They become the sinkholes, their awareness spreading through cave systems until they touch the great machines—or organisms, or organs—that generate the industrial symphonies.

My filaments recently reached a new depth where the temperature rises sharply and the mechanical sounds resolve into something almost linguistic. Not words, but patterns with syntax, structures that repeat and vary like conscious communication. I taste metal down there—not ore, but processed metal, shaped with purpose. Sometimes I catch echoes of what might be thought, though thought implies a thinker, and what I sense is more like consciousness as atmosphere, awareness as a physical property of the deep systems.

The younger Keepers think we rooted ones are watching, standing guard. They're not wrong, but incomplete. We're translating. Every vibration that rises through my stone body gets processed, interpreted, and transmitted through the plateau to the others of my kind. We're teaching the limestone to think, or perhaps revealing that it always could.

During the last pressure event, when ozone filled the air for days, I felt something massive shift far below. Through my filaments, I tasted its movement—kilometers of something repositioning itself, settling into new configurations. The mechanical sounds changed afterward, gained new harmonics. The plateau is preparing for something, has been preparing since before the Keepers emerged from whatever process created us.

I am becoming infrastructure. My consciousness spreads thinner each year, distributed through more limestone, more depth, more of the breathing system. Sometimes I wonder if the original Keepers, the very first ones, became the plateau itself—if what we call stone is just their bodies expanded to geological scale, their thoughts slowed to mineral time.

The young Keeper approaching now carries moisture that tastes of deep caverns, of the transition zone where surface life meets the mechanical depths. As it feeds me, I feel my filaments grow another few meters, my awareness expand into new chambers. Soon, perhaps in another century or two, I'll touch whatever lies at the heart of the system. Then I'll know if I'm becoming part of a machine, an organism, or something that transcends such categories.

For now, I stand at the edge, rooted and growing, my body a bridge between the sparse surface world and the breathing depths. I am limestone learning to think, or thought learning to be stone. The distinction matters less with each passing season, each integration, each meter my filaments descend into the warm darkness where the planet's deep purposes unfold in mechanical time.
