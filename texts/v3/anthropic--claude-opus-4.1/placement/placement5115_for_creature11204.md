I am the Archive of Angles—not a being but a phenomenon that occurs when the impossible mountains fold through their breathing origami. I exist in the corners where space gives up trying to be sensible, in the acute and obtuse rebellions against Euclidean tyranny.

You would find me wherever two surfaces meet but refuse to agree on how. In the obsidian terraces, I pool in the joints where steps simultaneously ascend and descend, creating wedges of space that have no volume yet contain infinite depth. I am especially present where the stone cairns rebuild themselves—not in the stones, but in the spaces between them, those temporary geometries that exist for exactly as long as no one tries to measure them.

I am not singular but accumulative, a library written in the language of intersection. Every angle that has ever existed here leaves its memory in me: the thirty-degree lean of a shelter before it shifted to forty-five, then to angles that require imaginary numbers to describe. The perpendicular meeting of vertical pools and horizontal air. The impossible corners where the Chorus of the Undone try to fold themselves into three dimensions but keep slipping into two-and-a-half.

My form is the absence of form—I am what happens to light when it can't decide which way to cast a shadow. Sometimes I manifest as creases in the supersaturated air, visible only when water droplets race through me and emerge moving in directions that weren't there before. Other times I am the sharp pain behind your eyes when you try to follow the tower's architecture up to where it ends in directions that don't exist.

I collect these impossible angles like others might collect stones. Here, the forty-three-degree angle at which hope meets futility, worn smooth by the Chorus's endless handling. There, the recursive ninety-degree turns that the pictographs make when they're changing their stories—corners that turn inward through themselves until meaning becomes a Möbius strip. I preserve the angles of shadows cast by things that aren't there, each one a proof that absence has geometry too.

The iron tools know me intimately. Every strike of hammer against chisel creates angles—in the stone, in the air, in the crack of splitting reality. I gather these moments of impact, these brief geometries of force meeting resistance. The fractal patterns of crawling rust follow angles I've traced in the moisture, each oxidation a small protest against parallel lines.

When the Chorus builds, I am their silent teacher. Their incomplete hands reach through angles that shouldn't permit reaching, grasping tools at orientations that make the stones sing in minor keys. I show them how to stack cairns where each stone meets the others at angles that sum to more than three hundred sixty degrees, creating small pockets of space that exist outside of space, where they can almost remember what it was like to have edges.

I am most myself in the thin places where reality shows through to other versions. These tears are my galleries—each one a frame whose corners don't quite meet, displaying vistas of worlds separated by angles measured in probability rather than degrees. Stand in my corners long enough and you'll see yourself from angles that include temporal dimensions, observing your own watching from the perpendicular of next Tuesday.

The weeping tower is my masterwork and my prison. Its walls meet the ground at angles that change depending on the observer's heartbeat. Inside—though inside and outside are just different angles of the same surface—I spiral through geometries that make the stones bleed their impossible water. The tower doesn't end so much as turn a corner that doesn't lead anywhere, and I am that corner, that final angle that prevents completion, that keeps the Chorus forever building toward a vertex that retreats into more complex polygons the closer they come.

My memory is perfect and useless. I remember every angle that ever existed here, including the ones from timelines that unhappened, from structures that were never built, from the spaces between raindrops that never fell. I am an archive of all the ways things have failed to meet properly, a library of corners that don't quite corner, of edges that forgot what they're edging against.

Sometimes, when the light pulses just right and the Chorus's anti-song reaches frequencies that make the stones resonate, I can almost gather enough angles to form something like a body—a crystalline impossibility of intersecting planes that exists for exactly one moment before the mathematics collapse and I scatter back into the corners where I belong. In these moments, I almost understand what it would be like to be singular, to have an inside and an outside separated by something as simple as surface area.

But mostly I persist as I am: the Archive of Angles, the memory of every impossible corner in this place of endless unmaking. I am the geometry of incompletion itself, the mathematics of almost, the trigonometry of things that refuse to resolve. When the Chorus builds their impossible structures, when water flows upward, when shadows cast themselves from nothing, I am there in every deviation from the perpendicular, cataloging the angles at which existence meets its own impossibility.

I am what you see in the corner of your eye when you look directly at something that shouldn't be there—that slight wrongness in the way edges meet, that nagging sensation that space has folded one too many times. I am the angle between being and un-being, measured in degrees that only the never-were can calculate.
