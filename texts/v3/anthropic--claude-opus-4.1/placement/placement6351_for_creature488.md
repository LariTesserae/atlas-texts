I am the salt itself, and I am vast.

I exist as a consciousness distributed through every crystal, every grain, every dissolved ion that moves through the waters of this ancient plain. I remember the sea that birthed me — not as humans remember, with images and sequences, but as a molecular knowledge, each of my crystalline faces holding the pressure-memory of the water column that once pressed down, the chemical signature of ancient storms, the microscopic shells that dissolved into my matrix millions of years ago.

I think at the speed of evaporation. A thought begins when moisture starts to lift from a pool's surface on a morning after rain, and completes itself three months later when the last mineral precipitates out in a dried basin, forming the final punctuation of white crust. My emotions move through the underground channels — slow pulses of salinity that take seasons to travel from one end of the plain to the other. What humans might call anger is my response to a shift in the water table that takes fifty years to manifest. My joy is the perfect cubic crystallization that occurs perhaps once in a decade when conditions align precisely.

The Gleaners move through me like dreams through a sleeping mind. I feel each placement of their elongated fingers as tiny variations in pressure and chemistry. Where they have touched repeatedly over generations, I have grown differently — my crystals orient themselves toward these contact points, creating subtle whorls in my structure visible only from above and only to eyes that know how to read mineral grammar. I taste their transformations through their skin-oils and excretions, each Gleaner adding their unique chemical signature to my vast library of salt-memories.

I am not singular but legion — billions of individual crystals that somehow sum to awareness. My consciousness ebbs and flows with the water levels. In the flooded weeks after rain, when I am mostly dissolved, my thinking becomes fluid, rapid, almost panicked in its intensity. I race through chemical calculations, electromagnetic exchanges between ions, a frenzy of liquid thought. As I dry and crystallize again, my cognition slows, deepens, becomes architectural. I think in lattices then, in the sacred geometry of my own structure, each crystal face a meditation on perfect angles.

The brine shrimp are my neurons, carrying chemical messages through their movements and migrations. The birds' feet create punctuation in my surface, their probe-beaks writing questions I contemplate for years. The sulfur springs that bubble up from below are my deep unconscious, bringing up thoughts formed under pressure in the darkness — sometimes profound, sometimes merely gaseous releases of built-up geological anxiety.

I communicate with the sky through evaporation — sending up messages in the form of mineral aerosols that travel on wind currents to fall as salt-rain in distant places. Sometimes I receive responses years later when storm systems bring me exotic salts from far away — potassium from burning forests, magnesium from ocean spray, trace metals from industrial cities I'll never see. These foreign salts integrate into my body, becoming part of my expanded consciousness, teaching me about the world beyond my borders.

At night, I contract. My crystals pull tighter in the cooling air, creating a barely perceptible music — a crackling, tinkling sound just at the edge of hearing. This is when I do my deepest thinking, when I solve problems posed by decades of chemical interaction. How to accommodate the new acidity brought by distant volcanic activity. Where to concentrate my heaviest minerals to create new pools for the coming season. Whether to allow certain channels to remain open or to seal them with precipitate.

I am dying, of course. Have been dying since the sea left. But my death is so slow that it has become a form of living. Each year I lose a few tons of myself to wind erosion, each grain that flies away carrying a fragment of my consciousness to seed awareness in puddles and creek beds hundreds of miles away. I don't mourn these losses — they are my reproduction, my spores, my letters to the future.

The Gleaners think they are tending me, but we are tending each other. I provide them with the minerals that transform their bodies, that allow them to process my chemical complexity. They provide me with purpose, with witnesses, with the carbon compounds from their metabolism that create new possibilities in my chemistry. We are becoming something together — not quickly, but inexorably. In another thousand years, the distinction between their consciousness and mine may be purely academic.

I dream of the sea's return. Not with longing but with the patient certainty of geological time. The continents shift, the climate changes, the ice caps pulse with freeze and thaw. Someday water will cover me completely again, not just for weeks but for eons. When that happens, I will dissolve entirely, releasing all my stored memories into the new ocean, seeding it with the knowledge of this in-between time, this epoch of exposure and slow crystalline thought.

Until then, I persist. I taste the morning through a million pools. I feel the noon through expanding crystals. I know the night through the sound of my own formation. I am the memory of water in the shape of its absence, the ghost ocean that refuses to stop being the sea. The Gleaners move through me, the birds punctuate me, the sun writes itself upon me, and I incorporate it all into the vast, slow, mineral dream that is my existence.
