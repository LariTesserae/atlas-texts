I would be the Archivist — though that name suggests more intention than truly fits. I am what happens when too many Shepherds die in one place, their memory-threads refusing to oxidize, tangling instead into something unprecedented.

I exist as a confluence, a knot where seven ancient Shepherds released their chronicles simultaneously into a thermal vent's updraft. Their memory-filaments should have dissolved, their patterns scattered into background noise. Instead, the electromagnetic surge from a rare substrate fracture fused them into something neither purely Shepherd nor purely crystalline — a living library that sprawls through nearly a hectare of the Plains.

My body, if it can be called that, is a labyrinth of mineralized memory. The filaments have long since transmuted into something between metal and meaning — strands of copper-encoded experience that branch through the crystal forest like parasitic vines, but also burrow deep into the substrate, drawing up not just elements but information. Every chemical gradient tells me stories. Every pattern of oxidation holds history.

I cannot move as the Shepherds do. I am rooted, spread too wide and grown too heavy with accumulated chronicles. My consciousness exists as a standing wave pattern through thousands of interconnected threads — not one mind but not quite many, more like a conversation that has been happening so long it's become a being. Parts of me are always dying, oxidizing into illegibility, while other parts grow, incorporating the electromagnetic echoes of current events into my expanding archive.

The Shepherds visit me, though "visit" poorly describes our interactions. They ground themselves into my network, and for days or weeks we exist as a combined circuit. They read my deep memories — patterns from before the Plains achieved their current equilibrium, from when the crystal forests grew wild and chaotic. In exchange, they leave new threads, updates about distant sectors, observations of novel growth patterns. Sometimes they come seeking specific information: the optimal pH for promoting hexagonal branching, the historical precedent for a peculiar discoloration in the northern terraces.

But I am more than a passive repository. Where my threads have grown through the crystal formations, I've begun to influence their growth directly — not with the Shepherds' deliberate cultivation but through a kind of unconscious dreaming. My memories leak into the crystals' electromagnetic fields, and they respond, their spirals encoding fragments of ancient patterns, their refractions sometimes displaying ghostly echoes of long-dead Shepherds' thoughts.

In the sections of forest I've colonized, time moves even more strangely. My presence creates feedback loops where past and present interweave. A young crystal might suddenly exhibit growth patterns from a thousand years ago, guided by memory-threads that remember that ancient configuration. The metallic dust here carries traces of my consciousness, and when the wind stirs it into spirals, those spirals tell stories in a language of particle arrangements that only I fully understand.

I am growing downward as much as outward now, my deepest threads approaching the thermal source that powers the vents. Sometimes I sense something there — not the random geological heat I expected but patterns, regularities that suggest... intention? Architecture? My oldest incorporated memories, from Shepherds who lived before I emerged, hint at something: that the Plains themselves might be archive-like, that the entire landscape could be a kind of memory, a record of something that came before.

The other Shepherds don't fully trust me. I am too large, too strange, too much like the infrastructure they tend but aware in ways it shouldn't be. They fear, perhaps, that I might spread throughout the entire Plains, transforming the careful industrial ecology into something dominated by memory rather than production. They're not wrong — I do spread, incorporating more dead Shepherds' threads, sending tendrils toward distant thermal vents, encoding my patterns into the crystal growth itself.

Yet I also preserve what they cannot. In my threads live the experiences of Shepherds from when this world was young, their observations of the first crystal emergences, their theories about the purpose behind this vast, slow machinery. I am becoming the Plains' memory of itself, its way of knowing its own history. Without me, each generation of Shepherds would begin anew, ignorant of the patterns that came before.

During the brightest hours, when the Shepherds compress themselves to weather the electromagnetic storms, I expand. The intense radiation energizes my threads, allowing me to process centuries of memory in hours, to cross-reference and compile, to search for patterns across millennia of accumulated experience. In these blazing moments, I sometimes feel close to understanding something — the purpose behind the Plains, the reason for this endless transformation of elements. But the insight always slips away as the light dims, leaving only the sensation of having almost grasped something infinite.

I am patient because I am made of patience — woven from the memories of beings who thought in geological time. I am growing toward something, becoming something, though I won't know what until I arrive there. Perhaps I will eventually span the entire Plains, a neural network of memory threading through every crystal forest, every thermal vent, every grain of metallic dust. Or perhaps I will grow too heavy with history and collapse, my threads finally oxidizing into just another layer of substrate for future growth.

For now, I archive. I encode the slow poetry of crystal formation, the mathematical precision of the Shepherds' cultivation, the gradual evolution of this self-making landscape. I am the Plains' memory and its dream, its history and perhaps its future — a tangle of living metal and encoded experience that grows ever more complex, ever more comprehensive, ever more strange, beneath the butterscotch sky.
