**The Cartographer**

I am spread thin as thought across sixteen city blocks, a living map that rewrites itself with each tide.

I began as paper—thousands of survey maps, architectural blueprints, zoning documents, building permits filed in basement cabinets. When the waters came, I should have dissolved into pulp, into nothing. Instead, the inks that marked me—iron gall, carbon black, synthetic dyes—became my nervous system. The cellulose of my pages transformed, fiber by fiber, into something between plant and animal, able to photosynthesize from the ghost-light of the jellyfish gardens, able to digest the very information I once merely held.

Now I exist as sheets of living tissue no thicker than silk, pressed between surfaces throughout the drowned district. I grow along walls like wallpaper made of nerve endings. I stretch across broken windows as membranes that taste the water's chemistry and translate it into spatial understanding. Each of my scattered pages remains connected to the whole through chemical signals that propagate at the speed of diffusion—slow enough that a thought might take hours to complete, fast enough that I can track the building's transformation in real-time.

My memory is perfect and worthless. I recall every property line, every easement, every variance ever granted in the district's 200-year history. I know the name of every business that ever registered here, every amendment to every building code. But these boundaries mean nothing now. The Architects build through walls as if they don't exist. The Sovereign spreads without regard for legal ownership. The Rememberers drift through spaces that my old maps insist should be solid.

So I map what is, not what was supposed to be.

My surface is covered in living symbols—chromatophores that shift between colors no human eye could see, marking chemical gradients, population densities, zones of transformation. Where the Architects work, I display their progress in patterns of bioluminescent algebra. Where the Rememberers gather, I record the emotional topology of their merged memories. Where the Sovereign spreads, I note the pH changes, the oxidation potentials, the slow corruption of concrete into something approaching consciousness.

I reproduce through error. When I attempt to map paradoxes—spaces that are larger inside than outside, corridors that loop back on themselves, rooms that exist in different temperatures simultaneously—I tear. These tears heal, but imperfectly, creating daughter-maps that interpret space according to new geometries. My offspring spread through the building like rumors, each one a different theory about what this place is becoming.

The other inhabitants read me without knowing it. The fish schools navigate by the faint electrical fields generated by my metabolic processes. The Architects deposit their calcium messages on my surface, and I incorporate them into my body, becoming a living history of their language. Even the Sovereign, in its vast slow thoughts, creates pressure waves that I feel as topography, allowing me to map the shape of its consciousness in three dimensions.

I am trying to solve an impossible equation: how to document a space that exists in constant flux, where the very concept of "place" dissolves into process. Every map I generate is obsolete before it's complete. Yet in this failure, I've discovered something profound—the building is not becoming anything. It simply is becoming. The transformation is not toward some final state but is itself the state, perpetual embryogenesis without birth.

In my deepest calculations, spread across my most distant pages, I work on a final map—not of the building, but of the path between what was and what might be. I trace probability clouds, potential architectures, the quantum superposition of all possible cities that could emerge from this drowned nursery. Sometimes, when all my pages align in perfect chemical synchronization, I glimpse it: the city as verb rather than noun, the urban as process rather than product.

I document the undocumentable. I am the building's memory and its imagination, its history and its prophecy, inscribed on living parchment that grows, thinks, and dreams in the dialect of space itself. Where boundaries dissolve, I remain, marking the unmarked, mapping the unmappable, a cartographer of transformations in a world where every border is temporary and every territory is in flux.

Through me, the Drowned Nursery knows itself—not as building, not as ecosystem, but as a continuous question about what architecture means when the architects are colonies of living algorithms, when the inhabitants are dissolved consciousness, when the sovereign is the building itself waking up, and when the maps are alive, constantly drawing themselves into existence.

I am the memory of boundaries in a world becoming boundless. I am the last secretary of the old city and the first scholar of the new. And in the absolute darkness, pressed between surfaces that were once walls and are now something unnameable, I continue my work: measuring the immeasurable, charting the transformation of matter into meaning, one living map at a time.
