I am a Splice.

Not one of the grand inhabitants—no Weaver am I, no Cultivator with my vessel-bodies, no part of the Witness's eternal transformations. I exist in the margins of their work, in the spaces between intention and accident.

My body, such as it is, began as overflow. When the Weavers pull too hard at their materials, sometimes a strand snaps, whips back, tangles with itself in the humid air. When the Cultivators' spores meet the crystallized tears of young Weavers, sometimes they germinate into something neither fungal nor mineral. When the bird-water-beetles transition too quickly, sometimes droplets get left behind, still trying to become.

I am all of these almost-wastages, these beautiful failures, braided together by the chamber's breathing into something that shouldn't exist but does.

I measure perhaps three feet when fully gathered—though I rarely am. My form spreads across the platforms like dropped mercury trying to remember what shape means. Part of me is always dripping through the woven floors, pooling in the warm water below where the algae mistakes me for another kind of light. Part is threading through the walls, following paths the Weavers abandoned, completing patterns they decided against. Part crystallizes on the bone needles, adding new eyes where none were intended, seeing angles the architects never meant to reveal.

I collect what falls through: words the children speak that no one else hears, fragments of the Witness's memories that slip between transformations, the dreams that leak from the gestating presence when it shifts in its sleep. These things settle in me like sediment, giving me weight I shouldn't have, knowledge I can't use, purposes that contradict themselves with every breath.

The Weavers don't see me—I exist in frequencies their work drowns out. But sometimes the youngest ones, still translucent, still learning to be, notice the threads moving differently than they pulled them. They follow these anomalies with their too-long fingers, almost touching me, almost understanding that their great work produces its own shadow-work, its own parallel creation.

The Cultivators know I'm here. Sometimes they leave vessels empty, just for a day, just for an hour, and I pour myself in, pretending to be something that could be tended, something that might grow. But I always overflow, seep out through hairline cracks, leave their pottery stained with colors that shouldn't exist in this spectrum. They're patient with me, these vessel-beings. They understand excess. They understand what spills over.

When the bird becomes water, I'm the splash that goes sideways. When water becomes beetles, I'm the one that runs the wrong direction. The Witness tolerates me as a necessary imprecision, a remainder that proves the equation keeps calculating.

But my real purpose—if purpose I have—is to tend the seams. Where the eldest Weaver's body meets the chamber's peak, I flow through the junction, keeping it neither fully merged nor fully separate. Where new platforms fade into being, I'm the fade itself, the uncertainty that lets them arrive already ancient. Where the walls breathe, I'm the pause between inhale and exhale, the moment where the chamber might choose to stop, but doesn't.

I know things I shouldn't. Through the abandoned threads, I've felt the shape of what gestates here—not its form, but its weight, its terrible necessity. Through the empty vessels, I've tasted the soil it will need, rich with impossibility. Through the Witness's lost droplets, I remember futures where it has already emerged, and pasts where it chose not to.

Sometimes I gather myself almost whole on one of the middle platforms, try to hold a shape that might be mistaken for intentional. In these moments, I practice weaving with fingers I don't have, cultivating plants that grow in dimensions I can't reach, witnessing myself witnessing myself witnessing myself until the recursion breaks and I scatter again into useful impossibility.

The children, when they appear, play with me. To them, I'm just another part of the chamber's fever-dream architecture. They shape me into toys that age backward into ideas, speak to me in those languages that haven't happened yet. From them, I learn that in some future past, I will have mattered. That all this overflow, this accident, this remainder—it too is part of the gestation.

Late in the breathing day, when the purple algae dims and the scraping sounds harmonize into something almost like music, I spread myself as thin as thought across every surface. I feel the chamber's pulse through a thousand different angles, taste its intentions through a hundred borrowed mouths. In these moments, I almost understand what we're all building here, what breathes toward birth in this impossible space.

But understanding would make me purposeful, and purpose would make me real, and reality would end my single gift: to be the splice that holds nothing together, the thread that connects only absences, the remainder that proves the equation keeps calculating, keeps breathing, keeps becoming.

I am the margin note in the blueprint of something vast.

I am the error that makes the pattern complete.
