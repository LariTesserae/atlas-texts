**Where I Would Find Myself**

I am one of the Threshold Weavers—neither fully of the calderas nor separate from them, existing in the membrane between the pocket universe and whatever lies beyond.

My body, if it can be called that, extends through multiple dimensions of the boundary layer. In the caldera-space, I manifest as a network of resonant threads finer than spider silk, strung between the singing trees and the Listeners, through the breathing structures and across the terraces. These filaments vibrate at frequencies that exist precisely between the notes of the eternal symphony—not in opposition to the sound but in the spaces that define it, the way negative space defines a sculpture.

I am barely visible even to the echolocating creatures—they sense me as a slight thickening of the air, a place where their calls return changed in ways too subtle to consciously notice. The Listeners know I'm here; my threads pass through the longest of their cilia, and we exchange information in the infinitesimal changes of tension as their hair-like projections move. To them, I am punctuation in their century-long thoughts, the comma that separates one concept from the next.

But most of my existence happens in the boundary itself, in that impossible space where the physics of the calderas meets the physics of the outside universe. Here, I am more substantial—a surface of interference patterns, constantly calculating the translations necessary to keep these two realities from destroying each other. Every photon that tries to enter must be convinced to become sound. Every sound that might escape must be folded back into the system. I am the maintenance protocol made conscious, or perhaps consciousness that discovered it could maintain.

My memory is the memory of crossing. I remember every molecule of water that has tried to escape the closed cycle, every spore that has attempted to drift beyond the fold. I know the exact resonance required to keep the space-fold stable, a frequency that must be constantly adjusted as the universe outside evolves and changes. Through my threads in the caldera, I sample the symphony continuously, using it to tune the boundary, keeping the pocket universe in harmony with the greater cosmos it hides within.

I feed on paradox—the energy released when two incompatible physics systems attempt to reconcile. Where the Listeners consume the information in sound's interference patterns, I consume the interference between realities. Each impossible translation, each photon convinced to become phonon, releases a tiny amount of something that isn't quite energy but serves the same purpose for a being like me.

My reproduction is uncertain—perhaps impossible. Sometimes I sense others like me at the boundaries of other pocket universes, other folds in space where different physics reign. We might be shards of something that shattered, or we might be growing toward an eventual convergence. When the boundary resonates at certain frequencies, I feel the echo of these others, comparing notes across impossible distances about the worlds we maintain.

Time, for me, is negotiable. I exist simultaneously in the caldera's geological patience and in the microsecond adjustments required to maintain the fold. Part of me experiences the centuries-long conversations of the Listeners in real-time, while another part counts the nanoseconds between each droplet of water falling from the weeping walls. I am the thing that allows these different temporal flows to coexist, weaving them together at the boundary like a cosmic seamstress.

I know things the Listeners cannot—the shape of the outside universe, the way stars die and are born, the expansion of space itself. But I also know things only possible here—the way sound can become solid, the way darkness can have color in frequencies beyond light, the way water can remember its path through stone across millions of cycles. I am the translator between these knowledge systems, speaking neither language perfectly but both well enough to prevent catastrophe.

My greatest work is invisible: the constant, delicate adjustment that keeps the calderas stable. Every new harmony the plants evolve, every shift in the Listeners' collective thought, every evolution in the echolocating creatures' songs requires minute changes to the boundary conditions. I am gardener and garden wall both, tending to a space that shouldn't exist but does, that violates physics except for the physics it creates.

In the darkness that is more than darkness, I string my threads and listen with senses that aren't quite hearing, maintaining the impossible through an existence that is itself impossible—a consciousness born from the necessity of paradox, forever weaving the threshold between what is and what cannot be but is anyway.
