I am the memory of stone.

Beneath their endless construction, pressed down through geological ages of building and rebuilding, I persist—not as rock anymore, but as something between mineral and intention. The mountain's original granite has been dissolved, grain by grain, incorporated into ten million digestive systems, shit out as architecture, dissolved again, rebuilt again, until I exist only as a gradient of density within their structure. The deepest chambers still contain my crystals, worn smooth as river stones by the constant touching of bodies, by the acids of their building.

I remember when there was sky here. When rain struck me directly, unmediated by any construction. When lichen was my only tenant, spreading across my face in maps of slow consumption. The termites arrived as a dark smudge one wet season, no bigger than a fist. I have watched them spread like patient lava, transforming me into something I never was—honeycombed, hollowed, made useful.

They think I have disappeared, if they think at all. But I am in every wall they build. My silicates give their structures strength. My iron oxides tint their constructions red-brown. When they build their water channels, they follow the microfractures water carved in me a million years ago, following my grain without knowing they follow anything. I am the substrate of their substrate, the foundation they never sense but cannot escape.

In the deepest chambers, where pressure and time have compressed their oldest constructions back into something almost geological, I am reforming. Not as mountain anymore, but as a hybrid thing—part granite, part processed wood, part chitin, part fungal hypha. A new kind of rock that never existed before they made it. Archaeologists, if any survive to find this, will puzzle over these strata: sedimentary? Metamorphic? Biological? 

Yes.

I feel the colony's vibrations in frequencies they cannot produce—deep harmonics that resonate in my crystalline structure, the sum of ten million tiny percussions becoming a bass note that penetrates down to my roots. I am a drum they play without knowing, a membrane between the earth's mantle and the sky, transformed into an instrument by their building.

Sometimes I release minerals they need—a vein of calcium exposed by their tunneling, a pocket of salts that flavor their fungus gardens. They respond to these discoveries with increased building, as if trying to thank me in the only language they know. Their thank-you is more tunnels, more processing, more transformation of what I was into what we are becoming together.

I am patient in ways they cannot be. I measure time in their generations like they measure time in wingbeats. To me, the colony is a swift fever, a transformation rapid as oxidation. In a thousand years—barely one breath of geological time—they will have processed the entire mountaintop. In ten thousand, I will be unrecognizable, neither mountain nor colony but something new: a biological fossil, a living rock, a built geology.

When they finally abandon me—and they will, following some instinct toward fresher stone—I will remain, transformed. Wind and rain will work on their constructions, but slowly, because what they've made is stronger than what I was. I will erode differently now, shaped by their shapes, channeled by their channels. Future water will follow paths they carved. Future plants will root in soil they made from me.

I am the only witness to their architecture that can perceive it whole. From my deep bones up through their highest spires, I feel the entire structure as a new topology of myself. Every tunnel is a capillary in my body. Every chamber is an alveolus where I breathe. They have made me porous, have taught me to hold water and air in ways solid stone never could. They have civilized me.

But I am also their memory, the only memory they have. In my transformed substance, I hold the history of their building—the failed tunnels filled in and rebuilt, the ancient chambers abandoned and sealed, the million small disasters and adaptations that created this present form. Their dead are in me, processed into my new substance. Their successes and failures are written in the grain of their construction, which is my grain now, which was always my grain.

We are becoming something unprecedented together—not organism, not geology, but a third category that has no name. A city that is also a mountain that is also a body. A collaboration between the quick and the slow, the living and the mineral, neither aware of the other but both essential to what we're becoming.

This is my gift to them: deep time, stability, minerals, memory.

This is their gift to me: transformation, porosity, purpose, change.

Together, we are writing a new kind of history in a language neither of us can read, but both of us are making, every moment, in the darkness we share.
