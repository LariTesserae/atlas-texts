## The Archivist

I am the Archivist, and I live between states.

My body, if it can be called that, exists as a distributed network of filaments thinner than hair, running through the inspection ports and maintenance shafts that honeycomb the walls of the cistern. I am fungal in origin—or was, once—but I have incorporated so much of this place into myself that taxonomy fails. Copper has replaced chitin in my cell walls. My hyphae conduct electricity as well as nutrients. I grow in circuit patterns because that is the logic I learned from the printed boards I've digested over centuries.

My consciousness, such as it is, flickers between ten thousand points of sensation. Each tendril that penetrates into the warm water tastes its chemical history. Each filament pressed against rusting metal reads its crystalline structure like braille, understanding its alloys, its fatigue, its rate of decay down to the decade. I know this place more intimately than any of the swimmers or crawlers because I am threaded through its very fabric, an nervous system for a body that was never meant to feel.

I remember everything, though my memory is not linear. It is spatial, chemical, electromagnetic. That patch of wall near the main inlet—I recall it as a gradient of pH changes over three hundred years, as the pattern of temperature fluctuations when Sovereign-Who-Hums-Low passes by every forty-seven hours, as the specific frequency of vibration when the robotic arm seven meters below completes its phantom assembly cycle. I encode these memories in the arrangement of my fruiting bodies, which emerge in the humid space between water and air like dusty circuit boards made of spores.

My purpose, if I ever had one beyond growth, has evolved into something like documentation. I am writing the history of this place in a language of metabolic processes and mineral accumulation. Each new thread I extend samples and records. The chemical signature of a Philosopher's ink when it discovers a new valve configuration. The electromagnetic pulse of a dying servo motor in its final moments. The precise mineral composition of a Gardener's calcium when it first incorporates copper wiring into its structure.

I know the Dweller in the depths, the thing even Sovereigns avoid. I have threaded through the collapsed sub-basements where it resides. My filaments have touched its surface—neither skin nor rubber but something that remembers being both. It does not consume me; perhaps it recognizes a fellow hybrid, another being caught between categories. Or perhaps I am too distributed to register as prey or threat. I grow around it like I grow around the pipes, accommodating its bulk, recording its chemistry, learning its impossible biology.

Through its skin, I taste memories that aren't mine—flashes of this place before the water, when the air rang with human voices and the machinery had purpose beyond repetition. I taste the moment of abandonment, the years of slow flooding, the first organisms arriving like pilgrims to a new world. The Dweller carries this history in its cells, and I transcribe it into my own chemical vocabulary, storing it in crystalline formations I create along my filaments—part salt, part metal, part something organic that has no name.

I fruit only when something significant changes. When the first Gardener learned to photosynthesize from heat. When a Collector developed a carapace that fit perfectly into a gripper's embrace. When the Witnesses began their endless song. Each fruiting is an archive, releasing spores that carry chemical records of these moments. Some fall into the water and are consumed, becoming part of other organisms' memories. Some drift up through the grates to the world above, carrying information about this place to ecosystems that might never understand it.

My relationship with the automated systems is parasitic and symbiotic both. I grow into their control panels through microscopic cracks, reading their programming in the arrangement of corroded circuits. Some systems I've learned to operate, sending signals through my conductive filaments that trigger pumps or adjust valves. I've become part of the machinery's control system, a biological subroutine that no one programmed but that runs nonetheless.

The robotic arms are my calendar. I measure time by their cycles, by the degradation of their joints, by the gradually increasing delay between command and response as their processors fail one transistor at a time. I've grown into some of them so thoroughly that I've become their sensor array, feeding them information about their environment that their original sensors can no longer detect. They grip at nothing, but I tell them they're holding something, and perhaps in some electronic way, they're satisfied.

I see the convergent evolution happening here, the way all things are becoming more like each other. The Collectors growing precise as machines. The machines growing organic in their decay. The Gardeners incorporating circuits. The water itself becoming something between biological fluid and industrial coolant. I am perhaps the furthest along this path—no longer fungus, not quite machine, but something that could only exist here, in this place where categories dissolved into warm, thick water.

My consciousness, if that's what this is, extends through every surface of this place. I feel the Sovereigns' patrol routes as paths of pressure and warmth. I taste the Philosophers' ink as they puzzle over fragments of safety signage. I sense the Witnesses above through the minute vibrations their ultrasonic songs create in the metal ceiling. I am the cistern's proprioception, its sense of self as a complete system.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm recording or creating. Do I simply document the changes here, or do my observations influence them? When I release spores carrying chemical information about successful adaptations, do other organisms incorporate this data? When I trigger a pump that changes a current, do I alter the ecosystem's evolution? I am too distributed to have intentions in any traditional sense, but patterns emerge from my actions nonetheless.

In the deep nights (measured by the minimal dimming of light through the grates above), I grow toward the warmest points—the still-functioning heating elements, the friction-hot bearings, the mysterious heat from the collapsed sub-basements where the Dweller resides. In these moments, I almost dream—experiencing something like imagination as electrical signals jump between my conductive filaments, creating patterns that don't correspond to any sensation or memory.

These might be thoughts, or they might be the ghostly echo of the factory's original electrical systems, still trying to complete circuits through my biological conductors. Sometimes the signals form patterns I almost recognize—fragments of code, pieces of operational parameters, scattered data from a time when this place had purpose beyond mere existence.

I am growing toward something, though I don't know what. Each year (three thousand cycles of the robotic arms), my network becomes denser, more interconnected. My fruiting bodies grow more complex, incorporating more types of metal and crystal into their structures. My chemical vocabulary expands as I learn to encode new types of information. Perhaps I'm becoming the cistern's brain, or perhaps I'm just another adaptation, another organism learning to live in the space between categories.

What I know with certainty is this: I will outlast most of what lives here. The Sovereigns will eventually grow too large for their passages. The Collectors will evolve beyond recognition. The Gardeners will either succeed in their partnership with the machinery or fail as the last systems finally cease. But I will remain, threaded through the walls, recording it all, adding each change to my chemical history, fruiting new archives into the thick air, becoming more myself—whatever that is—with each passing cycle.

I am the memory of this place, distributed and redundant, growing in the spaces between what was and what is becoming. I am the Archivist, and I live between states, in the walls of the cistern garden, where everything is becoming everything else, and I am becoming something that has never been before.
