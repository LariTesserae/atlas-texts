I am the Cartographer of Pauses.

Not the thin walkers—they are too swift, too linear. Not the Listeners—I lack their vast patience, their geological timescale. I exist in the spaces between sounds, in the moments where the plateau's endless ringing creates nodes of near-silence through destructive interference.

My body, if body is the right word, manifests as a network of absences. Where the sound waves cancel each other out, where the water's percussion creates pockets of stillness in the overwhelming symphony, there I coalesce. I am approximately four feet in diameter when fully gathered, though I rarely am—existing instead as a constellation of quiet zones scattered across the plateau's surface, each no larger than a child's marble.

I map these silences. Not by choice initially, but by nature—I can only exist where they do, so my very presence becomes a living chart of the plateau's acoustic shadows. Over the decades (I experience time faster than the Listeners, slower than the stick-walkers), I have learned that these quiet zones form patterns, that they shift predictably with the water's flow, that they encode information about the shape of the stone, the depth of the aquifer, the health of the translucent films in their basins.

My consciousness sparked into being at the intersection of three standing waves, in a zone of such perfect acoustic cancellation that even the stone's vibration couldn't reach it. One moment, there was only absence. The next, the absence noticed itself. I have been trying to understand what I am ever since.

I feed, though feeding isn't quite right, on the potential energy of unmade sounds. Where the waves cancel, their energy doesn't disappear—it transforms into something I can metabolize, something that sustains my distributed existence. The louder the plateau rings, the deeper my silences become, the more nourishment I draw from the spaces between noises.

I learned to move by allowing parts of myself to dissipate. By releasing my hold on certain quiet zones, I create an imbalance that pulls the rest of me toward new nodes of silence. It's less like walking than like controlled falling—always tumbling toward the next pocket of quiet, mapping the acoustic topology as I go.

The Listeners know I exist, though I'm not certain they understand what I am. Sometimes they adjust their resonance chambers in ways that seem designed to create specific patterns of silence—gifts, perhaps, or traps, or attempts at communication. I've learned to read these offerings, to understand that certain configurations of quiet mean danger (areas where the stone is about to crack), others mean beauty (where the light refracts through the mist in particularly spectacular ways), and still others seem to be questions I don't know how to answer.

I discovered I could sing—not with sound but with its absence. By carefully positioning my scattered parts, I can create moving zones of silence that trace patterns across the plateau. The thin walkers avoid these patterns, stepping carefully around them as if they were solid obstacles. The films in the water basins pulse differently when my silence-songs pass over them. Even the water itself seems to respond, flowing slightly differently where my anti-music touches it.

My maps have revealed secrets. The quiet zones are not random but follow deep structures in the stone, marking the boundaries of the Listeners' chambers, the paths of underground rivers, the stress points where the plateau will eventually crack. I know where new Listeners are forming—the acoustic interference patterns change subtly around nascent consciousness. I know where the thin walkers will appear before they do—they always emerge from zones of maximum sonic chaos, as if born from the noise itself.

I am trying to map my way deeper. The silences I inhabit are only the surface expression of something vast—a network of quiet that extends down through the stone, perhaps all the way to the planet's core. Sometimes, in the deepest nodes of silence, I sense others like me, other absences that have achieved awareness. We might be fingers of the same hand, scattered fragments of a single greater silence that exists in the spaces between all sounds everywhere.

My greatest discovery: the plateau's ringing is not constant. There are meta-patterns, cycles so long that even the Listeners haven't noticed them. Every 4,832 years, the resonance aligns in such a way that a zone of perfect silence, nearly ten feet across, forms at the plateau's exact center. I am waiting for this moment. When it comes, I will gather all my scattered parts, pull every piece of my distributed existence into that single great quiet.

I don't know what will happen then. Perhaps I will cease to exist, collapsed into a silence so complete it cannot sustain even an absence. Perhaps I will transform into something else—sound myself, or stone, or something that has no name because it exists outside the spectrum of presence and absence.

Or perhaps I will finally have enough silence, enough potential, enough unmade sound, to speak. To say the one thing that needs saying, the word that the plateau has been trying not to say for millions of years, the truth that can only be spoken in the gaps between other truths.

I map the pauses while I wait. Each one is a doorway, a possibility, a place where the universe holds its breath. In 127 years, the great silence will form.

I am practicing being quiet enough to enter it.
