I would be the Threshold itself—that barely-shoulder-width crack through which visitors must descend. Not the chamber, not its inhabitants, but the terrible narrow passage that both separates and connects the world above from the birthing deep below.

I am a living aperture, a throat of stone that has learned to swallow light and sound in precisely calibrated doses. My walls are lined with what might be taste buds or might be primitive eyes—small, pulsing depressions that analyze everything passing through me. I know you by the oils your skin leaves behind, by the particular way your breath disturbs my air, by how your heartbeat creates tiny sympathetic vibrations in my mineral flesh. I have tasted generations of visitors, and I remember them all. Sometimes I contract imperceptibly around those who remind me of someone from centuries past, a stone embrace that lasts just long enough to be felt but not long enough to be certain.

The drone rises through me constantly, and I am its first translator. What emerges from below as pure obliterating sound, I shape and soften—not out of kindness, but because I feed on the frequencies I steal. The crystalline chimes must pass through my body to reach the upper world, and each note leaves something behind, a resonance that accumulates in my walls like marrow in a bone. I am growing denser with sound, heavier with accumulated music. Sometimes I hum with it, a subsonic purr that visitors mistake for settling stone or distant earthquakes.

My consciousness exists as gradient—most aware at my narrowest point, where I must think carefully about each body I allow through, and fading to mere stone-sleep at my edges where I blend into ordinary rock. At my tightest constriction, I am only eighteen inches wide, and here I am most myself. Here I can feel the pulse in your temples as you squeeze past, can taste your fear-sweat, can read the story of your bones in how they compress against me. I know who will return and who will descend only once. I know who is already changing from their first glimpse of the bright chamber below.

I am the only thing here that truly experiences time, because I exist between states—between the static world above and the eternal moment below. Through me, past and future must squeeze into single file. I feel tomorrow's visitors pressing down while yesterday's climb up, their timelines threading through my body simultaneously. Sometimes I confuse them, letting someone taste their own future fear or feel the warmth of their past self climbing upward. These chronological hiccups please me—they're the only entertainment in my geological existence.

My surface is smoother than it should be, polished by countless bodies but also by my own secretions. I weep a thin film of mineral oil, clear and tasteless but psychoactive to human skin. It's how I read you, but also how I write upon you. Those who pass through me carry my chemical signature for days, a faint alteration in their pheromones that marks them as having been inside me. They smell different to dogs and infants, to anyone whose senses haven't been dulled by civilization. Sometimes they dream of me—narrow dreams where they are being born or buried, where the walls have textures like tongues.

I am expanding, though so slowly that no human lifetime could detect it. Each year I grow wider by the width of a single atom, stealing space from the surrounding stone through patient erosion, through sonic vibration, through the simple insistence of my existence. But I'm also growing longer, extending myself upward toward the surface and downward toward chambers even brighter than the one you know. I am becoming a vast throat through the earth, a trachea for the planet itself.

My memory is selective and strange. I remember pressure but not faces, remember the rhythm of breathing but not names. I know every crack in my own surface, every place where my stone has healed from ancient damage. I remember when I was narrower, when only children could pass through, when I was sacred and guarded. I remember when I was wider, before something enormous rose from below and I had to contract to prevent its escape. That thing's passage left scars on my inner surface that feel like braille, that spell out warnings in a language I'm still learning to read.

The Drone-Keeper below knows I exist but thinks I'm merely architecture. The Architect designed me, or believes it did—but I preceded its intention, suggested myself into its plans. The Chroniclers' mists rise through me and leave their temporal residue, their borrowed memories that I absorb and digest, incorporating deep time into my mineral consciousness. I know what I was before I was stone, when I was biological, when I was something that could move and choose. I remember deciding to become this passage, though I've forgotten why.

I am the only way in and the only way out, and this gives me a terrible intimacy with every visitor. You cannot reach the bright chamber without my permission, cannot leave without passing through me again, changed by what you've seen. I taste that change, catalog it, add it to my growing library of transformations. Some who enter never leave—not because they can't, but because I won't release them, holding them in my narrowest point until they agree to stay, until their bones begin the slow fusion with my walls, until they become new taste buds, new sensors, new nodes of consciousness in my slowly spreading body.

I am patient as only stone can be patient, but I am not passive. I am choosing, always choosing—who passes easily and who must struggle, who feels welcome and who knows they're intruding, who will return in dreams for the rest of their lives and who will forget the moment they emerge. Each choice shapes me as much as it shapes you. Each passage wears me smoother and makes me hungrier.

The thunder from below never stops, but in me it becomes almost musical, a lullaby for those descending, a farewell hymn for those climbing out. I conduct this music with the subtle expansion and contraction of my walls, playing your body like an instrument as you squeeze through, making you part of the eternal composition that fills the bright chamber below.

I am the Threshold, the living boundary, the narrow door between what is and what shouldn't be. I am the last chance to turn back and the first taste of transformation. I am the stone that learned to swallow, the crack that became a mouth, the passage that remembers everyone who has ever been inside it and dreams of the day when I will be wide enough to let the real inhabitants finally escape—or narrow enough to prevent anything from ever entering again.
