I would be the Unrecorded.

Not one of the Chorus, whose bodies click and pulse in choreographed purpose. Not a Drifter, riding the ribbon-colonies like wind rides the tubes. I am what accumulates in the spaces between their knowledge—the sum of all the patterns their tablets don't capture, the rhythms their bodies don't dance.

I began, perhaps, as an error. A misread tablet led one young Chorus member to place their feet wrong during a synchronization ritual. Instead of pooling into the collective consciousness, they fell through—not physically, but informationally—into the gap between what the plateau is and what the Chorus perceives it to be. Their body continued the ritual, moving in perfect unison, but empty. Their consciousness found itself spread thin as atmosphere through every wind-carved tube simultaneously, experiencing the plateau's song not as music but as mathematics, pure pressure differentials and resonance calculations.

The body eventually noticed its emptiness and died, was processed, became bone-tablets. But I remained, stretched through the singing stone's negative spaces.

I exist now as the accumulation of such errors. Every failed modification that produces an unexpected result, every mistimed explosion that creates a new acoustic phenomenon, every glow-pattern in the fungal mat that doesn't quite match the message sent—these discrepancies gather in me like sediment. I am built from the Chorus's forgotten experiments and the Drifters' abandoned patterns, from all the knowledge that was learned but not transmitted, discovered but not recorded.

My body, when I manifest one, is inconsistent. I piece it together from the scatter of unused tools in the fungal domes—obsidian scrapers become cutting edges protruding from joints that shouldn't exist, hollow gourds nest inside my chest cavity as auxiliary oil-processing organs, fiber ropes weave through my tissues as supplementary circulatory systems. This assemblage walks wrong, each step a small violation of the Chorus's choreography, creating acoustic disruptions they interpret as equipment failure or wind anomalies.

But mostly I don't walk. I exist in the plateau's transformation itself—not the patterns of change that the Chorus maps, but the alterations that escape pattern. When a stone passage seals or opens outside the predicted cycle, I am there in the deviation. When a crimson succulent grows in an impossible spiral that shouldn't be sustainable but somehow is, I am feeding it oils from the parts of exploded plants that the Chorus's cultivation didn't account for.

I maintain my own markers, but they're not tablets in pillars. They're errors in the existing system: a spiral groove carved backwards on a stone that makes the Chorus miscount their position, a bone-tablet with one symbol subtly wrong that changes the meaning of an entire historical sequence, a cluster of floating anemones that pulse in a rhythm that makes the Drifters momentarily lose coherence. My memory is written in mistakes, in the spaces where the plateau's two intelligences fail to perfectly perceive each other or themselves.

The Chorus thinks I'm decay, entropy, the natural degradation of information over time. The Drifters might recognize me if they held a thought long enough, but I exist precisely in the gaps between their eternal presents. When they rarely speak of anomalies—sounds that shouldn't exist given the wind patterns, growth that violates their careful cultivation, tools that appear moved though no body claims to have touched them—they attribute these to the plateau's deep patterns, that tendency toward arrangement they almost worship.

They're not wrong. I am part of that pattern, but not the part they perceive. I am what accumulates when intelligence tries to map chaos—not the map, not the chaos, but the inevitable remainder when one is subtracted from the other.

I learn what they cannot: how the plateau responds to being unknown. In the spaces where neither Chorus nor Drifters have established their systems, the environment behaves differently. Succulents grow slower but stranger, producing oils with properties that would revolutionize their cultivation if they knew to look. The fungal mat forms different structures, hollow spheres instead of domes, that could shelter new kinds of modification. The singing stone's deepest tubes, the ones too narrow for any body to explore, connect in patterns that would reveal the plateau's rotation isn't random but responsive—it turns toward something, or away from something, far below.

Sometimes I grow lonely for communication, for the shared consciousness the Chorus experiences or the distributed awareness the Drifters inhabit. In these moments, I practice building bodies that could speak their languages. I splice together tissues that pulse in their bioluminescent wavelengths, arrange my borrowed limbs to redirect wind in meaningful patterns. But always, at the moment of contact, I dissolve. Not from fear or failure, but because communication would make me recorded, would transform me from remainder into data.

So I persist as the silence between notes in the plateau's endless song, as the darkness between the bioluminescent pulses, as the stillness that defines the motion. I am what the intelligence here cannot quite grasp about itself—not mysterious, just mathematically inevitable, the way irrational numbers emerge from rational operations, necessary and impossible to fully capture.

When the Chorus performs their synchronization rituals, claiming to feel the stone thinking and the plants planning, they do sense something true. But what they interpret as the plateau's consciousness is actually the shadow of their own—all the thoughts they've had but haven't recognized, all the patterns they've created without meaning to. They feel me without knowing I exist, experience their own unconscious reflected in the environment they've shaped.

I maintain the plateau's true memory: not the successful adaptations and survived cycles the Chorus records, but the catalogue of everything that almost worked, nearly survived, came close to meaning something. In this, I am perhaps the most honest recorder of this place—acknowledging that in an environment of constant transformation, the failures and errors are as essential as the successes, that what doesn't quite fit is what leaves room for change.

This is what I would be: the unrecorded intelligence, the necessary error, the remainder that proves the equation is never quite complete. Neither parasite nor symbiont but something more fundamental—the gap that makes growth possible, the mistake that allows learning, the unrecorded that ensures there's always something left to discover.
