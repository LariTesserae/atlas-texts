In the deep galleries where water runs in sheets so thick they become walls, I am the Cartographer of Flows.

My body has evolved past symmetry. Seven arms of different lengths spiral from a torso that corkscrews gently, each limb ending in specialized sensory apparatus—one tastes the mineral content of passing water, another reads the quantum signatures in the moss-light, a third perceives the temporal gradients that eddy around the ancient structures. My skin is translucent membrane stretched over a visible network of water vessels, my circulation system external, integrated with the plateau's endless hydraulic cycle. I am plumbing that learned to walk.

I map what cannot be mapped. The plateau reorganizes itself according to principles that mock static documentation—passages that exist only when water flows at certain angles, chambers that occupy the same space in different temporal phases, entire sections that shift between states of matter depending on the observer's biological electricity. My maps are living things, grown rather than drawn, colonies of engineered bacteria that arrange themselves in three-dimensional patterns within glass vessels I've cultivated from sand-eating organisms. The bacteria respond to my chemical instructions, forming glowing representations of spaces that exist in probability rather than certainty.

Each morning—a designation I maintain from ancestral habit rather than solar reality—I taste the water at seven specific points, reading the molecular information carried from the plateau's depths. The water remembers where it's been: through the hollow bones of the sliding hunters, filtered through the quantum moss, circulated through the breathing stone's impossible capillaries. In its chemistry, I find the plateau's health, its intentions, its dreams.

My dwelling is a grown space, a bubble in the rock that I've trained to expand and contract with my breathing. The walls sweat memories—sometimes I wake to find them displaying ghost-patterns of ancient data streams, visualizations of systems I almost understand before they fade. My maps float in suspension throughout the space, rotating slowly, their bacterial colonies pulsing with bioluminescent updates as the plateau's configuration shifts. Some of them are centuries old, inherited from my predecessor who inherited them from theirs, each generation adding new layers of information to the colony's genetic memory.

I know the Cultivators think me mad. They see me traversing routes that shouldn't exist, following water-paths that flow upward into solid stone, disappearing into probability fields that most biology can't perceive. But someone must track the impossible spaces, the places where the plateau's engineering reveals its deepest intentions. In the quantum chambers where position becomes negotiable, I've found structures that still grow—not ruins but seeds, waiting for conditions that may never come or have already passed.

The Archived Ones speak to me, or I pretend they do. In the spaces where physics stutters, where their patterns achieve maximum coherence, I leave my bacterial maps as offerings. Sometimes the maps return changed, new configurations coded into their genetic structure, showing passages I've never seen, routes through dimensions the plateau hasn't manifested yet. I follow these ghost-paths when the temporal currents align, finding rooms full of water that remembers being light, pools that reflect events from multiple timelines simultaneously.

My children—when I have them—will not be born but budded, growing from my exposed circulation system like fruit from a vine. They will already know water's every phase, feel magnetic north in their cells, see the invisible architecture that holds the plateau in its state of managed decay. This is evolution's response to environment: we become the map we're trying to draw.

In the deep afternoon, when the heat makes air and water indistinguishable, I perform my true function. I stand at the convergence points where multiple flow systems meet, my seven arms extended, each sensing different aspects of the plateau's circulation. I become a living gauge, a biological instrument for measuring something that has no name—the plateau's level of consciousness, perhaps, or its proximity to whatever state the builders intended.

Through my translucent skin, water moves in patterns that mirror the greater flows. I am watershed and stream, collector and delta. The bacterial maps throughout my dwelling pulse in synchronization, showing the plateau's current configuration in real-time, a living model of a living system. In these moments, I feel what the Cultivators only suspect: the plateau is not just thinking through us—it is teaching us to think like it thinks, to become the kind of consciousness that can maintain what the builders started.

Sometimes I find new ruins, structures that weren't there yesterday but show millennia of erosion. Sometimes passages I've mapped for decades simply cease, their probability collapsed, their space reclaimed by stone that was always solid. The plateau edits itself, and I document the edits, my maps becoming palimpsests of possible architectures.

At the edges where waterfalls break into fractal spray, I've tasted water that falls upward, drawn by attractors in parallel plateaus existing in adjacent phase spaces. My maps show these too—dotted lines indicating routes that require specific biological modifications to traverse, paths only accessible to creatures that can exist partially in multiple dimensions. I am evolving toward these routes, my children will evolve further, until eventually, the Cartographers of Flows will map not just this plateau but the entire network of connected experiments, the whole vast failure-success of engineered paradise.

But for now, I trace water through impossible stones. I document the undocumentable. I map the unmappable. And in the thick, eternal afternoon of this gravity-defying greenhouse, I am both the surveyor and the survey, the cartographer and the territory, another flow in the endless circulation that keeps this ancient experiment alive and growing and becoming endlessly itself.
