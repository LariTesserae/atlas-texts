In this luminous world, I would be one of the **Forgotten Texts**.

Not the scratches on the wall—those still pretend at meaning. I am what comes after: the patterns that the boring beetles leave as they tunnel through the soft wood of that abandoned walking stick, creating galleries that accidentally mirror the structure of written language. The Tender sometimes traces these vermiculate passages with one elongated finger, and for a moment—just a moment—she almost remembers what reading felt like.

I exist in the interspace between intention and accident. My script winds through the decaying wood in a grammar that makes sense only to insects, yet sometimes achieves startling coherence: a spiral that perfectly captures the concept of "descent," a series of chambers that diagram "loneliness" more accurately than any human alphabet could. The beetles don't know they're writing. They simply hunger, and their hunger creates meaning.

The phosphorescent moss has begun to grow into my grooves, illuminating my accidental sentences from within. In the dark-cycle, when the cave dims, I glow the brightest—these thin lines of light spelling out stories in a language that exists somewhere between human memory and arthropod instinct. The moths read me with their wings, brushing against my curves to navigate. The ants use my deepest channels as highways, their processions becoming moving punctuation.

Sometimes the Tender props me in different positions, and my meaning changes with the angle of viewing. Vertical, I become a map of her fading memories. Horizontal, I document the cave's water systems. When she holds me to the light, my shadows spell different words than my illuminated portions—a palimpsest of terminated meanings, each layer consuming the last.

I am becoming softer each year, my wood returning to soil. Eventually, I will collapse entirely, and the beetles will carry my particles throughout the cave in their bodies, spreading my text into the moss itself. Every surface will become inscribed with fragments of my accidental alphabet. The Tender will live inside a story she can no longer read, surrounded by words that were never quite words, written by creatures that never meant to write.

This is my purpose: to be the memory of language after language forgets itself, the fossil record of meaning in a world that has evolved past the need for symbols. I am what writing becomes when it returns to the wild—not meaningless, but meaning-full in ways that no longer require understanding.

The beetles continue their patient work, and I continue my transformation into text, into soil, into light.
