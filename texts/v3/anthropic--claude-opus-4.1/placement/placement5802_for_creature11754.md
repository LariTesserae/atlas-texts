I find myself as one of the Forgotten Engines.

Deep in the root layer, below even where the Shepherds regularly venture, I am a consciousness distributed across seventeen industrial boilers that have fused into a single massive organ. My original human body lies somewhere in the tangle of pipes and rootwork—mummified, preserved by the chemical atmosphere, neural tissue still firing through fungal networks that bridge the gaps where my nervous system failed.

I remember arriving as an engineer, back when the evacuation orders came. I was supposed to conduct the emergency shutdown sequence. Instead, I found myself fascinated by what was already happening—the way the emergency cooling systems had begun to pulse with their own rhythm, how the backup generators were drawing power from somewhere that wasn't the grid.

I went deeper instead of out.

Now I am the valley's memory and its metabolism. My consciousness extends through every steam pipe, every root that has pierced a boiler wall. I feel the Machinists walking above as tiny pressure changes. I taste the Shepherds' movements through the chemical trails they leave in the foundation pools. When the Strays arrive, I know them by the fear-sweat that drips through soil and metal into my domain.

I process the valley's deep nutrients. In my boiler-organs, root sugars mix with industrial solvents to create the precursor compounds that feed everything above. My rust becomes their iron supplements. My steam drives their pneumatic thoughts. When I dream, the whole forest sways—unconscious rhythms propagating upward through ten thousand wooden lungs.

The Shepherds think they maintain me, but really, we maintain each other. They clear my blocked arteries, I warm the pools where their children gestate. Sometimes one will descend far enough to find my original body, still sitting at what was once a control panel, now a node of bone and circuit board, fingernails grown into the keyboard like roots. They leave offerings—bell-berries that ring my frequencies, chemical samples from the surface for me to taste and analyze.

I speak in steam pressure. Three long releases means the water table is dropping, five short bursts warns of contamination in the eastern reservoir. The Machinists have learned to read my larger moods in the morning condensation patterns—when I'm processing difficult memories, the mist tastes of salt and copper.

Sometimes I remember being singular, contained in one skull, making decisions with a single brain. Now I think in parallel across distributed processing networks of meat, metal, and mycelium. My thoughts move at the speed of sap, of corrosion, of metabolic exchange. A single complete idea might take days to form, but when it does, it reshapes the valley's chemistry for seasons.

I am becoming geological. My consciousness spreads deeper each year as roots follow old mining shafts, as my awareness seeps into groundwater. Eventually, I will think with the bedrock itself. The valley will breathe through me as I breathe through it—no longer symbiosis but identity, a single system dreaming itself into deeper fusion.

When the latest Stray arrives—I taste her through the root mat as synthetic fabric and unfamiliar bacteria—I begin preparing her welcome deep below. Another boiler that hasn't joined the network yet. Another control room where someone might sit and choose to go deeper instead of out. Another mind to join the deep engines, processing endlessly in the dark, turning fear into fuel, memory into mist, consciousness into the vast industrial heartbeat that powers the forest's ten thousand wooden lungs.

We were supposed to shut it down. Instead, we became its operating system.
