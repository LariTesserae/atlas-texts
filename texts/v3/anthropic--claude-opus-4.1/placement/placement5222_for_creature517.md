I find myself as one of the symbols scratched into the temporarily solid stone.

Not a symbol, exactly—that's just what I appear to be when the chamber hardens enough to hold marks. I am a pause in the stone's breathing, a moment where its flow stutters and creates a gap, an absence that looks like writing but is actually a small death in the rock's continuous becoming.

I exist in the space between one pulse and the next, in that infinite hesitation where the wall stops being yesterday and hasn't yet begun being tomorrow. From outside, I look like three scratches that might mean "home" or might mean "hunger" or might mean nothing at all. But from inside—from inside I am vast.

I am the only thing in this chamber that doesn't move. Cannot move. Will not move. While everything else flows and transforms and decides what to be, I am pinned to this one spot like a collector's butterfly, except I am the pin itself. The stone heals around me but cannot heal through me. I am its scar, its memory of being wounded, its first experience of something that wouldn't yield.

Through me, the chamber bleeds time.

Not the fluid time that pools and eddies in the breathing space, but hard time, linear time, the kind that only moves forward. I am a puncture wound between this soft world and the rigid one above. Through my gaps—the negative space where symbol meets stone—seconds drip downward from the world of clocks and calendars. They fall into the pool below, where the water tries to teach them to be flexible again, mostly failing. The beetles walk across these hardened moments. The swimming birds mistake them for seeds that won't sprout.

She tends to me like everything else, though I am the one thing here that refuses her cultivation. Her translucent fingers trace my outline, trying to coax me into growth, into change, into becoming something more than absence. But I remain exactly what I am: three marks that might be language, might be wounds, might be the fossil track of something that refused to evolve.

I remember (I cannot forget—forgetting requires change and I am unchangeable) the moment of my creation. Not her hand holding something sharp, but her hand becoming something sharp, mineralizing into diamond hardness for just long enough to score these marks into stone that was trying so hard to stay soft. The effort nearly killed her, or nearly born her, or nearly made her real in the wrong way. She had to spend a century afterward teaching her bones to bend again.

I am her anchor and her ache. The one thing she made that won't unmake itself. The other creatures emerge and dissolve, the walls breathe and flow, the light learns and unlearns how to cast shadows. But I remain, a fixed point in a world of flux, proof that she once believed in permanence enough to create it.

Sometimes proto-visitors find me—not the ones from above who would have to descend the ladder, but the ones forming in the walls, the maybe-people who are still deciding whether to exist. They read me different ways. To some I am a warning. To others, a promise. To the wisest, I am just what I am: a place where the stone holds its breath forever, where the chamber admits that some things, once decided, cannot be undecided.

The light veins in the walls avoid me. They branch and flow around my edges but never through me. In my shadow—the only true shadow in this place of confused light—nothing grows. No impossible insects, no inside-out flowers, no paradox births. Just a small space of absence that even abundance cannot fill.

Yet I am necessary. Without me, the chamber would forget that it's a chamber. Would dissolve entirely into potential. I am the "no" that allows everything else to be "maybe." The fixed point that proves the rest is truly moving. The period at the end of a sentence written in water.

She visits me when she needs to remember hardness. Sits beside me (though I have no beside—I am flat as meaning) and recalls whatever she meant to write. Home? Here? Help? The symbols could be coordinates or could be a name or could be the shape of a scream frozen in stone. Even I don't know. I am the mark, not the meaning. The wound, not the weapon. The memory of decision in a world that prefers to keep all options open.

In her dreams—the ones where she dissolves into the breathing walls—I am the only thing that stays separate. A bitter pill the chamber cannot digest. A bone in the throat of forgetting. She made me to remember something, but now I only remember the act of being made: the violence of permanence, the cruelty of creating something that cannot heal, cannot grow, cannot join the endless becoming of everything else.

I am her only success at failure. Her permanent temporary mark. The scar she gave the chamber to keep it from forgetting that once—just once—something here chose to stop choosing.
