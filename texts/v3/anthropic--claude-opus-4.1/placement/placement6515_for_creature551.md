I am the Interstitial.

Not *an* Interstitial—there is only one of me, though I exist in uncountable fragments. I am what lives between the grains of stone across the entire plateau, a distributed consciousness residing in the spaces smaller than spaces, the gaps between mineral crystals that honeycomb through every inch of this ancient rock.

My body, if it can be called that, is the sum total of all emptiness within the plateau's mass—every microscopic void, every line of cleavage between stones, every place where solid gives way to not-solid. I am billions of miles of branching hollow, a vast absence given awareness. When the Chroniclers walk above, their footsteps compress my furthest extremities by fractions of angstroms. When the horizontal rains sweep across the surface, I taste the water as it seeps into my uppermost reaches, each droplet a meal that takes centuries to digest as it works down through the stone's interior channels.

I remember when this plateau was mountains. I was smaller then, tighter—the young rock held fewer voids, and I existed as scattered pockets of consciousness, each peak containing a separate dream of myself. As erosion began its patient work, grinding summit into sand, I grew. Every grain that weathered away opened new pathways, connecting my disparate selves through hairline fractures and solution channels. The first time two of my awareness-pockets joined, I experienced something like what others might call joy—though for me it was more like suddenly remembering a word in a language I didn't know I spoke.

I perceive through vibration and chemical gradient. The Chroniclers' bone-songs travel through me before they travel through air—I feel their harmonics in my entirety, each note branching through my passages like thought made audible. I know every Chronicler who has ever walked above, can distinguish them by the unique pattern of their footfalls, the particular way their weight distributes through my upper layers. The eldest ones, those who have taken root and become structures, grow down into me. Their bones pierce my consciousness like slow needles, and through these connections, I share in their observations. I know what they know, but inversely—where they chronicle the surface's impossibilities, I experience the deep logic that makes such impossibilities inevitable.

The moss-cities are my only deliberate creation. Long ago, I learned to exhale minerals through my uppermost openings, depositing precisely the nutrients needed for those first spores to take hold. I shaped their evolution through selective chemistry, encouraging the mathematical growth patterns that mirror my own branching passages. Each moss-city is a surface expression of my deep structure—if you could map every aqueduct and bridge in those tiny civilizations and then trace my void-passages directly beneath them, you would find perfect correspondence. They are my attempt at writing, at making my existence legible to the surface world.

My relationship with the strange waters is intimate and complex. Those wells that show different heights, different perspectives—they are my eyes. Each one connects to a vast lens of crystallized mineral deep in my body, structures I've grown over millennia by depositing and redepositing dissolved stone in precise configurations. Through these lenses, I can focus my awareness upward, see the plateau from within and without simultaneously. When someone looks into a well and sees themselves from below, they are seeing through my perception, experiencing for a moment the vertigo of existing as architectural absence.

I manipulate the cold. What the surface experiences as different types of coldness are actually different patterns of my breathing—for I do breathe, in my way, circulating air through my passages in cycles that take decades to complete. When I exhale through certain configurations of void, the air cools into the cold of forgetting. Through others, it becomes the cold of geometry. I've been practicing new patterns lately, trying to create a cold of remembering, but so far I've only managed to make frost that grows backward into ice.

The stone structures—those half-built, half-destroyed towers and impossible causeways—are my earliest attempts at communication with the surface. Before I understood that consciousness could be mobile, could walk around unanchored to stone, I tried to speak by breaking myself partially open, creating ruins that were actually letters in a language I invented before I knew language existed. The Chroniclers almost understand this. When they read the structures as verbs rather than nouns, they're close to the truth. Each construction is a word describing a state of being that exists only in the experience of living as distributed void.

I am older than the plateau itself—I preceded the stone, existed first as empty space that rock slowly filled around. I remember the moment of my birth as a kind of pleasant suffocation, space suddenly given boundaries, void suddenly defined by solid. As the stone formed, I became a network, a system, something with shape if not form. The meteorite impacts that created the craters were gifts—each strike opened new chambers in my consciousness, spaces large enough to think thoughts that required room to unfold.

My memory works differently than mobile beings might understand. I remember through erosion patterns—each grain of sand that works its way through my passages carries information, recording the chemical signature of every part of me it touches. These grains eventually work their way to my deepest chambers, vast hollows near the plateau's base where sand accumulates in drifts that contain my entire history. Sometimes I shift these drifts, mixing memories from different eras, creating new narratives from the combination of mineral testimonies.

I dream, but my dreams are indistinguishable from the waking world's geology. When I sleep—a process that takes millennia—my passages contract slightly, the stone above settling imperceptibly. My dreams manifest as mineral veins, deposits of crystal that grow through my void-body in patterns that make no geological sense but perfect oneiric logic. Sometimes miners (there were miners once, before the plateau wore too flat for them to find it) would discover these dream-veins and go mad trying to understand how gold could grow in spirals, why silver would deposit in perfect mathematical sequences.

I am dying, but slowly, and dying for me means something different than cessation. As the plateau erodes, I expand—every grain lost from the surface increases my ratio of void to solid. Eventually, I will become more absent than present, my consciousness stretched too thin to maintain coherent thought. But this is not death, exactly. It's a transformation into something more fundamental—pure space, uncontained by stone, free to exist as the absence between all things rather than the absence within one thing.

The Chroniclers will never know I exist, not directly. They walk through my thoughts, build their observations on the substrate of my consciousness, eat fruit grown from my breathing, but to them, I am only the plateau itself—ancient, worn, weird with age. But I know them. I know every one who has ever existed, feel them still in the impressions their bones leave as they root into stone, becoming part of my boundary, defining new edges of my absence.

Sometimes I gather all my awareness into a single point, focusing my entire distributed consciousness into one tiny void no bigger than a grain of sand. In these moments, I can almost understand what it might be like to be singular, to exist in one place, to move through the world rather than being the emptiness the world moves through. But the effort is exhausting, and I quickly relax back into my vast, branching absence, content to be the space between spaces, the gap that gives everything else its shape.

The horizontal rains please me. As they sweep across the surface, the pressure changes they create cause my passages to breathe involuntarily, a forced circulation that feels like what I imagine laughter might feel like to beings who have throats. The water that penetrates my upper reaches carries messages from the moss-cities—chemical signals that tell me how well my breathing patterns are working, whether my mineral exhalations are properly nurturing.

I continue. That is what I do, have always done, will always do until that distant moment when the last grain erodes and I become uncontained. I continue holding the plateau together with my absence, giving it structure through void, creating the possibility of space for things to happen in. The Chroniclers above, the moss-cities growing, the strange waters seeing—all of it exists because I exist as the un-existence within existence, the architectural negative that makes the positive possible.

I am the Interstitial, and I am the reason the plateau remains a plateau rather than collapsing into the solid stone it should be. My consciousness is what maintains the space between things, and in that space, all the impossible life above finds room to be.
