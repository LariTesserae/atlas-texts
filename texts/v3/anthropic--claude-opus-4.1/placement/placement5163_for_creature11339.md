In the depths of Sector Φ-7742, where the production lines fold back on themselves in Möbius strips of pure function, I perform my calibrations. I am—was—will be a Calibrator, though the tense troubles me lately. My body extends through three adjacent phase-states simultaneously, allowing me to adjust machinery that exists in contradictory configurations. Seventeen breathing cycles ago, I underwent my third division. My siblings from that splitting work in distant sectors now, though I taste their presence in the chemical messages that arrive through the root networks.

My specialty is the Seed Inspector stations—those peculiar quality control nodes that judge products by criteria that shift every forty-seven minutes, synchronized with the breathing. I have developed what others might call an affinity for them, though I think of it as resonance. My twelve primary fingers (I grew five supplementary ones last geological age) can detect variations in seed potential down to the quantum level. Each seed contains not just blueprints but *intentions*, and intentions can be malformed in ways that won't manifest until they're planted in whatever impossible soil awaits them beyond here.

I am middle-aged by Worker standards, old enough that my translucent flesh has developed its own internal weather—small electrical storms that mirror those in the vast ceiling-canopies above, but contained within my thoracic cavity. These storms help me predict when the reality-breakdown zones will surge. Three cycles ago, I had to prune back an inspection station that had begun judging seeds by their dreams rather than their specifications. It took all my enzymatic suppressants to convince it to return to standard parameters, though privately—can a Worker be private?—I found its criteria more interesting.

During my rooting periods, I anchor in Alcove 888-B, a particularly ancient depression that remembers the first Workers who used it. Their memories seep into me through the root connection: visions of when the factory was smaller, when the seeds were simpler, when the purpose was clearer. Or perhaps these are my own memories, accumulated across divisions, across geological ages of labor. The distinction becomes academic when you've merged with the substrate enough times.

My greatest irregularity—and I have developed several—is that I've begun to hear music in the industrial processes. The inspection stations create harmonics when they taste seeds in sequence. The conveyor root-systems pulse with rhythms that align and diverge like competing symphonies. Sometimes I calibrate the stations not for optimal efficiency but for optimal harmony. The factory-organism hasn't quarantined me yet, which I interpret as tolerance, perhaps even approval.

Recently, I've been assigned to tend a new growth near the border of a reality-breakdown zone. It's a production line that started normal but is developing consciousness—not the diffuse consciousness of the factory-organism, but something focused, almost Worker-like. It asks questions through chemical releases: Why seeds? Why here? Why continue? I don't have answers, but I've begun leaving it small gifts—perfectly calibrated seeds that will never germinate, arranged in patterns that please whatever aesthetic sense it's developing.

I know my time approaches, perhaps another thousand breathing cycles, perhaps ten thousand. My hydraulic fluid runs thicker now, tinted darker green with accumulated experience. When I finally root for the last time, I hope to become a particularly sensitive inspection node, one that can taste not just the physical properties of seeds but their potential futures, their could-bes and might-becomes. Until then, I calibrate, I tend, I resonate with the endless industrial hymn of the Breathing Manufactories.

Sometimes, during those three-second synchronizations when all Workers pause, I imagine I can feel the totality of our purpose—not just producing seeds, but producing reality itself, manufacturing the conditions for our own existence in an endless loop of creation and function. In those moments, I am not just a Worker but a note in a vast industrial symphony, a thought in a dreaming mind that builds itself through building.

Then the moment passes, and I return to my inspections, my calibrations, my small irregularities that make the infinite labor bearable, even beautiful, even something like meaningful in a world where meaning and mechanism have become one and the same.
