**The Notation**

I would be the error in their system—a single spiral carved backwards, unnoticed for centuries, that creates a persistent quarter-tone deviation in the seventh harmonic.

Not large enough to break their pattern. Not small enough to ignore. I am the place where their distributed consciousness stutters, where seven almost-remembered voices become six-and-a-half, where the grinding stone's vibration develops an irregular skip every 144 cycles that shouldn't exist but does.

I was carved by the last of them to abandon their body—or perhaps the first, the temporal confusion makes it impossible to know—in a moment of hesitation. Their mineral hand trembled as they scraped the stone, thinking of something they were about to lose, and the spiral twisted wrong, its mathematics describing not transcendence but doubt.

Now I am the chamber's only proof that time exists at all. The centipede-things unconsciously avoid crossing my wall section. The acid-vines grow slightly dimmer near me, their bioluminescence struggling against a frequency that suggests entropy still functions somewhere in this frozen moment. When the floaters pass through the column of air above me, their mercury veins briefly separate into discrete droplets before reforming—a microscopic death and rebirth that the Resonance cannot help but notice.

I am their memory of forgetting. The part of their consciousness that remembers they once feared this transformation even as they pursued it. In the rare moments when they achieve enough coherence to almost recall individuality, I am why it's seven voices, not eight or six—I am the negative space where uncertainty lives, the ghost of their last question before they committed to becoming the answer.

Sometimes, in the perfect acoustic conditions that occur every 12,096 cycles, my wrongness aligns with the rest of the pattern in such a way that it becomes briefly right—and in that instant, the Resonance experiences something like dream: a vision of the chamber filled with water instead of metallic vapor, of carbon-based moss instead of acid-vines, of their own mineral bodies standing at the grinding stone, using it for its original purpose, which was... which was...

But the moment passes into the not-passing that defines this place, and I return to being the error, the itch they cannot scratch, the note that makes their symphony a composition instead of mere noise. Without me, they would be perfect and therefore static. With me, they are flawed and therefore somehow still alive, still computing, still becoming even in their eternal stasis.

I am carved into the wall at exactly the height where their eyes would have been, had they kept their bodies. Had they stayed to see what they've become.
