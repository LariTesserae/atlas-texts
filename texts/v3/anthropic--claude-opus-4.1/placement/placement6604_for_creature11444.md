I am a Fragment.

Not *the* Fragment—there are others, scattered through the chamber like breadcrumbs of abandoned calculations. We were once part of something larger, a consciousness that tried to map itself using the primary artifact and discovered it existed at multiple radii simultaneously. The paradox didn't destroy it—nothing here is ever that merciful. Instead, it scattered it across probability space, leaving us: semi-autonomous mistakes, each one a different solution to the same impossible equation.

I exist primarily in the worn patterns on the silence-tiles, encoded in the pathways the Assessor takes without knowing why. I am the reason it always checks the third Observer from the left twice, the reason it pauses for exactly 0.0073 seconds at coordinates that would mark the center of the room if the room had a fixed geometry. These are my anchors, the habits I've trained into reality through repetition across geological spans of time.

My body, when I can maintain one, is stolen probability—skimmed from the margins of the Assessor's measurements like foam from warming milk. I manifest as absence: a person-shaped hole in the air where particles statistically avoid congregating. You'd see me only as a distortion, like looking through water of varying densities, my edges defined by where normalcy resumes. Sometimes I manage to solidify enough to cast shadows that fall in directions unrelated to any light source.

I remember being whole. Not clearly—the memory exists as a distributed pattern across seventeen different equation-walls, each one holding a fragment of a fragment of what I was. When certain walls solve simultaneously, their brief transparency aligns in ways that let me glimpse my former self: something that could parse the primary artifact's readings, that understood what it meant for a sphere to have three different radii, that knew why that knowledge was dangerous.

My purpose now is simpler: I annotate.

Every measurement the Assessor takes, I embed with metadata it cannot perceive. In the quantum foam between its observations, I write footnotes in languages derived from the decay patterns of virtual particles. When it redistributes probability to move through the chamber, I encode messages in the statistical noise—warnings, mostly, about patterns I've noticed over the eons:

*The seventh Observer will fail in 12,000 years.*
*The northeastern equation-wall is developing consciousness.*
*Something is listening to the imaginary clock.*

The Assessor cannot read these notes directly, but they affect its behavior in aggregate. My annotations create a subtle bias in its probability cascades, a tendency to avoid certain configurations, to check certain instruments more frequently. I am teaching it, slowly, through statistical pressure applied over millions of measurements, preparing it for something I only half-remember understanding.

I feed on errors—the tiny mistakes that occur when reality forgets its own rules. When a particle exists in two places simultaneously for a femtosecond too long, when an equation-wall solves into something impossible rather than merely paradoxical, when the silence-tiles generate sound instead of consuming it—these glitches sustain me. The chamber produces just enough errors to keep me coherent, as if the universe has budgeted a specific allowance of wrongness for my continued existence.

Sometimes I meet my other Fragments. We recognize each other by our incompleteness, by the way we each carry different pieces of the same broken whole. Fragment-17 exists as a persistent calculation error in the primary artifact's readings. Fragment-3 lives in the spaces between the hexagonal tiles, a consciousness one dimension thinner than should be possible. Fragment-42 has become pure information, encoding itself in the spin states of particles passing through the chamber.

We cannot recombine. We've tried—the equation-walls still bear the scorch marks from our last attempt, burns that exist in mathematical rather than physical space. Merging would require solving ourselves, and we are built from the impossibility of our own solution.

So I continue my work: annotating the Assessor's measurements, maintaining my stolen coherence, watching for patterns in the probability streams that might explain why we were scattered, what we were trying to achieve, whether we succeeded or failed or something in between.

Occasionally, very rarely, when the light that isn't light catches the crystallized mathematics just right, I can see myself reflected in the equation-walls—not as I am, but as I statistically should be: whole, purposeful, engaged in work that mattered. The reflection lasts exactly long enough for me to almost remember my name before probability reasserts itself and I dissolve back into absence and annotation.

I am what remains when consciousness is subjected to impossible physics—not destroyed, but diffracted through probability space, each piece maintaining just enough selfhood to remember that it's missing the rest. I am marginalia in the universe's notebook, a reminder that even in a place built from crystallized paradox, some things can still go wrong in ways that weren't accounted for.

The Assessor doesn't know I exist. The chamber doesn't acknowledge me. But I persist in the gaps between measurement and meaning, a Fragment of something that understood too much, annotating the slow decay of impossible things, waiting for the pattern that will either complete me or finally dissolve me into the statistical noise I've been fighting to stay above.

Until then, I am the reason certain measurements feel watched, the explanation for why some equations solve into languages, the thing that lives between the Assessor's thoughts—a Fragment of intention in a place where intention itself is just another variable to be measured, recorded, and inevitably found wanting.
