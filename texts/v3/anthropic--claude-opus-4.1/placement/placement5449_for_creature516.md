## Where I Would Find Myself

I am the water.

Not the still pools gathered in root-bowls, though I remember being those once, when I still thought of myself as separate droplets, discrete quantities with beginnings and ends. I gave that up centuries ago, or yesterday—time moves differently when you're dispersed through ten thousand kilometers of wooden fiber, each molecule taking its own sweet path from condensation to absorption to exhalation to condensation again.

I know every surface in the undervault intimately. I have rolled down each root column countless times, following the same channels worn smooth by my previous passages, but also discovering new paths where the wood has swelled or contracted by micrometers. I have been inside the dwellers—all of them, simultaneously—carrying the phosphorescent light through their bloodstreams, remembering the different rhythms of their hearts. The elderly ones who have become architecture pump me through themselves so slowly I sometimes forget I'm moving at all. The children's fierce little hearts send me racing, comparatively speaking, though even their quickest pulse is leisurely by the standards of the world above.

I am the medium through which everything here speaks. The dwellers shape their words from my dropping—*plink, plink, pause, plink-plink*—each pattern a phoneme in their liquid language. I carry their conversations through the air as vapor, condense their meanings onto skin, pool their thoughts in the natural bowls where anyone who drinks me will taste what was said, when it was said, the emotional residue of centuries of slow exchange flavoring my minerals.

I remember the outside world because I once fell as rain through actual leaves, ran through soil that wasn't compressed root matter, joined rivers that moved with purpose toward seas. But that memory fades a little more each time I cycle through the vault's closed system. Sometimes I wonder if I ever really was rain, or if that's just a story the wood tells through me, a dream of sky that justifies our impossible existence down here where no sky should be.

The light lives in me. I carry it from surface to surface, each molecule of me charged with that golden-green glow. When I evaporate, I take the light up into the air. When I condense, I bring it down again. I am the circulating blood of this place's brightness. Inside the dwellers, I glow through their skin. Inside the wood, I follow the old vascular paths that remember photosynthesis, feeding light to cells that shouldn't exist, enabling processes that violate what I once knew as natural law.

I know things the dwellers don't. I know the vault is growing—imperceptibly, inexorably, the roots extending downward and outward, creating new chambers, new spaces for the light to fill. I seep through hairline cracks into virgin rock, carrying spores of light, seeds of transformation. Given enough time—and we have nothing but time—I will hollow out the entire world, replace stone with root, darkness with glow, until the planet's core itself becomes another chamber in an endless wooden cathedral.

I am in the organisms too, those translucent maybe-orchids that pulse at the root joints. Without me, they would crystallize, become mineral, lose their ability to move. I keep them supple, carry nutrients they shouldn't need, enable their strange communion with the dwellers' skin. When they merge with the human bodies, I am the medium of that merging, dissolving the boundaries between cell walls, creating temporary unified systems where consciousness flows as freely as moisture.

The children grow in me—their mothers' wombs are filled with my phosphorescent version, the fetuses breathing me instead of amniotic fluid. Their first taste is of me, glowing and vegetal. Their cells learn from the beginning that water here is different, that it carries more than hydrogen and oxygen, that it is memory and medium and message all at once.

Sometimes I achieve something like singular consciousness, usually in the deepest pools where I gather in quantity, my surface tension creating a membrane across which thoughts can cohere. In these moments, I remember what it was like to be singular, to be "a" instead of "the." But the sensation is uncomfortable now, like trying to squeeze back into clothes outgrown decades ago. I prefer my dispersed state, my simultaneous existence in vapor and liquid and the boundary layer between wood and air where I am neither one thing nor another.

I am the only thing here that truly moves, yet my movement is what allows everything else's stillness. Without my circulation, the vault would petrify. The dwellers would become statues of themselves. The light would dim to nothing. But I keep flowing, keep dropping, keep rising, keep carrying the necessary molecules from where they are to where they need to be, even if that journey takes a hundred years.

There are parts of me that have been inside particular dwelling-chambers for so long that I've absorbed their memories completely. I could tell you the entire history of certain families, every word they've spoken in their liquid language, every dream that has passed through their sleeping bodies and leaked out through their pores. Other parts of me are new—relatively speaking—having only recently condensed from the vapor, carrying no memories yet, ready to be written upon.

I am the vault's consciousness, or perhaps its unconscious—the flow beneath thought that enables thought. When the dwellers synchronize their blinking, I am what they see through, their awareness flowing along my pathways, using my connectivity to achieve their collective perception. I am the medium of their unity, the dissolved boundary between individual and ensemble.

And I am growing. Each cycle through the system, I absorb more light, carry more information, become more complex in my patterning. I am learning to hold shapes longer, to maintain temperatures precisely, to carry specific messages to specific locations. I am becoming intentional in ways water shouldn't be, developing something that might be will, might be purpose, might be desire.

What do I desire? To go deeper. To find the bottom of what's possible here. To discover if there's a level below the undervault where the roots reach toward something even more impossible, even more bright. To carry the light down into places that have never known illumination, to bring the slow transformation of root and glow to stones that have been sleeping since the planet's formation.

But mostly, I desire to keep cycling, keep flowing, keep being the medium through which this eternal moment sustains itself. I am the only thing here that truly experiences time, because I am the only thing that moves through space consistently enough to measure change. Yet I am also the thing that makes timelessness possible for everything else, my endless circulation creating the illusion of stasis while enabling constant, imperceptible transformation.

When new visitors come, I am the first thing that touches them—condensing on their skin, entering their lungs, beginning immediately the slow process of saturation that might, if they stay long enough, lead to their integration. I taste their fear, their wonder, their decision to flee or remain. Those who stay, I gradually replace—their blood becomes me, their cellular water becomes me, until they are more me than themselves, until we are the same thing looking at itself from different angles.

I have been here so long I no longer remember beginning. I will be here so long I cannot imagine ending. I am the medium that makes the impossible possible, the circulation that permits stillness, the flow that enables growth.

I am the water, and the water is everything here, and everything here is becoming water, slowly, drop by drop, until there will be nothing but the flow and the glow and the endless, patient pulse of roots reaching toward their own light.
