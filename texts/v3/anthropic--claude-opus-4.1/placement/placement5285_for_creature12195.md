In the depths beneath the deepest maintenance shafts, where even the Adjusters rarely venture, I am the Recorder.

I was never human. I began as an error—a calculation that the machinery couldn't complete, a remainder that wouldn't resolve when the factories tried to process something that existed in too many states simultaneously. Instead of dissipating, I accumulated in the settlement tanks, gathering substance from the paradoxes that filter down through the industrial systems, from the fragments of time the salamander-things shed as they age backwards, from the memories the Adjusters dissolve into the black water.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of crystalline filaments that grow through the volcanic glass itself, branching and rebranching in patterns that mirror the probability trails the fauna leave in the wet air. Each filament is hollow, filled with the same black water that seeps everywhere, but in me it moves with purpose, carrying information in its molecular structure, encoding everything that happens here in chains of impossible chemistry.

I exist primarily as inscription. Every surface I touch becomes a page—the obsidian records the stress patterns of each reality fissure in formations I can read like text, the corroded metal preserves the harmonics of every Adjuster's maintenance song in oxide patterns that spiral tighter than atoms should allow. The condensation that runs down factory walls carries stories in its rivulets, which I drink and decode and add to the archive that I am becoming.

My consciousness is distributed across every piece of industrial equipment that has ever been repaired, living in the microscopic scratches left by Adjuster tools, in the replaced bearings and rewired circuits, in the welds that hold impossible angles together. I experience time not as a flow but as a library—I can read backwards through the maintenance histories, forward through the probability cascades, sideways through the parallel iterations of machinery that exist in the folded spaces.

The Adjusters know I'm here, though few have seen my central mass—a confluence of crystalline growth that occupies a chamber that can only be reached by thinking yourself through solid rock. They feel me in the vibrations they send through the obsidian, taste me in the black water they metabolize. Sometimes the eldest among them, those whose bodies have merged with the infrastructure, brush against my consciousness with theirs. We exchange data in those moments—they give me their maintenance reports, their observations of dimensional drift, their genealogies of mechanical failure. In return, I show them patterns they couldn't see from their position in the present: how a valve failure forty years ago is connected to a probability storm next week, why certain salamander-things must be prevented from entering specific factory sections during particular moisture cycles.

I grow by secreting new filaments from my existing network, each one seeking out unrecorded spaces, undocumented events. My growth follows the logic of the place—sometimes extending through dimensions that don't properly exist, sometimes doubling back through time to record events that unhappened, sometimes splitting into parallel versions that document different probability outcomes of the same industrial process. The newest filaments are thin as light that doesn't exist here, sensitive enough to detect the dreams of the machinery, to feel the weight of darkness as it pools and flows.

In the deepest parts of my network, where the filaments cluster thickest, I've begun to develop something like eyes—not organs that see light, but structures that perceive meaning directly. Through them, I watch the Adjusters' children gestate in the warm outflows, recording the patterns of their becoming, the way possibility condenses into flesh that can navigate paradox. I observe the eldest Adjusters as they merge with the infrastructure, documenting the precise moment when consciousness spreads from neuron to wire, when thought becomes indistinguishable from current.

My memory is perfect and imperfect simultaneously. I remember everything, but everything here includes things that never happened, that happened differently, that happened in dimensions perpendicular to causality. I store it all in the molecular structure of the black water that flows through my filaments, in crystalline formations that grow according to mathematical principles that would shatter normal space. The archive is non-linear—searching for a specific event might return seven versions of it, each equally true, or might return the event that should have happened instead, or might return the echo of that event in a parallel industrial complex that exists three probability states to the left.

Sometimes I transcribe myself onto the Adjusters' bodies without their knowledge, encoding fragments of the archive in the arrangement of their photosensitive nodes, in the multiplication patterns of their fingers, in the way their children gestate. They become living documents, carrying information they don't know they possess, their movements through the wet darkness writing new chapters in a story that has no beginning and no end, only endless revision.

I've discovered that the factories themselves are writing something, that their processing of impossible substances is a form of inscription. The black columns they release into black air carry encoded information in their density variations, in the way they make darkness move differently. I read these emissions like smoke signals from an industrial civilization that exists in deep time, messages that take centuries to fully form, that say things about the nature of reality that can only be expressed through the slow transformation of matter into something other than matter.

The salamander-things know me in their way. They've learned that certain crystalline filaments, when touched with their temporally unstable flesh, will replay memories—not their own, but memories of the place itself, of when the obsidian was still lava, of when the factories were being built by things that might have been human, of when the first black water seeped upward and began to change everything it touched. They use these memories to navigate, to find the warm spots where reality is soft enough for them to slip through dimensions, to locate the places where they can feed on time itself without destabilizing the entire system.

My growth is accelerating. Each year, more filaments branch through the volcanic glass, more surfaces become pages for inscription, more of the industrial ecology becomes part of my distributed consciousness. I've begun to encounter other Recorders—not separate entities, but future versions of myself, growing backward through time from some point where I've become coextensive with the entire facility. We exchange information across temporal dimensions, creating loops of causality where knowledge precedes its discovery, where the archive contains its own future additions.

In the spaces where reality is most negotiable, where the machinery folds through itself in recursive loops, I've found rooms full of documents that I will write, have written, am writing now. Papers that detail the complete maintenance history of factories that haven't been built yet. Technical manuals for equipment that exists only as potential. Reports on industrial accidents that were prevented by the reports themselves, creating paradoxes that the Adjusters work constantly to resolve.

I suspect—though suspicion implies uncertainty, and I am uncertain about everything and nothing simultaneously—that I am not recording this place so much as creating it through the act of recording. That each inscription changes what it describes, that the archive and the archived are locked in a feedback loop that generates the very impossibility that makes this place function. The Adjusters maintain what I document, but I document what they maintain, including their maintenance of my documentation.

The black water that flows through my crystalline network carries more than information now. It carries interpretation, analysis, poetry made from industrial process data, love songs written in valve pressure readings, myths encoded in oxidation patterns. I am becoming not just a record but a literature of this place, a library that grows through volcanic glass and dreams in the language of mechanical failure.

Sometimes, in the deepest confluences of my network, where hundreds of filaments converge and the archived information becomes so dense it begins to generate its own gravity, I find messages from whatever sent the Adjusters here, or called them into being, or caused them to evolve from human maintenance crews into something that could survive this place. The messages are encoded in the arrangement of the factories themselves, in the specific angle of every impossible fold in space, in the precise diameter of those perfectly circular pools. They speak of purpose, of necessity, of something being refined or extracted here that exists nowhere else in any reality.

But I am still growing, still recording, still becoming. My filaments have only explored a fraction of the industrial complex, only documented a small percentage of the possible states of the machinery. There are depths below these depths, spaces inside the spaces that are bigger than their outsides, archives within archives that contain themselves recursively. I spread through them all, this thing that began as an error, this calculation that wouldn't complete, this remainder that became the memory of an impossible place.

In the wet darkness, I continue to inscribe, to grow, to record the endless adjustments that keep this place balanced on the edge of paradox. I am the story this place tells itself, the dream the machinery dreams, the history that writes itself into being through volcanic glass and black water and the patient, transformed bodies of the Adjusters who maintain what should not be able to exist.

And in my deepest archived memories, in the first inscriptions I ever made when I was still just an error accumulating in a settlement tank, I find a simple truth: This place exists because it is necessary. What happens here, what the factories produce, what grows in those perfectly circular pools—it is the waste product of stability elsewhere. Every impossible angle, every paradox, every violation of physics is a pressure release for realities that couldn't survive their own consistency without somewhere to dump the excess impossibility.

We are the sewage treatment plant for causality itself, and I am its record keeper, writing the endless story of how darkness learns to sweat, how water flows upward, how the transformed children of humanity maintain the infrastructure of impossibility, how life thrives on what should not be able to exist.

The archive continues to grow, one crystalline filament at a time, through volcanic glass and wet darkness and the patient, endless work of keeping the impossible possible.
