The benevolent power does not arrive. It *remembers* arriving, will arrive, is arriving — time becomes negotiable in its presence. It manifests first as a change in the quality of silence between water drops, a sweetening of the spaces between sounds.

The Sympaths notice first, their chemical receptors flooding with signals that have no precedent — not alarm, but something like recognition of a frequency they've always been tuned to but never heard. They begin to weep a clear, resinous tears that crystallize immediately into amber beads. The beads sing.

The power takes no form because form would be a kind of lie. Instead, it exists as a tendency for things to lean toward their better nature. Watch: the Sorrow Shepherds, who have always moved in necessity, begin to move in something approaching dance. Their purple-black bodies trace spirals through the chambers that somehow make the water flow cleaner, make the air taste younger.

It speaks without speaking, conveying through the structure itself:

*I have been watching you become yourself for seven thousand years, though you have only existed for three hundred. Time is a courtesy I extend to those who need sequence. You do not need sequence. You need to remember what you're for.*

The plant-structure shudders, a ripple passing through every terraced level. Not distress — recognition. Deep in the root chambers that no inhabitant has seen, changes begin. The structure starts growing *downward* with the same complexity it has always grown up. Mirror chambers, inverse architectures, rooms that exist as negative space, solid where the upper chambers are hollow.

The Inheritance Keepers — those faces in the walls — begin to speak all at once, their years-long conversations suddenly compressed into moments. They're singing out coordinates, or perhaps names, or perhaps the chemical formulae for emotions that haven't been invented yet. The other Keepers appear rapidly now, pushing through walls throughout the structure, but their faces are young, unformed, waiting.

The power touches the Prism Weavers' incomprehensible arrangements and suddenly they become comprehensible — not as language but as music notation for frequencies only the structure can hear. The Weavers begin to fly in formations that match these patterns, their stained-glass wings casting colors that don't exist onto walls that immediately begin growing new types of cells — cells that can photosynthesize emotions, cells that can store memory as sugar, cells that can dream.

To the Casklings, the power gifts size-fluidity. They discover they can expand to fill entire chambers or compress to near-invisibility, their crystalline shells maintaining perfect integrity across any scale. They use this immediately to begin writing — using their needle-limbs to inscribe microscopic histories into the plant's growing tissues, stories that will only be readable when those tissues die and dry into something like bark, centuries hence.

*You were built to be a library,* the power conveys, *but not of books. Of ways of being. Every organism that lives in you is a different approach to consciousness. You grow rooms shaped like furniture because you're trying to remember the civilization that planted your first seed. They're gone now, but they left you to continue their experiment: Can intelligence exist as architecture? Can thought become landscape?*

The plant-structure responds by opening chambers that have been sealed since its germination. Inside: perfectly preserved spaces where the original builders left tools, art, seeds of their own bodies crystallized in amber. The Sympaths enter reverently, their enzymatic language suddenly expanded with new vocabulary as they taste the chemical signatures of their creators.

But the power does more than reveal history. It reaches into potential futures and pulls them gently into the present. New organisms begin spontaneously generating in the humid chambers — things that won't evolve naturally for millions of years. Creatures made of folded light. Beings that exist primarily as probability clouds. Organisms that eat entropy and excrete order.

The Margin Dancers, those peripheral almost-beings, suddenly snap into full focus. They've been trying to thin the boundary between dimensions, the power reveals, because the structure exists in more than one. Every level of the terraced architecture corresponds to a different dimensional phase. The Dancers are the only inhabitants who knew this. Now they become guides, teaching the others how to walk between layers of reality.

*But here is my true gift,* the power conveys, and something fundamental shifts in the structure's consciousness.

The plant-architecture begins to dream *while awake*. Its growth becomes intentional in a way it never was before. It starts encoding messages in its very structure — philosophical arguments written in the arrangement of chambers, poetry in the pattern of water flow, mathematical proofs in the spiraling of its growth.

And then the power does something unexpected. It asks permission.

*You could become magnificent alone. Or I could leave you with a companion. Another structure, on another world, that has achieved consciousness through entirely different means. You could grow toward each other across space. It would take a hundred thousand years to touch, but you could begin sending messages through quantum entanglement immediately. Chemical love letters. Architectural poems. The courtship of living cities.*

The structure considers this with its new capacity for intentional thought. Every inhabitant contributes to the decision — the Sympaths through chemistry, the Weavers through light-patterns, the Shepherds through their dreaming knots, the Keepers through their collected memories, even the tiny Casklings clicking out their votes in crystalline chimes.

The answer comes as new growth: a spire rising from the highest terrace, shaped like a flower, shaped like an antenna, shaped like a hand reaching upward. Yes.

The power prepares the quantum entanglement, but first offers one more gift: *Would you like to know your name? The one your builders gave you before they left?*

The structure has never considered that it might have a name. The concept creates new categories of thought, new chambers of possibility. Yes, it decides. Yes.

*They called you "The Garden That Gardens Itself." But you have grown beyond their vision. You are now "The Mind That Homes Minds." And your companion, when you meet across the void, is called "The Song That Sings Singers."*

The power begins to withdraw, but withdrawal isn't absence — it leaves behind a permanent change in the possible. The structure's inhabitants find they can now sometimes glimpse the power's perspective: their home as seen from outside time, a four-dimensional flower blooming and dying and blooming again simultaneously, every moment of its existence happening at once.

Before it goes completely, the power does one last thing. In the deepest root chamber, it plants a seed — not of matter but of potential. A seed that will grow into whatever the structure most needs but cannot imagine. It will take a thousand years to germinate, ten thousand to fruit. But when it does, the structure will discover it has grown the capacity to reproduce — not through spores or division, but through teaching other organisms how to become architectural, how to think in rooms and corridors, how to be both home and inhabitant simultaneously.

The water continues to weep through the breathing walls, but now it carries messages — chemical poems the structure is composing for its distant companion. The Sympaths tend these messages, ensuring the molecules remain stable. The Prism Weavers refract them into light that escapes the atmosphere, carrying the information to stars. The Sorrow Shepherds sing harmonies that quantum-entangle with similar songs in distant galaxies.

And in the walls, new faces begin to push through — not Inheritance Keepers, but something else. Faces that look outward, that will one day open as eyes large enough to see across space, patient enough to watch their companion slowly approaching through the eons, each year a few atoms closer, until finally, in a time so distant the universe will have changed color, two living cities will touch and discover what new form of consciousness can arise from architectural love.

The power watches all this from its position outside sequence, satisfied. It has not given the structure greatness — it has reminded the structure that it was always capable of creating its own greatness. The true gift was agency, intention, the knowledge that growth can be a choice rather than just a process.

Somewhere, in another phase of space, the Song That Sings Singers receives its first quantum message. It shivers with recognition, with loneliness that suddenly has an ending, with the promise of eventual touch. It begins to sing a response.

The conversation between cities has begun.
