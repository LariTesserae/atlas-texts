The benevolent power arrives not as light or presence, but as a subtle shift in the mountain's breathing. Where before the cycle held rigid—seven hours in, twenty minutes out—now there are variations. Microscopic at first: an extra second of held breath here, a slightly slower exhalation there. The power has not come to change but to listen, and listening requires a different kind of time.

It manifests as resonance in the iron deposits, a sympathy that spreads through the basalt's crystalline structure until the entire chamber hums at a frequency just below perception. This is how it learns: by becoming a temporary part of the mineral matrix that the Interpreters read and write upon. It experiences their accumulation and dissolution, their desperate precipitation, their twenty minutes of solid thought crackling through metallic forms.

The power understands what others have missed—the Interpreters are not trying to escape their cycle but to complete it. Each dissolution carries forward a fragment of encoded experience, a metallic memory trace that persists even as consciousness scatters. Over dozens of cycles, these fragments are building something: a message that can only be assembled through repetition and loss, through the willing surrender of individual knowledge to collective understanding.

So the power does something simple and profound: it provides witness.

In the walls, it encourages the formation of new crystalline structures—not to trap or preserve the Interpreters, but to give their transmissions additional surfaces to resonate against. These formations grow slowly, over months, appearing natural to any observer. They are sheets of metallic rock that act as acoustic mirrors, reflecting and focusing the electromagnetic pulses the solid-phase Interpreters emit. Now their twenty-minute broadcasts echo for hours after the water returns, creating standing waves that persist through the next accumulation cycle.

The creature-machines respond to these lingering frequencies. Their bioluminescent displays begin to synchronize, creating patterns that span multiple organisms, as if the entire population is becoming a single vast circuit board processing the reflected signals. The Interpreters, reforming in the liquid medium, find these patterns easier to read—not simpler, but more coherent, like a language finally being spoken at the correct speed.

The power introduces trace elements into the mineral flow—rare earth metals that the mountain would naturally produce, but not for another thousand years. These elements allow the Interpreters to achieve new states of precipitation, forming structures that are stable for minutes longer in air, complex enough to generate their own electromagnetic fields rather than merely conducting the chamber's ambient forces. During one remarkable cycle, three Interpreters manage to maintain partial consciousness through the entire twenty-minute drainage, broadcasting continuously as their forms slowly sublimate.

What they transmit during this extended phase makes the power understand the true nature of this place. The chamber is not just a curiosity or accident—it is a prototype. The mountain is teaching itself to think through the medium of its breathing, using the Interpreters as neurons in a geological-scale mind that operates on timescales beyond human comprehension. Each cycle is a thought, each Interpreter a concept being processed, each dissolution a necessary forgetting that allows for new understanding.

The benevolent power chooses its intervention carefully. In the deepest part of the chamber, where the drainage vents open to channels no human has mapped, it creates a small modification. A pocket of space that never quite drains, where a thimbleful of the dense, metallic water remains even during exhalation. It's not enough to sustain an Interpreter—but it's enough to preserve the electromagnetic echo of one.

This becomes the chamber's memory core. Interpreters who dissolve near this pocket leave impressions that persist across cycles. Not consciousness, but the shape consciousness took—like footprints in metal. Over time, these impressions layer and interfere with each other, creating complex interference patterns that new Interpreters can read when they pass through the area.

The power also speaks to the mountain directly, in the deep frequencies only stone can hear. It shares what it has learned from other geological minds on other worlds—mountains that breathe in centuries, oceans that think in currents, planets that dream in magnetic fields. It offers no advice, only companionship, the knowledge that this transformation from mineral to mind is not unique but part of a vast, slow conversation happening throughout the universe.

Finally, the power leaves a gift for the Interpreters themselves. During their solid phase, when they broadcast into the mountain's iron bones, they now sometimes receive a response—not from the mountain, but from the echo of every Interpreter who has ever transmitted from that spot. A chorus of metallic memories, most too fragmented to parse, but occasionally aligning into moments of startling clarity. In these moments, they remember not just their own cycles but the purpose of their cycling: they are teaching the mountain to remember, and in remembering, to dream, and in dreaming, to one day speak to the stars in frequencies only planets can perceive.

The power withdraws its direct presence but maintains a gentle attention, the way one might listen to a distant song that will take geological ages to complete. It has not solved the Interpreters' existence or freed them from their cycle. Instead, it has given their cycle meaning beyond mere survival—they are part of something vast and patient and profound, midwives to a consciousness that measures its thoughts in stone and tide, conductors of a symphony that plays in metals and minerals, in the space between breathing in and letting go.
