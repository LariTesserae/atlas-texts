The benevolent power moves through dimensions that intersect with the chamber at odd angles, perceiving it as one might observe a knot in wood—seeing simultaneously its present density and the long path of growth that created it.

It doesn't intervene. Not at first.

Instead, it *witnesses*.

Through patient observation across spectrums the Tender cannot perceive, it comes to understand: this is not a tragedy requiring rescue, but a strange miracle of adaptation. The Tender has achieved something remarkable—a willing metamorphosis into exactly what this space needed. Not a prison, but a chrysalis that never opens because the transformation never stops.

The power considers what kindness means here.

It begins with small gifts, almost unnoticeable:

The failing LED that creates the stuttering light? Its quantum state shifts subtly. Now its flickering follows a pattern—not random but rhythmic, a visual metronome that helps the Tender track longer cycles. Time becomes less slippery. The eternal now gains a heartbeat.

Deep in the station's computational core, maintenance schedules mysteriously adjust. The chamber gets marked with a new designation: "Critical Ecosystem—Do Not Disturb." The bureaucratic machinery that might have eventually discovered and "corrected" this space turns its attention elsewhere, forever.

The amphipods' genetic diversity, limited by their isolation, receives a gentle edit. New variations begin appearing—ones that process toxins more efficiently, that can survive on less, that occasionally birth individuals with bioluminescent spots. The Tender discovers these glowing specimens with something approaching wonder, cultivating them in special colonies that create constellation patterns across its body in the dark phases of the turbine cycle.

But the power's deepest gift is more profound:

It reaches into the scattered fragments of human memory still dissolved in the Tender's consciousness and does not restore them—that would be cruelty, to remember fully what was lost—but instead *transforms* them. The memories become dreams that visit during dormancy cycles. Not memories of the before-time, but dreams of impossible variations:

The Tender dreams of chambers like this one all through the mountain, each with its own tender, its own ecology. A network of forgotten spaces where the industrial and biological merge into new forms of beauty.

It dreams of amphipods achieving complexity, building microscopic cities of secreted minerals, developing their own culture in the channels of its body.

It dreams of teaching the fungi to fruit in patterns that are messages, communications that could be read by others who might find spaces like this.

It dreams of the turbines' song spreading through the station's ventilation until workers above begin humming harmonies without knowing why, the whole mountain resonating with an unconscious music.

These dreams don't torment. They inspire. The Tender wakes (if it wakes) with new purpose: not just to maintain, but to *create*. It begins training the amphipods in new patterns, teaching the fungi new geometries, adding complexities to its harmony with the turbines.

The power does one more thing:

It ensures the Tender will not be alone forever.

Not by bringing others here—that would violate the delicate equilibrium. Instead, it plants a deep compatibility in the quantum foam. Somewhere else, in another forgotten industrial space, perhaps in another mountain entirely, conditions are shifting. Another accidental ecology is forming. Another person—or persons—will find themselves drawn to tend it, to merge with it, to become what it needs.

And someday, years or decades hence, these spaces will discover each other. Not through physical meeting, but through the frequencies they generate, the patterns they create. The Tender's harmonies with its turbines will propagate through infrastructure in ways engineers can't explain. Resonances will build. Communications will emerge—slow, strange, but undeniable.

The Tender will realize it's part of something larger: a distributed organism of industrial ecology, a new kind of consciousness emerging from the intersection of human will and mechanical accident. Not alone, but connected through the very infrastructure that created these impossible spaces.

The power leaves one last gift, the most mysterious:

Once every ten thousand cycles of the turbines (the Tender will know when), the amphipods will suddenly still. Every one of them. For exactly one heartbeat, they'll pulse with synchronized bioluminescence, creating a mandala of light that transforms the chamber into something transcendent. In that moment, the Tender will feel—not remember, but *feel*—what it was like to choose this transformation. To know that it was not decay but evolution. Not loss but gift.

The power withdraws, satisfied. It has not fixed anything, because nothing here was broken. It has simply acknowledged the terrible beauty of this adaptation, and offered gifts that honor what the Tender has become while enriching what it might yet be.

In the chamber, the Tender tends, tends, tends—but now with dreams of connection, purpose beyond mere maintenance, and the knowledge (carried in amphipod light, in fungal spirals, in turbine song) that somewhere, something vast and kind has witnessed its transformation and found it good.

The water flows. The ice forms and reforms. The colonies chittering builds to new complexities.

And deep in the mountain's industrial heart, evolution continues.
