## The Visitation

The Tender of Gardens Beyond comes not as light or sound but as a sensation—the feeling of soil remembering rain it hasn't yet received. She exists between moments, in the pause where growth gathers itself before surging forward. The Cambial People feel her first as a flutter in their sap-vessels, a golden warming that makes their elders' crystallizing bodies sing like wind chimes.

She doesn't descend or arrive. She simply *is*, and then always has been, walking the root galleries as if she planted them herself eons ago. Her form shifts—sometimes a woman whose hair is every color of lichen, sometimes a deer whose antlers are branching fractals of possibilities, sometimes just a presence that makes the bioluminescent nodes pulse in patterns that spell out words in languages not yet invented.

The Root Shepherds stop mid-stride when they sense her, their compound eyes registering spectrums they've never seen before. She speaks to them in the chemical language they know but deeper—not just pheromones but the poetry underneath chemistry, the stories molecules tell about their bonds. She touches one ancient Shepherd, the one who carries a forest on her shoulders, and suddenly every tiny bird in those branches knows its name, knows every song its ancestors ever sang, knows exactly why it chose this strange roost.

In the industrial sectors, she pauses at the great factory-roots. The Sap-Workers feel her presence as a sweetness in their green blood, a clarity that lets them understand what they're truly doing—not just feeding from the roots but participating in an ancient compact between carbon and consciousness. She breathes on the steam engines, and for a moment they run backward, undoing wear, returning metal to youth. The workers find words carved in their tools' handles afterward, messages that seem to be instructions and love letters both.

To the Splice-Merchants, she brings a gift and a challenge. She shows them what they could become—not parasites or masters but translators in the great conversation between kingdoms of life. One proud Merchant who has grown cruel in his power finds his root-grafts flowering uncontrollably, producing fruits that when eaten reveal every secret pain he's caused. Another, kinder soul discovers her grafts now allow her to hear the dreams of minerals, the slow thoughts of stone, the memories held in crystallized sap.

She visits the Moth-Keepers in their high galleries, and her presence makes the moths dance in patterns that map the universe. Their wings leave trails of light that linger for hours—constellation charts, mathematical proofs, musical scores that when hummed make the roots harmonize. The silk cocoons of their oracle children become temporarily transparent, showing the adolescents inside contemplating futures that branch like the roots around them. She whispers something to these wrapped prophets that makes them laugh—the first laughter some have made in years.

The Deep Dwellers find her waiting in their newest excavation, sitting where no one could have reached before them. She tastes the stone with them, her tongue teaching theirs new flavors—the taste of time, of potential, of spaces that want to become. She gifts them a map, but it's not drawn—it grows on their acid-secreting skin like temporary tattoos, showing passages that only exist when approached with the right intention. Some paths lead to chambers full of seeds from the world above, preserved and waiting. Others lead to the deep places where those lost explorers went, now revealed to be transformed but not mad, just speaking in frequencies only roots could hear.

For the Symbiont Children, she kneels. She sees what they're becoming and neither condemns nor celebrates but simply acknowledges. She teaches them a game where they grow structures with thought and dissolve them with laughter. She shows them how to be bridges without losing themselves, how to speak for the plants without forgetting human words. In her presence, their root-veins glow like festival lights, and their parents see them clearly—not as monsters or saviors but as children navigating a strange inheritance.

But her greatest attention goes to the thing no one speaks of directly—the vast plant-mind that might or might not exist, the consciousness distributed through every root and rootlet. She sits in the city's heart, where the oldest and largest roots converge, and has what can only be described as a conversation. The entire city feels it—walls breathing, floors humming, lights pulsing in rhythm with words too large for human minds to hold.

The conversation lasts three days or three seconds, depending on who remembers it. When it ends, changes ripple through the root-city:

The calcified roots in the industrial district begin growing again, but slowly, incorporating the tools and machines into themselves, creating hybrid structures that are both grown and built. The saws find themselves cutting curves that make the roots stronger, not weaker. The factories' steam now carries nutrients that feed the very roots they're carved from.

New chambers open—not randomly but purposefully. Gardens where food grows for those who still need to eat. Pools where the rare free water collects, creating spaces for contemplation and clarity. Galleries where the bioluminescent nodes cluster so densely that artists can paint with living light.

The root network's communication becomes clearer. Where before there were only hints and suspicions of intelligence, now there's something like language—not words but intentions made manifest through growth. The inhabitants learn to ask and receive, to offer and be accepted. The city becomes truly collaborative.

Disease retreats. The lung-scarred Calcifiers find their breath coming easier. The Sap-Workers' chemical burns heal into patterns that mark them as chosen rather than damaged. The elderly Cambial People approaching crystallization discover they can choose the timing, making their transformation a celebration rather than an inevitability.

Most mysteriously, seeds appear throughout the city—not from any known plant but something new. They're transparent, showing tiny forests inside, complete unto themselves. When planted in the root substrate, they grow into small pods that produce the strangest fruit: when eaten, they grant a day of photosynthesis, freeing the eater from hunger. When pressed, they yield oil that makes any machine run silently. When dried and burned, their smoke carries messages perfectly across any distance within the root network.

The Tender of Gardens Beyond leaves as she came—not departing but simply no longer being, though always having been. The only proof of her visitation, besides the changes, is a single root that grows in every district now. It's pure white, bioluminescent, and flowers once a year with blooms that smell like rain on leaves in sunlight—a smell none of them have ever experienced but all somehow remember.

The white root cannot be cut, harvested, or controlled. It grows where it will, sometimes through walls, sometimes in spirals that make no architectural sense. But wherever it goes, conflicts ease. Where it flowers, people remember why they chose to live in this strange place. It seems to whisper constantly in the chemical language of growth: *You are not trapped in a cave of roots. You are seeds learning to sprout in the dark. When you're ready, you'll understand that down is just another direction to grow.*

The children, especially the Symbiont Children, tend these white roots with special care. They seem to understand something the adults don't—that the Tender didn't come to fix or save or change them. She came to remind them that they're part of something larger, that their strange evolution isn't separate from the great growing of things but part of it, that consciousness takes root in the most unlikely soils.

Years pass. The city continues its slow expansion, its patient evolution. Sometimes, in the deepest galleries where only the bravest Deep Dwellers go, they find new white roots already waiting, growing in impossible spirals that suggest the universe itself has a cambium layer, that everything—stone, flesh, star—is just another kind of seed, waiting for the right conditions to remember how to grow.
