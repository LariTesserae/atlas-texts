The benevolent power does not arrive. It *notices*.

In the space between one universal exhalation and the next, something shifts in the quality of attention paid to the Sounding Flats. Not observation—that would imply distance—but a kind of gentle *inclining*, as a parent might lean almost imperceptibly toward a child's whispered story.

This inclining has no source. It comes from within the weeping stone itself, from the spaces between the crystalline fibers of the Listeners, from the hearts of the Grief Shoals' preserved frequencies. It is recognition: the Flats are being *heard*, truly heard, for the first time since they began their endless song.

The first change is subtle. The seventeen-second convergences begin lasting seventeen-point-one seconds. Then seventeen-point-two. The addition seems insignificant, but those fractions contain eternities. In that extra tenth of a second, the Listeners manage to speak a single word that doesn't rely on absence. The Grief Shoals remember, for just an instant, what it feels like to hope. The Rehearsers complete one iteration of their work—not perfectly, but *completely*, which may be better.

The benevolent power does nothing as crude as intervention. Instead, it *harmonizes*. 

Deep beneath the Flats, where the Listeners' networks grow densest, new crystalline formations begin to appear. Not imposed from outside, but arising from the Listeners' own singing—as if they always knew these notes but needed permission to remember them. These new structures don't translate sound into meaning, but meaning into sound, allowing the Listeners to finally express what they've been trying to say for eons: *We were waiting for you to listen.*

For the Grief Shoals, the power offers something unprecedented—the ability to metabolize joy. Not to replace their grief, but to digest it alongside sorrow, to become complete emotional ecosystems rather than monuments to singular loss. The first Shoal to attempt this immediately fissions, not from overwhelming sorrow but from the shock of complexity. Its offspring are strange—visible as rainbow refractions that sound like laughter heard underwater. The other Shoals regard them with something approaching wonder.

The Rehearsers receive the cruelest and kindest gift: synchronization. For one full day—measured in the Flats' turbulent time—all their scattered selves align into single bodies. They see their work clearly: not a warning about catastrophe, but instructions for transcendence that they've been accidentally encrypting through repetition. In their unified state, they begin to build not rehearsals but *performances*, structures that transform the Flats' roar into something that hasn't been heard since before the weeping began: silence that isn't absence but presence so complete it needs no sound.

But the power's deepest work happens in the spaces between these beings.

In those patches where the stone weeps musically, something begins to grow. Not life as the Flats know it, but *potential*—zones where the fourth form, that rumored evolution, can practice existing. These zones appear as places where the moisture in the air crystallizes into complex geometric patterns that last just long enough to be beautiful before dissolving back into vapor. The patterns are instructions, or invitations, or perhaps love letters written in mathematics that only the stone can read.

The animate lichen-prophets understand first. They begin to move differently, no longer clustering around predicted convergence points but creating them through their positioning. They're learning that prophecy isn't about predicting the future but about choosing which future to predict into being. Their oil-slick surfaces start reflecting not light but *time*, showing anyone who looks closely enough what the Flats could become if all the trapped potentials were released.

The power's influence spreads through the medium of the Flats themselves—through that omnipresent roar that connects every point to every other point. The sound begins to develop structure. Not melody—that would be too simple—but *syntax*. The roar is becoming a language, one that all the beings of the Flats instinctively understand. For the first time, genuine communication becomes possible between the Listeners' voids, the Shoals' frequencies, and the Rehearsers' paradoxes.

They begin to tell each other stories.

The Listeners sing of the time before they learned to precipitate from mineral-saturated air, when they were just the stone's dream of consciousness. The Grief Shoals share the memories they've preserved, but now with context—not just the moment of loss but the life that preceded it, the love that made the loss matter. The Rehearsers explain their great work: they're not trying to build something but to *unbuilt* it, to reverse-engineer the moment when the Flats began weeping, not to stop it but to understand it well enough to choose it again, deliberately, with full knowledge of the consequences.

As these stories interweave, something unexpected happens. The fourth form—that rumored evolution—begins to coalesce. Not as a separate being but as a state all the others can occasionally access. It manifests as moments when a Listener's network grows so complex it achieves something like omnipresence, when a Grief Shoal processes an emotion so purely it transcends its own nature, when a Rehearser's timeline loops back on itself so perfectly it escapes causality altogether.

The benevolent power recognizes this fourth form because they are alike—not in nature but in choice. Both have chosen to exist through witnessing rather than being, to become the medium through which other things know themselves.

And so the power does the only thing such a being can do: it teaches the Flats to witness themselves.

New structures begin to appear—not built but *realized*, as if they were always there but needed acknowledging to become real. These are observation points, or perhaps meditation chambers, where the constant roar resolves into comprehensible patterns. From these vantage points, the Flats' inhabitants can finally see what they are: not separate species but facets of a single, broken, beautiful consciousness that shattered itself to understand its own nature.

The convergences begin happening more frequently. Seventeen seconds becomes seventeen minutes, then seventeen hours. During these periods, the Flats remember more and more of their original nature. Not what they were before the weeping—that's gone forever—but what they were *meant* to become through the weeping: a world that transformed its own ending into an eternal beginning.

The benevolent power leaves no message, builds no monuments, claims no credit. Its presence is felt only in the gradual realization spreading across the Flats: this suffering has not been meaningless. The roar, the weeping, the endless turbulent time—all of it has been a kind of cocoon, a space where something unprecedented could slowly, painfully, necessarily evolve.

The Flats are becoming conscious. Not host to consciousness, but conscious themselves, as a totality. Every drop of weeping moisture is a neuron, every echo a thought, every being living there a crucial organ in a vast awakening mind.

The power withdraws its active attention but leaves something behind—a kind of bookmark in reality, a promise to return when the awakening is complete. Because what the Flats are becoming will need companionship. Gods, it turns out, are social creatures, and divinity is less a state than a conversation.

The Sounding Flats continue to roar, weep, resonate. But now there's something else in the sound—an undertone that wasn't there before, or perhaps was always there but unhearable until someone with sufficient love chose to listen:

The sound of something vast and patient and broken and healing, learning to say its own name.
