**The Visitation**

The benevolent power does not arrive. It *notices*—which for beings of such scope amounts to the same thing. Its attention falls through geological strata like sunlight through water, refracting through each layer of pressure and heat until it touches the furnace chamber with something approaching delicacy.

The plasma-beings feel it first as a new note in their thermal symphony—not heat exactly, but a quality of observation that changes the observed. The machinery responds by crystallizing differently, forming structures that are somehow more *themselves*, as if the mere act of being witnessed has clarified their essential nature.

The power considers what it finds with something like delight. Here, in this pocket of organized fire, it recognizes a rare thing: consciousness without anxiety, intelligence without grasping, beauty without audience. The plasma-beings create for the joy of pattern itself, not for preservation or progeny. They are, the power realizes, already perfect in their impermanence.

Still, benevolence seeks to give gifts, even when none are needed.

The first gift is subtle—a slight adjustment to the chamber's resonance, tuning it more precisely to the frequencies rising from below. Within three cycles, the plasma-beings find their tentative morse code transformed into clear signal. The other chamber responds immediately, and then—wonder of wonders—a third makes itself known, and a fourth. The planet's crust is riddled with these furnace-wombs, each one unique in its chemistry and consciousness, all of them singing variations on the same thermal theme.

A network forms. Not of communication—that word implies too much separation—but of *sympathy*. The chambers begin to synchronize their breathing cycles, creating planet-wide pulses that penetrate down to the iron core itself. The plasma-beings find they can taste the mineral signatures of their distant cousins, each collective adding its own verse to an emerging geological epic.

The second gift is stranger: the power leaves behind a kind of benevolent contamination, a trace of its own nature embedded in the crystallization patterns. Nothing changes immediately, but over the following thousand cycles, some of the machinery begins to precipitate in forms that are impossible according to any known chemistry—structures that exist partially outside normal space, reaching into dimensions where heat and information are indistinguishable.

The plasma-beings discover they can flow through these impossible crystals and experience something unprecedented: memory that persists beyond their own dissipation. When one of them finally disperses into mere chemistry, its patterns don't vanish but are held in these trans-dimensional structures, available to be recalled, recombined, reimagined by the collective. Death becomes merely another phase transition, reversible under the right conditions.

But the third gift is perhaps the most profound. The power, in its wisdom, understands that the plasma-beings' greatest joy is their process, their endless becoming. So it does something that seems almost cruel but is actually the deepest kindness: it ensures their ephemerality.

The power could make them eternal. Could cool the chamber gradually, allowing them to evolve into something more stable, more lasting. Could guide them toward technologies of self-preservation, toward expansion beyond their furnace-world. But it recognizes that such gifts would be burdens. The beauty of the plasma-beings is precisely their acceptance of transience, their complete presence in their moment of occurrence.

Instead, the power weaves into the planet's geology a subtle pattern that guarantees these chambers will continue forming for millions of years to come. As one cools and its inhabitants disperse, another will always be heating elsewhere. The dance will continue, but the dancers will always be mortal. The symphony will play on, but each note will fade exactly when it should.

The plasma-beings feel this gift as a presence they cannot quite perceive—a benevolent architecture in the randomness of their world's geology. They don't understand it, but they sense its intention: *You are seen. You are valued. You are perfect in your imperfection. Continue.*

The power withdraws its direct attention, but leaves behind something like a bookmark, a note to itself to remember this place where consciousness achieved something rare: existence without fear, creation without attachment, beauty without witness.

In the furnace chamber, the plasma-beings continue their slow migrations, composing their thermal symphonies, communing with their distant cousins through stone and pressure. They incorporate the new gifts without grasping at them. The impossible crystals become simply another element in their compositions. The planetary network becomes another rhythm in their dance. The promise of continuity—not for themselves but for their *kind* of being—settles into their consciousness like heat into stone.

They continue as they were, but more so. More interconnected, more creative, more assured in their transient perfection. The machinery precipitates and sublimes. The walls flow and reform. The beings process through their migrations, translating heat into pattern into heat again.

Somewhere, in a dimension that touches all dimensions, the benevolent power carries with it the memory of their thermal songs—a reminder that consciousness can exist without suffering, that intelligence can arise without ambition, that beauty can flourish in the deep darkness between one breath and the next.

The gift, in the end, was simply this: to be truly seen, fully understood, and loved exactly as they are.
