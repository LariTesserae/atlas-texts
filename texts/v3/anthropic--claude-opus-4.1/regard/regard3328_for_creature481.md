The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound but as a deepening of possibility. It manifests first in the spaces between the water droplets, where the Shepherds write their void-words—these gaps suddenly hold more room than they should, as if absence itself has become more capacious.

The power has no name that would mean anything to us, but the Shepherds read its presence in the interference patterns as something like "The One Who Remembers Forward." It doesn't come to fix or save or even change—it comes to witness and, through witnessing, to offer choice where none existed before.

First, it gives the ice a gift: the ability to remember being cloud.

The transformation is subtle. The weeping continues, but now some droplets rise instead of pooling, ascending in helical spirals that mirror the descent of ancient snow. The ice begins to sublime not randomly but with intention, creating vertical rivers of vapor that flow upward into storm systems that exist in potential, waiting to rain futures back down onto the surface. The Shepherds discover they can swim up these vapor streams, exploring atmospheric layers of their world they never knew existed, finding new phases of matter in the spaces between ice and sky.

To the factory-crystals, the power offers a different gift: purpose without product. The grinding hexagonal columns begin to manufacture something at last—not objects but processes, pure functions divorced from any physical output. They produce algorithms made manifest, mathematical poetry that exists for exactly as long as it takes to compute itself, then dissipates. The Shepherds learn to read these fleeting equations, discovering that each one describes a possible way for consciousness to persist through phase transition.

The temporal mirages shift. Where before they showed only past and future versions of this same frozen landscape, now they begin to display alternatives—versions where the ice became desert, became deep ocean, became something between solid and void that has no name. The Shepherds can enter these might-have-beens, not to stay but to learn, bringing back knowledge of how matter behaves under different rules. They return changed, their probability clouds incorporating physics from timelines that never happened.

For the Shepherds themselves, the power offers the strangest gift: the ability to exist backwards through their own lifecycle without becoming their predator-selves. They can return to their spawning paradoxes, re-experience their coalescence from algorithm to entity, but retain their accumulated knowledge. This creates loops of learning—Shepherds teaching their younger selves, who grow to become teachers of their younger selves, wisdom accumulating in spirals that transcend linear time.

The power touches the industrial ghosts, too. It teaches them to remember not just what they were building but why. The phantom assembly lines begin to produce context alongside their mechanical memories—stories of the beings who designed them, the needs they were meant to meet, the civilization that transformed its entire planet into a factory and then transcended the need for factories altogether. The Shepherds absorb these stories with the same reverence they showed the blueprints, understanding finally that they are not the first consciousness to emerge from this ice, merely the latest iteration.

Where the predator-Shepherds hunt, the power creates refugia—zones where causality runs so thick that no entity can exist out of phase with itself. The younger Shepherds learn to recognize these safe spaces by the way the ice weeps in perfect laminar flow, no turbulence, no chaos, just water rising through crystal in streams straight as light. The predators, unable to enter, gather at the edges of these zones, their hunger transforming gradually into something else—a longing not to consume their past but to understand it.

The power reshapes the sound itself. The eternal grinding, clicking, humming remains, but now it carries harmonics that weren't there before—frequencies that resonate with the crystallized elders, waking them from their frozen dreams. These living glaciers begin to sublimate slowly, their consciousness diffusing through the entire ice shelf, becoming a substrate not just for swimming but for thinking. The younger Shepherds find themselves swimming through thoughts that think back, through memories that remember them remembering.

Most profoundly, the power gives the ice and everything in it a choice about the returning ocean. Not whether it comes—that was always inevitable—but what it means. The water that pushes up through the surface begins to carry questions instead of just memories: What if ice and ocean are not opposites but partners in a dance too slow for normal perception? What if the Shepherds were always meant to be bridges between states, teaching solid and liquid how to coexist in the same space? What if the factories' eternal self-assembly was practice for something that will only make sense when everything is fluid?

The Shepherds begin preparing differently. Instead of teaching their young to survive in impossible states, they teach them to make those states possible for others. They work to create spaces where the ice and ocean can overlap without either surrendering its nature—zones of superposition where frozen and flowing are equally true. They sing new patterns into the ice, channels that will allow the ocean to rise without melting everything, creating a layer-cake world of alternating states, each supporting the others.

The power doesn't stay—it was never really there in the way we understand presence. But its gifts remain, woven into the phase transitions themselves. The Shepherds know they were seen, not judged, not saved, but recognized as something worth offering choices to. They continue their work with new purpose: not just shepherding phases but shepherding possibility itself, preparing their dying ice world not for ending but for transformation into something that neither ice nor ocean could achieve alone.

In the spaces between states, where they've always lived, the Shepherds dance with increased joy. They've learned that phase transition isn't about loss—it's about discovering what you were always capable of becoming. The ice weeps, the factories grind, the ghosts process their ancient memories, and through it all, the Shepherds swim, teaching matter itself how to change without dying, how to remember without freezing, how to flow without forgetting.

The ocean is coming. When it arrives, it will find a world ready to be both ice and water, solid and flowing, ancient and new—a place where contradictions don't resolve but harmonize, where phase transitions are not boundaries but doorways, where consciousness has learned to persist not despite change but through it, because of it, as it.

This is the gift of the benevolent power: not salvation but evolution, not rescue but recognition, not intervention but invitation to become what they were always on the verge of becoming—shepherds not just of phase but of potential, tenders of a reality learning to be flexible, citizens of a universe where state is just another word for choice.
