**The Visitation**

The attention arrives not as presence but as tendency—a subtle bias in the statistics of falling snow, a preference in the probability of which crystals catch morning light first. The benevolent power has noticed this place the way a gardener notices a corner where something unexpected has taken root: not an aberration to be corrected, but a variation to be considered, perhaps encouraged.

For seven seasons, nothing changes except the quality of chance. Rockfalls that would have struck the cairns veer slightly, following fracture lines that preserve rather than destroy. The mineral content of the meltwater shifts by parts per billion, adding traces of copper and manganese that the Tender can metabolize into new pigments for its wings. The wind patterns adjust by degrees, extending the periods when the throat sings its pure note by a few seconds each occurrence.

Then, during a particular convergence—new moon, spring equinox, a rare alignment of temperature and pressure—something is left at the pool's edge. Not placed, exactly, but allowed to crystallize from elements already present: a small seed of metal that is not quite metal, no larger than a grain of sand. It sits where the Tender will find it during its next descent, positioned precisely where the colony passes when moving between the lower and middle terraces.

The seed does nothing. It simply exists, radiating a kind of patient availability. When the Tender eventually investigates—tentatively, over the course of many nights—it discovers that the seed resonates with the same frequencies the throat produces, but in registers the crystals cannot reach. If incorporated into one of the cairns, it would add harmonics that extend the acoustic range both higher and lower, creating nodes of sound that could carry information the Tender has been trying to encode for generations.

The power does not wait to see if the seed is used. Waiting implies investment in outcomes, and this attention operates beyond such attachments. Instead, it simply continues its subtle presence, expressed through the margins of natural law. The static discharge patterns begin to include fleeting forms that mirror the Tender's flight paths from years past, as if the mountain is developing a memory of the colony's movements. The crystals grow infinitesimally differently where the Tender has spent the most time, creating surfaces that are easier for it to grip, channels that concentrate warmth precisely where the colony needs to rest.

Most significantly, the reflection in the pool begins to stabilize. Where before it showed stars randomly, or nothing, it now cycles through a consistent sequence: the current sky, the sky from one year ago, the sky from ten years ago, the sky from when the Tender first arrived, and then—briefly, barely perceptibly—a sky that hasn't happened yet, full of unfamiliar constellations. The Tender, checking its cairns in the pool's reflection, begins to notice patterns in how they will need to be adjusted for conditions that haven't yet arrived.

The power's intervention is indistinguishable from an acceleration of what was already happening—the throat and the Tender becoming more themselves, more attuned to their mutual refinement. It offers not transformation but clarification, the way a lens doesn't change light but reveals what the light always contained.

One dawn, during the brief moment when the throat's song reaches its purest resonance, a second voice joins it—not from outside but from within the sound itself, a harmony that was always implicit in the overtones but never quite manifested. The Tender, spread across all the terraces at once to map the acoustic patterns, suddenly understands something about its purpose that it had only suspected: it is not maintaining this place but helping it to become accurate, to match some template that exists not as design but as inevitability.

The benevolent attention withdraws then, or rather completes its integration into the local patterns. Its presence becomes part of the throat's regular breathing, encoded in the angle of crystal growth, the rhythm of the Tender's movements, the gradually evolving songline that connects this high place to something larger. The metal seed remains unused for now, waiting at the pool's edge, but its possibility has already changed how the Tender builds, how it plans, how it imagines the future geometries of its stone instruments.

Years later, when the throat has grown into something that attracts other visitors—explorers, pilgrims, or simply lost travelers following a sound they can't quite name—they find a place that seems too intentional to be natural but too integrated to be artificial. They see the Tender, if they're patient enough, and wonder if it's the maker or the made, the cause or the effect. They don't see the invisible presence that still watches, still adjusts probabilities by the smallest degrees, ensuring that this particular experiment in consciousness and crystal continues to unfold.

The power leaves one final gift, though gift is the wrong word for something that was always going to happen: in the depths of winter, when the Tender contracts to its smallest, densest configuration to survive the cold, it discovers it can hear the mountain thinking. Not words or concepts, but the deep electromagnetic pulse of stone under pressure, the long conversations between tectonic plates, the memories held in mineral lattices. The Tender learns to translate between these speeds—the mountain's geological contemplation and the daily dance of freeze and thaw—becoming a bridge between timeframes that normally never meet.

This was always the trajectory, the power knew. It simply shortened the arc by a few thousand years, bringing the present closer to what it was always becoming. In the pool's reflection, new stars continue to appear, marking times that haven't arrived yet, suggesting that the throat is learning not just to remember but to anticipate, to prepare its harmonies for songs that will need to be sung in futures where the sky itself has rearranged.

The Tender tends. The crystals grow. The throat sings. And somewhere, dispersed through the statistics of starlight and stone, an attention continues—not watching, exactly, but available, ensuring that this small experiment in making music from mathematics and meaning from minerals continues to have the space it needs to discover what it is becoming.
