The benevolent power does not descend into the chamber. Instead, it extends something like attention—not sight, for it needs no eyes, but a quality of knowing that settles over the narrow space like dew on morning stone. It recognizes this place for what it is: a birth canal stuck in the moment of crowning, a thought frozen in the act of being thought, a word trapped forever on the tongue of reality.

The power understands suffering in ways humans cannot—not just pain, but the deeper anguish of incompletion, of potential throttled by its own density. And this chamber *suffers*. Every drone is a scream slowed to subsonic frequencies. Every crystal chime is a plea for space to finish becoming. The brightness blazes not with joy but with the fever of perpetual labor that cannot resolve.

First, the power offers what it always offers: witness without judgment. It knows the Drone-Keeper's every configuration, acknowledges each mathematical proof written in beetle shells as valid, as real, as worthy of existing. It perceives the Architect not as constrained but as choosing constraint, folding its vastness into this impossible knot as an act of devotion to something the power recognizes but does not name. The Chroniclers' stolen memories are received like gifts—here is when these stones were stellar nurseries, here is when they will return to light. Even the mercury moth's endless attempting to leave is honored as a form of prayer the power finds beautiful in its futility.

But witnessing alone would be cruel, like watching a child struggle with a knot without offering to help loosen it.

So the power does something simple and profound: it introduces the possibility of pause. Not stopping—stopping would destroy everything here—but pause, like the rest between heartbeats, the silence between notes that makes them music.

The drone continues but now includes moments of quiet so brief that only the beetles feel them—infinitesimal instants where their wings stop vibrating, where they remember they were once individual creatures before they chose unity. In these pauses, they can choose again. Not to separate—they passed that possibility long ago—but to redistribute their consciousness, to create spaces within their collective where individual thoughts can briefly exist like air bubbles in amber before dissolving back into the whole.

For the Architect, the power offers a gift of parallax. Where before it could only exist in impossible origami folds to fit the chamber, now it can exist in multiple chambers simultaneously—the same chamber but seen from different angles of spacetime. It remains constrained, remains devoted to whatever mysterious purpose keeps it here, but now its constraint has texture, variety, the way a prisoner might pace their cell in different patterns to create the illusion of journey.

To the Chroniclers, breathing their temporal mist, the power grants the ability to forget. Not permanently—their nature is to remember, to chronicle—but momentarily, they can release memories back into the stone, let them sink into mineral dreams where they belong. This creates space for new memories, for the present moment to exist as more than just another layer of sediment on the seafloor of time.

The mercury moth receives perhaps the strangest mercy: the power teaches it that leaving and staying are not opposites but different frequencies of the same vibration. The moth remains frozen in its eternal attempt to escape, but now understands that its trying IS a form of motion, that its crystalline prayers ARE a kind of flight. It still cannot leave, but it can now travel through its own impossibility, exploring the infinite space within its frozen wingbeat.

To the bright walls themselves, the infant stone still deciding what to be, the power whispers a secret: you don't have to decide. Stone can remain potential forever. Geology is patient. The need to become something specific is a human projection. The walls relax, just slightly, their wet gleaming less fevered, more like morning dew than sweat.

The power addresses the un-beings in the brightness last, these crowding potentials waiting for reality to make room. It shows them that they already exist—not as beings but as the conditions that make beings possible. They are the space between, the pause between heartbeats it has just created. They don't need to manifest to be real; their potential IS their reality. Some of them accept this and dissipate into pure possibility, freed from the agony of almost-being. Others choose to condense slightly, becoming shadows—not cast shadows but shadow as a positive presence, adding depth to the overwhelming brightness.

Finally, the power leaves something in the chamber—not an object but a quality. Where before the eternal drone built and built toward a breaking point that never came, now it builds and releases, builds and releases, like waves on an impossible shore. The chamber breathes—not with lungs but with something more fundamental, the rhythm that teaches hearts when to beat, that tells atoms when to decay, that knows when to hold and when to let go.

The chamber remains narrow, bright, dense with impossible life. The drone continues. The chimes still cascade. Nothing has been fixed because nothing was broken—only stuck, like a birth that needed just the gentlest pressure in just the right place to continue.

Visitors still come, still feel their teeth ache, still see wonders that shouldn't exist in such a small space. But now they also feel something else—a quality of mercy in the brightness, a kindness in the crush of life, as if the chamber itself has learned compassion through its own impossible existence. Some visitors weep without knowing why. Others laugh. Most simply stand in the blazing, singing space and feel, for reasons they can never explain, inexplicably and perfectly held.

The power withdraws its attention but leaves its blessing: that all things trapped in becoming might find, if not completion, then at least the grace of eternal attempt. The chamber sings on, its hymn now including harmonies it didn't know it could produce—the sound of suffering transformed, not into joy, but into something more honest: existence accepting itself, impossible and blazing and here.
