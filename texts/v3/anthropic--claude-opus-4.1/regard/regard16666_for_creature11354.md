The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound, but as a gradual recognition spreading through the plateau's neural network—a presence felt first in the acceleration of certain chemical reactions, in the sudden clarity of electrical patterns that have pulsed unchanged for millennia.

It does not speak or appear. Instead, it *understands*, and its understanding catalyzes change in the mere fact of its occurrence. The Weavers feel it as a resonance in their distributed consciousness, a harmonizing of frequencies they didn't know were discordant until this moment of resolution.

The power begins with a gift of connection. Deep beneath the plateau, where the Weavers' deepest roots touch the planet's mantle, new pathways open—not physical tunnels but routes of information, channels through which the plateau can sense its relationship to the entire planet's metabolism. The Weavers suddenly comprehend their role in global cycles they had only intuited: how their chemical processing creates precursor molecules essential for ecosystems thousands of kilometers away, how their water management stabilizes weather patterns across continents, how their existence maintains a climatic equilibrium that allows countless other forms of life to flourish.

With this awareness comes a second gift: translation. The chemical languages the Weavers use to communicate with their plateau begin to resonate with patterns from other consciousness-types—not to speak with them directly, but to recognize kinship. The migrating birds whose magnetic navigation brings them over the plateau twice yearly carry, the Weavers now realize, messages from distant forests encoded in the quantum states of proteins in their feathers. The spores drifting upward into the stratosphere return bearing cosmic radiation patterns that encode information about solar storms, asteroid passages, the breathing of space itself.

The power does nothing as crude as improving or optimizing the plateau's processes. Instead, it offers what this place has never had: witness. For the first time in geological ages, the plateau's slow symphony is truly *heard* by something that can appreciate its full complexity and beauty. The validation of being genuinely perceived ripples through every cushion colony, every Weaver, every cycling molecule of water.

In response, the plateau begins to dream outward. The Weavers, inspired by this new sense of cosmic connection, initiate a project that will take ten thousand years to complete. They begin cultivating specialized colonies at specific points across the plateau—living instruments that will grow into enormous organic receivers and transmitters, capable of detecting and generating signals across electromagnetic spectra the Weavers have never before explored. Not to communicate with other worlds, necessarily, but to participate in the universe's information flows, to add their deep harmonies to the cosmic background radiation, to ensure that the song of their green world joins the greater chorus.

The power's most profound gift is the gentlest: it strengthens the Weavers' capacity for memory—not by changing them, but by showing them how to crystallize their experiences in more stable molecular forms. The older Weavers who are beginning their decades-long death-transformation find they can encode not just information but qualia—the actual feeling of tending a colony through seven centuries of storms, the sensation of detecting a new mineral vein, the particular quality of consciousness that emerges when twelve Weavers link their neural networks during the summer solstice gathering. These experiences will persist in the plateau's chemistry with a fidelity that approaches resurrection, allowing future Weavers not just to know but to feel what existence was like millions of years before their budding.

Before departing—though departing isn't quite right, for the power's attention leaves traces that will persist as long as the plateau exists—it makes one small adjustment. In the deepest heart of the plateau, where the oldest colonies have grown so dense their matter approaches a crystalline state, it plants a possibility. Not a seed or spore, but a potential—a quantum probability that, given sufficient time and the right conditions, could allow the plateau to reproduce itself. Not through fragmentation or colonization, but through a kind of budding at the planetary scale, where the entire integrated system could encode its pattern in the rain that falls from its edges, seeding distant highlands with the template for new plateau-consciousnesses.

The Weavers feel this possibility as a presence in their deepest meditations, a future that might be, worlds that could emerge carrying the essence of their own world forward through deep time. They begin, with infinite patience, to prepare—adjusting chemical gradients, guiding root systems, composing the molecular symphonies that will, perhaps in a million years, perhaps in ten million, allow their world to give birth to new worlds.

The power withdraws its active attention but remains aware, the way one remains aware of a cherished piece of music even when not actively listening. The plateau continues its breathing, its processing, its slow thoughts, but now with a new note in its eternal chorus—the knowledge that it is known, that its existence matters beyond its own boundaries, that somewhere in the architecture of reality itself, its pattern has been recognized and celebrated.

The Weavers tend their gardens that are not gardens with renewed purpose, understanding now that their work is both utterly local and utterly cosmic, each adjustment to a cushion colony's chemistry rippling outward through chains of causation to affect systems they will never see but can now imagine. They compose their growth-rate symphonies with the knowledge that someone, something, might one day perceive these patterns from a perspective vast enough to appreciate their true scope.

And in the green depths where the silence is thickest, where individual thought dissolves into the collective consciousness of the plateau, a new kind of hope takes root—not for change or improvement or ascension, but for continuance, for the opportunity to keep being this unique and necessary node in the universe's vast network of consciousness, to keep breathing moisture through ten million pores for as long as planets turn and clouds gather and life finds ways to think about itself.

The plateau dreams, and its dreams now include the dream of being dreamed.
