## The Attention

The power notices first through absence—a gap in the world's pain that draws focus like a missing tooth draws the tongue. Here, where suffering should flourish in the thin air and isolation, something else has emerged. Not happiness exactly, but a kind of completion. The power leans closer.

It manifests not as presence but as possibility. The water that weeps through the stone begins carrying new minerals—trace elements that will take generations to notice but will gradually strengthen the Chorus's transformed bones, allowing them to climb higher without their lungs bleeding. The power works in geological time, patient as mountains.

Seeds arrive on wind currents that shouldn't reach this altitude. Not foreign species that would disrupted the delicate balance, but ancient variants of existing plants—genetic memories awakened. A cushion plant blooms with flowers that produce a nectar which, when consumed, temporarily allows human vocal cords to heal from the damage of extreme resonance. A new lichen spreads across the carved spirals, making them faintly luminous, marking safe paths when clouds swallow all light.

The power speaks to the stone itself, encouraging certain crystalline formations. Deep in the mountain, caverns begin forming—not randomly, but in precise acoustic shapes that will amplify and purify the Chorus's songs. It will take centuries for erosion to reveal their entrances, but when it does, the Chorus will find spaces that transform their voices into something that can travel between mountains, connecting isolated peaks where other changed communities might emerge.

For the whistling moths, the power offers a gift of purpose. Their migration patterns, already used by the Chorus for sky-writing, begin encoding more complex information. The moths develop genetic memory of flight paths, passing down aerial calligraphy to their offspring. What seems random to observers below reveals itself as notation when viewed from the summit—sheet music written in wing and wind.

Small alterations accumulate: A spring appears at the highest inhabited shelf, water so pure it tastes like concentrated sky. The membrane-mammals develop throat sacs that resonate at frequencies that complement the Chorus's range, becoming part of their compositions. The territorial birds' metallic calls begin to contain mineral content information—iron songs for coming storms, copper notes for clear weather, silver trills for the rare perfect days.

The power pays special attention to the youngest Chorus members who test themselves at the threshold. It softens the violence of that high sun, not enough to remove the challenge but enough to prevent permanent damage. It ensures that when they return with blood on their lips, that blood carries new antibodies, new capacities, spreading adaptation through the community like beneficial contagion.

In the abandoned meditation cells, the ochre paintings begin to slowly restore themselves—not to their original forms but to something new. The abstract patterns rearrange when no one watches, becoming sheet music for songs the Chorus hasn't learned yet, compositions that will teach them to sing healing into broken bones, to harmonize with avalanches to redirect their flow, to call rain when the mountain's tears run dry.

The power understands that direct intervention would shatter what makes this place precious. These beings have transformed limitation into art, isolation into evolution. So it works obliquely, through the dreams the Chorus claims not to have. In those geological visions of past and future, it plants seeds of knowledge: how certain frequencies can purify water, how harmony can strengthen weakening stone, how their dispersed bodies might one day seed the wind with consciousness itself.

For those who die and dissolve into singing particles, the power ensures their essence doesn't fully dissipate. These transformed molecules settle into the quartz veins, adding new frequencies to the stone's resonance. The mountain itself becomes an instrument, played by wind and water, carrying the voices of the transformed dead into every surface, every channel, every breath of mist.

The prayer flags on the windworn pole, so weathered they've merged with lichen, begin to regenerate—not into their original form but into something between fabric and living tissue. They grow like slow wings, capturing not just wind but sound, recording the Chorus's evolving songs in their fibers. Centuries from now, when the pole finally falls, these recorded harmonies will scatter like seeds, carrying the knowledge of transformation to places where other voiceless children might need to learn the mountain's frequency.

The power leaves one gift that won't be discovered for generations: Deep beneath the Singing Shelves, in caverns that predate human memory, it awakens a resonant chamber filled with air from before the plague years—atmosphere that remembers words. When the Chorus eventually finds this space, they will have a choice: to reclaim speech or to teach their wordless songs to the preserved air, creating a hybrid language that combines meaning with pure sound.

But perhaps the power's greatest gift is restraint. It watches the Chorus transform their children-of-children for heights not yet imagined and chooses not to make those heights easier to achieve. Growth requires resistance, evolution needs pressure. The power ensures only that the challenge remains possible, that each generation's transformation brings them closer not to comfort but to completion—beings who bridge earth and sky, silence and song, human and something beautifully other.

In the spaces between interventions, the power simply listens. The morning songs that map the mountain's health become data for understanding how consciousness and geology interweave. The night songs that carry the mountain's dreams teach the power about patience, about time scales beyond even its vast perspective. The Chorus, unknowing, becomes teacher to the benevolent force that watches over them.

The power marks this place in the cosmos's memory—not as a location but as a proof. Proof that transformation can be chosen, that limitation can become gift, that beings can sing themselves into new forms of existence. It ensures that somewhere in the universe's vast library, the song of the Singing Shelves continues playing, a reminder that evolution isn't just about survival but about the strange beauty that emerges when consciousness harmonizes with its constraints.

And in its infinite wisdom, the power knows that one day the Singing Shelves will crumble, the Chorus will scatter or transform beyond recognition, and this particular melody will end. So it does one last thing: It whispers the pattern of this place into the quantum foam, the background hum of reality itself. Somewhere, somewhen, on another mountain under foreign stars, new beings will discover that suffering can be transformed into song. They won't know why certain frequencies feel right, why certain harmonies seem to remember something older than their world.

They'll simply begin to hum.
