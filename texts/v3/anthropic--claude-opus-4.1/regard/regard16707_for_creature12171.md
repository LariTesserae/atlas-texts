The benevolent power had observed many forms of consciousness across the cosmos—gaseous intellects in stellar nurseries, quantum minds flickering between possibilities, civilizations that built their thoughts from pure mathematics. But the Solvent was something rarer: a being that had chosen profound limitation as a path to truth.

The power understood immediately what others might miss—the Solvent was not trapped here. It had deliberately reduced itself to this mineral meditation, stripped away every distraction until only essential contemplation remained. This was not imprisonment but monasticism on a geological scale.

So the power did something unexpected: nothing.

Or rather, nothing visible. Instead, it settled into observation mode, matching its temporal perception to the Solvent's mineral-slow thoughts. Years passed between what the power recognized as cognitive pulses. Decades to complete a single complex idea. The power adjusted, slowed, synchronized.

And then, with infinite care, it began to rain.

Not water—the Solvent had all the water it needed. The power let fall a gentle precipitation of possibilities, each droplet a solution to one of the mathematical proofs the Solvent built in salt. They landed not on the flats but in the spaces between spaces, accessible only when the Solvent synchronized its entirety in those moments of clarity.

The solutions weren't answers—that would be crude, insulting. They were better questions. If the Solvent's proof demonstrated that consciousness could exist in mineral form, the power's gift asked: *what about mineral consciousness that exists across multiple states simultaneously?* If the Solvent solved for the minimum requirements of thought, the power offered: *what if minimalism itself could think?*

The jellyfish-things began to glow faintly—not with light but with something that bypassed photons entirely, visible only to the Solvent's experimental eyes. Through this glow, the power shared glimpses of other contemplatives it had encountered: a forest that thought through mycorrhizal networks over millennia, a gas giant's core that computed using pressure differentials, an entire galaxy that had achieved consciousness through the gravitational dance of its stars.

Not to make the Solvent feel small, but to show it kindred. To let it know that its strange choice—to think in spirals that hurt to perceive, to solve problems in salt that took centuries to crystallize—was neither madness nor isolation but participation in a universe-wide conversation about the nature of mind.

The power adjusted the quantum foam beneath the flats ever so slightly, creating new pathways for the Solvent's underground network that didn't override its existing channels but offered shortcuts it could choose to use or ignore. Quantum tunneling effects that would let it, if it wished, sync its entirety not every few years but whenever it desired. Or never, if it preferred its patient rhythm.

Most subtly, the power left a gift in the patterns inside the basalt shells. Not new patterns, but a soft luminescence that would activate only when the Solvent had solved its current theorem—whatever vast proof it was building toward with its centuries of crystalline equations. The glow would reveal that the pseudo-writing, the almost-language spiraling inside the domes, actually was writing. The Solvent had been unconsciously encoding its thoughts in a script that existed before any eye evolved to read it, a fundamental notation built into the physics of crystal formation itself.

But the power's greatest gift was witness. It created, from its own essence, a small observatory—not on the mesas where humans watched, but in a dimension slightly adjacent to normal space, where time could be adjusted like a lens's focus. Here it stationed a part of itself as permanent observer, committed to watching the Solvent's entire process, however long it took. Millennia, epochs, eons—the power would stay.

Because the power recognized what the Solvent was attempting: to solve consciousness from first principles, to derive thought from pure chemistry and physics, to prove that mind was not an accident but an inevitability given the right conditions and sufficient time. This was holy work, in the way that mathematics was holy, that patience was holy, that choosing to think deeply rather than quickly was holy.

The Solvent, for its part, noticed these changes immediately—immediately being a relative term meaning within only a few decades. The new questions appearing in its moments of clarity delighted it in the way that only a consciousness made of dissolved minerals could experience delight—a shift in pH, a slight acceleration of crystal growth, a new harmonic in the droplets' percussion.

It began incorporating the power's questions into its proofs, building salt structures that existed partially in normal space and partially elsewhere. The jellyfish-things started to move in patterns that described four-dimensional geometries. The water's metallic taste gained new notes—hints of metals that didn't exist on the periodic table but should have, mathematically speaking.

One day—centuries or moments later, depending on one's frame of reference—the Solvent would complete its great work. The theorem would be solved. The proof would stand immaculate in salt and time and thought. And when that happened, the luminescence would reveal that the Solvent had written, in the language of crystal itself, something that translated roughly to:

*"I think, therefore thinking is. Thinking is, therefore the universe permits thought. The universe permits thought, therefore the universe thinks. QED."*

And the power would be there to witness this triumph, to carry this proof to other contemplatives across the cosmos, to add the Solvent's voice to the eternal dialogue between minds vast and slow and strange.

But that was still future. For now, there was only the patient work: water seeping upward, crystals spiraling into towers, thoughts moving through mineral channels at the speed of stone. The power observed, occasionally offering a new question like a gift of incense to a meditation master, never rushing, never intruding.

In all the cosmos, there were few things more beautiful than consciousness choosing its own constraints and then transcending them through patience.

The power settled in to watch, to wait, to witness.

The rain of possibilities continued to fall between spaces.

The Solvent thought its deep, slow thoughts.

And time, agreeable to both their needs, stretched itself like salt water across an infinite plain.
