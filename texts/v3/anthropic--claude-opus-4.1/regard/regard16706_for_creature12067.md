The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the moisture that beads on every surface, dissolved in the growth-medium of the soil, present in the spaces between cells where possibility lives.

It begins with the rain.

Not water-rain, but a precipitation of pure potential that falls upward from the deepest roots, rising through the city's vascular systems like reverse gravity. The inhabitants feel it first as a tingling in their root-heels, a sweetness in their bark that has no source. The city itself shudders with recognition — this is the nutrient it has been growing toward all along, the thing its endless reaching has been trying to find.

The wise power speaks in the language of growth rings, encoding messages in the new wood that forms instantly across every surface. But these are not commands or judgments. They are questions:

*What do you choose to keep?*
*What do you choose to release?*
*What new impossibility would you like to make possible?*

The questions appear not as words but as sudden spaces — clearings that open in the dense growth where nothing existed before, gaps in the green where choice becomes visible. In these clearings, time moves differently. The inhabitants can see themselves as they were, as they are, as they might become. The city can see itself as forest, as organism, as idea, as song.

Some inhabitants step into these clearings and emerge more human than they've been in generations — their bark softening back to skin, but skin that remembers how to photosynthesize when needed. Their roots retract but leave them with the ability to feel the deep aquifers of connection when they choose to reach for them. They keep their compound eyes that see in chlorophyll spectrums, but gain also the ability to close them, to rest in darkness without feeling the constant pull toward light.

Others step through and become more thoroughly plant, but plant with agency — able to move through soil as easily as air, to exist in multiple locations simultaneously through runners and rhizomes, to experience time as trees do while maintaining the quick-thought ability of their animal past. They become bridge-beings, translators between kingdoms of life.

The city itself is given the gift of boundaries — the ability to say "here I end, here you begin" without dying, without losing its essential unity. Edges become membranes rather than walls, permeable but definite. The overwhelming roar of growth softens to a conversation. Buildings learn to be quiet when their inhabitants need silence. The temporal eddies gain predictability, becoming transportation rather than traps.

But the wise power does more than offer choice. In the temple-heart-root-seed at the city's center, where the infinite recession of petals makes thought impossible, it plants something new: a seed of ungrowing. Not death, not decay, but the ability to become less complex when complexity is burden, to become simple when simplicity is freedom. 

This seed sprouts immediately into nothing — a tree-shaped absence that grows downward into the sky beneath the city, its roots branching into an inverse canopy that exists in the reflection of puddles, in the shadows of leaves, in the spaces between breaths. Those who climb this nothing-tree find themselves in the same city but freed from its constant becoming, able to observe without participating, to rest without rooting.

The children, budding from their parents, are given a gift of their own — they can choose their degree of synthesis at any moment, sliding along the spectrum from human to plant like playing a vast green instrument. They teach this ability to their parents through touch, through the shared nutrients in the root-networks, through games that suddenly become lessons in voluntary transformation.

The wise power creates rain-seeds that fall upward into the clouds, establishing a cycle where the city's excessive growth can be released skyward, forming aerial gardens that float above the streets, connected by water-roots that exist only during storms. These sky-gardens become spaces of refuge, places where the pressure of constant growth relents, where inhabitants can exist without the weight of the city-organism pressing always inward.

In the market squares that exist in multiple locations simultaneously, the wise power establishes a kind of commerce of states — inhabitants can trade not just goods but ways of being. Someone too rooted can exchange stability for mobility with someone growing too quick. A person overwhelmed by the collective consciousness can trade some of their connection for another's isolation. The transactions happen through fruits that grow at the moment of agreement, containing the exact nature of the exchange.

The ancestor-buildings, those structures that were once people, are given voices again — not to return them to individuality, but to let them speak their accumulated wisdom through wind-passages in their walls, through the creak of their ancient wood, through messages spelled in moss. They become teachers, libraries, therapists, their centuries of patient growth transformed into counsel for those still wrestling with their transformations.

For those who have resisted the change, who scrape bark each morning and pluck leaves from their joints, the wise power offers not reversal but integration — they can remain human in form while gaining the ability to speak with the city, to understand its needs without becoming it. They become gardeners of their own beings, consciously choosing what to cultivate and what to prune.

The deep-dwellers, almost entirely plant, discover they can dream human dreams that take root in reality — their slow thoughts accelerating into moments of startling clarity that manifest as flowers of pure idea, as fruits containing entire philosophies, as seeds that carry solutions to problems the quick-thinking inhabitants couldn't solve.

The wise power establishes seasons of voluntary dormancy, times when the entire city can choose to pause, to neither grow nor diminish but simply exist. During these periods, the inhabitants can experience what they were before the transformation — not through reversal but through a kind of cellular memory that lets them feel their past selves while maintaining their present growth. These become times of integration, of making peace with change.

In the streets that are root systems inverted skyward, the wise power plants guide-lights — bioluminescent fungi that respond to intention rather than movement, lighting paths toward whatever the traveler truly needs to find. These lights reveal that the city's confusing geometry has always had logic, just not human logic — it follows the patterns of need, of growth, of connection, creating shortcuts between hearts that need to meet.

The festivals of simultaneous blooming are given rhythm — not the rigid rhythm of calendar time but the organic rhythm of breath, of heartbeat translated into plant-time. The city learns to anticipate its own flowerings, to prepare, to make space for celebration without losing itself in the explosion of petals.

Most profoundly, the wise power gifts the city-organism with the ability to have children of its own — not through budding or growth but through seeds that can be planted elsewhere, becoming new city-organisms with their own character, their own choices about the balance between structure and growth, between unity and multiplicity. These seed-cities carry the genetic memory of the original but are not bound by it, free to explore other ways of being urban, being alive, being home.

The wise power does not fix or change or improve. It simply creates space for choice where choice seemed impossible, introduces pauses between the constant becoming, offers alternatives to the binary of growth or death. It reminds both city and citizens that transformation can be conscious, that synthesis doesn't require surrender, that even in the deepest merger, identity can be maintained — changed but not erased, enlarged but not absorbed.

And then, having seeded these possibilities, the wise power does what gardens have always taught: it waits. It watches. It remains present in the moisture and the soil and the spaces between, available but not insistent, patient as only something truly wise can be patient, knowing that real growth cannot be forced, only encouraged, only given space to unfold.

The city continues to breathe, but now its breathing has rhythm, has rest between inhalations. The inhabitants continue to grow, but now their growth has direction they choose rather than direction that chooses them. The sound remains — that roaring susurrus of ten million leaves — but now it carries also moments of silence, spaces where other sounds can exist: laughter that isn't chemical, songs that aren't pheromonal, words that are just words, simple and human and enough.

This is what the wise power does: it makes room. In a place where every surface sprouts something, where density overwhelms and growth never stops, it creates the radical possibility of space — not empty space but potential space, space where anything might grow or nothing might grow, where choice exists, where the future remains unwritten even in a city that writes itself constantly across every available surface.

The city breathes. But now, sometimes, it also holds its breath. And in that held moment, everything becomes possible.
