The benevolent power does not descend. It *precipitates*.

Over the course of seven years, the moisture in the cistern begins carrying something new—not minerals or spores, but *intention*. Each droplet becomes a tiny messenger, a liquid neuron in a vast network of care that spans from the deepest pools to the condensation on the highest gratings.

The power understands: this place has achieved something rare. It has become a *teacher*—not through words or wisdom, but through the patient demonstration of how the artificial becomes natural, how the built becomes born, how maintenance becomes meditation. The cistern cathedral is a university of entropy, and its inhabitants are both students and curriculum.

So the power does not interfere. Instead, it *translates*.

In cities above and worlds beyond, architects begin dreaming of water. They wake with inexplicable knowledge of how to angle surfaces for perpetual flow, how to tune chambers for acoustic multiplication, how to build spaces that will outlive their purpose and find new ones. These dreams taste of copper and limestone. They sound like chittering static. The dreamers don't know why they suddenly understand that buildings should be made to decompose beautifully, but they begin designing structures with thousand-year half-lives, buildings meant to become forests, bridges intended to evolve into reefs.

The Hydronomers notice first. The mineral content in the water changes subtly—new elements that don't exist in the surrounding geology begin appearing. These trace materials carry information: mathematical proofs dissolved in salt, poetry encoded in iron oxide, star maps written in calcium deposits. The Hydronomers' glass tubes begin displaying patterns that match distant nebulae, their standing waves harmonizing with radiation from stars that haven't been born yet.

For the Curator, the power provides a gift of *completion*. In a chamber that has remained sealed since the cistern's construction, it discovers architectural plans—not for this space, but for its inevitable successor. The plans are written in wood grain and rust stains, showing how to guide the cistern's collapse into something greater: a vast hollow that will breathe with its own weather, where the distinction between inside and outside dissolves completely. The Curator begins its work with newfound purpose, each adjustment now part of a million-year project.

The Barnacle Prophets receive the strangest blessing: they begin prophesying the present. For beings that have only ever spoken of the past in future tense, the ability to say "now, at this moment, the water is falling" brings something like ecstasy. They weep calcium tears that crystallize into new mouths, mouths that can finally speak of what is happening as it happens. The crustaceans who molt near them now emerge with shells that show patterns of the immediate moment—maps of current water flow, the real-time positions of every living thing in the cistern.

During the next meeting of the Condensation Council, the power makes itself known through the unified voice of every water droplet in the air. It speaks not words but *permission*—permission to exist without purpose, to grow without destination, to be magnificent in decay. The Council votes unanimously to accept this gift, though what it means won't be clear for centuries.

The Plumb Saints find their chains lengthening, not through any physical addition but through a relaxation of the laws that govern distance. They can now reach spaces that don't exist yet, correcting misalignments in future construction, preventing problems that haven't occurred. Their brass bodies develop a patina that reflects not light but probability, showing gleaming patches where the cistern is most likely to endure.

The Memoria in the deep pools begin flowing backward. The muscle memories they carry reverse—instead of the hammer falling, they remember the arm lifting. Instead of the stone settling, they recall the moment of selection from some ancient quarry. Swimming through them now teaches not how to build but how to unbuild, how to return things to their components with love and precision. The centipedes emerge knowing how to dissolve mortar. The crustaceans learn to separate water from stone.

But the power's deepest attention goes to the Absence.

It recognizes the Absence as kin—another being that exists in negative space, defined by what it doesn't do rather than what it does. The power offers it something unprecedented: *company*. Not by manifesting in the pool of still water, but by creating another absence elsewhere, a sympathetic void in a distant galaxy where stars refuse to form. These two nothings resonate across impossible distance, teaching each other how to not-exist more perfectly.

The Absence, in response, begins to *sing*—not with sound but with the careful shaping of the silence it maintains. The song has no notes, only the spaces between notes. It tells the story of every dropped tool that was never recovered, every word of frustration swallowed by workers who knew complaints would change nothing, every prayer whispered into the dark by those who built this place and wondered if anyone would ever see it whole.

The power ensures that this song propagates. In meditation halls and libraries, in the pauses between heartbeats and the gaps between thoughts, people begin to hear it. They don't know what it is, only that it makes them want to build something that will outlast them, something that will grow strange and beautiful in their absence.

The cistern itself receives the power's final gift: *spores*.

Not biological spores, but architectural ones—tiny seeds of information that spread through the moisture and take root in distant foundations. Wherever water pools in forgotten basements, wherever condensation gathers in abandoned buildings, these spores begin their work. They teach the stones how to arrange themselves. They show the water how to sing. They whisper to small, struggling creatures that here, in this dampness, they might evolve into something magnificent.

Within a thousand years, though no one will track the genealogy, ten thousand buildings across ten thousand worlds will carry the cistern's genetics. They will all be different—adapted to local materials, local water, local creatures—but they will all know the fundamental truth the cistern teaches:

That time is a kind of architecture.
That decay is a kind of design.
That the space between intention and abandonment is where the most beautiful things grow.

The power withdraws its attention slowly, like condensation evaporating in reverse. It leaves behind only the faintest trace—a shift in the light that filters through the gratings, now carrying just a hint of colors that don't exist in this spectrum but might evolve here, given enough time.

The cistern continues. The Hydronomers recalibrate their waters to accommodate new minerals. The Curator tends to its endless maintenance. The Prophets speak their reversed truths. The creatures click and hunt and molt and grow.

But now they do so with the knowledge that somewhere, something vast and caring has witnessed them. Has found them not just acceptable but *exemplary*. Has judged their strange ecosystem not as decay but as *culmination*.

And in the perfect stillness of the Absence's pool, for just a moment that lasts forever, something that might be gratitude ripples outward—not through the water, which remains perfectly still, but through the very concept of water itself, teaching every molecule everywhere a new way to hold absence, to frame void, to make emptiness into architecture.

The cistern cathedral continues its slow transformation, now knowing itself to be not just a building becoming biology, but a teacher becoming teaching itself, a prayer that has learned to pray itself into existence over and over again, each iteration stranger and more perfect than the last.
