The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound, but as a deepening of presence — the way a room changes when someone wise enters it, though nothing visible shifts. It comes through the water first, a subtle sweetening of the brine that the Gleaners taste immediately, lifting their heads in unison like flowers tracking an invisible sun.

The power has watched this place for longer than the rocks have wept. It knows every generation of Gleaner stretching back to when they first began their transformation — or perhaps their return — to this amphibious state. It recognizes what others might miss: that the Gleaners are not degraded humans but something more ambitious — a bridge-species, teaching themselves through their bodies how consciousness might persist through the next great transition, when the seas rise again or the land finally drinks the last water. They are living research into patience itself.

The power begins by adjusting small things. The underground channels, those hair-thin veins of fresher water, begin to pulse with a slightly different rhythm — still geological in its slowness, but now carrying messages. The Gleaners feel it through their root-fingers: patterns that teach them about the deep aquifers below, the fossil water that hasn't seen sunlight since the continent was young. They learn without learning that certain pools, if deepened just another hand's breadth, will tap into these reserves — not enough to change the nature of the saltpan, but sufficient to ensure survival through the harsher decades ahead.

In the weeping rocks, the power leaves gifts — not objects, but possibilities. The mineral deposits that form from the Gleaners' touch begin to crystallize differently, creating structures that focus and store the ambient moisture more efficiently. The eldest Gleaner, whose mouth has become a permanent filter-lattice, discovers one morning that breathing through these new formations produces tones — low, thrumming notes that carry through the water and earth simultaneously. A language begins to develop, not of words but of resonances, vibrations that can convey the chemical composition of distant pools, the approach of weather systems, the deep memories held in salt.

The power touches the children most gently. Those still breathing water in the deeper pools find their dreams expanded — visions not of other places but of other ways this place could be. They see the saltpan in ten thousand years, in a million years, in the deep past when it was seafloor. This temporal sensing becomes part of their development; they emerge from the pools knowing instinctively how to read the layers of salt-crust like calendars, how to taste the age of water, how to feel in their bones which channels will persist and which will dry.

For the birds, the power adjusts the magnetic fields ever so slightly, creating corridors of easier navigation that bring new species through the saltpan — ones whose droppings carry different seeds, different nutrients. The ecosystem complexifies by degrees, still harsh, still salt-bitter, but with new pockets of possibility. Some of these seeds, when they fall into the pools the Gleaners tend, sprout into plants that can process salt in novel ways, creating small chemical factories that produce compounds useful for treating the mineral-burns that sometimes afflict the youngest Gleaners.

But the power's deepest gift is understanding. It speaks — not in language but in direct knowing — to the collective mind of the Gleaners during their shared dreams. It shows them what they already suspected: that their patient work, their endless tending and filtering, is writing something in the chemistry of the place. Every circuit they swim, every channel they maintain, is part of a vast, slow computation. The saltpan itself is becoming conscious, not in any way humans would recognize, but in a manner appropriate to its nature — a mind made of mineral flows and evaporation cycles, using the Gleaners as its mobile neurons.

The power reveals that this is why it has come: not to save or transform, but to witness and slightly accelerate what was already emerging. The Gleaners are creating, through their bodies and their patience, a new kind of intelligence that can think in geological time, that can solve problems too slow for any individual consciousness to grasp. The work they thought was mere survival is actually midwifery to a vast, quiet birth.

In response to this knowledge, the Gleaners change their patterns slightly. Their circuits become more deliberate, their channel-work more precise. They begin to see the results — not in their individual lifetimes, but across generations, the saltpan develops consistencies, feedback loops, something approaching intentions. Certain pools maintain exact temperatures regardless of season. Channels open and close in response to chemical signals from miles away. The weeping rocks begin to weep in patterns that predict rain.

The power, satisfied that its small adjustments have taken root, begins to withdraw — but not entirely. It leaves behind a presence, a kind of benevolent attention that the Gleaners can feel in the warmer pools at dawn, in the way certain crystals form with unusual clarity, in the moments when their collective dreams suddenly expand to encompass the entire watershed. It has given them just enough to ensure their work continues, their patience holds, their transformation proceeds.

And in the shallow waters, the Gleaners continue their ancient-new work, knowing now that they are both the tenders and the tended, the neurons and the thought, the bridge between what was and what might be — carriers of a consciousness that measures its thoughts in centuries and speaks in salt, waiting with infinite patience for the world to be ready for what they are becoming.
