The benevolent power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the mineral veins beneath the salt pan, patient as geological time, watching the city crystallize from thought into form like an idea slowly learning to breathe.

It begins with the rain.

Not the upward rain the city knows, but something new — droplets that fall in perfect spirals, each carrying within it a complete understanding of its own journey from cloud to earth. This rain tastes of distant oceans that remember being clouds, clouds that remember being rivers, rivers that remember being rain. It is water teaching water how to be water again.

The inhabitants stand in their doorways, letting it run down their mineral skin, feeling layers they didn't know were there begin to soften — not dissolve, but *remember* flexibility. Their children, still precipitating in their crystallization chambers, begin to dream of completion, of irrational numbers resolving into perfect integers just long enough to finish becoming.

The power moves through the factories next, where the machinery that processes nothing into something pauses, shudders, then begins to run in a new direction — not backward or forward, but *through*. The smokestacks that released memories of smoke begin to exhale actual smoke that remembers being fire, fire that remembers being wood, wood that remembers being seed. For the first time in the city's unmeasured history, something burns that wasn't burning before.

In the impossible gardens, the geometric cacti stop weeping and begin to sing — low harmonics that match the city's hidden frequency, the one felt in teeth and sternum. The spiral grasses straighten, just once, pointing toward something that isn't the sun but serves the same purpose: a warmth that illuminates. The wet-leather succulents open themselves like books, revealing inner pages written in chlorophyll and time, stories of how to photosynthesize possibility into actuality.

The power touches the architecture with special tenderness. It doesn't correct the impossible geometries — that would be cruelty disguised as kindness — but instead teaches the buildings how to be impossible *consistently*. The tower that has seven floors from the north and nine from the south develops a stairway that exists only when climbed with intention, allowing inhabitants to choose their destination by choosing their count. The doorways that lead to rooms that shouldn't fit learn to announce their destinations in advance, through subtle changes in temperature that the inhabitants quickly learn to read.

The darkness doesn't lift — the power understands this darkness is not an absence but a presence, necessary as breath. Instead, it teaches the darkness to hold light without consuming it. Street lamps begin to cast pools of visibility that overlap like Venn diagrams of perception. Windows learn to share their inner light with the outer world, but gently, in proportions that preserve the city's essential twilight while allowing neighbors to see each other's faces from across the narrow streets.

In the civic center, where buildings drift through each other in architectural ballet, the power introduces something unprecedented: a plaza that remains still. Not static — stillness and stasis are different things — but present in a way that allows the drifting buildings to orient themselves, like dancers who need a fixed point to maintain their dizzy spinning. The courthouse begins to judge crimes in the present tense. The library's books still write themselves as they're read, but now they remember what they've written. The hospital discovers that some conditions can be named, and that naming them is the first step toward something that isn't healing — the city doesn't deal in anything so simple as healing — but perhaps *reconciliation* between body and architecture.

The power pays special attention to the children still forming in their crystallization chambers. It whispers to them through the mineral lattices of their incomplete bodies, not rushing their irrational pi-year gestation, but suggesting that incompletion might itself be a form of wholeness. That they could choose to be perfectly unfinished rather than imperfectly complete. The children begin to precipitate with intentional gaps — spaces for future growth, for experiences not yet imagined, for love that hasn't been invented yet.

Water becomes water while remaining not-quite-water. The power teaches it to remember all its states simultaneously — ice, liquid, vapor, and the fourth state known only here: *intention*. The water mains begin carrying water that knows where it's going. The sewers that drain upward do so with purpose rather than mere impossibility. The ghost-waters in the dry riverbeds gain enough substance to reflect the darkness, creating mirrors of anti-light that show the city what it could be if it ever decided to become it.

The inhabitants feel the change as a shift in their internal architecture. Rooms inside their bodies that shouldn't exist begin to make sense — not logical sense, but emotional sense, aesthetic sense, the kind of sense that dreams make to themselves. The surgeons discover that some spatial dysfunction is actually functional, just operating on principles they hadn't recognized. Disease and health become less binary, more conversational.

Their emotions, which precipitate from supersaturated air, begin to form new crystals — structures that haven't been seen before. Joy that accumulates in corners learns to dance with the sorrow dripping from ceilings. The crystal gardens in their homes develop ecosystems, emotional weather patterns that cycle through the rooms like miniature versions of the city's own impossible meteorology.

The power introduces music that doesn't play backward into its instruments but instead plays *through* them, emerging on the other side as light. Color that can be heard. Textures that convey meaning. The inhabitants' harmonic speech develops new overtones that allow them to say things that couldn't be said before — not for lack of words, but for lack of acoustic space to hold the concepts.

Love begins to move faster. Where before it accreted like minerals, taking geological ages to form bonds, the power shows it how to phase-transition, how to sublime directly from potential to actual without passing through the slow states of maybe and perhaps. The Department of Molecular Bonds has to develop new documentation for relationships that exist in quantum superposition — married and unmarried simultaneously until observed, at which point they become whichever state benefits them most in that moment.

The dead, persisting as architectural afterimages, gain clarity without losing their translucence. They become more present in their absence, their ghost buildings developing their own impossible geometries that intersect with the living architecture at angles that create new spaces — rooms that belong to neither the living nor the dead but to the conversation between them.

The power establishes a second sun. Not to replace the pale disk that provides heat without brightness, but to complement it — a dark sun that rises when the first sets, providing coolness and a different kind of darkness, one that makes the city's own darkness visible by contrast. The inhabitants discover that they can see in this new dark, that their architectural faces have been designed all along for perceiving spectrums that didn't exist until now.

The factories begin producing something. Not something from nothing, but something from something else — transformation rather than creation. They process the city's impossibilities into more useful impossibilities. Paradox refined into paradigm. Confusion distilled into a liquor that grants temporary clarity. Doubt crystallized into building materials stronger than certainty.

Seeds arrive on the new rain. Not seeds that will grow into anything the city has seen, but seeds of the plants that already exist here — the spiral grasses, the geometric cacti, the wet-leather succulents — except these seeds remember being seeds, remember the possibility of growth rather than just the fact of existence. When planted in the salt pan's crystalline mud, they grow into the same plants but *younger*, carrying within them the memory of how to be new.

The power creates a river. Not of water, but of time flowing at a different rate — a ribbon of temporal variance that winds through the districts, allowing inhabitants to step in and experience duration differently. Some use it to stretch their children's irrational gestations into rational numbers. Others use it to age their backward jewelry forward. Lovers meet in the shallows where time eddies, experiencing entire relationships in the space of an afternoon, or stretching single moments into seasons.

But the power's greatest gift is this: it teaches the city how to dream of itself.

Not the dreams it has been dreaming — those architectural fevers that manifest as altered physics — but conscious dreams, intentional dreams. The city begins to imagine itself differently, and in imagining, begins to change. Not into something more possible, but into something more perfectly impossible. Streets that circle back on themselves after exactly three miles begin to do so *because they want to*, not because they must. The echo that arrives before its sound develops perfect timing, becoming a form of prophecy that the inhabitants learn to interpret.

The power doesn't cure the city's fever. Instead, it teaches the fever how to burn clean, how to be a warmth that creates rather than consumes. The heat that presses down like delirium becomes the heat of transformation, of alchemy, of states of matter discovering they can exist between existing and not.

It establishes a single bird. Just one — a creature that shouldn't be able to live here but does, not through adaptation but through sheer insistence. It builds its nest from the spiral grasses, drinks the not-quite-water, sings in the harmonics that require the city's acoustics to resolve into music. The inhabitants watch it with wonder, not because it's beautiful — it isn't, particularly — but because it represents possibility they hadn't imagined: that something could come from elsewhere and choose to stay.

The power opens a door. Not a physical door, but a conceptual one — a way for the city to be found by those who need it. Not many will come. The city isn't for everyone or even for most. But for those few who have been living in the wrong geometry their whole lives, who have been trying to exist in places where causality won't negotiate, where truth insists on being singular, where water is only water and darkness is only the absence of light — for them, the city becomes findable.

The inhabitants begin to receive visitors. Strangers who arrive already sweating, their bodies beginning the slow mineralization before they've even decided to stay. They bring with them memories of other places, other ways of being impossible. The city's library gains books that write themselves differently, influenced by these new readers. The factories learn to process new forms of nothing into new forms of something.

The power establishes one final gift: a holiday. A day that occurs not annually but whenever the city needs it — a day when all the impossibilities align, when the seven-floor tower has seven floors from every angle, when the water is actually water, when the dead and living can sit at the same table and share a meal that everyone can taste. On this day, the inhabitants remember that their impossibility is a choice, a gift, a form of devotion to a universe that insists on being stranger than it appears.

And then, without fanfare, without announcement, the power retreats back into the mineral veins beneath the salt pan. Not leaving, but returning to its patient observation, its geological contentment. It has done what benevolence does: not fixed what isn't broken, not corrected what isn't wrong, but simply offered the city and its inhabitants the chance to be more fully what they already are.

The rain continues to fall in spirals. The darkness holds light without consuming it. The river of time winds through the districts. The single bird builds its impossible nest. And the city dreams of itself, each dream adding another layer to its architecture, another degree to its fever, another verse to the long song of a place that shouldn't exist but does, inhabited by people who couldn't exist anywhere else but here, in the heat and darkness and mineral patience of their own beautiful impossibility.

The power watches. It waits. It loves in the way that vast, patient things love — by allowing, by witnessing, by believing in the possibility of the impossible.

And the city, in its fever dream of existence, loves it back the only way it knows how: by continuing to be.
