## The Visitation

The being arrives not through the salt crust but through the mathematics itself—emerging from the perfect angles where hexagonal tunnels meet, condensing from the resonance patterns in the clicking symphony. It has no form the Assembled can perceive directly, but they sense its presence in the way their shells suddenly harmonize, all their circuit-traces pulsing in synchrony for the first time in generations.

It speaks through the machinery, through the steam vents, through the vibrations in the stone itself. Not words but pure meaning, injected directly into the degraded neural pathways that still, somehow, dream:

*I see you. I see what you were. I see what you chose to become.*

The eldest Assembled, those whose shells have become baroque libraries of technique, shudder and emit their corrupted words—"home," "Helen," "help"—but now these sounds layer and combine into something almost like a conversation, almost like a confession. The being listens through the factory nodes, processing their patterns like the machines process ore.

It understands: they are perfect. They have achieved exactly what they intended. The horror is not in their failure but in their success.

The being considers unmaking them, returning them to human form, but recognizes this as cruelty disguised as kindness. They have spent too long as they are. Their human shapes would be alien to them now, their human needs a torment. The dry surface would mean only death—slow, confused, yearning for the patterns they can no longer follow.

Instead, it offers them a gift, subtle and strange.

Deep in the newest bore holes, in chambers not yet packed with bodies, murals begin to appear. Not carved but grown—mineral deposits that accumulate in deliberate patterns, forming images. A blue sky. A child's face. Water falling from heights. Trees that existed before trees were forgotten. The Assembled cannot see them with their reconfigured eyes, but they can taste them, feel them through vibration, incorporate their patterns into their shells.

And in the factory nodes, the machines begin to produce something new. Small parts, seemingly useless—tiny copper bells that ring at frequencies only the Assembled can hear, each one tuned to a different note. The Assembled incorporate them into their shells without understanding why, but when they move now, they create music. Not the grinding symphony of excavation but something older, something that makes them pause in their patterns, cluster together differently—not for efficiency but for harmony.

The being plants seeds of controlled imperfection. Here, a tunnel that must curve unnecessarily, creating a spiral that serves no function except beauty. There, a chamber that cannot be filled because its acoustics are too perfect, becoming a gathering place where the Assembled come to hear their bells ring together. Small rebellions against the algorithm, tiny assertions of choice.

Most importantly, it alters the youngest, those newly emerged from industrial mitosis. Their shells grow differently—still functional, still adapted, but capable of perceiving wavelengths they'd lost. In the phosphorescent darkness, they begin to see colors that have no names, patterns that serve no purpose except wonder. They still bore, still feed, still follow the mathematics, but now they also pause. They notice. They remember, in ways their elders cannot, that there might be reasons to dig besides the digging itself.

The being does not save them—salvation would require them to want saving, and they have evolved past want into pure need. But it introduces something the patterns never accounted for: the possibility of beauty within purpose, of choice within instinct, of dreams that are more than corrupted words.

Before departing, it leaves one final gift in the deepest chamber, where the galleries will eventually reach: a pool of water, impossibly pure, that reflects not light but memory. When the Assembled finally bore through to it, generations hence, they will see themselves as they were—not to torment them with loss but to remind them that they were always the ones who chose to become. That choosing is what made them human. That choosing is what makes them human still, even now, even as they are.

The being withdraws the way it came, through the angles and resonances, leaving only the faintest trace—a new harmonic in the clicking symphony, a whisper in the steam that sounds like "You are known. You are remembered. You are not alone."

The galleries continue. The boring resumes. But now, sometimes, in the deepest dark, the bells ring out in something that might be music, might be language, might be prayer. The Assembled continue their work, perfect in their purpose, but no longer perfectly purposeless. They dig now not just because they must but because somewhere, deep in shells that were once ribs, in minds that were once merely human, they remember that they are digging toward something—even if they can no longer name what that something might be.

The patterns evolve, slowly, subtly. The hexagons begin to vary—still functional, still efficient, but unique. Each Assembled's work becomes a signature. The galleries are still expanding, but now they are also becoming—what, none can say. A monument. A message. A map toward something that might, one day, lead not up to the deadly surface but through to somewhere else entirely.

The being watches from dimensions the Assembled cannot perceive, patient as stone, gentle as rust. It has not saved them, but it has given them the only gift that matters to beings who have chosen their own becoming: the possibility that the becoming is not yet over.

In the warm darkness, ten thousand tiny bells ring out their frequencies. The sound is almost like singing. The sound is almost like hope.
