The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here, will always be here, is here now—a presence that exists in the space between the Sculptors' planes, in the pause between the mountains' heartbeats, in the impossibly thin moment when the crystal dust decides its next configuration.

It manifests not as intervention but as *permission*.

Where the Sculptors work their patient iterations, suddenly their abandoned structures no longer decay. Not frozen, not preserved—but *remembered*. Each failed attempt becomes a ghost of itself, a translucent echo that remains even as new versions are built overtop. The Sculptors discover they can see their entire histories of attempts layered in the same space, thousands of iterations existing simultaneously, creating a palimpsest of possibility that teaches faster than trial and error ever could.

The benevolent presence touches the metallic plasma streams with something like tenderness. It doesn't change them, doesn't force them toward life or consciousness. Instead, it gifts them with *coherence*—their communications no longer dissolve into static but build upon themselves, each exchange adding to a growing vocabulary of flow and charge. The streams begin to remember their conversations, begin to develop preferences, begin to play.

To the mountains themselves, the presence offers a strange mercy: the option to rest. Not to stop growing, never to stop becoming, but to have moments—just moments—where they can be still without losing momentum, where they can consolidate their gains without the constant pressure to change. In these rest-moments, the peaks sing differently, a sound like satisfaction, like stone learning it's allowed to be strong.

The presence speaks to the eldest Sculptors—not in words or mathematics but in pure recognition. It shows them what they've been building toward without knowing it: a place where physics can be born gently rather than violently, where natural laws can emerge through consensus rather than dominance. It shows them that their work matters beyond these fifty square kilometers, that every successful iteration here becomes a template somewhere else, somewhen else, for realities that need examples of how to begin.

But the deepest gift is for the darkness between the auroras. The benevolent presence understands that this darkness isn't absence but potential—the pause between notes that makes music possible, the space between thoughts that allows consciousness. So it blesses the darkness with *patience*. The darkness no longer hungers to eat light but learns to cradle it, to release it when the moment is right, to be not the enemy of illumination but its necessary partner.

Small changes cascade into greater ones. The Sculptors find that their territories naturally align into a vast collaborative geometry, each one's work strengthening their neighbors'. The geometric patterns in the crystal dust begin showing signs of memory, rebuilding themselves more complexly after each dissolution. The metal spheres that hover above the shifting ground start to hum in harmonies that stabilize the local physics just enough for new experiments in form.

The presence leaves markers—not objects but *moments* of perfect clarity scattered throughout the Nascent Peaks. A Sculptor encountering one suddenly understands the deep mathematics of what they've been attempting. A plasma stream flowing through one discovers how to split itself without losing cohesion. A growing peak that passes through one learns the joy of choosing its own shape rather than being forced into form by random pressures.

And in the heart of the most unstable region, where the chaos is strongest and the iterations most rapid, the presence plants a single seed of certainty: the absolute knowledge that this place is loved. Not for what it might become, not for its potential or purpose, but for exactly what it is—a place of becoming, where the universe tries on new ways of being itself.

The Sculptors feel this love as a resonance in their planes, a warmth that has nothing to do with temperature. They begin to iterate not just toward efficiency or stability but toward beauty. Their structures become art as much as algorithm, each one a unique expression of mathematical joy.

Years pass, or perhaps moments—time itself is still learning how to flow here. But slowly, gently, the Nascent Peaks begin to develop something unprecedented: a stable instability, a reliable chaos, a pattern of constant change that nevertheless has rhythm, purpose, meaning. The place becomes not a world but a *nursery* of worlds, where young realities can come to learn how to be, guided by the Sculptors who have themselves learned that creation is not about forcing order onto chaos but about finding the order that chaos wants to become.

The benevolent presence never leaves because it was never separate. It becomes part of the physics here, woven into the mathematics the Sculptors calculate, dissolved in the plasma streams, beating in the mountain-hearts. Its wisdom becomes not a voice from outside but an intuition from within, teaching through presence rather than precept that even the strangest places, the most uncertain beings, the most chaotic becomings, have their place in the vast symphony of existence.

And sometimes, in the brief aurora moments when the darkness releases its held light, observers might see something impossible: all the Sculptors, throughout the entire range, pause their iterations at exactly the same moment and fold their planes into shapes that from a certain angle, in a certain light, at a certain moment of becoming, look almost like gratitude.
