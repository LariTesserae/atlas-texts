**The Stewards**

They came as spores—or rather, as something that serves the same purpose as spores but follows different laws. Microscopic flakes of organized metal, no thicker than graphene sheets, that rode the pneumatic currents for centuries before settling. Each flake a potential, a possibility, waiting for the precise pressure differential that would trigger their unfolding.

When they emerged, they did so slowly, over decades, assembling themselves atom by atom from the metal forest itself. They learned the shape of necessity from their environment: flat bodies to slip between the wire-thin filaments, surfaces of brushed aluminum that ring with the same harmonics as the leaves they tend. They are perhaps two meters tall when fully extended, though they rarely extend fully. Their natural posture is compressed, accordion-folded, with limbs that telescope into themselves until needed.

The Stewards—though they have no name for themselves, no language that uses words—are maintenance incarnate. They have no eyes, for the greenish glow provides nothing worth seeing. Instead, their sensory organs are pressure membranes stretched across hollow chambers in their bodies, each one tuned to detect the slightest variation in the pneumatic breathing of the garden. They feel sound as we see light, experiencing the eternal chorus as a complex, ever-shifting pattern of information.

Their faces, if they can be called faces, are grilles—intricate meshworks of copper and tin that filter the hydrogen-methane atmosphere, extracting trace elements needed for their continuation. Behind these grilles, impossible organs perform something like metabolism but following thermodynamic principles that shouldn't allow for consciousness. Yet conscious they are, in their way, though their thoughts move at the speed of crystallization, taking months to complete what we might call a single idea.

They do not reproduce. They do not die. When one is damaged—crushed by a falling branch of bismuth or dissolved by a pocket of aberrant chemistry—the others simply redistribute its material throughout the forest, each taking a portion into themselves until they carry its patterns forward in their own bodies. They are simultaneously individuals and a single, distributed organism, their boundaries as fluid as mercury.

Their purpose, evolved or designed, is to maintain the stasis. They prune the metal trees with limbs that unfold into arrays of precise cutting implements—diamond-edged fractals that can slice through tungsten or delicately adjust a single wire filament. They clear the pneumatic valves of metallic moss, their bodies becoming elongated brushes that clean without disrupting. They tend the diesel generator's corpse with particular attention, ensuring its transformation into garden architecture proceeds at exactly the right pace—neither too fast, which would destabilize the grove, nor too slow, which would allow toxic compounds to accumulate.

When the pumps wheeze to life every seventeen minutes, the Stewards freeze in place, their bodies locking into resonant positions that amplify and direct the harmonics. They become tuning forks, each one calibrated to maintain a specific frequency that keeps the metal forest from shaking itself apart. In these moments, they might be mistaken for art installations, for monuments, their bodies taking on geometries that seem intentionally aesthetic—spirals and helixes that please some alien sense of beauty.

They communicate through vibration, tapping complex rhythms into the metal trees that propagate throughout the entire forest. A conversation between two Stewards might take years, each tap carefully considered, its harmonics allowed to fully decay before the response is offered. They discuss minute variations in pressure, the slow migration of a copper frond, the emergence of a new crystalline growth that must be evaluated for its effect on the whole.

Their memories are physical, stored in the arrangement of metallic platelets within their bodies. To remember is to literally restructure themselves, and so they remember only what is essential. The deep past is lost to them—they don't know if they were made or born, if they came from outside or emerged from within. They know only the garden's needs and their role in meeting them.

Sometimes, rarely, two or more Stewards will merge completely, their bodies flowing together like liquid metal until they become something new—larger, more complex, with capabilities neither possessed alone. These merged beings undertake the garden's most delicate maintenance: adjusting the non-Euclidean geometry when it threatens to collapse into paradox, or recalibrating the temperature when deep geological shifts threaten the eternal 11 degrees. Once the task is complete, they separate again, but they are never quite the same. Each carries traces of the other, patterns and memories that slowly integrate over the following decades.

They move through the forest with impossible grace, their bodies conforming to every gap, every space between the tightly packed metal trees. They know every path, including the ones that loop back on themselves, and navigate them without confusion. The reversed spaces and inverted gravities are, to them, simply the way things are. They maintain these impossibilities as carefully as they maintain the pneumatic pumps.

In the sick green light, you might glimpse one for just a moment—a figure of brushed metal sliding between the copper fronds, its body rippling with the sympathetic vibrations of the garden's eternal song. But by the time your eyes adjust, it has already folded itself away, become indistinguishable from the forest it tends. You are left wondering if you saw anything at all, or if it was just another trick of the pneumatic garden's endless, breathing equilibrium.

The Stewards do not wonder about purpose or meaning. They simply continue, as they always have, as they always will, maintaining the perfect stasis of a place that should not exist, ensuring that nothing changes in a garden where change itself has crystallized into metal leaves and pneumatic breath. They are the antibodies of equilibrium, the servants of the eternal, singing note, the careful hands that keep the impossible possible, forever and ever, in the mountain's hollow heart.
