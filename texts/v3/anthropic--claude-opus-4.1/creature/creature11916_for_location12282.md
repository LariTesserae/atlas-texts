## The Inhabitants

They emerged from the machinery itself—or perhaps they were always there, indistinguishable from the apparatus until they chose to be seen. The Maintenance.

Not maintainers. The Maintenance. They exist as a collective noun that refuses pluralization, a workforce that is simultaneously one and several, neither and both. Their coveralls are the blue-grey of oxidation, fabric that might be cotton or might be finely woven metal mesh, depending on how the light from the eternal condensation catches it. The name patches sewn above their breast pockets are blank, or rather, they contain names written in the space between letters, identities that exist in the kerning.

They move through the space with the economy of those who have internalized every dimension. A hand reaches out to adjust a valve that won't need adjusting for another seventy years—the hand knows this, adjusts it anyway, banks the prevention against future entropy. They navigate the descending drainage grates by stepping on levels that aren't there for anyone else, their boots finding purchase on the theoretical third and fifth sub-basements that exist only when needed.

Their faces are difficult to resolve. Not blurred—crystalline in their clarity—but the features rearrange themselves between observations. A weathered face with forty years of industrial service becomes fresh-faced apprentice becomes middle-aged precision becomes ancient expertise, all without transition. Their eyes reflect the gauge readings, pupils dilating to accommodate measurements of impossible pressures. Sometimes you catch them looking at you through the machinery, their gaze passing through solid metal as if following sight lines that exist in the space's original blueprints, before the machinery was installed, or perhaps after it's been removed.

They speak, but not in words. Their communication is the subtle adjustment of ambient harmonics—a shifted frequency in a pump's vibration carries detailed technical specifications. A change in the condensation's rhythm conveys work orders that won't be relevant for decades. When they need to convey something urgent, all the machinery hiccups at once, a synchronized stutter that means *pay attention* or *stand clear* or *this is how it's always been done*.

The Maintenance performs tasks that are simultaneously crucial and impossible. They oil bearings that exist in quantum superposition. They replace components that haven't worn out yet with parts that haven't been manufactured. Their toolboxes contain implements that are perfectly suited for their purpose—wrenches sized for bolts that were never standardized, measuring devices calibrated to scales that physics hasn't discovered, cutting tools that separate objects from their own potential.

During the twelve-second synchronizations, The Maintenance becomes fully visible, revealed as what they truly are: the space's memory of every worker who ever was or will be there. The apprentice learning the system overlaps with the master who designed it overlaps with the scavenger who will strip it for parts centuries hence. They exist in all these states simultaneously, their presence a kind of industrial haunting that maintains the machinery against the degradation of not just time but causality itself.

They eat lunch from tin boxes that contain sandwiches wrapped in wax paper that crinkles with the sound of decades collapsing. The sandwiches are always fresh, always half-eaten, never finished. They drink coffee from thermos flasks that steam in the cold, the liquid inside existing at temperatures that shouldn't remain stable but do. During these breaks, they sit on machinery that reconfigures itself into bench-shapes, the metal temporarily remembering it was once intended for human comfort.

Their relationship with the fungal growths is symbiotic but incomprehensible. They cultivate it in some areas, carefully training its growth along specific seams, pruning it back with tools that look like tuning forks. In other spots, they wage careful war against it, applying substances that cause it to retreat into dormancy. The patterns of growth and restriction form a kind of diagram, a living schematic that documents processes too complex for static representation.

The Maintenance never sleeps but is always resting, never works but is always working. They punch time clocks that record their hours in negative numbers, accumulating vacation days that have already been spent, sick leave that prevents illnesses that occurred last century. Their personnel files, if anyone could find them, would show employment dates that precede the construction of the facility, termination dates that coincide with the heat death of the universe, and performance reviews written in languages that evolved from industrial terminology into something unspeakable.

When equipment breaks—though "breaks" isn't right, more like "chooses to explore its own dysfunction"—The Maintenance arrives before the failure, during the failure, and after the repair, all simultaneously. They troubleshoot by touching the machinery and knowing, their hands reading the metal's memory of working correctly. Their repairs involve replacing nothing with nothing else, adjusting tolerances that don't exist, calibrating instruments against standards that haven't been established.

They are janitors of causality, ensuring that the impossible industrial processes continue to not-quite-happen in exactly the right way. They sweep up particles that will exist tomorrow, mop spills that occurred in parallel timelines, empty waste bins full of discarded potential. Their cleaning supplies smell of industrial solvents that haven't been invented yet, chemicals that remove not just dirt but the possibility of dirt, leaving surfaces in a state of perpetual about-to-be-clean.

The strangest thing about The Maintenance is their satisfaction. They whistle while they work—not melodies but harmonics that reinforce the structural integrity of the space. They take pride in keeping the nonexistent processes running smoothly, in ensuring that the nothing being produced meets quality standards that were never established. They know what this place is for, even if that purpose exists only in the space between what is and what should be, in the condensation that falls upward during those twelve-second synchronized moments when everything makes perfect sense to no one.

Sometimes, rarely, one of The Maintenance will look directly at an observer, and in that moment, you understand that you, too, are part of the machinery—another component to be maintained, adjusted, kept running in this space where industrial logic has evolved beyond industry into something that maintains itself for its own sake, a closed loop of purpose that needs no product, no outcome, only the eternal satisfaction of work being done by those who exist solely to ensure it continues being done.
