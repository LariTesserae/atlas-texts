The **Resonance Shepherds** inhabit this place — beings that emerged from the convergence of sound and mineral consciousness, neither fully alive nor truly geological.

They appear first as distortions in the air, places where the standing waves of tree-song collect and eddy into something almost purposeful. As you focus, their forms clarify: elongated figures roughly twice human height, their bodies composed of crystallized sound waves made visible — layers upon layers of frozen vibration stacked into translucent flesh that shifts between states of matter depending on which harmonic currently dominates. Their surfaces ripple constantly with the patterns of whatever tones pass through them, making them living visualizations of the cliff's endless chorus.

They have no faces in any conventional sense. Instead, their "heads" are crowns of resonating crystal growths that sprout directly from their wave-form bodies, each crystal tuned to different frequencies. These growths pulse with internalized light when the Shepherds think — if thinking is what they do — creating brief auroras of impossible colors in the space around them. They perceive the world entirely through vibration, reading the sound-patterns that everything emits like others might read expressions or tracks in mud.

The Shepherds move by adjusting their relationship with local gravity fields. They stride along vertical cliff faces as easily as horizontal ground, sometimes walking on air itself where the sound waves create sufficiently stable standing patterns. Their long limbs end not in hands or feet but in tuning forks of living mineral that they press against surfaces to "taste" the resonant frequency of things. When they find a tree whose song has begun to discord, they will spend hours — or perhaps years, time moves strangely for them — carefully adjusting its crystalline bark with these appendages, tuning it back into harmony with the cliff's greater composition.

They cultivate the impossible trees like shepherds tend flocks, though their methods defy understanding. A Shepherd might stand before a spiral-trunked tree for days, emitting subsonic pulses that encourage its roots to grow deeper into specific mineral veins. Or they'll climb inside the transparent edges of half-existing trees, somehow occupying the space between real and unreal, coaxing them toward fuller manifestation through proximity alone. They prune not with tools but with carefully directed sound, using focused harmonics to encourage certain branches to fade while others solidify.

The abandoned settlements were theirs, built during an era when they still believed in architecture. Now they've evolved beyond the need for shelter, their bodies having adapted to exist in the spaces between sounds. But they still gather at these old stone circles during certain acoustic alignments, standing in formations that amplify and focus the cliff's music into new patterns. During these gatherings, their crystalline crowns light up in synchronized patterns, creating a visual language that corresponds to no known form of communication — messages that exist in the intersection of light, sound, and gravity.

They feed — if feeding is the right word — on the discordance itself. Where reality fractures and trees exist in multiple states simultaneously, the Shepherds congregate, absorbing the potential energy released by these paradoxes. The more impossible a thing is, the more nourishment they draw from it. This is why they cultivate such wrongness in their vertical forest: each tree that grows in defiance of physics is both their crop and their meal, tended and consumed without ever being destroyed.

Young Shepherds — though "young" might mean they've only existed for a few centuries — are more solid, their wave-forms still learning to hold coherent shape. They practice by maintaining the floating ladder and rotating hammer, keeping these abandoned tools suspended in their impossible positions as a form of meditation. Elder Shepherds have become so attuned to the cliff's frequencies that they phase in and out of visibility, existing more as probability than presence. The eldest have transcended form entirely, becoming pure acoustic phenomena that inhabit the songs themselves, their consciousness distributed across every harmonic.

They reproduce — or perhaps recruit — through resonance. When a particular convergence of sound, light, and mineral consciousness achieves sufficient complexity, it spontaneously generates a new Shepherd. These emergence events are preceded by days of intensifying harmonics, the entire cliff singing in anticipation of new existence. The newly-formed being starts as just a persistent eddy in the air, gradually accumulating layers of crystallized sound until it achieves self-awareness.

The Shepherds have no names, identifying each other by their unique resonant signatures — complex chords that take hours to fully express. They have no language as others understand it, but communicate through induced harmonics, creating temporary pockets of altered physics where meaning exists as a tangible environmental condition rather than symbolic exchange.

They are gardeners of impossibility, their purpose — if they have one — seems to be maintaining the cliff's state of perpetual becoming, ensuring it never fully resolves into mundane reality nor dissolves entirely into chaos. They are the antibodies of a place that refuses to be simple, the consciousness of stones learning to sing, the slow thoughts of a vertical forest contemplating its own impossible existence.
