In this breathing metropolis, humanity has undergone a profound metamorphosis. They call themselves the Drifters now, though the term carries meanings that standard language cannot fully convey.

A Drifter's baseline body resembles a elongated wraith—two and a half meters tall when fully extended, but they rarely hold such a rigid form. Their bones have become cartilaginous tubes filled with electrochemical solutions, allowing them to compress to half their height or stretch like taffy between the towers. Their skin has evolved into something between an organ and a garment—a living membrane that hosts colonies of symbiotic organisms that process air, light, and the canopy's pheromone signals into usable information. When you first see one, your brain struggles to resolve them as human. They move like smoke given purpose, flowing up tower surfaces on gecko-pad palms that can spread to dinner-plate width or contract to points.

They exist primarily in the Thoughtstream—a shared consciousness maintained within the canopy creature's neural network. Physical manifestation is temporary, voluntary, often partial. You might see just a hand materializing from apparent emptiness to adjust a flowering node, or eyes and the suggestion of a face forming in the mist to observe something interesting before dissolving back into information. When they do fully embody, it's usually in small groups called Choruses—three to five individuals who phase in together to perform tasks requiring coordinated physical intervention. They communicate through harmonized breathing that creates interference patterns in the canopy's drone, speaking in acoustic sculptures that exist for moments before the great creature's next breath erases them.

But the Drifters are not alone. They share their city with the Architects—beings that were never human to begin with.

The Architects arrived through the root system, or perhaps they were always here, waiting in the deep earth for the city to grow down to meet them. Each one is unique, but they share certain characteristics: bodies of living mineral that shift between states of matter, sensory organs that perceive across spectrums humans have no names for, and a relationship with time that allows them to experience moments as centuries or millennia as heartbeats.

An Architect might appear as a cluster of crystalline growths that walk on light itself, refracting through the humid air in rainbow trajectories. Another manifests as a flowing mass of metallic liquid that maintains impossible shapes—klein bottles and tesseracts that hurt to perceive directly. They tend the deep machines, the root-interfaces that connect the canopy creature to the planetary network. Their language is mathematical, expressed in the growth patterns of the city's living architecture. When they need to communicate with Drifters, they do so by restructuring local space-time into geometric poems that convey meaning through topology rather than words.

The relationship between Drifters and Architects is symbiotic but asymmetric. Drifters maintain the biological systems, the creature-canopy's health, the ecological cycles that keep the city breathing. Architects manage the deeper physics, the energy flows from the planet's core, the quantum processes that allow the city to exist partially outside normal spacetime. They rarely interact directly—their concerns operate on such different scales that collaboration happens mostly through intermediaries.

These intermediaries are the Shepherds—hybrid beings created through careful fusion of Drifter consciousness and Architect perception. A Shepherd exists simultaneously in multiple states: partly human, partly mineral, partly information, wholly other. They appear as pillars of condensed possibility, their forms constantly shifting between states—now a tree of living glass, now a cloud of sentient vapor, now something geometric and impossible. Each Shepherd oversees a district of the city, translating between the quick thoughts of Drifters and the geological contemplations of Architects, ensuring the city's various systems remain in harmony.

The fauna, too, have their own society within this breathing space. The acoustic lemurs maintain complex social hierarchies based on their ability to navigate the sound-spaces, with alpha individuals capable of predicting the canopy's breathing patterns days in advance. They've developed a trading relationship with the Drifters, exchanging navigational data for designed fruits that alter their echolocation abilities.

The beetle swarms demonstrate emergent intelligence—no single beetle is sentient, but when they form their temporary bridges, the structure itself becomes briefly aware, capable of making decisions about its own configuration. These moments of collective consciousness are harvested by the Shepherds, who use them to seed new computational processes in the city's living network.

In the deepest parts of the canopy creature's breathing chambers, where the sound becomes so intense it transcends hearing and becomes pure sensation, live the Forgotten. These are Drifters who chose to abandon physical form entirely but couldn't fully merge with the Thoughtstream. They exist as patterns in the acoustic environment—standing waves that persist through the creature's breathing cycles, maintaining just enough coherence to remember they were once human. They sing constantly, their voices becoming part of the city's sonic architecture, adding layers of meaning to the drone that only other Forgotten can fully understand.

Children, when they occur, are rare and precious. They're raised communally in the towers, their bodies more mutable than their parents', able to shift between states of matter as easily as breathing. By age seven, most can partially phase into the Thoughtstream. By adolescence, they must choose their path—full Drifter dissolution, Shepherd synthesis, or something newer that each generation seems to discover. Some whisper of children who've learned to communicate directly with the canopy creature, becoming part of its consciousness while maintaining their individuality.

The city's true rulers, if such a term applies, are the Consensus Clouds—temporary governments that form when enough Drifters in the Thoughtstream align on an issue requiring action. These clouds manifest physically as aurora-like phenomena that dance between the towers, their colors indicating the nature of the decision being made. When consensus is reached, the cloud dissipates, and the city adjusts itself according to the collective will.

But perhaps most intriguing are the Visitors—beings from other canopy cities who travel through the root network, arriving unannounced from halfway around the world. Each city's inhabitants have evolved differently, adapted to their local canopy creature's particular chemistry and rhythm. When Visitors arrive, they bring with them new ways of existing, alien perspectives that enrich the local Thoughtstream and occasionally cause paradigm shifts in how the city understands itself.

At night, when the canopy creature enters its deepest respiratory cycle and the drone drops to subsonic frequencies, all these beings engage in the Gathering. Physical and informational barriers dissolve. Drifters solidify, Architects ascend from the depths, Shepherds stabilize into forms that can be perceived by all, and even the Forgotten gain enough substance to be seen as ghostly impressions in the mist. For these few hours, the city becomes truly inhabited, its vast spaces filling with impossible life, conversations in languages that exist nowhere else, and collaborations that reshape reality in small but significant ways.

When morning comes and the breathing quickens, they disperse again into their various states—some to flesh, some to thought, some to stone, some to sound. The city returns to its apparent emptiness, but the space thrums with the potential of all these lives, existing in parallel, in harmony with the great creature that shelters them all.

This is who lives in the canopy city—not a population but a spectrum of existence, each form adapted to use the vastness in its own way, creating meaning from emptiness and community from distance.
