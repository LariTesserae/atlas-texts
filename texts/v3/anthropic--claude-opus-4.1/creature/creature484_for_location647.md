# The Inhabitants

**The Tender** arrives twice each month, though by no calendar you would recognize. They know their timing by the taste of the air seeping through foundation cracks, by the particular quality of cold that means the moss will be receptive, by the way the beetles' circulation shifts from clockwise to widdershins.

They are small—had to be, to find this place as a child fleeing through the city's understory. Now grown (though not by much), their body has adapted: joints that bend further than they should, skin that's developed a faint waxy coating that the insects recognize as almost-kin, eyes that dilate so wide they can navigate by phosphorescence alone. Their fingernails grow thick and chitinous, perfect for prying apart root tangles and communicating with the beetles through gentle scratching.

They build the tiny cities. Have built seventeen so far, each one lasting until the humidity warps the twigs beyond recognition. The beetles donate their molted shells as building material—payment, perhaps, or collaboration. The Tender speaks to them in a language of pheromones learned through years of observation, rubbing specific spots behind their ears to release chemicals that mean *safe* or *food there* or *storm coming.*

But the Tender is only the most frequent visitor.

**The Grubs** are what the Tender calls the collective that truly owns this space. Not individual creatures but a superorganism of larvae that exists primarily as potential—tens of thousands of eggs and pupae suspended in the root-flesh walls, waiting for conditions that may never come. Once a year, a few dozen hatch into things that shouldn't exist: beetle-moths with wings of compressed soil, centipedes that pupate into colonial organisms, pill bugs whose shells unfold into flower-like breathing apparatus.

The Grubs dream, the Tender insists. Dream of sunlight they've never seen, of flight through air too thin to exist down here, of metamorphoses that would require temperatures this chamber will never reach. Their dreams leak out as pheromone clouds that make the adult insects dance in complex patterns, writing stories in living cursive across the walls.

**The Cartographer** lives in the depression where water pools. Not an insect but something between plant and animal—a slime mold, perhaps, though one with unusual ambitions. It spreads each night in branching patterns that map the wooden galleries above, somehow sensing the city's structure through vibrations, through changes in air pressure, through the tales told by insects that venture up and return.

By dawn it contracts back to a palm-sized mass, but it remembers. The Tender has watched it for three years now, seen how its nightly expansions grow more elaborate, more accurate. It knows where the bakeries are by the yeast that filters down. It knows where the tanneries dump their waste. It knows which buildings have basements and which rest on solid earth.

Sometimes the Cartographer extends a pseudopod toward the tiny twig city, and the Tender allows it to explore the structure with something like curiosity. Once, it rebuilt a collapsed section with its own dried tissue, an act of repair that suggests understanding beyond mere reaction.

**The Memory Worms** live deepest, in the spaces between roots where plant matter compresses into something like peat. Pale, segmented things no longer than your smallest finger, they consume time itself—or rather, they eat the chemical traces of what has been. Each worm is a living archive of every creature that has died here, every spore that has taken root, every chemical message that has passed through the substrate.

Split one in half (though the Tender would never), and both halves remember everything. They share memories through conjunction, briefly joining their bodies to exchange chemical libraries. The oldest ones, the Tender suspects, remember when this chamber was solid wood, before the first crack let in the first beetle, before moisture and time hollowed out this pocket universe.

They surface only when something truly novel enters the space—a new species of fungus, an unknown chemical signature, a vibration pattern they haven't catalogued. Then they emerge in writhing clusters, tasting the newcomer with bodies that are mostly nervous system, filing the experience away in proteins that will outlast the city above.

**The Architects** are what become of beetles that live too long in the phosphorescent light. Their shells grow translucent, then transparent, revealing organs that pulse with borrowed fungal glow. They lose interest in food, in mating, in the normal beetle concerns. Instead, they build.

Using saliva that hardens into amber-like resin, they construct impossible structures in the corners where root meets root—spiraling towers barely visible to the naked eye, bridges between surfaces that serve no purpose except perhaps beauty. The other insects navigate around these structures with something like reverence, never disturbing them even as they grow to block established pathways.

The Tender suspects the Architects are trying to recreate something—perhaps a memory encoded in their cells, perhaps a vision received from the Grubs' dreams. Their constructions follow mathematical principles that shouldn't exist in insect minds: golden ratios, Fibonacci spirals, fractals that repeat at ever-smaller scales until they pass beyond even the Tender's ability to perceive.

**The Tender themselves** is becoming something new. Each visit changes them a little more. Their hair grows in segments now, breaking at regular intervals like an insect's antennae. Their breath comes in spiracles—secondary breathing holes opening along their ribs, though they still need their human lungs for the journey to and from this place.

They've started pupating, in a sense. Building cocoons of shed beetle shells and compressed moss in various corners of the city above, places where they can rest and transform gradually. One day, perhaps, they'll no longer need the gap in the foundation stones. They'll simply be here, part of the circulation, another species in the endless evolution of the chamber.

The tiny city at the center isn't just model or monument—it's a promise. When the Tender finally transforms completely, when they become small enough or strange enough to live within those twig walls, someone else will need to maintain the passage between worlds. The beetles know this. The Grubs dream it. The Cartographer maps the routes future Tenders will take. The Memory Worms encode the knowledge that will need to be passed on.

Until then, the chamber pulses with its slow life, and the Tender builds their tiny cities, and the beetles dance their chemical stories, and something new is always becoming in the darkness between the city's bones.
