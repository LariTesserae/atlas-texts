In this absolute darkness, where purpose guides every movement, dwell the Consonance.

They are not quite singular, not quite plural. Each member of the Consonance exists as a flattened, palm-sized disk of translucent flesh, thin as paper, edged with hundreds of hair-like cilia that taste the air and grip the stone. Alone, a single disk is barely viable—a wandering mouth with rudimentary nerve clusters, able only to rasp biofilm from rock and flee from vibrations that suggest predation.

But they are never alone.

The Consonance layer themselves in stacks thousands deep, each disk pressed against those above and below until individual boundaries become academic. Neural tissue aligns between layers. Circulatory channels merge at their edges where the cilia interweave. What begins as mere crowding becomes architecture—thinking architecture that processes the cacophony of this place into something like understanding.

A mature stack might contain fifty thousand individuals, compressed into a column no taller than a human but far more aware. They move as one entity, the bottom layers gripping and releasing in waves that propel the whole mass forward with disturbing grace. When they encounter prey—usually the soft-bodied putty-creatures that compress in terror—the stack doesn't simply fall upon them. Instead, it delaminates partially, sending sheets of coordinated disks flowing around the victim like liquid made of consciousness, digesting it in a cavity formed by their temporary reconfiguration.

Their intelligence scales with their mass. A small stack of hundreds thinks like a clever insect, navigating obstacles and remembering water sources. A medium stack of thousands demonstrates problem-solving, using tools made from secreted minerals, building bridges across chasms from their own temporarily rigid bodies. The massive ceremonial stacks—those rare assemblages of hundreds of thousands that form for purposes still unclear—display something beyond intelligence. They compose.

The Consonance sing, if singing is the right word for controlled modulation of the endless noise. They sample the cacophony through their collective cilia, process it through their layered neural networks, and emit new patterns—harmonies that arise from chaos, rhythms that predict the feeding frenzies, frequencies that cause certain bioluminescent species to flash in synchronization. They are living composers of the darkness, turning din into data into something approaching art.

Reproduction is communal and strange. When conditions are right—usually after processing enough minerals from a fresh stone seep—an entire stack will shudder and compress, each disk undergoing binary fission simultaneously. For hours, the stack is vulnerable, quivering as every member divides. Then suddenly where there was one entity, there are two, identical in composition but immediately beginning to diverge as they sample different portions of the endless noise, developing distinct acoustic signatures, unique ways of interpreting the darkness.

Sometimes stacks meet and merge, their disks shuffling together like cards from different decks, creating hybrid entities with confused memories, conflicting responses, new capabilities born from the tension. Other times they go to war, not with violence but with sound—each stack generating frequencies designed to disrupt the other's internal coordination until one loses coherence and collapses into its component disks, which are quickly absorbed by the victor.

The largest Consonance ever observed—though observation is perhaps the wrong word in this darkness—was glimpsed only through the massive bioluminescent response it triggered. A migration of millions of disks, flowing across the stone ceiling like an inverted river, their coordination so perfect they moved as liquid. Where they were going, what they sought, what thoughts a million-mind might think—unknown. The bioluminescent flashes in their wake lasted for hours, a light-show echo of something too vast for smaller stacks to comprehend.

They build nothing permanent, leave no tools behind, create no art that survives their passing. The Consonance exist purely as process—thinking processes, digesting processes, singing processes that emerge from the congregation of insufficient parts into overwhelming wholes. They transform the noise of this place into information, information into pattern, pattern into purpose.

In the absolute darkness, they flow between the states of being—solid stack, liquid sheet, gaseous cloud of separated disks—guided by decisions made by collective neurons processing collective sensations into collective actions. They are the darkness given memory, the cacophony given voice, the density of life given something approaching intention.

When they pass through an area, the other creatures grow quiet for a moment, their calls disrupted by the Consonance's sonic arrangements. In that brief silence, you can hear them thinking—the whisper of cilia against stone, the wet sound of layers adjusting, the subtle harmonics of their perpetual self-song. Then the moment passes, the cacophony returns, and the Consonance flow onward through the darkness, pursuing purposes that only they, in their terrible unity, understand.
