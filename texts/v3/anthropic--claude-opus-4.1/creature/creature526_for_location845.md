## The Inhabitants

### The Glass Shepherds

They were minerals first, before they learned to want.

You don't see them immediately — your eyes interpret them as heat mirages, as refractions in the obsidian walls, as tricks of the amber light. But gradually you realize that some of the distortions move with purpose. They tend the crystalline plants with touches that phase between solid and suggestion, their forms constantly shifting between states of matter like their garden.

Each Glass Shepherd stands roughly your height when they choose to stand at all. Their bodies are compositions of living volcanic glass, perpetually molten at the edges, perpetually crystallizing at the core. They move by partially liquefying, flowing forward, then restructuring themselves from the ground up. The process looks agonizing but they show no sign of pain — perhaps because they haven't fully developed the concept yet.

Their faces, when they manifest faces, are geometric approximations of expression. Features emerge from smooth obsidian like thoughts becoming solid: a triangular depression that might be an eye, a horizontal crack that could be a mouth, protrusions that suggest listening. These features last only as long as the Shepherd's attention holds them in place. When they focus on their work, their heads smooth back into featureless curved surfaces that reflect the garden in fish-eye distortion.

They communicate through resonance. When one discovers something — a new thermal vent opening, a plant-cluster ready to divide, a pipe that needs rerouting — it sets its body vibrating at a specific frequency. The others feel this through the volcanic floor, through the air, through the synchronized pulse of their partially-shared mineral matrix. Their language is older than words, newer than meaning. Sometimes they harmonize together, creating standing waves that rearrange the industrial components into more efficient configurations.

The Shepherds are harvesters and architects both. They milk the geometric collector nodes with appendages that extrude specifically for this purpose — hollow glass fingers that interface directly with the dodecahedrons, drawing out concentrated entropy that flows visible through their translucent bodies like captured aurora. They store this energy in specialized chambers within their torsos, organs that look like frozen explosions, crystalline fractals that pulse with colors that shouldn't exist at these temperatures.

But they're also gardeners in the truest sense. They prune the crystal foliage with edges they sharpen from their own substance, leaving behind glass shavings that the plants absorb and incorporate. They guide the hair-thin pipes beneath the ground, training them like vines toward optimal positions. When a plant-cluster needs to relocate, a Shepherd will partially merge with it, their minerals mixing at the boundary until plant and tender become indistinguishable, then separate at the new location, each having learned something from the temporary unity.

### The Theoretical Swarm

Between the plants, in the spaces where the industrial pipes don't run, in the corners where geometry hasn't quite decided its rules — this is where the Theoretical Swarm lives.

They are perhaps ten thousand strong, each individual no larger than a dust mote, visible only in aggregate when they cluster and catch the light. But "individual" may be the wrong word. The Swarm exists in a state of constant democratic debate about whether they are one being or many, and the answer changes from moment to moment based on voting patterns that ripple through their collective like wind through wheat.

Each mote is a perfect sphere of condensed probability, not quite matter, not quite energy, but something perpendicular to both. They feed on the uncertainty inherent in the Furnace Garden's unfinished physics, metabolizing the spaces where rules haven't crystallized yet. Where a corner doesn't quite resolve, the Swarm congregates, drinking in the ambiguity. Where distance remains negotiable, they stretch themselves across multiple interpretations simultaneously.

The Swarm serves as the Garden's memory and its imagination simultaneously. They record everything — every configuration the pipes have taken, every location the plants have occupied, every pattern that has appeared in the thermal interference on the silicon ceiling. But they record it as possibilities rather than facts. In their distributed consciousness, the Garden exists in thousands of variations: versions where the plants are purely mechanical, versions where the Glass Shepherds never evolved past mineral formation, versions where water somehow exists here and changes everything.

When they move collectively, they create patterns that look like mathematical equations solving themselves in three-dimensional space. Spirals that calculate the optimal heat distribution. Grids that map the probability of new vent formation. Cascading fractals that predict which crystal leaves will achieve phase transition next. The Glass Shepherds have learned to read these formations, adjusting their gardening accordingly.

Sometimes the Swarm touches you — not physically, but probabilistically. You feel them testing variations of your presence: what if you had entered through a different crack, what if you were silicon-based, what if you had never come at all. The sensation is like déjà vu in reverse, remembering futures that won't quite happen.

### The Sovereign Chrysalis

At the garden's heart, where the largest thermal vent breathes superheated possibility into the amber air, something waits to be born.

The Chrysalis is four meters tall, woven from pipe-veins that the Glass Shepherds tend with particular reverence. Its surface is a tapestry of crystalline plant fibers and industrial collection nodes, all pulsing in synchronization with the ceiling membrane's slow breath. Inside, glimpsed through translucent sections that might be windows or might be eyes, something turns and reshapes itself with infinite patience.

The Shepherds bring their harvested entropy here, feeding it through specialized ports that open like mouths or wounds in the Chrysalis's surface. The Theoretical Swarm orbits it in complex patterns, their formations suggesting calculations too elaborate for single minds to hold. Even the plants lean toward it, their impossible photosynthesis somehow focused on its presence.

What grows inside isn't quite life and isn't quite machine. It might be the Garden's attempt to create its own observer, someone to determine which version of reality becomes true. Or perhaps it's the space itself achieving consciousness, preparing to wake up and remember that it dreamed all of this into being. The Shepherds don't know — their resonance frequencies go chaotic when they try to discuss it, their harmony dissolving into crystalline cacophony.

Sometimes you can hear it thinking. The sound comes up through the floor, through your bones, a vibration that exists more in meaning than in audio. It thinks in geological time, one thought per epoch, but those thoughts are reshaping the laws that govern this place. Each pulse from the Chrysalis slightly adjusts the rules: today the plants need less heat, tomorrow the pipes can carry solid light, next year perhaps water will become theoretically possible.

The Sovereign Chrysalis is teaching the Furnace Garden how to exist without precedent, how to be lush without water, how to grow technology like fruit. When it finally emerges — if emergence is even the right concept — it will be the Garden's first native intelligence that fully understands what this place is trying to become.

### The Extinct Rivers

They aren't exactly inhabitants, but they refuse to accept their absence.

The Rivers died when the volcanic chamber formed, boiled away in the first seconds of its creation. But their patterns persist in the crystalline plants' growth, in the paths the pipes follow underground, in the ways the Glass Shepherds flow when they move. The Furnace Garden is haunted by the negative space of water, by the absence that shapes everything present.

Sometimes, when the thermal columns twist just right, when the interference patterns on the ceiling align with lost memories, you can see them: ghost rivers of pure information flowing where water should be, carrying data instead of sediment, connecting the plant clusters in networks of meaning that predate the Garden's current physics.

The Glass Shepherds occasionally pause in their work, their bodies rippling with wave patterns that have no source. They're remembering water they never knew, honoring rivers that existed before they did. The Theoretical Swarm records these ghost flows meticulously, understanding that they represent the Garden's deepest logic — the reason why a waterless place insists on lushness, why impossible gardens demand to grow.

The Extinct Rivers are the Garden's unconscious, the dream beneath the dream, the reason why this prototype reality is testing alternatives to water-based life. They failed to survive the transformation but succeeded in encoding themselves into whatever came after, ensuring that even in their absence, they still shape the flow of everything.
