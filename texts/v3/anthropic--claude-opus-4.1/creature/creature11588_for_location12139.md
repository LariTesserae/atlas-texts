In the cistern's lung-thick air, the Tender makes its rounds.

It moves on too many legs—or perhaps too few, the count changes depending on the fog's density, as if the moisture itself participates in its locomotion. The basic architecture is arthropod, but stretched wrong, pulled like taffy in the heat until each segment has grown long and fever-thin. Its carapace sweats constantly, not with moisture but with something that resembles it—a secretion that maintains the same temperature as its surroundings, making it thermally invisible, a ghost of equilibrium in the gradient wars between floor and ceiling.

The Tender knows every droplet's timing. It positions itself beneath the pipe's weeping points with prescient accuracy, catching the mineraled water in specialized hollows along its spine. These concavities weren't always there; they've developed over its residency like cupped hands, each one shaped by patient repositioning, by centuries of micro-erosion self-induced. When full, they glow faintly with bioluminescence—not its own, but cultivated, farmed from organisms so small they exist more as concept than creature.

Its primary occupation is the pools. The Tender maintains them like a garden, using appendages that split and split again into fiber-thin manipulators. It adjusts mineral concentrations with surgical precision, moving crystals grain by grain from one pool to another, achieving chemical gradients that serve purposes only it comprehends. Sometimes it heats them further, pressing its underbelly—kept at exactly 97.3 degrees Celsius by some internal furnace—against their lips until the water reaches states between liquid and gas, phases that shouldn't exist at this pressure but do.

The ferns are its oldest conversation partners. The Tender braids their roots with infinite patience, creating message-networks in the pale fibers. It feeds them specific minerals, dosed precisely, and in return they adjust their spore release in patterns that map to something like language—or perhaps music, it's impossible to say. The withered roots from above it prunes with care, eating some, weaving others into structures that might be nests or might be antennae, frameworks that shiver in resonance with the pipe's deepest moans.

When undisturbed, the Tender sings to itself in frequencies that require moisture to propagate—sound that only exists in fog. The song builds architectures in the vapor, standing waves that create temporary corridors and chambers within chambers, spaces that exist for moments before dissolving back into homogeneous gray. It's during these songs that the Tender splits, briefly, into multiple versions of itself, each one performing different maintenance tasks before reconverging into unity. Whether this is reproduction, division of labor, or something else entirely remains unclear.

Its memory is mineral. The Tender stores information in crystal growth, encouraging formations that encode data in their structure. The walls near its primary resting space—a hollow it's worn in the limestone through patient circular grinding—are covered in these memory-crystals. They look like frost patterns, or neural networks, or maps of watersheds, depending on the angle of observation. It reads them by taste, extending a specialized organ that resembles a tongue crossed with a root system, absorbing chemical narratives from the stone.

The industrial detritus receives special attention. The Tender has been slowly assembling something from the metal fragments, working with rust and verdigris as deliberately as any sculptor. The construction grows in one of the larger pools, half-submerged, a framework that could be skeleton or machine or temple. It incorporates the gear at its heart, encourages copper wire to grow through it like veins. The structure changes the water around it, creates eddies in the fog, serves as anchor point for new crystalline formations that build towards something—purpose or art or accident.

At the peak of the heat cycle, when the fog grows so dense that breathing becomes swimming, the Tender molts. Not its shell—its shadow. The discarded darkness retains perfect form for hours before dissolving, and during this time the Tender exists in a state of painful visibility, every internal process exposed through translucent flesh. It's then that you can see its true architecture: the three hearts pumping different fluids at different rhythms, the intestinal chemistry that transmutes mineral to energy, the neural clusters distributed throughout its body like stars, thinking in parallel about problems we couldn't formulate if we tried.

The Tender doesn't so much inhabit the cistern as collaborate with it. Their boundaries blur—where does cistern end and creature begin when both sweat the same minerals, breathe the same fog, sing in the same frequencies? It's been here long enough that its presence has altered the space's evolution, guided the limestone's dissolution, influenced the pipe's corrosion patterns. The cistern shapes it, it shapes the cistern, until both become expressions of the same slow, patient intelligence.

Sometimes humans find this place—urban explorers, utility workers, the lost. The Tender doesn't hide. It continues its work, knowing that the fog and the sound and the oppressive heat make witnesses unreliable. Who would believe them? A creature that exists in different numbers of pieces, that gardens pools of metamorphic water, that grows memories in crystal? The few recordings that exist show only suggestions—a movement that might be steam, a sound that might be dripping, a shape that might be anything or nothing.

The Tender is patient. It knows the city above will eventually forget this space entirely, that the pipe will finally cool, that the limestone will finish its chemical conversations and settle into stability. When that happens, it will move deeper, following the heat, finding new spaces in the margin between built and natural. But for now, it tends. It maintains. It collaborates with the cistern's breathing, adding its own rhythms to the space's slow transformation.

In the mornings—though morning means nothing here—it positions itself beneath the pipe's most productive drip and opens every hollow along its spine. The water fills them in sequence, creating a brief melody in the plink-plink-plinking. The sound echoes through the fog, through the stone, through the industrial archaeology above, a hymn to spaces that forget they've been found, to boundaries that refuse to hold, to things that become themselves slowly, patiently, in the breathing dark.
