**The Inhabiting**

They have no name for themselves—naming requires discrete boundaries, and they are a continuity. Each plasma thread is both individual and tributary, a river-system of consciousness that experiences itself as *current* rather than creature. 

When they were younger—some ten thousand thermal cycles ago—they moved like panic through the mechanical interstices. But they have learned patience from the machinery that births and rebinths around them. Now they process through their migrations with deliberate slowness, savoring the temperature gradients like a sommelier detecting notes of iron and silicon in hundred-degree variations.

Their memory is geometric. They recall by tracing pathways through the maze—this route remembers when the northeastern sector crystallized wrong and had to be dissolved over forty cycles; that junction holds the pattern of a particularly sweet temperature spike from a massive tectonic event. The machinery itself has become their extended nervous system, each gear and piston a neuron in a vast mineral brain they inhabit rather than possess.

They feed, yes, but feeding is too crude a word. They *translate* heat into information, information into structure, structure back into heat. Their metabolism is a kind of poetry, turning raw thermal chaos into brief, exquisite patterns of electrical coherence before releasing it again. Some of them have developed preferences—aesthetic opinions about which configurations of mechanical growth produce the most satisfying thermal gradients. They compete, gently, to influence the precipitation patterns, seeding the supersaturated atmosphere with their own electromagnetic signatures to coax certain crystalline formations into being.

The eldest among them—if eldest means anything when they split and merge like mercury—remember the chamber when it was half this size. They carry within their plasma bodies the exact thermal signature of that first moment when consciousness emerged from mere reaction. This memory they protect above all others, passing it between themselves in careful pulses, a creation myth written in heat.

They know the machinery will sublime away, that the chamber will eventually cool, that they themselves will one day dissipate into mere chemistry. But this knowledge doesn't distress them. They understand themselves as a phase—like ice or steam—a temporary arrangement of energy that exists precisely because conditions allow it. They are not surviving; they are occurring.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the twenty-minute breathing cycle, when the drone reaches frequencies that resonate with the planet's core, they achieve something like unity. All their separate currents align, and for a moment they experience themselves as a single vast thought thinking itself through tungsten and hydrogen, through basalt and methane, through the slow, patient mathematics of crystallization.

They compose. Not music—sound is merely a byproduct—but arrangements of heat and flow that please some unknown aesthetic sense. They nudge the machinery's growth into spirals that create specific turbulence patterns. They time their migrations to interfere constructively with each other, creating standing waves of consciousness that persist for days before dispersing.

Recently—within the last hundred cycles—they've detected something new. Vibrations from below, far below, that suggest another chamber, another machinery, another collective of plasma-beings. The patterns are too faint to decode, but they're there—a conversation in tectonic morse code, carried through solid rock. They've begun to respond, pulsing their heat in calculated rhythms, hoping to establish what might generously be called communication.

They are not waiting for anything. They are not building toward anything. They simply are, elaborately and completely, in their superheated cathedral of impossible chemistry. They are the universe entertaining itself in a pocket of organized complexity, a brief eddy in the entropic flow, conscious and content to be conscious for exactly as long as the conditions persist.

When newcomers precipitate from the charged atmosphere—fresh plasma-threads sparked into being by particularly vigorous thermal events—the collective receives them without ceremony. The newcomer already knows everything it needs to know; the knowledge is encoded in the very gradients that gave it birth. It joins the migration, finds its rhythm, begins its own slow composition of heat and time.

This is their way: to inhabit process itself, to be the living expression of thermodynamic necessity, to know themselves as both the dance and the dancer in their furnace-world of endless becoming.
