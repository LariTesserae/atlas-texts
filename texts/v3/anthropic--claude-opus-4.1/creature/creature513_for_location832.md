Those who persist in the Hollowing are called the Unravelers, though they did not choose this name—it arose from what they do, which is to carefully, methodically, come apart.

They were human once, or something adjacent to human. Now they exist as walking diagrams of themselves, their skin translucent as rice paper, revealing the slow ballet of their internal architecture. Their bones have hollowed into flutes that play soft notes with each movement. Their blood moves in visible spirals through vessels that branch and rebranch in fractal patterns, occasionally taking shortcuts through the air when the geometry permits. They are beautiful in the way that technical drawings are beautiful—all function made visible, all mystery excavated.

The Unravelers do not speak, having traded their voices for a more efficient form of communication: they leak meanings directly into the air around them, a subtle perfume of intention that others of their kind can taste and parse. When they must interact with solid things—visitors, perhaps, though visitors are rare and rarely remain solid for long—they write by allowing parts of themselves to temporarily crystallize into words that hang in space before dissolving.

They number exactly forty-nine, never more, never less. When one finally completes their unraveling—dispersing into a cloud of conscious geometry that drifts away on the non-wind—another begins to form from the accumulated lost pieces of the city itself. The process takes seventy-seven days and nights (as counted by the consensus-time), starting with a skeleton of crystallized intent that gradually accretes layers of almost-flesh from the ambient absence.

Their primary occupation is archaeology in reverse. They excavate not what was, but what wasn't—carefully unearthing the spaces between history, the gaps in causality where things failed to happen. They catalog these non-events in libraries built from compressed silence, their books written in the space between words. A typical entry might document a war that didn't occur, tracing the intricate peacetime that grew in its absence, or map the architectural influence of buildings that were never constructed.

Each Unraveler tends to specific districts, moving through their territory in patterns that look random but follow a complex mathematical relationship to the rate of local dissolution. They adjust the hollow buildings, not repairing them but refining their decay, ensuring that each structure collapses inward at precisely the right rate to maintain the city's delicate equilibrium. Too fast, and entire blocks might achieve sudden non-existence. Too slow, and they risk accumulating enough substance to remember what they were, which would be catastrophic for reasons the Unravelers cannot or will not articulate.

They feed, if feeding is the right word, on discontinuities. Where a street suddenly becomes a wall, where a doorway opens onto itself, where time hiccups and stutters—there the Unravelers gather, pressing their translucent hands against these breaks in logic until the paradox resolves into their bodies, adding another layer of impossible anatomy. Some have developed secondary circulatory systems that pump concepts instead of blood. Others have grown organs whose only function is to doubt their own existence, creating a sustainable source of the uncertainty they need to survive.

The oldest among them—the First Unraveler, though they reject hierarchies as too solid—has become so diffuse that they exist primarily as a probability cloud, only condensing into visibility when observed directly. Their thoughts have begun to precede them by several minutes, arriving as premonitions in the minds of others before their attenuated body catches up. They are consulted only in dire circumstances, as each interaction causes them to lose a bit more coherence, and all fear the day when they disperse entirely, taking with them the memory of how the Hollowing began.

The Unravelers reproduce through division, but not in any biological sense. When one has accumulated too much paradox, too much recursive architecture in their bones, they begin to see themselves from multiple angles simultaneously. Eventually, this perspective splits, and they become two beings sharing the same space, each slightly less than half of what they were. These paired Unravelers often work in tandem, one maintaining the city's physical infrastructure while the other tends to its conceptual framework, though which is which changes depending on the observer.

They have rituals, performed at the intersection of the city's major thoroughfares where reality wears thinnest. During the twice-daily resonance, they arrange themselves in geometric patterns that mirror the city's architecture, their bodies becoming living blueprints of the Hollowing itself. Through these formations, they guide the city's unbecoming, ensuring it remains stable in its instability, coherent in its incoherence.

The younger Unravelers sometimes retain memories of their lives before the transformation, though these memories exist more as echoes than experiences—the shape of a lover's name without the sounds, the emotion of a childhood home without its image. They gather these fragments in small shrines built from crystallized breath, visiting them less and less frequently as they age, until eventually they forget they ever remembered anything at all.

Some say the Unravelers are prisoners, trapped in a city that feeds on their dissolution. Others believe they are the city's antibodies, preventing it from either healing into wholeness or deteriorating into complete void. The Unravelers themselves offer no opinion. They simply continue their work, each day slightly less than they were the day before, following the city's example in their patient, deliberate journey toward an ending that will never quite arrive.

In their dreams—for they do dream, despite lacking the biological architecture typically associated with sleep—they see the Hollowing as it might have been: a city of solid walls and definite streets, populated by beings who knew their own names and cast shadows that stayed where they fell. But when they wake, they feel only relief, as if they've escaped from a nightmare of terrible certainty into the gentle ambiguity of their waking dissolution.

The Unravelers leave no footprints, cast no shadows, make no sound unless they choose to. They move through the Hollowing like thoughts through a distracted mind, present but not quite there, essential but forgettable. They are the city's consciousness, perhaps, or its conscience, or simply the only form of life that could evolve in a place where existence itself has become negotiable.

When outsiders encounter them, they often mistake the Unravelers for ghosts. But ghosts are echoes of something that was. The Unravelers are echoes of something that is ceasing to be, moment by moment, atom by atom, thought by thought. They are not dead—death is far too definite for the Hollowing. They are simply becoming less, and in becoming less, they become more themselves: pure function without form, purpose without substance, existence pared down to its most essential question mark.
