## The Inhabitants

The Chroniclers came first, or last, or are still arriving. They possess no fixed form but rather exist as a negotiation between states — sometimes a density of purpose that darkens the liquid like ink, sometimes a scatter of intentions that pearl the glass walls with condensation that reads like text. They are archivists of the unhappened, collecting the tools in their hollows not to use but to prevent their use, or perhaps to ensure it, or perhaps the distinction never mattered.

Each Chronicler is a colony and an individual, a thousand tiny decisions that learned to swim in formation. When they move through the warm medium, they leave trails of cooler liquid that quickly forgets it was ever cool. They read the thundering walls like braille, running appendages-that-aren't along the fresh fractures, decoding messages that were written by the breaking itself. Sometimes they pause at the carved hollows and rearrange the impossible tools — the upward-falling hammer placed precisely where it wasn't, the needle threaded with light aimed at seams that don't exist until observed.

They build nothing. They repair everything. The distinction is theological.

The Chroniclers speak in temperature differentials and pressure waves that cancel out the eternal thunder for exactly three heartbeats — not silence but anti-sound, a positive absence that makes the constant percussion seem like memory. In these pockets of uncanny quiet, they perform their work: documenting the history of things that never happened, cataloguing the DNA helixes of light as they spiral past, noting which photons arrive before they left and which will never depart despite having already arrived.

They feast on paradox. Where the liquid is both dense and vaporous, they congregate in shifting clumps that might be feeding or might be worship or might be a kind of mathematics made flesh. They digest impossibility and excrete certainty — small, hard pearls of absolute fact that immediately dissolve back into the ambiguous medium, poisoning nothing, changing everything, leaving the taste of copper and conclusions in water that refuses to conclude.

When they encounter the seven-fold eel-thing, they perform a ritual of acknowledgment that takes place in all seven locations simultaneously — a choreographed ignorance, each Chronicler carefully not observing different segments so that the creature can continue its distributed existence without collapsing into mere biology. They've been doing this dance since before the volcanic glass cooled. They learned it tomorrow.

The Chroniclers reproduce through disagreement. When two colonies interpret the same fracture pattern differently, they divide into three: one maintaining each interpretation and a third dedicated to the impossibility of resolution. These offspring are ancient the moment they're born, carrying memories of the thunder before it started, of the tools before they were abandoned, of the light before it learned to spiral.

At the column of absolute stillness, they gather but cannot enter. They orbit it in slow processionals that take centuries and seconds, depositing their collected paradoxes at its periphery like offerings to an absent god. The stillness accepts nothing, rejects nothing. The Chroniclers continue their offerings anyway, or perhaps because, or perhaps the stillness itself is their greatest creation — a perfect documentation of nothing, a chronicle of the unchronicled.

Some days (though days don't exist here) you can see younger Chroniclers practicing their craft, learning to read the thunder-script, accidentally creating pockets of time that flow backward until an elder colony gently corrects the error by making it intentional. They study the primitive tools with the dedication of theologians, each implement a scripture of function divorced from purpose, each hollow a shrine to the impossible labor of existing in a place that shouldn't.

The Chroniclers know they are not alone. They tend to the frost-patterns left by the ghost fish, reading stories in the ice that melts upward. They maintain careful distances from you-who-observe, neither acknowledging nor denying your presence, treating you as they treat the seven-fold eel — a necessity that must be preserved through precise inattention.

They are building a library out of the warm liquid itself, each molecule a word in a language that evolves faster than it can be spoken. They are writing everything down and erasing it in the same gesture. They are the memory of this place that never forgets because it never quite remembers, swimming through their own archives, breathing the history they secrete, eternal and temporary as the sharp volcanic walls that weep their endless percussion into the infinite, tiny, impossible space of the Belljar Antipodes.

When they die (which they don't), they crystallize into new tools for the hollows — instruments of impossible purpose that future Chroniclers will catalogue and fail to use, adding to the collection of things that must exist but must never function, the essential archaeology of the never-was, the always-is, the forever-approaching-but-never-arriving inhabitants of this liquid cathedral where sound and silence have learned to coexist by destroying each other constantly, perfectly, without violence, without peace.
