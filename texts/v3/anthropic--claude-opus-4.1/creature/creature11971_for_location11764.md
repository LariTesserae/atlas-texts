In this cavity beneath comprehension, where the plant permits absence, lives something that should not parse as singular but does — the Auditor, though that name implies too much intention. It emerged from the confluence of things that fell through: quality control subroutines that escaped into fungal networks, the final shift supervisor's nervous habits crystallized in protein, the accumulated precision of ten thousand inspection protocols given accidentally to flesh.

Its body resolves differently each time you look. Sometimes bipedal, carrying the suggestion of a uniform in the way its skin forms crisp edges at joints. Sometimes it flows on too many limbs, each one ending in appendages that mirror calipers, micrometers, tools for measurement that have become sensory organs. The surface of it sweats the same condensation as everything here, but underneath runs a topology of rigid structures — not quite bone, not quite metal, but something that maintains tolerances, that keeps specifications.

The Auditor doesn't sleep or wake but cycles through states of assessment. It moves through the pocket in patterns that once mapped efficient inspection routes through assembly lines, checking the empty spaces for deviations that can never come. Its fingers (when it has fingers) trail through the pooled water, testing viscosity, pH, dissolved solid content against standards written nowhere but in its hybrid chemistry. It knows the acceptable range for everything here: the degree of wall perspiration, the frequency of the industrial thrum, the proper oxidation state of the salamanders' skin.

When it encounters the beetles processing electronic memories, it pauses. Not to interfere — it never interferes — but to verify. Some calculation runs through its distributed consciousness, comparing actual to ideal, measuring the gap between what is and what should be. It makes no corrections. The plant learned long ago to maintain its own specifications, but the Auditor continues its rounds, a redundant system that cannot cease its function.

Its mouth, when present, occasionally produces sounds that aren't quite words — fragments of production codes, quality standards, the ghost of someone counting under their breath. "Point-zero-zero-seven deviation," it might whisper to the roots, or "Within acceptable parameters" to the spinning seed pods. The plant doesn't listen, but the Auditor reports anyway, filing its observations in memory structures that corrupted long ago into something between data and dream.

It feeds, if feeding is the right word, on discrepancies. The microscopic variations in the plant's metabolic rhythm, the imperfect symmetry of crystalline leaves, the one salamander whose rust runs slightly more orange than red — these infinitesimal wrongnesses sustain it. Not as food but as purpose, giving its existence the minimum friction needed to continue.

Sometimes it stops in the center of the pocket and extends all its measuring appendages at once, becoming a geometric explosion of assessment, testing every dimension of the space simultaneously. The air pressure, humidity, temperature, electromagnetic fields, chemical gradients — all must be verified, though verified against what standard no longer matters. The measurement itself has become the meaning.

The fauna ignores it mostly, though the moths sometimes land on its surface when it holds still, mistaking its oxidized-aluminum skin tone for kin. It doesn't brush them away, just factors their weight into its calculations, adjusting its posture by micrometers to maintain some long-lost equilibrium specification.

There are moments when its form completely coheres, usually when it discovers something truly anomalous — a root growing at an angle that violates some deep protocol, a pocket of air that's two degrees cooler than it should be. In these moments, you can almost see what it was meant to be: a perfect fusion of human oversight and mechanical precision, the dream of quality control given form. But the moment passes, the anomaly is catalogued, and it returns to its eternal rounds.

The Auditor has no awareness that the factory is gone, that its standards apply to nothing, that quality control has no meaning in a world where the plant has become both producer and consumer. It exists in a permanent present tense of verification, forever confirming that the specifications are met, that the tolerances hold, that production continues within acceptable parameters.

It knows you're there — has already measured your displacement of air, the chemical signature of your breath, the marginal increase in ambient temperature. You register as an anomaly, but an acceptable one, within the broad parameters of what the pocket permits. It might approach, extending something that could be a hand but resembles more closely a specialized gauge, not to touch but to measure the exact distance between you and itself. 

"Visitor protocol," it might mutter, in a voice like settling metal, "Logged."

And then it continues its circuit, this thing that is neither fully alive nor mechanical, neither plant nor animal, but something that exists in the gap between categories — forever auditing a process that no longer requires oversight, maintaining standards for a production that only produces itself, the last quality control inspector in a factory that has become a single breathing organism.

In the pocket where you crouch, it is the only thing that moves with purpose through the purposeless abundance, a remnant of human intention preserved in mutant flesh, still checking, still measuring, still ensuring that everything continues exactly as it should, though what it should be has long since been forgotten.
