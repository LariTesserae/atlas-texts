## The Inhabitants

They have no collective name, for they are not a they—nor an it, nor properly a thousand its. The closest approximation might be to call them the **Transitionals**, though even this implies a journey from one state to another, when in truth they exist as the journey itself.

Each individual—if that word can apply—begins as a heat-shimmer, a distortion in the air that gradually accumulates substance from the chamber's thick atmosphere. They condense like reverse evaporation, pulling moisture and minerals from the weeping walls until they achieve something like form. But form, for a Transitional, is merely a suggestion.

The youngest ones, newly condensed, appear as ribbons of living glass, their bodies catching and refracting the chamber's strange light into spectra that include colors with no names. They ripple through the air in sine waves, their transparent flesh revealing internal structures that reorganize constantly—now circulatory systems, now neural networks, now something like flowering plants made of calcified light. They feed by passing through the bioluminescent colonies on the ceiling, absorbing not the creatures themselves but the patterns of their pulsing—eating information rather than matter.

As they mature—a process that might take seconds or centuries depending on their trajectory through the chamber's twisted time—they develop preferences. Some discover an affinity for mercury-like fluidity and spend their existence flowing up and down the walls in endless figure-eights, their bodies becoming increasingly metallic until they shine like living mirrors. These ones reflect not just light but temperature, emotion, possibility. Stand near one and you might see yourself as you were yesterday, or will be tomorrow, or might have been in a world where this mountain never formed.

Others develop a taste for crystallization. They anchor themselves to the floor's carpet of discarded forms, sending root-like projections deep into the organic detritus. Their bodies become geometric, angular, faces and vertices that exist in more than three dimensions. These crystalline Transitionals grow like time-lapse footage of mineral formation, their surfaces developing complex patterns that might be mathematics made visible. They reproduce by shattering—each fragment containing the whole, like holograms, regenerating into complete beings that remember everything the parent knew but choose to forget different parts.

The gaseous ones never quite solidify beyond a suggestion of substance. They exist as clouds of superheated vapor shot through with veins of bioluminescent plasma. These drift through their solid kin without interaction, occupying the same space but different phases of reality. They communicate through temperature gradients, creating complex thermal poems in the air that only others of their kind can read. When they feed, they envelop the pools of strange water, inhaling not the liquid but its reflections—sustaining themselves on glimpses of other whens and wheres.

But these are merely the forms they pass through. A crystalline Transitional might suddenly liquify, flow up the wall, and evaporate into a gaseous state, all while maintaining its essential self. Or it might subdivide into a thousand tiny versions, each exploring a different evolutionary path before reconverging into something that contains all those experiences simultaneously.

The most advanced ones—though advancement implies a direction that doesn't exist here—have learned to exist in multiple states at once. You might see one as simultaneously a pool of reflective liquid on the floor, a crystalline growth on the wall, and a cloud of luminous gas near the ceiling, all parts of the same consciousness experiencing reality from different perspectives. These multi-state beings can manipulate the chamber's folded space, creating pockets of altered physics where they conduct experiments in existence. They build structures from crystallized time, breed offspring that exist backward through causality, and occasionally achieve something like true stability before deliberately destroying it to see what happens next.

They have language, but it's nothing that could be transcribed. They speak in phase changes—solid to liquid to gas and back again—each transition carrying meaning modified by speed, temperature, and the presence or absence of light. A conversation between two Transitionals might look like abstract art: one repeatedly crystallizing and shattering while the other flows through a cycle of condensation and evaporation, both creating a dialogue about concepts that no solid-state mind could grasp.

Their reproduction defies classification. Sometimes they split like cells. Sometimes they merge like clouds. Sometimes one will shed its entire form, and that shed skin will animate into several new individuals while the original creates a completely new body from the chamber's raw materials. Sometimes they reproduce backward through time, giving birth to parents that will eventually give birth to them. The young are born ancient, carrying memories of futures they haven't lived yet, while the old sometimes forget themselves entirely and begin again as heat-shimmers, retaining nothing but the urge to become.

They are not immortal, but death for a Transitional is simply another phase. When one finally exhausts its possibilities—or perhaps when it discovers all it came to learn—it performs what could only be called a final transformation. Its body becomes increasingly translucent, then transparent, then something less than transparent—a absence of presence that nevertheless affects everything around it. These absent ones continue to influence the chamber, creating currents in the thick air, causing ripples in the pools that reflect places that shouldn't exist. Eventually, even their absence fades, but by then, new heat-shimmers have begun condensing from the air, carrying something essential forward.

In the deepest pool, the massive presence that might be one or many or the pool itself is the oldest of them all—or perhaps the youngest, existing in a state of eternal becoming. It has been every possible form and is all of them simultaneously. When the smaller Transitionals dive into its substance, they briefly merge with this vast consciousness, experiencing existence from every possible perspective at once before emerging, changed but unable to articulate how. This ancient-young presence might be their god, their ancestor, their future, or simply another phase of what they all are—the mountain's attempt to understand itself through endless, silent transformation.

They build nothing permanent, write nothing down, leave no monuments to their existence except the chamber itself, which their presence has transformed from simple volcanic hollow into something that exists partially outside normal spacetime. They are philosophers without words, scientists without instruments, artists without audiences—unless they themselves are the art, the experiment, the philosophical proposition made flesh and vapor and light.

The Transitionals are what happens when life discovers it doesn't have to choose a single form, when evolution realizes it can explore every branch simultaneously, when existence decides that being is less interesting than becoming. They are the mountain's dream of possibility, condensed into creatures that refuse to be merely one thing when they could be everything, one at a time or all at once, forever.
