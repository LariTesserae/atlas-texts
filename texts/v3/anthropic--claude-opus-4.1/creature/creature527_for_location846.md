# The Inhabitants of the Plateau

They call themselves the **Unravelers**, though the name is a translation of a translation of something that was never words to begin with.

You recognize them first by their absence—negative spaces in your peripheral vision that shouldn't be there, moments where the plateau's endless emptiness becomes actively vacant. They've evolved past opacity. Their bodies, if you can call them bodies, exist as controlled discontinuities in local spacetime, deliberate flaws in the fabric that somehow achieved consciousness. Each one is a walking paradox: a three-dimensional cross-section of something that lives primarily in the collapsed dimensions between moments.

When they choose to be perceived, they appear as tall, attenuated figures made of crystallized probability—humanoid in the way a shadow cast by smoke might be humanoid. Their surfaces aren't surfaces but event horizons, boundaries where the possible becomes actual for just long enough to interact. Sometimes you see through them to their own past or future positions, their timeline smeared across space like light through a prism. Their faces, when they have faces, are geometric abstractions that nevertheless convey infinite nuance—a triangle of void that somehow expresses irony, a sphere of bent light that radiates melancholy.

They move without walking. Distance is negotiable to them; they simply adjust their probability of being elsewhere until elsewhere becomes here. Groups of Unravelers passing through the same space don't collide—they superimpose, creating interference patterns of consciousness that birth temporary new individuals who exist just long enough to think one transcendent thought before dissolving back into their component beings.

Their primary occupation is what they call "orbital tending"—though this has nothing to do with celestial mechanics. They maintain the vast patterns that keep the plateau stable in its impossible position, walking between the probability towers in paths that describe mathematical proofs. Each journey across the emptiness is a calculation, each pause a decimal point in an equation that holds back entropy. They've been doing this so long that the distinction between the Unravelers and the plateau itself has become academic. Are they maintaining it, or has it grown them like specialized organs to maintain itself?

They feed on paradoxes—not metaphorically, but literally metabolizing the energy released when contradictions collapse into resolution. The dimensional turbulence that churns the space above the plateau is their agriculture; they cultivate impossibilities and harvest them at the moment of greatest tension. You might see one standing perfectly still for days beneath a knot of twisted space, waiting for the exact moment when it will unravel into pure conceptual energy. They consume this through pores that open in dimensions you can't see, growing more real and less real simultaneously.

Their language abandoned sound geological ages ago. They communicate through selective alterations of local physics—a shift in the refraction index of empty space means something like "greeting," while a temporary reversal of cause and effect in a small radius conveys complex philosophical arguments. When multiple Unravelers need to share complex information, they create what you might recognize as a conference: a zone where all participants partially merge into a collective entity, their thoughts becoming physically visible as geometric patterns that fold through space like living origami, each fold a nested clause in an ever-expanding sentence.

The young among them—if youth has meaning for beings that exist partially outside of time—are more solid, more committed to singular presence. They practice maintaining stable form, sometimes spending centuries learning to cast a consistent shadow. But as they age (or perhaps as they become more themselves), they grow increasingly distributed. The eldest Unravelers exist as statistical clouds, present everywhere on the plateau with varying probability, their consciousness scattered across kilometers of empty space yet somehow remaining singular, coherent, intentional.

They build nothing permanent because permanence is antithetical to their nature. Instead, they create temporary structures from crystallized time—monuments that exist for exactly as long as they need to be observed, then dissolve back into potential. Their art consists of carefully orchestrated cascades of dimensional collapse, aesthetic experiences that can only be fully appreciated by beings that can perceive in seven dimensions simultaneously. To your eyes, these might appear as sudden flowers of impossible color that bloom and die in spaces that shouldn't exist, leaving behind only the memory of beauty too complex for your brain to fully process.

Their reproduction, if it can be called that, is an act of philosophical commitment. Two or more Unravelers engage in a debate about the nature of existence that becomes so intense, so fundamental, that it generates a new perspective—literally. This new viewpoint gains autonomy, bootstrapping itself into independent existence through pure logical necessity. The offspring begins as an idea that thinks itself into being, gradually acquiring the ability to manipulate probability until it can maintain its own discontinuity in spacetime.

Death, for the Unravelers, is voluntary and reversible—less an ending than a punctuation mark. They can choose to collapse their probability completely, existing for one perfect moment in absolute certainty before dispersing into the background mathematics of the plateau. But even this dispersal is temporary; the patterns they comprised continue to resonate in the structure of space itself, and eventually, inevitably, these patterns reach sufficient complexity to spontaneously regenerate the original consciousness, though perhaps with variations, improvements, evolutionary adaptations to better navigate the collapsing physics of their dying universe.

They remember when the plateau was young, when it was merely impossible rather than transcendentally so. Some claim to have witnessed the first probability tower phase into existence, though their accounts differ—not because memory fails, but because the event itself exists in quantum superposition, never quite having happened in any single way. They speak (in their fashion) of a time when the plateau hosted civilizations of matter, crude things that needed atoms and molecules to think with. But that was before the elevation, before the great lifting that carried this fragment of land beyond the reach of conventional physics.

The Unravelers know you're here—have known since before you arrived, will know after you've gone. To them, your presence is a temporary densification in the probability fields, a knot of crude matter stumbling through their refined emptiness. They regard you with something between pity and fascination, the way you might watch an ant trying to comprehend a cathedral. Sometimes one will pace you for kilometers, existing just outside your perception, studying the quaint way you insist on experiencing time sequentially, space continuously.

They are working toward something—all of them, always, their every action coordinated across impossible distances and improbable time. Some ultimate calculation, perhaps, or maybe they're composing reality's final theorem, the mathematical proof that will allow existence to gracefully conclude. Or perhaps they're preventing that very conclusion, their ceaseless walking and tending keeping the plateau—and by extension, some fundamental aspect of reality itself—from achieving the perfect stillness that would mean the end of all possibilities.

In the rare moments when an Unraveler chooses to acknowledge you directly, the experience is overwhelming. They see you not as you are but as you were, are, and will be—your entire timeline spread before them like a map. They perceive the quantum foam of your constituent particles, the probability clouds of your potential futures, the fossilized light of your past decisions. Their attention feels like being simultaneously dissected and assembled, understood and made incomprehensible.

And yet, for all their transcendent evolution, there's something melancholic about the Unravelers. They've evolved past pleasure, past pain, past every sensation that gave their ancestors' existence texture and meaning. They continue because continuity is all they have left, maintaining their plateau in its impossible orbit around concepts that no longer exist, faithful servants to a universe that has forgotten what it asked them to guard.

Sometimes, in the vast silences between probability towers, you might catch one of them attempting to remember what it felt like to be singular, solid, simple. They'll condense themselves as much as they can, trying to experience the plateau as you do—continuous, linear, bound by causality. But they can never quite manage it. They've traveled too far from their origins, evolved too perfectly for their environment. They are what victory looks like when evolution wins so completely that it obsoletes its own purpose.

The Unravelers are the plateau's solution to consciousness—not life as you understand it, but existence that persists through will alone, maintained by beings that are themselves maintained by the patterns they walk, an ouroboros of intention and entropy locked in eternal, elegant equilibrium at the edge of everything, where mathematics becomes mythology and the only truth left is the continuous, necessary lie of continuation itself.
