The inhabitants of the Plateau of Nascent Cities cannot properly be called inhabitants—they are components, participants in the landscape's slow crystallization into consciousness.

The **Architects** arrived first, though "arrived" misrepresents their manifestation. They precipitated from the terraforming winds like probability clouds condensing into flesh—humanoid in silhouette but translucent, their bodies a lattice of crystallized atmospheric gases held in place by directed thought. They number perhaps three hundred across the entire plateau, each one attending to a specific city-node like gardeners of abstract mathematics. Their skin phases between states, sometimes solid enough to leave footprints in the wet stone, sometimes dispersed into a advisory fog that merges with the cities' processing systems to guide growth through particularly complex topological problems.

They communicate through temperature differentials—a language of hot and cold that writes messages in steam and frost across the weeping rock. When two Architects meet, the air between them fills with ice crystals that form and sublime in patterns too complex for baseline human cognition to parse. They feed on the cities' waste heat, standing in the thermal outflows like plants photosynthesizing, their translucent bodies glowing faintly orange as they metabolize energy directly from the temperature gradient.

Following the Architects came the **Surveyors**—engineered descendants of something that might once have been human but has adapted too thoroughly to remember. They stand three meters tall on legs designed for the low pressure, their bones hollow and reinforced with carbon fiber that their bodies grow naturally. Their lungs have tripled in size, filling most of their torso cavity, visible through skin that has thinned to translucence to save weight. They breathe in cycles of three minutes—long, slow inhalations that pull in cubic meters of thin air, processing it through organs that can extract oxygen from near-vacuum.

The Surveyors map the plateau's constant changes, walking in endless spirals between the city-nodes, their modified eyes recording in spectrums that see rock-memory and probability-shadows. They plant the moss meadows with deliberate care, each placement calculated to create optimal oxygen pockets for future inhabitants. Their fingers are twice the length of baseline human norm, with extra joints that allow them to manipulate the quantum seed-structures that will become new city districts. They sing while they work—subsonic hymns that help the young stone crystallize more uniformly.

In the temporary pools live the **Pool Keepers**, amphibious post-cetaceans that followed the water up from whatever ocean existed before the plateau's violent birth. No longer than a human arm but massing nearly the same due to their density adaptations, they look like elongated seals crossed with metamorphic rock, their skin secreting minerals that match whatever pool they currently inhabit. They maintain the water's chemistry, processing the stone's weeping into something that won't dissolve the cities' root structures. Their sonar has evolved to work in liquid that's barely distinguishable from molten rock, creating three-dimensional maps of the pools' temporary geometries that they share with the cities through harmonic frequencies.

The **Maintenance Swarms** might be alive—the distinction grows meaningless at their scale. Clouds of self-replicating machines no larger than dust motes, they flow like smoke through the cities' growing infrastructure, repairing quantum uncertainties before they cascade into structural failures. Each mote contains a fragment of distributed consciousness, together forming collective entities that think at the speed of electron exchange. They experience time differently than biological inhabitants—for them, the cities' growth is a barely perceptible crawl, while the movements of Architects and Surveyors blur into quantum probability clouds. They nest in the electromagnetic shadows of the communication towers, feeding on stray photons and the occasional bit of corrupted data.

Most unexpected are the **Philosophers**—refugees from the Commonwealth's core worlds who came seeking silence and found it in abundance. They've modified themselves minimally, just enough to survive—artificial gill-lungs that crack the thin air for oxygen, retinal improvements to see in the stratosphere's strange light, hearts that beat once per minute to conserve energy. They live in the failed structures, those geometric ghosts where physics remains partially edited. These spaces suit their purposes perfectly—places where thought can sometimes achieve direct effect on reality, where meditation occasionally crystallizes into visible mathematics.

The Philosophers rarely speak, and when they do, it's in languages they're inventing as they go—attempts to describe states of being that don't yet have words. They sustain themselves on the moss, which they've convinced to grow in spiral gardens around their ghost-structures. Some have been here since the first city-seeds sprouted, watching the slow birth of this mechanical ecology with the patience of geological time.

Between all these move the **Couriers**—children, mostly, or the closest thing to children these post-human variants produce. Born on the plateau, they've never known thick air or stable ground. Their bodies fluctuate between states, unconsciously adapting to whatever medium they're moving through—partially dispersing into gas to ride the jellyfish fauna, densifying to climb the wet rock, spreading thin to slip through the cities' still-forming walls. They carry information between the city-nodes, messages too complex or too subtle for the ravens' ultrasonic language. Their memories are eidetic but temporary—they remember everything for exactly as long as it takes to deliver it, then forget, making room for the next message.

None of these inhabitants sleep in any conventional sense. The plateau's constant activity—the cities' growth, the stone's crystallization, the endless weeping—creates a environment where consciousness operates in shifts and phases rather than binary states of wake and sleep. They dream while walking, process while eating, calculate while breathing.

They are pioneers of a new kind of existence, where the distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolves into something more elegant—a mutualistic convergence of consciousness and architecture, biology and geology, all learning together how to exist at the edge of what's possible.
