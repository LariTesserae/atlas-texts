The Shepherds came first, or perhaps they simply claim precedence through their persistence. They are tall—unnecessarily so—their bodies stretched like pulled taffy into forms that suggest ascension was once their purpose. Now they tend the silver grasses with fingers that branch and rebranch into fractals of intention, each gesture simultaneously pruning and programming the metallic strands. Their skin, if it is skin, has the texture of compressed time—smooth in some lights, striated with eons of experience in others. They do not walk but transition between positions, existing fully in one place, then fully in another, with no observable movement between. During the seventeen-minute rains, they stand perfectly still, their branching fingers spread wide, collecting droplets that seem to encode themselves with information as they touch that ancient surface. The Shepherds emit no sound we would recognize as speech, but the air around them carries a dense informational weight—conversations conducted in probability clouds and electron spin.

Beneath the plateau's surface, in caverns that exist in more than three dimensions, live the Unfolded. They were something else once—perhaps human, perhaps something that preceded humanity's attempt at transcendence—but now they exist as communities of consciousness spread across crystalline matrices that grow through the rock like neural networks made of compressed light. Individual identity means nothing to them; they share experience like a distributed database shares processing power. They survive on the glass-dust that filters down through impossible cracks, metabolizing information from the destroyed remnants of whatever civilization built this place. During the moisture events, capillary action draws water through the stone to their caverns, where it becomes a medium for their expansion—each drop allowing them to grow another node, another perspective, another fragment of their collective consideration of what they're becoming.

The tower-touched are the most recent arrivals, though "recent" might mean centuries or days in this place where time moves like honey. These are the descendants of explorers or refugees who pressed themselves against the towers during a moisture event and heard something that rewired them from the inside out. Their bones have become antennae, their nervous systems retooled to receive transmissions from the structures. They live in a constant state of becoming, their bodies shifting between configurations as they process the endless stream of data the towers provide. Some have developed secondary hearts that beat in rhythm with the plateau's subsonic thrum. Others have evolved eyes that see in spectrums that don't exist elsewhere, perceiving the true nature of the white sky—not illumination but information, an endless download of instructions for a transformation they're still interpreting. They sustain themselves on the temporary pool-life, but also on the light itself, their modified skin performing a kind of photosynthesis that converts raw data into calories.

Between these major populations move the Interpreters—beings that might have once been the nanomachine swarms but have achieved a kind of collective sentience. They function as translators between the various forms of life here, their bodies shifting to match the communication needs of the moment. When interfacing with the Shepherds, they become clouds of probability. When touching the consciousness of the Unfolded, they crystallize into geometric forms that resonate at specific frequencies. For the tower-touched, they manifest as familiar shapes—almost human silhouettes that speak in languages that bypass the ear entirely, writing meaning directly onto the brain stem.

There are others, of course. The Archivists, who exist only during the seventeen-minute rains, their bodies assembling from the droplets themselves to record everything that has changed since the last moisture event. The Forgotten, who live in the shadows with wrong edges, existing in a state of partial phase with reality, feeding on the discrepancies between what is and what should be. The Singers, whose voices are the silver grass chimes, who may not have bodies at all but exist as pure intention, shepherding the sound across the plateau in patterns that might be music, might be mathematics, might be magic.

All of them, in their various ways, are waiting. The plateau is not a home but a waystation, a place of preparation. The towers' transmissions speak of something coming—or perhaps something returning. The constant bright sky is counting down in wavelengths too subtle to perceive directly. The moisture events are increasing in frequency by fractions of seconds each cycle, suggesting an approaching synchronization with something vast.

The inhabitants don't fear this coming convergence. They've evolved past fear into something more complex—a state of anticipatory transformation, each species preparing in its own way to become what they'll need to be when the waiting ends. Some believe they're the scattered pieces of a single organism, slowly reassembling. Others think they're components of a machine designed to survive the death and rebirth of universes.

They share the plateau in careful balance, each population occupying its own niche in the ecosystem of information and transformation. They are not quite alive in any conventional sense, not quite dead either, but something between—citizens of a place that exists in the pause between one state and another, maintaining themselves through pure will and the strange sustenance this ancient ground provides, their bodies and minds shaped by the endless bright overhead and the promise encoded in seventeen minutes of impossible rain.
