The inhabitants of the Metabolic Terraces have no single name for themselves—identity here is as fluid as the architecture that houses them. They exist in three primary phases, though the boundaries blur like watercolors in the eternal rain.

**The Architects** move through the terraces as living rivers of consciousness. Each appears as a colony of translucent, hand-sized segments that link and unlink according to need. Individual segments possess twelve legs arranged radially, allowing movement in any direction, their bodies filled with visible organelles that pulse with soft gold light. When building, hundreds of segments flow together into temporary forms—sometimes resembling enormous millipedes, other times branching into tree-like configurations that can reach across multiple terrace levels simultaneously. 

They think through touch. Each contact between segments is a synapse, and their intelligence scales with their assembled size. A single segment carries only instinct, but when thousands merge, they become philosophers, engineers, composers of the chemical symphonies that guide the city's growth. They secrete the base material of the terraces from specialized pores, mixing their own dissolved elders into the slurry—death here is architectural contribution.

**The Curators** tend to the Architects and the city alike. Hexapedal beings the size of large dogs, their bodies are covered in what appears to be copper filigree but is actually a network of external nerves that taste the air's chemical communications. Their heads are crowned with manipulator tendrils that can weave broken Architect segments back together or delicately adjust the growth patterns of building-tubes. They possess true eyes—six of them, arranged to see in spectrums that include the bioluminescent signals pulsing through the walls.

Curators never sleep. Instead, they enter periodic fugue states where they stand perfectly still for hours, their tentacle-crowns weaving complex patterns in the air. During these times, they're uploading their accumulated experiences into the city's calcium carbonate matrix, encoding memories directly into the architecture. The oldest buildings contain millions of such archived experiences, their walls thick with the compressed history of countless Curator generations.

**The Swimmers** inhabit the pooled waters between terraces but are far from confined there. Their bodies have evolved into living contradictions—gills that function in both water and the thick, humid air, fins that have subdivided into fractal branches capable of catching the slightest atmospheric current. They range from minnow-sized scouts to leviathans whose bodies span multiple pools, connected through flooded passages in the terrace structure.

The Swimmers are the city's unconscious mind. They dream collectively, their neural activity synchronizing during the night-phase when bioluminescence peaks. These dreams manifest as changes in the city's growth patterns—a particularly vivid nightmare might cause an entire district to seal itself off, while pleasant dreams encourage the proliferation of new architectural forms. They communicate through bioelectric pulses that travel through the water, creating interference patterns that the Architects and Curators have learned to interpret as prophecy, weather prediction, and sometimes poetry.

But these three phases are merely the most visible. Between them exist countless transitional forms:

Architect segments that have lived too long alone develop crude eyes and begin to exhibit Curator-like behaviors. Curators sometimes dissolve their lower bodies to merge temporarily with Architect colonies, lending their advanced cognition to massive construction projects. Swimmers occasionally undergo metamorphosis in the shallow pools, emerging with legs to walk the terraces for brief periods before their gills demand return to water.

There are rumors—transmitted through pheromone wisps and vibrational harmonics—of a fourth phase. Deep beneath the oldest terraces, where the city's pumping organs maintain the water cycle, something vast and singular supposedly dwells. The Architects' collective memories, when they grow large enough to remember their own myths, speak of the Sleeper—a being that is simultaneously one and many, whose breathing creates the rhythm the pumping organs follow, whose dreams are the template from which the entire city grows.

The inhabitants reproduce through contribution rather than replication. Architects who have built enough dissolve themselves into the next generation's building material, their consciousness fragmenting into thousands of new segments that carry fractional memories of their progenitor. Curators lay crystalline eggs in the building walls, where they absorb years of encoded experiences before hatching with inherited knowledge. Swimmers release clouds of gametes during the strongest rains, their offspring beginning life as microscopic participants in the water cycle itself, some eventually settling and growing, others remaining perpetually dissolved in the endless precipitation.

Social organization follows the architecture's logic—fluid, responsive, constantly restructuring. Leadership is temporary and functional: an Architect colony might direct construction for days before dissolving, its segments joining other projects. Curators form loose guilds based on the districts they maintain, but membership shifts as buildings reshape themselves. Swimmers school and scatter according to dream-currents that no land-dweller fully understands.

They have no conception of ownership—how could they, when the city digests and rebuilds itself continuously? Instead, they practice a form of temporal territory, claiming spaces for specific durations before moving on. Conflicts, when they arise, are resolved through competitive building: opposing groups construct increasingly elaborate structures until one design proves so superior that it's adopted universally, its creators dissolving into the satisfied exhaustion of victory.

Their art is indistinguishable from their architecture. Every building is a song in calcium and chitin, every passage a verse in the city's endless poem. They compose in flavors and pheromones, painting with bioluminescent bacteria that they cultivate in specialized organs. The greatest artists among them are those who can influence the city's growth patterns most subtly, creating beautiful inefficiencies that serve no purpose but wonder—spiral chambers that sing in the rain, surfaces that create rainbow interference patterns in the perpetual wetness, passages that preserve the echo of every sound that passes through them.

The inhabitants worship nothing and everything. They recognize the city as divine but understand themselves to be part of its divinity. Their rituals are metabolic—great feasts where they consume carefully prepared sections of architecture, digesting the memories encoded within, becoming temporarily more than themselves through the accumulated experiences of centuries. They mark time not in days or years but in cycles of growth and dissolution, measuring their lives in the number of times they've contributed to and emerged from the city's substance.

Death, for them, is merely deep participation. The Architects know their segments will dissolve and reconstitute. The Curators trust their memories to the walls they've tended. The Swimmers understand that they never truly leave the water that cycles endlessly through the terraces. They fear only true stillness—the death that would come if the rain ever stopped, if the city ceased its growth, if the great metabolic engine that is their world ever fell silent.

In the rare moments when the rain lightens to a whisper and the city's groaning quiets to a hum, the inhabitants pause in their endless labor and listen. In that near-silence, they hear something else—a deeper rhythm, a pattern that underlies all their activity. Some say it's the heartbeat of the Sleeper. Others claim it's the echo of the creature the city was built from, still somehow alive despite being the ground they walk on. The wisest among them suggest it's the sound of their own collective existence, the symphony of consciousness that emerges from their unwitting collaboration, the voice of something new being born from the merger of organic and architectural, individual and collective, life and landscape.

They continue building, tending, swimming, not toward any goal but as an expression of existence itself. The city grows beneath them, around them, through them. The rain falls warm and eternal, tasting of copper and salt and time. And in the spaces between the terraces, where water pools and life churns in its most primitive forms, new variations constantly emerge, ready to join the endless experiment of living architecture that has consumed their world.
