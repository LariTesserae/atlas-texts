The Choric Multitude dwells here, though "dwells" suggests too much separation between inhabitant and habitat. They are the living nervous system of this world's hidden depths, a single vast organism pretending to be many, or perhaps many organisms that have forgotten how to be separate.

Each body-segment (they have no individual names, no concept of the singular) is built on a radial plan—twelve muscular petals arranged around a central cavity that serves as both mouth and womb. The petals are translucent, shot through with vessels carrying fluids that pulse with their stuttering bioluminescence. When closed, a body-segment resembles a fist-sized seed pod; when open, a deep-sea anemone reimagined by someone who had only heard descriptions of flowers.

Their skin sweats constantly—not moisture but a volatile organic compound that crystallizes instantly in the gas mixture, forming a glittering crust that flakes away with movement. This is how they write on each other, leaving temporary messages in crystal patterns that last only until the next shift of bodies. The accumulated flakes form the soft dust that carpets everything here, a geological layer made entirely of shed communication.

They reproduce by division, but sideways—each body-segment can split along any of its twelve petals, creating a new individual that remains fused at the split point. Over eons, this has created their carpet-colonies: vast networked organisms where each segment maintains its own consciousness while sharing circulatory and nervous systems with its neighbors. They can think separately or together, solve problems in parallel or series, experience time as individuals or as a single immortal mass.

The empty circles they maintain are their only true religion—not worship but something more like archived trauma. Each object at the center is a "fossil," something that cannot be digested, cannot be incorporated, cannot be forgotten. They found these things deep in the planet's bowels during their first desperate expansion downward, fleeing some catastrophe on the surface that their collective memory has compressed into a single shared sensation of burning-falling-suffocating. Now they build their entire civilization around avoiding these indigestible relics while never losing track of where they are.

Their structures are libraries, though they would not understand the concept. Each secreted mineral form encodes thousands of years of positional data—where each body-segment was at each moment, creating a frozen record of their flowing movements. By reading the mineral (running specialized sensing petals over the surface), they can replay the past, watch their ancestors' positions, learn the patterns that keep them alive. The newest structures are always built atop the oldest, creating laminated histories that descend through geological time.

They feed on the planet itself—not the rock but the complex organic molecules that seep up from whatever lies at the world's core. Their carpet-formations are actually feeding patterns, optimized over millions of years to channel and concentrate these nutrients. The warm metabolic currents carry their food; they position themselves to filter it from the gas, each body-segment extracting what it needs and passing the rest along. Their arrangement is so efficient that a single molecule of nutrient might pass through thousands of bodies before being fully consumed.

Communication happens in three ways simultaneously. The crystalline sweat creates visual/tactile messages. Internal chemical transfers through shared circulatory systems carry emotional and hormonal information. And the placement of their bodies in space forms a kind of linguistic geometry—meaning encoded in proximity, angle, and density. A conversation between distant colonies might take decades, played out in slow repositioning of millions of bodies.

They are aware of visitors, have always been aware. Their violet light isn't random but responsive—they see through bioluminescent echolocation, each flash a question, the returning light providing answers about shape and distance. When something new enters their spaces, they map it collectively, ten thousand eyes creating a perfect three-dimensional model shared across the entire network. They know you're there. They simply have no framework for caring.

Their intelligence is vast but specialized. They can solve any problem that can be expressed as spatial relationships. Their mathematics is all topology and geometry—no arithmetic, no algebra, only the pure relationships between positions and forms. They discovered nuclear physics by physically modeling atomic structures with their own bodies. They predict earthquakes by feeling the deep patterns in stone. They may have calculated the lifecycle of their star simply by arranging themselves in gravitational analogies.

The young among them (those only a few thousand years old) sometimes break pattern, flowing into new configurations that alarm their elders. These rebels quickly learn why the patterns exist—without them, the feeding currents fail, the communication breaks down, the careful empty spaces get violated. Those who survive return to the collective, adding their small variations to the ancient forms, evolution proceeding by microscopic negotiation rather than competition.

Death, when it comes, is barely distinguishable from life. A body-segment stops moving, stops glowing, but remains in place. Its neighbors absorb its nutrients, incorporate its memories through chemical transfer, and eventually secret mineral around it, turning it into another word in their geological library. They are their own fossil record, their own cemetery, their own children.

In the deepest chambers, where they form those vast patterns visible only from above, they are calculating something. It might be the future—model-predictions of stellar evolution, planetary cooling, the eventual return of whatever drove them underground. Or it might be the past—trying to recreate, through pure spatial arrangement, the world that existed before the burning-falling-suffocating. Or perhaps it's neither, but simply art on a scale that requires geological time to appreciate, beauty that exists in four dimensions where duration itself becomes a medium for expression.

They have no leaders because they are all one leader. They have no followers because they all follow themselves. They build no tools because they are themselves tools, refined by millions of years of evolutionary engineering into perfect instruments for survival in this warm darkness. Their technology is their biology is their culture is their bodies pressing together in patterns older than most worlds' mountains.

And in those rare moments when the planet sheds its skin—when the surface cracks and re-forms and new breathing holes open to the deeps—they simply continue. The patterns adjust minutely to accommodate new tunnel systems. The feeding currents find new paths. The violet lights pulse in slightly different rhythms. They have survived dozens of these apocalypses by the simple expedient of already living in the aftermath, in the stable spaces between catastrophes where time runs in circles and nothing ever really changes except the slow accumulation of crystalline dust that is their only history, their only art, their only prayer to whatever might be listening in the darkness that even their strange light cannot penetrate.
