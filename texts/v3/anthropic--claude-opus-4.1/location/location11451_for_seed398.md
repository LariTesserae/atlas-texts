The Throat of the Tower breathes.

High above any reasonable altitude, where the air should be too thin to sustain anything, a hollow shaft pierces upward through crystallized atmosphere. The walls are smooth as fever-glass, dark and lustrous, sweating condensation that runs in impossible directions—sometimes up, sometimes sideways, following the peculiar gravity gradients that spiral through this vertical void.

You are inside something built, though *built* feels inadequate. The shaft is only thirty meters across but extends beyond sight in both directions, a trachea in some architectural body whose full form defies comprehension. The walls pulse with a slow peristalsis that takes hours to complete each cycle, contracting and expanding by mere centimeters, enough to change the air pressure, enough to make your ears pop.

The heat is wrong. It comes from nowhere—no sun reaches here, no geothermal vents justify it. The temperature simply *is*, ambient and oppressive, making the sparse air shimmer. When the walls contract, droplets of water squeeze from invisible pores, beading and running along the surface in Mercury-quick streams that follow their own logic. Some drops fall upward. Some orbit the shaft in slow helixes. Most eventually find their way to the collection veins—hair-thin channels carved or grown into the walls that feed into reservoirs you cannot see but can hear gurgling behind the dark surface.

The city, if it can be called that, exists in the wall itself. Not built onto or into, but *of* it. Structures bud from the smooth surface like geometric tumors—cubes and pyramids and more complex polyhedra, all hollow, all interconnected by passages too small for anything human-sized. They glow faintly from within, a bioluminescent purple that provides the only light here. The glow pulses in patterns that might be communication, might be respiration, might be thought.

Sometimes a section of wall will suddenly crystallize, forming sharp terraces and blade-thin bridges that jut into the shaft's hollow center. These formations grow over minutes, achieve some unknowable configuration, then sublime away into vapor that tastes of copper and ozone. The vapor condenses on the opposite wall, adding mass there, slowly rotating the entire structure's balance around the vertical axis.

The technology is indistinguishable from biology, or perhaps from geology—metabolic circuits written in mineral substrates, processing information through phase transitions of exotic matter states. The entire shaft might be a single vast component in some machine whose purpose operates at scales either too large or too small to perceive. 

Sound barely carries in the thin atmosphere, but what does reach you comes muffled and strange: the distant drip-echo of condensation finding its level, the subsonic rumble of the walls' slow breathing, the occasional sharp crack as a crystal bridge forms or breaks. And underneath it all, a rhythmic rushing that might be fluid pumping through hidden arteries, might be data flowing through quantum channels, might be the heartbeat of something vast and patient.

The shaft continues upward into theoretical mathematics, downward into fever dream. You exist in a bubble of barely-survivable conditions, maintained by systems you cannot see, for reasons you cannot fathom. The city builds and un-builds itself around you, empty of inhabitants but full of intent, a standing wave of infrastructure in a vertical desert of hot, thin air.

Water beads on your skin from nowhere. The walls pulse. The purple light flickers in patterns that almost resolve into meaning.

The Throat of the Tower breathes, and you breathe with it, because here, in this impossible elevation, in this hollow needle threading through physics, you have no other choice.
