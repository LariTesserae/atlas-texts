## The Churning Galleries

Deep beneath the surface, where the earth's crust thins and raw geology still writhes in its birth-pangs, vast cavern systems pulse with impossible life. These are not the quiet caves of limestone and time, but active wounds in the planet's skin—galleries of basalt and obsidian that crack and reform daily, hourly, sometimes by the minute.

The heat rises from below in waves that make the air shimmer and dance. Not quite volcanic, but warm enough that condensation forms constantly on the cooler upper surfaces of the caverns, creating a perpetual cycle of moisture. Water beads and runs down walls of fresh black glass, collecting in temporary pools that steam and evaporate, only to reform elsewhere as the stone itself shifts. The droplets catch what light filters down through fissures above—a normal daylight that seems alien in this embryonic underworld.

But it is the fauna that transforms these spaces from mere geological curiosity into something approaching madness.

They move in rivers, in torrents, in living avalanches of chitin and soft flesh. Creatures that should not exist in such numbers, should not exist at all in this plantless realm, yet here they surge in densities that defy reason. Millipede-analogues the length of your arm travel in chains of hundreds, their legs creating a constant rattling susurrus against the volcanic glass. Things like translucent cave fish swim through the air itself, feeding on the mineral vapors, their bodies packed so densely in some passages that they form living, writhing walls.

The creatures feed on the rock itself—or rather, on something within it, some quality of newly-formed stone that dissipates within days of its creation. They must constantly follow the geological turnover, racing to fresh surfaces as they emerge, abandoning yesterday's feeding grounds as new cracks open elsewhere. This creates a perpetual migration, a churning that matches the instability of the galleries themselves.

Where the stone has cooled enough, scattered structures appear—not ruins, but active constructions. Simple windbreaks of stacked obsidian. Crude funnels carved to channel the condensation. Marks on walls that might be territorial, might be counting, might be something else entirely. Tools of volcanic glass lie abandoned or cached: scrapers, points, things that might be primitive art or might serve purposes unseen. Whether these are made by some subset of the swarming fauna or by something else that moves unseen among them remains unclear.

The sound is constant and overwhelming—not quite a roar but an endless layered cacophony. The scuttling of uncountable legs. The crack of cooling stone. The hiss of steam. The whistling of creatures through narrow passages. Occasional booms as chambers collapse or reform. And underneath it all, a deep thrumming that might be the planet's own circulation, the peristalsis of these living caves.

In some chambers, the fauna density reaches impossible peaks. Creatures climb over each other in mountains of moving bodies, seeking something—heat sources, mineral veins, newly opened passages. These writhing pyramids collapse and reform, collapse and reform, creating their own weather systems of disturbed air and scattered moisture. Individual organisms become meaningless; only the mass behavior matters, patterns that emerge and dissipate like thoughts in a dreaming mind.

The light that penetrates from above through cracks and fumaroles creates scattered pools of normalcy—places where you can see clearly the madness of this ecosystem. But these illuminated spaces are temporary. The cracks seal or widen. New ones open elsewhere. The light chases the geology, and the fauna chases both, and the crude structures appear and are abandoned in cycles that might span hours or generations.

This is not a dead world beneath the surface, nor a dying one, but one being born constantly, violently, with a fecundity that belongs more to the ocean's depths than to stone and darkness. Every surface teams with life. Every new crack is immediately colonized. Every drop of condensation is contested. The galleries expand and contract like breathing, and with each breath, the living avalanche adjusts, flows, continues its endless, deafening circulation through the arteries of an infant landscape that may never mature, caught forever in the trauma of its own becoming.
