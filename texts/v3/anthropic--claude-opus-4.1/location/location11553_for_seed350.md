The drowned factory floor stretches beneath forty meters of blood-warm water, its assembly lines still running in the green-black depths. Nothing here makes the kind of sense it should.

The machines continue their work—great stamping presses compress water into water, conveyor belts carry schools of nothing between stations where robotic arms perform precise operations on absence. The industrial rhythm thunders through the liquid medium: a bone-deep thrumming that makes your organs resonate wrong, punctuated by metallic shrieks that travel too far, too fast, bending around corners that don't exist. The sound has texture here, visible as ribbons of distortion that spiral through the water like eels made of migraine.

Vegetation grows in impossible abundance—kelp forests erupting from ventilation shafts, their fronds the color of copper oxidation and petroleum rainbows. Vines thick as bridge cables spiral up through the flooded space, breaking apart into fractal branches that somehow occupy the same coordinates without touching. They grow so fast you can watch them move, sprouting leaves that photosynthesize darkness, fruiting with objects that shouldn't be organic—brass gears that pulse like hearts, glass bulbs that bud and bloom into more glass bulbs, recursive and wrong.

The water itself can't decide what it is. In places it's lighter than air, forming inverse waterfalls that cascade upward into hovering pools. Elsewhere it's so dense that light bends around it, creating pockets of twisted space where machinery folds through itself, where conveyor belts become Möbius strips carrying their cargo through dimensions that this place borrowed from somewhere else. The warmth is inconsistent—tropical near the floor, arctic in wandering patches, scalding where the phantom boilers continue to burn nothing into steam that's still somehow liquid.

The factory extends beyond sight in directions that multiply when you're not watching. Catwalks and pipes create a three-dimensional maze that reorganizes itself with mechanical precision every seventeen minutes—you can hear it happening, the groaning reconfiguration, the splash of displaced water, the scream of metal teaching itself new geometries. Support pillars grow like trees, sprouting I-beam branches. Some of them are trees, their bark patterned with rivets, their roots tangled in power cables that spark with bioluminescent algae.

Through the murk, the scattered remnants of human presence persist in loops of purpose. A control room, its windows blown out, computers still displaying readouts in languages that evolve as you watch—English becoming circuit diagrams becoming flowering script becoming emergency warnings in colors that shouldn't exist underwater. A cafeteria where the tables have become reef structures, lunch trays still circulating on underwater currents that follow the ghost of old ventilation patterns. Safety equipment mounted on walls—life preservers that have sprouted gills, emergency showers that run continuously, adding water to water to water.

The turbulence never stops. Currents fight each other, creating whirlpools that spin both ways simultaneously. Bubbles of air drift past, filled with more water. Schools of bolts swim through the maze of machinery, following migration patterns that correspond to the factory's original shift changes. The vegetation surges and retreats like tides running on industrial time—growth spurts every thirty seconds, die-backs every minute, endless renewal that feeds on rust and oil and the electromagnetic fields that shouldn't exist here but do.

In the dim green light that filters from nowhere—not from above, where there is only more factory, more water, more impossibility—shadows move independently of their sources. The silhouettes of workers who were never here clock in for shifts that never end, their movements traced in stirred sediment and the brief alignment of floating debris. The shadows operate machines that cast their own shadows, which operate other machines, recursive and endless.

The loudest sound is the factory's heartbeat—a deep pneumatic pulse that comes from everywhere at once, so powerful it creates standing waves in the water, geometric patterns of force that reshape the vegetation into industrial topiary. Between beats, you hear the others: the choir of pressure valves releasing nothing under infinite pressure, the symphony of pipes singing their stress fractures, the white noise of a trillion tiny mechanical processes that continue despite having no reason to exist.

Sometimes the water remembers it's supposed to be air, and for moments that last too long, you can see the factory as it was—dry, functional, rational. But these glimpses make less sense than the drowned version, because in those moments you realize the machinery is manufacturing the water itself, that this flood is not an accident but a product, endlessly produced, quality-tested, shipped to nowhere through pipes that dive into dimensions where water is something else entirely.

The factory grows. Not larger, but more itself. Denser with purpose that has evolved beyond its original design, each machine teaching the next a new way to be wrong, each flowering vine learning from the assembly lines how to mass-produce impossibility, everything here locked in a state of constant industrial metamorphosis, too loud to think, too real to deny, too wrong to ever make right.
