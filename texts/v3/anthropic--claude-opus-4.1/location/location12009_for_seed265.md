The black water seeps upward through fresh volcanic glass, finding every hairline fracture in the obsidian plains that stretch for miles beneath the starless void. This is new land, barely cooled, its sharp ridges and knife-edge formations still singing with the memory of their violent birth. The darkness here is absolute—not merely the absence of light but something thicker, a medium that swallows photons before they can travel more than a few meters from their source.

Industrial ruins punctuate the glassy expanse at irregular intervals: a corroded turbine housing emerging from a pool of condensation, its metal flowering with oxide patterns that shouldn't exist in these chemical conditions. A line of transmission towers marches into the wet darkness, their cables long since dissolved but their steel bones remaining, each one slightly wrong—too many crossbeams, angles that hurt to contemplate, shadows that fall upward into the black air.

The warmth rises from below, not from any sun. It carries moisture that condenses on every surface, beading and running in streams that flow in spirals, sometimes reversing course mid-channel. The water collects in perfectly circular pools that dot the landscape, each one exactly 7.3 meters across, their surfaces mirror-black and trembling with subsurface movement. Nothing should live here, yet the fauna thrives—pale, elongated things with too many joints sliding between the glass formations, their bodies generating their own bioluminescence in organs that pulse and fade in patterns that make the darkness itself seem to breathe.

Where vegetation manages to take hold, it grows in sparse clusters around the seeps and springs. These are not quite plants—more like crystalline structures that photosynthesize darkness itself, their translucent fronds reaching downward into the wet earth rather than up toward any sky. They tick and chime softly when the thick air moves, a sound like distant wind chimes made of teeth.

A factory complex sprawls near what might be called the horizon, though distance becomes meaningless when visibility ends so abruptly. Its smokestacks release columns of absolute black into black air, visible only as zones where the darkness moves differently. The machinery still runs, decades or centuries after abandonment, processing something drawn up from deep wells—a substance that exists simultaneously as liquid and gas, flowing through exposed pipes that branch and merge in patterns that follow no human industrial logic.

The creatures nest in the warm outflows from these facilities. Colonies of things that might once have been salamanders stretch and compress their bodies through dimensions that shouldn't exist here, their wet skin reflecting non-light, their calls too low to properly hear but felt as pressure changes in the inner ear. They feed on the condensation, or perhaps on time itself, aging backwards in their larval stages before emerging as adults that exist in several locations simultaneously.

The ground occasionally opens in fresh fissures, revealing glimpses of vast spaces below—caverns where the water runs upward in sheets along the ceiling, where defunct mining equipment hangs suspended in mid-air, oxidizing in defiance of the moisture that should accelerate its decay. Sometimes these openings seal themselves, the obsidian reforming with a sound like breaking bells.

In the most saturated zones, where the industrial structures cluster thickest and the water pools deepest, reality becomes negotiable. A corroded storage tank might contain a forest of copper pipes that extend infinitely inward. A maintenance shed's door opens onto the same shed, but aged differently, its tools arranged in patterns that spell out chemical formulas for substances that can't exist. The abundant fauna here have learned to navigate these spaces, their bodies flickering between possible states, their movements leaving trails of probability that linger in the wet air like scent.

The entire landscape pulses with a slow, geological heartbeat. Changes accumulate over months and years—a new spring emerging, a structure sinking gradually into the soft volcanic soil, the migration patterns of the non-things shifting in response to magnetic fields that point in directions perpendicular to reality. It is a place between states, where industrial decay and volcanic birth meet in permanent twilight, where life thrives on impossibility, where the darkness itself sweats and dreams.
