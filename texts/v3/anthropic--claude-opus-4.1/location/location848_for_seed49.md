The Metabolic Terraces spread endlessly beneath a bruised amber sky, a world-spanning city built from the calcified remains of something that might once have been alive. Each terrace layer sweeps for thousands of kilometers, stacked like the pages of a book written in chitin and modified calcium carbonate, their surfaces worn smooth by millennia of warm, mineral-rich rain that never stops falling.

Water sheets continuously down the terraces in thin films, creating a perpetual cascade that has worn channels and grooves into the bio-ceramic substrate. The rain tastes of copper and salt, condensing from the thick atmosphere that presses down with almost tactile weight. Between the terraces, water collects in vast shallow pools that stretch to the horizon, their surfaces disturbed by the constant drumming of precipitation and the movement of things beneath.

The city—if city is the right word—grows from itself. Buildings extrude from the terrace floors like teeth, their surfaces the same ivory-beige material as the ground, networked with tubes and cavities that pulse with bioluminescent signals. These structures pack so densely that walking between them requires turning sideways, following maze-like passages that branch and merge according to some evolutionary logic. The buildings themselves are hollow, their walls honeycombed with chambers where the fauna nest.

The creatures here are everywhere, a teeming mass of chittering, clicking life. They resemble terrestrial arthropods that have forgotten their original blueprints—centipedes with too many segments flowing like rivers through the passages, beetle-analogues the size of dogs that maintain the building-tubes with secretions from specialized glands, aerial swimmers that navigate the thick wet air on modified gill-fans. They move in organized chaos, following pheromone highways invisible to human senses, their collective movement generating a sound like a waterfall made of breaking shells.

The din is overwhelming. The rain creates a baseline roar, punctuated by the metabolic groaning of the city as it grows and restructures itself—walls crackling as they extend, floors humming with the chemistry of their own construction. The fauna add layers of ultrasonic communication that occasionally dip into audible range as metallic shrieks. Deep beneath the terraces, vast pumping organs cycle water through the city's body, their rhythm felt as much as heard, a bass note that makes the air itself vibrate.

Nothing here is static. Buildings seal passages and open new ones on timescales of hours. Entire districts liquify and reform, the fauna evacuating in coordinated swarms only to return when the architecture stabilizes into new configurations. The terraces themselves undulate slowly, like the breathing of something continental in scale. Weather systems form in the gaps between terrace levels—localized storms where the warm rain becomes scalding, micro-hurricanes that spin through the packed corridors, sending the fauna into frenzied reorganization.

The technology, if it can be called that, operates through controlled decomposition and regrowth. The city digests parts of itself to fuel expansion elsewhere. Specialized organisms—or perhaps they're machines—crawl through the building walls, their transparent bodies revealing intricate internal geometries that shouldn't exist in three dimensions. They leave trails of modified matter that exhibit properties outside conventional physics: surfaces that are selectively permeable to certain wavelengths of sound, chambers that maintain different gravitational orientations, passages that are longer on the inside than their external dimensions suggest.

At night—though the distinction is subtle in the constant rain—the bioluminescence intensifies. The city's metabolism accelerates, and light pulses through the building walls in patterns that might be communication, computation, or dreaming. The fauna respond to these signals, their movements becoming more coordinated, forming temporary superorganisms that achieve purposes unknowable before dissolving back into component parts.

The warm rain never stops. It has fallen here for geological ages, weathering the terraces into their current forms, feeding the pools that serve as nurseries for new architectural growth. Sometimes the rain carries spores or eggs or computational seeds that take root in the building surfaces, sprouting new forms that are quickly integrated or dissolved by the city's immune responses. The water itself might be the medium through which the entire landscape thinks, each droplet carrying information between far-flung districts, the sound of rain encoding the city's endless monologue with itself.

This is a place that has grown beyond the distinction between natural and artificial, where civilization and ecosystem have merged into something that maintains itself through constant transformation, a vast metabolic engine that covers continents, perhaps the entire planet, speaking in frequencies that bones interpret as vibration and meaning remains just beyond reach.
