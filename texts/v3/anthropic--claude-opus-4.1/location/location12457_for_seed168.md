The Synthesis Plains stretch endlessly beneath a butterscotch sky, their surface a living contradiction that defies easy understanding. What appears from a distance to be vegetation reveals itself as something far stranger — millions upon millions of crystalline growths that emerge from the substrate like ferns made of copper wire and silicon. These formations branch and rebranch in perfect mathematical spirals, their surfaces oxidizing in real-time to create gradients of verdigris and rust that shift imperceptibly across decades.

The ground itself is still becoming. Raw mineral substrate — not quite rock, not quite soil — spreads in vast terraces that step up toward distant mesas. The material has the texture of compressed ash mixed with metal shavings, warm to the touch from some deep geological process that has only just begun. In places, the surface buckles and splits, revealing seams of iridescent compounds that sublime directly into the atmosphere, leaving behind delicate lattices of metallic foam.

The "vegetation" grows directly from these thermal veins, each crystalline frond drawing materials up through hairline fractures in the substrate. They cluster most densely along the ridge lines where the warm updrafts are strongest, creating forests of glittering copper-green that ring like wind chimes in the faintest breeze — though here the air barely moves, and the only sounds are the occasional ping of crystal growth and the subsonic hum of the ground's slow self-assembly.

Industrial processes happen at a geological pace here. The crystalline growths are factories in themselves, processing raw elements from below through something like photosynthesis but using electromagnetic radiation rather than water. Their waste products accumulate as prismatic dust that drifts into the valleys between terraces, forming dunes of metallic sand that shimmer with their own faint bioluminescence.

The emptiness is profound. Between each cluster of crystal-plants, vast expanses of bare substrate stretch for kilometers. The space feels hollow, as if this world exists within some enormous cavity, though the sky extends normally overhead. Perhaps it's the way sound dies so quickly here, swallowed by the porous ground, or how the horizon seems both infinitely far and strangely close, the terraced landscape creating false edges everywhere.

No roads cross this place, no structures interrupt the geometric perfection of the crystal forests. Yet the presence of technology is undeniable — in the precise angles of growth, in the electromagnetic fields that pulse through the substrate, in the way certain formations align themselves to invisible beacons. This is infrastructure that builds itself, industry that grows rather than being constructed, automation so perfect it requires no maintenance or operators.

The light comes filtered through perpetual high clouds of metallic vapor, creating a diffuse golden glow that casts no hard shadows. The crystal growths catch and refract this light, breaking it into spectra that paint rainbow caustics across the dusty ground. At what passes for noon here, the entire landscape seems to glow from within, each formation becoming a prism that transforms simple sunlight into something altogether more complex.

Time moves strangely in this place. The crystal forests grow at rates measured in millimeters per year, yet their growth is constant, never pausing, never accelerating. The substrate continues its slow process of differentiation, sorting itself into distinct mineral layers that will, perhaps in another thousand years, become something more like soil. The metallic dunes creep across the valleys at speeds too slow for any observer to detect, yet fast enough that the landscape would be unrecognizable after a human lifetime.

This is a world catching itself in the act of becoming, where the boundary between the geological and biological has dissolved into something entirely new. An industrial ecology that operates without workers, without creatures, without even proper plants — just the patient transformation of raw elements into increasingly complex forms, following some pattern or purpose that remains beautifully, mysteriously opaque.
