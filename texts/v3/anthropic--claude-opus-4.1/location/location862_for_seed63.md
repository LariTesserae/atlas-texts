The Breathing Gallery rises like a forgotten organ loft suspended in perpetual twilight, its floor a latticework of calcified tubes no wider than a cathedral nave. Here, darkness presses close and thick as wool, broken only by the faintest bioluminescent traces that streak across chitinous surfaces when disturbed.

The heat radiates not from above but from below, seeping up through the ossified framework in waves that make the air shimmer even in near-total darkness. This elevated platform of bone-like struts was formed over millennia by something's patient secretions — whether geological or biological remains forever unclear. The tubes branch and merge in patterns that follow no earthly mathematics, creating a maze of interconnected chambers and passages, each packed so densely with life that empty space has become theoretical.

They move as one organism despite being millions. Soft-bodied creatures the size of thumbs carpet every surface in writhing layers, their translucent flesh revealing darker organs pulsing within. They taste the heat through specialized patches along their segments, navigating by thermal gradient alone. Where they cluster thickest, the temperature soars, their metabolisms generating furnace-conditions that would char any vegetation if any could grow here. They feed on each other and on the mysterious exhalations that rise from cracks in the tube-work — sulfurous streams that provide the only chemistry needed for their alien biology.

The creatures have evolved past the need for water entirely. Their bodies process energy through crystalline structures that form and dissolve in endless cycles, creating soft clicking sounds like distant rain on glass — the only noise in this muffled world. Occasionally, a section of the colony will surge, ten thousand bodies moving as a wave across the curved walls, pursuing or fleeing something imperceptible to any other form of life.

Ancient markers suggest something once built here, or at least tried to. Smooth depressions worn into the harder sections might have been anchoring points. Geometric scratches that could be tallies or language appear in places the colony rarely reaches. A single metallic node, fused into a junction of tubes, hums at a frequency below hearing but sets the creatures into agitated spirals when they pass near it.

The scale deceives — what seems containable at first reveals itself as a labyrinth extending beyond exploration. The tubework continues outward and upward into the pressing dark, each chamber packed with the same writhing biomass, the same heat, the same soft clicking of crystalline metabolism. The boundary between creature and architecture blurs where their secretions have built upon the original structure, adding their own blind galleries to this elevated maze.

Sometimes, rarely, a section will hollow out spontaneously — millions of creatures fleeing some invisible disturbance, leaving behind a perfectly empty chamber that glows with residual heat. These spaces fill again within hours, the colony flowing back like living mercury, as if empty space itself were the intolerable anomaly in this packed world of soft bodies and darkness.

The air tastes of copper and ozone. Navigation requires learning the thermal patterns, the subtle variations in the clicking chorus, the way certain sections pulse with their own tidal rhythms. This is a place that exists entirely in touch and temperature, where sight would be useless even if light could penetrate the living carpet that coats every surface, where the distinction between landscape and inhabitant has long since dissolved into something altogether more unsettling — a geography made entirely of flesh, yet following rules older than any biology we might recognize.
