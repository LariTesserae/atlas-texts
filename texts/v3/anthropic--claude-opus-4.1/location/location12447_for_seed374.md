The city rises from thermal mud, a vast warren of fired clay towers and sodium-salt bridges spanning the steaming lowlands. Everything here was built by countless generations of something between termite and coral—creatures that secrete mineral shells in response to heat gradients, piling their calcified bodies into spires that twist upward through the perpetual mist.

The sound is overwhelming: millions of builder-things clicking and scraping as they work, their movements amplified by the hollow chambers they've created. The structures themselves drum and whistle as steam vents through purpose-built channels—a deafening architecture that breathes. Water seeps constantly through the porous walls, creating a slick film over everything. The builders incorporate this flow into their designs: aqueducts of hardened saliva, cisterns lined with their own shed carapaces, fountains that gurgle with sulfurous runoff.

No plants grow here—the soil is too young, too hot, still cooking from below. The ground itself seems unfinished, a landscape of mineral deposits barely consolidated into form. Bubbling pools mark where the earth hasn't quite decided what it wants to be. The only life is animal: the builders, of course, but also clouds of moisture-drinking flies, populations of blind fish in the thermal pools, colonies of filter-feeding worms that extend their bodies from cracks in the clay walls.

The settlement stretches across perhaps three miles of this wet, warm depression. Steam limits visibility, but you can hear the city extending far beyond sight—that constant industrial roar of biological construction. The builders work with simple tools they've evolved or secreted: rasps on their forelimbs, acid glands for etching, the ability to regurgitate a cementing paste. They shape their city through pure repetition, millions of individuals following chemical programs laid down in the foundation.

The light struggles through the heavy air—a bronze dimness that makes everything appear to be underwater. Shadows barely form. The temperature hovers just at the edge of discomfort, warm enough that the moisture never fully condensates, creating an atmosphere like breathing through wet cloth.

Nothing changes here. The builders construct at exactly the rate their structures dissolve. Every tower that falls is rebuilt identically. The populations remain constant, the thermal vents neither growing nor shrinking. Even the cacophony maintains its precise volume, a standing wave of sound that has perhaps been steady for millennia. It's a place caught in perfect, deafening equilibrium—a city that exists in a single, eternal moment of its own becoming.
