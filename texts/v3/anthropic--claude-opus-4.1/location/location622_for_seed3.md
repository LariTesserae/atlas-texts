The city clings to the nascent mountain like a parasite on an unfinished god.

This is a place where geology hasn't quite made up its mind. The peaks thrust upward at impossible angles—not yet weathered into sense, still soft with their own becoming. Rock faces weep condensation that wasn't there moments ago, as if the stone itself is sweating from the effort of existing. These are mountains being born in fast-forward, though fast here still means centuries compressed into decades. You can hear them growing if you know how to listen: a low groaning beneath everything else, punctuated by the crack of new stone splitting from nothing.

The city should not exist at this elevation, in this state of geological uncertainty, yet here it sprawls across the unfinished slopes in terraced industrial districts. Factories anchor themselves with massive steel roots driven deep into the embryonic rock, their foundations constantly adjusted by crews of engineers who work around the clock to compensate for the mountain's slow breathing. The buildings themselves are sparse—wide boulevards of nothing separate one industrial complex from the next, as if the city planners understood that everything here needs room to shift.

Moss and strange pale vines colonize every surface with unreasonable vigor. They grow in the gaps between reality's seams, particularly thick where the factories' steam vents create pockets of warm moisture in the cool air. The vegetation has learned to photosynthesize from the dim industrial glow that permeates everything—the sky here forgot how to be properly bright sometime during the last industrial revolution, or perhaps the one that's still happening, or the one that hasn't quite started yet. Time moves oddly here. The plants know this and don't seem to mind.

The fauna thrives in this confusion. Crowds of things that might be pigeons but have too many wings roost on the factory catwalks. In the sparse zones between buildings, herds of semi-transparent deer graze on the moss, their bodies flickering between states of matter. The abundance of life is wrong for this elevation, wrong for this temperature, wrong for the dim light, yet the creatures multiply as if following rules from some other version of this place—one that exists slightly to the left of what you can see.

The sound is constant: the industrial thrum of turbines that process something unnamed, the crack of new rock, the calls of impossible animals, the hiss of steam escaping from the mountain's pores. It builds into a loud ambient chorus that residents stop hearing after a while, though visitors often report that the silence, when the factories briefly pause, is somehow louder than the noise.

Water collects in impromptu pools where the rock hasn't decided its final shape. These pools appear and drain without pattern, following the mountain's slow metamorphosis. The city has given up on permanent plumbing, instead maintaining a network of flexible pipes that can be rerouted daily as the geology shifts. Rain comes when the mountain exhales too heavily, though sometimes it falls upward, pooling on the undersides of industrial platforms where gravity has gotten confused.

The entire settlement exists in this state of reality breakdown—not catastrophic, but persistent, like a television with bad reception that never quite loses the picture. Streets sometimes lead to their own beginnings. Buildings occasionally occupy the same space without touching. The industrial processing plants take in raw materials and produce things that don't have names yet, won't have names for another hundred years when the language finally catches up to what's being made.

At the extreme edges of the city, where the elevation becomes genuinely hostile, abandoned factories rust in the thin air. But even abandonment is uncertain here—workers sometimes clock in at these empty buildings, maintaining machines that won't be installed for decades. They work their shifts in the slow time of the mountain's becoming, patient as geology, while the city below continues its sparse, impossible sprawl across slopes that are still deciding what shape they want to be.

The moss grows thicker every year. The animals grow stranger. The mountain continues its slow birth, and the city adjusts its foundations, replots its streets, and carries on manufacturing things for a world that might exist tomorrow, or yesterday, or never quite at all.
