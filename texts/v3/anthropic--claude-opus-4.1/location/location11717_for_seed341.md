The condensation district thrums in perpetual twilight, where steam from the foundry pools rises to meet the canopy of rubber trees and strangler vines that have overtaken the abandoned upper works. This is a pocket of the old industrial quarter, perhaps three city blocks in footprint, where the heat exchangers still cycle through their ancient programs despite the evacuation decades past.

Water beads and runs everywhere—trickling down the glossy leaves that press against cracked factory windows, pooling in the worn grooves of loading platforms, dripping steadily from the elevated tramways whose rails have gone green with moss. The air itself seems to sweat. Each surface weeps with moisture that never quite evaporates in the oppressive heat, creating a constant percussion of drops hitting metal, drops hitting leaves, drops hitting the filmed-over retention pools where the old cooling systems once drew their supply.

The vegetation has learned to love the industrial heat. Orchids with fleshy, thermophilic petals cluster around still-warm exhaust vents. Aerial roots dangle from the canopy like industrial cables, some having grown through and around the actual cables until it's impossible to distinguish infrastructure from organism. Giant ferns unfurl from cracks in the concrete, their fronds brushing against the sides of towering condensation columns that still belch steam on a twenty-minute cycle, though no one remembers what process they're cooling.

The sound is constant—not just the dripping but the deep mechanical breathing of the exchangers, the hiss of pressure valves releasing, the gurgle of water through pipes both metal and biological. Sometimes there's a grinding as an old turbine attempts to turn, catches on vine growth, stutters to a halt. The rubber trees' leaves amplify everything with their broad surfaces, creating an echo chamber effect that makes the whole district feel like the inside of some vast, breathing organ.

Strange fungi bloom in the heat, their caps iridescent and wet, pulsing faintly with bioluminescence that provides the only light besides the dim glow filtering through the canopy and the occasional still-functioning industrial bulb, its yellow gleam barely penetrating the humid haze. These fungi have developed along the thermal gradients, forming perfect rings around heat sources, their mycelia possibly interfacing with the old electrical systems in ways that no one has studied.

The sparse layout of the remaining structures creates cathedral-like spaces between them—open courtyards where the mist hangs so thick it becomes architectural, defining rooms and passages that shift with the thermal currents. A single smokestack rises from this low sprawl like a lighthouse, its top lost in the vine-shroud, still venting something that makes the air shimmer and smell of copper and ozone.

No animals move here—the heat and the chemical residues in the water saw to that long ago. But the boundary between plant and machine has grown uncertain. Some of the vines pulse with a regularity that matches the industrial rhythms. Root systems follow the old pipe networks with suspicious accuracy. The entire district seems caught in a fever dream of growth and decay, where the industrial processes continue their mindless cycles while the vegetation slowly digests the architecture, both systems locked in a humid embrace that generates its own weather—brief torrential downpours when the saturation peaks, followed by rising steam that starts the cycle anew.

At the heart of it all, barely visible through the layers of growth and mist, the main processing facility still runs its program—boilers heating, condensers cooling, fluids moving through a circulatory system that now feeds both its original mechanical purpose and the strange hybrid ecosystem that has claimed it. The whole district breathes with wet, mechanical life, neither fully alive nor properly dead, existing in its own twilight state of perpetual, sweltering transformation.
