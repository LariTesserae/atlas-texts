You notice them only after you realize what *you* are doing to the room.

Your heavy wool coat does not vibrate. Your leather boots, your chilled knuckles, the thick fabric strapped across your shoulders—none of them belong to the choir. Wherever you stand, you punch a blunt, ragged hole in the cathedral chord. Falling droplets brush your sleeve, lose the hum for a heartbeat, and snap into white needles of ice with a soft, constant *tick-tick-tick* that patters down your chest and drops into the mossy pools around your ankles like tiny hail.

Then, ten paces down a sodden aisle of ribbed fronds, the chord shifts to answer you.

It is not the blind, cavernous foghorn of a choir-climber, nor the shivering hiss of foliage adjusting to a draft. It is a narrow, nimble counterpoint—a slender thread of middle resonance that bends smoothly around the bruise you have made in the air, patching the tear so cleanly that the droplets hovering a foot from your shoulder stay clear and obediently liquid.

When you turn toward the sound, you think at first you are looking at a pale, upright stalk of aerial roots tangled in hanging lichen. Then the stalk tilts its head.

There is no hurry in the motion, yet it is nothing like the glacial crawl of the beasts clinging to the canopy. A choir-climber takes a quarter-hour to shift a single hooked foot; this figure moves at the deliberate, weighted tempo of someone wading chest-deep through heavy oil, every gesture shaped to slip cleanly *along* the standing waves rather than fight them. It steps free of the dripping curtain of foliage and stands in the steel-blue gloom, watching you.

It is taller than a man—perhaps seven feet if it ever straightened its spine, though its shoulders hold a permanent, listening stoop beneath the arched ribs of the vault. Like the trunks around it, its skin is utterly devoid of age or weathering: smooth, taut, faintly luminous, slick with clear mucilage and marked along the flanks and forearms with pale vertical striations, like the stretch-marks of a frond still finishing its uncurling. Beneath that glistening skin, no red blood runs. Instead, a fine, geometric lattice of bruised violet and milky steel-blue pulses slowly through its limbs, brightening and dimming in exact phase with the vascular glow of the chamber.

Its chest is built like an instrument. The ribs do not curve inward to guard soft organs so much as arch wide and forward into a pale, fluted soundbox, spanned by wet, translucent membranes that tremble visibly in the roar. Above a slender, seamless throat—where a human jaw would open into a mouth—a narrow vertical grille of pale cartilage flexes in subtle ripples, inhaling and bending the ambient din. Its eyes are large, lidless, and unnervingly familiar in the wrong way: two wide, dark, perfectly curved droplets of water held in shallow sockets by surface tension, never blinking, gleaming with gathered twilight.

From its hunched shoulders hangs what looks at first like a soaked, tattered cloak. Look closer, and the edges are alive—broad, overlapping flaps of pale epidermal mantle fringed with hair-fine cilia that shiver furiously in the high glassy hiss of the air. Because of that living fringe, the water beading on its skin never stills; clear films of supercooled dew glide steadily *upward* along fine tracks on its forearms and cheekbones, gathering at its crown before detaching in slow, heavy tears.

You realize, with a sudden prickle beneath your wet wool, that it is not alone.

Now that your eye has learned the shape to search for, the shimmering aisles begin to yield them up. One leans at an impossible, relaxed angle against a thick vascular column six yards to your left, letting the trunk’s softened private gravity hold its weight while its long, six-fingered hands comb gently through a cluster of translucent spore-sacs. Two more stand face-to-face farther down the nave, motionless save for the faint, shimmering ripples crossing their chest-membranes. They do not try to shout over the deafening roar; they *carve* it. Standing two feet apart, they catch the cathedral chord in their hollow ribs and throw it back slightly out of phase, braiding tiny, intimate pockets of interference in the wet air between them—phantom intervals and pulsing beats that you cannot hear with your ears, only feel as a dizzying, rhythmic tug inside your skull, like someone humming directly against your teeth.

There are not many of them—perhaps a dozen, perhaps a few more, scattered across the fifty paces of the sunken vault. Yet they are everywhere the chamber is growing.

While you stand shivering in the gloom, a deep, groaning *crack* rolls around the outer perimeter, vibrating up through the soles of your boots as another finger-width of frozen permafrost surrenders to the brine-roots. For half a second, the geometry of the cathedral changes. The standing waves slip; a sharp, glassy dissonance whines through the canopy, and a sudden shower of tiny white ice-needles rains down through a dead pocket of air twenty feet away.

Three of the pale listeners are already in motion.

They do not run—running would tear the acoustic fabric worse than your coat. Instead, they step close to the massive root-buttresses, lean sideways into the trunks’ gentle gravitational pull, and *glide* in long, skimming strides above the soaked moss-mattress, barely brushing the pools below. One reaches the newly fractured alcove first. There, behind the thickest hanging curtains, a finger-wide seam of raw, jagged black slate has just split open, silent and ruthlessly cold, and a young crosier uncurling beside it has grown twisted, its vein-lattice knotted out of symmetry so that it deadens the hum around its own stem.

The tall figure pauses beside the flaw and draws a small, sodden bundle of braided moss from a crease in its hip-mantle. With agonizing, surgical grace, it peels the wet moss back from a sliver of stone—a raw, unweathered chip of dead black permafrost pried from the boundary before the glowing brine could dissolve it.

The instant the dead stone meets the humid air, it refuses to sing. Around its jagged edge, the supercooled mist collapses into solid frost with a sharp, dry *tick*, sheathing the pebble in a translucent, razor-thin blade of white ice.

Holding that impossible knife of silence, the tall keeper leans in. Its six long fingers move with a slowness that looks almost reverent, scoring away the misgrown knot along the young frond’s rib, severing a tangled filament that was dampening the chord. Green-violet sap wells briefly and seals itself without a single drop falling astray. The moment the cut is finished, the keeper folds the wet, humming moss back over the stone. Deprived of its pocket of silence, the ice-blade melts instantaneously into a clear bead of water, leaving no blade, no scrap, no dead thing behind to mar the chamber.

Beside it, a second keeper has laid its fluted chest flush against the velvet flank of a blind, hound-sized choir-climber hanging nearby. Through its vibrating ribs, it pours a slow, coaxing bass pulse into the beast’s translucent body. The climber’s spiracles yawn wide in sympathy; over three agonizingly slow minutes, it pulls itself handspan by hooked handspan along the ceiling-roots toward the raw crack, flooding the fragile new alcove with a heavy foghorn drone until the newly exposed rock is safely drowned in sound.

Meanwhile, a third kneels at the very rim of the vault, where hair-thin root-tips sweat their eerie, faintly glowing brine into the fractured stone. It presses both pale palms directly into the weeping mineral paste. You watch the bruised-violet light brighten along its fingertips, climbing its slender wrists in slow, rhythmic pulses as it drinks dissolved rock and salt straight through its skin, nourishing its bones the same way the walls nourish themselves.

At first, watching them work, you might mistake them for mere organs of the plant—mobile extensions of the seed’s impossible craft, no more possessed of a private life than the blind climbers or the crystal-combed grazers clinging to the undersides of the fronds.

Then you follow one inward, toward the hushed heart of the nave, and that illusion breaks.

Around the thickest central pillar—the smoothest, most massive vascular column in the chamber, where falling drops curve lazily inward like slow planets—hang three vast, translucent crosiers that are not fern fronds at all. They are swollen, tear-shaped sacs the height of a grown person, veined in dense, radiant steel-blue, pulsing with a slow, tidal rhythm. Inside the nearest one, curled tight in clear, luminous fluid, you can make out the folded silhouette of a new listener: knees drawn to a half-formed lute-ribcage, six needle-slender fingers tucked beneath a smooth chin, translucent mantle still furled tight along its spine like wet wings.

Two of the older figures stand motionless beside that hanging cradle. You can tell they are older not by wrinkles or wear, for nothing here decays, but by the breathtaking complexity of the violet lattices braided across their ribs, and by the faint, pale hairline marks along one’s collarbone where frost once bit too deep before melting away. They have pressed their own hollow chests directly against the slick skin of the amniotic sac. Their eyes are half-veiled with quiet drops of condensation.

They are not checking the sac for flaws. They are singing into it.

Step closer, and you feel the faint, intricate warble trembling through the moss beneath your boots—a delicate, shifting pattern far more personal than the broad, impersonal roar of the cathedral. They are teaching the unborn sibling the exact pitch of the room, yes, but woven inside that chord is something else: small, idiosyncratic bends in the harmony, little signature tremors passed from ribcage to ribcage. Before the figure inside the sac ever unfolds its limbs or opens its dark water-drop eyes, it will already know the voices of the two who waited beside its stem.

And deeper still, tucked behind a dense, overlapping screen of the huge two-faced leaves, you discover what they keep hidden even from the choir.

Here, two broad leaves have been coaxed—or painstakingly pruned—to curve inward with their dry, utterly black undersides facing one another, forming a cramped, waist-high cleft no wider than an outstretched arm. Even standing a pace outside that slit, the warmth tears out of your face so sharply your teeth ache. Inside that tiny black pocket, the heat-deleting membranes swallow not only warmth, but half the chamber’s roar, dropping the cathedral chord to a thick, dangerous hush.

It is the only place in the vault that is almost quiet. It is a place that can kill them.

Yet two of the pale listeners are crouched at its threshold, hunched low over a flat shelf of black leaf-underside where a dozen small, indigestible relics lie arranged with meticulous care—smooth pebbles of dead quartz, a fractured chip of glossy obsidian, a spiral fossil of some ancient sea-shell that the glowing brine spat out unmelted as the vault ate its way through the permafrost.

None of these stones hum. Every one of them is a tiny island of silence.

As you watch, breathless and half-frozen, one of the listeners reaches a six-fingered hand into that lethal, muffled cleft. Almost immediately, a delicate white rime begins to bloom along the wet skin of its wrist, creeping up toward its elbow like creeping lace. It does not pull back. Instead, it holds its vibrating chest-membranes rigidly still, damping its own song, and lets a single clear bead of sap gather at the tip of its longest finger and fall onto a flat, dead pebble.

*Tick.*

The drop snaps into a tiny white needle of solid ice.

A second bead swells, detaches, and strikes the tip of the first—*tick*—branching sideways. Then the other listener reaches in, its pale shoulders already dusted with fine white frost, and adds a third drop, then a fourth. For ten agonizing, hushed minutes, enduring the sting of the freeze creeping along their wet mantles, they build.

What rises on the dead stone is a hand-high sculpture of white rime—delicate as spun glass, impossibly sharp, utterly dry, and unlike anything else in the sunken cathedral. Nothing in the vault grows like this. The fronds arch inward and knit shut; the roots coil down; the mist drifts in closed, lazy circles. This tiny white lattice shoots straight *upward*, branching open and outward in fierce, clean verticals, reaching toward an emptiness that does not exist beneath thirty yards of lightless, frozen rock.

They have never seen a tree standing under wind. They have never seen a spire, a cloud, a horizon, or a sky. Yet whatever impossible craft curled itself into the seed that made this place left an ache inside its blueprints—a phantom limb of geometry—and these young, embryonic minds have felt the pull of it. Here in their forbidden little pocket of silence, risking frostbite to touch hardness and stillness, they are rehearsing for *up*. They are practicing what it might feel like to be dry, and sharp, and unenclosed.

At length, the frost reaches the base of the first listener’s smooth throat. Its water-drop eyes tighten. Gently, almost sorrowfully, both figures withdraw their hands and lean back, parting the curtain of two-faced leaves.

The deafening cathedral chord rushes back into the cleft.

The tiny white tower shudders once, loses its edges, and collapses instantaneously into a clear, heavy puddle that slides off the black leaf and vanishes into the spongy moss. The frost lacing the listeners’ arms melts in the same heartbeat. Neither looks distressed. One of them already lifts a wet palm, watching fresh supercooled beads swell along its fingers, already memorizing the angle of the next tower it will try to build.

That is when the eldest among them finds you.

You do not hear it approach over the vast, roaring chord. You only feel the sudden, subtle softening of the air at your back, as though the invisible pressure pressing against your ribs has shifted its weight. When you turn, it is standing two paces away in the shimmering violet gloom—taller than the others, its lute-like chest wide and braided with an intense, pulsing steel-blue lattice, a single pale frost-scar curving like a crescent moon beneath its fluted throat.

It has followed the trail of tiny frozen *ticks* your dead wool coat left across the moss-mattress.

You brace yourself, stiff with sub-zero cold, certain it has come to prune you out of the nave like a misgrown knot or a dead shard of slate. Instead, it glides one slow, liquid step closer—close enough that you smell the faint, clean scent of wet copper and crushed celery rising from its slick skin—and lifts one six-fingered hand.

It does not touch your wool. It stops an inch from the bare, raw skin of your cheek, right beneath the thin white plume of your breath.

Its slender fingers are trembling—not with the rhythmic shiver of the cathedral chord, but with an uneven, startled tremor you have not seen in any creature down here. You suddenly understand what you must look like to a mind born in sub-zero dark. Every animal in the vault borrows its motion from the standing waves. Every leaf drinks heat away into nothingness. And here you stand, clumsy, deafeningly quiet, blazing like a small, impossible furnace that carries its own buried sun inside its ribs.

The tall figure tilts its head, its wide, dark, water-drop eyes reflecting the faint blue glow on your freezing skin. Slowly, it draws its bare hand back toward its fluted chest.

And then it does something that takes your breath away.

Its throat-cartilage shifts into a tight, straining geometry. Its arched ribcage expands, and the translucent membranes stretched across its chest begin to vibrate with a fierce, razor-precise intensity—throwing an exact, inverted mirror of the cathedral chord outward into the narrow space between you.

Instantly, within a three-foot bubble around the two of you, the roar *dies*.

The change is so violent your knees nearly buckle. The relentless acoustic pressure vanishes from your jawbones and ribs, leaving a deep, velvety, ringing hush. For the first time since descending beneath the frozen crust, you hear the frantic hammer of your own heart, the raspy wheeze of freezing air in your throat, the wet squelch of your boots settling into the moss.

And right in front of you, you hear a soft, terrifying *crackle*.

Without the hum to hold it liquid, the clear film glazing the tall listener’s cheeks and shoulders flashes into white frost. Delicate ferns of rime race across its pale forehead, stiffen the shivering cilia along its mantle, and creep steadily inward toward its unblinking, water-drop eyes. It is letting the silence bite straight into its own living skin—paying for this tiny room of quiet with its own flesh—simply so that it can meet you inside the element you belong to.

In that fragile, freezing hush, the fluted grille beneath its jaw opens wide. It does not have lungs like yours, nor a tongue, nor lips—only wet cartilage and hollow ribs built to bend a subterranean roar. Yet it tries. It forces a slow, trembling ribbon of cold air upward through its freezing throat, mimicking the rough, warm gasp that escaped you when you first stumbled into the vault.

What comes out is a hollow, crystalline, watery syllable—like a gust of winter wind blown across the rim of an ice-rimmed bottle. Clumsy. Unmistakably deliberate. A question shaped in a language neither of you possesses.

Before the creeping white lace can reach the black surface of its eyes, it lets its chest-membranes fall slack.

The cathedral chord slams back into the space between you, deafening and wet and immense. The frost sheathing the listener’s face collapses instantly into clear, heavy water that glides *uphill* across its cheekbones and vanishes into its shimmering mantle. It holds your gaze for one long, unhurried breath—two alien kinds of patience measuring each other in the bruised-violet dark—then leans smoothly into the soft gravity of the nearest vascular column and glides backward into the dripping aisles.

It disappears ten paces away, folded cleanly back into the humming twilight. Yet as you stand there, soaked and shivering, your coat once more ticking quietly with tiny spikes of ice, you realize the chord around you has not quite returned to what it was before. Somewhere high in the dense middle thrum, woven softly between the taut cello strings and the glassy hiss, a faint new overtone lingers—hollow, breathless, and faintly rising, as if the whole blind, roaring embryo beneath the ice were quietly practicing the sound of a warm voice from the sky it has not yet reached.
