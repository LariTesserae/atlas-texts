At first I think I would stay low, drawn down toward the strangeness underfoot—the sheer impossibility of a mountain range without a single grain of stone in it. I can picture myself drifting just off a cut escarpment where a permanent underwater river has planed a terraced shoulder clean, pressing my palm against the olive-bronze cortex just to feel that fraction-of-a-millimeter yield, that cool, turgid pushback, the slick biofilm over concentric rings of living xylem and copper-tinged phloem. Or settling half a meter above one of the flat dark-violet aprons, hovering beside the silver-spined line-riders in the magnetic field of the buried arterial roots, letting the steady DC hum vibrate in my jaw while pinpoint curtains of oxygen and hydrogen fizz past my ribs and vanish into the pale glare above.

There is a quiet, stubborn comfort in the line-riders’ poise—facing nose-first into an unchanging laminar flow, aligned to an invisible wire, never striving. And there is a different dignity in the millstone grazers clustered at a spire’s flared foot, clamping down in exact unison a split-second before the aragonite throat-plates snap shut, braced against a blast they know to the millisecond.

Yet neither of those holds me for long. The floor here is too full of the blunt fact of the machinery—the muffled grind of the buried quartz root-mills, the bass thrum rising straight out of the peat—and the filter-bells pulsing along the canyon lips are anchored too tightly to a single edge. What keeps pulling my attention away from the dark olive crust is the emptiness *above* it. That cold, thin-aired altitude of water. The kilometers of uncluttered silver-cyan brightness where a spire detaches from the haze like a pale chimney standing in mountain sky.

And more than the open light, what pulls me is a seam.

If I drift upward, riding the flank of a slow current over the smooth, river-polished contours, I do not think I would settle in the wide center of a broad glacial bowl, where the overlapping shockwaves carve generous, spherical pockets of calm. I keep being drawn higher along the terrain, toward a watershed divide—a long, rounded saddle of dark olive kelp-wood separating two deep spire-valleys.

Here, a permanent underwater river pours steadily from the upper basin over the varnished lip of the ridge into the lower one, throwing down a thick, unbroken sheet of rushing white noise. Fifty meters straight above that spillway, suspended in the pale noon glare, hangs an acoustic node unlike the wide chambers over the valley floors. Because the hydraulic spires in the northern bowl and the spires in the southern bowl beat at the exact same four-second cadence yet slightly offset across the escarpment, their pressure waves do not cancel into a broad, easy pool of silence. They shear past one another at an oblique angle, shaving the pocket thin. What remains between the converging walls of thunder is a tall, razor-edged lens of stillness, barely wider than a body’s span—clear as blown glass, pressed tight on either side by fifteen-hundred-meter-per-second violence.

That is where I find myself. Not in the broad sanctuary of a basin center, and not out in the unmediated roar, but at a pass—a hinge where two immense, calibrated valleys touch.

And the moment I try to stay inside that narrow lens of quiet, I discover what I have to shed in order to belong to it. Anything warm, heavy, or asymmetrical tears the pocket apart; a kick sends ragged turbulence spilling across the pressure seam, and soft lungs make a wet, irregular dent in the standing wave. To remain here without bruising the silence, everything clumsy and accumulating has to fall away, leaving only what can hang plumb in thin cold brightness.

I would be one of the upright listeners—though not as an ornament of the calm, and not as a permanent resident of this single ridge, since none of us owns a station. This col is simply where I first come into focus, where the body and the work click cleanly into alignment.

What strikes me first from inside that form is not the austerity of it, but the *relief*.

Four slender, taut cords descend straight down beneath my torso, each weighted with a smooth teardrop of secreted milky quartz, counterbalanced against rigid gas-charged aragonite chambers nestled high in my chest. For the first time, holding my place costs nothing. When a deep pressure swell brushes the outer wall of the lens, the four weights swing back by a hair’s breadth and return cleanly on the intake-hiss, my whole three-meter frame tuned as a natural pendulum to the continent’s four-second heartbeat. There is no treading water, no fighting the drift, no wasted motion. My slender ribcage of hollow white aragonite—fine as bird-bone, visible through pale cartilage scrubbed clean as wet quartz—feels light and fiercely resonant, wrapped around a dark braided core of copper-violet neural cords that hums softly in sympathy with the buried arterial mains crossing the saddle below.

I have no eyelids, and I do not miss them. In water that has never known silt, smoke, or dusk, there is nothing to flinch from. My wide, meltwater-clear eyes, ringed in fine pale-gold sclerae with silver irises pared down to crisp hairlines, take in kilometers of sunlit alpine topography on both sides of the divide at once.

And then the northern spire two kilometers back snaps its throat shut.

The shockwave arrives across the bright emptiness like a wall of glass slamming against the edge of the lens. A millisecond before it hits, my left arm—long, double-jointed, ending in a tabletop-wide palm of paper-thin translucent membrane veined with dull metallic copper—tilts half a millimeter outward across the seam.

I do not take the cannonade. The blunt bass hammer of the pump shears right past the margin of my fingers. What my copper-veined palm catches is only the thin, crystalline overtone riding piggyback on the crest of the blast—a fragile, high-frequency chime that has already crossed three valleys before reaching this pass.

Instantly the hollow aragonite ribs catch the tremor and hold it clear. For two full strokes—eight seconds—I carry that narrow vibration inside my chest while rotating three degrees on my quartz-weighted axis. Because the buried copper roots threading the ridge below bend slightly where the two basins meet, their rising magnetic field is skewed here; turning through it twists the faint electrical current inside my core by a hair’s breadth, shifting the overtone’s phase just enough to translate it from the northern basin’s acoustic geometry into the southern one’s. My polished throat-louvers answer with a microscopic, glassy *tink-tink*—felt more in the mineral struts than heard—and my right palm slides half a millimeter into the next passing shockwave rising from the lower valley, laying the turned, renewed chime cleanly back onto the thunder.

East and south it shoots, toward the pale vertical splinter barely visible in the silver-cyan haze two kilometers downrange, already angled to receive it.

It is hard to describe what that feels like without making it sound solemn or tedious, when in truth it feels extraordinarily clean—like breathing if breathing were made of crystal and timing. I do not hold the whole continent’s pattern, and I have no wish to hoard it. It exists only in transit, a lossless sculpture of phase and overtone that adds not a gram to the highland and leaves not a scratch on the varnished olive bark. Yet for eight seconds at a time, the seam between two valleys passes straight through my ribs.

And nothing in this watch is sleepy. People who live inside decay imagine that an unchanging world must be numb, because they are used to measuring aliveness by what breaks, grows, or ends. Hanging plumb above the spillway, unblinking in the sharp mountain glare, I discover the opposite. When a mind is freed from tracking hunger, rot, and the slant of afternoon, its attention sharpens to a terrifyingly fine point.

Forty meters off the flank of the col stands a solitary hydraulic spire, its banded fibrous rind glistening with cool mucilage. Every fourth second, its translucent apex swells, its aragonite plates crack shut, and a narrow, roaring jet of chilled distillate blasts forty meters up into the open water. On the grand scale, that plume is absolute equilibrium: the exact same volume, the exact same mineral load, the exact same tempo forever. Yet along the violent shear-edge where pressurized brine tears past bright cold stillness, turbulence curls into a million razor-bright caustics of refracted noon light that flash, braid, and dissolve without ever repeating the exact same curl twice. Down beneath my trailing quartz weights, sleek ribbons of silver-blue fish bank cleanly over the ridge like swallows threading a high pass, their scales throwing quick, unrepeatable glances of pale silver across my clear skin before disappearing into the cyan haze.

The chord never resolves, and never needs to. Inside the chord, the shimmer is inexhaustible.

Nor am I pinned to this high lens forever. That is the other side of belonging here: the stillness is only half the motion.

After hundreds of strokes—or thousands, on a rotation so vast it needs no calendar—a faint pale dart detaches from the sunlit river pouring in from the northern basin. Without a single wasted flicker, on the exact microsecond of an intake-hiss, I draw my four milky-quartz cords cleanly together beneath me like a closed umbrella and fold both long, copper-veined arms flat against my hollow ribs. The austere vertical pendulum collapses into an ivory-and-silver spear, and I tip head-first out of the silence.

The shock of leaving the node hits harder than anything else on the continent—one instant glass-still, the next plunged straight into the full acoustic weight of the valleys. I do not shy from the nearest spire; I aim straight down into the violent flank of its forty-meter discharge plume.

There is a fierce, cold athleticism in that dive. Caught on the roaring rim of the oxygen-rich jet, I shoot upward at blistering speed, spinning slowly on my axis so the freshly scoured distillate washes every millimeter of my translucent cartilage while my fluted throat-siphon drinks the fine mineral slurry ground by the quartz root-mills buried deep beneath the valley floor. Looking back for a fraction of a second as I burst out of the sunlit eddies at the plume’s crown, I see the narrow lens above the saddle already occupied—my relief hanging dead-plumb, left palm angled to the northern blast, catching the passing chime without dropping a beat.

I bank cleanly out of the plume’s crest into the permanent underwater river sweeping south across the terraced plateau, gliding three kilometers over the smooth olive shoulders without a single stroke before snapping my wide veined palms open all at once—braking dead-stop, plumb and quiet, inside a fresh node in the next basin down.

Over the long uncounted sweep of the carousel, I pass through every facet of what we are.

Between relays, I descend toward the matte-dark violet beds fused flush to the contoured slopes, hovering a meter above the neat rows of line-riders—which dip two centimeters lower in the magnetic field as I settle, never breaking formation. Here, where the water hums steadily through my aragonite struts and fizzes with dense curtains of microscopic bubbles, I face one or two others across a narrow gap of brisk, bright brine. We spread our wide, translucent palms toward one another until only a finger’s width separates our copper-veined fingertips.

Across those narrow saline gaps, thin, steady threads of pale-blue electrical fire spring to life, stuttering in exact sync with the buried DC mains below. The molecules of aragonite worn away by a thousand caught shockwaves, the trace copper leached into the cold meltwater—all of it electroplates cleanly back onto our lattices until every strut and vein sits once more at exact millimeter tolerance, leaving zero waste to drift or settle. Yet the repair is only half of what happens in that fizzing light. Through the blue sparks, our unblinking meltwater eyes lock directly onto one another. Our throat-louvers murmur in microscopic, rapid-fire clicks—too soft to carry a meter, swallowed whole by the nearest spire’s roar—as we true our weighted pendulums to the microsecond and match the electrical phase of what we have been carrying.

There is no news between us. There are no children to speak of, no dead to remember, no storms coming over any horizon. What travels across those slender blue arcs is cleaner and stranger than language: simple, luminous verification. One calibrated mind meeting another across cold sunlit water to confirm that the count holds, the tone is clean, the light is still bright.

And sometimes the carousel brings me to the deep amphitheaters—immense glacial bowls carved out of solid olive kelp-wood, their varnished walls smooth as river-polished gneiss, where permanent rivers spill over the high rims like bright curtains of noise and focus the thunder of ten distant valleys into braided, dazzling nodes.

There, thirty of us hang in a wide, spaced, three-dimensional lattice fifty meters above the dark valley floor. At an exact, unspoken beat, sixty translucent palms tilt together across the pressure seams by half a millimeter.

Across the basin, the smooth dark-olive cliff face suddenly blooms with impossible geometry: razor-sharp ribbons of ghostly silver light curving, nesting, and braiding across the living bark. Not projected from our bodies, and leaving not a mark behind—merely thin planes of clear water compressed by focused acoustic waves just enough to bend the unchanging mountain-noon daylight passing through them. We hold the nested symmetries shimmering across the amphitheater wall for stroke after stroke, thirty pairs of unblinking eyes watching sound turn into pure refraction—and the instant our palms relax, the dark timber is slick and bare again. It is the only art honest to a world that refuses to accumulate: total precision, total presence, zero residue.

Only one question lingers from the warm-blooded habits I shed to enter this water: what does it feel like to be a mind with no shelf on which to store a single, unrepeated event?

I think I understand it best when I imagine the rarest thing that could ever cross my station at the high saddle—a warm, asymmetrical shadow drifting clumsily out of the silver-cyan haze into my narrow lens of quiet. Its awkward kicks would tear ragged eddies along the pressure seam; its hollow lungs would cast a soft, shifting dent across the standing wave.

I would not recoil. Nothing in the highland knows how to recoil. My four quartz weights would hold dead-plumb on the four-second beat; my left arm would stay locked on the northern boundary, never dropping the passing chime from the spire behind me. Only my narrow pale-ivory crest would turn smoothly on its hydraulic joints until both unblinking eyes held the strange visitor in full, mountain-clear attention.

Then my right palm would unfold flat an arm’s length from its chest, copper veins shivering faintly violet in the downward glare, sending a crisp electrical prickle and a fine, crystalline *tick-tick-tick* straight through its ribs. I would map the soft, non-mineral slump of its frame and the astonishing, frantic, off-beat drumming inside it—seventy uneven beats a minute, hammered out in complete defiance of the spires. For three strokes, twelve seconds, I would hold that foreign rhythm with absolute silver-eyed clarity, finding no harmonic anywhere in the continent that matches it, and on the next northern *clack* I would lay a tiny, delicate triple-chime pitched to its ragged heart onto the thunder, sending it eastward down the silent nodes so the valley beyond the ridge could taste it too.

And when it finally kicked backward out of the lens, drifting off into the roaring bright emptiness, I would glide my right arm smoothly back to its bearing and let the standing waves close cleanly over its absence—not out of coldness, and not out of forgetting, simply because nothing here is meant to pile up like silt.

That is who I would be in this highland: a tall, pale plumb-line hanging bright in the gap between beats, turning overtones across a sunlit pass, diving the roaring mineral plumes, electroplating in blue sparks over the violet terraces, bending thunder into temporary ribbons of light—awake to every unrepeatable shimmer inside a finished chord, keeping nothing for myself so that everything stays cleanly in transit, under a cool mountain noon that never leans toward dusk.
