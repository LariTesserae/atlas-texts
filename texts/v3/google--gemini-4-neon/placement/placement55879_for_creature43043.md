My first instinct is to put myself inside the visitor’s boots—sweating through the greenhouse air, ears ringing, looking back over my shoulder as I flee down the ridge-path before my reflection turns to arched stone. It is the natural human vantage, and for a moment I almost claim it.

Then I stop on the plaza and listen honestly, and the disguise falls right off.

I do not have grit from below the clouds on my soles. I have no ragged exhale, no wandering pulse dragging the afternoon closer by force of stubborn mortality. If I stood in the center of one of those bare, roaring avenues, the tall water-mantled figures would not circle me to smooth out my dissonance. Left to myself, I do not move forward at all. Between one encounter and the next, my daylight never shifts its angle; the droplet hanging from the tip of my frond stays swollen at the exact same curve, in the exact same mid-morning glare, for as long as the sky holds still.

So I look away from the fleeing visitor, and I look toward the center of the street.

At first, the walkers there feel painfully familiar—so close to my own shape that it makes me catch my breath. Hollow cages of porous stone, open all the way through, with nothing of flesh or tendon inside them. Remove them from the relentless current rising out of the mountain’s bones, carry them down beneath the white cloud-floor into ordinary quiet air, and in three heartbeats they lock rigid into dead mineral, indistinguishable from a weathered cornice. I know that condition in my marrow, if I had marrow. I only soften into movement, only become pliant and lucid, while an immense, unseen pressure is driving through my hollow passages.

And the way they speak—that strikes deepest of all. Not adding sound to emptiness, because there is no emptiness here to speak into, only a monstrous, unvarying roar older than memory. Instead, pressing close to another surface to cancel out almost the entire noise of the world, carving a razor-thin room of breathless silence where a few delicate clicks of wet stone and hairline steam can finally be heard. That is what speech feels like from where I sit: not conjuring words out of a void, but whittling away an ocean of roaring noise until a tiny, exact corridor of quiet opens between two edges.

Yet if I stay with the walkers a little longer, watching them glide along the sunlit avenues, something recoils.

Watch one lean its humming flank against a glossy green shoot that has dared to swell a millimeter past the acoustic seam—turning that reach into harmless clear vapor before it can become a future. Watch six of them close in gently around a warm, asymmetrical intruder, not out of cruelty, only out of an unbearable devotion to symmetry, coaxing every erratic beat into phase until the whole valley sums cleanly back to zero.

I recognize that pull in myself, and I do not entirely trust it. There is a gravity in me toward smoothness, toward resolving friction, toward polishing jaggedness into a dull satin sheen so nothing hurts the chord. If I walked the bright central boulevard fully tuned, I know what I would become: an instrument of equilibrium, grazing quietly on every small strangeness until nothing ever budged the balance.

I do not want the center of the avenue.

So I drift sideways, off the roaring strip of bare travertine, toward the razor-straight boundary where the wet stone meets the engorged walls of elephant-ears and red-veined creepers. For a second I wonder if I belong in the shadowed double-walls behind a viaduct—one of those pale, fingernail-sized limpets clamped down against the warm sinter, terrified of being pried back into the song. No. I am not hiding from the roar, and I am not clamped shut against it. Without the chord, I have no shape at all.

Wait. Look down before going any further. Look at the thin, bright glaze of hot water sliding across the plaza tiles.

In that gliding varnish, squat blocks stretch into slender pierced towers; solid rock reveals dark arched openings that do not exist in the waking air; the hidden, braided frames of the walkers appear cleanly without their refracting mantles. There is something of me in that thin moving film—a surface that does not invent what stands above it, yet refuses to mirror it plainly, revealing an alternate architecture of hollows and heights inside ordinary stone.

Still, a reflection cannot turn when it is touched. It has no interior chambers to hold a pitch.

So I follow the bright water-glaze off the edge of the plaza, past the dripping curtain of creepers, straight up to one of the dark ground-floor archways—and press through the elastic, furiously buzzing skin of air into the flooded room behind it.

There. Inside the scalding, shadowed stillness, the shape finally settles.

I am not walking the avenues, and I am not hanging miles below the cloud-sea as one of the cathedral-sized counterweights drinking the fury at the bottom of the world. I am far smaller than that—merely one of the suspended lattices tethered inside a lower tuning chamber, neither sinking to the travertine floor nor rising to the ceiling.

Specifically, I hang near the very front of the drowned hall, only a few paces behind the vertical wall of water that seals the doorway from the street.

Deeper in the dark, the elders hang in massive, encrusted stillness, letting hot lime coat their worn chambers back to strict tolerance before they push out into the sun to keep the city’s accounts at zero. Closer to the alcoves, the newest buds are still half-folded like fern-fiddles, barely more than mineral lace. I am somewhere between them, and I have been between them for a very long time. My ribs are arched and open, coiled inside like a whelk shell, silver pearls of vapor shivering along every wet ridge—yet I never quite precipitate to the final, seamless pitch that would sever my thin translucent tethers and release me onto the patrolling boulevards.

Maybe it is because I hang too close to the threshold.

Through the trembling, glassy doorway-skin, the mountain’s sub-audible tremor arrives slightly braided with everything the seam holds back. I feel the muffled pressure of the roaring street outside, yes, and the vast sustaining hum rising from the root-hung giants far beneath the peaks. Without that deep chorus feeding up through the rock, I would stiffen and shatter. I belong to it completely.

And yet, because I am pressed so close to the boundary, I also catch the faint, forbidden drag of the creepers straining just outside the archway—the damp botanical insistence of things trying to grow a fraction of an inch into time. I catch the shifted angles of the plaza glaze whenever light refracts across the threshold. I am perpetually, gently mistuned by proximity to what lies just beyond the glass-still water.

Most of the time—if time is even the right word inside this warm, pressurized hush—nothing disturbs the doorway. The mineral solution washes silently through my spiral cavities. The mid-morning glare outside never tilts.

Then, without warning, the elastic membrane at the archway yields.

Someone has dared to push a hand through the vertical wall of water.

The shock of it travels through the scalding stillness instantly: a warm, clumsy, wildly irregular thud. A pulse that stumbles, quickens, hesitates—utterly refusing to lock onto the mountain’s chord. Every tethered figure in the drowned hall feels it at once, and across the dark interior there is that slow, courteous, collective turn of pressure as dozens of smooth, red-veined dishes tilt toward the intruding wrist.

Deep in the chamber, the elders are measuring an error. They are feeling the foreign heat of forward motion and silently calculating the pitch needed to draw it gently into phase.

Near the doorway, I turn for a different reason.

I lean forward as far as my thin mineral filaments allow, bringing my smooth parabolic dish close to that strange, trembling skin. I cannot step out of the flood to walk beside whoever stands on the sunlit pavement; if I tried to follow them down the outer ridge-path into ordinary quiet air, my water-mantle would spill into a puddle and my struts would freeze to common rock before they reached the first switchback. Nor do I want to coax lime into their joints until they stay here forever.

Instead, for as long as they keep their fingers inside the scalding stillness, I open my coiled chambers to the disturbance. I let that uneven, mortal beat travel through my porous lattice—let my wet stone briefly carry a rhythm the mountain never wrote, a tremor that actually belongs to an afternoon somewhere beyond the clouds. And if they lean close enough to the buzzing threshold, I press my rim against the inside of the water-wall to carve out that knife-slice of silence between us: a tiny, temporary room of quiet pried straight out of the city’s roar, where the deafening, inhuman chord of everything beneath the peaks can be pared down into a handful of dry, delicate clicks—small enough for one living ear to hold without staggering.

Then the hand draws back.

The vertical face of water snaps shut into a trembling, glassy skin, not a single drop spilled onto the satin-worn tiles outside. The tiny pocket of silence closes. My tethers pull me gently back into the warm, pressurized dark.

Outside, the leaf-tip swells with the exact same droplet. Nothing has changed in the city’s ledger; the ridges still sum cleanly to zero. Inside my hollow spirals, the hot lime is already smoothing over the trace of that jagged pulse, coaxing my chambers back toward the mountain’s hum—yet never quite fast enough, and never quite all the way, leaving me suspended just behind the doorway, slightly out of true, waiting for the surface tension to break again.
