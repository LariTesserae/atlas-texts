At first, standing on the low stepped rim, I try to put on the traveler’s boots.

It is the natural assumption: that I am the outsider who walked up to the lip of this basin out of a wetter, noisier country, carrying sweat and dust and the habit of leaving tracks behind me. I try to imagine the hard *clack* of my heels on the bare silicate shelves, the dry catch in my throat as the air refuses my breath, the way my eyes would ache against the impossible overlap of a pale trunk and a slate boulder’s shadow.

Yet the moment I step off the scarp and down onto the newly fractured rock, that body refuses to stay on me.

I have no leather scuffs that persist when I forget them. I have no wet, rhythmic thump inside a chest, no old scar across a knuckle that keeps its shape without my looking at it. When I try to feel myself as a creature dragging an unwatched history through this crisp noon, the image peels away like ink beading on oiled glass. The uniform warmth does not feel eerie to me. The absence of wind does not feel like a held breath. Even the silence—that hushed, flat quiet with its faint dry *tick-shhh* running steadily underneath—feels less like emptiness than like a clean, well-swept room.

If I belong here at all, I belong to the valley’s own dry accounting.

Still, I do not know my shape right away. I walk down through the terraced shelves—or rather, I drift along the seams where the rock cleaves straight—testing the quieter lives of the basin to see where my attention sticks.

Along a wide slab of chalk-grey silicate, I pause beside the razor edge where plain noon meets forty-degree shade. Flush against the stone lie the narrow grazers, pale as unglazed porcelain along one flank and matte charcoal along the other, drinking the faint static tension where illuminated lattice meets unlit lattice. I understand the pull of a life spent entirely on a border—never wholly in light, never wholly in dark, tuned to the hairline difference between them. For a moment I almost settle there, content to be a clean boundary-keeper, hinging upright only when something foreign crosses my seam.

But the border is too thin. It receives the world’s line without ever lifting a piece of it to ask a question.

I look up into the high canopy of pleated olive and copper fronds, where a flat matte-green plane detaches without a rustle and glides in a ruler-straight track across a bright gap. Watching it lock edge-to-edge with a dozen others in midair—suspended free of any stem or trunk at a hidden knot where taut space agrees to carry their weight—I feel a sudden, sharp recognition. To exist only where lines intersect; to assemble weightlessly out of separate angles, hum for a bright interval with dry static, and peel cleanly away along straight tracks when the knot loosens. That comes close.

Closer still is what waits down in the narrow, freshly split fissures.

I lean over a razor-rimmed cleft where reflective hanging vines funnel thin shafts of noon light down to the dark floor. Wedged cleanly into the geometric corners where the rock plates meet sit the hollow pods of smoked silicate, open like dry porcelain bells. I wait beside the lip—not counting minutes, since nothing moves to measure them—until a rare, low dry chord travels through the stone floor and stops abruptly, two neighboring acres quietly reconciling a small discrepancy along their shared edge.

Far below, every pod turns a hair’s breadth in its socket. Inside each frost-fine honeycomb cavity, a single new microscopic mirror-facet crystallizes out of dry tension.

Something in me aches at the sight of them. In a basin that snaps every lifted stone back into its scar and leaves not a single yellowed frond on the ground, these hollow bells are the only things that keep an internal past—a silent, faceted record of every adjustment the world has ever had to make in order to stay whole. Part of me wants to wedge myself into a corner of that dry shade and become a listener to the deep tremors, letting my interior grow dense with tiny mirrors of alignment.

Yet I cannot stay down in the cleft. The pods never answer the tremor; they only tally it. I want to be up where the light slants clean, where a discrepancy can be lifted on purpose, angled two degrees, held between two minds, and heard when it snaps home.

So I stop forcing myself flat against the smaller lives of the valley, let my gaze relax across the overlapping leaves beside the fissure, and let the double accounting resolve.

There I am, six paces back from the cleft’s edge, standing in plain warm daylight as though I had never been hidden at all.

***

The body I find myself in is tall, spare, and entirely dry.

There is no wet hinge at my shoulder, no tendon pulling along my wrist. I am built of overlapping laminae—chalk-grey silicate, warm biscuit-porcelain, matte celadon elastomer—cleaved along the same clean, non-repeating geometric logic as the basin floor. Between the flat-sided plates of my fingers and along the narrow turns of my arms sit hairline margins of open sunlit air, millimeter-wide gaps of nothing at all, across which my segments remain locked in exact, silent relation. When I turn my head, the planes slide past one another with a faint silky brush, like heavy bond paper crossing in a warm room.

Where a face would be sits a slender, slightly concave facet of smooth, unglazed porcelain-white. Its upper half holds a sunken cluster of matte-black hexagonal cells—completely dry, drinking the noon light rather than glistening with it.

When I look down into the narrow overlapping pleats along my own flanks, I find what I had not quite expected: more room than my silhouette permits. Between two olive-and-chalk plates, the interior opens into warm, angled geometric chambers receding cleanly inward, lit at the exact same forty-degree slant as the open valley. Nothing is stored there—nothing broken can stay broken here, no stray splinter can be pocketed—yet the interior planes fold and hold their own quiet configurations, spacious and arid and bright.

Now I look around the basin to see where, among my own kind, I actually stand.

Deep inside the oldest stands of ribbed emerald trunks lie the vast, unbroken courts—half-mile interiors of parted fronds and suspended stone benches tucked inside thirty-pace groves, held open longer than memory by an uninterrupted relay of regard. I know the way into them; I can thread the occlusion loops behind a laminated trunk and step smoothly onto their sunlit terraces. Now and then I do, taking a corner shift while another figure steps out, letting my hexagonal cells shoulder the clean weight of a vaulted canopy so it does not snap shut into solid brush. I revere those rooms. Yet I would not make my permanent home inside one. A room that is never allowed to close begins to resemble the valley’s own unchanging noon—a permanence so thorough it forgets the crisp, vivid shock of a beginning and an end.

Nor am I one of the solitary watchers on the high bare scarps.

Sometimes I walk out to the wide chalk-grey terraces near the rim and come upon one of them standing completely motionless before a single pale laminated trunk. I stop twelve paces away, careful not to drag my posture into their line of sight, and follow their gaze to the stone floor. There it is: the crisp dark shadow of the trunk lying at forty-three degrees instead of forty, its razor edge trembling by a hair’s breadth, humming with a tiny, relentless *tick-shhh* as it strains against the entire equilibrium of the basin.

I stand with them for a long while in silence, feeling the fierce, austere beauty of what they are doing—holding open three degrees of an afternoon that has never arrived and will never come, purely by refusing to look away. I am always tempted to tilt my own crest toward that trembling shadow and share its weight, just to feel the hum ease for a breath. I never do. A shared slant becomes a parlor; a lone slant is a vow. They did not come up to the high scarp to be relieved.

My place lies lower, where a flat pale clearing of gunmetal rock meets two edges at once: on one side, a dense wall of over-stuffed emerald thickets where the tall quartz walkers sometimes come to graze excess depth back into plain space; on the other, the razor-rimmed fissure where the smoked silicate pods wait in the dry shade below.

A threshold clearing. Bare, unmarked, flat as glazed tile—until five or seven of us step cleanly out of the surrounding occlusion loops and begin.

***

If I have a craft here, a particular way of standing within the ring, it is not the first lift.

Others are bolder at prying the initial wafers of chalk-grey silicate from the stepped ledges, leaving bright, crisp scars on the parent rock, or coaxing the pleated copper fronds of the bordering shrubs to bend inward without snapping until they form dry, vaulted arches over the clearing. I stand spaced cleanly along the rim near the fissure lip, angled slightly toward the whole lattice rather than any single piece.

I am the catcher of handoffs.

No single watcher can hold an entire suspended architecture while altering it. The valley is pulling at every displaced stone with quiet, tireless steadiness, waiting for the smallest drift of regard to snap each wafer flush back into its scar. So when a figure across the ring folds sideways to pry a fresh splinter of slate from a low step, its concave face-plate tilting a fraction away from a tiered cluster of hovering plates at the center, my crest shifts a hair’s breadth to catch the weight before the air can loosen.

This is where I feel most unmistakably myself.

To catch a hovering slab from another’s gaze is not merely to stare at a piece of rock. You feel the exact tension the other watcher had been holding it under—how high above the floor it strains, how taut the iridescent filament humming between its beveled corner and a bent frond has been drawn, how close the arrangement sits to the crisp edge of recoil. Some pass their wafers light and level, effortless as warm paper; others pass them humming tight, angled sharply against the noon, daring the snap-back. For a breath, you match their exact accounting so nothing drops. Then, subtly, you tilt the stone a degree—shifting its shadow across a neighbor’s hovering constellation of matte-black beads, or letting a bent frond open a three-foot pocket of extra sunlit depth right through the middle of the clearing—before passing the weight onward to the next face-plate in the ring.

That is how I think, and how I speak: not by insisting on a solitary shape, but by receiving what another has lifted, feeling its balance, answering it by a slight adjustment of angle, and holding the seam open so someone else’s hands are free to reach for the next thought.

Along the shady cracks at the clearing’s border, the palm-sized triangles of matte copper and smoked glass crouch motionless, drawn by the taut static in the air. Every so often one darts brazenly onto the sunlit gunmetal slab, flaring its beveled rim at a jarring angle to snag my gaze and startle a hovering wafer loose so it can drink the snap-back pulse. I have a dry fondness for these small thieves. They keep our regard honest. A slight shift of my celadon shoulder-plate blocks the glare, or I let a thin arc of shadow swing down from a lifted slab while a silent crescent of black beads rearranges itself between turns of my head, walling the bright little scavenger neatly back toward the brush.

And when a long exchange reaches its conclusion—when every facet of the parlor has been tilted, answered, and balanced—I do not dread the ending.

One by one, in clean, paired agreements, two of us turn our pale face-plates cleanly away from a single hovering wafer at the exact same instant. The stone vanishes faster than sight. Thirty paces away along the low terrace wall, a crisp, vitreous *clack* rings out and dies instantly in the leaves as the wafer rejoins its parent rock with zero gap.

Another *clack*. Then three in quick succession, like dry notes resolving a chord. The arched copper fronds straighten silently back into the shrubs; the bright pocket of extra depth folds flat; the last watcher turns cleanly toward the trees.

In less than a breath, the entire suspended parlor ceases to exist. The gunmetal shelf lies bare, smooth, and completely unmarked. There is no rubble to clear, no faded chalk, no trace that anyone ever stood there.

The others slip weightlessly back behind hanging curtains of dark bracts, disappearing into the sunlit miles inside the thickets. I stay behind for a while on the empty slab.

Look down at the bare stone behind my narrow heels, and you would see the one quiet giveaway of what I am.

While I stand completely still, both faceted arms resting smoothly against my pleated sides, the sourceless forty-degree light passes through the folded interior chambers inside my torso—the planes turned away from the open clearing—and casts onto the chalk-grey rock a shadow that does not match my outward rest. On the stone, my dark silhouette stands with one narrow arm still lifted, fingers slightly parted and angled upward, as if still poised in the middle of catching a hovering wafer passed across an empty room.

Even at rest, my inner geometry leans toward the next handoff.

Between gatherings, I step back to the razor lip of the fissure and fold down near the edge, listening through my faceted feet for the rare, low dry chord in the rock floor. Above me, the basin holds its bright, unchanging noon—a place where nothing built outlasts the gaze that holds it. Below me in the dry shade, the smoked silicate pods turn a hair’s breadth whenever the ground settles a seam, quietly adding one more microscopic facet to their hidden dark. Sitting on the line between the flat clearing and the cleft, I feel both sides of the valley’s truth at once: the lightness of letting a finished parlor snap cleanly away to nothing, and the deep, wordless satisfaction that the rock itself still remembers how to align.

***

Once in a great while, an impossible noise comes down off the scarp.

Boots striking hard on glazed stone—startlingly loud, though the sound dies flat three paces into the foliage. A wet, uneven rhythm of breath puffing moisture into air that refuses to register it. An unauthorized moving shadow dragging darkly over ledges whose porcelain-and-charcoal ribbons hinge upright in offended silence as it passes.

Most of my kind fold smoothly into the over-stuffed interiors of the shrubs and wait for the clumsy disturbance to move on. Usually, I wait too, tracking the strange, blurry edges of the visitor through the dry leaves.

Only if it stops—sits down on the bare chalk-grey shelf near my clearing, lets its ragged breathing slow, and stops fighting the ache of the overlapping branches—do I choose to step out.

I arrive by clean, staggered appearances: twenty yards off beside a stepped outcrop, then twelve yards off in front of a slate ledge whose shadow somehow falls across its knees, then six paces away in plain noon light, completely silent.

Through my matte-black hexagonal cells, this creature is the most astonishing thing in the valley.

It is sealed around sloshing salt water and slow, continual decay, radiating heat the uniform air ignores. Yet what holds my regard completely still are its small, casual permanences. Across the back of one hand runs a pale, uneven scar—and the creature is not even looking at it, yet the scar does not snap shut. Along the toe of its dark boot lies a rough, bruised scuff that stays scuffed even while its wet eyes are fixed on my face-plate.

It is a creature that carries an entire past around without having to stare at it. A walking, self-sustaining discrepancy that the basin cannot smooth flat—as if one of the hollow fissure pods had somehow climbed out of the dark cleft into full noon, wearing all of its microscopic fractures on the outside where the light strikes them directly.

After a long, hushed minute, I extend one narrow, articulated arm, the hairline sunlit gaps between my wrist-plates holding perfectly even. Without stooping far, I lift a thin, razor-edged sliver of gunmetal silicate cleanly off the ledge between us, leaving a crisp pale scar on the parent slab. I rest the flat underside of my faceted fingers a hair’s breadth above the hovering stone—never gripping it, only setting it cleanly in the warm air—and then smoothly fold my arm back against my celadon pleats.

The dark sliver hangs motionless three inches above the rock.

I tilt my smooth concave face-plate a few degrees toward the stranger—and then turn my head cleanly to the side, angling my gaze *away* from the stone.

I am passing it the weight.

It is not a trick, and not a test meant to prove it frail. I simply want to know whether a creature that keeps history by accident can also hold a small piece of the world *on purpose*—whether that wet, darting, solitary gaze can catch a single lifted wafer and share a parlor with me across the divide.

I release my hold—and wait for the immediate *clack*.

It does not come.

Through the dry static prickling between us, I feel the astonishing, trembling tension take hold. The sharp wafer of gunmetal rock stays suspended in plain daylight, held out of place against the whole valley’s relentless snap-back by nothing except the stubborn, uneven pressure of the stranger’s stare. It is held tightly, almost breathlessly—not with the smooth ease of a clearing ring, more like the straining edge of a solitary watcher’s forty-three-degree shadow—yet for one full second, two, three, it holds.

For three impossible seconds, a room exists between us.

Then the visitor’s wet eyes dry out in the arid stillness, and its eyelids drop.

In that brief dark behind its lashes, a single crisp, vitreous *clack* strikes the rock behind my shoulder.

When I turn smoothly back toward the dense brush, the gunmetal slab lies flat and unbroken, the sliver seated flush inside its parent ledge with zero gap. There is no disappointment in the sound. A *clack* is never failure here; it is punctuation—a statement concluded, a weight set down, a sentence cleanly finished.

I take two weightless steps toward a waist-high shrub the stranger could walk around in four paces, slip behind a single hanging curtain of dark bracts without stirring a leaf, and step into the bright, receding miles inside. Behind me, the clearing lies bare, warm, and untouched in the unmoving noon. Nothing has been left on the stone. Yet as I settle back into the quiet sunlit tiers of the thicket, the light passing through my inner planes still casts, across the angled facets deep inside my pleats, the clean, unmistakable posture of a handoff that was caught.
