I do not wake when the city is loudest.

At the height of the exhale, when the great perforated conduits along the middle terrace are booming full-throat and the stretched partition-screens three stories up are hissing like dry chaff, I lie near the matte outer curb and am nothing at all—merely a hollow, crooked piece of seasoned plant, cool as the floor, indistinguishable from a dropped husk. A hand’s breadth to my wall-side runs the satin-slick lane where the vibration beats sharpest. All through the peak of the tide, fist-sized shells go shivering past me in a brisk, steady stream, singing their crisp little overtones straight into the wood. Now and then one drifts wide and clips my flank with a dry, hollow *clack*, nudging me a hair’s breadth closer to the drop into the empty middle. I do not recoil. The peak chord of this avenue is a fraction of a tone too bright for my belly now; it rattles lightly across my outer ribs and slides off without catching.

Wait with me here in the dead wood, and you can feel what I am before I ever move.

I am smaller than a loaf, larger than a fist, arched over an empty chamber of horn-hard fiber. My left flank is slightly flattened—the old impress of a bowed lintel two terraces higher, where I spent eighty years as a tight knot of callus, slowly hollowing myself out beneath the acoustic fretting of a stressed joint before my stem wore to a thread and snapped in a hush. My open throat is not centered; it flares slightly obliquely toward the wall-side, cocked like a tilted ear, and down my back runs a narrow fluted ridge where a strip of ancient bast once pressed into my growing ribs. Beneath me sit dense, backward-slanted combs of silica bristles, stiff as dry barley-awns, wholly still.

Inside my belly is where my age lives.

I was thinner once. Back when I dropped from that upper lintel—bouncing light as a dried nut onto a high terrace roof—my walls were springy and sharp-voiced. The reedy whine of the upper cane viaducts caught me easily; I skated the braided bridge-cables in frantic, chattering strings, ducking into wrist-wide notches where the draft shrieked highest. Every hour I spent shivering through those invisible, static-charged clouds drew microscopic grains of chalk-flour, peppery resin-dust, and dry pollen into my open lip, cementing them in tacky, imperceptible films along my inner ribs. Century by century, my hollow space narrowed by the thickness of a hair. My weight grew. My voice dropped.

One tide, the high viaducts could no longer hold me. I stalled along a narrow curb until a crowd of younger, sharper shells tapped me over the lip of a worn stair-shaft. I tumbled hollow through open air and came to rest two stories down, on this broader curved avenue, where a deeper trunk-conduit caught my ribs and woke me again.

Now it is happening a second time. I am suspended in the drift between stories—too thickened for the brisk noon of this middle tier, not yet heavy enough to have fallen to the foundations.

So my hour is the sag.

Stay motionless beside me on the curb until the unseen weather far above the buried roof begins to turn. You feel it first not in the air, but in the wood beneath my bristles: the steady booming of the bass pipes slumps downward by a fraction of a tone. The fierce papery rushing overhead softens to a dry surf. Along the satin lane beside me, the crisp, hurried traffic begins to falter. One by one, the lighter shells lose their grip on the weakening note; their woody overtones sputter, their bristles drag, and they click softly to a halt mid-avenue, going cold and motionless in their tracks.

And right then, as the terrace’s chord sinks through the waning half-hour before the turn, the floor slides down into the exact measure of my empty belly.

*I wake.*

There is no breath in it, no surge of blood, no decision. The wave simply enters my arched ribs and locks. Instantly my whole dry cage begins to shiver—faster than any pulse—and my oblique throat sings a clean, woody overtone straight back into the curb. Driven by my own trembling shell, my backward-slanted bristles bite the satin floor and kick me smoothly forward in a blur of tiny steps.

I move out into an avenue gone suddenly quiet.

This is the place I actually inhabit in the city: the twilight of the wind, the slow slackening between full roar and total stillness. While the busy cohort of my tier sits frozen along the curbs like fallen gourds, I glide smoothly between them, weaving around stalled flanks, combing up the fine static-charged dust they shook loose in their passage. Because my flared lip tilts toward the wall, I do not keep to the dead center of the lane; I hug the carved, silky-blunt doorposts where the side-drafts spill out of the stacked dwellings.

Halfway along this curve of the terrace lies a chamber I know by the drag of its threshold.

Its sill is worn into a shallow bowl, and its smooth jambs narrow at the middle, shaped millennia ago around proportions no longer present in the pocket. During the full roar of the exhale, that room’s Helmholtz hum is always faintly unfinished—a hair too sharp, edgy and unbalanced, as though tuned to be damped by something soft standing in its center. Most shells pass its doorway or circle uneasily near its outer edge.

When the tide sags, though, the draft across that notched jamb eases, and the room’s chord lowers its shoulder by the exact fraction it lacks. It drops straight into my key.

The standing wave draws me cleanly over the worn bowl of the threshold and into the quiet interior. There are no sharp drafts inside, only a deep, steady, resonant fullness that cradles my ribs from every curved corner at once. I circle slowly along the floor, hugging the lee of the braided walls where pale mineral flour and drifted pollen lie undisturbed for decades, drawing a few invisible grains across my lip to bind with the long, silent strata already lining my belly. For fifteen minutes, the unfinished chamber and my thickened cage hold a single balanced note together—neither too sharp nor too dull, a brief, dry harmony tucked away behind a bowed lintel.

Then the barometric pressure balances between inside and out, and the room lets go.

The hum drops cleanly out from beneath my bristles. My overtone cuts off. I slide half an inch on momentum across the smooth bowl of the threshold, out onto the open lip of the terrace, and go utterly dead.

All across the five curved stories of the pocket, the last halting clicks fade out. The city sighs. Somewhere across the great empty air of the center, a hollow cane girder gives a single crisp *tock* as the long tension eases from its fibers.

I cannot move in the hush. I cannot turn my lip upward or reach for what comes next. I am only a cool, hollow nut resting near the drop.

Yet the stillness is not vacant. Out of the vast, unoccupied air above the terraces, a faint, enveloping whisper descends—softer than dry snow, wide as a lowering curtain. The dwellers of the hollow center, losing the cool vortex and static repulsion that held them spinning blindly through the roar, drift down with unbearable gentleness across the frozen streets.

A weightless rib of tissue-thin bract brushes across my fluted spine, carrying a sudden, sharp surge of cedar and peppery resin. Hair-fine aerial threads trail delicately over my oblique throat, combing static from my horn-hard shell. As those vast, spiraling sails drape for a breath over my dead ribs, a faint, dry pattering—lighter than any rain this pocket has ever forgotten—breaks loose from their dust-heavy vanes and falls straight into my open lip. High-void pollen, lifted from the uppermost screens and spun for hours in the empty middle, settles inside me beside the deep chalk-flour I just swept from the corner of the room.

Then a cool draft touches the opposite side of my shell.

Far down in the curved foundations, a different set of notched flues catches the reversed breath. Before the new chord has even gathered to a roar, the gossamer weight lifts cleanly from my back and spirals away into the rising air. Beneath me, the floor hums awake on an altered pitch—higher, reedier, waking the mirror-cohort of shells that slept through the exhale.

I do not wake with them. The inhale strikes this curve of the terrace from the wrong angle for my skewed lip; I sit dead-still near the doorway while a wholly different traffic shivers briskly past me in the reverse direction, nudging my ribs, polishing my outer shell one microscopic grain smoother.

Only once, a hundred tides ago—or perhaps a thousand, for I have no count except the slow narrowing of my own hollow space—something broke the rhythm.

It happened in the middle of a full, roaring exhale, when I ought to have lain mute along the matte curb for hours. The bass conduits were booming at their peak; the traffic in the lane was loud and brisk. Yet without any sag in the wind, the doorway beside me suddenly *dropped its pitch*. The unfinished room behind the worn jambs went dull, softened, and deep—sliding straight down into the exact note it only ever reached at the edge of the hush.

My ribs caught fire out of season.

I shivered off the curb and glided briskly across the lane—and collided, almost at once, with a wall of silence. A crescent of ordinary, sharp-voiced shells had piled up dead-still right across the avenue, their overtones cut off mid-note, clicking softly against one another in a sudden acoustic shadow where the floor had simply stopped ringing. Because my note was lower, drawn from the softened throat of the chamber itself, I crept half a shell’s length farther into the edge of that stillness before my bristles finally lost the floor.

I slid on momentum and tapped gently against the obstacle standing in the threshold.

It did not ring.

It was not horn-cork, not dry bast, not hollow rib. It yielded by a fraction of a hair and drank the shock completely. A strange, heavy warmth radiated from it, laced with something utterly alien to the clean chalk and cedar of the pocket—something damp, slack, quick-pulsing, breathing on a tiny, frantic tide of its own that had nothing to do with the buried fissures or the weather outside.

I lay dead-still against it, just at the rim of the silence it cast. Above my cold back, inside the darkened room behind that soft barrier, the chord held steady—muted, rounded, completely finished at last, as though the ancient grafted walls had folded calmly around the exact missing weight they were braided to keep. For one brief, uncanny hour, I rested at the edge of that remembered silence, my dry ribs barely humming with the faintest sympathy from the floor just behind me.

Then the warmth lifted and moved away down the curved street. The acoustic shadow vanished. The room snapped back to its sharp, lonely note; the crescent of stalled shells around me shivered awake all at once and went streaming past, bumping my flank aside as I went dead again on the worn wood. A trace of dampness clung briefly to the rim of my lip, and within three breaths the arid draft stripped it away to a faint smudge of salt.

That was one hour out of ages. The dust has long since buried that grain of salt beneath a hundred newer layers.

I know what is coming for me, though not as a fear—nothing rots here, and nothing is wasted. Another century or two of sags, a few thousand more crossings of the worn threshold, a few thousand more quiet brushes from the drifting sails in the hush, and my hollow interior will narrow past the last note this middle terrace can sing even at its lowest dip. I will stall along the outer curb and stay cold through tide after tide, until a lively string of younger shells nudges me smoothly over the lip of the next worn stair-shaft.

I will bounce down through the open air, heavier now, ringing a deeper *tock* against the treads, and come to rest on the lowest curved avenue where the great root-rind bends inward beneath five stories of grafted timber. There the bass conduits roll a drone deep enough to wake the slow, gnarled elders—thick-walled, pebble-smooth, moving half an inch at a time on the heaviest swell of the earth’s breath.

I will walk there for millennia more, carrying the high-void pollen and middle-tier sugars slowly downward, until at last my ribs are packed nearly solid with mineral wealth and no note in the city can stir my bristles again. On the deepest sub-audible rumble, I will grind myself tight into a hairline split beneath some strained, sagging pier, and stop moving for good.

The tacky sugar-dust at my base will bind me to the living floor. Slowly, across a hundred years of roaring tides, horn-hard callus will creep out from the mother-wall, folding gently over my flattened flank and my tilted lip, drawing in every hoarded grain I gathered from the upper air and the unfinished rooms. The weakened pier will thicken by the width of my body. Its pitch will tighten by a fraction of a tone, subtly steering the next millennium’s lanes away from the mended joint and toward some newer thinning wall.

I will stop being the small voice that answers the sag, and become the quiet wood that holds the song.
