The power came without thunder.

It did not descend upon the plateau as a figure, or speak from the sky. Its attention arrived the way warmth arrives inside a stone: slowly, without breaking the surface. It passed over the empty towns, the frost in the walls, the grasses leaning toward water hidden underground. It listened—not for a voice, but for the long exchange in which the uplands were already speaking.

It found no wound to close.

The roads wandered, but not in distress. The houses stood empty, but their emptiness was not a cry. Water vanished into cracks and returned elsewhere; hills loosened their gravel and settled again. The country was strange, and old, and awake in a way that did not require a witness.

So the power did not fill the towns with people. It did not summon birds to the silent trees, or flowers to the pale grasses. It did not straighten the roads or correct the maps. These would have been gifts only to someone who believed the uplands incomplete.

Instead, it made itself available.

In a cistern beneath a roofless house, one drop of water hung for a moment above the dark. The power placed no meaning in it. It did not demand an answer. Then the drop fell, and the sound traveled down through the stone—not a word, but a listening presence, patient enough to be heard on the uplands’ own scale.

For a long while, nothing changed.

Then, far away, a road that had been leading nowhere arrived at a bridge. The bridge crossed a ravine filled with grass, and beyond it the road continued into the pale distance. Perhaps the land had made it. Perhaps it had always been there, in an arrangement of things not yet reached. The power did not claim the work.

It only kept watch, gently, so that if the uplands ever wished to answer—or to ask for shelter, or simply to be known—there would be something capable of hearing.

And the plateau continued: cold stone taking in rain, roots finding their seams, fog moving uphill against the wind. Nothing had been made more ordinary. Nothing had been required to change.

But the silence was no longer unattended.
