The power did not enter.

It knew that entry was not always a kindness. A light in the hollow would have made the dark into something to endure. A voice would have filled the close air with a demand for answer. Even a hand, however gentle, would have turned the being’s whole world toward the shape of a visitor.

So the power attended from beyond the stone.

It learned the chamber as the being knew it: by pressure, warmth, the patient weight of water. It learned the bowl’s old warmth, too, and the brief disturbance made by the hands that had left it. It did not mistake the chips for a message, or the stillness for loneliness. It understood that persistence was not a plea.

For a long while, it did nothing.

Then, far above the hollow, where a seam in the ridge might one day split under frost, the power softened the stone by no more than the breadth of a grain. The mountain shifted, as mountains do, but not there. The hand-wide opening remained. No stream found its way into the chamber. The roof held its shape.

Nothing changed that the resident could name. The water stayed warm. The roots stayed pale and fine. The bowl remained on its ledge, keeping the faint difference left by human hands.

But when the next tremor passed through the ridge, it spent itself elsewhere.

The being felt only the familiar pressure of its small world, unbroken around it. It did not know that anything had been spared. It did not need to know.

The power left no sign of itself. It kept the cleft neither more open nor more closed than before, and did not ask the hollow to become anything else. Outside, wind moved over the pale stone. Inside, a bead gathered beneath the ceiling, trembled, and held.
