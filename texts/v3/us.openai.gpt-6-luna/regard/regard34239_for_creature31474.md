The power did not descend in a blaze.

It came as a change in attention: the basin, which had endured the gaze of weather and stone for ages, was at last regarded without being measured for usefulness. The power listened first to the machinery beneath the ground. It listened until the knocking and shuddering were no longer one great noise, but many: the strain of old joints, the passage of heat, the small tremors traveling outward through the dark.

Then it listened to the creatures.

A bead of water trembled under the stone lip. One blunt little body emerged and tapped the rock. From the fissures came answering clicks, faint and irregular. The power did not mistake them for a prayer. It did not mistake them for permission. It waited.

The basin offered no explanation. It was not a puzzle with a hidden answer. Its life had made a home of what was available: warmth, dampness, rhythm, shelter. The machinery had outlasted any purpose that could be seen, but its labor had become part of the conditions under which the small lives endured. To stop it might have been mercy to the metal and disaster to the living. To make the basin abundant might have unmade the balance that had kept it habitable.

So the power did not quiet the noise. It did not raise mountains, summon rain without end, or scatter seeds across the bare stone.

It found, far below, one worn bearing whose fracture would someday turn the steady labor into a violent halt. The failure was not imminent. It might have been many seasons away. But when it came, the force of it would have split the neighboring seams and sealed some of the basin’s oldest refuges.

The power touched the bearing once.

Not enough to make it new. Not enough to alter the machinery’s ancient rhythm. Only enough that the crack would not race ahead of the slow turning. The metal continued to knock. The ground continued to shiver. The vents breathed at their appointed intervals.

Above, the small creature tapped again. Perhaps it had felt the touch. Perhaps it had only found another bead of water.

The power left no message, no monument, no promise that the basin would be spared every hardship. It did not ask the creatures to understand what had happened. It gave them nothing they could become dependent upon, and took nothing away that they had learned to live with.

By evening, condensation gathered in the familiar places. In the fissures, the little bodies rested against the warm stone. Somewhere beneath them, the old machinery turned on—still strained, still obscure, still carrying its sound through the basin.

And, far below, one small fracture waited longer to become a catastrophe.
