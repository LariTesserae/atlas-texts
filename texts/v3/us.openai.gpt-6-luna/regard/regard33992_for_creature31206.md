The power did not come down from the sky.

It was already near enough to hear the water ticking into the basin. It listened to the town as one listens to a sleeping house: not for noise, but for the small differences between one breath and the next.

It saw the droplet beneath the leaf. The hand beside the cup. The cord waiting on the workshop bench. It saw, too, the Staying passing from seam to shadow, keeping every almost-change from becoming an event.

The power understood why.

The Staying had not made the town still out of cruelty. It had found a way to keep the world from being lost. Nothing wore away; no harvest failed; no one departed. It had mistaken preservation for care, as even the kind can do when they are afraid.

So the power did not tear it from the stones. It did not command the stream to flood the hollow or the people to rise all at once. It came to the workshop, where the Staying lingered most often, and made itself known in the one language the creature could not mistake: a change that was gentle, and could not be taken back.

The droplet fell.

It struck the stone with a sound no louder than a fingertip on a door.

The Staying gathered in the workshop doorway. For the first time, it had nowhere to hide from what it felt. The cord on the bench trembled—not from wind, but from the settling of the wood beneath it.

“You have kept them safe,” the power said. “But safety is not the same as a life.”

The Staying drew close around the unfinished gesture, as if it could shelter the hand forever. The power did not pull it away. It only let the room grow warm by one degree, and allowed the pale light at the window to shift.

In the town, a woman’s fingers closed around the cup beside the well.

She blinked. She drew a breath that belonged to no instant before it. For a long while she did nothing else. Then she drank.

The power gave the people back their motion—not as a command to hurry, but as a door opened from the outside. Those who woke found themselves able to choose their next act. Some wept. Some called out names. One man shut the door of the workshop against the cold, then opened it again because he wanted to see the sky. The town did not become busy all at once. It began, simply, to have a next moment.

The Staying could not keep the people inside their pauses anymore. But the power did not banish it. It showed it another way to belong.

There would still be quiet here. There would be rests between tasks, still air beneath the eaves, water held briefly in the hollow of a stone. The Staying could live in those intervals: in the hush after a bird’s note, in the patience before a seed opened, in a person’s chosen silence. It could keep company with what was still without preventing what was becoming.

For a time, it seemed unable to understand the gift. Then it moved across the courtyard—not as a seam of shade, not as a half-seen animal, but as something with a direction. It went to the edge of the terrace and watched the stream run on.

The stream had changed its silver fork around the dark rock. Only a little. Enough.

The power remained until the town had found its own pace again. Then it left no sign of itself except the fallen droplet, drying on the stone, and a pennant on the watchhouse roof that at last rose and fell with the wind.
