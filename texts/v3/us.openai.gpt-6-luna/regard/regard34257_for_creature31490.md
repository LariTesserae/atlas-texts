The power did not descend like a star.

It did not give the plateau a sun, or soften every stone, or make the dark afraid to enter. It had made worlds before, and knew how easily a gift could become a wound when it ignored the lives already arranged around it.

So it listened.

It listened to the slow pressure turning beneath the basalt, to the small creatures clicking in their shelters, to the hunters balancing on invisible currents. It listened to the water moving through cracks too narrow for any eye to follow. And it listened to the old grazer at the edge of the herd, whose feet remembered the plateau’s routes better than her failing legs could travel them.

For many seasons she had led the young between the wet basins. Now, when the deep clank changed pitch, she hesitated. The familiar route ran beside a fissure whose rim had begun to loosen. Below it, pressure gathered in a pocket that had never before filled so quickly.

The power could have held the stone still. It could have emptied the deep water and quieted the plateau forever. Instead, it gave the old grazer one clear sensation: warmth, not beneath her feet but to her left—a steady warmth along a ridge she had not crossed since she was young.

She turned.

The others followed, because they knew her, and because the ridge smelled of damp stone. They passed beyond the fissure just as its rim broke inward. A breath of vapor surged into the empty hollow. Blue light ran briefly over the rock. Then the pressure found an older channel, one that could bear it, and the plateau resumed its measured turning.

The power left no mark anyone could find. It did not make the grazer young again. It did not tell her why she had turned, or ask for gratitude. But for a while she led the herd by that ridge, and the young learned its slant beneath their feet. When her steps finally failed, they still knew the way.

Below them, the deep engines went on turning. Water gathered in the seams. Small lives returned to the wet stone.

The power stayed—not as a hand over the plateau, but as attention within its vastness: careful enough to know that sometimes the kindest thing is not to remake a world, but to help one of its lives find the path it has almost forgotten.
