The power would not lift the mountains out of the sea.

It would not still the currents, or call the old machines back to life. The water had become the country’s weather; the creatures had learned its language. To quiet it all would be less like kindness than like silence imposed on a conversation.

So the power would listen.

It would listen through the stone, through the kelp forests bowing in their green darkness, through the valleys where the deep currents gathered themselves. It would listen to the far calls of the traveling animals and the small clicks of crabs in the towers. And, in a narrow cleft high on the slope, it would listen to the watchful creature resting with its arms folded close.

The creature would not see the power. It might not even know that anything had happened.

A storm tide was coming. The creature felt it first as a tightening in the water: pressure changing against its skin, the mountain beginning to hum. It left its cleft and moved downslope, seeking the familiar shelter beneath a ledge. There, a young one was caught in a loop of old cable, thrashing whenever the current tugged it tighter.

The creature approached. The water shoved it sideways. The young one’s color flashed and faded against the rock.

Then, for the space of a few breaths, the current changed.

Not stopped. Not conquered. A narrow stream of water slid around the ledge and crossed the cable’s pull at just the right angle. The loop slackened. The creature reached in, found the young one, and eased it free.

The storm arrived. The two vanished beneath the ledge as the surge swept over it, carrying sand and broken fronds down the slope.

Nothing in the country would mark this as a miracle. The mountains would keep their shape. The old tower would knock and turn. The creature would remember the current as strange—perhaps only because it had been afraid. It would return to its cleft when the water grew quiet enough, and the young one would find its way into the kelp forest.

The power would ask for no gratitude. It would not make itself a new law of the sea, or promise that danger would pass them by. The creature still had to learn the mountain, as every creature did. It still had to listen.

But the power would remain attentive: not hovering over the country, not ruling it, but present in the same way a hand can be present without closing around what it touches. Where life met a passage too narrow, a little room might open. Where the current threatened to make an ending, it might sometimes be given another course.

And the high country would go on being remade by the sea—not protected from change, but accompanied within it.
