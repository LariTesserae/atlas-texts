The power does not mistake the basin for a wound.

It comes near—not as light, which would turn every hiding place into exposure, nor as warmth, which would alter the terms by which life has learned to endure. It does not smooth the ridges or still the currents. It does not lift the silt and reveal, all at once, what has spent ages becoming difficult to see.

It attends.

It follows the cold water through its crossing layers. It feels the pressure settle over stone, the grit slide through narrow seams, the concealed lives open and close beneath the plain. It learns that the basin’s quiet is not emptiness, and that its restless changing is not a plea to be made still. The being beneath the gravel has no single face to look upon, no one voice to answer. So the power does not ask it to gather itself into one.

For a long while, nothing happens.

Then, where the living network passes between buried stones, the power makes itself known—not with a message, but with a presence as slight as the difference between a current passing by and a current that has noticed you. It offers no command, no promise of rescue. Only recognition: *You are here. I have found you.*

A feeding crown rises from the silt. Somewhere beneath a ledge, a small blue-green light appears, goes out, and appears again. The signal passes from one hidden body to the next. It may be only the old language of warning or recognition. The power does not require the creatures to understand more than that.

Then it lets the current go on.

The basin remains cold. Sediment falls. A shallow furrow begins to soften. Nothing is made permanent; nothing is taken out of the life of the place. But for that brief span, the basin is not merely unseen. It is known—and the power, having learned its measure, does not ask it to become anything else.
