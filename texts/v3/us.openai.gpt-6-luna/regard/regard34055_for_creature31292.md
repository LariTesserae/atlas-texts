The power would not mistake emptiness for a wound.

It would not cover the plain in grass, raise mountains at the horizon, or call the builders back to finish what they began. It would not make the Settler speak in a voice it did not have. The land was not asking to become something else. The Settler was already at work, learning what its inheritance could bear.

So the power would watch.

It would feel the rain move through the channels, the warmth pass into the courses of clay, the slow attention of the Settler gathering in the seams. It would learn the difference between a crack that threatened a wall and one that merely let the land breathe. It would wait long enough to see what the Settler chose to mend.

Then, one year, when the rains came thin and the heat lingered, it would make a small allowance.

In a low court far from the nearest green patch, water would collect and not vanish at once. Nothing would shine there. No spring would burst through the paving. The basin would simply hold its rain a little longer than it had before—through the first hot days, then the next. At the edge, where dust had gathered in a joint, a single seed would settle. It might take root. It might not.

The power would not tell the Settler what the gift meant. It would only leave the water there, and remain near enough to prevent the basin from collapsing before the Settler could answer.

The Settler would feel the unusual weight in the ground. Its attention would come, slowly, from distant channels and walls. Perhaps it would deepen the basin by a fraction. Perhaps it would close a seam and keep the water. Perhaps it would let the court dry, as it had let so many others dry.

The power would accept either answer.

If the seed took, it would not make a forest of it. It would let the small leaves meet the light on their own terms, and let the Settler decide whether that patch belonged in the world it was learning to hold. If the seed failed, the power would not call the attempt a failure. The water would return to the sky. The court would remain.

And once the possibility had been given, the power would grow quiet again.

Not absent. Not waiting for gratitude. Simply present, as rain was present, as warmth was present: something the Settler might come to recognize without ever needing to name. The land would go on settling. The Settler would go on learning. And nothing would be hurried merely because a wise power had noticed it.
