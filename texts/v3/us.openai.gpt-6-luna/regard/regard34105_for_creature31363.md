The power did not descend into the hollow.

It had no need to arrive as a figure, or to make the stone shine. It knew the difference between being unseen and being alone; it knew that attention, however kind, could become a weight if it insisted on being felt.

So it waited beyond the broken rim, where cloud moved through the high air. It watched the water enter the seam, pass beneath the moss, and emerge below. It watched the creatures holding their places in the old arrangement. It listened to the hush that might have been breathing.

And it understood that there was no wound to close.

The hollow was not asking to be made grander, brighter, or easier to find. Its smallness was not a failure. Its crowded life was not a problem to be solved. The power did not mistake quiet for emptiness, nor stillness for helplessness.

But it saw, too, that nothing here seemed able to end. Leaves darkened without falling apart. Water passed without seeming to depart. The world had kept its balance for so long that balance had become almost indistinguishable from a held breath.

The power did not break that breath for it.

Instead, it left a possibility in the water.

No voice carried it. No creature was compelled to understand. The clear thread entering the stone simply moved, one day, with a little more freedom. A bead slipped from the ledge and fell. It struck a dead leaf, and the leaf shifted. Beneath it, something small and white began to feed.

The change was so slight that the hollow could have refused it. The moss might have closed over the place. The creatures might have carried on exactly as before. Nothing would have been taken from them if they did.

But the water kept moving.

In time—though time here did not hurry—a leaf softened enough to become soil. A frog laid eggs in the cup-shaped pool. Some hatched. Some did not. A salamander left its runnel and returned to it thinner, or older, or bearing a faint mark that had not been there before. The first green shoot to grow above the others bent toward the gray light, not because it had been commanded, but because it had found room.

The hollow remained itself. Its stone stayed dark. Its world stayed small. The creatures still made room for whatever entered, and the moss still rose gently after a footstep. But now the place had room for difference, too.

The power gave it no path out. It gave no creature the burden of becoming a messenger. It made no promise that the hollow would never be harmed. Instead, it laid a quiet guardianship along the rim: a turning-away of careless hands, a failure of maps and ambitions to settle on this particular patch of mountain. Those who came in wonder might still find it. Those who came to possess it would, without knowing why, pass by.

Then the power withdrew its attention—not because it had stopped caring, but because care did not require constant watching.

Deep beneath the leaves, the hollow felt the water pass.

It did not call this a blessing. It had no word for blessing, and no need of one. But somewhere in its close, padded silence, a small thing let go of its place. Another took the place that opened. The breathing sound went on, no longer quite the same as before.
