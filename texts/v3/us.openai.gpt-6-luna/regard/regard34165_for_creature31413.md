The power did not descend in a pillar of light. There was no voice in the clouds, no hand laid across the shelf to still its changes.

It looked—and in looking, understood that the place was not waiting to be made whole.

The rock was young, not broken. The lives were small, not forgotten. The spring was meagre, but it was shared. Even the fallen cairn had become part of the slope, and the moss was already making a quiet claim on its stones.

So the power did not flatten the ridges or fill every crack with water. It did not make the little creatures tame, or the birds gentle, or the weather kind. To remove every danger would be to erase the knowledge by which these lives endured.

It did only what could be done without taking the place from itself.

When the dry season lingered, a narrow seep continued beneath the spring wall. When a hard frost came just as the tiny winged things emerged, the cold relented by a single night. A slab above the sheltered nook—loosened, but not yet fallen—held fast through one more winter. No creature knew these mercies as gifts. They knew only that water remained, that the thaw came, that the shelter still stood.

The power watched the small animals choose their crossings. It watched the birds turn in the wind, and the roots find purchase in stone. It watched the scattered cairn-stones roll no farther than the next safe hollow.

And when, in time, the shelf changed again—as it must—it did not call the change a failure. It kept faith with what had lived there: not by preserving every shape, but by leaving room for life to answer what came next.

The wind passed through the split in the rim and made its thin, flute-like note. Nothing looked up. Nothing needed to.

The power remained, attentive and quiet, asking nothing of the place except that it be allowed to continue becoming.
