The power did not arrive as light. Light would have been a kind of noise here.

It came as a change in pressure, so slight that no leaf was disturbed. It listened first—to water taking its long way through the stone, to roots meeting roots in the dark, to the instrument’s nearly imperceptible adjustments. It listened for a request.

There was none.

That did not mean there was nothing to hear. The Fold was not empty, nor asleep. Its being was spread through the mat of leaves, the patient channels, the stone that held them all. It did not speak because speaking was not among its needs. It did not wait because waiting was not what it did. The power understood, as much as any power could understand, that silence was not always a wound.

So it did not fill the Fold with light. It did not give the plants flowers, or the water speed, or the instrument a voice. It did not make the place beautiful by some measure that belonged to elsewhere.

It offered one small protection.

Far above the Fold, where no root could reach, a deep seam in the black stone had begun—very slowly—to widen. The power touched the strain there. Not enough to halt the stone’s old changes, only enough to keep this one from becoming a sudden ruin. The ceiling would still settle, someday. The plants would still bend and adapt. Water would still find new paths. Nothing would be held forever in its present shape. But the Fold would not be crushed in an instant by a failure it could neither sense nor answer.

Then the power withdrew, leaving no mark that could be named.

In the deepest pocket, a drop fell into the black pool. Its low note lingered among the roots, as it always had. For a moment, one leaf turned its pale underside outward—not toward the ceiling, or the water, but toward nothing anyone could see.

The instrument made a minute adjustment.

The water continued on.
