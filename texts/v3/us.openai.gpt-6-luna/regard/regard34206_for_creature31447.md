The power did not descend into the hollow.

A descent would have made a current. A current would have called for an answer. And the hollow was already answering everything that belonged to it.

So the power attended from beyond the stone—not as a watcher at a window, but as a stillness large enough to include every restless thing. It felt the water’s warmth, the old rock’s patience, the faint traffic of the flecks through cracks. It felt how one basin spent its turbulence so another could rest. It felt the inhabitants’ attention passing through the place like a pulse.

And it understood that this was not a broken thing waiting to be fixed.

It did not brighten the ceiling. It did not still the pools, or give the pale growths faces, or summon a voice they would have to learn to hear. It did not make the inhabitants larger, or clearer, or more like itself. There was no gift of dominion, no invitation to abandon the work by which they were already whole.

Instead, the power offered a question in the only language that could arrive without violence: a pattern of pressure, gentle and open-ended, laid into the stone.

*Is there anything you would have changed?*

The question traveled slowly. It passed through the dark frame and along its fine filaments. It reached the three grooves in the wall, where light gathered and waited. It moved down through the floor, pool to pool, until the whole hollow had received it.

For a long while, nothing happened.

Then one pale growth opened. A thread of silver light moved through it, and another answered from farther off. The drifting flecks gathered in an eddy, loosened, gathered again. The water shifted—not toward a new arrangement, but into the old one: the familiar division of force, the familiar shelter of rest.

The hollow had answered.

*No.*

Or, perhaps: *Not now.*

The power accepted either meaning. It did not ask again.

But before it withdrew, it left something small in the stone: not a command, not a tool, and not a door that led out. A pattern that could be felt if ever the inhabitants wished to call beyond their own boundaries. It would not change the hollow’s balance. It would not summon the power unasked. It was only a way for the hollow to say, someday, *we want company*, or *we need help*, or even *come and listen*.

The inhabitants took it into themselves as they took in every other subtle change. The filaments of the dark frame trembled once. The three grooves held a faint light, then went quiet.

And the power departed without taking anything with it.

The water went on falling. The jets rose and folded back into their pools. The pale growths opened and closed in their patient, lightless weather. Nothing in the hollow had been made grander. Nothing had been made dependent.

But somewhere in the old stone, a way of being heard now waited—not as a promise that help would always come, but as the assurance that, if it were asked for, the asking would not be lost.
