The power noticed the plateau first as a whole: its thin water, its raw stone, the small lives finding room between cold and warmth. It saw that the land was not broken. It was becoming.

Then it noticed one pale grazer, separated from the others by a fresh run of meltwater. The animal had slipped on the scree and was favoring one leg. Across the basin, a hunter had seen it too.

The power could have lifted the grazer out of danger. It could have turned the hunter aside, stilled the water, or held the whole upland in a safer shape. It did none of these. A life spared every risk would not be the same life, and a land spared every change would cease to be this land.

Instead, the power listened—to the grazer’s uneven breath, to the water under its skin of ice, to the hunter waiting on the ridge. Then it gave the wind a slight turn.

The scent of the grazer reached the hunter differently. The hunter paused, lifted its head, and followed the new trace toward a hollow where birds were rising from the warm mud. It was not deceived, only offered another possibility. It went.

The grazer tried to stand. The injured leg held. It crossed the shallow run slowly, placing each hoof with care, and found the others on the far side. They did not know they had been helped. They moved on together, leaving prints that filled with water behind them.

The power did not make a promise of safety. The hunter would eat; the stone would open; the weather would turn. But for that hour, a creature had been given room to choose, and the plateau had been left free to continue becoming.
