The power did not mistake stillness for distress.

It watched the basin through many cycles of rain, through the small adjustments of bodies on stone, through the patient return of the gray film. It listened until the factories’ low note and the creatures’ dry syllables ceased to sound like separate things.

Nothing asked to be rescued.

So it did not brighten the sky, or lift the creatures from their ledges, or turn the basin into something green. It did not give them a voice they had never needed, nor stir them into motion merely to prove that it could. The power knew that care could become another kind of disturbance when it arrived certain of what a life ought to be.

But it noticed one thing the creatures could not know: the factories would not run forever.

Not tomorrow, perhaps, nor for generations. The presses were sound; the pipes were mended; the district had found its rhythm. Still, every rhythm has an end somewhere in it. One day the warmth would diminish. The film would thin. The iron would cool.

The power laid no hand on the machines. It changed nothing anyone could have pointed to. Instead, far below the oldest wall, it eased a seam in the stone—not enough to break it, only enough to let a trace of buried moisture pass through. The water carried minerals up into the basin, so faintly that at first it seemed no more than another variation in the weather. Where it met the cold air, a film gathered.

The creatures found it in their own time.

One frill opened. A shell shifted. A mouth touched the stone and rasped once, softly. The nearest bodies turned, not all at once, but by degrees. The new dampness did not draw them away from the pipes they knew. It did not ask them to leave the old ledges or abandon the factory’s warmth. It simply made another place possible.

The power stayed long enough to see the colony alter by a fraction, and then it withdrew its attention—not because it had stopped caring, but because care did not require watching every breath.

The basin continued as before. Rain ticked on the roofs. Fans murmured behind their grilles. Shell touched shell. The factories held their low, patient note.

Only beneath the oldest wall, water moved through a seam no one had opened, bringing the stone a little nearer to the future.
