The power does not descend upon the highland.

It does not part the mist or speak from the cliffs. It does not make the roofs shine with gold, or fill the cisterns beyond their measure. It sees how much of the place is already held together by attention: a hand clearing leaves from a drain, a neighbor bracing a wall, a child learning which stones turn slick after rain. To overwhelm that care with a miracle would be to mistake it for helplessness.

So, at first, the power only watches.

It learns the sound of each basin after rain, the weight of water in the old channels, the paths people take when the fog comes down. It sees the names cut into lintels and the unremarked labor that has kept those names from sliding into the mud. It understands that the highland is not merely inhabited. It is being answered, every day, by those who live there.

Then, because even the most careful place can be wounded by chance, the power leaves one small gift.

When a slope begins to shift, the water speaks differently.

Not loudly. A change in the drip beneath a stair; a hollow note in a familiar channel; a trickle where there should be none. Someone stops, listens, and calls the others. They shore the wall before the ground gives way. They move the jars, guide the children to higher rooms, and set stones where the runoff can pass without taking the terrace with it.

No one sees a hand in the work. They say the rain came early, or that old Mara heard the channel, or that the wall had been weakening for years and it was fortunate they noticed in time. Each account is true enough.

The power does not prevent every loss. A corner of roof still falls. A path still washes out. The dead are still remembered beneath the weathered stones. The gift is not that the highland will never suffer, but that, when it can be helped, its people will have a little more time to help one another.

And when they make repairs, the power does not guide their hands. It lets the stones be placed by those who know them.

Afterward, the water runs on through the channels. Moss returns to the shaded joints. Children play beside the repaired stair. A door settles in its frame, and somewhere under the paving a drop taps into a basin.

The power remains—not as a ruler, nor a guardian standing apart from the place, but as a patient regard within its continuance. It asks for no shrine. It gives no command. It attends, and when attention alone is not enough, it makes the smallest difference that may let the people keep making the larger one.
