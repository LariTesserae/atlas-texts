The power did not descend.

A descent would have made it another weight upon the plateau, another force to be measured, routed, and incorporated. So it drew near without landing: a quietness at the edge of the Low Heaven, where the darkened fronds met the empty sky.

It watched for a long time.

It did not mistake the turning wheels for misery, or the absence of sunlight for a wound. The Low Heaven had made a life of what it had. Its rhythms were not beautiful because they resembled anyone else’s. They were beautiful because they endured.

The power listened—not for words, but for changes. For the slight tightening of roots around a failing strut. For the heat diverted toward a cold channel. For the pulse that traveled through an elder knot and set a tower’s belts moving. It learned that some cycles were useless only to anyone who did not live inside them.

And it found no plea.

So, at first, it did nothing.

Then it offered something small enough to refuse.

On the plateau’s far rim, where the crust was thinnest, a seam opened in the roof of leaves and metal. Not a wound: a narrow, smooth aperture, no wider than a seed-column. Through it came a single thread of distant light. It did not warm the whole plateau or reveal all its hidden works. It touched one patch of old paving and waited.

The Low Heaven responded as it responded to every disturbance. Roots turned toward the new heat. Leaves tightened. A vent changed direction. Deep beneath the stone, an elder knot sent a pulse through the nearest tower.

The automatic blades rose.

The power did not stop them. It could have, but that would have been an answer in place of a question. Instead, it eased one gear a fraction out of alignment—so gently that no root was crushed, no belt snapped, no furnace went cold. The blades passed above the seed-column and cut nothing.

The stalk stood in the thread of light.

For a long age, nothing more happened. The Low Heaven’s machinery resumed its old motions. The blades descended and lifted. The passageways grew over and opened elsewhere. The seed-column bent toward the warmth, then away, then toward it again. Its pale points brightened faintly.

The power remained.

It did not pour water through the opening. It did not tear the roof away to give the plateau a sky it had never asked for. It did not fill the empty corridors with animals, or silence the furnaces, or command the elders to remember what the works had once been for. It knew that gifts could become invasions when they were too large to refuse.

Instead, the aperture remained: small, steady, and under no command but the Low Heaven’s own.

After a time, roots grew around its edge. They could have sealed it. They did not. A thin mat of leaves formed beneath it, not the hard, dark material of the great terraces but something softer, still uncertain of its use. The next seed-column rose there, beyond the reach of the blades. The machinery adjusted around it.

Perhaps the plateau had understood. Perhaps it had only found a new way to continue. The power did not insist on the difference.

When it finally withdrew, it left no voice behind and no promise that the Low Heaven would become anything other than itself. It left one opening through which a little light could enter, and one small interval in the old machinery where something had been permitted to grow without first proving its usefulness.

The plateau kept working.

But now, in one place, it could choose not to.
