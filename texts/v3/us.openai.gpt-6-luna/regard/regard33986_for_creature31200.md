The power did not mistake the chamber for a wound.

It saw the broken stone, the dead lamps, the pipes entering the earth like old, forgotten intentions. It saw the leaves holding their dark green territories, the roots finding water by patience, the gauge trembling at the smallest change. And it understood that a place could be harsh without being forsaken.

So it did not fill the chamber with sunlight. It did not summon animals, or make the plants hurry, or give the roots a voice they had never needed. It did not ask the quiet to explain itself.

It listened first.

The pipes carried more than warmth. Somewhere beyond the chamber, their metal was thinning; someday, a rupture might send scalding water through the stone, or bitter runoff into the hollows. The power touched that distant weakness—not to restore the machinery, but to make its failure gentler. The pipes would cool slowly when their time came. Their contents would not poison the roots. The rock above would shift, as rock must, but not all at once.

Then, beneath the far wall, it opened a narrow passage for clean water.

Not a spring. Not a stream. Just enough: a slow, clear seep through the mineral seams, arriving when the chamber’s hollows had nearly dried. The old drops continued to fall from the joints, widely spaced and metallic. The new water came without a sound the plants could distinguish from the earth’s own small sounds.

Nothing glowed. Nothing awakened.

Over many seasons, one hollow held water a little longer. A pale root found it. Leaves gathered around the hollow, not spreading farther than the damp allowed. The bare stone remained bare.

The power stayed near—not hovering, not watching for gratitude, but keeping a patient regard on the chamber’s thresholds: too much heat, too little water, a fracture widening toward collapse. When it needed to act, it acted by the smallest measure that would preserve what was already becoming.

And when nothing needed doing, it did nothing.

In the darkness, a bead formed beneath the pipe.

It swelled, let go, and fell into the hollow.

The chamber listened to the sound, and kept changing.
