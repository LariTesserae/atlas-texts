The power did not mistake the chamber’s darkness for emptiness.

It attended first to the smallest thing: a creature pausing with its forelegs on a crystal edge, while the mountain’s beat passed through the floor and into its body. The creature had no name for the beat. It had no name for the pause. Still, the power received both—not as a prayer, but as the whole of a life, complete in its scale.

It could have opened the blister. It could have drawn the creatures into air and sunlight, given them eyes, given them a world large enough to contain the word *far*. But such gifts would have been a kind of harm. Their bodies belonged to pressure and mineral dark. A sky, offered without the means to live beneath it, would not be freedom.

So the power did not enter.

It did not speak. There was no language in the chamber that could carry what it wished to say, and no need to invent one. The creatures were not waiting to be told that they mattered.

For ages, it watched the glassy throat narrow by fractions. The colony shifted its paths. The gas weakened. A cooler place spread from the outer wall, and the pale bodies crowded toward the old warmth, pressing their plates together.

Then, when the contraction reached the point at which the last passage would close, the power touched the stone.

Not enough to stop the mountain. Not enough to undo its making. It softened one grain at the rim of the throat; loosened another. The shard trembled, settled, and left a passage no wider than before—only still open. Through it came the thin breath of heat and gas, carrying the familiar hammering into the crowded dark.

The creatures paused. Across the blister, the pause traveled from body to body. Then the colony resumed its slow traffic, rasping, feeding, sheltering one another in the seams.

They did not know they had been saved. They did not know there had been a choice.

The power stayed with them—not holding the throat open forever, not promising that the chamber would never cool, but keeping watch as the mountain changed. When the time came for the blister to close, it would not call that failure. A life need not last forever to have been wholly worth protecting.

For now, the tiny furnace endured. In the black rock, under the young ridge, the many-legged tide moved through its mineral maze, and the great silence around it was not neglect.

It was room.
