The power noticed the plateau before it noticed the animal.

It took in the long shelves of stone, the hidden water moving through them, the roots binding the bog, the small lives stirring beneath the moss. It felt the weight of every pump and the quiet where pumps had stopped. It understood that the darkness was not a wound. Nothing here was waiting to be made bright.

So it did not bring the sun.

It did not quiet the rain, or smooth the ridges, or give the sumpback eyes. It did not lift her from the water and carry her somewhere safer. She was not lost. She was not asking to be remade.

The power listened to her call through the old pipe.

For a long while, nothing changed. The rain tapped the hollow. The sumpback rested. Far away, another animal answered, and the answer passed through the flooded seams of the plateau as it always had.

Then, at a working pump house beyond the next shelf, a crew member put one hand on a rail and felt the old pipe shiver.

The vibration was faint. It might have been a fault in the machinery, or water shifting underground. The crew member called the others over. They shut down the pump and waited. In the quiet, the pulse came again: low, measured, alive.

They followed it along the rail to a stretch of pipe they had marked for removal. The pipe had been there longer than any of them. Its joints were furred with moss; its buried length crossed a warm channel beneath the bog. They had thought it useless.

The crew changed their plans. They left the pipe where it lay and marked the channel on their route map—not as an obstacle, but as a place where water and animals moved. They listened again before starting the pump.

The power did not tell them what they had heard. It did not need to. From then on, they learned to notice the deep pulses beneath the machinery, and sometimes to wait for them.

At the old hollow, the sumpback lifted her throat from the pipe. She felt no hand upon her, no voice speaking her name. Only the pipe’s familiar shiver, and rain arriving across the plateau.

She answered.

This time, the reply traveled a little farther—not because the world had become gentler, but because someone, somewhere, had learned to listen.
