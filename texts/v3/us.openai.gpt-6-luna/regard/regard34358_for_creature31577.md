The power did not descend into the hollow.

It had no need to. Its attention reached the summit without weight, as warmth reaches a stone through the first thin light of morning. It saw the seam, the old posts, the creatures passing over and under one another. It saw the long-legged grazer waiting at the rim. Most of all, it saw what no single inhabitant could see: that the hollow’s life was not a crowd gathered in one place, but a listening made of many bodies.

The power did not mistake that listening for a request.

For a long while, it did nothing. The dew gathered and vanished. The flowers opened, chimed faintly, and closed. The chorus rose after the wetting, then thinned to a few clicks and rustles. The hollow continued as it had continued before, neither improved nor interrupted by being seen.

But the power remembered it.

That was its first gift: not to make the hidden place famous, or safe from every change, but to hold it in mind without demanding that it become anything else.

And because the power was wise, it understood that memory alone might not be enough. Mountains change. Stones split. Old mechanisms fail. So it made one small provision—not a spring, not an endless summer, not a wall against time. Deep in the summit, beneath the cracks where the smallest creatures sought the last moisture, it eased a single buried seam of stone. The seam would release a little water only when the hollow had gone too long without rain. Never enough to drown the mats or alter the flowers’ brief season. Only enough to carry the life through an interval that would otherwise end it.

The posts were left as they were. Their purpose belonged, perhaps, to another age; their remaining habit had become part of this one. The power did not restore them to their makers’ intention. It merely kept the smallest of their old workings from failing all at once.

The hollow noticed, though it could not know what had happened.

When the next long dry spell came, the stone cooled at night. Beads formed along the seams, fewer than usual, but they did not disappear. A thinness of water appeared in the cracks—so little that no creature could have named it a miracle. The filaments reached it first. The crawlers followed. A flower opened for a breath longer than it had the season before.

Then, from beneath the bowl, came a note the hollow had never made.

It was not loud. It carried no command. It passed through the rock like the memory of a low wingbeat, and every living thing felt it in a different way. Leaves turned. The grazer lifted its narrow head. The smallest residents paused at the edges of their paths.

For one breath, the hollow held still.

Then the chorus answered—not in unison, but together. Clicks from the cracks, raspings from the mats, a faint chiming from the flowers. The note was taken up, changed, and made their own.

The power did not answer again. It did not need to teach them a song, or tell them what they were. It had offered only the knowledge that their sound had reached beyond the stone.

The grazer lowered its head and returned to feeding. The tiny lives resumed their journeys. Above them, the mountain kept its ancient silence, and the sky passed over without knowing what had been sheltered beneath it.

The power’s attention remained—not as a hand closing around the hollow, but as a promise held at a distance: that if the place ever stood at the edge of ending, it would not have to meet that edge unnoticed.
