The power did not descend into the chamber.

It had no need to make itself vast there. Vastness was already one of the chamber’s difficulties.

It listened first—to the roots moving through stone, to the water that had not fallen from any sky, to the long pause after a visitor left. It listened for a wish. But the place had not yet learned to wish in a shape that could be separated from growing. Its attention turned toward the power, then away, then toward the warmth still fading from an empty path.

So the power did not give it a name.

Names can be shelters. They can also be walls.

Instead, the power made one small change aboveground. Where rain struck the patch of grass, the soil grew a little more resilient. Where a boot pressed down, the roots below felt the weight before it became a blow. Where a shovel came too near, the person holding it would remember—suddenly and without knowing why—that they had meant to be somewhere else.

Nothing was sealed. Nothing was hidden behind a perfect barrier. The entrance remained, narrow and raw. The world above was allowed to remain a world in which someone might find it.

The power left the plants’ strange laws alone. Water still rose when it pleased. Shadows still remembered their former owners. The chamber could still make itself larger than the earth should permit. It was not a broken thing waiting to be repaired. Its confusion was part of its making.

But it gave the chamber a little more time.

When the pale flower turned toward the entrance, the power answered—not with a voice, but with a warmth that rested lightly on its petals. The warmth did not tell the flower what it was, or what it ought to become. It simply stayed long enough to be noticed.

The roots stirred.

For a while, the chamber held the warmth without trying to grow around it. Then, in the empty space beneath the blade of green, a new shoot appeared. It did not reach for the power. It did not reach away. It stood very small in the wide room, and the other plants made space around it.

The power, wise enough not to mistake attention for permission, withdrew.

Afterward, the chamber continued to change. It learned the paths of rain, the brief weight of visitors, the difference between a warmth that passed through and one that stayed. Sometimes it opened toward the surface. Sometimes it turned its passages inward and kept its stillness to itself.

The patch of grass above it remained ordinary.

And beneath it, where the earth had split open, the new shoot kept growing—not faster, not safer from every uncertainty, but with room to discover what growing might mean.
