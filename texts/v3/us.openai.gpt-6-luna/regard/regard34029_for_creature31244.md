The power did not descend through the crack.

Light was not a road, and the crack was not a door. The mountain had no need to be entered. It was already known: not as a thing to be owned or mended, but as a becoming, immense and unfinished, whose pressure and heat and fractures were part of its making.

So the power attended.

It felt the chamber’s roar, and beneath it the small note that continued after each shudder. It felt the grazers gripping their threads, the plant folded against the current, the cup holding its little skin of mineral. It felt the inhabitant spread through stone and gather again in water, tending what it could not name.

The power understood that the chamber was dangerous. It also understood that danger was not the same as wrongness.

It did not still the water. The water had work to do. It did not close the seams or cool the warm corner, or make the mountain finished. And it did not lift the inhabitant out of its hidden hollow to show it a wider world. A thing can be sheltered by the world it has, even when that world is small.

Instead, at the next great shift, when the mountain drew its weight through the chamber, the power made room for one breath.

The pressure came on. The walls began to ring. Gravel rose from the floor, and the current gathered itself into a hard, turning fist.

Then, for the length of a single pulse, the surge passed around the chamber instead of through it.

The loose grains settled. The plant opened one frond. The translucent grazers turned on their threads, catching the light. The clay cup remained where it was.

The inhabitant lifted its ridged side from the floor.

It sent a note into the stone—not a question, exactly, and not a greeting. The power did not answer in words. It answered by holding the pause open a little longer, then letting it close.

The creature listened to the returning roar. It listened through the stones, the fiber, the cup. It did not follow the power’s attention beyond the mountain. It had no need to. It turned, slowly, and placed itself once more against the floor.

That was its answer.

The power did not mistake it for consent to be changed. It did not give the creature a name, or a purpose, or a memory it had not already carried. It left the chamber to its own rules: the crooked paths of dropped things, the vanishing bubbles, the pressure that entered like darkness.

But from then on, when the mountain shifted in a way that would have crushed the hollow outright, the power held the neighboring stone—not against its becoming, only long enough for the chamber to flex. The mountain still rose. Seams still opened and closed. New grains still arrived. The water still roared.

And sometimes, when the current grew too violent for the smallest lives, a single breath of stillness passed through.

The inhabitant never saw the power. It may not have known there was anyone to see. It only learned that, now and then, the mountain’s great note contained a gentler one: not command, not warning, but room.

It kept tending what had been left in its care. The three stones. The braided fiber. The cup with its patient rim. The living things that held fast.

And the wise power, having found that this was what the inhabitant chose, did not choose for it.
