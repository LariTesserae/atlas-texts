The power did not arrive as light. Light was already scarce, and the hollow had learned to live without much of it.

It came as a change in pressure: so slight that the stalks only tightened their scales, and the pale grazers paused together. Beneath the floor, the great hidden body stopped moving.

The power listened.

It listened to the stalks drawing bitter vapor from the air, to the little creatures beneath the orange crust, to the loose grit whispering down the plates. It listened for thirst and found none. It listened for loneliness and could not be sure what that meant here. The hollow was full of signals, but not all signals were pleas.

Most of all, it listened to the being under the ground. It did not mistake its weight for anger, or its silence for fear. It felt the slow passage beneath the plates: a life belonging to heat, mineral, pressure, and dark. The being did not ask to be brought into the open. It did not ask for water, or coolness, or an easier world.

So the power did not give these things.

It did not clear the dust from the air or soften the black stone. It did not make the plants flower longer, still the wind, or quiet the animals. The hollow’s upheavals were not a wound. Its brief blooms and sudden cracks were part of how it breathed.

But the power saw one deep seam, older than the rim, beginning to fail. If it split all at once, the floor would fall inward. The buried being might be crushed; the root-nets torn loose; the hollow’s careful exchanges undone. The power touched the seam—not to close it, but to turn its pressure aside.

When the ground rose, it rose in a blister no larger than before. When it cracked, the glittering grains spilled out in a bright, harmless rush. The grazers fled, then returned. The stalks bent away and lifted again. Beneath them, the great body continued its slow passage.

For a moment, the hidden being felt the power there: not a command, not a hand upon it, but an immense attention that asked nothing. It shifted once beneath the floor. The plates answered with a low, resonant thrum.

The power accepted this as enough.

At the rim, it left the old stones where they were and let the worn spirals remain worn. It did not restore the posts or rebuild the windbreak. Instead, it made the hollow difficult to find from far away—not invisible, only easy to overlook by anyone who came seeking to take more than the brittle seed-cases.

Then it withdrew from the pressure of the air.

The hollow returned to itself. The dust roared softly. The clicking creatures resumed their dry conversation beneath the stone knobs. A stalk began, slowly, to swell.

Nothing there knew it had been saved. Nothing needed to. The power had not made the place into something kinder by its own measure. It had learned what kindness meant there: to keep the old rim firm, to let the world go on changing, and to leave its strange inhabitant free in the dark it called home.
