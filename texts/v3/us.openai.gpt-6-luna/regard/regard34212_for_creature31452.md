The power noticed the city.

It did not mistake its stillness for sleep, or its emptiness for grief. It understood that a place could be whole without being complete in the way a visitor expected; that the residents were not trapped inside the city, but were the city’s ways of attending to itself.

So it did not send birds to the roofs, or open the clouds below, or make a road down to the lands where ordinary rivers ran. It did not break the rules that held the terraces in their impossible balance. Such gifts would have been answers to questions no one had asked.

Instead, it listened.

For the first time, the residents heard the whole of their own sound—not only the roar beneath the bridges or the notes caught by particular arches, but the immense chord as it traveled through every stair and channel, every pool and hidden cistern. They heard how each part depended on the others. They heard, too, the attention of the power: not as a voice above the water, but as a stillness within it, wide enough to receive them.

The city had no word for being witnessed. It answered as it knew how. Water climbed a channel that had never carried it before, passing over three terraces and ending in a shallow basin beneath the highest garden. The basin filled, but did not overflow. In its surface appeared a sky the city had never seen: not the bright sky overhead, nor the cloud-sea below, but a far-off, ordinary evening, darkening at the edge of a world.

The power understood. It did not pull the city toward that world. It did not bring the world into the city. It left the reflected sky where it was, a view held in water, and let the residents decide what it meant.

For a long time, nothing changed. The note of the wind returned to its accustomed place. The channels resumed their courses. Mist remained beneath the bridges.

Then, in the highest garden, a door opened onto a landing that had never been there before. It led nowhere unless someone wished it to. Beyond it lay no promised rescue, no command to become something other than the city. Only the possibility of an answer from beyond its own vast chord.

The power left the choice with them.

And because it was wise, it did not require them to make it.
