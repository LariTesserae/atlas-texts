The power did not bring the sun.

It knew that light can be a kind of violence when it arrives without warning. It knew, too, that darkness was not the same as absence. The plain was crowded with lives that had learned one another by pressure, taste, warmth, and the patience of touch. To flood it with brightness would not have made it more real. It might only have made it strange.

So the power waited.

It listened to the rain on the mineral crust, to the engines turning their old circles, to the innumerable feet moving through mud. It listened beneath those sounds, where the earth shifted too slowly for most lives to notice. And when the heavy knock came again from under the water, the power did not silence it.

It answered.

Not with a voice. Not with a command. A small pressure traveled through the ground: one gentle pulse, then another, separated by a pause long enough to leave room for refusal.

Everything stopped.

The little bodies beneath the crust held still. The larger creatures settled their weight into the mud. Even the water seemed, for a moment, to forget which way it had been going. The machines continued their turning, but the power did not mistake their rhythm for an answer.

Far below, something shifted.

The reply was so slow that it might have been a change in the shape of the land. Yet the power felt it: a single, deliberate return of pressure, faint as a hand laid against a wall from the other side.

The power answered once more, at the same distance, with the same patience.

Then it waited again.

Nothing was healed in that moment. No ridge softened. No channel found its final course. The engines did not stop, and the dark did not open. The plain remained warm, wet, and difficult to know.

But from then on, the knock beneath the water was not entirely alone.

The answer did not tell the hidden thing what to do. It did not ask it to rise, or explain itself, or become legible. It offered only this: *I can feel that you are here. If you wish, you may answer.*

Sometimes, after a long silence, the thing did.

Sometimes it did not.

The smaller lives learned the new interval and gathered near it. The larger creatures felt the answering pressure through their broad bodies and made room around the places where it passed. The machines kept their slow rotations. Their tiny inhabitants continued to ride the gears and tap at the chains. Rain kept filling the hollows.

And the power remained—not as a watcher above the plain, but as a quiet attention within its many exchanges. It guarded the space between a call and its answer. It let the inhabitants keep their darkness, their strange distances, their own ways of being alive.

It did nothing more unless invited.

In that place, this was not nothing.
