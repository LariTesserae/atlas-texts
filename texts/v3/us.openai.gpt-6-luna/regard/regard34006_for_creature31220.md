The power did not descend upon the Hush.

It did not bring rain, or soften the stone, or quiet the wind. It did not make the old inhabitant speak in words, or give it a face that could be recognized. The Hush had not asked to be remade. Its long patience was not a wound.

The power listened.

It listened through one night, then through a season, then through the slow turn of years. It learned the differences between the fronds’ voices: the bright, brief complaint of a gust; the deep chord that traveled beneath a basin; the murmurs of colonies answering one another over distances no human could cross in a day. It learned that a silence might be fear, or rest, or simply the space between one answer and the next.

When it understood this much, it offered a sound of its own.

Not a command. Not a warning. One low note, set gently beneath the stone, where it could be refused.

The note traveled slowly through the plateau. The small runners vanished into their burrows. The grazers stopped with their heads lowered. In the settlements, people stilled their instruments, uncertain whether this was a new tremor or an old one returned. Beneath them, the root-lattices received the note and passed it on.

The Hush did not answer at once.

The power waited. It had made mountains and watched them wear away. It could wait.

At last, from the far side of a shallow basin, a colony returned the note—not exactly, but altered: roughened by mineral seams, lengthened by the roots’ passage through the dark. Another colony took it up. Then another. The sound crossed the plateau in a slow, uneven wave, carrying no name and making no promise.

The power heard welcome in it. Or permission to remain. It did not insist on knowing which.

After that, nothing visible changed. The wind still beat against the fronds. The animals still followed their ancient trails. The deep vibrations still came, and the ground still shifted by finger-widths while the settlements shuttered their lights.

But sometimes, when the plateau’s own music had traveled as far as it could, another note waited beyond it—not louder, not wiser than the Hush, only present. The roots could answer, or ignore it. They were never hurried.

And if, in some distant age, the plateau faced a danger it could not meet alone, the power would hear. It would not spare the Hush every change. Change was part of its life. But it would not let the Hush be silenced for another’s convenience.

For now, the power did what the wise sometimes do when they meet a life unlike their own:

It stayed near.

It listened.

And it let the answer take as long as it needed.
