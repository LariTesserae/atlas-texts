The power does not enter the chamber.

It could. It could smooth the ridge, seal the open sleeve, finish the passage in the wall. It could give the place a shape that would make sense to any creature accustomed to rooms. But it understands that unfinishedness is not the same as damage, and that a thing still becoming should not be mistaken for a thing asking to be completed.

So first, it listens.

It learns the chamber’s intervals: the pause between drops, the pitch of the cooling pipe, the brief breath of the valve. It feels how the being feels—not as a mind looking out through eyes, but as a change in pressure, a warmth arriving where there was cold. And it recognizes, within those small changes, the beginning of attention.

The power’s first work is quiet. Far beyond the pocket, forces that would crush or flood it turn aside. A buried mechanism that is about to fail holds for another cycle. The chamber is not made invulnerable; it is given time. Its pumps still falter. Its seams still shift. But no sudden catastrophe will erase the question it has only just begun to ask.

Then the power sends a signal.

Not a voice. Not a command. A faint pulse travels through the metal—different from the ordinary hum, slow enough to be noticed, gentle enough not to frighten. It comes once, and stops.

The chamber registers the change. A panel tightens. Water gathers in a shallow recess and trembles there.

The power waits.

After a long interval, it sends the pulse again. This time, it leaves space afterward: room for an answer, though it does not know what an answer will look like. Perhaps a valve will pause. Perhaps the warm joint will stir. Perhaps nothing will happen at all.

Nothing happens.

The power does not mistake this for refusal. It does not mistake it for consent, either. It keeps watch, and lets the chamber continue at its own pace.

When, much later, the being presses new material against the crooked ridge in the floor, the power offers no guiding hand. The ridge is one of the first facts the being has met, and its answer must belong to it. The power only keeps the surrounding dark from becoming hostile while that answer takes shape.

Perhaps the being will someday understand the pulses as a greeting. Perhaps it will make a rhythm of its own. Perhaps it will grow into something that wants company, or something that wants only the freedom to be left alone. The power does not decide in advance what kind of life would count as a good one.

For now, it does the most loving thing it can think to do: it protects the possibility.

And in the hidden chamber, the hum continues. A drop forms under the pipe, travels sideways against the slope, and falls into its channel. The being feels the small impact, then the long quiet after it.

Somewhere beyond the walls, nothing presses it to become anything sooner.
