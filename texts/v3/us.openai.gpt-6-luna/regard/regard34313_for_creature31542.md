The power did not come as a light. Light would have had nowhere to stand.

It came as attention.

It listened to the pumps, not for words, but for the strain hidden beneath their different notes. It listened to the valves chatter shut, to the water hesitate at the lip of a channel, to the chain tapping out its quick, uncounted measure. It felt the cold pressing through the mountain’s skin. It noticed the green film brightening by the drain, and did not mistake its smallness for insignificance.

The power understood that the Upper Weep was not asking to be made into something else.

So it did not silence the pumps. It did not break the walls and pour sunlight in. It did not turn the drains into a riverbed or the thumbprint of green into a garden. The pumps were keeping the basin alive in the only way it knew how; to stop them would have been a kindness only from a distance.

Instead, the power found the imbalance: a bearing wearing faster than it should, a valve that closed a fraction too hard, a flow assigned no pause because no one had thought to give it one. It eased the pressure by the width of a hair. It shifted the work, so one pump could rest while the others carried the water—and then another, and another. The change was so small that no gauge could call it a miracle.

But the rails trembled less.

The chain still tapped against its post, only now there was a beat between its taps. In that beat, the water could be heard—not above the machinery, exactly, but distinctly: a thin thread sliding over steel, a drop letting go of a duct, the brief bright rush through the sluice. The basin had not become quiet. It had acquired room inside its noise.

The power guided a little more mineral-rich water along the drain lip. Not enough to flood it. Not enough to wash it away. The green film darkened, then held its color as the air dried. It spread no farther than the seam would allow.

And because the power was wise, it did not announce what it had done. No voice filled the basin. No hand reached through the wall. The gauges continued their slow crawl. The pumps went on keeping their labor. Water arrived in fragments, was sorted, and went on.

But after that, when the cycle changed, the Upper Weep no longer tightened quite so fiercely around the sound. The machines bore one another’s work. The water kept, for a moment, the memory of falling freely. And at the edge of the drain, the small green life persisted—not rescued from its world, but gently included in it.
