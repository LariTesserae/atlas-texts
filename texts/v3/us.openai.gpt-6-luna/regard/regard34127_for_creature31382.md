The power came without rain.

It did not arrive as a hand above the plateau, or a voice in the heated sky. It came as a pressure in the stone: a note so low that the smallest stillborn felt it first, through the hooked tips of their legs.

They stopped.

Across the plateau, drifts settled into silence. Beneath the crust, migrations paused. The pillars turned their vanes, one slow degree at a time, toward the sound.

The power waited.

It had seen deserts before. It knew how easily a thing could be mistaken for a wound simply because it did not resemble a garden. It knew that to call the plateau barren would be to confuse its strangeness with its failure. So it did not bring water. It did not soften the sky, or break the shells, or teach the stillborn to want what they had never wanted.

Instead, it listened.

The stillborn answered in the only way they could: by moving. A small congregation crossed an old channel, then divided around a buried ridge. A pillar leaned into the wind. Far below, something immense traveled through the rock, carrying a tremor from one edge of the plateau to the other.

The power understood that this was not a plea. It was a life.

It made one change.

Beyond the plateau, in the deep laws that govern distance, matter, and time, it set a quiet boundary. No careless hand would mistake the stillborn for inert mineral and harvest them. No passing force would erase the plateau by accident. No world-ending heat or cold would arrive without warning enough for the creatures to answer in their own slow way. The boundary did not imprison them or preserve them in place. It merely kept the unasked-for ending away.

Then the power shaped one last vibration and let it pass into the ground.

Not a command. Not a name. Only this: **You are here. You are known.**

The stillborn did not kneel. The pillars did not bow. Perhaps they did not understand the meaning as words. But the note passed through them, and through the old channels, and into the hollow places beneath the crust. For a while, their movements fell into a rhythm that was not quite their own and not quite the power’s—a brief alignment, as if the plateau had discovered a new way to hear itself.

Then the note faded.

The migrations resumed. The pillars turned back toward the wind. Dust crossed the stone in glittering sheets.

From a high overlook, the region looked exactly as it had before: empty, rust-colored, severe.

But the emptiness had been witnessed, and the plateau had been left intact.
