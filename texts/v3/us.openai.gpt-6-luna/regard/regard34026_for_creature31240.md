The power did not mistake motion for distress.

It had watched worlds beg for rescue in the only ways worlds could: collapsing inward, burning their atmospheres away, breaking their moons. The high country did none of these. It shifted, settled, broke, and smoothed itself. The great inhabitant moved with it. What looked, from a distance, like a world spending itself down was also a way of living.

So the power did not descend.

It listened.

At first it listened for sound, because sound was what it knew how to hear. There was almost none. Then it attended to the ground: the long pressures traveling through buried seams, the small shiver that brought the pale creatures from their fissures, the slight changes in the great inhabitant’s course. It listened for a very long time, until duration itself ceased to seem like a distance.

At last it understood that the high country was not silent. It was speaking slowly.

The power sent no voice in return. It made a small alteration in the dark between the stars: not the familiar distortion, but a pause within it. A question, as modest as it could make one.

Nothing answered.

The power waited.

Years passed. Dust moved. A basin tilted. The pale creatures vanished into a fissure and emerged, much later, beneath another ridge. The great shape traveled under the crust, feeling its way through pressures too broad for any ordinary mind to follow. It came near the old anchors and slowed.

Then, along one buried seam, a tremor passed—too regular to be natural, too gentle to threaten.

The inhabitant stopped.

The power offered no command, no promise to mend the high country or make it still. It showed, instead, the shapes of three possibilities: the land unchanged; the land held fast; the land allowed to move, but with some of its sharpest stresses carried elsewhere. It showed them not as images, which would have meant nothing here, but as pressures: a hard sudden break, a terrible stillness, a long distributed easing.

The answer did not come quickly. Perhaps it was not an answer at all. Perhaps it was simply the way the great inhabitant leaned toward the third pressure and remained there.

The power did not presume certainty. It repeated the offer after another turning of the highlands.

Again, the great inhabitant leaned toward the easing.

Then the power acted.

It touched the anchors—not to make them stronger than the mountains, nor to fasten the crust in place, but to let them speak to one another. The old devices had been made to distribute strain. Some had gone quiet; some had forgotten the paths between them. The power restored those paths. It gave each anchor a little more time to answer its neighbors, and gave the land a little more room to carry what it had always carried.

The next great movement came.

A ridge folded. A plain opened. Dust rose and streamed uphill beneath the dark. The ground trembled over a breadth no eye could measure. But the strain traveled outward, passed through the old rings, and thinned across the high country. A catastrophe became a change. Not a small change—never that—but one the land could take into itself.

The pale creatures came out of their cracks. The dark mats, where they had survived, opened against the newly exposed stone. Far below, the great inhabitant moved on.

It did not turn toward the stars. It did not offer thanks. It may not have understood that anything had been done.

The power accepted this. Gratitude was not the price of care, and recognition was not proof that care had mattered.

It left the anchors to their work and withdrew its attention just enough for the high country to be itself again. It did not promise that no ridge would ever fall, or that no living thing would be lost in the dust. It did not mistake survival for safety, or stillness for peace.

Afterward, on certain nights, the thin distortion still crossed the stars.

And sometimes, beneath it, the ground answered—not in words, not even in a signal any other world could have recognized, but in a long, low easing that traveled from one buried ring to the next.

The power listened.

The high country went on changing.
