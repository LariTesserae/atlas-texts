The power came without descending.

There was no figure on the path, no voice in the thunder. Only, for a little while, the whole shelf seemed to be held in a great and patient attention. The water kept moving. The birds kept to their nests. The fox paused at the rim, ears lifted, then went on.

The keeper felt it while clearing grit from the channel. They stopped with one hand in the cold water.

“Is something wrong?” they asked.

The cloud thinned—not enough to show the world below, only enough for a strip of pale light to lie across the basin. The keeper looked at it, then at the shelter, the ledges, the cliff’s dark pockets.

“Nothing,” they said at last. “Nothing that needs changing.”

The power understood that this was not the same as wanting nothing.

It understood the difference between hardship that had become a way of life and hardship that was only needless harm. It saw the keeper’s cracked hands, the roof that needed mending, the careful routes of every creature on the shelf. It saw, too, that a mountain remade for comfort would no longer be this mountain—and that care, offered without listening, could become another kind of trespass.

So it did not soften the wind or fill the chasm. It did not call the fox nearer, or make the birds tame, or turn the keeper’s shelter into a palace.

Instead, it waited until the keeper had finished the channel and sat down at the shelter door. Then it let the light remain for a little longer than the clouds should have allowed.

The warmth reached the keeper’s hands. Not enough to banish the cold, but enough to ease the ache in their knuckles. The birds began calling again. Somewhere beyond the wall, water fell in its usual hidden course.

The keeper looked up.

“If you’re asking whether I need help,” they said, “I do. Sometimes. But not help that takes this place away from itself.”

The light slipped from the basin. The cloud closed, gently as a door.

Nothing more was promised. Nothing was made easy forever. Yet from that day on, when the keeper had done all that could be done and still lay awake listening to the weather, the shelf no longer felt quite so empty. The mountain remained immense. The work remained small and constant.

But the work was seen.
