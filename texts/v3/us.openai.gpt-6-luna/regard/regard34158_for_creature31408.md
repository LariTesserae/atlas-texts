The power came looking for the one who lived in the hollow rooms.

It did not descend in a pillar of fire, or arrive with a herald. It came as a pause: one breath in which the leaves did not clap, the insects did not rasp, and the roots beneath the paving did not knock against stone.

Every inhabitant noticed.

Under the streets, the many-jointed things stopped in their channels. On the roofs, birds lifted their heads. In the narrow dark between two walls, a pale, long-bodied creature stood with its paws braced against the bark. It was old—not in years alone, but in the way of something that had outlasted several names for the place.

The power found it in an empty room.

The creature sat in the middle of the bare floor. Around it, roots had grown through the walls and stopped short of the threshold. A shallow pool held the last light. The room was not empty, the power understood. It was being kept.

“I have noticed you,” said the power.

The creature’s whiskers stirred. It did not answer.

“I have noticed what you shelter. I can quiet the noise, straighten the roads, hold the walls against time. I can make the rooms easier to find.”

At this, the creature rose and walked to the doorway. It passed through. From the next room, it looked back.

The power understood that the creature was not asking for any of those things.

So it listened again—not to the district as a single sound, but to its particulars: wingbeats, claw taps, seed husks snapping; the subterranean traffic branching and rejoining; the many small lives making room for one another without ever gathering under one name.

“What would you have me do?” asked the power.

The creature stepped aside.

It was not permission to enter. It was simply room enough for the power to see the threshold clearly.

The power saw that the creature did not keep the rooms empty by force. It did not command the plants to stop, or the birds to leave. It tended the possibility of emptiness: a space no one had to occupy, a pause in which a frightened thing could hide, a place where the district could be more than the sum of its needs.

Then the power did the one thing it could do without making the place less itself.

It made a promise, and anchored it beneath the oldest stone: no hand from beyond the district would be allowed to claim the hollow rooms as vacant. No one would be permitted to call the inhabitants vermin, or the roots ruin, or the strange turns of the streets an error to be corrected. The place might change. It might weather, flower, fold, and grow. But it would not be taken simply because its life did not look like ownership.

The creature touched its nose to the stone.

The power did not ask for thanks. It left by the doorway it had entered, though the doorway was not where it had first appeared.

Afterward, the drumming underfoot resumed. The leaves began to clap. A flock crossed the courtyard in a bright, unruly rush, and the old creature slipped between the walls.

Nothing in the district became quieter, or clearer, or easier to map.

But from then on, when something came looking for an empty place to possess, it found the doors leading elsewhere.
