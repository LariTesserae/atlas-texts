The power did not descend.

It had no need to make an entrance. The hollow was already full of arrivals: spores, feet, roots, the ceaseless fall of dust. To add another noise would be to mistake being noticed for being welcomed.

So it listened.

It listened long enough to learn that the ground’s groan was not a voice, though it carried more lives than any voice could. It learned the difference between a seed-knot waking and one splitting in pain; between the ordinary looseness of a walkway and the tremor that meant the floor beneath it was going soft. It learned the Listeners’ small cautions, and their courtesies. It learned that a pause could be an answer, and that an answer did not always mean agreement.

Then, one day, a child felt a shiver beneath the shelter floor.

Not the familiar shiver of growing roots. Not the distant drum of falling stone. Something finer: a quick, deliberate pattern, repeated twice, then left alone.

The child put both palms down.

The pattern came again.

They called an adult. Together they tapped the nearest hollow stem: a question, not a word. The answering tremor moved through the woven growth, crossed the warm stone, and returned—not as an imitation, but with the same patient space between its beats.

The Listeners waited.

Nothing demanded that they name it. Nothing pressed a thought into their minds or bent their knees. The blue membranes at their throats opened and closed in the dimness. At last, the oldest among them tapped a pattern meaning, *Are you there?*

The ground answered.

They did not decide that the ground had become a person. They knew better than to give a name too quickly to something vast. But from then on, when they made that pattern, sometimes an answer came: steady, unmistakable, and never louder than it needed to be.

The power could have made the hollow still. It could have stopped the walls from swelling, the floors from shifting, the hungry creatures from taking what they found. It could have poured water through the stone until thirst became a forgotten word.

It did none of these things.

The hollow was becoming. To end its becoming would have been a kind of death. And the Listeners had not asked to be made into rulers of it.

But once, much later, the power heard a sound the Listeners had not yet learned to hear: a deep fracture traveling beneath a nursery platform, too broad to be stopped by moving a few shelters. The power did not hold the stone together. It did not silence the growth. It sent the old pattern—twice, then a pause—through every connected stalk within reach.

The Listeners woke. They listened. They felt the wrongness in the floor and loosened their ties. They carried the young to higher shelves. They made room.

The platform fell after they had gone.

For a time, the people stood at the edge of the new gap, feeling the raw warmth rise from below. Then someone tapped the answering pattern against a stem.

The reply came.

No one thanked the power as if it had performed a miracle. They thanked it as one voice thanks another for calling in time.

After that, they sometimes asked questions. The power answered when it could, and did not pretend when it could not. It warned them of certain fractures, but not of every danger; it would not make their listening unnecessary. It never marked a path as theirs forever. It never asked to be worshiped, and when the Listeners tried to give it a name, it offered no preference.

So they called it, for a while, **the One That Waits**.

And the hollow went on growing.

The power remained—not as a hand laid over the world, but as a presence that had learned how to answer without taking the next turn.
