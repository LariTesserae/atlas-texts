The power did not arrive as light. Light would have been only another disturbance in the water.

It came as attention.

It listened to the plateau’s hollow note and felt the pressure moving through the stone: the sea leaning in, withdrawing, finding its way through every seam. It saw the small lives carried from shelter to shelter, and the broad plated creature resting in the lower basin, its feelers testing the changes before they came.

The power could have stilled the currents. It could have mended the broken walls and set every fallen slab back in its place. For a moment it imagined the plateau made whole, its passages dry and bright, its old design revealed.

Then it understood that stillness would not be peace here. The water was not an intruder. It was the life of the place.

So the power did almost nothing.

In one lower basin, where the current sometimes turned too sharply, it eased the pressure—not enough to stop the water, only enough to soften its sudden reversals. The basin still filled and emptied. The stone still wore away. But for a little while, a creature could rest there without being dragged from its shelter at every change of tide.

The plated being felt the difference. Its feelers lifted toward the quiet in the water. It remained for a time, then left, as it always had. Later, it came back.

The power did not make the basin its home, or the creature its charge. It did not teach the animals what the ruins had once been, or tell the stone that it was beautiful. It made no mark that could be mistaken for a command.

It simply kept listening.

And when the plateau shuddered beneath the sea, there was, within its vast breathing, one small place where the water was kind.
