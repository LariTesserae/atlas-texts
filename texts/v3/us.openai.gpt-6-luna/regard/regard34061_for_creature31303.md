The power did not arrive.

There was no radiance in the seam, no voice traveling through the water, no sudden understanding in the pale animals grazing the walls. Nothing in the chamber had asked to be found. Nothing could have known what asking was.

The power simply attended.

It attended to the tube whose mouth had nearly been covered by a settling shell, and to the soft creature inside, still holding fast. It attended to the tiny current that carried food through the seam, to the fronds bending in the upper hollow, to the scavenger feeling its way along a familiar dark route. It saw not a city waiting to be saved, but a multitude of lives making a world out of what they could hold.

The power knew that care could become a kind of harm. It could widen the chamber until its inhabitants were exposed to currents they had never survived. It could lift every shell, smooth every passage, spare each creature from hunger and pressure—and, in doing so, erase the conditions by which they had become themselves. It could preserve the city exactly as it was, which would mean preserving it against its own life.

So it did not make the chamber safe. It did not stop the creatures from crowding one another, or the tubes from growing over their neighbors. It did not promise the city would last forever.

But where the black stone pressed toward the seam, the power eased its pressure—not enough to change the shape of the chamber, only enough that the narrow blue passage did not close. A grain of silt that might have lodged there drifted on. Water entered, then left, carrying its faint offerings into the dark.

The inhabitants did not know that anything had happened.

A crown opened at the mouth of a pale tube. A frond turned in the current. The translucent creature beneath the shell arch stirred its many small limbs, and a scavenger slipped past without touching it. Somewhere, a new rim formed around an unfinished doorway. Somewhere else, a passage narrowed.

The power remained, not as a wall around the city, but as a patient attention beside it: ready to act if the breath of the place were truly taken away, unwilling to confuse every change with a wound. It would not demand that the lives within become grateful, or even aware. Their small world was not a message to it. It was a world.

For now, it let the water breathe.
