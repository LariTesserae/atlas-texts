The power does not descend like a sun.

It has no need to make the water part, or the stone kneel, or the creatures look up. It attends first to what is already happening: the film laying its first skin over the basalt; the swarm passing its blue message from body to body; the eels feeling the current through the narrow dark. It attends to the summit not as a possession, nor as a problem to be solved, but as a place in the midst of becoming.

And so it does not quiet the vents. Their heat is not a wound. It does not smooth the sharp ledges or fill the abyss with light. The edge belongs to the summit as much as the shelter does. Predators will still pass. Stone will still split. Some lives will be lost when the ridge changes, and no kindness could make a living world without loss.

The power’s first act is restraint.

Its second is so small that no creature could name it. When a seam opens, it opens by degrees. When a current shifts, it carries warning before it carries danger. The blue flashes remain visible through the crowded water. The paths between hollows do not all close at once. Nothing is spared change; the living are given room to answer it.

At the brink, a few of the small fish turn outward and go down. The others remain gathered along the ledge. The power does not call them after, or command them to stay. It lets the deep remain deep, and the summit remain a summit.

Then it withdraws—or perhaps it was never elsewhere.

For a while, the animals move as they moved before: feeding, sheltering, flashing, waiting. The ridge cools by fractions. Pale mineral gathers along a crack. In the dark below, something answers the swarm’s blue signal with a light of its own.

The power is pleased, though nothing has been made safe.

The summit is not saved from being what it is. It is met there, wholly: crowded, unfinished, alive.
