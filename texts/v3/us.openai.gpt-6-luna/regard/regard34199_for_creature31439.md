The power did not arrive as light.

Light would have been a change, and the hollow had not asked to be changed.

It was simply there: an attention broad enough to include the basin, the far ridge, the ring with dust resting on its inner wall—and the measures, gathered wherever distance had begun to feel occupied. The attention did not try to name them. It did not mistake their silence for emptiness, or their stillness for a plea.

For a long while, it watched.

It saw that the measures were not suffering. It saw, too, that this did not make them lesser or unworthy of care. The hollow’s balance was not a problem waiting to be solved. Its disagreements were not wounds. The ring did not need mending; the dry basin did not need water; the shadows did not need to be taught where they belonged.

So the power changed nothing.

Then it made one promise—not to the place, which might not want promises, but to itself: no one would be allowed to turn this hollow into a possession without first having time to understand what they were touching.

The promise left no mark on the stone. It did not close the way in, or hide the mountain more deeply. Anyone might still find the hollow. Anyone might still enter. But those who came intending to take, measure, or remake it would find their certainty loosening before their hands could act. They would have to pause. They would have to notice the dust, the displaced breath, the shadow that did not quite follow. They would have to ask themselves whether the place had agreed.

The measures gave no answer. Or perhaps their answer was the same as before: the ridge remained distant, the instruments disagreed, and the basin held its balance.

The power accepted this.

It withdrew without departing, leaving the hollow as it had found it: cold, exact, and free to remain strange.
