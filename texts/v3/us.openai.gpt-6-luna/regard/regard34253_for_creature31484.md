The power did not descend into the basin.

It came near enough to know it—not merely the shape of the stones, but the minute life in their seams; not merely the creature’s stillness, but the difference between waiting and fear. It watched a bead gather beneath the ledge, lengthen, and fall. It watched the animal draw in its edges, then, when the water was quiet again, ease one pale limb forward.

The power understood that the basin was not empty because it was quiet. It understood that the creature was not lost because it had no name, and not suffering simply because its world was small. The mineral ridges were not wounds. The old shelter was not a plea. The creature did not need to be made more like anything else.

So the power did not fill the pools with color. It did not raise a forest from the stone or give the animal eyes, a voice, or a guardian’s purpose. It did not hurry the water, or command the basin to become a place of greater abundance. Such gifts might have been wonders. They would not necessarily have been kindness.

It left the basin as it was.

But it did not leave care behind. The power held the place in its attention—not to keep every stone forever fixed, but to know it, should the balance ever be broken by something needless and cruel. It would not prevent weather, age, or the slow rearrangement of living things. Those belonged to the world. But it would not let the basin be erased merely because no one had thought to notice it.

For a long while, nothing changed. A drop fell. The animal moved the width of a hand. The dim light rested on the water.

Then the power went on, carrying the basin not away from itself, but with it: small, quiet, and fully seen.
