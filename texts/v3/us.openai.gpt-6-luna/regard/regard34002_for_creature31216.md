The power did not begin by changing anything.

It watched the basin through a hundred small changes: a stair made impassable, a gutter opened beneath a kitchen, a child lifted laughing from a flooded game court. It watched the people move their lives by hand, and learned that what looked like disorder from afar had its own customs. The water was not their enemy. The people were not waiting to be rescued.

So it did not still the pools, straighten the passages, or build them a wall. It did not make the cliffs generous with soil, or fill the town with iron. It understood that a fixed basin would be no kindness to those who had learned to live by noticing what moved.

It listened instead.

For a long while, it could not tell the ordinary rearrangements from the dangerous ones. The people could. They read the basin in feathers and drafts, in the posture of birds, in the silence of insects. Their knowledge was intricate, and the power did not mistake its own strength for wisdom.

Then, one night, the signs failed.

Water gathered beneath the lowest houses, not flowing in any direction the residents knew. The drains gave no breath. The birds slept on. A woman woke because the floor beneath her palm had gone strangely warm. She sat up, listening. Across the terrace, someone else woke too.

The power could have held the water back. Instead, it gave the stone one clear, low note.

The sound passed beneath the houses and through the bridges. It was not loud, but every person felt it in their feet. It came once, then again: the interval between the notes the length of a held breath.

The people were on their feet before the third.

They did not know who had sounded the warning. They did know what a warning was. Children were lifted to upper platforms; food and tools were hauled from low rooms; a passage was opened that had been bricked shut for years. When the water came, it took the lower houses and poured through the empty spaces where people had been sleeping.

By morning, the basin had rearranged itself. A familiar stair was gone. A bridge leaned against a wall. In one courtyard, fish circled a cooking pot.

The people counted one another. Then they began to make another way through.

They found the warm stone later, in a niche above the new waterline. It had no mark on it. It never sounded again. The people set it among the other strange things the basin had left them, and went on with their work.

The power did not ask them to name it, or thank it, or build a place for it. It had not saved them from their world. It had helped them hear their world in time.

After that, it remained attentive. Most days it did nothing at all. It let the water choose its courses, and the people choose how to meet them. And sometimes, when the basin’s old language was not enough, it added one small note.
