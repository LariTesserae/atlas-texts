The power did not mistake bareness for a wound.

It looked across the plateau and saw no unfinished garden waiting to be made green. It heard the great roar and did not try to quiet it. The stone was not asking to be softened; the water was not asking to be gathered into rivers; the little lives beneath the ledges were not asking to be made larger, brighter, or easier to see.

So, at first, the power did nothing.

It attended instead. It learned the difference between a current that fed and one that swept away, between a crack that sheltered and one that merely looked like shelter. It followed the tiny clear bodies through their brief passages, not counting them as a multitude, but noticing each life as it met the water: here, held fast; there, carried on; elsewhere, gone.

And because it was wise, it did not promise that none would be lost. Loss belonged to the way life moved here, as surely as the water did. To prevent every loss would have been to alter the place beyond recognition, perhaps to end the very conditions that let these creatures endure.

But the power made one small provision, too fine for the plateau to show.

In a seam beneath a distant shelf, where the water had always passed too swiftly for the young to settle, a little of its force was gentled. No new pool appeared. No stone changed shape. The seam simply became, sometimes, a place where a small body might catch hold and remain.

The power left the rest untouched.

The roar continued. The pale stone shone. Most of the minute lives were still carried away, and some were not. Nothing in the wide country could have told that anything had happened at all. Only, in one dark crease beneath the light, a few more small lives found shelter in the endless movement—and the plateau went on being exactly itself.
