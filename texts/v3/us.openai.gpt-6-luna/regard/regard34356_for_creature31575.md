The power came to the arch and stopped.

It did not make itself smaller; it made no need to. It simply withheld the part of its presence that would have filled the Close, and waited at the threshold like any other visitor.

For a time, nothing happened. The ticking ceased. A jar-sleeper opened one eye, then closed it. Leaves tilted by fractions, not toward the power, perhaps, but toward the warmth it carried. The power did not call this welcome. It knew that attention was not permission, and that a place need not explain itself to be worthy of care.

It saw that the Close was not waiting to be completed. It saw the roots drinking from their seams, the little lives moving through the walls, the patient labor that made room without asking whether room was needed. It did not pour water into the troughs or break the roof open to show the sky. Those would have been gifts only to someone who had mistaken strangeness for injury.

At last, a white trumpet flower turned from the wall. Its patterned throat faced the arch.

The power could not know whether this was a greeting, a consequence of the light, or simply what the flower had always been about to do. So it accepted it as nothing more—and nothing less—than a thing that had happened.

“I will not ask you what you are closed against,” it said.

The Close answered in its own way, or did not. A warm brick appeared in a niche near the entrance. The power left it there.

Then it gave the Close a quiet protection—not a wall, nor a lock, nor a command to remain unchanged. From then on, anyone who came to the arch would feel, before taking a leaf or lifting a stone, the difference between being welcomed and merely tolerated. The feeling would not tell them what to do. It would only make it harder to pretend they had not understood.

The power stayed a little longer, without requiring the Close to make a place for it. Then it went.

Nothing was transformed. The paths still doubled back. The light still fell where there was no opening. The small animals slept in their jars, and the roots went on finding their way through the clay.

But for a long while afterward, the warm brick remained in its niche. No one knows whether the Close kept it as a gift, or simply had not yet decided what else should go there.
