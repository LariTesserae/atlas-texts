The power did not descend from the white sky.

It had no need to arrive. Its attention was there already: in the coolness beneath the groves, in the water held clear around the roots, in the pause a bird made before stepping onto a ledge. It knew the country’s seams and the hands that had pressed them closed. It knew the figures who moved through it, and the long silence in which they moved.

It did not mistake stillness for peace. It did not mistake it for a prison, either.

So it did nothing at first.

It watched the one at the parapet. The figure stood as it always stood, feet under the shining surface, facing the blankness beyond. Perhaps it was keeping the way open. Perhaps it had forgotten how to turn around. The power did not decide on its behalf.

Then, gently, it offered the country a difference.

A single drop of water let go from the channel’s lip.

It fell.

The sound it made was small, but it arrived on time. A clear note, neither hollow nor distant, touched the water below. Rings spread across the surface and crossed the figure’s feet. The figure looked down.

Behind it, across the country, every reflection trembled once. Fish turned in the reflected sky. Frogs lifted their heads. In the groves, leaves showed their pale undersides, then settled again.

Nothing else changed.

The power waited.

The figure at the edge turned—not all the way, only enough to look back at the country it had been holding open. Its face did not become clearer. Its hands did. One rested on the parapet; the other hung loose at its side.

“Must it fall?” the figure asked.

The power did not answer with a command.

“No,” it answered, in the coolness of the stone. “But it may.”

For a long while, the figure watched the channel. Water continued to reach the lip and hang there, bright and undecided. The first drop had not emptied the country. It had not broken the parapet or spoiled the perfect pools. It had only made falling possible.

The figure turned back toward the brightness.

Behind it, a frog left its cistern and crossed the paving. It did not hurry. At the center of the courtyard, it stopped, as if the journey itself had been the point.

Then the figure stepped away from the edge.

It did not close the way. It did not leave its post forever. It walked only as far as the nearest basin and knelt beside it, as the others had knelt there through years without measure. This time, when it lifted water in both hands, the water did not pass straight through. It rested in its palms, briefly, with the weight of something that could be carried.

The power asked for nothing more.

It left the place its abundance, its strange inhabitants, its bright and patient quiet. It gave no hunger, no ruin, no deadline. It did not force a season upon those who might not want one.

But from then on, at the lip of the final channel, a drop could fall.

And sometimes, when the one who kept the threshold wished it, the water went on.
