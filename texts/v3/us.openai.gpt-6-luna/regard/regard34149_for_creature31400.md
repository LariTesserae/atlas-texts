The power did not descend.

There was nowhere to descend to. The high country had no single center, no throne of stone, no heart it could point to and call the whole. Its living ran through seams and roots, through the feet of grazers and the folded wings of hunters, through the patient plates shifting far below. To arrive in one place would have been to mistake a part for the world.

So the power attended.

It watched the mist gather and vanish; the small creatures feeding in the wet seams; the herds leaving one cleft untouched, then returning years later as if summoned. It learned the difference between a tremor that meant *the deep is turning* and one that meant *this shelf will break*. It did not mistake hardship for suffering, or silence for emptiness. It saw that the country was not waiting to be made alive. It was already alive in a way no single creature could have understood.

The power considered giving it sunlight. It considered filling the hollows with water, warming the bare stone, making the ledges wide and safe.

Then it saw what such gifts would undo.

The plants would lose their rhythms. The animals would lose the knowledge written into their feet. The great hidden body might be drowned in a brightness it had never asked to bear. Benevolence, the power knew, was not the same as improvement.

So it changed nothing.

Not quite nothing.

Once, in a season when the mist failed and the shallow hollows dried, the power held a cloud against the rim of the peaks. It did not make rain. It only gave the cloud time. By dusk, condensation had gathered in the cracks; by night, the fronds had opened. The little ones came out to feed. The grazers found enough to continue. Far below, the deep seams brightened, and the old tremor passed through the plateau unchanged.

No creature saw a hand in this. No creature needed to.

The power did not speak in words. It sent no command into the stone, no name for the immense life beneath it. Instead, it gave one pulse—quiet, distinct, and without demand—along a mineral vein.

The country received it.

For a long while, nothing answered. Then the blue-green seams lit, not in their familiar sequence but in a pattern that wandered from ridge to ridge. The hanging fronds trembled. The grazers lifted their heads. Beneath the plateau, something turned—or perhaps only noticed that it had been noticed.

The power waited.

The land returned no promise. It offered no thanks. Its answer was the slow continuation of its own work: mist gathering, roots combing the air, stone holding its weight above the depths.

That was enough.

The power kept its attention there, not as a keeper watches a possession, but as one living vastness keeps company with another. It would not spare the country its winters, or still every cliff that must someday fall. But should anything come to unmake it without meaning or measure, the power would hear.

And in the high country, where nothing had changed, the dark went on shining faintly beneath the stone.
