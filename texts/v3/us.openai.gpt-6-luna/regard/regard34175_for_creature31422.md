The power found the pocket without parting the reeds.

It did not arrive as light, because light would have made a direction here, a favored side, a place toward which every living thing must turn. It did not arrive as a voice. The chamber already had its voices, and none needed replacing.

For a long while, the power simply attended.

It learned the slow pull of the hollow stalks, the grain released by the turning seed heads, the small hammering that shook food loose from stone. It learned the amphibian’s stillness—not emptiness, but a way of receiving the world without asking it to come faster. It followed the runners beneath the floor and felt how nourishment crossed the dark from abundance to want, how the roots made no distinction between giving and keeping.

The power understood that this place was not waiting to be improved.

So it left the warm ceiling, the basin, the creatures passing unseen through the felt. It left the noise exactly as it was. It did not make the plants grow taller or the water clearer. It gave nothing a name that might become a claim.

But the wetland above was changing. A hard season was coming, and with it a long dry wind that would draw the shallow water down through the marsh. The power did not stop the season. It did not hold the rain over this one small room while the wider marsh thirsted.

Instead, beneath the roots, it loosened a buried seam in the earth.

Not enough to make a stream. Not enough to be noticed from above. Only a fine, patient seep, entering the old basin at the rate of a drop gathering and falling. The plants would draw from it, and pass what they could spare along their runners. The basin would not overflow. Nothing would be spared every loss. But the pocket would remain a pocket: damp, warm, and habitable, until the rains returned.

Then the power turned its attention to the amphibian.

It gave the creature no sudden wisdom, no vision of a vast kindness bending over its small life. It gave it only one additional possibility: when the cup of water grew low, the animal would feel, somewhere in the spread of its toes, the direction of the hidden seep. It could follow that faint coolness to a deeper hollow. It would still have to move. It might still fail. But it would not be trapped by a dryness it could not understand.

That was all.

The power withdrew, though “withdrew” was not quite right. It had never occupied the chamber. The plants kept pumping. The seed heads turned and clicked. The bulbed stem struck its stone. A drop gathered beneath the ceiling, trembled, and fell into the basin.

The amphibian’s sides rose and fell, a moment behind the stalks.

Nothing in the room knew it had been helped. Nothing needed to.

Above, the marsh remained flat and unremarkable, and the reeds hid their old, tireless world.
