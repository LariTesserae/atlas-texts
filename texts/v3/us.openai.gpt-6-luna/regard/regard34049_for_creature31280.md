The power did not descend through the water like a star.

It had no need to announce itself. Its attention reached the highland first: along the sunlit ridges, through the green hollows, into the places where the blue thickened and distance grew uncertain. It felt the slow sway of the groves and the faint warmth of the vents. It listened to the water passing over stone.

Then it found the pale being.

The creature was tucked beneath a broad frond in a basin where mineral dust settled softly on the leaves. It was small enough to vanish when the plants moved, and still enough to be mistaken for a pale stone. The power saw how it lived: the narrow paths it followed, the shelter it preferred, the places it avoided. It did not mistake quiet for loneliness, or smallness for helplessness.

For a long while, it did nothing.

This was not neglect. The highland was young, but it was not abandoned. Its plants were taking hold. Its warmth was gentle. The being had shelter and room to move. To remake the place would have been to answer a question no one had asked.

So the power left the ridges sharp, the valleys deep, and the sunlight wavering above them. It did not make the creature speak, or give it a name it could not use. It did not make the groves brighter or fill the empty water with an abundance the place had never needed.

But it made one quiet promise—not to the being alone, but to the whole living country: that should the balance be threatened by a force too large for its own slow means, help would come in a form that did not take the highland away from itself.

The promise settled nowhere that could be seen. It became no mark on the rock, no change in the current. The fronds continued to bow and return. The pale being emerged from beneath its shelter and crossed the basin, unhurried.

Above, the sun trembled on the sea’s surface.

And beneath it, the world went on becoming.
