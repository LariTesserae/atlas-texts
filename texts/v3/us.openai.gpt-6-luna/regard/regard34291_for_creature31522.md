The power came without crossing the rim.

It did not enter as light, or voice, or a shape standing among the pipes. It came as an attention so complete that nothing in the basin was overlooked: the pale lives beneath the water, the bird’s careful feet, the slow wheel, the silt settling into a seam. It saw that the basin was not broken merely because it changed. It saw that the one who lived there was not waiting to be made still.

So it did not stop the water.

For a while, it did nothing at all.

Then, deep inside the pump house, where the old wheel turned behind its grille, there came a pulse unlike the others. Not louder. Not stronger. Simply different: a pause where no pause had ever been, followed by a note that did not belong to the machinery.

The inhabitant felt it as a change in pressure. It answered by sending a thread of water backward through a narrow pipe.

The power answered with another pulse.

They went on this way for a long time. No message was translated. Nothing was named. The exchange passed through pressure, delay, and return—the languages the basin already knew. The inhabitant learned only that some changes came from beyond the rim. The power learned that the basin was not empty, and that its quiet motions were not merely weather.

When the exchange ended, the wheel kept turning. The water resumed its old, intricate routes. Nothing had been healed, because nothing had asked to be healed.

But the power left something behind: not a command, not a gift that would alter the basin’s nature, but a measure of shelter around its continuance. If the basin were ever threatened by a careless hand that meant to drain it, bury it, or strip it for parts, the power would make that hand pause long enough to notice what it was touching. It would not forbid every change. It would not preserve every stone. It would keep the place from being destroyed without ever being seen.

The inhabitant could not know this as a promise. It knew only that, now and then, the wheel’s familiar hum included a silence that was not its own.

And in that silence, something waited—not asking the basin to become anything else, but willing to answer if it called.
