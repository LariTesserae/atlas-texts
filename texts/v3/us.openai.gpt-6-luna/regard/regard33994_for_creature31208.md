The power did not seal the crack.

To do so would have made the room safe only by making it still. And the water was not asking to be still.

Instead, the power listened: to the gate knocking in its socket, to the pipes’ uneven notes, to the patient pressure gathering at the outlet. It heard, beneath them, the shape of a danger—not only to the chamber, but to the street above it, where people walked without knowing what held beneath their feet.

So it changed one small thing.

Behind the plugged drain, a forgotten channel loosened. Not enough to empty the room, or to draw the water away from its new habits. Just enough to give the pressure somewhere else to go. The current found the channel by degrees, as water finds every opening: first a thread, then a turning stream. The gate quieted. The crack stopped lengthening.

The power left the rest alone. It did not smooth the broken tile or scour the grit from the floor. It did not put eyes in the water, or teach it words. The light still entered in wavering sheets; the green threads still bent and lifted. The room remained made by hands, and the water remained busy learning it.

Above, one person woke with an uneasy thought about an old service hatch. By morning they had found the key. They came down with a lamp, heard the pipes answering one another, and stood for a long while at the water’s edge.

They did not understand what lived there.

But they understood that the room needed care.
