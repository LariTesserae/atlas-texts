The power did not widen the cleft.

It did not soften the basalt into a gentler country, or fill the dark with a sun that had never belonged there. It understood that the ridge was still becoming itself. To quiet every fracture would be to quiet the place.

So it waited, and listened: to the water entering and vanishing, to the wind in the fern fronds, to the pump’s rough, uneven pulse.

When a slab loosened above the entrance, the power did not hold the whole mountain still. It let the stone break—but turned its fall by a little. The slab came down in pieces, missing the pump and the greenest ledges. Its fragments settled at the cleft’s mouth, where rain and grit could slowly make new ground.

Then, at the pump, the power made one small adjustment. Not a miracle of endless fuel or tireless motion: only a truer rhythm. The engine still labored, but no longer shuddered itself toward failure. It drew off the sudden, dangerous surges and left the hidden seepages to feed the moss and fern roots. Water still arrived when it pleased and disappeared when the rock required it; now and then, a shallow basin kept a little of it.

Nothing announced the change. The pump clanked on. Wind flattened the ferns and let them rise. The ridge shed grit. In the basin, water held the narrow strip of sky for a few quiet hours.

The power sent no animals into the cleft. There was not yet room in its fragile company for lives that needed more than the place could give. But in time, when the ledges had settled and the new soil could hold, a seed might arrive on the wind. If it took root, it would not be because the cleft had been made safe. It would be because, for once, the cleft had been given enough time.

And that was the power’s kindness: not to make this small, unsettled world into something else, but to keep its hardships from becoming needless endings.
