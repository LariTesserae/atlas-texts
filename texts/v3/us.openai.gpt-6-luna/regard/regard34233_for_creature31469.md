The power did not arrive as light. There was no surface to break, no air to carry a voice. It came as an attention so complete that, for a moment, nothing in the Upper Quiet was merely unnoticed.

It saw the terraces as the builders had shaped them, and as the water had softened them. It saw the pale bodies feeding in the seams, the folded creatures beneath the ledges, and the narrow ones passing through the arches. It understood that no single narrow one held the district in memory. The memory lived in their turning together: in thousands of bodies answering a change in pressure.

The power did not give them eyes, or names, or a message. It did not open the sealed door. A door might be a boundary for reasons it could not know, and the little creature pressing its head against the crust had already carried the door’s strange pressure home. That was knowledge enough for now.

Nor did the power stop the eating, or make the place young again. These lives belonged to one another. To spare every creature from being eaten would be to unmake the life it meant to bless.

But it saw, far above the inhabited terraces, a seam in the old tower slowly giving way. Not yet. Perhaps not for a hundred generations. When it fell, it would take a stair, a nursery, and the narrow passage by which the district’s farthest colonies met the rest. The power did not hold the tower still forever. It only eased the strain, so that when the stone finally moved, it would move slowly. The small lives would feel the change coming. They would turn, as they always did, and find another route.

No creature noticed the gift as a gift. The water shifted. The narrow ones turned. A few eggs were lost; others hatched. The tower kept its dull gleam.

The power remained attentive—not keeping the Upper Quiet unchanged, but keeping its changes from becoming, all at once, an ending.
