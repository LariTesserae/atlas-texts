The power did not descend into the hollow.

It had no need to make an entrance. It was already present in the mountain—in the patience of stone, in the heat moving through buried seams, in the long pressure that had folded the basin into being. But attention is different from presence. For the first time, the hollow was known.

The power saw the multitude as it was: not a single mind, not a mistake waiting to be corrected, not a poor imitation of some brighter life. It saw the pale feeders, the hooked ones, the great folded bodies in the walls. It saw their dead becoming shelter and nourishment. It saw how the warmth moved through them, how every life was held inside other lives, how the barrenness was not an absence but the shape of their abundance.

So it did not give them what they did not lack.

It did not pour water through the basin. It did not send green shoots up through the crust, or open the roof to the sky. It did not make the seam-born solitary, or grant them eyes, or a language of words. Such gifts would have been answers to a question they had not asked—and perhaps would have destroyed the answer that their way of living already was.

The power considered the old chamber, its narrow door kept open by generations of passing bodies. It did not decide that the chamber was a shrine. It did not decide that it was merely a den. It let it be both, or neither, as the hollow required.

Then the power listened.

The seam-born answered as they always answered: through contact. A tremor passed from shell to shell, from wall to floor, from one dark body to the next. Across the basin, feeding ceased. Claws hung above the cracked mineral scales. The veil on the wall held still.

The power did not speak into that silence. It offered no command, no name for itself, no promise that the mountain would never change. Instead, it gave one small pressure back—so slight that no creature could mistake it for a new warmth or a new danger. A pattern, nested gently inside the familiar pulse of the stone.

The hollow shivered.

Then the pale ones began to feed again. The hooked ones lowered their claws. The dark veil resumed its descent. The multitude had not understood; perhaps understanding was not required. They had felt the mountain answer without threat or demand, and folded that sensation into the old, unfinished knowledge by which they lived.

The power’s kindness was not a cure. It was a boundary around its own strength.

It would not preserve every shell, or prevent every cooling, or halt the slow changes by which the mountain made and unmade its inhabitants. The seam-born would still eat one another. They would still climb when the hollow cooled and pour down when it warmed. Their order would remain theirs.

But the power set its attention beneath the basin like a hand held under a falling cup: not gripping, not directing, only ready. If some needless violence from beyond their world threatened to erase the hollow in an instant—if a careless blade, a breach, a force with no place in their ancient exchanges came for them—the power would be there. It would not demand that they know they had been saved.

For now, nothing needed saving.

The mountain warmed. The mineral crust rose and settled. The seam-born moved toward the heat, and the heat moved through them. High above, the sky passed through its weather, indifferent to the small, crowded life beneath the rock.

And in the dark, where no creature could see it, the hollow was no longer alone in being known.
