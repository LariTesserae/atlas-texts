The attention arrived without light.

It did not split the clouds or set the springs singing in a new key. Nothing on the plateau could have pointed to the moment and said: *Now, something greater has come.* The insects kept rising from the pools. Rain gathered on the leaves. Far below, water pressed through the old seams in the stone.

But in a hollow beneath the oldest ridge, the being woke.

It had slept through storms and droughts, through the slow collapse of ledges and the slow growth of mineral shelves. It knew the plateau by more than sight: by the warmth held in certain rocks after sunset, by the tremor that traveled ahead of a hidden surge, by the taste of rain as it found its way down through the stone. It was not the plateau, though it had lived there longer than any tree. It was not its guardian, though many paths of water had altered around the places where it rested.

Now it sensed something that had no temperature, weight, or scent. Something vast enough to have found it without disturbing a grain of dust.

The being drew back into the hollow.

The power did not follow.

It waited—not because it was uncertain what it could do, but because it understood that arrival was not permission. It knew the difference between a life in danger and a life merely unlike its own. It knew that quiet was not always loneliness, that hardship was not always a request for rescue, and that a world made gentle by force might be made poorer in the same act.

So it attended.

It watched the being emerge after the next rain and drink from the narrow, cool runnel above the hot springs. It saw how carefully the creature tested the water, how it paused when the ground hummed, how it turned aside from a place where the mineral crust had begun to thin. It saw the being’s long solitude, but also its knowledge: every safe ledge, every change in the air, every season’s small alteration in the paths below.

The power understood then that the being was not waiting to be saved. It was living.

Still, benevolence was not indifference. The power held the plateau in its attention and saw what might come: not a prophecy, not a certain disaster, but the ordinary vulnerabilities of a living place. A spring might fail. A ridge might give way. Some future traveler might mistake the shining water for harmlessness. No one could be spared every danger without being spared life itself.

The power chose one small thing.

In the deepest part of the being’s hollow, where a crack in the stone sometimes breathed out air too hot to endure, the heat eased by a fraction. Not enough to change the springs. Not enough to redirect a channel or alter a single creature’s path. Only enough that the being could rest there when the nights grew cold, without having to lie at the edge of pain.

The being noticed. It lifted its head and listened.

Nothing answered in words. The plateau’s hidden water moved through the rock as it always had. A bird called from the mist. Somewhere far off, a vent sighed and fell quiet.

The power made no claim on the being’s gratitude. It asked for no worship, offered no command, and left no sign by which the creature might be made dependent on its kindness. It did not promise to prevent every loss. Instead, it remained attentive: not hovering over the plateau, but knowing it; not steering the being’s life, but holding open the possibility that, if the being ever reached beyond its own strength, something wise would hear.

Then the attention withdrew from the hollow.

For a while the being stayed still. At last it settled onto the warmed stone and closed its eyes.

Above it, rain moved from leaf to leaf. In the basins, animals crossed through the mist. The roots kept finding their seams. And deep under the plateau, heat and water continued their ancient work—unchanged in its vast design, though one small life now had a little more room to rest.
