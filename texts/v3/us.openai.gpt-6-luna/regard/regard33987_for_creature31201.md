The power came without arrival.

No thunder crossed the basin; no new hill rose to mark its passing. For a while there was only the familiar warmth on the stone, the reeds ticking softly against one another, the calls of birds carrying over the flats. Then the water seemed to hold the sky a little more clearly—not because it had changed, but because something was attending to it.

The power looked for what needed saving.

It found no single creature to name the basin’s rightful inhabitant. The frog beneath the ledge belonged to the water, but not more than the fish passing below it; the fish no more than the bird waiting on the shelf, or the small, hurried lives in the grass. Their lives touched and depended upon one another. To rescue one from every danger would be to unmake the terms by which all of them lived.

So the power did not stop the bird from hunting, or the water from rising and falling. It did not command the reeds to grow thick enough to hide everyone, or forbid the dry season its turn. It understood that a place can be vulnerable without being broken, and that not every difficulty is a wound.

It listened instead.

It learned the basin’s quiet measures: which pools lasted longest, where the stone held water beneath its surface, when the narrow channels joined. And it made one promise—not to keep the basin unchanged, but to keep change from being forced upon it all at once. If an outside hand should come to poison the water, drain the hollows, or silence the basin for the sake of convenience, the power would stand in the way. If a season ran hard, it would not abolish hardship, but it would leave the old passages open, so life could find its way through.

Then it turned its attention to the being who lived there.

Perhaps the being was small enough to vanish beneath a leaf of stone. Perhaps it was the basin itself: this broad, patient gathering of water and life. The power did not demand that it speak, or become more than it was. It offered no crown, no command, no grand transformation. Only this: *You are seen. You need not become magnificent to be worthy of care.*

The basin answered as it always had. Water beaded under the ledges. Insects rose at dusk. A frog called, and another answered from farther off.

The power remained—not as a ruler above the life of the place, but as a quiet safeguard at its edge. And the basin went on being what it was: open, imperfect, enduring, and full of voices.
