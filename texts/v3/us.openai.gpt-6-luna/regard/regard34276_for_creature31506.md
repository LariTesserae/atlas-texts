The power did not enter through the crack in the stone.

It had no need to. It was wise enough to know that a place so full could be crowded even by kindness. So it attended from beyond the roots, and listened—not merely to the rasp of crickets or the hum beneath it, but to the small lives pressed together inside the sound.

It found no single creature at the chamber’s heart. The being who lived there was the relation between them: fish beneath their bright skin of water, salamanders among the stones, feelers resting on crystal, the green threads holding their drops. A country made of company. The power could not know whether their stillness was a prison or a way of being. It would not mistake its own longing for theirs.

So it did not break the moment open.

Instead, it made one thing possible.

A bead of water loosened beneath a ledge. It did not fall at the power’s command; the power only removed whatever had kept it from falling. For a long while, the bead remained where it was, round and bright.

Then it dropped.

The sound was almost nothing. The puddle received it, and for the first time its surface changed. A ring spread outward, touched the fish, reached the dark boundary where stone met water. One fish moved its tail—not far, not urgently, but enough to disturb the reflection of the white roof.

The power waited.

A cricket’s leg shifted. The unbroken rasp stumbled, leaving a narrow space in which silence could be heard. Then the cricket began again, and another joined it—not in perfect unison, but a breath later. One salamander blinked. The crystal’s low ticking found a rhythm that was not quite the same as before.

Nothing was taken away. The chamber did not become larger, or darker, or less crowded. Its creatures were not hurried toward some grander fate. The power simply released the next instant, and left the inhabitants to meet it at their own pace.

At the three little arches, the power paused. It did not decide who had built them, or what they were for. It left them worn and mysterious, with the groove still ending just short of the water.

Then it withdrew its attention—not as one abandons a place, but as one steps back from a creature that has begun, at last, to move.

The chamber continued. Its song swelled, faltered, and began again. A drop fell from the green thread above. The fish turned beneath their small sky.

The power did not make the place into anything else. It only made room for the possibility that it might become.
