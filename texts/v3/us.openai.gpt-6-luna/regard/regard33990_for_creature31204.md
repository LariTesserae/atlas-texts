The power does not descend onto the roof.

It does not put a green mantle over the stone, or still the wind, or fill every hollow with water. It does not make the people build permanent houses. It understands that the country is not waiting to be corrected, and that the people are not waiting to be remade. Their lives are difficult, but they are not mistakes.

So the power listens.

It listens to the long conversations between rain and rock, and to the brief, quiet speech the people use among themselves. It learns the difference between a sound that frightens and a sound that warns. It learns how much knowledge is held in a pause before a foot is placed, and how carefully an elder teaches a child which rumbles can be ignored.

Then, once, it helps.

A family is crossing a familiar ledge when the wind changes. The stone beneath them has begun to loosen—not with the usual little clicks and mutters, but deep inside, where no practiced ear can reach. The eldest pauses. The youngest does not. There is no time for a warning to pass between them.

The power lays a single note through the mountain.

It is low and clear, and it does not resemble thunder, or wind, or any ordinary complaint of stone. It passes through the ledge, through the soles of their feet, through the bones of their hands. All three stop. The child looks up. The eldest turns them back.

A moment later, the ledge falls away.

Afterward, the people search for a name for the sound. They do not find one that belongs to them. So they teach it as they teach the rest: quietly, with the young seated near a sheltered hollow, listening. They say there is a note that means *move now*. They say it may come only once in a lifetime, and not always in time.

The power does not promise that the roof will be safe. It does not promise that every warning will be heard, or every pool will last, or every lost path will be found again. It cannot offer certainty without changing the country into something else.

It remains attentive instead—not as a ruler above the plateau, but as a presence that does not look away. When the people leave a shelter behind, it does not preserve the shelter. When rain passes over bare stone, it does not command the clouds to turn. But now and then, in a place where one clear sound could matter, the old rock gives its warning.

The people never agree on what made it.

They simply listen.
