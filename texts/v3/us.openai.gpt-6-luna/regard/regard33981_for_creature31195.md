It would not mistake the basin for a wound simply because it was made.

First, it would learn the place: the hours of the pumps, the temper of the currents, the routes the fish chose when no machinery compelled them. It would notice where the water was generous and where it was merely efficient. It would watch the larger swimmers turn before a gate opened, and understand that this, too, was knowledge: a life shaped by the world it had, not a lesser life for being shaped.

Then it would look for what the inhabitants could not afford to notice. A pump that had no reserve. A channel that would become a trap if one gate jammed. A stretch of water where every refuge depended on a single machine continuing to work.

It would not stop the machinery. The machinery was part of the basin’s weather now. Instead, it would make the world less brittle. A failing gate would open safely rather than seal shut. A stalled pump would summon a second, quieter route for the water. Warning would travel through the channels as a change in pressure, early enough for the animals to turn away.

And, in one sheltered place, it would give the floor a little more permission to become something other than a floor. A rough shelf where growth could take hold. Crevices for small creatures to shelter in. A patch of living abundance that did not need to be trimmed to a line.

It would not make this place wild by force, or carry its inhabitants somewhere they had never chosen to go. It would leave them their familiar currents, their learned paths, their long blue distances. It would simply make sure that there remained more than one way through the world.

Then the power would withdraw from sight.

The pumps would hum. Fish would wheel around the towers. In the sheltered channel, something small would find cover in a new crevice, while a larger animal passed without knowing that anything had changed.

The basin would continue to be itself—only with a little more room for life to answer back.
