The power did not enter.

It understood, before it came close, that entry would be a kind of violence. A hand would be a mountain. A breath, a storm. Even a thought made too sharply might press the bead out of its crack.

So it watched.

It watched through the cycles: the seep swelling, the roof cooling, the rain gathering upside down. It watched bodies become threads and threads divide, watched creatures gather on the ridges and pour into the basins. It did not mistake their silence for emptiness, or their constant change for a wish to become fixed. The place had not asked to be made larger, brighter, simpler, or more like any world the power already knew.

For a long while, it did nothing.

Then it noticed the summit cooling toward a season in which the crack might close. The black stone would contract. The bead would slip away. Perhaps the tiny country would endure elsewhere; perhaps not. The power could not know, and did not pretend that all loss was a wrong to be corrected. But this loss would be an accident of scale: a whole living country erased without ever having been touched by the weather it knew how to survive.

So the power made one small adjustment.

Not to the creatures. Not to their bodies, nor their strange passages through stone. It did not give them voices, or a story of themselves, or a fear of ending. It warmed the summit by less than the difference between two ordinary moments. The crack remained open.

The creatures felt no hand upon them. They climbed when the water rose. They scattered beneath the falling drops. They combed the mineral films and slipped through fissures that opened onto distances no eye could measure. Their weather continued to change.

At the mouth of the crack, the bead trembled. The pinhole of daylight went dark, then bright again. Inside it, the inhabitants kept moving.

The power stayed near—not as a keeper holding the world still, but as a quiet attention, ready to learn the difference between a change that belonged to this life and a harm it could not survive. It would not prevent every ending. It would not decide what the creatures ought to become.

For now, it kept the crack open.

And because the power was wise, it did not ask them to be grateful.
