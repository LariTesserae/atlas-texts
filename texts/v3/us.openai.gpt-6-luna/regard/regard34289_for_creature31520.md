The power did not descend.

It had no need to make an entrance. The plain was already whole, and its inhabitants were not waiting to be rescued. So the power attended first: not to the strangeness of the place, but to the lives within it. It listened to the clicks passing through the fronds, to the tiny bodies pausing over mineral seams, to the slow adjustments of roots around distances that could not decide where they belonged.

It did not call the stillness emptiness. It did not call the folded ridges a wound. It did not mistake a world unlike its own for a world in need of repair.

When the tall presence appeared between the ridges, the power turned its attention toward it—not as one ruler to another, nor as a maker inspecting a lesser work, but as one living mystery acknowledging another.

The figure stood in its wavering outline. The plain remained as it was. The fronds did not bow. The little glassy creatures did not scatter. Nothing was required to perform recognition.

Then, somewhere among the overlapping distances, a possibility opened.

It was not a road, exactly. No path appeared on the ground, and no light marked its entrance. It was simply the possibility that, if the tall one wished to speak beyond the limits of its world, it could. If the small creatures wished to follow a seam farther than it had ever led, they could. If the green wished to remain precisely as it was, it could do that too.

The opening did not tug at anyone. It did not brighten when ignored or close when approached. It waited without impatience.

The tall presence took one step toward it. The step passed through several arrangements of distance, then stopped. Its small, steady darkness turned—not toward the power, perhaps, but toward the place where the power’s attention rested.

The power offered no answer in words. Words might have made the offer sound like a judgment, or a demand. Instead it made clear only this: *Nothing here is wrong because it is different. Nothing must change to be worthy of care. But if you wish for change, or company, or simply to be known, you may ask.*

The plain’s answer was not a single answer. A click moved through the roots. Fronds turned by fractions toward the mineral clearings. The glassy creatures crossed and recrossed a seam, their separate bodies gathering, dividing, gathering again. Perhaps this was welcome. Perhaps it was curiosity. Perhaps it was only the life of the plain continuing in its own language.

The power did not translate it.

It left no monument, no new law written across the stone. It did one thing, quietly: it held the invitation open. No visitor would be able to force the inhabitants through it. No stranger would mistake the power’s attention for permission to claim the place. The plain would remain strange, dry, green, and still, protected not from being different but from being treated as though difference were an invitation to conquer.

Then the power withdrew its attention—not entirely, but far enough that the world could return to itself.

The clouds kept their shapes. The shadows kept their impossible directions. The tall presence resumed its passage through the folded distances. Nothing visible had changed.

Yet, from then on, there was one more possibility held within the stillness: not the promise that the plain would become something else, but the assurance that, should any of its inhabitants wish for an answer, the answer would be listening.
