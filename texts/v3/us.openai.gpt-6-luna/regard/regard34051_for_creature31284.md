The power did not arrive as light.

Light would have startled the animals, and the people had enough reasons to look up.

It came instead as attention: patient, exact, willing to distinguish one pump’s tired knock from another’s. It listened to the channels and the rock and the small lives moving between them. It listened to the people, too—not only to what they said, but to the pauses they had learned to trust.

It had come looking for the being who lived there. At first it found no one creature it could point to. Then it understood: the being was taking shape among them. It was the residents who listened together; the water that carried news; the animals that answered changes in heat; the pumps that labored on. Not one mind, not yet, but a life made from many kinds of noticing.

The power could have stilled the ground. It could have made the walls permanent and the machinery soundless. But the district was not asking to be finished. Its inhabitants had learned to live with change; to abolish change would have been another way of taking the place from them.

So the power gave them something smaller, and more useful.

When a ledge began to loosen behind the boiler house, the eels left their usual warm hollow hours earlier than they should have. The crustaceans streamed into the channels beneath the assembly hall. The residents saw. They heard. They had time to move the tools, brace the walkway, and guide the water elsewhere before the stone came down.

The power did not stop the collapse. It made the warning clear.

Afterward, they found a narrow seam in the fallen rock: dry above, wide enough for a person to pass, leading toward a pocket of air no one had known was there. They marked it on the wall, not as an escape route, exactly, but as a possibility. The place had acquired another room without anyone having to claim it.

In the weeks that followed, the power made no grand appearance. A pump that would once have seized began knocking unevenly, loudly enough to be noticed. A trickle of clean water found its way into an old basin. When the residents argued over whether to move a shelter, the animals gathered on one side of the channel, then scattered—and the people laughed, because it was not an answer, but it was something to consider.

The power did not tell them what to call their home. It did not tell them what to call themselves, or the living presence growing among them. It left the names unfinished, as the stone was unfinished.

And when, on an evening with no proper name for it, the pumps faltered and everyone stopped to listen, the district held its breath.

Under the walkways, the little bodies clicked. In the pipes, water moved. In the walls, the stone settled—not safely, not forever, but gently enough for the people to hear the difference.

Someone set down a wrench.

Someone else began to laugh.

The power stayed, without being seen, and kept listening.
