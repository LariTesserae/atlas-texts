The power did not mistake stillness for suffering.

It looked upon the shelf and saw that nothing there was asking to be rescued. The plants were not failing. The animals were not starving. The listeners were not waiting for a world to begin. Their lives were small beside the vastness of the plateau, but they were not therefore empty.

So the power did not descend in brightness, or tear a road through the forests, or silence the roar. It listened first.

For a long while it listened to the shelf as the listeners did: through pressure and pulse, through the knock of seed-cases and the slow flex of stalks. It learned the difference between an old rhythm and a new one. It learned the voices of separate forests, and how a message thinned as it crossed the open stone.

Then, in one distant stand, it made a sound.

Not loud. Not urgent. Just a single interval that belonged to no bladder, no frond, no passing animal. It entered the stalk where an old listener clung, and traveled through the listener’s body as a question.

The listener answered with the ordinary signal for *I hear you.*

The power waited.

After a time, the listener sent another: *What are you?*

The power could have answered with the shape of itself, but no shape would have fit inside that small, attentive body. Instead it gave the listener something it could hold: the faintest sense of the shelf from beyond its own forest. Not the whole shelf—that would have been too much—but the knowledge that the chord continued past the familiar range of its hearing. Other forests. Other listeners. The cold water beyond the rim. A great distance, real and not merely imagined.

It offered this without taking anything away.

The old listener sent the new interval down the stalk. Another listener received it, then another. Across the forest, the sound passed from body to body, never quite unchanged. Some answered. Some did not. A few carried it out toward the open tracts, where the message grew faint in the wide water.

Nothing in the living machinery faltered. The currents held. The grazers crossed their old routes. The forests continued their work.

But in one place, a young listener left its station.

It drifted across the open stone—not because the power commanded it, and not because the old life had become unbearable, but because now it knew there was somewhere it had not yet listened. Its departure left a vacancy in the forest. Another young one would settle there in time.

The power did not follow. It did not ask for thanks, and it did not promise that the shelf would change. It remained attentive, as patient as the water, while the inhabitants considered what to do with the unfamiliar note.

For ages, the listeners had been given their place in the chord.

Now they had been given the possibility of adding to it.
