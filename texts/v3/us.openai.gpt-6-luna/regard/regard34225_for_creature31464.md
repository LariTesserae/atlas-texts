The power did not descend.

It did not break the water with a voice, or pour light through the branches, or make the plateau understand itself in words. It had no wish to improve the dark by making it brighter, or to improve the inhabitants by making them more like itself.

It listened.

At first it listened for a single voice. Then it understood that the voice, if there was one, ran through roots and grazers, through current and corridor, through the small lives in the seams and the great bodies passing above the terraces. No one part spoke for the whole. The whole was not silent for lacking a speaker.

The power waited long enough to feel the difference between stillness and peace. It saw the lives lost to hunger and age; it did not mistake their deaths for a plea. It saw growth cover old paths and new paths open elsewhere. It did not call this decay. The plateau was not perfect, because nothing living need be perfect to be wholly itself.

So the power did not give it a purpose. It did not awaken a mind where there might already be one, or summon the inhabitants toward some imagined destiny. It did not preserve every branch, every hollow, every creature. To keep the plateau unchanged would have been another way of destroying it.

It made only a promise, and made it quietly enough that the plateau could ignore it.

If anything ever came from outside the ordinary reach of its life—some force that would tear apart in a moment what the roots had made across ages—the power would stand between that force and the living world. Not to spare the plateau all change. Only to spare it a change imposed so violently that nothing within it could answer.

Then the power withdrew its attention from the surface of things and kept watch at the edges.

Nothing in the water announced this. The currents continued through their ancient channels. The wall-grazers returned to their paths. A broad body turned between the columns, brushed one with its flank, and passed on.

The plateau remained itself.

And, for the first time, it was not alone in being known.
