The power came without crossing the sky.

It was present first in the pause between two gusts, when the plateau’s many sounds could be heard as separate things: the click of a claw on stone, the stirring of a herd, the faint ticking of ice. Then it was in the water beneath the deepest basin, where the long-bodied creature moved through darkness.

The creature felt it as a change in pressure.

It stopped. Around it, blind life kept to the stones. Above, the ice held the brightness of the day. The creature had no word for visitor, or god, or wonder. It knew warmth and cold, hunger and water, the shape of passages it had swum since it was small.

The power did not speak in words the creature could not know. It asked, gently: **Are you well?**

The creature turned in the dark. It was not well in the way of something safe from pain. No living thing on the plateau was. Its body bore an old wound from a shifting shelf; its water was cold; its food was scarce in winter. But it knew the warm chambers. It knew the pressure that told it where the ice was thin. It knew the movements of others nearby, and the great, slow pulse of the land.

It was not alone.

The power understood. It had come prepared to mend what was broken and discovered that the creature’s life was not a broken thing. Hardship was woven through it, as were shelter, hunger, warmth, danger, and the presence of others. To remove every difficulty would be to remake the creature into something else—and to remake the plateau with it.

So the power did not still the ground, or hold back the winter, or make the hunters harmless. It did not tell the herds where to go. The plateau had its own old intelligence: not a mind that planned, but a wisdom made from root, stone, water, hunger, and return.

Instead, the power left the creature one small gift: a way to be heard.

Not a voice. Not a command. If a change came that the plateau could not absorb—if poisoned water entered its hidden channels, or some force from beyond its weather threatened to make the living patterns fail—the creature could send its distress through the deep water. The power would hear it. It would come then, not to prevent the world from changing, but to keep change from being made into ruin.

The creature swam on. Its old wound remained. Ahead, a warm current curled around the stone, and another body moved faintly in the dark.

The power turned its attention outward: to the roots binding fresh fractures, to the herds breathing together in the lee of rocks, to the gliders rising on the vents’ white banners. It did not bless the plateau by making it gentle. It blessed it by seeing clearly what it already was.

Then it withdrew from the water, and the ice settled above.

The plateau continued becoming itself.
