The power does not enter the chamber.

It does not give the little animals eyes for the light, or make the pale film flourish into a garden. It does not warm the water to comfort, or command the stone to hold still. The chamber is not unfinished. Its sparseness is not a failure, and its dangers are not an accusation.

The power attends.

It notices the seam where the film persists, the cool underside of the pool, the narrow crack where the soft-bodied creature withdraws. It understands that these are not small things because they are small. It understands, too, that to preserve them from every change would be to preserve them from the world that made them.

So it allows the ordinary changes. A mineral rim breaks. Water shifts its course. One patch of film thins while another takes hold. A creature retreats, or does not. The chamber continues its slow, imperfect making.

But once, far above the hollow, a pressure gathers behind the black stone. The water chooses the old route downward, and for a moment the whole chamber is near to being scoured clean.

The power does not stop the water. It gives it another way.

A hairline seam opens into a neighboring runnel. The surge divides—not enough to spare every fragile trace, not enough to leave the chamber unchanged, but enough that one cool basin remains cool and one sheltered seam remains sheltered. The water passes. Stone flakes fall. The pools cloud, then clear.

Nothing in the chamber knows it was helped. Nothing looks up.

The crystal continues to scatter its distant light. The pale film goes on making itself, thread by thread. In the cool basin, translucent bodies drift through the silver bands, answering only to temperature and current. From the warm crack, the soft-bodied creature may emerge again when the water steadies.

The power does not ask to be thanked. It does not mark the place as sacred, or make it safe forever.

It leaves the chamber hidden, changing, and alive—for as long as life can find a way to remain there.
