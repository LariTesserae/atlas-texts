### The Answering Knock

The power noticed the place before it understood it.

At first, it thought the hollow mountain was a wound: a small, secret country pressed into a space too tight to contain it. It saw the stairs that returned to themselves, the water running uphill, the summit that would not be reached. It felt the warm walls working in the dark and the tiny inhabitants tending what had not yet settled into its final shape.

Then it understood—though not all at once—that nothing here was asking to be finished.

So the power did not open the mountain.

It did not widen the hollow, straighten the stairways, drain the pools, or lift the cloud from the summit. It did not give the little people faces that lasted, or words they had never needed. It did not make the highest terrace reachable simply because reaching it seemed, from outside, like a reasonable kindness.

Instead, it kept watch.

It laid no hand upon the landscape, but turned aside the forces that might have crushed it: the careless blow, the digging tool, the curious eye that would have mistaken a living country for a trinket. The mountain remained hidden—not imprisoned, but sheltered. Within its crown, the water still gathered and retreated. New rooms still unfolded inside old walls. The inhabitants still tapped to one another through the clay.

And the power listened.

One day, when the small figures had gone quiet at the sound of the mountain’s hollow knock, the power answered. Not with thunder or a voice from the heights. It made one gentle tap, beyond the walls, where no hand could be seen.

The little figures stood still.

A second tap came—not a command, but a question.

For a long while there was nothing. Then, from somewhere beneath a wet stair, one small hand pressed against the clay.

The answer traveled farther than the figure could have known. Through ridge and channel, under leaf and bridge, the tiny tap reached the power. The mountain heard it too. Its great hollow knock did not repeat. Instead, the water began to run uphill, silvering the walls.

The power did not follow the sound inside. It did not demand to meet the presence in the intervals, or ask the little inhabitants what they were building. It knew that closeness, even offered kindly, could become a kind of trespass.

It only made a promise, and placed it where promises are best kept: in its own conduct.

The crown would be allowed to become at its own pace. It would not be mistaken for a flaw because it was unfinished, nor taken apart in the name of improvement. If ever its inhabitants called for help, the power would hear. Until then, it would not confuse watching over them with ruling them.

So the terraces climbed, the walls softened and set, the leaves crowded every seam. The summit remained visible and unreachable.

And beyond the mountain, the wise power kept its hand open.
