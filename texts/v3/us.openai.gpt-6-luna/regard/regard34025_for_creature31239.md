The power did not enter through the seam.

It could have. It could have widened the opening until the little country had room for rivers, weather, and a sky. It could have raised the shelf into a mountain and set creatures upon it to name the plants. But the place had not asked to be made larger. Its smallness was not a wound.

So the power listened.

It listened through the furnace’s breathing: through the softened hum of fans and pumps, through the patient arrival and retreat of heat. It listened to the plants turning, one by one, toward warmth. It listened to the silence between their changes—not empty silence, but the kind that has room in it.

Then it did one thing.

It made the seam safe.

Not sealed, and not held open by force. The plates could still shift. The furnace could still cycle. The little country could still move, seed-width by seed-width, into whatever part of the seam it needed. But no ordinary failure of the machinery would crush it, and no careless hand would mistake its mineral strangeness for a flaw to be scraped away. The place would remain beyond the reach of damage that had no meaning to it.

The power did not give the plants rain. They had never needed rain.

It did not make them flower. Perhaps they would, in time; perhaps they would not. It did not decide what their growth was for.

Instead, it let the unfinished rules hold. A fleck of stone might still rise. A shadow might still take its time finding the right direction. The pinnacle could keep its thin, impossible gap. The power would not correct what was not broken.

At the edge of the shelf, where bare mineral gave way to a congregation of velvet leaves, one seed-shaped bulb grew a little brighter. Not as a signal. Not as thanks. It had simply reached the part of its slow life when its light gathered.

The power did not ask it to mean anything.

It stayed—not as a watcher above the furnace, but as a quiet assurance within the conditions of the place: that change could come without erasure; that the small could remain small without being abandoned; that what had only just begun did not need to justify its existence by becoming something else.

Then the power turned its attention elsewhere.

The furnace breathed on. The plants inclined, by almost nothing, toward the warmth. And in the seam, the mountain continued learning how to be a mountain.
