The power noticed the district, and did not mistake its stillness for a plea.

It came near as warmth—not the warmth rising through the floor, but something finer, a presence that could have filled every seam in the stone and chose instead to wait at their edges. It felt the wheel’s slow turn. The roots holding the pipes. The leaves that stirred only when the water stirred. It felt, too, the patience in all of it: not the patience of something waiting to be rescued, but of something continuing.

The power could have lifted the city above, opened the hollow to daylight, set the wheel spinning briskly, and called the result a miracle. It could have made the old factory new. But it understood that renewal imposed from outside might be another name for loss.

So it listened.

It listened for a span of time no diver could have measured. Once, the wheel turned. The pipes warmed the roots. A row of bubbles rose from beneath the square. Nothing answered in words.

Then the power offered a question—not in language, but as a gentle loosening of the dark around the central pool.

*Would you have anything changed?*

The district did not answer at once. Perhaps it could not distinguish the question from the deep quiet. Perhaps the question had no meaning there. The wheel turned; its pause was longer than usual. Near the pool, the broad pale leaves folded inward, then opened again.

The power waited.

At last, one thread of green drew tight around the iron framework. Not enough to pull it, only enough to make its intention felt. The wheel completed its turn.

The power accepted this as an answer, though it did not presume to know all that the answer meant.

It left the district as it had found it: submerged, warm, shadowed, and whole in its own way. It did not clear the silt or polish the iron. It did not call the plants weeds or the machinery ruin. But it placed a quiet protection around the hollow, so the foundations above would hold and no careless hand would break through its roof. It made no wall against visitors; it only kept harm from arriving by accident.

And it left one other thing: a small, patient light within the pool’s reflection, which the inhabitant could gather or let fade. It would not brighten the streets unless invited.

For a long while, nothing happened.

Then, when the wheel turned again, the leaves around the framework angled toward the pool. The pale reflection trembled across their undersides. A few silver fish emerged from the ticket booth and hovered in the glow.

The power remained near, not to direct the district’s keeping, but to witness it.

In the factory, the wheel turned once.

This time, the pause that followed was not empty.
