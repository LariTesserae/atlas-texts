The power came to the rim and did not descend.

It had crossed worlds where need announced itself plainly: a flame without fuel, a body crying out, a land gone silent. Here, the signs were harder to read. The hollow was harsh, but not abandoned. Its life was sparse, but not failing. Even the noise, which at first seemed a cry of distress, was full of answers.

So the power listened.

It listened long enough to distinguish the stone’s deep roar from the calls laid over it; long enough to learn that a chime’s bright note was not loneliness, but inquiry. It watched the grazers follow their almost invisible paths and the gliders turn toward currents no eye could see. It watched the plants bend, recover, and bend again.

It did not bring water. Water would have changed the bargain this place had made with life. It did not hush the wind. The hollow’s creatures knew how to hear one another through it. It did not pull up the old instruments, or decide that strangeness was the same as damage.

But beneath the basin, where the wind passed through hidden clefts in the stone, one passage had begun to narrow. It was not yet a danger. Left alone for a long age, it might become one: the roar would alter, the currents would lose their old paths, and the life that depended on them would have to bear a change it had not chosen.

The power touched the passage—not to open it wider than it had ever been, but to keep it from closing. A single grain of impossible strength held in place the stone that was already there.

Nothing in the hollow saw this happen. No light appeared. No voice spoke from the sky.

Only the tower’s rings turned once in a new relation to the wind. A glider tilted, reconsidered, and took its usual course. On a ledge, one of the great creatures gave a low pulse. Across the basin, the fronds answered with their dry, silver rasp.

The power remained a little longer, not as a guardian standing over the hollow, but as a guest who had been welcomed without knowing it. Then it went.

The place continued to be cold, bright, and loud. Dust crossed the flats. The animals kept their distances. The old instruments went on measuring whatever they had always measured.

And far below, the stone kept breathing.
