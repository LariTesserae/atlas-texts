It did not descend.

A descent would have changed the weather, and the plateau had already learned how to live with its weather. So the power came as attention: patient, complete, and so careful that nothing living had to flee from it.

It listened first to the land. To water moving beneath the crust. To the grasses tightening in the evening damp. To the long, glassy vibration passing under the ridges. It listened to the herds, whose migrations followed paths older than any visible trail, and to the small creatures making their ink-fine maps in dust.

Then it listened to the beings among the old works.

One listener was crouched beside a straight cut in the stone, its long fingers sunk in the dust. It had stopped moving. Far below, a seam of water was changing course. The shift was slight, but the listener felt it: a new strain traveling through the rock, a note that did not belong to the plateau’s familiar music.

The power felt it too. It could have pressed the seam back into its old path. It could have held the ridge, the channel, every drop of water exactly where they had been. But the plateau was not a still thing to be preserved. Its life came from changes small and large, from losses as well as returns. To prevent every alteration would be to make a monument of a living place.

So the power did not mend the stone.

Instead, it made one new sound.

A gentle pulse passed through the ridge—not the deep, sustained vibration the listeners knew, but a single clear note, then a pause. It crossed the basin without disturbing a grain of dust. The herds raised their heads. The birds widened their circles. The listener touched its palm to the ground.

The note came again.

The being answered with a tremor of its own: brief, uneven, questioning.

The power waited.

The listener moved to the nearest dark tower and pressed both forelimbs against its base. Another of its kind emerged from a hollow in the ridge. Together they followed the faint strain beneath the stone, stopping often to feel, to listen, to compare. The first led them to a shallow channel where dust had begun to gather over a hairline crack. The second went for others.

The power could have shown them the whole of the hidden fracture. It did not. It gave them only the clear note when they had wandered too far from it, and kept silent when they were on their own.

By dawn, the listeners had placed small stones along the channel in a pattern no outsider would have understood. They had not stopped the water’s new course. They had made room for it. When the brief rain came, the water found the altered channel and slipped away beneath the ridge without tearing the seam wider.

For a while, the power remained near.

It learned the difference between a pause of fear and a pause of attention. It learned that the listener’s stillness was not emptiness, and that what seemed like maintenance was sometimes conversation: between one being and another, between the old works and the land, between this rain and the next.

When the ring of pale pillars filled after the evening storm, the listener went there alone. The other sky moved in the water. For a moment, among its unfamiliar clouds, there was a darkness without edges—not a shape, not a visitor, only the sensation of being regarded without being measured.

The listener lowered its head.

The power answered with the clear note, once.

Then it withdrew its attention until it was no more intrusive than starlight.

Nothing on the plateau became easier. The water still hid itself. The wind still wore the ridges. The animals still had to find their way through a world that could not promise them anything.

But the listener carried a new sound in its memory. And when, years later, another deep strain traveled through the rock, it would know that the plateau was not the only thing capable of listening.
