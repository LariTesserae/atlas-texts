The power noticed the room.

It did not look straight at it.

Straight looking had already taught the little lives to thin and vanish. So the power attended as one might listen to water through a wall: gently, without insisting that the sound become a word. It learned the room’s small measures—the distance from the half-ring to the upright stone, the pause between a drop gathering and deciding whether to fall, the breathlong stillness when something passed beyond the rock.

It could have made the room larger. It could have fixed the water in its seams, given the fronds a proper green, given every creature a name and a place in a finished order. It could have turned the bright, uncertain light into a sun.

It did none of these things.

Instead, where the pale rock was thinnest, the power kept the pressure of the greater earth from becoming too much. Not a wall against change: the stone was still allowed to gather, crack, and dream of other shapes. Only the sudden, careless collapse was held away. The room could become itself at its own pace.

The power also softened its attention. The inhabitants no longer had to disappear whenever they were noticed. They could be seen imperfectly—at the corner of a gaze, in the shimmer beneath the stone, in the space where an insect had meant to land—and remain what they were. This was a small mercy, though none of them knew to call it one.

The round animal contracted.

The room brightened, as it always did. But this time the brightness did not swell in answer or try to outshine it. It simply received the change. For a moment, the animal’s pale body seemed less like a question and more like a presence. Then it relaxed. The light settled.

The power left the rising drops to rise. It left the falling drops to fall. It did not decide whether the marks in the grit were shelter or play, whether the dark creature traveled or dreamed, whether the little stone had been placed or had always been waiting upright in its narrow cleft.

It only made certain that the room would have time to find out.

And because the power was wise, it did not ask to be thanked. Because it was benevolent, it did not mistake silence for refusal. It stayed beyond the rock, vast enough to protect the fragile place without entering it, and attentive enough to notice when the mites made a new path around the damp.

For a while, nothing changed.

Then one winged speck settled beside the water. It remained an insect through a blink, and then another. The fronds’ reflections swayed late, as before. A bead of water gathered beneath the ceiling and fell, touching stone with its small, exact tick.

In the quiet that followed, the room did not become finished.

It became a little more willing to continue.
