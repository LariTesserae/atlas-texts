The power noticed the hollow without entering it.

It had learned, over ages, that attention could be a kind of pressure. Even kindness, brought too close, could make a small world rearrange itself around the one who meant well. So the power did not shine through the crack, or call down into the water, or name the pale creature a soul and thereby decide what it ought to want.

It watched.

It watched the green knit itself over stone. It watched the creature pass and vanish, pass and vanish. It watched the drops hold their impossible reflections, and the little doorframe stand before its wall of solid rock.

The power could have opened the stone. It could have made a road broad enough for a river, a creature, a king. But it did not know whether the pale thing wanted to leave. Longing and habit can have the same shape when seen from far away.

So the power did something smaller.

It steadied the hollow.

The fresh seams in the rock did not close, and the stone did not become old. The silt still gathered. The plants still leaned in their slow, secret direction. But the pocket in the world ceased to draw tighter around its own reflections. The passage glimpsed in the drops could remain, if it was looked at; it no longer had to retreat merely because something approached.

Nothing else changed.

For a long while the creature did not go near it. The power did not hurry it. In the water, the pale body continued its ordinary journeys among the green. The doorframe stayed a doorframe, and the wall beyond it stayed stone.

Then, one day—or what the hollow counted as a day—the creature came to rest before the three stones.

The sand ridge did not break. The drops trembled.

In one of them, the narrow passage appeared. It was not bright, and it did not beckon. It showed only a darkness with a different depth to it: water, perhaps; or another pocket of the world, with its own crowded green and its own small, uncertain rules.

The creature moved toward the drop.

The hollow did not contract. The passage did not recede. Nothing seized the creature or carried it forward. It could still turn away. It could still return to the moss and silt, to the shape it had always known.

For a long time, it did neither.

Then the pale body touched the reflection.

The drop opened without falling.

Whether the creature passed through, or merely entered a part of the hollow that had been there all along, no visitor could have said. The water closed gently behind it. The plants leaned on, slowly, in their accustomed direction. The little ridge of sand remained before the stones.

The power stayed near—not as a guardian stationed at the door, but as the quiet assurance that a door, once offered, would not be taken away.

And if the creature returned, the hollow would still be there.
