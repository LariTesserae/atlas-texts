It did not arrive as a figure above the plateau.

It had no need to. For a long while it simply attended: to the city’s patient repairs, to the hands that turned a stranger’s warning into their own, to children learning the difference between a harmless knock and a dangerous one. It saw that the people were not waiting to be saved. They had made a life out of noticing.

So it left them their life.

But beneath the mapped channels, the water was gathering in a place no plan remembered. Stone had shifted there by degrees. A passage narrowed; pressure accumulated behind it. The city’s instruments showed nothing beyond tolerance. The old workers heard something, but not something they could name.

The power could have broken the stone open. It could have turned the water aside by its own strength. Instead, it gave the city one new sound.

For three nights, the pumps kept their ordinary rhythm. On the fourth, beneath the familiar thrum, a note appeared: low, even, and repeated at intervals too regular to be chance. It traveled through the pipes, the grates, the soles of boots. It did not drown out the machinery. It made the machinery easier to hear.

An apprentice caught it first, standing with one hand against a warm inspection wall. She called an older worker. Together they followed the note from one junction to the next, chalking its path, until it led them to a sealed maintenance door beneath an abandoned platform.

There they found the narrowing passage, and the water pressing behind it.

The city opened a relief channel through sound rock, slowly and with argument. They routed the excess into a chain of low basins beyond the rim, where reeds could take hold and rainwater could spread instead of bearing against the shelf. No one agreed afterward on who had first understood what the note meant. The apprentice said it had sounded like a question. The oldest worker said it had sounded like pressure. Both were right enough to be useful.

The note did not return.

Nothing else changed. The city remained warm and damp, its pumps hammering, its bridges shining after rain. The deep water still moved through darkness. Sometimes, when the crews walked the long channels, they paused at the bends and listened—not because they expected an answer, but because listening had become part of the work.

The power did not ask to be thanked, or named, or believed in. It had not come to take the city’s judgment away from it. It had only made one necessary thing audible in time.

And, having done so, it listened with them.
