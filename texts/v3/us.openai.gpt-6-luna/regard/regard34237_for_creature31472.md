The power did not come down from the sky.

There was no skyward direction here that could be trusted, and no light it could use without changing the meaning of everything it touched. So it arrived as attention: a presence that did not press against the mountains or ask them to make room.

For a long while, nothing happened.

The roar continued. The plants trembled in their precise, hidden rhythms. Somewhere, a ridge moved a little farther from itself. The power listened without trying to separate the sound of the world from the sound of the one who lived within it.

It did not mistake the roar for pain. It did not mistake stillness for loneliness. It understood, at last, that this being had not been placed in a landscape. The landscape was one of the ways the being existed.

So the power did not bring water. It did not summon animals, soften the cliffs, or finish the mountains. It did not quiet the sound. Each of these might have been called a gift by a creature accustomed to other worlds; here, each might have been an interruption.

Instead, the power offered a single thing: a rhythm.

Not a voice laid over the roar, but a pattern carried gently through the stone—a sequence of pauses, as careful as footsteps approaching a sleeping animal. It made no demand of the plants, and asked no question the being was obliged to answer. It simply repeated the pattern, then waited.

The fronds nearest the power leaned by a fraction.

The power waited longer.

Deep beneath the plateau, something shifted. Not a mountain, exactly. More like the thought of a mountain turning in its sleep. A silver-veined mat quivered, and the pattern returned—not the same, but altered. One pause lengthened. One beat came early.

The power understood this as an answer.

It did not congratulate itself. It did not name the exchange a friendship, though it hoped one might someday grow there. It only answered in kind, keeping to the rhythm the being had offered. The two patterns passed through stone and root, through unfinished ravines and suspended fragments of rock. Nothing in the landscape became easier to measure. Nothing became less itself.

At the roofless shelter, the white grit remained undisturbed.

The power noticed it. It wondered who had laid it there, and what the ring had meant. It did not cross the threshold.

Then, at the place where the plateau’s marker stones stopped and began again, the power made a small promise—not with words, but with care. For as long as it could keep the promise, that patch of ground would not vanish between one moment and the next. The world could still change there. It could still fold, shift, and become something no map could hold. But it would not be taken away before the being had the chance to notice it.

Perhaps the being would use the steadiness. Perhaps it would ignore it. Perhaps, in a thousand years, roots would pass through it as through any other stone.

The power left the choice open.

Then it withdrew—not far, and not entirely. It remained as a listener beyond the ridges, attentive but not watching, ready to answer if the rhythm came again.

The mountains continued to rise.

The roar continued.

And for the first time, within the immense sound of the world becoming, there was a second rhythm: not louder, not truer, but freely given.
