The power came quietly, because it had learned that arrival can be a kind of trespass.

It did not descend through the cloud ceiling or part the hanging gardens. It did not make a star, or a voice in the trees. It turned its attention toward the high country, and waited to discover what attention meant here.

For a long time, it found only the vast, patient exchanges of the place: water entering root and stone; old growth yielding to new; small bodies moving beneath the moss. Nothing asked to be rescued. Nothing was asking at all.

Then it felt a person.

The person was kneeling beside a pool where a stream passed beneath the ground. They had folded a broad leaf into a cup and set it at the water’s edge. Rain had filled it. They sat beside it with their hands resting on their thighs, listening through the soles of their feet.

Someone they loved had died there, the power understood. Not recently. The hollow had grown shallower since then. A new root crossed the path. The person had come to the pool many times and would come again.

The power could have made the dead walk. It could have lifted the cloud ceiling, called sunlight down into the groves, or made every buried stream speak aloud. It could have given the person a word for grief.

It did none of these things.

Instead, it let its presence settle against the ground: not a command, not a weight. A warmth, slight as the memory of a hand.

The person stopped listening to the pool.

Their fingers touched the moss. Beneath them, the warmth remained.

They pressed their palm more firmly to the ground, testing. The power felt the small uncertainty in that gesture and understood that it had been noticed.

So it waited.

After a while, the person folded a second leaf and placed it beside the first. Then they made a sound—a low note through a hollow stem. It was meant for no one living nearby.

The power did not answer in the same way. It had no stem, no breath, no voice suited to the country. It only held steady: here, and not demanding.

The person leaned their shoulder against the old tree beside the pool. The power leaned nowhere; it had no body to rest. But it stayed with them through the slow dimming and the silvering of the clouds.

When the person rose at last, they took neither leaf cup. They walked on by the firmer path, their steps light on the yielding ground.

The power did not follow.

It did not alter the pool or preserve the path from roots. It did not make the person’s loss smaller, or turn their quiet customs into a religion. The high country went on being itself: wet, crowded, patient. The old tree kept its chambers. The water found its way underground.

But for a while, wherever the person laid a palm against the living ground, there was sometimes a warmth beneath it—so faint that it might have been the tree’s, or the water’s, or the hand remembering another hand.

The power allowed the person to decide what it meant.

And when it withdrew its attention, it did so without taking the warmth away.
