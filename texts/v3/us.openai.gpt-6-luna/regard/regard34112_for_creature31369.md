The power did not mistake change for damage.

It looked upon the plateau—the raw ridges, the traveling rain, the flocks turning as one—and did not reach down to press the land flat. It did not seal the cracks or command the water to run in its proper direction. It understood that the plateau was not a broken country waiting to be repaired. It was becoming. Its uncertainty was not an error in its making, but part of its life.

And the being beneath it was not a machine in need of a better master.

So the power watched.

It watched through many cycles: pressure gathering in the deep stone, the pause before release, the answer of roots and wings and burrowing bodies. It learned the difference between a ridge rising and a ridge tearing open; between a storm that nourished one hollow and a collapse that would bury a nesting place. It learned, too, how little the inhabitants needed from it. They did not need the world made dependable. They needed the chance to respond.

Only after it had watched did the power draw near.

It did not enter the plateau as a figure. It entered as a note in the deep earth: a second rhythm, quiet enough not to drown the first. When the great pressure gathered beneath the stone, this note gathered with it. When the pressure began to turn, the note changed—not to stop it, but to make its turning perceptible.

The change was small. Before a major shift, the cracks gave a faint, clear glow. A tremor ran through the ground a little earlier than before. In places where sound vanished or bent, the warning traveled as warmth through root, soil, water, and bone. It did not say *run*. It only said, *Something is about to change.*

The plateau’s inhabitants were already good at listening. Now they had a little more time.

The grazers crossed the old fault before it opened. Burrowers carried their young to higher ground. The bright-eyed hunters left a hollow where the stone would soon fold inward. Sometimes they ignored the warning, because they had found food or shelter and judged the risk worth taking. The power did not punish them for this. A warning is not a command, and safety is not the same as obedience.

The deep being noticed the added rhythm.

For a while its cycles faltered—not from injury, but from attention. It tested the note, drew near to it, moved away. The power did not push. It let the rhythm remain available, neither fastening it to the being nor withdrawing it in offense. At last, the great process continued, carrying that faint extra beat among its own vast repetitions.

Whether this was acceptance, curiosity, or merely another way of changing, the power did not presume to know.

It made no roads. It raised no walls. It did not give the plateau a fixed horizon, or make its pools tell the truth about the sky. It left the strange reflections and the uphill streams and the shadows that arrived late. It left the animals their skill, and the plants their quick, restless growth. It left the deep being free to work.

But in the most dangerous places, where a shift could arrive too quickly for any living thing to escape, the power made brief sanctuaries: not permanent islands of stability, but moments in which the ground held its shape long enough for small lives to pass. A tunnel stayed open for one more breath. A ledge did not crumble until the last nestling had left it. A curtain of rain paused at the edge of a hollow, then moved on.

The plateau did not become safe in the way a garden is safe. It remained immense, unstable, and full of forces no creature there could master. But its dangers were less often sudden beyond answer. More lives found a next moment.

Then the power receded, though not entirely. It remained as attention: a presence that did not demand to be seen, a kindness woven into the interval between warning and change.

And because it had not tried to make the plateau into something easier for itself to understand, the plateau could go on being what it was.

The power’s gift was not stillness.

It was a little more time to move.
