The power did not descend through the opening.

It had the strength to widen it, to warm the stone, to lift every roof into the sun. It could have made the water run clear and plentiful, or turned the little gardens into orchards. It could have carried the people above in a breath and shown them distances they had only heard about.

But it looked carefully before acting. It saw that the hollow was not a wound, and its people were not waiting to be rescued. Their smallness was not a failure of ambition. Their quiet was not an absence of life. They had made a world in which a loose stone was noticed, a stranger could be fed, and even a frog in the doorway was allowed to remain.

So the power did not remake their home.

It listened. It learned the paths of the water, the weight of the roots, the names people gave their gardens. It learned which silences were peaceful, and which meant that someone was missing.

Then it made only one change: from that day forward, danger would give warning.

Not danger itself, which belonged to the world as weather and change did. But a roof under too much strain would creak before it fell. A channel running toward failure would show its trouble in time to mend it. A season of want would be preceded by some small, unmistakable sign, so that no one need face it alone and unprepared.

The people called these things good luck. They added them to their knowledge, as they added everything useful: a mark beside a channel, a warning passed along while sorting seeds. They did not look up for a giver. The power did not ask them to.

And when, now and then, someone wondered whether there might be a way to the world above, the opening seemed a little less distant—not wider, not brighter, only possible. The thought could be followed, or left alone. No one was hurried.

The power stayed near in the way a hand stays near a small flame: close enough to shield it from a sudden gust, careful not to smother it.

And the hollow went on being itself. The water gathered and slipped away. The moss took the edges of the lanes. The people mended what needed mending, and tomorrow came without having to become anything grander than another day they could meet together.
