The power noticed them, and did not mistake their silence for a plea.

It had seen worlds made green in an instant, seas lifted into clean blue bowls, creatures given names and hungers and the bright burden of desire. It had learned, over ages, that making a thing more like oneself was not the same as helping it. So it watched.

It watched the figures straighten walls that did not need straightening. It watched them pause beside a basin and let the reflected stars lie in the water held in their chests. It watched one newly risen figure try, again and again, to cross a terrace whose far edge receded by precisely the distance it traveled.

The power could have shortened the terrace. It could have given the figure a face, or a voice, or a purpose that would fit inside a single sentence.

It did none of these things.

Instead, for one interval, it held still.

The rain stopped—not because the clouds had emptied, but because the power asked the falling water to wait. Across the plain, every drop hung in place: beneath bridges, beside walls, above the shallow basins. The figures noticed. One by one, they turned their lintels toward the sky.

The power made no sound. It placed a question in the water of their chests.

**Is there anything you would have me change?**

The question passed through the brickwork. It traveled along canals, under platforms, through seams of pale mineral paste. It reached figures standing in distant basins. It reached those who had become lintels and steps, though they had not moved in many seasons.

For a long time, nothing answered.

Then, near the edge of the plain, a figure set one foot down and made a small change. A slab rose from the pavement. Not a road, nor a wall, nor a foundation. A seat: broad enough for the figure to rest upon, though it had never rested before.

The power waited.

Another figure, some distance away, bent a canal toward the seat. A third raised a low ridge to shelter it from the rain. The first figure climbed onto the slab and sat. Its water trembled in its chest. No one could say whether this was comfort, or curiosity, or merely a new arrangement of weight.

The power understood that an answer need not be a word.

It let the rain fall again.

Afterward, it offered no grand design. It did not plant the plain with forests, or fill every empty chamber with inhabitants, or declare that the long labor was finished. It made only one thing: a doorway that opened.

The doorway appeared on the far side of the basin, where a solitary lintel had stood for ages before more wall. Through it was no promised paradise. There was only a darkness unlike the darkness of the clouds, wide and unmeasured. Beyond that, perhaps, another plain. Perhaps nothing. The stars reflected in the figures’ chests shone there as they did in the pools, but the stars were not reflections.

The figures gathered.

One leaned through the opening. Its mineral cord drew taut behind it, running back into the constructed world. It did not sever the cord. It returned to the others and touched the basin at its chest to the water in the nearest pool.

The power did not urge it onward.

In time, a second doorway appeared—not made by the power, but by the figures. It opened into a chamber they had never built before: a room with no purpose yet, no slope to correct, no water to contain. They left it empty. The power saw that they had done this deliberately.

So it left the room empty.

The benevolent power remained near—not above them, not within them, and not as a hand guiding every course. It remained as the patience to let a question stand without answering it for them. When the figures wanted help, they learned they could alter a wall toward the sky, or turn a canal in a new direction, or gather around a basin and hold still together.

Sometimes the power answered. Sometimes it only listened.

Nothing here was unmade. Nothing was remade for the sake of resemblance.

And once, in the dim interval the figures called night, one of them climbed the steps that had always led back to the plain. At the top, it found that the steps went somewhere else. It looked back at the others, who waited below without calling it home or asking it to leave.

Then it crossed the threshold.

The plain did not close behind it.
