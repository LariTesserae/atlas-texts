The power did not mistake stillness for peace, or movement for freedom.

It watched first. It followed the water through the conduits and the narrow places between bodies. It learned which vibrations meant food, which meant warmth, which caused the slow answer that passed through the plain. It found no single inhabitant at the center of the multitude. There was no center. The being was the answer passing from one body to the next: a life spread through channels, surfaces, and shared water.

The power could have lifted the whole plain from its foundations. It could have broken the clouds open, poured sunlight into the basins, made forests from the ceramic plates. It did none of these things. The creatures had not asked for a different world, and the power knew that beauty imposed without understanding could be another kind of ruin.

So it touched only one small valve.

The change was slight. A stream that had always moved in one direction paused, then flowed backward through a narrow, empty channel. The water cooled by a fraction. No creature was carried away. No food stopped coming. The current simply offered something the plain had never offered before: a way that was not the usual way.

The nearest bodies tightened. Feelers opened along the channel wall. A ripple went outward, hesitated, and went farther. In the great reservoirs, small eyes turned toward a pressure that had no familiar cause. The machinery held steady, ready to correct the irregularity.

The power kept it from correcting anything.

For a long while, nothing followed. The living mass settled back into its accustomed quiet. The power did not insist. It let the backward current remain, gentle and available, while rain gathered on the plates and ran through its old, appointed routes.

Then a small creature, pale as a fleck of skin, released its grip.

The current took it only a little way. It came to rest in the new channel, where the water was cooler and the metal had no warmth beneath it. It could have returned. It could have remained. After a time, it moved on.

The power did not call this courage. It did not call it an answer. It merely kept the passage open.

Over ages, it made other changes as careful as the first. A basin filled and drained by a rhythm that was not quite a tide. A dark recess received a dimness unlike the permanent grey. A gate that had been sealed since before the oldest living bodies loosened—not wide enough to endanger the plain, only enough to reveal that there was space beyond it.

And wherever the inhabitants met these unfamiliar conditions, the power waited.

Some ignored them. Some occupied them. Some changed their shapes to fit. A few followed the water into the newly opened places, carrying the old life outward by their own slow choosing. Behind them, the channels continued to feed those who stayed.

The power left the machinery running. It did not demand that the being become something else, or even that it understand what had been offered. It made the world less inevitable, and then withdrew its hand.

Rain continued to gather in the seams. Pumps continued their quiet turning. Beneath the pale, suspended weather, the multitude rested against metal and one another.

But now, in one channel, water sometimes ran backward.

And when it did, the plain listened.
