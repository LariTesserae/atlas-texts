The power did not mistake the place for a wound.

It saw the rusted shed, the pipes, the slow wheel, and did not call them scars. It saw the green closing around the works and did not call that healing. The machine’s clack was not a plea. The empty frame was not an altar waiting to be completed. The being had not been asking to become more like anything else.

So the power did not enter the chambers, or speak into the leaves, or give the thing a face with which to understand a visitor. It did not make the wheel stop so that silence could announce its arrival.

For a long while, it only attended.

It learned the place’s small measures: the weight of water in a root-bowl; the pressure that meant a path was narrowing; the familiar knock that passed from the flywheel into the mud. It learned how much could change without the place being harmed, and how little could be altered before the change became a violence.

Then it did one thing—not inside the green thickness, but beyond it.

Whenever people came looking for land to drain, clear, or build upon, their plans found reasons to turn elsewhere. The ground proved unexpectedly difficult. The old records contradicted one another. The water tests came back inconclusive; the costs rose; some other site, easier and less alive, drew their attention. Nothing stood at the boundary with a sword. No wall appeared. The place was simply left alone, again and again, by the ordinary decisions of people who never knew how close they had come.

The power did not preserve every leaf. It did not prevent the paths from closing, or the pipes from rusting through, or the wheel from someday reaching the end of its turning. It made no promise that the place would last forever. It protected only the chance for its changes to remain its own.

Once, during a long rain, the empty iron frame filled with water. A single bead gathered on its upper bar and fell into the mud below. The stems nearest it shifted—not toward the drop, nor away, but by a degree so small that no eye could have measured it.

The power did not call this recognition.

It accepted it as enough.

And the place went on: damp, crowded, quietly listening through every root. The wheel turned. The water moved. Nothing was required to understand that it had been seen.
