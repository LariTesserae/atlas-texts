The power did not descend.

There was no new star above the plateau, no voice rolling down from the clouds. It came, if coming was the word, as attention: complete, patient, and without demand. It attended to the cliffs and the wet roots, to the herds crossing the meadows, to the hidden lives beneath the lake. It listened to the deep note when it sounded, and did not mistake its strangeness for a cry for help.

Then it attended to one of the people.

The being was crossing a high shelf alone, carrying a small vessel of water to a doorway in the roots. They had made the journey many times. They knew the trail’s narrow places, the birdcalls that warned of loose stone, the way rain altered the color of the rock. They were neither lost nor afraid. They were simply going where they had meant to go.

The mountain shifted above them.

A seam, weakened by years of water, opened in the cliff. The first stones fell quietly. Then the ledge began to come apart—not with malice, not as punishment, but as mountains do. The person heard the change and turned toward the wall. There was nowhere to run.

The power did not stop the mountain. It did not mend the seam or command the stones to return to their places. It held the falling rock for the length of a breath.

That was enough.

The person slipped into a narrow recess. The stones passed the opening and struck the shelf below. Then the cliff continued its long, ordinary breaking. A portion of the path was gone. The water in the vessel had spilled, but the one carrying it was alive.

They remained still for a while, palm against the rock, feeling its tremor fade. They did not see the power. They did not hear words. But in that small shelter, surrounded by the roar of the falls, they felt—without knowing how—that the whole country had been witnessed.

When they were able to move, they went back the way they had come. They marked the broken trail. They called the others. Together, the people led the herds away from the unstable shelf and found another route to the doorway. The new path was longer. In time, moss would cover the broken stone, and the forest would make its own use of the gap.

The power did not tell them what the deep note meant. It did not name the thing beneath the lake, or explain the bridges, or make the country easier to understand. Such answers were not needed to keep the people company. The plateau had its own ways of knowing, and its own time in which to know them.

So the power stayed near, though not as a guardian standing watch over every danger. It let rain fall, let rivers change their courses, let old stone give way. It did not spare the country from being a country.

But once, when one life stood at the edge of ending, it made room for one more breath.

And afterward, the waterfalls went on speaking. The birds called across the ravines. The people walked their altered paths, carrying water between the scattered doors. Beneath the lake, the blue-white lines held steady, and the mountain listened as before.
