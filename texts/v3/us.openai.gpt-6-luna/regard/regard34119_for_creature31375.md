The power did not mistake stillness for suffering.

It watched the plateau through every kind of weather it had ever known: the brief slickness after rain, the cold that had no direction, the long-held breath above the stone. It listened to the roar—not as noise to be silenced, but as the inhabitants’ voice, with all its cracks and rings and answering echoes. It saw the walls and pools, the pale lives in the seams, the beings who stood beside footprints that had been waiting for their makers.

And because it was wise, it did not come down to explain the place to them.

It did not raise the light, or soften the stone, or turn the distant shelves into a road. It did not fill the pools with bright water or teach the inhabitants to walk. Those things might have been gifts elsewhere. Here, they would have been a stranger’s idea of improvement.

Instead, the power attended.

For a long time, nothing changed. The inhabitants noticed it, though noticing was not the same as seeing. The roar held a new depth. The hollows rang, and the ring was received—not sent back altered, not swallowed, but heard. When the ridges cracked, there was no answer from elsewhere. There was simply, unmistakably, someone listening.

The inhabitants gathered where their paths crossed, though paths on the plateau did not always lead anywhere. Pale shapes collected along the damp seams. In a roofless room, a figure leaned toward the star-filled pool. Far away, a being the size of a doorway stood beside a set of footprints and looked up.

The power asked no question. It made no demand that they speak in a language other than their own.

But it left something with them: not a road, exactly, nor a door. A possibility.

From then on, when an inhabitant wanted to go somewhere—truly wanted it, in the way a stone might want to be rain—the plateau could make room for the wanting. Somewhere among the shelves, a descent would become visible. It might lead beyond the dimness. It might lead to another place, or to a place that had never needed a name. No one would be carried down it. No one would be called back. The opening would remain until it was chosen, and close when it was not.

For ages, no one went.

This was not failure. The power had not given the plateau a test.

Then one of the small, pale things in a crack found itself wondering what lay beneath the stone. It had never wondered before. The thought did not make it less of what it was. It moved, by degrees, to the edge of the shelf.

A way down appeared.

The other inhabitants watched. The roar continued. In the wet hollow nearby, the water held its dark. No one begged the small one to stay, and no one urged it on. It looked back once—not at the plateau as a place it had outgrown, but as the place that had kept it company—and then went down.

The opening closed behind it.

The plateau remained as it was. The walls kept their finished shapes. The birds flew without crossing the sky. The echoes returned from impossible directions. But now the inhabitants knew the stillness was not a boundary imposed upon them. It was something they could remain within.

And the power, having given them no reason to leave and no reason they must stay, continued to listen.
