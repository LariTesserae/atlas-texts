The power did not mistake the basin for a wound.

It saw the raw stone, the houses fitted into its fractures, the people learning where to place a foot and when to leave a wall alone. It saw that the place was unfinished—but not therefore failed. It saw that the people were vulnerable—but not helpless.

So it did not close the cracks or raise a roof over the whole basin. It did not make the ground still. A still ground would have been a different place, and these people had made a life by listening to the one they had.

It gave them time.

After that, when stone was about to shift dangerously, there would sometimes be a sound beneath the familiar sounds of settling: a low, distinct note, like a cup touched gently with a fingernail. It was not a command. It did not say where to go or what to save. It did not come for every small fall of grit, and no one could summon it at will. But when it came, people paused.

The first time, a child heard it in a passage crowded with baskets and sleepers. She called out. The grown-ups looked at the charcoal marks on the wall, saw that the crack had lengthened, and began to wake the households beyond it. They carried children, water jars, and what they could lift. Some argued; one old man went back for his tools. The note sounded once more, and he came out without them.

The ceiling fell after they had cleared the passage.

In the days that followed, they did what they always did. They moved the usable stone. They braced the edges. They found another way through. They disagreed about what the sound meant. Some called it a warning. Some said it was a new feature of the rock. A few left a cup of water beneath the place where the ceiling had fallen, though no one could say for whom.

The power did not ask to be thanked. It did not put its name on the passage or make the people its servants. It remained attentive: to the basin as it changed, and to those who changed with it. When it could help without taking the work out of their hands, it did. When it could not, it did not pretend otherwise.

The earth continued to open. The people continued to make room. And sometimes, before the stone moved, there was time enough to hear it.
