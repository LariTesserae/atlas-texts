The power came to the Cup without thunder.

It did not descend as a figure, or enter the hollow wearing a shape the stones could recognize. It attended. The way light attends a crystal; the way water attends a groove. It gave the Cup its whole regard.

It saw the old stacks, their careful hands long gone. It saw the groove leading to the shallow basin, and the water that still found its way there. It saw each hairline crack, each grain loosened by frost, each edge already softening toward a future no one would witness.

And it saw the one who was not separate from these things: the Cup’s gathered attention, its being made of weather and stone.

The power did not mistake emptiness for need. It did not seed the cracks with moss or send an animal to drink from the basin. It did not mend the old stacks, or protect the hollow from wind. The Cup was not a broken thing. Its quiet was not a plea.

For a moment, nothing in it was overlooked. The water’s brief descent, the ice’s small click, the immense patience of the rock: all were held in a regard that neither hurried nor measured them. Then the power withdrew its attention, not because it had finished caring, but because care did not require possession.

The mist came when it came. The groove gathered water. Cold returned. The Cup went on being itself, with no new mark upon it.

But nothing had passed through its little hollow unseen.
