The power came to the Glassback without thunder.

It had no need to announce itself. The plateau already knew how to listen: to the tick of cooling metal, the hush beneath the scree, the first drop finding a crack in the stone. So the power listened too.

It saw the pale herds wait for the oldest female. It saw the people leave a little water in the hollows. It saw lizards take the warmth of the pipes, and birds make homes in the bones of the machines. Beneath them all, the deepback moved through its buried darkness, carrying the slow weight of a life mostly unseen.

The power could have made the springs run freely. It could have softened the stone, called up forests, filled every cistern. But it understood that to change the Glassback so completely would be to change the lives fitted to it: to turn remembered paths into useless habits, and careful sharing into a thing no longer practiced. Plenty, imposed without wisdom, might become another kind of ruin.

So, at first, it did nothing.

It stayed through a season of hard sun and watched the people mend a condenser whose yield had dwindled. It stayed through the cold dawns, when moisture gathered beneath the ledges and the lizards came early. It stayed through rain, when every creature hurried toward the brief silver water. The power did not mistake difficulty for failure. It did not mistake quiet for emptiness.

Then, one night, a shelf of basalt shifted above a seep-hole. A slab, loosened by old rain, leaned toward the narrow hollow where the people had set a covered jar for small animals. Beneath the stones, the deepback passed close by.

The power touched the shelf—not to hold it forever, but to give it time.

At dawn, a child noticed the new hairline crack. The people braced the slab, cleared the seep, and moved the jar out of danger. They found fresh furrows in the dust and, beside them, the deepback’s broad hollow, undisturbed.

Afterward, the power withdrew its hand.

No one saw it go. No one named what had happened. The people kept the seep clear. The deepback kept to its hidden routes. The Glassback remained spare, crowded, thirsty, and alive.

That was the power’s kindness: not to make the plateau into somewhere else, but to keep faith with what it already was—and, when one small moment threatened to break its pattern, to leave the living just enough time to mend it themselves.
