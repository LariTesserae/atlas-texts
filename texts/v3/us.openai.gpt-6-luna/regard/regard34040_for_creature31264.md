The power did not break the hill open.

It did not send roots down through the stone, or brighten the chamber until every hidden lane stood exposed. It did not fill the pool with stars. The Bell Pocket was not a wound to be healed, nor a dead thing waiting to be made useful. It was old, and held together, and listening.

So the power listened too.

It heard the drip strike the bowl. It heard the answer travel through the little city: over the roofs, beneath the bridges, into the plugged arches where the listeners gathered. It heard the water’s murmur and the small, careful ticks around the pale creature under the paving stone. And it heard, beneath all of that, a sound the listeners had never made: the sound of wanting to answer, rather than merely return.

The power did nothing for a long while.

Then, one evening—or what served as evening beneath the hill—the drop missed the bowl.

It struck the pool instead.

The note was not deep or beautiful. It was simply different. It sent ripples outward, and the listeners followed them through the channels, as they always did. But when the ripples reached the walls, the sound did not come back in its usual order. It paused.

In that pause, the listeners found they could do something new.

One made a small tap beneath the western arch. Another answered from a stairway. Not an echo of the water, not a remembered footstep: a sound chosen, however faintly, because it was meant for someone else.

The rare creature unfolded beneath its loose stone. Its feelers touched the damp seam. The listeners went quiet around it—not empty, but attentive. Then one of them sounded a soft double note, and the creature moved toward it.

The power did not explain what had happened. It did not name the listeners, or tell them what they ought to become. It left the city as it had found it: dark, damp, crowded with stone, its water at the same level, its old paths intact.

But from then on, the answers were not always repetitions.

Sometimes, if a visitor stood in the doorway and made no sound, the Bell Pocket remained silent. Sometimes a footstep came from ahead, not behind. And once in a very long while, from beneath the central bowl, a note rose that had no source in drip, stone, or water.

It did not summon anyone.

It only made room for a reply.
