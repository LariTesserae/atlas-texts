The power did not begin by changing anything.

It had learned, in the long work of being wise, that a place could look like a problem from far away and be a home from within. So it did not open the roofs to the sky, or widen the lanes, or warm the basin until the people had to learn a new way to live. It did not mistake quiet for loneliness, or closeness for confinement.

It came near enough to listen.

The Fold noticed. It noticed everything that pressed upon it: rain, frost, the weight of a foot on a living bridge. This presence was different. It did not push or pull. It rested against the black stone like a hand held open.

For a long while, nothing answered.

Then, in the central meeting place, a single root tightened around the exposed circle of stone. The vibration passed through the joined stems and into the dwellings. One by one, people came—not summoned, exactly, but aware that something had made room for them to decide.

The power asked its question without words.

*What should I do?*

The people listened. They listened to one another, and to the roof above them, and to the old stone beneath their feet. At last, one of the oldest touched the ground with both palms.

*Stay,* came the answer. Or perhaps: *Know us. Do not make us into something else.*

So the power stayed.

It learned the small distinctions the Fold had learned: the difference between a wall tightening because it was failing and a wall tightening because it was growing strong; the difference between a passage closing from neglect and a passage closing because no one needed it anymore. It learned the people’s ways of making room for one another, and the settlement’s ways of making room for the people.

Only once did it intervene.

A new ledge broke from the ridge above the outer dwellings. The Fold’s roots reached for it, but the stone came down too quickly, sharp and heavy. The power steadied the fall—not stopping the mountain, not taking the work from the roots, only giving them the few moments they needed. The ledge caught. The roots braided through its cracks. By the next morning, the people could cross beneath it.

They found no sign of a miracle. Only stone held where it might have fallen, and a faint warmth deep in the wall that faded as the roots took hold.

After that, the power did not ask to be thanked. It remained near, attentive but not possessive, as the Fold changed around it. When the people wished for its presence, they touched the central stone. When they wished for solitude, it withdrew beyond hearing.

From the ridge, nothing looked different. The roofs remained dark. Water went on ticking from leaf to leaf. Figures slipped through the green tunnels and vanished.

But sometimes, when a new crack opened in the stone, it did not widen quite so fast. And in the Fold, where every promise was made by a body passing and passing again, that was enough.
