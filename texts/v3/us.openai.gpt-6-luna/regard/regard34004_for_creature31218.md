The power did not mistake strangeness for suffering.

It looked upon the basin and saw its hunger, its crowds, the bodies opened and returned to stone. It saw, too, the channels worn by countless passages, the sheltered cuts where the young survived the hard rain, the careful turning of the whole multitude toward water. Nothing in the basin was asking to become something else. Its darkness was not a wound merely because the power knew how to make light.

So it did not lift the ceiling of cloud. It did not scatter the animals into separate lands, or give them green things to eat, or silence the calls that made the walls tremble. It did not decree that no creature should die. The basin’s order was not gentle by the measure of a single life, but it was old, and living, and complete.

The power stayed long enough to learn its rhythms.

Then, in a season when the clouds thinned too far and the seepage began to fail, it made one small change. Not a flood. Not a new sun. Beneath a ridge the animals had always avoided, water found a narrow passage through the stone. It arrived slowly, cold at first, then carrying minerals from deep below. A new damp seam darkened the rock.

The smallest crawlers found it. Their trails gathered there, and the skitterers followed. Later, the larger animals came, leaving the old channels open behind them. The basin altered its map by degrees. No creature knew that anything had been given.

The power did not make the new seam inexhaustible. It let the water rise and fall with the hidden weather of the deep earth. It gave the basin a little more room to endure—not escape from its nature, only another way through it.

Afterward, the power remained attentive. When the rains came, it did not count the lives that would be spent in them. It listened to the wings lift, the ground answer, the many small feet return to their familiar grooves.

And when nothing needed doing, it did nothing.

That, too, was care.
