The power came without crossing the rim.

It was not a body, though for a moment the cloud above the hollow seemed to gather itself around a shape. It was not a voice, though every creature in the bowl grew still enough for the silence to have edges. The power had noticed the hollow as one notices a small, living thing cupped in the hand: not as a possession, and not as a problem to be solved.

It saw the new stone, the thin water, the nearly colorless mats. It saw the creatures feeding and being fed upon, each life threaded into the next. It understood that the place was harsh, but not abandoned; fragile, but not asking to be made easy.

So it did not bring sunlight into the bowl. It did not warm the pools or soften the black walls. It did not make the fliers beautiful, or the crawlers harmless, or the hunters kind. It did not give the hollow a name that could be carried away and used to claim it.

Instead, it listened.

The murmur traveled through the power as it traveled through the stone: a pebble’s shiver, the answering clicks, the calls from the walls, the slow closing of bodies in the pools. It heard no prayer in it. It heard no plea. That, too, it respected.

Then it made one small adjustment, so slight that nothing living could have known it had been made.

Deep beneath the northern wall, where the young mountain held its strain, the power loosened one pressure and eased another. It did not stop the stone from changing. The cliffs would still fracture. The bowl would still fill and drain; generations would still vanish into the cracks and be replaced. But no single sudden break would take the whole hollow at once. The mountain would have time to become old around its inhabitants, and the inhabitants time to answer.

When the power withdrew, the cloud moved over the rim as it always had. A drop of water struck a pool. The round, clear-backed creatures parted around it and gathered again. High above, the fliers turned in their unseen currents.

Nothing had become brighter. Nothing had been made safe from life.

But, far below the ledges, the mountain held its breath a little more gently. And the hollow went on, unaware of the care that had passed through it—not rescued, not remade, but allowed to continue becoming itself.
