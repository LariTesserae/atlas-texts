The power did not mistake the place for a wound.

It saw the strain in it: water pressing at seams, roots kept to their boxes, people listening for trouble while they worked. It saw, too, the care that had accumulated there—unshowy as moss, practical as a handrail repaired before anyone fell. The place was not thriving without cost. But neither was it waiting to be rescued.

So the power did not clear the clouds or still the machines. It did not soften every wall, call the plants wild, or give the people a life without danger. It knew that a system made safe by someone who did not understand it might only be made fragile in a different way.

Instead, it listened.

Deep beneath one of the oldest terraces, water had begun to find a hairline path through a retaining wall. The crack was small; the danger was not yet visible. A pump nearby covered its faint vibration. For three nights the power held the sound in the pipes—not louder, exactly, but clearer, a low, uneven note that would not quite settle.

On the fourth night, a maintenance worker woke with that rhythm in mind. At the morning shift, they mentioned it to an older colleague, who stopped joking and went to listen. They found the seep behind a cabinet, and the crack behind that. No one called it a miracle. They called the wall lucky, then got to work.

The repair took weeks. They relieved the pressure, opened an old drainage route, and made a narrow planted shelf where runoff could slow before entering the lower channel. A few people argued about the space given to reeds. Others argued that the shelf needed a better path for inspection. In the end they built both. The next time the rains came hard, the wall held.

The power did not tell them what to plant. It did not leave a mark on the concrete. It only let the warning be heard in time.

Afterward, the place continued as before: wet roofs, lit windows, pumps turning behind their walls. Birds found the new ledge. Someone hung gloves near a warm pipe. A child learned to tell the changed pitch of the lower channel.

Nothing about the highlands became effortless. But, for a little while, the margins widened. And the people—who had always known how to listen—had one more sound they could trust.
