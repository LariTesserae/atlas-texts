The power came to the ridge without crossing it.

It did not descend through the cloud in a shape the creatures could fear, or shine upon the hollow until every wet surface blazed. It was simply there: an attention vast enough to notice the crabs choosing their routes, the wings turning together, the striped animal waiting beneath the stone. It noticed the water climbing uphill. It noticed the towers measuring down.

And it did not mistake uncertainty for a flaw.

The hollow was not broken. It was becoming. To make it finished would be to take something from it.

So the power watched.

It watched through one pulse of the rods, and the next. It listened to the roar that followed, and to the quieter sounds afterward: claws returning to the channels, wings settling near the new warmth, water testing its fresh course. It listened for a single voice in all that life, but found no need for one. The hollow spoke in many bodies, none speaking for all.

Then, deep beneath the black stone, the rods began to gather a stronger pulse. The shelves trembled. A seam opened across the floor, widening toward the hollow’s rim. The creatures knew before the ridge did. Crabs streamed away from the channel. The wings rose in a trembling cloud. Beneath the shelves, the striped animal backed into the dark.

The power could have stilled the stone. It could have commanded the rods to fall silent. Instead it gave the hollow a moment: not an order, but a pause.

The pulse slowed.

In that interval, the water found a narrow crack beside the widening seam and poured through it. The smallest crabs followed. The wings turned after them. The striped animal emerged from its shelter and pressed one broad shoulder against the shelf—not enough to hold it, perhaps, but enough to change where the weight settled.

The seam stopped short of the rim.

When the rods pulsed again, the stone rose elsewhere. A low ridge unfolded beside the new channel, and warm water crossed it in a shining thread. The hollow answered with its roar. This time, the creatures scattered less far.

The power offered no name for what had happened. It did not tell the towers what they were for, or the hollow what it ought to become. It left no law engraved on the stone. It only made sure the choice had not been taken away before the place had learned it could choose.

At the rim, where the mountain was dry and the wind had nothing to catch, the power withdrew its attention—not leaving, exactly, but ceasing to press close. Within the hollow, the mist thickened. The rods dimmed. A crab paused at the edge of the new ridge, tested the water with one clear leg, and crossed.

For a while, nothing changed.

Then, somewhere below, the stone gave a small and thoughtful shiver.
