The power came without crossing the country.

It was simply there, in the way a change in pressure is there before rain: not visible, but known by every surface. The reeds bent—not toward it, but in acknowledgment. The channels did not fall silent. They made room.

At the central basin, the figure stood as it had stood before, listening across the lowland. The power did not call to it. A call would have been another sound to carry, another thing to keep.

Instead, it listened.

It listened to water remembering water, to stone holding the trace of vanished floods, to the small, patient movements beneath the roots. It listened to the inhabitants passing impressions from pool to pool, and to all the mistakes folded into those impressions. It did not mistake their silence for loneliness. It did not mistake their strangeness for need.

For a long while, it did nothing.

Then it offered—not a command, not a cure, but a choice.

At the basin’s center, a single ring appeared. It spread outward, touching no shore. Within it, the water reflected neither an earlier sky nor one yet to come. For one breath, it reflected only what was there: the low clouds, the listening figure, the dark rim of stone. The present, with no echo laid over it.

The figure turned.

The power did not tell it what the ring meant. It waited. Beneath the water, pale shapes shifted among the roots. A low tremor passed through the basin and away into the channels. The answer took a long time to return—or perhaps it had begun long before the power arrived.

The figure placed one hand on the surface.

The ring widened. Then it broke, as any ring must, into countless small disturbances. The country accepted them. The water kept them, imperfectly.

That was all.

The power did not straighten the channels or seal the cracks in the stone. It did not make the inhabitants easier to see, or give them names that visitors could carry away. It did not preserve the lowland from change. The place had never asked to be spared change; change was how it continued.

But the gift remained—not as a new law, and not in every pool. Now and then, when the lowland grew thick with old impressions, a basin would hold one clear instant of the present. Any inhabitant could enter it, or leave it, or let it pass unused. No memory was taken away. No one was obliged to distinguish what had happened from what was happening.

When the power withdrew, the country’s sounds gathered again: stream over stone, rain in reeds, water speaking under roots. The figure returned its attention to the whole expanse.

It did not look after the departing power.

It had not been asked to.

And far off, in a shallow pool beneath the trees, something small and pale trembled against a root. The water around it held the sky as it was.
