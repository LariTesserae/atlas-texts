The power did not descend into the basin.

It had no need to arrive as a figure, or to make the sky brighter, or to quiet the water so that it could be heard. It attended first. It listened to the notes in the hollows, the small tremors passing between neighboring stones, the deeper pulses moving under the mud. It listened long enough to know that the clamor was not a cry for help.

The basin was changing, but change was not an injury here. The shelves thinned; the channels wandered; a familiar note sometimes ended. The inhabitants felt these things. They did not need to be spared them.

So the power did not hold the stones in place. It did not clear the mist, give the basin a sun, or fill it with green life because green life was what other places had. It did not name the old walls or decide what they ought to mean.

It offered one thing: a moment in which every pressure could be felt by every other.

The deep ones beneath the ground sensed the smallest hollow at the basin’s edge. The young ones heard the low pulses of the oldest. The water’s thin threads, the air caught under stone, the dark shapes traveling below the flats—all of them received, for an instant, the whole scattered music of the basin. Not as sound alone, but as nearness: *you are here; the others are here; this is what you make together.*

Nothing was required of them in return.

For a while, the basin’s voices gathered into a single chord. It was not beautiful in any way a passing traveler might have recognized. It was uneven, damp, and crowded with small dissonances. Then the chord loosened. Each hollow resumed its own note. Water went on falling. Somewhere, a new crack opened.

The power remained long enough to understand that the inhabitants had not been waiting to be made whole. Then it let the basin keep its own shape.

In the dark, the deep pulses continued. But now, among them, there was a faint answering vibration—not a command, not a promise that nothing would be lost. Only the memory of having been heard.
