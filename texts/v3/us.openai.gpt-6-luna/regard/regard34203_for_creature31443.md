The power did not finish the mountain.

It came in the hour before dawn, when cloud lay over the height and the houses seemed to float without foundations. No door opened for it. No stone split. The watchkeeper on the upper stair only felt, for a moment, that the wind had stopped listening to itself.

Then the spring rang.

Not loudly. One clear note rose from the basin, though no one had struck it. The Keepers came out with their lamps and tools. They stood around the water, waiting for it to settle. It did. The surface was still, and the level remained where they had marked it.

The power made itself known then—not as a face or a voice, but as a presence that could be met without being understood. The Keepers felt it in the quiet attention between them. It did not ask for a name, or a vow, or a place in their stories. It did not tell them what the mountain was.

It let them know only this: when danger came too quickly for listening, they would have time to hear it. Not certainty. Not immunity from change. A warning, early enough to gather the children, loosen a wall, move the water, or leave a shelf untouched.

The Keepers did not ask it to keep the height from changing. They did not ask it to make the empty houses full.

In the morning, they checked the spring. Then they checked the bridge cords and renewed the chalk marks, as they would have done on any other day. One of them left a shallow bowl beside the basin. Another swept the steps. The watchkeeper went to sleep.

No one built a shrine. They made a place in the meeting hall where a traveler could sit out of the rain.

And the mountain continued to arrive at its own pace.
