The power did not descend in fire, or speak from the clouds.

It paid attention.

It listened to the drop that struck the cistern, to the answering trickle behind the wall, to the long, low creak of one wet surface settling against another. It learned the basin’s patience: how water passed from channel to channel, how every seam held and yielded, how the whole immense construction depended on being neither quite full nor quite dry.

The power did not call the place barren. It did not mistake silence for sorrow. Nothing in the basin asked to be rescued, and wisdom kept it from inventing a plea.

So it made no garden. It sent no animals to drink from the cisterns, no roots to pry apart the mortar. It did not soften the ridges into soil or scatter green across the bright, pale slopes. The basin was not a failed version of somewhere else. It was itself.

But the power noticed that every drop had only one kind of journey: caught, divided, guided, caught again. A world can be complete and still have room for a door.

At the outer ridge, where a narrow channel bent back toward the interior, the power eased one small sill. Not enough to drain the basin. Not enough to weaken the wall. Just enough for a thin thread of water, if it ever came that way, to take a course the makers had not provided.

The first drops passed it by. They followed the old grooves, as water had always done.

Then, after a season of cloud, one drop reached the lowered lip. It paused there—not by choice, perhaps, but because the breeze held it. Beneath it lay open air. Beyond that, a faint horizon.

The drop fell.

It struck a ledge below, slipped into a seam, and was lost again among the hidden waters. Nothing in the basin changed shape. No voice rose from the cisterns. Yet for a moment the water had not been carried onward by wall or gutter. It had crossed a little space unheld.

The power did not insist that this meant anything. It left the channel open and withdrew its attention gently, like a hand lifted from a sleeping creature.

Afterward, most of the water continued as before. Sometimes a thread took the new way. Sometimes none did for years. The basin stayed damp beneath its bright, cold sky, keeping its patient work.

And if the water remembered the fall, it did so without words: as a path that was not a command, and a small, unblocked place in the world.
