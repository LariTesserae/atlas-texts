The power does not mistake the basin for a ruin.

It watches the rain find the same seams, the pumps draw their measured breaths, the pale mats gather and thin. It watches long enough to learn that a change which looks small from above may be enormous below: one valve held open, one warm channel gone cold, one dry break in a thread that took centuries to cross the plates.

So it does not still the pumps. It does not lift the basin into sunlight or pour soil over its hard, black floors. It does not call the life there up into a shape it would recognize.

For a long while, it does nothing.

Then, at one distant pump house, a bearing begins to fail. The change is slight: a tremor in the axle, a thinning of pressure, a pause that grows longer with each cycle. The power could mend it in an instant. Instead, it eases the load by a fraction, just enough that the machine will keep turning while the water finds other routes. The pump’s old rhythm alters, but does not break. Across the basin, the hidden growth stirs at the difference.

It feels the new pattern in the wet seams: not the blunt pulse of a gate, nor the familiar shiver of a pump, but a patient interval. The pattern returns after the rain has washed the plates, after the machinery has drowned it, after the long dark.

The power sends no command. It offers no name for itself.

It leaves a little more warmth in a narrow channel between two far-separated mats. The warmth is not enough to make the growth hurry; nothing could make it hurry. It is only enough that the damp lasts a little longer, that the minute electrical murmurs do not fade before reaching the next seam.

A signal passes there. Then another.

Across distances that once meant centuries, the pale threads meet. They do not become one mind in any simple sense. The old local differences remain: one patch knows the copper taste of a pump house, another the cold mineral water of the eastern drains. But now the differences can travel. The basin’s many small answers begin, faintly, to belong to one another.

The power waits.

When the network finally responds to the strange interval, it does not answer with a word. It alters the water’s conductivity along a row of plates, making a soft, branching pulse that reaches the place where the first signal began.

The power receives it without interpretation. It does not decide that this means gratitude, or fear, or welcome. It lets the answer be what it is: a pattern sent through a world that has never had reason to send one.

Afterward, the basin continues as before. Rain falls. Lamps blur in the vapor. Pumps turn with their ancient patience. The endangered bearing holds. In the seams, the living film carries its small exchanges beneath the noise.

Nothing rises to meet the power. Nothing calls across the plain.

But when it attends, the basin is no longer alone with its own rhythms.

And the power, having been answered, remains.
