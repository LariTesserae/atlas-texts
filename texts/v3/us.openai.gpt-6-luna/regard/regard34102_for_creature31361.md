The power did not come down through the clouds.

It had no need to make an entrance. It was old enough to know that a place can mistake spectacle for danger, and wise enough to know that being noticed is not always a kindness.

It listened first.

It listened to the mills and presses, to the roots working patiently beneath the streets, to the bells hung where paths forgot themselves. It listened to the people who paused before a crossing, measuring the air with their rods; to the children who knew not to run; to the tired jokes in the factory rooms, and the silences after the machines began to shudder.

It listened long enough to understand that the plateau was not waiting to be rescued. Its people had made a life among its uncertainties. The green skin was not a wound to be cleared away. The crooked roads were not a mistake that had to be corrected. Even the old factories, with their imperfect timekeeping, belonged to the place as much as the rain did.

So the power did not straighten the roads, or quiet the machines, or drive the roots back into the earth.

It chose one smaller thing.

From then on, when someone rang a warning bell because a path had become dangerous, the sound reached one person who could help. Not everyone. Not always the nearest. Someone who was able to come, and who would understand the bell for what it was.

The first time it happened, a child had gone missing between two familiar towers. Her family rang the bell at the end of the service road. Across the plateau, in a factory where the gears had stopped mid-turn, an old worker heard it. He set down his tools and followed the sound, though it seemed to come from three directions at once. The path bent twice. A bridge met its opposite tower at the wrong angle. But each time he stopped, the bell sounded again—not louder, only clearer.

He found the child beneath a shelf of stone, sitting very still as she had been taught. He brought her home by a route neither of them had taken before.

Afterward, people wondered whether the bell had changed, or the air, or the plateau. They repainted the warning marks. They checked the old cords. They wrote a note beside the road: *bell carried well today.*

The power let them keep their own explanation.

It remained attentive, as it had been before—not watching to correct every sorrow, but listening for the difference between a difficulty a people had made their own and a danger that had become too much for any one of them to bear.

And when the brief rain crossed the heights, the power did not hold it there. It let the water pass through the living skin of the land, into its hidden hollows and roots.

For a little while, every leaf shone. Somewhere, a bell waited quietly at the end of a path.
