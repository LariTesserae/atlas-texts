The power did not send light down through the seam.

Light would have been a kind of trespass. It did not pour water into the hollow, either; the city had learned to live without it, and what looked like deprivation from above might be balance below. The power was wise enough to know that a gift can be a wound when it is given without understanding.

So first it listened.

It listened to the fine rasp of stems, the settling dust, the long groan moving through the rootwork. It listened for pain, but found no single place where pain lived. It listened for loneliness, but could not mistake emptiness for a request. The city held its rooms open because that was how it had grown. The power would not fill them merely to satisfy its own idea of company.

At last, it answered in the city’s own language: a tremor.

Not a booming shock, not a command. A small, even pulse passed through the stone beneath the roots. The oldest pillars received it; the younger fibers carried it onward. It traveled so slowly that, in one chamber, it might have seemed only another settling of the earth. Yet it did not fade as ordinary sounds did. It returned, after a long interval, in the same gentle measure.

The city answered without knowing, perhaps, that it was answering. A dry note passed through one hollow trunk, then another. The pulse changed—not much, but enough. The power waited. It had all the time that the city had.

In this way they learned each other’s nearness. Not thoughts, exactly. Not words. The city offered the knowledge of its shape: the weight its bridges could bear, the passages it had preserved, the places where its roots held fast. The power offered, in return, the knowledge that nothing above was reaching down to claim it.

Then came the only intervention.

Far above, the pale plain shifted. Ages of abrasion had been thinning the roof over the hidden hollow; one day, the seam might have opened too suddenly, and the falling stone could have broken what time had so carefully joined. The power did not seal the entrance. It did not entomb the city for its own protection. It steadied the ground around it—just enough that the seam would open or close by degrees, as the city could endure.

Afterward, the power withdrew its attention from the surface, but not its care. It left no lamp burning and no voice in the halls. It made no demand that the city grow, remember, forgive, or become more like anything else.

Sometimes, far below the mineral plain, a soft vibration crosses the roots. The city receives it, holds it, and returns a sound of its own: a low, dry resonance that travels from wall to wall and fades into the long quiet.

The power does not call this answer gratitude. It does not call it friendship.

It simply knows that the city is there.

And when the hollow listens, it is not listening alone.
