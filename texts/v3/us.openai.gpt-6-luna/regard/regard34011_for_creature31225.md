The power came near without arriving.

It was wise enough to understand that an entrance, even a gentle one, might be a kind of violence. Its attention passed over the pale enclosure and felt the dense agreement of its forms: ridge against wall, note against note, each presence held in the place where it belonged.

At first, it searched for pain.

It found none—not because the inhabitants were incapable of suffering, nor because their stillness concealed some plea too subtle to hear. It found no fracture asking to be mended, no hunger mistaken for quiet, no loneliness hiding inside their closeness. The ringing was not a distress call. It was their life, complete and continuous.

The power listened.

It listened long enough to hear that the voices were distinct, though no voice stood apart. It learned the slight difference between one held note and the next. It learned that the lichen’s gray-green strokes were not an invitation to make the whole place more colorful. It learned that the little world did not need a horizon in order to be whole.

Then it considered changing something.

It could have given them motion. It could have opened a passage into a larger country, brought warmth or water, softened the ringing into silence. It could have made them more like the beings it already knew how to help.

It did none of these things.

For a wise kindness does not assume that every closed door is a prison, or that every still life is waiting to be improved.

So the power made no mark upon the pale material. It added no voice to the ringing and took none away. It left the courts crowded, the hollows dry, the ridges smooth from age. The inhabitants did not have to receive a gift, or refuse one. Their whole world remained their own.

But the power did something beyond the enclosure, where its doing would not press upon them. It made a promise to the vastness around them: that no careless hand would break this small country merely because it mistook stillness for emptiness. If ever its inhabitants wished for change, change would remain possible. Until then, they would not be forced to become other than they were.

The power did not ask them to be grateful. It did not wait for an answer.

It listened once more—not to make the place its own, but to know it—and withdrew its attention as carefully as it had come.

The little world went on ringing.

Nothing in it knew that anything had happened. Nothing needed to.
