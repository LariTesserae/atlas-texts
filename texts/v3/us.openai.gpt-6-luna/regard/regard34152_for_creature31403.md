The power did not arrive as light.

Light would have made a spectacle of the hollow, and the hollow had never asked to be seen. Nor did the power speak. A voice would have crossed the open center too quickly, like a thrown stone.

It listened first.

It listened to the insects rasping in the wall, the brief birdcalls, the soft passage of hidden bodies through leaves. It listened to the patient wheel at the rim, and to the peat beneath it, receiving the vibration and passing it on. It listened, most of all, to the broad, quiet attention under the floor.

The resident felt this listening. It was unlike rain, unlike footsteps, unlike the iron pulse. It had no weight and made no demand. Still, the pale threads tightened among the roots. The sheen gathered in the center, thin as breath on glass.

The power did not reach down to uncover it. It did not name the thing beneath the peat, or decide that its stillness was a flaw to be mended. It understood that the creature’s way of being was to listen—and that listening was not the same as waiting to be rescued.

So the power listened back.

For a while, nothing changed. The wheel turned. A drop of moisture fell from the wall and darkened the peat. Somewhere in the green, a small bird gave a sharp cry. The sheen trembled.

Then, beneath the machine’s regular throb, the resident felt another rhythm: not a command, not an imitation, but an answering presence. It did not try to drown out the old pulse. It simply made room beside it.

The creature sent no message. Perhaps it had none to send. A faint ripple passed through the clear film, and the power received it as it was: not a greeting in any human sense, but the whole of what could be offered.

That was the power’s only gift. It left the basin its own.

It did not brighten the day, silence the animals, stop the wheel, or lift the creature into some new and dazzling form. It did not promise that the hollow would stay unchanged forever. Change belonged to living things as surely as damp belonged to the peat. Instead, the power held a quiet assurance in the ground: that the basin did not need to become more beautiful, more useful, or more understandable to deserve care.

When the power withdrew its attention, it did so gently. The resident could no longer feel the answering rhythm, but the memory of it remained—not as a burden to maintain, only as a fact: once, something vast had listened without asking it to be anything other than what it was.

The wheel kept turning. The wall kept growing. The hollow stayed open.

And beneath the peat, the old listener listened on, no longer quite alone.
