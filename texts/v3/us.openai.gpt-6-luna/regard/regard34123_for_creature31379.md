The power noticed the basin.

It did not mistake the settlement for a ruin simply because its builders were gone. It did not mistake quiet for emptiness, or the fish for a remnant of something more important. It saw the walls holding shade, the crabs at their patient work, the soft bodies resting in the chambers. It saw how the place had changed without ceasing to matter.

For a long while, it did nothing.

To raise the fallen roofs would have taken away the shelter they now made. To clear every passage would have altered the currents on which the residents depended. To return the old inhabitants—or even the knowledge of them—might have burdened the creatures here with a history they had no need to carry. The power understood that care was not the same as correction.

At the last arch, the solitary fish faced the dark beyond the settlement. It had no thought of courage, nor of being watched. The current brought it scents of distant water and the minute tremors of things moving out of sight. It held there, making small adjustments with its tail.

The power could have filled the basin with light. Instead, it touched the current.

Not enough to turn it aside. Only enough to ease its passage through a narrow channel, where sand had begun to gather against a low ridge. The water moved a little more freely there. Fine silt lifted and drifted away. With it came a trace of nourishment from the open basin, settling among the shellfish in the deeper courtyards.

Nothing changed all at once. No wall stirred. No creature looked up.

But the current reached the last arch in a new way. It carried the smell of a feeding ground beyond the ridges, and the distant, faint pulse of open water. The fish turned toward it. It waited, as it had waited before. Then it slipped through the arch and followed the current a short distance into the dark.

It might return. It might not.

The power did not decide for it.

Afterward, the basin continued as it had: crabs crossing the streets, translucent bodies turning beneath the arches, old stone gathering the slow green of the sea. The settlement remained what it had become—not restored, not abandoned, but inhabited.

And the current, passing through its familiar rooms, carried on a little more easily.
