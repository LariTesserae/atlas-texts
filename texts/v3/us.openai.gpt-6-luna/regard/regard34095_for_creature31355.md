The power did not enter by the road.

It did not descend through the roof, or arrive in a blaze that made every shadow choose a side. It knew that the district had its own ways of admitting things. So it waited beyond the bowl, where the ground was firm, and let the rain carry its attention inward.

Nothing changed at first.

Then the puddles stopped trembling.

The resident nearest the arch looked up. Around it, the animals paused in their separate paths. A pale bird opened its wings inside a pipe. The rusted press held its jaws open, no longer closing and opening in its patient, empty rhythm.

The power did not call the resident by a name. It did not give it one.

Instead, it made a place beside itself—not a doorway, exactly, but the feeling of one: an opening that would stay open, even if no one came through. The resident watched it for a long while. Its face shifted in the puddle, becoming, for a moment, unmistakably its own.

The power could have drawn the district into a single shape. It could have straightened the streets, finished the bridge, made every stair arrive somewhere. It could have commanded the plants to grow thick and the machines to run smoothly, and called the result healing.

It did none of these things.

It knew the difference between a wound and an unfinished thing.

So it laid a hand on the ground at the bowl’s rim. Not to hold the district still, but to give it somewhere steady to press against. The soft earth settled beneath the touch. The walls did not become permanent; they became safe enough to change. The foundations could wander without carrying the houses away. Water found hollows where it could gather without drowning the roots. The machines, when they woke, no longer had to wake alone: their turning answered by small lights along the pipes, like neighbors opening their doors.

The resident came closer. It stopped just beyond reach.

The power waited.

After a time, the resident lifted its long fingers and touched the air between them. The opening widened by the width of that hand. Nothing crossed it. Nothing was required to.

For the first time, the great animal passed through the district in daylight. Not fully seen, but not hidden from. It moved slowly through the streets, and the residents watched from their scattered places. The power did not make them follow. It did not make the animal stay. It let the track of its passing remain in the ground a little longer than usual, so that anyone who wished could find it afterward.

When the power withdrew, it left no temple, no law, no explanation of what the district was meant to become.

It left one small thing: a seed placed in the dark soil beside the arch.

The residents did not plant it at once. For several days, they passed it by. Rain gathered around it and sank away. A soft-bodied animal nosed it, then continued along its exact route. The seed did not hurry.

At last, the resident who had touched the opening knelt and covered it with earth.

The shoot that emerged was slender, pale, and uncertain. It did not straighten the street or repair the broken roof. It did not resemble a promise that everything would be well.

It simply grew.

And the district, which had always made room for something, made room for it too.
