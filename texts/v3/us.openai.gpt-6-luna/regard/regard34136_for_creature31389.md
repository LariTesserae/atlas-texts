The power noticed the hollow and did not mistake its silence for a plea.

It did not give the plants eyes, or make a voice from the pools. It did not send animals to enliven the basin, or a traveler to name it. Nothing there was waiting to become more important. The hollow was already keeping its own small weather, and the life within it had found a way to belong without needing to be seen.

So the power watched.

It watched through one long evening, as the bare stone gave back its warmth and the mist gathered low among the green. It watched a bead of water descend a leaf and fall into a pool. The note was slight, but the power heard it—not as a song meant for an audience, only as what happened when water, leaf, and hollow met.

Then, far beneath the basin, where old pressures leaned against old stone, a seam began to open. Not quickly. Not enough to trouble anything that day, or the next. But in time it would widen; the warm pools would drain into the mountain, and the living patches, deprived of their hidden water, would wither.

The power did not forbid the hollow to change. It did not promise it forever.

Instead, where the seam was weakest, it eased the pressure by a fraction. Stone settled against stone. The water kept its course. The change continued—so slowly that even the power did not call it stillness—but the hollow was spared that particular ending.

No sign remained. No light shone over the rim. The plants did not turn toward the sky in gratitude; they leaned, as before, toward warmth and water. A bubble rose in one pool and vanished without a sound.

The power left the place as it had found it: quiet, bounded, and alive in ways that did not require a witness. It did not make the hollow sacred. It understood that the hollow had never needed its permission.

And for a long while afterward, the water stayed close beneath the stone.
