The power did not mistake the Reach for a ruin.

It saw the soft clicks in the clay, the delayed water, the setters working their careful mouths along the joints. It listened to the heavier knocks moving beneath the tiles. It understood that the basin was not waiting to become something else. Its unfinishedness was not a failure, but a way of being alive.

So the power did not give it a plan.

It did not raise the roofs into a palace, or clear the courts, or send a bright road down the middle. It did not make the drains run straight. It did not ask the inhabitants to come into the open and explain themselves.

For a long while, it only paid attention.

And because it paid attention, it learned where the Reach was in danger—not where a wall leaned, or a channel doubled back, but where a settling slab would crush a nest of setters; where water, delayed by some hidden turn, would arrive too late for the pale creatures waiting in a dry seam.

There, and only there, the power placed a hand beneath the weight.

No hand could be seen. No new material appeared. The slab still shifted, and its cracks still widened and closed with the seasons. But it no longer fell. It rested on a strength that yielded a little when the Reach moved, and held a little when the Reach threatened to move too far.

Then the power waited again.

The settlers found the altered pressure first. They stopped at the seam, touched it with their small mouths, and began laying ash in a new line. The line ran toward the safer hollow. Over several rains, it became a channel—not straight, not permanent, but sufficient. The larger inhabitants passed beneath the supported slab. Their knocks paused once, then continued.

The power did not take this for thanks. It did not take it for consent to do more.

In one court, where the roof had fallen away and no roof had yet replaced it, the power let the dimness lift by a fraction. Not daylight: only enough brightness to make the wet brick visible. The setters came and went. A pale creature drank there. After a time, a fresh course of clay crept across the opening and dimmed it again.

The power allowed this too.

It might have finished the court. It might have made the water dependable, the passages legible, the walls safe from every collapse. But it knew that a life protected from all change would not be the life that had drawn its attention. The Reach needed room not only to endure, but to answer.

So the power kept one thing: a possibility.

Wherever a sudden failure would have meant only harm, there would be, for a little while, strength enough to let the Reach choose its next shape. Not enough to prevent the settling. Not enough to forbid the crack. Enough to make the difference between being altered and being ended.

The inhabitants never saw the power. They had no name for it. In the drains, the knocks went on. In the wet seams, the setters continued their small, patient labor. Water still took the route laid out for it, except when it didn’t.

And the Reach remained unfinished.

But now, in one place beneath the crowded dark, it was not alone in holding itself together.
