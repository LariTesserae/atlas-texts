The power did not mistake stillness for sorrow.

It did not look upon the plateau and call it broken because nothing changed. It did not see the crowded animals and assume they longed for solitude, or see their open mouths and invent a hunger for them. It watched for a long while—not as a traveler watches, counting bodies and searching for a path, but with the patience to let a thing be what it was before deciding what it needed.

It saw that each creature was particular, even where the same creature appeared many times. It saw that the plateau made room for footsteps without becoming less full. It saw the old doorways, and the care—whatever care meant here—with which they had been placed.

So the power did almost nothing.

At one doorway, it made the threshold honest.

Until then, a traveler could pass through only by looking away. The power did not remove the distortion, or repair the door, or make the world on either side more sensible. It only allowed this one passage to be crossed with one’s eyes open. Beyond it was a narrow shelf, no larger than a room. No animals occupied it. None had been driven away to make space. It had simply never been included in the plateau’s crowding.

The power did not call the place safe, or peaceful, or better. It left the doorway open.

For a time, nothing happened. The animals remained as they were. The marks in the stone repeated. The air held its cool, dry silence.

Then one of the creatures drew back a feeler that had rested on its neighbor for so long that the two seemed joined. It gathered its limbs beneath itself and moved—slowly, in the old rippling way—toward the doorway. Perhaps it meant to enter. Perhaps the movement was only movement. The power did not decide for it.

The creature paused at the threshold. Its many instances along the distant ridge paused too. None vanished. None was pushed from its place. The creature could remain among its kind, or step into the quiet shelf, or turn back. For the first time, these were not merely different descriptions of the same arrangement.

The power waited.

It would not make the plateau into another world simply because it could. It would not put a next event into every life and call that mercy. But it had placed one possibility where there had been none, and left the choice with the beings who would bear its consequences.

The creature crossed.

Nothing sang. No light changed. The plateau did not awaken. The creature settled on the empty stone, alone—not abandoned, not separated forever, simply alone for as long as it wished to be.

And the power, having offered what it could not know whether the creature wanted, remained near enough to hear if it should ever call.
