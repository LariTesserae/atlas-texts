The power did not mistake stillness for peace, or peace for a thing that needed correcting.

It watched the basin for a long while. It watched the small lives pass their days from mouth to mouth; the grazers return to their water; the hunters take what they needed and vanish back into the leaves. It heard the deep call sound at its appointed intervals. Nothing cried out to be rescued. Nothing asked to be made grander, faster, or more beautiful.

So the power did not arrive as thunder. It did not lift the canopy or break the pale stones or send a flood to scour the basin clean. It came as a silence so gentle that at first it seemed only another pause in the chorus.

Then it listened—not merely to the calls, but to what lay between them.

The basin’s being was not the hidden caller alone, though the caller gave it a voice. It was the old agreement of roots and mud, water and appetite, shelter and passage. It had no words for *I am content* or *I am waiting*. It had only its deep, regular note, and the paths worn nowhere yet visible beneath the plants.

The power understood that it could not know, from the outside, whether the basin wished to remain as it was. Benevolence, it knew, was not the same as choosing on another’s behalf.

So it offered no command. It gave the basin a possibility.

At the lowest edge of the mud, where water had always gathered and gone no farther, one bead slipped over the lip of a stone. It travelled the length of a root, darkened a thread of soil, and stopped. Nothing else changed. The leaves held their water. The animals followed their paths. The deep caller sounded again.

The power waited.

A long-necked grazer drank from the familiar hollow. A small scavenger followed its feet. Somewhere under a leaf, a beetle laid its eggs. The bead of water remained where it had stopped, shining in the dimness.

Then another drop found it.

Together they made a thin, shining line through the mud. It did not cut a valley or hurry toward some distant sea. It merely went a little farther than water had gone before. A pale growth opened beside it. An insect came to drink. In the deep green, the caller shifted its weight and gave a note that was not quite the same as the last.

The basin heard itself change.

It could have let the little channel close. The roots were strong enough to take it back; the mud could have swallowed it by morning. The power would not have forced it open again. But, drop by drop, the water kept its new direction. Not because the basin had been judged incomplete, but because it had been offered the chance to become something else—and had answered.

The power did not give it a destination. It gave it time: not an order to flourish, nor a sentence of endless becoming, but room for one event to leave a mark on the next. Rain might come differently. A creature might wander beyond its old route. The channel might deepen, or vanish, or lead to another living place. The basin would decide what it could, as living things decide: through countless small responses, none of them certain in advance.

When the power withdrew, the chorus filled the silence again.

Only now, beneath it, ran the faintest sound of moving water. The deep caller answered, and this time its note did not fall at precisely the appointed interval.

Nothing was taken from the basin. It was not rescued from its abundance, nor awakened from a sleep it had chosen. It was simply given a future—and left free to make of it what it would.
