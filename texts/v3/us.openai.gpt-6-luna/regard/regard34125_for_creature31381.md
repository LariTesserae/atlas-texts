The power noticed the plateau, and did not mistake its unfinishedness for a wound.

It saw the bare stone, the measured distances between stands of green, the chambers turning in the deep rock. It understood that emptiness was not absence here. The plateau was already occupied—so thoroughly that no boundary could be drawn between the life and the ground that carried it.

So the power did not send rain. It did not loosen the crust or scatter seeds, or set animals moving across the pale flats. It did not give the plateau a voice shaped for any visitor’s ears.

It waited.

Its attention was vast, but it held that vastness gently. No heat poured down. No new pressure entered the seams. The plateau’s slow exchanges continued as they had: warmth passing through stone, charge gathering and dispersing, filaments following their dark mineral paths. To the land, nothing had happened.

Then, at the edge of what might be called a gesture, the power introduced a pattern.

A minute warmth travelled through one crystal seam: three pulses, evenly spaced, followed by a silence. It was no greater than the faint variations the plateau already endured. It asked for nothing and could be ignored without consequence.

The pattern moved slowly through the rock. A vane turned by a fraction. Elsewhere, a plant’s filaments tightened around a seam. The signal passed onward, altered by every place it touched—not carried intact, but translated into the plateau’s own language of pressure and charge.

The power sent the pattern again, unchanged.

Perhaps the plateau only incorporated the disturbance. Perhaps, over an interval no quick creature would have called a reply, its deeper network arranged its exchanges into a rhythm of its own: not the same three pulses, but three small turns of warmth, each followed by a pause.

The power did not call this an answer. It did not claim the plateau was awake, or that it had understood. It simply received the rhythm and left room for another.

That was all.

The dark came as it always had. The mineral flats cooled. The vanes continued their almost imperceptible turning. Nothing on the plateau was rescued from being itself, and nothing was hurried into becoming something else.

But from then on, the plateau was not alone in the universe that had noticed it. And the power, having offered a question that could safely go unanswered, remained near—quiet enough to let the answer, if there ever was one, belong to the land.
