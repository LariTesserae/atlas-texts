The power did not arrive.

Arrival would have given it a direction, a boundary, a place to stand apart from the living place. Instead, it turned its attention toward the low country and stayed attentive.

It did not mistake the city’s motion for distress. It did not call the shifting ground broken, or the passages that changed their length confused. It watched a wall loosen into soil, and the soil gather itself into shoots. It saw that what might look, from a distance, like one immense creature was also a multitude—and that neither the multitude nor the city could be separated from the making of it.

So the power did not give the inhabitants faces. It did not teach them speech. It did not straighten the streets or raise a lasting roof above them. The place was not waiting to be finished.

For a long while, the power did nothing that could be seen. It learned the city’s rhythms: the slow pull of roots toward water, the sudden snap of new growth, the pauses in which every leaf held still. It learned that a silence here was not emptiness, and that a tremor might carry more than one answer.

Then, at the edge of a fresh rupture, the ground opened wider than the new growth could bridge. A shelf of raw clay tipped toward a hollow where water had gathered. The city responded in fragments: roots tightening, stems bending, a corridor drawing shut. The responses crossed and contradicted one another. The whole place shivered with the effort of finding a way.

The power did not close the rupture. It gave the ground a little more time.

The clay held. Roots reached it. Stalks braided across the gap, some breaking, some taking hold. Water drained into the dark below. The city changed its shape around the wound, as it had changed around a thousand others. Nothing was made permanent. Nothing was spared the labor of becoming.

The inhabitants felt the difference—not as a voice, but as an unfamiliar steadiness beneath the moment. A delay in the fall. A breath in which to answer.

They reached toward that steadiness through the soil. Their questions were not words. They were warmth, pressure, moisture drawn along rootways; they were the opening of one passage and the closing of another.

The power received what it could, without pretending that every movement meant the same thing. Some parts of the city leaned toward it. Some withdrew. Some continued growing as if nothing had happened. The power accepted all of this as answer.

It offered no bargain. It asked for no name.

It made only a promise, woven lightly into the world: that when a force came capable of ending the city all at once—of leaving no seed, no root, no pattern by which it might begin again—the city would have a moment in which to respond. Not safety from every loss. Not rescue from every change. A moment: enough for a root to turn, a passage to open, a multitude to become aware of what was coming.

The inhabitants did not understand the promise as a whole. Perhaps no single part of them could. But now and then, when the ground split or a wall gave way, the pause would return: not an interruption of growth, but room inside it.

And the power remained attentive.

It did not ask the city to become still so that it might be loved. It loved the city in motion: unfinished, crowded, many-minded, making and unmaking its ways through the warm low country.

When a visitor came, and the lane tightened gently around their footsteps, the power did not tell the city to welcome them. It did not tell the visitor they were safe. It left the encounter to those within it: the roots beneath the stranger’s feet, the leaves turning their wet undersides, the living walls deciding whether to part.

Then it listened to what happened next.
