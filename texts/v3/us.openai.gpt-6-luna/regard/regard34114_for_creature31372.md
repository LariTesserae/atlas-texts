The power did not make the plateau finished.

It did not smooth the ridges, still the wind, or quiet the water. It did not gather the shelf-menders into a herd where they could be admired. It knew that to be seen was not always to be helped.

Instead, it attended.

When a pump drew too hard from a shallow basin, it did not break the machine. It let the change travel farther than it should: through the stone, beneath the green mat, into the feet of a shelf-mender resting under a lifted edge. The animal moved. Its small knocks passed through the wet ground. Another moved, then another, until a whole stretch of the mat stirred—not enough to alarm anyone, but enough to send a worker back to check the level.

The worker found the basin lower than the gauge said. Later, the gauge was replaced. The pump’s schedule was changed. A strip of ground around the pools was marked for no vehicles, then left unmarked again, because the best boundary was one the animals did not have to understand.

The power made no sign of itself. It did not put a voice in the worker’s mind or a blessing on the water. It only gave the worker time to look twice, and the patience to believe what the land was showing.

After that, small things went differently. A maintenance track bent around a soft, busy hollow. A leaking pipe was repaired before its warmth began to draw the shelf-menders too close to the station. When the gantry needed replacing, the new one allowed the stream to wander beneath it. The uplands remained loud with water and machinery, but the machinery learned, by human hands, to take up less room.

For the shelf-menders, nothing changed enough to become a story. Their routes stayed hidden. The pools still warmed and clouded; the sedges still rattled in the wind. They went on rasping green film from stone, pressing their broad feet into the mats, and making small channels where water might pass.

Sometimes, when the shelf was quiet, a shelf-mender would tap once beneath the plants.

From somewhere farther off came two taps in answer.

The power did not answer for them. It did not need to. It had not come to be heard, or thanked, or made the center of the place. It had come to notice what was already there—and, where it could, to keep the fragile conditions of its continuance from being carelessly taken away.

The plateau went on becoming itself. The power let it.
