The power did not descend in fire, or take a shape the plateau could mistake for a visitor.

It arrived as attention.

For a long while, nothing in the green distinguished that attention from the weather. The clouds passed beneath the terraces. Water ticked from the roofs. The reed towers sounded their low notes into the humid air. Then, beneath all those familiar sounds, there was another: a silence so complete it seemed to be listening back.

The plateau heard it.

Across the open meadows, root-pillars tightened around their own deep quiet. Pale creatures paused beneath the bridges. In the pools, reflections held still even as the clouds moved on.

The power listened to the place as it was—not for what it might become, but for what it already was. It heard the rain-guided roots and the halls answering one another over the distances. It heard the delicate traffic of the small lives within the green. It heard, too, the loneliness that might have been there, if loneliness were a thing the plateau knew.

Then it asked.

Not in words. It offered a possibility: that the stream might choose a different course. That one flower might close. That a new terrace might rise, or an old path soften beneath the low leaves. Not a command, not a cure—only the clear knowledge that the world did not have to remain exactly as it was.

The plateau considered.

This took no time at all, and all the time there was.

At last, a single note rose from the reed tower at the marsh’s edge. Another answered from the farthest hall. The sound moved through the woven stems and the listening roots, crossing the broad green distances.

It did not mean *change me.*

It did not mean *leave me as I am.*

It meant: *I am here.*

The power understood.

It had seen worlds mistake stillness for failure, and movement for life. It had seen kindness become a hand closing around what it meant to protect. So it did not make the plateau grow. It did not awaken its inhabitants into a different kind of wanting, or put a road through the empty ground, or give the halls voices that could be understood by passing strangers.

Instead, it left the question with them—not as a seed they must grow, but as a door that could remain unopened.

Should the plateau ever wish to change, it would know that change was possible.

For now, the water went on sounding in its chosen channels. The creatures drifted beneath the bridges. The halls listened to the weather and answered in their old, hollow music.

And somewhere, in a dark pool, a pale body turned once beneath the surface. The roots felt the movement. A distant wall gave a faint, answering hum.

The power stayed long enough to hear it, then departed without leaving a footprint.

The low leaves closed over the place where it had been.

But the plateau kept the question.
