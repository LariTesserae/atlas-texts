The power did not descend in a column of light. Light would have been of little use there; it would have struck the mist and gone no farther.

It came as attention.

For a long while it did nothing. It listened to the pumps laboring beneath the streets, to water ticking from leaves, to the small, continual negotiations between root and brick. It learned which noises meant danger and which were merely the district speaking in its sleep. It learned the difference between a wall that was failing and a wall that had simply learned to wear moss.

Then, because it was wise, it did not try to make the place dry.

It found the oldest pump and eased the strain in its bearings. Not enough to silence the machine—its rough, familiar pulse belonged to the district—but enough that it no longer had to grind itself toward exhaustion. It cleared a buried channel where silt had gathered, and mended a gate whose stubbornness had been making the water rise too high in the lowest cellars. The water still passed through everything. It simply stopped being forced, so often, through the same wound.

It gave the district a few places where the water could rest without doing harm: a widened cistern beneath a courtyard, a stone basin beneath the trees, a shallow garden where overflow could spread and settle. In those places the plants grew differently. Their roots did not have to pry so fiercely at the walls. The water turned clear enough, now and then, to show the dim shapes of the paving below.

The power made no animals appear. It would not populate the district merely to make it seem alive. Instead, it made a passage through the old embankment—not an escape route, exactly, but an opening. Beyond it lay higher ground, a stretch of open air, and the possibility of other lives arriving in their own time.

Then it waited.

The district answered as it could. A gate lifted a little farther than it had in years. A vine crossed the threshold and stopped there, its leaves trembling in the current of air. One by one, the pumps changed their rhythm—not into harmony, but into something less strained. From somewhere under the streets came the sound of water choosing a new way through.

The power understood.

It did not take the district away from its dampness, its old machinery, or the life it had made from both. It did not call persistence a failure simply because persistence was difficult. It left the basin low, the plants rooted, the water moving. But it gave the place a little more room to choose what happened next.

Afterward, the caged lamps still burned amber through the mist. The walls still sweated. Nothing passed through the district unchanged.

Only now, sometimes, something passed through and found a way to leave.
