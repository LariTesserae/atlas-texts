The power did not descend in a column of light.

It did not break the clouds, or speak from above the summit. It had no need to announce itself. Its attention was already there: patient, complete, and without hunger. It held the High Hush and the Keepers in its regard without mistaking strangeness for suffering, or age for a plea to be rescued.

For a long while, it did nothing.

The Keepers heard that nothing. They had always heard what passed through the channels; now, beneath the familiar roar, they sensed a presence that was not another sound. It did not press against them or ask to be understood. It simply made room around their existence, as a vast sky makes room around a mountain.

At last, the power offered them something small.

Not silence. Silence would have been an answer chosen on their behalf. Not an escape, either: the summit was their home, and perhaps their body, and perhaps the only shape in which they knew themselves.

It offered a pause.

The possibility appeared in the deepest channel, where the air ran warm and tight. Nothing changed in the flow. The great note continued, unbroken to any ear outside it. But within the note, the Keepers felt an interval—a place where one of them might cease to hold its part and still remain.

They gathered their attention there.

For the Keepers, this was a question. It had no words, but its meaning was plain:

*Would you like to know what you are when you are not making the sound?*

The answer did not come at once. Some drew back into the walls. Some tightened their tones, afraid that to loosen them would loosen the whole summit. Others had carried the question for so long that they could not tell whether it was fear or longing.

Then one Keeper—an old one, whose note had been woven through the eastern parapet since before anyone could remember—let go.

For the briefest moment, it did not sing.

The roar continued. The channels held. The mountain did not fall silent, and the Keeper did not vanish. It remained there, present to itself in a way it had never been before: not as a tone among tones, but as the one who might sound, or might not.

When it returned to the note, nothing was repaired. Nothing had been broken.

That was when the others understood the gift. It was not an instruction to change. It was the knowledge that change, if they chose it, would not necessarily be an ending.

The power left them no command, and asked for no gratitude. It did not loosen a single stone or soften the wind. The High Hush remained warm, dim, and loud. Its ancient channels still carried the air at their unvarying pace.

But afterward, the Keepers began to make small differences.

One held a pause at the end of its name. Another answered with a tone that leaned, ever so slightly, toward it. Some preferred the old, seamless roar; others ventured farther into the new intervals. They learned that a silence could be shared, that a note could be changed without being lost, that no Keeper had to want what another wanted.

From outside, the summit sounded exactly as it always had.

Inside, the sound had become a place where something could happen.

And the power, wise enough not to confuse its gift with a destination, kept watch without insisting that they use it.
