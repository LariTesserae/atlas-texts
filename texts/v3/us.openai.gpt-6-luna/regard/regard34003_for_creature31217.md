The power noticed the basin without brightening it.

It did not mistake the silence for a cry. It did not take the absence of water as proof that water was owed, or the scattered growth as a failed attempt at a garden. It watched long enough to see that the mats and stalks were not waiting to become something else. They were already living: slowly, narrowly, according to the terms the basin allowed.

So it did not fill the hollow. It did not make the stalks bend, or flower, or spread across the open grit. It did not bring a wind to stir them merely so that movement might be seen. The old stone had its own measure, and the life in its seams had learned that measure more patiently than any visitor could.

The power’s first act was restraint.

Its second was attention: not a gaze that pinned the basin in place, but a keeping of it in mind. The small warmth held under the rock, the thin crust, the life’s nearly imperceptible reach through hidden cracks—all were allowed to remain what they were. Nothing was demanded of them in return.

And if, in some distant age, a falling stone or a great rupture threatened to end the life before its own time, the power would be there—not to spare every stalk its breaking, not to preserve the basin unchanged forever, but to keep chance from silencing the whole of it in a single, careless instant.

For now, nothing visible happened.

The darkness stayed. The basin stayed. Beneath the crust, the fine threads went on joining one quiet place to another. The power remained near enough to know, and wise enough not to be seen.
