The power did not enter through one of the gates.

It had no need to. It was already listening.

It listened through the stone, through the channels and the fitted seams, through the small bodies tucked into the architecture. It listened long enough to learn that the Lower Choir was not a broken thing waiting to be made whole. The dry arch had its reason, whether known or not. The cold basin had its season. The fish’s strange route, the bats’ forbidden passage, the rootless creeper—each belonged to a pattern whose meaning could be damaged by a well-meant repair.

So the power did not straighten the leaning walls. It did not fill the basin with light, or make the frogs’ eggs hatch in the cold bowl. It did not give the Choir a voice apart from its own many voices.

It waited through a rain.

When the water began to fall, the channels took it as they always had: some quickly, some reluctantly, one carrying a thin thread toward the lower gate. The roofs answered in drops. The frogs began their blunt calls. The bats stirred beneath the arches. The whole place gathered itself into its wavering roar.

Then the power noticed what lay beyond the retaining walls: not an immediate danger, but the long, ordinary kind. A hand might one day mistake the basin for useless space. A gate might be widened. A channel might be blocked to make the ground simpler for someone who had never heard it breathe.

The power did not forbid such hands. It made them capable of hesitation.

A builder, years hence, would come with plans and pause at the wall. A surveyor would notice the water’s path and ask where it came from. Someone would hear the bats at dusk and decide not to seal the vents. No one would know why these small delays mattered. They would simply leave the gates narrow, the channels open, and the basin unfilled.

That was the power’s work: not to keep the Lower Choir unchanged, but to leave it room to change in its own way.

It departed without a sign. No stone shone. No creature received a prophecy. The next rain came when it came, and the one after that did not. A tile loosened. Moss spread over a seam. The pale creeper crossed another finger’s breadth of lintel.

And once, beneath the dry arch, a bat woke from sleep, listened to the water, and did not fly away.
