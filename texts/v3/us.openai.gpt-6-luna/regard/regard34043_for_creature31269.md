The power did not open the seam.

It did not pour water into the dry stone, or send sunlight down the crack, or make the leaves flower so that life might look more familiar. It did not mistake stillness for a plea. For a long while it simply attended: to the faint charge moving through the rock, to the roots holding mineral and metal together, to the engine’s hammering and the pressure it maintained behind the grille.

It learned that the machine was not merely an old thing left running. Its steady failure to fail had become part of the place’s weather.

So the power made one small provision. Deep inside the engine, beyond any hand’s reach, it placed a quiet safeguard—not a new command, but a reserve. If the mechanism began to falter, the reserve would keep the air and charge from vanishing all at once. It would not keep the cavity unchanged forever. It would only prevent an accident from deciding the future on the inhabitants’ behalf.

Then it left the seam as it had found it: cool, dry, and dim. The leaves held their angles. The engine kept its rough note. No sign appeared to say *you have been seen*.

But the plate above the crack no longer strained toward collapse, and the small life spread through the stone was given what it had never asked for: not rescue, not transformation, but time enough for whatever came next to come from within.
