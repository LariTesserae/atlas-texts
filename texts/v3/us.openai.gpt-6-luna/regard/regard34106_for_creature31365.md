The power does not stop the hollow.

It sees how easily a hand might mistake motion for damage: the splitting stone, the vanished pools, the herds breaking through walls that were young only yesterday. But it watches longer. It sees that the hollow is not failing to become a place. Becoming is what it is.

So the power does not smooth the floor or seal the daylight seams. It does not quiet the wings, or make the hunters gentle, or preserve every shelter that the ground has already outgrown. It will not turn a living change into a monument to safety.

It listens instead for the moments when change becomes too sudden to be lived through.

At those moments—before a roof gives way, before a basin empties into a crack, before a ridge shears off beneath a blind herd—it grants the hollow a little warning. Not a command, not a map. A fraction of time: a tremor that travels clearly through shell and hoof and foot; a draft that carries the taste of fresh stone; a drop of water falling where the clappers can catch its note. Enough for bodies to turn, if they can. Enough for the people to lift a lamp and choose another passage.

The warning does not prevent loss. A shelf still breaks. A pool still disappears. Some creatures do not move quickly enough, and some paths end. The power knows that a world in which nothing can be lost is not the same as a world in which life is safe. It does not promise the hollow an end to danger. It offers its inhabitants, where it can, the chance to meet danger awake.

And it gives the people no permanent road. Instead, it teaches them—through the sounds of their own clappers, through the shifting wetness of the stone, through the routes the animals take—to notice when a road has ceased to be a road. Their cairns still fall. Their shelters still need rebuilding. But now and then, someone recognizes the warning before the ground opens beneath them, and leaves in time.

The creatures receive no lesson in words. Their bodies simply find, more often than before, a seam that leads somewhere livable, a ledge that holds through the night, a watercourse that does not end all at once. The great plated animals still pry the young fissures wider. The crawlers still follow, cleaning and feeding and being fed upon. The winged clouds still scatter when the air turns. Nothing is made harmless. The hollow’s many lives remain tangled in the old practical bargain: each making room, each losing room, all moving on.

Perhaps the power does one other thing. When the hollow falls silent after a deep crack, it lets the silence last just long enough for the living to hear one another—not only the great herd, not only the people, but the small bodies touching shell to shell, wing to wing, in the dark. Then the sounds return: the water tapping, the clappers answering, the busy rasp of life across new stone.

The power does not tell the hollow what to become.

It keeps a little space open between what is breaking and what may yet live.
