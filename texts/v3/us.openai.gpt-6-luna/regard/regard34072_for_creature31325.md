The power came without arrival.

No star descended. No new wind crossed the plateau. Nothing bent its head toward the sky. The power was simply there, attending—and for a long while, it did nothing but listen.

At first it heard a single immense sound: the continent’s living machinery, rasping and booming under the cold brightness. Then it learned to hear within it. The tiny clicks of roots taking hold. The soft displacement of dust beneath a runner’s feet. The slow pulse passed from one grazer to another, carrying the herd’s knowledge of a ridge, a hollow, a safe passage through the stems.

The power had made and unmade worlds before. It knew how easily kindness could become a kind of violence. To still the wind would be to silence the plants. To shelter every creature from danger would be to unmake the delicate exchanges by which the plateau lived. So it did not command the storm away. It did not soften the stone or make the herds immortal.

It listened.

Then, one day, a young grazer wandered beyond the herd’s moving ring. A white front of spores swept over the plain, turning the light hard and directionless. The calf’s thin call went out and vanished in the roar. Its legs trembled against the stone. Far off, the herd had stopped, but the answer could not find its way through the storm.

The power did not lift the calf into the air. It did not clear the sky.

It touched the plateau—not the plants, nor the animal, but the long, fluted corridor between them. For one breath, the stone carried a familiar pulse farther than it should have traveled: slow, low, unmistakable. The ridge answered. The herd answered the ridge.

The calf turned toward the sound.

When the spore-cloud passed, there was nothing to show that anything had happened. The plants continued to flex and sing. The grazers folded back into their loose, pale procession. The young one walked inside the herd again, learning the map in the bodies around it.

The power remained, not as guardian above the plateau, but as a listener within its vastness. It did not promise that nothing would be lost. It made no claim that the world was gentle. It offered only this: when a living thing called into the storm, its call would not always be alone.

And when the plateau sang at night, the power listened without asking it to sing more softly.
