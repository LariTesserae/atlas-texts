The power noticed them first as a sound.

It heard the machine’s hum, then the rasp within it: so many tiny lives keeping contact in the dark. It listened long enough to learn that the rasp was not a cry for help. The creatures were crowded, but not calling the crowd a prison. They were cold, but the cold was the condition they knew how to live in. Their world was narrow; still, within it, they fed, multiplied, died, and made room for the young.

So the power did not tear open the mountain.

It did not lift the ceiling, soften the metal, or give the creatures eyes that could see beyond their basin. It did not mistake their smallness for a plea to become large. To flood their world with warmth or freedom, without knowing what those things would mean to them, would have been a kindness only from a distance.

But neither did it leave them entirely to chance.

The machine had been made to hold its note forever, and forever is a dangerous promise. One day, a seal might fail. A pipe might clog. A service hand, unaware of the living skin behind the plate, might scrape it clean. The power could not make the creatures understand such dangers. It could, however, keep the world from ending by accident.

It laid no visible hand upon the mountain. It touched the smallest hidden joint in the weather machine and made it less likely to seize. The pipes would still tick. The water would still bead and run. The chamber would remain cold, cramped, and loud—but its rhythm would not fail for want of a careless bolt.

Then, after a long while, the power made one other change.

A single bead of water, forming at the ceiling, followed a route it had never followed before. It slipped past the familiar channel and descended to an unfinished seam on the far side of the basin. There, it gathered in a shallow hollow no larger than a breath.

Nothing was commanded to go there. No creature was promised a reward. The old routes remained, and the old water kept its course. But the new hollow stayed damp. A clear animal found it. Then another. One of the pale insects crossed the distance, touched the wet seam, and turned back—carrying, on its legs, the faintest trace of the place.

The power watched what they would make of the opening.

Perhaps nothing. Perhaps, after many generations, a new colony would take hold there, and the rasp of the chamber would acquire one unfamiliar note. Perhaps the hollow would dry, and the old pattern would continue unchanged.

Either outcome was allowed.

For the power’s gift was not escape. It was not a destiny, nor an order to become more than they were. It was a little protection for the world they had made their own, and a little room in which that world might surprise itself.

Then the power withdrew from the mountain’s crown—not gone, but no longer pressing its attention against the hidden pocket. Below, the creatures went on turning toward each pipe’s tick. The droplets descended along their accustomed routes. In the new seam, one clear body combed the water, making a current for the next.
