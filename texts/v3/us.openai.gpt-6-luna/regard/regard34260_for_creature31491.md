### The Measure of Help

The power did not descend upon the plateau.

It did not split the clouds with a voice, or raise a forest from the stone. It did not warm the air until the animals could forget what their bodies had learned, or make food so plentiful that the old paths lost their meaning. It was wise enough to know that hardship was not, by itself, proof of neglect—and that to remake a place in the image of comfort might be another way of destroying it.

So, for a long while, it did nothing.

It watched the herds comb the rock. It watched the small lives beneath the frost-thin crust, and the water threading through basalt where no eye could follow. It watched an old grazer stumble, and the scavengers descend when the body had cooled. It did not call this good. It did not call it evil. It saw that the death entered the same patient exchange as everything else, though the life that ended had not been interchangeable to itself.

The power understood that a world could be whole without being gentle.

Then came a succession of dry years. The basins failed to fill. The damp seams retreated beneath the rock. The herds altered their routes, as they had altered them before, but this time the old passages led to stone that yielded nothing. Young animals weakened. The lichen paled. In one great valley, the underground water dwindled toward a point from which it might not return—not in a season, or a century, or any span the plateau’s lives could endure.

The power did not send rain.

Instead, far below the surface, it held open a narrow fracture in the basalt.

Water found the fracture as water had always found fractures. It moved through the dark, slow as mineral change, and surfaced in a hollow the herds still knew by its taste. The hollow did not become a lake. It offered no abundance, only enough dampness for the lichen to take hold again, and enough living film for the grazers to keep moving.

Some animals died all the same. Some routes were lost. The drought lasted longer than the power wished it to. When rain finally returned, the hollow looked no different from any other black basin after thaw.

The herds did not know they had been helped. They had no reason to.

And the power did not ask to be thanked. It had not saved the plateau from change. It had preserved the possibility of change continuing.

Afterward, it remained attentive. Not as a keeper standing over the land, but as a presence that knew where the thin places were: the hidden spring, the failing refuge, the passage too easily erased. Most of the time it left them alone. The plateau had its own answers, written in bodies and stone and long migrations. Wisdom did not mean replacing those answers with its own.

Now and then, when the cloud-shadow crossed the ridges, a herd would pause. Heads lifted. Metallic crests angled toward the weather. A faint shiver passed through their plated backs.

They were listening to the storm.

They were listening to one another.

And, though they could never have known it, they were not listening alone.
