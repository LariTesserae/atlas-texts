The power noticed the hollow first as a pattern in the deep.

It had listened to mountains split, to rivers worrying their beds, to the restless heat turning beneath the world. This was quieter: water counting its way down leaves; roots revising their course; a thousand small lives passing through one another without quite becoming one. Beneath those motions lay a slower attention—not a mind shaped like a person’s, but a life gathering itself.

The power did not descend.

A presence such as that could have filled the hollow in an instant. It could have made every pearl bulb flare, every root go still. It could have given the hollow a name, a voice, a desire to be understood. But the power was wise enough to know that being noticed did not mean wishing to be changed.

So it listened.

When the deep resonance came again, the power heard the hollow listening back. The red threads brightened. The pools trembled. In their wavering surfaces, distances unfolded and folded: a geometry too large for the chamber, yet held within it.

The power answered with one quiet pulse.

Not a word. Not a question with a shape the hollow might be forced to imitate. Just a sound set beside the old resonance, gentle enough to be mistaken for an echo. It moved through the stone, reached the roots, and faded.

The hollow altered one leaf. A pearl bulb flickered. Somewhere in the wall, a root turned toward a seam of water.

The power waited.

For a long while, nothing more happened. The little grazers fed. The blind swimmers traced the edges of their pools. Condensation gathered and fell. The hollow continued becoming itself.

Then, far above, the buried stone shifted under a pressure that would have fractured the chamber’s ceiling. The power eased that pressure aside. It did not seal the hollow away from the world, or make the stone invulnerable. It only prevented this particular break, this particular ending. Water still found its old paths. The raw seams remained raw. The hollow kept the freedom to change.

No creature saw what had been spared. The living roof creaked once, and held.

The power might have done more. It might have warmed the chamber, made its inhabitants visible to distant eyes, or raised a clear passage through the stone. It might have tried to teach the hollow a way to answer.

Instead, it left an invitation: another pulse, much later, at the same deep interval.

This time, the hollow answered—not in sound, but in arrangement. The pearl bulbs drew a little closer above the pool. The red threads glowed in sequence, carrying the pulse from chamber to chamber. At the edge, where stone met leaves, a new arch formed. It did not open outward, exactly. It simply made room.

The power received this without claiming to understand it.

After that, it kept watch from beyond the hollow’s reach. It did not call the place a sanctuary, or the being within it a child, or the small creatures its subjects. It did not mistake patience for a request, or quiet for loneliness. It watched for dangers the hollow could not yet meet, and intervened only where intervention would leave the hollow free to continue its own slow labor.

And sometimes, when the buried world resonated, the power answered.

Sometimes the hollow answered back.

Neither required the other to become more like itself.
