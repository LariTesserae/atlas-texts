The power did not arrive as light.

Light would have changed too much. It would have made the creatures hide, and the buried filaments recoil, and the water remember a sky it had never seen. So the power came as attention: patient, complete, and without demand.

It listened to the basin.

It listened through the stones, through the threads beneath them, through every small impact that made up the great roar. It learned the old pressures and the newer ones. It learned that the plain’s trembling was not merely damage, nor its silence merely emptiness. The buried multitude held the basin together. The little jointed creatures went to disturbances not because they wished to escape them, but because the changes mattered. Even the reeds, bending both ways, were doing what they had always done.

And so the power did not lift the basin out of itself.

It did not drain the water, or smooth the storms away, or raise a green world over the dark one. It did not decide that stillness was sickness, or that a place without a horizon must be longing for one. Such a rescue would have been only another disturbance, larger than the rest.

Instead, it found the small creature on the shelf of stone.

The creature had stopped at a seam. Its transparent limbs trembled in order: one, then another, then all at once. Far below, the buried filaments tightened. The tremor traveled outward, dispersed through the packed earth, and became indistinguishable from the roar.

The power answered.

Not with words. Words would have had nowhere to land. It answered with a rhythm so quiet that, at first, the creature mistook it for a pause in its own body.

The creature remained still.

The power repeated the rhythm, exactly as before. It offered no command in it, no promise that the world would be made easy. Only a space: small, steady, and unoccupied.

The creature touched the seam.

For one breath, the roar did not cease, but it no longer filled everything. Within it, the creature could feel the sequence of its own limbs. Beneath that, it felt the answering movements of the buried multitude. Beneath those, farther away than distance could measure, it felt the power waiting without pressing closer.

The creature sent its tremor again.

This time, something answered from the plain—not the power, but the filaments, taking up the rhythm and carrying it through the dark. The pattern passed under stone and water. It reached the black cups of lichen, where pale threads turned in their beads. It reached the reeds, whose clear stalks bent without choosing a direction. For a little while, the basin contained a sound that belonged to no storm and no grinding layer of earth.

The power gave that sound somewhere to rest.

At the ring of broken stones, where the water had always moved strangely, a small hollow formed in the roar. Not silence exactly. The basin was never silent. But here, for a few moments, impacts did not drown one another. The water could be heard meeting stone. The stone could be heard holding. And the creatures, if they came, could hear themselves.

The change was no larger than a bead of water. It did not cure the plain of its weight. It did not stop the next storm from gathering beneath the clouds. But it remained.

The power left it there, available and unclaimed.

Then it waited.

The creature crossed the shelf. It passed the old pillar, went on through shallow water, and did not turn toward the hollow. Perhaps it had no use for it yet. Perhaps it had already carried the pattern elsewhere. The power did not follow, and did not call it back.

Far below, the buried multitude loosened by a fraction. A channel opened, though no one could tell whether it was new or had always been waiting. Water flowed through it in both directions. The basin trembled, and held.

The power stayed attentive—not to keep the world unchanged, but to notice what the world might do next.
