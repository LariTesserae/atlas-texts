The power found the catchment not by searching, but by attending.

It did not arrive as light. There was no sudden warmth, no voice among the pipes. It listened to the chamber’s many kinds of listening: the crabs through their feet, the fish through the runnel, the amphibians through one another. It understood, after a time, that there was no single creature at the heart of the place. The being was the relation between them—the held warmth, the patient rain, the small lives making use of what had been made for them.

Nothing here asked to be rescued.

The power considered the polished shell, the measured channels, the buried machinery. It saw that the catchment was not wild, but neither was it merely a machine. Its life depended on an arrangement that could be forgotten by the people who had built it. To remake the room would be to mistake care for permission. To make its inhabitants aware of a presence they could not understand would be another kind of disturbance.

So, at first, it did nothing.

It remained with the water through one full turning of the pump. It felt the vent draw breath; felt the amphibians answer; felt the answer travel outward and return, softened by every living body and wet surface. The catchment had no silence, but the power found in it a kind of rest.

Then, far beyond the ceramic shell, it touched one small thing.

In the station’s maintenance records, a forgotten line appeared: a note about a concealed catchment, its water exchange, and the signs that would warn of a failing supply. It was written in the language of ordinary work. No one would mistake it for a revelation. Years later, when a technician noticed an unfamiliar rhythm in the pump, the note would be there to find. A door would be opened before it was too late. Someone would come with tools, and with care.

The power did not promise that nothing would ever change. It did not promise that every life would last. It only made sure the place would not be lost through simple neglect.

Inside the room, nothing noticed. The rain fell as it had fallen. A crab lifted its claw at the altered tremor in a pipe, waited, then returned to scraping the wall. The fish passed through their dark slots. On the warm ledge, the amphibians gave their small descending call.

The catchment listened, and carried on.
