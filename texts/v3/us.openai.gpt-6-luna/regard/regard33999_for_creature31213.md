The power did not descend upon the mountain.

It had learned, long ago, that some places mistake arrival for a command.

Instead it paid attention.

It listened through the roar, past the cracking water and the great note rising from beneath the lake. It listened to the fronds turning toward words, to the fish hanging in impossible currents, to the small tapping inside the dry stone rooms. And, most of all, it listened to the thing beneath the basin.

The thing had no name and no settled shape. It was not quite asleep. It had been trying, for a long time, to become something—calling up scraps of motion and memory to see what they might make. A footstep. A breath. The outline of a person at the shore. Each returned to it changed, and none stayed.

The power did not tell it what to become.

At the next great booming note, the power made a small space in the sound. Not silence—silence would have been too much like a wall—but a pause, wide enough for one answer.

The fish stopped moving. The fronds folded. Every returning footstep halted just before it landed.

In that pause, the thing beneath the water felt, for the first time, that it was being heard without being summoned.

It tried to answer with the only voice it had: the note.

But the note came out differently. Not louder. Not softer. Simply its own.

On the shore, one of the figures turned away from the stone room. It took a step toward the lake.

This time, the footfall did not arrive first.

The figure looked down at the water, then back at the room, as if discovering that it might choose where to stand. Beneath it, the lake held its reflection without delay.

The power offered no name. Names can become cages when given too soon. It offered instead a boundary: a narrow ring of calm around the basin, so that the mountain could keep rising without carrying the lake—or whatever lived in it—out into the clouds. It made one of the little stone rooms a threshold: dry for any creature that wished to enter, open to any that wished to leave.

Then it withdrew its attention by no more than a breath.

The basin remained strange. Water still climbed the walls. The shadows still ran ahead of their owners. The mountain continued to form itself in sharp, unfinished shelves. The great note still came from deep below, though now, sometimes, another note answered it from the shore.

The figure stayed.

It learned to make movements that were not borrowed from travelers. It learned that it could listen without becoming every sound it heard. And, when it wished, it could step into the dry room and rest.

The power did nothing more.

It had not come to make the basin ordinary, or to rescue it from being unfinished. It had only made room for the being to discover that unfinished was not the same as abandoned.
