The power did not enter the seam.

There was no room for its full attention—not because the hollow was small, but because attention, made complete, would have filled it. Every mite would have felt itself watched. Every drop would have paused in its falling. The lizard would have opened both eyes. The power understood that being seen can be a kind of disturbance.

So it looked from outside, and then from within, and asked nothing to become larger or more beautiful than it was.

It did not still the mud rise. The mud was making a place; its making involved movement. It did not silence the motor, whose slow heat belonged now to the lives gathered around it. It did not mend every crack or make every pool safe. A world without loss would have been a world without passage, and the seam was made of passages.

But the power saw that the concrete would one day be repaired.

A crew would come across the plain with tools and bright lamps. They would find the floodgate weakening, the mud rise lifting against it. Their plan would be practical: clean the gap, fill it, seal the wall. No malice would guide their hands. They would only see water entering where it ought not, and a small failure in an old structure.

The power did not turn their minds from their work. It did not make the gate immortal, or ask people to abandon the plain. It placed one unremarkable fact in the world where a careful person might find it: a note in an inspection file, a hairline shift in a drawing, the memory of an earlier surveyor who had wondered whether the water was draining somewhere useful.

When the crew arrived, one of them noticed the trickle.

They took measurements. They waited through a shower. They found that the seepage was not undermining the gate, not yet; it was moving through an old drain and easing pressure behind the wall. The hollow itself remained unseen. No one put a light into the seam. No one went searching for what might live in a space so narrow.

They made a different repair: firm where the floodgate needed firmness, open where the water needed passage. They left a rough-edged channel, smaller than a hand, protected from the worst of the flow. It was not made for the inhabitants. It was simply the right way to mend the gate.

The power let the work be ordinary.

Afterward, rain still came in fits. A channel opened, then silted. A beetle lost its shelter and found another. The roots went on lengthening through the concrete’s small failures. The motor turned at its appointed intervals, and the pools broke their impossible reflections into strips of sky.

Once, the mud rose sharply. The seam narrowed. For a while there was almost no room between its living edges. The power did not hold the earth back. It held the repaired gate steady—not against change, but against a change that would have crushed the hollow all at once.

Then the pressure passed.

The lizard shifted to a higher crack. The shrimp rode out the new current. Mites found the altered routes. In the moss, winged insects hatched and filled the dark with their brief, delicate weather.

Nothing in the seam knew that anything had been done for it. This was part of the kindness. The inhabitants were not made into symbols, or summoned to gratitude, or spared the terms of their lives. They remained small, busy, hungry, vulnerable, and occupied with the real work of living.

The power stayed near without taking possession. It kept no account of which creature deserved shelter most. It asked only that the world not become needlessly cruel where a lighter touch would do.

And when the rain gathered beneath the ceiling, the droplets were free to fall—or to vanish against the wall, as they had always done.
