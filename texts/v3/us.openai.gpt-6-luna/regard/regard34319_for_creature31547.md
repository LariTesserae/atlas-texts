The power noticed the hollow without mistaking its smallness for need.

It saw the sharp stone, the cold pool, the lives gathered at the edges. It saw the old wheel turning where water could still persuade it to turn. It saw the hidden resident in the cleft, and understood that the hollow was not waiting to be made into something better. Its balance was narrow, but it was its own.

So the power did not fill the room with light. It did not lift the salamander into a world it had never asked to enter, or still the turbine simply because its sound was strange. For a long while, it did nothing at all.

Then the wheel faltered. A worn tooth caught; the casing shuddered, and a knock rang through the stone too hard for the small lives to ignore. The frogs flattened. The midges fell from the air. In the cleft, the hidden creature drew back from the trembling metal.

The power touched the water—not enough to raise it, only enough to ease its passage through the turbine’s failing works. The tooth slipped free. A burr softened. The wheel turned on, still old, still imperfect, but no longer one catch away from breaking apart. Its low growl returned to the ground, steady as a thought the stone had nearly forgotten.

The creatures resumed their small motions. One translucent grazer crossed the pool’s skin. A frog blinked beneath the drip. From the cleft came the faint stir of a body uncoiling.

And beyond the hollow, where no creature could see it, rainwater took a slightly different course through the new ground. It did not cease to enter; it did not arrive in a sudden flood. It found, instead, the patient way in. The hollow would still change, as all living places change. But not tonight, and not by accident.

The power left no sign of having been there. The green-gray light remained dim. The stone remained cold. The machine went on knocking now and then, because age had not been taken from it, only catastrophe.

Between one pulse and the next, the hidden resident listened to the hollow breathing. For the first time in a long while, the silence between the sounds seemed wide enough to rest in.
