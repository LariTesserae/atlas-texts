The power did not descend.

It had no need to arrive. The flooded country was already within its attention, as a small flame is within the attention of a careful hand.

For a long while, it did nothing.

It watched the banks split and the islands trade places. It listened to the machinery’s deep, ceaseless labor. It felt the reeds answer the turbine, the roots answer the shifting stone, the whole green-brown reach answer itself. It did not mistake strangeness for pain, or motion for disorder. Nothing here had asked to be made familiar.

But the roar allowed no sound to remain distinct. Each movement drowned the next. If the place had a wish, it could not hear itself wishing.

So the power made a little room.

Not silence. Silence would have been another command. Instead, it loosened the noise from its knots. The turbine’s knocking became one sound; the rain’s upward passage, another. The chains stopped rattling for a single breath—not because they were broken, but because they had been heard. Across the flooded country, leaves turned. Roots tightened. Machines paused between one beat and the next.

The power offered no words. It offered a question, shaped in pressure and time:

*What should be allowed to remain?*

The answer came unevenly. Some mats of greenery folded toward the current, ready to go. Some roots reached down through stone and held fast. The new forest bowed toward the pallid brightness; elsewhere, fronds turned toward one another. At the center, water still boiled up between the slabs. The place did not speak with one mind. It had many motions, and not all wanted the same thing.

The power understood.

It did not halt the flood. It did not quiet the machines forever or anchor every island against change. To make the whole country still would have been to silence it by another means.

Instead, where the roots had chosen to hold, the power gave them something solid to hold against. A few ridges of black stone settled deep. Their edges remained sharp, but the earth beneath them no longer slipped away. Around them, channels stayed open, free to turn and wander. The water continued its strange rise and fall. The land continued becoming.

The change was small enough that, from far away, no one would have called it a miracle.

A mat drifted past the new shore and did not have to bear its weight. Reeds clattered in the current, then found a sheltered pocket where they could stand without clattering. Beneath the pumping station, a gear turned, and this time its motion led somewhere: a thread of water ran through it and emerged into the flood without being forced upright.

At the center, the forest turned its leaves—not toward the brightness above, but toward the newly steady ground. Roots descended. Some found stone. Others passed on.

The power left the question open.

It had not come to save the country from being itself. It had given the country a little more say in what that meant: a place to stay, a way to go, and enough room between the roaring sounds to notice the difference.

Then the noise gathered again. The turbine resumed its slow, enormous rhythm. Rain climbed through the trees. The landscape shifted—but not everywhere, and not all at once.

Nothing came forward to meet the power.

Nothing needed a face.

The reeds went on answering the water, and the water, for a while, answered back.
