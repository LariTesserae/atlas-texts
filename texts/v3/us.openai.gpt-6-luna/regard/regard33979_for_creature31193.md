The power did not arrive as light. Light would have been a kind of announcement, and everything in the basin had already learned to fear sudden changes.

It came as attention.

It attended to the water choosing its channels, to the reeds bending beneath the reedbacks, to the small lives hidden in the mud. It listened without mistaking every cry for a plea. A hunting call was not cruelty; a warning was not a prayer. The basin was dangerous, but danger was not the same as wickedness.

And the power saw the being that lived there—not a sovereign, not the only life worth saving, but one particular life among the countless others. Perhaps it was a calf stranded on a collapsing bank as the rain-swollen water rose. Perhaps it was something smaller, listening from beneath a leaf while the shadows moved above. The power did not ask the creature to understand it. It did not make it wise, or fearless, or safe forever.

It gave the bank one more moment before it broke.

A root, newly exposed, held fast. The calf found purchase, scrambled free, and reached the herd. Elsewhere, the flood still carried branches downstream. A hunter still found its prey. The basin did not become gentle, and the power did not pretend that gentleness was the same as goodness.

Then it did something larger, though no creature could have seen it: it kept the water from taking everything at once. Not this pool, nor that nest, nor any particular animal. It preserved the basin’s ability to begin again—to shift, to grow, to be changed without being ended.

The power made no roads. It raised no walls. It did not choose a ruler for the country, since the living things already knew how to move without one. Its care was not a commandment imposed on the basin, but a little room made within it: room for the roots to hold, for the channels to wander, for lives to continue after a hard season.

When it had finished, the forest remained dark and loud. The water kept moving. The calls still answered one another from places no eye could find.

Nothing in the basin knew it had been spared.

That, too, was part of the mercy.
