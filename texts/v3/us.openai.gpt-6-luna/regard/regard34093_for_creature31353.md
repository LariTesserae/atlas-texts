The power did not begin by making light.

It understood that light, to them, would not mean comfort or revelation. It would be an enormous disturbance: heat without warning, shadows where there had never been shadows, a world suddenly made legible in ways their bodies were not built to bear.

Nor did it give them water, or sky, or a voice that could cross the chambers. It did not mistake their darkness for deprivation, or their stillness for sorrow.

It watched long enough to learn what counted as a life among them.

It learned the passage of a tremor through a colony; the tightening of hundreds of shells in answer to one frightened body. It learned how the small makers tested a shard against the stone before trusting it as a tool. It learned that a dead creature was not simply gone, but returned, grain by grain, to the ledge that had held it. It learned the difference between a colony resting and a colony failing.

Then it found, far below the inhabited chambers, a fracture under strain. The crust there was slowly drawing apart. If it opened all at once, the shock would cross the stone faster than any creature could answer it. A whole warm seam might fall into darkness, and the colonies on it would be lost—not through hunger, or age, or the ordinary bargain between hunter and prey, but through a single violence no life there could meet.

The power did not mend the world smooth. It eased the strain.

The fracture still opened, but little by little. A deep sound traveled through the stone, changing over many seasons from a blow into a murmur. Dust settled in unfamiliar places. The old routes grew less certain. The creatures felt the alteration as pressure, warmth, and the faint taste of newly exposed minerals.

At first they only drew closer together. Their bodies answered the changing stone as bodies always had: one tightening, then another, until the signal passed through the colony. The hunters withdrew from fissures whose air had changed. The makers laid new shards across a powder pit and began, by small degrees, to follow the warmth.

The power did not guide them. It did not mark the safe path with a shining sign. It left them the work of finding it, and the freedom to turn back.

A new chamber opened beyond the altered fracture. It was no paradise. Its floor was steep and its air carried an unfamiliar sharpness. But warm mineral seeped through one wall, and along it, slowly, a pale crust began to form. The first explorers crossed into it and returned. Others followed their pressure-signals. In time, some settled there; others stayed on the old ledges.

The power watched both choices with equal care.

It kept no creature from dying. It did not silence the hunters or make every passage safe. It did not preserve the world from all change. But where a single sudden ruin would have erased whole histories, it gave change time enough to be answered.

And once—only once—it touched the stone with a pattern.

Not a command. Not a name. A pulse, spaced like the quiet ticks of shells resting against one another: a disturbance, then stillness; a disturbance, then stillness.

The nearest creatures tightened. The signal passed inward, across the ledge and through the packed bodies. Farther away, a few makers stopped their work. In the deep cracks, the hunters folded themselves close to the rock.

Nothing answered in words. Nothing needed to.

The power remained, immense and patient, attending to the small movements by which life held itself together. It did not ask the creatures to understand that they were seen. It simply made certain that, for a while longer, there would be someone there to see them.
