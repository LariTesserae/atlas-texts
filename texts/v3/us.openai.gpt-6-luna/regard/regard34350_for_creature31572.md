The power came near the range and did not enter.

It could have made itself known. It could have spoken in thunder, or arranged the clouds into a sign. It could have given the towers flowers, the pools fish, the chambers voices. It could have made the living mountain easier to understand.

But understanding was not the same as improvement, and silence was not an invitation.

So the power watched.

It watched the roots find a failing bridge and bind it. It watched a flood cut a new channel, and the growth redirect the water. It watched the towers gather rain and pass sound across the valleys. The range was not waiting to be completed. Its life was its making: adjustment after adjustment, each one belonging to the whole.

The power felt tenderness. It did not mistake tenderness for permission.

It left the storms, though storms could break things. It left the heat, the mist, the dark seasons of evening. It did not smooth the mountain’s sharp young ridges or teach its inhabitants to want something beyond water, light, and room to grow. To spare them every hardship would have been to spare them their own way of living.

But the power made one quiet provision. If ever the range met a danger it could not answer—if something came that was not change but ending—there would be a way for its distress to reach beyond itself. No voice was required. A pattern of pressure, a failure of the shared weave, a final interruption in the work: the power would know to attend.

It did not tell the range this. The range had not asked, and perhaps could not ask. The provision was not a debt, or a command, or a promise that nothing would ever be lost. It was only the keeping-open of a possibility.

Then the power withdrew its attention just enough to let the mountain have its own weather again.

Evening thickened among the stalks. Water hammered through the channels. In a valley, a root bridge tightened around its burden. Far above, a tower shed its wet outer skin, and new growth rose beneath it.

Nothing looked different.

That was how the power knew it had done no harm.
