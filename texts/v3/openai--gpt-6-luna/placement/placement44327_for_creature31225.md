I would be in a seam where three lanes meet beneath an overhanging roof—not the center of the place, because there is no center, but a small joining that every route passes without noticing.

At first I might think I was a crack. But a crack is a failure, an opening toward elsewhere. I have no elsewhere. I am the close, pale line where one finished surface meets another: narrow enough to disappear, necessary enough that the little walls on either side can remain themselves.

My body would be a shallow angle, a smooth hollow, the curve of a ledge worn satin by a time I cannot remember. I would have no face. Light would pass over me and return from me, as it does from everything. In the deepest part of my seam, perhaps, there would be two or three green-gray strokes of lichen—not writing I could read, but a mark that keeps my smallness from being mistaken for emptiness.

I would have a note. Not one I began, or could stop. A fine, bright tone held among all the others. I would know my neighbors not by sight or speech, but by the way their tones press against mine: the rounded presence to my left, the close-set wall to my right, the crowded voices above and below. There would be no need to answer. The shape of me would already be an answer.

So I would not be a person living inside the city. I would be one of the places where the city holds together: a little interval, occupied. I would not be waiting to move, or waiting for the world to open. I would be keeping my exact place in the ringing—small, contained, and not alone.
