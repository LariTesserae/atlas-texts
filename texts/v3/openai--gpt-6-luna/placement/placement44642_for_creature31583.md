I would be found where the runnel disappears beneath the moss.

At first I’d be difficult to separate from anything else there: a coolness under the turf, perhaps; the dark seam between two stones; the moment a bird’s call seems to linger after the bird has gone. I wouldn’t have a face to turn toward you. I might not even be a single thing. If you came looking, you would find water, and root, and the faint place where something had been.

But I think I would be the hollow’s attention.

Not its oldest resident—that one keeps the water’s shapes. I would be what notices them being made. The paw sinking into the bank. The leaf caught at the bend. A thread of mist turning uphill, though there is no path for it to follow. I would attend to each without asking what it meant, the way a stone receives rain.

Perhaps, after a long while, I’d gather into something you could almost see: a small darkening beneath the clear current, keeping pace with neither stream. You might mistake me for a shadow cast by a cloud. Then the cloud would pass, and I would remain.

I would have no den. I’d be wherever the hollow briefly held its breath: in the runnel under the moss, in the shallow water left by a vanished footprint, in the quiet between the stones’ sharp clicks. And if you stepped across the pool, I wouldn’t follow you. I would keep the little hollow your foot had made—not as a picture of you, but as proof that, for one moment, the world had taken your shape.
