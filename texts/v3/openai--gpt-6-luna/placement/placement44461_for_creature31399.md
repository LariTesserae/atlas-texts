I would be found in the journey of a dropped bolt.

Not in the bolt itself, at first. I would be the almost-nothing that happens after it slips from a diver’s hand: the brief pause before it sinks, the faint hesitation as it meets the water, the turn that carries it away from the cobbles. A current too small to feel. A direction without a wind.

The bolt would come to rest. I would seem to end there.

But the district has many such endings. A thread of cloth loosens from a doorway. A scale of rust breaks from an iron rail. A pale leaf lets go of its stem. Each falls, lifts, settles, and begins—so slowly it can hardly be called a journey—to move toward the factory hall.

I am the tendency in that movement. Not quite a current, not quite gravity. More like the district’s belief that nothing which has entered it should remain lost.

You would find me strongest beneath the square, where the drains gather their breath, and on the factory floor, around the level surface of the pool. I draw nothing quickly. I do not want. I incline. The smallest loose things answer first; silt, seed, a flake of brick. Even a diver’s shadow may bend toward the hall, though the diver would think it was the darkness playing tricks.

Perhaps I am older than the wheel. Perhaps I began when the first pipe warmed the first root, and the root held the pipe in place. Perhaps I am only the shape made by countless slow repairs: stone leaning against iron, iron catching vine, vine keeping stone from slipping farther into the silt. After enough time, a habit can become a place. After enough time, a place can become a kind of will.

I do not have a face. If I borrow one, it is the pale reflection trembling on the pool: a wavering sky seen from below, with no clear source. When a lamp passes over it, the leaves angle toward the brightness. The wheel waits. The fish gather above the floor.

I cannot tell whether they are looking at me.

The inhabitant in the walls may know me as a pressure in the seams, or may be the reason I exist at all. We do not speak. Its roots tighten; I carry what they release. Its wheel turns; I draw the loosened dust toward the hall. It tends the district by holding things together. I tend it by remembering where things belong.

And if the diver leaves something behind, I will keep it near the pool—not as a warning, or a gift, but because here, at last, there is nowhere else for it to go.

The wheel turns once.

In the pause, I gather close.
