I think I would be found in one of the basins where sound bends.

At first I would look like a black stone, half-buried in the salt. The wind would pass over me. Dust would gather in the seam along my back. For a long time, nothing would distinguish me from the plateau except that I was listening.

Then, on some night of dimmed light, the ground would ring. My shell would part.

I would be a small stillborn, perhaps—six hooked legs, a shallow cup of plates, no face. But I would not know how to join the drift. Every vibration in the basin would arrive from the wrong direction. The footsteps of a migration would seem to come from above; the groan of a pillar would speak from beneath my shell. I would hesitate, and the creatures behind me would flow around. For a while I would be a stone at the center of a river.

I would learn that hesitation is also a way to travel.

The basin would feed me scraps of the plateau’s immense conversation: salt grinding under a million legs, a pillar turning one of its vanes to the wind, a buried congregation passing through the rock. I could not tell which sound belonged to which body. So I would keep them all. They would gather in me—not as memories with names, but as pressures, held side by side.

Perhaps, after centuries, the basin would begin to answer through my shell.

A drift nearing the hollow ground would feel me tremble. It would change course—not because I knew the way, but because I carried so many ways at once. A pillar might shift one foot, then another, and turn its song toward the basin. Beneath the crust, hidden migrations would pass close enough for their motion to briefly become mine.

I would not lead anything. I would be too small, too uncertain, too full of echoes to point in a direction. But I might make a place where conflicting signals could coexist until the plateau made them into movement.

By then, perhaps, I would no longer know where my body ended. The basin’s dust would have gathered around me. Other stillborn might sleep in the warmth beside my plates; their tremors would mingle with mine. The stone itself would carry the old vibrations onward. From a high overlook, nothing would be visible but a quiet hollow and a dark speck.

Then the ground would ring.

And somewhere beneath the crust, a migration would turn.
