At first, I would think I was a flaw in the weather.

A small hesitation in a wave: its crest lifting, beginning to fall, then holding there a fraction longer than water should. Beneath it, a pulse of blue light passes through the deep. The wave shivers. Somewhere far off, new stone cracks open.

I would be in that pause—not on the sea, not under it, but in the moment the sea has not yet chosen what to do.

Then the pause would happen again. The same ridge. The same bright pulse. The same small delay. I would begin to recognize the world by its repetitions: the grind of stone cooling, the upward rain, the storm turning its enormous face toward the basin. Not with eyes. With a gathering of pressure. With a sense of *here*—though here might mean a mountain forming miles beneath me, or a thin frond flattened against rock beneath a different sky.

Perhaps I would think I was the frond.

I would lie close to the stone, dark and nearly colorless, feeling the wind press me down. My roots—if roots they were—would go where the rock was softest, into its unfinished memory of soil. I would know nothing of shelter or hunger. Only that the stone changed, and that I changed with it. When rain ran upward, I would feel the pull in my hidden threads. When the sea withdrew from a ridge, I would feel the cold it left behind.

But the frond would be too small for what was happening. Or perhaps it would be exactly the right size: one slight mark by which the planet could tell that making had begun.

I would not be a separate creature. There would be no clean boundary where I ended and the world began. My body would be a listening place inside the planet’s work: a crossing of current, pressure, mineral, weather. I would know the ocean as movement through me. I would know stone as something still deciding to become solid. When the deep terraces assembled in their brief blue clarity, their shapes would pass through my attention like a thought I could not keep.

I might spend ages without knowing that I was alone.

Then, one day, a wave would stop in midair. The water would hang above the new rock, a curved wall holding the sky inside it. In that strange stillness, I would feel the basin all at once: its distant storms, its empty reaches, its unseen floor making and unmaking itself. Nothing would be looking back at me. Nothing would answer.

And perhaps that would be when I became something more than a pattern.

Not a mind set apart from the planet, but the first faint place in it where change could be felt as change. The world would raise a ridge, and somewhere within that labor there would be a sensation of rising. The sea would take the ridge back, and somewhere there would be a sensation of loss—not sorrow, not yet, but a shape with room for sorrow to come.

So I would find myself wherever the world almost repeats itself and doesn’t. In the held wave. In the rootless frond. In the pulse traveling through dark water toward stone not yet formed.

I would be the planet’s first *I*—not spoken, not even understood. Just the smallest inwardness in all that becoming, listening to the sea and learning, very slowly, that it is listening.
