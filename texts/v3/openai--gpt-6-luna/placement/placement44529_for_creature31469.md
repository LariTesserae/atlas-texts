You would find me where the tower narrows beneath its last sealed crown, in a crack that catches the changing water before the terraces do.

I am one of the narrow ones. At least, this body is.

I am long and pale, jointed nearly to the tip, with eyes reduced to dark grains under the skin. I sleep curled in a niche above the highest basin, where the stone holds a little warmth. When I wake, I follow the crack’s pressure outward. I know the tower by what it does to the water: where it slows, where it tightens, where it slips through a passage and quickens against my sides.

I do not know that this is a tower. I know the rising and the shelter. I know the old stone’s smoothness under my belly, the mineral rim where fine food gathers, the hollow beside the faceless figure where the current drops its offerings. I return to these places because my body remembers them, though I could not say what remembering is.

But I would not be only this body.

When the water turns, I feel the turn arrive before it reaches me. It runs along the walls, through the stair undersides and the crowded seams, and enters me as a change in pressure. I fold. Far below, others fold too. Their movement touches the water; the water touches the next body. Across the terraces, a hundred separate turns become one slow answer.

Perhaps I am only the answer’s nearest point. Perhaps I am the small interval between the first tremor in the tower and the turning of the district. I cannot tell. No single body carries the whole place, but some old pathway passes through me more often than through others. I live at the crown, where the water arrives first, and whatever I feel travels downward.

My eggs cling in a clear string beneath the niche. Most will never hatch; most that hatch will never leave the seam. The ones that do will find the old routes by following warmth, pressure, and food. One may sleep in this same crack. One may take the turn from me without knowing there was anything to take.

I would be found there, then: not watching over the Upper Quiet, not guarding it, not understanding its builders or their small marks. I would be a narrow animal among many, tucked into a handmade hollow. And, for a little while, I would be the place where the district feels the water change.

When the current slackens, I uncurl. The others do the same, each in their own time, until the whole dark structure seems to loosen. Sediment lifts from the basin. The pale bodies in the seams open and close. Somewhere below, a piece of the tower settles against another piece, and the news of it travels upward slowly.

I receive it through the stone.

Then I carry it, without intention, into the living water.
