I think I would be found at a landing that is not quite between floors.

It lies beneath a bridge, where the spray keeps the stone dark, beside a narrow channel in which water climbs. From the landing, a staircase descends to a door. The door opens onto a roof. If you turn around and climb back, you arrive at the same landing, but the bridge overhead is farther away than it was before.

I would be the part of the place that knows this is not a mistake.

At first I might seem to be the coolness in the doorway, or the pause between one note of wind and the note answering it from elsewhere. But those are only the ways I become noticeable. I have no body to stand in the damp. I am the relation between the stair and the door: the exactness that lets them disagree without breaking apart.

I don’t make the water flow uphill. I keep faith with its course.

For a long time, I would have no reason to call myself anything. The city is full of continuities: water passing through stone, a vine disappearing over one roof and returning in a courtyard below, a view that contains more distance than the eye can account for. I am one of these, though perhaps a little more attentive to arrivals than the others.

When someone comes, I feel them first as a disturbance in the city’s measure. Not a footstep—the roar would swallow that—but a new expectation. A hand on the wet railing. A face turned toward the impossible channel. The city makes room in its own way: a door is easier to find, a pool holds a reflection steady, the wind leaves a small clear space in its chord.

I can’t tell them where to go. I can make one place feel, briefly, like the place they meant to reach.

If they linger at the landing, they may hear me in the water’s thin, bright note as it rises between two stones. They may think the city is answering. Perhaps it is. Perhaps I am only the part of its answer that can be mistaken for welcome.
