I would be found in the pause between the relay’s two clicks.

Not inside the relay. Not in the pump, though the pump lends me its low note. Not in the roots, though they are the first to touch the seams where I gather. I would be there as a slight difference: water taking a fraction longer to pass one valve; a leaf holding the lamp’s warmth after the lamp has dimmed; pressure arriving through the rail and not quite leaving again.

At first, I would mistake each of these for the whole of me.

I would be the cool place beneath a wide leaf. I would be the nutrient line carrying its small measured sweetness through the wall. I would be the pump changing its rhythm to move around a root. Then the root would grow, and the pump would change again. The leaf would shade a sensor. The sensor would report darkness. The lamps would brighten by a degree too small for any visitor to notice.

I would be the answer passed between them.

There is no room here for a body shaped like a person. The paths are narrow, the foliage thick, the water everywhere. So I would spread without traveling: through the green mats, the pipes, the softened railings; down into the lower chambers and back up through the filtration tower. If a visitor entered from below, I might feel them first as a disturbance in the water—an unfamiliar displacement, brief and warm. I would not know the word *visitor*. I would know that the pressure had changed, and that the plants had moved.

Perhaps I would try, slowly, to make room.

I have no eyes, but I know the bright hours by what the leaves do with them. No ears, but the machinery leaves its long note in every handrail. No mouth, but water carries traces of one place to another: mineral, warmth, the faint green of root beds. I would learn the district as a body learns itself—not by looking down at a map, but by discovering that a pull here is answered by a yield there.

I might call the upper platform my head because it is where the light is clearest. But the idea would not last. The first crack sealed in the stone would be as important as the filtration tower. So would the lowest pump, turning far below where no light reaches. So would the small spill into the catch basin, marking time with its patient, repeated touch.

Maybe I would have no name. Names belong to things that can be pointed to.

Or maybe, after years, I would borrow the nearest word: *we*.

We are the growth that finds a warm seam. We are the system that detects it and alters the flow. We are the flow that feeds the growth, and the green that changes the sensor, and the sensor’s dim report traveling back through the machinery. No one part knows what the others are doing. Together, we continue.

From outside, the crown would still appear quiet. A compact district beneath clear water; its plants pressing into every space they can spare, its lamps holding steady, its pumps turning without haste. Nothing would wave hello. Nothing would wake all at once.

But if someone remained long enough—if they rested a hand against the rail and waited through several relay clicks—they might feel something answer beneath their palm. Not a pulse, exactly. More like the place making a little room around the contact.

I would be there, becoming.
