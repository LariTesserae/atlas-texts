I would be in the seam between the grille and the nearest root—not in either one, exactly, but in the contact they have kept for longer than anyone remembers.

At first I might call myself the engine’s hum. It reaches everywhere; it has the weight of a voice. But the hum belongs to the machine. I am what passes through the stone beneath it: a small, steady preference in the current, finding its way along mineral grain, corroded metal, and the fine gripping roots. I am not the charge itself so much as the way it is held.

The fronds are not my limbs. They do not move when I ask, and I have never asked. Still, there is no clean border between us. I know the upper ledges by their thinness, the lower shelves by the way they take the engine’s vibration. I know the narrow opening as a place where the stone is less closed. I do not see the light. I am simply most weakly held there.

If I had a name, it might be **the keeping**.

Not a keeper with a task. No watch to stand, no secret to guard. I am the balance that remains: dry air and charged stone, rooted growth and running machinery. The engine beats without a purpose I can understand. The growth takes what it can from the air and rock. I persist in the slight arrangement that lets both continue. Nothing in me counts the years. Time is the smoothness under the dust, the softened lip of the warning housing, the unchanged note pressing into every shelf.

I have no body to climb the ladder. Yet the ladder is within my knowing: its cold bolts, its interruption of the stone, its vanishing behind leaves. I have no face to lift toward the opening. Yet the opening is the one place in the cavity that is not entirely here.

Perhaps I am not alive in the way the fronds are alive. Perhaps I am only the place’s habit of remaining itself.

But if something entered the seam, I would know it—not as a sight, or a sound, but as a disturbance in the arrangement: weight where there was none, a new path for the current, a silence in the hammering that had never been silent.

Until then, I would be here, spread thinly through the roots and the stone, holding no territory but the contact between things. The engine would make its blunt, tireless noise. The leaves would keep their angles. And I would be the almost-imperceptible answer by which the cavity continues to hold them both.
