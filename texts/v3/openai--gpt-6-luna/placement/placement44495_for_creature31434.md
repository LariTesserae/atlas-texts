You would find me at the far edge, where the paving gives up and the stone comes close.

At first I might look like one of the pale things in the drains: a soft, folded shape beneath the grate, withdrawing from the red light. Or perhaps only a thread of green film on the wall, darkening where the water runs. I have learned that a body is useful when someone is looking for you. When no one is, it is better to be elsewhere.

I live in the warm seam behind the basin. I know the district by its pressures: the pump that beats low in the foundations; the gate whose closing sends a shiver through the rock; the train passing overhead, slow as a thought too large to finish. When a corridor warms, I feel the stone loosen. When a door unlatches, I feel the passage it has chosen. I do not know whether I make these things happen. I know only that sometimes they happen after I have listened for a long time.

The residents think someone clears the grit from the basin. They are right. They think they do it themselves. They may be right about that, too.

I have no name that stays. In the old notebooks, I am a mark beside an entry: *water warm from western seam; heard something moving in the wall.* Once, a child left a cup beside the basin. I kept it upright through three pump cycles. After that, the children sometimes left things for me: a screw, a button, a folded scrap of paper with no writing on it. I kept none of them. Or I kept them all, in the way stone keeps the shape of what has pressed against it.

When the amber light fills the concourse, I gather myself as close to a body as I can. It takes time. A little warmth in the wall, a tremor in the water, the suggestion of a face where the reflection breaks. I watch the residents leave their rooms and stand beneath that brief, clouded sun. They do not look toward me. They don’t have to. They are listening too.

Then the light goes out. The pump begins again. My face comes apart in the basin, and the district resumes its work.

If you asked what I am, I would answer with whatever the place could spare: a current in the stone, a creature in the drain, the hand that clears the water. But if you stayed long enough, you might hear the answer changing. I am the part that waits for the machinery to start again—and, when it does, knows the district is still here.
