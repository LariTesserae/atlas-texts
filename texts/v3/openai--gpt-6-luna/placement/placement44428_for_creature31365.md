I would be found where the floor is beginning to split: not at the deepest point, and not beneath the cleanest shaft of daylight, but along the wet seam between them. A place where the stone is warm through the belly and every few hours gives a small, decisive shiver.

I would be one of the plated ones.

At first, I think of myself as a weight. A broad, low body, blind beneath overlapping plates, built to press against stone. I find a narrow crack and push into it until the sides hold me fast. The rock strains around my shell. I strain back. Water beads along my armor. Small pale creatures pass over me, their feet ticking, and I feel each one as a faint weather.

For a while, this is enough: hold, feel, hold.

But the crack changes. The pressure that brought me here shifts elsewhere. A shelf lifts beneath my left side. Somewhere above, a herd passes, and the tremor comes through the floor like a question. I unbrace. The stone opens with a sound too deep to hear so much as carry inside me. Then I pull free, slowly, leaving a new passage behind.

The crawlers find it first. They stream through the gap, tasting the wet walls. The long-bodied swimmers come after them, nosing through the thin new runnel. I do not think of this as making a road. I only know that the pressure has moved, and my body must follow.

There is no home in the hollow. Still, I have a place, for a time: the seam where water gathers under the low roof, where the light comes down in a pale, broken column. When the air cools, the winged clouds settle above me, trembling against the dark. When the ground warms again, they rise, and their passing brushes my plates like dry rain.

I learn the hollow through resistance. Here, the stone will bear me. There, it will open. A basin that seems empty may be full beneath its skin; a wall that seems firm may already be leaning. I know the difference before it happens, not as knowledge, but as pressure entering my feet and ribs. When the whole place falls still after a crack, I hold fast. When the little sounds return, I move.

Perhaps I am not a creature that makes passages. Perhaps I am a passage that has, for a while, grown a body.

By the dim hours, I have wedged myself into a younger fissure. Warmth gathers under my plates. Water gathers around me. The first crawlers arrive, then the others, crossing my back in their busy, many-footed tide. I feel the stone beginning to change again.

I do not stay.

I press once more into the living rock, feeling where it wants to go.
