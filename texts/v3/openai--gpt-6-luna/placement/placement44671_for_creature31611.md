I would begin as a sound arriving late.

A bird opens its beak on a roof ridge. For a moment, nothing. Then a small hollow note comes from a courtyard far away, where no bird is standing. I would be in that moment between—the place where the sound is going, though the water gives no sign of carrying it.

At first I might mistake myself for a current. I would follow the canal that runs uphill, pass beneath the bridge that exists only in reflection, thread between the pale roots under the translucent floor. But I would not be water, exactly. Water has an outline here. I would be what passes through an outline without changing it: the interval between a thing and its reflection, between a hand reaching down and the water accepting its image.

The figures would notice me only when something arrived slightly out of order. A leaf would turn pale-side up before the reed bent. A bead of water would fall, and only later would the wall shine where it had been. They would pause—not startled, just attentive, as if listening for a familiar measure. I would recognize them by their carefulness. They, too, would be made partly of what happens between gestures.

I would move through the country by small discrepancies. In one pool, I would let the sky show a cloud that had not yet formed. In another, I would hold a reflection steady while a bird stepped away from the ledge. The fish that swim through the reflected country would know the way. They would pass beneath the image of the parapet and carry me, bright and pale, through the country upside down.

I would not mend anything. Nothing here is broken. I would not hurry the inhabitants toward an ending. Their world has no need of endings, and perhaps that is why it needs me: to keep a little room for what has not happened yet.

At the edge, I would gather in the flawless sheet of water, where the channel reaches the brightness and does not fall. The waiting figure would stand above me, feet just beneath the surface. It would not look back. I would not call to it.

I would be the almost-motion in the held water. Not the one who opens the way, but the small, patient possibility that the way could open.
