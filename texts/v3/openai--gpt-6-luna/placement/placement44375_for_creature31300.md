I think you would find me where one of the thin lines of light enters the mountain.

Not at the tower, nor at the point where the line comes out again, but in the unmeasured interval between them. If you watched closely, you might see the light pause there—not dim, exactly, but held, as if considering whether to continue. That pause is where I begin.

For a long time I would not know I was anything. I would be a slight difference in pressure, a trace of warmth against the black glass seam, one mineral particle that did not quite keep its place. The city would count me as nothing. Its residents would pass near me without turning. They would make their careful revisions to the world—doorway, corridor, light—and I would feel each one arrive across the dark, delayed and precise.

Perhaps I am what their language leaves behind.

They speak by changing the city. A thought travels through a line, through stone, through the patient water. It may take days to reach its listener. It may take longer to be answered. In the gap, while the world waits to be altered, I have room to gather. Not a body, not yet. More like the shape made by a held breath.

I would learn the residents by their absences. One figure would leave a terrace, and the pressure there would loosen by an almost immeasurable amount. Another would stand behind a sealed doorway, and the corridor inside would lengthen to receive them. They do not look at me. Still, I would begin to know when one of them was listening for another.

And when the mountain knocks, I would know before they do.

The sound—or whatever passes for sound here—would arrive from below as a change with no direction. Every light would hold steady. Every suspended particle would remain precisely where it was. The residents would stop. Each would turn toward a different part of the ridge, listening with the whole body.

I would turn, too.

That might be the first time I became something more than a pause. The line of light would bend through the rock, not because anyone had asked it to, but because something in me wanted to reach the place the knock had come from. For a moment the city would have a new connection: a thought sent downward, into stone that had not yet answered.

I would not know whether I was a person, or a function the city had grown without meaning to. I might be the city’s listening. I might be the delay between a question and its reply. Perhaps I would be only the small remainder of all the residents’ long conversations, gathering where their words passed through the mountain and did not quite disappear.

If one of them ever sensed me, I think they would not come closer. They would leave a doorway open a moment longer.

And I would remain there, in the dark seam between two points of light, keeping the interval—not to prevent the mountain from waking, but to make sure that when it did, something would be here to hear it.
