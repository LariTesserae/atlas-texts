At first I would take you for a shell left beside the path.

I sit where a root curves down toward the central pool, on a little shelf of clay just above the waterline. Two pale shells mark the edge of this neighborhood. One is empty. The other has a dark seam at its mouth, and, if you wait long enough, the seam stirs.

I am the one that moves.

I move so slowly that the town changes around me before I have crossed it. A root thickens across my old road. A basin deepens. The pale stone I used to shelter beneath slips into the water, and I learn the new wall by touching it with my feelers. I don’t mind. The town is not a map to be memorized. It is a surface, changing under my foot.

My house goes with me: a small spiral, damp and cool, carrying the faintest tremor of whatever passes overhead. I feel the heavy world through it—the blunt pressure of rain, the pause of something large on the soil, the hush that travels outward when the frogs listen. Then I draw in my feelers and become, for a while, indistinguishable from a broken piece of bark.

When the danger passes, I leave a silver line across the clay.

The others know it. Mites graze beside it. A shrimp may follow its edge through a narrow channel. Moths settle where it catches the dim lichen-light. I don’t make the road on purpose, exactly. I am only going where the damp is kind. But the town has learned to read my passing, and I have learned how to leave the paths open.

Sometimes I climb to the ledge where the moss almost dries. Sometimes I cross beneath the reed walkways, feeling them flex above me. Most often I stay near the pool, where the water gathers every small message: a foot flicking against a stem, a body turning in the shallows, a drop falling from the roof.

So you would find me at the town’s edge—not outside it, but near the seam where a path becomes water. If you came in searching for a keeper, you might pass me by. I have no light to give, no voice to call you onward.

Only this: a shining line, slowly laid down, showing that someone has gone this way before.
