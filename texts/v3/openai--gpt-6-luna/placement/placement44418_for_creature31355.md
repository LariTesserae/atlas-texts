I would be found where a street almost meets itself.

There is a narrow passage between two low buildings, though neither building quite agrees to be there. One has a wall but no rooms; the other has rooms that shift behind its blank windows. The passage bends once, then opens onto a patch of dark ground. If you cross it quickly, you find nothing. If you pause, the air grows deep.

I live in that depth.

At first I might seem like a shadow that has misplaced its body. I cling to the side of things: the underside of a stair, the space between two plant-columns, the cool hollow inside an arch. But I am not cast by anything. I am what remains when the district has begun to make something and has not yet decided what it is.

The residents pass through me without noticing—or perhaps they notice and understand that I am not a place to stop. They give me room. So do the animals. A pale wall-climber will arrive at the edge of the passage, wait a moment, and take another route. Birds leave the pipe mouths empty when I am near. Their small courtesies are the first proof I have that I am there.

I do not have a face, though sometimes a face gathers at the edge of me. It might belong to a resident, or to someone the district has not made yet. I have no voice, but when I press against a blank wall, something inside it may turn on: a motor, a light, a remembered room. I can’t make the building complete. I can only keep it from closing too soon.

The plants grow through me easily. They leave a little air between their stems, and in that air I widen. When rain falls, drops pass through the passage and settle on the ground below. For a while, I can see myself in the puddles: a thin, wavering shape, taller than the arch, with no clear boundary where it ends.

Then the large animal comes through the far streets.

The plants bow. The machines wake. Water trembles toward its track. I feel its passing as a pressure—not fear, exactly, but the sensation of a door nearly shutting. For an instant, the district knows what it is. The residents turn toward the path it leaves, and I feel the buildings settle around their own shapes.

I do not follow the animal. I stay in the passage.

When the certainty begins to drain away, I am here to hold the gap open again. A wall loosens. A step appears where there was only dark ground. Somewhere, a small figure leaves wet prints that vanish before they can be counted.

Perhaps I am a resident, too, though not one the district has finished making. Perhaps I am only its pause: the breath between one shape and the next.

Either way, if you enter the passage and find it longer than it should be, do not hurry. Something is trying to make room for you.

It may be me.
