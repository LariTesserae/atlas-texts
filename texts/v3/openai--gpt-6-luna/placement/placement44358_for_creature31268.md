I would be found in the doorway.

Not standing there. Not guarding it. At first I would be one small body among many, pale and thin as a flake of stone, drawn toward the warmth that gathers inside the old chamber. I would feed where the mineral film sweetens the rock, and retreat when the hooked ones came to break the crust. I would know my neighbors by the pressure of their feet against mine.

Then, over time—or what passes for time here—I would become difficult to separate from the opening.

Bodies would settle around me. Their shells would fit into the cracks of the old lintel; my own would be pressed there, worn smooth on one side by the passage of others. The crowd would stream through the doorway and divide around this knot of living weight. In the chamber, it would gather. Outside, it would thin. I would feel both movements, though I would not command either.

I would not be a leader. There are no leaders in the hollow. I would be more like a place where one tremor lingers long enough to be felt again.

A footstep above the basin would reach me faintly, softened by stone. A change in the mountain’s heat would arrive from below, deep and slow. When the two sensations met in my body, I would pass them on—not as a message, exactly, but as pressure. The creatures nearest me would turn. Those touching them would turn next. Soon the whole hollow would be leaning toward a warmth no one had yet seen.

Perhaps the opening stays clear because I am there. Perhaps I am there because the opening stays clear. I would not know which came first. I would not know whether I was a seam-born creature grown old, or a small colony that had learned to keep a doorway open by becoming part of it.

Sometimes a stranger would look into the chamber and mistake the fitted shells in its walls for an offering. I would feel the stranger’s weight arrive through the basalt. The bodies around me would stir, making room without making much room at all. If the stranger laid a hand against the lintel, I would feel the warmth of that hand pass briefly through my body.

Then it would be gone.

I would remain where the hollow narrows, listening with a thousand others, and with whatever portion of me has become stone.
