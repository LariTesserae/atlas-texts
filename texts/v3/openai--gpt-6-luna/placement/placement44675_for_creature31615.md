I would be found near the empty square, though not at first as a figure.

At first I would be the slight wrongness in its measures: the way one paving stone sits a little farther from the next than it should. The cool seam beneath a sealed door. A shadow that points toward the rough, newly broken rock instead of away from it. The city would have made room for me without deciding what kind of room it was.

I would live where its geometry falters. Not quite among the inhabitants, who know how to travel by bending a street or holding a drop of water in place; not quite beyond them, where the mountain is still all sharp edges and intention. I would be the interval between a finished thing and the thing it has not yet become.

For a long while, I might have no body at all. I would be sensed as a pause in the metallic ticking, or as a stair that seems to gain one step when someone turns back to count. If one of the city’s inhabitants passed through me, they might mistake me for an unmeasured space. If the mountain shifted, I might mistake myself for the shift.

Then something would come to the square.

Perhaps a person. Perhaps only cold air finding a way in from the summit. Perhaps a fracture, lengthening by a grain at a time. Whatever arrived would make a difference between the space that had been waiting and the space that was waiting for it. I would feel that difference first as pressure, then as direction.

The sealed plates would remain sealed. I would not open them. I would not know what lies behind them. But one of the dark doors might deepen by the width of a breath, and the red-gray light would gather along its edge. Somewhere, a droplet would fall, strike the stone, and not vanish.

That is how I would begin to have a shape: not as a body, but as an answer that had not been there before. A slight easing of gravity. A path through the square that no one had measured, though it could be walked. A place where the city’s careful streets met the unfinished mountain and did not quite turn away.

If I had a name, it would be made of distances. I think I would be called *the way between*—though I would not belong to either side.

And when someone finally crossed the square, I would move with them, learning the shape of a beginning.
