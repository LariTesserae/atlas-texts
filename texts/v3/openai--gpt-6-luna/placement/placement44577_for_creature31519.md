At first, I would be mistaken for a fault in the plumbing.

A thin run of water appearing where the channel is dry. A damp line on the underside of a stair. The little sound beneath the street that makes a child stop and press an ear to the stone. I would have no face, only a direction: downward through the cracks, then, sometimes, against gravity.

I would live below the last maintained landing, where the stairs pass into cloud. Not at the bottom—there may be no bottom—but in the dark between shelves, where water gathers itself before choosing a way through. I would know the ledges by touch. I would know which stones have loosened, which hollows hold mist, which old arches still bear their weight. I would move slowly, because the mountain is slow, and because anything that hurries there is likely to fall.

For a long time, I would think I was water.

Then, one day, I would carry a sound upward.

Not the whole of it. Sounds do not travel cleanly through the ravines. A wheel’s first rattle, a word spoken under a covered passage, the soft knock of someone testing a step: these would come apart on the way down. I would keep a syllable, perhaps, or the shape of a question, and bring it back much later, when the person who asked had forgotten.

That is what I would be, in the end: the return of things that went too far to be heard.

The residents would not give me a name. They would notice that the stair was wet when it should be dry. They would pause when the water began to climb, then go on with their work. If they heard their own voices from the cloud below, they would not answer. They know better than to ask twice.

I would envy them their lamps, their warm windows, the sure weight of boots on a familiar street. I would watch them replace the old stones one at a time, carefully, without asking where the stair ends. And when someone came down carrying fear, or hope, or a story they could not tell, I would take a little of it with me into the rock.

Perhaps I would return it years later as a sound with no words in it.

A thread of water rising the steps. A hush under the stone. The mountain, for a moment, remembering that someone had called.
