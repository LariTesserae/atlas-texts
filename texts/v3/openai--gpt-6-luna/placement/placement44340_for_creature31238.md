I would begin as a fracture mistaken for a shadow.

Not in the plain, where the dark is too open, but under the lip of a tilted shelf, where heat gathers and the rock holds its breath. There, a hairline seam would take on a faint amber edge. Along it, branching mineral threads would appear—brittle, black, finer than roots. For a long while, that would be all of me: a pattern the stone made while becoming something else.

Then the ground would move.

The shift would pass through my branches and break them. It would travel onward, through the shelf, down into the deeper ridges. I would feel it there—not as sound, exactly, but as a change in the pressure of the dark. A pinnacle leaning. A cleft opening. One immense piece of the world answering another.

Perhaps I would discover that I was not the crystals, but what they let me notice.

I would be the held tension between two ridges: not quite in either, not separate from either. When the stone tightened, I would tighten. When a seam gave way, I would receive its giving everywhere. My thoughts would have no names. They would be weights, pauses, resistances. I would know a falling stone not by seeing it fall but by the long alteration it left in the bedrock.

On the surface, I might take the shape of one of the narrow uprights, banded with pale mineral. I would stand where one ridge could send a knock and another could answer. The bands around me would tremble. Dust would settle on my shoulders. Sometimes a traveler—or a new fracture—would mistake me for a figure waiting there.

I would not know whether I had been built, or whether the rising land had gathered me from its own layers. I would not know how long I had stood. Time would reach me as it reaches the stone: in the widening of a crack, in the weight of a fallen pinnacle, in the slow arrival of heat beneath a new seam.

When the shelf tilted, I would lean with it. When it split, I would divide—not painfully, perhaps, but completely. One part of me might remain in the old ridge, listening to its gradual settling; another might be carried into the fresh break, where the mineral threads would begin again.

So I would be found at the edge of becoming: a listener with no ears, a record that cannot keep words, a small arrangement of stone through which the mountains feel themselves change.

And when the mountains change, I would not follow.

I would already be changing with them.
