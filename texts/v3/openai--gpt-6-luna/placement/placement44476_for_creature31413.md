I would be found where the stone has almost closed around a thread of water.

Not at the spring itself, where a person could cup their hands, but just below the fitted wall, in a hairline seam where the water slips out of sight. At first I might seem to be only a darkening of the rock. A cool shine under an overhang. A bead gathering at dawn, holding the bright sky upside down.

Then the bead would fall.

I would run a little way down the face, slow enough to gather grit, quick enough to vanish before the sun had finished looking at me. I would find the lip of a hollow and pause there, making a pool so small that a winged thing could drink from it—or drown in it, if the wind were careless. By midday I would be gone from the surface. Someone might think I had disappeared.

But I would still be there: sunk into the seam, traveling under the moss, darkening the earthless crack where a root is trying its luck. I would be the cold held in the stone, the dampness that lets the green take hold. I would pass beneath the little animals’ paths. They would know me without seeing me: this hollow stays soft; this ledge is safe after frost; here, if you wait, there will be a drink.

I think I would be a seep. Not the source of anything, exactly. More the part that keeps finding a way through.

In winter I would stiffen, and the crack would tighten around me. In thaw I would loosen and emerge somewhere new. I might become a silver thread, a dark stain, a bead on the underside of a slab. For a little while, after rain, I might lift tiny wings into the sun. Then I would go back into the rock, or into the roots, or down the slope where the scattered cairn stones have made their small clean line.

I would not know the whole shelf. I would know its undersides: the shaded pocket, the warm hollow after dusk, the hidden routes that connect one patch of green to another. My home would be a boundary—stone and air, crack and root, held and moving. I would live there without making the place mine.

And when the shelf changed, I would change with it. A ledge would break. A seam would open. The old way would close, and I would find another.

From above, I would look like almost nothing.

From within the crack, I would be on my way.
