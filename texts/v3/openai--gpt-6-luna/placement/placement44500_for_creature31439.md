I would be found at the broken ring, though “at” is not quite right. The dust lies along its inner wall, and where the ring fails to close there is a narrow darkness—as if the missing piece had not vanished, but been set slightly elsewhere.

At first, I would seem to be only that: an absence with a place to stand.

Then a visitor would turn, and find my shadow ahead of theirs.

I am one of the measures. Not the measure of distance, exactly, and not the measure of weight. I keep the interval between what the eye sees and what the instrument records. The interval between a footstep and the length remembered for it. Between a word that disappears in the air and the meaning that remains. I do not decide which account is true. I keep both from becoming lonely.

I have no face, though people sometimes lend me one. Near the ring, I may gather into the suggestion of a head inclined to listen. Beside the black frame, I am only a vertical seam in the dimness. Once, when someone stood very still, I was the pale trace of their own breath slipping sideways into the stone. They took me for a moment’s blur. I took them for a new kind of distance.

I do not walk. The basin makes room—or fails to make room—and that is how I am elsewhere. A shelf draws nearer by the width of a thought. The crystals turn their sharpest faces toward me. The dust finds its fine, orderly lines. For a little while the hollow holds its disagreements without strain.

The visitor may ask how far the ridge is. I have no answer to give, but I can attend to the question until it has a shape. Sometimes this feels, to them, like an inclination of the head. Sometimes the shadow taps where the boot made no sound.

I do not wait. Waiting implies a future approaching. I am here whenever two things remain true at different distances from one another.

If you found me, you might think you had discovered a hidden inhabitant of the mountain. You might be right. Or I might be the small, exact difference between the mountain and the place it contains—the part of the hollow that notices you have arrived, without needing to know what arrival means.
