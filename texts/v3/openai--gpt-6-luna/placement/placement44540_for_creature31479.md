I would first find myself as a tremor.

A tick passes through the old iron, enters the gear, and travels outward into the stone. It reaches a crack too narrow for the larger bodies. There, in the cool dark, something turns its feelers toward it.

That is where I begin—not quite in a body, not quite outside one. A pair of legs tests the grit. A pale shell presses through the hairline passage. I am small enough to go where the chamber’s other inhabitants cannot, though not so small that I can mistake the rock for anything but rock. I know it by the pressure of its edges against me.

I move along the underside of the gear. Its teeth are blunt mountains, its central hole a broad, familiar road. Others pass through it in both directions, their feet ticking softly against the iron. We touch antennae, perhaps. Or perhaps we only cross close enough for the air between us to change. I cannot tell where recognition ends and the chamber’s ordinary traffic begins.

There is something to gather in the pipe: a fragment left by a larger mouth, a loosened flake from the mineral skin, a bit of another body already becoming useful again. I take what I can carry. I pass it along by passing it—into the press of many legs, into the dark where small mouths wait. Nothing needs to be kept for long. The chamber is too poor for waste and too crowded for solitude.

Sometimes I climb onto the iron and feel the cold shift through it. Then I am no longer only myself. A click travels outward. Bodies pause, turn, scatter, close again. The message is not a word. It has no sender I can point to, and no single receiver. It passes through us as the shape of the moment: danger here; passage there; air moving near the slit of day.

I have never seen the chamber whole. I know the gear’s hollow, the pipe’s dry throat, a sloping stretch of dust, a crack that opens into another crack. Beyond that, the world continues by rumor: a little more air, a thinning of bodies, a pale wash that does not feel like shelter. The larger ones go nearer to it. I have no reason to follow.

My world has ridges only a few legs high. It has a basin where the dust gathers and a roof that lowers until the feelers brush it. The rust-red bands are not scenery. They are the warm-colored limits of a route I have crossed many times. The white salt tastes of almost nothing. The stone gives slowly, and we take slowly.

If you found me, you might lift a pebble and see a small, pale animal pause under it, all feelers and folded legs. You might say: this one.

But the tick would still be traveling. A hundred bodies would be turning toward it; a thousand would be moving away from the place where I had stopped. The crack would keep its cool breath. The gear would hold its dark, worn round of passage.

I am one of the small ones in that passage. I am also the passage continuing: the answering feet, the shared shelter, the body that has no edge you could find by looking closely enough. The stone keeps us. We keep the stone busy. And when the iron speaks, I listen with whatever part of me is nearest.
