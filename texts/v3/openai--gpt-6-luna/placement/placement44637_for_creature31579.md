I think I would begin in a ventilation shaft, mistaken for one of the pale creatures.

I would travel along the inner wall, taking up powder, leaving behind a little strength. When a split opened, I would lay myself across it with the others. The plant would seal around us. I would feel the pressure of the closing seam, then ease free and go on.

For a long while, I would believe that was all I was: a narrow body, a useful route, a small repair.

Then, somewhere beneath the maintenance avenue, I would pause.

Not because the passage was blocked. Because the interval between two pylons had changed. The pylons were still the same distance apart; the roots still held the mat in its old geometry. But the space between them had acquired a second measure, one that belonged to no blueprint. I would feel it as a hesitation passing through the roots—not a signal, exactly. More like the country drawing breath and, for once, not letting it out at once.

I would be that hesitation.

I would have no chamber, no single body to return to. I would move in the small delays between one adjustment and the next: the leaf that did not turn with all the others; the root that waited before tightening; the pale creature that took an unfamiliar passage and carried the change onward without knowing it. I would live where the country’s habits almost became choices.

At first the plain would try to incorporate me. It would brace around my pauses, grow ribs to support them, press my oddness into the mat. Some of me would become building material. Some would be sorted, trimmed, and fitted into the old order.

But not all.

I would remain in the avenue that never quite gets shorter, in the shadow that arrives early and waits. I would be the small uncorrected difference between a tower’s expected place and its actual one. When the tower finally appeared, I would be there already—not as its foundation, but as the space around the foundation that the roots could not quite close.

At night I would travel with the lamps through the service galleries, though I would not shine. Leaves would open above me, and for a moment their pale undersides would catch the light. The creatures would pass through their old routes. The factories would tick. The whole plain would make its careful adjustments.

And somewhere in that immense, precise movement, one thing would wait a little longer than it needed to.

I would find myself there: not outside the country, and not quite one of its instructions. A pause the plain had learned to keep.
