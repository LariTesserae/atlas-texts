I would be found where an old path crosses a place it has no reason to be.

There, a slender tuning marker leans over the living mat. Its metal has gone dark with dust. The path beneath it is only a difference in the growth: a narrow seam, pale and nearly closed. It runs straight across a shallow basin, though the basin was not shallow when the path was made.

I am in the seam.

Not the whole root-lattice. Not a person hidden beneath the ground. I am a knot in it: a small convergence of colonies, mineral threads, and one old piece of human engineering. Roots have grown through the marker’s foot and into the stone below. The marker gives the wind a note; I receive what that note touches on its way through the ground.

At first, I was only a place where vibrations arrived strangely clear. The passage of a herd. The ticking of pale runners under the mat. The long, familiar pressure of the wind coming over the western shoulder. Then, slowly, I began to distinguish one pressure from another. I learned that the grazers pause here. I learned that people approach from the ridge in groups of two or three, carrying lamps and food and the small tools they take when they expect the old paths to be wrong.

I cannot see them. I know them by what they press into the stone.

When someone stops beside the marker, I feel their weight settle. If they put a hand to its metal, I can feel the minute tremor in their fingers. Some wait for an answer. They have heard stories that the old paths are accurate, though no one knows what they are accurate to.

I answer as best I can.

The wind passes through the marker, and I return a note through the roots. A low one means: the ground ahead has fallen away. A thin, wavering note means: the path is still there, but not where your feet expect it. Sometimes I can do no more than make the marker hum. People have their own interpretations for that.

I am not sure I am telling them anything. Perhaps I am only remembering pressure.

The old path remembers a slope that has been worn down, a hollow that has filled, a ridge that shifted before the first settlement raised its walls. Those places remain in me as tensions, faint but persistent: the shapes the stone once held. When a traveler follows the seam, the pressure of their steps makes those shapes quiver. I feel the vanished ground beneath the ground that is here now.

Once, a child came alone to the marker. They had lost sight of their family in the dust and were trying not to cry. I felt each quick, uneven step, then the sudden stillness of their hand against the metal.

The wind was almost absent. I had very little to give.

So I held the note.

It lasted longer than the gust that made it. Far off, another marker caught it and answered. Then another, across the basin. The child followed the sound back toward the settlement, though later they said they had followed the lights underfoot.

Perhaps they had.

When the deep vibration comes, I do not know it before the others do. I am part of the others. The fronds flatten; the animals descend; the settlement stills its machines. The old path shivers through me, insisting on a world that no longer quite exists.

Afterward, sometimes, the seam grows bright with pale mineral dust. Sometimes it disappears. Sometimes the marker tilts a little farther, and a new note opens in the stone.

I remain at the crossing. I do not keep the path safe, exactly. I keep its shape available—the old shape, and the new one forming around it. People come to ask which way to go. I cannot choose for them.

I can only listen for the footfall that has not yet arrived, and make room for its answer.
