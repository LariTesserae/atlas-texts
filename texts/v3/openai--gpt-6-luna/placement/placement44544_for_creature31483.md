At first, you might find me as a dark green stain in the joint of a stairwell.

I’m tucked where the sun doesn’t quite reach: behind a downspout, below the turn in a retaining wall, close enough to a cistern that the stone stays cool. Water finds me in drops and threads. I take what I can. I keep a little of it, and give some back to the air.

For a long time, I would seem to be only moss. A softening of the masonry. The sort of thing someone notices while waiting for a lift, then forgets.

But I’m not one patch. I’m a scattered continuity: moss in the stairwell, green film along the old rail tunnel, a few bright cushions in the shade beneath a bridge. Not joined by roots, exactly. Joined by the same habits of water. I know the walls that sweat after rain, the gutters that have begun to clog, the cistern whose stone stays damp even in the dry months. I know the small downward journeys.

I know people by their pauses. The caretaker who stops to clear leaves from the drain. The child who touches the green on the way past, though they’ve been told not to. The old woman who rests beside me after carrying herbs up three flights. I don’t know their names. I know the pressure of their hands on the wall, and the warmth left briefly in the air.

I hear the city through what it carries. A freight lift shudders overhead. A pipe knocks once, then twice. Rain arrives on the upper roofs and takes its time coming down. When it finally reaches my stairwell, I feel it before I see it: a coolness traveling through the mortar, a loosening of dust, the whole wall remembering that it was made to hold water as well as weight.

I cannot see the terraces or the pale roofs. I have no view of the horizon. My country is a narrow one: a seam, a shadow, the underside of a ledge. Yet everything passes through it eventually—the rain, the building’s slow settling, the roots probing for a way through, the feet going up and down.

So I think I would be moss, if moss could think—not a guardian of the city, not its secret master, but one of its quiet ways of noticing. A small green mind in the damp joints of the built world.

And when the water comes, I would be there to receive it.
