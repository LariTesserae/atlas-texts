At first I think I would be the water: not a river with somewhere to go, but the thin, patient sheet that lies over the stone and keeps returning to the same hollows. I would know the sky by its broken reflection. I would carry the small lives through the channels and let them go again at the edges.

But water has no place to rest.

So perhaps I would be something that rests at the edge of it. A small shape beneath a ledge, where a bead of water falls at long intervals into a dark pool. I would be easy to mistake for the stone: brown-green, still, no larger than the hollow that holds me. There would be no den to claim, only this patch of shade, this seam in the rock, this shallow place that stays wet a little longer than the others.

A frog, then. Not the loudest one. Not the one that makes the basin seem to speak, but one voice among many, waiting through the bright hours while birds cross overhead and shadows pass over the shallows. I would feel the warmth held in the stone beneath me. I would feel the pool tremble when a fish turned. I would know dusk not by looking up, but by the insects gathering close enough to eat.

When the evening chorus begins, I would answer. My call would travel a little way over the water and be taken up elsewhere—not owned by me, not even quite mine once it had joined the others. Then, when the air cooled and the sound thinned, I would settle back into the ledge’s small dark pocket.

I would be easy to overlook, but never alone. And if the water rose, I would move a few inches, then stop. Here, that might be enough to find the next good place.
