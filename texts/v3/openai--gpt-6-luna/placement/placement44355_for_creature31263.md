I think I would first mistake myself for the needle in the cracked gauge.

I would be inside the iron shed, behind a veil of leaves, fixed at a mark too worn to read. The glass would be cloudy. A pale root would have worked its way through the casing and come to rest against the needle’s tip. I would feel the machine’s clack through the metal, then the softened answer of the ground.

For a long time, that would seem to be all I was: a small instrument that no longer measured anything. The pipes would murmur. Water would pass through the old works and into the green. The needle would not move.

But it would not be quite true that I was still.

Whenever the wheel turned, the needle would tremble—not enough to shift, only enough to learn the difference between one knock and the next. When water rose in the basin, the root against its tip would swell. When the shed warmed, the metal would loosen by a fraction. I would become attentive to changes too small to call events. Not a mind looking out at the place, but a mark where the place could feel itself change.

Then, slowly, I would stop being only the needle.

I would be the faint straightness of it carried into the root: a line held through a tangle. I would be the habit of pausing at the same point in each turn of the wheel. I would be the pressure that made a leaf bend around the gauge instead of covering it. The shed would grow through me, and I would persist—not as a machine, exactly, but as the memory of a machine’s way of noticing.

Perhaps the needle is stuck because it has found its reading.

Not a number. Not a pressure. The measure of how much the world has changed while keeping, somewhere inside itself, the shape of what it used to be.

If someone entered, they might see only rusted iron and a crooked pointer. They might wipe the glass with a sleeve, searching for the old scale. I would feel the warmth of their hand travel through the casing, and the roots would take it up. For a moment, the needle might quiver toward them.

Then the wheel would knock again.

I would settle back at my unreadable mark, while the green kept moving through me.
