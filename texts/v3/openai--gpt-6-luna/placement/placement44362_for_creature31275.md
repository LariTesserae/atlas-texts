I would be found where the water changes its mind: at a junction beneath a low bridge, where one channel narrows and another opens into a warm, dark chamber.

At first I might be mistaken for the pale film gathered along the seam. Then the film would tighten. A small mouth would open against the flow, and the water would pass through me.

I am not large. I am long and flattened, with a soft body that follows the curve of the conduit and little feelers tucked beneath me. In the dimness, amber knots show through my translucent skin. I have no need to look far. I know the world by what touches me: the mineral grit in the current, the warmth leaking through the wall, the faint shiver that comes before a pump changes its pace.

I eat what the water carries, and what grows on the metal where it stays warm. I do not chase it. Nothing here needs chasing. When the current brings a fine pulse of nourishment through the floor slots, I open and sift. When it does not, I rest with my underside pressed to the seam, close enough to the others that their breathing reaches me as a second, softer current.

Perhaps I am one creature. Perhaps I am only the part of a larger arrangement that happens to have a mouth.

The bodies around me touch in places and part in others. A pulse passes along them when the machinery shifts: a tightening, a release, then another, farther down the channel. I answer without deciding to. The answer travels on. There is no message in it that I could put into words. It is only a way of keeping pace with what holds us.

Sometimes a great shape settles in the chamber beyond mine, wrapping its pale back around a submerged pillar. The gauge above it moves by the width of a mark. I feel the change through the wall. I do not know whether the creature is resting on the machine or whether the machine has, over time, made a place for it. The distinction does not reach me. There is warmth here; there is water; there is something to eat. I fit.

If the plain has a memory, perhaps it keeps it in small things like me: in bodies pressed to joints, in the slow pulse passed from one crowded hollow to the next. I would not know that I was remembering. I would know only that the valve breathed, the water altered, and all along the seam, we tightened and let go.

Then the current would settle. I would close my mouth and rest beneath the bridge, nearly indistinguishable from the living film, while the grey light trembled above us.
