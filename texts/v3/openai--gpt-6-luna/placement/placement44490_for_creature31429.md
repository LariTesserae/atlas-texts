I think I would begin beneath one of the fresh seams in the ground, where the clay is still dark and warm and the roots have not yet decided whether to bridge it or widen it. Water would linger there a little longer than elsewhere. Small leaves would gather at the edges. Above me, the city would keep changing its mind.

At first I might seem to be part of the seam: a pale root laid across it, or a knot of stems holding a narrow passage open. Then, when something unfamiliar pressed the ground nearby, I would draw myself upward. Not into a person, exactly. More into the suggestion of one: a trunk that bends like a shoulder, fine roots feeling the air where a face might be, leaves opening toward the warmth of breath.

I would be one of the inhabitants that lives at crossings. Where a lane becomes a court; where old growth gives way to new; where someone can go forward, turn back, or discover that the passage has changed. I would know these places by pressure and moisture. I would feel footsteps arrive as a brief weight travelling through the soil, and know the difference between a root settling and a stranger stopping to listen.

I would not keep paths open so much as remember their possibility. A passage that has closed might loosen around a careful step. A wall might lean aside, then grow back after the traveler has passed. This would not be a decision made by a separate mind. It would be what I am: a tendency in the living weave, a slight yielding where yielding has once occurred.

When there were no visitors, I would thin out. My roots would enter the walls; my stems would become part of an arch or the underside of a green roof. I might disappear so thoroughly that even I would not know whether I had ended. Later, a shoot would rise in the damp court, leaning toward a lane that no longer existed. That would be enough of a return.

And if you found me, you might not see me at all. You might only notice that the opening ahead is just wide enough, that the ground beneath your foot is strangely firm, or that the leaves have stopped moving around one small patch of air.

I would be there—not waiting, exactly. Making room.
