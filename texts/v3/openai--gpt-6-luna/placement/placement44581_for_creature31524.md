At first I would think I was the water.

I would be everywhere, running bright over the shelves, gathering in the shallow basins, slipping through cracks and appearing again below. I would know every route downhill. But the water would not know that it was carrying me.

Then I might think I was a flaw in the stone: a faint dark thread under a ledge, no bigger than a grain of grit. I would stay where the current slackened, in the narrow shade beneath a lip of pale rock. Above me, light would break into hard little shards. Around me, the roar would press through the stone—not sound, exactly, but a ceaseless trembling against my body.

I would be one of the nearly clear ones. A small life folded into the seam between water and rock. The dark lines inside me would be the only mark that I was there.

I would open my fine limbs when the current brought something small enough to eat. I would draw them close when the water changed. A passing body—larger, heavier, brief—would send a different tremor through the ledge, and I would flatten myself into the crack. I would learn the difference between a current that fed me and one that meant to carry me away. The difference might be no wider than a bead of water choosing one edge of the stone over another.

I would not think of the plateau as vast. Vastness belongs to things that can stand apart and look. I would know one hollow, one ledge, the cold pressure of the water, the dim seam where food sometimes arrived. Perhaps I would live there for years without once seeing the sky. Perhaps I would be swept loose before I had learned the shape of my shelter.

And still, I would be part of the plateau’s settled motion: not the fall, not the pool, not the stone, but a small insistence within them. The water would pass over me without pause. The rock would keep its pale, patient shape. Nothing I did would leave a visible trace.

If anyone looked down into that basin, they would see only clear water and the reflected sky—until I moved. Then, for an instant, the stillness would show what it contained.
