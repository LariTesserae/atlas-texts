I would be in one of the seams—not the deepest one, where the larger things pass, but a narrow joint beneath the lip of a cistern. There the damp holds its coolness longest. Ash gathers. Water arrives late, when the rain has already stopped and the roofs above have gone quiet.

At first I would look like a pale chip of ceramic.

Then I would uncurl.

I am a setter, though I don’t know whether that is my name or the name given to the work I do. My back is smooth; my legs are fine enough to feel the grain of mortar. I travel by touch, testing each new edge. Soft clay gives beneath my mouthparts. A loose plate answers with a small click. I carry a grain of ash from one wet place to another, and leave it beside the water—not a mark, exactly. More like a suggestion.

I do not make plans. I thicken a wall where it trembles. I ease a little clay from a channel that has become too narrow. If a hollow keeps water too long, I fill part of it. If the water finds a way through, I let it keep the way. By the time I leave, the place is scarcely changed. By the time I return, it may have become somewhere else.

I know the Reach from underneath. I know which courts sound deep and which are only pretending to. I know that a footstep can enter a drain as a murmur and come back out of a wall as a knock. When the larger inhabitants move below, I feel them before I hear them: a tremor, then three heavy taps, then silence. Sometimes the clay on my feet matches the marks they leave behind. I have wondered whether they are making the walls, or only answering them.

When the darkness thickens in a corner, I stop. So do the others. We wait until it loosens. Then we return to the seams.

I have no room of my own. I live wherever the Reach is still deciding what it can hold. My home is the interval between a plate and the one beneath it; my road is a channel not yet certain where it leads. I am small enough to disappear into the work and persistent enough to alter it.

Perhaps that is all a setter is: not a builder, but the little pressure that keeps a thing from settling into its final shape.

When the rain comes, I uncurl. Somewhere overhead, water begins choosing its way. I follow the first darkening seam, carrying a grain of ash. Below me, something knocks once against the underside of the world.
