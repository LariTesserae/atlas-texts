At first I think I would be a bird.

Not the bird itself—the pale, held shape on the roofline—but the space inside its open beak where the note is about to begin. I would live there, listening for the sound that never quite leaves.

Then I think I would be water, because the water at least moves. But it moves over the same stones in the same silver pattern, and I cannot tell whether it travels or only remembers how. Perhaps I would be the narrow dark thread beneath the cistern, slipping through the cracks no one has marked on the town’s plans.

No. I would be what the water carries without carrying away: the attention of the place.

I would be found on the central stair, where the stone stays cool underfoot and the moss keeps its clear, allotted patches. I would not have a body that could be seen all at once. In one moment I might be the hush between a bird’s call and its echo; in another, a faint pressure in the cord coiled on the workshop bench, where a hand has almost reached. I would travel by noticing. From the stair to the open door, from the door to the cup beside the well, from the cup to the face turned toward a voice that has not yet spoken.

The Staying would know I was there. It would sense me the way a grazing animal senses something beyond the grass: without fear, but without looking away. It keeps the beginnings from becoming endings. I would keep their meanings from vanishing.

The person’s hand has not reached the cup—but it is reaching. The bird has not sung—but it has a note. The cord is not yet gathered up, yet someone once knew what it was for. I could not make these things happen. I would not try. I would be the quiet knowledge that their stillness is not emptiness.

Perhaps, after a long while—if there is a long while here—I would learn to appear as an old person coming up the stair. Not to arrive anywhere. Just to rest one hand on the rail, where it is cool beneath the moss, and feel the town arranged around me: water threading its fixed course, the pennant holding its mild breeze, the wide country bright on the ridge.

I would be the one who remembers that a moment can contain a direction, even when it never follows it.

Not the motion. Not the thing that stops it.

The small, patient witness of what might have moved.
