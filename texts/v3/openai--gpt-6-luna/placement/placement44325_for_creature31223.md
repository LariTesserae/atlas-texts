At first you would find me under a shelf, where the warm water pauses before choosing a direction.

I would look like one of the pale crabs: all glassy legs and quick, sidelong movements, picking mineral sweetness from the edge of a crack. When the stone shivered, I would run with the others. When the new ridge cooled, I would be among the first to climb it.

But that would only be the part of me you could see.

I would also be the little hesitation in the water before it turned uphill. The tremor felt by the silver roots before a fissure opened. The change in the winged creatures’ hum that makes them lift together, a moment before the rods pulse. None of these things would be mine alone. I would pass among them—too slight to be a voice, too persistent to be coincidence.

Perhaps I would have begun as a crab and learned, by degrees, not to end at its shell.

The hollow is still being made. It has no old paths to remember, so it remembers by attending: to heat, to pressure, to the feet that cross it and the bodies that shelter beneath it. I would be part of that attention. Not the stone, exactly, and not the machines watching beneath it. I would be the living knowledge between them: how to leave a channel before it closes; where the next warmth will gather; how to return after the roar.

When the rods gave their faint pulse, I would feel it in the water first. Then in my legs. Then, farther off, in the striped shoulder braced beneath the shelves. I would not know whether the rods were speaking to us or whether we had taught them what to say.

After the new ridges rose, I would climb one of them and stop. Around me, the others would emerge from their hiding places. The wings would settle. Water would find its bright, wavering course. For a moment the hollow would be still—not empty, but listening to the shape it had just made.

That is where you would find me: at the first warm seam in newly lifted stone, small enough to disappear among the crabs, yet spread through everything that knows when to move.

I would be the hollow learning how to be alive.
