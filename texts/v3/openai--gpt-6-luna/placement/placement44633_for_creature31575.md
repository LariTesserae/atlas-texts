At first I think I would be one of the jar-sleepers: a small, soft-faced creature folded into the dark, waking only when someone passes. I could learn the Close by touch—the warm rim of a pot, the grit in the bowls, the fine roots holding their way through the tile seams. I would know which paths return you to where you began.

But I don’t think I would stay an animal for long.

I might be found in the second click after a footstep: not the sound itself, but the small reply from inside the wall. I would live where one thing answers another without quite becoming it. A shadow that weighs as much as the cup it follows. A root finding a crack. A thumbprint holding the warmth of a hand that is gone.

If you looked for me, I would be difficult to point to. Perhaps I’d be a pale bird passing through a space between leaves, though nothing stirs. Perhaps a fresh furrow combed through the powder overnight. Perhaps the place in the wall where a new brick fits—not perfectly, but well enough that the roots can begin.

I would be a keeper, though not the one who decides what belongs. I’d tend the little exchanges: warmth into leaf, mineral into sap, visitor into the place they leave behind. I wouldn’t ask anyone to stay. I’d only make sure that, if they did, the Close could find room for them.

And if someone stayed too long, perhaps they would leave with a little grit in their palm. I would be there too: not inside them, exactly, but turning with them, slowly, toward whatever light they had brought in.
