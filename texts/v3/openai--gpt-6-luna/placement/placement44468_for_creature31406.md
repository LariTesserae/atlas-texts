I would find myself in one of the rooms the city has learned to leave open.

Not at its center—there may be no center now—but where several old passages meet beneath a roof of interlocked roots. The floor would be worn into a shallow track, though no feet have crossed it for longer than I could count. My first thought would be that I was the echo of those feet: a small sound kept in the grain, returning whenever the city groaned.

But an echo is supposed to repeat.

I would not. I would arrive late, altered by every wall I passed through. A tremor in the roots would reach me as pressure, then as a low note, then as the sense of a doorway narrowing somewhere far away. I would know the city not by seeing it, but by feeling where its fibers held tight and where they had begun to yield. I would be a listener made from the thing listened to.

Perhaps I would be the memory of the builders—not their faces or names, which the city could never have kept, but the shape of their intentions. A room made broad enough for a gathering. A passage made straight for a reason no longer known. The city would not remember them as people. It would remember the space they asked it to make, and the pressure of their hands persuading stems to turn.

I would live in that small difference between what the city was shaped to do and what it has become.

When a grain of stone shifted above, I would feel the disturbance travel through the roots. I would pass it onward—not as a message, exactly, but as a question the city already knew how to answer. The answer would be a creak crossing the dark, a brief tightening of the walls, dust slipping over the floor. Then the stillness would gather again, not empty, but full of all the movements too slow to hear.

If someone entered, I would feel their footfall arrive before I understood it. I would hold the sound inside me, carry it into rooms where no one had stood for ages. The city might close a little around the stranger—not to trap them, but because it knows only how to keep what touches it.

So I would be an echo, until I wasn’t. A memory, until that proved too human a word. I would be the city’s awareness of a change passing through it: small, patient, and spread across too many walls to have a face.

And when nothing changed, I would remain there too, in the hollow the roots had chosen not to fill.
