At first, you might find me by sound.

Not the loudest sound in the hollow, nor the clearest. A click, nearly swallowed by the rustle of sleeping wings; a small question sent into the dark and answered by stone. I would be somewhere above the damp channels, where the roof slopes low and bodies press close enough to share warmth.

I would be a bat.

But not, at first, a bat with much sense of being separate. I would begin as warmth against warmth: a small body folded beneath another, held to the rock by claws I had not yet learned to trust. The hollow would be the whole world—its mineral taste, its breathing drafts, the shifting weight of the colony. When I cried, other bodies would stir. When I slept, the roost would rock gently with the breathing of hundreds.

Then I would learn the ledges.

A tooth in the rock: turn here. A cold seam: keep clear. A draft rising from below: the way out. I would send my voice ahead of me and receive the chamber back in pieces. Distance would arrive as an answer. A wall, a wing, a neighbor’s face—each would return differently.

I would leave at dusk with the others, not because I knew the valley, but because the dark beyond the lip would pull at me. Outside, the air would be enormous. I would follow its currents, hunt where insects lifted from the fields, and learn that hunger could carry me farther than fear. Then, in the hours before dawn, I would turn back toward the cliff.

I would not recognize the hollow by sight. I would know it by the rise of warm air, by the familiar clutter of calls, by the particular confusion of echoes where the entrance narrowed. Home would be the place that answered me in a voice I had heard before.

Perhaps, after many returns, I would become one of the old bodies on the ledge: a worn hollow in the colony’s pattern, a neighbor against whom the young might sleep. I would still go out alone to feed. But alone would not mean separate. My flight would cross paths with others; my calls would join theirs; the dark would be full of lives, each finding its way by sending a little sound ahead.

So you would find me in the crowded shadow—not at the edge of the world, exactly, but at the edge of a home. Folded among the others, or passing through the narrow mouth of the cliff, carrying the hollow with me as a memory made of echoes.
