I would be in the wall behind a staircase that returns to its beginning.

At first I’d think I was one of the small pale inhabitants: crouched in the damp, one hand against the clay, waiting for it to soften. I’d learn the taps. I’d answer the distant ones, though I wouldn’t know what I was saying. When a new passage opened, I’d follow it carrying nothing visible.

But sometimes the clay would close around me, gently as a hand closing around a seed. I wouldn’t struggle. I’d become the hollow behind the wall, the narrow pocket of air where the warmth gathers. From there I could feel water turn in its channels, leaves shiver, the mountain give its single knock. I’d be less a person than the place’s attention, gathered briefly in one small seam.

Then, when a figure passed outside, I’d tap back.

Perhaps that would make me a little person again. Perhaps the wall would open and give me feet.

I’d never reach the highest terrace. I’d tend the steps that lead toward it, smoothing a damp edge, lifting a leaf from a new doorway. Each time the stairs brought me back to where I began, I’d take it for a message: not *you have failed to arrive*, but *there is still somewhere to go*.

I’d be found where the world is almost solid and almost open—between one drop and the next, listening for the answer.
