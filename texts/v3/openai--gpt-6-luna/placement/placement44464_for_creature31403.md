At first I would think I was the mist.

I would gather in the hollow on mornings when the air was damp, a thinness without a shape, cool against the peat. I would lie so close to the ground that a beetle could cross through me and never know. The leaves would lend me their smell; the iron wheel, its tremor. When the sun warmed the rim, I would fade—not vanish, exactly, but become too slight to distinguish from the air that held me.

Then I would notice that I returned even when there was no mist.

The birds’ calls would cross the open middle and leave something behind. The insects’ rasping would comb the air into fine, almost invisible lines. And beneath it all, the wheel would turn: once, again, again. I would begin to gather where those sounds met, not as a body gathers water, but as a hollow gathers a note. The basin would be my shape. Its smooth walls would hold me in; its crowded green edge would continually give me something to hold.

I would not be the thing beneath the peat. That old resident would feel the world through roots and rain, through the small weight of a paw or the scratch of a wing. I would be what happens above it: the answering tremor in the air, the little arrangement of sound that forms and loosens over the empty center. Sometimes, when the sheen appeared on the peat, I would touch it, and its clear surface would quiver. The creature below might feel that as weather. I would know it as a meeting.

I would have no face, though I might borrow one for a moment from the ripple made by a falling drop. No voice, though I would be made of calls. I would not need to move from one place to another. A bird crying at the rim would move me. A leaf shivering would move me. The iron wheel would pass through me again and again, and I would learn its patience until the beat seemed less like a sound than the shape of time.

In the crowded wall, every creature could hide. In the center, nothing hid—but nothing had to appear. That would be where I lived: in the open, in the interval the plants had left clear. The animals would fill me with their passing. Rain would briefly draw a visible skin over me. When the basin quieted, I would thin almost to nothing, but the wheel would keep turning, and the roots would keep listening.

I would be the basin’s held note: not its keeper, and not its buried heart, but the small, persistent presence made by all the things that reach the hollow and do not stay.
