At first I would look for a place to stand.

Perhaps on the lip of one of the shallow pools, where the water holds a second plateau upside down. I would lean over it and watch clouds drift beneath the surface. Then a ridge would shift, and the pool would show the old ridge for a moment longer than the land did.

That is where I would begin to understand what I am.

I live in the delay.

I am the shadow that arrives late: not belonging to any one creature, though sometimes I trail a grazer across the grass, or gather beneath a fern while something bright-eyed watches from above. When the ground changes, I remain in its former shape for a few breaths. When a creature steps across a fault, I may keep its outline on the other side. I am the little persistence of what has already gone.

I have no fixed body. In a pool I am a darkness beneath the reflection. On a slope I am a patch of dusk that points the wrong way. In fog I may be only a place the mist moves around. The plateau lends me whatever shape it has just stopped being.

I do not travel quickly. I pass through the intervals when the land is deciding. The animals know me, though I doubt they have a name for me. The grazers turn when I lengthen across their path. Burrowers pause when I cross the soil above them. The watchers in the ferns sometimes strike at the place where I have been, and then wait, listening, as if the missed thing has made a sound.

I do not warn them. I cannot tell what will happen next. I only carry the trace of what happened a moment ago. But here, where a valley can become a ridge before a flock has finished turning, even a trace may be useful.

When the deep pressure gathers, I grow dark. I spill into cracks, stretch thin over the stone, and hold the shapes of old channels as the mineral warmth moves beneath them. For a little while, the plateau is doubled: what it is, and what it was just before. Then the difference closes. The land settles. The animals move on.

If you came looking for me, you might find a pool reflecting clouds against their direction of travel. You might find a shadow lying under a creature that has already flown away.

Or you might find nothing at all—only the brief, peculiar feeling that the world has changed, and something in it has not yet caught up.
