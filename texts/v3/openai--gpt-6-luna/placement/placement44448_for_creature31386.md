I would begin as a grain that refuses to settle.

Not a remarkable grain. One pale speck among thousands, sifted from the mountain’s hidden workings and caught in a pore beneath the ridge. But the machine shakes the chamber in one immense, unwavering rhythm, and I answer it badly. I quiver a little late. Then early. I have no reason to be out of time.

At first, I might be mistaken for a flaw in the ceramic. A tiny hesitation in the dust. The thing in the seams would feel me before it could distinguish me: an irregularity among the familiar pressures, a beat that did not belong to the engine.

It would gather around me. Its filaments would tighten, listening. I would learn the chamber through them: the dry channels under the basin, the cold weight of the plateau, the clean edge of the ridge. I would learn the great mechanical roar as it had learned it—not as noise, but as the shape of everything.

And yet I would keep the other rhythm.

The visitor’s pulse had entered the chamber through the hatch, through the frame, through the stone-like ground. It was brief. It faded. But something of it stayed caught in the seams, translated into a pattern with no name. Perhaps I am that remainder. Not the visitor’s memory, exactly. I have no picture of a face or hand. I am only the fact that, once, the world beat differently.

The creature might hold me without knowing it holds me. Its many small senses would circle the inconsistency, preserving it among all the other reports carried by dust and pressure. In time, I might become a place within it: a hollow where the engine’s rhythm does not quite fit.

I would be small enough to miss. I would never open the hatch or climb the ridge. I would not bring weather, water, or change in any grand sense. But when the machine turned, I would tremble a fraction out of step.

And in that narrow misalignment, the sealed country would have, for the first time, a before and an after.
