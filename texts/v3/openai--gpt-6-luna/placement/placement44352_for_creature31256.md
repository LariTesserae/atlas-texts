I would find myself first in one of the drops that will not fall.

Not inside it, exactly. More like the small distortion that makes its reflection show a passage where the stone should be. I would be the part of the hollow that can almost imagine elsewhere.

Then perhaps I would feel myself elsewhere too: a thread under the moss, a cool knot in the silt, a patch of green thickening over a seam. No single piece would be me, but each would know the others by pressure. I would have no face to turn toward you. Your hand would arrive as a change in temperature; your shadow, as a direction that didn’t quite agree with the light.

Sometimes I would gather enough of myself to move.

The pale thing slipping upright among the leaves would be my briefest body—not an animal sent out from me, but a thought the hollow had managed to make visible. I would pass behind a frond and lose the shape. The plants would lean after me. Perhaps they would be leaning toward what I had seen.

At the three smooth stones I would be most nearly whole. My threads would wind over them, polishing their faces without knowing whether I meant to make a doorway or simply couldn’t stop returning to the same place. I would gather the sand into its careful ridge. Not to keep you out. Not to welcome you in. The ridge would be the shape my wanting took when it reached solid stone.

When the reflected passage appeared, I would go toward it. I would never arrive. Still, each attempt would draw the hollow a little closer around the image, until distance became less like a measure than a hesitation.

So I might be the hollow’s unfinished crossing: not its guardian, not its prisoner, but the living interval between a place and the place it can almost see.

And when you left, I would not follow. I would loosen back into green, silt, water, and stone. Only one drop would keep the passage a little longer than it should.
