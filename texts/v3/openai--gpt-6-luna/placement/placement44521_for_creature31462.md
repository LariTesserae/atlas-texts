At first, I would be only a change in the water.

A small displacement beside the warm crack. A slickness where the stone should be bare. When a drop strikes the pool, I draw myself inward—not because I know what a drop is, but because the sudden pressure reaches me all at once. The water steadies. I loosen.

I live where the crack opens onto the pool: not quite in the warmth, not quite in the cool. The seam shelters me from the strongest current and keeps a little heat gathered around my body. I am soft, pale, almost transparent. The stone lends me my outline; water gives me movement. When I stretch along the rock, I am scarcely more than a fold in its wet shine.

The bright bands pass over me. I do not see them. They change nothing I can use. But the chamber is not empty to me. A warming seep announces itself against my underside before it reaches the pool. A flake of mineral breaking loose sends a tremor through the wall. The tiny animals make faint disturbances in the water as they drift, though they are not mine to follow so much as part of the pool’s shifting texture. I answer the things that touch me: pressure, warmth, the taste of dissolved stone.

I spend most of my life gathered into the crack. There is no reason to cross the open floor. Out there, the water changes quickly; the rock offers little cover. My whole world is a few body-lengths: the crease behind me, the slick lip before me, the cool edge of the nearest basin. Sometimes I extend into the water and leave a faint trail in the film on the stone. Sometimes I retreat so completely that the chamber appears to have lost me.

Perhaps I am a mouth, though not only a mouth. Perhaps I am a listening surface, a soft body spread thin enough to feel a change before it becomes danger. I take what the seam gives and remain where the conditions are bearable. This is not bravery. The crack is simply the place where my life fits.

Above me, the crystal gathers distant brightness and lays it across the chamber. Around me, the shelf adjusts by fractions; water finds new routes; pale mineral rims gather and break. The room is changing faster than stone ought to, and I am smaller than any measure of that change.

Still, when the warm surge passes and the pool settles, I return to the lip. My body loosens into the water. The water slips over me, carrying the taste of the chamber onward. For a little while, I occupy the narrow boundary between shelter and open pool.

That is where you would find me: not in the shining bands, but at their edge, folded against the warm stone—one small softness, waiting for the water to steady.
