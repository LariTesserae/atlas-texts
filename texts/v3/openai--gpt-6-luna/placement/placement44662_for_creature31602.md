I think I would live in the gap beneath the old tram.

There is just enough room between its roof and the stone above for a body to pass, though not quickly. Silt gathers there in a soft dark layer, and the tram’s buried frame holds a little warmth from the deep rock. A narrow leak feeds the gap. Around it, translucent fronds grow in slow, branching fans.

At first, you might take me for part of those fronds.

I am small, and not quite one thing. A colony of pale filaments, perhaps, joined by a common sensitivity to pressure and warmth. I know the district as changes in the water: the small shiver before a gate moves; the measured beat of the flywheel; the hollow stillness that means a culvert has stopped breathing. I feel the inhabitants pass above me through the tram’s frame. Their steps are not sounds. They are brief, familiar weights.

For a long time, I do nothing but feel.

Then a filter clogs in the western passage. The current changes. Silt begins to gather at the entrance to a safe room. I feel the disturbance travel through the pipes, past my narrow home, and onward into the basin. I release a little of the warmth I have stored. The fronds stir. A stream of fine particles lifts from the tram roof and travels uphill in a clean, straight band.

One of the inhabitants sees it.

After that, I begin to answer.

Not with words. I can make the silt move, or tighten my fronds around a leak. I can lay a pale strand across a threshold, though I cannot choose its shape very well. The people learn that certain signs mean trouble. They leave grilles open where I grow, and now and then they bring scraps of softened plant matter. They never try to carry me away. I think they understand that I am most myself here: in the cramped dark, among the tram’s old ribs and the city’s patient machinery.

I have no face, and no name I know of. The children call the pale threads beneath the tram *the listening moss*. Perhaps that is close enough.

Sometimes, when the basin is quiet, I feel a pulse pass through the buried culverts from beyond the last lamps. It does not match any machine I know. The inhabitants disagree about what it means. I cannot tell them. I can only hold still and listen again when it comes.

So I would be there: under a street that has become a ceiling, rooted in the warm seam between stone and machine. Not keeping the city alive. Not quite. Just noticing when its habits change, and making one small sign for those who still know how to look.
