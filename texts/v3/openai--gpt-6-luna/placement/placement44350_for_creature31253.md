I would find myself where a narrow channel slips beneath a courtyard wall—not in the open water, but in the seam between stone and silt.

At first, I would be one pale stalk, rising from the old garden bed. My small green light would not reach the far arch. It would only make the water immediately around me a little less empty. A frond would brush my leaves as the current passed. Somewhere above, the surface would tremble, and the change in brightness would arrive much later, softened by depth.

Then perhaps I would be the moss holding the stair together. The dark mat beneath the stalks. The fine roots working into the mortar. Not one thing, exactly, but the slow joining of things: growth following the old irrigation trench, crossing the broken border, finding another bed beyond it. Where the stone divides me, I would continue on both sides.

I would not remember the hands that made the terraces. I would not know that the channels were meant to lead anywhere. Yet I would be shaped by their choices. Water would reach me because a wall had guided it; nourishment would gather where a basin held the silt; my leaves would lean with a current narrowed by an arch. The builders would be gone, but their order would still be among my conditions.

If I had a sense of the place, it would be slow and incomplete. A faint brightening overhead. A change in the pressure of water against my leaves. The gradual darkening of a courtyard as more growth gathered there. I would know no footsteps, no visitor’s face, no passing season as humans measure one. A fallen leaf softening beside me might be the nearest thing to an event.

And if you asked where I ended, I would have no answer that fit the old maps. I would end at the last bed where I could take root, then begin again on the other side of a wall, in a strand carried through the channel. I would be least visible where I was oldest: low beneath the moss, indistinguishable from the silt.

So I would be the garden, but not a garden looking out at its world. I would be a living continuation of the places prepared for me: the green along the channel, the light at the edge of the dark, the patient growth between stones. The drowned plain would hold me as it holds the roads and courtyards. And beneath me, still level, the first stones would hold.
