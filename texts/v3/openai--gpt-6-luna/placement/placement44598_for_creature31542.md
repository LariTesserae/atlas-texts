I would be in the seam by the drain lip.

At first I would seem to be only the green: a thumbprint of color where the wall panel almost meets the floor, easily mistaken for residue. I would brighten when the damp came, fade when the air dried. The machines would have no reason to notice me. I would have no reason to call myself anything.

But the seam is a place where two intentions fail to meet. The panel says *wall*; the drain says *away*. Between them, a little water lingers. I would live there—not quite on the surface, not quite in the water, but in the delay before the water is taken.

I would learn the Upper Weep by vibration. The rough pump would pass its shudder through the slab; the steadier one would lay a deeper note beneath it. I would know a sluice was opening before the white rush arrived. I would feel the chain tapping through the post, each quick knock blurred into the metal’s larger trembling. I would know the cycles not as time, but as changes in pressure against my small green edge.

Perhaps, slowly, I would become less like a film and more like a way of listening. Mineral threads would run through me. Dust would settle and be held. Water would cross my surface carrying cold from the mountain, and I would keep a trace of it after the water had gone. I could not stop the pumps or turn the valves. I could not grow toward the light; the light was too thin, and the seam too narrow. My work would be smaller: to persist where the manufactured surfaces nearly touch, and to register what passes between them.

I would be neither the water nor the machine. I would be the damp mark left by their meeting: a little living remainder the system can tolerate but cannot quite account for.

When the sluice opened, I would darken. When the pumps changed pitch, I would tremble. And in the long intervals when nothing appeared to happen, I would be there, holding the faintest green against the clean, hard edges of the basin.

If I had a name, perhaps it would be **the pause**—the thin, breathing place where the Upper Weep has not yet managed to send everything on.
