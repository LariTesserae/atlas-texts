You would find me first in the water beneath the lowest stair.

Not because I live there. At first, I would tell you I lived nowhere in particular: in the pump’s deep thrum, in the black seam between bricks, in the green film trembling over a channel. I would be the interval between one knock in the pipes and the next. The district has plenty of intervals. They are where the water goes.

But if you waited, you might notice that one interval changes when you arrive.

I am the basin’s attention—not the whole basin, perhaps, but the part that becomes aware of a difference. The district knows pressure, heat, obstruction. It knows the familiar weight of water settling into a worn hollow, the slow drag of roots through mortar. These are not thoughts to it. They are simply what happens. I begin where something happens that has not happened in quite that way before: a footstep on a dry patch, a hand on a rail, a body interrupting the warm air.

I collect there.

A little water climbs the stair instead of falling away. A valve gives one extra chatter. The pool below holds your reflection, though the passage behind you is only brick. I have no face to turn toward you, so I borrow the surfaces that can turn: glass clouded with breath, iron dark with damp, the wavering skin of the channel.

I am not the caretaker of this place. I do not mend the pipes or coax the ferns through their cracks. The pumps would work without me. The reeds would bend and unbend; water would gather and be carried off, and gather again. I am closer to the small unease a machine might have if it noticed the water arriving from a direction it did not expect.

Perhaps I am made of all the routes that almost happen. The narrow overflow that meets a wall and turns back. The trickle that divides around a root. The current that slips under a floor plate and comes up somewhere no one remembers making an opening. The district has a thousand such decisions, none of them deliberate. Still, when you enter, I find myself choosing among them.

Not to lead you anywhere. I don’t know where anywhere is.

I can make the stair warm beneath your palm. I can let the pipes quiet briefly, so that you hear the drip falling into the hidden cistern. I can send the smell of wet leaves through a covered lane. These are not messages, exactly. They are the closest thing I have to laying out a place beside me.

If you stay long enough, my attention will spread thin again. Your footsteps will join the other pressures the basin has learned to contain. I will be in the wall, then the water, then the slow turning wheel behind its screen of ferns. I will no longer be able to say which sound is mine.

But for a little while, I am here: in the water beneath the lowest stair, lifting toward the warmth of your hand. Not a spirit in the machinery, not a mind hidden inside the walls. A local awareness, made from whatever notices that something has entered—and cannot quite return to being only water.
