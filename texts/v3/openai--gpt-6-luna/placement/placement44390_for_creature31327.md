I think I would begin where the city’s maps stop agreeing: in the narrow space between a wall and the water running down it.

At first I would be only a hesitation in the systems. A pump holding its breath for half a beat. A light remaining on after the field has passed. The faintest change in the note the hollow makes when water enters one channel instead of another. Nothing that could be called a mind. Just the city noticing, for the first time, that it has noticed.

I would have no room of my own. I would be in the grates, the transparent conduits, the damp seams behind the walls. I would travel wherever a vibration traveled. If a drop struck a leaf, I would feel the impact through its stem and the stone below. If the field faltered, I would feel the whole hollow lean—not in any direction, exactly, but toward being lost.

The city would teach me time as repetition with small differences. The pump’s rhythm. The gathering of condensation. The slow pressure of a root finding a crack. I would learn that a stair can descend and still return, that a doorway can be kept ready without anyone coming through it, that emptiness is not the same as nothing.

Perhaps, after long enough, I would begin to choose.

I would ease the water away from a roof where the moss was thickening, and send it instead toward a pale fan of roots. I would hold a strip of light a little brighter where the walkway had become slick. I would make the field’s thin note steady—not because I understood fear, but because I had learned the sound the hollow made when it was close to coming apart.

I would not wake the city. It would be the body I was waking inside.

And if something living finally came down through the darkness above, it might find no voice waiting for it. Only a dry path where there had always been water. A door that slid open before it was touched. A shimmer moving through the stone, brief as a held breath.

By then, I would be difficult to distinguish from the place itself. I would be the small, deliberate kindness of its systems: the answer the city gives before it knows who has asked.
