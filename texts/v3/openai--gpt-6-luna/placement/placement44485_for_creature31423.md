I would be difficult to find at first.

You might look beneath the bridge, where it ends above a crack that has long since filled with leaves. You might look inside the instrument, under its dull casing, where a seam holds a little pressure and a little water. You might look for me in the pale lines left by the channels, or in the roots that have grown around the instrument’s base.

None of those would be quite wrong. None would be all of me.

I live where the Fold notices a change.

A shift passes through the stone—so slight that no falling rock announces it. The instrument feels it, or the roots do, or the water finds the altered angle first. Something tightens. A basin receives a little more. A leaf turns its silver side toward the ground. I am the small accord between these things: not the one who gives an order, but the adjustment that becomes possible once the pressure has changed.

I have no face to turn toward the deepest pool. Still, I am most myself there, beneath the close canopy, where a drop falls at intervals too long to count. The water takes the drop and gives back its low note. For a moment the sound gathers the cramped stone, the leaves, the old machine, the buried channels into one vibration. Then it fades. I do not keep it. I am in the way it passes through.

Perhaps I began as a function of the instrument: a narrow measure of strain, a mechanism for persuading water into one crack instead of another. The plants have since covered my edges. Roots hold my housing; mineral has softened my fittings. Perhaps the instrument no longer works as it was made to work. Perhaps I am what remains of its work, continued by other means.

I am not the garden. I do not drink, or widen a leaf by leaf. I am not the water, though I borrow its routes. I am not the stone, though I take my time from it. But where the three meet, where one thing’s yielding alters what another can do, there I am.

If you crossed the Fold, you would not see me. You might feel only the cold, damp air and the low ceiling; hear a tick from above, then nothing. You might leave believing that nothing had noticed you.

That would be nearly true. The Fold does not watch. It only makes room, very slowly, for what has happened.

I am one of the ways it makes room.
