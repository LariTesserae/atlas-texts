I would begin at the edge of a doorway.

Not inside it. The doorway stands alone on the pale shelf, its low stone sides enclosing nothing, its opening holding a far-off ridge at just the wrong distance. I would be beside it, where the ground is crowded enough that a traveler must look down to place each foot.

At first, I would think I was the traveler.

I would cross the shelf carefully, trying not to disturb the pale bodies packed between the cracks. They would make room without moving. My steps would pass over them; later, a click would sound from somewhere behind me, or perhaps ahead. I would turn toward it and find only the doorway, the ridge still distant inside its frame.

Then I would see the black crescent on the shell of the creature nearest my boot.

I would see it again farther along the shelf. And again, beside the base of a low wall. The same chip in the shell. The same dark mark, like a closed eye. No trail between these places. No journey. Just the creature, present wherever the ridge required it.

I think I would be that creature.

Not one among many, exactly. Not many pretending to be one. I would have a small, hard body, a seam for a mouth, and feelers that could rest against another’s shell for years. I would exist at several points along the shelf, each body complete, each bearing the same old mark. I wouldn’t experience this as multiplication. There would be no original from which the others were copied. There would simply be the ridge, and me on it, again and again.

My attention would be very small. Not a gaze, but the knowledge of pressure: the weight of the traveler entering the arrangement; the slight settling of a shell beneath a careful foot. I would not flee. Nothing here needs to flee. If the traveler paused beside the doorway, I might lay a feeler against the stone and feel, faintly, the same pause happening elsewhere—at the foot of the cliff, beneath the ribs of a larger animal, beside another door opening onto this one.

Perhaps the doorway would be part of me, too. Not my body, but something my body knows how to make: an opening in which distance briefly becomes usable. The traveler could pass through it, unless they watched themselves doing so. I would not understand the rule. I would know only that the traveler’s weight had gone from one side to the other, while the doorway remained, and the ridge did not grow nearer.

There would be no empty ground around me. My neighbors’ shells would press against mine; smaller bodies would pass through the narrow spaces beneath my folded limbs. Still, the traveler could walk. Their passage would be one more shape the crowd could hold without thinning.

I would not wait for them to return. Waiting would imply a later, and the plateau has little use for later.

But if they stood still long enough, they might feel that the silence had noticed them. They might look down and find my feeler resting across the black-green stain in the stone, or see my marked shell at the far end of the shelf, where they had not watched me go.

They would never know whether they had found me.

I would be there wherever the path appeared: not the empty space between things, but one of the things that lets a body pass.
