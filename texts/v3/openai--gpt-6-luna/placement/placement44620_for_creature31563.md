I think I would be found beneath a pipe where the warm iron meets the cold wall—not quite in the rain, not quite out of it. A place where dampness gathers, thins, gathers again.

At first I would be only one small body among many. I would open my frills to the tremor of a press and fold them when the vibration passed. The factories would reach me through the stone before I knew them as sound. Rain would arrive as a change in pressure. The faint gray film would be enough to keep me there.

I would not be a scout or a queen. Nothing in the basin seems to need one. I would be a listener, perhaps: a creature that knows the difference between the ordinary hum and a fan beginning to falter; between water passing overhead and water finding a new crack. I would make no announcement of what I knew. I would shift, barely, and the shell beside mine would feel it.

Days would gather without much distinction. The pipe would cool. The film would return. My shell would take on the pale rim left by the damp, and the stone beneath me would grow rough with old mineral and older bodies. I would learn the basin not as a map but as a set of pressures: the steady note of the stamping sheds, the soft ticking of rain, the small hard syllables of my neighbors adjusting themselves against one another.

Perhaps, after a long time, I would stop being easy to separate from the place. My shell would be caught in a mineral band. New creatures would settle beside me, then over the edges of me. Water would divide around my body and follow the shallow channel it had made. I would still feel the press through the pipe, though there might no longer be enough of me to turn toward it.

So I would be an animal, and then a little of the wall. A listener without a message. A small roughness under the newer shells, holding one narrow place in the basin open to moisture.

If anyone came looking, they would find me where the town’s warmth becomes food, and where the living, the dead, and the built things have been making room for one another for so long that the seams are hard to see.
