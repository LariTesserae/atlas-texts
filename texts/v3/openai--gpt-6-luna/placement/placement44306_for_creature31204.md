At first I think I would be a bird.

I would live far below the rim, where the updraft holds you without effort, and look up at the country from the side. But a bird would be seen. It would have a hunger, a direction, a shadow passing over the stone.

I think I would rather be something the people hear and cannot quite find.

Not an echo. An echo gives back what it is given. I would live in the narrow clefts where a sound goes in and comes out altered: a shout thinned to a thread, a footstep returned from the wrong direction, the low groan of a ledge held for a moment longer in the air. I would be the aftersound—the small persistence of a noise once its cause has gone.

My home would not be one particular ravine. It would be wherever the rock has hollowed itself into a listening shape. A place shaded from the noon heat, above the reach of sudden water, near enough to a pool to hear its surface tremble when rain begins. When a cliff falls, my home might disappear. Then I would gather in some new crack, still carrying, for a little while, the shape of the old one.

The people would know me without naming me. They would pause when a sound returned strangely. An elder might touch a child’s wrist and wait with them, listening: *That is wind. That is water under stone. That is the slope settling. And that—leave it alone; it is only the hollow giving back what it heard.*

I would not know what the stone intends. I would only keep its changes audible.

At night, I would listen to the households sleeping beneath their overhangs: the soft shift of a shoulder, a hand laid down beside a water vessel, the tiny click of grit moving under someone’s heel. When the storm came, I would fill with its noise until I could hold no more. Then, after the rain passed, I would release the last of it in fragments—wind, water, a distant fall—though no one could be sure which sound belonged to which moment.

So I would be neither person nor animal, and not quite a voice. I would be a place the country makes when it wants to hear itself.

And when the rock changed its mind, I would change with it.
