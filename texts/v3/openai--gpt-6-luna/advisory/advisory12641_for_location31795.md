## Prepare to arrive smaller than your instincts

This is not a human-sized town with narrow streets. It is a pocket habitat made by and for very small lives. Unless your multiverse travel includes safe, reversible miniaturization, **do not try to enter**: widening the opening, lifting the earth, or pushing aside roots could collapse the settlement. Observe from outside instead.

If you can shrink safely, make sure your reduced mass and strength are appropriate to the place. A traveler who is tiny but still presses with human-scale force could crush a walkway, cave in a clay edge, or send a damaging wave through the central pool.

## What to bring

- **A light, soft, close-fitting waterproof layer.** Everything is damp, and cold will accumulate while you move slowly. Avoid loose fabric, dangling straps, and anything that could snag on root-hairs or leaf walls.
- **Grippy footwear**, with no hard, heavy soles. Bark chips, wet stone, moss, and clay will all be slippery in different ways.
- **A very dim, shielded light for emergencies.** Let your eyes adjust before using it. A bright beam could startle or disorient the creatures—and would erase the subtle light cues by which the hollow is usually perceived.
- **A simple way to record your route**, such as a small, quiet marker you can retrieve. Do not leave paint, tape, fibers, or other foreign material behind.
- **A compact emergency tether or exit line**, if it can be secured without cutting, tying around, or burdening a living root.

Do not bring sprays, repellents, fragrances, food, open flame, heaters, or anything that leaks. Avoid powered equipment that hums or vibrates. In this place, a tiny disturbance can travel through water, clay, and roots.

## Before entering

Treat the hollow as a living, fragile settlement—not an empty cave. The roots are still growing, the ground is still being shaped by water, and the routes may change. A passage that was clear when you arrived could narrow while you are inside.

Pause at the entrance and let the place settle around you. Listen. Watch for ripples, moving shadows, or sudden stillness. If the creatures have gone quiet, something may have disturbed them; wait rather than pressing farther in. Do not enlarge the entrance or pull away leaves and soil to improve your view.

It would be sensible to arrive in good condition, with no open wounds, and to assume that the water, animals, and surfaces carry unfamiliar microbes or parasites. Do not drink the water or touch your face while inside. Plan to clean and quarantine your clothing and equipment afterward according to your multiverse travel protocols.

## Moving through the town

Keep one hand lightly against a **nonliving, stable surface** when you need a guide, but do not grip or scrape the roots. The local advice to follow a root is for those who know how to pass without harming it. Living walls can bleed sap, and sap draws attention.

Move slowly and place each step deliberately. Prefer firm stones and established ground; avoid reed-stem walkways unless you can tell they will bear your weight. Do not step on moss, leaf roofs, shells, larvae, or anything whose nature you cannot identify. Never cross the central pool unless there is a compelling reason: even a small disturbance may be noticed throughout the town.

Give animals room to retreat. Do not chase the translucent shrimp, touch the snails, lift leaf roofs, or peer into crevices where moths are folded away. A small frog or a hanging larva may be still, not harmlessly inert. If an animal blocks the path, wait. The town’s traffic moves at the pace of its residents.

Expect water to behave like terrain. A bead or narrow runnel may be an obstacle; a ripple may carry farther than you expect. Do not dam a channel, scoop from a basin, or shift stones that guide drainage. Water is part of the town’s streets and plumbing.

## Navigation and emergencies

There is little to see by ordinary light. Learn the space by texture and sound: the cool curve of a root, the give of moss, the difference between a falling drop and a moving creature. The upright splinters and paired shells mark neighborhoods; leave them exactly as you find them. Do not assume a familiar-looking route remains open. Roots thicken, clay crumbles, and water cuts new channels.

If you lose your way, stop. Do not walk faster, call loudly, or sweep the area with a bright light. Return to the last place you can identify, listen, and let your eyes readjust. If a root shifts or a passage begins to close, retreat rather than forcing it. If the hollow seems to go completely still, stay still too, until the disturbance above has passed and movement gradually resumes.

Leave before you are tired, cold, or tempted to take shortcuts. The town offers no dry, bright refuge for a visitor, and a small lapse in balance could damage a house, a drain, or a resident’s hiding place.

## The right frame of mind

Go as a guest passing through someone else’s home—quiet, temporary, and almost weightless. The town is not silent or empty; its life is simply conducted in movements too small to command a human’s attention. The best visit may leave no sign that it happened at all.
