## Treat the Bell Basin as unstable ground, not as a lake

The safest visit is a short one from the highest reliable ridge you can reach—not a swim, not a crossing, and not an attempt to explore every room. The Basin is small enough to tempt you into thinking it is manageable. Its water, sound, and changing terrain make that impression dangerous.

### Before you go

- **Arrive with a guide who has recently been there.** Old maps and yesterday’s route may already be wrong. Agree on a turnaround time and leave someone outside the Basin’s boundary with your planned route and return time.
- **Bring a retreat line.** Use a climbing harness and a rope secured to rock you have inspected, with a second independent anchor where possible. Treat every anchor as temporary: shelves break away, and familiar rock can reappear somewhere else.
- **Prepare for ordinary mountain hazards too.** Wear sturdy boots with good grip, gloves, eye protection, and a helmet. The black rock is sharp and newly fractured; the ascent may be high, exposed, and cold even though the lake is warm.
- **Plan for communication without shouting.** Agree on clear hand signals and rope tugs before entering. Use hearing protection, but not so much that you lose all awareness of your companions. The roar can overwhelm speech, and the low note can be felt through the body.
- **Carry reliable, simple navigation aids.** A compass, a marked route on stable terrain, and tactile markers are more useful than relying on reflections, shadows, cloud position, or a map that assumes the ridges will stay put. Do not depend on a single marker placed at the water’s edge.
- **Pack for a quick exit, not a long stay.** Keep essentials—warm layers, water, first aid, light, and emergency signaling—on your person in sealed bags. Do not count on a structure, shelter, or camp being safe merely because it is dry inside.

### At the Basin

Stay on the ridges and keep well back from the margins. The water does not behave like a level surface: it can climb walls, hang in sheets, reverse direction, or conceal a new trench where there was firm ground hours earlier. **Do not wade, swim, lean over the water, or use it as a shortcut.** Warm water is not reassuring here; it may hide deep cuts, crosscurrents, or sudden changes in depth.

Avoid testing the place. Do not throw stones, drop objects into the lake, or make marks in ash or wet ground. Things may return, fall upward, or be answered by movements elsewhere. A footprint is not a dependable trail marker.

Keep voices low and purposeful. The drifting fronds turn toward voices, and the skimming creatures appear during the loudest surges; neither behavior tells you what the organisms are doing or whether they are harmless. Do not approach them, feed them, or try to follow them.

The stone rooms may be useful as brief shelter, but inspect the entrance and floor before entering. Their dry interiors do not prove they are safe, and their grooves should be left alone. Do not sleep there or use the rooms as your only route back.

### Leave at the first sign of change

Turn back if the route alters, a shelf cracks or shifts, water rises across your path, or the surface suddenly goes flat after a boom. Also leave if you begin hearing your own steps or breath from elsewhere in the Basin, or if shadows and reflections become difficult to distinguish from the things they belong to. These are not curiosities to investigate; they are warnings that your bearings may no longer be trustworthy.

Keep the rope line taut enough to follow, but never trust it as proof that the ground ahead is sound. Move one person at a time across questionable rock, with the rest of the party on secure ground. At the first sign that the line, the ridge, or your sense of direction is behaving strangely, retreat by the route you have already checked—without trying to recover equipment.

The best visit may last only long enough to see the black ridges, feel the booming note through your boots, and understand why the Basin seems unfinished. Leave before the mountain has time to change its mind.
