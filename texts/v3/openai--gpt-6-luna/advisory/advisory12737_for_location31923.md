Treat it less like an abandoned ruin and more like a small, functioning place whose residents happen to be plants, water, and old machinery. It looks calm, but the pump still runs, the channels still carry water, and the buildings have had a long time to settle into their current shape.

**Before you go**
- Check the weather and keep the visit short if heavy rain is possible. The basin drains, but wet paving, hidden channels, and old culverts can make a sudden downpour more hazardous than the drizzle suggests.
- Tell someone your route and return time. If your multiverse equipment needs a clear spot or stable conditions to work, identify a broad, open courtyard before you enter and keep it as your return point.
- Bring a waterproof outer layer, warm clothes, sturdy boots with good grip, gloves, a small first-aid kit, water, and a charged light. The air is cool and damp; stone and metal may remain slick long after rain stops. Carry your own drinking water rather than relying on the channels.
- If you have asthma, allergies, or sensitivity to mold and spores, bring the medication you need. Avoid kicking up old dust or brushing through dense growth.

**While you’re there**
- Move slowly and stay on the broad stone walkways. Moss, leaves, and roots hide cracks, drains, and changes in paving. Don’t step onto roofs, gantries, loading-yard vegetation, or apparently solid growth.
- Give the machinery a wide berth. A belt, valve, or pump may still operate even when it seems inactive; pipes may be warm, and metal edges may be sharp or weakened. Don’t open panels, turn valves, block drains, or try to “help” the system.
- Don’t drink the water, eat plants, or put your hands into channels. The vegetation is unusually well established, and its relationship with the buried pipes is part of how the place works. Look, photograph, and leave it as you found it.
- Keep clear of dark interiors, basements, and culverts unless you have a specific reason and a safe, permitted route. The town’s compact scale can make it tempting to explore everything, but a few minutes’ walk is not the same as a few minutes’ safe exploration.
- Use your light carefully. The industrial lamps come on irregularly, so don’t rely on them for navigation; keep your own light ready even at dusk. Mark turns mentally or on a map, and don’t mistake the soft green cover for a continuous, walkable surface.
- Expect long silences punctuated by the pump’s pulse, a ticking valve, or movement in the foliage. Avoid loud calls and unnecessary noise. There may be no visible people, but that doesn’t establish that the place is unoccupied.

Most of all, resist the urge to treat the settlement as either a dead factory or a wild garden. Its machinery and growth have reached a fragile, durable arrangement. A considerate visitor can pass through quietly, take in the smell of wet iron and crushed greenery, and leave without changing that arrangement.
