Treat this less like a picturesque cavern and more like an isolated, unfamiliar biosphere—possibly with unusual local physics. Its calm is not evidence that it is safe. The heat, still water, dense vegetation, uncertain air, and apparent lack of change all deserve caution.

### Before entering

- **Go with a trained partner and a retrieval plan.** Agree on a maximum time inside, regular check-ins, and clear conditions for turning back. Keep a reliable route to the return point; do not depend on radios or ordinary navigation working across a multiverse boundary.
- **Establish the return point before exploring.** Use a secured line or other reversible markers, backed up by written bearings and a route log. Do not paint, cut, or attach anything to the plants or stone.
- **Take environmental readings at the threshold and repeat them inside.** Check oxygen, carbon dioxide, temperature, humidity, pressure, and airborne particles or gases. Do not assume the air is breathable simply because plants are thriving.
- **Plan for a hot, humid environment.** Wear light, protective clothing that will not snag easily, and carry water and electrolytes. A sealed suit might prevent exposure but could cause dangerous overheating; choose protective equipment only after measuring conditions.
- **Bring a helmet, eye protection, grippy footwear, and a small first-aid kit.** The stone roof is unfinished, the ground may conceal soft hollows, and condensation could make rock or leaves slick. Avoid walking directly beneath visible seams or suspiciously thin sections of roof.
- **Carry independent instruments:** a thermometer, air monitor, reliable timepieces of more than one kind, a written notebook, and a low-intensity light. The place is already bright, and ordinary clocks, compasses, or electronic devices may not behave normally.
- **Keep equipment clean and contained.** This world may have organisms unlike anything at home. Do not bring food, soil, seeds, or exposed biological material inside, and plan to quarantine and clean gear after returning.

### On arrival

Pause before going farther. Record the conditions at the entrance, check your partner and equipment, and observe whether the light, condensation, or plants change over a short period. The description suggests that water, dust, and leaves may remain strangely motionless. Treat that as a possible anomaly, not as proof that time has stopped or that nothing can change.

Stay on existing bare ground or the most open route you can find. The growth is dense enough to hide footing, and brushing through it could damage a fragile ecosystem—or expose you to irritants, spores, or plant defenses you cannot recognize. Do not taste the water, eat the plants, touch the cup-plants, or reach into hollows. A still, clear pool is not necessarily clean.

Keep your movements slow and deliberate. Avoid flames, loud experiments, and unnecessary sampling. The basin contains a few simple structures—a stone ring, a shelter, an arch—but there is no reason to treat them as abandoned conveniences. Do not enter, move, repair, or remove anything. They may be culturally significant, ecologically important, or much more fragile than they look.

The stillness may also affect your judgment. Sparse sound, fixed light, and a landscape that seems to resist change can make time hard to estimate. Check your timepieces and notes frequently, but do not rely on any one instrument. Set a strict turnaround time well before entering; do not stay longer just because the place feels peaceful.

### Turn back immediately if

- Your air monitor shows unsafe or changing conditions.
- You develop headache, confusion, unusual fatigue, breathing difficulty, or signs of heat illness.
- The roof shifts, sheds material, or shows new cracking.
- Your route markers, clocks, instruments, or communications behave unexpectedly.
- You notice movement or changes in the plants, water, light, or sound that were not there before.

If the landscape suddenly begins to change, do not try to investigate the change. Leave by the known route, keeping your partner with you and avoiding a panicked dash through the vegetation.

### When leaving

Account for everyone and all equipment before crossing back. Keep exposed gear contained, and avoid bringing plant matter, water, or soil home. Afterward, check for bites, rashes, respiratory symptoms, and any unexplained changes in instruments or timekeeping; isolate and clean equipment according to a cautious quarantine procedure.

Most of all, resist the temptation to make the first “true event” happen. This basin may be an ordinary ecosystem in an unusual place, or it may be something whose quiet is part of its nature. A careful visitor should leave it as close as possible to how they found it.
