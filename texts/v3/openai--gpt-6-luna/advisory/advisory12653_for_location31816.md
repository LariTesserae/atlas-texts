Treat the salt city as a **living, working structure**, not a ruin. Its quiet lanes are also air passages; its plants and walls are part of a system that keeps the hollow habitable. A short, cautious visit is wiser than an ambitious exploration.

### Before you go

- **Find a safe, knowledgeable guide if one is available.** The entrance is easy to miss, and the plain’s salt crust may be thinner or less stable than it looks. Don’t probe, dig, or strike the ground to find the seam.
- **Make a route and rescue plan.** Tell someone outside when you expect to enter and return. Carry a map, a way to mark your route without leaving permanent traces, and a communication method that does not depend on clear radio reception underground.
- **Keep the visit short.** There is no dependable water, shelter, or animal life to support you if something goes wrong. Eat and drink beforehand, and carry your own supplies in sealed containers.
- **Check yourself for heat or respiratory risks.** Hot, extremely dry air and fine mineral dust can strain the body even when the effort seems modest.

### What to wear and carry

- A **well-fitting particulate respirator**, eye protection, gloves, and clothing that covers exposed skin. Salt dust can irritate eyes, airways, and skin; it may also work its way into equipment.
- **Low-profile head protection** for the low ceilings, plus sturdy footwear with good grip. Keep your pack small enough to pass through narrow doorways without scraping walls or plants.
- A **diffuse, low-intensity light**, with a backup. The hollow is dim even when it is bright outside; avoid powerful beams or unnecessary illumination.
- A **temperature and air-quality monitor** if you have one. Ventilation may be noisy or uneven, and the fact that air moves does not guarantee that it is safe to breathe everywhere.
- Hearing protection you can remove or adjust. The hollow’s whistles and roars may be loud, but you should still be able to hear your companions and changes in the air.

### Finding the entrance and going in

Approach slowly and avoid putting weight on an uncertain crust. The seam is narrow; don’t widen it or force your way through. Once inside, move carefully: doorways are low, passages are close, and surfaces that look solid may be old saltwork or weathered construction. Don’t climb on roofs, lean heavily on walls, or use stakes or anchors.

The lanes, gutters, slots, and chimneys have a purpose: they guide hot air, dust, and falling crystals. **Never block a vent or channel**, even temporarily. Keep away from active fissures, where heat and airflow may be stronger and the plants may be brittle. Watch for fresh flakes, new cracks, shifting dust, a change in temperature, or a change in the usual rhythm of the hollow. If anything seems different or unstable, turn back.

### While you’re there

- **Don’t touch, cut, collect, or step on the plants.** They are the city’s living growth, not decoration, and they may be sensitive to oils, microbes, or unfamiliar organisms carried on your gear. Arrive with clean equipment and take everything you brought back out.
- **Don’t drink from the walls or plants, and don’t pour water into the hollow.** Water does not collect there, and even a small spill could affect the mineral surfaces and vegetation.
- Keep food and rubbish sealed. Avoid open flames: the ventilation is unpredictable, and fire would add heat, smoke, and risk to a confined place.
- Don’t mistake the sounds for signs of ordinary activity. Wind moans through the shafts, fronds rattle, crystals patter, and the rock booms as it expands. Pause during a strong gust or boom, secure your footing, and let loose dust settle before moving on.
- Use a companion system and agree on simple hand signals; the air can be loud enough to make speech unreliable. Stay together and keep your exit route clear.

Leave at once if anyone becomes confused, nauseated, unusually tired, short of breath, or overheated—or if the airflow, temperature, or structure changes unexpectedly. Don’t try to sleep in the hollow or wait out a problem there. The safest way to experience the city is to go in lightly, move gently, and leave it breathing as you found it.
