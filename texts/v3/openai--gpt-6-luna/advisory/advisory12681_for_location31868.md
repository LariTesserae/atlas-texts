Prepare for the Sedgeworks as you would for a **working, waterlogged district**, not an abandoned factory or an untamed swamp. Its greenery is carefully doing a job: holding the ground together, filtering water, and fitting around machinery that is still running. The place may look calm, but the paths can be obstructed, the surfaces slick, and the machinery loud.

### Before you go

- **Arrange access with someone local.** Ask which lanes and factory areas are open, whether any paths are currently blocked, and where the safe crossings and exits are. The numbered gates and service roads may help with orientation, but the district’s edges and walkways can change as plants grow and repairs are made.
- **Allow more time than the map suggests.** The Sedgeworks is compact, but roots have lifted paving into uneven shelves, and plankways may be laid over unusable ground. Don’t count on crossing it in a few minutes.
- **Check the day’s conditions.** Ask about water levels, active machinery, maintenance work, and any areas with poor ventilation. If you have asthma, serious mold or pollen allergies, or difficulty coping with heat and humidity, find out about the indoor air and bring any medication you need.
- **Carry an offline map and tell someone your route.** The low skyline and nearly continuous canopy make landmarks hard to pick out. Don’t assume you’ll have a clear view of the road or be able to hear calls over pumps and presses.

### What to wear and carry

- Wear **grippy, closed-toe footwear** with good ankle support. Expect wet brick, slick leaves, moss, metal grates, and unexpected changes in level. Sandals and smooth-soled shoes are poor choices.
- Choose **lightweight, quick-drying clothes** that breathe. The air is warm and close, and shade does not necessarily mean coolness. A light rain layer may help, but avoid heavy clothing that traps heat.
- Keep your pack compact and secured. Avoid loose straps, dangling cords, scarves, or anything that could catch on rails, grates, or machinery.
- Bring drinking water, a small first-aid kit, and a way to protect them from damp. Consider a light mask if dust, plant fibre, or musty air bothers you; hearing protection and eye protection are sensible if you have permission to enter an active work area.
- Use ordinary insect precautions if needed, but don’t assume the water is safe just because it looks clear—or that all the wet ground is standing water.

### While you’re there

Treat the district as **inhabited and operational**. Stay on designated paths and follow local directions. Don’t climb onto roofs, cross rails, step over channels, or enter the processing hall without an escort. Belts, rollers, pipes, and presses can be dangerous even when moving slowly; keep hands, hair, clothing, and equipment well clear.

Do not drink from channels or pools, wade through them, or reach into grates. The water is controlled and used in the district’s processes, but that doesn’t make every outlet or pool safe. Likewise, don’t pick, cut, or pull at the plants. They are part of the district’s infrastructure, not simply scenery. Avoid walking on the planted beds or placing equipment where it could block drainage.

Move deliberately. Roots and lifted paving can turn a seemingly level lane into a trip hazard, while rain or persistent damp may make a surface slippery even when it hasn’t just rained. The soundscape can disguise movement and warnings: pumps murmur below the walkways, water taps on metal, and machinery knocks at steady intervals. Make visual contact with workers where possible, and don’t assume someone has heard you.

### How to approach the place

Expect a subdued, enclosed visit rather than a dramatic expedition. The canopy hides the larger city; the light arrives in fragments; the machinery provides a steady pulse beneath the leaves and water. Pause somewhere safe, take in the sound, and let your eyes adjust to the dim lanes—but keep to the paths while you do.

The best preparation is to arrive rested, lightly equipped, and willing to follow the district’s existing routes. In the Sedgeworks, what looks like overgrowth is often doing essential work, and what looks like a quiet ruin may be part of someone’s shift.
