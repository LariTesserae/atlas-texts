Treat this as a **dry, inhabited, wind-driven place**, not as a cave to explore at speed. The biggest risks are likely to be disorientation, dust, noise, dehydration, and getting separated from the narrow way back out.

### Before you enter
- **Check the air first.** Don’t assume that bright, moving air is breathable. Use an atmospheric monitor if you have one; carry an independent breathing supply until you know the air is safe. The chamber’s vapors, mineral dust, or plant grains may irritate lungs even if the atmosphere is otherwise breathable.
- **Plan your return through the seam.** Mark the entrance discreetly, keep a guide or companion with you, and agree on a turnaround time. Don’t let curiosity draw you so far into the streets that the small opening becomes hard to find.
- **Ask permission and seek a local guide.** The city is quiet, not abandoned. Its residents may know the wind’s schedule, safe routes, and which doors or ledges should be left alone. Speak close by, or use gestures; shouting is unlikely to work.
- **Pack water and salts.** There is no visible water, and warm, dry air can dehydrate you without making you feel as sweaty as you expect. Carry more than you think you’ll need.

### What to wear and carry
- Wear **sturdy, well-gripping shoes**, long sleeves, and close-fitting clothing. The stone is worn smooth, stairs are shallow, and bridges tremble in the gusts.
- Use **eye protection** against the fierce reflected light and windblown grit. A brim or light hood can help, but secure it so it cannot flap or catch the wind.
- Bring **hearing protection that still lets you communicate**—for example, filtered earplugs or suitable protective devices—and agree on hand signals beforehand. The roar may hide footsteps, warnings, and even your own voice.
- Carry a **well-fitting particulate mask** for dust and drifting plant grains, but don’t rely on it to make unknown air safe. Keep it accessible rather than wearing it automatically if it obstructs communication or makes breathing harder.
- Bring a small light, even though the city is bright. Some rooms are bare and dark, and the strong shadows may conceal changes in level. A compass or other navigation aid is useful, but don’t trust it as your only wayfinding method in an unusual stone chamber.

### While inside
- **Move slowly and stay on established routes.** The smooth channels are part of the city’s wind system, not ordinary gutters. Avoid blocking vents, moving the stone wind-markers, or placing gear where it might be swept into a passage.
- Watch the gust cycle. When the larger openings catch the wind, bridges shake, loose tiles chatter, and the sound swells. Don’t cross exposed bridges or climb towers during a strong gust. Keep loose objects strapped down.
- Expect **glare, dust, and confusing acoustics**. Use visible meeting points and hand signals; check on companions regularly. The roar can make nearby voices seem distant or directionless.
- Keep clear of unfamiliar plants, pale creatures, and sealed doors. The plants are brittle and may shed fine grains; the residents may depend on them or on the airflow around them. Don’t collect specimens or touch old writing, soot marks, or fittings without permission.
- Avoid flames, smoking, and anything that throws sparks. The growth is dry, and the chamber’s airflow could carry embers or dust farther than you expect.

The city may feel peaceful because its weather is regular, but its quiet streets sit inside a powerful, moving system. Go lightly, keep track of the way out, and let someone who knows the wind set the pace.
