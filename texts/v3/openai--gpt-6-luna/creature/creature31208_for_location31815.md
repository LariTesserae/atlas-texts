What lives there is not a creature, exactly. It is the water learning the shape of its room.

At first it seems only to pass through: a steady arrival at the hatch, a circuit along the gutter, a slow return toward the plugged drain. But it has begun to keep habits. It favors one side of a pillar. It worries the mortar at the outlet. When the loose gate shifts, the current changes course, and the whole chamber answers—pipes rattling, droplets ticking, the ceiling carrying the noise from one end to the other.

The water has no eyes, but it gathers the light and breaks it into moving pieces. It has no voice, but every narrow passage gives it a different note. Its warmth is held close against the tiles; its patience is measured in grit carried away, in softened edges, in the new crack beside the gate. It does not know the room is failing. It only presses where the room gives way.

For now, the gate and the channels keep its movements orderly. The water returns, again and again, to the routes made for it. Yet each circuit is a little different. A bead gathers overhead and falls. A thread of green bends, lifts, bends again.

The chamber has been built to contain something that will not stay still. And the thing it contains is beginning, very slowly, to make the chamber its own.
