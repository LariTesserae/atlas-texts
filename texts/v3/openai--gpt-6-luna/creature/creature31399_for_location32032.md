The district’s inhabitant has no body that can be pointed to.

It is easiest to notice where the old factory’s wheel turns: one slow rotation, a pause, then another. The sound does not travel so much as enter the stone. In the pause, the roots tighten against the pipes. A broad leaf lifts from the machinery and settles again. Somewhere beneath the square, a drain releases its bubbles.

If these things are one creature, it is very large and very thin. It occupies the hairline seams in the masonry, the soft mats under the silt, the green cords threaded through the ironwork. It has no eyes, but it knows when water has been disturbed. A diver passing through the streets sends a message ahead in ripples: a tremor through railings, a stir among the fronds, a faint shiver in the factory’s hanging cables. The inhabitant receives this everywhere at once, or nowhere in particular.

It does not chase. It does not hide. It makes room.

A strip of cloth caught on a doorway slowly draws free. A fallen bolt rolls a finger’s breadth toward the central hall. The current remains too slight to explain either movement. If the diver follows, they may find the cloth caught among the factory leaves, or the bolt resting beside the shallow pool, placed as neatly as something set down by hand. Whether the place is collecting what enters it, or simply allowing every loose thing to find its way home, is difficult to tell.

Its home is the open iron framework above the pool. The leaves there are wider than those elsewhere, their undersides pale and faintly luminous. Fine threads run from them into the gears. They tighten and relax with the wheel’s turning, not driving it, exactly, but keeping its motion from being lost. Around the pool’s rim, mineral crusts have formed in delicate rings. They are thickest where the water should be moving fastest.

Sometimes a diver’s lamp will catch the pool’s surface, and the reflection will appear to show a ceiling far above: not stone, but a dark, wavering sky. The inhabitant gathers near this light. Ferns angle their fronds toward it. The little fish leave the ticket booth and hover above the factory floor. Even the wheel seems to pause longer, listening—or waiting for whatever the light has brought.

There is no sign that the district remembers people as people. A hand on a pipe is only pressure. A voice is only vibration. The inhabitant may respond to a diver with a slow opening of leaves, a slight warming of the water, the release of a row of bubbles from beneath the square. It may do these things for no one. Its measures of time are too broad for courtesy, and too small for ceremony. A turn of the wheel might be an answer. It might be no more than another turn.

Still, it keeps the canals passable. It keeps the pipes warm. It tends the edges of the pool, where the stones are smoothest, and the dark crowded with green. Its care looks very much like decay continuing in an orderly fashion: iron becoming reddish scales, brick yielding to roots, machinery made useful by the plants that have grown through it.

Perhaps there was once a purpose to all this, and the inhabitant is carrying it out long after the purpose has been forgotten. Or perhaps the district became its own reason for continuing: every pipe warming every root, every root holding every stone, every slow rotation passing the work on.

In the factory, the wheel turns once.

The leaves stir, though the water is still.
