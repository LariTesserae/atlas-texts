Nothing lives in the range apart from the growth that makes it. There is no population hidden in the chambers, no small life stirring beneath the roofs. The inhabitants are the roofs, the bridges, the towers: a multitude of plants grown so tightly together that the distinction between a forest and a constructed place has nearly disappeared.

No single kind of plant seems to rule. There are the tall, jointed stalks that hold up arches; the broad-leaved forms that spread into roofs; the corded roots that bind ledges and span gaps. Some grow as pale, flat shelves, catching water and passing it along. Others pack themselves into dense, dark walls. Their shapes differ, but their work joins up. A shoot that finds a seam thickens it. Roots seek the damp underside of a failing bridge. Leaves turn over one another until they shed rain into a channel instead of a hollow.

The range has no apparent center. Its coordination is spread through the living material: pressure felt in a stem, moisture drawn through a root, chemical signals moving along a shared weave. A storm arrives, and the response travels outward—not as a command, but as a succession of adjustments. Leaves close against the force of falling water. Stems bow and brace. Roots swell where the soil is loosening. By the time the flood has passed, the growth has already begun to mend what it changed.

The empty towers may be the strangest forms. They rise from wide platforms, open to the weather, with no obvious fruit or flower to explain them. Their bundled stems gather the rain and wind; their hollows carry sound across the valleys. Perhaps they are organs of the whole range, perhaps simply growth given a shape that has proved useful. They do not turn toward a visitor. They do not wait for one.

This is not a garden cultivated for another creature, nor a city left behind by builders. Its inhabitants make their world by growing into it. They have no hands, yet they raise terraces; no plans, yet they repeat forms that hold. They do not appear to want anything beyond the conditions that let them continue: water moving, light reaching, pressure answered with new strength.

When the evening mist gathers, the entire mountain seems to settle into itself. Leaves shift beneath the rain. Stems knock softly together. Far below, a root bridge tightens around its burden of water. The living structures keep adjusting in the dark, not working toward an arrival, but carrying on with the work that is already their life.
