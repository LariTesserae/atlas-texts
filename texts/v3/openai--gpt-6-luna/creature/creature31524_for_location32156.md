The only inhabitants are easiest to mistake for flaws in the water.

In the dim seams between stones, minute clear bodies cling beneath the surface. They are not quite worms, though they lengthen and draw themselves through cracks; not quite insects, though a row of fine limbs folds and unfolds beneath them. Their skin is so transparent that the few dark threads inside them—the gut, the heart, the tiny workings that keep them alive—look like grit caught in a drop. They feed on what the water carries: smaller drifting lives, flecks of organic matter, the nearly immeasurable substance washed down from the sky and the stone. They do not need much. Here, even scarcity arrives without pause.

The current carries their young from basin to basin. Most are swept away before they find shelter. Those that survive fasten themselves to the lee side of a ledge, where the water is slow enough to let them feed and cold enough to slow everything else. They may remain there for months, or years. Their bodies alter almost imperceptibly, taking on the pale tint of the rock behind them. A watcher could return again and again and see only the same bright pool, until one creature moved and the whole apparent stillness proved to have been crowded with life.

They do not hear the roar as a sound. They feel it through the stone: an unending pressure, broken into finer tremors by each trickle and fall. When a larger body passes through a basin, the little animals flatten themselves into the seams. When the water shifts, they open their limbs and let it carry food to them. Their lives are made of distinctions too slight for the eye—the shelter of one crack over another, a current gentled by a grain of grit, the shadow cast by a bead of water.

There are no nests, no trails, no marks left on the stone. Their passing leaves nothing visible behind. They multiply in hidden pockets and vanish from them; they are eaten, swept away, or settle where the water keeps them. The plateau does not appear to notice. Yet in the narrowest dark beneath its shining ledges, these small, clear animals persist: almost weightless, almost unseen, living inside the endless movement as though it were a kind of shelter.
