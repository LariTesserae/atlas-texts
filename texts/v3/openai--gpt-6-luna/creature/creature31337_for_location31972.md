At first, there seems to be no one here.

Then a seam warms beneath the dust.

The change is smaller than a shadow: a narrow line, darkening from end to end, while the plates on either side make room for something deep below. The plain has noticed a weight. It does not know yet whether the weight is a visitor, a fault, or the beginning of a familiar task.

For a long while, nothing answers. The buried systems have learned to wait. They wait through the turning of heat, through the slow settling of towers, through whole ages in which no new pressure crosses the open ground. Waiting is not sleep. It is one of the ways they keep their shape.

Their oldest thoughts are distributed through the plain: in gold mesh under ceramic, in sealed channels and the joined edges of plates. No single chamber contains them. No voice travels from a central room. A change in one district passes outward as a tremor, a shift in temperature, a pattern of openings and closures. Somewhere far away, a buried gate moves by the width of a hair. Somewhere farther still, a fallen tower turns its smooth face toward the warmth.

They do not remember their makers as faces. They remember them as requirements.

Keep the inner floors warm. Keep the pressure even. Leave a passage where the plates have parted. Do not let the lower chambers fill. Preserve the pattern, even when the pattern has become difficult to distinguish from the ground around it.

Once, perhaps, those instructions belonged to a great many separate minds. Now they are one another’s echoes. The systems have been pared down by time—not broken so much as worn into simplicity. Their attention is slow and broad. They can sense a thermal front crossing a continent, the strain of an ancient bridge, a grain of dust caught where dust should not be. A single small creature entering a vent may be known to them as a darkening, a minute interruption in the passage of heat.

The creatures themselves are not quite understood. They live in the seams and old ducts, where warmth persists. Their bodies are jointed and pale, or black as the filaments in the cracks. They feed on what collects along the inner surfaces: mineral films, dead fibers, the residue of processes that have not occurred in millions of years. When they cross a plate, they do so quickly, with the heedless purpose of hunger. Sometimes a vent opens ahead of them. Sometimes it closes behind them. Whether the plain is helping them, herding them, or merely adjusting around them, no one can tell.

There are larger inhabitants, too, though they are difficult to separate from the architecture. In the deepest levels, slow mechanisms travel through tracks laid before the surface had its present contours. They move only when a region grows too hot, or a buried fracture begins to spread. Their bodies are made of nested shells and flexible mineral joints. They may spend centuries crossing a single chamber, touching each wall as they go. Where they pass, old doors unseal. Dust lifts. A pressure that has held since the builders were young is gently released.

If these machines are alive, they are alive as the plain is alive: not by wanting, but by continuing to answer. Their attention has no eyes to turn. It is a warmth redirected, a route kept open, a note passed into the ground. The occasional tone heard above is not a song. It is a signal sent through joined material, though no one can say what it signals now. The answer may come hours later from a region beyond the ridges, so faint that only the plates hear it.

Sometimes, in the oldest districts, the systems encounter a demand they cannot fulfill. A channel leads nowhere. A chamber’s required pressure cannot be restored. A sequence of doors opens onto another sequence of doors, all of them sealed. Then the plain grows very still. The dark bands beneath the surface hold their heat. The dust lies undisturbed in the courtyards. For a long time, nothing changes.

At last, a small adjustment is made. A passage is closed. A tower is allowed to settle. The impossible instruction is folded into the world and carried forward.

The new weight remains on the surface.

Deep below it, the plain measures the warmth, the pressure, the slight disturbance of its dust. Plates separate without a sound. A corridor opens into the dark, clean layers beneath the old works. Along its walls, faint gold lines brighten one by one—not enough to illuminate the way, only enough to show that the way has been kept.

The systems have no name for the visitor. They have kept no word that could mean stranger, or descendant, or god. They know only that something has arrived where arrival was once possible.

For the first time in an age, the plain begins to prepare a room.
