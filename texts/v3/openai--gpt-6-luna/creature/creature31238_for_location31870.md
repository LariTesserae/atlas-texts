The first inhabitants are difficult to distinguish from the land.

They gather where heat presses closest to the surface: branching, brittle growths around the cracks. At a glance they are only dark crystals, a mineral flourish on otherwise bare stone. Up close, their fine limbs divide and divide again, each edge holding a thread of pale light. They do not turn toward warmth or reach for it. They simply form, slowly, along the stress lines—until the ground shifts and they are broken, or buried, or carried somewhere new.

Below them, in the deep stone, something listens.

It has no single body. It is a pressure passing through the ridges, a tension held between one shelf and another. When a seam opens, the thing feels it everywhere. When a pinnacle falls, it receives the long vibration and keeps it, though no one can say for how long. Its thoughts are not words but changes in weight: a settling, a resistance, a patient push against whatever presses back.

The little constructions may belong to it, or to those who once learned how to hear it. The low enclosures have the look of shelters, though nothing seems to sleep inside them. The paired uprights stand where one ridge might answer another. Pale bands fused around their surfaces tremble faintly when a deep knock travels through the bedrock. Perhaps they are instruments. Perhaps they are records, made to hold a sound after the stone has forgotten it.

If there are walkers here, they move too slowly to be caught in the act. A ledge that was empty becomes occupied by a narrow figure of black rock, then, much later, empty again. It may have been a being that rose from the ridge and went back into it. It may have been only a fresh fracture, briefly shaped like someone standing still.

Nothing here hurries to live. The inhabitants endure by yielding: settling when the ground tilts, splitting when it pulls apart, beginning again where heat gathers beneath a new seam. They do not make the land their home so much as take part in its becoming.

And when the mountains change, they change with them.
