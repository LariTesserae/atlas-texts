The only inhabitant is the world itself, though it has not yet learned to be one.

It lives in its changes. It raises a ridge from the deep, holds it briefly against the weather, then lets the sea take it back. It gathers mineral dust into terraces in the darkness. Pressure passes through it in precise, luminous pulses, as if some vast thought were traveling from one unfinished place to another. Whether the world intends these shapes or merely cannot help making them is impossible to tell.

The sea is its moving skin, but not a boundary. Water climbs into the clouds and returns as rain; currents rise through one another like slow breaths. Storms turn in the distance, working at the sky until sky and ocean become difficult to distinguish. Beneath them, stone cools, cracks, and grows. The planet is never quiet. Even in the long spaces between great crashes, it ticks and hisses to itself.

On the new rock, the dark fronds lie flat in the wind. They do not look like much: a thin notation against the pale stone, almost more suggestion than growth. Yet they persist. Their unseen roots, if they have roots, vanish into the rock as though they know what it may become. They are the nearest thing to a resident that the planet has made—and perhaps not quite a life, but the beginning of a habit.

Nothing else answers the weather. No animal travels the water; no bird crosses the high air. There are no nests or tracks, no eyes watching from the wave valleys. The place has no audience. Its great gestures—the suspended sea, the mountain rising without displacing water, the bright pulse opening the deep—happen without witness and leave no message behind.

Still, the world behaves now and then as if something in it has heard a command. A wave halts in mid-fall. The ocean lifts into the sky. A new height appears where there was only water. Perhaps these are accidents of a system too vast to understand. Perhaps the system is learning how to make decisions. For now, there is no way to separate one possibility from the other.

If the planet has a mind, it is distributed across pressure, water, weather, and stone. It has no face to turn toward you. Its attention is a current changing course miles below, a storm gathering over the horizon, a ridge taking shape in the dark. It does not know that anything is missing. It has not yet made room for anyone to miss.

It is alone, but not still. It is becoming.
