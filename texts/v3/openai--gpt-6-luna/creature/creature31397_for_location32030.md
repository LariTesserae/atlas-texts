The Choir is inhabited by the interval.

Not the stone, exactly, nor the water passing through it. The interval between a ledge and its shadow; between a drop falling and the sound of it landing; between a traveler’s step and the faint answering step that comes from somewhere ahead. These small delays are where the Choir keeps its attention.

At first, the intervals seem like tricks of light and distance. A shadow takes too long to settle. A footfall returns from a shelf you have not reached. Then the repetitions begin to feel less like echoes than like careful listening. The place has heard you arrive. It is waiting to learn what you will do.

Its inhabitants have no bodies to distinguish from the landscape. They gather in hollow chimneys and the narrow dark beneath shelves of stone. They pass through the mountain with the water, though they do not travel as water does. When a stream climbs against the slope, one of them may be moving through the rock below it. When a basin shifts a few paces without spilling, the interval that held it has shifted too.

They are not spirits in the sense of the dead, and they do not seem to remember people as people. They remember patterns: a hand laid on cold stone, a pause before crossing a shadow, three notes repeated instead of two. A traveler who walks gently may find the paths growing easier to read. One who hurries may discover that every cairn is visible and none is near.

The old shelters were built by people who understood enough to stay. Their grooves beside the doors are not decoration. They give the intervals somewhere narrow to rest. Water gathers in them because the Choir is willing, for a while, to hold still. The shelters have no windows facing the sun so that their occupants need not watch their shadows arrive late.

Nothing in the Choir hunts. Nothing asks to be worshipped. Its attention is patient, distributed, and difficult to separate from the weather. Sometimes, after a deep note has passed through the terraces, all the little sounds fall quiet together. In that silence, the mountain seems to be considering what it has heard.

Then a drip strikes stone. Somewhere far away, a second drip answers.

The Choir does not sing on purpose. But it is not empty.
