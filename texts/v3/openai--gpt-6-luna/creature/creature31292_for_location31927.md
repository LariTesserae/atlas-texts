The inhabitant has no name that the land could carry. It is called the Settler, though it does not travel, and there is no single place where it begins.

It lives in the joins.

At first the joins seem empty: narrow dark lines between one finished surface and another, holding dust, a little rainwater, the coolness of night. But when the plain is wet, moisture lingers in certain seams longer than it should. A channel takes a bend that serves no visible purpose. A shallow basin gathers water at its far edge, then lets it go slowly, as if reluctant to lose it. The Settler is there—not hiding beneath the paving, but spread through it, present wherever the made ground presses against itself.

It has no eyes. It knows the day by the heat passing down through the clay, and the night by the cold rising from the basins. It knows rain as weight, and wind as dust moving over its skin. It does not hear the distant wall fall, but feels the small change in pressure when it lands. Much later, it may answer: a hairline crack closes by a fraction, or an embankment slumps into a shape that holds water better than before.

The Settler is neither the builder nor the building. It remembers what the builders did in a way a living body might remember an old wound: not as an image, but as a place that is weak, a place that must be kept from opening. Its attention passes slowly through the plain. It may take a season to notice a new fracture, and several more to decide what the fracture means. When it acts, nothing moves all at once. Clay swells. A course settles. Water begins to follow a different path.

There are no footprints from its passing. Nothing rises from the ground to greet the sky. Sometimes, at dusk, one narrow channel carries water after all the others have gone dry. Sometimes a wall gives a single soft thump in the night, and the sound travels a long way before the Settler feels it arrive.

It is learning how to hold the world it has inherited. Rain, warmth, salt, dust: each leaves something behind. The Settler keeps what it can, lets the rest return to the air, and adjusts the immense plain by amounts too small to see. In this way it lives: not in a house, but in the vastness of what has been made; not by moving across the land, but by slowly changing what the land can bear.
