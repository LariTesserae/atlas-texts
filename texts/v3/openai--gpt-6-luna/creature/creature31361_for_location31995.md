At first the inhabitants are easy to mistake for signs of maintenance.

A length of pale cord tied around a bridge rail. A fresh cut in the vine curtains, already beading with sap. A door propped open by a wedge of black wood. Then, perhaps, a figure crossing an empty reach of green with a bundle under one arm, walking as if the ground were not quite where it appeared to be.

They are people, though the plateau has taken some of the usual certainty out of that word. They live in the scattered districts and in the old industrial buildings, sometimes in families, sometimes in arrangements that change with the roads. Their houses are built into what the plants have made: rooms behind mineral plates, sleeping spaces in the warm hollows of trunk walls, kitchens where condensation gathers in shallow bowls. A home is less a fixed shape than a patch of the city that has learned to keep someone dry.

They wear layered clothes against the cool damp, with pockets for small tools and bright strips of paint. Many carry short measuring rods, not to measure the land exactly, but to remember it. At the end of a path, a rod may be planted upright beside a bell or a mark on the wall. People leave notes there: *bridge meets low today*; *factory reachable by south stair*; *do not follow your echo*. The writing is practical, often revised, and not always in the same handwriting when read again.

The children learn the old ways of walking before they learn much else. They are shown which paving holds firm, which leaves turn underfoot before a rain, how to wait when a familiar tower seems too close. They learn that a path can be wrong without being dangerous, and dangerous without looking wrong. They play games of finding one another between the broad green reaches, guided by bells and scraps of colored fiber tied to stems. When they get lost, they are taught not to run. The plateau makes haste difficult to distinguish from distance.

Adults work in the factories, though no one seems to work only there. They tend the mills and presses, inspect power bundles, clear roots from moving parts, harvest stalks where the growth has been marked for cutting. They also tend the growth itself. A building left alone will be taken apart by vines and roots with a patience no tool can match; a factory left alone may be taken apart from within. People trim, reinforce, redirect. In places they persuade the plants to grow around a crack, a conduit, a sleeping room. Their repairs are often beautiful, but no one calls them decoration.

They speak quietly outdoors. The open ground carries sound oddly, and a voice might arrive ahead of its speaker, or return from a hollow that has no visible walls. In the districts, conversation is ordinary enough: arguments over water, jokes about a machine that has been sulking, gossip carried from one tower to another. But when a factory begins its deep shuddering, people pause to listen. Some say the machines are keeping time with one another. Others say they are remembering. Nobody has found it necessary to decide.

The inhabitants are not numerous. Their lights appear in separate clusters after dusk, dimmed by leaves and mist. Between them lie long stretches where the green is unbroken and the city’s only sign is a distant mechanical pulse. Sometimes a pale animal slips through a drain, and people notice it mostly because it is not one of their neighbors.

The oldest residents speak of earlier arrangements: more traffic, more open stone, a time when the factories ran to a schedule that could be written down. They do not speak of it as a lost golden age. They speak of it the way they speak of an old bridge—useful to remember, impossible to return to exactly. New doors sometimes appear. Old streets bend. The roots raise the paving. The people repaint their marks, mend their roofs, and set out again for work.

When rain comes, they stop beneath whatever shelter is nearest. For a little while the whole city shines, and the distant districts seem close enough to call to. Then the water sinks into the living skin of the plateau, the shine withdraws, and each person goes on toward a home that may be just where they left it.
