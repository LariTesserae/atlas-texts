The place is a hollow beneath the ground, no larger than a few streets laid end to end. Its ceiling is lost in a bright haze—not darkness, but a high, milky radiance that seems to come from the stone itself. The light has no visible source. It gathers on wet surfaces and breaks into pale bands where water runs down the walls.

The floor lies shallow under warm water. Here and there, dark shelves of rock rise above it, rounded smooth by ages of flow. Narrow channels cross the basin and meet again, pouring through cracks too small to account for their noise. Water falls, surges, and withdraws in restless cycles, making the whole hollow sound like a distant engine heard through a wall. At times a jet lifts briefly from a pool and hangs there, a twisting column trembling in the air before collapsing back without a splash.

The stone is old and settled into its shapes. Its surfaces are polished in the paths of regular currents; its edges have softened, its hollows deepened. But the water is never still. It churns in one basin while, a few paces away, a veil of droplets hangs almost motionless above a dry ledge. The turbulence has its own boundaries, as if the place has learned where it is permitted to be wild.

Vegetation is scarce. A few pale, fingerlike growths cling to cracks near the waterline, translucent as softened glass. They open and close very slowly, not quite responding to light or motion. In the deeper pools, minute flecks drift and vanish, too small to tell whether they are living things or debris. Nothing here crowds anything else. Long stretches of bare stone separate each patch of growth, each pool, each built object.

The objects are few, and none quite resemble ordinary machinery. A narrow frame of dark metal stands on a dry shelf, its four legs sunk into the rock. Threads of light pass through its empty center and bend as the water roars around it. Farther in, a small round structure has been set into the wall, flush with the stone: a sealed door, or perhaps an instrument, marked by three shallow grooves. Every so often the grooves fill with light, and the nearest current changes direction.

There are no obvious controls, cables, or moving parts. The constructions seem to work by persuading the place rather than commanding it. Water avoids certain stones. Droplets gather in clean geometric lines. A low vibration travels through the floor, and the entire basin answers with a brief rearrangement of its flows. Whatever was built here has been present long enough to acquire the same worn, patient quality as the rock. The system is not new, and it is not dormant. It is simply doing something whose purpose no longer needs to be announced.

The air is warm and heavy. Moisture beads on the ceiling and falls at irregular intervals, each drop bright for an instant before it strikes the water. The sound is constant and loud, but not monotonous: a broad rushing beneath sharper ticks, hollow knocks, and the occasional deep note that seems to come from inside the stone. Voices would carry poorly here. A whisper might travel across the pools; a shout might be swallowed at arm’s length.

Nothing suggests a passage to the surface. Yet the hollow does not feel sealed away. Its bright ceiling, its ceaseless exchange of water, and the measured adjustments of its strange instruments give the impression of a small, complete climate—warm, wet, and turbulent, held in an ancient balance by forces that are still at work.
