The place is hidden inside a high ridge, sealed behind a split no wider than a hand. Outside, the mountain is all wind and pale stone. Inside, a person could kneel and touch both walls at once.

The hollow is warm. Its ceiling hangs low and close, crowded with blunt, unfinished mineral knobs, as if the rock were still deciding what shape to take. Nothing here has been smoothed by a long river or ground down by weather. Surfaces end in fresh planes and soft-edged seams; dark clay gathers in the joins. The stone looks newly made, though nothing in the chamber seems to be moving.

Water sits everywhere without quite becoming a stream. It films the walls, fills thumb-deep hollows in the floor, and beads along the undersides of the rock. A shallow pool occupies the center, black and perfectly still. Its edges are intricate: tiny ledges, trapped bubbles, hair-thin roots, bright mineral grains visible only when a hand passes close enough to disturb the water. The pool does not overflow. The wetness seems to belong to the hollow rather than arrive from anywhere.

There is no light. The chamber has no glow of its own, and even the entrance admits only a brief change in the feel of the air, not a beam. Its geography must be learned by touch: a low shelf on the left, a narrow ridge of stone beneath the pool, a wall where the damp clay is warmer than the rock around it. Sound is equally close. A sleeve brushing a wall is loud. A breath returns from the ceiling almost at once. Now and then, a single drop gathers, but it does not fall; it remains swollen on the stone, held there by some patience of surface and warmth.

Vegetation is scarce: a few pale threads rooted in the clay, fine as old linen, with no leaves to speak of. They thread between grains of stone and vanish under the water. There are no visible animals. Perhaps small things live in the mud, but the chamber gives no sign of them except for a faint stippling in one patch of silt, too regular to be a crack and too still to be a track.

Someone has been here. On a dry ledge sits a shallow bowl, shaped from dark stone with hand tools. Beside it is a small arrangement of flat chips, perhaps a mark, perhaps only the convenient place where the chips were set down. No fire remains. No wall has been built, no path cut; the chamber is too small to need much making. The few human traces feel less like ownership than a way of asking the darkness to remember a visitor.

The hollow holds its conditions with unusual steadiness. Water does not run; stone does not crumble; the thin roots do not spread. A bead may tremble when touched, and the pool may briefly wrinkle, but soon every surface is still again. It is not a finished cavern, nor a place visibly forming. It is a small pause inside the mountain: warm, saturated, densely folded, and almost perfectly quiet.
