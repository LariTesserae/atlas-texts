The basin is small enough for its far rim to seem close, though the stone has been wearing down for a very long time. Its floor lies almost level, a shallow dish of dark mineral grit bounded by low shelves of basalt and pale, brittle crust. Nothing rises far above the ankle except a few blunt knobs of rock. The basin has no channel leading out and no streambed leading in. There is no water here: not a pool, not a damp seam beneath the crust. The pale deposits are the residue of a much older wetness, ground fine and left behind.

Darkness softens the distances. The rim is a smudge against a sky scarcely darker than the land; the nearest stones show only their edges, while hollows disappear a few paces away. Given time, the eye finds a muted glimmer in the mineral crust—not light of its own, only the faint return of whatever dimness is above. The air is warm and still. Heat rises gently through the floor, held by the stone as if the basin has been storing the day for ages.

Erosion has made everything rounded and unhurried. Rock shelves slope into the grit without sharp breaks. Wind has combed the floor into shallow, almost erased lines, then left them alone. A thin scatter of pebbles gathers at the base of each shelf; larger stones sit where they fell, softened at the corners. Nothing suggests a recent upheaval. The place has arrived at its shape and stays there.

Life is sparse. Here and there, low mats of dark, dry growth cling to cracks, their edges curled tight against the rock. A few stiff stalks stand apart from them, pale and leafless, drawing what little sustenance they can from mineral salts and the warmth below. There are no tracks between them, no burrows, nests, or calls. The plants make no movement that can be seen. Their scattered presence makes the empty intervals feel deliberate.

Sound is scarce. A faint sift of grit may pass across the floor, or a stone may settle with a small click somewhere beyond sight. Otherwise the basin is quiet—not the waiting quiet before something arrives, but the quiet of a place with very little to disturb. Even the wind seems to lose its force at the rim.

There are no paths or marks of habitation. No tools, walls, or worked stones break the natural arrangement. The basin asks for no particular way of crossing it: one can walk from shelf to shelf, or sit among the low rocks and let the darkness gather. Its few rules are plain. Keep to the firm crust. Do not expect water. Wait long enough and the warmth will become familiar, while the landscape remains much as it was.
