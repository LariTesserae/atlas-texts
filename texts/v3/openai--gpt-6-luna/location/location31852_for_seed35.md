The chamber lies beneath a shallow skin of stone, close enough to the surface that the ceiling still carries the pressure of the world above it. It is not a cavern so much as a pocket where the ground has opened and stopped: a single, compact room of amber-dark mineral, perhaps the size of a small hall. Its walls are sharp and young-looking, crowded with fresh fractures and glassy ribs. Nothing has had long enough to soften them.

There is no water. The low places hold fine pale powder, sifted from the walls, and the seams glisten with a waxy mineral that warms the air without melting. The warmth is steady and close, like the inside of a machine that has been running for years. Dust hangs in it. A dim orange light seeps through the stone itself, strongest along cracks and thin edges; there are no lamps, though a few old industrial fittings have been bolted into the walls as if someone once tried to make the glow obey a switch.

The chamber is densely occupied. Small creatures pack the ledges, the roof, and the narrow spaces between mineral blades. Some resemble pale beetles, with dark, reflective backs; others are soft-bodied and almost transparent, their internal threads faintly lit. They do not scatter from one another. They pass in close, interlocking routes, climbing over backs, slipping beneath bodies, joining and separating without collision. A few hang motionless from the ceiling, antennae trembling to the constant vibration in the stone. The colony is abundant, but not frantic. Each animal seems to know exactly how much room the others will leave.

Sparse growth takes hold wherever the mineral surface is porous: little fans of black filaments, stiff and rootless-looking, with coppery tips. They are not quite plants. They draw something from the warm seams and give off a dry, sweet odor, like dust heated in a workshop. Their patches are small, though the creatures gather thickly around them.

The sound is immense for such a small place. A low engine-note fills the chamber, joined by a steady clattering, a chorus of clicking jaws, and the thin metallic ringing of something vibrating deep in the wall. The noises overlap until they seem to have a texture. Near the floor, the air thrums against the teeth. The scattered machinery contributes its own rhythms: a belt passing over a wheel, a piston striking, a fan turning with a dry scrape. None of it appears to be powered by anything visible. The machines are squat, rustless, and half-swallowed by mineral growth—one press, a few pipes, a little bank of stamped metal housings. Their purpose is difficult to guess. They work without producing anything.

Nothing here changes in the ordinary sense. The creatures move, but their routes return them to the same cracks and ledges. A fan turns, halts for the span of a breath, and turns again; a loose plate shivers in precisely the same sequence. Dust lifts from the floor and settles into the same shallow drifts. Even the bright seams do not widen or fade. Time has not stopped: there is movement everywhere. Yet every movement closes its own loop, leaving the chamber exactly as it was.

At the center is a low mound of dark mineral, surrounded by the deepest concentration of life. Creatures crowd its sides, stacked in delicate layers. From its crown rises a narrow, jagged fin, too regular to be a natural fracture and too crude to be made by careful hands. It hums with the same note as the walls. The animals turn toward it together, though nothing signals them; for a moment their bodies make a single shifting pattern, then they resume their private circuits.

The place feels less abandoned than finished. Whatever happened here has reached a stable arrangement: stone, machine, and animal sharing one small, warm enclosure. The chamber does not grow, decay, or ask to be understood. It keeps its noise. It keeps its inhabitants. It keeps, with perfect precision, happening.
