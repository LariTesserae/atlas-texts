The place is a high, warm hollow no wider than a few minutes’ walk, cupped in a shelf of black stone. Its rim is old and smooth, worn to a dull, thumb-polished curve; the floor is not. There, the ground continually rearranges itself in little avalanches of pale grit. The hollow has no stream, seep, puddle, or damp soil. Even the deepest cracks are dry. Heat rises from them anyway.

The light is weak and coppery. A veil of mineral dust hangs above the rim, turning the sun into a blurred disk and making distances difficult to judge. Shadows pool under every leaf and ledge. At midday the hollow looks like evening; at evening it looks as though something has just gone out of sight.

It is unexpectedly lush. Clumps of tall, jointed growth crowd the sheltered places, each stalk armored in overlapping scales, each leaf narrow and leathery, colored bottle-green, bruised purple, or the yellow of old bone. They do not drink. They take in a faint, bitter vapor from the air and draw salts and metals out of the stone; their thick inner tissues hold the resulting chemistry in place. Some grow in fans close to the ground. Others rise in hollow columns, rattling softly as they swell and contract with changes in pressure. Their roots are more like nets of pale wire than roots, gripping the grit without seeking any moisture.

The plants are scattered rather than continuous. Between them lie broad plates of bare mineral, so smooth that the light slides across them, and patches of loose ash that shift underfoot. Here and there, dark knobs of stone push through the floor. They are old, rounded, and furred with a thin crust of orange lichen. Everything feels ancient in outline, as if the hollow settled into this shape long ago. But nothing in it stays still for long.

The wind enters over the rim in sudden, slanting bursts. It combs the grit into brief streams, bends the tall stalks nearly double, and sends loose leaves spinning in tight circles. The dust never quite clears. It makes a low, continuous roar, punctuated by the dry clatter of the plants and the sharp hiss of sand striking stone. When the wind drops, the silence lasts only a moment: the hollow is full of animal sound.

Small fauna are everywhere. Pale, many-legged grazers move among the stalks in quick, synchronized jerks, pausing together as if listening to a signal no one else can hear. Broad-winged things hang motionless in the dim above them, riding the warm updrafts. They have no visible feathers; their wings are translucent membranes veined with dark lines. From the stone knobs come bursts of clicking and churring, answering from one side of the hollow to the other. Something large occasionally thumps beneath the loose floor, sending a tremor through the plates. Nothing drinks, and none of the animals seem to miss water. They lick mineral dust from the rock, feed on the plants’ bitter husks, and take what they need from the hollow’s charged, dry air.

A few human traces cling to the rim and to one sheltered wall: a low ring of stones, a narrow windbreak woven from stalks, and three posts blackened by age. The posts are carved with shallow spirals that have been rubbed nearly smooth. There is no real settlement here, only the suggestion that someone once returned, or still returns, to collect the hollow’s brittle seed-cases. A chipped handstone lies half-buried nearby. It is the only tool anyone seems to have needed.

The strangest changes happen quickly and without warning. A patch of ground may rise in a slow blister, split with a crack, and spill out a cloud of warm, glittering grains. Within minutes, the grains settle into a new ridge. The plants bend away from it; the animals rush toward it. A stalk may flower all at once, opening a crown of pale, bell-shaped sacs that ring in the wind like glass. The sound draws the winged creatures down, and for a while the whole hollow seethes with movement. By nightfall, the sacs are gone, consumed or collapsed, and the stalk stands bare again.

Yet the place does not feel broken or newly made. Its upheavals have their own rhythm, and the old rim holds firm. The hollow is a small, balanced world of thirstless growth, mineral heat, dusty twilight, and noise: a sheltered pocket where every quiet surface is only waiting for the next gust, pulse, bloom, or sudden rush of life.
