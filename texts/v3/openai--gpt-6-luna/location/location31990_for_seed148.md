The district occupies a shallow bowl no higher than the surrounding ground. From a distance it looks almost empty: a few black uprights, a low roofline, long gaps of darkness. Then the eye adjusts, and the gaps turn out to be full of vegetation.

Plants have taken the place of nearly every material the city once used. They grow in the seams of paving, through the hollow frames of buildings, across the backs of signs and the ribs of unfinished bridges. Their stems are thin and pale, more like wires than trunks. In places they braid themselves into walls; elsewhere they hang in loose curtains that never quite touch the ground. Moss covers the machinery without hiding its shape. A rusted press stands inside a thicket of soft green blades, its jaws opening and closing at intervals too slow to hear.

The vegetation is abundant, but it does not make the place feel crowded. Most of it is narrow, vertical, and widely spaced. Between stalk and stalk there is an extravagant amount of air. Whole blocks are made from a few plant-columns and the memory of streets. You can see through several structures at once, though the view rarely agrees with itself: a stairwell appears behind a window, then seems to be outside; a lane continues beneath a building that has no upper floors. The district is small enough to cross in minutes, unless it decides that a particular corner is farther away.

The ground is not yet settled. It is a dark, fine-grained material—part soil, part soft stone, with the faintly wet shine of something newly exposed. It puckers around roots. Shallow puddles gather in depressions that were not there the day before, reflecting a dim sky even beneath roofs. Rain comes intermittently, a brief patter against metal and leaf, and leaves a smell of cold earth, sap, and old electricity. Water rarely runs anywhere. It beads on surfaces, hangs from wires, or disappears into the ground without making a sound.

The buildings are industrial in their arrangement, though not in their permanence. Low factories, narrow service halls, and squat housing blocks crowd the bowl’s edges, connected by pipes and conveyor lines that have been bent into trellises. A few machines still operate. Their motors turn with patient, uneven effort, pumping clear fluid through transparent tubes or pressing seeds into blank sheets of soil. No one appears to tend them. At night, which is most of the time here, their indicator lamps glow weakly behind leaves—red, amber, then a blue that seems to shine through solid objects.

There is no true horizon inside the district. Darkness gathers early under the roofs and between the plant-columns, while the open ground remains a dull gray. Shadows do not always fall away from things. Sometimes they cling to the object that cast them, outlining it in a second, slightly misplaced shape. A visitor may cast no shadow at all until passing beneath a certain arch, where it stretches ahead, long and thin, though there is no light behind them.

The fauna is more evident than the people. Small, pale animals move through the structures in sudden, exact routes: narrow-backed things that climb walls without gripping them, birds with folded wings that sit inside the open mouths of pipes, and soft-bodied creatures that graze on the green film covering the machinery. They are numerous, yet seldom gathered. A glimpse here, a clicking movement there; a flutter in the rafters followed by silence. Their calls are quiet and spare, almost indistinguishable from the ticking of cooling metal. Sometimes a large animal passes through the far end of a street and the whole district responds: plants lean aside, dormant motors start, puddles ripple toward it. By the time you reach the street, nothing remains but a clean track through the dust.

The place is always in the middle of becoming something. New shoots emerge from bare concrete; unformed lumps of stone lift beneath the paving, then stop. A bridge may acquire one more span overnight, though no one remembers the gap it was meant to cross. Pipes end in the air. Foundations descend into soft ground and return as roots several yards away. Nothing here is ruined exactly. Everything is unfinished, including the parts that look ancient.

Most of the time the district is quiet enough to hear moisture collecting in the leaves. Beneath that hush, its processes continue: a motor turning, a root splitting mortar, wings brushing the underside of a roof, water gathering in a hidden basin. The city does not seem abandoned so much as sparsely inhabited by the living, the mechanical, and the not-yet-real. Its empty spaces are not vacant. They are being held open for something.
