The place hangs in darkness like a bubble of sculpted night: a cavern so ancient the stone it once was has become only a dusty rumor, a memory of hardness long digested. What remains is the final exhale of a reef of living wood that, instead of rotting, decided to keep growing until the entire volume of the cavern became plant alone. No river ever came here; moisture clings only as an antique brightness, the faintest lick of vapour on glossy leaf-edges, too slight even to bead. One senses water merely by its absence—spirals of negative thirst between the stems.

Everything is plant, yet almost nothing is green. Centuries with no sun have bred black carapaces of leaf, hematite branches, vines like whale-baleen drawn into threads. Bole and vein and fibre fuse into buttressed walls, archways, vaults, flights of stairs that serve no one’s feet any longer. The city is tree turned city turned tree: avenues of vertical trunks, balconies of woody lattices, windows the thickness of a fingernail’s translucent lamina. All is ventilated by the slow breathing of the plant—it inhales the broken moon-breaths of the cavern and exhales a resinous warmth that smells faintly of iron and pine.  

Cool, but not cold. The temperature balances on the faint metabolic glow of lignin chemistry—just enough to keep sap migrating upward against impossible physics, like a clock that has learned to tick without gears. The air is thick with pollen motes that float like elderly dust. Each spore is the size of a teardrop crystal: heavy enough to settle, but given ease by the air’s near-vacancy. They land with gentle metallic pings on the empty boulevards, sounding like rain on a tin roof heard through a memory.

There are no animals. No footstep, no birdstartle, no gnaw of insect. But the place is loud with wood. Hear the deep groan of heartwood compressing under its own theology; the crack of a septum dividing a cell perhaps once a century; the whistle of capillaries drinking the last gastronomy of micron-damp air. It is the creak of antique timbers in a cathedral long buried, except the timbers are both nave and nave-wright, architect and architecture grown seamless. Every echo is a micro-dialect of timber tongue, rising through octaves too low for human ears, felt instead as shifting in the marrow.  

Scale is perverse. The city fits into a pocket of mountain root no larger than a small church, yet within its dark rooms infinity tessellates. Corridors shrink to knotholes, then widen to forests of carpeted roots. The traveler walks for an hour and returns to the same lintel without ever turning around; distance behaves like origami dampened by this breathing wood. One could crawl through a gloaming arch and meet oneself climbing another staircase that descends into the same root-cell from the opposite side of the blackest hour.  

Hand-tools forged of the plant itself—obsidian leaves honed to razor, lignin braces stitched with hair-fine roots—lie abandoned on benches of heartwood that have already grown around and through them, incorporating their offering without protest. Every surface keeps the marks of tools once used: chisels sunk into grain and forgotten; gouges left mid-stroke; hammerheads ingested by bark until only dents in the softer xylem remain. Technology here never advanced farther than the lever and the knife, and why would it? The organism supplies every need: pressure vessels that transpire edible ethers, hollow chambers whose walls glow faintly with luciferin, pleated bellow-vents that sigh to ferry heat between towers like slow blood.

Time moves as a glacier recedes: change measurable only in the slight thickening of one ring per long-century. Color fades by fractions of nanometers: black slightly more black, gloss slightly deeper gloss. The cavern is almost finished with change; its next aspiration is the stillpoint after the last resin has cured forever. Occasionally a city-block of root will autocorrect its tension with a snap loud as a gun gunshot, then silence drowns again. Dust follows dust. Resin weeps and cools into renewed amber. The only law remaining is the inclination of wood to keep reaching: upward through no light, outward through no space, inward through no center. In doing so it rewrites geography into biography, biography into eternity recognized only as a faint warmth under the palm.

Stand in the middle. Plant your hand on the nearest buttress. Feel the low thrall of lignin singing down the the subtle warp of what the space remembers. You have entered a metropolis that ended before it had anyone to name it; it grew its civic plan to perfection the moment life had no further use for it; every home is root-house, every plaza a cavity transfigured by woody overgrowth. Now it is architecture become mineral, become organism again, become something stranger still. And yet it welcomes, though no soul is left to offer the welcome. The sound rises: not urgent, merely present, the way waves speak without insistence.

Breathe the dark resin air. Breathe again. You are inside the inhalation of an age that has forgotten light but refuses to die.
