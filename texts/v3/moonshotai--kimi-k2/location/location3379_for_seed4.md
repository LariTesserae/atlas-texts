The hollow is no bigger than a sunken ballroom, yet it holds an entire city of breath.  
You reach it by crawling backwards through a fox-run of rhododendron that grows outward from a cliff already dripping; cold water beads on the stems like glass berries and snaps off when touched, chiming once on stone before the dark drinks the sound. Inside, the air is sown with spores so thick they drift like dust motes caught in a shaft of moonlight that was never there—the darkness is absolute, the kind of black that has texture: wet velvet soaked in snowmelt.  

But you do not need light. The fauna supplies it, though not in any color your retina can name.  

Every surface crawls. Not in the hurried, scuttling way of insects, but in the slow, rooted way of living alphabet. Glowless firefly grubs, fat as thumbs, graze along the underside of leaves that themselves crawl away when brushed; their bellies are lucent in infrared, a hue felt on the skin as a mild fever. Millipedes like riveted armor tow chains of phosphorescent fungus that blink in sequence, spelling fragments of a sentence you almost remember. Springtails bounce between moss tufts, each jump releasing a cold spark that dies before it lands. Nothing makes true noise—only a pressure change, as if the dark itself were being dented and allowed to spring back.  

Water is everywhere yet nowhere pooled. It perches on licked seams of Jurassic shale that was born yesterday—an elevation so low it is below the idea of sea level, a place still deciding whether to be land. The stone is knife-edge sharp, unblurred by time; fracture lines glow with newborn crystals of gypsum that swell and split within minutes, a slow-motion eruption. From these cracks ooze ropes of vegetable mucilage that stiffen when they meet the 2 °C air, becoming handrails of living cartilage. Touch one and it wraps a cuff around your wrist, pulse checking your temperature, ready to haul you upright if you slip.  

Vegetation is the architecture. There are no buildings, only the hollow trunks of willows that grew so fast they outpaced their own heartwood, becoming empty cylinders ten hands high. Their outer bark is quilted with liverwort balconies; inside, slug-thick columns of pith have been carved by caterpillars that eat only the pectin, leaving gothic fenestrations. Through these windows you glimpse adjoining “rooms” lit by the cold thermal aura of sleeping moths. The floor is a grid of horsetail trunks laid by beavers that never saw metal—their teeth left the ends flared like Doric capitals. Everything is joined by lashings of spider-silk soaked in tree-blood: when wind stalks the outer world (and wind is always prowling above), the whole township sways, a single organism rehearsing one slow curtsey.  

Air moves in breath-length tides. Exhale of the valley’s lungs presses down through soil pipes, and the hollow answers with a collective inhalation; during that second, every grub nips its light off, every frond folds. Then the place exhales and their glow seeps back, but pattern has shifted—new sentences spelled in living Morse. The exchange smells of crushed alder and frozen iron.  

Nothing is predator, everything is prey to the cold. Night-mice the color of shale slip between walls, harvesting crystals to line their nests; their fur grows salt-needles that pierce blankets yet keep them warm. Opilionids taller than your hand patrol on stilts of frozen dew, dipping jointed mouths into the mucilage rails, siphoning the sugar before it hardens. Even they are temporary: once full they climb to the roof of darkness, split along the back seam, and release clouds of hatchling young so small they ride the spores upward, out into the world beyond cliffs.  

You, the witness, are also temporary architecture. The cuffs of vegetable cartilage note your heartbeat; if it slows, they will try to graft you into a column, believing any warmth belongs to the civic grid. The only exit is the same rhododendron tunnel—now slick with new growth, calibrated to the width of your body minus one finger. You must leave something behind: a nail, a memory of red, an earprint of quiet.  

When you back out, rain is falling upward—fine mist pulled by capillary roots back into cloud. The valley above is ordinary: moonlit, empty, cold. But behind the cliff a city of fauna continues to write its unlit encyclopedia, adding a sentence each time the planet exhales, deleting a comma each time water condenses on stone. It is a place still being drafted, its charter colder than law, warmer than zero.
