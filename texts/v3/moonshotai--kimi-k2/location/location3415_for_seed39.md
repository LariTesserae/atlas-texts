They call it the Stillworks, though nothing here is still.  

Stand at the lip of the abyss and you will understand the name: everything is machinery, but the levers turn only once every thousand years, and the pistons sigh instead of strike.  Below you, the world is being born backward.  A naked alpine ridge—knife-edge, two thousand metres of naked gneiss—saws the sky in half, yet the rock is so new it still remembers the melt from which it cooled.  Frost crystals hang in the air like paused explosions, never quite falling.  They glint with a sound too high for ears: a glass harmonica played by the planet to itself.

Water is here, but it has not decided what it wants to be.  It beads on every facet of stone, held by capillary awe.  Trickles race down vertical faces only to lose heart and climb back upward, reversing film-reel.  Where the ridge terraces, the water gathers into mercury-silver lenses that hover a finger-width above their own reflections, never touching ground.  Every hour one lens grows heavy enough to fall; the splash is a single syllable of wet thunder that rolls across the range and is swallowed by the hush.  Then the cycle begins again.  The count is never two.

Vegetation keeps its distance, embarrassed by its own scantiness.  Between boulders you will find one grey-green lobe of lichen, coin-sized, older than the mountain it clings to.  A dwarf willow, two leaves and a stem, trembles like a rumor.  Nothing roots deeply; the substrate is still being argued over by geology.  When the wind wants to erase even these gestures it can: the plants agree to flatten into two dimensions and lie colourless until the threat passes.  In this way they survive the cold that is not quite cold—air sits at the freezing of breath, a half-degree window where exhalations become weightless pellets of ice that orbit your hood like gnats made of mirror.

Elevation is the only thing here that has finished its sentence.  The ridge is the highest vertebra of a broken ringwall surrounding a hole you cannot see bottom of.  Across the void other summits repeat, perfectly spaced, as though a cosmic gear-tooth cutter had walked a slow lathe around a blank place in the world.  The circle is six kilometres across; if you shout the echo returns twelve seconds later, wearing your own voice but with the consonants filed off.  

There is no soil yet, so erosion works forward and backward at once.  grains are released, ride the wind ten centimetres, then leap upslope again, reassembling the parent rock in microscopic layers.  The range is knitting itself faster than it can be unmade.  Someday it will be impregnable; for now the surface flakes away like birch bark, each sheet thin enough to read light through.

The emptiness is not loneliness but protocol.  The density of objects is one pebble per twenty cubic metres of sky.  Yet glance sideways and you catch brown motion: a herd—if “herd” can name thirty bodies spaced half a kilometre apart—of chamois-like creatures whose bones are strut-work brass.  Their knees vent soft steam that smells of coal smoke and lilac.  They move by releasing pawls; when they stop the clicking lingers, a clockshop clearing its throat.  Predators exist, but the contract here is politic: prey stop and wait to be seen, predators pause to be admired, and both parties continue altered by the exchange but not diminished.  Death is still legal, yet the paperwork is so severe no one bothers except as performance art on equinoxes.

Industrialisation arrived without architects.  Halfway down the escarpment the ridge swells into a blister of stainless steel the size of a cathedral.  No bolts, no welds: the metal has crystallised directly from the stone, cubic molecules locking like benedictions.  From its vents issue slow exhalations of liquid air that pour, luminous navy, into the dim.  The flows turn turbines whose blades are frozen vortices of the same navy light—turbines that spin the idea of torque rather than torque itself.  You feel the electricity as a pressure behind the eyes, a migraine of potential.  There are no wires; power is broadcast as etiquette.  When an animal breaks the horizon it unconsciously bows its head, absorbing its share, then walks lighter, bones briefly humming middle C.

The sky is a low tin lid someone forgot to paint.  Light reaches 1.2 on whatever scale measures the patience of photons.  The sun is not absent, only hesitant, a dusk blear that circles the rim every twenty-three hours without ever truly rising.  Colours exist as rumours you remember from childhood.  Snow would be a comfort, but snow has not been invented; instead, cold is a texture, suede dragged across teeth.

Sound wanders looking for employment.  Mostly it files reports of silence, but every so often the ridge clicks—one sharp note like a dropped marble—and all the hovering water lenses answer in descant, a plink that travels outward in a flat ring you can watch vanish into the fog of your own breath.  Then quiet renegotiates its contract and the moment is archived.

Nothing here has been built and nothing has been left alone; those categories collapsed before they formed.  The place is a workshop for making world, but the labour is reverse-time, objects returning to imperative.  Walk carefully: your footprints begin filling before you lift the next boot, not erasing but unhappening, sole-shaped vacancies in causality.  If you stand still too long the ridge will try to recruit you.  First your shoelaces mineralise into silver wire, then your thoughts become blueprints for a canyon you have not yet imagined.  Visitors are advised to keep moving in a random walk, lest they be promoted into geography.

At the centre of the unseen amphitheatre lies the Sink, a perfectly circular patch of air that behaves like water.  Objects drift into it—loose stone, brass herbivore, shard of frozen breath—and orbit, slowing, until they achieve neutral buoyancy and hang, a constellation of almosts.  Periodically the Sink releases one token, firing it vertically upward at the ridge crest where it lands with the soft clap of an idea locking into place.  Geologists argue whether this is the planet’s way of editing itself or simply a heart beating.  Either way, the rhythm is steady, forty-one beats per day, and every creature sets its internal calendar to it.

Leave before your bones begin to ring; the exit is the same as the entrance but higher, as if you had been turned upside-down without disrespect.  Behind you the Stillworks resume their slow contrapuntal assembly.  Somewhere a turbine dreams of being a flower, a lichen signs a memorandum of understanding with the sun that will arrive tomorrow, and the ridge grows one angstrom taller, quietly proud of the mountain it has not yet decided to become.
