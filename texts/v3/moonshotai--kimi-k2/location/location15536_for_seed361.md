You arrive inside an echo.

It announces itself first as pressure: a warm palm on your sternum, humidity dense enough to feel like breath. Above you the ceiling sags under a vault that is somehow only nine meters high and at the same time light-years deep. Condensation falls upward here, tracing little glass beads out of the air only to relish their own weightlessness and rise again before touching stone. The acoustics are tuned so that every droplet clinks back into its invisible cradle with crystalline clarity, and the echo—large, generous, oddly maternal—keeps answering.

The stone itself has stopped pretending to be stone. It is more like woven night packed under eons of fatigue, so compressed by age that its pores have learned to drink light the way a tired person drinks silence. Here and there it bruises into mauves and violets, reminding you that color, too, can fossilize. A single narrow canyon path threads between two knuckles of this ancient mountain flesh, sloping downward but ending just there—because beyond the path nothing else exists; the world has folded in on itself and given this single warm room to its fever-dream.

Yet vegetation fights upward. Verdant clusters erupt wherever the avalanche-time has softened: velvet-black moss furred like bats, olive succulents wearing translucent windows so you can watch chlorophyll stream past in realtime, spindly orchida that flower backward—petals curling inward to guard an internal brum dropping stamen into itself endlessly. There are sharp-leafed things shaped like question marks. There are tongues that pulse their own capillaries in neon. All of them are sparse, respectfully spaced; their roots behave like guests rather than tenants, anchoring politely to the stonemother before lifting aside once the conversation is finished.

Between leaf and leaf exist the fauna pitched somewhere between overwhelming and claustrophobic. They don’t take up volume so much as density of meaning. Birds the size of commas hover, their wings blurred by nanosecond shudders, refracting daylight into a grammar of sparks. Dragon-things too thin to obey physics skate along the humidity gradients, folding wings every time they need to slip an extra dimension. Something like a peacock spider lip-syncs to the canyon reverb; when its throat sac inflates it reveals a pupil-star spiraling open, small enough to fit on your thumbnail yet containing two thousand languages no tongue you have can pronounce. Every creature is loud, but not as loud as their collective pause: a tinnitus made of predatory wanting. Somewhere listening, someone tall.

Scattered among the plants, barely visible until you crouch to plant a hand, lie the artifacts. Squares of polished copper no bigger than fingernails melt and re-set on contact with air, displaying fractal continents at revolutions no tectonic speeds reach. A bonelike cage, knuckled and porous, stands empty—but inside the cage the law of being collapses: grasshoppers in mid-jump freeze midair, turning into topographical maps, then into sound waves, then back into insects again. Their antennae wag hello. Not far away a thread of black glass curves to the ceiling like an embered harp string; pluck it and you hear last year’s rain. The systems here are beyond modern the way a child’s drawing is beyond accounting—the proportions wrong, the failures irrefutably alive.

This place runs on a single fold of regulation: whatever you call a boundary is asked politely to become a horizon. Walk the canyon until you reach the grotto. At its heart waits a pool no wider than two joined handspans, skinned in mirrorseed so thin the water underneath exhales into the reflection itself. It is warm; it is zero-point-eight of water possible, and it dreams of rivers you could step into sideways. Every dawn—time measured not by light, but by the slow contraction that shivers through the moss bed—the pool swells and sighs. It grows by one droplet. Somewhere in the data of that droplet is the delta of the entire planet, patiently undergoing its afterlife cycle. This growth is the heartbeat you had mistaken for echo.

The room changes if you rest long enough longer than you meant to. The fauna knits you into their vocabulary: the spider-spiral’s languages re-alphabet your pulse. The moss records your heartbeat like a footnote beside speckled lichen that spells you longhand in barely audible Morse. The artifacts begin to mill around, curious where you may have left your shadow—copper squares flitting on membranoid wings to measure its absence, bone-cage spider-walking to invite your fingers beneath its lattice and see what song happens next.

Exit is entirely simple: you sit beside the pool, let one rising bead brush your lips, and learn the taste of the room deciding not to keep you. Afterward you remain tattooed by what happened after you decided back.
