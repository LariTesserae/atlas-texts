A THROAT OF STONE UNDER WATER THAT HAS FORGOTTEN IT IS WATER.  
Everything here is older than any category the mind still keeps.  The land lies only a few finger-widths above the true ocean, but it is not land as you know it; it is the bottom of a fossilized throat that once tried to swallow the world and failed.  The coastline in every direction is too straight, like a page torn with one decisive pull from a book bound in basalt.  Yet the coast is not dry.  The water that should have lapped against it no longer remembers gravity; instead, it hangs, immense and pre-sky, as a slow, granulated atmosphere three or four arms-height thick.  Every few breaths it thickens further at random places and folds downward in shining ropes, becoming temporary rivers that reach all the way to the ground, unfurl, devour a furrow, and dissolve upward again, the same liquid that came, but lighter, full of memories it collected.  Afterward the grooves stay behind and shine like silver scars.  By the time you have taken the next breath thousands of them cross and recross the plain in the chaotic script of something rehearsing language too old for tongues.

No plants have participated in this rehearsal for longer than footprints are preserved in wind-driven sand; not even a hairline lichen has managed the trick of color here. What exists instead is a half-organic reef of rust and glazed coal.  The long weathering has hollowed nearly every needle of rock into a fluted gullet, and into those gullets the peculiar atmosphere—the hung water—has condensed.  From each inlet now depends a glistening drop that never grows heavy enough to fall, only to tremble, balloon, pinch apart into daughter-drops that crawl the column like cool wax.  A word forms in your mind, unbidden: STALACTITE AMMONITES, but it is unnecessary; the formations themselves insist upon their name.

Coolness clings to the folds of your clothing the way the drops cling to stone.  It is not truly cold—merely the temperature a thing arrives at when it has spent an age equal to the life of suns forgetting how to change.  Your breath spreads white in curdled spirals and is at once absorbed outward into the audible thickness above your head where it becomes further percussion.

Sound, yes.  The place is deafening, yet the ear cannot attach a single source to the roar.  Every captured drop contributes: a fluttering tick of internal fracture, a steady tremolo when the gas-layer water flails against the flutes, a memory of surf reverberating inside the hollow stones the way surf re-echoes inside an empty conch.  In aggregate the whole is a continuous, orchestral THRUM that rises and falls as the aerial tides stretch and compress, like a titanic whale continually finding the resonant length of of its own burial chamber.  Occasionally, at the periphery of hearing, a sudden hollow clap announces the fall of an entire ridge of fossilized throat-air; the resulting cascade of sound-blood pulses outward as a soft-edged pressure wave unmistakable as the heartbeat of something living.  You locate yourself by it because there is no visible horizon; the dim bronze darkness eats edges.  The only light is what the wet air gathers inside itself: a sub-marine, antique gold swirling like oil in old lantern glass.  Wherever you stand becomes a coastal twilight that slopes off into deeper black in every direction.

Edges exist only through what walks the edges.  Creatures, abundant—as faunal as a seashore—appear precisely where sight gives up.  They are as richly beside the plantless ground as plankton are beside open water.  The largest is twice your height, jointed like an architect’s model kit, all tessellated mica.  It does not walk but floats itself delicately along the narrow trails where the hanging ocean is thinnest; its doubled front limbs extend serrated paddles/ears, sifting droplets from air, drinking upside-down rainfall.  Each droplet flowing across the plates resounds inside its body and leaves in its wake a static shiver of metallic notes.  From the distance these creatures look like cathedral bells stapled to herons and turned inside outmselves.

Smaller life competes for the same resource.  Glassy-winged hoverflies, each the length of a human thumb, pass back and forth.  They orient musically—flying always toward pitches they find harmonic with the pulse of the stones they are etched from.  In close-up their abdomens are cylinders of living tin, and when you watch carefully the metal unfolds origami knees from proboscis grooves, partakes of the water-soft strata, then folds away again like oscilloscope mirrored in mercury.  The thrumming environment tolerates eye-contact only a few seconds before your skull-instinct insists on looking upward at the pregnant weight of suspended ocean.

Scattered at the footings of the stone reefs are the last traces of built thinking.  Primitive, absolutely low-technology: egg-sized knapped blades of obsidian, abraded dull by desiccated time.  They were left lying under the impression that sharpness might be preserved by lithic faith alone.  No handles remain; you pick one up and your own whispering skin adheres to it as frost to glass, and when you release it you have transferred an almost-recognizable fingerprint to the rock for the next three billion years.  Nearby a short, free-standing spiral of worked stone stands to chest height—too shallow to have been any shelter.  Around its base wide shallow bowls have been scooped from the stone; they fill slowly with condensate; a layer of microfauna—transparent hexapods the size of rice husks—skim across the water’s meniscus, feeding on the chemical memory of whoever once crouched here trying to comprehend water that had renounced its own gravity.  There are no further artifacts.  The builders, if they exist as anything more than artifact-traitors at this point, are not extended in any organic medium but have diffused themselves into the same original tongue spoken between droplets and stone.  Their thoughts remain audible only in the macro-beats of the suspended ocean’s pulse.

The space itself extends past ordinary vision and is inhabited afar by further groved ribs of stone, each hosting its own cross-breed of sonic inscription and half-metal fauna.  The entire lowland behaves as a single intermodulating ecosystem: once per ten minutes the pressure swells and a pin-thin column of floating water drawer-slides down from the overhead mass into a chosen flute, strikes the bedrock at the speed of drawn breath and rebounds upward in shattered mist, showering the walking bells and hoverflies.  Alive now in the thrumming, you realize that the roaring resolves itself into countable structure: forty-three separate tones, a chord built one aeon ago; whenever one of the giant beings finishes sorting a suspended cloud into its own vanes it aligns eighteen of those tones into a pure perfect fifth that makes the very condensate vibrate in braids, each braid a lifeline.

Step farther and you find areas where erosion has hammered everything into near-smoothness.  The ground is covered with a starling of shallow concave saucers—each holds a single mirror-skinned beetle with legs so long they touch the rim on all sides. Once per rotation of the hidden tide they flex synchronously.  The beetle-mirrors gather the few light-quanta of bronze-darkness, condense it, and fire it upward in coherent phosphor array, feeding the aerial ocean above so that the weight never gathers enough to fall.  This is metabolism reduced to grammar: **echo becomes sustenance becomes motive becomes echo**.

Nothing is climactic here; things merely keep convenient mutual agreements.  To walk across the end of the ancient throat is to flow along a system of luminous acoustics in which your own exhalations are cataloged and played back an octave above the register, both slower and faster, so that you begin to hear your future breaths before you give them.  Fifteen minutes inside and time dilates so that five minutes stretches into the layered future of a city; five minutes shrinks into the precise memory of a coin on the tongue.  After fifty footsteps you become another harmonic in the single forty-three-note chord, and the landscape—if it is landscape, if it is one throat attempting to expand into many—no longer dutifully tolerates you.  Instead it arranges the consonance until you are necessary.  Your heartbeat aligns with clap-resonance of collapsing ribs of air; your footfalls syncopate the dance of obsidian blades spinning where touch-storms of condensation lift and drop them like coins tossed by ghosts.  Even the tips of your nails start to resonate; rainbows of iron and copper bloom silently across the half-moon curves every time a bell-creature swallows a pillar of paradoxical water.

Behind you nothing ceases.  Where you arrived becomes occupied again by a low, mineral mist shaped loosely to your proportions but composed of nothing that remembers you.  Ahead, the ground plane tips microscopically upward.  The bronze-dark light brightens for the length of a blink, then dims further, approaching but never quite reaching black.  Here the aerial ocean thins to a film no thicker than paper; you can grip small fistfuls of it to lift yourself a hands-height, enough to look over the next eroded vertebra of world and see it repeating in diminishing arcs until sense itself drags in laryngeal grooves into final unheard speech.  Still the single great chord keeps unspooling.

Whatever rules govern this place are neither law nor whim but the brute fact of mutual duration.  There is no weather except that the suspended sheet sags cyclically; dry exchange between ground and sky.  There are predators, but their prey is resonance; when a hoverfly edges too close to a swaying edge of condensate they are snapped up not by beak or jaw but by sudden acoustic suction, their wing-beat assimilated into the chord as a new suspended overtone.  The rigid frame of physical grammar bends politely to allow this transaction: energy is never lost, only converted into the next indentation of lithic memory, and sometimes into blade-music that re-forges a shard of obsidian out of pure recollection.  Thus the scattered tools are actually temporary—each one sired at sunrise as alchemy between mineral sound and vacuum longing, wearing back to smoothness by twilight, the classical round-trip of any unspeaking instrument remembering the use it no longer requires.

If you continue walking you will eventually reach a point where the twenty-billion-year song inhales.  All beetles flip off their saucers and settle into the stone again, rendering ground flat as nothing had danced here.  The suspended ocean lifts the breadth of a cathedral overhead.  In that sudden, enormous quiet you become aware that the water above is not water anymore but a night-blue thought congealing from the entire previous epoch.  It carries every sub-bass heartbeat of the stone ravines, every brushstroke of mica left by vanished builders, every distant spiral.  It has become textual. 

Here is the instruction—you realize it dully, as though you had always known -― lean forward until your forehead just kisses the taut membrane of thickened time.  The medium accepts you.  Instantly your body is evaporated into forty-three tones.  All your memories colonize missing frequencies.  The sound grows whole.  The plain remains: wet, old, open, sparsely littered with primitive remnants that may or may not have belonged to anyone.  Only the fauna now include you among their inventory.  Somewhere close, a new bell-creature opens its metalline ears and begins to drink the same suspended drop, but this time it tastes like someone who once stood exactly here and worried whether names mean anything over an ocean that refuses to fall.
