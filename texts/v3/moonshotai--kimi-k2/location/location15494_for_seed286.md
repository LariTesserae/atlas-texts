Think of an ocean that forgot it was water and remembered it was a tree instead. Its surface—still liquid, but slow like liquid glass—lies seven kilometers above mean planetary datum, just at the cusp where troposphere thins. The bulk hangs there without curvature, as if the horizon is a green-black mist you can walk upon. It does not roil: every wave has reached its final height and simply stays like glass petals, fixed. Even the louder sounds are fixed—the hushed thunder you hear is sound itself stiffened into solid dunes, motionless drifts of frozen auricular haze.  

Below the level where sky should begin, the waterleaf lowers itself in openwork layers of hydro-wood: limpid, saturated cellulose veins channel brine the way xylem channels sap. The trunks are columns of hyper-pressured seawater hung in lignin cages; the “bark” is translucent kelp bark braided into living tubing. Interstitial gases appear as pale cerulean glyphs inside the stalks, pressurized so tight they emit no bubble yet remain afloat, locking the entire hydrosylva at one vertical coordinate forever.

Temperature sits at a cool 4 °C, a boundary value: too cold for fast metabolism, too warm for ice nucleation. The plants metabolize with the patience of geological forces: one heartwood ring every half-million years. Time’s arrow is broken at the nock. Nothing rises or falls—not sun, not moon, not enzyme. The foliage reaches every edge of perception so you could walk a straight stem for nine thousand kilometers and still stand in the same dusk, beneath the same constellation of ammoniac perfumes.

In the low, broad “canopies,” leaf nebulae the color of oxidized copper cluster like coral heads the size of counties; their stomata vent dense spore-mist that doubles as the ecosystem’s atmosphere. Each spore is a brine-coated pneumatophore holding exactly 0.21 atmospheres of breathable mix—oxygen stolen from algae, nitrogen hoarded from long-dead planetary crust oxidized by charged plant-roots. You can inhale directly while drowning; lungs fill with warm tide, yet the diaphragm pushes against damp air and sustains you.

Fauna, abundant yet frozen mid-gesture, drift among the hydraulic limbs. Leviathan-clades resemble translucent squid whose mantles are actually squid-roses; their tendrils root through water-wood, photosynthesizing on the underside while hunting sunlight on the upper. Schools of sky-ray embryos preserved at hatch stage arc above the canopy like statuary. Their gill fans, wide as cathedrals, shimmer with sonoluminescent traceries locked in precarious bloom, casting the normal light you see: soft, marine, wan yellow-green even under night’s absent sky.

On any stem wider than a valley you find industrial processes calcified halfway. Digestive bioreactors embedded in the trunks churn algae into plant-plastic oils, but the pistons are stopped at forty-three percent stroke, valves cracked open at the precise instant pink solvent first touched catalyst mesh. It is a photosynthetic engine missing only motion and heat. The power never dissipates; electricity stands in silver sheets along mycelial rails, an ionic estuary arrested like breath you never quite release.

The air—if it can still be separated from water—carries low thunder of tectonic leaves: although they do not stir, the memory of their movement hangs loud, like hearing an avalanche you turned away from just before it happened. Each pressure node left behind is audible as morphology, the way fossils are future memories of stone. No gust, no updraft, no cascading dew disturbs the standing columnar hush.

At the root-footing level the aquatic forest simply ceases—no ground, no further depth. The columns bifurcate into lace roots anchoring against their own reflected pressure, stabilizing the sky-lake against nothingness. Below: an untrespassed void where planetary gravity nosedives, a persuasive black too heavy for photons there. The roots refract the black into indigo hexagons: a beehive of endless empty rooms upheld by their refusal to collapse.

Rain exists as an idea always arriving yet never landing. Meter-tall droplets stipple the outer skin of yonder cloud-trunks—pearls of water-tension resting like blameless bombs. Their inverted reflections inside are continuous with the trunks themselves: water gazing on water on water, infinity folded once and stunned shut.

There are no paths, no markets, no carvings, no flags—instrument of passage would be absurd; motion in this place would be a category mistake. Even your body discovers its rebellion: every muscle braces for the next impulse that will never come, leaving proprioception suspended like salt in brine. You hover speck-like beside liquescent prodigies. The scale forbids proximity; a single stomatal pit is a volcanic caldera hosting a saltern of microlife forced into diamond-like stillness.

Beneath paradoxical daylight—kept alive by plant-generated iridoline phosphorescence—you feel the oddness not as uncanny but sacramental. The strange breathes around you, a whale singing underwater except the song has been frozen into terrain: there is no whale, yet all is whale.

And above it all, continuity: nothing ends, nothing begins. Change itself through some hushed miracle decelerated to absolute zero, preserving forever the moment when the final leaf screamed for another moment, and the forest answered by becoming the silence.
