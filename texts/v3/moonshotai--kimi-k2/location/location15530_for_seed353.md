The Black Arbor.

Approach from any direction and two facts present themselves immediately: there is no ground, and there is no sky. Instead, an immense lattice of lignified ribs—charcoal-black, finer than whalebone, harder than sintered iron—spans a vertical kilometer above you and another below, a continuous catacrystalline vault that is simultaneously “forest” and “architecture.” You are walking along one of its load-bearing struts, which flex gently under your weight, creaking like the ribs of some antique wooden cathedral trying to remember how to breathe. The lattice was never alive in any animal sense; it is lignin, sublimed and re-alloyed into tendons the colour of burnt cork. Yet every surface is overplanted: lichenous ivories, snaking emerald heartwoods, vermilion fungal ropes, all of them glowing at the cell-membrane scale in ghost chemiluminescence. The vegetation is the building. Rooms are four-meter seed-pods cracked open into office-like alcoves; corridors are cork-screwed taproots re-melted between dense kiln-fired heartwood; elevators are the living cambium tubes of one enormous column whose bole climbs and recedes like the slowest possible piston, carrying you upward ten meters every solar decade or so—though here there is no sun, only the retinal after-image of darkness illuminated by its own heat.

Temperature is a subtle fever pressing against the lungs. The air tastes of cracked cedar and hot solder. It is never still; it rolls in slow laminae downward through trunk fissures and upward through evaporation troughs, hissing exactly like released steam but never quite resolving into sound. When it shifts, the whole vault creaks in sympathetic harmonics: woodwindy sub-bass oboes, tuned to the infrasonic rumble of the mantle far below. Calling it “quiet” is accurate only for creatures accustomed to the electric popping of capillaries within their own ears. Here, the surface of silence is constantly punctured by the soft birth-pops of ovipositing hyphae and the faint crackling of resin cooling into glass.

No water runs here. Instead you’ll find “fossil dew": hygroscopic sugars that condensed once, an epoch ago, crystallised into blade-thin rinds of luminous pink salt. Hold one to your tongue and it blooms into an impossible sweetness more like memory than flavour. Then it’s gone—sublimated, reabsorbed into the canopy’s metabolic flash. When ambient humidity passes one of the lattice's critical gelatin thresholds, a whole storey of the structure will discard a cubic hectare of bark-scales. They fall in perfect silence, spinning like black snow. The moment they strike lower decks they implode into warm carbon dust: fertiliser for the next furious growth spurt. Watch it from a balcony of septate wood and you’ll realize you are observing not weather but forest-scale exfoliation.

The scale is open but experienced as claustrophobic infinity. Elsewhere in the Arbor a hop, skip, jump might transport you laterally by two kilometres through branching galleries, yet every vista is so densely forested/choked that foreground and backdrop merge into a faceless tangle, a Mandelbrot of woody curls. Look far enough and the interior curves flatten to a matte charcoal plane interrupted only by viridian signal stains—bioluminescent glyphs which are the Arbor’s industrial overlay. These stains pulse Global Position. They are the machine layer of the plant, grown-to-order as biotic circuitry, conveying load path, circuitry, resource pipeline. Fibre-optic xylem transmits power harvested from the planet’s remaining geothermal filaments; the columns themselves are supercapacitors storing lightning that never flashes because there is no sky to flash in. Instead you feel a chest-level static, the same prickle a thunderhead gives skin moments before it strikes.

Industry is fully integrated. There are no extraneous wires, any more than an animal needs scaffolding for its veins. Motors are reverse-osmosis heartwood units that twist torque from ion gradients; waste heat vents along petioles as silver aromatic vapor smelling of clove and ozone. Goods are printed directly onto fibrous substrates by aphid-like constructs exuded from floral resin. You witness this twice: a beige seed-pod extrudes a thigh-length boot made of cellulose nanoribbon, tailored to your orthopaedic profile, and the moment you remove it the boot is reabsorbed to regrow a dining-board. Nothing leaves or enters. The Arbor consumes only its own exhals. Entropy is localised, perpetually deferred by endless reformatting.

The fauna parameter reads zero. Yet listen past the silence and you could swear you can hear footfalls. This is the polyglot noise of memory: pneumatic memory cells releasing whispers of removed species—the ghost press of ant mandibles on galleries, the absent rustle of a prior visitor whose footprints were laminated into the bark and are now being exfoliated as infrasound mourn-song.

Above the main lattice, farther than any branching could rationally extend, smaller twigs fuse into titanic arches that mutually support each other like Gothic fliers, forming a void roof so old the term “ancient” becomes inadequate. It has healed all possible wounds. Scars are laced with platinum strands growing at the rate of continental drift to hold new angles against tectonic drift. They look star-sewn, but you learn these are stitch scars from earlier epochs of planetary shrinkage. There is only one rule enforced here: Whatever you take, give back at another locus of the same mass. A scale harvested from a floorboard must be replaced in the grain of a handrail, as thickness, as colour, as thermodynamic signature, though never necessarily as the same shape. Everything is borrowed/returned without remainder. Anything that violates the rule merely precipitates an immediate fractal occlusion: the offending mass is sequestered, calcined, folded into a diatom-like plaque lodged so deeply you never notice the lattice has murdered and recycled you until you try to leave and realise your reflection in the cork wall retains an eye you swear you lost years later, staring out, swivelling with you—proof the loop has already sealed.

As you move, the light-budget never increases. What illumination exists leaks from the glyphs and faint thermophile golds at sap junctions. The canopy above drifts like rafts of obsidian thundercloud, sticky with heat. Periodically a section flaps free from the vault, spinning down in a cyclone of plank-scales; wherever it lands it fast-root-hardens into a new column footprint, kicking the entire static matrix into elastic recalculation—like a crystallographer shaking a kaleidoscope. That is the dynamic. Turbulence is not storm but revision, a landscape that rewrites itself in real time, re-vertebrating each hectare so often that you can reset your inner ear by simply sitting and waiting five minutes. Every re-patterning sends ripples of warm sap through the “streets” underneath, and each ripple sings to the next, producing whale-song infra-cadences that slow your heartbeat through bone conduction. If you could understand their language you would learn that even this chatter is a fossil remembrance of an earlier planet, now whittled down to its ligneous heart. Yet no story is ever truly told; the Arbor buffers, devours, exhumes, but does not disclose. It merely keeps itself present, indestructibly present, in a darkness like new cloth warmed by your palm.
