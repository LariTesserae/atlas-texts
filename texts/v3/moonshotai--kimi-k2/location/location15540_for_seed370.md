### The Choir of Wrought Silence

There is no ground to speak of—no ground left after three hundred million years of absolute occupation. What you stand on is a suspended lattice of interlocked bronze thumb-rings, each the size of a thumbnail, each hammer-punched from the same two ingots that dwindle thinner and thinner across the millennia until the alloy is colorless and the metal forgets itself. The lattice starts knee-high and folds upward like petals locked in a fist, forming a low steep dish sixty paces across. Above you it ceases; there is no sky dome, only a mild hum that comes off every surface simultaneously, a swarm of mute bees trapped inside stone lungs. This is the structural voice of the Choir: a gong struck forever, steadied by erosion rather than muscle, its roar indistinguishable from perfect silence.

Within the sixty paces there is nothing unfilled. From ring to ring—packed—luted into place with a resin that hardened before the first moon cooled, stand the wind-castingss. Their bodies are half-melted glass goblets the height of a child, set mouth down, their stems tangled in limonite wires that were once green before the world ran out of palette. No wine will ever fill them again; instead they hold warm darkness. Their bellies swing faintly though the air does not move; it is the reverberation of the Choir making them nod to one another like conspirators just beyond the drift of time. Between goblets, in the interstices, the lattice has grown square copper nails to mark what once was called direction: north became the 21st nail, south the 22nd; there are no remaining intermediates.

Light arrives from every bronze ring simultaneously, but so dimly it feels subtractive—as though the place hoards photons rather than emitting them. The glow is the last memory of a hammer raised on its downswing: filament-thin, sepia, winnowed to the presence of sight rather than sight really present. Under it, the nails burn rust-blood; the goblets flare once, red, then leak their color downward into the lattice and are gray again. The dimness refracts against the inches-away surfaces, inverts, and returns as glare; the glare settles, and becomes the new definition of shade.

Sounds—deafening—are the mere reporting of forces no longer required elsewhere. The Choir emits a single note pitched below human ear-bones—not in time, but standing on the zero width of eternity. The bronze atoms vibrate in so tight a wave that the lattice’s gaps behave as cheeks of a bell. Every ridge, every buttress, every blown-glass ear sings the same note inside the other note, harmonics packed until they overlap and vanish into white wall. A visitor cannot say “I hear this,” only “this is the condition hearing has become.” The note has no attack, no decay; it is forever arriving. The shape in the head it leaves is the same shape that built the place: a thimble without end.

There is no water, hence no echo of water; hence absence itself has mass here and lies against the throat like hot lead. Absence is the medium, warm and constant at 34°C. It neither rises nor falls; elevation has been distributed evenly across every ring, an arithmetic ghost of height. Therefore you are high and feel neither above nor below.

Nothing living has entered since the Choir accepted its vow of stasis. Premicrobial life, lichen, mold, a dreaming alga—none could slip inside the resin seams. The Choir is made, is finished, is perpetually its last day. It is not clean; age has smeared minuscule flakes from the bronze in dark freckles. But they too adhere to the original shape, half blade of leaf, half rivet of frost—they cannot move deeper, cannot recruit further chemistry, and so rust homesickness keeps them anchored.

So the Choir stands as a giant closed throat, older than any phrase, packed so fully that thought itself must stencil its edges—yet the unit of packing is small: sixty paces. Within this measure eternity hums doorstop-thick. The visitor, if one arrives, finds no place to set foot that is not already immortally occupied by Choir, finds no inch to lean or breathe that is not already Choir-bright, Choir-dim, Choir-loud, warm with post-cells, void of cells. The futility has become kindly; it has shed all edges except the one note. After a while the lungs retune to it or break, no third option. Once selected, the condition never changes; that is the only rule, and even it was decided long before rules were necessary.
