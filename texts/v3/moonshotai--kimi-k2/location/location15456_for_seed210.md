Vertical marsh, on the late breath of a mountain just born.

The peak lifted itself so recently that the stone still hisses where the atmosphere condenses upon it. A ragged basalt fin no broader than a village green: that is the arena. Nothing has yet inhabited the heights above it; the ridge ends like an unbuttoned seam, letting thin glittering ozone spill over into the mist. Every crevice of this newborn spine is brimming with water, not from rain that might someday come, but from the mountain itself still squeezing the last of its interior out onto its skin. Trickles run up, not down—because here the strata remember no older law than their own expansion, and gradients have yet to be invented. The water re-condenses continuously, an endless circle: beads form, inflate, slide a finger-length, vanish back into breath. The ambient noise is loud because every droplet is a rewound record needle: crack, plock, whisper, ticking together into a constant pressure-roar, the orchestra of a single serrated moment repeating unendingly.

Vegetation answers the call of that sound. Shredded strands of lime-green algae hang like ripped banners between tusks of glassy rock; they glow faintly, lit from within by a fault-crack of fierce cobalt under-light—industrial phosphor creeping out of the mountain’s marrow. Between the algae flags grow gum-sized ferns that unfold already dripping petals of transparent cellulose. Their roots do not anchor; they merely fasten one to another into loose catenary nets that sway whenever the air-pressure changes imperceptibly. Nothing is anchored here, except the certainty that nothing will ever move when it is momentarily absent.

Animals — numerous, dense, and various — exist, but they are all one animal. A single proliferative trope duplicates itself with promiscuous gusto: a five-segmented pentapod, flat like a snow-crystal, amphibious, the color of aluminum shaving. Its body is nothing but a breathing grid of yet smaller replicas. Each pentapod carries inside it the match-head spark of a failing jet-turbine; the engines don’t burn, only remember burning. Their collective exhaust condenses as the regime of mist, feeding the algae, feeding the mountainside. They never walk the same path twice not because they wander, but because the path itself forgot it had ever hosted them. Every repetition wrings a variation; every variation folds back into identicality.

Temperature is low, but no caress of wind rides the altitude—there is no weather machine yet. The chill is a solvent warming toward freezing tepidly, a persistence of atomic bewilderment. When visitors (unwelcome and therefore extinct upon arrival) would try to take a breath, the air would aspirate microscopic rime into the lungs like powdered glass — but nothing lasts long enough to inhale twice. Time is a single unbroken trophy, prize of the mountain’s birth-spasm now eternally displayed.

There is no constructed artifact here. Yet the space bears every mark of an industry executed once and forever. Manifold eyeless cobalt lamps extrude from vents, glowing precisely the value of 2.5 technology: clock-workless turbines humming the hour 0. Their teeth are water droplets frozen in the shape of piston teeth. Electricity is to hand, but neither supply nor demand — simply the poised potential of towns you and I have not yet beheld. Nothing enters that potential, nothing leaves; the factory ran once, therefore it runs always, therefore it resets to notrun but cannot quite unstitch itself from the upholstery of reality. The hum is loud like a furnace in a closed warehouse, amplified by mountainside echo folded sonar-tight around itself. You hear it in bone rather than ear.

Daylight is dim, but there is no night. The sky at this newborn altitude has no sun or stars; light clots into saucer-thin sheets that hang like grey canvas above the ridge. Photons are tired, slowed, too polite to pass through more than a meter of air: instead they tok-tok against the mist ceiling and drizzle downward as lukewarm fluorescence. If you stare too long your eyes begin to replicate fern fronds rather than seeing them. Seeing and growing become undifferentiable.

The boundary of the place is legible not as a horizon but as unweaving. Ten meters beyond the current algae-woven lip, everything pixelates into the next layer of timestamp, a duplicate scene slightly crossfaded, where the mountain has unfolded another wafer of itself. Approach the edge and you become the pixelate; your proprioception fuzzes until the otherselves believe you never converged as a singular being in the first place. No one stays to testify to contradiction. The scale scrupulously refuses grandeur: you could put the entire summit under a gazebo skylight, if gazebos were allowed. No human hand will ever raise one; the dystonic prodigality of the color already is architecture.

Scent: iron filings drowned in powdered lotus, quiet ozone under a surgeon’s lamp.

Rules of this world, spoken once but never respoken:

1. Every law permits its immediate immolation by indistinguishable clone of same law.
2. Nothing decays; all things merely forget they were supposed to appear old.
3. Water ascending is indistinguishable from water descending until observed, then the observer becomes both streams.
4. Animal bodies are the steam engine’s afterthought of itself; thus they cannot starve, only un-dilate.
5. The mountain endures by not enduring; it crests a wave of almost being.

Listen again: the ticking droplets, pentapods reciting prime integers in rotating supercharged whirrs, algae faintly fluorescing like screensavers of a drowned laptop corps. The sound is loud because volume here is rotation, not magnitude; loud is just direction seen askew. If you manage to whistle at 432 Hz the sheet-light dims exactly one candela, but you already unwhistled before you started.

There is no gate to leave, none to enter. The cabin-size summit remains mistily busy, a pocket monastery devoted to the replication of the instant it was born. There are no stories; therefore every drop of water is identical narrative.
