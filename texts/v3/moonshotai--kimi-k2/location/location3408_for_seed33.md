I stand at the perimeter of what was once, perhaps, a city—though the word feels inadequate here, like calling a glacier a snowdrift. The air is cool, thin, and carries almost no moisture; my breath leaves no cloud. Above me, the sky is not sky but a slow-moving cataract of pale stone, suspended in folds and pressures that suggest it forgot which direction to fall. The light is dim, sourceless, as if filtered through miles of calcified atmosphere. It clings to edges, never to surfaces.

Below this hanging geology, the city—if it is a city—spreads outward in three directions, but not downward. It is built along the inside curve of a canyon that is also a mountain: a single tectonic flake stood on its edge, its layers serrating the sky. Streets are not streets but polished striations in the cliff-face, wide enough for processions yet empty. Buildings have been planed off the rock by some impossible pladera, their windows left as negative space, their doors implied by abrupt absences of mass. Every block is a cavity; every tower is a shaft of air. The architecture is all silhouette, all echo. You do not enter these structures—you exit the idea of them.

There is no water. Instead, a faint static of mineral dust drifts downslope whenever the wind remembers to move. The wind itself is rare, and when it comes it carries a sound like glass remembering fracture. Otherwise the acoustic field is hushed, stitched only by the clicks of my own joints and, every few minutes, a distant ceramic sigh as some overhang adjusts its weight.

Life is sparse, but not absent. Things flicker between the hollow buildings—creatures the size of thumbnails, composed of overlapping reflections. They have no depth; when seen from the side they vanish like rain on old television. Their movements are jerky yet directional, as if each frame of their existence is rendered a few centimetres distant from the last. They seem to feed on verticality itself, nibbling the concept of height until ledges forget to rise. I watch one graze along the lip of a rooftop-shaped absence; the edge softens, droops, becomes gradual. A slow collapse upward.

No plants. No fungi. Just the rock, the dust, and these twinned glints of half-fauna. Yet the place is not sterile; it is post-organic, as though biology tried, failed, and the world innovated another way to metabolize time.

The elevation is so extreme that my blood feels carbonated. But the slope is incremental, terraced by ancient tectonic insomnia, so I can walk for hours without climbing—only the horizon drops away, exposing more of the inverse skyline. Distance behaves oddly. Two boroughs of negative building can appear stacked, but as I approach they shuffle laterally, refusing parallax. Sometimes the city folds back on itself; I round a corner and meet my own footprints coming toward me, still warm. I step aside to let myself pass. We nod, wordlessly.

There is no tech—no wires, pipes, signals, trash, graffiti. Yet everything feels engineered, as if the fundamental constants were persuaded rather than violated. Gravity negotiates. Mass forgets. Light keeps its own counsel. The place is urban not because people lived here, but because reality itself became dense enough to civic-plan. It holds elections between angles. It zones the intangible.

And it is active. Not lively—there is no life—but processional. Far below (or above—I can no longer assign down) a district of sheer planes tilts a few degrees, rearranging shade. A spire of absence rotates, opening a corridor through which colder air drains upward. The reflection-creatures migrate in flickers toward the new coolness, carving soft scars into altitude. Something large—maybe a statute of negative space—relocates in my peripheral vision; when I turn, only an additional quiet remains, shaped like apology.

I understand slowly: erosion here is not the wearing away of matter but the wearing into it of possibility. After enough centuries, the rock remembers every position it might have taken, and the city accretes choices rather than residues. The silence is not absence but a cautious consensus: every potential sound cancels its neighbour until only the hush of unconsummated collapse remains.

I keep walking because the alternative is to stand still long enough to become a reference point. The strata underfoot record longitudinal dreams; each step drums up a ghost percussion of worlds that decided against existing. They tug at my ankles, inviting me to subtract myself. I resist by counting breaths that do not fog.

Night—if night it is—arrives as a further dilution of the already dim. The sky-cataract dims until it matches the grey of the air, erasing horizon. The city phosphoresces in outline only: each hole for window, each slot for door, outlined by a colder shade of dark. The reflection-creatures multiply in this anti-illumination, eating height until the vertical begins to feel dangerously negotiable. I sense the buildings around me slouching, their absences rounding into caves. Soon the distinction between street and structure will dissolve, and the city will be one smooth declivity, a democracy of slope.

I quicken my pace, though directions are now arbitrary. Somewhere here must be an exit, or at least an entrance. Behind me my alternative self continues backward, footprints erasing under his/their heels. He will arrive at the moment I first appeared, and the loop will seam shut. Unless—

A draft of even cooler air brushes my face: the faintest memory of evaporated water. It carries no moisture, only the concept of having once been moist. I follow it between two slabs of withheld architecture, down a stairway that exists only as a consensus of lowerings. The reflection-creatures scatter like scattered mirrors, and for an instant I see myself from every unbuilt angle—knees bending sideways, shadow preceding body, eyes in the back of my mouth.

At the lowest point—though elevation feels negotiable—I find a single drop of water calcified into a perfect bead. It balances on a lip of stone that is also the negative of a gutter. Around it, the dust has arranged itself in tidy concentric filings, as if magnetised by the idea of a ripple. The city has produced this: not liquid, not solvent, but the fossil of wetness—an ur-drop to teach thirst to geology. It is the only honest measurement I have seen here, and I do not touch it. I record it in the only durable memory I own: the soles of my feet.

Then I continue, upward or inward, while the urban absences rearrange their tenancy. Somewhere, a roofline sags into a porch, a porch into a plaza, a plaza into the sky’s slow cataract. The cool air keeps its counsel. The silence tallies choices. The hush of reality forgetting itself settles over everything like dust that has never known weight.

And the city, gracious, begins to forget me even before I am gone.
