A pocket far above the world, cupped in the teeth of a mountain range like a held breath before speech.  
Altitude has bitten away most of the sky; only a bruised-red ribbon remains above the rim, and the stars already crowd the day. From whatever sea once existed far below, a single updraft climbs, turns suddenly chill, and then spits out its last moisture against the black stone. The stain spreads, glistening like oil on iron, and that is all the water here—thin skins that last a few minutes before they crawl into the air again to begin the voyage down.

The space is hardly wider than a plaza, but it is stacked: terraces of basalt four hand-spans deep, shelved inward, each one clogged. Life refuses to waste room. Legless lizards the color of fresh cut iron coil over one another, their scales rasping like drawn razors. Dust-colored moths hang perpendicular, wings folded so tightly they resemble dead leaves stitched to the rock. Lichens—black and copper—are embossed so thickly they have grown a second skin over the stone; in places hairline cracks become seams of darker flesh. The air is weighted, mineral-sweet, as though the mountain itself is quietly spoiling.

Density: Wanderers who claw their way up the final chimney say it feels like stepping into a crow’s skull—sound pressed inward, nothing loosened or thrown away. At the usual hour (one counts time here by the agitation of the moth-clouds) the lizards wake with a rasping chorus, tongues flicking for moisture. Their vibrating tails drum the basalt so that the whole basin rings like a drumone half-submerged in warm tar. Louder yet are the cicadas that were caught in today's updraft, lifted three thousand metres higher than their design. They lift and settle, lift and settle, producing a gong-like resonance inside the bowl; the rock returns the note multiplied into metallic thunder. The moths beat dust from their wings, and every down-stroke squirts capillaries of spores into the air; they glint when the ribbon of sky lights them like powdered rubies. Together these noises braid into something just short of language: a cadence older than words yet easy to misinterpret as warning.

Darkness rules. The sun is a coin thrown long ago that never struck bottom. What light exists leaks sideways from somewhere else—scattered violet from noontime thunderheads hundreds of kilometres away, or the soft crest of evening borrowed off some lower peak. Whatever the source, it arrives broken, bruised, sliced into panses by moths and lizard bodies. The rock drinks the remnants; only their edges stay wet and glisten like sharpened knives.

Temperature: Though almost no land remains between stone and stellar cold, the air is warm, astonishingly so. Each life-form carries insulation—lizards like bags of hot blood, moths with velvet skins, even the lichens hoarding daylight as copper heat. Touch one and you feel it sweat; bleed on it and light dims around the wound as if the place prefers to keep its temperature private.

Fauna overflows. The small bowl is a spawning ground for things mislaid at creation. There are rock-hares with blood-colored eyes that have forgotten their ears, yet tiptoe along the basalt with spider-leg fingers. Centipedes made of brass filings and ash link themselves in strings of twenty to cross gaps; each individual sacrifices its length until the column is thin as thought. When exhaustion comes the front bites the rear end, recycling metallurgic flesh back into the column’s bloodstream. Every suture leaves a new seam of environmental memory, and the line that results—a sigil no human has ever copied—skates along the shelf rim, marking this hour’s boundary between tomorrow and yesterday.

No structure has ever stood here; nothing came to the mountain bearing even a name. Yet technology flickers undeclared. The brass centipedes knot themselves into hinges, into arcs that briefly resemble guttering gutterpipes or the scaffolding bones of arches—except these are born of mouths, not chisels, and melt away before they teach any lasting grammar. Once, a lizard gnawed a flap of lichen and shaved it into a concave shadow darker than darkness; for seven slow minutes it served as a window above the red sky—until a moth landed, tasted the edge, and the shadow closed like a lid. Such artifacts come into being and vanish again; the mountain permits only stubborn processes—evolution grinding at one groove until the groove becomes the tool.

Erosion has reached its own agreement with gravity. The walls are fluted with the drainage of a thousand extinct springs; veins of salt and obsidian glitter inside those runnels, left behind when water squeezed itself out of existence. Chaos has polished the chaos; every break or crumble has been sieved until only the possible angles remain. Guests say that to stand still long enough is to risk becoming another crease in that diplomacy. Limbs cool, skin hardens toward lichen, heartbeat slows to match the rock’s metabolism. The boundary between flesh and basalt is negotiable, but the terms are final.

Cycles: Days flit in and out quickly by world standards, but here everything conspires to stretch them. Rain, if it comes at all, is a mist shaped like a migrating hand; a touch, a wringing, gone. Hunger is such that the mouth of the bowl opens instinctively to whatever updrafts gallop past—an invitation never answered. A cicada, newly arrived, will buzz for half a day and then quiet, realizing the mistake in altitude. The mountain’s slow grinding is audible only as a faint regurgitation stone years below human hearing. One must wait six human breaths to notice the chute in temperature that foreshadows the next incoming misty hand; one must wait six stone years to confirm it happened at all.

And yet movement accumulates. Dust lifted by moth wings eventually climbs the chimney and sails onto a distant glacier; lizard embryos, folded in the umbra of mother’s coils, hatch already adapted to thinner sky. The bowl is a forge, not by heat alone, but by density of experiment. Life so thick, time so slow, the result is like a lens plotting a diagram of the world to come—each creature finished at the moment it is first sketched.

Strangeness: Nothing is impossible here, but everything reckons. Thus a salamander mated with a ripple of shadow once—no one cheats time without paying; three years later the ripple carried back its debt in the form of a lizard born skinless, just nerve and ruby flame. The flame cannot warm anything; it consumes nothing, but merely swims the air like lamp-oil lit from inside. Each year it thins. Observers (rarely human) note that as the fire dwindles, the lichens on the lowest tier flush a deeper red. When the last thread of fire snuffs out, the entire basin will clap itself shut one simultaneous heartbeat, the stone folding in like an eyelid. But that hour drifts—drifts—just out of reach, preserved by the mountain’s own languor.

Sound again: if you put your ear to the stone you hear not the surf of its past but its future: the day heat will weaken and water will return. The note is soft as a fingernail drawing a line of chalk across glass. It does not comfort; it certifies.

There is a rule known only by the hearts of those who have trusted their weight to the basin floor. Even now, if you crane your head back until the bruised sky ribbon touches your vision like a dentist’s mirror, you will see an absence. That absence is the size of a child’s skull, and it does not change with clouds, with dusk, with flares from distant galaxies. It is the eye of the mountain; it never blinks. It has watched, is watching, will watch while everything dense and slow rearranges itself. One day the eye will regard the last centipede, the final moth, the tremor that opens enough space for the stone to complete one last thought. The eye will close. The bowl will invert, spilling its warmth and lichen history down the chimney like a genial belch, and the visitors, if any survive, will discover solitude only after all company has been digested.

Until then, day proceeds like the slow toggling of a switch in near-sleep. Moisture beads, moths cloud, lizards stir, the loud, overlapping density winnows itself finer each revolution. The rules stay simple: nothing built, nothing claimed, everything touched into pattern by time wearing the seasoning of its own breath.
