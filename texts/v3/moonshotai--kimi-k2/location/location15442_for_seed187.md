The Hollow Thicket

It is a single vaulted chamber, a depression no broader than a village square yet roofed by living timber so high that its crown is lost in dusk.  From outside it looks like any low knoll on a rainswept moor, but the mound is hollow, excavated ages ago by a smaller world that drew down its own sky and then forgot the habit of movement.

Vegetation, the only architecture here, has filled every cubic handspan of air.  Pale leafless vines thicker than a man’s thigh descend from the unseen ceiling, dividing and re-dividing until every lowest filament runs to root or else knots blindly back into itself.  Between these titanic ropes, paper-thin sheets of translucent lichen stretch like membranes, diffusing the light so that the whole interior glows the even green-white of milk seen through jade.  Nothing is trunk, branch, or foliage in the usual sense; it is all one organ breathing in slow centuries, consuming its own refuse, exhaling a liquor-cool vapour that smells faintly of bruised mint and old bronze.

Temperature clings at the edge of autumn: never warmer, never colder.  A fine condensation beads on over-saturated fibres and drops with metronomic patience – plink, plink – into shallow hollows worn at the roots.  Those granite scleroses, scoured mirror-smooth by two–three million years of dripping, now hold only enough water to reflect the single normal band of light that filters forty metres above, and enough sound to return a startling clap for every falling bead.  Together the drips compose a continuous, vast drumbeat amplified by the resonant chamber, loud as a factory floor yet never varying in thirty heartbeats or thirty millennia.

Nothing changes.  The erosive carving that first bled open this belly was the last spasm of geological youth; since then the scene has petrified.  Leaves do not unfold, seeds do not fall, fungi have long since given up on sporulation.  The system breathes, drinks its own tears, burns the slowest imaginable sugar, but it is locked in a single photon-for-photon budget.  Nothing can leave; a bird that once blundered in here four centuries ago is fossilised mid-wing-stroke against a tendril, plumage to ash, hollow bones rattled like wind chimes.

Yet life is abundant, for those bones are not alone.  Between the cables of vine, high up, squat colonies of copper-green moths that have also forgotten metamorphosis; their wings twitch open and shut every eleven minutes in perfect synchrony, as though wired to some ancestral central clockwork.  At the root bowls colonise blind glass-shrimp, slightly translucent, throbbing faintly to the same rhythm.  Every organism is part of the plant now – gut bacteria farmed in thoraxes, mitochondria re-hired as chloroplasts, symbiosis turned to permanent merger.  Even sound has been metabolised: the loud percussive pulse is farmed by hollow chambers in the woody walls that swell or shrink with air pressure to keep the drone constant; the plant has amplified its own nervous pulse to an industrial roar and calls it tranquillity.

Nowhere is there a tool, a brick, a mortared joint, yet the scene is unmistakably engineered.  It is plant-as-factory: braided vascular lines thick as factory ducts carry viscous, honey-yellow latex; gland-like switchells flick open and closed regulating flows finer than clock escapements; and across the vault, panels of laminar tissue stiffen and slacken in patterns that can only be read as circuitry – a lung, a bellows, a dynamo.  No coal is burnt, no wheel turns, but the laws of thermodynamics are obeyed in longhand, drawn across geological parchment, the last steam engine re-written as vegetable scripture.

Every breath you take within the Hollow Thicket tastes of slow thunder and petrified honey.  Sound never decays, only re-enters the spongy hearts of the vine-ropes and is carried upward, outward, inward, forever.  Light is the same soft noon whether the sun outside is dawn or dusk; the plant has stockpiled radiance like canned heat, rationing its single remembered afternoon.  Nothing will ever again break this equilibrium, for erosion itself has ceased; the erosion parameter has reached asymptote, the final grade of stones upon stones, where even touch becomes unnecessary.

Stand here long enough and the low thrumming becomes your bloodstream.  The plant has already decided, without hurry or ambition, that you too are fine raw material: within three centuries the calcium thought of as yours will be distributed through new pith threads, your carbon will smoulder gently in some future leaf, your last sigh will be admitted to the grand acoustic chorus, one more husk suspended beneath a sagging canopy that is sky, earth, mill, and memory.
