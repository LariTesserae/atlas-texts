The place is a half-born fruiting body stuck between spore and city.  
A single bulbous chamber, fifty meters across at its widest, hangs like an inverted bell under a sky that has not yet decided what laws of physics it wishes to obey. The walls are living cambium, pearly grey under granular starlight, still fusing radial seams together—stem walls thicken, then thin again, as if the whole structure is rehearsing its final shape in uncertain pulses. Against this cool flesh, condensation pearls up, trembles, detaches, and crawls downward along gravity’s vector until it disappears into the pores of the floor with tiny sipping sounds. Each lost droplet is instantly replaced by a new bead high above, so the slow rain is endless yet never amounts to more than a mist one must lean into to taste metal on the tongue.

Because it is total vegetation, every surface imitates every other. Pillars that look like corroded cast-iron are in truth lignified tracheids packed into ferrous mimicry; mortared bricks reveal, when touched, the soft give of chitin. Floorboards are broad stripes of resurrection moss curled into parquet. Curtains hang as slips of chlorophyll-tinted membrane, and if parted they exude a faint green light that drips onto the intruder's shoes like melted phosphor. Every edge is simultaneously growing and being trimmed back, so the city is forever under renovation by the city itself. New balconies bud, then partition into windowed, then harden into louvers stiff as horn, all within the time it takes for a breath.

Coolness dwells here, but it is the cool side of decomposition more than weather. The air is always ten degrees above freezing; shivering feels voluntary. It smells of bruised petiole, wet ink, and galvanized lightning, a scent that collects just above the larynx so that one almost tastes ions.

Sound arrives in narrow packages: a distant hydraulic sigh that might be lungs or pumps, the taut droning of scarab-shaped beetles that keep every seam clean, the flutter of translucent moths patterned with urban maps whose wingbeats clack like typewriter keys against the dark. Most minutes are nothing but the hush of plant metabolism, the faint pulse of stomata sigh opening and closing like eyelashes against the night.

Yet this is industrial too. Down the slant of a spiralling root-staircase widened by oxides, furnaces with staminal filaments arch like cathedrals of orange tongues. Their heat never radiates beyond an arm’s reach; the cold is contractual. They transmute cellulose into cable, exude pith like coagulated latex that beds arm-thick copper veins delivering unknown wattage upward to lightless sockets. No coal, no smoke—only continual fabrication of parts whose purpose has been forgotten.

The light here is not darkness in the ordinary sense. It is negative luminescence: shapes become darker than the surrounding murk, darker than nothing, so that one sees silhouettes by their subtraction of sight. Occasionally motes of inverse brightness spiral, drawing afterimages that look like crowded tenements on another planet. These motes land as bioluminescent graffiti, white against the dark, spelling slogans that will be creeping vines by morning.

Fauna crowd the emptiness with paradox. Mice the colour of rusted iron squeak along airshafts composed entirely of hollow fungal tubes. Their eyes are gear wheels, spinning though frictionless; every squeak clocks another inch of corridor into existence before the mice vanish in a rip of fastest air. Silvery newts skate on the surface of condensed water films, forming circuitry patterns that survive only as long as the moist skin lasts. Higher up, glass-hard manta rays swim through the three dimensions, feeding on the shadow-images of the moths; when a ray devours a map-wing, for an instant one can see the floor plans of ten unbuilt cities spark in its transparent gut like embers.

Rules accumulate like frost.  
—Nothing is allowed to dry. Anything reaching absolute dryness becomes architecture. A fallen glove would petrify into balustrade by dawn, so organisms lick continually at surfaces to keep them alive.  
—Any straight line, held for more than a count of seventeen heartbeats, begins to germinate centripetally, growing toward the middle until the organism who drew it is cocooned inside trunk. Hence the avoidance of edges except by explorers who count out loud.  
—Electricity is traded like gossip. You can bribe a beam of moths for a volt of glow, or, if you are slow, you can be harvested by the furnaces for your thermal gradient.  
—The place is small: one can walk from one end to the other in less time than night lasts elsewhere. But its shape is subtractive; corridors are spaces a larger oak once occupied and then decided to vacate. If you desire more hallway you must seduce the oak to hollow further.

Though dynamic, the cycles are off-beat to any outer clock. A day here lasts until every resident agrees on the length of a ‘day’; when consensus fractures, midnight flips backward until agreement re-gels. Visitors feel temporally seasick, watching sunless shadows elongate to spider legs, retract to child height, lengthen again. Seasons exist as attitudes in conversation. When optimists prevail, the walls blanch and push out pollen storms of tiny airborne vault keys; when cynics gather, bark cracks open to reveal the unfinished layers beneath, loud as glaciers calving.

Built and unbuilt nest together in promiscuous layers. A spire that had once been a spinal cord is now becoming a district clock tower; its columnar neurons crystallize into bronze gears while the bell, still pink flesh, sways like a heart. Narrow dwellings hang on the underside of leaves that dangle like wet laundry, the lintels woven from ferrous xylem, the keys living beetle husps. Curved balconies are curled outer seed coats, lacquered by sap into slick ginger tiles. Streets join balcony to balcony, yet they are no thicker than capillaries; the only ones who travel them are the wards—blind fireflies tattooed with street names who negotiate the landlord leaves and the tenant insects, holding property deeds in their bioluminescent Morse.

The overall tone is a hush so dense it feels like water you can lean upon before it absorbs you. Sound is rationed: drums and saws exist only in pockets, threads of noise that sink into loose soil when no longer productive. Thus near-silence is covenant and utility in one.

And everywhere the weirdness that scored the limit: rules of perspective collapse stubbornly. If you stand still for long, the room’s corners converge toward your pupils, as if the whole chamber proposes to turn itself inside out. When that happens, you discover the other side is another room, also turning inside out—ad infinitum, like infinite nesting dolls inverting their skins. Travelers caught in such folds normally reappear days later woven into the pattern of a carpet of grass-assassins: herbaceous blades sharp as razorwire that sway provocatively in the cool draught—though the cut patterns they perform are only mural, never penetrate skin. You can walk over them barefoot and emerge with hair trimmed in perfect circles.

At the origin point, the umbilical well of the place is still open: a smooth bore in the floor, vertical as a bullet path, descending into ink. Sometimes, coils of steam rise carrying unfinished cities stuck like confetti to vapor membranes. It is said that if you hold a mirror over this vent, the mirror will birth more mirrors behind you, each showing a different depth of the chamber. After seven reflections, the viewer sees the chamber before it existed, merely a damp seed resting on a hillside lumped with clay birthed by no worldbound weather.

No exit signs. No horizon. Only are given in leaf-shaped directions you swallow; once inside the body they unfurl like tiny dire maps. You leave when the temperature cools another degree, the condensed rain congeals to hoarfrost on your lashes, and one of the mantas, heavy with digested wing-maps, hovers, offering you a visa encoded in the sweep of its caudal fin. Accept, and the darkness tilts open like a lid; decline, and the city keeps you on retainer, a clerk of humidity and seed amendment, inking borders that move faster than language.

So it stands, half-finished but perfectly complete, a municipal spore suspended between self-invention and self-erasure, compact yet inexhaustible, cool but never cold, breathing in dreamwork and exhaling half-remembered geographies—an underground fruiting body that insists, quietly, forever, that cities are merely a stage of plant matter choosing architecture as next year’s flower.
