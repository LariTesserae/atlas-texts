At the flare-lit bottom of a crevasse wider than a city lies a plain of black mercury sludge—the thing that bleeds from the planet’s wounded crust whenever continent-making has paused in mid-breath. Neither hot nor cold, the place sits at a constant low fever: 1.4 on a scale whose warmth remembers bodies, but not theirs. Vapors rise in geometric fans that cool immediately into glassy lattices; these brace themselves momentarily on nothing but humid air, clink, and collapse back into the ooze without ever having been truly solid. It sounds like chandeliers falling at a sub-oceanic party miles below sea level, yet the volume is constant, nearly orchestral, ninety-three decibels of flawless breakage every eight seconds, as if nature had learned to conduct herself with instruments of catastrophic patience.

The water is everywhere but wary of becoming water. It seeps upward and sideways, more like thought than liquid, unable to decide whether it wants to be graphite-slick pond, salt sheen, or frozen breath. Dusty condensate hangs in marbled sheets just above head-height, forming corridors and domes that you can walk through if you don’t mind an invisible cheek slapped by near-opalescent moisture. There is no vegetation to resist or welcome you. Instead, the ground dull-bright with platelets of telluric nickel offers fungal-looking tumors, but microscopy would reveal them to be inanimate—fossilized reactions between leftover volcanic glass and paleogenic fog. Biology has declared this alcove off-limits or perhaps seven billion years early; you stand on a pre-biome, a draft of a world.

Scattered towers stick out of the flood-plain's vertebrae, but “tower” is generous: each is a single upright column of re-crystallized stormwater exactly ten meters tall and no wider than your thigh. They appeared when lightning once struck the ooze, froze it into tapering splinters, then retreated. The towers hum faintly in hidden bass so deep it is felt inside joints, not ears. They are the only built things, and the only fauna ever seen are slow eel-shaped ice tongues that loll out of cracks once every few centuries to lick the minerals from the column bases, spreading fresh frost where the surface ought not to freeze. When an ice-tongue retracts it leaves dull red iron-on-iron questions, slow swimmers against time.

Mercury tide updates: every eighteen hours the natal magma beneath sips a little heat from the ooze above, causing an imperceptible net shift—the plain silently slides east-south-east by the width of a hair, but then the entire sky-water canopy negotiates with it in microscopic retreat toward the opposite direction. Two slow-motion scars sliding past each other like tectonic lovers in glacial estrangement. Nothing measured by human calendars will outlast the shrug between them. Gravity itself catches colds here, drooping under the strain of pointless loyalty.

Colors are mostly shade. Indigo dragging behind itself the memory of red that never arrived, a cold charcoal that suctions light from the gray-glowing vapor domes. Here and there a coppery rangeflash: prismatic spill from nickel flakes. The loudest sound is the collapse of glass vapor bridges; the softest is your own heartbeat amplified by mercury seashell acoustics. If you lie down long enough the ooze will print your silhouette into nickel-lace and the decay of your warmth will sponsor one additional tower somewhere else—new architecture by lost thermonyms. It may take a millennium, but the plain has already subtracted that into its patient timetabler.

Within sight of this all, a perfect circle of absolute dryness floats half a meter above ground: floorless shadow, radius two steps, ringed by a rim of bristling frost. Anything stepping inside stops hearing the orchestra; the silence folds around like stunned silk. Time in that cylinder stretches, contracts, yawns. Rumor holds that yesterday’s rain went into it and never returned, and tomorrow’s light will be invented there first before escaping into the larger dim. The plain doesn’t comment on what isn’t yet given permission to exist. It prefers to collect your every footfall as a latent crystal of sound yet to shatter.
