It is not a cave, exactly, because nothing here is finished.

A hollow hardly bigger than a coffin sits halfway up a cliff whose rock remembers liquid. The cliff pushes straight out of cloud; the same warm haze presses in from every side, so thick it tastes like steeped stone. Water is everywhere yet nowhere collectable: it sheathes surfaces as a rolling sweat, gathers into pregnant droplets that never quite fall. The ceiling – once the cliff’s outer skin – has peeled away along sheets the width of a tongue, leaving concave pans that hold almost-black pools not deeper than a knuckle. Each pool lies at exactly the same level, reflecting nothing, owning no ripple. They are blind mirrors, stoppered by surface tension and time.

Sparse vegetation is accounted for by a single thing: a stem as thick as a finger that climbs unseen for an inch then splits into twenty translucent fronds. Every frond supports its own reservoir of water – not dew, but water condensed into the leaf itself, in sacs that pulse with slow internal tides. The plant has no colour; when the air is still, its outline vanishes against rock vapour. Occasionally a sac will release a droplet into the void, but the droplet never lands – it shrinks in mid-air, loaning its mass back to the air, so no evidence accrues of breakage or growth. The plant neither thrives nor withers; it was formed with this exact count of sacs and holds it forever.

Sound is a cupped silence: the cliff swallows every echo before it arises and so the only reports are a faint dripping, too slow to mark rhythm, and the occasional sieve-breath of vapor condensing on your own ears. Every two or three breaths there is a dry scrabble, like fingernail on slate – a single, thumb-sized beetle-creature, wings soldered together by mineral film, dragging the useless blades behind. It never walks twice in the same direction, yet its path retraces itself by dawn: a slow ark ending where it began, erased in advance.

Only three artifacts breach the living rawness of the space. A thumb-scraped hollow in the wall once held pigment – you taste iron rust if you press your tongue. A sliver of worked agate, little longer than a toothpick, lies balanced across one of the mirror-pools as though forgotten earlier in the hour that built the world; the pool underneath neither reflects nor wets it. And a bundle of moss-felt, compressed into a square the size of two palms, tied with unraveling fibre. When lifted it yields the weight of a bird skull, and inside the bundle sleeps a fossil syllable of ash, no larger than a comma.

There is no entering; one simply becomes here, as if the pocket caul once grew around you. Likewise, no leaving. The cliff does not weather, droplets do not fall, the eternal plant does not complete a lifecycle, the beetle finishes its arc forever unfinished. Warmth is steady, darkness complete; warmth and darkness share the same silk and ask no division. All densities of matter and myth are packed to the thickness of air still within inhalation, yet the space feels roomy, like an unarrived thought.

The rule is simple: in this nursery before action, nothing changes until everything does, and everything is not yet licensed to begin. The world has not been granted its first tick.
