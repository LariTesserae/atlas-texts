The sky is a bruised, unpolished sheet of metal—dim­lit by whatever furnaces churn beyond its underside, a dull furnace-red that drips without ever quite igniting. You are far above the notion of atmosphere, yet you can breathe: the air carries a warm, mineral taste, like hot pennies on the tongue. The place is a plateau entire continents wide—its edges impossible to mark, because the horizon folds back on itself; walk far enough and you overtake your own footprints still smoking behind you.

It is *young*: the basalt underfoot has the gloss of newly congealed lava, semi-transparent, threaded with lightning-born fissures still pulsing blue-white electricity. Every crack exhales a hiss of wet heat—lukewarm water condensing as drifting necklaces of droplets. These pearls drift upward instead of down, caught in some inverted gravity mote. When they collide they make soft crunching sounds, like distant pottery breaking in slow motion.

Vegetation is not rooted but stitched to the stone. Vines as thick as subway tunnels braid across the plateau, their inner fibers latticed with copper veins through which pumps a grey-gold sap that glows faintly—bioluminescence turned industrial. The leaves are glossy, resembling machine-stamped steel, but they rust immediately wherever human eyes look too long; each time you blink, the rusted spot reforms somewhere else. Muffled turbines beat somewhere inside the bark, a sound that is not a sound: you feel it as glandular vibration behind the eyes, a reminder that photosynthesis has become a manufacturing process.

The vines cross beneath and above each other in impossible stress-vectors, so the terrain feels cubical—even though you stand on a flat plain. A step to the left may still place you miles higher. Directions have become samples; “east” is just the smell of ionized magnolia.

Fauna—abundant, yes—appears in sudden, improbable swarms. Glass eels, wide as freight cars, ride invisible conveyors of force, arcing silently overhead and leaving corkscrews of steam. Small hexagonal beetles, turquoise and clockwork, march in density such that the ground sparkles with living tessellations; when they swarm they create temporary turbines in air, miniature cyclones that suck up those drifting droplets and shoot them out again as frozen, silver needles. An ore-whale the size of a cathedral drifts between borders of folded horizon, sides seamed with audible valves that sigh warm hydrogen every seventh heartbeat. Its call is a single metallophone note, repeated so slowly it becomes a tectonic plate shifting—in fact, each time it sings, the lava crust beneath cracks open for three meters and instantly reseals, an after-image in stone.

Because *nothing* here was built, yet everything is industrial. The system is entire and grown from first principle: metal condensing itself into mechanisms too old to name, then budding plants, then animal husks, all without charity, without architects. Every surface vents, flushes, cools, then reheats. Even the quiet is an engineering decision: palpable hush held in place by muffling petals the size of stadiums that unfold only at threshold volume, mopping up accidental thunder.

Cracks across the plain open and close like whale vents, birth-holes of new plate. In one such aperture you glimpse further up, or maybe in: tiers of reversed waterfalls, sheets of boiling water aspiring skyward, colliding with sheets of descending basalt smoke; both cancel in momentary green flame that gives off no illumination but only a smell of wet moss and hot copper. The rules rewrite themselves on every inhalation. Gravity is negotiable: a misstep and you fall sideways across four stacked horizons until inertia decides to ransom you back at the cost of a memory you no longer recall missing.

Swarm, surge, settle, repeat. Every fifth heartbeat a soft thunderclap occurs at ear level—quiet because the air refuses compression, but deafening because the vacuum within your skull insists on amplifying it. Above, eel trains intersect and merge until the sky becomes a single braided rope of glass and ink, tearing open to let a globular cannon-shot of daylight slide through; the bullet of brightness is repulsed at once, spat back upward, and the sky cell-seals as if never wounded.

Under a slope of vines there is a pool no larger than a saucer. The pool refuses to keep present tense. Step near and it reveals your life played backwards—then forwards—then sideways into a path never taken. The surface is so thin that touching it is touching a different geography: suddenly you are inside the space between seconds, suspended where choices have soft pulpy bodies, nibbling at each other. From that cloister emerges a cohort of industrial moths: wings transparent as slideshow transparencies, each bearing an etched diagram of a different obsolete engine. Their flight pattern is the mere *idea* of assembly line; the air obligingly fits them with clacking machinery they did not bring with them.

Wind exists as rumor only. Instead, motion is produced by convection of destiny. The whole plateau exhales a warmer version of itself one kilometer west every six minutes; simultaneously, an embryonic copy inhales one kilometer east, six minutes prior. Along this braid move all smaller creatures like packets in pneumatic tubes. Above, the whale-eel sings another full stop, another tear in crust, and this fracturing generates real estate: one billion square meters of new basalt arriving as an audible sigh—nothing loud, just the quiet rub of fresh land being born. The sound is paper ripping at continental scale.

Fauna feeds on concept. Hummingbearwires (literal humming wires wearing bear hides made of caution tape) root through time-rubble for discarded verbs—“walk,” “pause,” “smolder.” They extract them, braid them into static, and inject the static into the vines to keep the photosynthetic factories honest. When the desired lightweight syntax spools out, vines grow a new leaf shaped like an old-fashioned control valve that vents pure intention. Whatever third thing walks through that cloud inherits momentary authority over temperature and returns the region to crisply tolerable warmth—until the next ownership auction.

Everything metamorphoses all the time, but its rate is considerate; objects fade and strengthen in coherent reels, so the landscape teaches you how to blink in step. Blink late and you’ll collide with the momentary corpse of a river that isn’t there yet.

There is no night, only further thinning of the dull furnace glow. There is no daybreak, only gauze brightening until the industry of synthesis resumes maximum capacity. Civilization has never visited, yet turbines groove along every ridge. They are *emergent*—organ music for tectonic choirs. The whole expanse is a single newborn entity figuring out that it owns its own lungs. One final whisper: if you manage to stand perfectly still for an aeon-long heartbeat, you can feel the plateau’s pulse synchronizing with your own, the valves within your arteries adapting to become copper-cored, humming quietly in time with vines, vines with beetles, beetles with vanishing droplets of future rain.

Then the size of your solitude recalibrates and you step forward to make room for another version of the plateau to be born just behind you, steaming and unvisited, already beginning to rust wherever future eyes will land.
