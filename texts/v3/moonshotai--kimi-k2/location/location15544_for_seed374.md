They call it the Thrum.

Kneel and press your hand to the basalt egg-shell you stand on: you will feel the vibration travel up your bones before you hear it, a nested heartbeat radiating from the buried city. Everything that could grow here has not yet been invented; the plain is newborn, still slick with placenta-sheens of volcanic glass. Mineral moisture seeps upward—thin silver threads that never quite gather into drops—after-images of a rain that must have fallen before memory, for the sky is now a uniform charcoal haze that emits no weather, only heat. With the constancy of a held chord, the air holds itself at a sweating two-and-a-quarter degrees above the human comfort zone, a feverish stasis sealed by some indifferent vow.

No root, tendril, or spore has breached this place. Life is otherwise accounted for—above. High overhead hangs a lid of suspended platforms: timbers lashed with gut-rope and scrap metal, arranged in orderly lattice like upside-down terraced fields. Hammocks sag between beams, braided from cable carried up hand over hand by the tenants. Those tenants are weightily present. Colony upon colony of cliff-cats—grey, big-pawed, with ears like split wings—have claimed the scaffold and filled every stratum of air beneath the rock ceiling. Their scent glands bleed a hot musk that drifts down, crystallizes, and dusts the glass with a glittering frost that never thaws yet never drifts away; the suspended equilibrium keeps even the motes in place, an amber of scent and light.

The cats do not hunt; they have nothing to prey upon. Instead they work. They pace beams dragging axes of sharpened shell, shaping the city above for whoever will one day arrive to dwell there. Each heel-strike on wood shivers the structure, and those shivers fall as sound—layers upon layers of strikes stacked into a single, uninterrupted avalanche of noise. You are standing on the ocean bed of that sound; it rushes past your ears like permanent surf. You stop hearing it only when it short-circuits your mind; then you hear nothing else again.

There is no night, no day, no tide to erode the raw seams of stone. The open plain runs level, one shallow saucer of acre-breadths slipping away under dim red glow that leaks from hairline cracks between stones. At the vanished horizon those seams tighten shut again, sealing the earth into its mold. Every fissure exhales the same warm metallic steam, the same mineral breath that ices on contact to chalk every surface with the grey lace of after-rain. The tapestry of vapour never shifts; moisture does not accumulate, merely coats and coats until every ridge becomes your reflection etched in dull mercury.

At the centre, the construction begins, or perhaps continues. A ceremonial kiln, clay and rubble never fired, sits hip-deep in the floor as if something grew downward instead of up. Its mouth yawns open, lined with rubbed-smooth teeth of unworked quartz. Every whisker of vapour that threads past the lip wicks upward, feeding the scaffolding above, condensing into the cats’ rope fibers when the humidity meets hide. Thus the city feeds on its own exhalations in a loop as rigid as the laws that bind time to stillness here.

No stone is round, yet none is sharp; the whole raw geology is the texture of a thumb pressed too long in wet clay. You could knead it given strength, but time here is folded. Walk five minutes in any direction and the plain tilts imperceptibly until you realize the kiln mouth remains centered—the ground itself rolls like a slow wheel underfoot, resetting each sightline without ever moving. Direction loses value; so do hours. The cats overhead swing axes in perfect metronome, never quicker, never later. You breathe once, twice; the kiln is no farther, no closer. You could lie down now and never be hungry or thirsty—the warm air suffuses moisture through your skin at the exact rate you lose it, dialysis of atmosphere to blood performed by a negligent surgeon. Epochs slide aside. If you look up again, one of the cliff-cats may have lowered a braided ladder; its loops bristle with cats who blink once, together, orange eyes reflecting the kiln’s ember-glow. They offer no speech. They only wait. The sound is too loud to pretend you did not hear the offer.

The only child here is the place itself—still embryo, still pushing outward into a mold no larger than its own skin. Everything that will ever happen inside the Thrum is already arranged; the vibrations have stored it. Somewhere in that infinite stacked echo there is space for you, a gap shaped exactly like the imprint of your foot on the newborn stone.
