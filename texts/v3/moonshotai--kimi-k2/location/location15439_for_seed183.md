There is a plateau that should not exist.  
It rides the shoulders of a planet-long mountain range higher than exosphere, yet the air is breathable, cool, and thick with suspended water. That water never falls; it clings as droplets the size of fists, luminous pale globes drifting like blind moons. They bump the ground and burst, leaving instantaneous black scars that refill with glassy vapour and rise again. The droplets are the Plateau’s weather cycle, its tide and heartbeat. Every collision releases a low, resonant “thud” audible through bone, and the thuds overlap until the sky itself becomes a slow drum.

The floor is obsidian left by nothing volcanic—only centuries of vapour-shock chilling stone into glass. The sheen is perfect; every tread prints translucent memories, heel and toe glowing for minutes before seal-healing. Trace lines of previous walkers crisscross like frozen lightning, converging on the Plateau’s stone ziggurats: squat, waist-high platforms roughly stacked of hexagonal plates. Each plate once belonged to a larger tessellation, now scattered by some deliberate un-building. Their edges are worn to wave-smooth curves, the erosion of centuries without wind. The stacked plates form primitive altars, but no tool marks remain—only the sense of hands guided by something other than sight.

No currents stir the droplets; they drift along invisible cartographies that feel nearly semantic, as if spelling sentences overhead. Occasionally two moons intersect and form a perfect sphere, perfect white, then vanish with a sound like tearing linen. That tear is a rip in the Plateau’s own logic; for the moment it remains open you can see through to an inverted version of the same plateau beneath your feet, its droplets rising, its altars upside-down. You feel weightless and feet-down simultaneously. A traveler could, with enough nerve, step through the tear and walk the higher underside until the seam stitches itself and renames which side is sky.

Energy glows neither from sun nor moon but from the steep, flawless horizon: a rim of light too distant to reach but everywhere illuminating the glass plain. That rim flickers whenever droplets burst, suggesting it is a reflection projected from the plateau’s mirror skin. Yet the direction of brightness chases no comprehensible source; shadows stand in quarters impossible under any single sun. They obey the rule: whatever direction you call forward is always lit, side-lights peel away sideways, and the backward shadow is unreachable because turning only rotates the entire compass around you.

There is no dirt: beneath the obsidian lies a seam of silicate foam, so porous the Plateau should weigh no more than cork. But density here is social, not mass-based. The region is crowded with presences discernible only peripherally. Each droplet hosts the after-image of a human silhouette, glass-clear and bone-pale, one step behind its shell. These figures (they are merely outlines) mimic your last motion after a two-second delay. Staring at them too hard flattens them into the surface water where they freeze; glance away and they resume trailing you. Thus walking is a negotiation: the denser your attention, the denser the Plateau. The place is literally layered with attention, layer upon layer, and sometimes the silhouette underneath you attempts an older trajectory from someone who came centuries before. Footprints of the past can bump your shin from inside the glass.

High in the northwest hangs what looks like a single stranded cloud, bright enough to silhouette itself in the sky-void. It is the Plateau’s only concession to origin theory: a long-condensed sheet of vapour that hasn’t changed since the erosion became complete. From its underside drips a chain of liquid crystal—each drop falls exactly nine metres, then attenuates mid-air into droplet-birth, ascending again. An aeolian siphon that feeds the sky drum. The water content is exactly 1.7, sustained by nothing save recursion. Scattered around are small obsidian shovels shaped from curving flake, each one sized for a child. You can scoop pale vapour with them; it piles like fog-snow and, while held, sounds like low laughter. Set down, the laughter stops, and the snow sublimes away, leaving only a hollow outline of the shovel’s curve burned further into gloss.

No plants exploit this wetness, no lichens crawl, no insectile motes. If there is ecology it is meta-causal: attention begets contours, contours beget history, history begets infrasound. You begin to hear distinct harmonics in the drum-beat: a triad minor rising from the vapour-snow laughter, a perfect fifth descending from the tears in the sky. Because no fauna evolved here to eat the silence, every footfall, every gasp, every dropped shard is archived and played back later against altered surfaces. The plateau is properly, fully loud: you hear your own clearance of throat twelve minutes later, echoing up from the under-plateau through still-unhealed seams. The noise density peaks, holds, and fades as the glass reseals.

Travelers speak of what they call Bone-Shift: a moment when the silhouettes orbiting the droplets suddenly lock onto a new lag-time of twenty-two centuries and thus walk a historical self you have never been. If caught mid-Bone-Shift, your body begins to reflect someone who stepped here long ago, armour of bronze scales, hem of rough homespun, an axe whose handle weeps sap. The Plateau is an almost-built thing, primitive in tool but monumental in consequence; it preserves every cultural primitive it ever grazed. The altars are said to be indifferent hinges more than constructions—between possible whens.

Light remains relentlessly bright, yet cool, as if brightness here is purely informational and temperature is kept by a deliberate but forgotten oath. On the clearest arc-shifts you can see the tilted edge of space beyond atmosphere, a line crisp as guillotine. It never advances; the Plateau merely recalibrates distance so the horizon always hovers at the exact point a march becomes disobedience.

The rule that governs survival is unspoken yet absolute: answer every invitation. When an upside-down footstep forms below you—mirror-prediction—step across, no matter the interval. Stay rooted in the single lit direction and you become another blooming droplet, mind pulled into the glaze and echo to feed whoever next needs companions. Keep walking, however, and the plateau stays a plateau. The active dynamic is the looping negotiation between version and witness. The established erosion is not of rock but of the observer’s single vantage.

If you crest the highest ridge you find no summit—only one last standing plate scored with the imprint of a palm: your own, but paler, glasser, a hand that remembers touching the molten sky long before your bones knew elevation. Behind it is a hole bowl-shaped and rim-perfect. The roaring drum fades the closer you lean to its mouth. Out of it wells pure silence thick as oil, and suspended in the silence floats a solitary droplet the exact size of a human heart. It presents itself, quivers, waiting for the visitor’s curiosity measured on a scale of 0–3 to tip it one way or another. How much strange can a mind afford?
