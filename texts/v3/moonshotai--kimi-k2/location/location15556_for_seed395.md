It does not grow from the ground but from vacancy itself.  
Across the otherwise empty grey of a salt-flat never yet moistened, the land is only ankle-high; yet the word *flat* is wrong, for every square thumbnail of the panorama is occupied by feather-thin stalks—colourless at a glance, but purple-green iridescent if you squat until your knee-joints glow from that warm touchless sun.

The stalks are leaves without trunk. They grow so densely that a dropped pebble would lie suspended between them like a mote in mesh. No stem is thicker than a parchment edge, yet the mass carries the traveller: a footstep lands on a mattress of living struts that bow, sigh, rise again, always returning to strict horizontal. The sensation is firm but soft, like walking across a library of sleeping spines.

Because every cubic span is already colonised, the beings that move here do so on, through, or inside this leaf-carpet. Ant-things the length of an arm slip between the laminae like sewing needles. Their translucent abdomens bulge—hose-bags full of liquid that is not water, a pale chlorophyll-ether acquired from the purple cuticles. They drink by resting a mouth-hole against the fibres and waiting for osmosis to commit its slow theft. No predators pounce; something about the density itself is deterrent. You cannot fall upon what you cannot thread between.

What would normally be a desert silence is instead a silence full of micro-noise. Each leaf blade is a resonator so thin that exhalations alone set it trembling: a continuous low concertino of rubbed glass. The volume is almost nothing—nearer to zero than to quiet—but the frequency drifts with the air pressure into exhalations you *feel* in the sternum. Locals hear it as a single permanent vowel, “ee”, trembling, unsatisfied, and turning only slightly when clouds pass.

For clouds do pass. This vast sheet rests at a planetary low: a sun-warmed trench of planetary crust ringed round by slightly higher crust until it acts like the floor of an impossible sky-basin. Weather lives in folds only six meters up: almost imperceptible cumuli made brush along the top of the leaf-forest, condensing nothing (for water is absent) but bulging enough to filter the light from abrasive white to citrine. Shadows tip, elongate, vanish; the leaves track them micro-exact like a broadcast antenna array, sipping the shadow’s coolness with small cellular inhalations. The pulse of light change becomes the tide by which the landscape’s chemistry proceeds.

Under this tide the salt beneath is still bare, raw, first only resolidified magma that has never yet seen rain. The roots do not descend; they *lateralise*. Each purple-green leaflet extrudes root hairs sideways until the whole canopy is inter-laced into one lattice-seaweed suspended a millimetre above salt. When the wind surges west-east, the lattice entire rolls like breath in a museum carpet, a seamless wave hundreds of kilometres across. From orbit it looks still—a greenish fingerprint pressed into mineral—but on surface the traveller feels episodic locomotion, earth turning in bed.

There are no tools here and none seem necessary: the place already conducts all flows through itself. Energy arrives light-lanced, leaves ferment it into runny sugar they share freely, insects distribute and dissolve excess back into air directly as carbohydrate vapour, so thick at dusk the sky rouges slightly the colour of bruised peach. Building—at a score of 2.1/3—is subtler than pipes and walls. It is the accumulation of centuries of photosynthetic paper: layers compress upon layers until occlusions appear in the tissue: ribbed columns that have *forgotten* to stay horizontal. These columns thicken, angle, finally *stand*, becoming cortical trees that nonetheless remain 99 % leaf by volume. Their trunks are sheaths of petioles glued by their own resin. Inside, the resident fauna bore tubular apartments like salt-water worms that eat brick. The columns host curtains of pale orchid-like flowers whose fragrance is volatile pure sugar; they draw clouds of leaf-birds no larger than thumbnail. Birds do not fly above the canopy; they drive themselves inside it as if tree trunks are streets.

Orbiting observers call this worldwide thicket an *Autotropic Polis*, a city that needs no builder. Yet if the visitor sleeps rolled in the continuous mat the impression is inverted: the polis is the act of *using you* as material. Overnight the flat shifts imperceptibly; in morning a shallow hollow perfectly outlines your body—you have settled between leaves like coin in slot, and they have locked you into historical record. The longer you stay, the more accurately the lattice resumes your shape when you vacate it: a negative of yourself waiting underfoot forever.

It is a warm place. In the subtemperature 1.7 noon, heat rides the leaf vibration and beats against clothes like an engine-fan. Yet no wet; all moisture is bound in pectin-skins inside insects and leaf-cells. Thirst arrives as cotton mouth but is soon irrelevant—the inhaled air brings sugars, the very breath is aliment. After two nights here you cease to sweat, wound edges do not scab, skin dries to a suede transparency that refracts the purple-green upwelling of groundlight. Anthropologists who enter months later mistake former humans for translucent ghosts moving inside the lattice.

The scale is such that horizons roll upward like folded tarpaulin. What counts as horizon is not where land meets sky but where the tints of leaves shift phase: from viridian to chartreuse to celadon and back like planetary gears. A journey across a continent translates into the crossing of one single colour carriage in the slow chromatic wheel.

Every rule here is a rule of contact. Stand too long and root hairs will reach the soles of your boots or your bare feet (latticework having parted politely for skin). In little more than a minute, capillary action finds capillaries, pulls trace iron, manganese. The ground-light dims minutely in a radial star as resources migrate towards you: you are gently plucked of mineral by whispers too small to feel. Turn and walk away fast enough and no theft occurs—the place gives up easily, seeking only what drifts unused. Animals respect the etiquette: graze, move; pause thirty minutes and your nibbled tail-scales regrow. Sit contemplative on the same patch a week and the leaves below begin stitching a copy of your folded legs.

Mornings arrive with leafwarm wind beginning from the east—the planet rotates under the thicket, friction is no sunrise, only colour rotation. The sound gains volume but not loudness: every-tenor of glass fibres modifying pitch with passing pressure. Children, if they existed, could map cardinal points from harmonics alone. Near dusk the phase reverses: lattice exhales in west-east sigh, lays tiny chill of vacant sugar dust which birds and insects lick away through nostril slits.

There is no border. A traveller can regard one step left as *entering* again and again into the same city, the city so packed it has abolished distance. Two metres sideways is identical in species depth—no houses repeat, only *meanings* recur, a fractal civic pattern. Some speak of descent by elevator into empty basements of air beneath salt, but no floor has ever been reached; dig down, and root hairs re-root from the walls and the shaft converts to a hollow tree-womb plugged with soft sugars you can eat like foamed bread while the leaves overhead close back together above your head and you realise you have never left, only *inverted* inside.

Eventually the coming-into-being will ossify. The whole carpet is still embryonic, only a few centuries old by orbit count—an aeon too young to have keratinised to true landscape. But already petioles are lignifying; soon the glossy wafers will stiffen into plates. When the plates lock rigid they will vocalise; already small crackle-pings underfoot herald the moment when memory will begin *remembering itself aloud*—every footstep ever left replayed as cymbal, every conversation phonographed into anatomy, the Autotropic Polis become instrument and archive. Those who remain will move atop brittle glass singing their ancestors’ steps. Skyfuls of purple-green leaves, now hundreds of metres thick, will arch like a cathedral roof of living emerald, no longer touching the ground anywhere, the salt beneath at last revealed again—bare, untouchable, possibly never wet.
