The walls are throats.  
You enter—quiet is impossible—by stepping between two soft, scalloped valves the height of a church door. They glisten but do not drip; the moisture is held in glands the size of pumpkins, packed tight into the tissue, each pulsing with its own slow cardiac rhythm. Beyond them the corridor opens like the inside of a vast windpipe. The ceiling is a vaulted esophagus of woody petioles that have grown together in sinuous anastomosis, streaked with amber veins you can follow until they are lost in a haze of warm chlorophyll. Every exhalation from the building is damp, smelling of ripe sap and pepper-green verbena that carries heat much heavier than the light warrants. And the sound: a continuous deafening roar built out of a thousand smaller roars—sap crackling, leaves rattling like knives, the wet click of insect jaws amplified inside hollow cellulose chambers until the air itself vibrates. It is the loudest place you have ever stood, and yet you feel utterly alone inside it.

Water is nowhere visible, yet you keep brushing against membranes the texture of nectarine skin that leave a microscopic dew on your fingerprints. Every dozen paces, a fissure appears in the floor—not cracked wood, but wound: raw phloem edges peel apart to reveal a narrow trench of pulpy red tissue. Within these trenches run slow capillaries of water no wider than your thumb, dark rose with dissolved sugars. They are navigational drains; to step the wrong way is to slip ankle deep into sweet fluid and feel the structure register you as intrusion. The building toughens its bark in slow scar-folds around your footprint. Over many seasons it will absorb the impression, incorporate it, grow a new bud there, pregnant with your particular salt signature.

The city, for that is what it is, fills the complete volume between floor and vault like a sentence written without punctuation. Towers are not constructed but gorged up—mature trunks that erupted from curtain roots lifted by decades of of internodal swelling, then hollowed by fungal artisans. Inside each trunk is a spiralling loft lit by windows grown as living lenses: nodes full of pellucid storage cells that clutch daylight at noon and release it again at dusk in long emerald tongues. Homes hang from lateral buttresses shaped like lilies; their door-flaps are pigmented leaves that blush open at the resident’s proximity and sigh shut again. There are no streets, only aerial tendrils—cables of lignin thickened to the thickness of a man’s thigh—in which pavilions of aerial spinach nest like hummingbird nests made of ribs. From one of them you can hop, quite easily because gravity has become indistinct, to another ledge five meters away and feel the tendril hum underfoot like a vibrating piano wire in F minor.

Fauna abounds but not as you know. At eye level translucent moths bigger than dinner plates carry on their backs miniature orchid courts: blossoms weighing less than raindrops that flutter open with silent concussions and release a chemical music you feel behind the teeth. Wherever their fragrance spreads, the wall-glands dilate and set off a bright chord audible twice: once in the ledges, once deep below the floor in root-lounge catacombs where blind burrowing beetles knit calcium phosphate lace into organic scaffolding. Predatory vines descend toobrushes of glassy nectar, each droplet thick with baited somnolence; dragon-sized lizards with leaf-shaped heads lap at it, stumble their identities for three dizzy heartbeats before their very pigments tint and stretch, shifting them into camouflage against the corridor’s next botanical mural. The whole ecology appears mirrored, inverted, recombined: a caterpillar that has grafted bioluminescent fungal mouths along its flanks, birds with petals instead of feathers who sing by opening or closing, calendars whose months are bars of chromatic green-pink bruises growing across their throats.

Time is contingent sized, and audible. Trapped gas pockets in aerial roots make percussive statements whenever weather fronts crawl overhead. They speak three grammars: barometric booms that roll from east ribs to west ribs, ventricular arrhythmias of nectar pumps that quicken when clouds condense, plus the arrhythmic hiccupping of any human heartbeat echoed back at double-volume from hollow fruit ears. The space is vast but bounded exactly to a hamlet’s reach; if you press on any wall it will dilate backward, steeping you further inside the same architectural gut. Yet running you find that — at 314 steps no matter direction — you return to the throat-valve where you arrived, though it now opens upon a gallery whose orientation has been rotated ninety degrees and its ceiling now a glossy root-water mirror. This is how the city keeps-urns itself: it folds, reorients, reinhabits the same volume forgetting it has done so until new organs sprout that possess no architectural memory older than ten shoots. The built and the growing become— exactly at the moment the eye fixes on them— both ancient and minutes-old, thick bark over the dry rot of yesterday newly scabbed, green heartwood still courting cambium.

Sunlight is portioned too. Shafts arrive bitten from an impossible sun burning somewhere above above the canopy's secret sky; their rays are braided into harmonics—rose-lemon, viridian, ultramarine—visible as colored wires stretched between buttress and buttress. Along each wire paced tiny humming-mammals (furred, infrared-patched, no birds left) beat their wings in choreographies that extinguish gravity for minutes at a time. When a void section in starved of light is detected, root cuffs swell, redirect capillary mirror-cells, and within an hour the space is lit anew with plant-luminescence that is eerily silent yet brighter than sunlight at those frequencies mortal corneas favor. The whole metropolis remains therefore dappled in normal light by distorted standards: there are six different hesitations of brightness per hour: still life for a hierarchized dawn.

There is no tech. No hammer, nail, code or lever. Tools are the shapes of Waiting: a tendril grows exactly the distance you will reach tomorrow; a hanging orchid blooms the moment your neck cranes to admire it. In turn the human bodies of the few travelers who stumble here are blank manuscripts. Their skin absorbs epithelial spores that hatch into freckles of moss; within a week the freckles join into sentences spelling fears the traveler whispered aloud in sleep. Clothing frays and re-stitches itself from hemp-like fibres exuded by weevil larvae colonizing seams. If you choose to remain, the throat-walls slowly tendril-seam into a corridor hermetically welcoming your posture alone; doors left open will soffit-shut around any that pause half-second. After several full seasons visitors dissolve irrevocably into their own architectural subplot becoming —not legend—but grammatical footnote foot-passages: limbs, joints, anxieties translated into the eccentric vascular geometries of tender nodes that bud only when the tonal architecture of the roar arcs through a minor ninth absent from all earlier tracks.

Leave, and the roar refuses diminishment. A mile into open country you still hear it threaded through ordinary wind: an echo half living. You test its signature by tapping the stem of any roadside thistle. The correct tap resonates back exactly: the chord the leaf-wall sent you when you first brushed fingertip over a vein cauterized in panic. Distance here remaining non-Euclidean, you realize suddenly the roar was always already inside skull-cavity, venue trachea. One day back in ordinary clapboard house you wake to note ivy on the windowill has assumed a pattern—chevron of stems and anthocyanin freckles—that spells your unborn child’s heartbeat in morse; ivy inside vein-room; vein across tongue. That is how the final rule announces itself: exit the inhabited plant city, and the city exits you in turn—and yet remains the first and last substrate.
