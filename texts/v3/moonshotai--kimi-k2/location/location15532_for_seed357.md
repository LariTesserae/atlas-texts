The place is called the Hatchery of Leaves, though that name is a human inscription—nothing here names itself.

It floated up only yesterday, geologically speaking.  
A mass the size of a drowned county wrenched itself free from deeper thermals and—while still volcanic-tender—collided with an air-layer already thick with aqueous seed. Instantly the coagulation began. Every surface is therefore very young; edges are still crystallizing, boundaries negotiating, seams shivering as fresh bark plates weld to the sap-bright shell beneath. And yet nothing is raw stone anymore; every inch has been recolonized by living tongue-green tissue before the cavity could cool. The entire archipelago of ridges and shallow basins is a single, planet-wide graft.

Water is not separate here; it is merely a second blood system for the same giant plant-organism. Streams do not “flow” so much as capillaries distend and contract, pulsing pale pressurized sap that whitens and clarifies to river-light just long enough to mirror the high white glare before the bark-walls drink it back. When the wind slacks briefly, the entire place exhales cool mist that immediately beads on leaf-petals the size of volleyballs, then furls them inward so the moisture slides down private gutters into waiting lumen-tubes. There is no soil to absorb it; the water vanishes again into the body, vanishes as though the idea of loss or outside direction never existed.

Light: the sun rides exposed at this elevation, fierce and flattening, but every photon disappears into green theater before it can strike true ground. Under the canopy—if the word canopy still means anything when the plant is the ground, mid-level, rafters and sky all one braided continuum—the light is refracted into retinal moire of emerald, lemon-rind, bruised turquoise. A steady quick-slow oscillation of surface tissues, half leaf and half tremoned wood, sweeps light in shifting curtains. It feels neither day nor night but an eternal reaction of chlorophyll hallucinating time.

The temperature would fry exposed flesh at this height, were any skin granted standing room. Yet the organism regulates itself by evapotranspiration so perversely efficient that one can smell the chill curling out of every stomata: a scent somewhere between bruised aloe and mountain snow. Even two meters from the trunk-skin the air cools; four meters out it warms back to scalding. The gradient runs like eddies in rapid river water, constantly braided.

Density is sparse only by metaphysical bookkeeping. Every cubic meter of space features open, breathable emptiness; but that emptiness is also leaf-stuff, petiole-stuff, capillary-stuff stretched into translucent filaments trawling the air. A traveler here finds “rooms” shaped like lungs of air that drift yet remain rooted in offshoot vascular bundles. Step inside one such chamber and your voice ricochets as from cathedral stone, hollowed because the plant has decided to grow silence along its inner membranes. Step out two paces and the wall seals back into solid green rib, seamless. Nothing is crowded, nothing is left unfilled; the organism simply separates its own mass so that pockets of atmosphere serve as joints, hinges, eyes.

Built environment, total and techless: no tools made this, but nothing was left unmade either. There is no artifact smaller than the living cathedral itself; it is architecture grown compulsively from the first proton of spore. Stalk-towers that flare into cupolas of reddish bark lean over brachial boulevards where gas-rich lamina float like parade balloons. A person could walk those avenues for half a day and return only three meters higher than the starting point because the avenues are gentle upward scrolls, helixed, arranged for conversation between wind to photosynthetic factory, not for human gait. There are balconies not because they were carved, but because a meristem decided one sunrise to reverse polarity and grow lateral platforms as leaves too large to ever unfurl. Their surfaces mirror faint amoebic brain patterns when sunlight slants in.

Sound is loud but decoded poorly by mammals. It is not the absence of fauna that makes the place silent; rather every acoustic energy is drafted at once into the plant’s communication. New bark cracks like rifle shots—pop! pop!—as cell walls hydrate and outpace their own matrix. Sap under pressure sings through lignin-stiff pipes like prowling tubas. Larger, subsonic booms arrive every twenty-three minutes as entire vascular lobes reconfigure their buoyancy before daytime thermal updraft; air whooshes into the new vacuums with a basso sigh that rattles every diaphragm-shaped leaf. Thunder never arrives from the sky; the sky is inside these movements, the sky is the exhalation of plant muscle.

Material palette: still-warm pumice coated by the thinnest glass of lignin sap. Leaf edges microscopically serrated like obsidian flakes yet wet enough to dissolve skin oils. Spore dust fine as baby powder, magenta, released in sudden puffs whenever two stalks rub in cross-wind. Resin stalactites harden mid-precipitation, arrested motion caught as orange tear-glass. In hollow bladder-chambers one finds liquid marble—water held in tense meniscuses by reversible membrane sinew, glistening orbs the size of goats that roll when the floor tilts but never burst.

Rules of the world (never declared, merely enforced):  
1. Footfalls are answered. Plant neurology interprets pressure as interrogative, replies with osmotic shift. Where you step becomes momentarily softer and greener, as though the ground thanks you but instantly forgets.  
2. No edge lasts. Creeping cambium extends finger-petiole outriggers overnight; by dawn the gorge you crossed is a single bridge of netted veins. Paths are erased faster than memory.  
3. Respiration is currency. If you breathe deeply, the tissues nearest to your exhalation swell, reddening in ecstasy of CO₂. Several lungfuls and a coral frill will unroll above your head, shading you with deliberate grace. But take in the plant’s own fragrant oxygen and the reverse happens: a soft fold of leaf peels back to let you walk onward—payment accepted, onward fare granted.  
4. Fire is impossible. The air is too loaded with moisture bound in colloidal polysaccharide; but more subtly, the organism exudes anti-oxidant volatiles that seek and suffocate every flame-thought before ignition. Heat bends, steam gathers, but combustion is voted down at the molecular quorum.

Dynamics: hourly, the tilt of the whole floating mass adjusts. No tectonic plates clutch beneath; instead, buoyant corms deep in the core dilate or shrink like monstrous lungs. When they exhale, the entire edifice lists five degrees west, sending waterfalls of epiphylls sliding; when they breathe in, dew runs uphill. It happens in quick surges—ten seconds of world-shoulder shrug, then twenty of stillness—so that any human standing here would adopt the rhythm unconsciously into their own heartbeat after a single day.

Weirdness clusters around the vertex folds where the plant’s self-repair cannot keep up with its volume of ambition. Black-green crevices sometimes swell until they become swallowing mouths, then calcify into vaulted basilicas of petrified resin within minutes. Inside, older versions of the Hatchery are visible as amber ghosts frozen mid-motion: fossils of yesterday’s growth spiral, caught kaleidoscopically like a pressed book yet still half-alive, respiring as dark glass.

And no animals—so the occupant who somehow reaches here confronts an ecology of pure mirror. The plant developed every niche that an animal might have filled: photophore spots blink like owl eyes to scare off nothing, because nothing flies. Cilia arrays wave like insect antennae sampling pheromones no one emits. It is a theatre perpetually rehearsing roles for an audience that never arrived, and the fear it creates is not of loneliness but of the suspicion that, eventually, it will succeed in inventing you.
