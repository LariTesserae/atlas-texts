The **Deep Green Vault** is a hollow in the crust, a bubble of air trapped two hundred meters beneath the surface. It is not natural. No miners' pick broke it open; no aquifer ate it away. The vault was *exhaled* — a lung-shaped cavity left behind when some buried engine cooled and shrank. Its walls are cast iron, finger-thick in places, paper-thin in others, oxidized to bitter reds and bruised purples. Rivets the size of melons march in spiral seams, each one sweating a slow tear of condensation that never quite becomes a drop. That is the only water here: 1.3/3, just enough to keep the air tasting like metal left on the tongue.

Temperature is 2.8/3 — hot — but the heat is dull, metabolic, as though the iron itself is still digesting its own creation. Moisture flashes to vapor against the plates, re-condenses, and runs down the curves in silence. There is no echo; sound is 0.2/3. The emptiness sponges noise the way velvet sponges light. When you scrape your shoe, the scuff arrives late, flattened, already embarrassed.

Elevation 0.5/3 — below surface — yet the floor is not level. It has the sag of a deactivated diaphragm, lowest at the center where the engine once rested. There, the plating has peeled back like shed snakeskin, revealing a sift of black slag that behaves like soil. From this industrial detritus grows the only vegetation the place allows: one grove of greenhouse escapees, small as a parish chapel. Their scale is 1.0/3 — cathedral only if you are the size of a thumbnail.  

The plants are lush (2.2/3) but rationed; density 1.3/3 keeps them polite. Broad, wax-skinned *solanums* shoulder between copper pipes. Vines of unripe vanilla cling to thermometer wells, roots tickling mercury. Leaves are glossy, almost patent, engineered for low lumens. The single light source is a grid of cracked biolumes screwed into the ceiling — old safety strips, half-dead, giving 0.5/3 darkness punctuated by bruise-colored halos. Inside each halo the leaves glow swamp-green, as though lit from within by their own chlorophyll memory. Nothing photosynthesizes here; the plants live on heat-shocked sugars seeping from the rust, on vapor-phase nitrates, on time.

There is no fauna (0.2/3). No midge, no rust-mite, no bat. The only footsteps are yours, and they stop whenever you do.  

The built environment is scattered (1.4/3). Along one rib of the vault stands a line of five riveted lockers, doors ajar, interiors quilted with pale fungal lace. Inside: a single cracked face-shield, a tin marked "SAWBlast 1947," and a folded paper instruction for a boiler that was never installed. Nothing else. The lockers look like birds that returned to find the colony gone.

Tech is 1.9/3, the highest score on the ledger, yet it feels spent rather than present. Cast in the iron itself are ghost-impressions of gear teeth, embossed warnings, conduits ending blind. The technology has already happened; what remains is its outline pressed into metal like a fossil of intention. You can trace the negative space where a piston once punched, but the motion is missing. The dynamism (1.4/3) is slow: the creep of rust mountains growing one atom high per hour, the sag of plating by microns per annum, the eventual collapse that will take ten thousand surface rains to complete. The vault is young (erosion 1.3/3) only in geological comparison; by human years it is middle-aged and quietly expiring.

Weirdness (1.4/3) accrues at edges. Condensation sometimes nucleates around nothing, forming perfect glassy spheres that roll uphill toward the hot spots, then burst with a soft *tick* you feel in your teeth rather than hear. Certain angles of the iron show you reflections that arrive a second late. If you stand at the diaphragm’s center the grove aligns into a single symmetrical tree, an impossible composite, and your own silhouette becomes its missing heartwood. When you move, it falls apart again into separate stems.

Exit is not forbidden, only unprovided. Ladders climb the curve but stop ten meters short of the roof, rungs sliced clean as if by a giant tin-opener. A circular hatch overhead is dogged shut from the outside; its wheel spokes cast shadows like a frozen sun. No draft whistles through. Air circulates by a creep too gentle to feel.

Rules of the world, then:

1. Heat is a memory stored in metal; it can only be spent, never earned.  
2. Water exists only as the moment before a drop — surface tension is sovereign.  
3. Life borrowed industry’s garbage and made it briefly green; repayment will be exacted in iron oxide.  
4. Silence is absolute property; any sound you introduce is merely a loan, soon reclaimed.  
5. Light is feudal: each plant holds fealty to a single diode and dies if that lord fails.  
6. Nothing leaves intact. Even thought condenses on the walls and rolls back down as faint rust tears.

Stay long enough and you begin to suspect the vault is not a ruined chamber but a nursery for a future geode: iron skin, quartz heart, the eventual cavity lined with crystals grown from your own evaporated breath. You are the first seed of fauna, 0.2/3 moving quietly toward 1.0.
