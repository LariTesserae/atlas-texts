The lithosphere has folded itself into impossibility.  
Towering silhouettes of stone—no, stone-equivalents—rise and rise until they puncture the night sky, vanishing like stitch-ends into black felt. Their flanks are stilled avalanche faces, stepped and terraced by ten thousand years of salt wind rather than any hand of history. Each terrace holds a thin silver stream that does not roll downward but slithers laterally, bankless, as if gravity had agreed to sprawl sideways after long negotiations. From the cracks between these sideways creeks, condensation bleeds in steady drops you can watch ascending rather than falling. They coalesce into beads that float up the tower’s cold hide until, at some invisible altitude, they snap open into pale sheets of mist that re-seed the lacework of stream. The cycle is water-chewing its own tail, a slow, disciplined respiration.

At the crude elevation where alpine geography admits the idea of air thick enough to bear sound, every sound you make births a second copy, a half-second delayed, one octave lower. You speak and hear yourself answer from the void of a deep throat. Yet the world’s wider volume stays hushed; only the negative branches of noise—echo-gaps, inhale-creaks, the vacuum left when tiny claws retract from basalt—reach the ear in uneven pulses.

Plants have consented to fill the empty‐between. In the blackness, leaves open with the metallic click of shutters exposing long-latent emulsions. Each leaf is a lpped disk, glass-thin but lambent with its own cold cerise glow, which the flora use to photograph the darkness. A year is the time required for one full exposure; when the image is finally printed in the pigment of the leaf’s under-vein, the leaf dies, curls, detaches, and kite-flies downward as a single rigorous negative of the sky it served. There are forests of such negatives caught in the up-drafts of underground vents, spiraling in reverse gyres, silhouettes layering into a faintly quilted moonlight.

Because elevation is absolute, temperature is an abstract currency continuously confiscated by distance from ground. What heat remains has taken refuge inside living bodies. Even the few ice-craters glow faint blue, fed by the industrial metabolism of dense microbial mats vibrating at just-organ temperatures. These living furnaces generate microclimates large enough for lichen-owls and mirror-skinned salamanders who neither hunt nor flee but simply overlap territories—predator and prey notes written in the same key. Once every two centuries the residue heat is siphoned upward in a climbing storm of bioluminescent spores; the sky briefly unbuttons into a thousand spreading constellations that labor like furnace pistons, then close again.

There are no tools, no engines, yet every aspect suggests industry. Vast mineral shelves, weathered smooth, support what look like the negative spaces of abandoned cyclopean calipers left in bas-relief against the bedrock. Here and there, aquamarine currents run within perfectly circular grooves—pipes with no pipes, fluid remembering the pathway of machinery long dissolved. These veins shed turquoise phosphor light, bright enough to read the temperature by: the colder the hue, the colder the vein. One can place a bare palm against a channel and learn that one's own blood is in fact warmer than geothermal spindrift, though not by much.

Scattered across the terraces stand oxblood fungal towers fifty meters tall. Their caps perpetually drip a resin that hardens into shells. Each shell is a hollow mute bell. When a shell finishes hardening it detaches, rolls languidly to the void's edge, and is swallowed. Wind instruments perfected without breath.

A perceptual law governs scale here: whenever you attempt to fix a limit—horizon line, vertical edge, lattice boundary—the attempt itself doubles the perceived distance. The valley you believe lies within walking distance becomes a folded country by the moment you name it. Only by refusing to name it are you allowed to step across. This inversion is the quietest tyranny of the place; it keeps its towns and valleys unpopulated because thought keeps expanding them.

The fauna are patterns of calcium light weaving among plants, a density felt more as after-images on retinas than by actual head-counts. Some specimens you can only observe by their shadows cooling the condensation that wets your eyelids—ephemeral dark patches sliding across the membrane of the world. Others coil in fractal loops around the vertical rivers, looping because they grow faster than the rivers flow, never quite escaping themselves. They mute the last possible sounds by absorbing pressure waves into the lapidary inventory of their bones.

Time does not move horizontally; instead it composts in vertical columns. Reaching taller means growing older, not vertically but genealogically. A seed at a higher terrace grows into the adult memory of its lower ancestors; thus, the summits are senescent and crevices are juvenile, and the clouds are the senility of the entire chain.

Visitors (there are none) leave no footprints because anything that pressed downward today sinks by morning, refrozen into freshly minted negative space. The landscape forgets instantly; its balance is a slow mechanical respiration made of failing light, cold metabolism, and the luminous refusal of emptiness.
