The cliffs have not yet decided they are cliffs.  
Jet-black pumice, still half-viscous, climbs to the stratosphere as though somebody interrupted a fountain and froze it at the apex. These are the newborn bones of a place that doesn’t fully exist; each pinnacle loses upward light-years of height the closer you stand to it, because perspective itself is drafting the architecture. The elevation is extreme only while you do not look away—turn your head and the land slides back to shoulder-level plateaus, then slumps again when your attention returns. No summit lasts long enough for a name.

Everything echoes here, because the sky is a drum. The sound is not wind or thunder but an absolute, marrow-rattling silence inverted: a palpable over-pressure, a noise so loud it becomes antimatter. You feel it pressing the blood outward from capillaries. Your skull bones whistle their own thin cross-sections like flutes. Speech would crystallize mid-air and fall, ringing, but there is no language adequate; the tongue forgets watery beginnings and forms only rectangular phonemes that spawn right angles in frost on your lips.

Nothing drinks. Water is abolished: moisture is alloy. When the concept of thirst arises, it precipitates as an odorless aluminum nugget at the base of the throat. Collect enough of these and you can barter with the built scatter—shallow huts balanced on groins of cooling stone. The builders were here once, perhaps, or will be; the huts are intermittent memories of architecture more than finished dwellings. Stones still fold themselves around the idea of rafters, vertebrae solving into arches. Hammers, not yet smelted, lie latent as iron shadows on the ground.

Inside one half-formed cabin, a stair begins, carved from a single block that hasn’t acquired the trait of hardness. Tread it and the risers rubber-melt away under weightless steps; descending becomes ascending, becomes circling in place until dizziness chooses a new cardinal direction. There are hand-tools left waiting—flint, obsidian greased green, bone dragging salt on its own marrow—each one unset from gravity’s consensus. They vibrate in anticipation, primitive machines waiting for laws of physics to sign their contracts.

Between the huts the land is open yet starved. A town-sized emptiness contains five immutable trees. They bleed nitrogen petals in slow combustion. Their roots braid back into the air, drip upward to braid again, an endless fountain-braid whose tip fuses with thorax of the highest unstable cliff. From every flowering node hang gourds like tear-shaped bells. When the over-loud sky pulses, the bells try to ring out water, but achieve only subtraction: each knell leaves a negative space more parched than before, so that cheering the landscape drier becomes a ritual for the lush.

For the place is lush. Leaves darken because chlorophyll invents color it has never needed. Veins luminous teal conduct absence of water like ore. Fruit the size of hips sags without sap; split one open and you find only mirrors folded mirrorside in, reflecting the mouth that would have sucked them. Photographs of the place rot immediately into poems about thirst. The vegetation is extremely precise in its indifference.

There are no animals—never have been. Tracks exist anyway, crisp crescents printed in dust no wind moves. They fill themselves with expectancy, deepen, acquire depth as though expecting a weight that precedes the creature. A skeletal line of calcium is strobing in metamorphosis—vertebra elongating to plesiosaur, then collapsing to puzzle ring. Each slow metamorphosis leaves a lattice of pearl that will be among tomorrow’s primitive building blocks when someone finally steadies a hammer.

Light is dim but arrives sideways. Every angle of incidence bends 73 degrees, then fractures into moiré that writes backwards alphabets across stone. You can read these poems only by exhaling hot air; condensing breath reveals the letters as frost in reverse. Night never falls, only thickens from cobalt to black patent gloss. The stars are all exactly one copy of the same star, pasted repeatedly on a barrel vault that keeps rolling left.

The prime rule governing behavior here: Nothing completes itself. A rock only begins to shatter; a sentence forms its first consonant then forks off in silence; a life prepares ambition but arrests before act. Completion is thief of existence. Visitors who insist on finishing scaffoldings are gently unmade—edge by edge, joint vaporizing back to vague ambition drifting like smoke from an unused wick.

Get lost here and you remain lost only half-way; wander for eternity and you walk never more than forty meters in any direction, yet each step rolls the horizon farther open. There is comfort in the slow dynamic—every inconsistency just part of the long manufacture. Eventually the cliffs will elect a footing, the sky will dial down its freighted drum, the vegetation will condense into an orchard of mirrors nourishing ghosts of animals. But that is scheduled for a period well past the heat death of the observer.

For now, breathe the deafening hush, pocket one aluminum thirst-nugget, and leave the huts their half-roofed dreaming. When you cross the boundary back to where water behaves normally, the nugget will melt into an aftertaste of deliquescent laughter, and you will recall that the first thirst was never about water, but about the possibility of thirst itself—a negative space where desire trains itself to speak in angles still unborn.
