A grotto the size of a chapel clings, impossibly, halfway up the shear cliff that was born only three nights ago. The cliff is so new it is still proving its own angles: knife-edge ridges of basaltic glass flex like cooling fins; slabs the size of houses hiss and sing as the last detail-heat leaks out of their lattice. Every exhalation carries the smell of hot iron carried on a wind whose breath is cool and wet.  

At the water-rating 0.6 there is no standing water inside the grotto in the ordinary sense; instead, every surface is beaded with uncommitted moisture. Clear droplets cling to the needle-points of basalt, grow to perfect spheres, detach with deliberate slowness, then drift—no, jet—sideways; each droplet is charged from the stir of the roughened new stone, so they chase one another like plasma beads in miniature Van de Graaff storms. Where they burst, grey moss erupting from nothing drinks them down in single audible gulps that sound like fingers snapping, again and again. Vegetation 2.5 means the moss has companions: fern fronds only a finger-length long unfold and refurl within seconds, a living cinema loop; miniature banyans embroider the ceiling with aerial roots that drip condensed silver; red-black vines creep across the floor like spilled quicksilver. They fill the confined space until the air is heart-high and eye-rich, until the walls disappear and sight is only foliage repeating foliage, wet black stems reflecting themselves like dark glass. The air temperature, at 1.2, is just enough above freezing for the sap of a young world to move without breaking its skin.  

The grove-chapel is tech-level 1.8 without a single right angle or rivet; the mechanized here is botanical-industrial. Every branch is a copper filament armored in bark. Chloroplasts spiral inside like miniature motor-generators, flashing teal sparks at each heartbeat of photosynthesis. Tiny turbines of stamen spin in those same flashes, throwing out a low constant thrum—loud: 1.7—layered white noise just below the threshold of separating one pulse from the next. Static electricity arcs from leaf to leaf, seasoning the air with ozone warmed by sweet root-sap. The entire biome is a single closed-circuit engine, converting the cliff’s newborn elevation into pressure, gravity into sugar, sugar into pure voltage.  

Elevation 2.6 means only one way in exists for an outsider: the lip of the grotto is two human body-lengths below the outer lip of the spire, and beneath that, three hundred metres of still-cooling night. There are no paths and nothing built; even the thought of handholds would be an impertinence. You arrive perhaps by being born with wings, or by falling upward the night the cliff punched skyward and by pure fluke drifting in through the slit. Once inside, the space feels larger because density is 2.1—materials, sounds, smells, and plants press in close; every surface is another surface; small clearings open only to be braided shut again by a creeping vine or by the return drift of water-fog chased by progressions of sparks.  

Young erosion at 0.9 means the stone changes in front of one’s eyes. A hexagonal column detaches from the ceiling like apprentice marble, swings once like a pendulum, wedges into the vines. Before it can cool further its micro-fractures have gone green as the moss storms the crack, stitching it into living mosaic. Behind the new obstruction, an elliptical cavity appears: a fresh cell, belling open with the hiss of steam meeting dew. Every minute the shape is different; dynamic sits at 2.9, driven not by geology’s slow whim but by biology’s heedless acceleration on virgin substrate.  

Fauna is starkly 0.2—there are no metazoans—yet the fullness of vegetation with its own current loops, pounding pistils, and resonating fibers gives the impression of something alive beyond plant. If movement in the corner of the eye seems like the quick sidestep of a salamander it is only a leaf snapping its own shutter, harvesting photons in a feral mechanical reflex. The sound: continual, layered, loud, but strangely unlocated, the gurgle of copper blood in living bark, the sputter of wet electrum, the gasp of microstalactites losing their first infant droplets.  

No human language exists for time here. Clocks cannot survive the static. Every spark records and immediately overwrites. Sunlight arrives as remembered brightness from the opposite side of the cliff face; below that angle everything is the 0.2 darkness of depth and rock, except for the electric green pulse of foliage. In that rhythm a person would measure heartbeat by leaf shutter, dream of wristwatches hatched as ferns, and forget that there is a world outside with horizons. The of silence the space contains none; instead a storm of production, one engine spanning the scale of a cathedral closet, building its own inheritance every ten seconds.
