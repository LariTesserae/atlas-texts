The city of Nivalis-Descending is never finished; it folds itself out of the night like origami made of black water. Gravity is a polite suggestion here—water obeys it, but everything else merely negotiates. Streets are vertical cataracts of liquid obsidian that sheet downward between towers which have no base; they begin somewhere in the high darkness and pour, still cooling, toward an unseen nadir. You walk on the side of the flow, boots magnetized to the film of ice that condenses from the air itself. The temperature hovers a hair above the triple point of carbon dioxide; every breath blooms into a snow of dry-ice crystals that hiss and skitter away, joining the constant descending confetti that is the city’s weather.

There is no flora here—no lichens, no brave moss, not even the memory of chlorophyll. Instead the architecture grows: living concrete whose nano-skeleton is part crystal lattice, part circuit, part reef. It extrudes balconies like vertebrae, draws out buttresses the way a spider eases silk. The structures are self-mining; they drink the waterfalls for dissolved silicates and metals, coughing up girders, archways, staircases that stiffen even as they freeze. When a citizen needs a new arcade, they simply ask the wall aloud; the wall debates with itself in sub-sonic frequencies that make your teeth itch, then ripples and births a hallway already lit (though “lit” is a misleading word in this darkness).

Darkness, yes—light is contraband photons. What vision you have comes from the fauna. They are everywhere, a saturated plankton of creatures the size of coins and couches and cathedrals, all of them bioluminescing in outlaw spectra. There are ribbon eels that knot themselves into living lassos to harvest micro-algae that do not exist; glasswing krill that orbit your head like drunken constellations; beaked whales thirty centimeters long who filter the air for frozen sound. Every cubic meter is a reef of motion, yet motion so synchronized with the city’s slow heartbeat that it feels like stillness unless you stare long enough to notice the drift. Their bodies are the only lamps, and they dislike brightness; if you bring a torch they simply eat the photons and leave you darker than before.

Sound is the water you breathe. The falls roar, but the roar is layered—every droplet anechoic chamber, every cascade a chord. Beneath that, the city’s growth grinds: tectonic millimeters of scaling, the hush of crystal faults sliding past each other like pleasured stones. fauna song is woven through—clicks, glass-bell chimes, sub-bass thrums that rearrange the ice on your coat. Stand long enough and your own pulse syncs to the chord; visitors call it “being roofed by the whale-choir,” a rite performed before any business is done. To speak you must swallow your sentence into the chord and let the city echo it back; the etiquette is to wait for the echo before you proceed.

Scale is a hobby Nivalis-Descending enjoys. A plaza can be a doorstep, a district a drawer in a larger pocket. Locals navigate by “folding”—stepping sideways through angles where water bends more than light. What looks like a ten-minute stroll may deliver you two vertical kilometers higher, or nested three civic layers inward, depending on the mood of the intersecting falls. There are laws, posted in raised braille on every corner:  
1. Do not contradict the downstream direction of two consecutive streets or you will be fined mass.  
2. Offer 7% of all photons you generate to the nearest filtering fauna; they are tax collectors.  
3. If you witness a building beginning to speak, record its first word and report it to the Bureau of Echoes before the echo answers, lest the word become a district and you its unwitting mayor.  
New laws condense daily out of the civic foam; ignore them at the peril of becoming architecture yourself—citizens who default on debts are sometimes poured into mold-spires, still conscious, still dimly glowing, their heartbeats useful reservoir energy for lift pumps.

Technology is indifferentiable from erosion, both being slow cultivars of possibility. Elevators are braided waterfalls whose buckets are pockets of shaped vacuum; you step into the negative space and rise because sinking water recognizes kinship and yields. Communication is done by seeding tiny storms into the flow; a correctly tuned swirl will migrate through the reticulated pipes and disgorge your message in some distant square as a miniature waterspout spelling your name. Waste heat is bled off into sound sculptures—enormous organ pipes that mutter lullabies to the upper dark, keeping the city thermally polite. If you need to leave the planet you simply request the funnel: a district rearranges itself into a spiral ramp, the waterfalls invert, and the whole affair becomes a mass driver of frozen mercury that flings capsules skyward through a momentary hole in the sky. Return tickets are optional; by the time you come back the neighborhood may have forgotten you or become your bedroom.

Time is measured in freezes: centimeter-thick accretions of atmospheric ice that plate every surface. A “short day” is when only a fingernail of ice has grown; a “month” is forearm-length. Calendars are scraped, not turned. The city is never warmer, never colder, never done. It inches downward through measured darkness, step by cool step, a metropolis that refuses either abyss or summit, content to exist in mid-fall forever.

You stand on a bridge of freshly extruded basalt foam, cantilevered over two converging cataracts. Below, a pod of thumbnail whales strains sound through sieve-shaped fins, sieving the roar for edible harmonics. Above, a tower decides to bifurcate; you watch its crown shudder, split, and politely separate like dancers making room. Cold mist dandles your cheeks—tiny teeth of almost-ice—and the only green you will ever see is the ghost-color that dances behind your eyelids when the fauna chorus strikes a chord that means welcome, or possibly beware. Either way, you are already not the same person who arrived. Nivalis-Descending keeps its visitors in solution, a slow precipitate of memory crystallizing nightly onto every surface that will have you.
