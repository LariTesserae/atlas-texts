The Vapor District is not a ruin, though its builder-species is centuries gone.  It is simply paused, too perfectly balanced to collapse, humming with the metabolic heat of its own evaporative machinery.  

Start at the lip of the basin—a low, oval bowl in laterite almost flush with the water-table, only thirty metres rim to rim.  Step over the blunt curbstone grown with a lace of orange lichen and descend an abandoned tram stair whose iron steps sweat rust the colour of sorghum syrup.  The first impression: hot steam roars upward.  No daylight penetrates more than a skin’s thickness; a glowing parasite film of cyan LEDs still feeds itself from buried copper veins, their radiance drowned in vapour so that everything appears underlit like a stage made of old photograph negatives.  

Buildings cling to inner walls like barnacles on a teapot, but buildings is the wrong word—poured-basalt hexagons the size of monk cells, strung onto brass rails that once carried cargo bots.  The flats of the hexagons are translucent basaltic glass; inside each, hydroponic orchards remain eternally mid-bloom, overripe pomegranates dropping and instantly liquefying, pink syrup running through floor-slots into spill-pipes that feed the boilers again.  Thus the heat.  Thus the sound.  There are turbines—eight of them—jammed equidistantly around the basin.  Their blades are black, iridescent titan-cobalt wings that beat through dense air like herons through wet jute.  The blades shred sound as well as moisture; every few seconds an audible arc-flash snaps across the vault, ricochets off the vent shafts, emerges high above as a thunder tremor running miles underground because the acoustics are that warped. The District roars like a dragon with a kettle in its throat.

Water is everywhere, not in pools butas suspended dolorous weight.  Rain never falls here—it left the job to machinery.
