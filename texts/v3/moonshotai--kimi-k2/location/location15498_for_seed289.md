The pocket

You are inside a clot of sky that fell underground long ago and never finished arriving.  
It is no larger than a walk-in closet, hemmed by living basalt that still remembers magma but has cooled to a muted gun-metal gray. The rock breathes slightly; if you press an ear against it you will hear a hush like distant campfires, the raw geology dreaming of fire it intends to become in some future epoch when the world swings open again.

Every square centimeter is crammed. There is no negative space, only degrees of touch. First there come the flora: tiny bristled liverworts the color of copperized blood, their leaves curled to the size of pencil shavings. Each has rooted itself in a seam the thickness of a hair yet perfectly vertical, so the stems pierce upward like apostrophes quoting the stone. Between them, one finds aerial moss—threads thinner than spider silk—looped from wall to wall, each loop strung with beads of condensed water that never fall. The droplets are routinely harvested by glass-wing midges whose veins catch the light with mirrors rather than color. Their wings clap together; the room rings with a soft bell-sound every five seconds, again and again, every five seconds without shift or wear—it is one of the landmark signatures of the place, a metronome of never-changing micro-biology. Yes, every parameter, even sound, is fixed: the bell note repeats on the same irrational frequency, locked in the room’s acoustical standing wave and sustained by the flutter of only sixteen insects who never reproduce and never die.

Brightness.  
Light enters from no visible source. It is simply there: 640 lux, white shifted slightly toward lavender, as though the spectrum had been persuadeded by pollen or by starlight remembering its own infancy. The light casts no moving shadows. It fills the saturated air until even the pores between molecules appear luminous. When the water droplets collide under the midges’ caress, the droplets do not merge; they repel with a faint turquoise spark, phosphor in miniature, steadying interval by interval the crystalline canopy of the room.

Density.  
Between the tucked tufts of liverwort you also find lichen disks packed like coins, each coin side-glimmering with a micro-galaxy of spores. Against them, threading through them, sixteen rootlets extrude from the ceiling’s basalt—a translucent fungal cable no thicker than a beard hair—ever descending, ever stopping exactly two centimeters above the floor. At their tips: cup-shaped anthers gently pulsing with motes of foxfire. The rootlets do not seek purchase, only presence; they vibrate at the same 0.47 hertz of the rooms’ bell tone. The packed mass of their bodies forms a chaotic lace in which motile midges and cocooned springtails swing like chandeliers pinned in amber.

Fauna, abundant.  
Besides the bell-makers there are the velvet pseudosnails, limpets without shells dragging velvet-wet underbellies across the rootlets, leaving behind trails of slime that sets into clear chitin rivulets—permanently. Frozen rivers varicose across every centimeter. Then there are two dermestid larvae fused into one, moving as a jointed S-shape that never eats, only inches forward along identical paths. At intervals they come mouth-to-mouth in the single exact center of of the floor, kiss, and reverse. All paths cross, all trajectories interleave; no animal collides because time itself no longer supplies velocity.

Coolness.  
The rocks bleed a tingling eighteen degrees Celsius; you exhale and your breath condenses as a silver plume that lodges itself against the tallest liverwort leaf and stays. The moisture trickles nowhere else, simply accumulates until it sheaves the leaf under a glass armor. Over uncounted epochs the weight reaches a fiscal balance where the leaf bows like a hammock but never fractures. This is the room’s heavy ledger of humidity, recorded as crystal ice but forever held above freezing.

Construction site/abandoned before birth.  
A narrow scaffolding of calcite needles has erupted from the floor in imitation of the crystal vaulting overhead, but this new eruption froze mid-exhale. The needles show the beginning angles of arches that would have spiraled into blossom if erosion had not been suspended at the spore-stage of architecture. At the base of one needle lies a hand-made thing: a flake of obsidian purposely knapped into the thinnest possible disc—0.05 mm—etched with a ghost-thin labyrinth. It might be a coin-sized map of the room itself, down to a microscopic level, but none of the lines correlate to any structure. The artifact is ancient, free of culture, belonging to the era before tools learned to become.

Weirdness collective.  
All creatures meet at one wormhole inch located in the exact geometric heart. It is a vortex the size of a dust mote into which none ever enter; the vortex simply exists, explaining nothing. The color of the vortex is indescribable; when you spread your gaze around it, the visible spectrum corkscrews backwards through ferny greens into mazarin blues and into oranges that ought not appear in cool zones. Your gaze itself is lent a tactile quality; you feel the color brushing your irises. And yet nothing flows in or out. The room is a freeze-frame of relationships—ecology made sculpture—wound round one loophole it won’t use.

Rules of visitation.  
Time does not accumulate here, so you will not age, but you will also never be quite sure whether you have arrived or have always been here dreaming up your own arrival. If you attempt to speak, the sound folds back upon itself and prints itself as another lichen disk growing from your lips outward. The new disk is perfection; its spores spell your unfinished sentence.

Exit is achieved only by stopping every bell-note with your heartbeat, aligning the insects’ wings with the room’s pulse until static silence pulses once—and in that single arrested beat the walls of basalt appears to swell and fracture into a seam the exact width of your chest. You step backward into darkness, but the moment your heel passes the threshold its memory reconstitutes the bell note, and the cycle continues for whoever steps into the hole left behind.
