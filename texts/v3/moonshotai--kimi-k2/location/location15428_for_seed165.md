Everything is at the wrong angle.

From the saddle ridge you stand on, two impossible ranges lean into each other like the halves of a roof that were never meant to be seen from directly underneath. The cold is not sharp—it is horizontal, sliding stiffly along the basalt and hoarfrost in long sheets. Blood warms and then cools again within seconds; heartbeats echo dully inside boots because sound has nowhere to go but sideways along the tilt. The air is thick with it—the Loud carries on and on, though no new event makes it. It is the accumulation of every avalanche that has finished falling, every glacier moan that has finished ending, every shriek of tectonic shearing that has finished shearing, but instead of dying the noises are knitted into the space itself. They exist because the space remembers.

The rock underfoot is uneven but already shaped by ages of exposition: gull-edges smoothed, relief crests polished to sensuous humps, every step reminding you that erosive completion has happened long before you arrived. Deep stress-cracks in the flanks of the bordering peaks have filled, eon by eon, with ice not as a guest but as an inserted organ: frozen arteries keeping the stone stable yet monstrously heavy. Each seamed overhang has sprouted its beard of stiff grey lichen—vegetation only in the strictest taxonomy, because the cold has arrested all motion inside their cells. They photosynthesize nothing; they only hold the shape of yesterday for tomorrow to examine.

Water is a coded presence, an old story rather than current event. Droplets hang suspended in the seams between one tilted strata and its neighbor, clinging to both at once, unwilling to complete the act of falling. When wind intrudes—a wind you do not feel because it exists in a drift-plane three meters above your skull—the suspended droplets wriggle but do not drop. You perceive them as holes punched into visible reality through which the last steam of far‐off oceans still refuses to vanish. Between cracks, thin films freeze, thaw, refreeze in micro-cycles happening too quickly for eyesight, but the aggregate effect over millennia has been only confusion: grooves inside grooves, re-etchings overwriting the same whisper. Rain discovered this place once and still hasn't quite decided whether it is allowed to finish descending. The evidence knots itself into tiny pressure domes of glass-bright ice no wider than a pinky nail; they bristle everywhere like transparent sea urchins.

Built things—false gods or forgotten markings, impossible to say—appear partway down the slope at distended intervals. They are stone pedestals, waist-high, each bearing a single object no larger than an infant’s heart: a basalt wedge fitted into the stone so precisely that light refuses its gaps; a nothing-shaped hollow drilled exactly into the stone’s core and then left empty; a cylinder of ice frozen once into position and never allowed to melt. These artifacts are scattered by the same law that governs meteorite distribution: few, far between, inevitable. Whatever culture touched them lacked tool-use—shots of worked flint are absent—only the intentionality of placed void. You are not welcome, but neither are you forbidden. You are one more inanimate datum in their ledger.

The peaks climb visibly while you stare, contradicting horizons until you forget which way remains “up.” Clouds, or things pretending at clouds, squat directly on the summits—grey inside grey, shapes never assuming the cotton-wool of normal atmospheres but instead stretching down like the mould-stuff that grows on spoiled fruit. They possess no internal motion: the light is too weak to illuminate them in volumes, merely a muffled egg-blue glaze of dusk that never advances into night, never returns toward noon; the place forbids suns and moons alike. Yet the Loudness of the sky matters more than any remembered illumination. It flags raw-pressure gaps in the atmosphere that your ears reinterpret as organ chords, rumbling forevers braided together without origin.

Fauna visits rarely—more correctly, it deposits pieces of itself and vanishes. During the descent you come across a single white vertebra the size of your fist lying incongruously on a shelf of volcanic glass. A quarter of a mile further you meet its twin on the other side of the ridge mirror, mirror-position alignment. The bones are weathered to identical smoothlessness, edges ringed with faint quartzite corona as though the calcium retroactively mineralized into stone once exposed to these airs. You have no explanation for how the beast that owned them traversed the gap between places two mountains wide when there is no ascertainable path. You simply understand that they were placed at equal distance from a centerless center, where the loud quiet keeps perfect count.

At the lowest visible saddle two valleys yawn open. They are identical but misphased; by lifting your head you can look into one at ground level while seeing the other superimposed as a translucent afterimage spiraling in opposite direction. This is where weirdness articulates its clearest tonal argument: space has doubled back, not folded because folding implies motion; rather the two trajectories are present at once like train tracks fused side-by-side without touching, each an absolute duplicate occupying only a half of reality’s attention span. Walking toward either valley changes nothing inside the larger dynamic, which is Never Changing. The equilibrium has arrived and is sitting on its own momentum, perfectly satisfied to remain itself. You feel the tug of a decision without the promise of consequence—every step belongs to the same untold choir, already past.

Sometimes the Loud manifests as a distant horn blast of inverted gravity, and every droplet nearest the ridge simultaneously experiences a hiccup of weightlessness. They hover half a millimeter further from the rock for one immobile instant, reverse direction, and settle back as though applauding the idea quietly. These instants are the only heartbeat allowed here. They repeat at non-time intervals—perhaps every eleven thousand years, perhaps seventeen seconds—units that the ridge refuses to disclose because duration itself has been smoothed out among the strata.

There is no technology to distract creatures into forgetting. No hammer, no fire, no scratching of symbols—not because they are forbidden, but because they have already been resolved—like an equation whose answer is “zero” but the approximation process continues for eternity along shelves of eroded correctness. Knife edges of frost hold, not cut. Gaps between striations ink themselves in with permanent twilight. And even though the place is at last precisely what it intends, dimness scratched upon dimness by a sound older than telescopes, it does allow you one small intimate signal before it releases you or doesn’t: the faint click of a left-behind vertebra somewhere behind you fitting back into its ghost skeleton with a sound you will never remember hearing except as dream-temperature, exactly one degree colder than your last heartbeat.
