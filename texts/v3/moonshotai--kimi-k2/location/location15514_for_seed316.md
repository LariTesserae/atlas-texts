It begins as a sound.  

Not a noise, not yet—just pressure, a memory of thunder pressed flat against the ear-drums of anyone who wanders this place. A sound already broken into its constituent resonances, older than ears, so vast it forgets it was ever vibration and becomes plain existence. A standing wave that occupies the whole sky, the ground, every droplet and vein. A roar that went hoarse eons ago yet keeps roaring all the same.

Under that sound the low, messy plain spreads out forever, a continent collapsed into its final breath. There was mountain here once; now every remnant ridge is polished to a knee-high lip, surfaces so worn they reflect dim light like cataracts lakes. The water is under everything: not lakes or rivers but a-floods, capillary sheets lying millimetres beneath an eggshell crust. A footstep brays out circles that run inward forever, collapsing until they meet and annihilate each other in sighing fractures. Every sundered crust immediately wicks water up its inner walls and reseals with a soft lip as though the world were tonguing a wound closed.

Vegetation is goblin-sparse but huge: solitary hangers-on roseate with impossible blood-purple leaves, each leaf the size of sails. They stand tripod on half-dissolved stalks—three per plant, perfectly spaced so nothing else can crowd between. Between them the land is utterly occupied, but not by things that can be trusted to remain solid. Every square pace contains:

- one stoma-breathing slab, sweating warm distilled water into the air

- a lattice of microbial films no thicker than breath, packed so densely they behave like porcelain

- the auroral trace of some collapsed insect lineage remembered only by the shape their exoskeletons left in the bacterial jelly

Nothing wastes space; the density is theological, matter and spirit jammed so tightly reason turns into liquid and drips out of thought.

Temperature is equatorial-slow, the kind of heat that makes joints dilate and memory soften. Condensation beads on nothing visible. You will never be dry; your clothing grows second skins of scale-like fungus overnight, sweet-smelling lilac molds that ring like bells when you break their seals.

Fauna is statistically extinct. Yet the roar itself sometimes folds, and within the fold something scuttles—an afterimage of a crab built only from the fossil sound of a claw scratching basalt. Blink and it’s a ripple in the standing wave, vanishing back into whatever frequency gave it this hour’s version of bones.

Light is an heirloom, handed down by retiring suns. The sky heaves with continent-sized sheets of antique glow the colour of candle stubs. Sometimes a tear in them falls, thudding to the ground as a bruised luminance that puddles waist-deep before dissolving back into the empiric overcast. It is never bright, never dark—only always half-remembered.

Embedded in that dimness stand the built things: not architecture but gestures someone long past left half finished and the planet then spent three billion years erasing from language. A single arch of silk-thin stone in mid-collapse, arrested so completely that erosion itself wore smooth against time and lay down to rest as its own residue. A floor of hexagonal glass tiles sunk flush with the skin of the marsh, each tile incubating a trapped atmosphere from a previous world-model. Some mornings you can see another sky spinning inside them like fish in lenses. There are never two in sight at once; they sequester themselves behind one another like polite ghosts.

The weirdness is environmental gospel. Gravity wells have blemishes here. Every few leagues the horizon hiccups, slipping several metres downward with the sound of innumerable jaws closing all at once; whatever was crossing the lip simply reappears elsewhere, reconstituted from its own echo. Water sometimes condenses horizontally, forming curtains that spin out hundreds of kilometres then fold into harp strings of crystal that squeal at the standing roar until they explode into steam, each explosion adding one perfect fresh layer to the flat continent.

Continents migrate too. The plain is so old and the erosion so absolute that each square is a platelet in a colloidal fluid. You may step onto one section and realise minutes or months later that your camp now sits on what used to be an ocean trench three thousand kilometres to the west. Nothing tips over; the land rearranges imperceptibly the way myth rearranges a culture: same rhythms, new latitudes.

Noise. Remember the sound. On clear nights it modulates into almost words—half a lullaby from when the world was molten, half the groan of the planet’s copper core thinking in its sleep. If you stay long enough the ear stops looking for omens and recognises the roar as biology: a planetary heartbeat riding on silt.



Rule #1: Every action takes volume. Shout and you risk condensing enough water out of the saturated sky that your cry falls back as freezing rain. Open a notebook and the displaced air births a temporary delta where reeds made of quiet unfurl and begin pollinating speculation. Nothing is propulsion without also being excavation.

Rule #2: No tools longer than childhood last. A hammer left handle-down hydrates slowly from the inside, wood cells drinking until it bursts into a bouquet of feathery pulp and alloy petals. Metal, stone, bone—all become raw material for the next scheme of flowers. Technology unhands itself.

Rule #3: If you witness the same landscape twice a week apart, apologise quietly; the land is only trying different bodies the way a dream tries new clothing. Respect its embarrassment. Address condensation patches as guests, call the glass tiles poems, and do not ask the standing wave whether it is mother, monster, or machine. 

Leave.  
The place does not mind either way.
