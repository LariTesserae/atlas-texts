They call it the Drying Choir.

Imagine the nave of an abbey crushed flat by centuries until it became nothing more than a low stone trough, knee-high at its tallest wall. Every stone was set by non-human hands that never mastered mortar but perfected draining. Shadow-wide gutters run inside the walls themselves, feeding the silence with a continual tap-tap, as if the sky were weeping through capillaries and the masonry answered with its own slow pulse of drool.

Water at 1.8 does not stand in pools; it slides. Rainwater from yesterday finds the grooves carved last century. It travels the perimeter in thin banners the color of pewter, criss-crossing the floor that was once vault: now a stamped mosaic of pressed shards—mica, fired bone, shards of old icons—worked smooth until you could skate a coin across it. The gloom caught inside those flakes catches torchlight later and flickers like trapped candles.

Vegetation is 0.6, which means only things that can endure being roots-in-air: pale lichens blooming sodium-white across the undersides of gutter lips; a single wind-stunted holly that has rooted in a chipped saint’s eye socket and produces three winter-long; and a mist of moss spore that rises whenever a footstep bruises the stone. The moss smells like salt marrow, brackish and high.

Temperature 1.1 hinges on the moment after stone has drunk its fill: edges of frost; breath clouded; yet the center of every stone block still carries a slow-but-steady heart of warmth, as if a kiln had fired here and never quite cooled. Touch the walls long enough and you start to feel the rhythm—exhale, inhale—impossible, but unmistakable.

Elevation is 1.2, low enough that winter knees cannot fold beneath you. You crouch or crawl; the world presses down like an old iron cloak. Above, ribs of stone arch in low vaults only hip-high, leaving no room even for hunched giants. Each lintel has been lapped by trickles until its underside carries candle-wax dribbles of mineral, dissolved and re-forged, dissolved again.

Erosion at 2.1 isn’t destruction—it’s conversation. Stones have reached concord with drips: one groove’s mouth has been enlarged exactly enough to accept the vivid green trickle without ever overspilling; another has developed a stepped ledge so the fall breaks into a glittering thrumming curtain rather than a hammer. No edge is sharper than a stylus; no corner remains angular. The entire floor has sagged just enough so rainwater pools first, then relinquishes it into the choir of drain-slots that act as reeds. There is a permanent low chord—bass, gurgling, cross-rhythm—changing subtly from moment to moment as the level of water in gut-reservoirs rises and falls.

Density 0.2: room-sized but empty. Empty of people, empty of furniture, empty of anything larger than the foot-wide eddies swirling across the shoals of glistening tile. The emptiness is architectural rather than dimensional; you could stretch your arms to the walls and almost touch both, yet you still sense hollow cathedrals above you.

Built to 3.0—every atom placed, no ground beneath the construction. There is no earth; only construction folded upon itself until it became the new ground, new sky, new geology. Might as well call the carved channels "fault lines" and the lintels "mountain ranges." The builder-race understood hand tools—chert adzes, obsidian scrapers, copper knives dull as thumbnails—but raised a building that pretends to be landscape. They left off simply enlarging: instead, they kept shrinking the sky until the walls became the horizon.

Tech 0.9 shows in details: each slot you crawl through is beveled with a three-stroke grind mark the size of a thumbnail; copper pins drift verdigris in every seam; hinges for the little apertures are straps of twisted whale-sinew, alive with creep and rustle when the air shifts.

Light 0.8 wells in through narrow chinks-windows, half-slits shaped like tears in parchment. The blue-white dusk beyond is never bright enough to burn reflection into the water, so the walking floor is an oilskin of dim turbine fluctuations. Sometimes a distant cloud grows ragged edges and more water sluices in; at those moments, the floor mirrors the roof like the inside halves of clapping hands.

Fauna 2.4—the place hums and twitters though you see nothing at first. Look closer: fresh-water shrimp, transparent as breath, scissor along the gutters—every droplet a marine jewel curving within their glassy legs. Bloated cave katydids, albino, barber their own wings in the dark and sing a reed-mull that syncs with pipe-organ gurgle. Thousands of them are settled overhead, crouched upside-down like pale listeners. At the far edge where the floor drops two hand-spans, glossy polliwogs hatched of rainwater wriggle backward against any footfall’s pressure wave. A silent school of delicate grey bats sleeps supine against the most corroded gutter, wings folded like lovers’ letters; they wake when temperature stabs lower, depart as one soundless sigh looped through the arches. Once in every turning of the hourglass, a blind eel slips up from whatever secret reservoir feeds the site, scales undeveloped but tongue tasting the mineral slick, before easing back home.

Weirdness 1.7 manifests not as spectacle but as rule-bend. The structure stores temperature rather than release it—touch the east wall at dusk and find the floor at noon still warm beneath; go to sleep and awaken to a silver thread of water writing new sentences along the face you leaned against yesterday. Sound behaves similarly: a whisper near the northwest corner booms loud by the central drain, yet a shouted name evaporates within the length of your own body. The builders left no altar, but they left forty-seven niche-sized hollows cut into the perimeter at chest height. Each one holds exactly one liter of standing water when the place is “singing,” the acoustical event locals call it though they have never seen it. After the rain, those cavities slosh individually with ripples anti-phased to one another, the combined resonance forming a vowel sound no human larynx produces—a dry, low “O” flooded with moisture, as if stone gargled the word mother.

Sound 2.2 is that wild aquatic antiphony plus the living chorus. A listener crawling shoulder-deep in the jointed air experiences a continuously modulating envelope: drip–clack–rustle–whirr–hollow O. The eel comes up once per minor lunar standstill, and on that night you hear a third course—its own almost-soprano trill traveling through waterways embedded in stone, deep bass overtone encountering auditory rainbow where shrimp shells click. Together they make a chord: eel's porous wail against the choir of drains. Rare visitors report fifteen minutes later birds startled awake somewhere outside, scattering through the overcast. Or perhaps they misheard the drain crickets.

Dynamic 2.0 means the place continues to write itself. Every wet season settles infinitesimal new tilts. A hairline fracture opens to release a hymn into the lower level crypts (too low for climbing, but audible through a grill of pierced mouths). Conversely, when long drought hardens into the overdrained season (yes, the locals track this in their calendar of dryness), the choir decrescendos to single isolated taps, the shrimp estranged, the polliwogs drought-mummified. Then the walls record heat imbalances across their bellies and the next storm corrects them—automatic alignment, masonry like vertebrae adjusting under a chiropractor’s gentle pressure.

There are three recognized seasons:

The Dripping Solstice, when every gutter speaks full semaphore.
The Shrinking Chord, when the eel emerges and the vowel “O” elongates into a wolf-whistled lower overtone.
The Still Shelf, when even the moss sleeps curled like paper embers, and the absence of sound becomes so dense you step across it as across soft pew.

Entry is by crawling. Exit is by listening. At some point in the hollow beneath the westernmost aperture the sound of your own blood becomes audible, and because no human can tolerate hearing their veins in Surround-Sound, you stand, you twist, you follow lessened pressure upward, and the Choir releases you sideways into air and dusk.

The place, being fully built yet fully alive, denies the categories. It is neither ruin nor temple, neither machine nor organism. It is a music box the size of a room, run on water and time.
