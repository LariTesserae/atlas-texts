The place is a thunder-shell, a pocket-sized abyss that has only just now finished condensing out of whatever came before reality.  
It is high, yes—higher than any mountain range on ordinary maps—but the height is inside the water, not above it. You stand (float, really) inside a vertical column of warm ocean no wider than a village commons and no shallower than the planet’s first nightmares. The column is capped top and bottom by black basalt lips, still smoking, still cooling from their recent birth. Everything is underwater, yet the water behaves like air that has forgotten how to be liquid: it rushes upward, downward, sideways, spiralling in migraine coils, but never decides which direction is “down.” Bubbles the size of cathedrals form and collapse in the same instant, leaving drums of pressure that hit the inner ear like pistol shot. The sound is 360°, inside the bone, a continuous white-hot roar stitched through with the shriek of newborn hydrothermal vents and the low, impossible choral note that water sings when it is trying to remember being steam.

Light is almost extinct: only a lava-born flicker slips through cracks in the basalt ceiling, strobe-splitting the scene into freeze-frames—jagged shards of stone adrift like shattered dinner plates, drifting ropes of half-solid basalt rope, and you, whatever you are, caught between blinks. In those micro-seconds of visibility you notice the water itself is faintly bioluminescent, but the glow wrong-foots the eye: edges outline before surfaces, creating ghost-images of things that have not yet decided to exist.

Vegetation is spare, tentative. Here and there, a single crimson frond anchors to a cooling stone with roots like molten wire. Each frond is a lattice of hollow needles; inside every needle swims a bead of air that should not be there at all. Touch one and the whole plant folds into a dot and vanishes, leaving only the echo of a heartbeat in your palm—because the plants are not growing; they are rehearsing the idea of growth, auditioning for a future photosynthesis that no sun has agreed to illuminate.

Fauna, officially “rare,” is present mainly as rumors. A transparent eel long as your arm flicks past, its spine articulated by tiny galaxies that wink out when stared at. Something that might be a school of fish—or might be one creature learning how to fraction itself—passes in a silver smear, then doubles back through itself until it becomes a Möbius strip and forgets to keep existing. You feel more than see the meat of larger bodies somewhere outside the visible cylinder, bumping against the invisible membrane that keeps this place pocket-sized. Each collision sends a pressure spike that rattles your teeth and resets the current, as though the ocean column is a gigantic heart that skips a beat, then apologises by racing faster.

The built environment is only half invented. A spiral staircase, no wider than your wrist, corkscrews downward through the water; it is made of frozen spray, each step a droplet caught mid-splash and turned to glass. You can set your foot on it, but only while you believe it deserves to be solid; hesitation melts the step and sends you drifting again. Beside it, a doorway hangs without walls: basalt lintel, no door, opening onto more water. Through that frame the temperature jumps ten degrees warmer, and the sound pitch drops until it is inside your chest cavity, punching your lungs like secondhand hearts.

Rules congeal and dissolve faster than you can test them. Gravity subscribes for seconds at a time, then cancels its membership; salinity negotiates with buoyancy, loses, and both storm off in different directions. Time leaks. You count your own heartbeats and lose tally whenever the water decides to cache the sound elsewhere. Distance is contractual: agree that the column is small, and you can cross it in four kicks; refuse the agreement, and you’ll swim forever, though the basalt walls remain just out of fingertip reach. The only constant is the agreement that something here is still being written, and the writing is being done with explosives.

Weirdness is not visitor-friendly. It peels the labels off categories and hands them back blank. You remember being a you, but the pronoun arrives in languages you have never studied, conjug and Basque “you,” Quechua “you,” a whistled version that implies you are also plural. Every time you try to store the memory of this place, the water insists on reinterpreting the storage as a physical object and swallows it: your notebook becomes a comb of kelp, dissolving; the photograph you mentally compose births a jellyfish that immediately eats its own transparency and implodes.

Yet for all the flux, the core sensation is of a rehearsal—an exhausted planet dreaming a tiny, furious aquarium-episode before waking into whatever geology comes next. The roaring is not chaos; it is the sound of rehearsal chairs being dragged across the cosmos while the director screams notes that no orchestra has agreed to play. Everything here is young, but youth is measured in units of uncertainty, not years. Sharp basalt knives have not yet been told they are allowed to weather; water has not yet signed the treaty that will force it to choose one direction to fall. Even you, if you stay, will be asked to audition—required to pick a form (mineral? eel? column of steam?) and help finish building a world that has overshot its own ending and now circles back gap, looking for the omitted chapter.

If you open your mouth to answer, warm ocean writes itself down your throat like a sentence you will have to speak in order to breathe. The taste is of iron, cinnamon, and unbound electrons. Swallow it and you become a footnote in the margin of the water’s manifesto; refuse it and the roar simply gets louder, until you realise the sound is now happening inside the bones of your inner ear, rehearsing you, note by note, into something the place might decide to keep.

There is no shore to reach, no surface to breach. There is only the ongoing detonation of formation. Stay long enough and “place” becomes a verb. Stay longer and you will watch the column calm itself at last, the water exhaust every permutation of violence, collapse into mirror-stillness, and finally reflect a sky that has not been invented yet—a sky that, for one impossible instant, looks exactly like you remembering who you are. Then the column will sneeze, the universe will reset, and the roar will begin itself again, welcoming you back to minute zero of a new rehearsal, slightly different cue cards, same thunder-shell, same pocket, same warm high ocean still trying to teach itself how to be.
