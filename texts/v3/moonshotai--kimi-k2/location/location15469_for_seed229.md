**The Grove of Hollow Eons**  
*An airborne thirteensome of living islands afloat three thousand meters above a burnt-white salt pan, where the only water is a memory pressed into crystals and the only sky is dim gold dust.*

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––

You arrive on the ladder of wind.  
A rope-net lift woven from the hair-thin tendrils of the *sheargrass* clatters as it climbs, every gust sounding like hawk bells. The lift has been here since no one quite knows when: the knots are pre-metal—stone toggles, beetle shells, wax-stiffened cordage—yet the way they adjust their own tension each swing looks oddly recent, as if the net were dreaming its own improvements.

When the platform heaves over the rim you step out onto **leaf-light ground**. The islands (you begin to count them—thirteen impossibles stacked like coins seen edge-wise) are not solid. They are great bole-like sheaves of vegetation braided around bones of bluish pumice. Roots the color of aged parchment grip the air itself, crystallizing it into anchor points; if you stare past them the salt pan below is visible through translucent gaps, flat as an erased thing.

______Water  
No pools, no trickle. Life gets by on **fog-veins**: twisting hollow vines whose inner ribs condense the low-lying gold dust‐mist (itself nothing more than microscopic salt prisms and exhaled resins) into glass-thin streams. Each vein ends suspended in mid-air; droplets bloom ating and are **licked away at once** by toucan-moth larvae older than any story of continents. Drinking is audible: a soft *chuk-pah* every few heartbeats, like sap taps.

______Vegetation  
Lush is a shy word. The growth is a screaming green. The “trees” are **tower-ferns** whose trunks grow downward, questing for invisible anchorage; their fronds angle up, overlapping like the pages of misplaced books. Every frond curls at the tip into a **palate-bladder** that grows closed again after it hisses out a three-note chord—this is where half the *loudness* comes from. The grove mutters, it chuckles, it suddenly roars when six bladders strike the same harmonic and a flock of *hex-parrots* takes off in polychrome panic.

The understory is sheets of **mirror-moss**: glassy algal films that record the day behind you as a silvery after-image rolling at ankle height. When you step on one a faint image of your boot scuffs forward half a pace, as if you have shed a translucent ghost. It fades in seven heartbeats and the moss heals; yet for tonight the island carries ghost-libraries of every creature that passed.

______Temperature  
Pleasantly warm—skin temperature of a sleeping animal. A swarm of **sun-sachets** (ballooning seed-pods) rises evenly each dawn, acts as a variable roof of organic prisms that refract distant desert heat, then deflates every dusk into edible pulp. You could call the weather domesticated.

______Fauna  
Abundant but never crowded. Every branch hosts paired **manticorids**—kitten-sized symbionts with three fox-plumed tails and a single hawk eye in the sternum. They trade gossip by slapping theirsing each other with fleshy fans of tail-tuft; the slaps crack like bamboo firecrackers. Larger things move in spacious aisles: shy **Spire-lopes**, ruminants whose horns are coiled hollow reeds each containing an orchestra of bead-sized crickets. When a Spire-lope lowers its head to browse you hear Bach hocketed in diminuendo inside the spiral.

______Tech & Built  
Scattered indeed. Someone once placed stone **grain-bulletins**—curved slabs no heavier than an infant—that cup sound in parabolic bowls. Talk into the concave side; words travel round the island’s rim and slide back to you five heartbeats later translated into any tongue that last addressed the stone. They have acquired glossolalia; on three slabs you hear your own speech returned as rust, birdsong, and drumskin. The only other built structure is a watch-platform: a single cedar stilt-stand high above everything, its ladder made from the shed skin of a river-serpent long extinct below. Tools left there—obsidian flake knives, string smeared with amber—invite you to carve new aerial glyphs on bark still growing.

______Weirdness & Dynamics  
Here time is something scraped by claws. The islands rearrange. At dusk sometimes two landforms **crawl on their under-roots**, grating like vast wicker centipedes, to dock side-by-side; next morning you breakfast on a new sunrise-facing cliff. On full-storm days the auditory mesh becomes so loud the fog-veins vibrate to visibility, turning air into harp-strings. Then the **mirror-moss replays last year**; shadows of the previous season’s creatures walk backwards through you. The tether-vines that bind these islands to one another are themselves slipknots of **living calendar**: segments of their cambium record decades in visible rings, but now and then a ring corkscrews free and becomes an **instant-lizard** that runs shouting the year-number before leaping off into cloud void.

The entire system is held in an **equipoise of erosion half-undone**. Islands have been weathering for centuries yet accrete biomass faster than they disintegrate; the balance is audible—quiet yielding undergirding each triumphant trumpet blast from the fronds.

______Rules of the World  
1. Nothing may remain the same for two consecutive midnights unless it has made a sound loud enough to echo off the salt pan below.  
2. Anything recorded in mirror-moss **owes a song** to the network; the first visitor to tread that imprint tonight will sing it in sleep. Snoring counts.  
3. To drink, you must first speak to the fog-vein; if the vein agrees, it will wait until the moment your mouth is open and fire a crystal bead across the gap. Taste is faint—like licking your own memory of thirst.  
4. The ladder down only manifests at dawn for whoever has left a true name cut into root-wood. Names vanish by twilight, handed back free.  
5. Finally, **height is negotiable**. An island can descend only by detaching a portion of its vegetation to trade sky-for-salt; new growth below replaces loss above, but every descent costs one of the thirteen. Rumor says civilizations of the salt tried to ride these barges upwards; their desiccated harnesses often tangle in lower vines, bronze-green with oxidized hope.

––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––

Stay long enough and the grove will stitch its sound-track into your pulse. You will find yourself humming dissonant chords in the dark, shaping pathways that the manticorids follow like lantern guides. Each step eats its own echo. Every night a different island swings close enough that you might step across the brief bridge of cool air, beckoned by the smell of its alien blossoms—carnations that open only to footsteps spinning them into compass roses.

In the mirror-moss tomorrow you will witness your own arrival again, speaking to fog with palm cupped, but you will not recognize yourself—your face flickers too quickly through masks formed from every mouth that ever drank here. Still, the reflection bows politely, finishes your sentence, and is swallowed by the living green all around.

Leave any time before the next descent.  
Or stay, carve another name, and someday feel the island you stand on heave, roots cracking free, beginning the thousand-meter drop toward the white silence that remembers everything in absence of water.
