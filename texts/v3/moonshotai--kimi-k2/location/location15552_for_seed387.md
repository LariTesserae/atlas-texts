The under-district is the drowned fist of the abandoned furnace-city.  
When the glass dome above the valley cracked during the God-Warm centuries, the ocean pressed down through fissures the width of avenues, slid street over street, and sealed eight layers of brick-and-iron architecture beneath warm salt.  
Nothing grows—nothing that would be recognized as living. What remains is better described as architecture that won’t admit it has died.

You arrive by pressure-lock so small it fits inside a cathedral door.  
Air colder than the water soughs past you into the drowned shaft, equalising with the murmur of billions of weighted drips. Beyond the gate, everything is interior: the city drowned inside itself.

First impression: it is almost empty in the classical sense. Kilometres of vaulted masonry housing only the memory of bodies. But the density of absence is overwritten by the roar—infrasonic hammers, the turbines of drowned machinery still turning somewhere, breeding rumours of motion that echo through halls like whalesong trapped in iron lungs.  The sound is not a background; it is a liquid you must swim through. It siltates the ears, deposits a film of vibration on every averted thought.

Light is whatever the surplus industry provides—dim turquoise bioluminescent tubing, snaking through cracks in mortar long after the last keeper died. Foundries of forgotten purpose glow through cathedral-sized windows, their furnaces drowned but still weaponising algae for a fire that no longer burns. A faint chartreuse tide breathes from cracked pipes, revealing in slow pulses the grand boulevards that now operate as submarine canals. No surface shines; all is matte and barnacled by metallic oxides.

Materials are stubborn relics: oxidised rivets the size of fists, bulbous cast-iron pillars fluted like organ pipes, arches of aragonite salt accretion that copy the geometry they please. Drifts of rust powder settle into dunes that ripple whenever the factory-heart exhales. Every wall is half-consumed by contradiction: both eroded smooth and freshly scabbed, as if the city quarries its own debris to pave tomorrow’s foundations. A form of balance, yes—decay and refurbishment achieved by the same act.

The water itself is warm-blooded. It is thick with dissolved metal and dissolved memory: copper ions tint it rose, magnesium gives it weight. Swirls of micro-bubbles rise in geysers from unseen boilers, collapsing again into hot rain before they reach what might have been ceiling. There are therefore perpetual, noiseless storms no bigger than a fist taking place between floor stones. The city circulates like an organism whose blood has decided to go sightseeing.

Fauna is rare, but what exists has been promoted to tool status. Glass eel-bots—biomechanical things with fused vertebrae of osseous mercury—ribbon through shafts, replacing the need for plant roots with iodine-scavenging. They nibble rust, depositing refined ingots at central depots that have no more foremen to pick them up. Occasionally one gives birth to an eye the size of a dinner plate, blinks once, detaches, and drifts away like a lonely searchlight.

Rules that hold the place together:

1. Every machine that once consumed coal now drinks entropy. Whether entropy is conserved or manufactured is unclear; the black water keeps track.  
2. Gravity is negotiable inside any room whose door still bears an intact crest. Step across threshold and one can rise along veins of warm, pressurised air that smell faintly of spices forgotten by the surface world.  
3. Sound can be collected. Catch it in a bucket and it thickens into a quicksilver tablet, chewable, providing four hours of hallucinatory blueprints. The architecture eaten this way regrows in a new arrangement overnight, so explorers wake to find yesterday’s street looped into their bedroom.  
4. The deeper you descend (and you are already below the surface of everything) the louder it gets, but paradoxically the clearer individual voices emerge—if you can call them voices. A child reciting train timetables. A piston explaining why loneliness accelerates corrosion. Listen too long and you become punctuation in their sentences.

There are quarters named only by resonance: Furnace Canticles. The Hall of Thirteen Impossible Pitches. The Silenced Agora (where nothing is heard except the beating of your own skull). Paths between them are vertical as much as horizontal, bounded by catwalk chains gone slack and writhing like kelp, or freight elevators whose gates have petrified open, interiors filled with bleached coral that mimics pushbuttons.

Every few hours, a great piston the circumference of a city block shudders awake deep beneath the foundational magma chambers. Pressure ripples outward, forging tsunamis inside the water. The fabric of the drowned city flexes; a cupola tilts three degrees; a palace of soldered boilers sighs a fresh rivet popping loose. The sound when that rivet shoots free is thunder layered over childbirth layered over a dart hitting bone.

Over years, pockets of caught air have evolved into politics: anarchist dirigibles made of vulcanised cow-bladders orbiting the great shafts, their crews trading silence as currency because compressed lack-of-noise can be injected into sick machines to lull them into gentler behaviour. They speak in sign, because spoken word bloats in this medium and detonates.

Exit, when you must, is rumoured possible only through the Pinhole Foundry—a chamber where the wall drips molten brass into spoon-sized goblets. Drink one and the heat contracts perception so small the drowned city becomes a bubble in your bloodstream, a cochlear implant city you carry forever. Others claim there is no surface anymore; the ocean rose until the world itself slipped inside this pocket of warm industry, and the city is merely the first metaphor to admit it.

Regardless, the place retains a blushing hospitality. Stand still long enough and the sound quicksilver will weave you a balcony, an anvil, a question. Whatever you leave behind—molted skin, unneeded heartbeats, the memory of daylight—becomes brickwork for tomorrow’s unplanned annex.

This is the Undersuburb, continuously assembling and continuously drowning, an urban fetus wearing armour of rust and lullabies.  
Warm, liquid, loud, and so secretly single that it fits inside the echo of a dropped wrench.
