It begins where the foot sinks ankle-deep into plant-matter rather than stone.  

A shelf, four kilometres above any sea-level you might once have recognised, juts out above a vaporous latitude: an enormous travertine balcony stretched colossal between two mountain-ranges that forgot how to be ranges.  Their peaks have dissolved into terraced, dripstone petals four-foot thick; the calcium has folded and re-folded so long that limestone has the fibrillation of muscle.  Between these inverted monument-roots lies a city, but one spoken rather than built.

The city’s roofs are the crowded crowns of succulent trees.  Every trunk is equal parts arboreal and aqueduct.  Inside their pith-channels water travels red-hot—near boiling, but not boiling, held just before coalescence by some covenant that no one here ever wrote.  The liquid rises, unaided and without pumps or wheels, evaporating at the moment it touches the leaf-blades, and condenses again on the undersides above, raining back upon itself in silver stipple.  Thus perpetual monsoon hangs inside the forest canopy which is also the sky of the streets.  Streets themselves are only aisles of air, two metres wide and infinitely branching, filled by a speleothete mass of aerial roots braided into bridge, handrail, and arch.  No wind; sounds move as though the air is gravid, too humid even for motion.  The voice you might raise is snuffed instantly into the weave of green.

Step down one shelf — that is, slip through a gap where the foliation is so closely overgrown that it has formed a living, flaking hallway — and you are inside an avenue-square whose sides drip like stalactites turned inside-out.  From their tips hang waterfalls of pale, fluid pollen that never falls further than fingertip length, because at that distance gravity halts and begins its instant, absolute reverse, so that the pollen climbs back like tears returning to the eye.  Both directions are visible: descending and ascending as stroboscopic spirals.  Your retina retains both trails for several heartbeats, a double-exposure that never resolves.

The humidity is close enough to drink; temperature clings like animal breath, thick at 40 °C.  Yet nothing rots.  Petals, leaves and human footsteps leave no mark.  A verdigris patina, diffuse azure moss, covers every soft stem, petiole, and joint of architecture.  Under it the stone lives too, mildly and slowly; you can place your palm to the wall and after five hushed seconds feel its faint pulse regulating to match your own.  There is no fauna.  Insects were banished at the same moment language was.

Sound does not decay; instead it stacks.  The waterfall/roar/siren of gathered condensation – steadily roaring with the same roar it has always roared – is the only noise, yet it manifest in every audible register at once.  Frequencies resolving into tones that are also tastes (citrus-run machinery, green sap clotting metal).  If you cover your mouth it does no good; the sound is already behind eyes, between coaxial bones, behind tomorrow’s thoughts.  It has been described as “deafening” but it starts gentle and merely refuses to end; a pressure, not a volume, as if every cubic centimetre is a microphone knee-deep in gain.

The city repeats its mid-dimension perfectly on every conceivable layer.  Walk far enough upward (that is, climb a stair of arches where epiphytes have decidified their leaf-blades into worn steps) and you reach terraces that are exact mirror-echoes of terraces below, rotated 180 degrees.  The rule is simple: inversion equals retelling.  A child standing in the lower promenade can see her reversed self—grown to adulthood—placing the same foot, same hand, same breath, but always ten metres up.  The two selves never acknowledge each other, because they are the identical page printed on transparency and laid upon itself.  No window, no door, no inked line allows divergence.  If one weeps, the other weeps the same drop a second earlier, which also means later.  Causality collapses into a pond’s surface tension.

Every twenty paces a pool surface gulps open.  Into it slides the dusk-blue water drawn downward through those capillary trunks, here pooling only ankle-deep, continually replenished.  Mist drifts from the meniscus like slow blossoming smoke.  If you touch it your fingertips burn to exactly 40 °C: not hotter, not cooler, forever.  Beneath the membrane of each pool, harder than glass, floats a plate-glass city: some architect’s dream of cantilevered plazas, thunder-bridges, copper mirrors.  The upside-down metropolis is lit by the same afternoon that illuminates this one, except inverted, aged slightly.  When you step away, the unseen city’s faint bullhorn-roar surfaces for the space of one echo, then is swallowed by its bigger, above-self.

Though nothing ever changes, everything is most intensely present.  Season does not waver; the sun does not travel, only vibrates slightly in place, like a note held so long it ceases to sound.  Growth occurs because every leaf already exists; it is measured only in the knitting of matter from one equilibrium to the same equilibrium.  Dust, were it admitted, would settle.  But there is no dust and therefore no testament of time.  The air itself has the viscosity of heated glycerine, yet what liquid the fibres draw is exactly the weight they need to draw, so there is no slosh.

Any inward path funnels eventually to the Lustral Heart: a void ringed by a lower lip of vine-grown stone exactly three metres thick.  Cross that lip and you will fall out of the city into what should be sky but is instead the inside of a silent drain, where the roar has been siphoned away for one surreal instant.  You fall forever six microseconds before the sound rolls up after you like an avalanche, regathering the whole city's tsunami of echo.  But you never hit bottom; instead your impact is the sound itself, and sound becomes your falling, and you are the waterfall pollen climbing, failing feeble wings against the inevitable inward breath.  You exist, then, as tinnitus within the walls’ memory, as amplitude in the eternal standing waveform.  You do not die—you are merely rewound and replayed into shelf after terrace after shelf after shelf.  That is citizenship: being housed inside the resonance.

Yet none who remain here recognise themself as citizen.  The only words used are the footstep following the footstep, the leaf following its leaf.  Each is the law repeated.  Each is the city.  The landscape has taken human shape long enough to mimic the density of a metropolis but has forgotten to unshape itself back to wilderness.  Now it is neither.  It is the wet, the green, the hot air that carries no breath belonging to anything still named elsewhere.
