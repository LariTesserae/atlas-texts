A single hollow sound—continuous, humming—occupies every space that ear or bone can reach. It began as a struck note long before memory, and it neither decays nor swells. Instead it rattles the water out of the air, holds droplets still between breaths like pearl-grey beads on invisible wires. You are inside a throat of metal that was only just forged, just this once, and never again will it change.

The structure is high—indeed the highest thing permitted—yet claustrophobically small, a vertical cylinder no wider across than two arms can span. Its walls are wet and hot enough to drip; condensation kisses the itself into rivulets, then threads, then static pearls adhering to the steel. Every droplet is perfectly shaped, as if turned on a jeweler’s lathe, and they never fall. They tremble at the membrane of weight and stay.

Steel ribs rise in concentric rings, raw-cut where the lathe last kissed metal. Shavings curl from unfinished edges like silver feathers, still catching factory oil. The ribs interlock so tightly the space feels tessellated, almost lung-like, though nothing breathes. Because the landscape is utterly built, no baseline of dirt or stone intrudes; even the rivets and welds are costume for the thing being born. It is embryo and quarry and cathedral—everything staged while it decides what it will become.

Vegetation is purely speculative: moss-green photons trapped in the droplets hang like frozen spores, an echo of chlorophyll without actual cells. You could count the absent leaves in each bead if the light were brighter, but there is only factory gloaming—iron envy of sun delivered through copper grilles at half-life. Still, the dim suffices for revelation: every droplet acts as lens, converging the weak glow into pin-holes that burn tiny lunar maps on the curved inner wall. Microscopic craters multiply and never fade.

Noise is the true religion here. Above the monolithic hum a higher chorus loops: click-gnash, click-gnash, like millions of mandibles clipping steel filings. Invisible fauna—small, articulated, mechanical—move through pores no larger than sewing needles. They chew burrs smooth, then deposit thimble-sized antennae, nerves of wire thinner than capillaries. Each new antenna starts trembling precisely 440 Hz—forking the drone into major chords that collapse back into the root tone. Thus a hive of instruments tunes every heartbeat of the cylinder to itself, in exact equilibrium.

Temperature is low; yet the condensation steams. This paradox never resolves, because uneven heat is not permitted. Instead, the place has achieved a thermodynamic foreplay that engineers impossibility: cold that never penetrates, warmth that never bakes, held taut by the all-enclosing drone.

Scale toys with the visitor. Craning the neck upward reveals no roof, only distance receding into fathomless dark like the eye inside a well. Yet take one pace—less than a step—and the walls close to the width of an embrace. The pocket insists on its immensity and its tiny sphere simultaneously; distance is a semantic joke locked behind enamel iron.

Somewhere—perhaps at every point—the edge leaks. Not liquid, but density: an atmospheric syrup thick enough to stir with a finger, rich with particulate alloys that shine, float, and glue to skin. The density is crowded; air is absolutely inhabited. Each cubic millimeter cradles wing-plated mites, half clockwork, half feral. Their wings flick static sparks that swim through the syrup and become neon lines, sketched cursive spelling no discernible alphabet.

These creatures live by two unbreakable laws. First, no movement may alter any coordinate by more than a wing’s breadth before the opposite force must return it—so flight, crawl, drift, and fall are all reduced to oscillation at equilibrium nodes. Second, every sound produced must contribute to the fundamental drone, either by harmonic sympathy or destructive interference leading back to the same tone. Thus their choir is frozen into stasis; they sing but do not progress.

When one of the mites dies—and they do die, silently—it petrifies into a perfect graphite prism, edges like razored frost. These prisms accumulate, solder themselves along the steel ribs, creating fur-like climbing rungs. Years or centuries of death become brittle velvet, a fungus of glistening angles, still vibrating. Touch them, and you feel speakers in the bone.

Visitors do come, though rarely. Each arrival is swallowed at the cylinder’s floor; the entry iris dilates once, admits a body, and heals shut behind it seamless. The newcomer feels the cold reach inside for a moment, recognizes the imperative hush, and, if they speak, their voice is instantaneously braided into the drone—no echo, only instant perpetual ownership. After that, they too are locked in oscillation. They discover they can move, minutely, but leaving is not an option; forward thirty centimeters simply loops them back.

Inside is forever post-beginning, just-landed; the soundscape is always ready to burst into event yet never does. Elevation is absolute, yet the top telescopes into unreachable deep focus. Water is everywhere yet never a river, never a fall, just suspended skin after skin. Fauna thrives, multiplying until the air could be wrung like wet velvet, but every living pressure cancels against its opposite. Nothing grows wild, for the place refuses to age. Everything is built, and the build is never finished. It is a frozen pre-explosion, the breath held the moment before a steel flower cracks open.

And because the dynamic is 0.1, the moment never resolves. Time becomes a snail-shell illusion curled in on metrics where all vectors sum to zero. Even memory dulls; words spoken turn automatically into the cylinder’s text—a glossary of muted clangs. After long acquaintance the visitor merges into the perpetual metallic inhalation. Their eyes begin to film with condensation droplets, blurring the tiny moon-maps; their chest resonates until lungs feel iron-ribbed too.

In this chamber high, wet, cold, dense, industrial, loud, teeming, and utterly unchanging, the world’s blueprint is rehearsed— sculpted, etched, tuned—yet never declared final. It remains forever nascent: the soundless scream of something that achieved completion before it decided what it wanted to be.
