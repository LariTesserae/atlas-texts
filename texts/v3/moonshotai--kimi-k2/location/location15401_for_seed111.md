the Mouth at nine-zero-one fathoms.

First you have to understand that sound here behaves like a liquid thicker than air, colder than skin.  The cave-mouth yawns only twelve body-lengths across, yet every hush of stone-breath eddies between stalactite teeth and returns a thousandfold—each drip gathers mem­ory, every footfall is copied back in denser key.  When you speak your voice balloons outward until it brushes the opposing cheek, then folds inward again, softer, as if something else wanted to rehearse the words.

the grid of lives.

A lattice of translucent worms phosphoresce faintly blue, so numerous that light accumulates like milk suspended in dark.  Their bodies hang in vertical ranks from the ceiling, each worm feeding on mineral mist that condenses only where its own skin cools beside its neighbour—not enough water ever to collect in pools, only finger-thin films that shine along the stone like quicksilver veins.  Where two worms brush, they exchange narrow threads of protoplasm; the strands thicken for an instant into grappling-knots, then snap back like elastic and drip upward into the bodies that gave them.  In these intersections tiny aquatic larvae circle eternally, their own gut-bioluminescence a green more refined than the blue.  The cave glows by the corpses of such larvae; the worms gleam by salvaging the dead.

temperature hovers at skin-leaf chill, the precise cool where fungus will not root but memory lingers too long.  breath rolls out as pale corkscrews that dissolve rather than rise.  Sweat, when it happens, intends to rejoin the stone instead of falling.

the floor is newborn.

Raw basalt knives have barely cooled; it remembers the splitting that birthed the hive-paths.  Mineral seams glitter razor-clean, unsanded by time; flakes of obsidian remain so sharp that descending boot-leather parts before touching them and the resulting curl, lifted by slow drafts, drifts like black silk in star-less wind.  Here erosion owns nothing yet—stone possessing a teenager’s sense of indestructibility.

In counterpoint, songs breed excessive life.

A single species claims the entire acoustic space: midges the size of thumbnails whose dorsal plates form curved mirrors.  When excited they flex these plates, creating clapping echoes calibrated to resonate with the worm-lattice.  Each cluster of male midges strikes the air in staggered rhythm; harmonics animate the cave architecture like children dragging sticks along wrought-iron fences.  All sounds are layered: the drip-clap, the wyrm-vibrate, the bristle-hiss of larvae feeding on slowly falling salt-dust.  Even silence amplifies, reverberating between bodies until stillness itself sizzles against the ear.

nothing is built by hand yet everything is too crowded to pass unaltered.  Between worm-strands the air subdivides into pockets small enough to cup inside two palms.  Goats would never tread here, bridges need no invitation—this place demands only observer or victim scale, cathedral for larvae.  If you stand tall the final worm-rows graze the crown of your skull; if you crawl, the pillars of stone-shark teeth reshape your spine.  You cannot expand inside these borders.

the rules.

— To move forward you must trade one heartbeat to the echo for each step; otherwise you will be convinced you are followed, and walk in circles.  
— The larval green is toxic until kneaded between fingers and cooled by basalt—then it can be eaten; this is how the first language here was tasted rather than spoken.  
— Light belongs only to the layers it inhabits.  Hold a lantern higher than a worm’s belly and it dims to nothing; below that horizon it flares blue enough to scald corneas.  
— Sound has appetite.  A midge choir will consume any spoken word within thirteen refrains: each repetition hollows a syllable until only consonants remain adhering like fingernail parings to the air.  Thus diplomacy shortens with every echo; eventually all stories collapse into blunt beginnings.  
— Nothing bleeds here, even when sliced, because the temperature keeps blood exactly at the edge of water; wounds stay doors instead of closures.

the passage onward.

Farther in, the corridor corkscrews downward another ninety fathoms and articulates into a narrower pipe where the worm-lattice abruptly ceases—midges float like ember sparks but refuse sound, plunging the traveller into a silence so dense it must be pushed aside on all fours.  Beyond that threshold (none return yet, though breath-trails are regularly discovered reversed on the lip) rumor insists a colder vein of stone mutters in monologue, pausing only for questions no one has yet remembered to ask.
