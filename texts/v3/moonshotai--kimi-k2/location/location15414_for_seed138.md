Between the drains of two industrial districts lies the Overflow: a fry-low depression only forty hectares wide, kept permanently half-flooded by the steaming outflows of dye-works and sugar boilers. It is a freshly-lifted piece of lagoon no older than the seventeen cooling towers that ring its edge––a geometric shoreline still sharp, still exact, as if stamped yesterday into the slab of the city. Here everything began-noon and simply stayed there: vapors ascend forever, but never disperse; drops form, slide and slip, yet the water line refuses tide or fluctuation; clocks built into the weirs all read 14:37, moving no hand.

The ground is a rough mosaic of newly-cooled sulfate crusts the colour of boiled lobster and pale conveyor-porcelain spilled by an earlier generation’s traffic. These fragments float on a lens of warm water only finger-deep, water so saturated with dyes that it throws up films of cochineal, anthraquinone, copper-phthalo: one can walk above them wearing rubber soles and leave shoe prints that dry in the sun into brittle pigment dust, forever unwashed away.

Sparse vegetation is almost embarrassingly well-lit—yet only three prescribed species have gained hold: ruby duckweed along rails of rusting hot-run pipes where air feels like sauna exhalation; dwarf mangrove cuttings grafted from engineered seeds, issuing aerial roots so pale they seem silver needles; and single-stalk bromeliads that accumulate mist evened from inverted jet condensers, their vases always full and never overflowing. Each plant is an anchoring spore rather than a colony—one plant per square metre, chalked on the municipal map, numbered, bar-coded.

Fauna, by contrast, teems as noise made flesh— a city ordinance guarantees refuge for steam-borne copepods, metallophilic barnacles, ruby mysid shrimp that breed to the tempo of valve-hammer. They populate every pipe-mouth, bloom in the shallows, and feed a population of sodden pigeons and throaty gulls too greasy for flight, their wings giving slow, laboured applause in the hot unchanging air. Loud: stacks sigh coarsely, pumps thud and retreat like clockwork hearts, vent whistles never deviate from perfect fifth intervals, and every creature chorus locks into theered rhythm.

The settlement is small but stubborn: three parallel rows of pre-cast workshops shaped like vessels run aground, their slanted roofs fitted with catchment gutters feeding into the bound lagoon. Each shop houses one continuous process—brushes dipped, dried, trundled forward on belts—so that unless the machinery stops, a finished broom (or unlike, yet always manufactured like) emerges every thirteen minutes since the town was founded. No worker stands at the machines today or ever; the machines themselves mutter and sweat.

Street pattern? There is a grid, but all cross-streets terminate down two wide stone-lipped v-channels that drink the ceaseless condensation and guide it back to the centre, a ceremony of return. Everything is reachable in two minutes from anywhere, yet no one chooses to inhabit it. Here is industry escaped from its people, processing with no forward march. Awaiting nothing; remembering nothing. It is an almost-normality, if you stand still and accept that time itself is the product being refined—rendered into featureless low-grade duration, boxed and sent nowhere.
