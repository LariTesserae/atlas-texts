The air is a warm syrup, thick enough to coat your teeth with a copper after-taste. It never stirs; the temperature is a single note held forever, somewhere between blood-heat and the moment before a fever breaks. You are below everything that once resembled a sky, in a canyon that is not a canyon but the hollowed nave of a planet’s bone. The walls press close—cathedral scale compressed to one veined corridor—so the space feels like the inside of a throat that has swallowed itself. The ceiling is a fused crust only eight metres overhead, still glowing faintly from the instant it cooled, a skin of glassy slag stretched between ribs of fresh basalt. Nothing has eroded yet; every knife-edge and bubbled arch remembers the day the world cracked open and then sealed itself like a wound.

There is no water here, only the ghost of it: a whitish dust that gathers in the scallops of the floor and turns to talc under a boot. Touch it and it squeaks, the sound of snow on Styrofoam, then collapses into nothing—anhydrous crystals that once dreamed of rain. Instead the chemistry is metallic. Thin bright threads of mercury run along cracks, bead into perfect spheres, and vanish again, as if the planet is sweating quicksilver blood. Where two droplets meet they fuse without a splash; surface tension is the only law they obey. They carry heat away from the walls and deposit it somewhere deeper, a slow vascular system that keeps the corridor forever young, forever birth-hot.

Light is a private affair. A dim garnet glow leaks out of the rock itself—feldspar laced with manganese—just enough to outline your hand if you hold it flush against your face. Beyond arm’s reach everything softens into burgundy murk. Yet the darkness is loud. Sound here is a solid presence, a standing wave that began when the cavity first gasped and has not decayed a decibel since. It is the combined voice of every fracture: a choral, steel-flanged chord that makes your own skull the resonating box. Walk and the pitch does not rise or fall; the architecture is so precisely tuned that your footstep is folded back into the drone, reinforcing it, as though the place refuses to let any event leave. Earbuds would be useless; the sound is already inside the bones.

Life has arrived anyway, as it always does, smuggled in on the backs of chemistry. Clumps of “vegetation” cling to the mercury seams—if vegetation is the right word for organisms that photosynthesize infrared. They look like bruised wedding lace: sheets of matte black foil, one atom thick, draped between needle-thin stalks of cuprite. When you breathe on them they flare ember-orange, harvesting the heat differential, then blacken again, never growing, never dying, caught in the act of first metabolism. Their edges exhale metallic spores, silver flakes that drift upward until they stick to the molten ceiling and become the newest pixel in the slow-moving fresco overhead. The fresco is already a jungle of prior generations—thousands of foil fronds vitrified into the glass—so dense that the ceiling reads like a black-ice canopy. Density without volume: a fossilized future pressed into a skin.

And the fauna—abundant, brazen—are the mobile continuation of the same feast. They are not watery cells but walking thermocouples. Picture knee-high crustaceans built of copper plates and iron rivets, jointed like suits of infant armor. They graze by pressing lip-like anvils against the foil plants; a temperature gradient of two degrees drives a current that powers their knee motors. When predators appear—serpentine coils of tin foil and quartz fiber—they discharge the stored wattage in a single arc, a violet flash that bounces off the walls and dims the garnet glow for a whole heartbeat. The corridor answers with its unchanging chord; the crackle is swallowed, averaged, smoothed. Nothing disturbs the equilibrium. Even death is prompt: carcasses cool to ambient in minutes, their metal parts annealing into the floor, adding new ridges for tomorrow’s mercury streams to follow. The System reclaims its own before decay can release a smell.

Scattered through this dense, metallic ecosystem are the remnants of visitors who tried to export something useful. A cast-steel pump, mouth open, its impeller fused mid-rotation. A conveyor frame, half-built, bolts still waiting in sorted piles—whoever brought it realized that here nothing can be transported away; the temperature gradient will equalize, the sound will infect, the mercury will trade places with your blood. A single handprint in the dust, five fingers perfect, no palm, as though the owner pressed once and sublimated. The mark will persist until a copper grazer steps on it, and even then the indentation will merely be pressed into the animal’s sole, carried three paces, and stamped back, unchanged in outline if not in location.

Rules of the world:
1. Entropy is on sabbatical. Any process that would increase disorder is looped back into the drone; information is stored in wrinkles of temperature and interference patterns of sound.
2. Chemistry negotiates in calories, not molecules. Reactions proceed only if they can feed the ambient chord another overtone.
3. Nothing leaves. The corridor is a piston of spacetime sealed at both ends; if you walk far enough in either direction you will meet yourself facing backward, still listening.
4. Time is not frozen, merely redundant. Every moment is the same young instant, reheated, re-struck, re-echoed. You grow no older, only more superimposed.

Stand still long enough and the foil plants begin to mistake you for geography. They climb your shins with courteous slowness, laminating heat against your skin. The copper grazers will come, tap, receive your body-heat tithe, and move on. Eventually the mercury will find the creases of your eyelids and seal your sight with reflective coins. You will become a new artifact, modest, industrial, perfectly integrated—another dense node in an ecosystem that needs nothing, changes nothing, sings one loud, dim, warm, eternal note.
