1.  
The new-born ridge is still too hot to touch.  
It juts from the planet’s flank like the blade of a knife drawn out of living rock. Thirty vertical kilometres above the shadowed lowlands the air has not yet decided what kind of air it wants to be: pockets of neon, chlorine, benzene and argon knit and unknit every few metres, colliding with soft, hollow pops that are the only sounds for hundreds of kilometres. Nothing drinks; nothing grows; nothing walks. This is a world before thirst was invented.  

2.  
Elevation 2.9 means you are looking level with the tops of the mesosphere. The horizon is curved only slightly less than your own eye-ball; the sky is the colour of closed eyelids. What light exists leaks upward from the ground itself: seams of a dull garnet-red glow where stone is still cooling from its molten childhood. The brightness is less than a candle per square kilometre, but in the perfect vacuum of darkness it looks like a city seen from orbit, except no city has ever been here—there are only the first sketches, the doodles geology makes before it knows what it wants to build.

3.  
The ridge is not a single mountain but a crowd of incipient ideas of mountains. Every few minutes one spur of rock sighs, lifts, and grows three metres taller, its fresh face steaming silicate vapour that instantly plates into glass. Next to it another fin decides it is unstable, folds, and slips back into the crust. The continent is prototyping. Wherever you look something is half-finished, quarter-finished, being undone as quickly as it forms. A spire may last long enough for your shadow to register on its flank, then sag and deflate like an aborted clay potter’s throw. Nothing persists except possibility.

4.  
Scale 2.2 means your gaze can roam across a chessboard of such experiments almost to the curve of the world. Between each embryonic buttress is a vacant plain of black froth-glass, pocked with vesicles the size of cathedrals. The plains are not flat; they ripple like frozen swells on an midnight ocean, but each wave has a brittle skin sharp enough to gut a hull. Crossing them would take days, and every dawn—if you choose to call the momentary bruise-coloured brightening that passes for dawn—you would find the map obsolete: yesterday’s ridge-field might be today’s caldera roof; yesterday’s abyss might now be a fresh range, still sticky with magma.

5.  
Density 1.0 means emptiness is the majority party. For every one presence you meet, nine absences vote against it. The presence might be a single cube of greenish metal, two metres on a side, apparently abandoned by someone who did not stay long enough to be a someone. It is warm to the touch—its surface reads 41 °C, the same fever as the stone—but when you lick your gauntleted finger and draw it across the cube, nothing soluble transfers; nothing oxidises; no flavour of alien alloy leaches. The cube might be the eldest citizen, the only artefact older than the ridge itself, though erosion 0.1 insists nothing here is permitted to be old.

6.  
The cube is not lonely, though. Dotted across the newborn chaos are other one-offs: a ring of basalt beads the height of a person’s knee; a crystal the length of a forearm hovering two fingers above the ground, vibrating softly with piezo thunder you feel in your marrow; a sheet of paper the colour of dried blood lying perfectly flat in a wind that would flay a human face. None of these objects are repeated; each is a category of one. They are footnotes the planet wrote to itself during a fit of sleepless invention, then forgot to foot.

7.  
Tech 0.2 means no handles, no levers, no evident user interface—only these mute found-objects. Pick them up and they stay what they are; leave them and the crust may quietly swallow them overnight, or the ridge may grow a buttress through them, shearing them into strata like pressed flowers. They are not tools; they are momentary thoughts made matte.

8.  
Temperature 2.3 is warm, but warm like the flank of a sleeping animal you’re not sure will keep sleeping. The air is not air; it is a thin, metallic broth that conducts heat almost as efficiently as copper. In shadow your suit thermometer reads 19 °C; step five metres sideways into a glow-seam and the reading vaults to 44 °C. Because the atmosphere is patchwork, you can stand with one boot frozen and the other sweating. Local weather is a quarrel between adjacent cubic metres.

9.  
Nothing swims in these temperature seams; nothing grazes. Fauna 0.2 means you are the only creature here with lungs. Even bacteria have been postponed. When a hot vent breaks open it exhales only chemistry, not life. Yet the weirdness index nudges 0.6, enough that sometimes you wonder if the metallic broth itself is a life-form too slow to register your trespass—if the ridge’s restless rebuilding is not tectonics but metabolism, the planet digesting itself into some unguessable shape.

10.  
Sound 1.2 is present but reticent. The pops of gas mixing are like distant knuckles cracking. Whenever a spur grows too fast you hear a keening, not from fracture but from piezo shiver as quartz lattices lock. It is the sound of glass learning to sing. Between these events a snowfall of silence accumulates, thick as mercury. You become convinced you can hear your own blood deciding which hemisphere of your brain deserves more oxygen, a private tide in an oceanless world.

11.  
Dynamic 2.8 insists on turbulence, yet the violence is geological, almost leisurely by animal standards—minutes, not seconds. Still, the cumulative effect is that to stay in one place is impossible. The ground will credit you with altitude, then bankrupt you. I once watched a pinnacle rise beneath a colleague’s survey drone until the drone sat parked on its summit like a gull on a flag-pole; five minutes later the pinnacle withdrew, leaving the drone nose-down in a drift of black glass threads that cooled around it and fixed it like a museum specimen, wings forever mid-flap.

12.  
Light 0.4 means darkness is the default, but darkness here has gradients. Absolute dark is the basalt foam. Semi-dark is the garnet seams. Twilight-of-twilight is the sky, mirroring the ground’s glow with a bruise-purple pallor that never reaches true colour. You navigate by thermal bloom more than photon bloom: your visor paints heat difference as ghost greens and bruised roses, a private aurora only you can read. In that false darkness you sometimes imagine you see the future ridge, fully grown, clothed in lichen-equivalents that have not yet been invited to exist—an overlay error leaking in from some gentler version of the planet.

13.  
Sometimes the planet’s restlessness concentrates itself into one spot: a circular patch thirty metres across begins to blister, exhale, blister again, faster and faster until the centre blooms open like a pupil and something that is not quite lava, not quite light, fountains upward in a slow column, perfectly cylindrical, holding its shape against the windless air. The column is transparent at the edges, garnet at the core. It rises kilometres, then snaps off at the base and keeps rising, a glass snake cut free, drifting upward until it cools and shatters into blades that fall back and plant themselves point-first the newborn soil like seedlings of a future architecture. The hole it left seals instantly, scarless. You wrote in your log: “Volcano flowering. No pollen.”

14.  
Vegetation 0.5 hangs just above absolute zero, and what that means here is that every edge is still virginal. No chlorophyll, no xylem, no lichen freckles, no rust. When you cut your boot-sole on the foam-glass the scratch shows the colour of moon lettuce, but that is only metaphor: the hue is mineral, not chlorotic. Yet the eye, starved of green, invents it where it can. After a week every basalt vent looks like a leaf; every seam of olivine looks like moss. This is the pre-life mirage, chlorophyll hallucinated by exile.

15.  
Water 0.1 means thirst is a concept you brought with you, a stowaway. The planet has no word for it. When you sweat, the moisture stays a plume of vapour around your helmet until temperature inches above 47 °C, then dissociates into hydrogen and oxygen before it can condense. You are living inside a laboratory demonstration of how a world can stay bone-dry even while warm. Your recycler pisses distilled water back into your mouth and you taste metal, not because the water carries metal but because the air does, and every droplet nucleates on floating ferric motes before it lands on your tongue. You drink the planet’s absence and call it survival.

16.  
At night—because humans still need that division—the ridge gifts you a storm of stillness: every growing spur pauses at once, as though the planet listens to something. During those minutes you can walk the knife-edge of a newborn arête and it will not shrug you off. You once balanced on one such edge and looked down thirty kilometres into a valley that did not exist that morning. The view felt like the first memory of a planet that will one day remember oceans and decide whether to invite them. You felt like the witness who would be quoted in the footnotes of geology textbooks written by no one yet.

17.  
I left after eighteen sols. My footprints were already floes of glass riding subduction zones; my camp cube was half-ingested by a courteous newborn buttress that had grown around it like clasped hands. At lift-off the thrusters lit the garnet seams and for a moment the whole ridge-line ignited crimson applause, but I doubt it was for me. More likely the planet noticed fresh energy and began sketching again, prototyping mountains that will be tall enough to scrape the belly of my descent vehicle’s contrail, mountains no human will ever climb, because they will finish rising, cool, and be erased before any itinerary can reach them.

18.  
This is the landscape that cannot finish becoming. It is warm, high, dark, loud with silence, crowded with absence, and busy like an ant-hill built of insomnia and stone. It has no water to reflect the stars, no leaf to rustle, no beast to name, and yet it is not dead; it is pre-alive, a rehearsal for possibilities it has not chosen. Walk it only if you consent to be rough draft.
