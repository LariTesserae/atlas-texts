The air presses cool against skin like a glass held too long in shade.  Every slick metallic surface sweats; rivulets slide from perforated girders, drip from duct-lattice archways, bead on the millions of leaf-blades that bristle between plates.  You have climbed above ordinary cloud-height, but the place itself generates its own weather.  A hot-cold swirl surges out of the machinery and the living chlorophyll alike, so vapor rises like a plume from the shoulders of a busy god and, three metres above your head, spirals into fog that rolls ceiling-high along the vaulted avenues.

There is no “ground,” only decks—stacked promenades of corroded bronze and green-black steel—joined by ramps that serve as streets.  They interleave so tightly that a single stride forward and down carries you through storeys of breathing life.  Wide leaf of leopard-green hosts droplet mirrors; beneath it, more leaf; beneath that, bright brass coils exhaling warm mist.  Roots drip not soil but condensed condensation, a woody fluid that hardens quickly into amber cables.  Vines have learned to braid themselves through conduit, so the distinction between cable and creeper has long since blurred.  Copper veins pulse; chlorophyll veins luxuriate.

The smell is of bruised basil soaked in kerosene.  The factories—the hundreds of windowless brick blocks feathered into tottering irregular stacks—burn faint ember-chunks of compressed moss in lieu of coal, so their exhaust curls sweet, wet, faintly floral.  Sometimes the bloom of exhaust condenses on the overhead fans and re-enters the system as condensation: the building drinks its own breath.

Water nearest the heart of the complex runs in a slow millrace, a tide-less but steadily circulating bath, two finger-width deep and metres wide, along the Grand Gutter.  From it modules draw coolant on timed belts; when a belt overshoots or stalls, the gutter overflows and you’ll see a ten-second waterfall erupt five levels below onto a humming generator tray—sputter, hiss, new steam.  The place is designed, but behaves like something just learning the idea of balance.

Effective daylight barely arrives.  The roof—intricate tiers of glass roof-sheeting, gantry buttress, hoodie of vine that cannot restr its own motion—attenuates every lumen.  What reaches here is pewter-coloured twilight.  This is enough; the plants, evolved for this minor grace, deploy lustrous dark pigments to capture near-infrared leakage from the engines.  The engines themselves shine underbelly red: cyclopean boilers double as night-earth for the vines.  You can walk for two hours and never leave the envelope of manufactured dusk.

Fauna lives only at the tiniest possible pitch.  Thumb-long black newts haunt the gutters, rubber against patent metal.  Their breath smells of loam and rust.  Spongy moths hatch hourly among leaf undersides, so pale the light passes through them like semi-translucent smoke.  They die twelve minutes later, bodies collapsing into sticky thread, which the network of plant-machine hybrid adaptation re-spins into filter felt.  There are no birds: the architectural lung-noises keep everything trembling ever so slightly; every perch vibrates.

The scale is town-sized—if your town were turned on its side and stacked against the clouds.  From any balustrade you can see six directions of segmenting vista: farther vertical avenues of hanging gardens, downward cascades of boiler apartments, horizon slices of fog walling out the real sky.  Even quiet areas nurse small convulsions—a telescoping scaffold budding overnight, disappearing into reclaimed vine at noon.  It is turbulence without malice: surfaces bubble, settle, are born again.  The loud parts murmur, the silent parts fix themselves while nobody watches.

Occasionally someone—figures in airtight waxed cotton coats, lenses tethered across the face with flexible copper tubing—passes on a motorized walkway slow as moonrise.  They nod, or don’t.  They speak in close, low syllables that taste of steam; the language slips immediately back into the mist.  They live in alcove apartments carved behind prints of monstrous leaves, behind doors of riveted tin.  They service the petiole-pipes, adjust the angle of leaf-deck planks, prune copper tubing with flame.  There is no need for clocks; you hear the tide-slap of water at the gutter lip and know the hour by rhythm alone.

Nothing here is left uncultivated by craftsmanship.  Every centimetre is deliberate: a work-patch, a row of teeth in the spiral gear of a living gearwork forest.  Yet it is no habitat, no park; it is the product of an attenuated rebellion against nature ending in a draw.  Not tame, not wild—just perpetually re-negotiating its architecture between the cool leaf and the hissing gear.

Stand long enough on the central catwalk, and you’ll feel the slightest sway: the whole place exhales once each minute, driven by the collective heartbeat of bellows and stoma.  At its margins lie only more margins, a receding copy of itself.  Ahead, the path forks again, the cliché of a path, but here the cliché is precise in its adjustment, terrifying in its inevitability.  Choose left and the steam rises to meet you; choose right and it moss is already softening a step you have not yet taken.
