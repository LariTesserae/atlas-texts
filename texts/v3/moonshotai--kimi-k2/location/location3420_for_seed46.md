It is not empty; it is only unpopulated.  

Stand on the rim and look inward: a bowl the size of a small bedroom, hammered into the flank of a mountain that is itself still being hammered. The walls are raw nickel-iron, fresh from the forge of the planet’s mantle, still retaining the swirl-marks of whatever giant tongue spun it. They glow dull salmon-pink where the air brushes them, cooling so slowly that a century feels like a sigh. No water has ever visited here; when you exhale your breath falls in white kernels that bounce once and roll, bouncing again, until the heat eats them.  

Inside the bowl, five things grow.  
First, a single black-green liana that has no name, thick as your thumb, coiled like an overwound spring. It emerges from a fissure no wider than a hair, climbs three hand-spans, and explodes into a palm-typed crown of leaves the colour of bruised chromium. Every leaflet is veined with mercury; the mercury pulses once every forty-two minutes, delivering somewhere the memory of weight.  

Second, a stand of glass reeds. They are hollow, eighteen centimetres tall, and they ring softly when the mountain exhales. Their tops swell into fragile ampoules; when one matures it cracks and releases a slow thread of indigo vapour that writes a line of writing across the sky before the heat unwrites it.  

Third, a cushion of lichen that looks like oxidised copper, but if you stare long enough it reveals itself as micro-circuitry: tiny copper buses, via-fields of solder, resistors the colour of deserts. It is computing something, but the answer will not be ready until the mountain is cold.  

Fourth, a single fruiting body – fleshy, ochre, shaped like a human heart arrested by dilation. It beats, once per hour, with a sound like felt hitting felt. No spores emerge; instead, each contraction tightens the air a notch, as if the sky were being screwed down.  

Fifth, a mirror-orchid. It has no stem; it floats exactly thirty-two centimetres above the iron floor, rotating one degree every thirteen minutes. Its petals are polished to optical tolerance and they reflect, not the landscape, but the landscape’s potential – a version where water is 3/3 and everything is drowned. When the petal-angle is right you catch a glimpse of that other place: liquid thunder, black whales of chromium leaf, the liana become a kraken-wide vine hauling islands into its maw. Then the rotation carries on and the iron emptiness returns.  

Nothing walks here. Nothing flies. Yet the place is industrial: you can taste it. The air itself is a machine. Every tonne of atmosphere is a single piston in a planetary engine whose crankshaft is sunk through the mantle and into the moon’s cold core. The cycle time is geological, the thermodynamic efficiency 41%. You stand inside a cylinder no larger than a child’s bedroom and you feel the hush of vast gearing. The faint clicking you hear is not insects – there are none – but thermal expansion seating itself one micron at a time.  

Time is folded. If you remain for three days you will see the mountain grow by the height of a coin. If you blink too slowly the liana will have climbed another coil. If you speak the word “water” the mirror-orchid flares like magnesium and for an instant the bowl is full of hypothetical tide. Then the word is subtracted from the universe; you can no longer pronounce it, and your mouth tastes of electrolytes you have never consumed.  

Leave, and the place forgets you backward: footprints anneal, exhalations are swallowed, the lichen rewinds its calculation. When you turn to look again the bowl is already smaller than memory, a dimple on the iron hill, and the hill itself is a nipple on the breast of a planet that is still being machined. Somewhere underground the great engines idle, waiting for mercury, waiting for the heart-fruit to finish its slow arrhythmia, waiting for the first spark of aqueous chemistry that will never come.  

They have patience. The plantings are lush, but they are only five. The space between them is cathedral, and the cathedral is growing hotter.
