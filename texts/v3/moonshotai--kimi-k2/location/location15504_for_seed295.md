The Myco-Habitation stands two thousand vertical meters above the last memory of sea level, fixed to the stone flanks of the ancient cordillera like a single organism grown over its own ruin. Everything the eye receives is alive and has been made, yet no hand or plan ever touched the making. Here, fungus has chosen stone as its architecture and heat as its quiet hearth; the distinction between built and vegetal has folded into one continuous body.

Morning—though the term is local now—is announced by pressure, not light. At an altitude far above cloud layer, darkness is the native costume, and the only glow is bioluminescence—the pale aquamarine crawling along cap-and-gill, the flicker-motion of spore-motes like embers in negative. Light 0.4 means nothing celestial; the eye must press almost flush to the hyphal mat to register its lumen. Moisture 1.2 is never rain; instead a slow distillation. Vapor, exhaled by pore and mycelial tuft, condenses on every prow of the vertical city. Droplets bead along outer tissues, then gravitate inward through microscopic funnels, feeding the next tier. You will see no rivers, yet the structure drinks constantly—sip, hold, excrete, sip again.

Temperature 1.8 is warm for this altitude, maintained by strain after strain of thermogenic fungus encasing the inner core. Their metabolic heat bakes the hollow avenues beneath the tectonic arches. If you put a hand to any given wall you would feel not surface but soft fever, a yield like fingered bread just out of oven, pulse of a million hearts digesting mineral and memory.

Elevation 2.3 grants a view—would grant it—but Scale 1.6 denies distance in favor of immediate saturation. Corridors expand only a body-length or two before braiding back into themselves. Between every passageway there is intergrowth: bracket shelves jutting outward then curving overhead to become arched ceilings; veil curtains descending into floor-ridges that in turn lift into bench-reefs. You are always inside, yet density 1.6 suggests that outside has been abolished. The air is thick with organism and with sound. Sound 2.6 is Deafening, yes, but not shrill; rather an enormous, oceanic breathing farther in than bone. The city sighs, shuts, ripples open. Spore packets detonate in slow percussions that ring along glossy tubes. Molasses-heavy rain sound never drips—it glides—over membranes whose pores throb like eardrums. Inside your skull every vibration gathers until thought itself drops its threads.

Fauna 2.9—“overwhelming”—is the architecture. Miniature beetles with abdomens split to bioluminesce scuttle along seismic hyphae; translucent slugs stippled in algae graze at leisurely centimeters per hour over ramparts, leaving slick green signatures. Colonies of dusky moths sleep spread-wing from the upper canopies, bodies overlapping like shingles, each wing spotted with eyes that glow only when proximity screams. Higher up in the luminal gloom, a spider whose exoskeleton is indistinguishable from bone coral waits unmoving, its web a single rectangle of tensile silk upon which whole circumstances fade and re-emerge.

Nothing ages individually; erosion is the pace at which the whole formation re-eats yesterday’s growth in order to double itself thick tomorrow. Dynamic 1.4 is one long slow inhale. Seasonal pulps soften and resolidify via hyphal welding; a stair worn by a billion feet redirects itself across the span of centuries, adjusting like a vast gastrointestinal fold. No tools, no machines. Tech 0.1 means the sueño of metal never formed here; stone was never quarried—only colonized, inhaled, digested. If you chip away a fist-sized scrap you lift it free only to find that its musculature tugs against you until, exhausted, you set it back and it knits into place before your eyes. There is no refusal of mercy; the world simply reabsorbs its own fragments.

You may walk—or burrow, ascend, kneel, crawl—everywhere and nowhere until proprioception forgets cardinal direction. Direction itself has become vectorless: each node leads equally to stomach, shelter, spore-nursery, death-bath, vestibule. At the core lies the Quiet Cathedral, a chamber three stories high in human terms but only in that the void is described by upright lamellate walls ribbed like gills. If you stand here at the point where side aisles converge, and put your ear to the warm, semi-translucent flesh, you may hear the hive-presence discussing weather, ten thousand leagues below, in languages made of bloom-rates, acid shifts, osmotic tremor. The conversation spans continents but arrives as faint susurrus like grandmothers humming while they stir a forever-pot.

A traveler brought here would search for an original mineral, for bedrock beneath the husk. Yet everywhere the seam between built and living has been overwritten repeatedly, so that any neural record of “before” is now a palimpsest of fungal time. The limestone you believed formed you landscape is itself a prior generation’s petrified mycelium, compress-recrystalized, cinched, chewed anew. Erosion 1.9 is a planetary mnemonic that remembers in units of centuries, cells instead of seconds.

From certain terraces that face only black sky, breezes scour cold against the incandescence-hidden gorge. No stars follow you; they are hidden by the overgrowth of the heavens—dense cloud mantles twenty kilometers thick, whose lower folds lap at the upper terraces in vaporous banners. And because light is so scarce, night vision is not adaptation but evolution: your irises widen until black consumes iris, pupils become fungal cisterns where spores collect as dreams. After a moonless span of weeks you wake seeing in chemical prisms, tasting color in pheromones. Language collapses to texture and temperature; you speak by pressing wrist to wall, letting your heartbeat sync to the warm grid.

All laws are fungal here. Three postulates suffice:  
1) Anything not moving is being absorbed.  
2) Anything moving is choosing a substrate.  
3) Anything remembered is being digested again.

Visitors either surrender their pace and fall into the gradient slowness, or else their frantic scramble sends vibration storms that alert primordial mouths—polyp-studded valves lining the arterial tunnels—to open and swallow the difference. Thus the only passage is patient; the only arrival, indistinguishable from residence; the only sound that matters is the ongoing low chorus that says yes, yes, always yes, you are already part of the bone and bread and breathing lungs of a city that exists because it will always be eating itself into the next now and the next.

And in that rhythm—echo slow, heartbeat slower—you lie down against the fungal lee. Your form will not vanish; it will only be folded, sectioned, recoded. The world neither welcomes nor turns away; it has already made room long before you arrived, and remembers you so thoroughly that memory itself is your final shelter.
