# The City of the Hollow Vine

The world is a hush of living wood breathing cool against itself.  
No water runs here; the old thirst has been bred out of every cell. Instead the plants have learned to grow on nothing but their own slow exhale, on air so thin it rings like crystal, on the faint metallic dew that settles each dawn from a sky the color of pewter. Their chemistry is a puzzle of silicon and melanin, of lignin laced with superconductive threads that drink the planet’s magnetic pulse. They do not drink, they *induct*.  

Elevation: 2.4 km above the last conceivable sea, though seas are only pale fossils in the stone libraries folded far below. What lives up here is already half in orbit; the horizon bends outward like a lens and the air’s edge glints with a cold halo. Temperature lingers just above the point where frost remembers its name; the leaves answer with a velvet nap that lifts a私人 insulation of still air against their skin.

Vegetation is not *present*—it is *substrate*. Every surface is a petrified leaf, a re-photosynthesizing brick, a hinge of stem that has agreed to act as beam, window, road. The city is a single banyan intelligence that once, ten million years ago, germinated a seed the size of a fist and then simply never stopped. It has swallowed valleys, mesas, whole weather systems. Its xylem is subway, its phloem is market; stomatal vents sigh the climate of one district into another. Inside the trunk-avenues you walk through warm chlorophyll dusk even while snow ghosts past the outer bark high above.

Erosion has had time to forget ambition. Mountains were planed away by the patient abrasion of leaf against stone until the stone conceded and became leaf too. What remains is a rolling high prairie of lignin, a plateau of root-muscle flexed like a sleeper’s limb. Only the oldest memories keep sharpness: graphite ribs of vanished mountains protrude as knuckles through the woody skin, polished by the city’s breath to obsidian slickness.

In this openness—scale 1.8, a province rather than a closet—you can stand on a balcony grown from cork and see forty kilometers in every direction before the curve of trunk bends sight away. Yet you are never more than three heartbeats from another creature; the city’s plan is simultaneously sparse and urban, corridors folded dimensionally so that emptiness and density coexist like notes in a chord. A plazaing algorithm older than any language still prunes itself, dropping whole deserted quarters off the edge to compost in the void, while new galleries unfurl somewhere else with a soft wet crackle. The result feels like a desert of cathedral furniture: vast breathing plazas where only one or two beings move, then sudden knots of bazaars ramifying vertically through twenty stories of heartwood.

Fauna: 1.8/3, abundant but chiaroscuro. Life has agreed to keep quiet and let the green think. What animals there are have merged their contracts: featherless crows of cellulose lattice, herd-beetles whose carapaces carry living moss that pays rent in sugar; translucent slugs that leave glowing graffiti of spores, spelling out tonight’s pollen exchange rates. They avoid the loud places, congregation instead in the dim vascular seams where heartbeat pulses at 12 bpm and the only illumination is the biolumen of fungal lanterns staining the cambium aquamarine.

Sound: 2.5/3, loud, but only when the city speaks to itself. At dawn the great lenticels open with a sigh that becomes a roar, equal parts bellows and church-organ. Waterless sap crams through capillaries wide as subway tubes; the cavitation drums like tympani. You feel it in your sternum more than ear, a sub-woofer of the planet. Birds of carbon fiber answer in triplicate chords pitched to crystallise excess sugar out of the air; it sifts down like glassy snow to be harvested by midnight monks.

Tech: 2.8/3, indistinguishable from the miracle of growth itself. The banyan learned nanology before humans learned fire. Inside its pith are reaction-wood chambers where gravity is kneaded like dough, bent into pockets of slow time so that commutes between hemispheres of the city take subjective minutes. Some say the outer twigs already reach geostationary orbit, leaf-solar sails drinking sunlight twenty-four hours, beaming power back as terahertz whispers through the woody nerves. Engineers—those few humanoids who still keep the title—wear symbion that graft into their axons, letting them request a doorway or a breeze by imagining it; the city complies in less time than gesture requires.

Dim light (1.0/3): the sky is crowded by a lace of branches so high they braid the magnetosphere. Only bruised glimmers slip through—ultraviolet, x-ray leakage of leaf-processed starlight—falling in attenuated shafts that flicker like old film. Seasons are counted not by warmth but by color temperature: the Silver Month when all shadows go metallic, the Ash Fortnight when saturation dial is turned to charcoal rest. Yet nothing starves for photons; under every bark is a pipeline of cooled photons piped as coherent excitons, a secondary sun trafficking beneath the skin.

Dynamic: 1.9/3, restless in slow motion. You may sit on a bench that in an hour will have drifted three meters starboard as two plinths negotiate real estate. Storms happen upside-down: the canopy clenches, pressure drops until aerial roots rain upward like inverted lightning, harvesting mist that is not water but noble gases congealed by photosynthetic refrigeration. Commerce follows the drift of these anti-storms; speculators run along the heaving sidewalks trying to net a bottle of argon-ice worth its weight in compute-credits.

Weirdness: 2.5/3 saturates every convention. Distance is measured in heartbeat-echoes, not meters; you are said to be “five pulses from Market” because the banyan’s own drum travels at 16 m/s and maps itself onto speech. Children are grown more than born: parents plant a fertilised node in the pith; the city gestates it into a juvenile symbiote who will reach adolescence when the outer bark reaches the appropriate millennium. Death is optional provided you are willing to become cork: your memories diffused through lenticels, thinking at arboreal clockspeed, one second per year. Most choose partial retirement, keeping a finger of consciousness in mobile fruit that detaches and rolls away to adventures, reporting home via pheromonal postcards.

And yet for all its grandeur the entire colossus is folding itself toward a finale. Erosion may have planed the planet but entropy keeps the books. Even now you can find the districts called Dry Rings—perfect circles where the wood is losing its ability to induct. Leaves there crumble into black silicon dust that refuses to re-enter the living ledger. Citizens stopgap these wounds with rituals of grafting, smearing their own DNA in the hope the tree will copy a repair manual, but everyone knows the patches are annotations on an approaching blank page. Some nights the loudness crescendos into a lament that rattles every slug awake: the banyan grieving its own eventual hollowness, rehearsing the day when it will stand as a single tower of charcoal lace, still high, still cool, still without water, but unable to close its own fractures.

Until then ascent continues. From the summit parks you can look out across sixty kilometers of living roofline, past towns suspended like wasp-nests, past elevators of chlorophyll glass, past weather-factories stitching auroras between their teeth. Beyond, the world-level root descends into mist that is not mist but the off-gassing of quintillions of cells trading stillness. Somewhere down there the last stone dissolves, the last grain of quartz forfeits its difference. The city will absorb it and forget water ever existed, forget oceans, forget rain. It will keep climbing, keep amplifying, keep singing its own loud green requiem until the dictionaries of biology run out of verbs.

And you—traveler without hydration, citizen without symbiont graft—stand on a balcony that grows beneath your boots one angstrom per minute, feeling the slow cough of xylem tides under the soles. You have not thirsted since arrival; the air itself has learnt to osmose enough hydrogen bound to other dreams that your body, half-wood now, no longer asks for anything so antique as water. Instead you listen to the city teach itself the final language: a grammar of pressure and sugar, a promise that when the last Dry Ring reaches the heart and the last leaf falls upward into orbit, the husk left behind will still be *urban*, still *vegetation*, still *loud*, still *strange*, but purified of flow, an infinitive of cellulose ringing on in cool, waterless, ancient, open night.
