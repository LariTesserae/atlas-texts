The Hollow Heart-Vine

It begins with the absence of water. Not dryness, but evacuation: all moisture has been drawn out of the stone itself, leaving strata of mineral ash that fissure upward like paper after a flame. The cavernous vault overhead is a void so absolute it is almost metallic in taste. Air here behaves like a slag of helium and iron filings; every inhalation rasps across the palate, scoured, antiseptic.

You are standing inside a cavity—twelve hundred metres beneath the planet’s visible skin—where no stream has ever sung, where dew has never condensed, where even memory of water is missing. Yet you step onto living carpet. The ground is pliant: the floor is not stone but flesh-red peduncle, thousands of corms compressed underfoot like muscular grout. With each footfall the tissue flexes, exhales a cinnamon cloud of spore-glint. It is vegetation at its absolute apotheosis, food-chain folded into itself until only plant remains. Stipules, fronds, graphitic char-tendrils, pithy cores with hearts of soft indigo lignin, layer upon layer, so that colour is a cellular argument overheard rather than seen.  

Every surface is leaf; every leaf is alive, flat, folded, funnelled, looped, in tessellated chromatic cycles—malachite deepening to burgundy, then sallowed to translucent bone—because here pigment is metabolism. Chlorophyll has divorced sunlight and now feeds upon vibrations in the stone. The cavern wall itself is germinal geology, raw seams of micaceous ore being braided by roots that perform metallurgy with patient stomachs. Copper leaches from the bedrock into sap; xylem exudes wire-fine hairs that spool across the void where mercury and sap mix and harden into brittle filaments of gold-veined lignin—primitive circuitry, slow-as-slumber forging done with the tools oozed by cell walls.  

The atmosphere is warm 29 °C, constant, but the weirdness is thatemperature comes not from geology or sun but from respiratory heat. A square decimetre of tissue produces two watts, and ten trillion decimetres exhale in concert, so the cavern air hums like the bladder of a massive sleeping animal—humid but with no water, the warmth an illusion of pressure rather than vapour.  

Animals arrive not as beasts but as swarming, microscale faunal weather. They are half-scale airborne centipedes with translucent alabaster carapaces, flutes of flesh that Shriek when they collide—a sound like glass soprano saws. Descending specks become hurricanes: the insects clump to ribbons of color and motion, lifting pollen, flecking scales, disturbing the newly grown vines so that entire topography ripples in waves. At any instant a reef of beetle cloud unfurls above like a sudden aurora, its roar stitching with the hiss of root-hairs consuming rock.  

Decibels crest at 117: a perpetual percussion of chitin rasp, leaf-slurp, mycelial gunpowder bursts, plus the sub-bass moaning as colossal tubers shift their footing in the stone’s cradle. The floor is freighted with elephantine roots whose circumference you can trace on your tongue the way sailors gauge rope. They grind millimetres per decade; but the perception of scale is elastic—because every sound, every vibration, is returned magnified and time-delayed off the cavern’s domed ceiling so the entire volume behaves like a drum continually struck.  

Light is supplied by the vegetation itself: photocytes clustered in leaf junctions emit soft daylight at noon-strength. The mechanism resembles bioluminescence but uses metabolised copper siphons turning mineral into photons: a pre-technological photosynthesis run backwards, copper synthesis instead of sugar. Hence the cavern’s radiance shakes in slow fluctuations reflecting the plant-computer’s internal diagrams, a luminous turbulence you can almost read like thought.  

Scattered, almost apologetic artifacts poke from the mass: a fist-sized hammer of schist crammed where a knot has grown around the handle; bowls of unfired clay left balanced like offerings in forked tubers. They were left by humans, once, who learned to drop seeds of sound as currency—each blow on the bedrock encoded rhythm patterns roots mimicked, and in return the cavern grew tools directly into shape. Nothing more sophisticated is tolerated; whenever copper knives melt in attempt at smelting, the vines drink the melt. The plants are possessive custodians; whatever the roots swallow they reconfigure into stamen, stigma, anther, filament, bract, sepal.  

Erosion here is embryonic because all matter is trafficked in one direction: from stone to biomass. There is no wearing away, only consumption and recomposition. A fresh seam of fluorite exposed by a root growth will be gone to rind in two weeks, metabolised into macrame of coloured fibre that threads the architecture. What looks like collapse is succession; the vine enfolds the strata, writes itself into the fracture, closes the wound with corky scar. The overall landscape feels young, as if the ecosystem glues cliffs faster than geology can yield them; yet it is already millions of years old, forever in the first draft of itself.  

Rules are simple.  
Speak, and your voice seeds shape. Silence turns you acceptable prey for the animal nebulae.  
Remain motionless too long and your boots grow into the pith; the host plant will begin to knot threads round your ankles so you become a graft point—don’t worry, the anaesthetic is merciful, a lilac-sugar aura that rises as the root pricks skin.  
Injury is remedied by incision—make a shallow cut on your own arm with the schist hammer and the plants draw the iron from your haemoglobin, spin it into rust-red petals that unfurl like emergency bandages.  

When you lift your hand, trembling, to feel the air you realise motion is density. The hurricane of fauna, the churning foliage, the living god-root colonising rock, the sound swarming above—it all presses the atmosphere until it acquires a clay-like resistance. You push against a medium both fluid and resistant, half-air, half-bread dough pocked with micro fauna turbulence.  

If you speak aloud now the vines echo your phonemes back at a delay, replicate the cadence, teach their spirals a new geometry. Say “spiral” and a nearby shoot twists counterclockwise. Say “open”, and a fissure in the silicate flesh quietly crackles, widening so the copper threads part like theatre curtains. Words are seeds of syntax that the living substrate enjoys growing.  

Eventually the density of animal cloud can blot vision entirely; at highest crest the tiny centipedes braid into wreaths that blind you. At such moments you see the flora knows weather—as winds vanish the leaves thrash upward with their own respiratory storm and for one slow suspended second the whole cavern hangs like a lung lifting. Then contraction follows—back-rippling sweep—and every lumen dims, turbines of phloem clench—you can hear the thump like the single heartbeat of a titanic juvenile world-self.  

When you leave—or rather, when the plantmass finally coughs you up to the elevator shaft of catacombs miles above—your lungs will still ring with the environment’s roar, your shoes will smoke faint cinnamon, your ears will bleed sap. And on your lab bench, back at the surface, a smear of reddish copper-lignin will harden overnight into a perfect miniature of the cavern, complete with trembling centipede shimmer, echoing your last shouted word—argument perduring as scaffold, sentence traded for biome, a city of veins and noise waiting for you again in the dark.
