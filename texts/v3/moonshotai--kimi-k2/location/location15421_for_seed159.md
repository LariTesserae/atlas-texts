I.

All color comes from damp.  
The roof of the world is thirty metres of naked basalt, riven by capillaries no wider than hair. Condense‐breath drips in slow chandeliers; wherever droplets touch basalt they etch channels that glint red-brown, like oxidized bone. The warm rain never reaches here—this water is psychology, the cavern sighing its own fevered dreams into being. Every few minutes a bulb of moisture grows heavy, trembles, separates, and falls the full height with the decisive smack of punctuation.  

Nothing grows.  
Instead, tectonic milk‐glass buds up through cracks, translucent like pulled sugar, already cracked and splintering at the edges. They are still giving birth to themselves, shell cooling so rapidly it weeps flinty dust that turns the air into diamond pepper.  

Inside this capsule-under-rock, the scale opens sideways: galleries and blow-holes, loops and sudden plunges that vanish into hush, corridors partitioning and budding again. You could walk a valley folded into the thickness of a wall, yet no surface is older than a hundred heartbeats.  

II.

There is a rule: crystallization = population.  
Where the basalt floor fissures, milk-glass rises, solidifies; and at the very instant the surface vitrifies, animals erupt.  
Not as phantoms—bodies in full collision with the air. They are gleaming, warm-blooded, skin like raw aluminum stained with weld-flash. Six limbs, double-jointed wrists, needle hooves that carve perfect hieroglyphic commas on the glass as they land. Forty, fifty at a time, platforms of newborn fauna tumbling over each other with the delighted velocity of dice thrown by a cosmic shooter. This is their first second and already they outnumber every other presence.  

Sound bloats inside the enclosed sky: hoof-clatter ricochets in braided echoes, overlapping into metallic surf. Rough breathing—warm fog ejecting from spiracle flanks—joins the dripping and sizzles when it touches hot glass. The collective exhalations bloom into micro-clouds; droplets precipitate on stalactites; the cycle reboots.  

III.

They do not eat. They sculpt.  
Each creature bears a tool in one fore-claw: affixed crudely with leather twine, tiny hands of dark chert no larger than a coin but ground to microscopic wedges. Working in partnership (chitter, pivot, hit) they shave the cooling crust. Chips fly like insect wings. They are plankton trying to build a continent, and the continent keeps growing childishly under them. Under such density no stage remains empty; even the cliff-faces over-head glint with upside-down carpenters clinging by toe-hooks, filling every square handspan.  

What they build looks like mist turned rigid—lattices too thin to cast shadows, fins curved to the exact sine of their own cry. The work is never finished because the substrate is still dreaming and the alloys shift when the fauna are disturbed. An arch finished a second ago trembles, decides it prefers solitude, shears free. Towers topple, become sleds for daring adolescents who surf chaos down precipices. From the rubble comes the next foundation.  

IV.

The builders are legion, but not immortal.  
Pause long enough (ten seconds is long) and you notice the casualties—beaks crushed under crystalline avalanches, antennae pocked by spitting glass. Still warm, their silver hides begin to dull, and from the split seam at the throat ooze black granules finer than moon-dust. The granules creep uphill, magnetized to iridescent veins in the wall. They reassemble themselves into slugs of flowing midnight that fuse again with the basalt.  

The coda: glass columns catch this dark seep, react with bright moans. New lacunae open. The echo of a requiem replaces the hammer-cluster—just long enough for nascent hooves to begin again. In turbulence the numbers stay mathematically constant: however many fall, newborn beasts gouge space and replicate the cadence before the final drumbeat of glass has ended.  

V.

You, observer skulking in mineral dusk, feel your own breath condensing along forearms and turning to miniature armies marching back into your own pores. The animals glance at you only once. Their eyes are oval mirrors polished to the limit of pre-metal technology: you see yourself—but reversed, upside-down, grainy, and slightly older. None of them speak, yet their collective inscription reads clearly in the fluency of non-stop construction:  
YOU ARE THE MEDIUM NOW.  

The rule rewrites. Where your feet touch, warm rock starts to sweat, and within the frame of your echoing footstep a plate of half-cooled truth begins to rise. Tiny chert tools hang ready in small wet jaws.  

Will you lift the tool? Will you join the endless forging?  
You have two heartbeats before the next bulb of dream-water detaches from the ceiling like a punctuation mark, choosing exactly where the next cathedral must begin.
