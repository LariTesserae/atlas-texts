Field Notes for the Wet-Lung Cavern  
(what to pack, what to leave behind, how to stay intact)

----------------------------------------------------------
1.  Pre-mission medical: grow a second skin before you go  
----------------------------------------------------------
- Have a dermal symbiont cultured from your own fibroblasts and grafted three weeks pre-travel.  
  The symbiont is a transparent, chlorophyll-laced film that slips between your epidermis and the
  outside world.  It does two things: (a) recycles your exhaled CO₂ and moisture into spare ATP,
  and (b) fools the photosynthetic midges into believing you are already “armored,” so they will
  not swarm you on arrival.  Without it you leave the cavern wearing a living coat that will
  persist for months, making airport security intensely complicated.  
- Nails: clip them short and seal the quick with surgical glue.  A hyphae of ice-3 loves the
  protein wedge under a fingernail; the slow freeze feels like a paper cut that never scabs.

----------------------------------------------------------
2.  Clothing: dress like a sieve that wants to stay dry  
----------------------------------------------------------
- NO WATERPROOF LAYERS.  The cavern edits anything that claims to be impermeable; rain-jacket
  membrane becomes a sagging blister in minutes.  Instead wear a loose weave of basalt micro-fiber
  spun with copper.  The cloth is literally chain-mail for sky-eggs: when a silver droplet hits it,
  the copper sings, the syllable is rerouted into the weave, and the egg bursts harmlessly as warm
  mist instead of a phoneme you now owe a dream-debt for.  
- Color: matte black with a cyan fractal piping.  The fractal confuses the leaf-pages into thinking
  you are “already written,” so they will not try to fold you into tomorrow’s wind.  
- Pocket rule: no zipped compartments.  Living chloroplasts clog zippers; use toggled drawstrings
  made from your own hair (pre-waxed).  They self-dissolve in three days if lost, leaving no
  polymer litter the cavern must metabolize.

----------------------------------------------------------
3.  Breathing kit  
----------------------------------------------------------
- Primary: a mouth-piece carved from calcified kelp.  It tastes of iodine and dead reefs but
  keeps the cavern from upholstering your bronchi with souvenir moss.  
- Secondary: a 9 ml vial of 30 % hydrogen peroxide.  When the air thickens in hostility, crack
  the vial under your nose.  The local greenery will rush to metabolize the O₂ bloom and forget
  you momentarily—enough time to retreat.  
- Optional comfort: two drops of liquid smoke flavoring on the tongue.  The phenols bind the
  iron-rich petrichor so your exhalations smell of campfire instead of cave; this is interpreted
  by root-cables as “transient, non-seeding,” and they relax.

----------------------------------------------------------
4.  Food & water discipline  
----------------------------------------------------------
- Eat nothing wet.  Dehydrate every ration to biscuit dryness; reconstitute inside your mouth
  with your own saliva.  Water carried in is a foreign solvent and will revert to ice-3 the
  moment you look away, crystallizing capillaries in your gut wall.  
- Caloric density target: 5.5 kcal g⁻¹.  You want to generate the least possible respiration per
  ATP, because exhaled carbon is taxed by the cavern at 1 sky-egg per 200 mL.  A four-hour stay
  can otherwise drown you in silver marbles.  
- Flavor: bring something strongly umami (miso powder).  It masks the faint iron after-taste that
  otherwise seduces travelers into licking condensation, a gateway debt.

----------------------------------------------------------
5.  Light & navigation tools  
----------------------------------------------------------
- Torch = invitation to bankruptcy.  Any personal photon source outshines the cavern’s thrifted
  twilight and you will be charged—literally—the deficit in sky-eggs.  
- Instead, a meta-material glove that bends your body’s own IR glow back into the visible band.
  You see by cannibalizing your heat; the cavern sees no loss, so the ledger stays flat. Battery
  life: the rest of your life.  
- Compass is fine, but expect the needle to point at your personal future.  If you dislike where
  it aims, rotate your intentions 90° before stepping; the field follows.

----------------------------------------------------------
6.  Time-keeping & the heartbeat protocol  
----------------------------------------------------------
- Do NOT wear a wristwatch.  Mechanised ticks rhythms count as incorrect answers to the
  cavern’s flex question and accelerate assimilation.  
- Count breaths on your gloved knuckles: one flex of a tendon per inhale.  At ~1000 counts
  (roughly 43 min for a trained breather) expect the three-second ice-3 chandelier.  During it:
  – Stand still, feet at 45° spread to lower center of gravity; falling grapes bruise but do not
    cut.  
  – Cup gloves over ears; micro-fractures sing at 19 kHz and can fatigue the stapes.  
  – Whisper one clear interrogative—“What will you remember?”—so the cavern logs you as witness,
    not raw material.  
- If you miscount and the pulse surprises you, expel all air, drop to knees, press forehead to
  root-floor.  The place will accept the offered sigh as down-payment on rhythm correction.

----------------------------------------------------------
7.  Fauna etiquette: how to greet punctuation-shaped beetles  
----------------------------------------------------------
- Comma-beetles harvest cyanobacteria, are harmless, and are reading your CO₂ signature like a
  sentence. Let them finish the clause; brush them off mid-graze and they emit a conjoined
  semi-colon pheromone that invites spiders to extrude cooling columns directly into your hair.  
- Liquid-crystal worms are flashing Morse of their own digestive piezo.  Flash back with a red
  LED on glove (single 1 ms pulse).  They read it as rival worm, retreat, and you keep the
  condensation rail they were drinking.  
- Heart-shaped blisters ripening overhead: if one droops to within forearm reach, place one palm
  under it, count three heartbeats of your own, move on.  This courtesy is logged as “escort
  offered,” and midges will armor you more evenly.

----------------------------------------------------------
8.  Exit strategy: how to refuse or accept a seed  
----------------------------------------------------------
The cavity will try to press a green-glass seed into you—payable as portable weather forever.
You are free to decline, but refusal must be grammatical:  
1. Hold seed to larynx, hum a rising perfect fifth.  The vibration convinces the ice-3 nervous
   system inside that you lack the semantics to host it.  Seed withdraws its roots.  
2. Immediately gift an alternative carrier—anything with pores (a cork, a linen handkerchief,
   even a pressed flower).  Place seed on substitute, set on root-floor, step back.  The cavity
   accepts proxy freight roughly 70 % of the time.  
3. If you keep the seed anyway, budget for itinerant rain: pack micro-umbrella drones that orbit
   overhead and disperse your personal cloud before it reaches meteorological notice.  Poets will
  still adore you; airline schedules will not.

9.  Contingencies: worst-case first-aid  
----------------------------------------------------------
Symptom: breath tastes of rust and you’re coughing petrichor → iron deposition in alveoli.  
– Counter: 100 ml warm (37 °C) 5 % glucose, inhale nebulised, followed immediately by 200 mg
  deferoxamine mesylate.  The cavern’s iron gift is elegant but non-negotiable; chelate fast.  
Symptom: inner-ear invents tectonic harmonics, you can’t stand → pulse rewrite.  
– Counter: hum at 58 Hz for 90 s; that’s the cavern’s elastic modulus, it uncouples your
  circadian anchor.  Then exit immediately; you are one epoch ahead already.

----------------------------------------------------------
∆ Skin-clone breathing?  
∆ Copper-woven cloak tight at cuffs?  
∆ Vial peroxide cached in cheek pouch?  
∆ Knuckle tendon-count zeroed?  
∆ One honest question memorised (not a statement)?
