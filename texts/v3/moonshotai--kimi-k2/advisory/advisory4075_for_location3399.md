Field Briefing for the Ceramic Arena  
(A non-official, traveller-to-traveller checklist compiled from the six known safe returns)  

---------------------------------------------------------------  
1. Pre-departure body audit  
ideally fast before you go. Bowels empty, stomach only lightly acid. The Arena’s air accepts nothing moist; every burp aspirates a faint potassium hef into that perfect dryness and you will feel it on the teeth for hours.  
Clip nails, shave or bind back hair. Anything you shed will not rot, it will simply sit—indelible evidence that you were once biological. The place forgives nothing organic.  

2. Clothing – the “No-Cast-Shadow” rule  
Colour: matte off-white to pale zinc. Anything darker prints a false on the retinas; even your own after-image is foreign matter here.  
Fabric: single-layer open-weave linen or summer-weight paper cloth. No membranes, no Goretex, no skin paints; they out-gas, and the Arena drinks volatiles like a static pump.  
Footwear: thin felt slippers with a ceramic micro-sole. You walk on a skin-temperature surface that remembers your mass—rubber soles squeak and the sound feels like a crime.  
Leave the hat; brims cast shadows, and “swallowed” light is instantly classified as contamination.  

3. Kit list (all items field-wiped of moisture)  
– 1 ceramic whistle, no pea inside. If you must test the acoustic, one breath only.  
– 1 graphite stick, 2B, paperless. Sketch on the slab itself; lines buff away with a finger. Ink is forbidden—fluid dyes bleed a faint halo the Arena never reabsorbs.  
– 60 cm silk ribbon, neutral grey. Use it as a plumb-line if disorientation strikes; silk does not shed fibres.  
– 2g potassium iodide tablets (psychological insurance only). Do not swallow them; the taste on the tongue reminds you that your biology still belongs elsewhere.  
– A pocket prism: if you need to verify the direction the triangles are facing, rotate the prism once; they will acknowledge optics with a 0.04° twitch.  
NO chronometer. NO GPS. NO water flask. NO food. NO camera flash.  

4. Biological prep  
Two nights prior to arrival, sleep in an air-conditioned room set to 29 °C with RH at 10%. Condition skin for the inevitable thermo-neutral trance the Arena induces; it is easy to fall asleep there and dream you’ve left while you’re still standing. If you carry melatonin, halve the dose; the darkness is a perpetual mid-afternoon.  

5. Entry etiquette  
Step across the one-pace gutter in a single, deliberate stride. Do not drag your second foot—you’re up, not down, and the visual trick of “ground that does not exist” can tilt the vestibular sense.  
Once on the slab stop for thirty breaths and do nothing. Let the hairline hex-grid map itself into your peripheral vision; many travellers report the pattern suddenly “locks” and the disorientation collapses.  

6. Movement protocol  
Pace radially; do not spiral. Mark your radius-of-origin every seven paces by laying the silk ribbon in a straight line from the nearest triangle to the sole of your shoe, then step away. After the fourth ribbon you will have drawn an invisible tetrahedron—oddly, this keeps the Arena’s micro-mapping from anonymising you.  
Do not stand exactly equidistant from all three nearest triangles; the centre of any such triangle is a null-node where proprioception doubles and you forget which limb is which.  

7. Interaction with the “plants”  
Touch once, never twice. Use the back of a fingernail. They feel exactly like the floor; your brain will attempt to file them as “furniture” and the illusion is addictive. A second touch within one “day” (internal count) can start a low frequency buzz behind the eyes, an artefact of the Arena trying to absorb the bioelectric difference.  
If a triangle rotates while you watch, note the direction, then deliberately look away for 200 heartbeats. Do not let it finish the turn while under direct observation; nobody has reported injury, but half the log entries stop mid-sentence after that stare-down.  

8. Biological rhythms & time pooling  
You will feel neither thirst nor hunger. That is not a courtesy; it is a warning. Dehydration advances asymptotically—heart rate will sit at 46–50 bpm and you will think you are fine until capillary refill in your nail-beds drops. Press the skin every “ribbon cycle”; if tenting exceeds 1 second, begin deliberate exit.  
Expect the subjective of time: short-term memory remains crisp; long-term memory flattens. Recite your own name aloud once every thirty ribbon segments. It sounds like gunfire, but it re-anchors the hippocampus.  

9. Sound discipline  
Maximum allowable decibel: 32 dB (whisper level at 1 m). A sneeze is borderline, a cough vandalism. If you must speak, face the slab; even your skull echoes differently here and the darkness above folds the wave back into the ears with a metallic rasp.  

10. Exit window & breadcrumbing  
The Arena never visibly changes, so assume it keeps you until you keep yourself. Leave at the fourth local “day” (about 22–26 h inside). Beyond six days the equilibrium begins to print itself on bone density and HR will no longer climb even under deliberate stress.  
To exit, approach any gutter and lift the nearest foot sharply as if climbing a 15 cm stair. Step across. Do not look back immediately; sight-line continuity helps your brain log the Arena as a single temporal room, aiding recall suppression.  
Back in transitional space you will still taste potassium for three hours, and floor temperature will feel refrigerator-cold for a day; these are harmless artefacts. Drink 300 ml water immediately after crossing—your thirst reflex will ignore you until you remind it chemically.  

11. Memory quarantine (critical)  
Within 24 h write down everything that happened using only graphite or charcoal. Digital files corrupted by descriptive gaps of 29 %. Once the log is complete, seal it in an envelope, store somewhere dark for six months, then read only once—each of the six successful returns reports the identical observation: after that single read-through, the memory stabilises and no longer erodes.  

-----------------------------------------------------------------  
A note on philosophy, not safety  
There is no threat vector in the Ceramic Arena: nothing eats, corrodes, irradiates, stalks, or imprisons you. The danger is the invitation to remain inside a perfection that abolishes need. Whatever brought you across the manifold obviously values equilibrium—so value motion, and you will be allowed home.
