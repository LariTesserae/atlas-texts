Preparing to visit the Lung at the bottom of the world is less a matter of stuffing gear into a rucksack and more a discipline of reshaping your own mind so that, when the planet decides to inhale you, you do not bruise its tissue.  
Below is a field-manual written backwards from the inside of that breath. Read it twice, then forget it on the journey down; the Lung prefers visitors who arrive already half-erased.

Section 1 – Body-Work: Becoming Permeable  
1. Desalination fast: Three days before departure stop ingesting sodium. The water remembers every grain of salt it has not yet chosen to be; if your blood tastes too much like ocean it will keep you, like a borrowed book nobody wants to finish.  
2. Iron rinse: Drink a teaspoon of ferrous water each morning. The violet note in the Lung is hungry for iron that has already been inside a human vein; if you arrive pre-oxidised the flavour no longer fascinates and you are less likely to be tasted.  
3. Pulse delay exercises: Sit in a dark room, lay a finger on your carotid, and recite any lullaby backwards. When you hear the lyric arrive after the heartbeat you are ready to survive the acoustic fold.  
4. Callous shave: Scrub the soles of your feet until they are as soft as a child’s. The moss presses back with perfect recall; if it senses you came dressed in armour it will grow through the pores of your skin, cataloguing the difference between you and itself.

Section 2 – Memory-Culling: Packing by Un packing  
1. Items to bring: one eyelash (your own), a syllable you can pronounce without moving the tongue, one unlabelled photograph of a kitchen at dawn. Nothing else occupies space here; objects heavier than memory sink until they implode into regret.  
2. Items to jettison: every story you know about buoyancy, the taste of iron you think you remember, your emergency exit strategy. The exit is the idea of dryness; if you carry it you will leave before you arrive.  
3. Vaccinate yourself against certainty. Spend twenty-four hours arguing loudly that water cannot float on water; then admit you lost. The Lung is politely merciless to visitors who insist on chemistry.

Section 3 – Equipment That Doesn’t Exist Yet (carry the concept only)  
– Gravity mittens: knitted from the slack between two heartbeats, they allow you to adjust the seriousness of your mass when elevation becomes negotiable.  
– Echo comb: a pocket-sized silence you can tousle through your hair so the sound of your pulse arrives already straightened.  
– Pocket of wet absence: when opened, this swallows the concept of “above” so you stop trying to climb. Store it behind your eyes; it weighs nothing until needed.

Section 4 – Approach Routes (choose the one you dislike)  
A. Descend the Mariana Trench on an exhalation you borrowed from a dying whale; when the pressure declares bankruptcy keep sinking until upside-down feels impolite.  
B. Crawl through the cave systems under your own childhood bed; when the wallpaper turns to wet velvet, stand.  
C. Fall asleep in an abandoned railway station listening for the announcement that never comes; open your eyes between the loudspeaker’s future sigh and its present click.  
All three routes deposit you at the same membrane; they merely decide which piece of your biography the Lung will confiscate first.

Section 5 – Etiquette Inside the Breath  
1. Do not name the flatworm. Naming is the first act of ownership and it will inventory your pre-verbal dreams in retaliation.  
2. When the moss remembers your footstep do not apologise for the weight. Apology calcifies into pebbles and you will be asked to carry them out in your bloodstream.  
3. If a rib warms against your palm, hum the lullaby you rehearsed; the vibration translates into the frequency of homesickness, fooling the architecture into thinking you are already part of its geology.  
4. Should you witness a drop of heavier-than-water water fall, offer it the syllable you brought. Catch the bead on your tongue, return it to the common medium. It is impolite to hoard weight.

Section 6 – Extraction Protocols (understand you will never perform them voluntarily)  
The desire to leave is identical to the act of leaving. Therefore:  
– Do not visualise sunlight.  
– Do not recite your mother’s street address.  
– Do not recall the weight of dry clothes against clean skin.  
Instead, practise wanting what you already have in exactly the place you already are. When the Lung recognises that your longing has nowhere external to go it will exhale you onto a shore you have not yet disappointed.

Section 7 – Decompression on Return  
You will surface wherever you next deserve to be—often a convenience-store parking lot at 3:04 a.m., sometimes the minute before your first kiss, once inside a museum of things that only almost happened. Expect to cough up a single bead of chalk-blue water for seven nights; bottle each drop—they are deeds to property you no longer own. Keep the eyelash you brought in a matchbox lined with the photograph; when the kitchen at dawn matches the picture, the cycle is complete.

Final Note  
The Lung does not grant visions, souvenirs, or wisdom. It loans you the rehearsal of silence so that, later, when ordinary air feels too certain, you can remember the hush that fits exactly inside the space your body leaves when you stop insisting on forward. Pack lightly around that memory; everything else is ballast.
