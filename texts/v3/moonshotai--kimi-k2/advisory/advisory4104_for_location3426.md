Preparing to Visit “The Wipped Summit”  
Human-friendly designation: Site-17-α (“Brink-Wound”)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  Mental Packing (do this first, or the rest is useless)
1.1  Abandon the instinct for “finishing.”  
    The place will keep you forever if you insist on tying a single knot, completing a single sentence, or closing a single circle.  Before departure sit in a quiet room and practice stopping every action one gesture short:  
    –   Begin to write your name, omit the last letter.  
    –   Lift a cup but never let it touch your lip.  
    –   Inhale until the lungs feel “almost” full, then exhale.  
    Do this daily for three weeks until the feeling of “incomplete” is no longer frustrating; frustrating is what cracks visitors in half here.

1.2  Memorise the sound of your own pulse.  
    At altitude you will hear nothing else for long stretches; treat it as base-line music so you don’t mistake the plateau’s quiet sucking for silence.

1.3  Adopt a small courtesy phrase.  
    Something you can say without breath—through closed teeth—because exhaled words fall and entangle your legs.  A two-syllable murmur such as “minding” or “guest-ing.”  Whisper it whenever you feel shapes move at the corner of your eye; it is the only tip that has consistently kept the basalt-coloured hands from rearranging a visitor’s anatomy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  Equipment (nothing that insists on "done")

2.1  Suit – a three-layer soft exoshell, matte-black, no metallics (metal tastes amplify the coppery air until travellers faint).  Outer surface must be beaded with silicon bumps so your frozen breath-shell can be snapped off like cheap candy every twenty minutes.  Include elbow-length mitten-shells; fingers inside individual pockets become individual popsicles.

2.2  Heat package – not to warm you but to feed the plateau.  
    One kilogram of slow-release chemical hand-warmers (the kind shipped to arctic fishermen).  Activate two pads every half-hour and drop them onto the stone like cat-calling a diner.  The place metabolises the warmth and, satisfied, lets you keep your own for a while.  Bring forty kilos—more than that and the plateau gets greedy; less and it begins nibbling you.

2.3  Snow-shoes of parchment.  
    Yes, parchment: the breath-ballast will glue itself to woven fibres but slides off animal skin.  Stiff vellum soles let you lift your feet before the accumulating glass-thread anchors you.  Discard and replace when the sheets grow opaque.

2.4  Eye-cages.  
    A titanium ring holding two layers of ultra-thin polarising mesh, set 5 mm in front of the cornea.  You need them because the tin-bright sky erases depth perception; walkers who lose that faculty step clean over the edge of nothing.  The mesh blinks for you—alternates opacity—so every other second is unseen, giving the plateau its due fraction of invisibility and preventing the “rotation while you blink” disorientation.

2.5  A pocket hourglass that runs for exactly one minute.  
    Not for telling time—for making time small enough to hold.  When you feel eternity bleeding in, up-end the glass; while the sand runs you may complete thoughts.  When it stops, stop.  That contract keeps cognition from fraying.

2.6  Souvenir tether.  
    A loop of silk round the wrist, the other end tied to absolutely nothing you value.  When the plateau offers you the temptation to stay and finish “one last sketch,” snap the tether; the audible break reminds you that nothing here deserves completion, including yourself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  Biological Adjustments

Start a low-grade fever 12 h pre-arrival: ingest 250 mg Ferbrifuge-β every four hours.  Your core temp will sit at 38 °C without harm and gives the plateau surplus to steal, extending the time before it begins harvesting extremities.

Empty the colon.  The cold slows peristalsis to zero; any retained mass turns to concrete and must be cut out on return.  A single 24-hour fasting window with isotonic broth is sufficient.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4.  Behavioural Protocols on Site

4.1  Never circle a cairn completely.  
    Approach only to 120°, retreat.  A closed loop invites the loop to close on you, and the builders will stack you higher than your hip.

4.2  Breath discipline.  
    Speak through clenched teeth; exhale sideways so threads fall away, not between your boots.  Every tenth breath, inhale through the nose only, warming the incoming air and confusing the plateau’s count of how much heat you owe.

4.3  Gift exchange.  
    On arrival, select one tool from the scattered constellations, lift it one finger-width, set it back down offset by a hand-span.  You have accepted their host-offer.  On departure choose a second tool, lift, then replace exactly atop its original frost-shadow.  Thus the ledger reads zero and nothing follows you home.

4.4  Regard the water column as holy procrastination.  
    Admire it, but do not desire to understand; curiosity is a form of finishing.  If it shivers, stand still until the ripple dies—movement during a ripple is interpreted as willingness to replace the column, and travellers have been found upright, frozen, cylindrical.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5.  Extraction Checklist

–   Check the parchment snow-shoes: if vellum has turned glassy, shed them, tramp barefoot ten paces, then don the next pair.  
–   Snap souvenir tether; audible crack = permission granted.  
–   Turn 157° counter-clockwise from magnetic north (the deviation is intentional) before first downward step; this is the only heading that reliably subtracts altitude, else you circle the lip forever.  
–   Do not look back.  A backward glance is an attempt to finish the memory; memory shards form the plateau’s newest cairns.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6.  Post-Return Decompression (mandatory, ignore at psychological peril)

Spend six hours in a dim 5 °C room lit only by incandescent bulbs (no LEDs—they’re too finished a spectrum).  Eat nothing warm; allow the body to re-learn how to generate heat.  Speak in single sentences, never paragraphs, for twenty-four hours; it persuades the nervous system that incompleteness is still safe.

Do NOT watch recordings or view photographs of the site for thirty days.  An image is a finished object; the plateau reclaims its debts through any representation.  Half-loaded thumbnails, stilled at 98 %, are how it leaks back into local reality.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
Final Note  
The most successful visitors are the ones who pack lightly in expectations.  Bring nothing that requires closure—no letters to loved ones, no to-do lists, no prayer beads whose final click would mark the prayer “done.”  Travel with an open parenthesis and, whatever happens, do not close it.
