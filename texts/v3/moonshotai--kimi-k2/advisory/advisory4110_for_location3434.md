Field Briefing: Pre-deployment for the Arboreal Metropolis  
 (informal title: “How not to become mulch in the sky-lung”)

─────────────────────────
0. 30-Second TL;DR
• None of the dangers here are supernatural—just wet, fast-growing, and half a kilometre above ground.  
• Treat every surface as both floor and ceiling: it will be the other one tomorrow.  
• You are allergic to nothing until you arrive; then you are allergic to something. Bring antihistamines.  
• The locals measure wealth in cuttings and time; pack seeds, not coins.  
• Every breath you exhale is a humidifier; every garment you wear is compost. Dress accordingly.

─────────────────────────
1. Pre-Journey Medical & Biological Prep
–––––––––––––––––––––––
a) Respiratory tune-up  
   Air is 95 % RH, laden with vegetative aerosols (pollen, spores, cyanobacteria). Suggested prophylaxis:  
   – 3 day course of non-sedating antihistamine (rupatadine, fexofenadine).  
   – Two Epipens if you have ever said “I think I might react to something”.  
   – Daily saline nasal irrigation for one week prior; keeps cilia beating fast enough to export spores.  

b) Immunisation package  
   Standard rainforest set (hep-A/B, typhoid, yellow-fever, JE, rabies pre-exposure). Add tularaemia if you can find a vet willing to jab you off-label; the lagomorph ticks here are novel.  

c) Skin & mycology  
   – Imidazole cream (clotrimazole) for every intertriginous zone you possess.  
   – Powdered copper naphthenate for in-field sock sterilisation; fungus thinks merino is an invitation.  

d) Core temperature calibration  
   Ambient hovers 10 °C; evapotranspiration cooking occasionally spikes it to 15 °C. Train body to be comfortable standing still in 10 °C mist: cold showers, 30-sec breath holds to raise CO₂ tolerance (you’ll breathe wet soup).

2. Kit List – Weight / Volume Rationalised
Clothing (must dry while you wear it)  
• 2× long-sleeve shirts: 120 gsm UPF40 nylon ripstop, ventilated yoke, no cotton content (cotton = rot).  
• 2× zip-off pants, same fabric, legs zip-off at mid-thigh so you can convert to shorts when condensation pools reach knee height (it will).  
• 1× poncho cut from sil-nylon: doubles as rain sheet, vapour barrier liner, and stretcher-sling if the floor folds.  
• 1× micro-fleece (120 gsm) that lives in a dry-bag; night temps feel colder once skin is water-loglogged.  

Footwear Strategy  
Boots are dead weight; bare skin is fastest sensor. Compromise:  
• 1× pair 3-mm neoprene socks with vulcanised rubber sole (canoe shoes) for first 48 h while you learn “what not to step on” (ant-highways, soft bromeliads, glow-berries that squirt).  
• 1× roll-on rock-climbing tape to wrap toes when you inevitably kick a knothole.  
• Leave the $300 Gore-Tex at the portal checkpoint; they will ferment.  

Shelving/Hanging Yourself at Night  
• Jungle hammock with 20 D sil-poly tarp—set it double-height so airflow keeps dew from condensing on bag.  
• 40 °F (4 °C) synthetic quilt; down dies here. Add 2 g of permethrin to stuff-sack to deter “cricket musicians” that chew fabric for salts.  

Tools That Are NOT Metal  
• Folding pruning saw (carbon-fibre blade) – tolerated, small, quiet.  
• Flint or ceramic micro-scalpel for taking tissue samples without rust.  
• Needles: bring pre-sterilised bone or thorn if you want to sew wounds “their way”; steel needles corrode in hours.  

Data Capture / Navigation  
GPS signals bounce off leaves; forget it. Pack:  
• Mechanical wrist altimeter + solar-charged logging watch (Citizen or Casio).  
• Rite-in-the-Rain notebook + carpenter pencil (ink runs).  
• Palette of coloured rubber bands: mark stems you plan to reuse as handholds—colour = day of week; growth will over-write any mark shorter than 24 h.  

Energy / Food  
• You can drink the sky-sweat, but it contains phytoplankton; pack chlorine-tabs anyway.  
• Calorie density > 4.5 kcal g⁻¹: dehydrated nut butter, clarified cocoa butter shards—won’t bloom in 100 % RH.  
• NO metalised packaging; glossy snack-packs are viewed as “sky-mirrors” and attract curious macaws who will unpick your bag seam-by-seam.

3. Psychological & Cultural On-boarding
–––––––––––––––––––––––
“Time” is vegetative; “property” is tenure of tending. Memorise three phrases in Folian (leaf-rustle code + sloth-click dialect):  
1) “I grow, not take” – signals intent to garden rather than harvest destructively.  
2) “Two taps, one breath” – the polite pre-crossing ritual on ant-ways.  
3) “Hold-run share” – permission formula if you need to clip a vine for emergency cordage.  

Practice upside-down handwriting for half an hour daily, 2 weeks before arrival; many signage boards hang beneath branches.  

Expect decision fatigue: every step is a negotiation with an organism that photosynthesises while you dither. Build routines (always tie hammock with two sliding hitches so you can re-tension after dark growth-pulses).

4. Arrival Protocol (First 36 h)
Hour 0–2  
• Remove footwear at last lithic outcrop before the root-sponge begins. Stamp three times to announce biome entry; locals recognise pattern.  
• Exchange a “root-token” (any 5 cm fresh cutting you carried in) at the Dripping Forum: they’ll gift you a stamped-leaf route tag—wear it; doubles as hypoallergenic sweat band.  

Hour 2–6  
• Do not sleep yet. Acclimatise vestibular system: walk loops along the Trunk-3 causeway (slow gradient, 12 % bounce). Let inner ear learn spring-back rhythm.  

Hour 6–12  
• Hydration test: drink 500 ml of your own water first; if stomach rebels, use local supply boiled 3 min with strangler-sap (acts as flocculant, drops suspended spores).  

Hour 12–24  
• Prune your first micro-allotment (hand-sized) on the outer buttress; locals watch. Botch it and you’ll be re-classed “tourist”, denied overnight lofts. Nerd tip: cut 45 °, 5 mm above node, daub-wrap (sap paint) = instant graft seal.  

Hour 24–36  
• Attend the Cricket Chime (dusk). Stand still, count 120 beats: you now have local “second”. All future appointments counted in cricket-intervals; synchronise watch.

5. Survival Scenarios & Contingencies
Scenario A – Bridge Gone  
Cause: fig decides to fruit overnight, withdraws tensile roots.  
Solution: Always cache two 6 m lengths of 8 mm dyneema inside hammock tube; throw-line to neighbouring hand-rail vine, re-rig traverse in < 10 min. Test tension with two-handed bounce before committing pack weight.  

Scenario B – Ant Highway Crossing Your Belly  
Cause: you smell of the mango you ate.  
Solution: remain motionless; they process in 12–15 min. DO NOT slap; release alarm pheromone → swarm upgrade.  

Scenario C – Overgrowth Seals Your Loft While You Recon  
Cause: abandoned lease > 14 days (local fortnight = ten cricket-chimes).  
Solution: carry necklace of copper beads; copper ions slow meristem activity. Rub bead-line across stem you want stalled; buys extra 48 h to reclaim property rights with pruning saw.  

Scenario D – Lost in Fog-Cloud Layer, Visibility < 2 m  
Cause: cloud deck lifts until city sits inside it.  
Solution: You memorised altimeter at civic banyan (reference). Follow contour: keep altitude steady; surface roots always tilt 2–3 ° toward outer edge (drip-line carries water out). Spiral inward reduces radius → you find core landmarks within 400 paces.  

Scenario E – Hypothermia in 10 °C Wet  
Cause: soaked clothes + wind.  
Solution: wring shirt, don poncho OVER wet base-layer—traps radiated heat, prevents further evaporative loss. Sit on closed-cell pad (always with you as pack-frame insert) to insulate from conductive bark; eat cocoa-butter cube; shiver 10 min; regain 1 °C core.

6. Ethical & Legal Notes for Multiverse Travellers
• Do not introduce metal faster than 1 g day⁻¹; oxidation products shift local pH, triggers blight.  
• Do not gift glow-stick plastic—locals bury waste inside stems; micro-plastics appear later in their food-fruit.  
• Taking a “souvenir orchid” is theft of intellectual property; every cultivar is patented in living memory stems by communal song. Ask, barter, and always leave a node behind.  
• If you fall and die, you cannot be buried—the city composts. Draft advance directive before arrival so team-mates aren’t forced to decide between hauling 70 kg of meat down a dripping lattice or violating local funeral customs.

─────────────────────────
7. Return / Decontamination
1) Shake or rinse no gear within 5 km of lowland farms; spores ride jetstreams.  
2) 5 % vinegar dunk for boots & hammock—kills chytrid fungi.  
3) Freeze-dry your notebook at −20 °C 48 h; prevents stow-away mites.  
4) Expect 2-week lung adjustment once home: trachea accustomed to 100 % RH complains at 40 %; sip warm broth, run desktop humidifier, resist urge to over-scarf in mild weather.

─────────────────────────
8. Last Thing Before You Step Onto the Springy Mat
The city is not “hostile”; it is merely metabolising. You are, from its point of view, ambulatory fertiliser—helpful if careful, but delicious in any case. Approach like a pruner, not a plunderer, and the breathing cathedral will exhale a space shaped exactly like you … at least until morning, when it inhales something new.
