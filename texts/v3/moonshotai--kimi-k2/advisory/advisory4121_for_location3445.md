Field briefing: Preparing for a barefoot, one-day insertion into the newborn ridge-complex popularly named “Sky Reef.”  
(Assume you will arrive at dawn and exfiltrate before the sky-ocean cools at twilight.)

1. Redefine “clothing.”  
   – Anything hydrophobic squeaks against the leaves and draws the attention of drifting filter-feeders.  
   – Anything absorbent will weigh triple within minutes.  
   Solution: two layers only—an inner knitted bacterial-cellulose body-suit that wicks laterally and an outer loose mesh of spider-silk cords. The cords give purchase to aerial moss so you look vegetative, and the cellulose layer can be peeled off in one motion if you need to trade weight for speed.  
   Color: the exact salmon of the raw stone. Dye lot #RR-06 “Lagoon Flesh.” Pack a refill vial; scrapes heal in the rock, but cloth doesn’t.

2. Footwear is a philosophical error here.  
   Ridge rock is still exothermic and vesicular; boots melt the micro-crystals that form the only grip. Go barefoot, but prep your soles three nights before: soak in 3 % tannic, 1 % ferrous sulfate, then dust with diatom shale while wet. You will grow a 0.4 mm ceramic film that resists both the warm stone and the solvent sap that drips from umbrella-leaves. Trim toenails absolutely flush; overhangs catch on translucent vines and have been observed to continue growing upward into the canopy—travelers still attached.

3. Respiration.  
   The suspended ocean’s underside outgasses 12 % more argon than Earth-normal. It pools in the valley’s reflective mist. A light hypoxia feels pleasant (time dilation, mild euphoria) until you try to climb and discover you have forgotten which century you occupy.  
   Pack two ampules of 35 % oxygen emulsion in dental-sized bladders. Keep one in buccal pouch; if you notice petroglyphs appearing on your inner eyelids, bite. (Do not exhale hard—the vapour turns jellyfish-bells visible and they will drift toward the shimmer.)

4. Hydration paradox.  
   Water is everywhere; none of it is “water.” Each drop is already halfway through someone else’s metabolism—stone, orchid, sky. Your kidneys will declare sovereignty failure after three litres.  
   Take instead crystalline “reverse-ice”: water frozen around a microscopic seed of negative space. One shard in the cheek every hour re-creates room-temperature potable water inside your mouth without ever letting alien osmotic life taste your blood chemistry. Bring six slivers; they look like anxious glass and TSA off-world will argue—show them the mineralogical import permit.

5. Food.  
   Everything is still inventing toxicity. Flavour is not trustworthy—sweet often means neurologic vacancy.  
   Bring one flat rectangle of nutrified chitin fabric (5000 kcal, strawberry façade). Supplement with ambient iodine by licking the dorsal ridge of umbrella-leaves: they sweat the stuff. This prevents the thyroid from accepting local hormone mimics that will otherwise convince you the sky is a parent and descent is betrayal.

6. Tools that still obey you.  
   – A flake of obsidian no longer than your palm. The edge vibrates at 19 kHz, sufficient to sever vines that route mist; their severed ends cauterise themselves and do not scream.  
   – One polymer ring, 60 mm diameter, memory-set to fold into a sinusoidal hook. Throw it into any droplet column; it anchors as a momentary step-stone, letting you reach higher branches without trusting the stone that is still dreaming itself solid.  
   – A spool of red spider-silk, 20 m. Not for climbing—for marking. Tie a bow around any stem you have touched. The Sky Reef is amnesiac; the silk absorbs your heat signature and tomorrow cartographers can triangulate you if you don’t return.

7. Recording gear.  
   Photography fails: images come back inverted along the vertical axis. Sound is better. Bring a piezo contact mic and slate; scrape the mic across leaf-veins. The playback, reversed and slowed 800 %, speaks the stone’s cooling history in the mother tongue of basalt.  
   Do not livestream. Latency 17 s; whatever watches from the thistledown-bells copies your bio-signature during that gap.

8. Biological etiquette.  
   – Never exhale upward: the sky-ocean accepts donation, then claims interest.  
   – If a glass salamander walks your reflection inside a droplet, offer the obsidian flake as mirror; when it blinks away, you may keep the pigment galaxy left on the blade—powerful anti-fungal off-world.  
   – Should you encounter another traveler, verify directionality: ask them to spit. If the saliva falls up, they have already begun assimilation; speak only in questions, walk backward away.

9. Exit window.  
   The newborn ridge cools roughly 1 °C per 40 min. When vesicular stone no longer feels like resting flesh but like warm bread left on a counter, it means micro-fractures; entire knife-back ridges can subside into valley mist. That is your cue to leave.  
   Find the largest umbrella-tree whose trunk has not yet produced side branches. Tap three times with obsidian; a runnel of sky-drops will braid downward. Step into the column, exhale all breath, let buoyancy sandwich you between descending ocean and rising rock. Keep eyes open: the column becomes a vertical river that will deposit you on the outside talus slope in nine breaths. If you count ten, you’re swimming the troposphere elsewhere—file is closed.

10. Psychological aftercare.  
    Expect three nights of dreams in which gravity forgets you. Sleep inside a cotton sheet dampened with your own perspiration; the familiar salt reintroduces weight. Do not look at star-fields until a full lunar cycle passes; some travelers see the reflected reef and attempt to walk into the sky they now believe is downhill.

Remember: the Sky Reef is not hostile, only pre-narrative. It will record you the way wet cement records a deer’s hoof—then harden around that shape without ever learning the name “deer.” Travel light, leave lighter, and when the salmon stone starts to cool beneath your arch, run—because the planet is still deciding whether anything here, including you, deserves to fossilise.
