Field brief: the “Stone-Gullet Lung”  
(Thirty metres under some erased surface; an engineered, tide-locked micro-ocean the size of a cloister; no life larger than your thumb; every law of physics respected, every assumption of “how to visit somewhere” quietly undermined.)

------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  EXPECTATION RESET  
You will not “arrive” here; you will be decanted into it. There is no shoreline, no beachhead, no surface shimmer to breach. The whole volume is already inside a single held inhalation—water as air, air gone. Picture the moment after you swallow but before the gulp hits your stomach: that is the permanent ambient state. Treat the place as weather, not terrain.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  BODY ENTRY PROTOCOL  
A) Fluids first  
You must pre-fill every tissue space with isotonic saline or you will wring yourself with osmotic bruising inside six minutes. Arrive hyper-hydrated; void nothing.  

B) Blood temperature  
Water is held at 34–35 °C, i.e. warmer than most tropical reefs and only 2 °C below core temp. You will not feel “cold,” you will feel nothing—until your metabolism, robbed of its delta-T, begins to coast. Pack endothermic chemical heat: two 50 g packets of CaCl₂ in pierced plastic straws tucked under each clavicle. They dissolve on demand and buy you +1.3 °C for 40 min; enough to remind your thyroid it has a job.

C) Breath cycle  
The lotus-root nodes exhale pure O₂ when fondled, but they do it like capricious valkyries: one touch = one 3 cm³ bubble. You cannot sip from atmosphere because there is none. Carry a collapsible oral straw:  
- 400 cm³ micro-hose with duck-bill check valve  
- 2 mm nitrile bulb at the tip  
Dive, press bulb, rake a node, sip 8 cm³; repeat every 18 heartbeats. Practice the timing in a dark warm bathtub until it feels like a hiccup reflex, not a decision. Hyperoxia risk is trivial (the place never exceeds 28 kPa O₂) but dry-out is not—your mouth will taste like wet limestone eight hours later.

D) Ear, Nose, Throat  
Pressure at datum is 3 atm absolute—about the same as 20 m seawater, but because you reach it instantly via the submerged gate you will not have a “descent” to alert your Eustachians. Pre-equalise on entry: 40 cm³ of thick 50/50 glycerine/lidocaine gel squirted into each ear canal; also coats the tympanum against calcium spicules that occasionally drift free.

------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  MIND MAINTENANCE  
A) Visibility white-out  
There is only one “colour”: 7000 K glare delivered by stone-reflected light pipes, no particulate scatter. In 7–10 h your cones bleach; after-images will project corridor gratings on your retinas for three days. Countermeasures:  
- Wrap pupils in variable-tint goggles (0–2 % transmittance, auto-switching)  
- 2 h on / 20 min “shadow rotation” (eyes closed, drift inside roofed funnel)  
- 2 mg slow-release melatonin ocular insert (legal in six jurisdictions) if you need to sleep inside.

B) Vestibular confusion  
Neutral buoyancy cancels weight, but gravity still exists; stone blocks remain oriented “down.” After 30 min your hippocampus decides that what you feel is a giant lateral fall. Vertigo often becomes euphoria, then nausea. Fix mental horizon: strap a 1 g haptic pebble to your sternum; tiny constant load gives the lizard brain a floor.

C) Acoustic feedback loop  
There is no ambience, only reverb. Every click from your watch case the hallway like a dropped wrench in cathedral. The pulse grows: a local named the place “the Throat” because it literally speaks back to swallowing noises. Pack soft gum-rubber mittens for every hand action; tape zippers; bring a polymer writing board (waterproof tablet) fused to your forearm—no loose pencils.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4.  TIME AND NAVIGATION  
A map is impossible. Corridors curve back on themselves in three axes; any “exit” staircase terminates against blank roof. Think in drift-field instead: warm feels = core = in; cool feels = periphery = out. Navigate by micro-thermocline alone. Mark walls with sterile biolum tag (cold-light vibrio culture in wax pencil) that turns blue after 4 h; you can read your own breadcrumb breadcrumbs and see which loops you have fouled already.

------------------------------------------------------------------
5.  RESUPPLY & EVAC WINDOW  
The flood has two gentle respiratory tides every 25 h. 0.24 m/s current flows IN-ward for ≈ 9 h, then OUT-ward for same. The tide is powered only by kelp/algae metabolism—never faster, never fails. Schedule depart within 1 h of outbound peak; you will not win the swim fight otherwise. Gate back to your arrival lock sits behind a 1 m² iris of coral blocks; they lever inward when pushed against current, outward when pulled with it. Work WITH the exhale or you will lock yourself inside.

8.  ETHICAL FOOTNOTE  
You leave no cells (they die in the supersaturated CaCO₃ water and become micro-stone) and no metals (they dent the trilithons). The place is a closed lung: your breath is literally borrowed from masonry. Take no souvenirs except maybe a single lotus bubble on your tongue, and even that feels like theft.

------------------------------------------------------------------
9.  LAST REMINDER  
Once inside, the outside world feels like something you exhaled minutes ago and cannot inhale again until the tide turns. Do not hero-swim “up.” Do not wait for a surface. The surface is gone; the sky is the ceiling of someone else’s mouth. Treat your own lungs as visitor passes. When the water forgets you are foreign, exhale once—then accept the invitation and leave quietly, before the throat chooses to swallow.
