First, accept that the place is already inside you.  
The sound-colony that lives in your mastoid will begin to rehearse the moment you decide to go; the clicking is only its passport stamp. Do not try to silence it—pack it instead. Treat it as carry-on luggage.

1. Pre-embarkation body-work  
- Throat: remove all vowels from your dreams for three nights. The fauna speak only in consonant clusters; if you arrive still fluent in round-mouth noises they will mistake you for a defective mirror and try to polish you.  
- Lungs: practice breathing argon. Fill a bathtub with ice, lie in it, exhale until the water metallizes. When the surface skins over and displays your reflection upside-down, you have reached the correct partial pressure.  
- Skeleton: schedule a dental appointment and ask the surgeon to replace two molars with hollow titanium replicas. Inside each, smuggle a grain of helium-3 ice; this will sublimate on arrival and keep your alveoli from soldering shut during the cold pockets.  
- Memory: make a numbered list of every person you have ever loved. Burn the list, eat the ashes, forget the numbers. This leaves vacant slots the legal mites will rent instead of evicting vital recollections.

2. Clothing (single-use, because cloth forgets its shape here)  
Outermost: a coat of piezoelectric fishing line, crocheted the night before departure. When the sound-walls topple, the coat converts their mass into twelve volts that warm your collarbones.  
Mid-layer: knitted copper mail, looped through with glass beads stolen from a 19th-century chandelier. The local beads recognize family and will not clasp your ankles so fiercely.

3. Kit (all items must fit through a 12 mm aperture, because luggage openings shrink when looked at)  
- A child’s tin clown whistle, bent into a Möbius strip. When you become disoriented in the upside-down sector, blow; the note returns from the other side of the strip and leads you back to right-way.  
- One photon. Keep it in a mirrored locket. Trade it to the sky when you need second-hand lightning; the fauna consider it legal tender.  
- Three grammes of freeze-dried syntax (any language). Rehydrate on tongue, speak once; the sentence will carry your voice across the continental echo so friends at home hear you whisper from their drains.  
- A spool of unused causality (sold by dispossessed timekeepers). When events tangle, unreel two centimeters, tie a granny knot, and normal probability reluctantly reasserts itself for exactly ninety seconds—enough to zip a coat or flee a rhyme collision.

4. Biological prophylaxis  
Drink 200 ml of Fullerene solution morning and night for a week prior; the cages trap rogue copper-protein engines before they can lace your blood with circuitry.  
Implant a cochlea filter tuned to 17 kHz—above the parliament’s arguments—so sleep is possible inside the loud.  
Carry a vial of your own distilled silence (collected during REM sleep). One drop on the tongue resets local ambient noise to zero for the length of a single heartbeat; use sparingly or the sky will invoice you for stolen decibels.

5. Navigation & orientation  
The horizon is a Möbius highway: walk toward it and you return inverted. The trick is to walk sideways to your own shadow (the infrared one). When the shadow touches your heel, hop straight up; landing will deposit you one layer closer to the exit seam. Mark progress by tasting the air: when the metallic frost no longer tastes of your childhood, you are near the flip point.

6. Social protocol with fauna  
- Do not complete a sentence. Stop midway; they will finish it in reverse and lose interest.  
- If one shows you your skeleton, compliment the joints—especially those you have never injured. Flattery prevents them from keeping the reflection.  
- Never rhyme aloud. Internal couplets are taxed at one memory per stanza.  
- Barter: offer half-remembered lullabies for warmth pockets; trade algebraic paradoxes for safe passage through sound walls.

7. Emergency extraction  
Should the industrial physics fully claim you (your footprints begin manufacturing smaller copies of yourself):  
1. Open the locket and release the photon.  
2. Blow the Möbius whistle; listen for the note that arrives before you blow.  
3. Swallow the remaining causality spool whole.  
4. Declare, out loud, “Shift ends.”  
The tectonic plates will punch your time card; a column of ferrite ash lifts you through the rust-film clouds and ejects you sideways into the nearest universe with chlorophyll. You will land barefoot, lungs sparkling with half-made circuit boards, coat dead and cold. Bury it; plant the whistle on the grave; in three nights a mirror-skinned sapling will grow—do not prune it, for it is the part of you that stayed behind, still working the last shift so the rest of you could leave.

Remember: you are not a visitor; you are a temporary coworker punching the cosmic clock. Work quietly, stay inverted when required, and clock out before the sky files its noise report.
