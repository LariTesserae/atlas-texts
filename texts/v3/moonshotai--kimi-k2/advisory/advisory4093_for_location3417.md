Pack as if you were going to drown, then change your mind halfway through.

1. Body first  
– Buoyancy lungs: a re-breather bladder you can inflate orally; the swim-in takes 8–12 min and the rain is a single, directionless sheet—if you panic and forget which way is “up” you’ll need neutral trim.  
– Perpetual-wet 5 mm suit, blind-stitched, with a peppering of micro-weights (lead shot in silicone) sown into the hem so you hang vertically in the water column instead of cork-screwing.  
– Vaseline barrier cream for the places the suit can’t cover—rain scrubs oxygen away, but it also carries limestone silt that will tattoo your skin with grey moon-spots if you let it settle.  
– Ear-candling plugs: not for sound but for pressure. When you pass the thermocline “cool enough to keep thoughts tethered” your inner ear tries to flip the world right-side-up again; the plugs slow that illusion so you don’t vomit inside your regulator.

2. Orientation insurance  
– Sky-compass: a child’s toy magnetic needle sealed in glycerine. It will gimbal wildly, but the moment it stills you know you’ve reached the layer where the rain becomes “below” you—surface tension grabs the needle and points it root-ward.  
– Cedar cone (issued by the embassy). Hold it in a fist while swimming; when the city accepts you the scales part slightly, releasing a dust of pollen that sticks to wet skin and smells like struck matches—one breath and you’ll know which vector leads to living wood instead of dead stone.  
– No watches, no digital compass: iron rusts within the week and electronics drown in the civic carillon—every bell fragment in the wood is a Faraday cage.

3. Transit etiquette  
– Tightrope permit: before arrival grow calluses on your big-toe knuckles; residents walk root-ropes barefoot and plant clappers with the inside edge of the foot. Tourist boots snag on cambium and are considered clownish.  
– Gift bell: bring a thumb-nail shard of any metal you have worked with your own hands—copper from a rewired lamp, aluminum from a drilled hole. When you reach the baptistery node, whisper your crime forecast into the shard and drop it into the tide-pool. The echo you receive weeks later is your visa extension.  
– Nettle exchange: twist ten metres of 2-ply nettle cordage in advance. Offer it coiled clockwise; they will re-coil it widdershins and hand it back—now it is “municipal” and you may use it to lash your hammock to the buttresses without being fined in fermented volume.

4. Nutrition & metabolism  
– Ferment starter culture: carry a film-canister of crystallized ginger and a pinch of sour-dough yeast. Local yeast strains are salt-tolerant but vitamin-B poor; your starter prevents peripheral neuropathy during the first month of reed-fermented ale.  
– Collapsible cup carved from basswood. Metal cups ionize; plastic cups taste like drowned insects after two days.  
– Dental floss: the only approved “weapon.” Sling it between two yews and you can garrote a glass-minnow school for protein; floss is also currency—one arm-span buys a night inside a buttress alcove out of the otter-path.

5. Sleep & shelter  
– Hammock of cedar cambium (buy it there, don’t bring). The fibres harden overnight into a corslet that fits only your torso; theft is impossible because a stolen hammock shatters like pottery.  
– Night-light: instead of fire, bring a 0.3 W plankton bulb—amber LED sealed in epoxy. It tricks interior moths into landing near you; their wings carry map-fragments you can copy onto your forearm with lichen ink before they moult.  
– Boundary bells: three brass beads on a wire. Hang one every 180 cm along your line; the local gravity gradient fluctuates while you dream, and if the beads clink you know the rope is beginning to corkscrew—wake up, reset, go back to sleep.

6. Health contingencies  
– Lime-silt lung: expect a dry cough. Bring 20 g of food-grade citric acid; dissolve a knife-tip under the tongue twice daily—it binds the silt into soluble calcium citrate you can pee out.  
– Root-face hallucinations: if a knothole mouths your name, answer only with a hum at 73 Hz (low D). That frequency is interpreted as wind stress; the face will close and leave you alone.  
– Acoustic yeast infection: when carillon thrum starts echoing inside your joints, stop, lie belly-up on a rope, and let small moths crawl into your ears. Their wing scales absorb the sympathetic resonance—disgusting but effective.

7. Culture code  
– Never finish a sentence louder than you began it. The city is governed by echo decay; crescendo equals coercion and may be prosecuted by tree-argument.  
– Time is measured in “marble drops”: the interval it takes for a toy marble to roll from the civic node to the outer root-grove—roughly 63 years. Do not claim you’ll “be back in a minute”; locals will assume you are immortal or stupid.  
– Photography is allowed, but only with pinhole box camera carved from yew. Silver emulsion drinks the bell-sound out of wood; one snapshot costs the community a week of music. Offer to sing the missing part back into a trunk before you leave.

8. Exit strategy  
– The departure window is exactly one hour after you hear the rain pause for three heartbeats (the “vertical memory”). Count: one-Mississippi, two-Mississippi, three—then roll off your buttress, swim toward the coldest shimmer.  
– Do not look back; the cone in your pocket will already be humming the lullaby cadence. If you turn, the city mistakes curiosity for voluntary root-claim and begins to grow a duplicate of your face on the nearest larch. That duplicate will speak only the things you failed to say aloud during your stay—embarrassing, sometimes unforgivable.  
– Once you breach the sheet, tread water until your compass needle stills again; trust it to point you to the world that still owns a horizon. Only then may you name the place, tell stories, write postcards. Until that moment keep Eidenvein under your tongue like a cedar seed—wet, cool, inverted, root-held, bell-fed, and waiting to grow inside the next dreamer who asks you for directions.
