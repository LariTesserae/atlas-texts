Practical briefing for an ordinary human who intends to set one ordinary boot on the Plateau of the Hollow Choir.

1. Pre-departure medical checks (mandatory if you want to leave again)
   • Zero-moisture physiology audit  
     – Plateau air is <0.05 % relative humidity; the body will donate water faster than CO₂ freezes. Start a three-week “dry-down” at home: cut daily water to 900 ml while maintaining 4 % saline diet so plasma volume collapses but osmolarity stays high.  
     – Stool-culture lab before departure proves you can form a solid bolus at 20 % normal gut water. Keep the report; customs asks for it on return.  
   • Epithelial sealing  
     – A week prior, coat skin in two layers of pro-keratin nano-wax (pharmacy name: DermSilent). Re-apply to nostrils, lips and eyelids every 6 h; this slows sub-zero sublimation of mucous.  
   • Breath-training  
     – A human normally loses 400 ml water/day through lungs—here it exits as frost that never re-enters. Learn 4-2-4 “pool-breathing” (inhale 4 s, hold 2 s, exhale 4 s) at 8 breaths min⁻¹. At 25 % normal minute volume you stay oxygenated yet exhale only 40 ml water/day, mostly bound to expired ketones.  
   • Cardioversion magnetics  
     – Local crust leaks 0.3 mT—enough to swamp the SA node. Request a temporary titanium-threaded pacemaker vest; set it 5 bpm above your median so magnet-induced ectopics never capture.  
   • Hydration substitute  
     – Drink 300 ml D₂O (heavy water) the night before. Deuterium binds cytoplasm for ~14 days and is not lost to the Choir frost-field (H₂O sublimates, D₂O does not). Gives you a two-week lossless “water-memory” layer; after that, leave or become resident dust.

2. Kit list (nothing that relies on phase change)
   Clothing  
     – Outer shell of aluminised aerogel (4 mm) aluminised both in for IR reflection, out for charge dissipation. Inner liner is graphite-silk that equalises skin charge so micro-fauna don’t mistake you for a moving capacitor.  
   Head  
     – Scleral lenses cooled to 5 °C before insertion; you’ll need a store of “stares”. Blink-rate will fall to 2 min⁻¹—carry saline ampoules that can be cracked without sound; glass ampoules are silent, plastic clicks.  
   Hands  
     – Over-mitts of corundum gauze; lets you handle magnetic shrubs without slicing arteries that instantaneously dry-seal and shatter.  
   Rations  
     – Caloric bricks: 60 % long-chain MCT, 30 % beeswax, 10 % K-Ca micro-salts. Zero water activity so they neither freeze nor suck moisture from oesophagus. One brick = 900 kCal; three per day. Flavour is irrelevant—taste buds surrender in first 12 h.  
   Water (yes, still bring some)  
     – 250 ml in a collapsible graphene pouch worn under clothes, never opened on Plateau but kept as legal ID that you remember what water is. Customs won’t let you re-enter without it.  
   Tools  
     – A cold-hammered nickel-iron wedge: doubles as compass-calibrator, cutting utensil, bargaining chip. Carry communal spare; returning without it brands you “rude”.  
   Data  
     – Memory is better documentation than image. Bring a finger-string: 13 knots pre-coded with your coordinates in case you walk the Möbius and need to argue precedence with yourself. (Tape recorder won’t work—no air-to-membrane coupling.)

3. Arrival sequence
   Step 1 – Edge negotiation  
     Do not look for a cliff. Close eyes, walk until foot-echo vanishes, stop, open eyes. The Plateau now “agrees” you exist. (Keep blindfold for children’s game but do NOT sprint; tourists who run past sonic-death have no sound-shadow to locate themselves, fall into mirrored sky, and are found months later as thumbnail black discs on the village chime.)  
   Step 2 – Gravity calibration  
     Place both wedges point-down 1 m apart; if they lean toward one another the local g-vector is concave—do not build camp there or you’ll wake in Eudoxia hanging from the ceiling of chandeliers.  
   Step 3 – Respiration lock  
     Turn pacemaker to magnetic-safe mode. First exhale will frost-seal your lips; crack the graphite-silk collar, break ice away from mouth. The nano-wax keeps skin flexible—move jaw every 30 s to prevent ice-weld.

4. Behavioural codes (more survival than etiquette)
   Speech = theft  
     Locals have forgotten sound; any word you utter is extracted from their collective silence budget. Speak once and you owe the community: 3 h garden-plot raking (using only a wedge) or one thumbnail disc of yourself. Decide beforehand how much of you you can spare.  
   Magnet-shrubs  
     They align to pole; your iron-rich blood does too. Stand at right-angles or suffer migraine as haemoglobin swings compass-ward inside skull. Do NOT pick flowers—pollen fails to germinate but will still embed in cornea as diamond grit.  
   Ribbon-cat right-of-way  
     20 m segmented ribbon crossing your path means pressure discontinuity. Freeze until it knots; if you breathe you’ll create a local low-pressure node that may fold the cat through your chest cavity—leaves no wound but you’ll cough iced jet-streams for days.  
   Time pools  
     Sitting still >55 min bifurcates identity. Keep finger-string in motion—knot every half hour; the act of tying re-affirms single timeline. Two knots an hour = insurance against chandelier of selves.  
   Relic etiquette  
     You will meet your own thumbnail disc sooner or later. Tradition: greet silently, press it with your thumb—transfer current life-tension into it. Villagers record that as “debt paid.” Refusal = the disc reclaims you immediately; guides watch for this, will confiscate your remaining water ration to minimise mess.

5. Emergency exits
   The Fold-day Boom (annual)  
     One week before, sunsets acquire double echo. Mark calendar on sleeve: any camp-site isotopes you possess (pacemaker, wedge) will vibrate in unison—countdown 7 days. Twelve hours before boom, move to the northern “Lie”; step off immediately AFTER the sound—Plateau relaxes gravity for 3 s, tossing you 4 km sideways. Locals ride this current to trading posts; tourists get one chance to glide beyond horizon, land on an escarpment that remembers water exists. (Carry flare pellets of Cs that ignite in normal air—prove you’re sky-fallen, not Plateau-born, so ground crew let you down.)  

   If injured  
     – Oxygen is plentiful but frost seals lungs before blood loss matters. Break a caloric brick in half, smear warm (body-warmed) beeswax inside cheek, breathe through that: wax coats alveoli, slows sublimation.  
     – Fractures don’t bleed—snap back, wedge both sides, keep walking or they’ll glass-weld crooked.  

   If lost  
     Find a magnetic shrub, align your wedges parallel to needles. Walk perpendicular path exactly 1 km: probability says you’ll intersect relic chandelier within 1.5 km anywhere on Plateau; once there, follow the swing—pendulae point toward Eudoxia because that’s where largest mass of discs hangs.

6. Contamination protocol on return
   1. Declare frost-freckles on cheeks—they are seeded with Plateau micro-fauna eggs that hatch at 30 % relative humidity and will colonise lungs in 48 h. Mandatory decon shower at –30 °C for 20 min kills without melting (protect crotch—cold kills fauna but also fertility).  
   2. Pacemaker explant: magnetised leads will swipe credit cards 3 m away indefinitely; airport scanners view you as rail-gun.  
   3. Speak continuously for one hour—breaks silence frost in larynx; failure leaves you permanently arrhythmic in a world of sound.  
   4. Drink 1 L water under supervision; customs requires proof you remember how to swallow.

7. Psychological after-care
   You will wake for months expecting personal constellations on bedroom wall. Keep curtains closed initially; normal photons feel feeble.  
   Thirst re-enters like grief—sudden, shameful. Schedule hydration reminders or you’ll dehydrate to 2 % before noticing.  
   Nightmares often replay ribbon-cat knotting; sleep with knees elevated to remind body it now occupies single timeline.  
   Plateau’s mantra (“finished work, finished work”) may erode ambition. Counter with small unfinished tasks—half-read book, unmade bed—prove you still live where revision is possible.

Last truth  
No one leaves the Hollow Choir unchanged; most leave part of themselves still there, swaying politely next to ancestors. Follow the checklist, keep the wedge, remember water when you can, and you have a 70 % chance of exiting with only the silent anthem—shh-shh, shh-shh—rolling beneath every future footstep, reminding you that somewhere inside your pulse a whole plateau is keeping space for everything that has already happened.
