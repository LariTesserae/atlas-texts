PREPARATION BRIEF FOR A CIVILIAN MULTIVERSE VISITOR  
Destination: Auralara, the Floating Basalt Plateau (alt. c. 3,000 ft / 914 m, still rising 0.7 mm / local year)

This is not a sightseeing trip; it is a temporary citizenship in a place that has opted out of most things travellers think they need—noise, exchange, growth, weather, even time as you know it. The plateau will not hurt you, but it will edit you. Pack for that edit.

------------------------------------------------
1. PERMISSION & PATH
- There is only one sanctioned approach: the 2,147-step cliff stair cut into still-cooling basalt. No flyers, no portals, no jump-gates. The stair is administered by the Descending Office (one elder, two water-clocks per week).  
- Send a silence-offering 29 days before arrival: a single unknotted cord soaked in your home water, left at the valley base cairn. If the cord is gone when you return, you may climb. If it is still there, do not come. The plateau has refused you; accept the refusal.

------------------------------------------------
2. CLOTHING & GEAR
Textiles: Anything that rustles, swishes, or squeaks is taboo. Bring felted moss-cloth (it grows in most temp-zone worlds; ask a local tailor to felt it double). Colour must be monochrome grey-green; dyes are read as speech.  
Footwear: Basalt is glass-sharp and vesicular—like walking on frozen bubble-wrap. Soles must be moss-felt two fingers thick, lashed to your foot with reed braid. Leather is offensive (animal absence). Rubber soles squeak; leave them behind.  
Rainwear: There is no hard rain, only standing fog. A cape of loosely woven wire-sedge works; it drinks the mist and releases it slowly, preventing the “wet-cold” that settles at 5 °C.  
Pack: One shoulder satchel, basalt-chip fabric, volume 12 L—enough for one cycle (29 h) but not enough to import temptation.  
Tools allowed:  
- Basalt flake knife (they will knap one for you on arrival)  
- Reed-straw water straw (filters fog-borne micro-arthropods)  
- Memory-cord (9 knots, blank; you will add none, only loosen)

Banned: metal, coins, paper, bells, clocks, mirrors, musical instruments, cosmetics, incense, photographs, written language.

------------------------------------------------
3. BODY PREP
Ear-training: Spend the week before departure in a muted chamber; use noise-cancelling foam not circuitry. Learn to hear your own intercranial pulse; it will be the loudest thing you hear on the plateau.  
Lung-training: Jog while breathing through a damp sponge. The plateau air is 98 % RH; your alveoli must extract oxygen from near-water.  
Hydration: Begin drinking fog-collected water only. Minerals in plateau condensate are sparse; if you arrive saturated with iodine/sodium you will feel vertigo.  
Footskin: Walk barefoot on chilled glass or coarse cat litter to toughen skin; basalt vesicles will otherwise blister you within three hundred paces.  
Caloric ration: Reduce intake to 1,200 kcal/day. The plateau diet is aerosol nutrition; your gut must shrink to match. Expect three moss-cakes and 400 ml of vine-exudate per 29-h cycle.

------------------------------------------------
4. TEMPORAL ADJUSTMENT
Auralara keeps 29-hour “water days.” Starting one week out, shift sleep by 30 min a day, but stretch subjective noon to the new 14:30 mark. Do NOT use alarm tones; wake instead to a slowly dripping tap beside your bed.  
There is no annual calendar; events are measured in flex-cycles of the rock (≈ 3 local days). Memorise your visit in flex-cycles, not Earth weeks.

------------------------------------------------
5. CULTURAL CODA
Silence protocol: On first step of the stair, cease purposeful speech. After 400th step you will find whispers vulgar. By 1,600th step you will notice thoughts arranging themselves as shape, not word. Do not resist.  
Path law: Stay inside the pale stripes worn into grass. Carry a 20 cm reed to test distance: place it heel-to-toe; never exceed four reed-lengths sideways.  
Gifting rule: The city gives you weightlessness of noise; you give back mass. Consume your carried food silently, scatter the wrappers/clips into the perimeter abyss at Half-Fill Ceremony. Nothing organic may remain.  
Photography: Light behaves as pearl-grey silk to most sensors; images come out blank. Do not persist—attempts are read as theft of reflection.  
Music: If you must record, record only below 20 Hz. The plateau’s own flex is 13.7 Hz; overlaying higher frequencies is perceived as mockery.  
Departure ritual: When leaving, stand on the rim at Half-Fill, face inward, exhale until lungs burn, then walk away without looking back. The exhalation is considered your signature on the contract: “I was here, I made no echo.”

------------------------------------------------
6. HEALTH & SAFETY
Altitude: 3,000 ft is negligible physiologically, but fog-pO2 is 4 % lower than marine-level air; hyper-ventilate for first hour.  
Basalt cuts: Sharp as obsidian. Wash immediately with condensate; spores entering bloodstream cause “glass-fever” (rigors, translucence of nail-beds). Locals treat with moss-poultice; bring no antibiotics—there is no gut flora left to balance.  
Hypothermia: Feels like drowsiness, not shivering. If you stop hearing your heartbeat, move—stamp three steps on any axis.  
Psychology: Expect “mirror-drop” — the moment you realise the city is reflecting your absence of noise back at you. After 48 h (two cycles) most visitors lose time-signature. Counter with finger-tapping on your reed: one tap per 13.7 s, matching the plateau hum. If you cannot maintain it, ask an elder to walk you to the stair—descent must begin before disorientation makes steps impossible.

------------------------------------------------
7. SOCIAL INTERACTION
Elders speak in sub-audible phonemes (≤ 40 dB at 1 m). Lean into the vapour; sound rides fog.  
Children play memory games. Join by echoing yesterday’s footprints exactly; do not invent patterns.  
There is no trade; barter is insult. Accept whatever is handed (usually a single basalt bead); wear it inside clothing until you leave, then cast it into the valley at the Half-Fill ledge.  
Romance: Physical affection is expressed by synchronising exhalations—stand close, breathe in-out until pulses coincide. No vocalisation, no eye contact beyond three heartbeats. Sexual pairing is private to the point of secrecy; do not query.  

------------------------------------------------
8. CONTINGENCY & EXIT
If the plateau shears early (probability in any given week ≈ 0.0003 %):
- Drop prone on moss, cover head with moss-cape, grip reed braid.  
- The drop is ≤ 300 m in a single flex; survival odds are > 80 % if you avoid tumbling into stair chasm.  
- After shear, wait. Citizens will re-string memory-cord, realign paths. Follow their footprints exactly; they mark safe ground post-shear.  
- Do not request evacuation; off-world extraction is classified as noise. Walk down the re-cut stair when—and only when—the Descending Office reopens it (usually within one cycle).
