Field Guide & Survival Kit  
for a First-Time Human Visitor to  
The City That Drips  

0. Epigraph (whisper this before you step off the transport)

1. Pre-Travel Medical & Biometric Tuning  
- Bone-density scan – You will carry 6–11 % extra water-weight in your tissues by day three. If your T-score is < –1, drink 700 ml of 0.9 % Ca-Mg saline nightly for two weeks pre-arrival; it pre-loads your skeleton so the city’s percolating minerals don’t leach you hollow.  
- Ossicle-dampening graft (optional but recommended) – A one-hour outpatient procedure installs a 4 mm viscoelastic disk behind each eardrum; it converts the city’s infra-10 Hz “bone-noise” to harmless heat. Without it, 42 % of baseline humans develop vibratory vertigo and spontaneous dental micro-fractures within 96 h.  
- Dermal symbiont spray – Genetically inert lichen spores suspended in silicone. One application to forearms and shins forms a breathable lattice that traps the city’s constant condensation into a personal micro-climate. Stops trench-foot and “drip-rash,” the itchy dermatitis caused by copper-rich sweat.  
- Metabolic decalibration pill – Slows your metabolic rate 18 % for ten days. In a place where pigeons have two necks and lunchtime has lasted three centuries, thinking and digestion should keep the same tempo.  
- Vaccines – Standard multiversal cocktails plus:  
  – Terracotta beetle chitinase (reacts to powdered beetle shell inhalation)  
  – Oxide-eel serum (neutralizes glowing eel proteins if you accidentally drink the canal water)

2. Clothing & Gear: What Will Not Rot, Rip, or Re-shape  
- Fabric: spider-silk baselayer + recycled PTFE oversuit. Cotton rots, wool felts, synthetics stiffen from mineral accretion. Spider-silk wicks the drip away without ever feeling “wet.”  
- Color: choose the same shade of damp ochre the city itself uses. Too bright and every signage-lizard will try to climb you; too dark and residents assume you’re a moving shadow and step off elevators through you.  
- Footwear: felt-soled reef boots with replaceable carbide studs. Streets are slick troughs; you need friction but also the ability to kick free if a millipede-awning tries to cuff your ankle.  
- Rainwear? Pointless. The buildings rain on you. Bring a “drip-brella”: carbon-fiber half-umbrella that straps to your pack and keeps your head dry while letting shoulders breathe.  
- Field long-line harness – 30 m of 4 mm dynamic rope dyed oxide-indigo. Can double as:  
  – lizard-payment dispenser (dip in brine, they lick, you pass)  
  – emergency descent into a pressure-separate elevator shaft  
- Headlamp – Red-only LED. Living eel-light is enough 90 % of the time; your own torch is for signaling, not seeing. White light triggers territorial displays in double-necked pigeons.  
- Hygiene kit – Nail clippers (canals clog nails first), cotton ear-plugs (bone-noise), pH-neutral soap (re-establishes skin acidity after daily metal dust film).  
- Pocket archive trowel – Credit-card stainless. The only legal way to “read” municipal records; they will confiscate anything bigger at the Archive Facade gate.

3. Daily Italogical (money, etiquette, time)  
- Legal tender: 5 mm calcium cubes stamped with the year. They dissolve in two centuries—perfect slow currency. Buy a packet at any elevator terminus; pocket lint speeds decay so keep them in the desiccant tube.  
- Tipping: lick a salt tablet, press to a helpful lizard’s back for 3 s. That’s a day’s sodium for them; they’ll remember your scent and rearrange letters for you tomorrow.  
- Timepiece: set your chrono to 14:30 permanent solar. Locals divide the day not by hours but by “drips per pace.” When in doubt, simply echo the boiler thump under your breath; everyone synchs to it.  
- Verbal greeting: “May your drip meet ground in patience.” Response: “And yours harden when needed.” Do NOT shake hands; the humidity already did it for you.

4. Navigating the City Without Becoming a Stalactite  
The map is a topology, not a topography. Remember three axes:  
- Vertical (pressure layers)  
- Chromatic (color coding in the stone)  
- Sonic (echo-bounce between dripping façades)  
If lost, whistle once. Count echoes: even number = you’re inside a convex district (bowl-shaped), odd = concave (funneling to canal). Lizard-glyphs appear within 90 s to spell your next move.  
NEVER trust 2-D street placards; they were accurate last Tuesday, +/- 7 cm sag since.

5. Menu & Hydration  
- Safe drink: condenser-vapor collected from municipal chimneys—posted taps glow faint teal from resident eels. Carry a 0.1 µm filter flask; otherwise the metallic-celery aftertaste lasts weeks.  
- Food chain:  
  – “Brick-fondue” – calcium shavings scraped live off walls, melted with pepper-oxides, tastes like gruyère and rust. Eat hot; once it cools it petrifies into actual brick.  
  – “Two-neck broth” – only order if you see the cook snip both heads-buds; otherwise the pigeon keeps singing in your stomach (echo-y burps for days).  
  – Aged eel jerky – translucent, LED-bright while fresh; safe 48 h, then the protein lattice collapses into allergenic fibrils.  
- Portion rule: stop when your shadow thickens. The humidity plus mineral load slows gut motility; you feel full ten minutes later than you are.

6. Night & Rest  
Hotels grow downward nightly. Confirm your room still has ceiling clearance > 2 m before paying. Preferred chain: “The Sag Inn” – they laser-score a drip-crease each dawn so doors still open.  
Sleep with feet slightly elevated; condensation pools at floor level and can weld shoes to tile by morning.  
Earplugs useless; use the bone-dampers (see section 1). White-noise apps aggravate the fauna—pigeons respond to synthetic hiss by nesting in your hair.  
Sleep cycle: residents nap in 2-hour “shadow-segments.” If you need an 8-hour block, book windowless drip-closet; they’re quiet, coffin-like, humid but motionless.

7. Hazards & Mitigations  
- Drip rash – copper salt dermatitis – scrub with pH-neutral soap, air-dry, apply symbiont spray.  
- “Slow swallow” – vertigo from 0.2 Hz boiler resonance – crouch, press palms to stone until you feel vibration in wrists > elbows > shoulders; once symmetry is restored, stand.  
- Calcium intoxication – hypercalcemia from aerosol – symptom: you start tasting every color. Reverse with 500 ml citrate solution (carry two sachets).  
- Millipede-awning roll-up – if entangled, freeze; rapid struggle triggers tighter constriction. Exhale, compress thorax, slip out like shedding jacket.  
Elevator-shaft barometric drop – always clip the handrail before doors open; pressure islands can differ 60 hPa in 3 m, giving explosive ear bleed.  
- Mental bleed (“lag of long now”) – you forget urgency. Set timed alarms for departure flights; travelers miss departures believing “afternoon is everlasting.”  
- Fossil-flu – psychogenic calcification delusion; you think you’re turning into dripstone. Antidote: conversation with a child (they laugh you back into motion).

8. Interaction with Liminal Fauna  
Beetles: say thank-you if you see them patching a wall; gratitude increases patch lifespan 17 % and local crime drops (they reinforce vaults).  
Lizards: never offer table salt; iodized salt burns their pigment cells.  
Pigeons: do not feed. One neck drinks moisture, the other memorizes your face and will follow you cooing for days, broadcasting location to every pickpocket on the skyline.  
Eels: scoop only if they turn pale; bright ones are maintenance crews. Aged dull eels are free protein; ask permit from adjacent boiler clerk (verbal okay, no paperwork).

9. Souvenirs That Won’t Get You Arrested or Fossilized  
- 10 ml vial of certified wall-drip (ochre layer) – legal if sealed in neutral-gas ampoule.  
- Molted double-neck feather – export limit two; more requires CITES-analogue permit.  
- Credit-card pigment scrape from Archive Facade – allowed provided you scrape yourself and don’t exceed 1 mm depth.  
DO NOT remove live beetles, millipede segments, glowing eels, or lizard-signage. Fine: 15 000 Ca-cubes OR 3 months geological service (chiseling next century’s color strata).

10. Emergency Exits  
There is no “outside” gate—only elevation change. When departure hour hits:  
1. Reach any elevator terminus.  
2. Board up-line car marked “+300 m Datum.”  
3. Pay with 4 Ca-cubes + 1 ml of your own sweat (the city likes closure).  
4. As doors close, whisper “ground soon.” The car rises until condensation ceases, doors open onto bare rock prairie at perpetual 14:30. You are out. Do not look back immediately; barometric whip will dizzy you. Walk 200 paces, breathe thin dry air, then look: the plateau lip sags like candle wax you once carried in a pocket, and you will understand why nothing that leaves the city ever completely dries.
