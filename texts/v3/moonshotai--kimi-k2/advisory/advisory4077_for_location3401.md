Pre-trip Briefing for the Tideplant Underworks  
(For baseline-human cross-multiversal tourists, not corporate contractors)

The briefing is long because the place is trying, very patiently, to kill you.

0. Meta-Rule  
The Underworks is a negative-altitude, high-humidity, high-particulate, rock-still-moving, light-fluctuating sump. Every cubic metre is somewhere between “wound” and “machine.” If you treat it like a mine, it will drown you. If you treat it like a factory, it will bury you. Treat it like an unstable spaceship that happens to be bolted to the planet and you will live a little longer.

1. Visa & Liability  
- The operating company, Tideplant Drilling & Thaw Ltd., will let you in only if you sign Form 27-B: “Sub-Surface Activity Death/Disfigurement Waiver (Tourist Class).” Bring a waterproof pen.  
- Insurance underwriters back in the poliverse void all coverage below sea level; arrange third-party “wet darkness” riders before you board the cage.  
- You must deposit a full genome scan at surface medical. If the sump claims you, they’ll grow a replacement from whatever they dredge out. (They never dredge enough.)

2. Physical Conditioning  
- Lungs: you will breathe 93 % humidity air at 7 °C for up to eight hours. Train on an assault bike in a cold steam room, 30 min daily, three weeks prior.  
- Knees & Ankles: catwalk gratings are 25 mm bar centres; a stiletto will wedge, a boot shaft will twist. Spend time duck-walking on chain-link fencing to learn the wobble.  
- Grip: everything condenses. Squeeze tennis balls while holding them under running cold water; that is the friction coefficient of every tool, ladder, and handrail.  
- Ears: pressure swings when the Stack exhales. Practice Valsalva reps upside-down in a swimming pool to equalise quickly without letting go of the rail.

3. Vaccinations & Prophylaxis  
- Tetanus, leptospirosis, atypical pneumonia, and “copper-throat” (antibiotic-resistant Pseudomon every driller carries).  
- Anti-fungal prophylaxis: the white skim-milk plant sporulates micro-spicules that lodge in alveoli. A single course of voriconazole before descent reduces colonisation chance by 60 %.  
- Bring two tubes of zinc–dimethicone skin seal; any scratch turns soggy in minutes and the stone bacteria are hungry.

4. Clothing Matrix (layered out→in)  
a) Hardshell – company-issue aluminised PVC poncho (to knee) with gutter-weld seams. It is neon yellow so rescue can find you through steam.  
b) Insulation – 200 g synthetic quilted mittens & socks; down collapses when sodden.  
c) Cut-resistant base – para-aramid coverall; cuffs tuck into gauntlet socks so drips run past the boot collar, not into it.  
d) Optional fashion – none. Vanity sinks here.

5. Personal Kit (allowed past security)  
- Waterproof headlamp, IP68, minimum 800 lm with red-safe mode for ultraviolet-flicker blackouts. Bring two spare battery pods in a vacuum pouch.  
- One non-magnetic wrist compass; the deck plates are mild-steel and will lie to a needle.  
- 60 ml squeeze flask of liquid butyl-rubber: paint-on glove when you need both palms free of slime.  
- Phosphate test strips: if the drizzle turns pH<5, Stack gases are spiking; mask up and move up-current.  
- Collapsible trekking umbrella (carbon spokes) – workers laugh until they borrow it.

6. Forbidden Items  
Anything “organic leaving” is contraband. That includes: paper notebooks, wooden toothpicks, cotton bandanas, leather wallets, protein bars, you. Empty your pockets into the poly-seal autoclave before boarding the cage. They give you slateboard & graphite for notes; both are inert and can stay.

7. Mental Set  
- Light Strobe Vertigo: sodium lamps swing 30–40 cm on power cycles. Concentrate on stationary rock, not on bulbs; sing counting songs to stabilise inner ear.  
- Constant Drizzle Panic: humans feel irrationally “roof-leak unsafe.” Practise meditation under lawn-sprinkler drizzle for thirty minutes daily pre-trip; when real underworks rain hits, you will have a reference frame.  
- Sump-Vertigo: seeing black water move ten metres under your toes triggers ape-brain “cliff plus predator.” Drill yourself on open-grate balconies at height, looking down at dark water, until your heart rate no longer spikes >120 bpm.

8. Arrival Protocol  
- Cage Drop: 110 m in 85 s. Chew gum; swallow to keep eardrums intact.  
- First Breath: the air feels drinkable—metallic, savoury, like putting a 9-v battery on your tongue. Exhale fully; you want new, cool, low-ozone air, not that first warm lungful.  
- Orientation “Tin-Can Rose”: on arrival, stand still, plant both feet, turn 360° slowly, pick THREE immobile landmarks that pierce the steam (usually Stack piping corners). These are your breadcrumb home.

9. Movement Discipline  
- Stay yellow-painted mesh; count gratings between turns; call them aloud to your partner so you both know when you have drifted.  
- One hand for yourself, one for the ship – always.  
- Audio cues: learn the hiccup rhythm of the diesel pumps. When it stops, head for the nearest up-riser within 90 s. Do not wait for spoken orders.  
- Lamp code: three quick off-ons = “I need help.” Two slow = “evacuate.” One long = “hold still, I see danger.”

10. Hydration & Nutrition  
Water is potable only from sealed bladders; the drizzle contains copper ions >2 ppm and will emetic you inside a shift. Eat only vacuum retort pouches designed for submariners; they float if dropped and will not clog filters if lost. Energy-dense, no crumbles.

11. Bio-Encounter Drills  
- White skim-milk plant contact: brush off, do not crush; spores released under pressure. If juice touches skin, flood with 70 % ethanol, scrape with plastic card, seal under butyl paint.  
- Lamp-eels: improbable but morale-critical. If a pinkish grey head pops through a grate, remain still, lower any food. Do NOT attempt selfie; HR calls it “feeding distraction,” fines you for life.

12. Ultraviolet Flicker Escape  
Three flickers is 8–11 s warning before 180 °C steam pulse.  
1) Close eyes, look down – prevents corneal burn.  
2) Pivot on heel toward the Stack (you memorised the pipe corner landmark).  
3) Walk, DO NOT RUN, against airflow; hot gas will pass overhead. Count to five after hiss ends before reopening eyes.

13. Social Protocol  
Workers are polite but superstitious:  
- Never say “final shift,” “dry day,” “quiet night.” Speak of the future as temporary: “see you next circuit.”  
- Trade only in slate-pencil promises; they believe ink runs away like water.  
- If offered a sulphate frost-flower, break a tiny piece, pocket it, crunch the rest; refusal is bad luck, hoarding is worse.

14. Contingency Handlers  
- Flood faster than pumps: climb pylons toward roof, clip to safety rail with twin lanyards; water rises colder than you expect and steals dexterity in minutes.  
- Roof-sheet buckle: iron will clang like a gunshot. Hit the deck, face down, hands round ears; thermal contraction can fire rivets like bullets.  
- White-out (steam bank >99 % obscurity): kneel, switch headlamp to red, point straight down, cover with umbrella; this creates a dim cone that rescuers recognise as “conscious human.”

15. Souvenirs  
Allowed: a 50 ml vial of genuine sump water (boil first—declare at customs as “mineral brine”), a pressed copper rivet (issued by Supply), one photograph on company camera (printed on plastic sheet). Everything else belongs to the wound.

16. Decompression  
After you surface you will still hear pumps for six to twelve hours. Sit in the dry sun, unzip cuffs, let moisture wick away. Do NOT shower immediately; abrupt temperature shift drives copper salts into pores, triggers green-black plaques. Rinse with lukewarm water only, pat dry, then moisturise. Ear-ringing is normal—the planet’s heartbeat takes time to fade.

17. Final Intuition  
The Underworks is a place that negotiates: it offers the human need to build, the Earth’s need to seal. Your presence tips a microscopic balance toward one side or the other. Walk softly, count your breaths, and leave nothing of yourself behind except footprints on wet yellow steel. The planet already has too many open wounds—and enough stitches that won’t hold.
