PRE-LAUNCH BRIEFING  
A single-page summary is impossible for this place; the mountain edits paper as you hold it.  
 therefore treat what follows as a living document—fold it, crease it, let it breathe against your wrist while you read.  The text will update when the dome inhales.

1.  PHYSICS YOU MUST UNLEARN  
   •  Distance is polite suggestion.  Maintain three separate methods of measuring where you are: a pedometer that forgets every tenth step, a knotted cord you cut and burn as you walk, and a song you make up on arrival (the chorus becomes your return bearing).  
   •  Cause and effect swap without notice.  Pack a “causality band-aid”: a child’s wax-paper party horn.  When you hear your own future footstep, blow the horn once to reassert chronological seniority.  
   •  Cold is not absence but species.  It metabolises.  Dress in layers that can be fed: an inner mesh of copper wool the mountain will lick for minerals, a mid-layer of sealed algae packets it will sip for sugars, an outer shell of aerogel it cannot digest.  You are not keeping warm; you are keeping the cold too busy to notice you.

2.  PERMITTED & FORBIDDEN MATTER  
   Allowed metals: bismuth, lithium, gallium.  They are already native blood.  
   Proscribed metals: iron (the mantle will graft it into a new vertebra and you will carry the mountain forever), silver (it mirrors too well—your reflection will step out and lead you astray).  
   Bring zero plastic; the dome will polymerise your memories into a slow-oozing statue.  
   Bring water that has already been inside your body: saved urine in screw-top vials.  Offering it—one capful at each mirror-pool—buys temporary anonymity from the landscape’s census.

3.  ANTI-TRACKING PROTOCOL  
   •  Brush the sole of each boot every kilometre with a paste of garlic mustard and charcoal; the mantle dislikes the scent of disturbed succession.  
   •  Drag a magnetized needle behind you on spider silk; it writes false magnetic stories for any entity trying to read your trail.  
   •  Swap dominant hand every hour; signature gait dissolves when the body cannot decide which side is forward.

4.  NEGOTIATION TOKENS  
   Currency is thermal autobiography.  
   – Carve your childhood winter into a 1 cm ice cube (use a rewarmed paperclip as chisel).  Offer it to any valve-hole that exhales warm breath; passage granted for exactly the length of that remembered season.  
   – Carry three “future moments” written on rice paper.  When cornered by inverted after-images, release one sheet; the paper flash-freezes and shatters into seconds you can run across.

5.  BIO-CONTAINMENT  
   The photosynthetic mantle wants your metabolic song.  Never bleed openly; a scraped knee will sprout laurel-leaf tongues that recite your DNA aloud until the whole dome harmonises and fixes you in place.  
   Carry sterile clotting foam and a roll of lichen-based bandage cultured from your own skin flora before departure.  The mountain recognises self-symbiosis and will usually accept the graft as settled debt.

6.  TEMPORAL FIRST-AID  
   If you arrive before you left:  
   1.  Hand your earlier self the wax-paper horn (see §1).  
   2.  Do not speak; instead press your thumb to their forehead long enough to chill the skin—this synchronises timelines.  
   3.  Leave in a direction that feels uphill (even if visually flat); that is the direction of accrued consequence.  
   Carry a cheap wristwatch set five minutes fast on departure day; when its hands spin backwards you know a revision is in progress—check pockets for objects you did not acquire yet.

7.  SUSTENANCE  
   Food is legal only if it has already died once.  Bring:  
   – freeze-dried krill blocks (marine snow—already part of the planet’s reflux),  
   – dark rye rusks burned at the edges (carbon = pre-eroded memory),  
   – a single cube of candied ginger for emergency; sweetness is a foreign dimension and will eject you from the dome’s digestive tract if things go wrong.  
   Do NOT eat the mantle; it will taste of every extinct season and you will burp ice varves for weeks.

8.  SLEEP ENGINEERING  
   Night does not fall; instead space narrows until your tent poles scrape both “sky” and “ground.”  
   Use a hammock of aerogel mesh hung between two of your own bootprints (prints are loyal to creator).  Zip yourself inside a bivy-bag impregnated with melatonin and iron filings; the mountain will sniff the iron, assume you are inedible ore, and move on.  
   Dream in seven-minute cycles; set a metronome synced to tu-pah valve beats.  Interrupt dreams before narrative climax or they harden into bladelike frost inside the bag.

9.  EMERGENCY EXITS  
   – If the horizon inhales and your trail becomes a knife edge, sprint perpendicular to the breath (you will feel which way is “thicker”).  Continue until you hear yourself step in the past; stop, turn 180°, kick the ground once—this tricks the dome into exhaling space again.  
   – Should ice reach your heart in the central disk, cough deliberately three times; the first cough fractures the forming shell, the second cough names the mountain (it has many, answer to none), the third cough ejects the frost-script map onto your retina—blink twice to transfer it to memory, then RUN.  Do not stop until your sweat smells of your parents’ kitchen.

10.  REPATRIATION & POST-MISSION QUARANTINE  
   On re-entry to baseline Earth you will carry a constant sub-audible crackle—like wet cellophane—inside the Eustachian tube.  This is the mountain’s legal claim: it has sublet your skull for advertising space.  Accept tinnitus for thirty days; do not visit audiologists—they will record the sound, amplify it, and accidentally invite the dome into the clinic’s air-conditioning.  
   Instead flush daily with warm saline plus one drop of your own urine (the mountain remembers generosity).  After a lunar month whisper your actual name backward into a metal box; slam the lid shut—this repatriates your signature and closes the account.

OPTIONAL GESTURE OF GRATITUDE  
When you are safely home, pour a glass of water onto a bed of potted basil at dawn.  The plant will shiver once, in recognition.  From then on, harvest only at twilight; every leaf is a thank-you note forwarded to the oldest mountain, telling it you still remember the etiquette of cold.
