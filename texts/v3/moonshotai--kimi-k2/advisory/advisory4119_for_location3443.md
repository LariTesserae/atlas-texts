Preparing to travel to the Drizzle-Works is preparing to be swallowed by something that has not yet decided whether it is alive.  
The “visa” it issues is not a stamp on paper but a slow mineral audition: you will be measured by how willingly you let your edges dissolve.  
Pack, therefore, for permeability, not protection.

1.  The Drizzle-Works Passport  
Bring no documents the valley cannot read.  
Paper wilts, ink bleeds, electronics corrode in the suspended mist.  
Instead, carry a thin wafer of slate (5 × 8 cm, 3 mm thick) hung on a raw-silk cord.  
Scratch your name on it with a steel nib—no ink—then leave it on your windowsill at home for seven nights.  
By the time you step into the basalt bowl the slate will have grown a pale down of micro-limestone; that film is the only identity the locals recognize.  
Do NOT seal it in plastic; the valley will read the plastic as a refusal and ignore you.

Coordinate system: latitude/longitude fail here; GPS satellites slip off the newborn basalt like wet glass.  
Navigation is acoustic.  
Learn to hum at 38 Hz (lowest note on a contrabass flute).  
When you are one ridge away from the rim, your chest will answer with the same note returned one octave lower and three seconds late.  
Follow the echo downhill; the gap will narrow as you descend.  
When echo and voice converge you are at the lip—step through without hesitation.  
Hesitation leaves you circling warm basalt that smells of struck flint but never yields an entrance.

Clothing
- Outside layer: a second skin that can photosympathize.  
Weave yourself a coat from living orchid fiber—easier than it sounds.  
Three weeks before departure, plant tropical epiphytic orchid seeds on a rectangle of coarse hemp gauze (30 × 60 cm).  
Mist daily with cooled black tea until microscopic roots grip the fibers.  
The orchids will open and close their stomata in response to barometric pressure; in the valley they trade data with the fungus-lamps and keep your temperature half a degree warmer than the ambient drizzle.  
Dead cloth is permissible but will itch: cotton becomes clammy, down drools feathers, synthetics accumulate static sparks that attract curious bats and earn you a leathery cloak of thumb-sized commuters.
- Footwear: soft basalt slippers.  
Grind a fist-sized piece of volcanic scoria into two flat ovals 5 mm thick, felt them into raw wool while still porous.  
The slippers will exhale the valley’s own breath, equalizing pressure so the ankle-high shrinkage polygons do not rock beneath you.
- Head: no hoods.  
The drizzle wants your hair; it uses keratin as nucleation sites for the limestone film.  
Within hours your locks will be flocked white-grey.  
Accept the disguise; shaving it off is read as impersonating a native child and may trap you in a pedagogical conversation conducted through your radius bone at 17 Hz.

Hydration & Nutrition
Water: do NOT carry bottles.  
The suspended droplets reject containment; any canteen sloshes itself empty in minutes, the liquid fleeing to rejoin the communal mist.  
Drink the way residents do—mouth open, pace at 1.2 km h⁻¹.  
Your tongue must sprout 0.3 mm papillar ridges.  
Start training now: twice daily brush tongue with coarse sea salt, then rinse with plain water.  
After ten days the ridges calcify; only then will droplets adhere rather than skate.

Food: metabolism here is communal.  
Bring a digestive starter culture: a pebble of sourdough-sponge dried to wafer, carried next to the heart.  
On entry, crumble it into your left palm and let the drizzle rehydrate it.  
The resulting paste colonizes your gut flora, persuading resident mushrooms you are walking brickwork eligible for mycelial rations.  
Without this symbiosis every meal tastes of cold pennies and leaves you trembling after two days.

Cash
Official currency is limestone mass, but possession is nine-tenths of the law—weight can accrete on you as well as on a tablet, making you wealthier than intended and too heavy to leave.  
Balance the risk: carry a brass coin 2 mm thick, diameter 18 mm, pre-weighed at 3.1415 g.  
Etch a perfect spiral on each face.  
Trade it for the first service you need, then immediately incise the recipient’s initials into the spiral’s third whorl—this prevents the weight back-walking to you during sleep.

Language
Human hearing bottoms out around 20 Hz; valley gossip is 8–18 Hz.  
Pack a 70 cm length of 4 mm steel rod.  
Hold it between clenched teeth while gripping another person’s forearm; the rod conveys subsonics via bone conduction.  
Practice with a friend: translate the sentence “I mean no harm, only passage” into a 12-second vibration pattern (three pulses, pause, five pulses).  
Mispronunciation is lethal politeness: an accidental 19 Hz upswing is an invitation to merge households.

Electronics
Leave anything silicon-based at home.  
If you must document, carry a box camera made of walnut, basalt pinhole, and damp-sensitive photo-paper.  
Expose for one full breathing-cycle of the fungus-lamps (≈ 11 h).  
The resulting image will record not only light but the exact weight of drizzle that landed on the paper—an exquisite geologic receipt.

Health & Safety
Gravity ambiguity: objects <50 g may drift.  
Secure cufflinks, wedding rings, earphones.  
If you feel lighter than memory, exhale sharply; breath-fog is denser than drizzle and will re-anchor you.

Monthly clang: occurs unpredictably but never within 26 h of the previous beat.  
When felt in sternum, drop into a crouch, palms on basalt; the sound can tilt unsecured joints.  
Visitors who remain upright sometimes fracture a kneecap aligned to local time and spend the rest of their lives limping in perfect 4/4 geological meter.

Leaving
The valley's subtlest trap is comfort.  
After roughly three weeks you will wake coated in only the thinnest limestone duvet; your orchid coat will have learned your biorhythm; you will hear 12 Hz arguments as clearly as sparrows.  
This is eviction notice.  
Wait for the next clang, scrape every pale freckle from your shoulders onto the brass coin (now grown heavy with exchanged stories), and walk uphill humming 38 Hz.  
Do not look back until the echo detunes by a major third—if you do, the bowl inhales and the path seals.

Return
Back in ordinary air you will notice:  
radios sound like broken glass, tap water tastes electrified, calendars lie.  
Keep the slate wafer; it will continue accruing film at home, one molecular letter per day.  
When the inscription grows illegible—roughly a year—the Drizzle-Works has finished its sentence to you.  
Crush the slate, scatter powder on a houseplant, and the plant will orient its leaves toward wherever you next need to listen more than speak.

Pack for permeability, apprentice yourself to hesitation, and you may pass through the lung-valley without becoming grist to its slow bright teeth.
