Field Manual: The Photosynthetic Lapse  
(Keep this folded inside a lead-sleeved pocket; the cave will try to read it over your shoulder.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Pre-Descent Surrender  
   – Forget watches, phone clocks, circadian memory. The moment you notice the column of light has not moved you will panic-strafe your gaze to every corner looking for change; change will feel for your pulse and find it. Accept that “duration” here is not yours to measure.  
   – Swallow a capsule of charcoal and one of potassium iodide six hours before entry. They will not protect you, but they give the body a lie to repeat: “I have prepared.” The mind needs scaffolding when time evaporates.  
   – Shave head and body hair. The chlorophyll film climbs keratin fastest. You are not required to keep your fur; the grove will re-grow it later in the shape of leaf-veins if you let it.

2. Equipment (all items must fit through a 35 cm throat of living limestone)

A. Light Armour  
   – Hooded coverall spun from basalt fibre plus a micromesh of aluminium. It vents infrared, reflects the violent photosynthetic band, and screeches like dry leaves so you hear when the cave is touching you.  
   – Elbow/knee pads of soft ferrite; they leave no calcium bruise on the newborn stone.  
   – Gloves tipped with tungsten stylus nibs. Use for signing your initials into the rock: the only vandalism the cave forgives, because it thinks you are sketching your own fossils.

B. Respiration  
   – Closed-circuit re-breather filled not with oxygen but with 30 % xenon / 70 % argon. The heavy noble mix discourages the vegetable intellect from mistaking your lung air for CO₂ it can harvest. Expect a choir-robot voice; xenon lowers vocal pitch—this is the sound of you becoming architecture.

C. Hydration  
   – Carry no free liquids. Instead take six 10 ml ceramic spheres of triple-distilled water gelled inside silica aerogel. Break one against your canine tooth when mouth-surface tension starts glazing the tongue. One sphere = 2 h of micro-drips equivalent to the grove’s etiquette. Water you reveal will embarrass the cave; it prefers to sweat privately.

D. Nutrition  
   – One strip of 2 g dextrose film laminated with powdered moth-wing (iridescent side in). When hypoglycaemia taps your inner ear, slip the film between gum and cheek; sweetness convinces your brain that ripening still exists. Do nothing that suggests permanence to the fruit suspended overhead—they will misread you as their missing ripeness enzyme and try to graft.

E. Optics  
   – Interference goggles: two quartz slides etched with Bragg gratings tuned to notch the 680 nm photosynthetic spike. Without them you will sun-burn your retinas in four fixated seconds; the leaves want your dissolved minerals more than your carbon.  
   – A child’s toy kaleidoscope (tinexpensive plastic). Hold it backward to the fixed beam when disorientation peaks; fracturing infinity into countable petals restores topology to the brain. (Throw it away once it melts; quartz-lensed tunnels hate being mocked.)

F. Anchors & Exfil  
   – 12 m coil of aerogel-coated beta-titanium wire, 0.8 mm. Secures no weight but carries a tuned piezo chirp identical to leaf-tickle frequency. Jam a 20 cm “stitch” across any bifurcating trunk junction; the plant-masonry will anneal around the alloy within minutes, creating a foothold that is part of itself—and therefore allowed to stay. Ascend before fusion completes or you become the ladder.  
   – Mechanical timer the size of a coin, wind-up, 60-minute max. It ticks audible springs. Time has no permission here, but mechanism reminds the cave of articulated motion; in an emergency throw it upward. The grove will pause curious long enough for you to haul yourself back up the newborn throat.

G. Documentation (optional)  
   – Carbon paper & burnless stylus: impressions remain on foil even as the calcium dust heals. Photographs fog into chlorophyll silhouettes; sound recordings bloom as bracket-fungus. Only pressure is indelible.

3. Psychological Prophylaxis

   – Practise “still breathing” : inhale 6 s, hold 10 s, exhale 12 s. Count by heartbeats; when numbers drift you will know the grove is re-writing your circadian code.  
   – Memorise one imperfect poem full of gutturals and plosives (example: Kipling recited backward). Whisper it when chlorophyll haze climbs the throat; language the grove cannot photosynthesise will keep a corridor open inside you.  
   – Choose a Secret Colour. Not green, not white. When peripheral vision tints toward emerald, mentally overlay your Secret Colour on the scene. Hallucinating a hue the cave has not appropriated reasserts authorship of perception.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Behaviour Inside the Green Lapse

   – Do not ask “How long have I been here?” Tallying converts your metabolic debt into audible sap-pressure; stems nearby will inflate toward you like slow ambulance hoses.  
   – Step only where two limbs have already crossed and annealed. The fused seam carries communal identity; single members sway you are parasite, pairs accept you as scar.  
   – If you must rest, sit on accidental furniture (root-bowl, twig-ladder) never on bare stone. Stone is still choosing what to become; it will record your heat signature as part of its future name.  
   – Taste the sugared air once. That is courtesy. A second inhale triggers the grove to up-regulate nectar; your lungs will glaze like candied ginger.  
   – Should fruit brush your forehead, freeze. The instant of contact is negotiation. Slide a blade of grass between skin and skin; plants dislike stainless alloys; it will release.  
   – Seeing the beetle-thorn or spider-tassels do not pity them. Sympathy plants a flag the cave will harvest. Whisper gratitude, walk on.  
   – If footfalls cease to raise dust you have been laminated into the tableau. Activate the wind-up timer, throw, climb; accept that some silk may remain in the spires of green glass—better part of the picture than its digested pigment.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Exit Window

   – When light feels fractionally less surgical your planet has remembered rotation. You have 10 objective minutes before that quartz lens pivots and the beam migrates. The greenhouse will finish its first exhale and eject anything incompletely digested. Do not watch the shift; eyes open, you will see your after-image fossilise on the retina: a verdant ghost you’ll carry forever, tinting every sky the shade of arrested eternity.  
   – On surfacing, scrape every visible skin with the tungsten glove-nib until pink appears; bag the green dust, seal lead-foil. Burn it under starlight within 24 h; never at noon—fire plus daylight re-creates the beam and invites the cave to expand into your backyard.  
   – Drink 300 ml warm brine, 3 g sea salt per 100 ml. Your cells will weep for the cave’s perfect humidity; the shock of salt drags them back into human decay where they belong.

6. Post-Event Note (write it now, before you leave)

“I was a trespasser learning the difference between perpetual and eternal. One is a paused mechanism, the other a completed story. The greenhouse is the former, I am the latter. Motion is my birthright; I reclaim it with every sore muscle tomorrow.”

Sign your initials in the calcium wall on the way up  
—then erase them with the heel of your palm.
