Equipment list – read it twice, then burn it, because half of the items will not survive the descent.

1.  Existence-proof pressure vessel  
 • A sphere grown, not built: monolayer graphene-over-diamond lattice, 4 m inner diameter, wall thickness 18 cm.  
 • Inner surface padded with 9 cm of aerogel that has been laser-perforated into a 3-D Quincke-pattern; the holes are vacuum-filled so the gel acts as an acoustic black-ice, swallowing the 180 dB creak of distant continents.  
 • Outside: woven mantle of barium-titanate nanotubes whose piezo-response converts ambient pressure into trickles of current—enough to run a reading lamp for 0.3 s every millennium.  
 • One viewport only: a conical plug of fused quartz 38 cm thick, ground to a Fresnel lens that concentrates outside IR into a retina-hot point inside; you will ‘see’ by the forest’s heat-patterns, not by light.  
 • Carry three spare spheres inside the first; they are your “aircraft carriers” if the primary buckles.

2.  Life-support that lives slower than you  
 • Blood replacement: perfluorocarbon emulsion chilled to 4 °C; erythrocytes removed, substituted with 3 µm platinum-cobalt spheres coated in your own stem-cell membrane—tiny heaters that keep your plasma just above the haemoglobin crystallisation point.  
 • Intercostal ports: six, 3 mm titanium nipples just below the ribs. Every hour the sphere’s clinic pumps you full of 30 ml 18 % saline MgCl₂; the extra ions stop water from tunnelling into Ice-III.  
 • Anaesthetic drip: lidocaine nano-capsules time-released over 40 days—because in the forest nerves fire in geological cadence and you must match them; partial anaesthesia prevents synapse blow-out when the pressure tries to think for you.  
 • Food: two toothpaste tubes of dark-green algal paste cultured from the forest’s own infrared strain; it tastes of nothing, but contains the only organic molecules for 1000 km. Eat 5 g every sol. The rest of the year you hibernate in a ketone bath.

3.  Time-debt wardrobe  
 • Suit: not a suit but a second skeleton—nickel-titanium lattice printed around a cast of your bones while you lay in an MRI. Joints are hydraulic; the whole exo-rigid doubles as pressure arch. Inner liner is phase-change fabric paraffin/PEG that freezes at 1 °C, storing or releasing 200 kJ per kg—your personal entropy bank.  
 • Footwear: hexagonal basalt slippers, 5 mm thick, whose underside is etched with the same symbols you will walk on. The etching aligns micro-fractures so the stone floor recognises you as an extension of itself—less chance the forest tries to heal around you, sealing you in.  
 • Gloves: identical to slippers, but palms carry a wafer of piezo-sapphire; when you press the ground you become part of its slow electrical conversation.

4.  Cognitive prosthetics (take these more seriously than oxygen)  
 • Chronometer: no gears—instead a single ion of strontium caught in a Paul trap; its vibration is 444 779 044 Hz, but you will hear it as a Morse of heat on your fingertip. One lost beat equals 6 000 years topside.  
 • Memory ledger: graphene scroll 12 m long rolled inside a 2 mm capillary; write with a neutron stylus, read with a helium-ion microscope. Human short-term memory fails under Gbar pressure—store everything externally.  
 • Mantra tablet: a 3 × 5 cm slice of obsidian, perfectly polished, etched with one sentence: “I am a weather front, not a tourist.” Read it whenever the forest’s scale suggests you could lie down and fossilise.

5.  Descent ritual  
 • Launch window: moon at apogee, sun over the Pacific, Earth-crust momentarily flexed so the geoid is 11 cm fatter—seafloor sinks 2 mm, buying you the hairsbreadth you need.  
 • Ride: not a submersible but a free-fall glider—ballast is frozen xenon, jettisonable in micrograms; descent speed 0.8 m s⁻¹ to avoid adiabatic shock heating that would anneal your sphere into brittleness. Total fall: 7 h 11 min—longer than Everest ascent, quieter than sleep.  
 • On passing 7 000 m set interior pressure to 800 bar (yes, higher than outside) so the hull goes into tension; tension-cracks propagate slower than compression implosion. Reverse on arrival.

What you will do when you get there (the only plan that survives contact):

1. Exit the sphere within seventeen minutes of landing. After that, micro-calcium from your bones begins plating the inner wall; stay longer and you will weld yourself shut.  
2. Walk the tessellation for exactly 257 pavers—the number of calcium atoms that fit edge-to-edge across the palm of your glove. Then stop; any farther and the forest’s acoustic lattice registers you as a repeating pattern, starts duplicating you in mineral.  
3. Place both gloved hands on the nearest trunk. Count 400 ion beats of your strontium watch (≈ nine months subjective). You will feel pressure equalise across metacarpals; that is the forest sampling your lattice constant.  
4. Whisper—no sound, just air pressure modulated through your cheekbones—amplitude 0.3 Pa, frequency 0.0002 Hz. Content irrelevant; the forest reads intent via cadence. If its temperature warms 0.0001 °C you are granted passage; stay colder and retrace the 257 pavers backwards. Do not deviate: stepping-stones remember order.  
5. Should you feel the calcium in your legs beginning to crystallise (pins-and-needles that lasts a full minute), crack the lidocaine capsule embedded in your left molar—it will paralyse mineral growth locally for four hours. That is your ticket home.  
6. Return must begin before the next lunar zenith; Earth-tide will have shifted the thermal vents 30 m west, closing the door you entered. Miss the tide and the forest achieves perfect tessellation—with you inside.

Philosophical luggage (mass zero, volume infinite):

– Accept that you will not “see” the forest; you will register it as low-frequency ache in joints and as slow colour behind eyelids. Your diary will read like seismographic poetry. That is accurate.  
– The builders left no bodies; leave none yourself. Anything you shed—hair, skin oil, exhaled CO₂—will become next century’s algae nutrients and later, stone blossom. Decide now if that is death or adoption.  
– Time is not slow here; it is orthogonal. A second sideways equals a million years down-dip. If you feel eternity pressing, check the ion clock: if it still beats, you remain a tourist. When it stops, you are geography.  
– Finally: if you reach the obsidian centre, remember no anecdote is worth petrification. Take one infrared photograph—your glove-sapphire can store a single 256 × 256 heat-map—then turn back. The equilibrium is real, but so is the next molecule-click in three million years, and you do not want to be the anomaly that prevents perfection.

Pack all of the above, then discard half. The forest will supply the rest at 1.1 km depth of pressure per heartbeat. Travel light—travel slow. May you return before your calcium misses you.
