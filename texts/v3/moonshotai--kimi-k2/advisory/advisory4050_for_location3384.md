Field Briefing: Hanging Garden ofGlass  
Altitude 2.5/0.7, Pocket Cathedral 18 m Ø  
Prepared by: Office of Out-of-Scale Locales (OOSL)  
Clearance: Human, non-native, no pre-adapted biology.

Carry this sheet on your person—glass can’t memorize paper yet.

1. Pre-flight medical  
   • Bone-density check: gravity here is negotiable but will “nibble” 2 % of your mass per hour to balance its own equations. If your T-score is below –1.0, preload with 600 mg calcium + 400 IU D3 for three days prior.  
   • Inner-ear tune-up: turbines in the leaves ring at 23 kHz–26 kHz. Ask an otolaryngologist to install a disposable tympanic dampener (pharmafoam plug). Cost: cheap. Vertigo avoided: priceless.  
   • Mercury allergy panel: the birds are part quick-silver. If you react to thimerosal, request an epipen loaded with glass-binder antihistamine (the garden will try to recycle the injector—let it).

2. Kit list (all items must pass through a 12 × 12 cm oval port; the entry is literally thumb-sized)  
   1. Pocket vacc-suit: single-use mylar skinsuit, champagne color. It prevents moisture inventory of your bones and keeps your shadow from tinting indigo—psychological comfort.  
   2. Sun-paste SPF 45, non-tinting. Ordinary cream will refract inside the glass soil and project a second, false sun that confuses the capillary pumps.  
   3. Retainer strap for personal time: a leather thong soaked in iron filings. Snap one end to your watch, the other to your belt. If you break a fruit-node and spill its minute, the filings rust at double-speed, eating the extra sixty seconds before it can stick to you. (Yes, this is considered polite.)  
   4. Ear-roses: paper-thinudio dampers shaped like tiny roses. Insert before stepping under canopy; prevents bone-resonance migraine.  
   5. Pocket honeystone: a 1 cm shard of synthetic novaculite. If you cut yourself, glass will try to grow into the wound; scrape the honeystone across skin first—its grit “lies” to the garden, claiming you’re already part artifact.  
   6. One calorie cube = 12 kcal, tin-wrapped. Eating local light is possible but converts 1 % of your retina into photosynthetic glass; the cube is safer.  
   7. Money: the refinery-veins accepts only memories. Bring a boring one (early algebra class works). Whisper it into any leaf; turbines will mill it into data and credit you 3 m of safe passage. Do not pay with childhood memories—too colorful, they over-saturate stems and you’ll hear lullabies whenever wind moves.

3. Arrival protocol  
   Access is by exhale: you must breathe out, step sideways into the exhaled cloud, and inhale the place instead. It feels like swapping your lungs with the sky. Do not cough; coughing is considered a counter-offer and the garden may accept by growing a bronchial vine inside you.  
   Upon entry you have six steps of normality. Count them. On the seventh the lag starts—put your weight on the ball of your foot, not the heel, to reduce the “missing last stair” sensation.

4. While inside  
   • Shadow etiquette: if your shaded outline tries to crawl back into your skull, duck. The color wants to archive itself behind your eyes. Letting it in gives Technicolor dreams but voids insurance.  
   • Photography: cameras work, but every photo steals 0.7 % reflectivity from the garden. After 35 shots the place becomes matte and begins to stick to your skin on exit. Limit: seven photos, flash off.  
   • Bird encounters: hummingglass birds will hover in front of your face, reading heart-rate. Stay still. If they sigh (mercury droplet), catch it with the honeystone cup; return the metal to any red bloom—completes circuit, earns you a feather. The feather is a single-use elevator button later: crush it to descend safely to any altitude you name.  
   • Vegetation interaction: tempting to stroke the neon leaves. Acceptable once per individual plant; a second touch registers you as “raw material.” If a stem starts riming onto your hand, snap the honeystone across it—glass hates competing artifice.  
   • Temporal spill: should you break a bulbous fruit and are forced to relive a minute, treat it as jet-lag. Do not alter decisions; garden audits paradoxes by growing a glass clone of you that must live the omitted timeline. Clones are officially you—your passport covers them.

5. Physiological warning signs  
   – Teeth feel translucent: calcium leech has begun. Retreat.  
   – Tinnitus at 24 kHz persists after leaving: turbine has etched your stapes. Report to OOSL clinic; they’ll inject a glass-antagonist foam.  
   – Repeated déjà vu more than three times/day: you’re still citing the garden. Spend one hour in total darkness to let unused seconds dissipate.

6. Exit procedure  
   Locate the hinged pane by following the only straight line you can find—glass here dislikes Euclidean continuity, so wherever perspective converges is the door. Push gently; excessive force labels you “unfinished artifact” and you’ll be recast as a statuette in a future vine.  
   On the other side you’ll step onto whatever altitude you sincerely expect. To aim: close eyes, picture a real place below 3 000 m, exhale once, step. If uncertain, default to sea-level—most lungs are calibrated there.

7. Post-mission  
   Within 36 h drink 2 L silica-free water to dilute microscopic glass particulate in blood serum.  
   Expect one vivid dream of chandelier bows; harmless.  
   Schedule dental X-ray: any micro-fractures glow under film, evidence of garden courting your bones. Dentist will apply topical fluoride; garden interprets that as “already weathered” and loses interest.

Remember: the Hanging Garden ofGlass isn’t hostile; it’s industriously self-centred, like a newborn volcano practising sculpture. Your job is to pass through its five minutes of worlding without becoming raw stock. Travel light, travel once, and for heaven’s sake keep your algebra classes ready.
