Traveler’s Field Guide  
to  
The Ridge-That-Was-A-Range  
(commonly glossed “Aveld Spine”)  

Bring nothing that expects a world.

1. Temperature etiquette  
The cold does not attack; it infiltrates. Dress in layers that can be abandoned one at a time, because the chill waits inside the cloth itself. Two suits are recommended:  
an inner cocoon of wool silked with carbon thread (to trick the cold into thinking you are already stone)  
and an outer shell of matte-black aerogel felt thin enough to crinkle like paper.  
Never seal the shell; if perspiration cannot migrate outward it will be collected by the ridge and returned as rime beards that glue the hood to your face. Carry a plastic scraper of monofilament polycarbonate—metal screams here, and the ridge answers.

2. Water that is not water  
Conventional canteens are useless: liquid open to air sublimates into the hovering midge-fog and is rolled away as glass beads. Carry instead a concertina inhaler of saline mist. Spray twice on the tongue whenever the ghost-metallic taste appears; the droplets will be adsorbed before the ridge notices. Mark the cartridge; when the printed millilitres begin to drip off as silver numerals, swallow them—they taste of noon and will hallucinate warmth enough to prevent countable hypothermia.

3. Food  
Calories must be flat. The ridge abhors volume. Bring sheets:  
compressed nori of lipidised protein  
sugar glass laminated with caffeine  
and a slab of zero-porosity chocolate the exact thickness of one sedimentary lamina.  
Chew with the mouth open to the sky so the sound escapes upward and does not echo down the wells; otherwise the city will catalogue your jawbeat as a foundation tone and rearrange a staircase into your dental arcade.

4. Footwear  
Boots must be of dead material—vulcanised rubber reclaimed from truck tires, soles tattooed with the mileage of highways. The ridge recognises its own extinct fauna in the tread and permits passage. Lace with Kevlar twine impregnated with iron filings; when the negative gravity spots appear the filings will drift first, tugging the boot-top a countable half-second early—your warning to adjust stride. Socks are to be worn inside-out so the lint can record reversed footprints for later reference if you meet yourself walking backward.

5. Sleeping  
No tents; fabric becomes a sail for katabatic draughts. Instead, burrow a shallow trench exactly one body-length along the lee side of a chlorophyll blade—the stone leaves flex outward and form a cradle. Before lying down, speak the day’s total footstep count aloud; the ridge imprints this number as a temporary centre of gravity, preventing nocturnal roll-off. If you miscount in your sleep you will wake standing upside-down beneath the ridge watching your own dream continue above; cut the tether by spitting the forgotten digit into the void.

6. Navigation  
Compasses spin alphabets. Maps must be sung; therefore memorise one twelve-note throat-ballad corresponding to the crest from Obelisk-in-the-Day to Sky-Well-at-Dusk. Hum under breath; when the echo changes key you have crossed a synoptic weather cell—update the lyric or the ridge will cite an outdated dusk and strand you in fossil daylight.  
If lost, plant your bronze chisel (see 8) blade-up in a gutter of soil; wherever it leans is now “down’ for 137 beats—walk that vector until the flute-tone of crystal leaves returns to B-flat; you are back on the published route.

7. Count discipline  
Carry two tally systems:  
a knotted chlorophyll cord that records tens  
and a pocket abacus of chilled mercury beads that records ones.  
On every tenth step toggle the cord; on every twenty-first sip a bead, roll it across molars, swallow if it tastes of turmeric—this confirms you still occupy forward chronology. Should a bead taste of nothing, freeze; do not move until you taste the next one, even if hours pass. The ridge sometimes borrows your timeline; interest is paid in moments.

8. Toolkit  
One basalt chisel (bronze-hard, city-standard issue)  
three metres of woven chlorophyll rope (works only when unobserved—store in an opaque sleeve)  
a quartz lens polished to 6 % light-gathering; use to darken shadows, not to brighten light  
and a folding scraper for personal rime.  
Keep each item in contact with your skin so it learns your mass; otherwise the city may enrol the tools as citizens and you will wake to find your hands replaced by neat oval wells.

9. Etiquette in Aveld  
Do not claim any chamber as “yours”; ownership is carved in negative space and you are still positive.  
If you encounter fossil hikers mid-peel, greet them by reciting their own future mistake—read the rock script upside-down. They will step aside, letting the wind finish flaking them.  
Should you meet yourself walking backward, offer the duplicate a bead of mercury; whichever swallows first becomes the original and the other is annexed by erosion. Accept the outcome gracefully—both will feel equally real.

10. Emergency exit protocol  
When the ridge begins to quote an unsustainable dusk (sky bronze at noon, stormlight strobing the wells) perform the following:  
a) hum the map-ballad in retrograde  
b) eat one mercurial numeral to hallucinate warm colours  
c) walk into the hallucinated colour that exactly matches your birth-weather  
d) keep walking until the colour fades to ordinary cold grey—this is the threshold.  
Look back once; you will see the ridge as a single thread inside your past life. Do not wave; gestures are stored as silhouette laminae and billed against your future centre. Step forward. The filament will snap like frost and you will be home, minus one continental layer of self.

Remember: you were never here. The ridge merely rehehearsed you.
