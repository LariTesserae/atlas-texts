Go alone.  
If you must bring someone, bring only the person you would trust to close your eyes after death.  
Do not bring light; the place keeps a ledger of every lumen it is owed, and collects in dreams.  
Do not bring offerings; they will be re-gifted to you later, wrapped in your own fingerprints.

—— PREPARATION ——  
1.  One week before, stop naming things aloud.  Practice pointing to what you need: the kettle, the door, your reflection.  The pocket dislikes labels; they stick to the bricks like price-tags that can never be fully peeled.  
2.  Eat nothing fermented for three days—no kimchi, no yeast breads, no wine.  Your gut must reach the crack already emptied of its tiny civil wars; their echoes upset the rust filaments.  
3.  Sleep each night with a spoon of iron under the mattress.  This is not superstition; it is calibration.  The minerals will gossip beneath your dreams and acquaint your blood with the dialect spoken below.  (If the spoon rusts completely, postpone the journey; you are not yet porous enough.)  
4.  On the final morning, walk the city from rim to rim without crossing the same street twice.  Collect every echo you hear—footsteps, snippets of quarrels, bicycle bells—zip them into an imaginary purse.  You will spend them as coin when silence demands tribute.  
5.  Dress in the clothes you wore the last time you sincerely changed your mind about something.  If you cannot remember such a time, undress instead; the pocket accepts nakedness as evidence of good faith.

—— DESCENT ——  
At the tenement, choose the stair whose banister wobbles like a bad tooth.  Count steps downward until you lose count; then subtract two, and you are there.  
The air will taste of pennies sucked in a child’s mouth.  
Kneel first.  The floor is slicker than betrayal; humility is traction.  
Crawl to the crack on all fours so your heart stays lower than your head; otherwise it will fibrillate from the humming bricks.

—— CONDUCT INSIDE ——  
Breath:  
Inhale for a slow count of seven, hold for the count it takes a promise to break, exhale for eleven.  The filament-water drinks the difference; let it.  If you feel vertigo, remember gravity is merely another municipal ordinance; here it is quietly repealed.  
Time:  
Do not consult your pulse.  Count instead the varieties of grey until color becomes a fairy tale you once half-believed.  When you forget what red was, the pocket begins to recognize you.  
Rust filaments:  
Allow them to taste you.  They sample the salt-calendar of your past.  If one lodges beneath a fingernail, do not pull it out; it is taking dictation.  Months later you will find a metal thread in the lint trap of your dryer—memory repatri as jewelry.  
Speech:  
Limit yourself to monosyllables older than the city.  “Hush,” “stone,” “wait,” “still.”  They are passwords that expire the moment they are spoken; use them sparingly.  
Your Name:  
Swallow it.  Hold it under the tongue like a pill too bitter to bite.  When you leave, it will taste different—renamed by absence.

—— EXIT FEE ——  
When you feel the certainty draining, do not clutch it.  Let it bead out through your palms; the water will carry it upward and wedge it quietly into the next stranger who passes overhead.  You may later meet that stranger in a sun-lit café and see your own abandoned conviction flicker behind their irises—nod, but do not reclaim it; gifts to the pocket are non-refundable.  
Turn away from the climbing rope of droplets; do not watch the moment it knots itself into the floorboards above.  That knot is your memory of the place being severed.  You are granted just enough amnesia to survive daylight.

—— AFTERCARE ——  
1.  Do not shower for twenty-four hours.  The rust needs to finish corroding your timetable.  
2.  Speak your first full sentence aloud to running water—sink faucet, drinking fountain, rain gutter.  If the water flinches, you carried something out you shouldn’t have; return within a week and leave a coin pressed into the basalt as interest.  
3.  Buy fruit you have never tasted before.  Bite into it in bright daylight; let the sugars argue with the metallic aftertaste.  This reintroduces the concept of “ripe” to your calendar.  
4.  Expect statues where no statues were.  Read the plaques.  If you find your own name, do not correct the error; instead lay flowers—white ones, flavorless.  It keeps the fiction harmless.  
5.  When insomnia comes (and it will), lie on your left side and listen for a low bee-hum in the mattress.  It is the pocket’s postcard, reminding you that stillness is not the opposite of motion but its eventual, patient spouse.  Hum back until you both fall asleep.

—— LAST WARNING ——  
Some visitors leave lighter, yes.  Others leave unfinished in the most literal way: a ghost coil of rust shaped like a silhouette, propped against the wall, waiting for the next drop to nudge it into motion.  The pocket is indifferent; it merely keeps the ledger.  Make sure your account closes in your favor.  

Traveler, go gently.  The flaw has outlived every edifice that tried to pave it over; it will outlive your riddance as well.  Pack emptiness, owe nothing, and when you feel the sour breath rise—breathe back.
