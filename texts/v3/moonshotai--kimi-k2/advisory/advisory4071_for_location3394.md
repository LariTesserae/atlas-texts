HOW TO PACK FOR A THUNDER-SHELL  
(And how to keep from becoming scenery)

0. PRE-DECISION CHECK  
Read the paragraph above twice.  
If, after the second reading, you can still remember your surname, you are already underprepared. The place has begun its soft edit of you; the instructions below are for the version of you that still thinks it is going to “visit” somewhere. Print them. If the page begins to moisten, burn it immediately and memorise whatever words survive the flame.

1. BEFORE YOU LEAVE HOME  
1.1 Depersonalise your belongings  
Remove brand logos, country-of-origin stickers, monograms, anything that asserts “I come from somewhere else.” The thunder-shell treats labels as unfinished footnotes and will try to complete them—with you inside.

1.2 Vaccinate against ontological drift  
Obtain a vial of sterile, distilled paradox (any reputable multiverse clinic keeps it on ice). Inject 0.3 ml into each ear canal; you will hear a faint chord that refuses to resolve. That is the sound of your worldview becoming soluble. Keep the vial; you will trade it later for directions.

1.3 Write a will that forgets itself  
Lawyers will protest. Insist on disappearing ink. The document must become illegible within twenty-three hours—same half-life as memory in the shell. This prevents the water from citing you as precedent.

2. THE THREE-BODY PROBLEM  
You will need three bodies, but only one may be biological at any given moment.

2.1 Prime Body (biological)  
- Lungs: Replace alveolar lining with reversible osmotic mesh so you can breathe saline as easily as O₂.  
- Bones: Inlay each long bone with a hollow tungsten reed tuned to 432 Hz. When the roar inside your skull reaches critical mass, uncap the reeds; they will accept the excess resonance and whistle it out as harmless melody.  
- Eyes: Fit interchangeable irises—one pair polarised against strobing basalt-flicker, one pair fully transparent so the bioluminescence can write on your retina without asking permission.  
- Mouth: Dental caps of cooled obsidian. You will be invited to bite chunks of newborn stone; obsidian teaches them manners.

2.2 Secondary Body (astral)  
Pack it like carry-on luggage: folded into a solitaire diamond worn on the non-dominant hand. Do NOT polish it; frosted facets give the soul something to grip when its owner is being re-written syllable by syllable.

2.3 Tertiary Body (mineral)  
A thumb-sized shard of your future fossil. (Quarry it from a timeline where you already died peacefully; most grave-robbing statutes only apply to the living.) The thunder-shell accepts bribes in the currency of “possible corpses”; offering yours ahead of schedule buys you three safe malfunctions of physics.

3. CLOTHING  
You will be naked within three seconds of arrival—water that behaves like undecided air abhors textile precedent. Dress anyway; the garments are not for you, they are mnemonics for the place.

3.1 Outer shell: A coat sewn from sharkskin sandpaper (sharks that evolved to graze on legal documents). The coat pockets must contain impossible objects: a square circle, the sound of one hand clapping, the gender of Tuesdays. Unclip these and scatter them when you feel categories dissolving; the shell will pause to fact-check.

3.2 Mid-layer: Thermoregulatory myth. Spun from campfire stories—every thread a tale your grandparents never quite told. It keeps you lukewarm in water that forgets what temperature means.

3.3 Base-layer: Tattooed directly onto the dermis: the entire periodic table, but all atomic numbers shifted +1. This prevents local chemistry from bonding with you long enough to steal your mass.

4. KIT (All items must fit through a fist-sized hoop; dimensional etiquette insists)  
A. Pocket anti-ontology (lemon-shaped) – Squeeze when you catch yourself becoming metaphor.  
B. Eight ounces of dehydrated calendar – rehydrate one day at a time when sequence betrays you.  
C. A miniature hurricane bottled in a baby-food jar—use as compass; it always points toward the greatest instability, i.e., the exit that may or may not presently exist.  
D. Spare’s Universal Solvent (brand slogan: “Dissolves anything except regret”)—one drop opens doorways, two drops close them, three drops forgets you ever wanted passage.  
E. Pack of chewing gum flavoured with your childhood phone number; chew to remember how to call for help before phones were invented.  
F. One honest coin—any culture, any epoch—the shell respects a voluntary exchange. Flip it when you can’t decide whether you’re predator or set dressing; obey the coin, but don’t look at the outcome—knowing collapses the waveform.

5. IMMERSION PROTOCOL (ARRIVAL)  
5.1 Breach  
Step through whatever transitional membrane your transport uses. Do NOT inhale during traversal; the interim vacuum between worlds is a copyright buffer—breathing there incurs royalty fees payable in personal memories.

5.2 First thirty seconds  
The column will try to teach you its accent. Hum the note you heard during the paradox injection. Match pitches until the water decides you are a dialect, not an error.

5.3 Orientation  
Pick a direction and call it “aft.” Stick to this delusion; consistency is the cheapest form of gravity.

6. SURVIVAL CHECKLIST  
☐ Count heartbeats only in prime numbers; the shell hates perfect rhythm and will leave you alone out of artistic spite.  
☐ If you meet yourself swimming the other direction, do not synchronise limbs. Offer the duplicate the honest coin and swim on; it’s negotiating for your plot armour.  
☐ Should a frond fold into your palm and leave a “heartbeat,” tuck it behind your left incisor. There it will grow into a story you can trade for safe passage when language finishes mutating.  
☐ The spiral staircase of frozen spray: ascend, never descend. Descending implies narrative closure; the thunder-shell hasn’t written that page yet.  
☐ If you begin to suspect you are the director of the rehearsal, fire yourself immediately. Responsibility is the first step in petrifaction.

7. EXIT STRATEGY  
7.1 The sky-mirror interval  
Watch for the impossible instant when the column calms and shows you an unborn sky containing your own face. Memorise the face but NOT the sky. That memory is your return ticket.

7.2 Timing  
You have exactly one heartbeat after the reflection appears. Close your left fist (it holds the tertiary fossil). Whisper the childhood phone number into the gap between seconds. The lemon-shaped anti-ontology will activate automatically, tasting of iron, cinnamon, and unbound electrons. Swallow the taste. You will surface inside the transport you forgot you boarded.

8. POST-RETURN CARE  
- Do not rehydrate the entire calendar at once; days will gang up and beat present-tense senseless.  
- Expect inner-ear static for six weeks—percussive echoes of the shell’s roar. Teach it lullabies; it makes excellent roommate once domesticated.  
- Check your reflection in any mirror: if the pupils are flickering basalt-orange, schedule an immediate audit with a licensed ontologist—you left a body behind and it’s moon-lighting as landscape.  
- Finally, write the experience down, but print it on rice paper, store it underwater in a sealed jar, and never open it. That document is the only reliable map for the next traveller. The thunder-shell keeps expanding its footnotes; someone, somewhere, will need your version of the script.

Remember: the place is not hostile; it is under-rehearsed and you are a walk-on role that hasn’t decided whether to become cast, crew, or curtain. Pack for all three.
