Travel advisory: The Low Plain Before the World Forgets Itself  
    (informal working title: “The Last Flattening”)

words are maps – but this country is made of breath.

0.  First principle  
 cannot arrive here.  You can only be exhaled into it.  
 Therefore the journey is 80 % preparation of the body-mind membrane,  size of the holes you allow yourself to have.

1.  Passport & bureaucracy  
 There is no border station, but you still have to cross.  
 Bring an object that proves you once needed height – a spent bus ticket from a mountain tram, a pebble from a summit cairn.  
 Hold it in your mouth for the final kilometre.  
 When it dissolves (it will) you are granted a kind of viscosity-citizenship.  
 Do not try to keep the stone; swallow.

2.  Footwear – none  
 Bare soles are the only visa stamp the plain recognises.  
 Boots remember ridges and will try to recreate them underfoot, causing micro-waves that upset the flatness equation.  
 Trim toenails perfectly round; any sharp corner will be mirrored months later by a corresponding ripple in the water-table.  
 If you must walk through civilisation first, carry your boots strung around your neck like a pair of shellfish that died of shame.

3.  Clothing: become a sponge  
 Fabric should be untreated hemp-linen, cotton, or reclaimed fishing net.  
 Synthetic fibres repel water; the plain interprets that as resistance and will slowly increase the air-pressure around you until the seams implode.  
 Colour: only those which already appear in bruise or penny.  
Stitch a small outside pocket on the left hip; keep it empty.  
 That is the place your reflection will later sit when it decides to stay.

4.  Time-piece  
 Leave every instrument that counts seconds.  
 Instead, bring a shallow ceramic saucer.  
 Every morning pour into it one drop of your own saliva; the rate at which it dries tells you how much day you have left.  
 When the saucer crumbs, you go home.

5.  Food – do not bring any  
 The plain eats for you.  
 Breathe through the nose only; the mist carries a caloric content of 3.2 kJ per cubic metre, mostly in the form of dissolved pollen and historical flour.  
 After the third day your stomach will forget hunger and begin to accordion gently in sympathy with the ground-lung.  
 If you must carry emergency r true ration, let it be a single teaspoon of salt.  
 Hold it under the tongue until hallucinations of potato fields appear, then spit it out – the whiteness is needed elsewhere.

6.  Hydration paradox  
 Water is everywhere and nowhere.  
 Drinking it is considered theft; inhaling it is etiquette.  
 Bring a reed the length of your forearm; use it only to sip the condensation off grass tips at dawn.  
 Never twice from the same blade – that would constitute storytelling and the blade will curl into a question-mark that has no answer.

7.  Biological etiquette  
 Every organism here has already paid the entry fee (height).  
 You owe them the courtesy of low profile.  
 Crawl for the first hour each day; this aligns your inner ear with the regional datum of 0.00 m.  
 If a frog the colour of old parchment calls, reply by closing one eye – left for male, right for female, both for undecided.  
 They will gift you a temporary name that smells of iron; forget it immediately or it will follow you home and rust your dreams.

8.  Sleep system  
 No tent.  
 A tent is a pocket of un-flattened space and will invert like a blister during the night.  
 Instead carry four sewing needles.  
 At dusk push them into the ground at cardinal points-points; they conduct the planetary sigh into your spine and keep you anchored while you deflate horizontally.  
 Lie supine, mouth open; the mist will settle on your tongue and form the exact dream you are not allowed to remember.

9.  Light & fire  
 Fire is categorised as “vertical provocation” and will be extinguished by consensus of evaporating soil.  
 Bring no lamp.  
 Night vision arrives automatically once your retinas absorb enough dissolved silica.  
 Expect a grey brightness in which your own bones are readable; do not read them, it counts as prior publication.

10.  Waste  
 Everything you excrete is already present here in archaeological form.  
 Dig no cathole.  
 Instead, deposit onto the centre of a glasswort mat; the plant will encyst it, turn it into a small green prism, and donate it back to the water-table in 2–300 years when you are no longer copyright holder of the smell.

11.  Photography – impossible  
 A lens attempts to impose focal depth; the plain responds by flattening the glass.  
 All cameras will produce images of the inside of their own casing.  
 If you must record, prick the ball of your thumb and press the blood onto the saucer mentioned in §4.  
 The resulting clot is a true monochrome of the horizon; mail it to no one.

12. EXIT protocol  
 When the saucer crumbles earlier than expected, or when you notice your footprints beginning to precede you by half a step, walk backwards along your incoming track.  
 Do not turn; the horizon is a sentence that finishes only in your peripheral vision.  
 At the first sound of zipper (any zipper, even someone else’s in another year) insert the spent bus ticket/pebble between gum and cheek again.  
 It will have grown.  
 Spit it out: if it falls upward you may leave.  
 If it sinks, sit back down – there is still a paragraph you owe.

13.  Re-acclimatisation upon return  
 For three days after homecoming do not climb stairs.  
 Drink only water that has been left uncovered overnight in a shallow vessel; this re-introduces the correct lag between sky and reflection.  
 Tell no stories; the plain collects royalties in the form of altitude from whoever speaks its lines.  
 When you finally yawn wide enough to feel the heron-coloured wings rust inside your mouth, you are free – until the next breath.

Remember: the place does not exist.  
 It is the space that remains when the world has finished exhaling.  
 Your job is to arrive as pause, not as punctuation.
