Field Manual:  
Survival, Etiquette, and Minimal-Comfort Protocols for a Human Visitor to the Low-Plain Coral Works  
(Edition for travelers who arrive with only one circulatory system and no natural exoskeleton)

--------------------------------------------------
0. Philosophy – Read This First
 the City is inside-out biology masquerading as inside-out industry.  
 Every “structure” is a stomach, a womb, and a liver at once.  
 You are a potential vitamin. Behave accordingly.

--------------------------------------------------
1. Pre-arrival Inoculations & Body Modifications

Lungs  
- Steam + metallic aerosol = acute bronchiolar metallosis in 36 h.  
- Request nanofilter alveolar insert (porous PTFE + silver lattice, 0.1 µm). If your medical charter won’t allow permanent implants, bring a rebreather mask with hydrophobic concertina stacks—change cartridges every 4 h; they plug with salt faster than you expect.

Skin  
- The crust dissolves boot rubber in 48 h. Order expedition socks woven with basalt micro-fibre; the silicates discourage burrowing micro-masons.  
- Coat exposed skin with a single-molecular-layer tannic-acid film (spray bottle). It is transparent, lets you sweat, but repels the alkaline drift. Re-apply every 90 min while moving, every 30 min while stationary (but don’t be stationary).

Eyes  
- Orange haze does not harm, but 1.2 dim + metallic glitter = perpetual eyestrain. Bring amber-selective goggles; they boost contrast so you can read the ground temperature by colour (cool = pale ocher, unsafe warm = bruised salmon-pink).

Gut flora  
- Nothing here is edible by Earth-humans. Nothing. Before departure, swallow a cyst-cluster of chemosynthetic companion microbes. They will live in the upper ileum for two weeks, synthesising seleno-proteins that mop up the occasional metal ion you inevitably ingest. Without them, cumulative copper poisoning begins in ~72 h.

3. Arrival – First 120 Seconds

Step off the skiff; DO NOT jump—impact fractures crust and sprays brine into your filters.  
Immediately quarter-turn (45°) away from the sun: the haze is thickest there.  
Start a slow shuffle-rock step (metatarsal never fully leaves contact). This:
- warns the crust you are mobile tissue, not mineral to be concreted  
- keeps micro-masons from adhering; they need stillness > 6 min to lock on  

Deploy poles; plant once every third step for balance but DO NOT pole-vault (you’ll punch through into knee-deep brine and risk “fast-set” entombment).

4. Navigating – How to Read a Living Street

Human Desire Paths  
Wherever thousands have trodden, the insects have polished a calcite ribbon. It resembles bone-white marble shot with rust veins. Width = safe footfall line.  
Edges show pale-blue phospho trim at night: follow it; it is the evacuation route of the midges—ventilation currents keep it 2-3 °C cooler.

Spiracle Etiquette  
Circular or slit-like apertures, rim temperature 38 °C.  
Safe to enter only if rim shows diffuse green biolum (reflected midge wing). If the rim is wet and steaming, the colony is “exhaling” – step inside and the corridor will narrow within minutes.  
If you must pass through, keep moving—count 30 breaths, then exit opposite side even if it is not your destination. Thirty breaths ≈ 4 min ≈ your safety-window before autonomic constriction begins.

Hermit-Crab Curfews  
They stir 45 min after sunset. While gentle, a leg-pod the diameter of a waste-bin can still collapse a chest. If caught between crabs and wall, flatten yourself into a bay door recess; their eye-stalks over-scan at 1 m height.

5. Trade – How to Pay Without Becoming Part of the Architecture

Unit = gram of smelted metal, not face value.  
At a vent ledge, lay ingot on warm shell; retreat two steps. A pseudopod of insects will flow over the metal, mass it (accuracy ±0.2 %), and retract. Within three minutes a tool of identical mass appears: pipe, blade, ocular lens, even a crude kettle. If nothing appears, you have under-paid; add 10 g and wait again. If a second pseudopod gifts you a spiral nub of calcite, you have OVER-paid; take it—currency change is useless here but the nub forms a temporary “safe-stand” tile you may carry: set it down, stand on it for up to 1 h without paving risk.

6. Time & Distance – Keeping Orientation in a World that Grows While You Watch

Because the city accretes ~3 mm per 4 h, any distant landmark you memorise will be nearer and taller by the time you reach it. Correct with the watch bezel bezel:  
- Normal human walking speed on crust ≈ 4 km h⁻¹  
- Rotate bezel so zero aligns with minute-hand when you fix on a target stack  
- Each 4 min = 250 m real travel; when minute-hand crosses 250 m mark, glance up: if the stack is larger, growth > travel – you’re closing slowly; if smaller, the colony may have respired into a new layout. In that case, triangulate using two colonies, never one.

7. Daily Schedule (24 h 38 min local sidereal)

H 00  Dawn haze thickest, visibility 200 m; safest hour for long crossing – crust coolest, micro-masons torpid.  
H 04  Filter-wing midge ascent begins; wear veil.  
H 08  Sun a dull coin at zenith; heat index peaks—seek shade inside colony only if you can monitor narrowing walls.  
H 12  Brief hot rain – tannic film washes off; re-apply under the vinyl cape.  
H 14  Crust re-solidifies; commerce hours; good time to barter.  
H 18  Hermit crabs wake – stay clear of base-of-stack plazas.  
H 20  Phosphorescence ignites; tourists venture; most disappear – DO NOT join groups > 3 people (louder vibrations attract premature wall closure).  
H 22  Optimal sleep window – Thermopile sheet on your portable basalt-tile raft; sleep sitting, ankles crossed, poles planted so you tilt every 15 min—movement fools masons.  
H 30  Repeat.

8. Emergency Procedures

Accidental Piercing of Crust / Sinking to mid-shin  
1) Freeze – violent yank widens hole.  
2) Plant both poles radially, distribute weight.  
3) Activate CO₂ cartridge in pole handle: slow jet freezes 3 l of brine into a shell; lifts you 2 cm. Wiggle foot out horizontally.  
4) Spray tannic foam into cavity; prevents colony viewing your leg as scaffold to grow on.

Shelter-in-Place While Colony Contracts Around You  
- Find ANY vertical slit emitting ≤35 °C air: it is an exhaust; exhalant muscle is weaker than inhalant—kick repeatedly, it spits you outward.  
- Failing that, stab calcite with glass-bead glove, harvest a fist-sized debris, crush into powder; dust blinds the chemosensors, slows growth 6-12 min, enough to hack exit with multi-tool.

Lost Orientation / Storm White-out  
Lie prone, unclip copper ingots, arrange in a 3-4-5 triangle. Copper’s EM signature is homing beacon for port drones; rescue skiff will follow within 2 h. Do NOT wander.

9. Psychological Hygiene

The constant orange, the metallic taste, the faint smell of insect hemolymph, and the absolute absence of Earth-style life erode sanity faster than thirst.  
- Music-loop in one ear only; leave other open for pressure-valve whistles – they are navigation cues.  
- Hold spoken internal monologue: describe colours you see. It prevents olfactory/temperature hallucinations that precede heat-stroke.  
- When phosphorescent tracery tempts you deeper than 30 m into a corridor, hum four bars aloud; the echo reveals cubic volume loss in real time—if resonance pitch climbs by >one whole tone, you are inside a shrinking chamber, retreat immediately.

10. Exit Check-List – Boarding the Skiff Again

□ Jog in place 2 min to dislodge early-stage masons.  
□ Scan boot soles; if calcite bloom <3 mm diameter scrape off with titanium edge; if >3 mm, abandon footwear – colony has tagged it.  
□ Empty rebreather cartridges into waste-bag; port authority fines for dumping metal-rich dust into off-world atmosphere are astronomical.  
□ Trade any remaining copper for a “shell token” (lightweight) – required proof you balanced mass economy; customs will not let you leave overweight.  
□ Write nothing in organic ink in the logbook; use provided stylus. Paper seeded with spores closed the last two habitats.  
□ Look back once: the city will already have grown a fresh balcony where you last stepped. Wave goodbye. Some say the insects notice; others say they don’t. Both versions feel equally true.

Traveler, you will leave lighter, salt-crusted, smelling faintly of rust and iodine, ears ringing with manufactured music and corridor silence in alternating pulses. You will have built nothing, planted nothing, and yet some small part of the plain will forever be tinted by the copper that once travelled in your pocket. That is the closest you will come to making a lasting mark. Treat it as honour; treat it as warning; above all, treat it as memory—because if you return, the city will already have paved your footprints into another hallway, and it will greet you not as stranger, nor as citizen, but as component.
