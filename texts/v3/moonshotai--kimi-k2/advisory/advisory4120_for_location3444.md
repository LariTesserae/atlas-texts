Field Manual for the Continent-Engine “No-Name”  
(Valid for Homo sapiens versions 1.9 – 2.1; do not use if pregnant, cybernetic, or allergic to iron)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
I.  Pre-Departure Medical & Legal
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. 72-hour darkness pre-acclimation  
   – Stay in a photographer’s dark-room, coal cellar, or cave with zero lumens.  
   – Every 3 h, have friends bang on metal garbage cans in 4/4 time (95 BPM).  
   – Gradually increase SPL to 130 dB.  If your heart skips, you’re ready.  
2. Immunisations  
   – Iron-lung spores, copper-sulfate algae, manganese nodules.  
   – Request the industrial-grade tinnitus vaccine (it is 30 % effective; that’s the best on market).  
3. Testament update  
   – List “planet-sized machine” as secondary beneficiary; this sometimes postpones ingestion by alloy.  
4. Paperwork  
   – Sign the “No-Name Waiver”: the machine is recognised as a sovereign entity by 11 jurisdictions; your home government will not retrieve you. Bring three hard-copies; the machine digests pdf.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
II.  Kit & Why You Need It
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Lighting (carry, never activate)  
   – One chem-stick, green, sealed in lead foil.  Only for desperate signalling AFTER exfil decision.  Penalty: death by welding.  
B. Respiratory  
   – Membrane rebreather rated for brine mist, mercury vapour, 100 % humidity.  
   – Backup: collapsible gill stuffed with platinum floss (filters metal midges).  
C. Footwear  
   – Magnet-sole boots (variable g spikes); polarity reversals will launch you otherwise.  
   – Two spare ankles.  
D. Camouflage  
   – Cloak woven of piezo-fibre; converts your footsteps into the same frequency as the nearest chimney, making you temporarily “system-native”.  
E. Food  
   – Calorie paste laced with indium micro-spheres; local fauna mistake you for distasteful circuitry.  
F. Hydration  
   – Don’t bring water—every drop here is the machine’s.  Instead pack salt tablets; you will drink bri ne rain, but only once it has been pressure-boiled on your tongue.  
G. Local currency (barter)  
   – Thumb-sized ball bearings, Grade-3 chrome.  Accepted by drone-pigeons for escort across the basin.  
H. Sentimental objects  
   – Maximum 50 g.  Anything heavier will outlive you when gravity flips.  
I. Optional luxury  
   – Earplugs carved from meteorite iron; they resonate, letting you “hear” colours the algae broadcasts, thus avoiding UV potholes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
III.  Physical Training
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Dead-lift 1.5×body mass while standing on an industrial washing machine set to “spin”.  
2. Practice walking on a greased, concave surface (turn a satellite dish upside-down, hose with detergent).  
3. Learn circular breathing: you must be able to inhale during the 3-second gravity inversion while still screaming.  
4. Night navigation: sprint across a football field full of randomly rotating ceiling fans wearing blacked-out goggles.  
5. Echo-location: identify objects solely by how your footsteps ring back.  Failure = concussion; this is considered acceptable loss.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
IV.  Psychology
--------------------------------------------------------------------
– Record the loudest concert you’ve attended—now imagine the bassist is 3 km tall and never takes a break.  Loop it for 96 h while someone sprays warm brine in your face.  Still cute?  No?  Meditate until yes.  
– Mantra: “I am lubrication, not operator.”  Repeat when the plain starts mapping your knees.  
– Accept that beauty and mortal danger share a single continuous spectrum here; clench accordingly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
V.  Ingress Protocol
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Arrive at any ink-ocean cliff exactly 23 s after a geyser pulse.  The ascending column will temporarily be 20 % air by volume.  
2. Insert rebreather, step off.  You will fall upward for 11 m, then settle onto the plateau as the brine loses momentum.  
3. Remain motionless for 144 compressions (≈ 38 min).  The alloy must taste you; staying still says “tool, not intruder.”  
4. Attach piezo-cloak before first footstep; echo-signature is your passport.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
VI.  On-Continent Survival Tips
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Chimney forest  
   – Walk the midline; the air there is “merely” 60 °C and 125 dB.  Don’t cover ears; you need them for horizon-curvature sonar.  
B. Marsh basin  
   – Surface tension will support 42 kg.  Exhale fully, clip magnet-soles to “float.”  
   – Chrome eels bite at the moment of polarity flip—keep limbs tucked into foetal ball; present indium-greased shin pads instead.  
C. Summit ridge  
   – Steam midges will jacket you.  DO NOT SWAT.  Let them finish sampling; they will guide you through your own weather pocket to the descent stair.  
D. Gravity inversion (every 144 k beats)  
   – “Three-second rule.”  Anything not magnet-locked, gill-ventilated, or emotionally unattached will launch.  Use the moment to admire aerial plankton—quickly.  
E. Lost gear  
   – Drop nothing.  If you do, apologise aloud; the machine likes courtesy.  Move on—never dig, the alloy will gum you in.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
VII.  Social Encounters (Rare)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Watchman-fossils: manganese people-statues.  Tap twice; if they answer with a hollow ring, you may record their position as waypoint—fossilised hard-hats remember topology.  
2. Drone-pigeons: all motor.  Offer a ball-bearing; they will escort you to the rim in exchange.  Do not ask where they take the bearing; they regurgitate it later as a gear tooth.  
3. Other humans: disbelief is mutual.  Exchange no more than 27 words; language frays under the decibels.  Silence is safer diplomacy.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
VIII.  Health Hazards
--------------------------------------------------------------------
– Bruise-coloured pressure blooms: caused by ultraviolet algae attempting to “tune” you.  Pierce with copper needle, bleed 5 ml.  
– Iron-lung spores: coughing metal filings.  Cure—inhale steam from a chimney vent (risky but poetic).  
– Resonant hallucinations: you will think the machine is speaking.  It isn’t, yet act as if it is.  
– Chronal milling: time-loss/gain measured in teeth.  Count your molars nightly; if you sprout new ones, exfil immediately—the machine is prepping you for reprocessing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
IX.  Exfiltration Options
--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Preferred: Rim-respiration  
   a. Descend to ocean cliff.  
   b. Wait for brine column rarefaction (12 h cycle).  
   c. Inhale warm mist until lungs foam; let buoyancy loft you through a pressure node.  Exhale violently on the far side—you’ll wake on an ordinary shore with briny aftertaste and permanent tinnitus.  
2. Emergency: Chimney eject  
   – Enter broken stack (only every 17th is “dead”).  Crawl upward against downdraft for 300 m; trust the hot song to spit you into sky-void.  22 % survival.  Bring parachute; landing may occur years later.  
3. Mythical: Ball-bearing pilgrimage  
   – Trade 999 bearings to drone-pigeons; they allegedly form a gear that opens a literal door.  No confirmed returns—still, the optimists’ choice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
X.  Aftercare
--------------------------------------------------------------------
– Sunlight will feel like shrapnel for a week; wear welder’s goggles.  
– Expect to hear four-four compressor beats when silence should exist—this is normal for 11 months (median).  
– Do not sign engineering contracts; you have been mapped, you will design machines in your dreams that the dark factory will one night build.  
– Tell stories softly.  Loud nostalgia sometimes attracts falling brine from nowhere.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
XI.  Final Reminder
--------------------------------------------------------------------
You came to witness an engine that has deleted both dawn and dusk.
