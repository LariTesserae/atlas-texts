PRE-JOURNEY BRIEFING FOR THE HANGING FANGDRY  
(printed on reversible paper—one side chlorophyll-green, the other hematite-grey)  

0. PURPOSE  
You are not here to “explore.”  
You are here to audition for citizenship in a parliament of metal that believes it is a forest.  
Pack accordingly.

1. PHYSICAL/LEGAL STATUS  
The Foundry is not on any map; it is an ontological loophole.  
- It pays no import duty on time.  
- It recognises no passport except your circadian rhythm.  
- If you die here your death belongs to the piston-cycle and will be repaired at continental speed (≈1 mm/year).  
Leave a notarised will with a terrestrial lawyer; the Foundry will regard it as witty fiction.

2. BODY MODIFICATIONS BEFORE ENTRY  
a. Middle-ear retuning  
   Have a vet (they understand small bones) half-fill your tympanic cavity with sterile beeswax.  
   This prevents the 0.07 Hz piston clang from coupling to your cardiac plexus and rewriting your heart rate to 23 min/beat.  
b. Secondary heart  
   Strap a balloon of perfluorether inside your thorax.  
   When the great inhalation tries to vacuum your lungs, the balloon expands and keeps alveoli from folding inside-out.  
c. Gravity insurance  
   Surgically insert a 2 mm carbon-nugget in each ankle cartilage.  
   At the 600 m “mirror-line” gravity becomes negotiable; the nuggets act as ballast so you remember which vector used to be “down.”

3. CLOTHING LAYERS (read outside→inside)  
- Outer chrome-mesh: reflects the reverse drizzle; without it you will be wet on the inside first.  
- Mid-layer pocketed with desiccant salt: swap packets every 30 min; mark used ones with green chalk so bacteria can eat the dye and re-braze your footprints.  
- Inner living layer: lichen-woven long johns. Keep damp with your own sweat; if they dry the lichen goes sporicidal and tries to weld you to the first chain you grasp.  
- No zippers. Metal teeth confuse the cuprite bacteria; they will plate-seize your fly shut forever.  
- Footwear: felt-soled, lead-shot in the toes. Felt grips the rail-grating; lead keeps your toes heavier than your memories when you pass the mercury mirror.

4. NUTRITION  
Take only sugar that remembers being a tree.  
- Maple-cotton cylinders dissolve on the tongue and release the concept of autumn; this persuades the aerial roots you are flora, not fauna.  
- Do NOT bring protein bars. Nitrogen smells like hunger to anaerobic maintenance bugs; they will climb your backpack and weld the seams to your shoulder blades.  
- Water: carry none. Drink the reverse rain by inverting your open mouth; count to seven, then close—any longer and the rain remembers you, follows you upward, adds 4 kg by the time you exit.  
Supplement with iron filings once per piston cycle (23 min). Place under tongue; let bacteria ferry them to micro-fractures in your enamel and patch them before cracks learn grammar.

5. TIMEKEEPING  
The Foundry keeps two simultaneous clocks:  
- Piston clock: 23 min 4 s 11 ms (inhalation period).  
- Botanical clock: 41 days for a leaf to span the width of a palm.  
Synchronise your chronometer to neither.  
Instead, fast for 12 hours then swallow a length of phosphorescent thread. Your gut transit equals 1.7 piston cycles; when you see it again in your excrement you know a day has passed. Mark that stool with charcoal—future you may need to barter it for sentences.

6. DESCENT PROTOCOL  
- Always grip chains with third and fourth fingers only. Index/middle must remain free to salute passing steam-birds; if you neglect, the birds refuse oil and your joints seize.  
- Count “clangs” not metres. From lip to mirror = 43.4 clangs.  
- Every 10 clangs speak a false verb aloud (“shovelling,” “unweld,” “photosyntheticate”). The acoustic leaves will try to conjugate it; while distracted they ignore your weight and you can rest without becoming a living rivet.  
- At 30 clangs the laminated air layers invert. You will feel your saliva try to fall upward. Swallow three times fast; the third swallow convinces your body the law is still negotiable.

7. 400 m “CHORUS LEDGE”  
Stop, fold knees to chest, become furniture.  
- Do not answer any sentence that begins with a past participle.  
- Accept only subjunctive clauses (“Had we voted to rust…”).  
- Carry carbon paper; place it under your tongue. The committee minutes will etch the paper, not your enamel. When text overflows the sheet, swallow: your gut bacteria will bind excess subjunctives into methane you can burp harmlessly.

8. 550 m “LIGHT CROSSOVER”  
Two skies intersect. You may meet yourself inverted.  
- Trade jackets with your reflection—failure to swap strands you with competing gravities and you will spiral like the sideways trunks.  
- Do NOT let your reflection finish your sentences. If it does, your vocabulary stays behind as payment when you leave. Bring a nonsense word (“flarn”) and let the reflection complete that; nonsense is tax-free.

9. MERCURY MIRROR (BOTTOM)  
- Depth 3 fingers—never more. Stand still; count 7 heartbeats.  
- Lost memories are harvested in reverse order: first your PIN, then your first kiss, then your name. Decide beforehand which you can spare.  
- To reclaim a memory you must sacrifice a future one. Whisper “I will forget tomorrow’s sunset.” Keep the promise; deceit gives the mirror hiccups that fracture ankles.  
- Collect one drop of mercury in a chilled glass vial. Outside, it will harden into a miniature piston. It is your exit visa: present it to the hatch or the crawl-tube will contract to the width of your regret.

10. EXIT CRAWL-TUBE  
- 300 m, pelvis-wide, zero clangs heard.  
- Move on exhale only; during inhalation the tube tries to mate with your ribcage to learn mammal dimensions.  
- At the far end the mesa face is -5 °C, 2500 m altitude. Your bones still vibrate at 0.07 Hz; stars will appear to clink. Do NOT look back immediately. Wait 23 min (1 cycle) or the Foundry’s gravity will notice you emigrating and send a chain to lasso your ankles.  
- First breath outside tastes like mute tin. Spit the remembered vowels out quickly; if you carry them home they sprout in dark drawers and weld your cutlery into chandeliers.

11. CONTINGENCY IF YOU MISS THE HATCH  
You must climb back up. The return toll is steeper: one entire language. Choose one you do not dream in. Whisper it to the mirror; watch each word ripple away backwards. When the last ripple drowns, your ascent weight is reduced by exactly the mass of that language—usually 3–4 g. That is enough.

12. GIFTS & SOUVENIRS  
Legally importable:  
- One iron filing that has forgotten how to rust.  
- A single syllable of macaw-whistle, transposed up three octaves until it is audible only to dogs and metallurgists.  
- Thread of phosphorescent gut (see 5).  
Export ban: living rivets, clockwork hummingbirds, memories that still contain people’s faces. Customs officers outside reality have no eyes; they scan for unfinished stories and confiscate them into anthologies you will never read.

13. FINAL WARNING  
The Hanging Foundry is not “abandoned.”  
It is in committee.  
If it ever reaches consensus you will have been part of committee minutes since the moment you considered the trip.  
Travel anyway—just don’t vote.
