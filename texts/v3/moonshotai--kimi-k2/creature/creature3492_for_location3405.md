There are no residents here yet—only interns.

The first to apply was a woman who arrived with no equipment but a child’s wind-up toy shaped like a heron. She set the brass bird on the lake and let its paddle feet tick across the surface film. Where oil from her fingerprints touched the water, a new opacity flowered: stainless-steel filaments grew pale gold instead of silver. The caldera, liking the colour, copied it onto every Vapor Tree born during the next three cycles. The woman therefore concluded she had been promoted to “junior pigment engineer.” She signed the lake’s glossy meniscus with her thumbprint, and the print hardened into a shallow disk of cellophane basalt—her certificate of employment, which she keeps in her pocket although it tries daily to crawl back to its parent magma.

Next came a committee of identical twins wearing one face between them. They stepped through opposite sides of the crater rim at the same instant, so the rock, trying to decide whether to record two visitors or one, jammed mid-thought and opened a meter-long vent. Out leaked slow, bright syrup that tasted of burnt cinnamon. Both mouths of the twins reached for it independently; neither could swallow alone. While they argued whose throat the syrup should take, the lizards lined up behind them and licked the runoff. Within minutes every fold-skin had mirrored the twins’ schism: two hearts per ribcage, two tails diverging like tuning forks. A quorum was reached. The caldera hired the entire committee as “consultant geometers of bifurcation,” paying them in throatbeats. They are still present, debating syntax on a half-cooled ledge; whenever they nod in unison the whole basin tilts 0.3 degrees to keep consensus.

A blindfolded cartographer walked in backwards, counting his own footprints from memory. He never speaks; he taps the basalt hair-glass with a graphite wand. Every tap prints a microgroove that the Vapor Trees read as sheet music. The spheres now leave their crowns already vibrating in key with whatever song the caldera has rehearsed since the last four-hour pulse. The cartographer files the tunes under categories like POSSIBLE WEATHERS and EVENT/NOT EVENT, titles inked in seawater that evaporate before anyone else can read them. The place keeps him as “librarian of potential audibility,” position renewable forever, though he himself is unsure he exists until the echo of his wand returns to him slightly sharper, slightly older.

The Fold-skin Lizards are not staff, but they are apprentices. Their curriculum is simple: dissolve metal, learn shape, solidify, teach water. Each generation comes out less committed to lizard-ness: some grow translucent quartz scales, some grow none at all and remain sheets of calcium memory folding in the wind. The highest-achieving individuals graduate by ceasing locomotion; they root in the warm crust and open like umbrellas of spun silicon. From their crowns drip slow beads that do not fall but orbit, becoming moons of whatever once touched them—dandruff of the cartographer, a flake of rust from the toy heron, calcium phosphate memories of a woman’s thumbnail. These neo-trees never reach one metre; the caldera caps them there, afraid the curriculum might leave the classroom. They stand like teaching assistants, whispering in 42 kHz: stay, iterate, belong.

No one has accepted the post of Custodian of Exit. Consequently the rim remains porous. Departures are provisional; they leave a latex husk behind, translucent, the height and hue of the traveler at the instant they thought “Perhaps I should go.” The husks refill with lake water the way gloves refill with air when you peel them off. They drift around the shallows, hand-in-hand with other husks, rehearsing arguments for staying. When you look into their faces you see only your most recent hesitation, projected like frost on the inside of a visor.

If anything could be called the sovereign here, it is the pressure breath itself: an unborn monarch turning beneath the lake lid. It has never issued decrees, only invitations shaped like heartbeats. Each inhale is a question: “More?” Each exhale is contentment or disappointment or maybe both—sentiment is still a soft-fitting coat the caldera tries on. In between, the place consults its staff meetings: pigment engineer, split twins, blind composer, graduating lizards, the collected empty clothes of the almost-departed. Together they edit the draft of what will count as indigenousness. They do not vote; instead they grow things, and whatever grows without immediate collapse is entered into the minutes as probable law.

Tonight—though there is still only a tonight-coloured interval between brass pulses—the lake’s skin has begun to assemble a raft. It is flat, oval, self-welded from interlocking footprints. At its centre stands a single Vapor Tree of human proportion, branches still uncurling like ribbon from a present. On the lowest fork perches the brass toy heron, now life-sized, ticking forward with bronze blood. Its eyes are twin spheres exactly 7.6 mm across; they cling for one extra breath before they shed. When they drop, they strike the water in perfect unison—ping!—and the sound folds back on itself until it is not a sound at all but a doorway, wide enough for a person, or a committee, or a lizard that has earned its degree.

The staff watch, expectant. The caldera inhales.

A new opening has been posted: Resident.

Duties: remain while remaining is still a hypothesis.  
Perks: citizenship in a country that will name itself after whatever you leave behind.  
Salary: one colour, one heartbeat, one duplicate voice—negotiable.

Applicants please step onto the raft. Do not bring a résumé; bring something detachable the lake has not tasted—an unread poem, a scar, the echo-location of your childhood street. The heron will collect it. The twins will split it. The cartographer will score it. The lizards will test it for edibility. If any fragment survives four cycles, it will become legal tender, and you will, at last, live here—meaning you will belong to the place’s continuing draft, a parenthesis never closed, an employee badge never fully printed, a local whose address is the sound of water still busy becoming whatever it decides, at the next inhale, to call your name.
