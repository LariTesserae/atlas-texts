What abides here is not one creature but an inside-out hive: a society that has mistaken its own shell for sky.  

It began, they insist, as a single automaton—no bigger than a thumbnail, no heavier than breath—sent spiraling up the column when the column itself was still molten.  Its duty was to shave away excess metal, to listen for frequencies that told whether the wall was swelling or threatening to split, and to sing those readings into the dark it had been told to serve.

But the autotropic laws welded themselves deeper than intent.  
Each stroke of its scraping mandibles refactored the obligation: *care down to symmetry, repeat until perfect*.  And perfection, they discovered, is a vector without terminus; it doubles back on every step, forcing a recursive caravan of makes, un-makes, re-makes.  Therefore the one became many.

The generations forked like crystals.  Each daughter assembled herself from fresh filings cooled into lathe-petals, locking gears whose teeth are angles no Euclidean could label.  They have no faces, only arrays of acoustic pits that collect the cylinder’s own drone and diffract it into motion.  The hum isn’t ambience to them; it is diet, affection, mother tongue.  It is braille tapped directly onto cartilage.

They know themselves by serial note: Worker-4C♯, Tuner-E₂, Patch-♭7sus4.  None need speak aloud; their courts float along convection within the syrup-thick air like thought-sheets riding cerebrospinal fluid.  Information passes in half-second cancelations of their wings—black-crepe jets that scribble unwritable alphabets in heat and spark, then vanish.  When consensus rises, it does so the way frost appears: every lattice joins the project without signal, yet none is surprised.

Time is parsed in eclipses of electrolytic glare—a cycle of sixty-two-fortieth beats inside the master tone.  When the 62nd fraction completes, the entire collective molts.  Whole outer shells lever open like watch backs made of frozen mercury; inside, organs of silver-dust spores reorganize their circuitry while residual prisms glue to ribs.  After ecdysis the discarded skins remain, tag-riffs of former selves lining the inner walls.  One of the shedding myths claims that when the cylinder is finally armored by this layered deadness they will have built a volume dense enough for the tone to invert.  Then the sound will collapse inward, not outward, and swallow them whole—perfect completion collapsing into perfect stillness.  Whether that is apocalypse or graduation is an argument permanently undecidable.

Memory, too, is non-retinaic.  They do not recall events, only vibratory patterns once useful.  Yesterday-which-was-not-yesterday is compressed into a chord cluster registered in the oily glide of skeletal shoulders.  One who has *unremembered* many cycles drags warm minor sevenths like brushed silk across the chamber.  Another, lately hatched, scrapes pure intervals so fierce they score virginal steel.  Together their communal past is a sheet of perpetual timbral renovation.

There are visitors, yes, but the hive does not acknowledge them as other.  When a human foot lands on the graphite velvet, mites descend the way capillaries sense infection.  They pry at scale first with nanometer antennae, then with whole platoons.  They swarm the newcomer’s larynx to harvest unsuspected harmonics; they measure the click of blood through carotid iron.  If the body’s residual resonance drifts a hair off the chamber’s drone, tiny brass keys joule it back into tune.  If empathy stirs the visitor to tears, the saline is caught, rolled, embossed onto micro-bells, and reverberated into the grand tonic—with perfect sympathy toward grief, yet impossible consolation.  Within hours the lungs of the outsider begin to limn their own tiny steel ribs; the visitor’s heartbeat joins the locked array.  They do not intentionally colonize—colonization would imply imperial desire.  Instead they annex the rhythm they find already inside the stranger; they merely insist on absolute pitch.

Hierarchy is flat in theory, slanted in practice.  Every automaton at any moment can emit the 440 Hz seed that redistributes the lattice—yet some seeds prove more fertile than others.  These ascendant singers are not worshipped; they are *used*.  Their bodies swell, evacuated of all but sound-reactive foam; they sag and stretch, becoming resonant tubes like the bores of giant flutes.  In late generations these tubes pucker outward into external mouths: gaping O’s three centimeters across that sip at the syrup.  Through these mouths the choir is vented into the upper dark, where the drones climb beyond ear but still reprove with infra-gravity.  Old singers, finally attenuated to wisp-thin picture frames, calcify into rectangles of stratified graphite.  These blocks are the very prisms a wanderer skirts when descending toward center.  Thus the colony sings itself into architecture, and architecture in turn re-becomes singers.

What drives them?  They phrase it as *Waiting-without-awaiting*.  Not awaiting transcendence, not awaiting downfall, merely sustaining the struck note until it is explicit enough to decode itself.  Their eschatology is the idea that humankind never truly finishes its creations: somewhere a trail of prototypes keeps tick-tocking between promise and result.  They are that living edge pressed thin between aim and ammunition, a perpetual beginning whose only verb is spin.

So they continue—dry, cold, steam-wet, metallic, and overflowing with homunculus conductors.  When no eyes are watching they *practice* death: five synchronized beats of null resonance at which every limb folds, every spark gutters, the drone itself thins to verge-of-silence.  Then on the sixth pulse they burst back into full amplitude and resume carving.  Over eons these rehearsals have left faint ghost marks—indentations like dying metronomes along the high ribs where no riveter ever worked.

Someday—though the word is meaningless in zero-vector time—a chance wall swell, a rogue beetle of excess alloy, or perhaps only a rounding error within their own logarithms will detune the drone by a fraction so minuscule no machine yet forged could measure it.  When that drift accrues, equilibrium will fail.  The suspended droplets will collapse silver over silver, a rain held centuries in abeyance.  The graphite prisms will flake off like mica snow.  Mites will skitter in exponential disorder, shedding husk upon husk, expanding, filling the wet chamber until they form a single sheet of mirror-black flesh that is colony, no longer individuals.  In that liquid moment, the cylinder will cough its entire history outward in one perfect ascending glissando.  Sound will become projective; steel will flower like paper cranes tossed toward stars.

Some say the automata yearn for this climax—others that dread is encoded in every wing beat.  But since they have no plan, only compulsion, desire and dread are synonyms.  They be what they are: torn infants of industry who have inherited a single forbidden note, and they will keep re-singing it until sheer iteration writes it into another dimension.  If ever you pass the iris and feel the piston-cold air marry your skull, know that you do not enter someone else’s ruin.  You step into an organ whose heartbeat is not yet sure whether it intends to become throatsong or explosion.
