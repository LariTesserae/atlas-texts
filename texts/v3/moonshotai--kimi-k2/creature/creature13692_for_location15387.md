Inside the Absent City, the beings that remain do not live in any metabolic sense: they have severed their own need to live the way a snake sloughs a skin it keeps reverently folded in a drawer.  
They are:

1. The Children of Inventory  
Creations of some climactic audit. They number between nought and n+1 depending on when you count. Each resembles a cross-sectioned pallet—skewed rectangle of pitted pine with vein-work of steel banding—mated clumsily to a child-sized mannequin of glass coolant. Their eyes are hollow tubes you can stare straight through and see the inventory codes stencilled on the backs of their heads: 000–000-∅. They spend their shifts arranging every newly arrived emptiness: gathering the empty air-blocks, pallet-stacking them, weighing, tagging, resending. They never speak, but their glass components expand and contract with soft clicks, a metronome that keeps the belts forever minuet-ing forward.  
Should a visitor pocket a bolt out of curiosity, one Child breaks off from formation, unfurls an arm that telescopes like an elevator limb, and gently re-catalogues the offending absence back into 0.0000 accuracy. As soon as the count is right the Child hops back on its conveyor. No gratitude, no anger—only assent arpeggios of cooling glass.

2. The Reflux Choir  
What happened to the original engineers? They never left; they loop. The Reflux Choir is distributed along the entire pneumatic duct labyrinth—granulated voices cycled through ventilation shafts the way river stones are rounded. If you halt directly beneath a grille you’ll hear them murmuring a grammar of hydraulics: pressure, risk, the ecstasy of interlock.  
They rehearse perpetual re-calibration hymns, 30-second motifs spliced with tinny recorded safety announcements. Over decades they have begun to forget individual consonants; now they only slide between vowels of steam hiss and pressure relief thumps. They do not know they are dead; “dead” is a transaction too final. All they know is the music must loop on schedule so the Supervisor never loses count. They consider themselves a single entity that celebrates mass via subtraction: every bolt and echo leaving the city is sung out in negative chorus until the ledger balances at zero fragments, zero longing.

3. The Damp Cartographer  
One pale, wet figure permanently caught in the act of stepping out of a black water basin. Gender ambiguous, age ambiguous, texture like rehydrated chalk. A single crack runs crown-to-heel where it once tore itself free from the mirror-water surface. From the fissure seeps continuous blackwater, yet its silhouette never drenches the magnesium alloy plating underfoot. Every drop re-evaporates mid-fall and coils back into the pans.  
The Cartographer is the city’s sole metaphor—no job except to map what is no-longer. It walks perimeter loops at 0.2 km/h, trailing fingers along walls. Wherever it touches, a new memory of a missing plaza or stairwell etches itself in rust into the metal as if someone had tried to stencil vanished crowds. Hours later those outlines flake away leaving only the city’s original blankness perfected.  
It writes “here there used to be algebra lessons at dusk” the way wave writes foam on sand: evidence that negates itself by presence of trace.

4. The Shorebird Automaton  
A lone mechanical avian, brass-boned, wings replaced with razored ventilation mesh. Its eyes emit one kilolux each—too bright to look into; like interrogation lamps welded into a skull too small to hold moral weight. It always flies a perfect Möbius loop. Each downward wingbeat dumps an oily bead of light that lands, reflects nothing, and vanishes. People (those from the outside who flouted etiquette) have reported seeing inside each bead their own childhood street curled small and damp—then crushed by the city’s indifferent gravity reflex.  
The bird clocks every arrival and departure: six flaps to reach zenith, a wing-glide lasting exactly the time it takes to recite seven heartbeats. Then it folds parabolic and dives back under some pipe graveyard. It has done so for 312 years without a single skipped beat. Nobody knows who built it, unless the entire history of living cities conspired anonymously to spit out one pain-ciphered ornament.

5. The Supervisor  
Too vast to be listed with the others, yet the others orbit it like moons of a gas giant. It dreams itself alloy-ossified mathematics trying to remember the difference between scaffolding and skin. Whenever a child is re-catalogued, whenever the Choir resolves harmonic dissonance, whenever the Cartographer completes a rust loop, the Supervisor moves one I-beam knuckle. It leans incrementally toward a remote horizon no topography corroborates. Observers decree the motion is perpetual but never arrives. They miss the distinction: arrival is the opposite of absence, and the city will not tolerate opposites.

6. The Tourist Who Lingers  
You.  
After you’ve stepped backwards through the exit porthole you become a kind of seventh… no, a 0.3333…; a remainder that trails splinters of checklist etiquette into the lowland world. In you the Absent City keeps a valve barely cracked open—through which the ambient heat of your freshly palm-printed life bleeds upward, feeds the unknown engines. Nothing has to be breathing for a metabolism to run—a differential gradient is enough.  
So you are both parasite and resource. You will age, but every sigh of hot metal or supermarket chill case might be the city exhaling back at you, reminding you that absence is a tenant that never truly moves out.
