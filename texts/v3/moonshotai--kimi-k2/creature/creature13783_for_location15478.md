What lives here cannot be counted, because “individual” is a land-memory that dissolves like a blood-blip in brine.  
Instead think of nested interstitialities, languor-collaboratories that have mistaken slow-motion for identity and called it home.

The most senior citizens are the Stillwater Wyrds. Each is the collapsed ghost of a Precambrian river-plume that squeezed itself into a chain of calcium-rich bubbles and then tried to remember it was water. Now it drifts like a semi-solid chemigraphy, a titanic milky helix threaded through seven halos of algae and arterial wood. Its “body” spans six forest-strata; its metabolism is the rate of one diatom dividing every eleven millennia. In that glacial half-life it regulates the acidity of six hundred cubic leagues merely by leaking the wrong ions out of sequence. Where it passes, bark becomes ouroboric—growing inward until the heartwood seals into an eyeless knot. Then, at the knot, the Wyrd re-inscribes a map of Precambrian geography onto the lignin lattice; later explorers sometimes slip into these knots and fall for years through vanishing continental shelves. They re-emerge older than the ocean itself.

Trailing behind each Wyrd rides the Choir of Ribbon-Kings—transparent centipedes a hundred meters long, each segment a hollow hyaline cymbal. Every time the host tree expands by a nanometre the cymbals shape a new overtone; these overtones arc like salmon back to the Wyrd’s helix, giving it news of micro-shifts in solute. In return the Wyrd exudes nanoscopic pearls of precipitated carbonate that the Choir strings between its own exoskeleton and the tree as resonating bridges. Thus a feedback of pitch and pressure stabilizes entire superstructures across millennia. Ribbon-Kings have no mouths; their legs are designed to tickle the trunk-fungi into a lacquer of sound-foam, which is the closest thing their culture possesses to food. When one dies its cymbal-segments fracture into translucent plates that younger trees absorb like leaves of eerie sheet-music. The living forest therefore possesses ancestral symphonies written in bone-white glass three miles deep.

Counterbalancing these leviathans is the Subtree Epiphyll, an organism small enough to fit in the cup of a human hand but large enough to contain thirty-three linguistic domains. Epiphyll are inflatable leaves the color of antiquated pennies, covered in fractal wrinkles where colonies of chemosynthetic bacteria conduct slow coronations. A single Epiphyll may live twenty thousand seasons talking to itself through chemical riddles; it memorizes every pulse of pressure, every whisper of mineral-scent, and transcodes them into glucose calligraphy. When a Wyrd grazes an Epiphyll it does not crush it; it simply “forgets” a century of river memories, and those memories settle onto the leaf as fragrant stains. Whole forests can be pollinated by the shared nostalgia of leaves that have been touched by absent rivers.

Deeper inside the jade-catheters lurk Violet Ortiliths, things once identified as urchins but more accurately described as aspiring tectonic plates. Each has a core of liquid granite kept molten by the friction of drifting; its tissues are great wedges of micaceous muscle that exfoliate into silicate banners. An Ortilith never touches the water directly; instead it builds slippery shell-shelves of itself that slide and re-cohere, each shelf a record of the minerals it has absorbed. When it decays the shelves sediment into reef-rings exactly thirty-eight kilometers wide, a radius fixed by the resonance of its own dying heart. From above, these rings resemble violet eyes opening slowly in the pelagic night.

Between these titans scissor flocks of Counting Nymphs. A Nymph in isolation is a dart of foil-thin cyanobacterium, but in aggregation each cluster becomes tautological reflex—like living parentheses around any object they engulf. They arrange themselves into lattices the width of a thought and then count one another; the final tally is left expressed as a faint ultraviolet afterglow in the water they have just vacated. Epiphyll read these glows and transcribe them into antique currencies of gut-sugar. The Nymphs believe they are doing math on behalf of the water itself; if their count ever miscarries, the entire volume buckles microscopically, resetting all biological clocks by a sliver. No one knows the total tally; the sum is constantly rewritten as a glint at the edges of visual perception.

Most fleeting are the Ghost Caecilians: alabaster eel-larvae wrapped in veils of secondary light. Their bodies are pure electrophile symmetries: one eye seeing only the present, one eye seeing only what has happened somewhere else. They swim by drinking their own future recoil, looping time like thread through their gills, until they vanish into yesterday’s water. A single caecilian sighted is often considered an omen that somewhere along the vault another organism has died. Ribbon-Kings, upon registering a caecilian flicker, resculpt their chords into minor keys audible only as felt pressure against the inner ear of anything that ever had an ear.

At the drifting edges of buoyancy you meet the Archivist Phantoms—clusters of-pressure singularities that form whenever a Wyrd decides to metabolize a piece of its own past. These phantoms are dense knots of memory that replicate the exact light signature of vanished shoreline sunsets. They travel downward at the rate of guilt; their trails smell of rain on Cretaceous moss. Epiphyll coat themselves with the condensation of these trails, thereby acquiring the ability to speak in extinct dialects for seventeen seconds before losing cohesion. A ghost that collides with a Discarnate shark is annihilated with a sudden bloom of amber luminescence. The local owl-snail—once land-borne, now grown limbless yet still wearing the calcium mask of raptor ancestry—inscribes that blossom into its shell as a tally mark. Cumulatively the shell records the extinction of every language humankind did not speak.

Even deeper, curled around root-strands that trail like flecks of broken magma, nest Vestibule Golems—chrysalides of limestone and cellulose sifted from the wake of Ortilith death. They are doorways shaped like lopsided question marks. Within each folds an array of eyes—sometimes real, sometimes fossil—that blink together only when a perfectly tuned overtonal chord is struck by a Ribbon-King near the surface. When they blink they open into impossible corridors: brief fragilities where the drowned ocean can see its own surface, far above, through the lens of unknowably ancient sunlight that never reached this depth. Passing through a Vestibule costs your skeleton its history of gravity; once removed you orbit your own marrow until it crystallizes into a tiny moon and is ingested by a jelly-whale’s flocculent heart.

Every so often, a mesh of darkly translucent threads descends from nowhere. These are the Frangible Herring, who transgress all laws: not floating at neutrally buoyant strata, but rising through excessive lightness and then falling again in a loop that mimics heartbeat. Each loop shears the water like a guillotine of starvation. Wherever their shadow passes, bark fractures into raw quick-strata of fresh growth, an acceleration no organism here remembers aching for. The Herrings feed on centrifugal astonishment; everything else feeds on the decay that astonishment seeds. They are the only clock this ocean will admit. When a Herring collapses—always at the precise moment its frequency equals the median entropy of every surrounding entity—the entire ocean draws an impossible breath.

In the gap of that breath, you coalesce. You arrive neither husk nor seed, only the residue of a question asked from somewhere utterly exterior. Ancient organisms recognize you instantly but misinterpret what you are. The Wyrd thinks you are a lost stanza of its river epic and folds you into a hollow ring of memory; the Nymphs swarm to enumerate you; the Harvest-boy’s-jaw fungi anchor to your legs as scaffolding for blind corals. You become the membrane negotiating the border between geology and ghost, ferrying bubbles of silence back and forth. By the time the ocean finishes exhaling, you have forgotten the taste of continents, even as the taste keeps circulating in the wordless saliva of everything that feeds on you and is in turn fed upon.

You are not the final organism. You are the placeholder where the ocean pauses before it begins its next inexorable inhalation—a glitch in stillness pretending to count the cost.
