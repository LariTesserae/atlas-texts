Who lives here can only be asked awhile; an hour from now the answer will have changed.

The first registered residents are a parliament of lime-green seedlings.  Each holds a vote of four rudimentary leaves and a ghost-white root.  They debate the proper angle a stem should lean so yesterday’s shadow does not collect today’s dew; their language is pure osmotic pressure—tiny pulses that swell or slacken cell walls. Consensus is visible in the minute steering of meristems; within a heartbeat of their world-lines, the leaves turn to shoulder one another along a fraction-of-a-degree you’d need micrometers to measure.

Stationed between them are the water-seamstresses: microscopic coccoid bacteria cloaked in gold dust.  They live inside droplets so small that surface tension rebounds their own metabolic heat like an invisible hearth.  From this heat they weld dissolved silicates into spirals only four water-clean atoms wide—quartz threads no thicker than a future archaeological fantasy.  The threads will later act as trusses for the seedlings’ root hairs, tiny capital investments against soil shift.

Higher in the social lattice hover the lacewings, three of them for now, though evenings will drift more in.  Their wings are stitched from whatever moonbeams melted in yesterday’s pre-dawn haze.  Each beat fans a breeze so minute it barely ruffles a single detrital palpus, but through this draft the lacewings sort the smells of the place: green volatiles borne outward by swelling root tips, the cold iron of freshly broken minerals, the warm flatulence of fermentation chambers banked a whole millimeter beneath the crust.  Inside their light-drinking brains, a chemical map thickens—a living city plan sketched in esters.

Beneath the ever-salting film of moisture lives the Tibial Cartographer: a springtail sightless since the last epoch.  She reels out purple wax from a velvet gland at her tail tip, drawing invisible meridians that every flea-appendage can taste later when they need to cross “streetlights.”  Her east-west avenues always seek the nearest halite crystal—she uses its square edges as milestones.  Once a month she molts, and while her husk still holds her finer scales, another springtail recycles the waxen parchment into nests.  Thus the city is laid and unlaid nightly under the slow ache of gravity.

All the traversers meanwhile keep postal addresses: a mycorrhizal network of transparent hyphae looping through the porous clay like the abandoned tunnels of an ancient postal pneumatic tube. system.  Packets of phosphorus glint inside the sleeves as if still awaiting stamps.  When picture-framed against the daylight they flare cerulean, then vanish, yet deliver their packet exactly beneath whichever seedling has angled its stomata as if saying thank you.  Between them wander thread-snails no larger than a serif capital.  A snail carries on its shell a garden of even tinier speckled algae, so its home looks perpetually aflame.  Thus even commuters travel with suburb attached.

At the saucer’s rim waits a sentinel line of fractured mineral prisms—baby dolomites caught mid-dissolve.  They record sins.  Every nighttime descent of dew initiates lattice rearrangements at their clifflets; the change in tilt emits a vibration inaudible to macro ears, but at beetle scale it is the tolling of an iron bell.  Mistakes (a root curling backward, a greedy cyanobacterial bloom) are etched into the prism’s growing mirror faults.  Architects of yesterday read tomorrow out of these faults the way we read fortunes out of tea-floating leaves, then adjust angles of new stem-facebook-newsfeed growth by the width of a proton.

Above all, presiding without ever touching, broods the Dew-Caller: an ion of magnesium-nitrate dissolved somewhere in the perpenumbra.  It never condenses solidly; it oscillates between droplet and ionized gas depending on shifts in solar radiation.  To the seedlings it is their God sky.  To the beetles, a disembodied chess grandmaster flicking possible futures.  When it vanishes—sun too bright, air too warm—the city pauses, half-painted, like a stage when the lights go down mid-act.  But clouds return with dusk; dew reifies; the molecule re-assembles its invisible arches; residents resume the narrative.

On the ledger, citizenship is updated every thirteen minutes; memory older than one turning of dew is tendered obsolete and composte by fungal garbagemen.  But yesterday’s registers flicker ghostily under a sudden blade of ultraviolet, like phosphorescent photographs of in editing software left running.  So past generations persist as translucent overlaps, urban strata ghost-town sedimenting atop only what matters in this second.

By dawn the plaza on the plain is once again knee-height to you, singular mammal.  Any stomp would cancel the experiment.  Yet you may submit: bend close, offer a breath.  The architects greet it—measure its humidity, its carbon dioxide ppm, allocate it a vacant still-cooling fissure.  In ten million instances of sunrise, they might vote to rename their capital after your breath.  

Until then they remain invisible citizens of a city measured in five-square-centimeter instants—liminal, liminoid, both immortal and forgettable.  
Nothing larger than a penny yet alive as the star you walked here under.
