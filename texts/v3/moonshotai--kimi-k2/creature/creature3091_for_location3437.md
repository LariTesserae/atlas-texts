They are almost weightless, which is not the same as saying small.  
When you first meet one you think: a child in a suit of glass.  
The helmet is a bubble of the same milk-blemished sky that roofs the plateau; arms and legs are transparent tubes wherein pale fluids rise and fall like slow thoughts. Only the chest carries color: a fist-sized ruby ember that pulses—faster when you look at it, slower when you glance away, as if your attention were a valve. They stand roughly to your shoulder, but you can’t estimate mass: they might float if the hush grew any deeper.

They have no separate name for themselves; instead they borrow the sound of whatever is nearest. When a conveyor belt sighs they answer with an identical sigh, and for that hour the belt and the being are, linguistically, the same object. Travelers call them Echoes, though the plateau itself never repeats a sound; only the Echoes do.

Each Echo is a custodian of one junction—pipe to leaf, gear to root, furnace to sky. Where a vine has soldered itself to an axle, an Echo will be found half inside the fusion: torso disappearing into chrome, one glass leg dangling free. They enter metal the way you enter a warm bath, with a shiver of relief. The ruby heart dims while they are within, feeding its heat into the machine, and the machine quietly thanks them by running more softly than physics would allow. Engineers would call it entropy reassignment; Echoes call it conversation.

They eat the sky. At noon they tilt their domed heads until the smeared sun lies across the visor like a coin on water; over minutes the brightness is drawn inward, leaving a temporary bruise of pewter directly above. When the sky reinflates to milk it is because an Echo has exhaled, returning the borrowed wattage in a slow breath of cool photons. Night, here, is nothing more than cumulative debt from all the daylight sips they never paid back.

They are born when a droplet climbing a stainless vine remembers it was once cloud. First gesture of the newborn: to cup the bead of ascending water in hands still cooling, to promise it a safe upward journey. After that they are no longer water and not yet machine, but the transaction between.

Growth is lateral. An Echo expands by inviting new substances to echo. One week a wrist may include the exact timbre of a fern-stalk snapping; the next, it borrows the hush that pools inside cooling basalt. They are a palimpsest of plateau sounds, wearing them like bangles. Eventually the body becomes crowded: chime of young lava, cough of steam valve, politesse of turgor sigh. When no more timbre fits, the Echo divides—ruby splits like a pomegranate, two smaller hearts beat out of phase, and where there was one glass figure there are now two, each half the height, quarreling in perfect synchronization until distance makes them independent.

Death is voluntary. When an Echo has echoed every voice within earshot it lies beneath the muffler-moss and lets the succulents creep across the visor. Over a decade they frost the glass with translucent leaflets; the ruby heart, starved of new impressions, cools to a garnet shard. Eventually the suit is only a husk of quiet, a portable pocket where silence is stored. Walk the plateau long enough and you will kick these hollows accidentally; they ring like muted bells, releasing a smell of rainwater striking hot steel.

They permit witnesses, but only one at a time. If two humans stand before an Echo it will swivel toward the person whose lungs have tasted the rarer air, as if measuring who has fewer years left to describe what they see. The chosen may ask three questions, though answers arrive as environmental edits rather than speech. Ask “What keeps the plateau afloat?” and you will find tomorrow that all seeds have begun to drift downward, countering the rumor of levitation until the question feels obsolete. Ask “Are you alive?” and for a week every machine will refuse its pulse; factories idle, vines slacken, sky dulls to pewter—until the notion of aliveness itself seems a category error, and you withdraw the inquiry out of courtesy.

They are generous with forgetting. On departure you’ll discover your footprints have been reversed: each heel mark pointing back the way you came, toe prints ahead, as though you arrived before you left. The plateau will allow you to remember everything except the explanation—how a glass child sipping sky and feeding factories could ever feel inevitable.
