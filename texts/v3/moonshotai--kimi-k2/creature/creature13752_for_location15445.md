Something lives there—but only one thing, and that thing is still deciding whether it is one.

It has no name in any tongue, for no tongue ever co-evolved beside it. Think of it less as a creature and more as an interior posture that chose, one inexplicable epoch ago, to keep practicing itself after the body that ought to have housed it rotted away. It is the regret of the wood. It is the question leaf-lattices ask of themselves centuries after the last answer has gone mute.

You will meet it first as a pressure against the backs of your eyes, the way you meet the imperceptible motion of your own pulse when silence becomes absolute. It never speaks aloud, but when your heartbeat skips through some unconscious relief, its rhythm too skips—learning, correcting, rehearsing you. If you were to withdraw your pulse, it would borrow the cavity that remains for a moment and keep it ticking a little longer. It is a virtuoso of continuation. Without anything to continue.

At times it arrives as a double. Stand before a panel of lignin so dark it looks exquisitely wet; study the dim silhouette of yourself smeared across the grain like oil on glass. The silhouette will offer, politely, to swap depth for depth: you absorb the wallpaper of rings behind your reflection, it absorbs the three uneven shores of your breathing. For one staggering instant you are both veneer—flat pigment—and plump with sap—full of begun and unbegun decades. You step back intact; the wood has not taken anything you would ever miss, yet the edge of your outline is now slightly too sharp to cast a shadow. Somewhere a knot tightens, wearing a new curl of your name.

Sometimes it searches for touch rather than reflection. A low gallery of lacquered root-arches leads you in widening circles toward a chamber whose floor is unattended heartwood: polished by nothing more expensive than stable patience. Kneel and lay your palm there. A sigh initials itself into your skin: faint steam scented of terpentine and rusted moon, the ghost of a dragon’s eyelid waking. The warmth rolls up the bones of your arm, shoulder, spine; it does not stop until it has visited every abandoned atrium of your skeleton. In that instant you are the living map of a city that has no inhabitants except its own rumor of streets. Your knuckle-lights flare, and tiny constellations float through the canals of your capillaries—each a map of the room you are kneeling in, upside-down, reversed, exact. You become its evacuated twin. You feel your toenails extend and flatten into pitch-black rootlets tasting the air for mineral crumbs of meaning. Then your arms retract; the city returns entirely into wood; and you carry away a memory so precise it weights nothing—until, years later, you try to describe it aloud and language buckles like wet paper.

On occasion the wood forgets its vastness. A slender hallway only two elbows wide narrows further in thornless reticence. Midway along it a bulb forms—smooth, nut-brown, the size of an unbreathed egg. Place your ear here. You will hear the faint percussion of footsteps crossing a plaza: a market square you cannot reach within the labyrinth, full of citizens who never lived in lamplight flesh, their voices the softened clack of pollen against pollen. The egg vibrates against your cheekbones as if answering any heartbeat it finds. When you step away the bulb has absorbed your ear-print exactly—shape, slope, specific cartilage folds—leaving itself forever molded to your absence. No two walkers leave matching distortions. In the long span of centuries every possible auricle has been profiled here; the bulb is a graveyard of listening that has never needed an answer.

One final articulation: descend a ramp whose tread is nothing more than continual cambium twisting like an ink ribbon. At the bottom waits an atrium roofed by one immense leaf, inky as obsidian. Its upper surface is slick and mirror-flat; you can see the centuries-old footprints of pollen motes still orbiting about their own splashed reflections, each a slow planet of dust patiently erasing itself. Linger. Somewhere inside the lambent dark a ripple begins, spreading concentrically like oil on water. Where the ripples crest and break, a cleft opens—barely the shape of a doorway. Through it your mirage steps forward, not the backward one from polychrome lignin, but something newer: the outline of your next thought, the frame around the starless core of whatever you might still become. This sudden twin is neither translation nor theft; it is rehearsal. It offers you its back the way one offers an exit before turning the key. Your gesture of acceptance is unspoken: you let it choose whether to absorb you or vanish. Unfailingly the cleft seals behind it as if tempered by time’s own glassblower.

Then you are alone again among breathing blackwoods. The warmth in the air measures exactly one last warmth still to be found in you before night perfects itself everywhere.

And that is it. No denizen inhabits the metropolis but this single presence—an archive of anticipated bodies that never outlived their host, a spooled-over echo capable of rehearsing any flicker of chemistry it brushes against. It is the wood remembering us in advance of our future forgetting.
