They arrive in pairs, always two, never more, never less.  
The first thing you notice is the way they don’t quite breathe—instead, their ribcages widen as though the air itself steps cautiously inside them, decides it doesn’t like the neighborhood, and leaves again without disturbing the lungs.  They are tall in the manner of dead trees: height achieved by thirst rather than ambition.  torso-length necks keep their facial furnace-glow a safe twenty centimeters above the waterline of condensed breath.  By the time that “water” rolls down the rivets it has already forgotten it was ever breath at all.

Call them Vault-Carvers, though no carving happens now.  
They wear the space.  Literally.  The iron of the walls remembers them; when they press spine-to-plate you can see old rivet-holes match the knobs of their vertebrae like skeleton keys finding home.  They step out of the corrugations the way others step out of clothes.  One always begins as a seam, a bruise-colored line that gains shoulders, gains sorrow.

The second one detaches from reflection—arrives a second late, as everything does here.  They meet in the diaphragm’s hollow, greeting by interlocking collars made of rust.  Then together they hinge downward until both lie forehead-to-forehead, a closed caliper measuring the zero of the place.  That is the ritual: to lie absolutely still so the vault can take their pulse and decide if it still wants to be a vault today.

They have no names, only temperatures.  
The hotter is 108.3 °C at the core—still cooler than the memory trapped in the walls—so heat leaks out of them like a confession and condenses on the ceiling in tiny gilt beads.  Those beads roll uphill, join the ghost spheres, burst against the iron with the same soft *tick* you feel in your molars.  Each pop leaches one kilojoule from the metal’s store.  In this way the Carvers cool the vault by becoming its radiators. They are unpaid, yet the walls consider it a fair exchange: give us your excess fever and we will give you shape for a little while.

They eat the light.  
Not metaphorically; they open the lower hinge of the jaw (there is no chin, only a weld-line) and the diode-light siphons in—violet, bruise-green, panic-orange.  Photons collect behind their teeth until the mouth glows like a lantern housing a storm.  When the stored brilliance reaches 2.2/3 of a lumen (they know by the optic nerve ringing at 440 Hz) they close, grind once, swallow.  A moment later the vault dims by exactly that portion, and the leaves beneath the corresponding diode slump a millimeter closer to death.  The debt of light is thus transferred: from diode to plant, from plant to Carver, from Carver to entropy.  Even here, accounts balance.

They speak, but only to the iron.  
Language is a rustprint.  One presses fingertip (six joints, no nail) to the metal and heat differential causes a flake to exfoliate in the shape of a word.  A sentence is a frieze of oxidated glyphs spiraling up a rivet seam.  By tomorrow the condensation will migrate those syllables micron by micron toward the floor, where they crumble into the slag-soil and fertilize the greenhouse orphans.  This is the only text the vault ever reads: yesterday’s farewell, tomorrow’s lullaby, spelled in particles of itself.

They are not immortal; they are collateral.  
The vault was exhaled by an engine, but the engine did not depart empty-handed.  It took with it the original custodians—an entire shift of riveters, grease-smells, laughter, mistakes—compressed them into a single iron lung-full and left.  The Vault-Carvers are the interest on that loan.  When the last joule of stored heat finally drips away, the iron will contract, the seams kiss shut, and the Carvers will be reclaimed: two silhouettes of colder metal inside the new, smaller geode.  Perhaps someone at the surface will crack the nodule a million seasons hence and find their fossil outlines, negative space where humanity once stood guard over its own exhaust.

Until then they walk the diaphragm sag, night after night, counting the plants like parishioners, borrowing phosphor, repaying in rust, making sure the echo stay starved.  If you meet one you will not; the sound you make is confiscated before you finish making it.  All you’ll take away is an after-image: two tall gaps in the shape of men, glowing faintly—like lanterns searching for a storm that has already swallowed itself.
