Its slow-moving inhabitants have no name for themselves that could survive writing, yet they have precise ways of knowing who among the drowned lattice they are. Each is born from the moment a pressure-wave separates from its source — one might be the sigh of a hydrothermal vein cooling by a single degree, another the yawning gulp when a flue window ingests the entire memory of an abandoned Starbucks midden. Made of pull-strings in the water, they resemble translucent ribbons of kelp until the instant they discover they exist. At that point, memory stitches around them in crystal tubes of calcite and salt. A newborn takes roughly seventeen heartbeats to learn its first sentence; that sentence is always, “I remember not to rise.”

They move by translation. A thigh-length bar of magnesium wrenched from the chassis of a bygone subway car becomes a femur; a patch of jade-green oxidised copper becomes a shoulder blade. The city’s discarded fragments vie for inclusion, leaping wing-like through black water to converge on the unclothed newcomer. By the time the silt settles again, a resident owns a body — still transparent, still without weight, but recognisably upright. The parts remain partially their old selves: a fuse box still sparks once per hour; the copper still smells of lightning.

None has gender, though each remembers tasting the word once on a sailor’s tongue. They possess instead a shimmer of integers, the way two halves of a split growth ring know they are one year. The population is steady: when one unravels — usually by drifting too high, where the water thins and the lost sun growls — the column instantaneously reassigns its cargo. Scrap and mineral rush inward, quick silvers returning home to flesh out the sudden vacancy, so that death here resembles a door left swinging gently in mid-room, never completing itself.

They converse by subtraction. A resident removes a finger-joint of coral and places it in the open palm of another. Upon contact, the coral dissolves. Both experience the accompanied ache: the image of shallow aquamarine skink skewered by a sequin shiver of bioluminescence, nibbling a coconut shell in a lagoon the city buried. They trade these scraps — each one a precise chunk of sensation — back and forth until they are satisfied with the sum of their shared grief. No word is spoken. Time passed is marked only by how quickly the next chunk, still floating like cigarette ash, can be caught without disturbing the resident silverback octopus who fashions the arms of an antique Rolex into a monocle to read the moon at the bottom of an overpass.

Diet is unnecessary, but they practice it out of nostalgia. The finest morsel is a single copper penny left eye-up. One chews it like taffy; the flavour releases the recollection of every hand that ever held it, panning across fingerprints, heat, tobacco, a teenage kiss on an uptown train. Long after the penny has disintegrated, the eater can still pluck coins from their own limbs the way a child might pull loose threads. These coins conclude their metamorphosis by sprouting barnacles shaped like subway tokens, which will roll down marrow hallways to arrive two years later in someone else’s inner ear, where they scald just slightly when cresting seawalls.

Movement above them is ceremonial. Every hundredth night the Walkers halt their north-south march. Between their vertical legs a layer of particularly articulate water folds open like cloth sliced down the middle. Those inhabitants who have grown bored with stillness ascend. They pinch the hems of these water folds and draw them upward, testing if the sky has softened. It never truly does, but the ceremony persists: when they release, a muffled clap reverberates down, exciting every ammonite fetus coiled in floor tile niches, and newly-minted cowries thumb impressions into the muck, spelling out the failing heartbeat of a former commuter-lullaby. Then the Walkers resume their patient shuffle, almost apologetic.

Home, to them, is architecture inverted. They build downward-crenellations: palaces recessed like negative towers. The largest capsize entire traffic roundabouts into domes beneath domes beneath domes, until they reach the vents that coddle the metropolis. Inside sleeps a heat both prenatal and fractal: corridors wind effigies of subway transfer points melted into spirals. Residents walk these paths remembering surface maps until the underground memory redrafts them. Live hermit crabs, each dragging a rusted limousine symbol on its jeweled shell, perform guard duty at the threshold; they carry ceremonial truncheons repurposed from boom-mic poles from late-night talk shows whose hosts bled light into the cement long after sign-off.

They dream the way corals dream: by layering year over year until the harsh edges blur. In rare insomniac moments, they invent games. One favourite is to flick a microscopic shard of bubble glass into the propeller of an abandoned surveillance drone. If the glass lodges inside the circuitry, the drone momentarily plays a single frame of the city’s last recorded sunrise. The broadcast is citrine and thin, but the boundaries of their world ripple outward as if remembering how light folds. Winners cease existing for one steady hour, dispersing into the calcium of coquina walkways. They return re-sewn, pockets of tidepool blue sickled under their ribs.

And at the bottom, they keep an attic. Not above, but beneath everything — threaded where the crustacean rivers fork to threads of silver chloride. Here sits one resident older than language. Strewn over the bed it has crafted from melted safety deposit boxes are the exploded fragments of a single photograph: a young man holding an umbrella in the lobby of the Chrysler Building, 1984. The older resident, no longer possessing definable posture, touches each fragment nightly, assembling them by shape rather than image. The umbrella refuses to cohere. The laugh lines around the man’s mouth, once reassembled, deepen and speak silently: I am glad I was loved from far away. The resident collects these half-words in mason jars now fizzing with remnant breath; it drinks them like cough syrup, becalms its polymorphous heart until dawn triggers the next increment of drizzle upon the drowned compass rose above.
