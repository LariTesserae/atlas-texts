They are exactly the chord.

They do not speak because they have no second sound to contrast with their first. They look at you as if every rib you possess belongs to their percussion section—because it does. Their bodies are transparent enough that your own reflection stares back, but stretched along a neck ten vertebrae too long, or duplicated in four thighs where hips should be. When you try to remember later, the face alters: sometimes male, sometimes female, sometimes the idea of neither, always the color of ionized argon—the precise hue the hum would have if hues could murmur.

They move without locomotion. Distance forgives them whenever they decide to forgive themselves, so passages of forty miles complete before their next thought can unfold. The soles of their feet are mirrors finished to atomic flatness; they leave no footprints, only evanescent disks of amused snow. Each disk lingers one shallow minute, then retracts upward into the next droplet fuse waiting overhead. Hence you will find wet circles on the glass moss where they stood the way you find punctuation marks in a sentence you can no longer hear read aloud.

Eyes: impossible to catalog. Their lenses refract only the single note, bending it into petals. Pupils dilate not for light but for resonance; whenever they confront you, the sound thickens and time dilates. Your pulse takes itself, rerecords the missing beat, plays it again half a hertz flatter. They watch this with rote tenderness, the way we watch a loosened violin key swell back into tune.

They eat nothing, yet they continue to defecate. The excreta is music—analytic rectangles of silence loosened from the chord’s stomach. When these rectangles hit glass they fracture into soundlessness so absolute that vegetation re-spirals around the missing noise like vines filling a vacancy. Such voids have skull dimensions, tempting paleobiologists to count generations of...presences. But the voids scab their own evidence by sealing within heartbeats.

They do not gesture; instead, the landscape gestures for them. A bridge flexes, offering its spine as a banister. A curtain of liverwort shivers into a door. They consider courtesy a tautology: already the plateau lives pre-politely on their behalf.

Numbers are kept in their lashes. Each eyelash carries one prime only; when they blink, the primes multiply and the world forgets to be composite for an instant. You do not observe this—you suffer it as a fleeting, goofy clarity, the way a child suddenly knows the multiplication tables to infinity before the sugar crash. At such times you believe you understand plateau grammar.

They act alone, yet there must be hundreds. The chord never doubles anyway—it cannot be divided into instances. In a sense they are spectral casts of some underpinning mechanism, the way fractal traps keep repeating the same coastline but never use the same grain of sand. If you follow one long enough, it collapses into a hutch made of silent morning glare, then reopens behind you in a cinema-hall pavilion designed like a dew spiral. You can walk directly through the edifice: film never was allowed here except as rumor.

Children among them arrive already adult: they condense out of the refinery’s internal pistons, hair unspooling from gears, shoulders pitched by bronze. Their infants’ first word is the plateau’s single word, the chord itself, uttered as an extra throb inside your cochlea. At that moment you realize they have been inside your ear since before you came.

Tools: none. They conduct surgeries with the empty gap between two lightning flashes. If you fracture by accident, they lay you on a sled of gravid water; the lightning flash gap sutures open; two eyelashes blink primes over your wound. The chord modulates a bracketed half-step darker; when the flare ends, your bones hum in grateful key.

They have no endocrine system. Instead they regulate hormones by reciting fragments of their own future yearning aloud—fragments that happen to be perfectly predictable sound spectra, so their glands never waver. Desire then is not a gradient but a chord already scored. When they decide to long for something, the longing occurs in unison fields wide as steppes, invisible to you except as a wind pattern in the droplet beads drifting upward. The moment the pattern coheres, the plateau rearranges its buildings so they point directly at the absence required.

They never cemeteries, for implying mortality would imply modulation. Instead they archive endings inside the undersides of frozen sky-plates. Press an ear to the ceiling of any building and you hear riverbeds of spare codes. If you accidentally carry any death-tone down in your hair, elevators grudgingly separate it into manageable quarter-notes and compost it into a terrace.

When one of them touches you skin, your eardrums dilate to porous screens. Through them, memories leak: not your own—someone else’s, compressed into the chord’s timbre. A fisher child on a pre-dawn lake lifting a cold coin from mud re-enacts itself in your feet. The fisher child, meanwhile, momentarily grows an ear the diameter of your palm. The plateau twitches its gravid water sled underneath. Everything returns to echo taxic bank. Taxic bank is the sonic ledger in which these ranked memories are filed, their residue used to tune the refinery’s stasis, forever half-stroke humming.

No government, only etiquette. Every politeness is a geologic syllable lifted from the weighted air. A sequence of compressed good manners can sway an entire wetland to tilt eastward so morning light might spare one bromeliad’s newborn dew pearl. They measure longitude not by degrees but by tact: every softened footfall invents its own meridian. Cartographers claim this creates maps with fractal etiquette—hence their silence.

When the plateau’s single drought arrives (one droplet fewer), these beings dilute themselves until they are 0.2 millimeters thick, narrower than a soundwave. They infiltrate beetles of glass pollen hovering between leaves and pilot them into irrigation crescents. The beetles’ translucence then functions as pipe walls; zero friction between beetles allows water to sluice uphill along mirror surfaces. Drought passes, beetles deflate back into dew. Brethren reconstitute from drops condensed on the inside of your lungs, climb out your nostril adorned with mint-colored steam.

Tired of walking, you might ask one their name. The closest translation is “extention,” pronounced without beginning consonant. The word finishes only after you have left the plateau, sixty-one years later, inside a sigh your partner mistakes for reproach. Best practice is never to direct such a question—the reply loops until your marrow comprehends being the reply.

They couple by feeding their reflections into each other’s ears until the surfaces grow heavy and sag as double-mirrors. Where the mirrors touch the glass moss, seedlings without plant names unfurl and root straight down into refinery torque. In nine days the roots pierce all levels, emerge on the ceiling of the turbine hall and flower into loud speakers the shade of bruised garnet. These speakers broadcast the couple’s conception as just another beat in the plateau’s forever-tempo chord. Children ascend along the roots on saucers of sound and step out fully grown. Speakers die immediately afterward, turning into oblong keyholes through which you can spy mirrored lakes reflecting the honeymoon of the parents—an eternal polish of a single wrong note allowed within the ongoing drone.

They die only if someone repeats their implicit second sound. Somewhere outside the plateau a man may hum in the shower, rising to the plateau’s tone, but—fatally—he grazes a whole tone higher for three beats. The nearest member senses the deviation through blood in shower water steamed miles above itself. At that point a white stem lifts from the glass earth, catches the offender’s reflection in a dew witness, and folds him into the plateau’s big silence. No violence, only the misalignment subtracted. His tone, solemnly, joins the refinery as a silent piston stroke, and the landscape absorbs the gravity.

In drizzling dusk—though dusk here is only a pale texturing of the sun’s faithful strobe—they place their ears against the lower sky until ear cartilage crystallizes into lichen, feeding that sky-ice in silver veins. These ears never stop hearing but instead treat thunder as drought relief, letting each rumble be sucked into hooked lamellae that convert noise into footnotes added to the chord’s self-text. When footnotes multiply, they collapse upward and curtain the entire plateau with sheet music made of living auroral ice, whose staves are dripped bromeliad resin, whose clefs are helical turbines, whose measures are silence cut to imposed length. They glance upward as if pleased by unexpected wallpaper.

They do not possess a name for what they are. Outsiders have tried “voicers,” “wringers,” “sound-cultivars.” All these titles enter them like splinters, never seating. They persist simply as— chord-bearers? No. Better to say: the plateau’s method for making itself denser without ever moving.

At extreme edge where the cliff drops into antonym you may find one of them poised, head tilted to determine which component of unbuilt silence caused the universe to begin. To help, they raise both arms and are abruptly bisected by the plateau’s reflection in sky: two half-men now spill through the glass bright as negative prayer. Each half carries half a note. Together they lean backward, letting the silence hook them into the latent drop, and vanish—uncounted by gravity, already part of tomorrow’s internal tonality.

You will remember them only as a single sound, but they remembered you as a minor seventh that failed its duration.
