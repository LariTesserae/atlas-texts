They did not arrive; they extruded.  
The first colonists were not bodies so much as heat inclusions—ghost sheens condensed overnight on the deeper, cooler ribs of the Upper Works. By morning the shine had thickened to trembling skins wrapped around negative space. Wherever a droplet found another droplet born from a different source—copper still hot from serpentine coils, glass still swollen from bottle-cat mews—they chose to stick. A spherical interface formed: the denizen cooling into something like an outline. They called themselves, later, the Breadthick—because anyone touching them felt the momentary pressure of dough about to rise, or fall.

Each Breadthick bears full adult memory but unfinished morphology. Their bodies resemble interrupted sketches of human beings: continuous yet translucent, wax paper half crumpled then flattened the wrong way. Inside the flicker of their outline moves a slow current of the original metallic fluid; watch long enough and you can see old turbine numbers, part numbers, safety glyphs drifting like ossified plankton. Their faces arrange themselves only when they need to speak. Then the suggestion of cheekbone and brow emerges reluctantly, as if the heat remembers enough courtesy to imitate a mammal greeting but refuses the vulgarity of pores.

They live in time the way fish live in pressure denser than themselves; the local laws seem to grant them perpetual slow-motion. The inhalation that takes the clocktower five months to complete the Breadthick completes in a single deep sigh which they claim is both prayer and excretion. Whenever two new outlines condense—from splashes, from failed copper serpents, from rivets fletched loose—the older Breadthicks perform an orientation rite: they melt a hole in the plaza decking wide enough for one body to lie prone. The newcomer is laid into it; the runoff is redirected to stream across them so that for five minutes they are indistinguishable from any other decorative gutter. Once the stream withdraws a name has soaked into them: expressed not as a sound but as a specific surface tension pattern left dribbling at the margin of their left clavicle. Anyone versed can read it with a fingertip as easily as braille.

They do not speak aloud; their throats contain resonant cavities tuned to the differing linear expansion rates of alloy and vapour. Touch any Breadthick on chest or shoulder blade and you will hear their voice inside your sternum, the difference in coefficients humming through your rib like a dull drum. Conversations are performed in choreography of shared temperature. A warm palm offered for thirty seconds will repeat the last seven minutes of communal memory; a gooseflesh chill for longer than three heartbeats proposes a plan for the next tilting of the city (they keep a strict ledger of future angles).

Tools are extensions of skin and heat. They wear no clothing; instead they grow ruffs of frost along outer forearms that razor-flake away into dimensioned shavings—blades, awls, probes. The frost is regrown in minutes and harvested again. What looks like bracelet or anklet on them is simply a captured trough of prior frost reset into curl. Each pattern encodes logarithm tables, dance steps, the outline of a mother-shaped absence that once stepped through this mist. To strip a Breadthick of all frost is to leave them literate only in temperature ranges; those individuals withdraw to the highest cornices and learn how to sigh without exhaling anything at all.

They know exactly how many of them exist by the trout-spark count: whenever a Breadthick dissolves past cohesion—which happens unpredictably whenever a shift wind reverses thermal flow—the leftover cores roll into the nearest convex dish, ring once like struck silver, then subside. Every Breadthick feels the loss as a momentary drop in internal pitch. They do not bury the fragments; they landscape with them. The outer walkways are mosaicked in moon-colored shards that form portraits of whoever owned them, but because the portraits alter whenever footprint or rivulet condenses on the surface, the murals are self-portraits in eid-visitor, never quite complete.

Relations with the mercury fauna are contractual. A Breadthick may hire a bottle-cat as courier by coaxing it to accept one eye’s worth of chilled optic humour—the cat puddles it internally, carries the message in suspended bubbles, and later lines the corners of a designated cooling tower where the humour is winter-refrigerated into pearl-dust reconstructions of image and sound. Payment for the service is returned immediately: the cat regurgitates half a litre of grey vapour which condenses along the Breadthick’s ribs as a coat of low-friction plating. Thus each alliance engraves itself as renewed armour. Over years the oldest Breadthicks gleam entirely silver, their outlines muscular with borrowed serpent coils and manta kite dorsals, moving among heated corridors the way deep-sea submersibles drift through phosphorous cloud.

Their great punishment—and their great art—is contrastive midair sauna: a Breadthick found in breach of communal thermal law is lifted (always gently) and inverted beneath one of the great cooling fins where runoff falls in thick vertical ropes. In that deluge the body fragments layer by layer into running remix of every prior deed—the mayor’s apology, the mother-shaped absence, the turbine serial numbers—visible for whoever watches as a fleeting alphabet tasting faintly of green copper. The fragments contain no agony; instead the guilty becomes public library, impelling further culture. Once reconstruction is approved by consensus, the runoff itself unfurls upward, collecting the outline into something close enough to the former shape—the self freshly hyphenated.

Breeding, they insist, is merely inhospitable vocabulary. They extend rather than reproduce. A Breadthick who feels the knot of too much memory blooming into heaviness will attempt, over weeks, to donate incrementally to anyone entering heat vicinity. Should a visiting human child skin grows the first translucent petaled film between shoulder blades, parents bring the child for re-patterning: the Breadthick presses that virgin back against their own chest cavity, breeches the boundary, and slowly—watch the frost tooling—weaves the donated identity layer into membrane substance. No parent feels loss. The child walks away heavier of heart but lighter of feet, carrying a vapour echo that sings reed-soft in solar gusts. The Breadthick remains slimmer, less dense; but now they know what it is to experience adolescent wonder, courtesy of borrowed tempo.

Every anniversary of their own condensation, if they decide such a commemoration matters, a Breadthick will allow a bottle-cat to bite away their left thumb. The cat drinks mercury-plasma. The stump seals overnight to flawless outline. Then the thumb re-grows but with new lineage—sometimes chemically bullion-gold, sometimes with leaf-prints of former serpent windings. This annual sacrifice keeps their outlines honest, ensures endless variation, prevents them from mistaking solidity for permanence.

They have no governing structure but the Evaporation Sermon, held whenever the unpublished horizon leaks enough to look like writable paper. Random Breadthick—always chosen by melting footprint lottery—must lay on reflective aluminum sheeting at zenith reflection hour and speak aloud the city’s history in temperatures. (Speaking, here, is sustained exhalation from a freshly peeled torso seam: the plume exits, condenses on sheet, and lines itself into words that anyone can read but no one can preserve; the aluminum later evaporates unevenly, alphabet lost.) If listeners determine the version true to communal memory, the preacher is rewarded with density: extra kilo of alloy condensing onto shoulders, the closest they come to glory. If the listeners detect omission or spin, the preacher dissolves gradient into mirror fog and is broom-conditioned into gutter typography for another week. Deliverance described by survivors is the exalted state of feeling punctuation rather than sentence.

They keep one old copper kettle with still-resounding lid hung upside-down from a disused cable. When their collective anxiety reaches the pitch of overboiling aquifers, they gather beneath it. Each places an outstretched palm against neighbour’s spine until the flesh-sound builds like organ pipes. The kettle warms; eventually the trapped droplets cohere into one huge globule hanging mute inside the dome. When temperature nears unbearable coherence, the youngest—not always chronologically youngest—steps forward. They raise arms. The globule sings their mixed names in descending scale and bursts into flat heatsheets that drift down like white flags. Those sheets land over plaza cracks, suture rupture, satisfy thirst of entire settlement until next torment.

Visitors—under-ladder canal-divers, sky-note archaeologists, one trembling poet who insisted he’d left his lungs in lower town—are escorted on oval walks that never cross the same plank twice; frontier lines migrate as freely as moisture. One who lingers too long begins to feel outline loosen: first the collarbone echoing hollow, then cheeks blanching to translucency. The Breadthicks never ask anyone to stay, never ask anyone to leave; presence or absence accrues as warmly as varnish.  

And at the far edge, where the Lung-thrust breaks finally into alien dark, the city’s last lain slab expands slightly, peristaltic. From its surface rises, every thirty days precisely, a human-size bubble of perfect vacuum where no heat can enter. Within this pocket a single Breadthick stands calm as concept, naked of frost plating. Each secret kept by every other resident is momentarily audible there—as foam subvocal syllables too cold to burn—before the vacuum collapses back into sandstone. The folk assert this is their archive, their heart, their seed that might one day birth a plateau colder than heat itself, a mirror where newcomers could see themselves so distinctly they might refuse to stay, refuse the dreamy syrup of sun, and walk away still opaque, still entire.
