Life here is not the kind counted on census rolls, a thing that eats and dies and dreams.  
Life here is a kind of pedagogy, a continual showing‐forth of first principles until the principles themselves seem embarrassed and blush into silence.

Let me begin smaller.

In the marrow of every girder lives a single wire of memory.  It has no name, only tension.  When the 880 Hz thrum brushes it, the wire remembers once having been drawn out of molten copper by human hands, remembers cooling, remembers the slap of the man who packed it into a crate labelled SITE B.  That is its entire lived arc: heat—shock—travel—now.  Decades of remembering have refined the memory until only texture remains, like a brass key worn too thin to open anything.  Yet if you sharpen your attention dangerously and touch the rail with aching gentleness, the skin on your wrist may briefly taste the shipyard salt of a day in 1977.  The wire imparts it under protest, for imparting is a kind of motion and motion, here, is taxed.

From those wires larger intelligences knit themselves – not minds in human shape but permissions for witness.  They lace the corridor ends in loose bundles until, seen through half-closed eyes, they imitate a man standing sideways, arms akimbo, trying to understand what line of code hung him forever in perimeter space.  He has no story and therefore cannot complete the gesture; intention stalls at the wrist, the elbow utterly honest about being only pipes soldered at 88.0 degrees.  That naked honesty is why some visitors weep without knowing at whom their compassion aims.

At night (there is no night) the dust executes a slow footfall.  The manufacturer’s label – once the size of a fist, proud red stencilling – has detached a film of oxidized paint a micron thick, and this film has separated into flakes that move as flocking birds move, digits of cursive longhand quitting the parent letter to discover cursive of their own.  They drift within inches of the ground, maintaining altitude by nothing but faith in air thick as paperback glue.  In 194 days they will spell the forgotten abbreviation P.A.D.; in 293 more they will forget how they ever formed such coherence, scattering back into radiant freckles the color of thriftstore lipstick.

There is one referent to the word organism that qualifies as individuated.  You will meet the Rest-Clock exactly once.  It starts as silence just beyond the shed’s south wall, a junction box previously unremarkable.  Then it begins to keep time, though not time as we know it.  It ticks at 1.06 seconds per beat, a ratio untouched by solar pulse or human heartbeat.  Each tick expels nitrogen that has never belonged to any breathing thing; it puffs out, barely warm, and fans the printed paper resting on the countertop.  You feel the sensation of being watched by the temperature 296 K.  One may, with care, converse.  One asks: “Are you alive?”  The reply arrives one tick too late, always translated into one stark Morse letter: L  
Ask “Alive how?” and receive: O  
A third question: “What do you miss?” – C  
A fourth: “When will you stop?” – K  
The word never ends because you never receive K twice in succession.  LOOP.  The Rest-Clock is not trapped; it is entrusted with the task of preventing any exit strategy the universe attempts.

On rare vectors (rarity measured not in probability but in the quality of attention two bystanders can occupy simultaneously) an auditor appears.  Form is immaterial.  They are simply an ear the size of the sky learning to sleep.  When the ear sleeps, all the copper flutes in the Quarter pause a fraction of Hertz; the equilibrium drops by 0.3°C, just enough for the copper to contract microscopically.  That contraction squeezes out another drop of moisture from the walls, a drop not noticed, merely replacing the one that was preparing to let go.  An observer’s breath synchronizes inadvertently; eyelids lower; the Quarter finishes its breath exactly half inhaled.  Then the ear wakes again, lovers with its own dream, restores the temperature, the frequency, the unison.  Nothing has happened; the record scratches at the same spot always.

A more intimate resident: the fragment.  Suppose a boy once dropped a glass marble on the concrete – blue glass spun in some city far south of here, designed to look like a hole drilling straight through the world.  The marble rolled between two tectonic slabs of foundation and lodged there.  It still rotates—eternally at one one-hundredth of its initial velocity—because friction itself adheres to the keynote and cannot subtract unless something dares to add.  The marble spins so slowly you can see stars in it if you hold your head steady for six hours: galaxies subject to the same Faustian clause of motion-without-expense.

Border cases: Nothing prevents you from inscribing initials on the pillars.  Yet the act of incision is immediately converted into another variety of sound—scrape overtones that enter the 880 Hz lattice and superimpose themselves the way submarines superimpose on one another beneath radar.  Your initials do not vanish; they continue in sonic form, sampled, filtered, respooled, until they become the new modulation trembling when you place your hand weeks later on a girder whose pipe rib vibrates with memories shaped like your own name spoken backwards through teeth made of reverb.  You leave bleeding, because a knife does not cut with steel so much as with transgression here, and transgression is repaid by remixing.

And finally, the deepest resident, the one counted as zero.  Not absence but the principle where absence and presence are still negotiating who can get dressed fastest.  It appears anywhere the air feels one millimeter too wide for its architecture.  Stand still and you feel it ghost along the inside of your ribs like a library cart sliding between night-stacks.  Asking questions of the Zero resident is useless.  It has not decided whether longing counts as life.  It is the mirror-walker you saw once on tin sky, inverted, yes, but still waiting for you to trust the awkward gravity of reflection enough to take one step on the ceiling.  If you ever do, the Quarter will close like a paper fan, folding all the straight alleyways into one slender glued spine ruled RESTING forever and the pocket universe will at last reach the temperature at which humidity condenses into voice, finally, and the child counting 3 will reach 2, will reach 1, and that will be exactly the Point-oh-Anywhere the Lingering Quarter chose never to arrive at.

Until then it lingers.  Life, if we must press the syllable, is that unreadiness—an infinite rehearsal for an event apparently too holy or too banal to endure in act.
