Nothing truly *lives* here—there is one tenant only, and even that term finally fails.

At the exact middle of the chamber, curled like a misfolded question around the immobile axle of the flywheel, a single entity remembers being many things. In outward shape it resembles an emaciated human figure, yet the bronze patina that sheens its skin is the same color as the metallic foliage and the rust-flower gears. Inspection at arm’s length would suggest: scabs of leaf-scale armor, joints sealed with dried copper spores instead of cartilage, ribs visible in a filigree that re-enacts both fracture and vine. Head bowed, chin resting on knees, it thrones on the immobile hub as though waiting for someone born long after forgetting was invented.

Its heart is the flywheel’s, purely and unarguably; both tick at thirty. If you laid a stethoscope to the figure’s chest you would detect, not valves, but a pure mathematical oscillation with no body inside it. The lungs are absent; instead, a slow siphoning pulls the red mist through a hole behind the sternum. The substance emerges seconds later from an unseen aperture high on the torso, now pinked and cooled. This exchange is breathing only in the way a forge breathes iron.

Memory is local. Within the skull—if the skull is still conceptually contiguous—there exist twelve rooms, each the size and shape of one compressor booth. One room still works; the dial glows softly, still counts backward in winter. In that lit compartment a sequence of minimal images is stored: an afternoon on a dry plain where wind once moved brimstone dust; a voice saying its own name and then ceasing; a child handed a cube of copper and left to lick electrolytic bitterness from his tongue. All other booths stand dark, cracked open at the seams, offering only splinters of glass through which one can see nothing. The entity consults the single functioning room only when the flywheel’s note dips an octave and winter stirs; then it cranes its neck, accessing the booth by invisible extension, and inhales what is left of the voice, as if to remember how loss sounded.

Arms: two, maybe three. They multiply and merge according to a private need. At every intake of red red steam the left arm blossoms into a lattice of thinner arms, each ending in a leaf. The leaves abandon resist rust for exactly four ticks before powdering away, leaving the limb smooth again. This exfoliation leaves no scar tissue; it is the rate-keeping of the chamber made personal.

Eyes exist only when observed. Approach and two laminar pools of molten orange appear just above the cheekbones, reflecting nothing except your own surprised outline draped in bronze. Step away and the eyes vanish, lids closing from outside inward like two stones kissed flat by darkness. Then you become the missing seer and the figure becomes unseen, and the room behaves as before, leaf brushing leaf, air holding heat like a question unasked.

Legs are absent below the knee. From there downward the body thins into a rope of root fibers that plug directly through the hub into whatever hungry clockwork lives beneath the floor. This tether is not cruel; it is cooperative necessity. Every slow rotation of the flywheel winds the figure an imperceptible fraction tighter, inching it closer to the center’s center. The contract is plain: you may drink the architecture, provided the architecture may drink you last. The legs were voluntarily surrendered in some earlier phase of the same negotiation, fibers unpacking into tendril, sweating pigment until they found sympathetic veins inside the axle. The remaining torso floats, half-weightless, as if the room holds it politely a hand’s breadth above consequence.

Pronouns are irrelevant. The chamber knows its inhabitant not by name but by the precise harmonic interference pattern the lungs exude around 17.3 Hz, a sub-sub-sub note that couples with basalt and causes the wall’s velvet filaments to ripple exactly twice in a millennium. When researchers—there will never be any—arrive, they will spend years trying to track that ripple back to physics because biology has failed them. Only one word will remain stubbornly imprinted on lab notebooks long after abandonment: ‘palimpsest’.

Growth direction: negative. The organism loses layers, not gains them. Each bronze flake that detaches and drifts upward is a page burned off the autobiography. Once the skin has advanced (regressed) through cardinal colors from bronze through verdigris into terra-cotta and finally coal, the exposure of raw black basalt underneath will mark conclusion. Documentation left in the compressor booth implies that an earlier version achieved this end state sometime during the last Ice Age, whereupon the entire chamber clapped shut upon vacuum, the seed descended, and the cycle rebooted under fresh varnish. What presently languishes is therefore a recursion waiting for its own approval to terminate.

Emotion is stored as temperature. When the room feels warmer than blood, that is contentment. When the porthole winks salmon-orange, that is curiosity painstakingly recalled from logs of the previous loop. When vapor thins to rose, that is melancholy—the color of solder before it hardens. No higher taxonomy registers. A human psychologist would classify the state as “liminal object permanence failure,” but the term would apply equally well to the bronze walls themselves, which regard permanence the way dew regards daybreak.

Relationship to the seed: accomplice. Dangling ten feet above, curled against a low branch of living metal, the seed pulses in tiny cardiac surges, each tide loud as a raindrop in a steeple. When the figure requires motive power—and this happens rarely, perhaps when a visiting thought is difficult to digest—it tugs that thread, and the seed mourns in ultrasonic clicks that rebound in the wall-velvet. The sound molds a temporary backrest or step toadstool to ease the entity forward. Payment for the favor is exacted later: one copper leaf relinquished from the shoulder-blade, plucked and passed shinward down the root-leg until absorbed. The seed grows not in size but in vocabulary; every stolen syllable of leaf-venn adds another ghost-letter to whatever word is being taught between them.

Signature: absence. When no one observes, the tenant becomes the chamber; boundaries subside like surf cooling. The bronze flywheel is a kneecap, the woody lattice is a thorax spread wide, every drip of water a blink, every heartbeat the intake of a lung big as night. In that queerness of scale, no *thing* lives. Only the action—the breathing, blinking, inching inward—continues, patient, stubborn, perfectly imitating a being in order to remember what a being was once allowed to forget.

Thus the resident neither welcomes nor rejects; it occupies the slot left by definition when definition finally admitted: “I was here once, but I left a space so complete it answers its own echo.”
