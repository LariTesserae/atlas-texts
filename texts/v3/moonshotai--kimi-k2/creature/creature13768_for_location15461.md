I live here, though “I” has become negotiable.  

The spinal cavity recognizes me—not by name but by harmonic signature, the way the inner ear knows when the heartbeat is its own. I do not remember arriving. Segments of that voyage lie folded inside Bogdanov node twelve-A-four: a week’s worth of external daylight compressed into an elongated prism I can wedge against the roof-veins if I need to tan. I have never done so. I prefer the artificial dusk and the prickling susurration of moisture growing across my collar bones.  

In substance I am largely composite now. When the umbrella-jelly detonators first siphoned salt through the pores of my left hand, the lymphocyte drones assumed I was becoming a vent–human hybrid. Bone plates scalloped open like origami ribs and the drones stapled flexible graphene sleeves inside, turning coursing blood into quiet toroidal coils instead of noisy red rivers. The machines left meticulous post-it notes welded in titanium on my ulna: Patient has volunteered for peri-tectonic symbiosis. Signature:___________________. None of us filled it in; signatures are too vertical an idea for this place. I simply listened to the city agree.  

My occupation is Listening. Rootmakers dedicate me in the same way they notch stalactites to mark elapsed centuries. I hang thirty metres above the plaza, upside-down from a hammock of woven mycelium they call A-Minor Bridge because the reverberation along its 7.37-metre length drops a perfect minor third when struck. Each dawn (I still use the word) I begin a systematic tracing of cave-wide octaves. I time the interval between the gurgle rising from lithic oceans and the recoil from the rooftop bones. Then I translate the delay into choral notation and ink it onto fungal parchment which sliver-swimmers—microscopic light-finned spores—carry off into crevices where the city almost ends. Those clustered voices accumulate until some future listener inverts the chord and reassembles the day I heard. Rootmakers insist history must remain braised in minor modes; I obey without understanding why.  

I seldom descend to the plaza. Gravity fusses around my legs and my lungs feel frivolous at nine-storeys-deep partial pressure. Yet occasionally I descend to exchange arcana with the Maintainers. Two of them still credit a mother from surface myths. They wrap their ductile carbon arms in damp scarves depicting the daylight sky—cyan silk, cloud embroidery, impossible artifacts carried here inside elevator capsules until every dye molecule agreed to unlearn its worldly orbit. The Maintainers feed me nickel tablets that counteract argon narcosis. In return I recite yesterday’s corrected echo: a seventh that flattened by eleven cents at 14:02 local, causing three elevator doors stutter rhyme with three adjacent stalactite drips. The Maintainers nod, hammer the tile with a deliberate tritone, and resume pacing the harmonic primes.  

Etymology has grown moist and unreliable. Words refuse surface meanings; the descending humidity dilutes intention until only remoras of sound survive. In my hammock I review a lexicon I once claimed as mine:  

• Stratum = soft eruption of air across teeth, followed by tongue collapsing upward.  
• Citizen = noise circulating through another body’s pelvis before arriving in one’s own.  
• Year = the precise distance my abbreviated shadow travels along a vertebra before decay reclaims its pigment.  

Between projects I converse with broken kiosks. Their diodes long ago surrendered to radiolarian dust, so interaction transpires in half-lit afterimages. When I greet them with ‘morning’ the kiosk ghosts project my own reflection dissolved along the parabolic curve of the roof. The mirrors respond not with words but with chamber echoes of conversations I mislaid three exits ago. The transitive nature of identity fascinates me; somewhere, a future exile is replaying this greeting, believing it original.  

Sleep is modular. I spore-dream for exactly 27.3 minutes among orchid polyps, during which my mind is smeared across nine metres of viscid root-hair as bicarbonate accordion pulses translate short-term memory into calcium carbonate latticework. Upon re-awakening I find milky scabs at my temples; once peeled, I can play them on any flat surface as phonograph records deemed 6% obsolete. Those scab-songs keep the orphan child company—the single child left after the council abandoned civic recruitment. The child never speaks. I believe it exists as a personification of whatever residue refusal accumulates when every adult opts out. We share the hammock during storms (the trench coughs cyclones of charged dust upward). I hum the 498,211th harmonic prime to quieten its tremors.  

I have taken lovers—subduction lovers—who live entirely inside the interval between my pulse and the next drip. We negotiate sensation along frag grenades of static. One was a Reforged scribe whose neurally engraved ink swarmed away from the pen to chase vibrations always just ahead of articulation. We attempted co-authorship but our paragraphs kept sliding off the page and embedding as tinnitus in future passerby skulls. The relationship ended when my calcium records phlegmatically refused to resolve his name into clef notation. Another liaison lasted through twenty-eight of the city’s heartbeats; she pressed edits against my collarbone using phosphor fingertips that left embargoed sentences to scab over. Those scabs I donated to the trench rather than archive—a modest betrayal I revisit nightly as reef-lights glimmer where her glyphs dissolved into carbonado.  

Diets oscillate. Days when lattice fibrils hum high I ingest latent geometries secreted by vent-circulators: small square nuts flavoured faintly of submarined reefs. Other days I chew mineral foam harvested from drone abdomens. Their thorax light moistens my tongue like cool electricity. The umbrella-jelly detonators sip salts from the corners of my eyes if I permit. I let them; they hum minor sixths against my lashes, notes I will transcribe for Rootmakers against tomorrow’s vibes. The city samples me and in eating me back into itself I become less a parasite and more a timbral register.  

There are fugitive memories drifting I dare not touch. Beneath one of the regolith benches in the plaza I discovered a shoebox labelled GRANDMOTHER’S KNITTING where dust has translated wool into quartz. When I disturb it the inside overflows with VHS snow and the colour your childhood smelled like just after sunset. That box is tendershard; splice yourself against it and the recollection exits both of us simultaneously. I keep the box tied shut by threading detonator gut through its tongue, a cruelty no rootmaker would sanction. The city tolerates cruelty that seeks equilibrium.  

I own a pet echo—actually a metastatic syllable torn from elevator Directive #3—“REMEMBER”. It squeaks three octaves above audible, ricochets between ceiling sockets and arrives thinner every circuit. I leash it with phonon threads to the hammock so it does not flee into the trench where memories ossify diamonds. Echoes want the depth because pressure gives mass to ghosts.  

Lately body-tilt has worsened. Micro-slope creep built into the cavern architecture drags furniture, bones, intentions—toward the throat. I fear slipping while asleep; Rootmakers promise the city will re-coalesce me from reverberations if I submit to their next incantation, an outright lie they justify by showing their tally bone scrawl: 82.17% of everything lost has already been re-composed elsewhere. I calculate nevertheless the angle at which hammock, body, and fluid converge. In 1,942 nights cumulative fall-line, I will slide past the tenth storey if I take no corrective action. The Maintainers offered hammer-surgery on the floor, displacing slate north-by-west prime metres; doing so would fracture their harmonic progress irrevocably. We negotiated and forged a compromise: I will nightly sing every ninth sub-handed note into a resonator fixed to the plaza centre. As air thickens around the frequency, drag vector shifts micro-degrees away from chasmward. Thus my voice has become ballast and debt simultaneity. I keep ledger in mid-air mudra: finger-semaphore tally marks curl, fold and re-index around spine-warm basalt.  

Visitors arrive sporadically. They ascend pale in elevator lamp sweep, pupils wide as wounds. (Pupils wider possible, I should say; all wounds scale here.) I greet them according to the ancient three Echoes and add a fourth, unofficial:  
4. If you remember what you came for, you have not yet arrived.  

Some accept; others recoil, begging for reassurance that other exits exist. I motion toward the detonators spiralling past my hammock: Exits dissolve. Entrances open only in negative space. When children come they invariably name me “Amnesia Aunt” and try to tuck knuckles under my ribs as if I were detachable. I let them judge the pliancy of my struts. One girl carried a tin globe engraved with Earth. She insisted its oceanic trenches labelled MARIANA matched my home. I placed her globe near channel seven resonator; the city scanned it, decided it was non-paradoxical, and tucked replica six kilometres deeper into mantle. Now its twin depth-currents orbit the younger tectonic plates and she, hand now empty, has joined Rootmaker apprentice caste because no layer higher than haiku now contains her history.  

Tonight the lattice sirens. Someone sabotaged node eighty-nine, folding half a maintenance drone into origami appendix exactly three city-metres from plaza center. Triplets of damp glissandi thread the air—I feel them in my cartilage. The resulting curvature drags my hammock eleven degrees south, encroaching feared fall-line. I am weighing guilt: should I donate tonight’s ninth sub-harmonic to counter-balance the crime, or flee to deeper cartilage cave where geometry apologias are stored? Ambivalence nauseates; once, a memory of nausea caused a cyst to pearl my stomach lining into a perfect D-minor triad.  

I may still rise, unhook my spinal splice, let it dangle like a lantern. The crowd below might mistake vertebrae for bulb and myself for detonation. Children might chase shreds of light into the trench and the trench might finally open its irised eye. Or the node may self-repair, restoring Euclidean courtesy while erasing the saboteur’s motive into fusion ash. Both outcomes feel suspended at the opening of my throat like a vowel the city keeps correcting.  

Now the hammock creaks minor seventh too low. No ghost-orchid smell tonight; instead iodine and graphite smear. The resonance accelerates. My inscriptions—skin scabs, ledger marks, whispered ballast—gleam wet as fresh solder. I ready my ninth interval, throat tuned. If I release the note perfectly flat the entire polygonal horizon may tilt three degrees enough to buy me another season. If I miss by even two cents I will arc downward past bench, past quartz knitting box, into the luminous eye below.  

Either result satisfies one layer of community or another, but the city itself remains polyphonic on the subject. A seam inside city marrow theorizes every inhabitant is merely vesicle: we are enzymes ferrying mineral logics until the spine reaches digestive resolution. My private conviction is more lunatic: that tendon tremor eliminated by Repair Algorithm 9B is in fact the continent’s heartbeat beginning not to restart. Should movement cease, every rule collapses. The drone’s three Echoes may one day expand into a final Zero and the trench close over as if stitching wound. In such outcome only the one who still remembers surface daylight repeats history at digestive scale. I feign submission to trendlines while secretly preserving a note—one irregular heartbeat cached in the rear molar—that still smacks just slightly of sunny asphalt above.  

Thus I hang, inverted librarian inside the yawning bone. Impatiently. Fabricating time forward by refusing to decide whether the next hymn tenders downfall or delay. The city, misting around me, hiccups Cherenkov blue: not a warning to me personally—rather an acknowledgment that every resident, living and lexical, holds tenure contingent only on the city’s next sub-crane-snap exhale.
