What lives here is not an empire, not a breed, but a kind of etiquette.  
No resident stays longer than it takes one leaf-edge to inch across a fingertip, yet the life of the place never falters. The city is a relay of interim monarchies, each creature inheriting the throne only for the instant it can keep its footing upon the shifting board.

—The Aerial Masons  
Near the canopy hover the *knitting kites*: wingless, pale lianas that unhook themselves from yesterday’s height, twirl in the warm syruped air, and spiral downward until they become—by precise angle—trellis, railing, or balustrade. They begin transparent as dew, thicken to apple-green translucence by the time they touch the root-pavement, and calcify overnight into a pale marble lattice for tomorrow’s commuters. These kites were birthed not of seed but of surplus: every leaf overfed by sunshine thrusts a waste-packet—crystalline cellulose—out through its midvein; wind catches it, soffits it, shapes it. None of yesterday’s balconies remain today; the forest applaudes only new work.

—The Hinge-Timekeepers  
Where root-arcade meets root-arcade you may see shimmer-tags appearing like copper countersink screws—only, if you squat, each reveals itself as a *time hare*, jointed in seventeen places, the size of two thumbnails pressed together. They do not progress so much as *decide*. A hare folds until its body forms a bezel, fits itself into the seam between two root-facades, and begins to *sign*. The motion is invisible, but the consequence is perfectly legible: the adjacent corridor tightens or widens by a single millimetre. One more brick in the endless revision of municipal bylaws. No hare repeats a measurement; after its act it dries to parchment and shoots up as a three-petalled orchid up a drainpipe yet to be invented.

—The Migrant Statues  
Salamanders you saw, but the true aristocracy are the *quartz trufflehounds*, obsidian-skinned quadrupeds with windows of amethyst between their ribs. When mother-of-pearl in the Red Hollow begins its next faint rotation, the hounds emerge from tunnels beneath crystal crust, each bearing a sandstone effigy of its own future self. They set the sculpture on a chosen pedestal of bark-stair, then in a single draught lick five years of calcite out of the air and reassemble it mid-spine. Thus they age backwards: the statue grows heavier, the body lighter, until collapsing day when dog becomes pebble and effigy lumbers off on newborn legs. The cycle is three-thousand nights. They speak nothing, but carry in their pupils the light-blue morning of a world that may yet exist.

—The Envelope Guild  
Consider the foliage: every leaf sown perceives tomorrow’s weather and seals the mail it must deliver. Its upper surface sketches the temperature gradient of three cycles hence, encoded in micro-corrugations; the underside presses out a perfumed varnish-mail to the groundlings. Who decodes it? *Langoustines of the air*, insects grown cube-shaped through ancestral painstaking. A cube lands upon a leaf as though it were a postage stamp. It frays one edge into filaments fine as dust, soaks up the corrugated map, and rolls its own newly memorised edge into a scroll. Before sunset the scroll is donated to the wind—where it blooms like a jellyfish, falls, is sieved out by the root-pavement and becomes the thread that keeps one shard of daylight from falling through. One shard only. All others fall and are lost to the red rim beyond.

—Archivists of the Mirror Disc  
At the gyration’s midpoint, often between three blinks, *mirror-archae* slip from foot to foot across the iridescent disc. They resemble children until you notice their shoulder-blades are eyelids. Every thirty-third step they blink downward; a camera-flash of memory ejects from inside their spines, fossilised as a flake of pearly mica. This flake is shaped precisely like them but one millimetre smaller. Children pick up their own miniatures, arrange the replicas into concentric rings that gradually swivel outward across the clearing until the distance between stone and origin-child equals the radius of the disc itself. Then they press both soles to the surface; polygon plates slide apart, file the flake into an amber case overnight. Morning leaves no trace—except that the children are now shorter by that same millimetre. They continue walking, receding from themselves, until a single elliptical crescent carved into the undersurface of the disc reveals the aeon-long archive compressed in wafer-thin growth lines. Anyone lowering an ear to the crescent will hear the exhaustive laughter of previous travellers learning they had forgotten they came through once already.

—The Guests  
Yes, you—whenever you arrive—occupy the *suspension layer*. The etiquette is thus: stand long enough for a vine to ask your posture, motionless until a verdict is reached. The city does this politely; never later than the length of a thought it assigns you a public role no future walker will notice. Perhaps today you will become the hinge in a salamander’s wing-joint; tomorrow, an extra chord struck inside a hound’s papyrus hymn. Even the ejecta that drift away from you—skin flakes, carbon from your lungs—are drafted into the nightly revision. The ammonia scent adjusts itself so you will not recognizably smell—as traveler—but only confirm the parfum you gave upon entering.

No one ends. No one begins. The Forest of Nod is a city of tenures forever being handed off before the pen can blot. Its citizens are the commas between paragraphs only the forest writes and only the forest reads, pausing for the moment it takes a full-stop to become a colon, then collapsing into each other like the slowest applause.
