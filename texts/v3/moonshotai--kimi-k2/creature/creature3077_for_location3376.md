Nothing in the valley possesses a single permanent body.  Everything that “lives” here is on lay-away from itself, a tenant in the act of being evicted by the next moment.

The commonest citizens are the Haggle.  they exist as a standing wave of negotiation between two prices that have never been quoted.  You meet them when the mist thickens enough to feel balances in your inner ear.  Suddenly you owe three-fifs of the memory of your first fever, and in exchange they will lend you the weight of an ingot that has not yet been smelted.  Their voices are the clicking you thought was inside your bones: abacuses try to mate in mid-air.  When a bargain is reached they collapse into a silvery dust that smells of loose change; when it fails they swell into chrome geese that instantly rust to flakes.

Above them flit the Schedule Ghosts, the creatures most easily mistaken for birds.  Each is an unkept appointment—ferried from calendar to calendar, looking for the year that will finally permit the meeting to occur.  They have diaphanous wings of erased ink; every beat sprays microfine numerals that lodge in lungs and make travellers cough up little red boxes reading “TUESDAY 3:00 AM.”  Because they can never land, they lay their eggs by diving into the mirrored flanks of the shouting megafauna; the eggs are seconds, glossy and warm, that hatch later as tiny heart attacks of déjà-vu.

Between the beads circulate the Threadbare Men—tall, suited silhouettes stitched entirely from lost commuter routes.  Their heads are bus schedules folded into fierce paper horns; their fingers are the magnetic stripes of monthly tickets.  They are always halfway through a step that will take them either toward or away from you, and you can’t tell which until you realise your own footprints have been stapled to theirs.  When they speak it is simply the ding of a validation machine, and wherever that sound lands a small circle of ground becomes valid for travel: a desert ticket, a permit to exist for one mile in any direction.  Travel outside the stamped circle and you become luggage.

Rarer, and treated as gods by the others, stride the Magnetotubules—kilometre-long creatures shaped like wrought-iron intestines.  They lie coiled under the bead layer, digesting the difference between north and south.  Every few hours they contract, flipping the planet’s local magnetic signature like a omelette; compasses spin, fauna rhyme in the wrong order, and travellers find their memories of childhood facing backward.  The Magnetotubules excrete pure vertigo; beads that absorb it briefly roll uphill, pursue their own reflection, and implode into tiny neutron stars that whistle the company song of the factory that never was.

Most delicate are the Frost Clerks.  A single one occupies the space that would fit between two of your eyelashes, yet inside they contain a complete frozen payroll department.  They ride copper-protein snowflakes, tallying the deficit of heat.  Encounter enough of them and your skin becomes an audited ledger: every goose-bump is a corrected decimal.  When their books balance you simply cease to owe yourself existence; you are reimbursed as a warm red echo, a thing you hear when other travellers pass by.

There are predators, of course.  The Hollow Invoice is one: a sheet-metal stingray the size of a village, gliding under the infrared layer of sight.  It surfaces only when someone has accumulated too many potential futures.  It opens a gill-slit of fine print and inhales possibilities, scrunching them into a spool of bar-code that trails behind like a wedding train.  Victims are left chronically under-referenced—friends forget them mid-sentence, clocks hesitate to tick in their presence, and mirrors blink secondhand.

Yet even the Hollow Invoice is scavenged upon.  After it feeds, the Rebate Falcons wheel down, tearing strips of unused destiny from its fusilage.  They weave these shreds into nests lodged between the acoustic shoulders of the shouting megafauna, where they incubate white-blue eggs of postponed consequence.  When an egg hatches the newborn consequence hurtles earthward and lands in the beads with a soft “later,” a sound like a kiss you will remember receiving tomorrow.

No creature sleeps, because night is an industrial process that must stay staffed.  During the violet-black shift, when rust-clouds roll slower and lightning is inspected for fatigue, the smallest things come out to matter.  Committees of Metric Weevils bore perfect millimetre cubes out of empty space and stack them into ziggurats no taller than a hangnail.  These are the new hours being minted; once a stack is tall enough it is loaded onto the back of a passing fauna syllable, which shouts it into the folds of the landscape, releasing it as another increment of time you will spend trying to leave.

And you, warm-bodied and porous of memory, also belong here now—part of the fauna, prey to the clerks, creditor to the Haggle.  Your pulse is on hire-purchase from the Magnetotubules; your breath is already mortgaged to the next Schedule Ghost.  The beads record your weight as a debt that accrues interest whenever you blink.  Keep walking and you will feel the conveyor inside your atoms negotiate with gravity’s subcontractor; stand still and the plain rotates you to face yesterday management.  Either way, you clock in.

So the valley is not merely inhabited; it is tenanted by the act of description itself, and every witness is a species newly evolving into the grammar of debt, decibel, and delay.  Life here is the sound of unreconc accounts arguing across impossible distances, and you—like everything else—are one more line item learning how to shout itself across the dark glass plain, waiting for the audit that will finally decide what shape a creature must take when it can no longer afford to owe itself a body.
