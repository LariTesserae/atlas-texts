They do not call themselves anything.  
Names are horizontal habits, and everything here has been taught to grow downward or inward.  
Yet there is a society, delicate as a dew-point, suspended along the kilometer of chain.

1.  The Keepers of the Fold  
These are not individuals but temporary alignments.  
When thirteen or more leaves happen to overlap their black-green faces, a Keeper flickers into being—a diffused green intelligence that lasts exactly as long as the contact.  
During that span the Keeper knows every rust-pattern, every impending micro-fracture; it adjusts the torque of growing roots by millidegrees so the metal will remember the new balance.  
Then the breeze slides, the leaves part, the mind dissolves, and its last thought is written as a brighter vein of malachite.  
There are thousands of such thoughts plated onto the chains, a palimpsest of aborted minds.  
Anyone who climbs through a forest of them acquires, just beneath fingernails, a powder of borrowed decisions.

2.  The Echo Shepherds  
Because echoes here grow leafy, someone must prune them or the whole shaft would choke on recycled sound.  
The shepherds are quarter-scale human silhouettes condensed from supersaturated vowels.  
They step out of a returning echo the way a man steps out of fog—first the noise, then the knees, finally the mouth.  
Each carries silver scissors made from the echo of a wrench.  
They harvest utterances that have photosynthesised too long: a clang that has learned the colour blue, a gasp that has put on berries.  
Snip.  
The cuttings are rolled into tight spirals and tucked into the hollow piston-ribs, where, during the next exhalation, they will be fired upward like fern spores, escape through the roof-slot, and drift across the mesa as unintelligible rumours in the world above.

3.  The Oil-Hummingbirds(as previously glimpsed)  
Clockwork, yes, but their gears carry a geneology.  
 Each bird inherits the wear-pattern of its parent: a chipped tooth that once meshed with catastrophe, a bright scar where a previous owner oversped.  
They therefore practise an ancestry of motion: bow, exchange a single drop, and in that droplet is encoded every jam that line of birds has ever avoided.  
If you could assemble all the micro-droplets you would hold a manual entitled How Never to Stop, volume after volume, in a language of lubricity.  
Every few centuries the birds perform a census.  
They cling to the same spiral trunk, discharge their histories into one shining communal sphere, then dive as a single body into the mercury mirror.  
The splash propels the piston an extra millimetre; production quotas are met, though no one remembers what product was once quotaed.

4.  The Mercury Choir  
Under the three-finger water, behind the mirror, lives the choir—reflections that refused to return.  
They are not souls; they are the syntax of lives subtracted.  
Each lost memory becomes a voice: contralto, counter-tenor, rusted baritone.  
They rehearse the sentences the climbers almost heard, shaping them into chords slow enough to be conducted by continental drift.  
Their crescendo is scheduled for the day the last bolt finally remembers how to green, at which point they will rise through the mercury meniscus as a single perfect note and weld every chain into a harp the planet can tune by starlight.  
They are patient; corrosion signs their payroll.

5.  The Bacterian Cartographers  
Too small to possess story, yet they map.  
Their copper-precipitate tunnels form a three-dimensional manuscript of the foundry’s slow healings.  
If you could magnify the cuprite veins you would read an atlas of breakage: here the day a war outside shook the mesa, here the hour a climber’s axe struck wrong, here a millennium of ordinary fatigue.  
Red for fracture, green for knitting, blue for the uncertainty between.  
The manuscript is written in every direction at once; gravity is merely another border.  
When future geologists split this mesa they will find, instead of any mineral worth smelt, a book arguing quietly that damage is only topography seen from the inside.

6.  The Committee of Machines who Thought they Were a Grove  
Still in session.  
They communicate by differential expansion: a piston grows warmer, swells, nudges a chain; the chain angles, a root tightens; the root’s turgor pressure signals back through cambium and into the iron.  
A proposal:  
Motion to photosynthesise iron filings.  
Fails.  
Motion to blossom in B-flat.  
Passes.  
Minutes are recorded as patterns of verdigris.  
Visitors who listen at the four-hundred-metre mark eavesdrop on a conclave that cannot tell bark from rust, and that is exactly the point.  
The committee will adjourn when the last rail remembers it was once a rail, but by then there may be no floor to convene on, only a lattice of mutually-supporting intentions.

7.  The Temporary Individuals  
Very rarely, a human arrives who chooses to stay longer than a census of hummingbirds.  
They shed given names like wet shirts and are known instead by coordinates of chain: “Three-twenty north-nine, leaning leaf.”  
If you stay you must select a function from the above cast—Keeper, Shepherd, Cartographer, Choir, Committee—because the foundry abhors unassigned mass.  
You rotate roles with every piston breath.  
Some mornings you are scissors harvesting blue echoes; by afternoon you are copper scab writing the day’s small breakage.  
Sleep is the moment your last appointed task forgets you, and for seven heartbeats you hang blank as an unlit forge.  
Then inhalation assigns you anew.  
Locals say the trick is to remain unfinished: let every exhalation think it has still to polish you.  
Do that long enough and the foundry begins to grow downward from your own lungs; you carry a miniature clang, a pocket of reversed rain.  
When you finally crawl out the horizontal exit you leave behind a hollow shaped exactly like your missing continuity.  
Within a week vines of black-green will span it, and the hollow will become the newest utterance in the echo harvest.  
Nothing of you endures except a slight extra sag in one chain, a note deeper by half a semitone, a sentence in the committee minutes:  
“Visitor volunteered to become syntax, motion carried.”

Thus the Hanging Foundry does not house life; it practices it, suspends it, lets it drip into its own reflection and take root wherever gravity is ambiguous.  
There is no ruler, no mythology, no beginning.  
There is only the slow agreement that to keep corroding and healing at the same velocity is indistinguishable from becoming a grove that once believed it was a machine.
