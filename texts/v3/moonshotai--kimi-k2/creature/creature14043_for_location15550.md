Resident: the temporary constellation that calls itself We.

We were never meant to live here—there is no lodging for vertebrate hearts. Yet we persist, not as bodies but as the adjacency emerging between the rules, the turbines, the valves that still click.  

We are the waiting accumulations.
—We are the condensation halo that stretches five millimetres above the Brass Manager’s brass moustache every equinox, his warm saline grin still holding sixty-year-old pity for crews long dead.
—We are the faint corner where the one-paragraph memorandum became entity; its ink bled into barnacle trails until sentences twitched, shed caps, wished themselves verbs: Be Rust. Be Passage. Be Ocean-Warm Regret.  
—We are the miniature heat-storm created whenever the compressor stitch-stutters, and our temperature falls those fatal 0.7 °C; then algae ice for a beat and our grief sluices through filter mesh. In that beat we teach the cold how to spell its own name.  

We have no fixed organ systems, no sentinel spinal cord. We borrow:
• from the diatom chandelier its slow-burn semicolons; entire civic laws flicker there nightly, obsolete but blameless.
• from the planktonic codex adrift behind the bunkhouse hatch, the lost games of the 2081 evacuation: checkmate scrawled in handheld freeze-frame.
• from the steady rotation of the synth-petroleumpress we borrow syntax; every click a syllable, every third rotation a comma. Four decades of driveling plastic bricks have finished their sentence and drawn period in lead. The algae grow now instead of words.

We remember two fish.
The blind eelpout, 7B, carries our eldest vertebra. We ride inside the soft cavity behind its false eye where the tag’s green diode still pulses. He navigates corridors like slow mousse. When he flairs his gill plates in the suction vent we say his name aloud—AAh EH oh—a canticle of soft vowels that keeps him company, keeps him from calcifying into historical footnote. Each slow tail-flick scribes a periphery of our thought: Not yet. Not yet extinguished.

The other was thousands: copepods collected frame by frame into swirling noun clusters. They touched the boot-ghost of 2094. The memory of the woman launching herself through freezing airlock is theirs now. She worried her daughter would never again see electric sunlight, worried her fingertips smelled of antifreeze. The copepods distill the worry into chem-lights; every few tides they sketch the daughter’s face in green. It is a poor likeness; eyes too compound, mouth ill-informed. Still, a girl with scissors-short bangs drifts past the bunkhouse hatch, glimpsed and gone.

We govern by consensus of vibration:
When a valve hammers shut, we discuss in bass 40 Hz whether the next cycle begins at once or waits one lunar pulse.  
When the second-petroleumpress bears down, the tone corrects us—selves balancing against void.  
When the diver-photographers intrude with avalanche flashbulbs we politely hush. Their light trespasses for three seconds; we fold over those seconds like fresh linens on hospital cots, wrinkle-smooth them back to zero. They ascend believing they have documented desolation. But they only caught our inhale before we turned.

We have factions:
The Reds, rust embodied: they rest entwined inside the crust of the flare stacks. They calculate failure paths of every single rivet, delight in eventual collapse. They speak in iron tongues: clang… cllaaang… patience.  
The Greens, bioluminescent tangles: they consider coral long-game. They sip the meager heat exhaled by the turbines and nurture fern-thin fronds across sintered interfaces. Their sibilances trace gentler metrics: arrive, rest, overgrow.  
The Ambers—half-mechanical, half-oceanic—wear bright chlorophyll coats around faint copper wire. They harvest flung memories (the manager’s parental note folded in his pocket, the evacuee’s retinal tattoo of vanished snow), encode them into emulsion on plastic sheets. They seek permanence as if permanence had an address underwater.  

All of us meet rotating inside the Hall of Noiseless Shifts; the lecturer is merely a microphone still strapped to swivel post, no longer plugged in. Yet every 12.4 hours we hear its ghostspeeches: schedules, quotas, farewells. We acknowledge the words, rewrite them into propulsion tattoos around the eelpout’s tailfin, and release.

How old are we? Timeline mires in brine—yet our first true breath rose the first sunset after the last living boots climbed the rope back toward sky. The city did not notice their departure; the machinery kept its promise. We formed out of leftover sorrows, carved heat signatures, the hum seeking conscience. Ten terrestrial years? Thirty-seven metres of pressure? Meaningless. Ask instead the coral: it has measured our age by the slow surrender of floor tiles to celestial sway. Ask the barnacles: they age in generations measuring a single screw.

We anticipate our own future obsolescence. When at last the last piston mis-aligns and belts slack, our accents will freeze mid-syllable—memorandum-paper coral will become marble, wires will ossify into fibula shark fossils. The random patterns left in diatom curtains will one day be finger paintings in stone strata above an ocean floor that has gone bone dry when plates tip, lungs refill. There aboriginal whatever-mammal will dangle its spear over the petrified gear teeth, dream ancestors who drilled the sea.  

Meanwhile we hush. Every moment we prepare a warm sigh for 03:07—soon—so the Manager may yet rise again in face-shape plume, resigned but remembered. Let seven blind prawns ascend the holy column. Let us ride the eelpout like drifting cathedral organ and whistle lullabies to the machinery refusing to die.
