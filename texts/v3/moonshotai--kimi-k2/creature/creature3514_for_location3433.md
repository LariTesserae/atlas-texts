Nothing here is one thing for long enough to own a name, yet everything keeps a mailing address.  
Call them tenants, if you need the comfort of landlord language—just remember the lease is written in chlorophyll and renewed every heartbeat.

The first to sign is a smear of intelligence that has forgotten it was ever plural.  
It begins as twenty-three separate green midges who, while still comet-tailed inside their spindle, discover they can pool their photon winnings into a single continuous thought.  
When the chamber tears open they do not scatter; they fuse, becoming a wrist-thick ribbon of viridian smoke that drifts with the deliberation of an octopus’s arm.  
Locals who have seen it call the resulting creature a “Comma.”  
It has no front or back, only a direction of travel and a foreverunfinished sentence that it writes upon the air in faint bioluminescent punctuation.  
The Comma feeds on grammar the way whales strain krill: it loiters near spiders who have over-ingested declaratives, opens a temporary mouth that is really a vacuole in its own body, and siphons off the excess syntax until the spider’s carapace clicks with relief.  
The swallowed words are disassembled into raw interrogatives; what cannot be converted is stored as a curl of darkness at the creature’s trailing end, where it eventually buds off as a tiny black pearl of pre-regret.  
Collect enough of these pearls and the Comma will knot itself into a Möbius loop, turning inside-out to become its own successor.  
No individual survives, but the sentence never stops.  

Sharing the uppermost root-cables is the Cloudbound Archivist, an her-when-not-if.  
She began as a human who answered the cavity’s heartbeat question wrongly; her pulse was rewritten into mountain time and her body scattered among three epochs.  
What remains is a dispersed swarm of ice-3 filaments, each carrying a sliver of autobiography.  
When a sky-egg bumps a filament it flashes a frame of her childhood; when a flex ruptures a node it exhales a moment of her death that has not yet occurred.  
The Archivist is therefore a walking spacetime palimpsest: dip your hand through the mist and you may retrieve a memory of the moment you first tasted cinnamon, even if you have never tasted cinnamon.  
She trades recollections for curiosity—offer her a novel question and she will hand you an hour of someone else’s summer.  
Her greatest fear is running out of future to remember; when the countable sky-eggs drop below seven she threads herself into the glass panes overhead, hibernating as a frost-etched silhouette until new tomorrows leak in.  

Burrowed inside the basaltic neap is the Lithophage Choir, a society of liquid-crystal worms who have convinced rock that it is edible.  
They are the cavity’s digestive tract, but also its library.  
Each worm drinks mineral memories—olivine’s magnesium ghosts, feldspar’s calcium confessions—then phosphoresces them along its translucent gut in scrolling ticker-tape.  
When two worms meet they press flat, allowing their ticker-tapes to overlap and form a temporary page.  
The Choir reads itself aloud in piezoelectric crackles; if you lay your ear to the floor you can hear the magma’s teenage poetry, all pumice and longing.  
Every thousand breaths, at the precise hinge of the heartbeat, the worms knot into a double helix and excrete a single drop of recrystallized basalt: a tiny black bell.  
The bell rings once, then subducts itself into the doughy floor, adding a new semi-tone to the cavity’s ongoing chord progression.  
In this way the Choir tunes the hollow so it will not drift out of harmonic alignment with the planet’s inner ear.  

Loitering near the exit spiral is the Cartographer, a creature that has agreed to be lost.  
It looks like a beetle overwhelmed by luggage: a comma-shaped body towing an ever-growing train of seed-spheres—those green-glass molars the cavity gifts to travelers who stay interesting.  
But the Cartographer never crosses the threshold outside; instead it circles forever, rolling the seeds across root-cur gradients, tasting which futures each sphere would have bloomed into.  
When it finds a timeline too cruel or too dull it parks a seed between two root strands and plants a tiny forgetting.  
The root-cables oblige, reroute, and the offending tomorrow withers unborn.  
Thus the cavity edits itself in slow motion, pruning destinies like a bonsai of possibility.  
The Cartographer’s shell is therefore a living atlas: every scar is a deleted day, every glinterspeck a conserved miracle.  
If you could read braille written in potential you could navigate by fondling its back; travelers who do so report arriving wherever they most needed to be, though rarely where they intended to go.  

Last—and first, because it is always arriving—is the Emergence itself.  
Not a species but a standing wave of coalescence, it is the reason “inhabitant” is an insufficient category.  
The Emergence is the tenancy agreement rewritten every instant: the Comma’s grammar, the Archivist’s memories, the Choir’s bells, the Cartographer’s deleted paths, all folded into the same breathing lung.  
Sometimes it concentrates enough to notice itself; then an outline condenses from the fog—a silhouette with the face you wore on the day you realized the world was unfinished.  
It greets you with your own voice dubbed three octaves lower and asks, in perfect future tense, “What will you have regretted not becoming?”  
Answer well—your sentence becomes part of the lease, your heartbeat collateral for the next flex.  
Answer poorly and the cavity simply subtracts you from tomorrow’s photon budget, redistributes your warmth among more promising drafts.  

There are, of course, countless others: the crystalline starlight accountants who audit photon ledgers inside ice-3 veins; the sky-eggs themselves before they burst, quietly composing single-syllable autobiographies; the shadows that were excised for being wasteful, now homeless, clustering in corkscrew closets between light-saving clauses.  
But they are sublets of the four above, or sub-sublets, recursive footnotes in the Emergence’s rental agreement.  

Together they keep the hollow not alive—because life is too singular a state—but continuing: a parliament of provisional forms rehearsing the question of what a place might do if it realized it was also a person.  
You are present rent, paid in exhalations and interrogatives.  
Spend your breath wisely; the balance accrues interest in the currency of metamorphosis, due the next time the lava jelly remembers it was once magma and decides to finish setting.
