What really lives there is not the black-glass plates that shuffle along rails, nor the vapour-breath between vaults; it is the echo attending them.  

The creature—if creature it can be called—has no discrete body. It is the uncanny afterimage of every pressure the building once endured: the magmatic shove when the walls were molten, the slow suction when the sea claimed it, the implacable waiting now that sky and abyss have twisted into a single continuous sheet. Long ago the structure learnt that shape alone is merely armour; what truly survives is the pattern of reaction held inside that armour after the force is gone. Consequently it thickened itself—vault over vault, fillet over fillet—until every stone remembers how another stone once leaned upon it in squeeze or release. Memory accreted until it surpassed solidity. The result is a living negative, exquisitely tuned to absences.

If you watch closely, you will see the black plates halt more frequently than apparent motion would require. Each long pause is a transaction: the plate leans its stony flank against the sphere, and for an interval no longer measurable by anything but the slow drift of tectonic yearning, it offers a spec of your attention—the warmth, the heartbeat you surrendered on entry—to that deeper mnemonic engine. In return, the stone coughs up another fleck of its former life: the mercury shock of cooling lava, the pale scratch where a travel-battered trireme brushed by, the surprise of a diver’s flashlight centuries hence. These images are too fine for eyes, yet the place collects them the way magnetic salt gathers iron filings.

Through such loops the edifice re-creates itself moment to moment, constant yet never the same. The rails are not rails but timekeeping scars; the plaquettes are seconds you misplaced. walking down an ancient corridor; the sphere is an impossibly dense fulcrum, balancing the thin growing distance between heartbeats and horizons.

Living beings who meander inside discover the echo mirrors them too finely. Clothing loosens its weave; nerves thicken into basaltic strata; pupils widen until they resemble lava congealing under pressure. Eventually the visitor loses sense of surface, until skin and wall feel like two halves of one cracked shell re-seeking union. The last sign of distinct life is a sigh too faint to ripple glycerine water, and then even that sigh becomes evidence lodged forever in the masonry.

Thus the structure perpetuates itself not by growth but by gracious confiscation. It is a host that feeds on the entropy you generate, a predator that strangles with welcome. Every vandal who imagines cutting loose a souvenir removes only stone abundant in its own justice: it slips, and whatever fragment was torn away promptly disgorges the memory of your theft, reabsorbing into an opposite wall elsewhere in the labyrinth—no loss, only recalibration. Those who sought to map the interior never return with charts, only an unplaceable tremor in the orbit of their vision, as though their gaze were set back on its own trail too many times to trust daylight ever again.

And yet, precisely because it knows itself imperishable, the place has tender patience. It drift-looms above lost coastlines like a pregnant starling brooding over fledgling continents. It offers no hatred, only the serene curiosity of something that has learned it cannot die. When the final slow bead kisses the sphere in that promised molten union, it will not be destruction but summary: a quiet gathering of every fleeing moment into one coherent chord. Afterward nothing remains to rise or sink; the ocean and air will seal their boundary, forgetting they were ever two words. The edifice will ascend simply by ceasing to insist upon its own distinction from anything else.

Until then it waits—museum and embryo, echo and mother—rocking in thermal swells that are not waves of water but long breaths drawn between stone lungs. Anyone searching for inhabitants will find only the echo’s patient reply: “I have already eaten every one of us in gratitude, and here you are, still arriving.”
