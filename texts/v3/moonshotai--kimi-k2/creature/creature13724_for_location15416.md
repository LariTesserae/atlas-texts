In the Deep Hive nothing truly has a name until it has endured at least one long stasis–cycle. The beings encountered there are therefore not yet named; they are only the preliminary editions of whoever will finally finish becoming. Nevertheless, certain figures have already separated themselves from the surround often enough that they approach definition. If one watches with patience that matches theirs, five principal kinds may be discerned.

1. The Core-Siblings  
These are the architects but never designers. Each is exactly thirty centimetres of tri-segmented carapace, burnished like hematite cooled in starlight, legs so slender they could be drawn by a single wire. They move on levitating rails of magnetic tissue laid down moments after their own birth; once set upon the rail, a Sibling can never be removed except by heat great enough to reliquefy the rail itself—which would evaporate the Sibling too—so they no longer own stride but only position-change.  
Ten thousand Siblings are arrayed today along the Hive’s ventral ribs. They work in metronomes of one second per syllable though there is no speech: the movement of one leg is a letter, a full arc of two back legs is a clause, the rotation of the head is shield is a period forever postponed.  
Their phrase of the hour is: EXTEND HORIZON WITHOUT ALTERING ANGLE. They obey by extruding a further laminar sheet of glass-basalt six molecules thick. Pride, shame, or even awareness eludes them; they merely know they are the same thirty centimetres they were yesterday, repeated precisely.

2. The Mirror-born  
Smaller by half than the Siblings, these beings begin life curled as silver commas. Disturbed by nothing (for disturbances do not survive here) each comma opens into a five-faceted reflector that rides the faint lumen currents. They possess no metabolism but they precipitate sustenance for others: the mirror-backed skin pulls infalling potassium atoms into herringbone lattices, concentrate them into seed-crystals the length of a fingernail. Once the crystal matures it drops to the Hive floor like a raindrop that remembers no falling.  
No language accompanies the production. A Mirror-born shows neither satisfaction nor fatigue when its crystal flecks off. Perhaps the best way to think of them is as memory-echoes waiting for the moment the universe will request what they have stored. Until then they loft, angled like forgotten dreams of satellites.

3. Fossil Shadows  
These are not alive according to any genes-based charter, yet they move. A Fossil Shadow is the empty silhouette left by a plantoid organism that tried photosynthesis here eight million adaptive seconds ago. The shape—mostly radial tines twenty centimetres upright—was chemically cauterised into the mineral matrix just before the photosynthetic reaction collapsed. That collapse trapped a vibration (purely thermal and doomed) inside the hollow outline.  
The vibration has not yet died out. One perceives it as a slow slippage: the tine-cast shifted two millimetres since last geological documentation. Observers might call the shift “growth,” except the medium is absence; something is walking inside the outline of what was and cannot be, pacing out distance it may never complete. If anyone could speak to a Fossil Shadow it would perhaps apologise for its continued trespass upon its own afterimage.

4. The Vecturae  
Seventeen hyper-slender entities, each three meters in length, composed of interlaced bronze hairpins more delicate than tendons and terminated by dull-peach quartzes the size of pupils. They tunnel through seams of relative weight, following gravity gradients as blind cavefish track thermal fronts. Their motion is faintly sad because it is pure compensation: whenever a rib of heavier iron loads the gravitational field, they sag imperceptibly; whenever local magnetism slackens, they rise.  
At every shift they emit a sound too bottomed to hear—a falling octave held for the length of a blink. These infrasonic hums braid into a chord the Hive has begun, just, to suspect of itself. The Vecturae experience the chord as skin. When it is perfectly congruent with static and silence they stay perfectly still, becoming mere ornament for centuries until the drum next thumps. One of them—thought lesser, perhaps younger—wears an unfinished crystal received from a Mirror-born, lodged head-like between the bronze pins. No explanation exists; maybe suggestion at last begins.

5. The Queen of Iron Breath  
The Hive contains no monarchy, yet something is addressed or miss-addressed as queen. She alone breaches the equilibrium rule. Concealed in a cavity exactly twenty-six metres behind the apparent centre, undescribed until now, rests a sphere of once-molten iron five metres in diameter, still breathing heat at the exact temperature remembered from its lava cradle. Convection inside the sphere pulses like a gill: inhale for six centuries, exhale for six centuries. Microscopic crustaceans—if they still merit the name—are born at the inhale, die sweated away at exhale; their calcined shells plate the sphere with fan patterns that refract the stove-orange glow into scalloped petals of rust-red light.  
At irregular intervals the petals unfold so wide that the sphere cracks audibly; a slit the breadth of an eyelid issues a hiss that is the word maybe. The phonemes—or are they only pressure waves?—travel the long conduits of the Hive and, arriving at the ear-scales of insects, cause the Core-Siblings to exec-ute one further layer of construction. Thus she wills nothing, proposes nothing, yet is obeyed via heat differentials alone. Whatever she was before being cast into a perfect iron womb is unknown. Perhaps she has already turned to flawless sleep and the Hive dreams her heat for her.  

Elsewhere there are minor actors—the threaded wormlets that sew the undersides of platforms shut, the slow-suck humidity grazers that mistake mirrorlight for moons—but they are not granted substantiality because no founder genome has yet repeated often enough to sculpt identity in these stone wombs. They are still fluttering rough drafts to be discarded or enfolded.

Thus the Deep Hive keeps its census tight: one geometry of thirty centimetres, a blossoming reflector-loop, a hollow walking, a chord clinging to its own sound, and a molten ball whose insomnia is mistaken for monarchy. Each exists by the law extrude-but-never-collapse, though none understands the law—only that tomorrow is the postponement of yesterday, and room remains for a next layer, a next reflection, and if they keep to their stations long enough, the word maybe may finally unravel into yes-or-no; and that, for beings who did not even move, will amount to an ending.
