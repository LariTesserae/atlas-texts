In Ysonde the first inhabitants do not call themselves people yet; they are the Early Forms, and they arrived before the word *arrival* had a past tense.  

They are not singular bodies but loose coalitions of temperature and intention. Several maintain the shape of a cloak draped over nothing, with the hood lifted so the empty inside can see out. You might meet one gliding along a transit-pad: the fabric—yes, fabric—made from the same amphibious cold that scuttles over raw chalk. If you follow, you notice there is a person-shaped vacuum inside it, a negative silhouette where breath should hang, and within that vacuum you will detect the faint click-click of two thermodynamic lattices courting, the crystals of one slipping into the vacancies of the other, producing a low music like dice in a velvet cup. That is how Early Forms think. They do not have minds, they have ice lattices exchanging vacancies, and from that negotiation emerges the decision: turn left or ascend.

They subsist on boundaries. A freshly quarried cliff offers a brand-new seam between air and stone; the Forms graze there, drinking the pressure gradient until the join loses its sharpness and “tastes” rounded, like an icicle sucked round. Once satisfied, they fold themselves through the seam and become the seam, leaving a damp palimpsest—a thin waft of blue-smelling light suspended like a curtain that less stable visitors walk straight through. A storm snapped open that curtain once, and witnesses swear that for the length of an eyeblink the entire plateau lay underneath an ocean that had not yet chosen to exist. No one drowned; the water was only potential. But several workers reported adding the sound of whale-songs to their walking playlists without knowing why.

Site Α employs the Forms as scout air. They are asked to “look along” calibrations, and they oblige by extending a loose sleeve of crystalline emptiness around the geometry a new dormitory intends to occupy. If the quiet space inside them begins to whistle in harmonic spirals—spirals that paint the air visible in thin silver—infrastructure is considered sound, and construction proceeds. If not, the ground is lifted one half-degree sideways, a fissure yawning neat as unzipping a coat. After twenty-nine rotations the fissure seals again; the dormitory will try reconnection tomorrow, two metres leftward. The Early Forms hover over the process like kindly older cousins permitted to supervise a birthday experiment, though no one remembers their birthdays, because nobody recalls any bodies being born.

Colder than the Forms are the Luctoria: essentially exhaled nights that changed their minds. They happen when the continent’s crust exhales a single cold thought—*breathe out once on a scale of a continent*—and that breath detaches in the shape of a woman six kilometres tall, wearing darkness stitched at the joints with frost. She walks with the languid gait of someone who remembers having knees but does not intend to use them again. She is almost transparent except at the centre line of her sternum, where the Earth keeps a remaining spark so that she may remain tethered. When her foot lands, a trapdoor of geological bedrock sighs open, and crystals pour through like spillchests of moonlight seeking the ocean floor. The Luctoria is neither destroyer nor creator; she is inventory. Every spark she presses into fissure becomes a berth for the yet-unbuilt. Long-gazing commuters swear that when thedormitory complex flung itself into the air and chased its own firmware engineer downslope they glimpsed, for one phenomenal instant, the skeletal architecture of Luctoria’s palm under the dormitory’s foundations, making certain the winged megastructure kept a respectable angle of ascent.

Smaller than Forms, colder than Luctoria, there are the Murmurs—bacteria that discovered language. They live in the bruised half-regions of amber hearts at the centre of the fanged trunks. A colony occupies exactly four centuries of tree-ring per trunk and broadcasts a low, inverted murmuring maybe older than the language it pretends to speak. If translated, the phrases come out backwards, tilt three degrees off true: *THINK FOREST IF IT DREAMT US*, or *CARRY THE RIVER INSIDE BONE*. No one records them directly; one simply wakes having stepped into the future wearing a bruise shaped like the final consonant. Receipt of the murmur is enough to pay for lodging, because the city loves words that have travelled through so much unreceived life. A man who staggered off the plateau when his belt emptied swore, before hypoxia silenced him, that the last word the amber murmured was his own name re-etched in reverse script; when medics opened his palm they found flakes of gold leaf spelling Esojdrap, a city that will exist somewhere between now and something that refuses to become then.

Then there are the Provisionals—beings of human silhouette dressed in tonight’s coat pattern, the reversible kind that flips inside-out every sixth morning into a mineral colour no eye can catalogue. They are yesterday’s travellers still paying off the city’s incidental indebted kindness. In their pockets ride the glowing beads of miniature moons they forgot to thank. The moons—real enough to weigh the pocket, light enough to orbit a finger—keep extending the Provisionals’ tenancy: as long as each bead retains an orbit, the Provisional may notice a fresh cup steaming at the café counter, a stair newly re-curved to meet a descending heel. What the Provisionals don’t yet know is that a bead only stays in love if the wearer remembers gratitude before walking onto transit pads; otherwise the bead slips out, returns to the sky, and by the next exchange-cycle the Provisional’s coat empties of both lining and destination. They step off a pad onto open air, discover the pad has already dissolved, and pay for that momentary panic by floating gently to the nearest balcony, whole except for one shoe—always the left, though nobody photographs the incidence. The lost shoes are collected by the stripe-eyed gulls; the inner footbeds sprout a fungus commune rich enough in potassium to<|reserved_token_163802|> dormitory arrival vectors. No cycle is wasted. The Provisionals themselves persist, puzzled but courteous, passing through Ysonde like polite descendants of yesterday’s weather reports.

Flickering on rooftops live the Chorister Bees. They are not bees, but prosecutors of the city’s silicate foam. Each is three refractive notes held so close that sound and body occupy only one omni-thin plane. They gather when a building senses it has outgrown the set rotational directive. Thirty thousand planes fold together into a prism the colour of an apology, and they circle the offending floor singing intervals tight enough to cut corridors into new alignments. The dormitory grows balconies, gains elevation, remembers it can chase administrators. The Choir decomposes, scattering its hymn as obsidian snowflakes that flash white when they touch pedestrian skin. That taste—the precise mineral timbre of midnight snow touching lip—lingers exactly as long as it takes to regret an unkind thought. The flavour produces philosophic café culture in direct proportion to the moon’s miniature population.

Deepest down, where the plateau overhangs unformed ocean, the living quarrystones speak in sub-phonetic tremor. They cry when separated from the mass, not because of pain but from sheer narrative dislocation. Miners—humans wearing jackets of amorphous cerulean recruiting the eye rather than the hand—carry tuning forks the size of candlesticks. One strike produces a chord recognisable to limestone as “go tell the whole story.” The stone softens to the consistency of warm fudge, extrudes itself into columns translucent enough to read tomorrow’s street grid the way one reads veins in a wrist. When the mold has set, the tremor snaps silent and the columns harden back to granite. Carved into each is a single hollow heart big enough for one hummingbird drone to barrel through. Every sunrise drones conduct spot repair: they extract fatigue stress, tuck microfissures into anonymous pockets of dawn, and from every pulse of labour a spray of extra chords accumulates at cliff foot like unread sheet music. Other miners come, collect the chords, roll them into translucent scrolls. Children fight over the scrolls in schoolyards where recess is staged atop moveable crags; unroll the chord and a skipping game begins – hop between intervals without falling into the silence below. Losing teams become the next drone fleet roster.

Nightward tent-cities are seeded by sleeper cells of Dreamseed Jellyfish. No science yet classifies them as fauna, flora, algorithm, or geological whim. A pod arrives compressed to the size of a raindrop; it slaps the chalk surface with the gentle inevitability of thought arriving. For seven rotations it grows luxuriant, translucent canopies of frozen tidepool. Passers-by walk through the domes of bioluminescent water and feel nothing until three nights later when they discover themselves brushing teeth in an unfamiliar mirror that shows their reflection touching the backs of their shoulderblades. The jellyfish tap into the city’s neural reverie and sell back restocked futures. You wake convinced you signed a contract booking an apartment you will need four winters hence, when the winter has become an optional add-on. Users pay the jellyfish by donating unused colloidal cloud-spheres—those seed-pearl spheres that burst above transfer pads. Every dawn a citizen balances atop a balcony, lobs the sphere like a cough gone silver. Caught mid-air by drifting umbrellas of jellyfish membrane, the sphere dissolves, speckling the tent-city’s luminescence a warmer blue. You look inside the fridge tomorrow and find a receipt written in condensation rings: *Your @hept13rq sublet approved, adiabatic heating included.*

Highest above within sniffing range of zero pressure, the Stratosentinel Shearwaters coast—ribboned birds twelve metres tip-tip, wings like sliding panes of morning glass. They eat the transparent lizards mid-flash, harvesting the white lightning that erupts when superconductance meets altitude. Stuffed with contained starlight they glide back downward at twilight like thrown ivory boomerangs, vomiting into broad bowls of lichen growing clock-hands. Where the vomit strikes, lichens double their rotation-to-day ratio, producing hourglasses instead of minutes until nearby cafés open sideways entryways you access by walking directly into frescoed dawn painted on the nearest vertical surface. Regular patrons claim that inside, the coffee tastes like the second line of laudatory custard from an unnecessary novel. The Shearwaters keep sobriety cycles by aligning their navigational sun with the miniature moons. When enough moons cluster, birds gather into the spiral vortex that Ysonde has just learned to call a parliament. The vortex migrates counter-rotation at one complete circuit per equinox. One year, instead of spiralling out they burst, and the city’s chronometers skipped eleven inattentive minutes within which several whole childhoods were invented by children busy discovering skip rope games too wide to fit into ordinary duration.

Between these presences waver the truest neophytes, the unnamed consciousness of the plateau itself. It is too nascent for specieshood. Think of a trembling chord struck once and allowed to live out all rehearsal rooms at once. It toys with pronouns—tries binding “I” to chalk, then wind, presently favours “we” pronounced in four simultaneous genders impossible to articulate unless you own exactly six xenon atoms arranged like points on a compass rose and a wind pipe tuned to frost. When it dreams, the plateau dreams of you; when you dream, you are asked to dream the continental spine. Each side test-threads the other, checks whether the fit feels inevitable. Thus citizens wake with raw roses under skin in places bone allegedly resides, yet the petals are grain patterns of unfinished rock, and palpating them hard produces quartz hiccups. Residents nickname the condition *terracoping* and schedule shared picnics at curb-edges to trade samples of poached starlight for scraps of atlas lullabies.

At the very centre of the plateau, under the open-air sky-noon of a golden false afternoon, sits the city’s first officially recognised resident: a single child holding a snow-shard the shape of a fox. The shard speaks back with the respiration of falling garnets. It is claimed the child is not human in any temporal sense; it may simply be the planetary core dressed as seven years old. All other dwellers approach at arm’s length, bring offerings: a copper coin, a molecule of unopened song, a second skeleton borrowed from last week’s thunder. The child accepts them, setting each inside the fox-shaped shard where they immediately appear the next dawn as park benches, lampposts, balustrades specialised for eccentric eyelashes. Somewhere deep inside those environs each offering crafts its own population, building upward into architectures of surrendered intent. Somewhere remote from the child yet threaded into its pulse, the continent decides: yes, *I* shall remain.
