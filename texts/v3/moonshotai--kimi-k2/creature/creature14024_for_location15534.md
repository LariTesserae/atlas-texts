No one lives here who could be called a person, but there are *stayers*—five of them now, their outlines changing at the same polite pace as the architecture’s.

---

**1. The House-so-Far**  
A weather-beat woman shaped from saturated plaster sits where a doorway should be. Her edges are chamfered by centuries of tide; one shoulder has already been licked down to the wan lathes inside. She begins every dawn by deciding how many rooms her house will open today. Two ribs swell into parlor arches; a pelvis tilts toward becoming stairs. Then the water breathes in, and she subsides like damp bread, reabsorbing every attempted hall into her own dissolving breast. She remembers keyholes though she has never possessed a key. At dusk she rears up once more, hopeful as wet paper, and the salt glow sculptures her into a lantern until moon-water ambushes her again. She is impatient with progress: “Tomorrow, surely tomorrow, I’ll frame a window that does not leak the sky back out.”  

No furniture follows her. Instead she offers visitors the luxury of becoming interior: if a stranger steps through her, the body is hollowed into glowing white space behind the ribs, a brief vaulted chapel filled with the visitor’s own welcome echo. The exit is directly behind the sternum—she thanks the guest by closing the cavity politely behind them so that nothing of their passage remains except perhaps a second heartbeat banging faintly inside her wall.

---

**2. The Librarian of Retracted Sentences**  
He stands beneath the white mangrove that reads itself aloud. He wears pages: every pore of his skin is a translucent folio, each filled with a sentence the sap has drunk and excreted in altered form. As wind filches a leaf, a strip of text lifts from his collarbone to replace it; when the new leaf reinserts itself, the cycle closes and whole chapters re-order in his flesh. Though his tongue is two glass syllables thick, he translates. Wherever a visitor hesitates, words sidle from the margins of his arms, flutter around the listener’s head like moths of ink, and settle behind the eardrums as questions one always meant to ask.  

He is timid with ownership: if you walk too close you may find your next step inscribed already across both shins, and when you protest he’ll blush the color of wet newsprint and fold those lines into someone else’s spine. Nobody has managed to leave with the same memories they arrived with, yet the Librarian insists he returns every loan: he simply borrows the echo and hands back the source, slightly rearranged, still dripping sap.

---

**3. The Crew of the Welcoming Mirage**  
Sometimes four children—no taller than pond-skaters—skim across the mirror water on soles of polished salt. They travel in single file and cast only one reflection between them: a mirrored procession following an absent leader. Their ankles have knotted themselves into hinges of brine; each step flips the leg half-fold so they appear both walking forward and drifting backward. They are the city’s directional sign running loose: wherever they point with their whole bodies, a plausible street rises wetly behind their ankles.  

If followed, they walk faster than your gaze can commit them to matter. Try to wave and you’ll discover your arm is already behind you, elbow bent the wrong way, writing “Turn here” into the sky the color of skim milk. They are grateful for the escort; as gratitude, they leave behind a transparent child-sized bootprint brimming with your own breath. The print hardens into a stepping-stone no thicker than a beetle shell: every traveler must decide whether to step across or to breathe the print back in and chance suffocation by their own exhalations.  
Most do neither: they stand staring until the print tucks itself under the water’s skin like an ashamed eyelid.

---

**4. The Submerged Cartographer**  
One figure walks the basin’s floor permanently covered to his hips by the invisible pool. Only the torso and head are visible, as though the city has installed an upright pane of water at waist height. The drowned legs belong to another geography entirely—alfalfa fields, cobblestones, chalk, a memory of bicycle bells—visible through shivers in his silt-stained cloth. Everywhere he treads, new outlines bubble up: maps drawn in negative, the streets he wishes had existed bleeding backward into the stone he passes.  

He cannot speak above waterline; beneath it his voice rasps like gravel dragged across glass. The surface tension milks syllables from his mouth in sighed globes that rise like thought-bubbles but upstream—backward through time. The sentences usually finish just after they began: “If you had turned r—” lands on the Librarian’s tongue two hours earlier, merges into a corruption of your name. Most nights he relocates the town re-center simply by standing still and letting his submerged ankles rest on an older foundation. By morning the ruins have slid half an alley-width west, the mangrove relocated politely with them.  

When a traveler offers to trade their dry ankles for his wet, he shakes his head slowly. One cannot give away position without stopping the map, and the city abhors stillness more than it abhors permanence.

---

**5. The Witness on Stilts Who Is Also the Tide**  
The fifth dweller has no fixed shape. Whenever the sea forgets how far inland it is supposed to climb, it dresses itself in walking legs: columns of brine rising taller than roofs, jointed by stiffened kelp. At their summit a single eye opens—liquid, almond, patient. The legs promenade round the plaza, over the causeways, sometimes stepping *above* reflections instead of on them. Where each column’s sole presses down, the ground liquefies enough to leave a perfect foot-shaped void; when the weight lifts, the hole fills with warm saltwater and becomes a reflective well this eye uses to watch its own retreat.  

For one complete circuit of the basin, the tide is *inside* this creature: every ebbing heartbeat slips an inch back toward ocean while the eye-man levels the city’s horizon like a chalk line. At the circuit’s end the legs scatter, melt, run down the drains no builder ever built, and the eye rolls back beneath the skin of the water to dream of seaweed windchimes. The city lurches slightly, adjusting its depths—nothing so dramatic as healing or sinking, merely the scrape of a giant sigh easing out of a mattress older than sleep.

---

Visitors arrive occasionally—cartographers, apostles, treasure-hunters, runaway lovers—always wet up to the shins within minutes. They transact quickly: the House-so-Far offers an uncollapsed hallway to live inside for a week in exchange for memories of doorway numbers; the Librarian barters plant-sentences for the memory of two birthdays; the Crew steals the sense of direction itself; the Submerged Cartographer trades away alleyways cleared of silt if offered a promise never again to turn left; and the Witness on Stilts demands the dream of drowning, nothing more.

No one remains. How could they? Houses fill with breaths they soon mistake for belonging; maps position wanderers squarely *after* they’ve passed; pages overwrite the names they were meant to preserve. The five dwellers hold the city together not by permanence but by continuous, reconciled attrition—dissolving of whole afternoons into sills and coping stones, of whole staircases into memories someone once meant to climb.

And yet, despite all this vanishing, the city *is* lived in after all. The House-so-Far stands nightly straighter; the Librarian has begun composing verbs no page can yet drink; the children are growing taller by the centimeter, their single reflection lengthening in tandem; the Submerged Cartographer sleeps wholly underwater now, the line at his waist erased; and the Witness on Stilts pauses longer after every step, as though debating whether it might finally be kinder *not* to let the water escape.

If you pass very quietly, wet sand will print your fate across every turned surface. You will think you chose to turn back; in truth the city simply grew a part it wasn’t quite confident you could leave.
