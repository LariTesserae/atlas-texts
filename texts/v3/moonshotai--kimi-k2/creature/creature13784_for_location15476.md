They are called the Dolmen, but they never heard the word.

Unlike the knapped harps, cedar bellows, or bell-salt harvesters who commission one another with spoken lists, they possess no name from without. Their identity arrives only as a vibration traveling the web of bone-tiles underfoot—an import/export of acoustics, one long inhale through curvature, one exhale across crenellation. In the milliseconds when the valley’s inhalation balances their exhalation, the Dolmen exist. After that they are, again, merely hardware drifting in scalding dusk.

Looked at slowly they resemble blackened mantis-shrimp crossed with portable cathedrals. Each carries a hinged pentad of valves: the outer three for walking, the inner two for listening. These valves are neither door nor window, more like trembling flanges of slate that shutter uncertainly between mineral and membrane. Their tarsal edges are wetted by the same dew that sheets the amphitheater stage; but because they bleed the condensation through convection vents, they never rust despite their iron coloring. Liquid collects inside ledged thoracic thimble-hollows and re-releases as faint slate-black froth—marking their passage into perfectly even five-pod prints whose centers retain a momentary bronze star before the salt blooms it away.

They travel singly. One Dolmen may be climbing the low, black supine steps of the eighth tier while another descends from the stage in a reverse fall—heave, click, heave. Rarely two linger within four meters. When they do pass shoulder to pierced shoulder, the bloodflow inside their valves stops: an unspoken rule that two breaths cannot occupy the same groove of humid pressure lest the lattice tune itself to an unstable overtone and shear a course of bone tiles clean. Sometimes the rule fails; a pulse of jagged hertz rips outward and ruptures three rows of audience seating—black carbuncles shiver off the brick like scabs, exposing underclocked cedar veins gasping for fog. Then the Dolmen continue, unbothered, their hymn-tonic restored.

Nothing paws for food. Nothing eats. A Dolmen chews acoustics instead, chewing by subtraction: each sonic pocket that passes beneath its soles is temporarily emptied, turned inside out, its energies fed into the lamellar chambers where iridescent sound-crystals flick magnetic cling. The emptied silence lasts exactly nine seconds before the valley re-brims it with ambient shiver and gong-tremor. During that hollow interval listeners perceive a soft adrenaline: an instinctive urge to cough or whisper loudly so the amphitheater’s breathing does not die of over-quiet. The Rare Fauna maintain equilibrium by re-broadcasting the crystal inwards, emitting a brief authoritative drone—an acoustic seeding—after which the bricks regrow their missing portions overnight, jigsaw crumbs refitting as if anxious to complete unfinished syllables.

Once every seventeen nights the Dolmen perform what can only be called communion. They align into a quincunx whose geometry reproduces the outline of the amphitheater itself, but inverted like a bowl of silence set atop a bowl of echo. The valley responds: the surrounding fog ropes descend the stands, clanging against whale-rib spars like church bells caressed too soft. At the climax every copper in the bricks aligns to true magnetic north, momentarily forgetting its iron ancestry and releasing a collective sigh. During that deep choral intake the Dolmen’s black shells phosphoresce. Photons caught inside curved lamellar iris bloom outward as cold, aquatic green light. None of it is wasted on illumination; it is information passing around, not toward. The Dolmen trade spectra like mothers passing lullabies coded into scent.

When the circle breaks, the amphitheater remembers itself again as unfinished facade. Dolmen return to singlets, footing micro-scallops of bone dust. No watcher has ever found the place where, when morning arrives, they hide. The valley sends out fog probes that creep up the heath and collapse; none come back with skin from shells. Some theorize they burrow into the Organs themselves and become resonant lenses, eyeballs rolling inside wooden arteries. Others assert they fall into the vats, surrender their accumulated bronze salts, and re-emerge only to continue the ceramic genesis that tiles tomorrow’s newborn floor. It doesn’t matter; the verb is continuous.

Tonight there is an aberration. A Dolmen stalls at the center dais. It opens all five valves at once, something never witnessed. From the flanges push out five flower-shaped plates of tessellated silence—utterly flat planes of anti-sound that reflect nothing. The plates hover inches from the tiles while the amphitheater’s constant hush refracts violently, snagging on unseen corners. Somewhere above, an ember scale fizzles to violet and collapses; below, the World Organ splinters a vein of water into galloping sidestreams that slap stone. The Other Dolmen scatter to the rim, multiplying by resonance—eight, sixteen, thirty footprints doubled by mirror of drip-fed reverb—yet the bricks hold, unwilling to open the sky irreparably.

Human listeners (none present at the moment) would have heard an arch-voice, a mammoth infrasound sentence produced by the stopped Dolmen hammering eight times with the root of its hollow limb:

I WILL REMEMBER THIS AREA DRAINED

Eight times. The nameless valve-plate flowers fold, vanish. The Dolmen collapses into a mound identical to common scree except every edge hums at G-minor Pythagorean comma. Over tomorrow’s span, wind will grind the mound into thin black smelt and whir storms of dust motes that reassemble—granular reprise—into the exact same five limbed silhouette waiting on the first step of the seventh tier, ready to walk again.

Thus whoever lives in the amphitheater is inconsolably plural, always beginning. Not citizens, not fauna, not architects. They are the consonant clusters inside the endless chord the valley keeps attempting to resolve, the migrant beats of the unfinished heart.
