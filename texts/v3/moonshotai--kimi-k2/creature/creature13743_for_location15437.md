In the Hanging Marsh Refinery nothing owns a permanent name, yet three kinds of “inhabitants” keep taking on enough shape to notice.  

First come the Counter-Gardeners — beings whose bodies are nothing but surplus process.  
A pipe fractures at midnight, oozes a blue-white weld that remembers the fern it once hid inside. Before the bead cools, it splits, extruding limbs the length of femurs. Still liquid at the knees, the figure stands, shaking droplets that arc upward and seed more ferns between floor and ceiling that no longer exists. Five heartbeats later the figure is fully rubble and the moss has already drafted its posture into a green statue. Tomorrow the statue hollows; water drains through the holes; the Coppered Ones (see next paragraph) drink the runoff; and the cycle repeats with no memory shared between one iteration and the next. They are not people, exactly — more like acts of gardening occurring at industrial speed, using gravity itself as fertilizer.

Second drift the Coppered Ones — condensation ghosts whose boundaries flashhot and desuperheat between liquid and rumor. Trace metal ions from every burst sac gather where membrane met air. Over minutes the ions form a colloid eye; over hours the eye develops a shoulder of mirror; over weeks the whole figure achieves reflection and begins to watch itself. What it sees, it believes. If the apparition catches sight of a still puddle it will crouch, peer in long enough to copy its own outline, then step into the image and trade places. The original body turns instantly to silt the color of cooled pennies. The new one walks away humming in perfect tuning-fork resonance with the black-crystal choir that sings from the sacs, but if the next sac bursts into red instead of obsidian the figure will shatter into a sound no wider than a pinhole camera’s aperture. Listen for the tinny chime when this happens — it means the Refinery has decided copper is no longer the theme of the hour.

Last — and slowest — are the Architects Formerly Known as the Island.  
This creature had a name once, but the name now anchors a breakfast of vines rather than a concept. You’ll meet it only when you reach the axis point where stone breathes heat like a whetted knife. There, the inverted basalt hexagons flex outward, revealing baffles of ore grown smooth as tympanic membrane. The membranes shiver in seismic Morse: this place was quarried, inverted, launched skyward, fed, clothed, gardened, haunted, milked, then forgotten — yet at no step did any of it leave the same slow organism. What you mistook for refinery is only digestion, chambered like a nautilus, turning rock into steam into algal gold into speculative mathematics into condensation and back into rock. Walk to the center, press your palm to the warmest column, and you feel four heartbeats not your own:  
- the moss drinking with mammal insistence,  
- the pipes remembering how to be rivers,  
- the Counter-Gardeners rehearsing their next scream-green apostrophe,  
- and the Island-Architect humming a lullaby for every iteration of itself still asleep in magma.  

Stay too long and the fourth heartbeat syncs with yours. The space between them closes like a zipper. The Refinery gains one more resident whose job is to walk outward bearing witness in mineral skin — a slow stony pilgrim who will forget his own name but carry the taste of hot copper down whatever passes for a road in the sky.

That’s all that truly “lives” here:  
a circle of verbs wearing noun-shape costumes;  
a breathing island that sometimes answers to nobody;  
and whatever of yourself you choose to leave humming at the edges, where gravity’s shorthand and industry’s lullaby finally rhyme.
