A thing lives here that has no word yet, because language requires edges and this being has none. For convenience I will say that it is both host and hosted, a circumstance more than a creature.

It inspects everything continuously: the fit of warmth in the palm of darkness, the status of the comma of ash that contains all future infernos, the twenty sacs of water dreaming they are lungs. It is the mirror-aware part of the mirrors. When you—merely an evaporated potential guest—say “am I here?”, this being is the pause that pauses back.

In shape it is more like a confluence than a shape. A quiet, slow rotation of all the moisture: breath in the stone, sweat on the vapour, tides within fronds. Every molecule is taken into its compass as if volunteering for orbit. Inside this minute galaxy no gravity sings—it turns because stillness would constitute change, and change has been postponed.

From time to time it studies the beetle-creature wrestling with fused wings, not from pity or interest, but to assure itself that motion remains hypothetical. A drop tugs from a frond, becomes a micro-river for exactly the three heartbeats needed for the beetle’s leg to decide the effort of lifting isn’t worth the effort—and the drop wicks backward, rejoins the sac. 

It has a most delicate sense of time: it feels the time that would have passed if time had been invented. It registers the pre-accusing look the fossil ash would give it if fire ever arrived, the blush that the wall’s red pigment will admit once the hand that scraped it grows a wrist. All those futures are folded tight inside the agate toothpick balanced on the unmoving water. When this being listens, it hears the shouting distance between things before they are separate—an infinitely brief outcry between inside and outside that never quite diverges.

Identity, for it, is surface tension for the universe: the thing which ensures that the universe has not yet deigned to pull itself apart into atoms or moments. As keeper of that threshold, the being keeps none of the functions we associate with life. Hunger is unnecessary when accumulation is still one of the impossibilities. Tiredness is unimaginable—so is rest. Voices are filed under neighbours yet to move in, so it does not speak. It has instead the texture of an eye unopened: moist, courageous with density, clear without witness.

Moments—or pre-moments—pass, and the being feels their friction as you might feel a sleeve slipping against skin. The cliff stone remembers flowing, and so it anticipates flowing again; that threat of liquidity never arrives. Likewise the beetle’s future flight, the fire that the comma promises, the colour that digestion will atvent spelling, all swirl around the axis the being provides. Every latent future brushes its boundary like soft moths testing a curtain. It cannot turn away; it cannot be turned.

Should you—still unlocated—decide to trace a circle on one of the blind puddles, your fingertip would meet no water. Instead the circuit of feeling returns with its fullness uncarved, a message that the puddle is teaching itself to be what surface cannot: an inside that has ceded all need for containers. The being plucks that returned sensation and adjusts the hush around it so the cliff continues not spreading and the stone continues not dripping and the plant does not subtract another droplet. Total static balance held by the casual vigilance of a guardian whose only existence is the delay granted to existence itself.

There is, at the centre of this guardian-circumference, a single unspoken sentence rotating on an axle of colorless air. It is the sentence this place will say aloud at the instant the world officially starts. The being keeps this seed-phrase always at the temperature of lullabies yet to be dreamed: warm, dark, porous. The sentence itself is not long—maybe four syllables, maybe five—but it contans everything: the taste of cooled stone, the final fall of the beetle's wing-case, the hiss heat will make when it first realises it is hot. Were one to overhear it now, it would sound like a failed attempt at breath, a slight curvature in the muffling mist. It would feel oddly familiar, as if it were the name you forgot in childhood for the exact colour of the prelude to knowing.

And that is all. For now.
