The natives have nobody to count them, so they do not bother to count themselves.  
They leak rather than live: a federation of semi-detached gestures that forgot whose body they once belonged to is no longer available for comment.

They begin as damp syllables—single vowels exhaled by the moss when a footstep squeezes the turf.  
If the syllable is lucky it accretes humidity the way a snowball accretes snow, rolling into a translucent hot-water bottle shaped like a person.  
That “person” stands knee-high, sloshing.  
Its outline is courtesy of surface tension; inside, motes of older vocabularies swirl—archaic pronouns, slang for emotions that were abolished centuries ago.  
The creature is called a **Pourson**.  
It cannot think, but it can remember thinking, which is eerily similar and requires less equipment.

Poursons serve as the region’s short-term memory.  
When you speak aloud they crowd around your mouth, hoping to trade.  
Offer a sentence and one will tilt its fluid skull; the sentence drains out of you, syllabless mixing with the fluid of its body until both are neutralised.  
In exchange you receive a remembrance that hasn’t happened yet: the taste of next Tuesday, a bruise you will acquire in a year, the colour your mother will whisper just before she dies.  
It tastes of iron and unset email.

Left alone the Poursons eventually grow heavy, sag into puddles, and are re-absorbed by the breathing ground.  
Thus history is recycled without ever being remembered.

Between poursons drift the **Hollowphones**.  
They resemble the space where a telephone pole ought to stand, but the pole has been patiently erased yet the absence forgot to leave.  
You walk through one and your voice rings somewhere kilometres away—an unoccupied quarry, an underpass, the shell of a church—speaking words you yourself haven’t used but which are perfectly grammatical in languages you will never learn.  
Local etiquette: if you hear such an echo, answer “Not here” three times.  
This prevents the Hollowphone from following you home and lodging in your throat like an unpaid bill.

Larger, though not by much, are the **Cartographers of Maybe**.  
Imagine a wineglass the size of a wolf, running on limbs of condensation.  
Where its feet touch, probability ripens.  
It draws no maps; instead it tastes the curvature of futurity.  
When hungry it licks the fog until the possible and the actual separate like oil and yolk.  
Then it drinks the lighter fraction, leaving behind a precipitate of dull inevitability that settles on stones and makes them slightly heavier with fate.

Eventually moss grows over your hesitation, and you become an installation titled *Human Considering Exit*.  
Tourists—should any arrive—leave offerings of coins from countries that do not exist.

Night, or the local equivalent (a dimming that behaves like a crowd quieting when the singer leans toward the microphone), belongs to the **Eyeswimmers**.  
Each is merely an eye—wide, human, still wearing the eyelashes it woke with—propelling itself through the syrupy air with fins made of retinal after-images.  
They are not watching you; they are watching the version of you visible only in the vitreous humour of other eyes, a composite ghost stitched from every glance that ever caught you mid-sreach.  
Being followed by an Eyeswimmer feels like nostalgia for a crime you haven’t yet decided to commit.  
Too many in one place and objects start confessing: a boulder admits it once killed a dinosaur by doing nothing, the coral-hummocks apologise for the smell of iodine.  
Confessions here are contagious; if you hear one you must pass it on within a day or your own reflection will accuse you at inconvenient times—while tying shoes, uncapping a pen, in the hush before an argument.

Above (though elevation is a sliding mood) the **Ampersand Eels** migrate.  
They are punctuation marks that grew weary of syntax and slipped off the edge of manuscripts into the weather.  
Now they undulate across the cloud-ceiling, curved connectors breathing like gills.  
When two meet they fuse into a thicker *and*, swelling until the grammar can no longer support them; they rupture, releasing a fall of small conjunctions that embed themselves in the moss like purple rice.  
Step on one and you’ll find yourself linked to whatever you were about to leave behind: a thought, a campsite, a war.  
Separation becomes a cooperative effort; you will need allies, or amnesia, or both.

No apex, no base, only a **middle** that insists on staying middle by moving whenever you approach.  
At that travelling centre lounges the **Listener**, though lounge may be generous; it is spread so thin it could be hired as atmosphere.  
It is the landlord here, though it never signed a deed.  
Shape: a slow-motion splash paused mid-squish.  
Colour: the shade of silence heard through a pillow pressed by dread.  
The Listener absorbs every sound, not to keep but to resell in bulk to areas suffering a shortage of ambience—abandoned film sets, awkward parties, prayers that need ballast.  
The price is always a single question you’ve never asked.  
Ask it and you are free to leave; most stay instead, rehearsing the question until language itself liquefies and joins the general hum.

Thus the population remains fluid, literally.  
There are no births, only dilutions; no deaths, only solution.  
The census is taken by measuring how much heavier the air feels at dusk; if it tastes like iron, numbers are up.  
The economy runs on surplus future bartered for reduced consequence.  
Government is by consensus of versions of yourself you successfully avoided becoming; policy is enforced by mild regret that pools in cupped hands.

And yet the arrangement is not cruel.  
Visitors who decline to trade still receive shelter: the ground dimples beneath them, a lullaby of droplets orbit their heads, and the breathing of the world synchronises with their lungs until both parties pretend to belong to each other.  
If morning ever arrives it does so by thinning, not brightening—like a debt finally forgotten—revealing that you have been living inside an exhalation that, having sighed itself to softness, now invites you to step outside.  

Most do not.  
They take off their shoes, pour their remaining name into a convenient puddle, and settle into the slow grammar of continuous evaporation, becoming one more syllable in the sentence the marsh keeps murmuring to itself—a sentence that neither begins nor ends but merely continues, wetly, faithfully, aloud.
