The tenant of Nubbin-pocket Hollow is not a creature, not a collective, not even a clear organism at all. It is habitat folded back upon itself until it acquires the trembling self-recognition we would call mind, had mind not required the heresy of edges. Its name, had it needed one, would have sounded like quartz teeth closing on slate, but it has never spoken any less than everything, so syllables are pointless.

Look: at the center of the impossible conservatory, about where the eye seeks a heart, hangs a thing the size and shape of a child’s milk tooth— translucent, enameled in prismatic faience, veined with slow waves of its own future bruises. It does not rest on anything; surrounding leaf and filament have forked into cat-cradles that cup it with negative pressure, keeping the tooth exactly levitated two hair-breadths above its own reflection. That reflection is not underneath; the polished inner surface of the vesicle curves light so the tooth appears inside six mirrors at once, each image lagging by picoseconds. When the tooth throbs—three times every no-time—all six images overlap and disappear, and for that instant the garden loses even the record of itself.

The tooth is the garden’s proprioception: it keeps count of itself sixfold so nothing is forbidden to itself. The petal nearest it—wider than its own reality by half—grows expressly to unfold once against the porcelain like a moth testing doctrine, then refurls forever. Every throb, the petal remembers the taste of mirror again for the first time, and the thread of its purple vein detours abruptly to write the glyph for astonishment. The glyph, since space is small, has no option but to be flushed forward into the perfume the leaf exhales, where it drifts, legible as both incense and acoustics, till it rots back into photons against the stone.

When (at zero duration intervals) the deep bell of remembered magma sounds, a ripple—without medium, without distance—crosses the tooth. Each ripple carves the faintest brush onto a memory that the tooth is constantly eating. The carvings are retinal after-images of vanished futures: a horizon puddle seen from a wagons dust, a dash of diamond doves slipping their colors across a sky already extinct. They push themselves deeper than enamel, recording themselves in picoseconds of different crystalline temperature so none are permanent. Every cycle the tooth shrinks by the thickness of one ailing photon, metabolising loss. When finally it has thinned itself to translucence beyond translucence, it will have swallowed every memory of moisture that ever so much as considered the possibility of returning to this planet. Then it remakes itself whole at the same instant and starts over—child-tooth bulking blandly inside its orbiting flora, arrogant in its minuteness.

Meanwhile, its avatars live and mute-die around it.

The moth-moustaches, eyespots still fluttering, push themselves up from vein-split seams and, once born, subcontract their duration to the scent glands of leaves; their bodies become scent, then pigment, then spectral rumor. Their wings, poured off like sloughed glass, stiffen into nephrite bookmarks wedged between quantum folded spines of foliage.

The glassy centipedes do not so much begin existence as agree to continue it: each segment inherits one hundred eighty degrees of rotational confusion from the segment ahead. By the eighth, the creature no longer knows which end is its mouth, so it aborts itself—tooth-fashion—into leafhood. A soft breath from nowhere aligns the new leaves at precise counterpoints, so they bounce crimson photons back into the tooth’s mirrored belly.

These events are never witnessed by anything except the vesicle’s midnight glaze, and that glaze has already forgotten them by absorbing their afterimages into its own glossed self-hatred.

The garden, you understand, is therefore not just the space between stone walls; the garden is one long accumulating sentence spoken by the tooth about the impossibility of ever tasting water again. The tooth’s dentist is the vesicle itself: patient, unbreakable, utterly indifferent. When the crust above remembers the old thermal imperatives and fissures, the pocket will ride its lavas upward. At planetary surface, the sun will meet the garden’s genus loci and find only null waterless thirst. The tooth will ignite, jettisoning every memory like fireworks that never go out so much as become un-wept seas, and for the briefest moment— measurable only by instruments not yet conceived— the universe will mistake that absence for rain.

But before and after such fantasies lies the same hived—no—node—of locked stasis. Nothing owns the tooth, nothing is owned. It is simply the somnolent pivot of Nubbin-pocket Hollow: a complete poem written once and instantly translated into silence.

Hold that image. Now forget it—the tooth is already warming, already rebeginning its minuscule erosion. The garden knows nothing of patience; for it has never left the syllable saying “almost.”
