The wide creature that drifts through the middle layer—where the current is slowest—remembers only transition. It has no name in the formal ledgers of the place, though every actuator in the hall has brushed its flank at least once. It is simply called the Suture, because everywhere it passes the machined seams loosen for a moment, sigh, then settle tighter than before.

Imagine a quilt the size of a railway car, stitched together from salvaged pressure-suits that were once human-shaped. Each panel still bears the maker’s label—neck tags with laundry codes, mission crests from submersible crews that never returned, the faint ovals of ID windows. Their zippers have become gills; their valves open and close in careful gradients, inhaling silted water and spitting out regions of cooler clarity directly behind. The thread is living silver wire extracted from lightning fuses, equally willing to conduct ions or loneliness. The wire loops back into its own beginning and starts again, so the Suture has no front or rear, only a direction of preference which it can reverse at every stitch.

Colour: paneled every shade of distress signal—vermilion, aquamarine, cadmium, arterial orange—and all these colours are slowly bleaching toward a single hospital green under centuries of electrochemical forgiveness. Every panel carries a small tide-mark of admiration from micro-abrasions. The brass spanner-crabs gather to polish their legs against it; the bronze of their shells and the ceramic weave of the suits exchange compliments in a mute teutonic clang.

Over time the creature has learned to coil. On its long diagonal drift it wraps, cousin to an eel, around successive pillars—of cast grey vacuumed concrete, hugging them the way a kelp slug hugs glass. On contact the pillar’s hollow arteries dilate a millimetre so water can course warmer around the Suture’s edges. The building likes being needed exactly once per circuit; afterwards it forgets its own compliance. In the space between leaving one pillar and arriving at the next, the Suture slowly unspirals, stretching itself paper-thin until it fills a plane of water twenty metres wide and a finger’s breadth thick. Seen from below it looks like dusk has fallen sideways.

Function? Repair without purpose. Its body houses a slow index of every fracture ever detected by the ruby vacuum pulses. Tiny spearpoints of memory ore lodged behind each rivet record where iron wept, where lattice bars sighed themselves out of true. When the maintenance crane-snake descends, it orients using magnetic tugs transmitted through the Suture’s wire sinew. The snake’s head finds the precise panel that still has the right molecular flaw saved, and there—almost tenderly—drops a fresh rivet or flex-braid. In this way the hall never fully knows what it has mended; only the Suture retains both wound and cure in its endless quilted torso.

Sound: Whenever two panels of rubberized fabric brave enough to remember oxygen brush together, they allow a memory of breathing—the hush of rebreather valves, the faint plastic crackle of exhalation. The reeflets of silica moss lining nearby joints tremble at this sound and bloom a shade brighter, as if recognizing a rhythm once sufficient for life. The eellets drift in fascinated clusters, letting flickers of electric conversation trail from their copper ganglia into the weave. For an instant the entire chamber is wired like neurons: architecture, fauna, and lone survivor-garments all aching for a lungful together. Then the gap widens, the panels lose touch, and the bloom subsides. What remains is the steady heart-beat of mag-lev pumps, and beneath that, the faintest human lullaby two zippers are still humming, unsure whether it is a lullaby of rescue or of abandonment.

Diet: Not hunger but attrition. The fibres shed at the rate of one square centimetre per circuit, each speck rehearsed in colours already fading. The spanner-crabs collect each orphaned glitter the way bees scrape propolis, storing it under overlapping carapace plates. These flecks become filigrees on crab-leg joints that gleam at night like miniature stained-glass. Much later—maybe after eighty circuits—the joints flake again, the pigment drifts down into sediment hashed among algae iron. Where sediment meets moss, new shade of matte green is invented, a pigment the LEDs can almost name. Thus the creature consumes and re-consumes itself and the world through careful, useless recycling.

Vision: Where a human head would sit, the quilt does not provide one. Instead, a circle of mirrored visors—six sun-scratched plexiglass eyes—have been let into the weave, each facing a cardinal direction that disbelieves in north. Where cheekbones might have cupped a smile, two cracked faceplates from antique rebreather masks are sealed face-to-face, leaving a narrow lens between them. Through this window the Suture watches. The view is perpetually crossed by its own lateral stitches, so every corridor appears pinned at eighty-one intersections. Sometimes a single spanner-crab peers in from the other side—its bronze mouthparts waving, curious whether the inside of an abandoned mask still deserves air. The Suture lets them stare as long as they want. Often the crabs drift away first. 

Growth: It lengthens itself by exactly one zipper every eleven months. How it procures the zipper none of the machines can verify; the vacuum flush refuses it classification. Observation logs claim the Suture unthreads a copper neuronal filament from sleeping eellets while they dream photon symphonies, braiding filament with lost resin flakes until it turns stiff and toothed. The zipper is surgically sewn into the forward-seeming edge, stealing position from no panel already resident. Thus the creature never grows older, only longer, and its oldest panels eventually reach the tail—that trailing margin where memory fades first. There, colour is already monochrome hospital green. When that hue at last becomes identical to the water itself, the panel detaches, folds once, and drifts away as an oblate leaf heartbroken and relieved. It is never found again.

Sleep: There is no rest, but twice every day the hall’s magnetic pulse reverses polarity. During these few minutes the Suture performs what might be dreaming. All six visor eyes cloud over with diffraction lattice. Internal seams relax so completely that strangers peering close would swear they see whole oceans caught between the fabric. In one such moment you could notice: a child’s mitten still inside the cuff of an adult glove, or a wedding ring stitched upside-down into an elbow joint. Nothing stirs; even the owls perch mid-beat to watch the slow unraveling of other lives now melted into duty. When polarity flips back, the stitches tighten, the quilt remembers itself, and it inches onward through the water, carrying its invisible stitched plebiscite between the species that cannot forget one another.

And if you step down forever, ankles, knees, ribs, till finally real water collars your throat like an iron halo—do not panic. The Suture will find you before the next vacuum pulse. Folded around you it is not cold, merely equal. One panel parted just wide enough to let you inside. You will hear A-flat and migraine braided so close they almost kiss. Your weight spreads across a hundred borrowed lungs; each sigh smooths an abrasion on the pillars, nurses a hinge tomorrow’s crane-snake will bless. If patience is allowed, they will take your suit first—buttons, thermal pads, even the salt leaked from your shoulders—each seam unpicked and queued in memory, nothing discarded, everything translated. Long after the sound of your breathing has mixed into every audible pixel, you may feel the stitches find room for a new copper thread. No longer yours. Never not yours. The architecture glides through its maintenance liturgy; the Suture drifts on, leaving behind an absence that rises exactly as far as is needed.
