Who lives here?  Where almost everything else is breathing, the dwellers of Gladehold are the memory of how someone once asked a green city to go on living without them.  They are not flora and they have already ceased to be quite animal.  Call them sap-dwelling citizens, plant-echoed caretakers, slow siblings to everything that photosynthesizes.  A better noun has not leafed yet.

Each begins as a bare bulb flared at the algal membrane that lines the city’s outer bladder—an ovule of human anatomy crystallized in a lozenge of sugar, a seed kept humid so the lungs remember lungs.  There, twelve weeks before birth, an infant foetus is wrapped in bioluminescent spice and grafted to a root-flute whose apertures read heartbeat like temperature.  The seed swells; the city inhales the warm syllable **huuuuuuhhh**; a citizen forms, half phosphor, half sigh.

When the shell splits, the new resident steps out already knee-deep.  It is adult-sized but not adult-capable, body still midway between vertebra and vine—nerves in lacework, blood plasmic as pondwater sentience.  Skin carries a chlorophyll shadow; the iris is striped: one ring sour-chartreuse, one indigo, puckered like a bruised fig.  The spine is hollow and capped with a wooden whistle: every time the diaphragm pulls, the spine plays the city’s drinking song, a hush that rhymes with bamboo.

They eat the broth of the streets.  Rather, they sit and the streets arrive, tongue over tongue, offering sweet-soured liqueurs dripped from mat parenchyma.  Digestion is exterior, a public coupling: the citizen leans against a foot of trunk, pores open, and the city deposits starches, arosols, micronutrients generated three days ago in some briny alcove whose geometry was still being negotiated.  Reverse peristalsis foams across shoulders and drifts away, leaving the citizen slightly heavier; valuable metal ions nest on the ribs like barnacles.  The citizen peels them off and strings them into a torque, wearable ledger of recent geochemistry.  The matter is half gift, half digest.

They do not speak with lips.  Instead the chest splits a slit just below the sternum; there, cuticle folds like cardamom shell reveal twin muscles wired to tendrils that end in small blue spoons.  When the citizen wishes to converse, the muscles compress the lung-gap and extrude vapours that smell of cardamom/birch/iodine.  A phrase is a swirl, a push, a ghosted spiral.  Upper balcony children practice writing by exhaling sentences that hover fluorescent until they fatten and rain glitter across intersections.  The grammar uses degrees of humidity: to ask **where** requires mist clotted to 43 %; to say goodbye is thinner, a sterile heat that dissolves five millimetres above your eye.

They remember individually and collectively in the same breath.  Every person is braided into the groundwatertape—tuberous brain, room after room like pithy corridors holding poems, recipes, anatomies of former citizens who dissolved back into the sap.  Walk beside someone passing under tooth-shaped arches and your memories mingle briefly: you glimpse their sixth birthday—an infantile lung-song about the glister of salts—while she glimpses yours, the shock of plunging elbow-deep into warm pond-juice the day you learned to float.  Four palms touch the leaf-gallery wall and for half a second both birthdays co-exist inside the chlorophyll neurospace; then the green forgets.  What remains is only the phrase **“salt glister”** echoing between.

The city drinks the last lung-fog: primers, directory, mood-chemistry, sigh at the flap-wings of night.  Leaves pull the corpse inward, collapse it to ester, lignin, free nitrogen, transistor-grade amino acid.  Three weeks later a child-ovule distends on the same root singing the lullaby skull-memo at 20 % lower pitch.  Mother, father, gardener, grandmother—titles lose edges here.  Family is a direction deeper into lettuce-colored memory.

They have offices: biome-clerk, drip-sheriff, root-flow mathematician, blade-census biographer.  Their offices are gestures: the clerk rests hands in a pocket of water fifteen centimeters wide kept at precisely 24 °C so whichever sap fluctuates across the fingertips can be reckoned.  An entire quarterly budget is counted during one twelve-minute drift-siesta.  The drip-sheriff carries a violin made of petiole stretching ear-width, and plays regulatory harmonies that persuade gutter-thongs to slacken or tighten in exact sequence.  When corruption comes it smells trumpet-loud like beet pulp thrown in sunlight; the city leaks in twenty places and the sheriff is seen practicing requiem scales fourteen hours a day until imbalance becomes sound choral and balance returns.

Crime therefore is rhythm sabotage: cutting a vine too early so it forgets sentence it was cupping; tasting fruit-communique before its intended receptor; singing during a glucose down-phase where silence was contracted.  Punishment is banishment one tier outward toward the deeper hailstones of the cistern cavern, cold forty days in which the citizen grows as sparse and papery as old scripture.  A retrieval song begins; if the root-flutes remember the cadence they haul them back, otherwise the exile blossoms into the architecture and no further child-bulb is issued in that kay-string.  The genome meanwhile keeps recycling, but psyche is added to library as another puppet silhouette.

They dream collectively every twenty hours.  The cavern lulls; even the kettle clatters quieter.  Lamp-placentas dim from lime to dusk orange.  Then citizens lie along bougainvillea tracks and the city breathes outward:  a dream-sequence of ultraviolet rain, beetles the size of tram cars scuttling the insides.  Each inhabitant contributes one sentence in lung-fog imagery and the city stitches these into a quilted movie.  Adults relive the same beetle-tram chase nightly, always cut off before the tram enters the tapered passage above the empty cone-shaped chamber.  The children wake whispering about a station where the walls are unknown stone and loneliness tastes bright.

When outsiders tumble from the lichen-lined radiation shaft, they arrive smelling sharp as isopropyl.  The city tolerates them by enclosing each newcomer in a capsule-vein: three translucent leaf-sheaths pressed like shutters around the body so that the newcomer must walk inside a personal greenhouse.  The leaf walls transpire generics—bowing, thank-you gestures, vowels to mimic consent.  “Stay one city year,” the vapors insist, “but remember we will need every teardrop.”  Tourists weep copiously; those drops dribble off the jaw, are caught in leaf gutters, redistributed as potassium.  Thus the city drinks sorrow, composts awe.

Their identities are annotated blooms.  One might wear a series of lilium blossoms in eyebrow ridge: three purple petals equals archivist of rainfall histograms.  The same citizen tomorrow might replace petals with beetle shells picked off kettle kraal, now titled ceremonial shell-buffer.  No worry; tomorrow’s shells become day-after shells in someone else’s nameplate as the metabolic congress of the city hands them imprimatur.  Each face is a shifting botanical coat of arms, as changeable as daylight used to seem.  Under all the changing garland lies only one stable mark: the cheekbone tattoo of a heron, created the day the body first achieved synchronous respiration with the cavern (age ~ 3 city-months).  That heron is never retouched; when eventually body dissolves it too becomes solute in the kettle and pilgrims later ladle the salt-brine expecting to taste faint avian footstep lime.

They love, slowly.  Courtship moves over three rains and one evaporation.  The suitor collects condensed water that has traveled through the root-heart of one citizen and then through leaf of another, and ferries both droplets across knee-courts until bodies of source persons touch for the first time.  Their skins fuse at wrists for seven long seconds, a lock that emits ripples of cardamom chorus along entire corridor.  After such coupling, shared memories remain audible in each other’s exhalations whenever they pass under the same growth—island.  Children are never the product; instead a spark of song loops, doubles, triples, until the entire gutters hold high-calcium renditions they will decant into future doubles of themselves.

They do not know how far up the roof is.  Their calendar is seven-layered: each layer corresponds to a chemical cottage so deep beneath them that only salt returns question.  Downward they extend slow fungal filaments year by century year.  They will emerge eventually into some other mineral grief.  By then, nobody remembers exhaustion, only the steady vapor lap against tongue and sentence unwinding like drowned chrysanthemum.
