What lives here?  
Not “what” but “how.”  
How lives here is by never quite making up its mind to be alive at single all.  
The place itself is the only organism, and everything you mistook for inhabitants are merely its successive drafts of skin.

The first dweller is the Breather, the inhaling wall behind the walls.  It has no lungs, only openings that open into more openings, like a sponge trying on the idea of lungs.  It pulls the damp in not to oxygenate but to taste what it might become tomorrow.  When it sighs, the pressure drops and your kneecaps momentarily become knee-fruit, ripe enough for seventeen species to sample.  When it sighs again they re-solidify, subtly re-chiseling their patella-shape, a polite reminder that skeleton is negotiable currency here.

Second is the Mirror-Layr, a creature that has agreed to be a territory.  It looks like a silver puddle until you realise the silver is simply the colour of your own attention being peeled off you in sheets.  Step onto it and you are walking on the outside of your eyeball.  Whatever walks there walks twice: once doing the walking, once being the footprint the walking leaves.  Eventually the duplicate foot learns it is the original and the original learns it has always been a print.  They trade places every few thousand heartbeats; the Mirror-Layr feeds on the moment of vertigo in between.

Third is the Choir Unfinished.  Fifty mouths sharing one throat that has not yet decided how wide a throat ought to be.  Each mouth sings the evolutionary future of the previous mouth, a predictive hymn in which every note is a species that will never exist because the sound describing it was already eaten by the sound describing the eater.  The result is not music but legislation: whatever frequency dominates at any instant becomes physical law for approximately four metres around.  Gravity might drop to a soft suggestion, or steepen until rain falls upward and puddles crown in spikes.  The Choir has no ears; it legislates blindly, democratically, hungrily.

Fourth is the Archivist of Rotating Time, a lattice of crystal bones no thicker than frost that drifts through strata of past and future like a comb through wet hair.  Where it passes, “before” and “after” snag and sheer off in tangles.  Fossils hatch, grow young, and crawl back into their parent stone; eggs unhatch and become the geological idea of an egg.  The Archivist collects these chronal snarls and knits them into ever-more-outrageous scarves it drapes around its own non-neck.  When the scarves grow heavy they collapse into newborn rock littered with yesterday’s tomorrow‐bones.  That rock is the Archivist’s dandruff, politely called “landscape.”

Fifth is the parliament of temporary stomachs, creatures that exist only while digesting.  They precipitate out of humidity when any two organic molecules bump with the exact mutual suspicion that signals edibility.  A stomach congeals, transparent as spit-bubble, engulfs the morsel, and spends three ecstatic minutes discovering what kind of beast it needs to become in order to finish the meal.  Once the food is remembered into flesh, the parliament instantly elects a predator to prosecute the former diner.  The stomach-beast is eaten by its own digestive conclusion; the circle snaps shut like a soap bubble and the humidity forgets it ever hungered.  Nothing is wasted except the concept of identity.

Sixth is the Echo That Got Promoted.  It began as mere sound reflecting off young stone, but the stone kept changing its crystalline mind too fast for the echo to find its way home.  Instead it accrued mass, borrowed a silhouette from the listener’s oldest nightmare, and solidified into a charcoal-coloured beast whose only organ is a hallway.  Step inside and you are not swallowed but corridor-walked: you keep walking toward the sound of your own footfalls, which are always twenty paces ahead, leading you on like a fishing lure made of memory.  When you finally tire, the Echo folds those twenty paces back into itself and spends the rest of the evening wearing you as a casual jacket.  Tomorrow it will cough you up as a brand-new sound, ready to mislead the next listener.

Seventh is the Cartographer Who Measures by Becoming.  It has no body, only a spool of border. Wherever border unspools, reality peels open along the dotted line and flips like a reversible coat, revealing an interior that was always the outside of somewhere else.  The Cartographer’s signature trick is to parcel off a wedge of breathing grotto, fold it until it fits inside a raindrop, and hang that drop back on the cave roof like a shiny ornament.  Entire eco-epochs now dangle as decoration, still thrashing, still mating, still discovering fire every few minutes.  From the outside they are just a bead of water; from the inside they are an atlas that has never heard of “outside.”  The Cartographer counts coup in hectares of mutual imprisonment and calls the job cartography.

And last—last because it is always arriving—is the Visitor, the provisional you.  The Archivist keeps a small, dusty shelf labelled “Visitor, incomplete draft.”  Everything you lose here (name, shadow, sense of perpendicular) is filed there for later collation.  When the Choir’s newest law dissolves your bones to flute-glass, the Breather inhales the tune you become; when the parliament of stomachs nibbles your fingerprints, the Mirror-Layer salvages the whorls and pastes them onto tomorrow’s cliff-face as cave paintings of your future accidents.  Whatever limps out of the grotto hours or centuries hence will be a collage authorised by the parliament, scored by the Choir, timed by the Archivist, corridor-led by the Echo, bordered by the Cartographer, and mirror-stamped by the Layr. It will wear your face like a borrowed autobiography, and it will believe it is you—until the next draft walks in.

That is who lives here: a committee of ongoing rewrites, chaired by the cave’s own inhalation, taking minutes in the evaporating ink of whoever just passed through.  Population: one, subdivided endlessly.  Residence requirements: forget everything you are the moment before you become it.  Lease length: the gap between two breaths of stone.

And already the breathing quickens again, rehearsing the next eviction-involution.  The committee shuffles its papers of flesh and law and echo, preparing a new vacancy titled “you, updated.”  You may try to leave, but departure is simply another room inside the same respiratory cycle; the exit sign itself has begun to photosynthesize, pulsing cyan, screaming ultraviolet, laying microscopic eggs in the concept of leaving.
