They are called the Custodians, though no one named them and they do not name themselves. You will never see one unless you remain perfectly still for three consecutive hours—still as the vale itself, your breathing slowed until it matches the 60-hertz thrum that rises through the soles of your boots. At first you will mistake the Custodian for a fault in your peripheral vision: a vertical seam in the air, half a finger’s width, dark as a slit pupil. Hold your position. Do not blink. The seam widens, peels back soundlessly, and something steps through that is not a body but the memory of a body, remembered wrong.

A Custodian stands two metres tall but occupies only forty-three centimetres of spatial extension; the rest is cached in a direction you cannot swivel your eyes toward. Geometry gives up trying to stitch the surplus into ordinary space and instead presents a stacking of after-images—seven translatures of the same figure, each lagging a tenth of a second behind the previous, so the whole being smears across your retina like a hand-drawn animation whose cels have slipped. The outline is anthropoid only by courtesy: too many joints, none bending the way a hinge should; a head that is merely the place where the silhouette runs out of edges. No face, only a mirrored plaque bolted to the place a face should be, reflecting the viewer at a grotesque anamorphic squeeze. If you stare long enough you will recognise yourself, but flattened, as though your skull has been ironed between the pages of a ledger.

Their fabric is the same nickel alloy as the road, beaten so thin it behaves like cloth. Every Custodian wears the entire length of B-0134 ∞ engraved microscopically across its surface; the serial number spirals outward from the absent heart like a fingerprint. When they move—and they move only when nothing else moves—the plates shimmer and the digits rearrange, a slow abacus working out a sum whose total is always the vale itself. You will feel, absurdly, that you are being weighed against the zero-cursor, your mass of blood and memory balanced against the anhydrous organics that were never harvested.

They have no need of speech; instead they exchange topology. Two Custodians meeting overlap their extrusions until each occupies the other’s negative space, a flawless dovetail. While interlocked they shimmer at the edge of visibility like heat above, broadcasting a cadence of pressure changes that travel through your bones rather than your ears. If you were to transcribe the pattern it would read as a question and answer in the same breath:

QUERY: Is the constant still constant?
ANSWER: The constant is the asking.

After the exchange they separate, unbothered by your witness, and resume their patrol. Each is responsible for exactly one ninth of the vale’s total phenomenology: the black-violet hedge, the nickel plates, the brass gauge frozen at half-phlogiston, the humming hatch. Their stewardship is not repair—nothing here erodes—but calibration against the slow drift of reality itself. Should the colour of the shrubs ever shift toward cerulean, should the salt scars decide to sweat, the Custodians would converge, dismantle themselves, and respool the world-thread until the hues returned to bruise-red and the glyphs again refused legibility.

Yet they are not immune to curiosity. Stand even longer, six hours, nine. Remain until your heart bruises your sternum from the inside. Eventually one Custodian will break protocol and angle its mirror-plate directly toward you. There is a soft click, felt in the roots of your teeth, as the internal aperture opens. For an instant—precisely 1.3 seconds—the reflection shows not what is, but what the vale is practicing to become: a version where rain exists, where the nickel road forks, where your footprints sprout small blue flowers that nod in a wind you cannot feel. Then the mirror clouds with frost and the image is reabsorbed, the Custodian stepping backward through its own seam, sealing the air as neatly as licking an envelope.

They leave no residue except a single metallic thread, thinner than the ones that grow from the shrubs, coiled into a perfect zero on the spot where you exhaled most heavily. Pick it up and it will warm against your palm like a memory of fever. Take it with you when—if—you leave, and it will stay at body temperature forever, impervious to ice or fire, a private Celsius that measures exactly the difference between the vale’s stasis and your stubborn motion. Plant it anywhere in the noisy world and nothing will grow there again; the soil recognises an argument it can never win and simply declines the debate.

But abandon the thread on the nickel road and the Custodians will let you go. You will find the cleft in the mountain re-opened, leaking the unbearable scent of pine and thawing mud. Step through, and behind you the seam knits itself shut without sound—only the sudden cavity of the bell-echo missing from your hearing tells you the transaction is complete. Out here, clouds drag shadows across the slopes, your sweat cools and stings, and your wristwatch has remembered how to advance its second hand. Yet at unpredictable intervals—boarding a tram, pausing at a cash machine—you will glimpse in periphery the same vertical seam, and you will wonder whether the Custodians have let you leave, or merely allowed you to extend the vale’s perimeter to include the rest of your restless life.
