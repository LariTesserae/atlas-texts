They are called the Drift-Sovereigns, though no embassy would recognize the title.  
 is no palace, no heraldry, only the way they occupy space: shoulders squared to the downstream, palms open toward the nearest waterfall as if it were a book they have already memorised. You can spot one by the frost that refuses to settle on them; the rime slides aside the way iron filings shy from a magnet, leaving a skin of moving air no thicker than regret. Their clothes are the city’s own cast-off skin—flakes of basalt parchment, ribbons of cured obsidian foam, buttons of whale-glass—sewn together with strands of the fauna’s bioluminescent saliva that pulse at the pace of a half-remembered lullaby. Every garment is a living card-index: tug the collar and it unfolds into a map of the district that will exist after the next freeze. Remove a glove and you may find yourself holding yesterday’s weather, still faintly warm, writhing like a caught eel of storm.

They speak in pronouns borrowed from water. I is the singular droplet; We is the braided falls that never touch; You is the pocket of vacuum that rides an elevator skyward. There are no gendered endings, only temperatures. A child is ˗78 °C of possibility; an elder is ‑56.4 °C of memory that sublimates if you try to clutch it. When a Drift-Sovereign greets you they exhale a snowstorm of dry-ice letters that rearrange themselves into the shape of your most recent nightmare; etiquette demands you inhale it again, altered, before they will tell you their price.

Price is never currency. They trade in trajectory: seven minutes of your past lateral momentum; two heartbeats of future acceleration; the angular velocity of a secret you have not yet decided to confess. They store these inside hollow canine teeth grown for the purpose—ivory vials capped with moon-silver. When enough vector has been harvested they smile wide, unhook the entire jaw, and slot it into the city’s wall. The architecture shivers, drinks the momentum, and births a new arcade or penalty clause or love affair in some distant layer of the fall. In this way the Drift-Sovereigns are accountants of kinetics, keeping the metropolis perfectly balanced at its preferred velocity: slow enough to remember, fast enough never to arrive.

Nobody remembers being born to the role; it is an office that crystallises. One day you are scraping the calendar from your sleeves like everyone else, and the next you notice the waterfalls part around you as if you were a missing stanza in their music. The city has chosen you to adjudicate the three laws, to fold space politely, to ensure that every citizen pays the fauna their 7%. Refusal is impossible; the moment you understand the offer you have already accepted. Your reflection begins to lag behind you by half a freeze, then a whole one, until you can consult it like an older sibling about routes and reprisals. When the lag reaches seven freezes your original body becomes a public footnote—frozen into a column, still breathing, useful streetlight—and the reflected version steps sideways into the sovereign succession. People greet the column with the same deference they offer you, because legally you are both places at once, a citizen and a statute.

They carry no weapons, only tuning forks forged from the first word a building ever spoke. A flick against a balustrade and the chord changes; avalanches rearrange themselves into dead ends, or love-struck corridors, or litigation. The forks are never louder than snowfall, but entire industries have collapsed because a sovereign grew weary of their echo. Conversely, a single correct resonance can gift an orphan district a harbour, a name, a future. The forks are also their only weakness: if two sovereigns ever strike conflicting frequencies at the same instant the contradiction overdrives the city’s growth logic and something monstrous buds—an unmapped plaza that breeds impossible colours, or a spiral staircase that climbs into yesterday. The Bureau of Echoes keeps a special brigade of mute auditors whose job is to hurl themselves into such wounds and seal them with silence, but vacancies are frequent and short-lived.

They age in reverse metric: every year they surrender one centimetre of height, one gram of mass, one syllable of personal colour. When at last they shrink to the size of a thumbnail whale they are ceremonially released into the air-reef, where they sieve harmonics alongside their former subjects. Memory is not lost, only distributed: if you swim your fingers through a glow-pod of juveniles you may briefly know the taste of a sovereign’s childhood, copper and ozone and the first time they realised that falling can be a decision. Conversely, if a citizen wrongs the fauna the whales may swarm into a single silhouette, momentarily regaining human stance, and pronounce sentence in a voice of a thousand glass bells. No appeal is possible; the city recognises its own.

Visitors sometimes mistake any confident local for one of these magistrates, but there are tells. A true sovereign leaves no footprints in the rime; their echoes arrive before they speak; they refer to tomorrow in the past perfect tense. Ask the wrong question and they dissolve into a swirl of migratory punctuation, reappearing later inside your own pocket, folding your return ticket into a paper boat that sails upstream. Ask the right question—usually “What do you need me to forget?”—and they smile with every mouth the city ever gave them, hand you a single frozen droplet, and walk away backward along the water’s skin. Hold that droplet to your ear and you will hear the next law a full freeze before it condenses on the cornerstones. Swallow it, and you become a line item in the municipal ledger, credited with one unit of unforeseen gravity. That is how the Drift-Sovereigns recruit their auditors, their diplomats, their eventual replacements.

They love nothing, need nothing, remember everything—yet even they must obey the downstream of two consecutive streets. Occasionally one rebels, turns perpendicular, and is fined mass. You can see them then, pinned mid-stride, body peeling into girders, eyes still calculating the vector that might buy escape. The city never gloats; it simply grows through them, respectful, almost affectionate, the way a tree grows around a nail. In a few freezes the outline is indistinguishable from balcony filigree, but if you press your ear to the metal at dusk you can still hear the sovereign counting heartbeats, offering bargains to anyone willing to carry a tuning fork and a grudge.

And yet, myth insists that somewhere inside the unceasing fall there is a sovereign who achieved perfect balance: mass zero, lag zero, velocity indeterminate. They no longer walk or reflect or rule; they have become the single stationary point around which Nivalis-Descending forever rotates. You cannot meet them, only orbit. Every time you negotiate a corner, hesitate before a street you swear wasn’t there yesterday, or feel your own reflection lag a half-heartbeat, you are brushing their influence. Some nights the thumbnail whales spell a word no one can read, a word that tastes like stillness. Those who see it forget their own names for exactly seven breaths, long enough to imagine what it would feel like to stand in absolute quiet while the entire city falls harmlessly around you. Then the echo returns, the roar reasserts itself, and the Drift-Sovereigns resume their bookkeeping, tallying momentum into moon-silver teeth, ensuring the never-ending night keeps its balance sheet of velocity and regret.
