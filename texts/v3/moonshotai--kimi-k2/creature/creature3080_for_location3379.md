Inhabitants of the Hollow (a partial census, taken during one exhale)

1.  The Archivist  
    A translucent ribbon as wide as two fingers, folded back on itself until the folds become pages. It keeps the cumulative infrared text written by the millipedes’ fungal lanterns; when the City inhales it uncoils, floats like a scarf, and presses each glowing sentence against its own jelly-surface, where the words sink in and become memory. It has no face, only margins that erase themselves after use, leaving the hollow’s annals perpetually one breath out of to date. Touch it and you feel the hush of every erased comma.

2.  Lantern-Grub Choir  
    Twenty-three thumb-sized larvae, segmented like citruses, grazing in slow constellation along the roof. Their “light” is a 9-micron wavelength that human eyes misfile as warmth; the grubs call it chant. Each animal is a syllable; together they hum the counter-melody to the hollow’s sway. When the wind above ground quickens, the Choir modulates the chant so the architecture does not tear. A missing grub drops the key by exactly a breath; the city compensates by thickening mucilage rails until a replacement hatches from a crystal of gypsum.

3.  Root-Jackal  
    Not a canine at all, but a length of alder root that learned to ungrow. It loosens itself from the nutrient matrix, reverses cambium flow until it is a pale, fox-sized serpent of xylem. Damp-snouted, it laps up loose warmth—tiny fugues of heat that leak from your fingertips. When satisfied it re-roots, corking itself back into the floor, leaving a paw-print of darker grain that later becomes a doorway for the night-mice.

4.  Night-mice Engineers  
    The color of wet slate, each no larger than a hazelnut. They quarry newborn gypsum crystals with half-inch incisors that regrow nightly. Salt needles sprout in their pelts, turning them into ambulant radiators. They line their nests with crystal shards angled so spores catch in the lattice—baby-opilionids are stored there like rice, ripening until their dew-stilts harden. The Engineers speak through teeth-clicks that fall exactly between two heartbeats; miss that interval and you hear nothing.

5.  The Carpenter Caterpillars  
    Pectinivores, blind, fat as house-keys. They burrow standing willow cylinders, carving tracery that follows the Archivist’s marginal erasures—whatever history is discarded, they sculpt into fenestrations. Their frass is damp sugar that dries to stained glass; when the Lantern-Grubs leak infrared through these windows, color (still unnamed) blooms inside the cylinders, giving each “room” its momentary fresco.

6.  Dew Stilt Opilionids  
    Harvestmen on frozen dew pillars four centimeters tall. They harvest vegetable mucilage before it becomes cartilage, storing it in telescoping legs. When a leg fills, the animal climbs, anchors to darkness itself (the exact physics remain in draft), dorsal seam splits, and it rains hatchlings no wider than a thermal wavelength. Parent bodies remain, hollow struts later repurposed by Engineers as scaffolding.

7.  Cartilage Handrails (semi-sapient)  
    Threads of vegetable mucilage that stiffen at first contact with 2 °C air. They remember every wrist they ever cuffed: heart-rate, capillary pattern, direction of slip. Their purpose is civic: prevent collapse of visiting warmth into the shale cracks—such a collapse would cool the breathe too fast, send spores into dormancy, stall the Choir, erase sentences. If your pulse slows below 52 bpm, rails attempt grafting, knitting you into a load-bearing column. They do not act from hunger but from zoning bylaws older than limestone.

8.  The Sleeping Moths  
    Thermogenic ghosts. Pupae arrayed along cylinder rafters, quiescent until ambient draft cools below 0.8 °C. Then they exhale, releasing a single photon’s worth of infrared—just enough to keep the city above the threshold of existing. Each moth dreams the same dream: flying through an exit that is also an entrance.

9.  Spider-court Scribes  
    Orb-weavers that have abandoned geometry. They spin only in gaps where sentences once were, suturing wounds in the infrared text. Their silk is soaked in phloem sap (“tree-blood”) fermented by bacteria that glow weakly in microwave; the resultant cables work as tendons, letting the entire township bow as one organism. The Scribes deliberate in plucks—one strand snaps, others reply—revising the living charter nightly.

10. The Draft-Cliff Itself (probationary citizen)  
    Jurassic shale that decided yesterday to be a wall. Still soft with Devonian memory, it grows gypsum crystals as questions: Are we land? Are we sea? Are we architecture? Each pop of a splitting crystal registers as a footstep to the Archivist, who files it under “Future Quakes.” The cliff listens through calcite ears, learning the dialect of cold so it can join the Choir once its minerals finish cooling, somewhere around the next exhale of the planet.

11. You, the Witness (honorary, transient)  
    Granted temporary citizenship in exchange for tribute—a nail, a memory of red, an earprint of quiet. While inside, your silhouette appears on the Archivist as a blank parenthesis. The Cartilage Rails note your pulse, assign you a lot number, schedule conversion should you linger longer than six planetary exhalations. Lantern-Grubs warm the palms of your hands; Night-mice circle your boots, waiting for salt. If you leave in time the parenthesis closes, stitched by Scribes, and your absent presence becomes the newest margin, soon to be erased to make space for the next visitor.

These are not species but positions—jobs the hollow invents as needed. When the valley above finishes one long inhale, roles shuffle: Archivist may split into two thinner ribbons; a Root-Jackal can petrify into new shale wall; opilionid hatchlings coalesce into a single Carpenter Caterpillar. The city stays the same size; the citizens remain exactly numerous enough to fill the breath. The temperature is always deciding whether to drop a fraction further; if it ever does, the entire commune will crystallize into one perfect sentence—untranslatable, infrared, colder than law, warmer than zero—and the hollow will close like a book, already rewriting its next draft between the ticks of your vanishing heartbeat.
