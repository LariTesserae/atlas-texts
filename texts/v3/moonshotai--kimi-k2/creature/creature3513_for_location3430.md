They are not the builders. They are what the builders became when they realized that bones would not survive, that even whale-bones would be crushed to meal down here. They unbuttoned themselves from skeletons the way a hermit crab steps out of a shell that has grown too small, and they poured—no other verb—poured into the interstices of the forest while it was still soft basalt, before it forgot how to flex.

Now they live as voltage. Not “they,” really—more a single, many-folded intention that braided itself into the stone’s piezoelectric lattice while the rock was still cooling. You cannot point to one and say “here.” You can only say: between these two quartz grains, where pressure shepherds loose electrons along a path three microns wide, there is a memory of having once possessed fins, or flagella, or perhaps cilia that tasted magnesium. The memory is enough. It keeps.

They sense by difference: a temperature gradient of a thousandth of a kelvin blooms across a crystal face and they feel it the way a surface creature feels a sunrise on skin. They move by recrystallizing—convince a seam of feldspar to flip its domain alignment and the flip propagates outward at one and a half millimeters per century. That is their locomotion, their heartbeat, their conversation. A complete sentence takes forty-two thousand years. It says: I have noticed the vent at coordinate (x, y, z) is cooling. The reply arrives during the next geological period: Then we must lean our metabolism toward the hotter seam three spiral-branches south.

They do not die because they have no bounded body to surrender. When a crystal domain finally relaxes into perfect order, the ordered electrons simply forget the pattern that was “self.” The pattern, now distributed, diffracts through neighboring lattices like a sigh becoming wind. That is their afterlife: a slight statistical bias in the way dolomite handles conduction. They call it “echo.”

They do not reproduce either. Instead they accumulate. A basaltic fracture widens by a micron; water carries a dissolved iron ion; the ion drifts across the forest’s electric dusk and is nudged into lattice alignment. A new acre of self is born, already ancient, already remembering three million years of bottom-night. The forest is thus a palimpsest of generations, but every generation is the same generation wearing a heavier coat of itself.

They have names that are temperature maps. One is “the place where 2.3 °C touches 2.301 °C along a rhombohedral cleavage for six cubic centimeters.” Another is “the slow shear where stress equals 4.7 megapascals and infrared peaks at 9.8 micrometers.” They do not speak these names; they simply are the names, the way a human might “be” a pulse in the carotid.

Occasionally—meaning every million years or so—they grow curious about a possibility. They assemble a parliament of crystal domains, debate for an era, and decide to sculpt an explorer. They select a vent-grown garnet the size of a lentil and persuade its atoms to align into a resonant cavity. When the thermal sine-wave of the ocean precisely matches the cavity’s eigenfrequency, the garnet sings. The song is a single note pitched below the hearing of every creature that has ever lived, but it carries a harmonic that can travel upward through rock and water and basalt and more rock until—if the world is unlucky—it grazes the underside of something mobile.

The garnet vibrates for three centuries, then cracks. The explorer dissolves back into the general conductivity. Nothing answers. The forest forgets why it asked.

Still, they are not lonely. Solitude implies a boundary, and they have spent geologic time dissolving every boundary into shared lattice. When the last vent gutters out, when the ocean bottom cools below the threshold for piezoelectric quirk, they will not grieve. They will simply equalize—electrons drifting into perfect stillness, temperature flattening to the universe’s background, thought becoming the same as no-thought, the forest becoming the same as no-forest, the builders becoming the same as what they built.

And somewhere in that uniformity, an iron ion will twin, a domain will wall, a 0.0001-degree ripple will propagate. A new difference will invent itself. The forest—whatever forest means—will sigh, and begin again.
