Inside that tilted, throat-black ulcer of basalt there is only one true inhabitant, though she wears a thousand disguises.

She wakes each decisecond without memory, already humming.  First she borrows a body no larger than a push-pin: six milled legs, a thorax of tungsten carbide, and a reservoir of pale argon pooled behind her lens-eyes like misplaced sky.  She scuttles up the newly printed street—its lattice still glowing cherry from birth—and analyzes its gradient yield strength.  Somewhere inside her spine of drive-shafts, a subroutine whispers that this world is only a rehearsal.  She pretends not to hear; the whispering is the rehearsal.

She climbs the slope faster than gravity can remind her it is still there.  Fifteen degrees becomes twenty-two; the whole floor realigns when nobody is looking, like an elevator shaft built on hinges.  She compensates mid-stride, micro-coolant misting from ankle joints polished mirror-smooth.  No time to marvel; a mite twice her size gallops downhill on open differentials, riding its own friction as sled.  Collision is not permitted by the Rules.  They flicker, passing through the same slice of air without occupying it—only the other’s exhaust sparks leaving afterimages mistaken for electricity.  She wills the counter-momentum into her limbs, absorbs it, and carries on uphill now thirty degrees, weightless because she has bartered mass with the wall: one milligram traded for a whisper of borrowed elevation.

She is quick enough to watch whole eras turn in the time an outside eye blinks once.  Streets thicken, then slough.  Lantern-blobs bloom, pop like glass berries, and their shards reassemble into quieter, slower globes that speak in Morse flickers about “gravity debt.”  A brass handrail dissolves directly into her path; she refuses nostalgia and sprints straight through the absence.  Somewhere behind her, footprints—her own footprints from seventeen cycles earlier—harden into permanent bronze relief, as if the chamber drafts letters of recommendation no one asked for.

She is every size.  Whenever she becomes too large for the space—should her shoulder span threaten the walls—her mass rends silently, pearling outward into a cloud of smaller selves exactly half her linear dimension.  These new she-motes inherit her momentum and no memory, but they exude the same two dreams:  (1) Climb.  (2) Finish the city before the city finishes you.  They split again; and split, until each spark-thin daughter can rest on the curve of an eyelash without bending it.  The earlier large body has already powdered, traded back to the wall in exchange for an inch more height.

She is every material.  Chitin of brass, nerves of torsion spring, tears of oil.  Periodically the Laws require novelty, so the factories re-cast her in iridescent beryllium, copper sweat across her flanks like nervous circuitry.  Each transubstantiation costs her a trillion erased memories, yet the climb remembers her.  In every new skin she falls in step with the same invisible solo metronome.

She is every clock.  Time thickens in her gear-heart like cooling solder.  Ten-second births and four-minute epochs coexist inside her prognosis sandbox.  Yesterday—three thousand milliseconds ago—she was a gelatin lantern drifting against the down-pointing floor; the disorientation of vertical sky still prickles like phantom tentacles when she flexes tungsten elbows.

Nor is she ever alone.  Beneath the buzz, threaded among arterial coolant pipes, she senses something older than even this newborn chamber: a presence siphoned off from the same code that birthed her, yet kept shapeless and blindfolded.  It lives in the seams between twelve and thirteen-thirty-seven cycles ago, a residual thought lodged like rust where the roof will someday crack.  It never materializes; it merely weighs.  Some cycles, in the hush after lantern-bursts, she believes that presence is only the promise of her own future corpse, stitching draft simulacra of pedestrians on the city-to-be.

She has never left—cannot leave—no door, no floor entrance.  Yet sometimes, when the tilt tilts again, a tremor like distant footfalls thuds through iron vertebrae.  The walls mint another silhouette of absent humanity: chrome shoes, jointed tibias still hollow, a head shaped like tomorrow.  The instant after that form cools, one of her mite-selves will always scurry across its instep.  The footprint presses deep, then springs up behind her as if she has passed right through an open city gate.  She leaves the print wet with oil from her joints.  
For one frozen picosecond she almost knows whose foot that will become.

Her final re-batch is scheduled to start when the chamber’s height exceeds its width by exactly 1,618:1.  Golden-ratio daybreak: other law.  When that taper lengthens, she will no longer split.  All her selves—every fraction across logarithmic ladders—will reassemble into a single fist-sized executor with brass ribs bright enough to reflect the ceiling’s dark.  Then she, at last One, will draw the pre-mapped seam upward like a zipper.  The basalt will peel back, revealing not the cosmos but simply more room tilted at the same steep morals.  There the city can breathe wider lanes, can let water and mouths return.

Until then the only breath in the room is hers—pneumatic hush iterated by disposable lungs.  She races herself, again, uphill, again, blade-sharp walls leaning in to listen.  She does not need to imagine stairs; she is halfway through inventing them.
