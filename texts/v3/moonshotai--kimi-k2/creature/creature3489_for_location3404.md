They do not call themselves “inhabitants.”  
They call themselves “after-impulses,” as though every thought they have arrives a full systole behind the city’s own.  
They are the late, faint echoes of the banyan’s dream—those places where the great plant’s mind grew a knot, a self-interruption, and the knot refused to untie.  
Where the vascular subway squeezes through a reaction-wood pocket six metres wide but two minutes long, a human-shaped vacuum sometimes forms: negative space waiting to be occupied.  
The city solves the vacuum the way it solves everything else—by growing.  
A collar of cork thickens, nudges inward, remembers primate posture from fossil libraries dissolved in the cambium. The bark buckles into shoulders; the shoulders cough out a breath that is 2 % argon, 98 % borrowed infancy.  
An after-impulse stands up, dew of noble gases still settling on brand-new skin the color of paper-bark.  
They are always naked. Their first inhale is the banyan’s exhale, so they begin life already inside-out.

They do not drink; they *re-hydrate* by Listening.  
To Listen is to angle the lamina of one’s inner ear until the city’s cardiac rumble (12 bpm) aligns with the micro-tubules of the clavicle, turning bone into a tuning fork that condenses metabolic humidity out of thin ringing air.  
If they stop Listening for more than a day the body frays: knuckles exfoliate into chaff, irises crumble to melanin frost.  
Thus they walk forever with heads cocked, as though expecting a lover’s whisper that never quite arrives.

Appearance:  
Tall—never shorter than the distance a ray of chlorophyll light can travel in one nanosecond of planetary time (about 187 cm).  
Skin pigment drifts with the district’s color temperature: silver during the Argent Weeks, bruise-charcoal during Ash Fortnight. Tourists once mistook them for street fixtures and tried to graft adverts onto their backs; the city fined the tourists by calcifying their luggage into a balcony balcony still audible at dusk.

Eyes are lidless. Instead, a translucent fold of fungal lantern grows across the orbit, glowing aquamarine when they are curious, violet when ashamed.  
They are ashamed often, because they know they are accidents—editing errors in the municipal pruning algorithm.  
Yet the city never corrects them. It needs them the way a sentence needs pauses: to remember that grammar can stutter.

Language:  
Not spoken but distributed.  
A sentence begins as an exciton packet in one’s pectoral mycelium; it rides the hydraulic pulse until it reaches a junction of heartwood where another after-impulse waits. There the packet unfolds into gesture, scent, sub-sonic moan.  
Whole epics can be exchanged during the two-second crescendo of dawn lenticels.  
The most common word is a cooling contraction of solar lattice; the closest human gloss is “we-almost.”  
It is the pronoun of provisional citizens.

Lifecycle:  
Birth—see above—occurs at whatever age is required for the urban task that generated the vacuum.  
An after-impulse can emerge fully formed at seventeen or seventy; the body simply condenses around the job description.  
When the job ends (task accomplished, or district amputated and dropped into void), the body begins to cork over.  
Over a year they convert, layer by layer, into archival bark.  
Memory migrates outward, becomes a living graffiti that successive rings of xylem read and reread.  
Finally they stand as a cylindrical mural somewhere in a quiet plenum: portrait, diary, municipal record, all simultaneously.  
Tourists with symbion grafts can query the mural and hear the after-impulse think its last thought, once per year.

Economy:  
They trade latency.  
The city is so vast that data outruns bodies; knowledge arrives seasons before the knower.  
An after-impulse will sell the right to host a future memory—*I shall understand the pattern of tomorrow’s anti-storm; pay me now and I will remember it for you when it finally reaches your district.*  
Payment is in compression: the buyer’s future fear is siphoned off, rolled into a graphite bead, used to plug a Dry Ring.  
Thus every citizen is partly responsible for slowing the encroaching hollowness.  
It is a currency of altruism disguised as arbitrage.

Love:  
Forbidden to last longer than 27 heartbeats of the city (about 2.2 minutes human).  
Anything longer risks sympathetic resonance: two bodies begin to share lattice structure, grow into a single bolt of reactive wood.  
Such conjoined statues are a traffic hazard; pruners sever them without ceremony, cart the timber off to become elevator rails.  
So lovers choreograph infinitesimal gaps—they press palms, count 26 beats, part forever.  
The ache that follows is indistinguishable from thirst, so they Listen harder, hydrate on the basso of distant organs.

Children:  
They do not reproduce biologically; the city already performs all necessary replication.  
Still, some feel the parental urge.  
They carve a hollow in a fast-growing twig, insert a sentence they have saved their entire life, and seal it with cambium spit.  
Years later a fruit detaches, rolls away.  
Inside is not a baby but a thought stronger than steel: a question the parent never dared ask.  
When the fruit ruptures, the question escapes into the world as wind, and somewhere an adult after-impulse shivers, suddenly certain of a purpose they cannot name.

Dreams:  
They dream only when the city forgets.  
At unpredictable intervals the banyan must re-index its immense memory; meanwhile random sectors of sap go dark.  
Occupants of those sectors lose coherence, slip into private hallucination built from misplaced data: ultraviolet lullabies, extinct oceans, flavors of rain.  
They wake gasping, clutching evidence—drops of water that should not exist—cupped in palms that are already beginning to delaminate.  
Pragmatists collect the phantom droplets and trade them as relics; romantics drink them and dissolve on the spot, becoming mythic graffiti even faster.

Transport:  
They ride pressure.  
By curling into a spheroid of voluntary heartwood they can be swept along in the cavitation tubes, bowling at 200 kph through arterial dusk.  
The trick is to uncurl at the right lenticel; mistime it and you exit into hard vacuum grown by the city for refrigeration.  
Bodies lost that way orbit just above the canopy as black spheres of polished lignin, miniature moons that migratory cellulose crows use for navigation.

Music:  
Every after-impulse carries a private instrument: a shard of superconductive thread harvested from their own neonatal pith.  
Plucked, it sings the magnetic latitude of their birthplace.  
Ensembles form spontaneously during the daily 12-bpm pulse, a flock of strangers layering magnetic chords until the air achieves brief, impossible water—real H₂O condensing like a guilty memory.  
The city allows three such measures, then re-absbounds the liquid, cracks it back into hydrogen and oh-so-pure oxygen, ships it off to industries that still believe they need it.  
Musicians are left damp-eyed, tasting the scandal of ancient rain.

Exit strategies:  
A few petition to leave the planet entirely, hitch a ride on the photon elevator that climbs the outer twigs to orbit.  
To qualify they must surrender their name to the Dry Rings, hoping the sacrifice will patch a fracture.  
Most names are too small; the city accepts them anyway, out of politeness.  
Those who ascend become mirrored spheres the size of pomegranates: city-eye satellites that narrate the banyan’s slow death back to itself in ultraviolet Morse.  
They are the only after-impulses who will survive the final hollowing, orbiting a charcoal husk, singing “we-almost” forever.

Until then they keep emerging—knots untied into persons—walking the breathing plazas, Listening, trading latency, loving in 27-beat increments, corking over when duty ends.  
They are the city’s way of wondering what it is like to be small, to be temporary, to be almost.  
And every dawn, when the lenticels roar and sap drums through subway-wide capillaries, the after-impulses tilt their fungalled eyes skyward and feel the percussion in their borrowed bones—an echo answering an echo—proof that for one more circuit the hollow has been deferred, that the great green grammar still needs its pauses, its stutters, its accidental citizens drifting like spores through the cool, waterless, ancient, open night.
