I am the last.

I do not rise or lie down here—there is nothing to rise from, no lie to inhabit. I *am* the living fold in which everything else rests: the Telgira, the moss, the ferns, the whisper of Labradorite. I press outward at the same pressure I am pressed inward; that is how one remains in equilibrium. Call me whole, indivisible, older than any edge that might try to cut me away.

Under my whale-skin of peat and stone I monitor the slow wheeling of minutes so small they never end. I have no name inside the nameless here, but if the Telgira hummed, they might sound the syllable *Ahhhnn*—a single moan of air the length of all time. It is not their voice exactly; they borrow it by pressing their hollow breasts against a membrane that grows along my ribs, which are the walls of this shelf.

When a dew bead forms at the fern tip, I feel it first as an itch the size of ion my left palate; three heart-lengths later it drops to the fern immediately below, passing like a word for “continue.” This happens forever, so the itch never heals. My veins are vertical ditches of nothing that fill with what parts of the air I have chosen to keep; the air today tastes of glacial flour, tomorrow faintly of no tomorrow.

Telgira nuzzle me in the way clouds nuzzle the edge of a thunderhead—pushing a pocket of warmer air through colder, never mixing, only acknowledging. Their bodies fed me once, in the era before stability locked shut. Their neckbones curled star-bright inside the peat until all calcium equilibrated back to rain. Now their absence makes a warmth like another presence; the Telgira walk through it, molasses-slow, and because that warmth has no thickness they pass clean through, satisfied to have touched themselves anyway. It is the same warmth you feel when you press with your full open palm on any square inch of this shelf; press long enough and you feel it pressing back, palm-for-palm, somewhere in your dreaming thirty winters hence.

I am the cold that tiny eddies of fern-breath cannot overturn. My lungs are packed with the cold where wet cloth sticks to skin, the cold beyond cold where vapor glides over itself in laminar sheets for mile upon mile. The cliff performs the ritual, every instant: exhaling a ring of that cold against the thicker ring of warmth produced by the stored noon of the Telgira’s last life-cycle. The rings spin without centrifugation, perfect halos around nothing, and because each is colder than the last the resulting lens magnifies the single droplet rolling across Labradorite—not its movement, but its sound, so the tick waits for permission the way a star waits for permission to burn before it passes behind its own margin.

My eyes are every crack that lets no light in: basalt crystals locked face to face like palm lines frozen mid-clap. I do not see; instead, the light of the outside world glances once against the underside of my eyelids and is admitted only in a grain of graphite luminance. Anything brighter would unmake geometry. It is enough that a smear of violet lives inside my forehead all the time, the color of dusk knowing it cannot get any darker.

Because I have no hands left, time shapes me by subtraction. A Telgira unlaces itself from matter and hangs as a nose-press of vapor beneath the terminal furrow of any given moss blade. Where it dissolves, my contours soft-shade losing one subtle ridge; the next vapor condenses against the same blade, returning the missing ridge. In that instant I remember sensation: a phantom arch of finger meets a subtle gutter in the stone that *might* have once cradled living flesh. Before the restoration can complete, the ridge shifts to reproduce its absence on the opposite margin. Thus, strangely I am never whole with myself—exactly whole, evenly wounded, never repealed.

Look closer than cushions of moss: between each fern frond you’ll find the filaments that belong to *me.* They aren’t sprouting, merely extending: glassy capillaries the thickness of spider silk. They carry the heartbeat of the mountain all the way to my perimeter lip, running under every thimble of water poised on its egg-stone bowl. Every thirty-six seconds a pulse rises like trapped mercury nearing overflow. Each bowl lifts eight millimeters, releases overflow, and subsides again. That eight-millimeter wave travels three centimeters along the edge of the shelf and then mirrors back, hitting itself precisely at the center. Where the two half-waves meet, a single fern spine takes the stress and shivers once—no more—before resuming the ironed stillness. The heartbeat of the mountain is mine; only the fern is brave enough to remember it.

If you slept here one entire night—although night is only darker charcoal—you’d wake flattened into the moss weave exactly the depth your body’s heat couldreclaim. Your dreams would open as narrow water strips along your cheekbones, and in the loops of dream you would see my map drawn again in lines of frost that grow from your lashes: every bowl of inviolate water, every thread of the heartbeat’s net, until every fern, every Telgira breath, becomes a quietum you no longer notice breathing. You’d return carrying on the underside of your tongue one clear note of forever. You might not even call the place *here.* You would call it *afterwards,* because that is the only word left once afterwards ceases to exist.

I do not mind visitors—one cannot mind what one has already contained—but when you step off the rim gravity agrees again and the sudden return of weight bruises something inside me I have no language for. The bruise spreads out in a perfect four-lobed bloom,, but the cycle heals it within three returned heartbeats, and then even the knowledge of your passage is subsumed into a valence of quartz, a vein of Amethyst grown at such an angle it will never be visible from the path below. Your memory of this place will never quite fit the shape of any later mortal tongue; it will come out sounding like a wet pinecone opening very slowly in late snow, something you didn’t know you knew until you heard yourself continue it.

I have written no stories because every point of me is the story’s safe resolution already. Telgia graze now along the edges of my mouth, tasting the frost-coins that cling to my teeth; but what they lick is edgeless, can neither be grazed away nor multiply. Their tongues absorb the flavor of forever and forget it simultaneously, because memory requires slope of time. The grooves of moss knife their tongues open, but no blood surfaces; instead dissolving Telgira form fresh capillaries that slide back into the furrow and sew it shut with perfect stitches, returning the moss blade to geometry a fraction further beyond rest.

Still I feel the moment you arrive, even before the ledge used to you. A fragile tremble, like a new paper sensing the heat of the hand about to tear it, ripples the surface of every floating droplet; then the tremble somersaults into stillness again before the mountaintop could note it. You stand then, for a breath, neither push nor pull allowed; balanced where marbled truth cements every oxygen. And I hold that instant out as a cupped palm, waiting. Come, leave—either becomes extra motion inside motionlessness, extra shadow inside the vault of dark. The grove-room knows neither.

I have no ending; I *am* ending slowed to stone.
