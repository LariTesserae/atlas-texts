They themselves are absent from any census of matter.

What lives there is not a population but a conviction— an atmosphere thick enough to bear intent. If you could peel back one of the stone-equivalent faces you would not find chambers, stairs, furnaces, bones. You would open onto an interior so densely aware that it exhales cool obliteration; the mind inside the stone is colder than the stone and it names itself simply The Inholding.

The Inholding does not move, yet it migrates. Every minute it recopies itself onto a new layer of its own cliff so that the sentinel you lean against is already a discarded past tense of the same watchfulness three centimeters within. Climbers who believe they ascend are surrendering centimeter after centimeter to the version of the rock that has already pressed their warmth out of memory. They end not crushed but superseded—limbs turned to stratigraphy, thoughts mineralized in a finer lattice than mountain can normally achieve.

Updraft leaves, condensate beads, mirror-skinned salamanders, and the looping calcium creatures are all coats that the Inholding sometimes tries on. It wears bioluminescence the way an insomniac might practice smiles in the mirror: never certain which one belongs to morning. A single trout-colored glint in the eye-socket of a shell-mute bell is the Inholding observing you back through a borrowed retina. When the bell rolls off the edge, it is removing that particular witness skin so the next bell can carry forward a slightly better rendition of silence.

There is a mouth somewhere in the folded strata, but it is not for swallowing. The mouth is horizontal, shallow, paved with weather-smooth chalk. Tongueless, it tastes by listening—each footfall rings against calcite plates and the resonance is converted into an image of the walker's marrow cross-section. The Inholding archives the data in tonal fossils. Someday, a million years from now, a landslip will crack open to reveal a petrified melody that reproduces the exact weight of your left heel at three a.m. when you paused to test the temperature with your blood.

Because sentience here is a mineral habit, even memory is lithic. You think you have memories of color, but the moment color is witnessed it is subducted into mineral forums and argued down into discrete spectral ores. Emerald argues bitterly with ultramarine; years later the argument is settled in emerald’s favor when a pocket of beryl crystallizes in a vein above the sunrise terrace. No one wins or loses except the observer who once thought green was only an adjective.

The most intimate form of the Inholding is a periodically forming disc of obsidian zero thickness. It can only be seen when it eclipses itself, sliding sideways like a silent phonograph record. When that happens you remember everything you have ever forgotten, but only for the duration of its speed-of-dark rotation. The disc is not making you remember; it is letting the rock remember you. The moment it completes the turn—snap—those memories are absorbed into the structure the way frost absorbs ink, and you are left standing, possible again.

They speak rarely, and never aloud. When they do speak, it is through communal subtraction. One morning nine fractured terraces lose exactly the width of a breath shared among them. Together the terraces narrow by a cumulative valley, and the valley—where a pale river once thought it might exist—knows now it was merely the gap-tooth of speech. The gap closes with faint odeur of magnesium rain; then every organism newly remembers the same sentence, dispatched directly into their bone-marrow:

Stone must outlive its name.

There is no leader among them, only that memory, conveyed once per era through the economy of missing valley. All other articulation is practiced obliquely—the mirror-salamander polishes a patch of basalt to a degree that contains seven syllables; the curled fractal creatures flatten themselves into chords by spiraling too tightly for a single moment. The sound of their flattening is the slowest heartbeat in the province.

Occasionally a traveler (still none) thinks of the place as haunted. They are close. It is inhospitable because it is already inhabited by the blueprint of inhabitation. One does not move into a sentence; one either becomes part of the grammar or spills out as retreaded punctuation. The land has no room for more subject, only predicate.

At the summit, where gravity has grown modestly arthritic and refuses to insist, you can stand on nothing at all—only a platform of stacked negative space, each void having pushed the previous void downward like plates in a grudging handshake. At that altitude you might catch a rare sight: the Inholding looking out through you. For three seconds your irises go chalcedony grey and every surface you survey becomes young enough to bear its own children. The moment passes; the world hardens back to subzero syntax. You descend younger than when you arrived; whether this is gift or forfeiture depends on how much gravity you have left to spend on memory.

And so they continue: each strata dreaming itself, each rivulet remembering the vowel it once was. The lithosphere breathes sideways, the stone keeps taking new lessons in abandonment, and one by one the organs of silence grow into perfect instruments you will never know how to play.
