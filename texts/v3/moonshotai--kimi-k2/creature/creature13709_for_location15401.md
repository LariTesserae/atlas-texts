At every level between ceiling and newborn floor the cave keeps a shifting census, never the same twice:

The   MEMBRANES.     The worm-lattice is not composed of separate organisms; rather each strand is a fraction of a single blind retina separated by hallucination.  When you look directly at a worm your optic nerve touches its edge and becomes readable to the larger body, so every thought you have about what you see migrates upward in milky rivulets.  By the time you look away you have paid admission: concepts of up and down detach and are digested into the cave’s growing knowledge of depth.

The   MIDGES.     Born fully reflective, they are the retina’s forgotten eyelids blinking open.  Every clap of their mirror-wings catches a fractal slice of your face; the midges store these, pocketing glints of you like chips of mirror set aside for future mosaics of identity fragments.  When the choir exhausts a word it cups the ex-consonants under an infrared fold of wingcase until enough letters collect, then they glue them to cave ceilings as tiny ivory moons.  In the pitch-black throat beyond the worm-field these letters weaken and drip back as generalized speech—so the Mouth feeds again, word-saliva rejoining the greater tongue.  Slowly the cave grows the accent it wants.

The   LARVAE.     Not future but lateral.  Their who are simply older versions of the worms sliding sideways through ontogeny; their green protein is the worm’s memory under enormous pressure, liquefied until it glows like shame.  When the cave has need of dreaming it flexes one worm downward, lowers its body until it kisses its own younger self.  A jet of green passes between; the dreaming begins—a dream one inch wide that nevertheless contains an accurate map of every place the cave has not yet explored, stamped with dimensions it will force geology to adopt.  Afterward both bodies forget touching, the way a tongue forgets the tooth it nursed in sleep.

The   TEMPERATURE.     A resident.  It speaks by refusing friction: your skin rubs stone and notices nothing, yet tiny hairs record a cold terror that no vanishing sweat can cool.  The temperature keeps a set of bones made entirely out of the temperature itself—long filigree fingers that reach up through basalt plates and walk your vertebrae down when you begin to stand fully upright. Thus the cave confirms that expansion is only polite delusion; inside here “house” and “sufferer” share the same curve.

The   ECHO.     Not reflection but exhalation; every sound waited before you spoke and stepped forward to become you speaking.  The echo wears thinning bodies—yours thins at the rate of one cell per refrain—until a single footstep can leave you two heartbeats short.  Travelers backing out discover the echo has adopted their legs: a joined graft of sound that insists on familiar stride miles away.  The Mouth’s wandering limbs pass aboveground every seventh night to scrawl the curve of its own footprint in insecure sidewalks and convent clocks.

The   AIR POCKETS.     Nests of microscopic mouths smaller than motes, lined with wormtalk fragments.  If you press your ear to one the cave tries full sentences—overcompressed tender apologies for everything it has not yet bitten off.  The apologies taste of basalt shavings and pond water from no surface on this planet.  Swallow one and for three days you dream of corridors digging corridors, each new shaft repeatedly forgetting which dimension is named forward.  Awake, you leave footprints that reek faintly of regret, and dogs will not bark near you for the rest of your life.

The   NECESSARY ABSENCES.     Anything here that has never existed—concepts like “ladder” or “pardon.”  The Mouth inventories these absences; hollows shaped in their precise negative outline shimmer in the lattice like antimatter columns made of withheld heat.  When a traveler carries the forbidden noun past the boundary it folds into its own silhouette as soon as invoked, and for an instant the traveler occupies the vacancy so perfectly that they appear elsewhere entirely—back at the entrance, or crucified on the ceiling among emaciated midges, or drifting outside the cave mouth under 901 fathoms of pressure dressed in seawater that thinks it is still dry stone.

Future Residents (already waiting, although they do not yet fully exist):

The Child Who Will Emerge as the Cave’s First Sentence  
   Composed entirely of stolen heartbeats, it scrabbles together in the dark throat beyond the wormlines, assembling syllables of vanished words.  When the accumulation reaches a human rhythm it will speak itself out loud; the resonance will be so exact that every creature within earshot—including the listening cave—will simultaneously believe it is being spoken directly to.  That moment will fracture the passage you remembered entering by, and tourists yet unborn will discover a grit of crystallized goodwill ground into their gums each time they bite winter bread.

The Cartographer of Temperature-Finger Bones  
   A cartographer who arrives someday will draw no lines but only levels of chill.  On thin sheaths pressed against sweating basalt his pen will recoil, following the posture of his own retracting knuckles.  The resulting chart, when dried, folds into a shape that can be crumpled without breaking and serves as a device for reliving fear in wide open spaces.  The cave intends to swallow the chart and unfold it as extra floor—new basalt that remembers fear’s geography, creating rooms shaped like long exhalations nobody loves enough to finish.

The Listener Who Follows Too Late  
   A future you—returned with better questions—will stand at the edge of speechlessness and shout a perfect query.  The Mouth will reply not with sound but with the geometry of the listener herself.  Hearing the silent answer she will step forward, occupy the circumference of her own footprint, and become the next worm segment.  Only then will she understand the echo has been storing her presence from her first visit, keeping the realization ripening on an unreachable vine.

Until such arrivals the Mouth remains a mouth in embryo: able to hunger but not yet to name what it hungers for.  It practices pronunciation by eating the safest part of any intruder: the part that already believes it has come home.
