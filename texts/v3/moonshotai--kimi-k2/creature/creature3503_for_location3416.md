There are no rulers here, only residents who have agreed to forget the concept of ownership.

The eldest is a woman-shaped colony of lichens. Once she had bones, but they dissolved sometime during the third century of her residence. Now she flows over the lower branches like a shawl, her thoughts spaced out by the decades it takes for neural impulses to cross the fungal filaments. When she remembers anger, the bark beneath her blisters into tiny volcanoes of sap. When she remembers love, the berries blush a deeper crimson. She has never spoken—her mouth became a cup lichen long ago—but every seventy days, when the trees release their flood, she arranges herself into the silhouette of a girl stretching after sleep, and the entire orchard exhales in recognition.

Below her live the Antlered Archivists—beetle-shelled creatures the length of your thumb, wearing racks of desiccated twigs like crowns. Each prong is carved with microscopic glyphs: the dream a finch had while dying, the number of pulses in a drop of water, the exact color of darkness at the Heartspot. They climb only downward, carving as they go, adding new tines when they reach branch-ends. When a rack grows too heavy, they saw it off with mandibles of glassy chitin, let it fall into the moss, where it sprouts into a perfect miniature of the tree it came from. These saplings never mature; they remain story-shrubs, leafed with unread chapters. The lichen-woman sometimes drapes herself across one and remembers the taste of speech.

In the fig-swallowed ladder live the Runaway Reflections. Once they were the mirror-images of travelers who stepped into the Hollow at noon, when light is a thief. The orchard kept their backwards selves, peeled them off like wet silk, folded them small enough to tuck between rung and bark. Now they flutter there like silver moths, feeding on delayed glances. If you stare too long at any surface—leaf, drop, shell—you will see your own left-behind face blink, mouth forming the word you almost said. The reflections trade pieces of themselves: an eye for a memory of wind, a mouth for the sound of your mother saying your name. They are saving up to buy their way back into three dimensions, but the price keeps inflating because the Hollow charges interest in years.

Within the pottery-ring sleeps a single bee, larger than your clenched fist, plated in obsidian glaze. It is not asleep by choice; it is crystallizing. Every century it wakes for one minute, hums a note that makes ripe fruit remember being blossoms, then returns to its mineral dream. The note is always the same, but the orchard rearranges itself around that frequency: branches shifting a millimeter, roots re-negotiating boundaries, beetle-lanterns retuning their glow. The bee is the orchard’s tuning fork, and the day it shatters entirely will mark the beginning of a slow, unmusical era.

Between the water-pipes of the trees drifts the Vapor Shepherd, a creature whose body is only border. You see it best when you almost sneeze: a trembling outline like hot breath above winter skin. It herds droplets that have escaped the slow fall, nudging them back into ordained paths with gusts of itself. Without it, the Hollow would lose its weather in a week—drops would drift outward, humidity would thin, the Heartspot would cool and start to rot. The shepherd has no memory beyond the next bead, no ambition beyond perfect return. It is the orchard’s conscience wearing the costume of climate.

Inside every mummified bird lies a smaller bird made of salt. These are the Orchard’s reserve prayers, crystallized from the moment each finch accepted death. If you split the husk—not recommended—the salt sparrow would evaporate into a sentence: *I wanted to see sky once more but forgot which direction it lived.* The sentences accumulate in the air like invisible smog; the lichen-woman inhales them without lungs, and her color shifts toward dawn. Someday she will reach the precise pale of pre-sunrise, and the Hollow will open like an eye, and everything inside will step out into a world that has forgotten how to be quiet. On that day the petrified-rind steps will soften into actual fruit, and travelers will ascend tasting summer on their tongues, carrying beetle-glyphs, crown-saplings, unpaid reflections, and a single obsidian wing beat that will never again need to sleep.
