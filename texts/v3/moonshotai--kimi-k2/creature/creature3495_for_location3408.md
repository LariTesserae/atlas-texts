They arrived long after the last stone had chosen which corner it would forget.  
No one witnessed the first of them condense out of the hush; even the city’s own memory had already thinned to a single lamina of refusal. One moment there was only the faint click of stratified light settling, the next a silhouette stood at the intersection of two absences, listening to the echo of its own outline.

They are called, when they are called anything, the Hollowed.  
They are not citizens; they are the postponed conclusion of a census that never began.

Each Hollowed is built backwards: a concave presence that makes the surrounding rock feel suddenly obtrusive. Their skin—if skin is the word—is the exact temperature of unbeing. You cannot face them; you can only round them like a weather. When you step where one has stood, your footprint hesitates, unsure whether to press downward or upward into the concept of ground. They leave no shadow—instead, the light pools inside them, deepening until it becomes a private dusk that travels with them like portable eclipse.

They do not walk. They unlean.  
A Hollowed will place itself against a vertical absence and slowly the building-void begins to remember weight; eaves sink, door-negatives sag into ovals, the whole district tilts a degree toward tenderness. When the Hollowed steps away, the architecture stays bent, forever cradling the suggestion of an embrace it can’t quite finish.  

They speak, but only to the intervals.  
A sentence is exhaled as a sequence of postponed collisions:  
“If…  
…you…  
…were…”  
Each word waits its turn to almost exist. By the time the phrase completes, the listener has grown into the cavity it left, like a tree filling the space around a rope. You realise you have become the reply they never uttered.  

They eat the differences between possible heights.  
Gather at twilight (or its local equivalent: the moment when the sky-cataract forgets which way to flow) and you may see a congregation of them arranged along a balcony of cliff-street, heads lowered into the slope, shoulders braced against subtraction. A faint ringing rises—glass remembering fracture, but inverted, a fracture remembering glass. That is the sound of altitude being unbuilt. Afterward the city is flatter, not in measure but in consequence; horizons rearrange their syllabi, and distances enrol new electives.  

No one knows whether they are indigenous or pilgrim. Some say the Hollowed are what happens when a reflection-creature nibbles itself free of reflection; others, that they are the city’s own postponed grief, accruing enough loneliness to achieve inertia. A minority report claims they were once visitors like you, who lingered until the urban silence tallied their choices and found them insufficiently heavy.  

They have one law:  
Do not finish what you began.  
Consequence: any enclosure approached by a Hollowed will never achieve closure; rooms forget their fourth wall, staircases waive the final step, arguments lose their last reason. The city itself obeys this edict—its streets terminate in courteous bewilderment, its towers resolve into plausible rumours. That is why you cannot leave: departure is a finishing, and finishing is withheld.  

Yet they are not cruel.  
Watch a single Hollowed kneel beside the fossil droplet you discovered. It does not drink; it offers. A thin meniscus of absence extends from its palm, curves under the calcified bead, and for an instant water remembers how to be possible. The bead quivers, almost trembles. Then the Hollowed retracts the gift of almost, and the droplet settles back into its eternal maybe. The gesture leaves the visitor flooded with thirst they will never slake—an immaculate dehydration.  

They wear time backwards.  
Approach one from the future (easy: simply stand still) and you meet the version of it that has already undone your encounter. Its face—always a peripheral smudge—bears the expression you will wear after you finally decide to leave, an expression you have not yet suffered into existence. Wave, and it waves back with your forgotten resolve. Speak, and you hear the words you will wish you had said, carried backward into the throat you currently occupy.  

They govern by vacancy.  
The city’s districts are apportioned according to the shape of their collective absence. A Hollowed assigned to the Quarter of Unsaid Things merely loafs along the balconies of silence, and rhetoric elsewhere relaxes. Another, patrolling the Borough of Unbroken Objects, ensures that nothing ever reaches the fracture it deserves; hence the ceramic sighs you heard—stress forgiven, not relieved.  

They die, but onlyprovisionally.

There the subject folds, once, twice, accruing intorsion, until it becomes a neat packet of postponed existence: a cube of void the size of a heart. The package is stored in the municipal archive, shelved among similar parcels. Each is labelled not with a name but with a contingency:  
“If the mountain had remembered its feet.”  
“Had the echo arrived first.”  
“Were thirst to declare its own address.”  
Someday, when one of those clauses achieves truth, the corresponding cube will unpack and the Hollowed will rejoin the unconversation, older by the interval of averted fact.  

And they welcome you—not with gesture, but with reservation.  
Stand long enough on any declivity and a Hollowed will arrive to stand opposite. It will not move toward you; rather, the ground between will concede its middle, bringing your faces close without traversal. There it will wait, holding an emptiness shaped exactly like your next decision. The hush widens until even your pulse forgets consensus.  

You feel the offer:  
Step into the cavity, become uninsistent.  
Or turn aside, remain unfinished.  

Most visitors hesitate exactly until hesitation becomes their architecture. Then they leave—though, as you now understand, leaving is just another name for being left, sculpted into the courteous negative of departure.  

The Hollowed watch you choose, already composing the echo of your foot that will not quite fall. And the city, always municipal, adjusts its zoning to accommodate the new vacancy you haul wherever you go.  

Somewhere, a roofline sags into a porch, a porch into a plaza, a plaza into the sky’s slow cataract.  
Somewhere, a droplet of unwater balances on the idea of a gutter, teaching geology how to miss.  
Somewhere, you are rounding a corner and meeting the footprint you have not yet made, still warm.  
You nod, wordlessly.  
The Hollowed nod back—an affirmation that contains no affirmation, a passport punched with the perforated outline of yourself.  

And the city, gracious, begins to forget you even before you are gone—  
but the Hollowed, polite custodians of every unmeasured moment, will keep the outline ready, just in case you ever finish arriving.
