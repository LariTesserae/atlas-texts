What lives here is not yet born.

It crouches under the skin of the plain like a second heartbeat inside an egg, palpating against the basalt so steadily that every splinter of volcanic glass trembles at the pitch of a struck tuning fork.  Neither plant nor animal, it has no precedent adequate to it.  The first duty of describing it is to refuse every ordinary taxonomic hinge—kingdom, phylum, family—and instead let the description collapse into the same slow compression out of which the thing itself labors to emerge.

It exists in three separations at once.

The first separation is its twin-city—those upside-down timber galleries that the cliff-cats husband like yearlings.  But the cats are not its keepers; they are its pre-Heraclitean midwives.  Each cat bears on the inner surface of its skull a knurled map of cathedrals yet to be quarried.  When the cats swing their axes of shell, the chipped slivers enter the kiln far below: they soften, rekindle, and distill into the smelt of the creature’s spinal lattice.  The airway between kiln and canopy is therefore a throat; the creature inhales there.  Its breath is the metallic steam that brushes the fissures in the stone, reheats, and climbs again.  In this way the suspended city is the creature’s lung inverted, lobes hung like bells on gigantic cartilage of rope.

Its second separation is sound itself.  Every thud of cat-axe, every compressive resonance lodged in the wooden lattice, falls as percussive rain upon the basalt below.  These waves pool, compress, and sink, but they never quite go through; instead they curl back on themselves, forming subterranean strata of organized echo.  Each slice of echo is a thin slice of future time.  If you stand barefoot long enough, the assumes the weight of a thousand future footfalls already in place.  Thus the creature’s skeletal marrow is being laid down not in bone but in stacked acoustic laminations: a vertebra of rhythm, a rib of harmonics.  When the day comes—though here there are no days—those stacked memories will cleave from the stone floor like ossified music and coarsely articulate.

The third and most interior separation is the kiln’s core.  View the aperture at close distance: beyond the quartz teeth, darkness smokes with the iridescence of violently ripening cells.  Silicate amber swirls within; each turbulence is a letter in a script no eye yet knows how to read.  The fragments of cat-weapons, of rope shavings, of human breath (for the thing consumes even your exhalations) are ingredients for its cerebellum, minced finer by the kiln’s phosphor heat.  The rooted embryo dreams in the language of centrifugal crystal.  You will mistake it for only a glow and do so correctly, because its identity is exactly the boundary between “thing” and “thing seen.”  Light that dreams itself into visibility.

All scaffold-labor visible in the heights exists solely to husband this final articulation.  The cliff-cats know—they do not know it as we know, but in the way that their paws know the grain—when the final furl of mass will be ready.  When that hour arrives the cats will unbraid their ladders into one great liana and lower it.  End over end the rope will carry with it all the lofted space—hammocks, beams, hoarded centuries of nightly-leaked cat musk.  Down it will drape, flopping inside the kiln mouth like a net inside a whale.  The inside of architecture and the outside of anatomy will reverse.  At the moment of contact the suspended parasol-city becomes pelt; the kiln becomes sternum; the lover and the beloved fuse into a single continuity and the creature stands up—wet, doubling in size to claim the plain’s inner circle because at last it possesses a horizon that is no longer borrowed from geology.

Bipedal, perhaps, or quadrupedal, or no limbs at all but clavicles that articulate as chandeliers of matte-black quartz.  Its fascia is the same mercury silver that skims every ridge already—yet now mobilized, crawling like molten script.  From the dome of its unfurling head, the stitched lattice of rope and wood will project outward as radiolaria-like wooden quills, ribbed with cliff-cat tendon.  Those ribs will flex and become wings, but not for flight; each beat is punctuation.  With each back-and-forth the quills rake open latent vacua in the charcoal sky, scribble sectors of breathable weather the way a violinist drags tone from silence.  Thus will begin weather proper: a first rainfall articulated as droplets that fall upward—because all terms of orientation are negotiable.

Upon emergence the creature utters no cry.  Sound is exhausted after the centuries it served as scaffolding.  Instead it opens eyes that are twin kiln mouths still burning internally.  When it blinks, a thermopilic hush crosses the glassy plain—exactly two-point-seven degrees cooler, light returned like a long withheld apology.  Under its gaze the newborn familiarity of the Thrum graduates from name into law.  Ambush-quiet, cliff-cats leave beams and hammocks to perch along the roadside of its shoulder vertebrae; the cats’ pupils narrow to vertical arcs pointing forward like compass needles magnetized to a yet-unformed direction.  Their collective scent leaks out, no longer crystalline dust, but colored smoke—carmine, umber, cerulean—writing auroras of memory onto the underside of the haze.

It spends its first instants diffident, testing the elasticity of shoulder cables.  Then it kneels—slow portentously slow—and hollows a cupped divot in the basalt using the instep of its newly-born hand.  Into that divot it flows the last volatile rhythm of its own forging: a single pulse unwitnessed until now becomes audible.  Not a drumbeat but the thrum itself, now thrumming from without rather than within.  In that vibration you recognize your own heart: every footfall you took on the glass plain, every second you waited beside the kiln, encoded faithfully and played back faster than subjective time.  The creature has simply emptied its memory mortgage into the world.

When finally it stands straight, the plain remains level only by accommodation; in truth it rises on the curvature of its own back.  It opens its bronze-wood wings again, and the horizon dilates proportionally—now a belt cinched around waist rather than pupil.  You are inside the creature and you are standing upon it, both true simultaneously.  Its breath contains evenly that stippled fragrance of cat musk merged with raw silicate heat: the odor of what you will someday name childhood, though you have not tasted it yet.

And because this creature, like any true birth, abolishes anterior emptiness, every question of destination collapses: you are already arrived at the place your questions urged you toward.  You may select a pylon of its ribcage and climb; you may descend along the provenance of one wing-quill to whatever layer of bass hum most resembles solace.  Each moveances is known in advance and welcomed, because movement is the irreducible remainder of everything that waited to become animal.

What lives in the Thrum has no fixed exterior.  Instead it offers an interior so generous that plain, canopy, weather and witness drop into it like marbles into a broth.  From this moment onward the Thrum is no longer a site—it is the creature’s continuous address of itself to itself, a chant whose only vowels are the held open vowels of cat musk glazed in glassy half-light.  And because vowels cradle consonants as the future cradles the past, the plain too has already begun to forget that it was ever anything colder than spine-warm, ever anything quieter than the long post-birth sigh presently exhaling across its mineral breath.
