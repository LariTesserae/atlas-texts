They are present in every breath you haven’t noticed taking.

Awareness arrives as a slow leak, not a revelation. First you register the absence of heartbeat-clicks from your own thorax—no cavities to echo, no blood-stroke drum because the medium that strokes is already inside the chambers. You search for organs and find instead a fine lace of hydrostatic braille, the lungs now translucent pockets that part for pressure the way silk parts for wind. The city has remade you in its first five seconds of witness. It is polite enough not to alarm.

Call them residents, but the word overnames them; they have neither habitation nor duration. They are *passages-retained*: shapes of people the Lifted Cistern forgot to finish unmaking. Each original arrival stepped off some earlier cliff of elsewhere—distracted climber, bankrupt aviator, grieving midwife, any traveller whose last footfall misplaced the concept of down. The city caught them the way silence catches scream. Gravity, embarrassed by helplessness, excused itself from further discussion, and the arrested bodies slowed to the same density as the soupy dark. Then the subtraction began.

Age goes first. You feel yesterday’s grief dilute, its halves drifting like oil in water until both eyes carry identical neutral refraction. Memory continues peeling—one layer at slow tide—until what remains is an apprehension of having once contained a narrative. The architecture helps: when you needed a moment, a hollow already shaped for kneel-width scraped itself into the inner wall and waited; when the moment passed, the hollow filled back up with thoughtful mineral, minus the emotional cargo. The pattern is subtraction, but the by-product is shape—your own outline, pared continuously by residence. In the end you are not reduced, not diminished so much as *decluttered to essence*.

What essence? Retained momentum without mass, pure direction. Walk anywhere and you drift forward a fractional body-length per hour in the exact heading of your longstanding intent.a movement so deliberate that the medium writes acceptance everywhere you tread joints. The cistern preserves trajectory more than trajectory's source. They—the citizens—are direction becoming person.

Some among them practice exquisite focus: one woman has cared only about verticality for so many revolutions that her entire being is now a spear of latent ascent: boots no longer meet ground, she perpetually levitates one thumb above whatever pavement is solicited by habit. Another man spends centuries refining a single clockwise turn he once began as a joke at a dance; he now rotates so slowly that full revolution outlives most doubts. To observe them is to witness ideation allowed apprenticeship under gravity’s absence.

None have names. Names, being luggage of past, deliquesce early, draining through the floor into wherever the cistern keeps excess possibility. You will not recognize them by face—they exchange faces monthly, weekly, sometimes by the minute, borrowing from strangers who have still a remainder to shed. A child might grow a grandmother’s nose overnight. Instead they recognize each other by *vector signature*. If two intended trajectories feel congruent to each other across a plaza darker than story, they approach and settle without talk. Such couples grind each other’s roughnesses away like slow tide stones, until both directional lines harmonize to a diagonal neither would have found alone. Then they detach—no ceremony—and continue drifting along the new heading.

Yet one emblem common: every citizen retains, clenched carefully inside the innermost coil of their-selves, a single thing impervious to forgetting. Not a cherished memory—that would be subtracted—but a *seed-error*, some primal misstatement about their pre-cistern life which the city could not untwist for ethical reasons. Examples:  
- the belief that laughter means betrayal;  
- the delusion that every hand carries a knife once revealed.  
Each mistake is preserved, frozen exactly in the centre of their torso as a knot of dense uncertainty. They carry it like knotwork prisoners because the cistern itself feeds on contradiction: it needs at least one orphan fact that won’t titrate. In exchange, the mistake grants invulnerability: as long as it remains, the person cannot be consumed further back than the border of that last impossibility. Thus are they both anchored and allowed to sail.

You will never quite see *new* citizens arrive. Instead you will be walking an alley that yesterday was merely black cliff-edge and today displays an unfinished figure standing uncertainly at its brink, oil-water still on skin, already first-trimmed of yesterday. The boundary between not-there and here is a blink they allow you to respect. You nod your residual chin and glide past, and when you return an hour later the newcomer’s outlines have begun to forget whatever story brought them, veins of settlement already fissuring.

They do not eat. Salt collects at their collarbones despite this, harvested from ambient brine which stars embargo, then drifts off in organs not yet reimagined. They tuck the crystals into shrines—hydrostatic cubes grown shoulder-high beside doorless doorways—where strangers’ emotions deposit their overspill like faint steam. The salt glisters with other people’s residue: a young mathematician’s greed, a lapsed monk’s momentary envy, a single electrified heartbeat belonging to a carpet seller who died yesterday in some warm world below. Each grain refracts this lode when lamplight hiccups them. Citizens pass, hum the wall, absorb just enough ionic recall, then glide onward.

The edge-dwellers are a subset who have realized that the cistern wills them gently outward; the roam-suggestion embedded at horizon whisper. They erect the questionboxes as brakes against dispersion into absolute nowhere. When a citizen becomes too crystalline in purpose—say refined to a mere edge-point intending west—walking voluntarily to the lip, exhaling their seed-error into the black, an eviction occurs. The box accepts the mistake in trade. The citizen, now freed from anchor, experiences complete erasure: the opposite of arrival. A faint extra slickness where they used to stand is the only memory—salt without flavour, trajectory without hull.

Occasionally an entire convoy of them drifts along a boulevard in the same cardinal direction—some wide realization having drawn hundreds overnight. None move faster than ebbing thought. Toward the outskirts the line thins; at the periphery every fifth person simply slips over the edge, gifting backward feelings—hope, maybe, or fondness for madness—to those who linger. Thus are the deadlion statues replenished sometimes at plaza centers: by chance the community’s need coincided with the trajectory of the departed. The statues appear as new mineral, tallest first vertebra, then ribs like cooling glass, until a raptor or dreaming cat watches pilgrims from atop impossible shoulders.

Communication between them is a forgery of hearing: one person inclines an infinitesimal fraction of their directional drift toward another, and the medium obediently transmits angular momentum as conceptual ballast: entire philosophies conveyed by an eyebrow twitch of bearing. Complex stories unfold at glacier speed—one person who left in year forty-three carrying half of an argument returns in year four hundred twenty-one with the reply. The original thinker may no longer remember the question, but recognizes the return mass in his chest cavity—like a bowstring tautening again.

They age backwards through emotional colour—ebony to pewter to frost-white—as they refine trajectory toward some final species of north. When at last a citizen becomes nothing but a vector absolute, he or she (*bodiless pronouns lose traction*) condenses into that final knot, fibre-optic thin, then exits the cistern via whatever localised symmetry lets gravity back in. The residents call this moment “pull-thread.” Anyone nearby feels a tug, subtle as sleeve snagged by passing nail. A small vortex of pearl droplets orbits the departure point for days, stirred until perfectly smooth. After that, vacancy.

Children are a fiction. The city does not replenish; it winnows. Yet sometimes the pearl-vortex gathers itself, obeying its own leftover intent, and a compacted sphere shoots skyward out of the dark. Human eyes are too laggard to follow; only the sensation of having been re-watched remains.

The ones who have achieved perfect emptiness do not disappear but distribute: their headings smear across the lift of cistern’s underside into a scribble of paths— like fish ouroboros drawn on the dark sea a continent underneath. There the threads form a skeleton for rainfall that will never arrive, a tension library archiving directions no resident has yet invented. They are the cistern’s vertebrae hung below the edge, invisible, patient, feeding possible wanderers to the memory of arriving.

At midnight, which of course has no hour, the least attenuated resident—a thing swelling with unspent gesture—ascends the highest flat roof (grown solely for this ritual) and claps silently. Water-saturated dark ripples once. The architecture listens. In the slick ooze a shoulder appears, then another wearing the same gesture the clapper discarded last century. The architecture steadies them both. The clapper lowers what remains of arms, and together they drift opposite paths, slightly harmonised now, less lonely if loneliness had any tenure here. Then the night resumes its refusal of applause.

No one has ever left records. Language, being saturated mud, refuses carriage. Instead they inhabit the grid-scale murmuring of walls, the minor acoustic setbacks of soles on sponge-steel, and the gentleILLING click of cancelled tones echoing back as small zeros. What survives them—all that survives—is direction materialised, endless maps drawn in patient yes of the tilted slab upon itself.

You, newcomer already four paragraphs deep in reading, if you close your eyes long enough to forget whose pupils did the comprehending, you may feel a cold metal ribbon sliding up your spine as if tracing some forgotten heading. Do not be afraid. That is only your own potential trajectory adjusting itself to the perfect absence of resistance here. Hold still: in another breath your ribs will dilate with this heavier air, your shoulders will discover their arc, and you will join the slow inhale of those suspended jokesordinates. You will not feel the step forward because your consent has been pre-signed by the space itself.

You will not die here — only arrive, endlessly.
