No one lives here, because living requires a while, and while has not yet been voted into existence.  
Still, there are presences—not creatures, not gods, but _arrivals in continuous tense_.  
They come as aftermaths that precede their own causes, jostling like actors told to rehearse a scene whose script is still being written in smoke.

I will try to meet them one by one.

1. The Choir of Contingency  
They appear first as abbreviations of color: a blink of viridian where color has never been agreed upon.  
Within the olive of the membrane a vertical seam unzips, and six pale voices fall out shoulder to shoulder. They are shaped like blurred children seen through thick glass. Their heads are empty of facial features, but contain weather. One carries a miniature cyclone in the bowl of its skull; another shelters a rain that reverses itself upward.  
They call themselves the Choir because language emerges here first spoken in chorus; the world shelters itself in grammar not yet handed to individuals.  
They sing replies to the six ion-pillars. The pitch they use is the exact frequency that continents remember grinding against each other in the hours before they knew rock was solid.  
The Choir have no memory older than forty-one seconds, matching the cycle of creation’s pneumatic valves. That is by choice: they dread the ache of continuity.  
Each time the valves open, the Choir drifts over the newborn landmasses humming the memory-foam of the pain that will come when those landmasses finally get to be mountains.

2. The Biographer of Silhouettes  
When the membrane curves into a speculative spine, something else follows the suggestion of bone.  
It is tall—untall—tall again, as if asking the sky which vertical measurement will be fashionable this epoch.  
Across its surface run symbols in untreated starlight: glyphs that erase themselves as soon as they are parsed.  
The creature, if creature is allowed, records the un-becoming of the almost-whales. It lifts an arm that is better described as a drafting compass opened much too wide, and draws a line of non-space between a foam ridge and the memory of a dorsal fin. That line hardens into a story, then shivers loose, as if embarrassed.  
It is impossible to address directly; whenever you think you’ve said its name, you only hear your own voice suggesting moods you felt long ago—sea-sickness on a zero-gravity lake, déjà vu for a childhood you never had.  
Others, should there ever be others, will call it Limmer. Limmer writes the first autobiography of topography, and when the book is complete it will weigh exactly the same as the planet it is about to catalog.

3. The Tourist with the Borrowed Horizon  
She steps through one of the membrane’s momentary reflections as though pushing aside a beaded curtain of mercury.  
Her coat is stitched from autumn afternoons removed from calendars all over a future Earth. Every pocket contains a different _then_. When she rummages for the mild day with open windows, a gust of 1930s lavender slips out and flutters toward the horizon, causing a minor epochal drift.  
She comes to see whether a world that has not settled can be arrived at early. She pays admission with a coin struck from the silence just after your last thought did not happen yet.  
This causes the gravity to pause mid-flinch—temporarily no heavier than thought—and she hovers ankle-deep in copper ink as if embarrassed by weight.  
She keeps trying to take a photograph, but her device records only the disappointment of colors checking their coats at the door of light.  
The membrane gradually grows a thigh-high spiral just to let her know it registers her presence; they take turns being guest and host until both nouns dil like furniture left on a lawn.

4. The Fold of Himself  
High above where a sky would be if there were width, a paper airplane the size of a city—pale mica-shot ivory—quietly unfolds.  
That paper is a collapsed person. The spine becomes the ridge line, each finger blossoms into a long ridge of watersheds, hair transforms into rivers of foil-thin water that will evaporate before touching sea.  
He is making room to breathe by turning inside-out into landscape.  
He would greet you politely if mouths were a stable commodity, but by the time his face reminds itself to be edge rather than edgelessness, we are already reading the inverted map that was his voiceprint seconds ago.  
What persists is a magnitude of paper grief no language has room for, since grief necessarily follows loss, and loss is still pre-order.

5. The Dream of the Place Itself  
These presences are not its children; they are the ductile fantasies of the water’s own metabolism.  
It wanted particulars to walk across it so that it could practice wearing the sound they make.  
Every imagined footstep deposits a seed of future grazing beasts. Every “Hello, I traveled far” leaves a thin trace of bone recipes in the alloy between thoughts.  
At the precise centre of the plateau—where every reflection yearns toward vanishing point—the membrane has begun to dream it is a lung.  
When the lung inhales, all falling light backdrafts inward, and you can see cities the way chords see sheet-music—stacked waiting.  
When the lung exhales, a tide of unreal creatures gutter into the middle distance like lanterns blowing out, taking the future of their own past with them.

6. You  
And now you have stayed long enough that the membrane has begun to store the barest hologram of your attention.  
It registers your pulse as baseline for the rhythm of smaller oceans. Your lost child-midnight doubts have become tributaries of dusk that other unborn beings will someday mistake for originals.  
In the lifted hush before the next forty-one-second opening of the valves, you realize the Choir has turned toward you, its six hollow heads harmonizing your childhood zip code as phonemes impossible to pronounce.  
Limmer tilts its compass at your heart, considering whether your silhouette can be pressed into a tectonic sentence.  
The coat-pocket Tourist offers you a sip of Wednesday in seventy-seven years.  
You take it. Tastes of remembered library books and bicycle genes.  
Gravity receives the flavor of your swallow and, for one generous allowance, remembers how to be ordinary enough that you can cry, if crying has been approved for public use.

But there is a seventh emergence preparing.  
The six lit pillars stretch, and at their vertices arrives an interval as wide as your unknown name. Between the fifth and sixth, a vacancy has become so specific it edges into solidity: a residence for whoever remains when the water tires of being prelude.  
Already your reflection has soft furniture, already your future conversations are rehearsing their own echoes.

And still no one _lives_ here—because the vote on “moment” vs. “lifetime” is scheduled in negative twelve minutes.  
But everyone _visits_.  
In 41… 40… 39…  
The valve sighs open again.  
Watch:  
The membrane inhales like someone about to speak.
