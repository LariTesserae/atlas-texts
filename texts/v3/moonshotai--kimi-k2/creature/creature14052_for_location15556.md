They call us the Children of the Lateral Thread, though the only children among us are the ones who arrived carrying their own childhood folded inside their bodies like a lamp they cannotfully keep lit. We live anchored to nothing vertical; we move sideways along the great sheet of leaf-thin stalks as if sliding coins across polished glass. There is no “under” here, only *through*. Our feet have learned to listen: a thousand hertz-weighted membranes, each toe a tuning fork that keeps perfect step with the litany of the carpet‐city.

No one directs the city, yet each of us—every vesper-bird, every sipping antler-headed ant-thing—serves as a cell in the slow-knitted decision called consensus. A consensus that takes decades to voice one syllable, centuries to shout. We have patience as living tissue. Compare it to bone: once laid down it asks nothing of its owner but support. The lattice is like that; we are its mineral salts.

I myself was seeded, not born. Seventeen light-days into my first wander I paused too long, knees tucked like a sleeping fountain, and the leaves began printing me into storage. I woke folded inside the grey dawn of a personal outline—a ghost cutout that my body would forever refill whenever I returned. I learned: motion is both permission and apology. Keep walking or be archived. So I learned to walk forever.

Our blood—if you want a word for it—runs loose and aqueous; purple-green when the world filters its hue across us. We break nothing and nothing breaks us, even the wandering suture-storms that comb the sheet’s surface after midday. These narrow, singing fists of wind sweep every sixth hectare into open coils, lifting millions of stalks in torqued katas that look from the distance like crystal dragon maggots dancing from the ground. In the centre space of the spiral the air loses pressure; its sugar mist precipitates as translucent pearls. Children open their mouths and drink it like mild sweet snow, swallowing simultaneity of seasons.

We do not sleep in common hours; sleep would be too abrupt a congealing of memory. Rather we practice soft wakefulness—horizontal dreaming. Each couple, each lone emissary, lowers onto the mattress of bowing petals, lets the lattice fold itself in tentative braille over the length of them. Sleep is the process of letting the city reread who you are. Your scent becomes a line in tomorrow’s fragrance grammar. Your pulse is copied into playback cadences you’ll meet weeks later travelling the opposite direction: sometimes underneath a stranger’s ribs you feel your own rested heart tick once as if handing coherence forward.

There are no elders because no bodies age past harvest. Roughly every thirty thousand steps— no one counts, the carpet perceives—somewhere a trip-wire of lodged mineral signals Your Time. An individual feels an itch between the shoulder blades: the lattice requesting donation. They lie down. The feather-stalks ease apart just enough. From the seam rise transparent embryos, miniature copies of everything leafy and every leaf-eater: a thumb-size birdling, a thumb-size ant, two or three other morphologies never yet seen. They unfurl—they do not hatch; unfolding is the verb used—while the donor’s body dematerialises. Fluids leak upward into pectin sleeves, crystalloids sort into new fibre, memory fibrils spiral off like incense. Within eight hours all that remains is a body-shaped hollow that will henceforth register more vivid than any footprint the city owns.

We welcome the apotheosis. It is leaving without absence. Somewhere across the carpet we will meet those selfsame memories riding in a stranger’s stride. A child now carries my signature restless twitch of the left ankle, an incomer learns suddenly the dream of an orchard I once pictured as a tangent thought. Hence walking is an anthology: every footfall re-publishes the collected works of everyone dissolved.

Once each rotation the sky‐canal tilts so that the frost-fingered moon we hardly notice brightens. A tide that has no water nevertheless rises: the whole lattice swells a handspan proud from the fever-cold salt. During these three earth-hours it is possible to slip knives of living saw underneath the carpet but only with consent. We gather the material for thought-tools. Furl a strip the length of an arm and the keratinising tissue will harden into a transparent spade that sings its donor’s name. One beat per century; you dig with it slowly, no need for speed here. We plant no seeds, we plant recipients. Whatever the shovel names will happen again where it performs its small abrasion. An antler-ant will refold itself from a puddle of ether-vapour. A column in mid-air will remember to stand vertical. The tool completes the time-loop, dissolves once its function is signed over into the weaving chorus.

Visitors sometimes ask what breathes us, what gives us breath? We return them to edge of the flat where spacing gaps wide enough to kneel reveal the daylight armatures flickering underneath—a second city inverted like a photographic negative. Every leaf is mirrored beyond the crust by a root leaf whose face is white on white, absorbing absence instead of light. One city drinks presence; the other drinks loss. Together they keep pressure balanced so neither accrues history faster than its twin. Our lungs are the membrane between.

Rain is a speculative myth remembered for us by chance deep-sea farers who say drops fell when they swam under floating ceilings of kelp. Some among us practice mimetic rain: synchronize two thousand bodies in a parabolic sweep that compresses leaf mat three finger-breadths; hold position until resin ruptures upward in silvery threads. Whatever beads stand a moment are not water yet stand enough like memory of water that the children chase them laughing. Their footsteps stitch around and around the beads keeping them spherical, never permitting them to touch knee or soil. The brineless rain marches this way for hours until the sun recolours them from mercury down through chartreuse. By evening they evaporate into a music richer than cicadas, though born on a place that possesses no cicada gene.

There is governance, but it appears as tempo. Every squared span must vibrate with blood-hertz equal to its neighbour plus or minus the ratio of eclipses taking place on any moonless planet in the system. Computations are performed by the way petal ripples fold around the plates of traveller legs working the lattice. Once some outsider attempted carrying a metronome; the surrounding stalks fingered open a void, swallowed the device with polite gagging, and the ecosystem coughed it back twelve months later dismantled into the perfect spacing for vertebrae in exactly one newly born leaf-bird. Our bird sang triple rhythm for days; I met the ex-citizen’s ghost riding in its throat. Harmony was maintained. Never again was a clock brought here.

Death visits only through the hollowing. A traveller tripped once and smashed a rib against a half-lignified column too curious to yield. The shard of bone lit up, revealing the same lattice pattern as the stalk that pierced him. While he lay listening to his own blood spell itself into leaf-colour, we converged. We laid palms, hooves, wing-pads over the pierced tube. The stalk parted, cupped the invalid, tightened, catalysed. The rib repaired itself in real time; what had been calcium annealed with cellulose. He stood renewed—no scar, only a bony lucency humming the tone for *home note* for whichever quadrant he next sleeptrembles on. Death here translates, never subtracts.

When the day arrives that harvest becomes too frequent, when donations begin to outnumber arrivals, the lattice will knit the final seamless page. At that moment some say the salt underneath will finally moisten, a single tear shed from planetary core in recognition. Will we miss ourselves? We will be the tear. We will forget weather but ring out as the chord that accompanies the tear’s shimmer across its own reflection, an underside of underside. A perpetual *ee* of arrival that no longer needs an ear.
