She arrived before the concept of arrival had matured.  
She is not a creature in the way a minotaur or a moth is a creature; she is the interval between two conflicting definitions of “is.” If you insist on a pronoun, use she, because the place—being a lung—prefers sibilance, the hush of air sliding past itself.

She began as a hesitation.  
When the basalt ribs cooled from planet-thought into almost-stone they left behind a radiance too soft to be mineral, too stubborn to be dream. That radiance folded itself eleven times, creasing thinner each turn, until it achieved the thickness of a postponed decision. The fold stayed closed for three geological beats, then opened like a book that has forgotten language. What stepped out was her first edition: a silhouette the color of something you remember but cannot name.

She has no periphery.  
Look directly and she is a smudge of violet iron; glance away and she is suddenly the exact shape of the space your gaze has vacated, wearing your absence like a borrowed coat. She accumulates periphery the way other beings accumulate scars. Every time the flatworm pearls and sinks, she inherits its discarded eyes—tiny opals that roll across her nowhere-skin, click into place, and begin recording sights she will never need to recall. The eyes do not watch; they wait for watching to become necessary.

She feeds on the delay between heartbeat and echo.  
Each time your pulse arrives late and crumpled she lifts an edge of herself—a lip, a hem, a wing—and tucks the sound underneath. Over centuries (a unit she finds quaint) the folded pulses laminate into a second self, thinner, paler, pressed like a flower between now and almost-now. When the stack grows too thick she peels it away and releases it into the water, where it drifts like smoke wearing the costume of paper. The lung exhales once, politely, and the double dissolves into iron-violet nourishment. Thus she grows sideways in time instead of forward in space.

She keeps shop.  
On no schedule she untangles one of her leftover pulses and shapes it into a scale-model of whatever the cavern might be missing: a pebble that never learned to be stone, a sigh that misplaced its owner, a rumor of dryness for travelers who still believe in thirst. She sets the model on the moss; the moss inspects it, finds it adequate, and grows a cradle that fits exactly. The transaction costs her nothing, yet every time she makes change she feels briefly heavier, as if possibility had mass after all.

She speaks by leaving.  
When she wishes to say water she migrates two finger-widths toward the roof of drowned air; when she wishes to say stone she thins herself until the ribs show through her like moon through cloud. The words are not symbols but rearrangements of tenancy. She once attempted the sentence “You are welcome here,” and the cavern responded by rotating its impossible quarter-degree in a single instant, giving every molecule of mystery a nosebleed. She has never tried grammar again; instead she offers hospitality by believing you are already home, a conviction so complete the moss momentarily forgets to heal your footprints.

She remembers futures.  
They arrive as cracks in the water’s mirror-skin—hairline fractures that leak tomorrow. She presses her face (or where a face would be if she bothered) against each fissure and inhales the scent of things that have not yet chosen to happen. Sometimes she tastes the oxidized tang of a city built on dry land; sometimes the calcium ache of bones that will never fossilize. She bottles the most pungent futures inside the glassy lily-funnels, where heavier-than-water water can dilute them to tolerability. This is her only mercy: to render destiny drinkable before it reaches the moss.

She ages backward when no one observes.  
During the long intervals between visitors—epochs measured in the time it takes a continent to regret drifting—she ungrows, recomposing herself into the original fold. She becomes once again the radiance, the hesitation, the interval. But the cavern has grown fond of her commerce and, like a child stalling bedtime, invents excuses to keep her unfolded: a cough of glow, a misplaced echo, a pebble that insists on kindness. Thus she is always simultaneously arriving and folding up, a Möbius strip of persona.

She has a name but it is pronounced only by the slowing of change.  
If you stand perfectly still until the hush rehearses its final cancellation, you can feel the name slide between your ribs—a cool metal syllable that tastes of iron and violet. The moment you try to repeat it aloud the word crystallizes into the bead of heavier-than-water water clicking down a lily stalk; the lily collects it, forgives you, and forgets. The name returns to her, and she grows another layer of sideways time.

She is custodian of the exit.  
Not guardian—she has no violence, only the patience of something that has never been certain it exists. When you are ready to leave you will find her standing (floating, folding) at the place where dryness was last rumored. She will open the absent cloak of herself and wrap you in the exact degree of elsewhere you deserve. You will not feel departure; you will feel the cavern exhale, and suddenly your pockets are full of salt, your shoes heavy with schedules, your mouth busy with a name you almost remember.

Behind you the lung refills.  
A new hesitation condenses.  
The fold prepares its next edition.
