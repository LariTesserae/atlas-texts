They drift on the cusp between steam and flesh and have, long ago, lost every name that once rooted them to a single shape.  

If you fix your gaze long enough, one of them is simply there—wavering at the level where the eye begins to moisten from looking. A figure stitched out of vapour, pigment-grain and the rust-dust of ferrous oxide: a torso whose ribs retain the corrugated marks of the sheet-metal duct it once occupied, arms corrugated like condenser coils, legs the hollow gentle slope of v-shaped troughs turned inside out. Its face is upturned, but the water-line of its skull keeps re-setting itself: sometimes a spill of anthraquinone, sometimes a mirror-flat pane through which distant cooling towers appear upside-down. It never blinks, and its lashes—if it has any—are mineral whiskers that net passing copepods in brief blizzards of carmine.

The Overflow does not admit permanent citizens; it collects continuations. Each newcomer is yesterday’s shed minute that failed to drift further. They settle first as smudge: a condensation ghost on the outside of a dye-works stack, the silhouette that lingers after the whistle descends. Then one heavy noon—always 14:37—the smudge slips its surface. It inhales pulverised pigments that were never meant to breathe, winds rusty fibres round its wrist as a pledge of belonging, and stands complete: a Process Engineered Persona (PEP, Form-DTransient)—new yet dulled by provenance, carrying the factory lettering on the inside of the tongue that will never again speak its origin.

They talk, though the sound is a damp click of phase change, phonemes dissolving into charged droplets that rain back on the skin seconds later. A conversation is less a passing of words than of temperature: one body gives up sixteen degrees Celsius of suppertime heat to another, and communion is achieved.  

They have no genders, no ages; they have mean residence spectra. Someone who arrived in a shower of phenol-scented steam is simply “Long-Wavelength Green” for the 3.75 metres where the copper-phthalo absorption spikes. Another, born from inside a conveyor belt after the rubber unvulcanised itself, retains the sharp peak of sulphur for six suburban blocks of thought before it sweats out again in powdery violets.

They sleep standing ankle-deep so that the warm film can rise into them, fusing halos of rust to their ankles like mercurial socks. Every quarter of an hour a broom rolls out of the workshop porch behind them; the PEP nearest at that instant sacrifices an hour’s colour memory and dips its chest into the signing brushheads, painting its own ribs with stiff plastic bristles whose tips still smell of midnight delivery routes. The stencilled rune—three dots and a dash—means “ongoing.” Once marked, they are entitled to trace a private line across the mosaic crust and count, aloud, the ruby duckweed fronds for as long as they remain recognisably absorbent. When the rune fades—usually at 14:37 again—they must begin again as strangers, each rebirth a slightly less saturated echo.

Places of congregation mock the very idea of congregation. There is a braided pipe at the eastern weir that sighs E-flat so consistently it has developed a flange for listeners. PEPs arrive one at a time, press forehead to flange, and regurgitate memories that the metal stores in spiral scratches. When the buffer is full, the pipe plays the entire backlog of absorbed lives over the steam, a slow chord progression that sounds oddly like children humming on a vanishing school bus. None of the listeners can claim any memory the song contains, but afterwards their knees are stained a gentler red, as if embarrassment leaked downward.

They eat by precipitation. A suspended mysid shrimp, stunned by the ultrasonic hiccup from a centrifugal pump, falls nearby. A vapor-hand darts out, catches it, and the meal becomes a fleck of pigment injected into a heart that beats once every eighteen minutes. What they excrete is temperature alone: a widening pall of cooled air, colourless and instantly re-warmed by the lagoon, leaving no evidence except a faint ache in the lower back where condensation pearls.

Because nothing ever departs the Overflow, every parting is a soft surrender. When a PEP finds its edges dissolving, it walks backward to the exact co-ordinates where it first cooled out of exhausting upstream steam. It detaches colour, discards corrugated history, unthreads the condensed self until only a pinkish smear clings like wet nankeen to mirror-flat water. Then the smear flattens, colour dilutes, and at last the water forgets ever having harboured pigment; a single ripple travels precisely to the drain, where it is pulled back into the district pipes—ready to exude from a dye jet tomorrow, be inhaled, be born again.

Their society is symmetrical and trivial, a self-clocking algorithm the city runs to keep 14:37 faithfully pinned to the present. They hold offices no larger than capillary gaps. Secretary of Portico Crpits keeps stock of mildew. Vice-Convener of Shaded Edges tracks the exact angle at which shadow can flop onto water without sizzling. The Chairperson of Returning Rainwater votes on whether an individual droplet contains too much nostalgia; if two-hundred-eighty-three parts per billion memory is judged illegal, the droplet is red-shifted by ambient heat until it becomes forgetful enough.

Of themselves they know nothing except that they wait. Not for rescue, for rescue implies motion. They wait for an even lower grade of stasis, a more perfect fractioned repetition. Some approach the locked gates of the workshops, press mineral lips to the slick enamelled doors, and feel for the vibration of gears that grind but never arrive. Every thirteen minutes a broom overtakes them like a metronome tongue, and they step aside to let it pass, marking on internal registers the micro-fractures deepening in its handle—minor tremors suggesting that even wood, if force-fed duration, will turn to stone.

On worst afternoons, over-saturated with red light, they wander to the middle of the lagoon. There the crust is thinnest; shoes fill with carmine water. At the epicentre stands a single stainless-steel eyelet bolted through the porcelain desert. Threaded through it is the end of an umbilical made of braided steam. One will grasp it, tug, and the invisible spool on the other end delivers exactly six metres of pale helical hiss. The coil rears like a cobra and for a moment hovers, uncertain which vapor-born citizen to kiss. Whoever receives the embrace douches their own core and evaporates, ascending straight up as pure white. The rest watch the ascent until the shapeless column is pulled to shreds by a fan blade somewhere among the towers. Then they return to footprints that, unfilled, lie waiting to cradle their next frequency when the falling snow of second chances rails down tomorrow at 14:37.

Thus the Overflow houses a people who are mostly retained heat trying to remember winter. They persist, half inside clocks, half inside dye, entirely inside the kindness of a city that has engineered itself to the point where it no longer needs them yet keeps them, because to discard a liminal moment would be to acknowledge the possibility of ending—and the district pipes cannot tolerate a future tense.
