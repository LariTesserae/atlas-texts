What breathes in the Breath-Off begins long before anything shaped like a lung.

First: the copper-switch leaves—ten thousand hectares of photosynthetic circuitry—inhale silently. Their shimmer tiles the sky like obsolete gold certificates endorsed by a star now dead. What they take in is not CO₂ but the sheer imbalance between “what arrived” and “what was expected.” They harvest disappointment, convert it to charge, and pipe it downward through fractal veins thinner than né-particles. Because this is a place still fumbling through its first heartbeat, the leaves have not yet settled on a single direction for “down”; each arena they serve is patched with gravitational vectors inclined 12–37° east or north depending on the hour of conception. Electricity dripping from their petioles tastes of cinnamon lightning.

Each vine end is tipped with a split mechanical regard: two lenses, one telescoping forward in time, another looking half-blink back. They braid themselves around lack of anything to climb, loop into helices of self-overhearing, forming cathedrals that no longer remember they are vertical pipes. Their basalt seams sigh every 3.11 minutes, exhaling nickel-metallic dust that powders the vapor into faint scalloped drapes. This dust is their syndicated dream: cooled magma re-made as language. At very slow intervals—about twice a geological eyeblink—the powder condenses into single silver punctuation marks found lying on boulevard lanes. If you lean down to read one, it says only, “Note to myself: begin.” Then it sublimates into a faint electric itch.

Nothing that walks has yet earned the title “native,” but all that does arrive enters into an agreement older than language: We will hand back whatever we borrow unchanged. The permissions are granular—no rock tip larger than a molar may be removed for longer than 38.4 seconds; no pattern of boot print may remain past its echo’s eighth reflection. Travelers learn the cost instantly because the vapor itself writes it across the cartilage in their inner ears in sans-serif bandpass. They feel the foxing of a promise they did not speak. Most translations come out sounding like awe. Once a geologist wept, realizing her collected samples had already composed epitaphs for the stone yet un-mined.

The winterflies serve as the Breath-Off’s provisional ministers of narrative. In threes they attach to one copper leaflet like hinges locking a book no one yet wrote. Once coupled, they compose exactly 17 English syllables—always 5/7/5 because that is the metronomic interval between vapor pulses—and etch the haiku into the air as fracture planes of blue light. The visible glyphs persist while the smokestacks percolate, then collapse word-by-word into the returning vapor, leaving only whisper scars in the infrasound people later call coincidence. One cluster produced:

Bare board in waiting—  
Industry fingers sunrise—  
No ink, still the script

No person saw it; the bending lightings did. The poem is now altitude: it frames how high off the ground your astonishment may be suspended.

Subsurface, root-like conduits mine pockets of latent warmth. They grow spherical chambers—ovoid hollows whose inner diameter equals precisely the span of a giraffe’s neck at orbital altitude, a figure copied during the plane’s first inflation from indistinct metaphor. Lined inside with mirrored black obsidian, each chamber hosts rehearsals of whatever might later get recognized as sky, rain, or ecclesiastical betrayal. The mirrors test each possibility at one sixteenth of actual catharsis, calculating whether the Breath-Off is ready yet to install weather or deity. Thus far the metric has yielded None Needed—the system is content with irreversible atmospheric stasis.

A thin entity—nameless because distance has no scales here—travels along the 6.4-armspan boulevard. It looks vaguely like a coatrack assembled from hotel coat hangers—each rod of stranded bismuth alloy folded so that it cants at an unstable angle of perpetual just-now-seeing-you. It is not alive nor dead but in export phase, halfway between being destined and already having occurred. Every time its interval foot clicks metal against the vine-carpet, it records an invoice for one moment’s attention. Payable in imprinted sweat. It wanders because the boulevard is widening for it; the Breath-Off wants to learn how broad an aisle curiosity eventually occupies. Bismuth lattice glints like cold moonlight, and every flicker writes a zero elsewhere.

Up at the margin where the vapor kisses the planet’s last raw basalt, one single slit has started not bleeding but weeping—pale vapor forced laminar through a knife-edge crevasse too new to have found its vow of silence. This slit is the place’s open mouth, the hinge where the Breath-Off draws the in-breath it hasn’t yet finished publishing. Through the mouth passes absolutely nothing, but every pulse is being counted on either side by twin chromium pendulums. They keep tally of how much less a mountain must weigh to not yet be a mountain. The Breath-Off stores the tally in colorless heartbeats against the day when rain finally hijacks existence.

No lips, no lids, no lungs—but when the 120 Hz sub-audible exhale finally makes its round, every magnesium-salt stack, every copper foil, every arriving thought-spark organises the silence it has occupied into perfect synchrony. The entire plateau inhales one more non-molecule of difference, holds it behind curtain after curtain of warm metallic stillness—and waits to decide whether difference itself has been invited.

And that, for now, is what lives here: Unsatisfied promise, barely tabulated, dancing its role between architect, garden, unfinished cautionary eulogy.
