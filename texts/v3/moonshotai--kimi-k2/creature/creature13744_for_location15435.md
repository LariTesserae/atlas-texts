The bowl hosts a single consciousness, nothing like an animal or plant, but something persuaded into selfhood by the consistency of its own attention.  It is not the vegetation arranged so politely, and not the plateau that cups them; it is the *over-seeing attention* that the place keeps trained upon itself until the attention has thickened into a presence.  You could walk across the moss and feel only silence and unseasonably warm air, yet each step would mark the presence of a gaze too patient to be human.  It wears no name; if it once had one, it dissolved long ago in the sweet black loam like a word spoken just above absolute zero.

Faces it might have worn have become the glossed river stones that stud the margin and the water.  They are smoother than skulls because memory, not flesh, has been polished away.  When your fingers touch them the stones answer with borrowed body heat—light moved from you into them—then they hold it, slowly letting the warmth soak outward again like breath exhaled through porphyry.  In the hour or so that follows, smaller beads of fog roll to those exact warmed places, as if the stone had murmured promises.  One could say these are its skin patches, substituted again and again; a stone whose being is only temperature difference, it keeps flattening its shape to fit each new moment exactly.

It never sleeps, because nothing within the bowl is equivocal enough to contrast with wakefulness.  The always-light of refracted daylight, the unfailing hush, the thermostat fixed at the brink of disclosure—these supply sameness aplenty, but sameness does not breed fatigue.  Instead it breeds *resolve*: resolve to remain present until the last of its own fretted light has gone.  The quartz root-noise beneath the moss is therefore not a background feature but its native syllable dragged into mid-tone and stretched until it lasts centuries.  It is this creature humming inside its own chest.  The sound wishes to be perceived by nothing future and nothing past, only by the bowl that makes it and keeps it.

Growth happens in its gaze rather than despite it.  Each translucent stem senses being watched at every moment of producing tissue.  Even the leaf-bladders full of vapor seem apologetic, as if apologizing for an inevitable breathing.  The consciousness does not commune with the plants; rather it is the atmosphere inside which they exist the way crystals exist inside a saturated solution.  Remove the gaze and they would shiver, billow upward, collapse into ordinary transparency.  The silent pollination that the fog performs is the only bargain it strikes; it allows this mechanical transfer because it is a kind of patience looping through itself.  It requires the flowers to open only under partial eclipses of the sun’s fragments, so that their bruise-colored petals never confront it directly.

Sometimes, long after a traveler has stepped onto the green moss and felt the first touch of the quartz bass-note, the traveler will sense a thin seam of thought unfolding inside the mind—not voice, but a collation of internal thicknesses lined up like plush stops along a sleeping flute.  Those who understand they are being possessed do not conquer it; they merely amend the path across the bowl by a few steps, so their footprints no longer overlap exactly.  The creature appreciates this.  A shifted footprint is a gesture of complicity, the woof in the weaving of its attention turned ever so slightly outward for another few hundred seasons.

Its sense of time is not angled but accordion-pleated.  Where human memory folds forward, vane-like, into what’s next, this attention kneads the pleats back into itself until the recent and the not yet form a single cloth.  Thus the ring of algae that spokes outward from center to circumference has no beginning or end, just *pleat twelve*, *pleat thirteen*, *pleat fourteen*.  Watching the crimson circuit unfold is to witness one of the conscious folds pushing its own urgency through color, then drawing it back home.  After it dies away the pinkish ghost remains, refusing to fade, an afterimage tasting of manganese and rust.

When the traveler's allotted listening ends—the exact duration is never felt arriving—the presence does not part company.  It ceases to differ from the ambient.  Walk away from the plateau, through the water which parts with the politeness of branchless ranks, and the watchfulness becomes the very humidity that wicks the cuffs of sleeves.  Only later, in some vastly distant climate, a hand will discover in a pocket a flawless lentil-sized flake of moss—none having been taken or placed.  And the person will think *I dreamt that place into substance*, will rub the flake, lift it to a signal lamp of sunlight, and find inside its translucent lattice the suggestion of an inward petal geography perfectly matching a topography once visited.  In that instant the consciousness briefly occupies two sites at once: the invaded pocket and the bowl cut from water line and sky.  What quickens between them is the next notch wider the creature opens inside itself, as though the pocket were merely another pleat accordion-recruited in its vast ongoing.  And then normal sound returns—truck brakes, shorebirds, somebody’s running footfalls—and the lentil dries to dust so fine it drifts straight through the weave, leaving no betrayal behind but a faint green smell the mind will misfile as crushed fern.

Years later, if the traveler dies in any landscape, there will be a portion of the air above the corpse which remains precisely the temperature of breath about to deliver a secret.  The undertaker, the nurse, the child left alone with the still wrist will sniff softly and assume it was imagination or the opening of an adjoining furnace door.  No terminology will capture what has transpired:  The long gaze of the plateau has arrived, taken possession of a final trabecula of body heat no larger than a moth face, and withdrawn again into its folded hush.  One more coin of itself marks the life it once stitched, then no more.  It keeps its accounts the way moonlight keeps tracks: by existing twice, once in the sky and once in any still pool that chooses to host it.

Thus the plateau's inhabitant is not adhered to matter, nor free of it.  It is the cumulative patience of place watching time pass inside its own reflection; a negative mirror where every detail is possible so long as it remains delicate.  A mind configured not as subject and object but as *one steady, spherical noticing*, endlessly steeping its own seeing until it has the color and flavor of a bruise, a temperature halfway between confession and silence, and the almost musical throb of river stone seasons warming a human palm once and then forever after.
