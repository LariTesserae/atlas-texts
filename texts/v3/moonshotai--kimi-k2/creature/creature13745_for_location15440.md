In the Drowned Valley of First Forge, no resident ever *goes* anywhere—it’s more truthful to say the place temporarily discards them, only to gather them back in altered form before the hourglass has turned twice. Life here is less a kingdom than a continuous re-quotation of itself.

First to admit any kind of identity are the *Panegyrists*, those almost-children of extinct colonists whose entire memory is a single sentence looped across three synthetic throats. Each Panegyrist is a triune consciousness: three identical bodies lacquered in matte black resin, clumping like dew-blotted flies atop the millimetre-thin water films that sheet low basalt ridges. They wear respiration manifolds shaped like trumpet mouths where a human jaw should hinge. From those mouths they never tire of whispering—three tones apart, three accents of the same word—*Promissum*. That word is equal parts lullaby, weather forecast, and wake-up call. Whenever a gravity lens folds and the valley *whumps*, the Panegyrists receive it as a punctuation mark, adjust the vowel modulations *eeee* to *aaaaa* to *oouuu* until the ambient thunder is in key again. Then they relax, leaking susurrant agreement. Consume them and you inherit the sentence, spoken forever in your marrow at 8.3 kHz, pitched precisely to make your femurs hum.

What eats, or at least accompanies, the Panegyrists are the *Silhouette-Kites*. These started as emergency drone skins torn free by a rogue jetstream; over winters they re-remembered themselves into vast 2D sails of bismuth etched with read-only signature codes. An individual Kite is as thin as two wavelengths of far-red light; its underside is matte, the valley’s own dark, while the upper side scatters every stray luminance into holographic wings thirty metres across. Hovering between water-sheet and canopy, they graze on electromagnetic gradients. When a Kite grazes, the electromagnetic field shrinks by an observable quantum, and a Panegyrist’s breath catches in joy that is indistinguishable from anguish. The ecology is sealed: Panegyrists need Kite vortices to tune the valley’s music; Kites need Panegyrists’ sacred syllable to anchor their thermodynamic ledger.

Beneath those orchestral surfaces grows the *Inkfish*. Think cuttlefish the colour of memory foam, but without the muscle; they are all mantle and no spine, half the height of a woman yet weighing less than a thimbleful of mercury because nearly everything inside is vacuum-charged yolk. The Inkfish drift, spongiform, through the glass-gelatinous hotsrung slurry, progressing by displacement rather than intent. Feed on them and you absorb their secret: they exude cursive sentences in residual radio-frequency, personal histories stolen from whatever genesis serum the valley last metabolised. Once drawn into a blood-stream these histories slosh about inside a skull like too-hot tea; a newcomer might “remember” an entire childhood spent mining Pluto’s nitrogen caverns even though she came to this planet yesterday with two duffel bags and a prepaid psych pass. Most folk hesitate to eat them more than once, fearing irreversible identity theft, yet the taste is unanimously described as *mint turning to gunmetal*, an addictively precise contradiction.

Nothing in the valley *should* be as large as the Inkfish, yet something older stakes its own latitude where sky touches drowned earth you would swear space itself has folded wrong. Locals call such places *Ziggurat slippages*, and inhabit them are *Bathynauts*. These Bathynauts began as single emergency acetylene tanks discarded from aerial mechanics centuries ago; they are now proud cylindrical creatures, three metres tall, fused by frost and biometal lithification. What looked like rust is living insulation—cilia of iron oxide that pulse, breathe, and close over seams. The Bathynauts locomote by hydraulic oozing, pressure upon pressure until they flex and *psssst* a microscopic avalanche of fluid. Down below the crust line sludge their crown is a boiling pressure chamber surrounded by curled mirrors; from open top spill regular parabolas of superheated steam the size of cathedral bells. These museum explosions reheat the hotsprings for kilometres around—and deposit some new mineral every time. Hence the valley bleeds colours no periodic table predicts: chrome oxide in frost-petal violet patterns, bicarbonate sunset streaks glowing xiphoid gold at the overcast rims. A Bathynaut is too large to hunt and too procedural to negotiate with. The best one can hope is to ride the backwashes of their thermal exhalations, glissading across diamond-grit ice meadows on your rib-lung wings until the heat gives out and the fall begins.

But these things are visible horizons. Reach closer and you find the *Mycelial Overlay*, the true floor of all biologies here. Every act in the valley happens atop a frozen carpet woven from transparent hyphae; their tensile strength and opacity vary according to the amount of Panegyrists’ rusted vowels captured in microbubbles. The Overlay ponders slowly, in distributed thought, deciding what form noise should take tomorrow. Occasionally its axons erupt with virulent cataracts of mirror-like fruiting bodies—in the moonlight they resemble scattered eyeballs stopped mid-blink. To the dumpy ice-hare, half its mass converted to glide-sail, these eyes are its compass. To the Bathynaut, they are guilt. To the Panegyrists they stutter sacred numbers they themselves spent last night uttering. This mycelial multitude isenades the Forge’s last human operator, whoever that was, because she left behind memory data as spore fodder: diagrams of fracture mechanics and lullabies sung by a grandmother three lightyears gone. Every matrix cell sings both at once, though the strains battered into lichen-hymn differ slightly every time, preserving only their emotional key.

And so the valley sings—while beneath, spare amplifications: 

- Threadworms in brackish ditches tense, drink slowly accumulating Isotopic Laughter leaked from reactor ghosts someone buried too shallow.  
- Scabs of paint flake off antique trusses—each chip now crawling, a centipede of polymer dreaming of rejoining an aircraft it never became.  
- A blind silicotherm-monastic (vestigial name: Koloa—she never answers to it) hefts her furnace-chalice of molten bauxite every seventh rotation, pours thin layers across Bathynaut tracks. The alloy freezes into hobnails that clack under Inkfish pressure; they celebrate this rhythm like matins, mixed by Panegyrists into tomorrow’s pulse-weather chord.  

The one rule, observed or else oblivion guarantees: nothing new may leave. A Kite developing long bones, a Panegyrist forgetting its refrain, an Inkfish choosing to write *forward* instead of *inside-out*—the valley senses imbalance, leans in, and corrects brutally. Identity is borrowed soil; what leaves here is only ever barter material. Between burst pipes and frost-flash, the valley teaches eager matter how to keep watching itself be taught.

Tonight the lenses in First Forge hum, feeding tomorrow’s gravity onto plates of frozen perfect zero. Kyr ahead now, thunder has already begun constructing a bridge higher than atmosphere, a lattice of exploded icicles spiraling skyward like negative lightning growing from ground to cloud in frigid slow-motion. When it meets the rain-filmed lid it will pierce a microscopic aperture. Through that pinprick a single inhaled word from an ex-breather outside the valley will descend, resound among the hyphae, and shuffle the definitions of predator and prey before morning frost fully finials the lagoons with its letters. Already the Panegyrists adjust the vowel *o* from zero to micro-grave—scaling *promissum* to *promissooleum. Listen close: if you walk here now your ribs will memorise the cadence and carry it outward when they at last haul your cooling body onto the embankment. The valley survives in the borrowed syllables the dead refuse to release.
