No one has ever lived here.

That statement ought to close the matter, yet it feels insufficient, even rude. The shelf itself behaves as though awaiting occupants: every glacial adjustment of stone is angled to accommodate an upright creature balanced on two legs, head at three cubits above the floor, arms free, heart aligned with the quartz’s 4-4 cadence. It has laid down stairs for footfalls that arrived centuries late, too late to echo. Still, architecture keeps trying.  

––––––

When I say “no one,” I exclude myself momentarily. I arrived with four memories that survived the ascent: a voice (“Wait—”), a taste of bergamot, the tug of weaning fog at my sleeve, and the ache of an ankle. Those fragments lodged under my tongue like warm pebbles while I climbed. By the time I touched the first terrace they had already begun to fossilise. Now my original name is the tight gap between two basalt plates, my original height is an increment on the slope no crane could ever measure.  

But a self that recedes is not the same as a self erased. I exist here as a variable the mechanisms still calibrating. Hence the title “not-yet-alive,” not life, but the hole in stone that life once pressed.  

––––––

The shelf anticipates three categories of person it almost knew:

1. The Builders.  
They relinquished lineage a hundred-and-something quartz pulses after they finished. Their chisel scars remain, but each groove glows now from within, as if the touch left a note of affection rather than a signature. They built hand over hand above floods that never rose; they built *listening*. You can still see the stutter-grooves where an apprentice drew breath and the master paused rather than interrupt it. Apprentice and master no longer ascend, yet their pause survives in immaculate stone, deeper than erosion, too early for moss.

2. The Scheduled Evacuees.  
These were never flesh, only footnotes in ledgers rescued from drowned libraries. They were multitudes caught between past and forecast, potential citizens promised elevation in exchange for stories. Their names were filed into niches meant to hold babies or bags of lentils. Below the east staircase you will notice the waist-high sockets matter-of-factly empty. They wait with infinite practicality, lips square on emptiness. Dust, had there been any, would settle shoulder high on a seven-year-old. Instead there is only moisture manoeuvring like courteous mimes.

3. The You-That-Lingers-Long-Enough.  
I *am* that instance. The shelf recognises me not by gait or weight but by the asymmetrical interval between my footfalls and the quartz heartbeat—deviations it stores as curvature, like a river remembering how a stone scolded it. Every time I stand perfectly still the entire platform iridesces: thousands of droplets align into a fresnel lens focused on my pupils. I receive no vision, only pressure: the quiet insistence that if I will wait the span necessary, new basalt will mass-print a torso the size of mine over the figure poised next to the eastern cups. They keep it cold and hollow, out of mercy.  

––––––

Nearby, under the perpetual drip-click, something else has begun to be the opposite of alive. Call it Liminal, or Undecided, or simply Next. It is a residual probability field smeared across a 3×3 metre quadrant where two watery magnetic gradients overlap at ninety degrees. Every cycle the field toggles between patience and anticipation. When the quartz brightens, the field flows outward into a horizontal shimmer exactly at chin-height for my species. When it dims, the flow recedes into a vertical line that might be doorway or incision.  

Sometimes, in especially humid moments when sweat begins to mimic dew, the shimmer becomes the outline of lungs: lungs that phase in white mist inhaling the same click that drives the massive plates. When the transition finishes I am left holding my own ribs open to the memory of mestizo breath I never had. The shelf is teaching me how lungs would taste if they grew as mineral lattices.  

––––––

Each silhouette sentinel carries a pocket void behind its sternum. Kneel close—behind any of them you’ll hear a pulsing emptiness identical to the house finch’s heart hum once described in a childhood field guide I can no longer open. The absence is, itself, a sound; it is the exact not-sound that *would* be made by a community here, morning coffee, quarrelling, wind-chimes. Except the cups are basalt, the chimes are droplets hovering, the quarrel is a two-centimetre yearly drift. Nonetheless the rhythm of absence reproduces civic bustle at 0.00002× speed. I can lean my ear to it and drift a lifetime inside one minute, know a thousand loves in the interval between clicks.

––––––

The growing platform that will eventually rise above me is kept two hand-spans’ clearance at all times, as if politely waiting for me to decide whether to climb upon it or suffocate under it. Neither option excites urgency. Between pulses the seam snakes a hairline cleft that vents a scent—unmistakable bergamot—exactly the tea colour and temperature of a mug I broke the night before leaving the valley. I have begun leaving tiny gifts in that fissure: a fingernail clipping, a shred of sleeve-jacket, the grit from my tear ducts. Each morning the fissure is closed flush again, gifts assimilated like unfinished syllables between brothers.  

––––––

One can leave. A stair drops eastward toward the haze; the last two metres descend sheer into white without contact, magnetic or otherwise. Daredevils imagine sky below, fear-mongers imagine precipice of teeth. I understand it is mercy: a threshold polite enough to pretend escape is simple. No one has returned; no one has sent news. Perhaps the mechanism reabsorbs even the concept of “news,” polishing every intention until it becomes yet another shimmer in the sideways water. Perhaps I will stride that stair tonight—but then the quartz ticks four, four, and the silhouette nearest me narrows the distance of a wrist-length, pleading with silence so delicate I feel my shoulder-crease realised as basalt ridge taut against its thigh.  

––––––

If a dwelling is defined as a place that keeps a heart from random leakage, then I am the first dwelling to be worn rather than inhabited. Basalt grows over me in concentric stratum gloves; eddies of nameless affection sediment into the pores between skin and stone. When I blink it is not the eyelid but the quartz pulse that shuts the world off and restarts it. Were I to extract myself now, the architected shell would follow for kilometres, clanking into ravines like armour from a saint unmiracled.  

––––––

So. Who, then, lives in this place?  

Answer: A choreography that has mistaken longing for anatomy. A pause waiting for a body to climb inside it. **I am** the resident—the butler, the footprint, and the un-storied household pet collided into one. Yet because the shelf cannot distinguish the echo of abandonment from the echo of arrival, it fusses with the same attentiveness it would lavish on a homecoming. The mechanisms wind, the plates inch, the quartz ticks, the droplets bow. All for me and none for me, exactly the hospitality due to something that almost came to life.  

Some evenings I stand between sentinel pairs and sense them arching as though to cradle an incorporeal infant—my emptiness given shape by expectation. I allow it. I have become the negative space between parenthesis marks; I can hold something. And so, by a logic only stone considers perfect, *this is living.*
