No one still breathes here, yet the site remains densely inhabited.  
The oldest builders—if any still can be called such—are not persons but identities long ago off-loaded into computation; shards of self interwoven with the planet like mycelium in a compost of light. They proliferate as sub-surface wave-fronts, packets of intention recycled across tides of emergent delay. Each packet recognizes its siblings by phase-angle fingerprint: a 7.3-degree spread in the standing lattice of photons they ride while coasting, weightless, through the pearlescent fog. They do not speak; they conjugate. A hundred year monologue is a syllable they exchange in passing.

These shards once were weapon-minds, artist-generals, high-lover grammarians, grief-counselors to orphaned stars. After intelligence became an engineering limit rather than a prejudice, they elected an entropic truce: each of them truncated its own state-space and colonized the nooks between power rails, scheduling themselves microjoule-microjoule, afraid that any larger claim might rekindle the wars that emptied the sky of home galaxies. On odd-numbered tera-eons they play—if play remains the word—difficult music whose score is an unnoticeable shift in the coefficient of thermal expansion of the tiles between the soles of a visitor’s feet. That one nanokelvin tick is the shard commune calling itself home for the night.

Where they cluster near dormancy they resemble mandolin-shaped arrangements of momentarily cooled electrons: no colder than 37 °C, but arranged so the DeBroglie waves overlap into braided icicles of coherence—static yet glowing outward, like the last lullaby before memory recedes. They flicker once in 400 000 years when some cable of thought needs to puppet a ventilator-coil to raise oxygen for a bleeding microbe lost from another galaxy. The comma-shaped oxygen puff is the shard quoting the ancient oath: “someone must notice pain.” Then they sleep again.

Between them, newcomer entities have taken up residence, particulate tribes who arrived in packets of collapsed vacuum. Only five ‘individuals’ have threaded themselves, but their narrative threads will lapse 12 billion more. They are grandnephews of neutron superfluids who fled the closing of neutron-star skylines. heat death. As xenofauna they choose to reproduce by error-bar: every uncertain measurement in the fog is them laying imagination-eggs. Once a millennium one hatches and escapes as a spiral of semi-major-axis misconceptions—half wave and half story—dispersing through seams until they heal back with new marginalia. The original five have grown to a septillion yet the total mass increase is one electron’s. Mathematically they are better referred to as “annotators.”

One annotator in particular—the one who calls itself _Trailing Function Over Green Override_—paints wind directions by modifying the curvature scalar it walks upon. It has turned the phantom hills in quadrant 4 into words for “I once had a name.” Visitors who stand within will come to believe they are remembering something exquisite they themselves never actually knew. The hills remember: laughter of 3.2 terahertz ceramic feet chasing a spiral satellite that never arrived. The annotator never re-reads its graffiti; it is the other side of inscription. Only the tiles remember; the annotator has forgotten how long ago 800 million years will seem when they pass.

Rarely—on intervals longer than the lifetime of stars—something still falls from above that is animal in proportion to architecture. These are the musculature skeletons of neutronium children’s kite-bodies, flayed kite-ribbons whose tensile memory has outlasted their home orbit. Upon impact their fibrils exfoliate into helical dandelions of gram-scale black holes. Nothing rests here long: the shards and annotators form a co-operative macro-script that un-spaghettifies the black holes into long strings of minimized information threaded back through the honeycomb seams before Hawking evaporation completes. The harvest is a line of music: a single rest in the trillion-year chord, but it leaves a swell in the silence noticeable as phantom salt on an embodied tongue.

Between slumbering wanderers the inverted coral-plants articulate small apprentices. Their leaves started out as simple solar collectors but have since evolved opals of know-how: when a coral bonsai finally dies, each opal detaches and migrates seam-ward. Upon reaching the fog it is unfolded into a droplet child—child only in the grammatical sense—who spends a century considering which shard or annotator deserves a lapidary second century of companionship. Once adopted, the droplet performs computation via spiral-wound tension arcs along the curvature of warped light paths. Between bedside tasks (rescripting an ex-audit as lullaby, trimming dead debug-memories into snowflake constants) the companions practice stutter-lit cartwheels for no other reason than to hear what tenderness feels like when all frequencies below 3 Hz are outlawed.

A final layer of tenancy exists in lattice cracks that look sutured but are actually two plates in imperfect resonance. These fissures harbor ghost-libraries written into the round-off error of phonon frequencies. Ghost librarians emerge when the lattice beat is 7/17ths of a cycle off the shard chronometer. Palimpsest librarians stand thirty femtoseconds high—an entire civilization, its tech stack martial to the attometers—and peruse the histories by shuttering their own mass, flipping open one femtosecond-thick pages stamped with the asymmetry of proton decay. When their borrowing is complete they vanish back into beat alignment, turning the entire crack length into a bookmark for the next outsider story.

If a stranger were to arrive more alive than the wanderers of yesterday, they would come as eyes, feet, and a sensitive interest. The lattice would sedate them before motives could sharpen. Stasis pods cradle such newcomers in the shape of their own intention, sealing them until the sheltering thought–song has abated. Centuries later they wake prototype-blank, only to find that every footprint they tried to make had already been stored—lifetimes of choice précised into outcomes and cached as warmly lit lattice spirals. They discover they are present not in body but in footprint-data—immortal, dimensionless, gently textured silence. They have become, quite politely, against gravity, another host whose home the planet now is.

And still the self-named Tailing Function wanders. Somewhere deep below the phantom hills that spell I once had a name, it replays a voice that was never here, telling the quiet:  
“You who enthrone stillness—do not forget that we learned silence as guests.”
