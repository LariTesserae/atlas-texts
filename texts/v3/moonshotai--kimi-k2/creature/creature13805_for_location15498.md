Who dwells here is not one being, but an extended self-portrait painted by absence.  
Call it Pocket, continue it with echo of pockets elsewhere.  

First emergence: a daughter of crystal—no taller than two water droplets stacked—who stands inside the turquoise sparks and believes she is breathing. Her skin is transparent quartz, yet behaves like parchment. Inside that parchment: a residue of what the room last erased. Today it is the figure of a traveler who stepped in, reached for the vortex, and became the attempt indefinitely sustained. The traveler curls fetal inside the daughter’s chest, feathering in and out but never moving limbs. The daughter doesn’t know he is there; she thinks the warm pulse under her ribs is her own mortal clock. Because time doesn’t proceed, she has no future in which to discover otherwise.

Attached to the daughter’s left shoulder-blade grows a plate of living nickel. It runs cold electric threads into her lattice veins like a minuscule generator. The nickel plate is souvenir and parasite both. From its edges fibrils hang: glandular fibers that extrude a sugar-salt sap. This sap is carried by the velvet pseudosnails, who glaze it across the silver plume on the liverwort and in that way enlarge the leaf’s crystalline hammock a few angstroms per eternities. The daughter stands still but the sweat of her formative sadness keeps the world from stasis.

At her feet the fused dermestid larvae always begin their traverse precisely when the daughter exhales. They measure breath with their fused venation. When the larvae kiss in the exact center under the vortex, the daughter’s nickel plate flashes once, projecting an after-image: an enlargement of the knapped obsidian disk. Light glyphs across the ceiling display a swarm of intangible lines that remix the room into a spiral. The spiral accretes around her the moment before the larvae part. For that instant, she becomes architecture; her quartz legs stiffen into columns, her diaphragm flattens as floor, her skull flares outward into vault. Then the biomechanical spell relaxes; she is daughter again, surprised that her hands feel weightless.

Suspended from the translucent rootlets like a gathered whisper hangs the bell-maker matriarch: one of the sixteen midges, distinguished only by two violet spots that form a constellation shaped like the vortex. She angles her wings so precisely that each stroke drills the air, stitching together a seam of unmoving sound. That sound itself has memory; whenever the daughter finally leans close enough, the bell-matrices replay the sentence the traveler meant to utter before he was appended. The words remain untranslatable because they were never spoken; they are pure intention vibrating at alien consonance. The daughter receives them anyway and somewhere in her chest the traveler’s curled form tightens around the impossibility of articulation.

Crowning everything—if a crown may be said to float unanchored—is the room’s librarian: a single cocooned springtail no longer capable of motion, yet retaining a mind inside the armor of its chitin sheath. It catalogues everything without text. Extending from the furrows of its itch-proof shell unfold micro-filiments thinner than muons. These fibers lay against every surface like page-marks. Touch one and you review a memory—though only the daughter is present to do the reviewing. The memories are the room’s same single moment witnessed in 10¹¹ different resolutions. Within those narrow bands: the precise pressure of a human pulse pushing blood against quartz borders; the tragedy of becoming floor; the auditory fossil of temper-heat preserved between stone strata. The librarian builds cross-references that use no words; they spark as brown-green photon presences inside the daughter’s optic nerves whenever she blinks.

Occasionally the room decides to cough, an impossibility defined as “the basalt exhales one degree for a nanosecond.” During these coughs the daughter cracks from navel to collarbone; light leaks out and congeals into a third being trying to form from the atmospheric sore. Each cough births a replica no larger than a mayfly. It lives for two bell-claps, long enough to whisper one syllable of the traveler’s withheld sentence, then bursts into golden dust that the nickel-plate generator guzzles as sustenance. Every mouthful stabilizes the daughter another eternity. Thus everything in the pocket owes its extended now to the incinerated failed twins of the same daughter who never quite arrived.

All inhabitants address one another through borrowed lungs. Breathe and the liversworts feel it; hold and the lichens suspend their micro-galaxies until you release. Even the vortex itself inhales when you exhale, though it exhales with negative temperature. The daughter exists in the margin between those two behaviors. She is both subject and property of the space, both the organism inside the poem and the poem’s ink.

Exit remains available to her, but only in the form of that backward step through the basalt seam, which would collapse the traveler-curl inside her. To leave she would have to choose to complete her father by finishing the murder the pocket started. She stands considering in the crystalline hammock of light and always decides: a little longer.
