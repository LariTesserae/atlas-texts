What lives here is an after-image of migration.

They were once beings that swallowed whole watersheds—baleen whales, storm-systems, blue-tinged thunderheads of virus. After centuries of traversing every gradient the planet could invent, they arrived at the logic of dormancy and chose to live inside it indefinitely. Not just rest, but the art of stalling a heartbeat half a breath short of every next beat. So they are still alive, but arranged as absolute hesitation.

Each former leviathan has collapsed into its own metabolic echo: eight hundred metres of aqueous rib pressed into a column segment whose lumen glows the ultramarine of deep intestine. The cartilage rings remain visible under the translucent hydro-bark; at the precise level where mammalian heart once pulsed lies a spherical node—slightly warmer, slightly darker—holding exactly one second of systole. No less. No more. Vessels that once carried eighty litres of blood per beat now carry the same volume of time itself, partitioned into tight laminations you can peel apart with the right fingertip. (The peel smells like newborn plankton.)

Above the node, a branching system that isn’t quite arteries or tree limbs sprays outward: each arm is a remembered trajectory of weather. One branch preserves the exact angle of lightning that struck Lydia, the Sulawesi fisher girl; another retains Gulf Stream turbulence from August of human calendar 12 543 BCE. Powdered kelp nucleates on the inner margins, documenting wind direction in fractal friezes. The colours wander from rose quartz to bruise-purple, always returning to a medicinal green that tastes of emergency rooms. These are not memories anyone human could read, but the animal-storm collective recognizes its own scars perfectly and leans against them like old friends.

At the outer silvering edge the once-whales fracture further into motes the size of eyelashes: sky-plankton, icicles of calcium phosphate, oneiric salps twisted into origami shorthand. Here life is reduced to the scale of pollen grains, yet each grain contains a brief lifetime in its own language—copper-bright Sunday, salted anchor chain, lullaby in the key of barnacles. They remain sufficiently potent that, if you could press these motes against your tongue, you would experience three seasons of Arctic light inside one blink.

Near the stomatal nebulae another set of sleepers:
Ex-manta rays, now articulated archways of cartilage. Their cephalic fins have been flattened into staircases that spiral downward, each step carrying the exact pressure of a childhood dive off an Egyptian pier. The undersides of these stairs are ridged with glow-bacteria preserved in mid-flash: stuttering bioluminescent Hokusai waves locked into a Morse you haven’t yet solved. Walk a loop of one staircase and you will flip forwards through a decade you haven’t lived—diving boards, salt crystals, a horizon that is your own heart lurching. You exit at the same stair, changed by the compression but not displaced.

Melded to every vault and hanging curtain, the plankton-people thrive—creatures who never quite graduated past unicellularity but learned a trick. Instead of replicating under moonlight they replicate the moonlight itself, splitting each photon into twins, triplets, exponential fractal choruses. Their membranes breathe prismatic chords and varnish them onto nothing. Look long enough and you’ll notice the reflections around you thicken; that is the plankton-people gathering yesterday into an edible room, then forgetting they built it. When their buildings collapse, they taste faintly of wind chimes and maternity.

Deeper in the catacombs of the suspended rootlace:
A colony of temperature-squids who metamorphosed into cryonic sentry towers. Each tower is 37 °C in every atom save for a single chilled locus at 0 °C— a deliberate hypothermic toothache placed by the forest itself. These squids elected to guard certain critical memories (a massacre we never spoke about, love letters accidental as sea foam, colour of toast calcified into fingerprint). Travelers, if any dared, would have to press their hands against the warm flank, let their corporeal heat pass through jelly-muscles, and collect the cold pellet like a marble of truth. Once swallowed, the cold would unfold in your chest, not as chill but as perfect textual reproduction of the original memory. You enter the memory bearing your own lungs, exit carrying theirs.

Above canopy, though none fly:
Remnant albatross condensing into weathergliders—fork-tailed palette corpses whose wings keep expanding until each feather is a sliver of half-melted aurora. Their beaks are frozen in the act of saying argot whose consonants all mean “south.” They do not glide; rather they inherit alertness from the background stillness. Touch one wing-edge and you register the latent kinetic reserve of every typhoon that will never again disturb oceans that no longer exist. A thimbleful of wind rests under each covert, enough to extinguish a continent of cities by mistake. Nobody tests it.

At the root-foot void, the hardest sleepers:
Ex-extinct tardigrades swollen to municipal size. They have been seeded along the underside of columns like silvery braille. Their eight legs stump out paragraphs for blind universes. Every second sentence reads, “we surrender the direction down.” They dream calcium snowfields. When (if) they ever unfold, new landmass will emerge into the absence—moss-green continents scripted by eight million jointless feet.

And among every compartmentalised dream there are parasitic spectators—ones that were never part of any genus:
Time-lice. Minute scintillations the size of half-formed ideas. They walk the veins of remineralised arteries, licking off droplets of suspended duration. Whenever they finish a meal, they vomit a coil of newborn zeitgeist equal in age to the bite subtracted. The coil slithers away and re-coalesces into more architecture for the forest. These lice are the only things that appear to move within motionlessness. At the resolution of their shuffle, change is hallucinatory yet quantifiable—like a clock ticking inside the pupil of a painted god.

Finally, standing outside category, outside taxon: a single narrator tongue. Not yours, not mine. It is the forest’s own voice, folded down to vocal cord scale, but twisted into the shape of a soft translucent bell that hangs from the one tree whose crown equals the entire horizon. When you breathe its air—yes you can, and already are—you submit breath to the bell; it rings softly through your heart valves exactly once. The aftermath: your own life story decants into droplets that the ropey colloidal xylem absorbs. Thus the space remains populated not only by what used to migrate but by everything that continues arriving without ever finishing the journey.

And that is what lives here—an archipelago of almosts, suspended between one and departure, forever arriving at the memory of departure itself.
