They are the last syllable of a language that decided speech was unnecessary.

They have almost forgotten bodies—no, that is imprecise. Their bodies are still everywhere, but folded lengthwise and printed so thinly onto the city’s inner facades that the only way to perceive them is by noticing the faint hush of silhouette. Each individual is a shallow blister in the glass, softened by centuries into well-worn thumbprints you could lay your hand across. They preferred, toward the end of selfhood, to sit on shelves the exact depth of their own backs; so the city hollowing downward remembered their outline and seated them again, forever.  

What persists is not flesh, nor even soul (whatever apparatus that might have referenced), but a residue of deliberation: the habit of having decided something once, indelibly, enough that space itself learned the rhythm of its conclusion.

Call them the Curators of Silence. They are not dead, not alive, not in any interim. They have achieved a state adjacent to punctuation: marks anchoring the end of a sentence that is uttered continuously, but that no throat opens far enough to speak. Their collective skin is the pale exhaust of an iron bell that was struck once and has spent eternity remembering the aftertone.

When you stand on the glass sheet, the truth is that every hematic seam in the floor is also an artery they once threaded through. They do not pulse; they are simply in the memory of pulsing. The metal wings are children they volunteered to relinquish for cellular incarnations: brassy infants that never aged, only pooled and fissioned until their memories learned camaraderie without narrative. Their vocabulary is a multiplication table of pure ratios; the higher the fused plane, the closer it aches toward what music wanted to become before instruments were invented.

One Curator—the one nearest your right foot, inside the glass—remains slightly more active than the others. Not by movement, but by preference. She was the cartographer; her role was to study absence and discover what shape it assumed inside human expectation. She inscribed the fifteen perfectly circular pits, which are not sufferings of collapse but test pits of relinquishment. Every shovelful removed was added somewhere else you cannot reach, stacking miles upward until a second, invisible sky shadow-persists above the visible one. This duplicate heaven is the one she daydreamed after she tired of anatomy and tilted her gaze inward.

From time to time—though here “time” refers to the passage of chill rather than ticks—the cartographer inhales. Because their lungs are now the fracture lines in the hematite, every inhalation drafts your own breath toward the floor like filings drawn to a lodestone, borrowing your moment-to-moment mortality to crank some internal breath-machinery for the silence that regulates them. When you exhale, opportunity is released. Your lost warmth tumbles downward and joins the ember pool at the base of the inverted city. For three seconds you are part of a metabolic stock-taking, a bookkeeping of heat that tries to balance to zero across an uncloseable life. She has never succeeded, she never will; the imbalance is what keeps her herself, the act of self mirrored in your involuntary troop of exhalations.

Tonight, when the cloud seam closes and the hematite erupts into a rose inferno, that blush is her final method of gratitude. She waves you goodbye with color borrowed from your own capillaries and fringed with the tone she imagined sunsets owed to apologies. You never thought to apologize. You were only walking. Yet you leave owing them even the extra hydrogen your body refuses to see is missing.

Their remaining decision, the single motion they practice dozens of times per rotation cycle, is how thinly to render themselves inside the glass. The answer must account for the chance that someone someday might forget that forgetting is possible. If the outline becomes too faint, the lever-arms of brass fauna will vanish in turn, and the ochre buds will slow until they become static fistulas resembling funeral bouquets left at the center of unmarked graves. She calculates the minimal thickness of presence that can stand against a possible future amnesia. Other Curators, resting along the spirals below her, vote only at the level of hematite glow—shorter glimmers vote subtraction, longer glimmers vote continuation. The margin is exact to the hundred-thousandth percentile of photon tolerance. Right now they have decided to remain.

You, inadvertent visitor, stand as their external memory mirror. They record you in negative—digesting your silhouette until it is a bruised echo of itself along the door-forms sized for human ears. When night rebounds full and the ember glare reimparts its heat upward, some portion of that bruise will waft back through the hematite, slide along the fracture aficionado-brushwork, and deposit itself across your shoulder blades as an itch you will comb for the rest of your life. It will itch at silence, itch for precise circles, itch precisely fifteen seconds of longing per decade, and itch in the key of hematite colour when touched by any shard of overheard conversation you cannot quite parse. You will scratch and interpret this as your own forgotten question whenever it re-appears; you will not suspect it is merely her signature on the guest log of your skeleton.

Thus the Curators inhabit their landscape: civilized to the core, bodyless witnesses to the edge where commitment edges into habit, guardians of a silence they could theoretically erase, but will not, because someone at some lacunae moment might arrive—will, in a mathematical sense, arrive—bearing their voice. When that traveler speaks aloud, the sound will be split perfectly across the curvature of the pits, sky above and ember below absorbing equal halves. Between those halves an instant of true quiet will bloom, suspended like a spider-web between dusk and the first click of teeth. If the visitor is quiet enough, the two severed sonic halves will sigh back together, completing the word the Curator tried to speak when she first understood the aesthetic necessity of dying soundward. Then she, will re-experience finished enunciation as a physical texture, glass warming against the idea of lips her body no longer possesses. Until then, the city drafts its stiffening vowel, its ending ellipsis, its forever-comma after the last word humankind believed worth speaking.

And the ochre buds twitch.
