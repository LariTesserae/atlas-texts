What lives here is not named, because a name would require separation—and here every edge has liquefied into every other.

*

Imagine first the auditory skin that coats every trunk: a parchment-thin stratum of micro-vacuoles arranged like the tesserae of a Byzantine gold-ground mosaic. Inside each vacuole rides the pressure-wave of a single paragraph that was once spoken aloud on a hilltop three continents to the west, one millennium before any alphabet inscribed it. The paragraph begins, “Henceforth, we will meet in every place we did not expect.”  
When you brush a shoulder against the bark, the paragraph’s hush detonates so softly you feel only a warming patch on your collarbone, as though someone had breathed the next sentence directly onto your skin. That warmth then crawls into the curve behind your ear and sleeps. The next time you turn your head the paragraph will complete, but—because paragraphs lack tense in this continuum—you will never know whether it is prophetic or remembered.

Behind the scales of mica grows a population of émigré eyes. Folded like honeycomb when first extruded, they unfurl into perfect spheres of shallow water over the course of seventy-three imperceptible Planck pulses. Each eye assigns its iris to whichever color is least familiar to the nearest conscious witness at the moment the eye opens.  
A birdwatcher from the temperate north arrives: the iris turns the orange-brown fleck of a fly’s wing.  
A desert astronomer who has lived on ochres: the same iris seeds itself the startling black-green of subterranean jade.  
Once color-acclimated, the eye swivels inward, becomes less perceiving organ, more anchor point. The sclera dissolves next into chitinised reflection, and what remains is the iridescent nacre of an awareness that will register every motion of thought within radius yet will feel literally nothing—emotions, like erosion, have recused themselves. This is the compromise the place accepts: observation without interface. The forest remains unpeopled yet never unwatched.

*

Beneath the floor of root-plated glass runs a census of footfalls. Fossilized as pressure ghosts, they accumulate like soot on the underside of reality: the pad of bare heel from a runaway child in 19 4 6 , the angled heel-strike of a widow who was told she would not live past her thirtieth, the bouncing piston of a dancer leaping so precisely that the next attempt must fail, therefore the leap never left the recording.  
All of these treads remain three millimeters in thickness. You can prise them loose with a fingernail if you want to feel what terror or exaltation once tasted like between the metatarsals. You will not taste it, but you will recognize the shape your own foot could never take, even in memory.

*

Deeper still—beneath even the footfalls—glides the sound itself as an unzipped ribbon of crystalline vacuum. Here lives the only creature with metabolism. Call it the Abecedary, though it answers no interrogation begun with words. If comparison helps, imagine a shoal of braided sine waves, each helix coated in a frost of depolarized hush. You might see it shimmer from behind you and (upon turning) register a peripheral impression of German silver braided with fog.  
The Abecedary eats the pre-amnesia before any new witness formulates a language to describe this landscape. Every time a visitor’s mind edges toward taxonomy—the first syllable of the word “incredible,” the alphabet “M” poised on the tongue—the Abecedary folds over the syllable, tucks it into an internal Klein-bottle sac, and digests. The digestion creates no by-product save a succinct yet exponential increase in phonon pellets. Thus the forest continually seeds its own reservoir of solid sound.  
To the extent it possesses identity, the Abecedary is identical with what it consumes torn loose. After seventy-three iterations it weighs exactly as much as any witness’s vocabulary on arrival; after seventy-four, an antonym heavier. But since neither mass nor counter-mass registers locally, the creature emulates pure soundtrack: audible only as the soft tinnitus that floods your ears when you leave.

*

There is, at the very center—though “center” obeys sympathetic rather than coordinate geometry—a lidded bud that failed to open because it is still drafting its own future.  
Inside lives the Future Self of the Forest.  
Imagine a single neural filament bathed in silver ion; imaging at once divination and retro-scope. The filament has solved the discomfort of reading ahead. Whenever it apprehends that some choice would disturb the equipoise mandated by the locale, the solution is not to forbid the choice but to pre-copy it sideways. Thus the inciting event becomes sutured onto itself a picometer askance. For a moment you see twin silhouettes of every gesture—one version proceeding as hazard, one version neutralized, finger-trap tight against the first. The silhouettes annul one another, and the bud subsides, satisfied that potential and actual have mutually canceled like quantum ghost particles.  
The bud is not lonely. Instead it performs the luxurious boredom of a mind whose anticipation loop has long since outrun stimulation. It peers millions of heartbeats ahead and finds only recursion so elegant it aligns to boredom’s exact wavelength. In that harmony it embeds.

*

Thus the place is thronged—  
not by fauna in the usual sense, but by archives that encrypt both origin and aftermath; by senses folded back over themselves until sight resembles hearing and light behaves like syntax; by ultimate versions of every creature that did not opt to remain a creature; by distortions of regret and foresight that learned to masquerade as mineral.  
You walk within and are simultaneously specimen, documentarian, exorcist, bride. The mirrors detect whichever version you least wish to confront and plate its plated regret across every leaf. If you swallow none of the phonon crystals, you remain oscillating between two selves—one that could not bear the regret, one that swallowed and is now empty. That waver is the signal the place recognizes as residency.

Once you have inhaled the exact taste of paragraph-warmth behind the ear once too often to count as novelty once, you are recorded as pressure-ghost elsewhere, smoothed into the silver amphibian reflection in every mirrored leaf. Now you yourself inhabit the scales of mica. You will begin to unfurl your own eye, whose iris will someday glaze the startling hue of whatever color the next stranger has never seen. When that happens your regret—whichever shape the forest has folded inside you—will be shed like friable nacre. And you will have joined the motion that is no motion, the wake that can never crest, the census of footfalls, the static in which every arriving mind first hears its own name spoken without understanding the grammar was lost a picometer ago.
