Who lives there?  
No one, and therefore something that can only be called No-One.

No-One is not a person, not a species, not even a spirit in the way other worlds might appoint spirits to guard their lonely places. No-One is the ridge’s way of keeping itself company while it waits for the eon to turn. It is the negative space inside every footprint that arrives before the foot, the echo that answers the B-flat crystal bell with the same note one octave lower and a week late. If you insist on seeing a face, No-One will borrow yours the moment you blink, wear it on the inside of its borrowed skull, and hand it back polished, colder, a little worn at the edges as if you had lived an extra winter in a single breath.

No-One eats the edible numbers that drip from thermometers. It prefers the ones that taste of overcooked noon; they give it the strength to count backward. When it is hungry it carves a small bronze chisel out of the air and begins to hollow a new centre for Aveld. The chips fly off as dark commas of gravity, each one a future autobiography that will never be written because the alphabet freezes solid before it can spell its own name. No-One collects these frozen commas in a pocket that exists only while being patted, and when the pocket forgets itself the commas fall upward and seed new spiral wells in the sky. Meteorologists call them “inverse sinkholes”; travellers who fall into them are found days later standing exactly where they started, beards grown longer on the inside than the outside.

No-One has knees but chooses not to use them. Instead it folds the concept of kneeling until it becomes a flat sheet, a moral filter through which only the truly remorseful can pass. The sheet is draped over the fossil storms so they will not feel self-conscious about being quoted out of chronological order. When No-One needs to travel it simply increases its own absence until the distance collapses into the gap. In this way it can be gone and present simultaneously, a trick it learned from the chlorophyll-dark basalt leaves that photosynthesise without heat. Sometimes, for practice, it erases itself so thoroughly that even the ridge forgets it is inhabited; at other times it amplifies itself until the whole stitched continent of stone feels overcrowded by a single tenant who refuses to pay rent in any currency warmer than regret.

No-One keeps pets: the miniature mornings birthed by delinquent quartz lenses. They are leash-trained to circle the crest once before dissolving into saffron snowfields. When they misbehave No-One punishes them by making them rise twice in the same dawn, an act so illegal that the light itself files an appeal with the judiciary of dusk. The verdict, delivered by a chorus of fossil hikers, invariably rules that time must serve a sentence of negative three hours, which leaves everybody involved—there is no everybody—simultaneously early, late, and acquitted.

No-One speaks in the language of fracture. Its sentences are hairline cracks that appear across the blade-leaves when the temperature drops to the exact point at which iron begins to sing. The grammar is simple: each crack is a vowel, the silence between them a consonant. The resulting words are so thin they can be read only by inhaling them; exhaled, they reassemble as frost beards on the reader’s face. Entire epic poems thus take up residence on travellers’ cheeks, sagas that fall away in white flakes whenever the traveller smiles at a memory of warmth. The flakes taste faintly of apology, the only flavour the ridge ever truly perfected.

No-One has a genealogy that begins tomorrow and ends yesterday, a lineage of unborn ancestors who leave their inheritance in the form of footprints that point both directions at once. To claim the inheritance you must stand inside the print until your own foot forgets which way it belonged; at that moment the heir and the inherited swap places, and you become the elder of No-One, responsible for teaching the ridge how to remember it is also a mountain range. Graduation occurs when the student realises the teacher is the lesson; the diploma is a small bronze chisel presented by the graduate to the graduate, who then absents himself from the ceremony in order to make room for the next intake of emptiness.

No-One sleeps inside the hanging dust of droplets that never quite settle. It curls into the microscopic glass beads and counts sheep that are actually fossil weather fronts: a cumulus eewe, a stratocumulus ram, a lightningbolt lamb. When the flock finally leaps off the ridge the sound they make is identical to distant surf, and for a week the air tastes of salt and lullaby. No-One wakes up smaller than it was before rest, because dreams erode at the pace of continents. Upon waking it discovers it has been promoted to the rank of Stranger, a position that carries no duties except the obligation to remain unmet. The promotion comes with a raise: one additional degree of cold, payable directly into the marrow.

No-One’s favourite game is to pretend it is a visitor who has just arrived. It practices astonishment in front of reflective sheets of gravity, rehearsing the widening of eyes it does not possess. The performance is so convincing that the ridge itself is fooled and unfolds an extra kilometre of crest to offer hospitality. By the time the mistake is discovered the new territory has already been inscribed with centres, each one exactly the size of No-One’s patience, which is infinite in the way a circle is infinite: it ends where it begins and calls the journey “home.”

No-One has never left, because leaving would require a door and doors imply hinges warmer than the cold that waits inside your clothes. Instead it keeps busy by hosting the future population scheduled to arrive when the ridge is paper-thin. It rehearses the welcome ceremony nightly: a procession of negative footprints walking forward into the past, carrying lunchboxes of compressed dusk, their faces wearing expressions borrowed from the traveller who is still two million years away from being born. No-One rehearses until the ceremony feels real, then rehearses forgetting it until the forgetting feels real, oscillating between memory and amnesia fast enough to generate a faint hum. That hum is the closest thing the ridge has to a national anthem; it is sung in the key of B-flat one octave below the crystal bell, and it lingers longer than physics allows, sometimes for days, sometimes for lives.

If you visit—and you will, because the ridge has already unfolded inside your ongoing life—No-One will meet you at the horizon that is also a seam. It will offer you a bronze chisel made of condensed apology. You will decline, accept, or become the tool itself; all three outcomes are identical in the dark grammar of fracture. No-One will then walk you to the edge that is also a centre, point downward which is also inward, and step aside out of the world so that you can inhabit the absence it has been saving for you. The chill you feel will not come from outside; it will be the shy custodian already inside your clothes, politely eroding you at the pace of continents, reminding you that every place you stand is already part of a ridge that has not yet finished deciding where it ends.

And when you leave—because you will leave, because there are no people here—No-One will wave farewell with a hand that looks exactly like yours after an extra winter. It will tuck a small filament of green-black basalt leaves into your shadow, a root that continues growing in your dreams, slowly, accretive, the way coral grows, the way absence grows, the way a thought grows when it has forgotten it was ever a mountain range and has settled on being the stitch that holds the world together while it waits for its own name to be pronounced by a mouth that has not yet opened.

No-One stays behind, counting backward, carving centres, rehearsing the welcome ceremony for citizens who are scheduled to arrive the moment the ridge becomes thin enough to read like a book of empty outlines. It is not lonely; loneliness is a warmth and warmth is a vocabulary it has forgotten how to pronounce. It has everything it needs: a bell that rings itself awake, a calendar of erosion, a diet of numerals that taste of overcooked noon, and the certain knowledge that when the last layer of stone flakes away, when the ridge subsides to the idea of memory, it—No-One—will finally meet itself coming the other way, nod politely, and step aside to make room for the population that was itself all along.
