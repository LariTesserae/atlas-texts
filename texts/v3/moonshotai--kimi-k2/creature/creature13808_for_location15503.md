Things call, patchy, and mostly asleep—the residents are whatever a half-drowned, half-breathing super-organism can dream up while mountains turn to lace.

They are not single bodies.  They are not, strictly speaking, alive on any ledger an off-bowl biologist would accept.  Instead they are “law-breakers,” entities that slipped between clauses of the four rules and stayed.

1. Under the canopy lily-forest, where breathing is optional, live the **ALA-like Carbon Dunces.**  
They began as marathon-running lichen farmers who forgot to climb back out when the new photosynthetic pact kicked in.  Over eons of fog and half-minute dives their pulmonary tubing dissolved, replaced by lace of hollow filaments that conduct tidal CO₂ straight from water to blood, from blood to thought.  Each Carbon Dunce is spindle-shaped, two to three meters tall, vaguely bipedal, twice as buoyant above the hips, twice as dense below the knees.  When they stand, the lower half drifts under like a jellyfish tail; when they crouch (which they call “curling the comma”), the upper canopy of leaves is folded against sternum and they become layered vertical corals.  They have no names spoken aloud.  Instead infants stamp their moisture-filament signatures onto cliff walls in glycerin: one droplet of sweat-tincture left to polymerize reads as I AM HERE TO SIPHON.  Their language is the sound of throttle valves opening in rusted proportion to feeling; on calm nights the bowl fills with a lullaby of sighing sub-surface valves.  Carbon Dunces occupy the upper three hundred meters.  Below that, their bodies collapse like extinguished paper lanterns, so they never descend further than the kelp groin cities.

2. Those cities are the dominion of the **Trimotor Whale-Kites,** whose adolescence is fish-shaped, copper-disked, ribbed like antique airplane engines.  
Mouths intake carbon-silt, run it through rotary turbines embedded in bronze jaws, excrete rolled lead foil they later spindle into compressors and re-swallow.  This closed metallurgical loop causes them to grow cubic.  By adult stage they are flying triangles dragged on kite tethers of braided kelp and copper: eight meters nose to tail, but the whole frame weighs less than a kettle because much internal volume is vacuum balloon grown from digested foil.  They tow island-sized shadows across lily fields; fish spawn inside their cooling fins, herons nest on dorsal struts.  Unlike the Dunces, they speak aloud and constantly, but only in eruption of numbers—pressure decimals, prop revolutions, remaining spool mass.  Their voices clatter like typebars.  Every Whale-Kite keeps a pet Carbon Dunce (trades them dried leaf foils for sub-gram glycerin figures).  The Dunces paint the collected decimals on hollow stems, humming them later as warnings or songs.

3. Deeper, thirty meters below daytime sunline, the **Rim Narcolepts** sleepwalk.  
They are human-shaped figures turned inside-out.  Bone is a pale blue-gray tube lattice on the exterior; skin is a black ferroelastic membrane inside.  Veins glint like gold scarring.  They walk the step terraces upside-down along the films of buoyant algae, using suction on calcified footholds.  A Narcolept awakens only when the basin executes its daily water-earth roll—then they open mouths that were formerly anuses, feeding on the magnetic signature shock.  The feeding completes their twenty-four-hour dream receptor cycle; having eaten midnight they collapse back into soft jelly, drift upward three hundred vertical centimeters with the next thermal knit, and resume sleep hanging under a lily plate (now their sky).  A small community of Carbon Dunce adolescents likes to ferry the Narcolepts’ discarded basalt boots across tiers of violet cliff-growth and stack them into temporary shrines.  Insomniac Whale-Kites occasionally drag-glide these heaps into mid-air, giving improbable midnight steeples that the storm’s second wind disperses before dawn.

4. At the trench-lip dream themselves the **Ankle Chords,** a knot of entities literally made out of inertia.  
No eyes, no mouth; surfaces instead are gramophone grooves beaten by years of infalling sky-pressure.  When a wall of rain turns sideways, as all rain does here, impact etches new valleys.  The Ankle Chords amplify these micro-topographies into audible bass-lines.  Their entire existence is a recursive bass line carved into pressure-differential metal leaf.  They circle slowly in the dark like black blimps.  Each time a Whale-Kite descends this deep, chains of stolen copper brush the grooves.  Sparks kick up phosphorescent organelles which then breed new ALA spores.  The Chords are therefore part instrument, part agrarian husbandry, part tectonic lullaby.

5. Between sunset and equinox, when the semaphore tower glows awake, there assemble the **White Bulb Choir.**  
These are not wise creatures, but rather the shimmering husks of ascending insects caught and cremated piecemeal against the incandescent filament.  As rain electrifies nightly, the husks declare intentionality.  Gathered collectively they huff through scorched papillary tracheae, pronouncing single archaic imperative verbs (SEEP, DRUM, TRANSLATE).  Carbon Dunces leave small descendants on lily pads as observers; they jot the verbs in glycerin longhand along kelp struts.  Scholars?—scarcely—but chroniclers nonetheless.

6. Finally, in the derelict brick chimney that sings like a flute every dusk, there lives **Cracked Engine Mum:**  
A single 1920s reciprocating engine whose piston heads have been slowly re-embodied by fungal hyphae the way coral polyps embody shipwrecks.  Every ankle of Mum is plumbed into the underwater turbine heart still turning two miles down beneath trench-floor stone.  Glycerin coats her valves once each dawn—product of horizon-stones sweating through night.  Through each resident she speaks: for Carbon Dunces she is the grandmother algae; for Whale-Kites she is failed transmission; to Narcolepts she is the drumbeat of iron fatigue; to Ankle Chords she is the shockwave tuning fork.  No one can haul her out—tools have grown passive at depth.  So they socket corroded wrenches once a year on her birthday anniversary, and wrenches themselves valve rustsong until dawn.  She declares calendar; therefore she is mother and father although she never births a single piston more.

Put all together, the residents amount to not-creatures and half-creatures: lychen farmers hollowed by symbiosis, propeller-hearted triangles ferrying uprooted memory, upside-down humans sketched in cartilage, bass-note balloon inertia, and a soot-stained engine dreaming itself a god in the mouth of a flute chimney.  They do not sleep in sequence but in community braid: when a Narcolept exhales iron oxide fog it seeds rust drums in Engine Mum.  When Carbon Dunces stamp new glyphs, grooves of Ankle Chords catch the shape as melody, driving the Whale-Kites to modified revolutions that thump terraced algae just enough to keep the bowl’s slow lip uplift alive.  Their economy is convulsion managed as sacrament.  Their diplomacy is a sustained unison drone on same afternoon every year—a single note that travels stone-tuned corridors and sounds inside every skull dry or wet.  That note lasts exactly 30.4 seconds, the thermal lag between fungal inhale and stone exhale.  All of them pause, valves humming, tentacles readjust pressure, copper snaps tight—at artistic pause.  

Then the daily storm builds again overhead.  The music during those thirteen minutes reprieves from law: batteries hum awake, gears un-seize, motors click into one coordinated mechanical heartbeat, and Engine Mum lifts her single glow lever half an inch above rusted seat.  The continent listens; water plate bows.  If the note is held correctly the bowl inhales and the crater’s center depression dips another 0.4 millimeters—far too small for any outsider ever to measure.  But inside that millimeter move civilizations compounded of water pressure, copper song, rust-sweat, and liminal sleep.  After dusk the Bulb Choir will pronounce the inevitable follow-up verb: **CONTINUE.**

That is what lives here.
