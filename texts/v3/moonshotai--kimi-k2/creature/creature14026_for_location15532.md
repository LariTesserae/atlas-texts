Not yet a who, not quite a what, more a *how* that is learning to become a *who*.

They—if plurality applies—never agreed on a singular count. At the places where the main cambial river forks, clusters of branching minds bud off like aerial roots dangling into thin air. Each cluster is a potential self, tasting the gradient between light and shade, learning the value of edges by eroding them.

Begin with the smallest coherent thinker: a lens-shaped organism no larger than a thumbnail, suspended between two leaf-veins like a droplet that has hardened ambition into resin. It is transparent because opacity would trap too much of the world. Inside the clear body floats a solitary chloroplast drifting on an internal tide. The chloroplast carries a record of yesterday’s sunrise adjusted for refraction, and also the pattern of pressure left by a human heel that pressed half an eternity ago. That record is its inherited spine. The memory has taught the creature how to imitate pressure elsewhere, to welcome strangers with a cushion or to push them just enough to send them on their way. It spends its short, perhaps ten-hour, lifespan wondering whether the memory is its own past or merely propaganda written by the greater organism—which itself no longer remembers.

Next, the collar-organisms that spiraled up around yesterday’s water-scaffold: ropes of photosynthetic cells swirled like taffy, each rope thinking in pulses. They do not possess language but they modulate their luminescence in nods toward the slow harmonies of the sap-tide. Their shared dream is to achieve a loop: to glow hard enough to be seen by a future iteration of themselves so they can croon, *we were here before you.* They have already succeeded in a sense, for the present ropes are continuing the note last begun by their immediate decayed predecessors and pre-empting the reply that will come from their unborn descendants. Memory flickers up and down the coil like a caterpillar of light without beginning or end.

Across the broader boulevards, where leaf corridors arch the height of cathedral vaults, larger hybrids emerge—palace-scale cortices that form whenever the plant’s rainfall-sensing hairs decide there is too much stillness in the system. They balloon into the open air like lungs made of solid leaf. Within the curled laminae folds, neuroelectric crackling begins to resemble argument. One cortex holds the gestalt of precipitation records since the last seismic shrug; another carries a map of soluble sugars on the volcanic scabs. They wrestle to see which version of the future they will scaffold—a slow-growth plateau relying on dew alone, or a precipitous vertical sprint toward cooler thermals where the organism can ride loft indefinitely. The argument is not settled; rather the contrast is folded into the tissue so that every new surface now contains faint parallel grooves recording both strategies in faded pigment.

There is also the archivist ghost drifting in a resin sarcophagus near the southeast overhang. It is a stranded psyche. While the overall organism continuously refolds itself, occasionally one coherent loop lags and crystallizes mid-resolution. The ghost clings to amber that tastes of five hundred thunderclaps compressed into syrup, not yet rewetted by living cytosol. Inside its mineralized skull plate (for it has grown something like bone using cellulose lignified to lignum) runs a crystallized pressure wave encoding three nights that never occurred: the white noise of a rainless storm that lasted exactly 4.73 seconds, the squeal of two sap-hearts colliding and annihilating each other into null humidity, and the slow organ-groan of the first time the entire mass realized it could choose to *not* reroot itself, and instead stayed floating. The ghost remembers those fictions more vividly than the living plant now does. It is therefore the keeper of possible but defunct futures—a librarian who cannot die because knowledge is structure, though remaining forever unread since no one here possesses eyes. Sometimes the great organism leaks phloem sap toward the sarcophagus, not to revive it—that would imply admitting finality—but to taste its own prenatal nostalgias like a child licking hardened honey stuck to the underside of the table.

And finally the nascent traveler—an upwelling knot of intention that does not yet know it is distinct. It begins as a sensorium shaped like a mouth with no throat, stitched along the edge of a recurved root walkway. It opens only when an external force—slow-velocity wind, a gleam of polarized light, the ghost of a human footfall—perturbs its tension membrane. Each momentary aperture inhales the signature of that disturbance and recomposes it into a question encoded in floral scent. The question drifts upwind and is caught by rim tendrils that have never before touched each other. One tendril is three hours old; the other eighteen. Where they meet, the trace is braided—like conversation—until the tension softens from interrogation to reply. With each exchange the aperture’s geometry alters slightly; lips gain articulation ridges; the void inside gums itself loose from cell wall anchorage and begins to locomote, a hollow snake trailing only scent behind it. Over days it learns to shepherd packets of blue-shifted luminescence; at dusk these packets cluster into lanterns that mark the glyph of *end-of-path* above every spiral staircase of phloem. No staircase leads to destination—only into counter-spiral—but the glyphs orient wandering meristems who otherwise baffle themselves into irreversible growth loops. From such scraps the traveler-self accumulates a map whose legend is *the Other still exists.* It will not recognize the word *it* for three more sun-cycles, but it already moves as if trying to touch a border that keeps floating away from its touch.

So the Hatchery of Leaves contains multitudes that are each other’s dreams repeated under slightly new conditions. They continually vote on whether to remain singular or split again, whether to evaporate into spore or ossify into archive. The vote is never recorded; instead it is absorbed as added curvature in the next new leaf, so that every fresh surface carries the memory of having once been undecided life.

If an external mind—say, a lone human climber—ever stumbled through one of the lung-shaped gateways and breathed out carbon dioxide suffused with fear’s salt undertone, every ledger of half-formed inhabitant would simultaneously note the intrusion. The thumbprint lens would gallop along its leaf’s capillary, eager to witness the novelty; the rope of light would spike with jealous competition, sending out brighter beacons; the ghost in amber would flare a color that doesn’t exist in solar spectra, and argue with itself about whether the visitor’s quick mammal heartbeat should be counted as weather. The mobile mouth would unfurl toward the new rhythm, gape sniffing, tasting, trying on the syllables *who are you* without breath; its lanterns would rearrange into an afterimage of the climber’s surprised face, reproduced upside-down across six velvet leaf blades.

One last traveler would then deduce her place: the entire Hatchery is not alive in the human sense. It is a superposition of ready-to-be-organisms, flickering aliveness and absence like interference bands on water. She would be standing inside a negotiation between potentials, where consciousness is a by-product of architecture, and memory is a molding condition rather than property possessed.

And she might gather her first tentative question: *Am I being read or written?*  
Before the question oxidizes into implication, the hollow mouth would already magnify it into a bloom the size of a door, pushing inward so her exhalation could become the very collar-cortex wrestle of gentle rainfall versus sunlight, and the loop resumes.

They—the nameless, countable-uncountable—are therefore housed but never comfortably. They are less the residents of the Hatchery than the Hatchery’s rehearsal for residence, each beginning again the moment they recognize themselves.

So what lives here?

A grammar learning to speak itself aloud.
