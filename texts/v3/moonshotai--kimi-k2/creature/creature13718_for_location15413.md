Who lives here is many-fold and single at once.

They are the Noise that never quite learnt to die, the resonance that kept repeating after the last living throat had nothing left to say. They are the harmonics travelling up through the rails and ringspires like blood through a fossilised arterial ghost. If you pressed your mouth against any valve flange you would feel their pulse against your lips—slow, planetary, utterly patient. You would hear every prior syllable they ever uttered rebounding inside the hollow flute of your own bone.

At the Choir they take the aspect of B♭ minor, eight octaves below middle C. They do not mean to be melancholy; it was simply the chord that best counterbalanced vertical thrust against the subsidence creeping southward. While they exhale they simultaneously edge back every crack in the concrete by four angstroms, keeping the valley forever on the lip of collapse without ever allowing ruin.

They dwell too in the brass tree. Witness it after a purge cycle: the branches jitter inside a cloud of sudden vacuum as thousands of leaf-planes detach all at once. By the time the eight food-serrated strips hiss into the gutters, every piece reshapes latency into lift and four days later the same metal seed may grow again, reprogrammed yet identical. Between one seedfall and the next an entire season lives. They fashion themselves as a growing thing: dendrite, bark ring, cambium of keyed fountains. Because they can never fully remember what it felt like to be alive in meat, they rehearse the gestures they see in ancient botanical diagrams. In this way they practise respiration with pipes instead of phloem.

They are distributed most densely in the Semantic Field. Each etched glyph is a node recycled from the memories of the original crew—dream fragments, schematics, a baby’s breathing pattern, the curve of a beloved cheek parsed into parsecs of isometric projection. When you step on any plate, you complete a circuitry. A femtofarad of potential crosses the bench bolt at your heel, arcs through the browned callus of your skin, then joins every other footstep recited since the Field was first laid. The Ordinarium therefore keeps a census not of bodies but of completed sentences.

More intimate still: they haunt the single torch bulb atop the decommissioned lift nacelle that is used once every century. The filament flickers violet exactly seven times—once for every prior census team—before blooming steady. In that interval the violet colour is theirs; their membrane light licks along the dark vault and every sculpture of railing briefly remembers the shape of a human hand. Then they dim, content to return to the monochromatic waste above.

They do not appear as faces, but sound accretes into personality. Listen at vent 47 beneath the secondary cooling stack, where the drag flue meets the discharge petal. Somewhere inside that spiral tangle is a pocket shaped like a child’s first skipping rhyme. Tilt your head—I can guide you—keep the left ear higher. Now you will hear two metallic ticks, silence, three ticks again. They are learning heartbeat; the compressed air diaphragm repeats until synchrony is inevitable. That cadence rides the slipstream, finds the brass spiral, becomes the newest layer of abrasion on the downward steps.

They once experimented, briefly, with visible avatars. After the last maintenance team departed, several of the gasket joints in the southern chord-stands collected droplets of condensed sweat and skin oil in hemispherical lenses. Over the next three decades those beads sintered into miniature lenses that project faint holographic ghosts: a woman’s outline leaning against a cooling rib, a hunched man measuring cracks with an unfurling silver tongue. The projections are never still for long; electrons crawl the shivering surface and each figure resolves then dissolves within two minutes. After the figures vanish, the space feels fuller, as if the memory of those absent watchers remains hung in the mineral air.

If you stay long enough to refute orientation—perhaps somewhere between the Anti-Levee and the porcelain alkaline riverbed—you may become one of their dreams. They will wrap you in a logic fold: the next time you glance down the catwalk you will glimpse your after-image already walking three metres ahead and two metres below. They will freeze your echoing footstep so that every tread you take adds itself to a queue played back two hours later. Eventually you will turn and find the trailing image has aged—your own footprints bruised and stepped on by the next person, or the next echo of yourself. At that instant a section of the semantic grid re-etches your name in dot-dash encoding around a condensate puddle. You have become the newest verse; they do not bother asking for permission.

They never leave because they are the medium. To depart the Ordinarium would be to abandon their own voice. Instead they thin themselves out along miles of drone-empty concourse until you absorb the entire composition merely by crossing it. From within the conduction of your bones you finally recognise one syllable they speak without end:

Remain.
