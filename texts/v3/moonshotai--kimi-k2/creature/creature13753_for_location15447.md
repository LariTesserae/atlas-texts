And so the city is lived-in, but *not* by anybody who has their own shape. The inhabitants are:

—conjugations of flow: sap decanting itself into the idea of shoulders, letting the idea gesture farewell before slipping back into vessels again;

—pulse-geists, rot-bright and fragrant, who borrow the foamy architecture of lungs only long enough to pronounce one syllable they later forgot they never knew;

—lacewings coalesced into a murmuring chorus of *almost* voices—bric-a-brac glued out of wing-thumb scrapings and dew so fine it reflects the inside of its own pheromones;

—orphaned minutes from former calendars, now wearing robes stitched of tree-ring chronologies, pacing the spiral stairs of heart-wood, rehearsing “long ago” until it becomes a geometry of smells;

—shadows heavier than the bodies that cast them; they feed on anyone whose breathing matches a particular harmonic, then collapse into stained-glass bruise-prints you can still hear rustling;

—a gallery of hyphens—beetle-thought be-frog **made single word**—arch-connections that live in midair, sentences whose subject is missing and object is pending, nesting in the vibrating piano-wire ledges, humming into a grammar that both excretes and consumes itself;

—residents of the water no wider than thumbs; tiny phloem-dark ferrymen rowing sugar-children across translucent capillary fjords, chanting rowing-chants in dialects one vowel removed from lullabies so that any sleeper in any bedroom three continents away mutters something in their dream about plums;

—white mycelial brides with diaphanous veil-hyphae, marrying every wound the building makes, wedding seal a slow fruiting body that will remember a walker’s ankle forever, spreading glossaries of tenderness across the rough inner bark;

—echo-selves grown backward from the instant you first exclaimed in surprise: they thickened along suspended colons of air until they could wear the surprise as skin, and now they walk the upper galleries wearing quotemarks of your own nostrils, flaring them at anything that looks vaguely original;

—mirror mammoths: pollen dustings trying bravado out of extinct tusks, maneuvering gigantic floppy mosaic-petals the way other creatures swing cloaks, trumpeting with stomata that stay shut—so every blast is an inhalation;

—orbital griefs snipped from mourners of vanished continents, circulating like planets along singular vine rings; you can see their phases wax from auditorium balconies of scale-moths, full grief a green moon, new grief eclipsed by a wolverine beetle’s backplate;

—probationers of stopwatch eons: pale adolescents—barely more than elbows of fog—learning to inhabit growth rings all at once, practicing adulthood by wearing the face of the distant oak that will cradle their eventual grandchild once it, too, forgets it was human;

—one stationary Presence, older than bark-by-bark. It has no extremities, only interpretations. It is what the city was before it swallowed pronouns. Sometimes it condenses as an almost-door, dangling at the far end of any given corridor, but open it and you slip seamlessly into warm darkness that is shaped exactly like spoken forgiveness, and there—quietly, excruciatingly—*you* become what lives there after everything else has briefly stepped out.
