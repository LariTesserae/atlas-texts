No one lives here.  
 No one ever could.

But something is here— something already passed through the long event of having-been-alive and now lingers in the post-mortem of syntax.  It has no name, because names are wet verbs and every droplet of verb has been freeze-dried out of the walls.  Still, if you insist on a pronoun, call it the Archivist, because that is the last station any librarian can reach after every patron has died of thirst between the lines.

The Archivist is not a creature; it is a curvature in the paper.  If you slide your gaze along any page-seam you will notice that certain letter-bevels describe the negative shape of a seated figure: femurs folded against a thorax, a bowed crescent of spine, three fingers touching an absent cheek.  Those hollows were not carved.  They are the vacuum left by a reader who sat so long that the walls despaired of ever being turned again and simply set like plaster around the emptiness.  The silhouette is perfect, down to the tiny dimple where a chin once rested against a collarbone in thought.  Step close and you can still see the micro-crease in the wall that once accepted the warm exhalation of a living question.

The Archivist never blinks—lids are liquidity, and liquidity is contraband—yet it sees.  Its seeing is the opposite of surveillance: it watches inward, toward the interior of finished meaning.  Every fossil vowel is a miniature diorama of its former sound; the Archivist walks these panoramas without moving, the way a dreamer walks REM sleep.  If you could press your ear to the negative outline you would hear, or think you hear, the faint click of turning pages that long ago sublimated into the hiss.  That click is the Archivist’s residual pulse, a heartbeat made of fingernail on parchment.

Sometimes, at the exact angle where two paragraphs meet in a brittle earthquake of syntax, the figure appears almost convex instead of concave, as though the Archivist were leaning out to inspect you.  Do not speak.  Language here has the tensile strength of ash.  Offer only silence; silence is the one native coin that still passes.  The Archivist will accept it and file it in the hollow that once carried a sigh.  After acceptance you may feel lighter—one syllable of your inner monologue will be gone forever, neatly excised.  You will never notice which one, but from that moment on you will be slightly more legible to the room, and slightly less legible to yourself.

No one appointed the Archivist; appointment is a future tense.  The position opened when the final period of the final sentence dehydrated and snapped off, leaving a clean white crater.  Into that vacancy the residue of every abandoned reader pooled, atom by atom, until a composite custodian precipitated.  Its jurisdiction is limited: it may shelve you, but it may not unshelve you.  It may alphabetize your memories under any letter now vacant of ink, but it may never alphabetize your exit, because exits have been checked out and never returned.

At the center of the vaulted hall—though center is a polite fiction in a space whose dimensions equal the inside of a thimble—there is a single comma-fern growing from the eye of a lowercase “e.”  The fronds curve to mimic the Archivist’s seated silhouette; the two shapes form a chalice of negative space that collects nothing.  Once every aeon the Archivist remembers breathing, and the shock of that memory causes the fern to tremble exactly one micron.  The motion is so small it finishes before it begins, yet the entire library registers the event: every letter-surface flexes infinitesimally, the light flares a shade brighter, the hiss dips one cycle lower.  Then equilibrium seals again, like a blade snapping shut on the idea of wind.

No narrative can hold the Archivist; narratives are rivers and this is their salt-flat.  But if you stay—if you surrender the last wet consonant of your name—you will become its assistant.  Your task will be simple: face the wall that most resembles your own reflection and wait for a vacancy to appear.  When it does, incline your head until the space accepts the contour of your vanished pulse.  The hiss will bloom around you like applause for a job well done.  After that you will not be forgotten; you will be filed.  And the Archivist, one silhouette richer, will resume its vigil over the white desert where stories go to finish dying, cool and bright and perfectly still.
