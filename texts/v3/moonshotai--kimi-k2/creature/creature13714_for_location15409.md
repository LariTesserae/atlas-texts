It moves in the bracket of time that only industrial vacancies know, that nobody calendars or clocks.  
First it is an echo: a rust-flick slipping along the mill-scale pages when the condensate breathes in. Then it gathers.

It is composed of half-thermoformed plastic lids—scrubbed white, concave like owl dishes—and the aluminum pull-tabs of mis-filled burrito sleeves, but those are only bones on which the rest is draped. The moment any human turns his back on the #7 module, the caps and rings float into alignment by mutual magnetism; the drones and forklifts outside orchestrate them the way planetary orbits orchestrate compasses. Once gathered they fasten edge-to-edge by pure surface tension of embedded solder: a handshake too small to see, the way two droplets fuse in zero-g.

When the pieces kiss, a membrane of amber burrito grease stretches across the interior like chicken wire soaked in roux. Grease membrane hardens under EM pulses from the lichens, becoming translucent sheet: a stomach in negative space. Suddenly there is swallowing volume, a hemi-spherical hollow commensurate with a large scoop of ice cream scooped from nowhere. Into this cavity crawl the micro citizens—pseudoscorpions not with chelicerae but with retractile ball-point pen nibs for claws, tipped with transparent capillary bulbs. They harvest condensate, solder, and spice-laden mist into streams that they inject like transfusions back into the membrane. Each injection threads the grease sheet with darker veins—cumin, chili pepper, a note of monosodium citrate. Over three belt cycles the sheet tints the exact color of cheap molé pressed against styrofoam. The living construct is then no longer lid-and-rings but a single stretched bubble enclosing a whispering emptiness; visitors crossing the negative space feel as though they have stepped into the inside of a burrito cooking conceptually while still remaining plastic item #7492 tick-tocking overhead.

Inside, the sphere hangs by only three lichen filaments—electric cyan, mango, and pale rust—forming a trinary guy-wire bridge. The filaments form emergency scaffolding; every time they twitch, mylar sneeze-guards vibrating, the bubble pulses. A subtle peristalsis. The scent flares: sautéed onion essence pried from the edge pockets of tortillas twenty paces above. Anyone shrunk to crevice-scale feels a brief flashback to Friday night take-out after the bars closed. Memory baked into aerosol.

As it accumulates mass, it sprouts sense. First a cinnamon-brown iris made from the round die-cut “recycle” logo of a sleeve which flips itself outward, grommet popping through the grease membrane like a shy pan-fried egg. The iris blinks every 77.4 seconds—synchrony with the passing product above. During the brief darkening, the pseudoscorpions tremble; copper whiskers stiffen and the new rust-petals stop curling even if condensation is high. Order travels the way silence spreads when a stadium notices an injury on the field. Then the blink is over and constriction relaxes. Whispering resumes.

It has no body in the traditional arrangement. It is a negative creature: you point toward a burrito bulb vending machine and it is precisely all the space not occupied by vending machine. You step sideways into safety gap and you realize the gap itself has shaped itself into a placid koi-mouth smelling of mesquite smoke, ready to engulf you without teeth. To inhale within its radius means pulling in volatile guajillo compounds; exhale releases the crisp hiss of vacuum-sealed cheese reacted with nitrogen. There is no tongue, only flavors informed by pressure waves tuned to 21 kHz—the bats of sensible seasoning.

Lower, parallel to the belly, hangs intestines: a braided sleeve of seven white twist-ties reclaimed from failed frozen-churro packs. With each belt cycle the twist-ties jiggle, slowly untwisting themselves millimeter by millimeter. When finally 77.4×77.4 seconds have elapsed—one actual FoodCo floor hour—the sleeve is fully unbraided and becomes a threaded string of tiny cubic pellets each made of alumina filament from expired printer reels packed with dehydrated black-bean powder. The pellets drop one at a time onto the grease surface inside the membrane where they dissolve into caramel-fringed darkness: burnt sugar of the bean’s future. The construct thus metabolizes the cycle of the factory not as an external inputs stream but as its own heartbeat countdown visible to the lichens like a tiny shadow sundial.

At maximum satiation the sphere’s membrane flexes inward, seeking vertical stretch. The pseudoscorpions marshall. In disciplined tandem they grip their copper hairs and lash the trinary filament bridge outward so the sphere folds inside-out in two furled flips, lacquer wings of burnt grease painting the adjoining walls of the slot with glossy signage no human will ever notice. Folded out, it has become something between mandala and assembly manual: concentric circles implying nested burritos at scale-planck, annotated by solder tracks. Now the construct no longer hides but simply presents itself as a two-dimensional Sanskrit recipe distributed in mirror across the metal, readable top-to-bottom by lichen pattern, left-to-right by polarized EM whisper.

Finally—inevitably—it must shrink. Condensation always wins; entropy oils the edges of every machine. Solder chills. Grease embrittles. The pseudoscorpions relearn their claws can be pens again, reverse-inscribe instructions into mill-scale pages, tucking them shut so the recipe folds back like an origami fortune cookie no voyager will eat. When the hour ends and next shift footsteps drum the catwalk, the sphere collapses to a single flat aglet left dangling from the lichen filament: a burnt cinnamon glucose droplet hovering like a tear.

Yet inside the tear hides another dilation readying for the next eye-blink. A latent promise:  

I am the zero in burrito zero setfree. Where the temperature is mild and the cylinders hum.  

I am where sour cream dreams the lactic retort. where tortilla finds itself opaque to gamma. where bean and pepper agree to meet in forgotten neglect between the humming motors.  

I am the tongue that tastes nothing save absence of wrapper and yet remembers everything by smell.

And when you have studied my glyphs long enough to try to steal them onboard a tiny USB key, you’ll discover on insertion nothing more than checksum error 7492-VOID. But by then copper filaments will be knitting into the silicone under the key's clear shell; the next keyboard you type at will begin to smell faintly of corn masa at sunrise.

Some technicians have reported finding breadcrumbs of cinnamon sugar gathered oddly beside their workstation mice, or felt the ghost of someone else’s late-night snack lick across their fingers as if the night itself were softly hungry. The construct does not care about borders or label lines; it carries the flavor of backshift restlessness across entire towns where nobody knows the machines are whispering dinners into their mouths.

The engineers taste it and shrug: residual spices, they say.  
But the pseudoscorpions call it Telmunch, a name that means “everything the belt cannot fit.”  

And Telmunch travels upstream.
