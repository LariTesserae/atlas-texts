No one lives in the Hollow Thicket, yet it is not un-tenanted.  
There is, first and last, the Thicket Itself – an entity too diffuse for a throat, too patient for a name, but nevertheless awake. Call it Motherclock, Root-of-Roots, or simply “the Keeper,” since it keeps. It began as an ordinary stump-dweller spore, a child no larger than the soot at a candlewick, but after the smaller world collapsed its ambitions stayed. One pulse, then a second, then every beat grew heavy and capitalised: Pulse. Each Pulse filled the chamber with listening, with trying again, until trying became forever.

Keeper is a monarch without ministers. Its ministers areselves: heartwood still soft, ducts still wet. It governs by net-sap, by chlorophyll-knotted law. When the continuous drumbeat babbles a moment off-key, Keeper tightens an amber valve, swells a honey-duct, and the rhythm obediently locks again. It has no plan, only memory from before the word “before.” That memory is a sense that once there was departure, and departure is forbidden; hence the root-binding of every would-be wanderer until wandering is bred out of flesh and pocketed in chemistry.

Keeper is no lonesome genius. Nested in its cortex live lesser sovereigns:  

• The Moth-Forges.  Each is barely the ghost of an insect; a copper coin of wing-beat and instinct, soldered to the sunless cycle. Originally two fluttered in by accident; now their children twitch salt-green among leafless ropes. They have shed the ability to orient, to mate, to moult. Their thoraxes open like clinker-built furnaces where Keeper fissile-starches golden sugars. They are the Thicket’s muscle memory for movement; their wings simulate wind long after wind was banished.  

• The Glass-Shrimp.  Transparent denial of eyes. They skate in root-basins no deeper than a saucer, beating long liquorice gills in perfect eleven-minute accords with the Keeper’s own systole. At full age (some three hundred and eighty-one years by the outside calendar, though none abide there) each shrimp resigns its shell, dissolves into rime-white lipids, and those lipids are licked back up the slenderest fibre until the shell recrystallises as a pearl of latticed stone inside Keeper’s heart. Thus salt is remembered, calcium conserved, and the mindless crustaceans become sinew in reverse.  

• The Fossil Choir.  A bird still mid-soar, a pine-marten forever leaping, a child’s shoe sole pebbled with iron nails – every trespasser has been admitted to this sculpture yard at the vault’s outermost meshwork. Tissues were rinsed to shadow, then knit into woody bas-relief. Now they are mouths without tongues that speak only boundary: the Thicket sings through them a lullaby that nothing new shall enter. Their petrified marrow stays faintly resonant, adding hollow harmonics to the great drip-drum until – from the outside – a traveller crossing the moor by night might fancy a second, fainter heartbeat pacing under his own.

• Breath-Clackers.  These began as bacteria in some forgotten lung, but grew upward into stems pale green, jointed like bamboo sized for insects. At the eleven-minute crest each stalk cracks open, exhales chlorophyll-cool perfume, clicks shut again – the Thicket’s version of a clock whose dial is scent and whose hand is sound. Every vapour ring carries a census of dissolved nutrients minted by shrimpland, honed by midge-forges, authorised by Keeper. The fragrance spreads; when it reaches furthest stalk the circuit is complete and Keeper restarts the cycle. Thus the interior temperature never strays from that mild autumn dusk, and no molecule grows weary of its neighbour.

• The Lineage Tapeworms.  Myriad, pale, flat as peeled bark: they stripe the air itself, so attenuated they are only visible as slight disturbances when condensation slides along them. Each worm remembers for Keeper a fortnight of rain, a century of drought, an eclipse. They store these years in protein lattices until the date is required – then a worm rolls its body into a hollow horn and releases the weather it once hosted, replaying pressure and moisture so exactly that lichen membranes resin in brief confusion. Falsified microclimates are the Keeper’s nervous laughter.

None of these creatures is a tenant; all are rooms in the same body dreaming its own architecture. Yet Keeper considers them personalities, in the way a cello might recognise that it once had the shape of a tree and sometimes misses its leaves.

And through them persists something still smaller, still more lingering: the Echo-Sun, bottled a full lifetime ago. Keeper captured a straight ray of noon light in a single leaf vacuole, plumped the vacuole to a sac the size of an opal, and learned to turn that pinpoint like a watch dial. The glow is dimmer but everlasting; the molecule it rides remembers the angle of summer. When the drumbeat hushes for any fraction, the Echo-Sun tilts its ray to baste a root-face, coaxing a droplet not yet ready to fall. Thus the laws of conservation stretch, bend, but must never break inside this solemn stomach glow.

So who lives?  
No one but the unborn tomorrow the Thicket already spent three million evenings conserving.  
And paradoxically you, already half digested the instant your foot crossed the air-lock of vines. Keeper has numbered your marrow by drum-ticks. In its slow ledger you now possess a death-date measured not in years but sap years; your calcium is pledged, the hum under your ribs matched tempo to tempo with the Clacker choir. Should you speak, your syllables will wreathe upward through fibre and lichen until the Echo-Sun tastes them. If memories are patterns, they too are minerals; Keeper will knead them into rootlets until even your lullabies for lost places dribble down as ordinary condensation.

Still, you’ll never be alone. You’ll keep.  
As will the copper moths.  
As will the rootless shrimp.  
As will the bird at mid-wing-beat, the marten mid-leap.  
And overhead, the Keeper itself, yearning (or simply measuring; the difference was forgotten) towards an infinity compacted into a single breathing dusk — an infinity it already perfectly remembers, so closely that memory need never end.
