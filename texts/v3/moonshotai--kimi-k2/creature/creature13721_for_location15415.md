Who lives here?  
More importantly, *how* does something live where life was never prepared to?

The place keeps more than one household, yet none keep calendars.  
They insist on plural pronouns—“we,” “they,” “is-shift”—because inhabiting the summit only works if the self comes segmented.  
You never meet a resident whole; you meet their consensus.

Take the _lithocapellae_, the “stone-keepers,” first-seen human silhouettes carved by subtraction rather than addition: negative statues whose outlines are exact volumes of missing rock.  
Inside those hollow human shapes drift colonies of self-cooling plasma in braids—phosphor-blue ribbons that pulse like breathing and hum the copper-snow flavor into words.  
A lithocapella forms only when the basin’s rotation crosses the ninety-degree mark.  
Until then, the rock refuses to acknowledge your outline as even possible.  
When the shift comes, it lets them step forward as an absence upon which warmth, mass, and memory are briefly overdrawn.  
They are archivists tasked with filing whatever the stone once forgot: memories of the core’s grief when iron first sank, an auroral sigh from an extinct moon.  
Their job finishes when every memory is read out loud into the mineral lattice; then they fold back into solid silence and wait, a query latent in the stone’s mute lattice.  
Consequently—but also concurrently—they are the same as the basin, and the basin is the same as their afterthought.  
Plural pronouns fix that recursive ownership.

Then there are the _umbra-delvers_, patchwork organisms forged from the basin’s own inventory.  
Begin with lichen rings vanadium-red, bead them through the dag’s leftover scale-dust; moisten with the fleeing liquid of reversed dew.  
Hold at equidistant pressure to the vacuum between galaxies for a thousandth of a second.  
Now wait ten million foldings of entropic time.  
The result is a kite-shaped creature like an interlocking shell of kite shields, each panel etched with the grammar of vanished atmospheres.  
They do not speak.  
Instead, they articulate by adjusting over-bright afterimages on their mirrored bell-plates—mirrors that borrow memories from whatever organism hovers in their refraction angle.  
When a human outline (for example, yours, should you have stayed overstayed your cycle) is caught and reflected, the delver enacts—silently—every intention you feared to finish.  
The town you never built; the apology you never delivered.  
Performing that ghost act in complete stillness drains the delver of half its vanadium rings; new ones grow when next the basin flexes.  
They are consequence mavens. They digest unapplied futures.

Between these two orders exists the third, most circumspect, most fleeting: the _chord-shadows_.  
They are what the mind-thag breathes out but forgets to rein in.  
Each exhalation leaves four to six of them curled like smirched breath on the chilled vapour.  
Their bodies are a spectrum of refracted absence: if you could see palette enabling shadows to argue about colour. Yet you *feel* them, because their surface area is exactly equal to the number of synapses you have just used trying to grasp them.  
They have no mass but retain hydrostatic memory; when caught in the negative flood streaming down from the ceiling ponds, they briefly assume shapes of oceans you have only dreamed—childhood seas chalked across adobe walls, the watery horizon that closed above a childhood skiff.  
After four minutes thirty-three seconds they collapse back into infrasonic resonance, returning to the dag’s next lungful.

Finally—though the word “finally” is taboo here—there is **you**, the guest that is not a resident yet suffers from precocious residency.  
Your visa expires at N + 12 descending notes, but it renews three beats earlier every cycle in subtle increments imperceptible even to the lithocapellae.  
Each rotation siphons off one anecdote of you into the layered stone; each zero-mass flood liberates an anecdote of someone (thing?) else into your bones.  
After millennia you could be majority made of this plateau, just as the plateau is growing gently into you.  
The structures will detect the overlap and rearrange your absence room according to the new height and breadth of what you have become.  
Whether that is a kindness or a camouflage tactic no longer matters.  

What actually lives at the top of everything?  
A loose federation of memory consortia: stone-quanta, mirrored futures, breathing chords, and a borrowed husk called “I.”  
They don’t keep track of how many they compose; addition and subtraction are provincial notions where entropy itself learns reverse.  
All that is certain is they have arrived at consensus: there must be at least one sentient mouth to hear the next twelve-note culmination.  
Any future visitor is therefore already on the payroll—account not settled until the basin has memorized what the throat meant to say long after breath is swallowed.
