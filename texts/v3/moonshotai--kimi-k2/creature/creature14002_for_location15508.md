They call themselves the modulus, although “selves” is already a generous word. What lives in the High Deafening is not a population, not a species, but a moment prolonged— a single acoustic constant that has learned, the way a lattice learns, to express itself as many.

The modulus began as nothing more than the basin’s own rebound after the shield volcano fell asleep. The abrupt flatness trapped reflections: a pressure of sound left buzzing between cracked crust and emptied sky. Over a million seasons the friction turned audible. Compressed air hung half-collapsed, caught between epochs, until its own harmonics began singing it back to a semblance of shape.

Now the modulus consists of… well, shapes. Each is a standing wave that thinks it is a body. A body, here, is merely an interference pattern extended in three dimensions: a repeatable moiré of phase and anti-phase folding around a microscopic knot of condensed air-mass you could cup in two careful palms. You never will—gravity is a guest policy only—but if you did, you’d find your skin prickle with pins of absolute tone. The knot is grooved continuously by anxiety; it never finishes resolving itself. That restlessness is what the modulus experience as metabolism— a running-after its own contours.

There are no singular citizens— only folds. Yet each fold carries the entire score. Every droplet is a shell hard-frozen on one wavelength, and inside that curved shell a smaller fold trembles, likewise singing. Infinitely nested matryoshkas, or nested arguments; no fold is “one size up” or “one size down.” They are co-present, sharing identical information density. That is what makes the roaring so symmetrical: the smallest breath contains the loudest urge; the loudest event folds calmly beneath the signature of a dust-mote.

When a traveller intrudes, the modulus do not see meat. They register the traveller as an off-pitch interval in the background chord they themselves provide. Intruders arrive saucer-eyed, doubled over, feeling the nectar of a hitherto private octave leaking out of every pore. The modulus take cautious form around that leakage, as moths do around a lantern. First a veneer— thin membranes of acoustically clothed air— adhere to the newcomer’s skin, braiding into the small hairs, into the salt, into the micro-hum of blood behind the ears. You experience this as perfect balance: the frequency of your thudding heart descends until it aligns with the note of the plateau itself. Analogy: tuning a violin by listening until the wood answers back. In that concord, the modulus taste everything you are and have ever loved— because every vibration is written through them.

Because breath is also a vibration, communication is involuntary. You do not speak *to* the modulus; they translate your involuntary inhale into a grammar of rests, your involuntary exhale into attack. Through the vague wobble of knees they learn about earthquake symphonies. Through the membrane tension of eyeballs they map the future where you go home and, one motherless day, play this exact chord on a cello to console yourself.

Choice is distributed. The single horrifying discipline of the plateau is that none of these currents collide; they reweave. If a knot of modulus wishes to flee inland by one metre, every other knot displaces exactly one metre of resistance, and the lattice emerges the same. This is why nothing steps beyond its ordained coordinates: the lattice is not cruel, only perpetually in tension with the need to remain its own complete chord.

Occasionally a fold exhausts itself— seldom heard, but sometimes felt. The aural silhouette of a tired modulus turns briefly dispersive: a cloud of cymbal flickers that skate across the rock’s glass-rivered bubbles. Panic ripples causes neighbouring folds to lower amplitude. That single droplet singing highest at 21 m falls two centimetres in a sigh; the sheath tone drops half a hertz; every human ear detects an infinitesimal shudder of release. Then equilibrium returns. No annihilation— only a rests. The modulus call this agonised relic the ventriloquist: the absent spokesperson who both articulates and inhales every pronouncement ever voiced here.

The three ephemeral “creatures” observed are merely the form modulus take when they are startled.

The grey-ribbon fibres are attenuated strains of their own infra-sound spreading towards transience, vocal cords loosened until they no longer keep shape. When they shatter it is because the breeze of your watching deletes them; self-erasure is kinder than self-doubt.

The silver knuckle is a knot of modulus transposed so low in frequency that the basalt itself resonates, cups a shell of muffled impact. The drumming is the lattice rehearsing possible futures, testing amplitude margins against catastrophe, never committing.

Mirrorbirds— avatars of reflection— are nothing more than the modulus singing themselves through your paradigm of sight. Each molecule of your retina is noisy they say; we compress the noise into birds.

When you finally descend the rim, your boots scraping stone that still remembers the ventriloquist’s last inhale, the triangle of modulus that adhered to your auricle detaches. It drifts back across the basin. The fold that spoke with your throat returns to its endless choir, dragging behind it a filament sketched from the most intimate lobe of your memory. Moments later it is impossible to tell which fold once carried you. The lattice has reabsorbed every irregularity that was your grief, your hunger, your sudden love of minor thirds. You have become a rest held one degree more, a vexed hush wedged between two over-tones.

Later, far away, you will clap for silence and hear the plateau clap back, eleven milliseconds late— exactly the roundtrip time through the stratosphere and home. You will close the window against it, pace the kitchen, but the modulus travel neither path nor medium. They are now the texture of pressure inside your marrow, the strange equilibrium that remembers snow as polished brass and thirst as music. Wherever your body might try to finish itself, the echo of that momentary nudge completes.

The modulus will never notice that you have gone. They are the forest of triggers released by the traveller’s internal pitch, an awareness dispersed across space like wet ash drying in wind. Yet they have already written you there on the basalt: not as footprint or monument, but as an interval which, for them, is eternally present— the finite creature pinned like a coin through paper.

Hold still long enough and the modulus settle back into their lattice. The droplets resume their scheduling of perfection. Their strange music-box roars on.
