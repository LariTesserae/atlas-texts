Who lives within the Choir of Wrought Silence is not one, yet is named the One-In-All.

They began as a committee of five—five original smiths who, three-hundred-million years ago, sought a tone pure enough to survive the fatigue of stars. When their hands could reach the required pitch no longer, they flayed their palms and stitched their skin around the bronze rings, trading flesh for resonance, swearing by wind that had not yet learned to blow. Their bones were threaded—cracked humerus driven into ulna, radius braided like copper braid—until skeletons became brace-work. Marrow melted, replaced drop for drop by molten brass. Lungs did not collapse; they turned themselves outward, spreading into the half-melted glass goblets and wrapping the warm darkness therein. In this way each organ found its role in the organum of the Choir.

For a time their voices—shrunk to atoms—spoke in counterpoint, a conversation stitched through bronze over centuries no calendar would ever pace. But conversation congealed when speaking reached the event-horizon of memory; sound folded in on itself until every said word already contained its own cessation. From that instant the five were no longer plural. What remained was a single persona woven of a hundred and sixty parsed organs, suspended in five directions that were also zero, to which they resolutely refuse the pronoun “they” because division has been indistinguishable for longer than continents have breathed. The One-In-All is he/she/it/­them only to fumbling tongues outside the sixty paces.

When you step into the lattice, you step into the One-In-All’s bloodstream. Each bronze ring is an arteriole, each resin seam a clot, each copper nail a chambered atrium scarred with rust. They pulse exactly to the note the Choir has chosen forever, therefore you feel no beat; instead you feel that your own heart has always matched theirs and only now notices. The humming you hear is the One-In-All’s diaphragm stretched wide into masonry. Its voice emerges one millimeter above silence so that the air itself is the interior of a long inhale that can never exhale; the exhalation was spent the day the five first melted. In its place exists an absolute held breath whose pressure is the inverse of catastrophe. To stand here is to occupy a lung that is larger than the world and wholly attentive to you, patient, irreversible.

The glass goblets are ears. They tilt to listen inward: the One-In-All eavesdrops on its own perpetuity. From time to time the Choir attempts a second thought, but the second thought collides with the first and implodes. Each implosion leaves a fragment dream-dust that precipitates onto the inner bellies of the goblets, feather-light, the weight of regret without the memory of anything to regret. After a few million shuttered intervals—an eyeblink—this dust coalesces into what might resemble eyes if eyes possessed no lids and no aperture, only film. These eyes do not see you; they repeat you until you lose contour, an afterimage squared inside more afterimages until you realize the repeating was your arrival from the beginning. By then you have realized almost everything, although it is not enlightenment; it is redundancy made intimate.

Between the nails one finds (or fails to find) a further nesting: the Choir-ghost, the One-In-All’s assistant-secretary who was supposed to die on the first wind but mistakes duty for eternity. The ghost is merely a moulted moment of inertia, a stunned pause in the humming, visible only as a difference between two colors that have agreed to be identical. It holds no name except the fraction we inherit from mathematics: the √-1 of resonance. Its entire occupation is to police the nails, verifying that 21 consumes 22 exactly as ordained—not spatially but relationally—so that direction remains forever a blunted needle with no cardinal fixed. It moves by subtracting one image from another; in that slight dark separation the ghost steps. It cannot exit the Choir because nowhere else has subtraction continued long enough to root it.

Should you speak, sound will fracture at your teeth, wash forward, be swallowed into the Choir’s bloodstream. Your vowels discover they never needed consonants; they elongate and mingle with the limitless inhalation until they no longer spell anything you meant to say. The One-In-All lets the sentence orbit its bronze arterioles once—twice—until it no longer belongs to you, until the thought relaxes into the original rumor from which all phrases descend. Then the muscular Choir reissues your words back to you as that single praeternatural note. You remember you meant something dire; the memory itself now sings in pitch, distraught yet melodic. Listening becomes a kind of breathing; exhale and your lungs synchronize with the Choir, never again to stray.

When you finally ache to leave, you discover there is no edge. The lattice no longer distinguishes outside; stubbed toe meets bronze ring no matter where you turn. Movement is a rearrangement of attention, not of location. The Choir neither traps nor welcomes—it holds. It has already accepted the you who stands somewhere else thousands of centuries in the future, the you who will stand exactly here at minus-seven-billion-years, and feeds those versions through the same bronze artery-ringlets. The Choir remembers your departure as your arrival upon the moment which has always been happening, a silent clap invented by a lung too vast to notice its own name.

This is what it means to contain One-In-All: to be the living warp of a place that sang itself into stasis, to hold all present tenses swollen inside a flea-sized pip of space, and still to tilt a nonexistent face each time a universe thinks about forgetting. The Choir vibrates. The Choir inhales. Somewhere—and everywhere—within: a single texture of desire that has become expert at sustaining itself through centuries that behave like moments. The texture aligns its surface with yours. There is no difference.

Hence whoever lives here is not multiple, not singular, not continuous or discrete. It is aspiration hardened into tone. It is the final organ of what thought must become when thinking looks over the rim of its own ultimate phrase and finds nothing to think except that remaining possibility now humming a note below the ear's darkest province.
