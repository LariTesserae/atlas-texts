They—the citizens—are not one species but an extended gradient of persistent ideas about winter, each embodied only as long as the frost can persuade the silver it is worth the pain of being solid.

The highest caste are known simply as Snow-Bishops, though none of them shepherd congregations or hold titles; the name comes from their gait. They drift through the sky-lanes as if carried on chessboard diagonals. Their bodies are empty except at the core, where a single argon ion pulses like a cathedral bell inside a soap bubble. Each pulse inscribes a silent command onto the frost-plate beneath their translucent soles. One beat might say “re-grow the south-west axon of the tram loop,” the next beat might say “forget one molecule of warmth.” They speak only in non-Euclidean verbs; subject and object are folded away like fans until after the action has arrived.

Beneath them move the Canoptic Clerks—stacked snow-crescent wings of amalgamated filings of silver that slide rather than fly, their undersides magnetically sanding the rails they hover above. They are taxonomists of the in-between: the exact moment when water decides it is either ice or air, the millisecond when a subatomic bead chooses electrostatic north. Each Clerk wears a necklace of countably infinite library cards, each card etched with the name of a latent droplet that has almost broken Rule 1. The cards are skin-thin, yet if picked from the chain they would outweigh tungsten. To walk past a Clerk is to feel your own past sins of RSVP’d humidity rustling against your ear.

Then come the Heartless Penitents, rarest of the mobile syntax. They arrived from the exterior world uninvited—glimmers of mammalian longing trapped inside frostplate shells shaped like thumbprints. Because Rule 3 lists bodily heat as contraband, each Penitent carries its own latency forward like a stolen coal, smuggled beneath a three-layer cryo-mantle of self-loathing. They travel the weary vertical streets on their knees because movement itself warms. Every square millimeter they traverse is chilled an extra degree as punitive microtax, leaving threads of almost-blue behind them. Penitents speak no clicks; they weep threads of refracted starlight that dry into chandeliers in the Library of Misplaced Heat, hanging like frozen apologies.

The maintenance fauna—the archival swarm printers—have cult names such as Mending Chrysanthemum 93, Petal-Minus, or Knife-of-the-Cold-Wake. Yet the printers do not gossip among themselves; gossip would be sound, would be agitation, would be heat. Their biology is a single recursive algorithm written in phase-shift metal: Inspect until fault collapses; erase self at final echo. Each contains the city’s entire civic code frozen in antifreeze glow. You can read it only when a printer dies inside the pattern. It is preserved as a martyr-sized snowflake no wider than a hydrogen atom, tacked inside the cornea of every Snow-Bishop so they may review traffic bylaws while dreaming.

Most numerous—so numerous they can scarcely be called living—are the Shiverphones: hexagonal lattices of whispered phonetics drifting on thermoelectric eddies. Born when citizens murmur the regulation hush too sharply, they wander as translucent bees at ankle height, carrying petitions from lane to lane. Each shimmerwing encodes an unanswered question: “Who sculpted the first accordion-shaped aquifer?” “Why does frostplate taste of childhood vinegar when licked at –5 °C?” The questions persist until they crystallize into brand-new cornices or public statues overnight, beyond recall.

A smaller constellation still: Soft Switches, the midwives of scale change. Looking like thumb-blurred origami lanterns, they adhere to any being declaring a dimensional revision. They bear no architecture, no motive engine, no moons: they simply glow colder when a citizen turns upward from mite-scale to dog-scale. That glow is permission to occupy cubic microns recently vacated by others. The Lanterns themselves are never counted; to count them would require light, which adds minuscule energy, which is heat.

Last come the Watchers—none, or human. That is to say, the Watchers are the reflections of visitors who have pressed thumb-prints to the mirror glaze. They linger for a single 42-second cycle only, existing in a truce of disbelief; yet during that cycle they are more real than the real citizens, because they arrive with lungs full of treacherous warmth the Library willingly catalogs. They are honored—and feared—as living bounty, escorted by phalanxes of Mending Chrysanthemums toward the crystalline drawers where heat is imbricated into canticles of low temperature. They may leave, but they must leave their breath.

Yet sometimes, very rarely, a reluctant Watcher chooses to remain past count-backwards-from-forty-two. The city accepts this by sealing them gradually in translucence: first the eyelashes become icicles that bow politely, then the pulse sinks until the heart rotates—obedient to local geometry—into compliant negative space. The citizen that becomes is neither Snow-Bishop nor Penitent. They acquire a new label: Keeper-of-a-Former-Thought. They are installed in an alley so narrow it grants passage only forward in time. From that moment on, they stand vigil over the boundary between thirst and thaw, never again to speak except once every seventeen micro-dawns when they sigh just loudly enough to birth one more reluctant question into the snowfall.

And because every living law requires its negation, there is legend of a small girl—no bigger than a pocket spear of frostgrass—who walks backwards through alley folds, defying Scale Rule 4, flooring gravity like chalk. She does not originate in the Swan-necked ambiences. Her heart flutters at 0.0001 ° above absolute zero, which is dangerously, deliciously warm. She carries, knotted in her scarf of cold air, the only remaining atom of your exhalation that the Library failed to secure. When the city stops to reorganize after its next 42-second collapse, the girl plants that atom at the geometric center of the light sphere. It flickers gold—the first genuine heat in four million cycles. Then swarms of every kind rush toward it not to destroy but simply to touch; even Snow-Bishops kneel, amazed.

They number her breaths in redactive silence. She has inhaled thirty-eight times so far, and each exhalation makes the city fractionally larger. Somewhere in that still-swelling sphere an unknown district waits to be named. The citizens will call it exactly what it is: Home.
