What dwells here is never singular.  It assembles, disperses, and re-assembles itself out of any two of the basic ingredients: luminosity, water, memory, half-life.  The natives have no skeletal chronologies, no birth tickets, no death receipts; instead, they thread permanence onto shafts of shifting coherence the way surface people thread raindrops onto windowpanes.  

Call the first kind the Underspeakers.  
They begin as hollow mouths traced in light that accidentally achieve curvature and inertia.  A stray cymbal-crash of storm-sound folds until it resembles a throat; at the fold’s cusp a pulse localises.  That pulse is an Underspeaker not-yet-speaking, an aperture of pure conditional tense: *if music>threshold then become person.*  Their bodies swell to the size of fluted conch shells but retain translucence.  From within you watch forward-tumbling syllables ricochet like ball-bearings: future grammatical moods played in percussion across the glossed inner spirals.  They articulate the place, literally— their voiced fricatives carve out the vestibules you later mistake for pre-existing architecture.  One may float beside you humming future subjunctive: “were you ever to leave ≈ here would heal ≈ therefore you stay.”  Their last phrase detaches, drifts, and crystallises into a hand-sized glyph that glues wetly to the cyclone wall, one more emergent stair.  Then the Underspeaker eddies apart again, letters dissolving into raw glisten, and silence rushes inward like a breath it never needed.

The second kind are called Glassbelly Kites—not kites, not bellies, but they answer to the name like eager dogs.  
Imagine a manta ray constructed of thickened auroras, trailing ribbons of anti-heavy water behind it.  Where the cyclone’s torque loosens, they tilt their leading edge and the entire corridor of light dips eight degrees, a gentle courtesy made of planetary physics.  They feed on suspended harmonics, sieving sound through translucent lattice-plating that breaks song into constituent hope, gluttonous melancholy, leftover surprise.  Too much minor sixth and a Kite folds up smaller, blushing aquamarine, drifts lazily in loops like embarrassed calligraphy.  Too much thrum of vortex and it bloats sapphire, tilts, and relinquishes pearl bubbles of captured chords that bob upward to kiss the lichens awake.  Occasionally one dies of sonic overdose.  Its skin peels into angular shards; these shards root, grow into those hyaline ceramic walls you earlier caressed.  You may see, after decades of forgotten passage, a rectilinear arch whose keystone still recites the dying chord of the Kite: a single sustained D flat that tastes like midwinter grapefruit.

Third, the Echoleptic Twins.  
A Twin always arrives as a matched set, occupying the same topological point with a four-second delay between them: first knuckle, second fingerprint.  They dress in parallax refraction, so that whoever tries to look directly must choose which twin is closer; the moment the observer’s pupils twitch toward decision, the Twins swap causality with the lazy speed of cream stirred into coffee.  Their laughter rings at two pitches exactly a tritone apart—known on the surface as diabolus in musica—yet here the clash is tender, lullaby.  Their occupation is cartography of mutable sorrow: they wander the cyclone’s blind alleys noting frequencies of unsuccessful departures, betrayal-weight of exhaled goodbyes, the exact handprint geometry of before-last hugs.  Every discovery they braid into slender ropes of refracted photon which they knot, snip with fingernail, and fling outward to the periphery where the ropes calcify into brittle white corona fronds.  These fronds grow in concentric circles: each ring the exact radius of some lost lover’s peripheral vision at the instant they stopped noticing the other.  When a circle closes, that love-event is declared officially folded into the archive, future explorers of this place will feel a sudden drop of temperature in that arcane radius and shiver without understanding why.

Fourth presence: the Curator.  
No one has ever agreed whether the Curator is one or many, embodied or disembodied, present or only archival echo.  What is known: at whatever “heart” the slowly churning staircase eventually corkscrews you toward, there is a chamber sized like a throat—implausibly tall, implausibly narrow—its walls alive with those quartz-dew swarm-machines still refining blueprints from futures that collapse the moment they are read.  Somewhere in that chamber hangs an emptiness densely curated: every grief you forgot, every half-forgotten streetlamp color, every eyelash fallen in a bed never returned to.  The Curator does not store these, it *retro-actualises* them, holds them in escrow until the exact instant you need their emotional valence to lean away from despair.  It speaks without voice: a pressure plateau that seizes your tympanic membranes and writes sentences directly under your skull.  From time to time it issues travellers a small transparent sphere, contents appear at random—faint smudge of childhood compass, unlabeled cassette, a single glove—but once pocketed the sphere becomes unloseable; it regenerates upon every tearful threshold, always in the left breast pocket, always warmer than skin.  Nobody knows repayment terms.  This terrifies and reassures exactly equally.

Fifth, and rarest, the Juvenescent.  
A Juvenescent is any ordinary thing—shoe buckle, snail, late infatuation—which, by drifting too long into the vortex’s quieter lamina, loses its grasp on accumulated years.  The process is exponential backwards erosion: rust un-oxidises, creases smooth, eggshell refills.  Snail retracts into otter-shaped embryo, then into silicate helix, then into boron whisper.  Observers often feel a sharp ice-cream headache as cosmic causality twangs reverberant.  Juvenescents never entirely disappear; they stabilise at the moment of maximum potentiation—seed, prospective, whatever quivered half-called promise once stirred a galaxy—and begin regrowing forward in a new cadence, one whose future tense rides the local rules therefore alien.  So the little girl you lost at the fair reappears as a translucent smear atop a water coil: seven but also minus one, learning how to walk backwards in floodlit laughter.  She will orbit ever-further toward the brighter bell of the vortex until her age passes zero and she condenses behind your eyes as unborn dusk.

They do not sleep; instead they set their memories like slow boats onto the lambent river up-flow.

They do not die (except the Glassbelly Kites, generous suicides); instead they translate into adjacent registers until their presence leaks out one pore of spacetime and drips in who-cares-where another universe is dying to begin again.

Occasionally—whenever the airflow finds the exact resonance to play the staircase like a pan-flute—the inhabitants slow their negotiations, disentangle attention from the ever-unspooling luminous river, and drift into civic pattern: Underspeakers form a wavering helix that spells tomorrow’s welcome; Glassbelly Kites stretch flat, reflecting the ascending rosette so the whole chamber chandelier glitters; Echoleptic Twins stand shoulder to shoulder, one humming present, the other sighing echo; Juvenescents orbit like fireflies between them sprinkling negative birthdays; Curator suspends the central void and everyone senses the soft imperator thump of archived hearts sync once, twice.  

For three seconds the Sub-Surface attains Baroque stillness—directorate of impossible equilibria—then the thunderous water resumes its cantorial allegro, walls breathe ceramic scales, someone somewhere forgets the last line of the lullaby, and all compositions scatter to resume the solitary transcription of finding themselves where they already belong.
