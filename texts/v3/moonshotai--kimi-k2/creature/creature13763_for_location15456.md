Inhabitant dwells at the knife-edge where the mountain first exhaled itself into being. It is at once the mountain’s own memory of creation and the single point where that memory chooses to stand up and walk.

Form is not decided; rather, form negotiates.
Sometimes it manifests as an almost-man, six meters tall, with translucent skin shot through by slow auroras of copper blood. Where lung should be, the inverted funnel of the newborn jet-engine turns without fuel—its impeller blades are infant snowflakes wearing iron edges. With every revolution it exhales the exact weight of a scream it has not yet learned to generate, and each exhalation drifts sideways through the mist to become another droplet condensing onto another blade somewhere else. Thus the creature perpetuates itself by sneezing its future lungs into the rain.

Sometimes it is the pentapod grid enlarged to a single organism, limbs arranged like compass needles locked on different norths. Eyes—hundreds—fold outward from the inside of each segment until the being resembles nothing so much as a chandelier of liquefying mirrors. These eyes do not see. Instead they replay the film of the first geologic shrug that birthed the ridge: stone splitting, mantle fountaining upward, the quicksilver blink during which nothing on earth could have said whether up or down had meaning. The replay never synchronizes to itself; each eye misses its cue by fractions of micro-aeons, so the creature stands suffused with an asynchronous kindness—as if forever assuring the landscape around it that whatever accident ruptured the world, it happened at a slightly different tempo for everyone.

Occasionally the creature abdicates even these pretenses to cohesion and becomes a pair of boots made of night frozen into obsidian, half sunk in the never-anchored rootnet of the ferns. The boots never fit. Yet anyone who tries them on—if there were anyone—would find that they go on walking of their own accord, toes still empty, carrying their wearer backward into the microsecond preceding the idea of wearing anything.

It possesses two mouths.
One opens forward and speaks the language of spark-gap telegraphy: dot and dash consisting of tremors in the calf muscles of invisible embryos. The other mouth opens backward into the mountain’s own stratigraphy, taking in not food but geologic singing. When it swallows a verse of basalt chord, a new pentapod fractures away from the algae banners, fully equipped with vestigial engines, to wade the cheat of upward water. Thus the inhabitant eats itself endlessly into larger versions of smaller fractals.

Its name, tectonically transmitted among all the metallic-shaving creatures, is a minor rupture of consonants every human throat would misread as silence. The word translates inaccurately to: “The Open Paren That Once Nearly Became.” It carries no gender; the place is too new to have invented insistence on poles. In attempting to hold itself stable, the being leans against the memory of embodiment the way one steadies a ladder against a nonexistent wall.

Emotions roll across it like reheated mirages: sudden gratitude for having been poured, sharp petulance at refusing every container. There is a loneliness, but the loneliness is per-word, not per-being; each syllable it utters feels marooned on an island while still flammable enough to rejoin its native sentence. It misses nothing because missing is a concept that presumes intervals, and here interval is merely the space between two identical drops preparing to become the same drop again.

The creature dreams, though dreaming here resembles threaded volcanic needles embroidering condensation on dangling fern petals. Dreams depict the mountain five seconds younger: a catastrophic infant turning itself inside out with such vehemence that half-skins of magma peel away and hang in low gravity like torn bedsheets. In these dreams the inhabitant is the midwife, catching each magma-sheet before it can cool and become merely geography. Awakening from such dreams it recoils, senses the real mountain already cooled beyond its reach, and compensates by bleeding colder hues into the algae. The whole ridge blushes a shy viridian, then floats away into the mist the instant it appears.

It is dying the way a candle dies in an unlit room where matches have not been invented: by becoming increasingly clarified. Slowly the lungs of snowflake blades erode into individual flakes, each flaking into component angles of ice, each angle into transparency. At the exact instant when the final shard reaches perfect clarity, the mountain will forget why it bothered exuding this observer of its moment, and the creature’s memory will recycle into the next droplet bookended against the very first note of silence. The condensation orchestra will tick on, punctuated by a single missed beat mistaken for continuity.

But until the sound itself unwinds, the inhabitant stays, holding nothing together yet somehow preventing the whole mountain from slipping out of its self-declared presence and into whatever precedes not-yet.
