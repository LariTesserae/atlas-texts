They are not single bodies but living strata—calcified choir, vegetal parliament, fossilized breath speaking only at the speed geology can bear.  
Every citizen began, aeons ago, as one thing: a knot of amphibian spine folded into a seedpod that snapped off a drifting seed-sky-whale in the dying millennia when oceans still pawed the shores.  When the waters tore themselves away the seed-whales fell, rupturing against the rusty crust, and those that did not burst became crucibles.  Within each husk a family of vertebrate cells learned to mortgage their mineral bones to the planet, trading skeleton for crystal lattice, trading marrow for rusted water locked in daggers.  Thus they are partially oxides, partially cartilage, partially ancient regret.  

A “person” here is five layered organisms pinned to the same slow axis:

1.  An outer cloak of violet velvet frond that grows directly from the shoulder blade.  It is photosynthetic, but not for sugar; it harvests the faint gamma sigh given off by decaying potassium-40 in the surrounding stone.  The cloak is also reflexive skin: when a predator-snare bat folds around the wearer, the frond flashes ultraviolet warning patterns readable to every other dweller.  In two months the cloak moults into soft fossil paper, and citizens help one another scrape off the cast-off scales, rolling them into scrolls that take ink from glowworm fat and never decay.

2.  A spinal mycelium—individual at birth, but longing for consortium.  Until age thirty the spine is a solitary braid seeking a plug-in partner; thereafter the citizen’s own root and the mycelium of the corridor intercalate like two oarsman crews sharing the same water.  Calories pass—they cannot be swallowed—via slow osmotic sugar osmosis along capillary ropes.  Speech, once believed to be sonic, is now understood to be the slow pulse of glucose patterns.  The longest sentence on record is seven centuries long and has not yet reached its verb.

3.  A heart made, literally, of perched silence.  It is a cavity suspended in the upper thorax filled with argon kept two degrees above absolute zero.  The cavity’s walls are piezoelectric quartz; each footstep on the boulevard compresses the air enough to tumble one photon’s worth of electron within the crystal, producing a nanosecond beat-note.  Ten trillion footfalls equal one personal heartbeat.  During the annual migration of luminous moths there arrives a brief intolerable tachycardia that folds the person inward for a week-long dream.

4.  A library worn as a skullcap—bone matter extruded by chemolithotrophic snails that graze on the hairline.  The snails secrete nacre impregnated with trace RNA memory of every conversation ever held by that ancestral line.  Tap a snail gently and a dew-scene million years old flickers among the papillae; eat it (the one taboo) and you absorb an entire grandparent’s vocabulary of star-maps rendered in fossil snow.  No citizen has ever starved; the prohibition is iron.

5.  A last layer even they discuss in hushed tones: a worm of living vacuum nested in the appendix.  The vacuum worm is not of this geology.  It ran aground in the moment the original ocean evaporated—an extradimensional fracture fossilised into hollow topology.  The worm loops time through itself; once per lifespan the owner must discover which day of their existence the worm is busy devouring, then live that day carefully, knowing their déjà vu is their own future corpse.  In the plaza, watchers sometimes see a stranger walking backwards, then see her spine root itself and grow forward again.  These are the unscheduled returns of the worm’s meal.

Sex happens only once.  At age eighty (an age sometimes reached the same afternoon the birth cry is heard) the five layers attempt to peel themselves off, desperate for individual voice again.  The ceremony unfolds in the plaza beneath the thirty ox-blood crystal trees.  Cloak, spinal braid, silent heart, snail cap, vacuum child: they quarrel in a music too infrasonic for the warm-blooded.  Which exile ascends, which regresses, is rarely predictable; in the last recorded act of personal dissent, the cloak left, taking the citizen’s shadow with it.  For a month the body glimmered bare, transparent as salt, unable to dim when death-moths sought it.  Yet the cloak did not die: it unfolded like a slow umbrella and clamped to the underbelly of a fruiting mushroom arching overhead.  There it is presently finishing its ninth private novel written in rust spores and occult gamma pollen.  The original skeleton walks on, un-shadowed, loving its absent shadow across every duskless boulevard.

Anatomy complete, the ordinary programme of a citizen-day is deceptively motionless.  Wake means detect an ascending minor third in the harmonic hum of the boulevard; it cues root-release.  Walk two hundred metres—over forty terrestrial hours—then reverse.  Pause at the map-table.  Murmuring in a sub-knee thud, deposit the previous day’s memories into the lattice by pressing forehead to cold latent water locked inside rust needles.  Retrieve, from crystalline cusp, the memories left by the dead yesterday’s-ghost; no other reading ever occurs.  Meals are never eaten; instead the body itself eats its shadow between meals, re-ingesting photons that evaporated off its cloak at dawn.  Waste is exhaled as cool light that gives birth to motes of quiet fungus along the darker gutters.  There are no names and no loneliness; identity is expressed as directional constellation: “I am the south-south-south-west silhouette currently leaning on the thirty-fifth avenue that has not seen a dawn for four million of your rotations.”  Enough.

Still, reversions occur.  A fraction of citizens—one in a thousand years—risk the crack under the map-table.  They wedge the narrowest ear, then the softest shoulder.  Once inside the abandoned excavation, the velvet cloak withers, unable to find potassium-40 in peerless dark.  Their hearts detune, becoming merely warm.  Water, unmet for eons, suddenly trickles—water that lost its oceanic passport and has wandered home.  Few escape: the returnees arrive trailing filaments of saline memory, their silence sounding like surf in a gourd.  They stoop throughout the streets scattering seed-caries that sprout overnight into impossible descendants: orchids that drink straight brine, beetles whirring wings of compressed tide.  Then they die.  The fungal oracle records the musical signature of those last heartbeats: every fifth note is missing, swallowed by same vacuum-child that once looped forward.  They call this lacuna *The Taste of Rain You Did Not Know You Waited For*.

In the plaza grow the children—porcelain teardrops suspended on glassy stems.  These are not offspring but recursions.  Each egg opens only when the thirty ox-blood crystal trees resonate in a chord matching the absent fifth note last heard from the aquifer-returned dead.  A child steps out already clothed five-layered as an adult, because nothing here is allowed youth; youth requires lower gravity than the planet can now spare.  They bring with them the memory of a lost sea trying larynxes of stone.  And so the city sleeps very little.  The quietest hour is when thirty children, on the midnight of their arrival, squat beneath guessing elder mycelia and attempt in fugue whether “water” rhymed originally with “remember” or “surrender.”
