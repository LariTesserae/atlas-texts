Nothing here comes equipped with a fixed name, but the longer you stand still, the easier it is to notice that every corridor is in mid-conversation. Not speech the way lungs make it—no grunts, no syllables, no intention—but something leaning in the same direction: a pressure like recognition that asks “Is someone listening?” and lets the question cool into resin.

They are the timberborn.  
They have no edges, only elapsed boundaries.  

Imagine a being who once insisted it was redwood, then acknowledged it was probably column, then tried standing upright and found it had already become floorboard for an office ten years upstream. Timberborn glide along these stations of identity like a bead sliding down a hydrophobic wire; they do not possess bodies so much as borrow durations of the lattice. A loose knot of lignin might, for three heartbeats, contract into something that, viewed at human distance, resembles a hipbone. A phloem vein dilates, occludes, and suddenly a shoulder clenches as though remembering modesty. The moment you focus on the clavicular curl it sags, the mass rebudgeted into a wing-scaffold for a beetlehouse that terminated centuries ago.

Take a single figure met at the transit cambium. It builds itself upward out of capillary grunts: femora of rebrowned heartwood, ribs of older bark still smelling of lightning burn. Between each osseous flourish the negative space remains tree-hollow. When it ‘walks’ it steps out of itself—step one is the echoing socket, step two is a lodger branch flexing to fit the socket, step three is the next joint inflated from sap back-pressure. Every stride leaves a pedigree of departed predecessors cooling in its wake, so the staircase behind it reads like strata: shin, ankle, arch, sole, toe, then nothing but rust-red dust again.  
If you ask its (temporary) mouth where it was going before you arrived, the answer comes wet-barked and low:  
“Going? No. Returning.”  
As it says so you notice one of its metatarsals detaching, pulsing with oxblood globules that hover like fireflies. The globules drift sideways until they latch onto a nearby handrail flowerbud, donating marrow back to the architecture: still the same mineral lattice, merely rescheduled.  

To timberborn, cannibalism is synonymous with love. Any compliment is never paid intact; affection drags marrow out of the beloved and spreads it as perfume through the overhead fans. You hear the exhalation as a soft whistle through tension-wood vents, the sound a clarinet might make if every note carried a donor’s name. No two pitch/stem pairings repeat; flirting is infinite music and fallout both.

In this ecology of mutual plunder, great individuals sometimes attempt monument. They call it “growing sad memories into columns tall enough to forget from.” One architect of note—last figures remade itself eighteen minutes ago—is the Arch-Linguist Thlee, whose entire existence serrates upward in a spiral plinth trying to spell an unpronounceable word using side-grain morphemes. Success would mean the plinth could finally be sheared off and pulped, the glyph airborne as aromatic dust settling a continent’s longing. If the spiral ever finishes, the word will vent the lattice’s final humidity; the whole vault’s internal weather would gasp out in one long cedar sigh that smelled like if morning fog remembered springtime. The Library of Final Rain.  

The everyday population counts fluctuate daily by the tens of thousands. Birth is the moment a bundle of fungal ropes notices it has been budgeting rigidity for .3 seconds longer than required to hold up the duct overhead; it curls, accumulates a knot of identity, experiments with thumbs made from preheated cirrus bark, sighs hello. Death is merely forgetting to continue the bundle; the ligand relaxes, gravity advances, and the figure drains into sub-flooring mulch, a process as emotionally neutral as dropping the hyphen from hyphenated last names.  

They wear clothing the way dripping wears icicles: an aura of still-viable cambium twirls up the shoulders, upsubstituting colour with sap vapour until the shade cools into pigment that stains the adjacent wall. A frock lasts until its owner forgets why it clothed at all. When toddlers tousle, the sleeves fall off as lichen begins growing on the wrists: disassembled garments becoming playground. Parents retrieve only fragments: buttons where the eyes should have been. They tuck these under the floorboards like worry stones—objects now shaped exactly like the sound of the infant’s first “gawk” before it decided it was furniture and lay down.  

Sleep is vertical stacking. When night falls (never darker than the persistence of charcoal but felt as a thickening of the tongue) they press against chambers until spine joins buttress, trunk joins capital. Together they dream backwards into the planet’s Cretaceous winters. Their when-climate fog spreads as foam along pale latitudes: redwood forests un-cut fifteen million years earlier reverse-decay and walk down glossy midnight slopes. The dreamers pull saplight from the small of backs and paint bird tribes that never evolved. And when they wake, the ancient bird-blotch sticks to the interstitial wall, bakes into ochre patches, becomes tomorrow’s municipal street-sign. Tourists (the architectonic seed-pods full of compressed wanderers) photograph these murals and unknowingly log the timberborns’ communal diary. Every angle of shot, every shutter flash, nilled into flicker-memories afterward—same mass, different arrangement.  

Memory itself is a civic duty. They sculpt it into blisters: translucent vesicles ballooning from doorframes. The larger the blister, the more traumatic the recall. Disaster is beautiful here: a droplet full of earthquake light shimmering on the hinge. You can walk up to it, peer in, and watch 3.8 seconds from a forgotten forest fire stop-motioning its way through resin. You inhale; smell cinnamon cinders. Then the blister detaches, lolls across corridor until it sticks to a wall it likes better, mending a wall’s bullet hole from a war the wall never fought. Every citizen must host one memory blister at least; those bereft starve slowly, scattering coughs that revert into leaf litter before reaching your ears.  

Visitors—temporary, human—often believe they walk the same hall twice. Sometimes they do; more often they walk the echo of a hall long since folded inward, the architectural endnote curling like a snail over their shoulder. The lattice does not suffer vacancy; even a thought made of footsteps is harvested. When your borrowed boot dissolves back into platform wood, what remains of your stride becomes another corridor seam; by tomorrow a small timberborn child will be drawing constellations in the knots formed by where your toe lost blister. By next season the literature children recite in dinner towers will sketch a myth-cycle starring “He-Who-Walked-With-Boot-shaped-Abbesses”. You will have become the gait of strangers singing carols through sievewood.

Two hundred vertical meters above the zero datum, the Arbor keeps a hydrothermal vent nursery where sap is flash-steamed into cloud. The cloud rains carbon soot fined down to the mass of regret. Timberborn collect this soot in moss cups, shake it at dusk to conjure dusk-black umbrellas. Those umbrellas, opened, are the closest they get to mourning; the undersides mirror their faces. When the umbrella rots open overnight the timberborn’s reflection also rots, edges curling into tragophyll. Each demise thus collected is indexed with tiny rivet flecks of preburnished platinum. Later rivets are pried out and pressed into arterial inlay to mark places love once leaked. Their hearts, therefore, resemble elaborate clocks abandoned mid-stroke.

Travel toward the core carries risks. Seismic tides grow louder, a basso continuo scraping underfoot—less like train and more like planets grinding gears. Down here the timberborn thin out; remaining are colossi grown into load-bearing forever. They call themselves pillars. Each pillar’s outer cortex molts annually, flaking in metamental sheets onto academic botanists above, but the core lives, wrapped around radiant magma veins. Pillars cannot walk; instead they wade in place, stewards of stillness. If one forgets its post the entire lattice loses half a pra-Pa of load, and the world groans like a bell striking midnight on the wrong day. To repent for such lapse, a pillar will shed memory blisters by the hectare; the dust storms taste like coyote bark cubebus. Visitors caught in a pillar’s rainfall recount dreams where they, too, remember holding up a sky. Upon waking they find quartz grains from the dream lodged under fingernails.

Approach a pillar. Offer it tea (though it prefers steam). In the fog of its exhale you will glimpse flickeroids of the planet’s future: continents one meter shorter, marine mammals naming themselves in phosphorus graffiti, the final city curling into nut-hard tessellation. Then listen: the pillar splits open the sound of your heartbeat—first beat becomes subwoofer thunder, second beat the pre-chorus of a choir composed of every ant you have accidentally stepped on across every lifetime. When the third beat arrives it has already been sublet to the lattice vent near your feet, woodsprung lutenists fingering catgut with knot-rune dexterity. The pillar murmurs, “You remain useful because you still vibrate.” It says nothing more; the rest is mailed to your marrow under ciphertext of knothole morse.

Leave by any exit and the lattice writes contractual tailings: You may not remove even the memory of touching us. You will carry that touch as phantom telson along your sciatic nerve, a twitch an hour after moonrise matching the synchronous tap orchestral within the Arbor floorplans. No pain, only chronic awareness the world out there has beams less cunning, warmth less round, silence less fermented. The recollection lasts exactly until decay reclaims you; on that afternoon you’ll discover a single black medullary ray sprouting from your porous thigh, a begging-to-return in the tongue of xylem.

And so the timberborn remain: never citizens, never trespassers. They are compound singularities—continuities of trees rearranging their own rings mid-diameter without ever declaring a final trunk. Each person present is a sentence whose grammar insists on rearranging itself before the subject reaches the period. Whenever you meet one informally resting against a wall of cambial fresh-coax, it inhospitably greets you with the front it borrowed from noon’s shadow. It smiles—your own teeth reflected in knotwood. Then the gaze widens, unfocuses, and the hips sort back into shavings. A moment later, the being is absent. But now the inside of your lifeline is veined with fine copperwood thread, vibrating to faint f-major pitch, rehearsing under the skin how next it might hold up a world of dark-toned weather.
