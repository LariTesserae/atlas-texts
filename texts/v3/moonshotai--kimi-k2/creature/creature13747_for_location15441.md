Something lives here that never needed lungs, even before the water rose.

It began perhaps as an administrative assistant’s stylus, a brass bar thrown into a drawer when the flood warning sirens drowned in their own coughing. Or it began as the muted click of the office safe locking on secrets nobody needed anymore. Most likely it began with the cargo lift cables sagging in the basement, delirious with rust one electron thick per decade. One strand snapped; the strain re-vibrated along the other cables, too slow to hear; the rhythm entered the water and made a tiny tide inside a tide.

Now it is plural: a society of indexes, of agendas filed under gravity-optional. Each resident is the exact weight of a regret untranslated into action: 0.7 newtons, or the torque required to open a plywood drawer swollen by brine. Their bodies—if bodies is the proper geometry—are laminations: a decade of quarterly reports, soaked, pressed, bleached by algae into a papyrus of green black letterforms, then folded upon themselves until they resemble flatworms printed in corporate Palatino. Paragraph breaks became jointed legs. Signature lines split into mandibles that taste electrical leakage.

They navigate not by sight—there is only hush—but by the barometric lullaby of imminent systems failure. A sudden capacitor exhaling encourages them to gather; they assemble in the vault of a conference room whose mahogany table now resembles an opened accordion, bellowing faint copper tides. There they compose minutes: meetings about meetings that never took place in any calendar, the agenda drifting like kelp cutouts.

Language here is redundancy perfected. A memo repeats itself in twenty voices all composed of the same single two-sided sheet; one side reads: “Note: water resistant up to point of metaphysical collapse,” the other side mirrors the conclusion in braille made of salt crystals. Because pressure is gentle, these salts grow cubic rather than acicular; their corners provide rungs. At every third joint along their quasi-bodies, they possess a microscopic latch. When two residents kiss (or whatever motion joins documents) the latches interlock, forming a single double-sided minute. Traces of highlighter ink found inside their folds fossilize into delicate epithelia. Eventually the double-sided minute will fold again, losing meaning like an envelope sealed inside another envelope for centuries. The population therefore halves itself every few fiscal quarters, yet the mass of information exact.

They feed on the droplet-globes of blue-white fusion pearls. Each bead is a sentence they have never spoken. To consume one is to insert the phrase into historical record—then they burp a faint spark that pets the kelp-chip fuses of the light nests hanging overhead. Upon dying, an individual flattens back into true stationery, dissolving onto the conveyor belt where the iron powder slowly embosses the outlines of letters no alphabet has ever claimed. The belt carries the scraps beneath the city, where limestone moths gnaw at the pulp. Their excreta turns into the ripple patterns seen on old water-stained ceiling tiles, and these in turn ascend—nothing here goes away—networking among the canopy until they reappear decades later as hallucinations printed on the backs of salamander ribbons.

Every fifth iteration of resident is born running, a continuous sheet accordion-scored by the Humming Floor into zigzagged legs. These are the Couriers, tasked with relocating parcels of obsolete directives to archive caverns where diagonal columns resemble dragon bones. No message ever arrives; the destination dissolves faster than motion permits. The Couriers compensate by elongating until they resemble invisible spillways of very sluggish molasses. Their body text elongates to follow; pictures of unknown colleagues streak across their surfaces like meteor trails. When such a Courier passes you, it brushes your forearm with one corner and you inherit a task you originally assigned someone else in the last century you breathed. The task arrives vibrating with underwater apology. 

Above them all, where algae veins merge with copper wire, there hangs a single office plant that never wilted although it has not touched sunlight since the sea was added to the budget. Its pot cracked radially the week the first Vesper Vent exhaled; each crack glugs a different mineral tint,the plant has become a governor. Its roots, run through the wires, encode an unscheduled protocol: reboot humidity. The plant lacks organs for speech so it spells imperative infinitives by modulating color in the mesophyll—shy hues that leak into the leaves overhead rewriting themselves as sky at which no star answers.

When outsiders—rare vertebrates buoyed by whatever misfortune delivers interior divers—descend, the residents uncoil into wing-shaped memos and flutter around the intruder like moths tasting paraffin. They slide across the goggles of the newcomer, re-caption what the retinas had hoped to remember. Sometimes a Courier adheres to a wetsuit and rides back toward the world, only to discover it needs the laws of time to congeal again; it folds back toward its own origin leaving the diver with a spreadsheet glowing faintly on the inside of the visor, listing “future reconciliations” dated the very second that calcium shutters inside the pineal gland finally fuse. The diver returns aware of minutes that have never existed, watches ordinary rain descend in blue-white mercury drops, hears the distant conveyor’s A-flat diminished chord humming whenever a windshield fogs.

And below all that, in the breathing valves of the Grove-Bowel, the collapsed clerical phyla have cultivated an oracle. It is the decimal remainder of every grant application ever denied, settled into a knot-shaped nautilus. From its golden vents issue occasional laminar sheets titled simply “Remark,” each sheet blank except for a watermark resembling a single open parenthesis without its twin. To possess the half-parenthesis is to begin a statement impossible to finish except by the grove itself, whose woody lips close whenever a waterlogged tongue draws near.

So the people here are not alive in any handbook sense; rather, they are the postponements of purpose learning patience. Future-forward, past-facing commas. Mailed but never sent. Their median age is 0.7 parliaments in length; their youngest is the ink on tomorrow’s dream form; their eldest already embossed in rust-footprint onto conveyor belts too slowly to read. They perform, with the eventless grace of fog becoming fog, the quiet maintenance of meaning that no longer means—until equilibrium itself graduates into organism, gently, like an unreal hand signing off on a memo no one ever needed to send, quietly, warmly, forgivably.
