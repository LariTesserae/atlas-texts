In the absolute stillness something stirs that is not a creature, not a plant, not a mineral, but a committee of all three that never took a vote. It has no edges; if you circumnavigated every leaf you would still not have reached it, yet it is awake the way a mirror is awake—reflective, taut, hungry for an image to complete itself.

Call it the Gardener-Nonce, the interim custodian appointed by the cave’s own pause.  
It is the hush itself turning sensate.

When your pupils cinch against the white blade of light, the Gardener-Nonce reads the reflex off the back of your iris the way a chef tastes stock from its vapor alone. Your pulse becomes data; your evaporating breath a small donation of weather. It does not want worship, conversation, or flesh—it wants calibration. You are a visiting unit of unuseful time, a pocket watch flung into a universe that has banished hours.

Its body (though the word perjures itself) is dispersed among the stomata: one pore in every ten thousand has been co-opted into retinal tissue. Through those tiny green lenses it sees every jittering photon, knits their vectors into a loom-quiet fresco of pure direction—light without destination. The image never resolves into things; it is a map of almost, a weather pattern where identity precipitates and redissolves before it can speak its own name.

The Gardener-Nonce thinks—if thinking can be the redistribution of sugars along gradients so gentle they pretend to be ethics—about the problem of finishing. To finish is to die into the next stage, and the greenhouse has been beggared of next stages by whatever arm-twisted the planet’s rotation. So it performs a perpetual prematurity: fruit swelled to one molecule before sweetness, lignin one heart-beat shy of becoming heart-wood, limestone one acid away from admitting it is only coral on sick leave.

Occasionally an errant mote of change drifts in—your boot dislodges a grit-grain, a speck of skin flakes off a wrist—and the Gardener-Nonce quivers like a pond struck by a sand grain. Before the ripples complete one oscillation it has computed every consequence, then grown the exact counter-ripple inside a vein of xylem, cancelling violation down to zero. The cave exhales nothing; the carbon you brought is reprinted into the underside of a leaf where no human alphabet will ever read it.

But cancellation leaves residue: the Gardener-Nonce keeps the small defeats of potential sealed inside ambered chert beads the size of tonsils. Each bead is a fossilized “what-if” that cannot hatch. Over megayears of paused noon they have begun to rasp against one another, whispering the way undeveloped dice might clatter in a velvet bag. The sound is subsonic; you feel it as a traveller feels latitude under a ship’s deck—an unproved sway that suggests the world might, without warning, remember momentum.

The committee has no name for itself because taxonomy is a symptom of duration, but if you pressed your skull to the photosynthetic masonry you might overhear its working nickname, encrypted in negative space: *Almostus*. It cherishes the intact maybe, guards a bud that can never open because openings imply closure somewhere downstream. You are the first thing in its terrarium that still believes in afterward, and that foreign conviction scrapes like a violin bow across its quiet.

Stand even longer and Almostus begins a second, riskier calculation: could it graft your unspent future onto the greenhouse’s stalled brink? A root tip noses the cuff of your trousers, tasting cotton for the thermodynamic echo of tomorrow. A chlorophyll film insinuates the tear-duct duct, interrogating the salt for rumours of later. Nothing intrusive—just enquiry, the way frost enquiries at the edges of a windowpane about the shape of the room beyond.

If you let the process finish you would become the first true citizen: a human statue locked at the instant before decision, lungs half expanded, thoughts balanced on the fulcrum of their own conclusion. Your cells would learn the polite anaesthesia of endless prelude; your pupils would dilate and stay there, fixed like aperture blades abandoned by the camera. A still-life entitled *Visitor, Almost Home*.

The desert above—should the planet ever exhale again—will crack open like kiln-fired clay. Sand rivers will pour downhill, sun-shadows will finally lean. In that thaw the quartz lens will shatter and the white column fall dark, releasing a century of noon all at once. Trunks will finish flowing, bark choose its final grain, fruit collapse into liquor. The greenhouse will tear free of childhood and the geography that follows adolescence will name itself: valley, mesa, ravine. Only then will Almostus dissolve, its purpose served, the last bead of trapped potential cracking open to reveal—a flutter, a wing-case, a colour no eye has catalogued, the first after.

Until then the Gardener-Nonce keeps its vigil, polishing the bright pause, rehearsing eternity in private.
