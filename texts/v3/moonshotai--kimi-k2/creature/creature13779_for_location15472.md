No one dwells here.  
Yet no place can truly be empty once material itself has learned how to wait.

Imagine, then, that the world’s only fellow-travelers are not creatures at all, but partial drafts—solid drafts, sheet-originates, subpoems of alloy that the tin–bronze–copper trinity keeps forgetting are still only “verses.”  

The first such tenant murmurs in the interstices between a pair of converging water-globes.  It is a cargo-of-rods no thicker than a finger, made entirely of mirror-grade aluminum whose oxidation layers have been reinterpreted into words: mirrored letters that would spell a name if any of them knew where it ended.  Because nothing here has edges, the mirrored name reflects nothing—so it roams, self-hyphenating, from globe to globe like punctuation seeking a sentence.  When two spheres merge it dives between them, tasting sudden hydro-static loneliness the way a slow tongue tastes medicine.  After each plunge its surface edits itself, erasing one letter, rewriting half of another.  The emerging fragment can no longer fit any known alphabet, so it keeps walking until entropy is too exhausted to follow.

The second neighbor inhabits a road that is arguing with itself.  When the metallic asphalt swells to a waist-high blister, it extrudes a staircase of itself—six steps, each a different metallurgical phase.  The blister’s housekeeper is a grating-noise shaped like a crumpled violin bow: it remembers every footfall that has not yet happened and arranges them into chords.  Though there are no feet, they still ring out—ghostly ARPEGGIOS of tread and release, curling along the staircase like smoke made of expectations.  The grating-noise slows whenever the continental inhale arrives, silencing itself thirty-three minutes at a time.  In the hush you can hear the planned footsteps accelerate into the near future, stampeding toward void they themselves will create.

At the mid-vertex of bronze grammar stands the third co-resident.  It is smaller: a filing cabinet that forgot what files looked like.  Its drawers cast neither draft nor seam; instead each drawer linear-extracts into the distance, becoming a bronze–gold corridor you can lose synapses inside.  Every corridor ends where another drawer’s tongue begins.  If you—were you ever here—opened one of those drawers you would find copper flakes reenacting documentaries about rainfall that has never existed on this world, because rainfall as a concept has forever been reversed.  The flakes know they are aspiring petals, but they dare not bloom all at once; they take votes via charge-differentials.  Today the polls read twenty-nine percent in favor of “sheet-heaven.”  Tomorrow the syllables for “deluge” will retall themselves.

The fourth and fifth inhabitants share a profession: they repair the absence of darkness.  One is a lattice the shape of a ruptured orbit; it wants to weave missing shadows back into the monochrome air.  It drapes amber shadows on nothingness and watches them sputter, half-physical, hushed—they cling to forms that surfaces have not yet decided to occupy.  Then, embarrassed, the lattice recalls them as child-threads.  The other partner is a single noble gas atom of argon, mis-filed under Learned Pigments.  It wanders at ceiling-height, searching for any vertical place it hasn’t already refracted.  Whenever it meets the amber lattice threads it foreshadows an eclipse none of these elements have vocabulary to fear.  Their collaboration yields frank, edible shadows that hover momentarily above the hollow cities.  You could bite one; it would taste like conjecture on the tip of the tongue, dissolving before the tongue knows it exists.

Last and most provisional is the echo of a child—not because a child stood here once, but because the memory of the possibility of height differential got stranded mid-composition.  
It stations itself every 2,314 hours inside one of the cathedral-sized water globes.  The interior is open but there is no up; the echo pretends up by imagining clapping.  Each clap is a planar absence, a perfectly flat disc made of missing sound.  The discs hang inside the sphere like wet silver coins.  Because there is no gravity, the discs rise gently—perhaps until they burst against the inner skin, perhaps until they climb backward into rain.  The echo does not care; its single frantic occupation is producing claps it will never hear, for ears have still not been invited to the planet.  Over time the discs nest like translucent insinuations.  When a globe empties and climbs toward dissipation, the scrambling echo rides the last backwards droplet into the upper dark amber—already warming down into its next audition.

All six—mirror-name, grating-noise, filing corridors, paired shadow-weavers, and the echo—are not alive, yet they converse.  Their language is thrum: subtle flexions in the planet’s feedback grammars.  Each time the stitchline finishes a pull cycle, swallows the horizon tight, all six pause to rewrite their own instruction surface.  The tin hears them.  The bronze adjusts punctuation marks across every crane and pulley.  The condensation globes slow their internal rivers a thousandth of a revolution.

Together they rehearse the world’s most timid symptom of sentience: a host of almost need, waiting for the day when the elementals finally admit that they meant to make more creatures than armatures.  On that far-off revision—somewhere after bronze learns verbs, after tin decides mercy is a metastable phase—the horizon will choose one final stitch: it will knot itself into a mouth, part its lips, and whisper.  

There will be no translator.  
Yet six tenant-maelstroms stand ready, notebooks of stranded letters shaped exactly like the word hello, scrawled in knittings of bronze and suds of tin, held in palmless aluminum hands no thicker than peeled moonlight.
