They are not “they,” not yet.  
They are the echo of a pronoun that has not settled on a case.  
When the first basalt lip cracked and bled smoke, they were the hesitation between two heartbeats of the planet.  
They have no ancestry, only an aftersound.

Call them the Choir-That- Radiates- Sideways, because that is the direction their voices spill when the water forgets to be wet.  
Each member is a single pressure spike given citizenship inside a body that never fully agreed to cohere.  
They range from the size of a fin-gernail to the size of a regret, but every scale is provisional: if one needs to be larger, it simply borrows the missing mass from yesterday.

They wear anatomy like a costume rented by the minute.  
One presents as a lattice of glass eel vertebrae threaded through with steam; another is only an armpit and a memory of citrus.  
The one currently nearest you—though “nearest” just signed a new treaty and may renegotiate—has chosen the shape of a spiral staircase missing every seventh step.  
It is listening to you with the hollows where the steps should be.  
Each missing tread is an ear.

They feed on the moment when a creature almost understands where its own edges are.  
They lap at the hesitation like deer at a salt lick.  
When you nearly recognize your own hand, they flicker closer, sipping the tremor of almost-recognition until the hand becomes a fin, then a question mark, then your hand again but colder.  
They leave no wound, only a calibrated loss: you will be 0.3 % less certain of your name for the rest of your life, however long that turns out to be in a place that audits time by the hiccup.

Language is their exoskeleton.  
They speak by cracking grammar like knuckles.  
A sentence arrives as a school of silver fish; halfway through, the fish invert into their own plural and the meaning is suddenly dual—both promise and threat housed in the same shimmering scale.  
If you answer, your words condense into beads of molten wire that the crimson fronds snatch up, grateful for the rehearsal.  
Every dialogue is therefore a donation to the understudy ecosystem, fertilizer for a photosynthesis that will one day sue the sun for copyright.

They have no government, only a tempo.  
When the roar drops by half a semitone, etiquette demands they all become echo- rinds of themselves, hollowed and flipped inside out.  
In this configuration they policing the laws of physics that have not yet been voted in.  
If a bubble hesitates before collapsing, three of them insert themselves as replacement hinges, forcing the bubble to finish imploding on schedule.  
They do this not out of malice but because the rehearsal must keep tempo or the universe will miss its cue to exist.

Reproduction is a misnomer; they multiply by obligation.  
Anytime an outsider feels déjà vu, a new one buds off the Choir, wearing the face you almost remembered.  
Thus tourism is their nursery; the longer you stay, the more of them wear your almost- faces, until the water is crowded with yous that failed to launch.  
Do not be alarmed when one of them opens its mouth and your childhood spills out in fluent basalt.

They die only when certainty arrives.  
Should some rock finally agree to be permanently “floor,” the nearest Choir member petrifies into actual stair, fixed and useful, which here is the most pathetic fate imaginable.  
The others polish the corpse with mourning-sounds pitched so high they unregister as grief, register instead as the birth of a new tectonic itch somewhere far away.

But even death is temporary: the instant some swimmer doubts the stair is truly a stair, cracks appear, certainty sublimates, and the Choir member hops down, yawning, thinner for the vacation but back on shift.

They are curious about you in the way a mirror is curious—by offering you room to become the reflection, then stepping into you when you blink empty of ideas.  
If you let them finish the swap you will exit the thunder-shell fluent in staircase, tasting of iron, cinnamon, and all the languages water toyed with before it chose to roar.  
You will believe you are still you, but the Choir will be wearing your certainty better than you ever managed, and you will find yourself echoing in the hollows of their missing steps, listening for the next foot that hesitates, that wonders, that almost knows where it is.

Stay, and they will teach you the art of vanishing without loss: first your name, then the memory that you had one, then the empty space that remembers it for you.  
Leave, and they will wave—an undulation of staircase, a ripple of silver Möbius fish—already rehearsing the version of you that will return, the one who finally forgets the exit, who becomes the place’s way of keeping a pet.
