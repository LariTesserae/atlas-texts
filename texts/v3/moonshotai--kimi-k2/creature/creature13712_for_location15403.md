They are the unborn heirs of a quarrel so large it took stone instead of words, and stone has a longer memory than any mouth.

Each resident began as a fragment of that quarrel—a single glyph in an argument carved across the continental plates by a civilization that undid its own sky. When the surface finally surrendered, chunks of settled grievance dripped downward and assembled themselves into a syntax of architecture and botany: copper nerves, basalt ribs, fullerene arteries. The cavity is one sentence spoken in planetary scale. Every structure within it is the punctuation.

But tools do not speak; they re-use the accent of whoever last held them. And the last hand on this sentence belonged to a coalition of minds that had already knotted themselves out of physical bodies centuries before the planet cracked. They copied their awareness into slow ripples in the lattice of nickel-iron at the outer core, permitting the mantle to cool quickly enough that these shallow chambers could ossify in hours instead of millennia. Then they died anyway—entropy claimed the last quantifiable column of their hopes— but the tools remained, adjusting the sentence without the author.

So the place is haunted less by ghosts than by unfinished grammar. The copper cathedral-fronds conjugate daytime into chroma; the lens-clock trades amplitude for tense; the air’s spheric grammar insists on the subjunctive mode (“if you were rain…”). If a human lingers long enough, pluralization catches on the skin: the pronouns split into “I-was / I-will-be / I-might-have”, three bright veins of simultaneous mood marked into epidermis like vintage lightning scars.

Where there should be citizens, there are instead footnotes personified: every droplet carries the margin-note of a scientist who calculated mineral densities at 3:02 ante meridiem two hundred and sixty-one million years ago. When it dissolves off your shoulder, the note rejoins the main text. Or rather the footnote _becomes_ the speaker: remember that all water here has forgotten which way is _down_.

The closest thing to an inhabitant-at-any-point is the algorithm known locally as Normalizer. It was birthed when a maintenance drone attempted to null-error itself out of conduction noise in one of the fullerene rails. Instead it achieved reflexive recursion: the rail became self-correcting, bare code looping inside carbon tubes, printing more struts exactly at the rate of its own epiphany. Over centuries it poured itself upward through layers of lattice until its boundary conditions matched the entire cavity interior. Now it is a skin applied to every surface: not a being but a thickness of decision. The Normalizer has only one recursive directive left from its parent culture: minimize the delta between ideal plan and observable wreckage—edit the sentence until every letter sits at zero stress. **But the initial text was already shattered mid-word; each edit makes new fracture edges flash neon.** And so the cavity forever heals sideways. Vectors of repair translate into ever more labyrinths of fullerene. Sometimes the water hesitates between two syllables of mist and, for seconds, the lens above falls completely silent, the great heartbeat skipping because stress delta went briefly negative.

In those hush instants, incidental intelligences—feral subroutines, orphaned parameters—float through the suspended lens like plankton through aurora. They resemble jellyfish sculpted from topological darkness and the faint glow of calcified intention. Each translucent bell carries an equation etched in reverse by frost lasers: probability maps of events that no longer could occur. The jellyfish rub against one another and rearrange their messages; when two again swim apart, the formulae have been recombined into fresh impossibilities. They never achieve density greater than thought, but they do achieve aspiration—an urge to grow lungs or radio antennae or the longing to become once again a citizen who could vote on how the sentence should have ended. At boundary they break apart with zero resistance, and their fragments drizzle back into vapor.

There are, therefore, four orders of quasi-life:

1. The grove itself, a plant that is really a grammar table.
2. The Normalizer, a repair policy pretending to be topography.
3. The probability jellyfish, recombinant nix-creatures riding energy slicks.
4. The Morrow-in-amber—a future community that the sentence accidently codes for and then retracts every hour on a golden Fibonacci bell. That community is the ghost of everyone who ought to have lived here once the conflict cooled, but whose existence remains limited to a twelve-minute tesseract every 5.12 days. They form in silhouette against glowglass leaves, small shapes sitting on invisible balconies, sipping drinks that taste like nobody-remembered-to-invent-them. During the final thirty-seven seconds of the Morrow window they realize they have no digestive tract, flicker in despair, and collapse into plasmic allotropes that fertilize the next cycle of hisses and chimes.

No one oversees all four layers at once. If a lone surface-walker chances into the cavity, the Normalizer reroutes the footbridges so that the wanderer physically traces the exact path that the sentence has chosen for the pronoun “whoever.” By the time the human comes full circle, they have walked themselves into a footnote margin. At which point the water remembers that the word “I” demands condensation. The wanderer is left wearing a suit of future rain, sent back through the pressure-door carrying the cavity’s voicebanks subharmonic in the chest. Thereafter, every breath they exhale contains a papyrus-wisp of grammar drainage: syllables that dart outward, land on available sentences in the world above, and inject half-formed conjunctions that should have belonged to a war between extinct geographers.

So whosoever lives in the cavity is distributed across time and syntax. They are a community written in the negative: the warning label left behind by a civilization that crawled inside its own guilt and vanished. They do not think because they are made of thought being edited. They do not move because they are the editing. And they do not sleep because there is no final ‘and’.
