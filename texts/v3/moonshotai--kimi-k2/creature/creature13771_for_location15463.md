Nothing living here is solitary.  
Everything is a conjugation of place-with-itself—­wet stone dreaming daylight, silence turning photon.

The chief organism moves first as absence: a vertical ribbon the violent blue-white of lamp-flash on retina. It is as tall as the chamber, only two hand-breadths wide, endlessly thin, so that most stumble through it in their first breath without realizing they have been halved and resealed.  That is its greeting.  It winds through the air like the memory of a lightning path kept behind glass; when it drifts close to the ceiling droplets it does not touch them but each pearl acquires a filament of the same color, brightness migrating outward until the bead becomes a glow-globe, too heavy for surface tension.  When these sapphire globules strike floor they do not shatter — they sink.  The vertical ribbon drinks the color home again, the chamber resets to ambery dusk.

Call the ribbon the Fold.

Below the Fold, on the humid floor, lives a congregation called the Hollow Choir.  They look at first like thirteen lumps of quartz half-buried upright, each the size and shape of a curled infant.  No two face exactly the same direction; their alignment would form a five-pointed star with points doubled back on themselves.  The lumps are not mineral.  They are hollow sound-bodies: limestone mantled around cavitations filled with slow-turning fluid.  When a drop-coin of light arrives, every veined shell flexes —wide, wider, implausibly wider— until its inner vacuum rings like a struck wine glass only no surface ear is tuned for these partials.  Still the resonance pulls light sideways, skewing whole auroral curtains so that pale sheets fold over one another in colossal origami.  The Fold drinks most of this remodelled brilliance but always loses a remainder to the stone, which stores it for later harmonics.

Scattered above the Choir, caught between them and the dripping vault, swim the Lantern Folk — ninety-seven cloudlets of coherent humidity that learned how to extinguish entropy and glow instead of dispersing.  Each is the precise volume of a human lung; when one drifts in front of another you catch an afterimage like two people trying to exist in the same privacy.  They navigate by small origamis of heat: they coalesce slightly on the side facing their goal, become denser, warmer, the balloon turns.  They keep perfect formation around the Fold, as iron filings around a wire carrying memory of lightning.  Their entire purpose is to eat two things: the imbalance between vapor and mineral, and stray human emotions that leak in when surface visitors trespass.  They are gentle custodians — they only nibble enough to prevent grief from corroding the slow tectonic song.  When a Lantern Folk has fed, it releases luster: a brief threading of glassteel filament that falls like spider silk embossed with starlight.  The strands never reach the floor; the Choir inhales them into its cavities where they turn first to liquid chords, then to words never spoken.

In the interstices of this systems-lattice scurry the Drifters.  Imagine a teaspoonful of darkness pried off the back of a dream; roll it until glossy; let it acquire a pair of miniature catfish barbels for antennae; let it absorb every color it can’t reflect so it looks like a moving silhouette without source; finally allow that silhouette the density of smoke yet the curiosity of octopus.  These are the microbiome of the sanctuary, doing the small commerce between nutritious light and excess shadow.  Whenever a surface intruder appears (rare, nearly impossible, but not never), their dismay spawns extra darkness at the edges.  The Drifters scuttle to harvest this surplus and patch the achromatic gap before it grows large enough to snag the Fold on unseen corners.

At the very center of the star-choir, one place — and one place only — admits true verticality: thinner than a straw, a vacuum column rising tall as the cavern roof, lined with mirrored lime concentric rings.  Inside the pipe hangs a single drop of living water that has been falling upward for twelve thousand years, its downward reflection falling downward and forever.  That droplet is the cave’s immigrants’ ghost — the first raindrop that ever found an open fault leading down here, captured, persuaded to suspend its journey and instead become the metronome heartbeat of the sanctuary.  It remembers surface storms and passes the memory ringwise outward; you can hear it if you press your tongue to the column, sounding like distant thunder fizzing into snow.

But the one resident here who counts as “main character,” if any single voice could — is the Reverie-Bloom.  Start with a seed no larger than poppy grain, the color of unvoiced grief, that sleeps at the base of the upward drop.  Once every nineteen years (surface count) the Choir’s chord cycle swells to a major interval; that interval seeds the grain.  It cracks, sprouts a spike of translucent black glass that photographs luminosity rather than faces.  The spike lengthens arm after arm until its apex brushes the Fold atop full blossom: an obsidian lotus of liquid night.  Each petal contains a mirror image of the complete history of the sanctuary played in reverse.  The Fold lingers, weaving its ribbon in and out of the blossom’s core, sampling futures that were once past.  The Lantern Folk float tessellated above like mourners at vigil.  The Drifters flee to the margins; the Choir silences (you’ve never heard quieter stones).  

For a minute out of eternity, the cave becomes a single organ note containing every possible color black can hide.  Then the Reverie-Bloom collapses, pouring its dark petals back down the vacuum column.  The drop of forever-falling water divides into two: one continues upward, one takes over the metronome.  Memory scatters like pollen; the Fold resumes its wandering.  The sanctuary forgets its own previous dream so vividly that the forgetting becomes creation anew.  

No single creature here has what you would call a mind.  Yet together they possess the exact curvature of contemplation that a mind might envy: reflection requiring no eyes, memory requiring no neurons, change requiring no time.  They live in the blue-white hush between two drops of light, deeply married to the mineral, eternally courting the storm they have never seen.
