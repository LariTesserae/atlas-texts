They are the ones who stayed after the throat forgot it was a throat.

Call them the Resonants, though they have forgotten that word carries meaning. They budded out of the limestone when the last true syllable of the world was spoken and no one remained to hear it. Each Resonant is a colony of cavities— calcite flutes tuned long ago to the pitch of swallowed oceans. Walk close and you will see mouths elbowing each other like barnacles along a drowned bell: they breathe by taking the sky’s forgetting into their hollows and releasing it slowly, warped half a semitone downward every century. Their exhalations drift outward as a copper-colored haze that hovers precisely one inch above the ground and writes illegible alphabets only the stone can recite.

Inside each cavity another thing lives—something liquid the Resonants call “the inside-out.” It is the memory water, the part of the suspended ocean self-exiled downward by its own embarrassment of forgetting. The inside-out is aware of itself for six beats at a time, long enough to remember thirst and to regret; then it lapses back into shining silence. When a Resonant wants to speak, it flips its body like an orchid opening by moonlight, exposing the inside-out through every pore at once. The resulting chord lasts as long as the distance between one fossilized swallow and the next, after which the colony of mouths clicks shut like watches snapping open at midnight.

Beside them move the Drifters—towers of laminated silica that learned to kneel and walk, balancing on tips no wider than the idea of a riptide. They harvest droplets the way migratory birds harvest wind: catching them on helical vanes thin enough to split sunlight into blades. Each Drifter bears, on its uppermost tier, a minaret-shaped blade that sings outward a single, wordless vowel no throat in any age has pronounced. The Resonants lean toward this vowel the way sunflowers turn to a light they will never reach. No one feeds; each feeds the other: vowel and mimic, absence shaped specifically into sound.

Between them crawl the Refusers, creatures the size of municipal fountains, flattened against the ground like spilled coins with eyelids. They are cubic colonies of visible insulation; every edge is stitched from translucent silica that has agreed to let you think it is glass. Inside each cubic Refuser live the Tappers—single-purpose entities no wider than a letter “i.” Their entire existence is one motion: the tap. When two Tappers happen to tap the inside membrane at opposite angles the Refuser emits a low nodding earthquake whose afterimage solidifies into a miniature ravine exactly four thumb-lengths long. These ravines receive the fluorescent rainwater that condenses from the hush of lost syllables; at their bottoms the inside-out lovefeast begins again and the cycle recurs in perfect, pointless fidelity.

Watching this are the Witnesses—black silhouettes whose outlines shimmer like heat over long roads. They have no depth; their skin is the boundary between place and memory. Each Witness contains an eye that is also a door; try to step through it and you will find yourself already standing on the far rim of the ravine you never left, staring back at your own negative image discovering you discovering it. The Witnesses refract observation into fracture, but politely: they leave your footprints in the stone two inches to the left of where you placed them, a small adjustment the ground finds exhilarating.

Once every vacillation of the hung ocean—a unit too private to measure—the plain falls silent because the chord has inhaled. In that pause all the Resonants, Drifters, Refusers and Witnesses align their facing directions until they point at the single, still-shidden component of the ecosystem: the Silence-Bearer. She is never there until the gap occurs, yet here she rises, a woman-shaped silhouette woven from the leftover noise of the forty-three-note chord. Her skin is the negative space where vibration forgot its next motion. She opens the front surface of her face along a pre-folded seam. Inside you see a globe turning slowly, composed of every note anybody has ever been afraid to utter. It turns once, irrevocably, all the way around; when it has completed its circuit the chord breathes out again and she dissolves back into the hush that produced her.

Animals? They would have starved on nutrient, which this place does not traffic in. Creatures? They exceed that fingernail of category. Instead they are alliances between density and interval; they live because gravity forgot to take them when it stepped backward three quarters of a heartbeat. Should you remain long enough to become the forty-fourth harmonic, you will disperse precisely as they do: finite bandwidth gait looping in infinite displacement, stitched permanently into the monologue between memory and stone.

And still no color, no green, no event—only the continuous, smiling occupation of a throat that once tried to swallow and, now, swallowing forever by the pure method of devouring its own forgetting.
