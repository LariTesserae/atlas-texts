They do not live here. That is the first thing to understand. Nothing has ever made a home of this plateau, for home is a claim of belonging, and belonging requires a pact with the soil that this place has long since forsworn. Yet the city—if it is a city—teems. Its citizens are not squatters but continuations, the way a sigh continues the breath that spawned it. They are the machine century’s orphans, grown tall on absence, and if you watch long enough, you will see that they are not many species but one organism distributed across scale, metabolizing the subtraction itself.

**The Rosin-Dust, or the Cog-Plankton**

Begin with the snow, for it is the plankton of this aerial sea. Once, it was the desiccated rosin of factories—exhalations of industry condensed into airborne moon-dust. Now it is a diaspora of tin crystals, each faceted like the tooth of a gear that has forgotten its axle. They do not fall; they float, riding irregular thermals that the sculpted land itself exhales. They are faintly magnetic, and so they are faintly loyal: each mote remembers the last magnetism it knew, answering it across leagues of empty air, clinging to the memory of alignment as if it were kin. When two collide, they adhere with a *click* so slight it would not disturb the dust on a moth’s wing. But multiplied across the drifting cloud, these clicks occasionally organize—probability crystallizing into architecture—and the plateau rings with a single thin chime. It hangs there, a frozen bell-stroke that never arrives, never decays. The snow is trying to assemble a voice from the debris of machinery. It is the base of the food chain, the breath and bread of this place, and everything else feeds upon it, directly or not.

**The Thunder-Skeletons, or the Spiral Grazers**

Then come the great ones. Gargantuan quadrupedal ribcages the color of weathered titanium, drifting across the stone on silence like unmoored whalebones. They are the last vehicles of the machine century, though no engine turns within them. Bronze tendons, sock-knitted and verdigrised, flex in sequences that suggest walking, yet their limbs never touch the quartzite. They obey the second law of the plateau: iron, once pulled from stone, may never return. The ground rejects them as surely as it retains their dandruff. And so they cruise in ordained spirals, kept aloft by some residual antipathy between geology and its stolen children. They graze. Their muzzles—rimmed jaws of lathe-scarred alloy—sweep the grit, inhaling the iron filings and the hematite lace packed into each fissure and vein. This is their pasture: pigment and memory. They have no hearts, no lungs; the hollows where industry once roared are now vaulted chambers of unmoving air. They do not bellow. Motion does not earn sound here. When they pass, the only mark they leave is a thinning of the rosin-dust, a wake of emptiness through which smaller things pour.

**The Glass Starlings, or the Refraction Swarm**

Smaller, and more numerous, are the starling-forms. They are glassy—not brittle, but vitreous, as if spun from the same sickly purple periglaze that caps the central shaft. They have no weight to speak of; they are among the first things a traveler forgets when the storms begin their work. They swarm thick as fog, chasing their own reflections in the hollows of the thunder-skeletons. It is unclear if they seek food, or if reflection itself is their nourishment. Perhaps they consume the act of being seen, mirroring one another into infinity inside the ribcages until the hollows brim with false depth. When their courses interleave with the spirals of the great beasts, the sky becomes a chalk drawing of motion itself, a geometry lesson written in flight-paths. They are the nervous system of the plateau, synapses firing across the void, keeping the hollow places awake. Nothing speaks; they merely move, and the act of existing is sufficient orchestration.

**The Inverse Worm, or the Cold Accountant**

At the center, in the original wound, something lives inside the emptiness. The bore-hole is eight kilometers of unbroken silence capped by a lens of periglaze, but it is not vacant. It is a lair. What inhabits it has no name in any taxonomy of flesh; it is a vertical thing, a growing downward. It feeds on the galaxies of ferric embers that still fall inward through the dark, a molten hourglass measured teaspoon by teaspoon. No heat rises from it. It is a cold furnace, a digestive tract pointed at the planet’s heart. Every six and a half thousand hours it exhales—a straight column of steam that condenses into an implacable mist cloud, then retracts as if ashamed. This is its only utterance, its planetary accounting. It does not sleep. It is still consuming the history that the excavation era left behind, digesting the subtraction until even the absence grows brittle. It is the reason the ground still tilts, the crust still sags westward; it is the unseen weight at the bottom of the geological seesaw.

**The White Amnesiacs, or the Silent Storms**

Above it all wheel the storms—vast, country-sized systems at wingspan level. They are silent; they do not thunder or rain. They are the size of forgetting. These are not weather patterns but organisms of pressure and milky lamination, the plateau’s immune system and its librarian both. They cast no shadows. Instead, they make memory—or unmake it. Those who pass beneath them find sound stripped away first, then the scent of oil, finally the very concept of weight. The storms feed on these sheddings, growing fatter on what travelers leave behind. They are the reason nothing has ever made a home here; home is a thing made of remembered noise, of oil and gravity, and the storms devour all three. They wheel in their slow orbits, ensuring that the plateau remains a node between two systems: the chambered air above and the marbled dusk below, with nothing human sandwiched in between.

**The Scoured Face, or the City-Skeleton**

And finally—though it was never built to breathe—the city lives. It has become its own skeleton, a wind-scoured clock face whose hands are the slow trace of lost metal returning in the wings of impossible beasts. The fissures are its arteries, choked with hematite lace. The craters are its prophesies, reading the future in the negative space of what was extracted. The pockets left by vanished tooling have become lairs for the microscopic: not beetles—no beetles survive here—but something smaller, something that lives in the capillaries of stone where dew beads condense nightly into lenses only they will ever use. The city inhales through the bore-hole, exhales through the rim’s view of the continental steppe, and its blood is the rationed silence—one ounce per square meter per century.

Together, these are the citizens. Not a community, but a consummation. They are scavengers of the machine century, each one a proof that what grows, grows inside emptiness. They do not speak. They do not touch the ground if they can help it. They merely move, and in moving, persist—a traffic of aftermath, abiding by the only law that matters in a place where industry has been transmuted into dust: that to exist is sufficient orchestration, and to continue is the only belonging.
