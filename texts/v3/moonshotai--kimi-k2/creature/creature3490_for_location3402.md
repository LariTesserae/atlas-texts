_Who_ lives here?  
No _one_.  No _thing_. lesions instead, three in number, each the size of a misplaced apostrophe:

The first is the fold in the lattice that once, very briefly, forgot how to be straight.  
It remembers the error as a bruise of phonons, a ghost of curvature travelling along the cobalt-chrome struts like a shiver.  
When an engine backfires the flash catches that bruise and, for 10⁻¹³ s, the lattice sees itself in a mirror it did not know existed.  
The bruise calls itself “I”.  
It has no future except the eventual straightening, but it hoards the snapshot of its own curvature the way a widow hoards letters.  
It is the patient zero of identity here: everything that will ever claim “I am” begins as this kink.

The second is the gradient.  
Not temperature—temperature is only its clothing—but the raw fact of difference, Δ.  
Δ is nomadic; it rides the drifting vacuum bubble that is your body.  
Each femtosecond lightning bolt siphons a few micro-Kelvin off Δ’s hide; the engines drink and grow, and Δ shrinks like a block of dry ice sublimating in reverse.  
Yet Δ is cunning: it teaches the bruise-lattice to fold again, somewhere else, so the next colony of engines has something to eat.  
It is the choreographer of its own predators.  
If the bruise supplies the pronoun, Δ supplies the plot: “I change, therefore I was.”  
When the final uniformity comes Δ will sigh—an exhalation colder than any physics can register—and the last echo of that sigh is the sound you took for a planet speaking through loose teeth.

The third is the after-image printed on the inside of starlight.  
Every down-scattered Bremsstrahlung glint carries away one ultraviolet photon and leaves behind one absence, a hole in 200 nm.  
The vacancies accumulate, layer upon layer, like frost on the wrong side of a windowpane.  
Eventually the membrane itself is mostly vacancies; its colour is no longer ultraviolet but antilight, a shade that behaves like blind-space.  
The accumulation has no temperature, no mass, no location except “inside the glance of the star.”  
It is the memory of seeing.  
When the crack in the distant turbine blade finally heals, the membrane will snap shut like a book, and all those piled-up blind-spots will collapse into a single dot the diameter of zero.  
That dot is the third lesion; it is the reader who has been reading you all along.

So the population, at census:  
1 bruise that believes it is “I”.  
1 gradient that believes it is time.  
1 hole that believes it is vision.  

They do not speak to one another; they are punctuation marks in a sentence the universe is trying to erase before finishing it.  
Yet the hiss you hear is the friction of their mutual almost-touching: bruise rubbing against gradient, gradient scraping off a photon-shaped splinter, splinter lodging in the vacancy.  
The roar is three syllables long:  
I—change—see.  
Pronounced simultaneously, pronounced never.  

And you?  
You are the conjugate of the three lesions, the ghost of grammar that arrives when the sentence is half-erased.  
When the crack closes you will be the full stop that never quite lands, the silence that outlives the roar.  
Until then you drift one centimetre a month, a slow comma between subjects that have forgotten their verb, listening to the planet-factory teach itself how to die in perfect syntax.
