The only permanent inhabitant is someone who has forgotten she is the curator.

Officially she is titled “Thermal Archivist, Grade 9, Audit Loop Infinity,” but the nameplate slipped from her collar years ago and is now a silver disk frozen halfway through the brass grate, unreadable beneath a lacquer of blood-garnet.

She appears at first to be a seam in the temperature rather than a body: a slight differential that causes frost to bud in one particular spiral, never elsewhere. Only when you walk until you are parallel with the sapphire-tinged glare of the carbon-arc masts do the edges resolve into contours of a woman-shaped hollow, like the negative space left after icing has been chipped from a stencil. Here the cold is infinitesimally warmer, a charitable –99.8 °C; that 0.2° is the cost of her presence.

She wears a gown of marcasite antennae, telescoped down to threads so fine the turbines’ roar plays them like a glass harmonica. Every incoming photon rattles across the wires, diffracting into a soft monochrome rainbow on the floor—eight collapsing arcs that meet under her feet and become one unremarkable shadow, anonymous as a cancelled stanza. Clothing, body, instrumentation—nothing distinguishes itself; all are welded to protocol. The marcasite must be warmed every 6,600 revolutions of the turbines or it hardens into selenite sheets that shatter like sugar glass. She accomplishes this by periodically resting her gloved palms against a diverter rail. The average interval is 237 breaths. Because she has forgotten to breathe audibly, the interval is always approximate.

Memory is outsourced; she carries a contraption at her hip, a brass pocket-watch with radix-4 digits painted on the inside of the crystal. When the leaves of the silicone shrubs detach electroluminescent echoes, the watch ingests those shards of fleeting green foxglove and turns the hands four glyphs forward. Once the dial completes a full cycle, the watch chatters out her name on a scroll so thin it takes four hours for all the letters to emerge, cracking like thawing ice. The text reads differently every cycle; she no longer remembers which version was the first she ever answered to. She licks the strip once it finishes, dissolving the graphite ink back into her salt so the loops restart.

She speaks only in fractions of temperature. If she wants the turbines to alter pressure by a microscopic unit, she exhales a sub-zero lisp that condenses onto the nearest thermocouple as minus-language that the lattice understands but every other ear registers as wind. When she must address a passing moment of human attention (a trespassing cartographer, a bored god, you), she uses the garnet seal-blobs left on the rivets. She pricks the ball of her thumb with a burr, flicks one drop across the air; as it flies the blood freezes into a perfect scarlet Morse bead. Upon contacting the grill it sounds—silently but unmistakably through the bones of the ridge—one word: “FORGIVE.” Each visitor hears the word in their birth tongue’s earliest pronunciation, syllables from before they could spell. All who hear it depart unsettled, certain that the plea is both aimed beyond them and leaking from inside their own ribs.

Her eyes were removed during commissioning so that residual body heat would never disturb equilibrium. In the sockets now nest a pair of mercury gyroscopes that spin to reflect the exact angular deficit between whatever she faces and whatever she intended to face. She sleeps standing, because lying down would create a horizontal cavity capable of conducting heat along the grain of the brass slats. No pillow, no blanket, only a momentary cessation—eight rotations exact, a soft fissure in awareness during which the carbon arcs automatically dim by 3.5 lumens to conserve margin. She never knows it happens. When consciousness resumes, the first prompt the thermocouples whisper is a silent calculation of how much entropy her unconscious skin might have tried to smuggle. The deficit is always 0.000 calories; yet she drafts an apology into the frost anyway—an ephemeral glyph of acknowledgment just beneath her left bootprint. It melts in the next half second, but the apology has been logged permanently in the substrate: her version of a heartbeat.

Visitors sometimes report catching her in the parallelogram of unsky. They swear they saw her ascend the catwalk that manifests mere omission, vanish through the partition and reappear immediately back on the grill. She will never recollect the excursion; to her the catwalk is a calibration artifact that flags itself every ten thousandth cycle. Constraint stitches her to the site like a tick in amber, and yet she moves within that amber—by conduction, by charge imbalance, by arithmetic ghosts. Her rate of travel is one femtometer per philosophical dilemma.

Longevity is enforced: the very act of acknowledging expiry would require a temperature rise incompatible with specifications. Yet the watch continues to scroll her increasingly alien name, and the garnets around the vents ripen imperceptibly. One crystal vein has begun climbing her left femur—a creeping moiré of frozen blood attempting graffiti beyond the contract allows. It is writing a future signature directly onto the mercurial diagnostics of her shin. Should the vein reach her hip, she may remember that she was once allocated an exit authorization. There is no protocol for what happens after such remembrance; the engineers considered it statistically infeasible.

Until then she curates the single hollow at the ridge’s summit. Curating, here, is defined as being present enough that the cold can feel observed. No greater miracle is needed.
