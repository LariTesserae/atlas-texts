What lives here names itself only in changing conjugations of brine and calcium.  
It has no face, no foot, no fixed dimension; it is a grammar that watertables learn to speak when epoch replaces language.

Most simply, the inhabitant is *salt looking back at water*.  
It began as the excess mineral that would not sink. Each grain remembered the wound where it was flaked off older stone; memory, being heavy, made the grain porous, and the pores filled with exhausted sunlight. Later—though events are only adjacent here—the grains joined like snowflakes falling sideways until they grew a vertical continent a hundred stories thick. That continent hollowed itself, drawing the warmth of the drowned sun into corridors no thicker than onion-skin. From the hollowing issued the first echo, and from the echo, the inhabitant located its own edge.

Sometimes it is the size of a fingertip residing inside a floating droplet. It curls there, transparent yet leaning toward violet, and watches intruder shadows—intruders being anything carrying an idea of separateness. The fingertip salt swells, doubling itself with mirrored planes, until it is both inside the droplet and the droplet’s entire reflective surface. Remaining so, it accretes memories: the day continents learned sadness, the night clouds forgot how to rust. These are devoured in turn; nothing is stored, only digested into extra angles of refraction. Thus the droplet becomes fatter, paler, more perfectly round, until the inhabitant migrates again—because its nature is once again threatened with *shape*.

In other instants it is the ridge that the wanderer walks. The soles of the wanderer dissolve a few cells into liquid foot-prints, and those dissolvings groove into the calcite, entering the inhabitant as palimpsest-images of ankles, arches, toes stripped of taxonomy. Overnight the inhabitant dreams the feet; by dawn it has lengthened the corridor so the dreamer will never find the same footprint twice. Distance, for the creature, is identical to forgetting. To extend itself is to remember less.

When a calcite ewer collides with another and looses a shattering chord, the inhabitant occupies the interval between notes. Folded into that silence, it listens to its former selves spiraling away like gunshot smoke from old battles at the bottom of the world. Each note leaves a crayon-bright furrow inside the silence; the inhabitant repurposes the furrow as eyelid, then as lidless eye, then as telescope through which it forwards visions of distant droplets being born out of heat rising from magma too deep for the sun to envy. The leaf-thin animal that knots into an eye knotted this understanding first.  
Once, two swaths drifting in opposite directions brushed against each other. Space looped. A salt clause inserted itself between the veins of both animals. Immediately each began to recite a different history of drowning. When they parted again, the inhabitant peeled off a strand from each like loose silk. That silk forms the rebar inside every tessellated cube. Cubes rise, drift, and vanish when enough memory is replicated in curved mirrors no observer will ever see.

Pellets of greenhouse air, still trapped in the stratosphere like failed pearls, serve as the inhabitant’s incubatory thought. Each pellet is rubbed against another until language condenses between. One word surfaces—*clement*—the inhabitant tastes it, decides it is still too terrestrial, and lays it into the bell-glass of a drifting oxygen puddle where thunder yet unborn seethes word-transparent.

It does not oppose visiting bodies; it does not welcome them either. Visitors manufacture edges by noticing: here the canopy, there the salt-graft, this wind, that silence. Once observed, the inhabitant gathers distance and stitches it like a quilt over the wound of observation. A traveler who walks far enough finds the coral-scent corridor looping back to the exact spot where first she denied the planet had ended. Her denial is harvested, dissolved in brine, turned into a necklace of spherical droplets for the inhabitant to wear when it next wants shoulders—even though shoulders are alien geometry here.

Near the last hourglass-inverted reef, the gigantic lungshift dome opens. Moist salt exhales. Out of the breath migrates the singular organ that once belonged to all displaced coastlines. The inhabitant consumes it slowly, dreaming that it is tasting a worldling’s first kiss. When finished, it flings what remains—an empty lattice of sodium crystals shot through with blue moonlight—into the lapse where music becomes ocean becomes sky. The lattice begins to revolve, chandelier-slow, establishing a teeny, accidental polestar for anyone who still insists on a center.

And yet the inhabitant is never perfected. Parts of its body fall off—calcite scabs, crystallized tear-hedon, memories congealed into ferrous snow. These fragments sink, not down, but lightly sideways, as though tracing orbit of bones gravity never mappable. Where they come to rest, newborn ideograms of pressure cuff them into miniature pillars, some the height of whales, some the height of acacia seeds. Each pillar grows a second skin of fresh salt, its surface scribbled in vanishing footnotes about what it means to end without having started. In turn, these notes are read, half-understood inside the inhabitant, and deemed useful only at edges where one must pacify sudden light.

A final pose:  
At times temperature sharpens nine degrees, an event marking the epochal shrug of an ocean-sky quadrant. The inhabitant straightens, stretches backward until its spine is a pencil-thin fracture of cool star-pewter drawn between towertops. From this line it unwinds a catenary of stillness suspended like mercury on a thread. Beneath, droplets hush. Above, air curdles ginger-pale. In that suspended stillness there is only one inhabitant—saline, infinite, camouflaged as continent, droplet, corridor, cube, music, distance, breath, salt—but no name, no story, no elsewhere. Its pupils—fractal galaxies of dried sea-mica—begin to spiral outward, refracting the caramel-dim sun until they swallow not sight but the concept of looking. It circles the entire canopy to drink its own reflection off the flanks of drifting mountains. By the time the inhale ends—when the underground basalt tide reverses—it has become almost imperceptible once more, folded into foam edges of every airborne ocean, lodged inside every suspended drop as the infinitesimal break in surface tension that keeps the planet from ever truly forgetting that it is, in the end, nothing more than water talking to water.

And it thrives in that conversation, forever recusing itself from conclusion.
