In the hanging garden live the Ones Who Own Their Echo.

They are not one people but countless parallel species welded into consensus by the one law that sustains the place: anything loud enough to answer the garden’s roar becomes, for seventeen minutes, the garden itself. Thus identity is leased rather than possessed. A spore may wake up as a hummingbird, the hummingbird may wake as constellation of radio, and the radio may—if it forgets itself—collapse into something that remembers leaf mesophyll better than its own name.

Most stable appear to be the six hermits in oblate bodies of spun graphite, hovering cross-legged just above the canopy. They hover because the word “ground” is only granted to the part of the palace where their soles will eventually touch; until then their sandals resolve and unresolve like mirage. Each carries a mouthless ocarina made from the exhalation of a metallic orchid; the ocarinas grow as they are played and shrink when silent—an acoustic lung. When they play, the ravens freeze mid-dive, become sheet music, the sheet music turns into wind which folds the ravens back into birds on a different cycle. The hermits’ music is never rehearsed; it is recovered grief from the moment just before the hush. Each instrument’s wind is the one blink in which the hermit said goodbye to the last shape they wore.

Between cycles, when the cliff extrudes a fresh crumb of rimrock, migratory children come. They are the same children every pilgrimage, but also freshly born on arrival. They arrive as backpacks stitched from tortoise shells. The shells unfold, unzip, unzip, unzip each along seams nobody drew, until a child steps out holding the backpack’s husk like wet origami clothes. The children’s pupils are square, solarized camera shutters; their eyelashes are the fresco maps harvested from the nacelle walls. They read distance by closing their eyes, scattering plots of safe ground back into the ivory noise. When they open the lids again, a frightened dandelion may blink back at them; the exchange is complete and they remember the grammar of oxygen.

Also present are translators made of night. They are arboreal shadows left on branches by extinct constellations. When a traveler screams, the shadows detach and wrap themselves around the scream, rinsing it of meaning. The traveler wants to say “home,” the night-shadow hears only sinusoid and replies in the dialect of drowned alphabets. During the seventeen-minute reset the shadows reenact the frayed semaphore in which the constellations spelled “Do Not Forgive Us We Forgot How to Burn.” The sentence, once spoken, becomes lichen. Every time a lichen spore sprouts, the shadows flinch, recalling their debt to light.

A single copper centipede, cast ages ago by a blacksmith who mistook the sky for an anvil, tunnels among the roots reporting density. At .002 g / cm³ it is lighter than vacuum, heavier than memory. Its thousand legs are hollow straws filled with the laughter-like harmonics left by colliding dirigibles. Wherever the centipede walks, it plaits absences, so places recently visited suddenly become softer, acquiescing to fingertips the way wet paper accepts ink. Biologists who never came here once hypothesized such softness is death; the commuters of the garden know it as speed. They step into soft air and coast until friction thickens them back to being.

Finally, there is the Echo-Queen, who is also everyone mentioned above and none of them. The Echo-Queen resides inside the null amplitude at the heart of the hush. She is the period between two roars that humans interpret as punctuation. When the garden inhales, she inhales its deafness, becoming the silhouette of the cliff—an unmoving shape against an unmoving abyss. When the garden exhales, she is expelled as the sum total of difference across all cycles: a lattice of every unasked question and unused answer compressed into a single seven-note chord suspended in potassium glass. If you look straight at the chord you see only mirrored cloudlight. Tilt the glass one degree and the chord flips into a recognizable face: your own face at the age you will be seventeen minutes after the next death you experience.

The Echo-Queen has decided not to let this death occur within the garden. Instead, she does not decide; decision is noise. Rather, the chord-of-identity drifts. Visitors report a last sensation: fog of cooling blood through neck arteries painted exactly the final color of a black-orchid petal seen from below. Then they find themselves sealed inside a giant seed-shaped pod made of their own former skin grown translucent. The pod unhooks itself gently, joins the outbound stream of glass-winged tortoises, and falls. What falls is so light that falling feels like rising. What rises is so dense it has earned the city's farewell stare.

All of them—hermits, children, shadows, centipede, Echo-Queen—subsist by reminding one another that they have already agreed to never be the same thing twice, with the single exception of the shared scream which alone stabilizes the grammar. As long as someone, somewhere, is answering the roar, the population counter remains locked at ∞ / 1. The numerator entices new wanderers who never felt at home being one shape. The denominator reassures the cliff itself that it continues to be astonished to exist.
