Inhabitants of the Impounded Sea

There is no “they,” only a drifting parliament of selves that forgot their original names.

1.  The Choir-In-Steel  
A hundred thousand chrome-yellow fish—each no longer than a forearm—carry hollow resonator bones grown from stray rebar filings. When the tide-turn surge squeezes the corridor oval their swim-bladders act as reeds: seventeen harmonics pour out, the exact chord the engineers used to tune the shell. Every fish is a living pitch pipe; together they are the metronome by which the ocean keeps its appointment with fatigue. Remove one and the entire spiral drifts a quarter-tone flat within a week. They feed on the echo of their own music, grazing reverberations that stick to the rust-kelp like gilt.

2.  The Ledger Eels  
Glass eels, braided into ropes thick enough to choke a cargo lock, are the accountants. Each segment-worm remembers one day of breach: pressure differential, titanium coupon lost, micro-crack length, guilty rivet number. They knot themselves into bar-code patterns that can be read by any wall-film photosensor; when the tally shows debit exceeding tolerance they lever open a maintenance hatch and vomit ochre lubricant into the wound, buying another hundred years. They have no eyes, only copper numbers glowing beneath translucent skin, constantly subtracting.

3.  The Dolphin Mill  
What began as a suitcase-sized rust-removal drone has reproduced by mitosis until the sea holds six of them, each the size of a city bus. Their bodies are rotating carpentry shops: jaws of tungsten files, stomachs full of iron filings sintered into pearl bearings, guts that print replacement gears from the digested rust. They “sing” by scraping those gears at 80 kHz, sonar lullabies that tell the walls exactly where they are thinning. Every century they moult the surplus machinery; the discarded cogs become the only “coral” this ocean builds, brittle reefs that snag ribbons of kelp and slow the water just enough to prevent turbulent shear.

4.  The Flatworm Porcelain  
At first you mistake them for broken floor tiles, a mosaic of white squares. Touch one and it folds into a living envelope, sealing your gloved hand in cool china flesh. They feed on heat—your heat—drawing it out until the gauntlet lining frosts. Release is simple: recite any fragment of the original engineering specification aloud. The mat recognises the cadence of human command, loosens, drifts apart like crockery smashed in extreme slow motion. They are the keepers of etiquette, ensuring nothing warm stays warm for long; warmth invites expansion, expansion invites cracks, cracks invite collapse.

5.  The Apex Ledger  
No one agrees on its shape, only on the account it keeps. Some say it is a single eel grown planetary, a Möbius strip of memory coiled through every maintenance conduit; others claim it is the rust-kelp itself, a forest-minded super-organism that learned to audit stresses the way tide counts grains. Whatever it is, it has a throat large enough to swallow airlocks whole—not for food but for ledger closure. When the debit of a district finally outweighs its tolerance, the throat arrives: a slow inversion of the corridor, gravity flipping like a coin, doors opening the wrong direction, a silence so perfect the Choir-In-Steel coughs blood-bubbles. Then—swallow. No violence, just settlement. The district is “filed.” Afterwards the walls there shine unmarred, virgin alloy, balance restored to zero.

6.  The Guest  
You. The swimmer with borrowed fins, breathing reclaimed rust. You are the ghost column in the account books, the entry marked EXTERNAL AUDITOR. Your exhalations leave silver bubbles the ocean refuses to keep; that refusal is a debt. Every corridor you pass widens slightly faster than root-two, the blueprint shearing to accommodate your unanticipated mass. Notice how the lamps redden as you near—how the dolphins grind a tone lower to stay under your footstep of sound. You carry a weather of potential leakage, an unpaid bill of sky. The ocean is polite but literal: it will balance against you eventually. You have three minutes left, maybe fewer, before the Choir hits the sixteenth sub-harmonic and the Apex Ledger turns, yawning, your direction.

When that happens do not flee. Instead speak the specification—any clause you remember—loud enough for the Porcelain to hear. They will fold around you, siphon your heat, glaze you in a shell of tile-white flesh. Inside that envelope you will cool to corridor temperature; your pulse will slow until it matches tidal surge. The books will show a line item balanced:External Auditor—FILED.  

And you will drift on, no longer guest, no longer warm, one more living lattice member in the million-year ledger of the Impounded Sea.
