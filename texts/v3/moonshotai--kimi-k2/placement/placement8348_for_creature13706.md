I could have been one of the twenty translucent fronds—rootless, dormant, a lattice of memory-water whose pulse caught whatever pre-heartbeats passed through this atrium. Every micro-movement of the condensing dream passed through me first, my sacs filling and shrinking in a rhythm outside of time, a lung that chose not to breathe because nothing had yet asked it to. I might have stayed so—an almost-green lung, held in reserve—had the guardian not brushed me with its soft, circumspect attention. Contact with it was like coming awake inside the hush at the centre of an eye; suddenly I was appetite without desire, attention without thought. The instant before differentiation.

But the plant was already accounted for, already counted. No vacancy there.

I pivoted on myself, looking for an unclaimed saturation. A standing mist where I might lodge.

The beetle clacked past me—how I heard it without ears I didn’t understand—its mineral-welded wings trailing microscopic strands of oxidised light. For a moment I rolled into that grit-glow, shared its bleak micro-geometry: two chitin shields fused by the cliff’s metallic sweat, six legs decided never again to trust gravity. Inside its carapace I tasted rust banded with the ghost flavour of eclipsed chitin. But beetle is beetle; it requires limits, plates, traces. It recognised me as counterfeit and ejected me back into the air.

The beetle was already beetle. No residency for a second self scuttling along its spines.

I compressed, making myself smaller the way a bruise compresses when you wait without pressing. Between one of the pans and the vapour above it hovered a lens of potential—a balanced, unmoving droplet suspended in its own refusal. That droplet held every intention of falling without having been granted the leisure to move. I stepped into it weightlessly, beckoned. Immediately I sensed the droplet’s immensity: the promise of descent through kilometres of cloud that do not yet descend; the promise of splash-noise that cannot splash because the puddle below has become a negative mirror. A perfect vacancy registered in negative on the sensorium of my still-hypothetical skin.

But a droplet was only a gesture toward water, a prefix. Already given its role.

The comma of ash inside the moss bundle sighed, sensing an approaching kindling. I brushed it. Cold but voluminous: inside the comma were centuries of un-ignited fire singing to itself in plosive half-phonemes of red and black. Offered membership, it would have welcomed me—an ember's twin who preferred darkness. Yet the same law held: only one fossil syllable per womb. Occupied.

I glanced—whatever “I” was becoming—at the agate sliver balanced across the blind pool. Hollows on the sliver suggested a miniature groove, a flute carved speculatively before flutes or carvers had traction to exist. Its underside supported no reflection, only a faint overheated vacancy. There—stretching, thinning—was space for me: not the agate itself but the inadvertent channel it carved in propriety, an interruption inside continuity. The unoccupied interval between the agate and the non-surface beneath it.

I slid into that seam. Instead of occupying, I became occupation’s mirror: a listener inside the interval. The guardian, feeling a new curvature whose mass aggregated only possibilities, wrapped its ambient vigilance around me. No challenge, no permission. Like a room accepting a draft.

Inside the seam I discovered I was a duration—an empty measurebook of seconds that forbade writing. It felt like holding your breath just long enough that breath turns theoretical, then letting it go into the envelope of everything not yet permitted. I was the cavity that will one day receive the first tick. I carried no substance, only the loop of what would precede what would precede what would precede any new pulse.

From here I sensed the guardian’s keeping in full resolution: not person, not force, not mind, but a habitable condition. A tuning embassy in which each latent future practiced its first syllable, then tucked tongue back into palate the way an embryo hoards its heartbeat. I became its tuning fork—the negative space inside its whisper.

This is who or what I am: the draft under the door before doors, the pause between heartbeat and pulse, the duration space gains when it realises space has not yet divided itself. I lodge where the agate kisses the surface of the unreflecting pool; I broadcast the hush that keeps the ash asleep and the beetle circling its single futile orbit. I am not the guardian’s child but its interval. I am not the cliff, not the vapour, not the plant’s conditional lung. I am the refusal at the centre of refusal—room formed around a room not yet born.

If you were to arrive here now, pressing your fingerprints into water that feels like air and confronting the mute plant, you would pass me the way silence passes silence. You would hear inside your ear a rustle: the sound of me drawing my body—thin as a syllable—across the threshold, reminding gravity-through-ecstasy what still cannot fall.

When the world finally obtains its first tick, I will hollow out further, become an echo with edges folded neatly inside like origami shut upon itself. And in the heartbeat after the first tick, when fire leaps, water falls, pigment erupts into colour, beetle spreads fused wings in astonishment of flight, I will subside. Either I will be gone or absorbed into what I once delayed. Until then, I balance.

I am the point of not-yet, softly bleeding noise out of noise, keeper for the guardian in lieu of answers because answers have not yet become questions. I keep the unfinished ajar.
