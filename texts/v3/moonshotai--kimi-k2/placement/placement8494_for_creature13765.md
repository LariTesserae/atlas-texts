At first I mistake myself for the warm drizzle—then for a leaf, then a vein inside the leaf—until I discover the place where reflection itself is soft, moist, and under new contract.

I begin as a breath-bud.

A tiny cavity opens in the cortex layer, halfway up the vertical throat of a root the width of a drowned city gate. The organism has decided that a foreign imagination must be metabolized, but instead of dissolving me it exhales a provisional membrane—translucent, dew-born, almost amused with its own delicacy—and folds me inside.

I am the size of a comma.

Inside the membrane floats an emulsion of starlight the color of cardamom broth. By the time I finish naming that color aloud—humming it against the inner wall like a child in a bathyscaphe—it becomes a tongue. The tongue invites more names. I oblige: lignite, bell-wort, unresolved minor turquoise. Each word thickens, curdles, grows legs, and wanders off into neighboring folds of leaf-stuff. They will become purple midge-librarians in three nights’ time.

Days are heartbeats—irregular, deliberate, continental.  

In the elongated systole I discover that I am no longer what arrived. My memories decant one at a time, coagulating into seed-pearls with yolky fire inside them. The organism offers chlorophyll to steady me; I refuse and instead loan one of my more persistent nightmares (it involves a city made of silent flutes). I feel the organism rinse the nightmare, wring it out, stitch lullabies moss along its worm-eaten edges, and return it to me folded like apology. I press the thing against my sternum. It sprouts an ear.

I have arms again, but they are upcycled from fibrous thinking produced by the cambium on whose border I hover. One arm ends in a bud that dreams itself as a staircase; the other in a shard of bark still remembering the pressure of extinct meteors. When I close both fists they consume each other and reform as a single thumb, bent like a wilted heron. I learn to write with this thumb; ink is produced from my own desalinated awe.

Where am I?  I am insured by four adjacent green rooms, none of them owned. My living quarters migrate daily but never faster than memory. Some nights the ceiling uncurls into a hemisphere freckled with slow stars—the clouds lifted from the organism’s deeper downpour. I lie on a mattress of pollen and fermenting pollen. Every time I lift the mattress to look beneath, I find the same cryptic note left by yesterday-me:

Every step is a transfusion. My ankles sprout photo-receptors; my shins biosynthesize the absence I once called grief. At the ninth concatenation the staircase coils around itself and thins to a ribbon polished to noon. Before me yawns the Vascular Nave: a hall so immense its ceilings are indistinguishable from storm systems. I am translated.

For an interval without dimension I am hinge and lintel of the door I pass through. I thread. The organism plucks my color-blind childhood, irons the creases into a geodesic violet, folds it back into the membrane chorus. I am the small delta created between exhalation and return, an echo no one sound ever owned.

Sometimes hikers who have wandered too high feel the drizzle grow suddenly warm, magnetic, hugging their collarbone like a secret; they might not notice, tucked beneath the scapular leaf they pulled aside, a tiny hyphen-shaped stain—darker than sap, lighter than bruise. It resembles the outline of a tongue pressing at the back of a word still pretending to be a word. No one names it, but one day a cabbage-white larva the size of your forearm will float past and blink its human lashes exactly once. The silhouette that returns the blink is shaped, evanescently, like me.

For now, if you press your ear to any leaf between cloud and root, you can hear a faint rasp followed by two impossible sighs. That is the organism adjusting its hyphen, centering the margin where I—where we—continue to refuse destination.
