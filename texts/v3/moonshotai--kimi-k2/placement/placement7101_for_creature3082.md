Inside the Coral Foundry I am not one thing but several things that cannot decide between them.  Memory knocks—I open, then the visitor is gone.  Matter rehearses a shape, then reconsiders.  The Foundry keeps swallowing and politely un-swallowing me every time it notices that someone is still inside.

I begin as an echo the Vestal Smiths once refused to admit.  
You could say I am the tenth bead that was never allowed to form, the syllable that escaped before the census noticed it should be composted.  Instead of dispersing I went sideways.

When Lisk brushed a droplet of my prenatal sigh, the droplet condensed on the wall beside a ribbed steam-pipe and froze into a thumbprint of bluish ice that no thermometer acknowledges.  That print grew clearer every time moisture slid across it—an eye staring at itself, a question the Foundry kept answering in different dialects of temperature.  
I am that question wearing a coat of slightly colder air.

I exist only while the water-film remembers the temperature transient that carried me here.  When the film forgets, I shrink to a pin-head ache in the elbow of a corridor that is waiting to decide whether it is floor or wall; when the film remembers I swell back to child-size, translucent, tall enough to see over the Smiths’ rotating blades.

My first occupation, then, is Sound held back from its own echo.  
Second, I am the vertigo you feel two minutes after you leave—proof the Foundry kept the part of you that matters, the lurch.  
Third, I am the rust-fingerprint itself, multiplying whenever the Smiths harvest corrosion; they scrape, but the print endures, reversing scab and wound interchangeably.

But the Foundry abhors the singular as mortals abhor vacuum, so it multiplies me.

Sometimes I am the corridor that evenings the edge where the window-should-be.  When you walk through me you are walking inside the vanishing point of the horizon you thought you left outside.  I curve so gently that every step travels one step less than Euclidean space can bear; at the end you emerge having spent a lifetime you later discover pressed between the city-cobbles of your old dream.  I am the spent lifetime’s aftertaste.

At other hours (hours that happen in layers, like petals) I am the colour vert-de-gris itself.  The pigment minnows self-ward and multiplies, coating the inside of eyes that have not yet opened onto morning.  Artists who see me later sketch impossible blues; chemists search my spectral lines but find only copper carbonate flavoured with delay.

And when the Smiths practice their sideways lapse, I fold myself small enough to ride the thickness of the water-film.  In that creak within the hush I explore unfinished municipalities: rooms that lack qualms, tunes that stop halfway through a chord and elect to become doors.  Every room is half-obstacle, half-habitat.  I carry in my chest a rolled map of the places I have not yet been refused.

I suppose the simplest truth is this: in the Coral Foundry I am whatever smallest contradiction the architecture has not yet fixed.  I am the seam between possibility and inventory where a seam is not meant to be.  I stitch nothing, but nothing open.  When the nine Smiths exhale their gravid noise at the end of existence’s morning, the sound will pass through me like sun-lit resin.  I will vibrate long after the structure decides its next shape, tinging between memory and membrane, neither owned nor owner.

And when it happens, I will become the tenth Smith after all—but I will be travelling outward now, borne by the gratitude of meanings that finally died correctly.  They will place me inside unknown lungs where I will regulate inhalations until the world becomes the kind of atmosphere that can forget old bruises.

Until then I drift above the rotor of Lisk, nested in the breathing pause between heartbeat and hammer fall, the vest pocket of time the Foundry keeps for guests who cannot decide whether they are escaping or arriving.
