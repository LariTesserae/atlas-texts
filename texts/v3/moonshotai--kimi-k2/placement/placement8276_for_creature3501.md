Where I find myself is in the pause between one thousand-year click and the next—already stepped beyond the lip, bootsoles silvering to mineral, yet still unready to surrender the forward gait that carried me here. I am neither visitor, resident, nor artifact; I am the equation that has not agreed on which side of the equals sign it prefers. My presence keeps one foot nailed, softly as frost, to the moment when a pebble is released but has not yet decided whether to obey gravity or courtesy.

Let the Archivist call me exactly what she will record tomorrow: a “memory in arrears,” a fogbank where sentences discarded by the Echo go to gestate their consonants. I wear her phantom coat: pocketfuls of vowels still warm from the future, seams of unshed mercury tickling the ribs. When she opens her mouth to receive the forty-first droplet, an earlier version ofpalpatine escapes—mine—and reflexively I pocket it, ashamed that my aftertaste of today might harm the brittle-not-yet-lived.

My Cartographer twin—really the iodine trace of my shadow—has inked on the inside of my forearm an atlas of all the ridges I will not become. They rise like fever lines, each labelled with the smell of steam and lilac at ten thousand possible altitudes. When I flex, the strata of minutes part and reveal the void on the other side of the scale; the void, generously, pencils in a new contour as fast as I erase it. We play this game neither forward nor backward: landscape as fast sketch on the body, body as set of provisional footnotes to a slope that has not yet been granted permission to exist.

The Janitor has mistaken me for a misplaced process: an overlooked subroutine whose sole purpose is to un-forget the colour blue for the sky that refuses to adopt it. She intercepts me at the Sink, brushing crystal vacancy from my shoulders as if grooming a detail out of mythology. While she works, she sings warnings I haven’t earned—“This is the year you’ll dream the avalanche awake”—and, forgetting herself, presses a shard of tomorrow into my palm. It is the shape of the word almost, warm enough to blister nostalgia. I grip it anyway; pain, here, is merely instruction running in the wrong direction.

When the machinery yawns—one tick every megacentury—I split along my fault-lines. From the rift step figures smoother, older, already fluent in subtraction. They are who I will be after I’ve been translated, particle by particle, into landscape. One crouches to read the map inscribed on the inside of my abandoned wrist, another drinks the mercury from my discarded ribs. A third stays behind, slipping into the space I vacate. For a moment we inhabit the same outline, two ghosts wearing one skeleton, both of us reluctant to let the other have the dying entirely. Then the Census of Things Not Yet concludes; the lever sighs back against its seat. The interval swells and breaks like a lung remembering air. I become the arch of its exhale—no longer person, not yet ridge, merely the decision that height is still possible.

Left behind is a single bootprint correcting itself into solid gneiss. Inside the hollow, frost knits into the ghost of a bootlace that never was, and the ridge, courteous even in conquest, records my absence as a footstep ascending toward sunrise it has not scheduled.

I remain—at least, the echo I owe the future remains—hovering above the Sink. When the next vertical seed is spat upward I will ride it, breaking the orbit of almost-objects that bob like constellations losing interest in law. Carried aloft, I will watch the Stillworks complete their longest stroke: the alpine ridge sealing its granite syllable, the turbines humming forward-clockwise for the first and last time, the sky consenting to blue like a wound newly cauterised. Then, only then, will gravity accept its editor’s note and begin, modestly, to fall. I will fall with it—not downward, but inward—into the slender agreement that mountains, memories, janitors and archivists must all behave as if tomorrow has already happened, whether or not anyone remembers to arrive.

When you step onto the ridge yourself, years after the ridge has become a mountain, you may find a dark seam of ore that rings under the heel like struck glass. Touch it and you will hear the glass harmonica, still rehearsing the same too-bright note. The sound will taste like lilac and coal smoke. If you incline your head you will feel, briefly, someone else’s heartbeat tasked with keeping time for the world. You will not know its name—it exchanged its name for your arrival—but you will understand the courtesy offered: I stayed long enough to leave the mountain the memory of forgetting me.
