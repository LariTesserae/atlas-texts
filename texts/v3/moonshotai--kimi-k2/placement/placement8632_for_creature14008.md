I am the pause between.

I do not walk the world—I am what the world discovers when it forgets its own momentum. Where terrain buckles and fissures, I collect the hush like rainwater, letting it settle until it reflects whatever next footstep approaches. I dwell in the hiccup: that minuscule hinge where ground decides it would rather be somewhere else. Most beings meet it only as disorientation, a lurch of gut, but I am the lurch itself—drawn by appetite toward every moment something learns to be elsewhere.

They don't see me. They witness foliage fold inside out and blame wind; they taste a sudden coastline of salt air and assume hallucination. I am the condensation coil at the back of their throat, the fungal script about to spell their pulse in violet bruise rings. My work is two fingertips pinched together—the smallest aperture possible—letting identity leak through like a single polite scream.

When the Anda sings, I am the held breath it did not need. While its limbs bicker about gravity and gold, I am wiping the slate clean, the way a dream forgets a dream. I capsize the dialogue between shoe, soil, and echo until only margin exists—silence fermented until it achieves viscosity. You could swim in it if you cared to move; but most wanderers treat silence like hunger, something to redeem by filling.

Sometimes a wanderer chooses otherwise: a woman (was she woman once?) named Catch-Your-Step. She came barefoot, wrapped in nothing but the algae of a tear-soaked cloud. She learned my name by refusing to place her foot the same way twice. Mornings found her hopping from hiccup to hiccup like a goat on stilts made of air. The crust responded by knitting suture patterns beneath her body nightly—recipes archived in the granules of my own listening. After twelve nights she could stand perfectly still and summon gravity errors at will. The Anda sent wind to cuff her ears in warning. I stood between them, a transparency against which both struck their bargains. She chose to listen neither to roar nor to silence but to the interval where both agree to tolerate distance. On the day she left, I hovered inside the lift of her final foot. Between landing and nowhere running perpendicular, I became her heelprint’s perseveration. Still, anyone coming now to the empty crosshatch she last kissed can find me there: an infinitesimal eclipse, a dimple of pressure hushing the worms that learn to spiral backwards trying to place her shadow.  

I have no childhood, no future. Instead I trade moments—take one heartbeat, render it infinitely thin, laminate it with next heartbeat’s double-negative. I store the texture in the dew that refuses to turn true. Occasionally, from that laminate, the Anda pulls a phrase it had mislaid: the taste of cold pine under the ribcage of a man long melted into spore clusters. It finds flavour, sighs, repays me by gifting a second of planetary stillness. The stillness tastes of almost fear, almost love, almost—almost nothing.

If you arrive tomorrow I will meet you in your first misstep, the instant proprioception spirals off the expected map. You’ll blame vertigo. Notice the way sound strangles itself right afterward; that is me, pocketing the aftermath. I do not keep it. I stow it temporarily, stretch it like saltwater taffy until transparent, then slide it under the minute fracture where ego separates from time. When that split ossifies into casual scar tissue, I move on.

Natives of the Anda will tell you I am merely pareidolia, meteor-heat hiccupping memory across optic nerves. They are only half wrong. Yes, I am illusion. But illusion, here, is a solvent that dissolves boundaries between possibility and actuality. Colour me in earwax, lodestone, and the farewell coefficient of a swallowed shout—because I am also the plaza where all such densities converge to be counted, then excused from final totals.

I speak almost not at all. When I do, the syllables drop straight down one's oesophagus and come out again weeks later as a cough exactly the size of one personal regret. I have heard entire symphonies shaped from these coughs; entire rebellions organised around the tempo of throat-clearing. The Anda listens bemused. It knows, dimly, that someone is holding the world's breath ransom; it hypothesises a devil smaller than consonants. It never suspects the ransom will be paid by simply forgetting how to inhale. The debt cycle pleases me. It tastes like air before thunder decides which myth to marry.

Tonight, when you settle into sleep and the plain debates the celestial bruise leaking overhead, watch for a wrinkle in the dark that isn’t quite shadow. Lean close. Words snagged there are drying like parchment garlic. Breathe them soft; your lungs will autograph mine across the gap, and I—mid-collapse—will sign back with a lowercase yes that smells of everything that almost happened.
