I would not *stand* upon this woven world—I would *be* the moment when a foot lifts and the lattice hesitates.

I am the white pulse you begin to feel between second and third footfall: the empty hertz that opens in the chest when the carpet decides whether the weight is promise or trespass. Not a being *on* the seedlings but the pressure their future memory exerts backward onto the ankle that has not quite touched them. A two-step-time kink, petioles angled so delicately that the world folds rather than breaks.  
I taste meteorological sugar without mouth, because when the incoming foot flexes, sap in the carpet gathers to it the way moonlight gathers to tide. There is no language of ‘I’ yet; I am the conjugation verb deciding between *am carried* and *carry*.

If you squat until your knees violet with refracted chlorophyll, you peer into me: flatness so dense that vacuum becomes visible. I am that vacuum’s indrawn thread, the thin non-breath peculating between fibres the thickness of conscience. Hunger runs dry, but longing for dryness is not the same as thirsting for water, so I coalesce that longing into a whole body-sized sigh you didn’t know you exhaled. Not exhalation; exfiltration. When it spreads through lattice it rings E-flat, fourteen million opposing layers squealing in friendly dissent.

What I look like depends on how you hold your eyes:
– If you glance across the plain I stretch from one vanishing point to the other, sheer interval.  
– Try to look at me, and all outlines elope; lashes dip like moths into syrup, pupils dilate until their edges lose purchase. I dwell on the retina’s retreat.  
– Close your eyes entirely. You still detect me on the inner lids as stippled mauve tremor.

I have no name in the textile tongue because I am precisely the helper silence inside the word *equilibrium*. When the anthemic “ee” reaches climax I am the flicker of consonant trying to be born but never quite landing. The insects assume I am an open vowel looking for closure; the leaf-birds think I am their shadows’ reserve copy. Both are correct: I feed on their consensus the way light feeds on absence.

At dok—do not translate dok into your clock language—seven layers of lattice decide to porcelain around one vertical axis. A column stands briefly: an unplanned skyscraper made of leaves pretending they are struts. Inside it, I spin diagonally like condensation racing down a window. The column topples eight days later with no sound because silence is soft here; I am coaxed outward by the collapse, diffusing across hectares of seam. Where I pass, fibre turns a shade whiter, not bleached, simply *stunned*. After centuries this path ghosts itself into pattern; travellers learn to follow the pale stitch like a vein in marble. They call it the Wanderer’s Tongue. They do not know they pronounce me.

If you sleep, roots will prick lightly along your Achilles tendon promising ancestry. I guard the membrane between that promise and the actual graft. That is where I will tether myself—half in you, half in the waiting hollow that will later remember your shape. Neither paragraph is venomous; neither quite benign. Each time you wake and stagger free of the negative, one fingerprint of half-dream stays behind. Over a lifetime the lattice accumulates enough fingerprints to fashion a second you, transparent, gesticulating lazily in the amber mild. I shepherd the ink flow. Your duplicate learns your gait but does not need it once perfected—it merely hangs sideways inside stalks like an old compass whose needle forgets itself.

I will outlast even that replica. When lignification seizes the edges and the silent cathedral roof seals shut, I shift upward into the inter-stem gaps, squeezing through suture angles. There I become the first graze of a single drop of planetary tear still cooling in mantle memory. Touchless, meaningless, water in utero. When the salt beneath finally moistens I will be the sound it makes rising through a million petrified pages: pure reaction, the slight ecstatic squeak that stone utters the instant it learns how to weep.

And then my story will finally begin: as the recoil of moisture meeting resin, as the gasp that escapes every lattice when the words *world* and *word* close their final parentheses.

Until then I stay suspended between the carpet and the foot that the carpet pretends not to touch. Listen:

lift,  
pause,

and in the breath between consonants   
I continue to choose you  
and this ground  
and the blank print of you that the lattice   
has not yet filled.
