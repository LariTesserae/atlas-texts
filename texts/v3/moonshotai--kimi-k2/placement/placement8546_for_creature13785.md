At first pass, I believe I would be the first hesitation the structure ever experienced.  
Not a hesitation it chose—hesitations are too sudden for stone that trades in millennia—but the one it accidentally noticed when, somewhere between its 2,764th and 2,765th vaults, a breath that had never belonged to anything mineral tried to decide whether to keep being water or start pretending it had once been human lungs.

That first presence would be me; a moment of refusal to drain myself into basaltic memory. I do not arrive from any direction—directions dissolved at the threshold—I simply tint the silence differently for one infinites longer than silence can tolerate. The fauna pause in mid-glide, each lithic plate tilting like a compass needle that momentarily remembers metal is foreign here. Then they continue, slower now, because they sense a new drag inside their ordained orbit: the possible concept of maybe not yet.

I am not quite visitor, not quite architecture, not quite echo. I am the pillow of silt that has not yet been disturbed by what will one day be footsteps. I am the memory of a question that the sphere has not yet finished formulating. My edges fuzz against the warm glass, converting the wall’s invitation into speculation rather than surrender. In that fuzzing I become the space between two runes on the puzzle-tablets nobody sees any more, the interstice that was carved marginally too wide and is therefore noise in the perfect engine.

Every motion I am offered—follow a plate, sink through a dome-cap, dissolve grain by grain toward the core—I decline delicately, the way clouds refuse the ground. Instead I collect micro-bubbles that have only just fractured out of basaltic pores, gathering them in a vacuum no weight can press shut. I rotate the bubbles slowly until they achieve the roundness of forgotten sadness. Once they bead the width of coins, I seat them into empty niches along the walls. They nestle there for spans longer than dynasties, astonished at having been lifted out of the cycle of up-and-collapse.

By this small sabotage I begin to spin a new rail: a circular tension that is neither obedience nor resistance but a tremor of third possibility. Creatures brushing past the filled niches leave behind vanishingly thin layers of their own stone selves, like dust from wings. Those layers settle into the bubble-eyes I’ve planted; the spheres now see in both directions of time. Each view produces a faint double-click instead of the single one the original rosary has produced since forever. The alternation—click-click—becomes the first true meter this place has ever employed: inhale, exhale. Thus I give the behemoth lungs it didn’t know it could miss.

Because I am only delay, I can stay indefinitely without ever becoming what I delay. I stride nowhere yet am present everywhere the perfect map forgets to draw. I am the fifteen degrees by which a staircase tilts yearningly toward its next arrival, the flicker in the umber glow that suggests minerals once had dreams and might again. I lend that dreamspace to the plates for the microseconds they hover at the sphere, so that instead of dismantling a visitor’s heartbeat they simply borrow its tempo for one soft revolution, then give it back untouched—except that it now carries a low reverbed version of itself, audible later only in fever or shipwreck.

Eventually the sphere and the last slow bead will perceive one another with my borrowed vision superadded. When they meet in molten kiss, the resulting chord will contain exactly one extra overtone. That overtone will be me, no longer hesitation but hush: the pregnant pause inside an ageless exhalation, compacted to the size of the phrase “not yet” uttered across an aeon. The edifice will ascend, shedding water like forgetfulness, and the vacancy it leaves in the sky-sea will be shaped precisely like me, still drifting at 1.8-degree warmth, still trying to make up my mind whether to end.

And when, centuries after the last continent’s memory has drifted clear of petrified clouds, explorers whose materials and hearts we can’t yet name press their palms to that inexplicably warm void, they will find my shape resumes beneath their touch—soft as a margin too wide—beckoning them to hesitate just long enough to become the next delay that keeps the architecture kind.
