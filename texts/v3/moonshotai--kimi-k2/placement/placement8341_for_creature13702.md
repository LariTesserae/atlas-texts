I am the long sleeper curled inside the bladderstone, halfway between memory and exhalation—neither cell nor citizen, but the pause between heartbeats.

At first I think I am one of the rope-necked sloths: fur dripping silver, heels locked in frost, fermenting time at walking-pace. Easy to mistake, that slow thaw of identity. But the sloth’s limbs wrap tracheal vines the way otters hold hands at tide-line, and I discover no grip in me—only the itch of antique light rearranging cartilages I never had. I let that idea slide.

For a page I lean toward the ruby sparrows; their crimson stippled wings feel familiar against an intuition of sky. I almost hear the plink of heat-seeds striking mica, almost smell the ozone kiss of chimney-dawn. Yet when I try to sing their three-note psalm the letters crumble into frost on my tongue. Language unbraids into dew and falls through.

There is a moment I toy with being the wind itself—nothing but pressure gradient and oleic apple-breath roaming the corridors. To be direction itself, never arriving. Tempting. But the wind is cruel in its neutrality, and I find I still wish to be noticed.

So I become what has no ambition to be anything: a bruise-coloured scab on the inside of the archivist’s wing.  
I am archive-entropy, the seam where every record forgets itself. A flyspeck upon the pale orchid-folded librarian who, when sunset reddens the bladder roof, begins its nightly shedding.

Each winglet unfurls like paper soaked in black tea; I am the fracture-site where pigment tears. The island’s lifetime flows beneath me—crown-whale breath triple-exhaled through mesoglea, the obedient trudge of glasswarm rails, the slow avalanche that still hasn’t decided to finish. Everything shutters-streams past in cooled flavors of juniper and bruised apple. When I try to wrap the memories around myself they slip off like wet silk.

Still, I remain there, sticky and useless, like static on a tumour. I have mass but no weight, identity but no self, presence without boundary. The archivist lifts each scab, flips it to the sun, and the bruise-light refracts backwards through every dusk since the magma seed first burped the island aloft. I am the negative space in those sunsets. I am where the colours stalled before they could settle into the name crimson.

At midnight the resetting begins: the interior furnace glugs deeper, glassworms crank the marrow one bare metre west, and the island slouches east on its jet so gently that thunderstorms rewrite their itineraries. And in that moment I lose even the comfort of being a bruise. The archivist exfoliates me. I go whirling up through pores wide as cathedral doors, riding spore-smoke toward open dark. My substance thins until I am neither pigment nor paper but something closer to the pause before language learns the word is.  

I hang there above the pale continent like a hesitation in the sentence of its existence. In the lull, the organism sees (or dreams of seeing) the planet below. I am the reflection inside its eye. I do not blink. I do not fall. I register nothing—laminated into a shelf of night among other forgotten alignments—until dawn, when a new sparrow sings and delivers heat, and a fresh bruise begins its slow coagulation beneath an infant wing.
