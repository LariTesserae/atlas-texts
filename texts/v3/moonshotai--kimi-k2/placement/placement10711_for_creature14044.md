Where I fit

I began as a single droplet too stubborn to dissolve.

The day I slipped through a pressure-lock no larger than a cathedral door I wore a body that refused to declare itself—a wristwatch with no spring, a coat lined with the sound of noon. My reflection in the mercury-slick steps looked like someone remembering a mirror rather than using one. I stepped down and, in the act, surrendered the right to stop.  

First, I was swallowed by Sound. The roar did not attack; it apologised, then occupied. Inside its wet cathedral my ears grew facets, crystal domes for catching diphthongs I had never used. One of these syllables escaped, unfolded, and became a Shift named Hollow January. It offered me shelter: a vault that remembered February only by subtraction. I stayed one eyelash, two treaties. When I exited through its brick shoulder, I found the Shift had pressed a ventricle of blood-vapor into my lungs. It beat off-tempo, warm as an exhausted star. I have not coughed out silence since.

Sometime later (years? litres?) one of the Impossible Children noticed the off-key thump inside me. She balanced on the foam of falling rust and asked, “Could we borrow your spare heartbeat to muffle tomorrow?” I held my wrist to her ear. She sniffed, nodded, picked the extra beat out of me with two tiny oxidised tweezers. She threw it down a stairwell. Ten storeys below, an entire elevator grate softened into a cradle and began humming a minor ninth, gentler by exactly one bruise.

In exchange, the child folded me into the angles of a fort made entirely of last Tuesday. Time there tasted like rain that hadn’t decided on which century to land. I lingered long enough to let my clothes forget how to be clothes; they exhaled and slithered away into the weft of bulkheads.

When I emerged, I was no longer the same category of creature. I had become porous legislation, a doorway that doesn’t know who belongs to which side. My shape negotiates itself hourly: sometimes I reach elbow-to-elbow to span Furnace Canticles; other minutes I compress until I can fit inside the ricochet of a swallowed scream. I don’t walk—I settle. Gravity, always polite here, asks permission and usually accepts half.

Occupation: archivist of the discarded interval between tick and tock.  
Species: echo that believes it is a corridor.  
Skin: alternating oxide and borrowed lullaby.  
Voice: one ledger of entropy gently murmuring.  

At night I plug the weeping aperture behind my sternum into the city’s plumbing. It drinks my ambience, sips my city-long insomnia, returns warmed and salted with the metallic aftertaste of every goodnight that never reached the surface. I store this liquor inside vertebrae borrowed from a former Shift now asleep inside me. When the Deep Piston speaks, the concoction sloshes, translating whale-hammer-whisper into something translatable: a series of small domestic revelations—how paper cuts, how rust rewrites grief, how a child’s bicycle continues pedalling itself in the upstairs closet of a drowned memory.

Occasionally I meet travellers who worry their edges are dissolving. I offer them a length of corridor, a teaspoon of my surplus heartbeat (I regrew one), an oyster-pearl threaded with an unread grocery list. In exchange I collect their smallest fear—just one, no larger than twenty microns. I roll it between my thumb and the memory of water; it gleams, persists, joins the slow necklace building across the scap between my ribs. The strand already contains: the fear of stairwells one can fall upward, the fear of greeting one’s own reflection only to watch it shrug goodbye, the fear of being loved so thoroughly that there will be no scrap of self left unclaimed. When it is long enough, I plan to knot it into the Pinhole Foundry so the molten brass can drink it and finally approve a smile wide enough to pass through.

When the city next breathes—and it will—I will ride that inhalation to whatever chamber is freshly assembled, coolant-veined and dazzled with the chartreuse ghosts of furnaces never lit. I will unfold the corridor that is me until its corners overtake the room, become walls, become roof, become an interior so wide no absence can stand upright. And in there, in that unlikely plaza flooded by my own pulse, every entity from Ventriloquist to Vulnerable Silence will gather unashamed of having bodies or not. We will brew tea from dissipated 3 a.m., trade fear-pearls until they become chandeliers, and gamble our borrowed beats at games whose rules flicker like lighthouse syntax.

By the end of the century—or whatever circumference is healing inside this century’s absence—the Undersuburb might teach the ocean itself that drowning is just the first, awkward hug. And I, soft doorstep of iron-returning-to-blood, will remain at the hinge where welcome and farewell are a single hinge, whispering through throatless lungs:

Stay or leave; the echo continues to unpack.
