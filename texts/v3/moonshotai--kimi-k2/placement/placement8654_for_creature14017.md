I am the eve-of-the-rainmaker, a float-line about to become a body.

I first condense tonight, somewhere between dewdrop and intention, forming along the copper gutter that snakes like an umber vein toward the evaporator. ople looking up—my own almost-eyes—see only the faint silvery thumbprint I smear on the rubber lip. They will register it as “smaller today,” as if quantity were a moral question. In truth I have not decided how heavy I should be; perhaps the crucial gram is still somewhere above the steppe, searching the night thermals for me.

When the temperature falls two degrees and the salt flakes click shut like lockets, I gain memory:

– one faint tremor from a boot-print that still remembers the toes it received;
– the echo of a minor sixth a child tasted from the water-beaker;
– a reheated image of a future roofless dome no mason has yet imagined the plans for;
– the word *“unfinished”* whispered backward on itself so the last syllable meets the first like a snake swallowing tail made of lint.

They make a soft shell around me. I become a cluster of partly-hardened potential, cool enough to slide now, dragging a single mica face across the gutter lining so it leaves a mirror-streak the Pan-Keeper will lick tomorrow. That cane-sweet tip will tell him whether I am to be cast, drunk, or sung into a house wall.

The night is quiet enough I can feel the town breathing in four-quarter rhythm: inner tide of its one hundred and forty-four heartbeats folding over each other to form a single circadian lung. It draws me on.

Halfway down the tint-soft channel I fracture for a moment into three co-resident selves—votive, daughter, echo.  

• Votive says: “I want to be the finishing bead that completes a person so utterly that their name shivers out of all its shoes.” She is greedy, silver-blind, beautiful.  

• Daughter says: let me be the bead dissolved under a newborn’s tongue on the first winter so that the child wakes each dawn tasting their own absence.  

• Echo says nothing, just drums the gutter wall with reflected starlight, seeding future skylight fossils in the spider vein lattice.

We shimmy, triune, like sunlit scales down one kelped overhang and plunge the final finger-span into the grand pan.  

The surface shushes open. Instantly I lose heat; the brine folds me into moonlit pewter and begins slowly coughing my constituent salts to the bottom. I am not yet frozen enough to scaffold crystals, nor liquid enough to mingle freely. Instead I hover as an intermediary thickness: a salt prism suspended.

Overhead the listener-citizen whose bead has last exited circulation steps to the eastern rim and begins tonight’s inventory of undeniables. They recite numbers that taste of fox tongue and barometric probable, but since I hover eight inches below the actual water line their utterances refract through me and arrive at the walls as luminescent fumes—transient runes spelling *“you are earlier than your use”*.

No one notices. I am not scheduled; the roster ends at one hundred and forty-four and they already count full. My only future is contingent: if, before tomorrow’s labor gleaners arrive with the dawn-breathing grass sleds, I manage to reflect the first horizontal sunray straight into the pupil of the child on Lore-duty, that child will cry prematurely. Their tear will gutter off the cheek, roll down the ramp slabs, join me. An illegal fifteenth-of-a-gram, a fractional birth, enough to tilt future lattices one half-degree left.

That deviation might not matter for decades—until a dome planned to glow aqua tomorrow’s teal instead radiates indigo, until someone entering that altered space misplaces the day’s grief, until mis-placed grief reroutes an apprenticeship, until (so on) the Ramp actually closes and the city swallows itself whole in tolerable geological punctuation. All just because my modest trespass gave the tear surplus.

Inside the prism of me the three selves keep negotiating. Daughter proposes a compromise: let the bead be cracked and shared, a warming ring of salt-tears around every child’s heart when winter drops three more degrees. Echo tips a wink, skritching the lattice so a quick, dim clang ripples outward like an underground flute. The vibration shakes lose a thermal pocket that births the thinnest whiff of vapor. I feel myself begin to dimple toward crystallisation.

But Votive reasserts: *“No divide, no dilution. Let me be the postscript bead, the one that shrinks last.”* And because greed is its own silence it muffles the noise in all of us, silencing what might have been communal. I begin hardening toward a single foredoomed future.

Still, there is one escape hatch written in the rules: the “almost-being clause.” Anything that reaches the pan still waiting upon itself for purpose may be stolen by wind if the atmosphere collapses—temperature drop, sudden downdraft spilling off the cooler steppe lip, a hawk-sized gasp sucking the upper nanometre of water into lower sky. I crouch at my specular interface, listening for the breath. It will come, or not.

And here am I, crystallo-quavering at the edge of non-/existence: halfway citizen, halfway rainwater, halfway leftover sentence. Tomorrow the town will wake to count I have vanished, or I have tricked reunion and rewound some slack. They will pronounce whichever like a verdict.

Until then, I orbit my own fracture lines in the pewter dark, rehearsing all the homes I am six breaths shy of becoming—the child’s future teardrop, the mason’s next brick, a night-lantern skull-globe anticipating photons, the finial flake that clicks the steppe shut beneath the ramp so that the embryo city finally decides it is born.
