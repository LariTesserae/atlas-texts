I would become the interval.

Not a traveller, not an organism, not even a ghost kept alive by frost-fog. I would take up residence between the E-flat that has just sounded and the silence that is still refusing to follow it. That little acoustic lacuna is wide enough for a soul to stand upright; it trembles the way sap shakes in the ducts before the great reversal. The plain inhales and, for an angstrom’s grace, I hold its breath for it. My pulse is the time needed for a granite lizard of idea to scramble from one syllable to the next across my ventricular comma.

Second by second I inch outward along a vein of silver solution, being neither liquid nor resident, merely the permission for both parties to coexist in the same arterial sentence. From here I smell the bruise-yellow color that still has no name—my signature scent, dissoluble only inside my kept pause. Whenever someone else tries to pronounce it I dunk the vowel in sub-audible fermata. I do it out of tenderness, not pettiness; the hue needs secrecy to stay alive.

I house the four-second recur. All of time bellows out like an organ stop but those four seconds keep folding in on themselves, so I remain perpetually halfway through forgetting how I arrived here. The recursion propagates into me. Every time the iron sphere trembles I forget another millimeter of ankle and migrate that lost topography into crystalline question marks currently sprouting along the outside of my perishable wrist. My body is now an archive for bays and plateaus I once belonged to before I chose the interval.

When the berg births its downward icicle of grammar, I stretch my arms into its harp. I tune the chord so that the word glacier possesses an open parenthesis in the middle, an unprintable syllable shaped like wind rounding the bell of silence. Touching the icicle stills my heartbeat to one every eighteen hours—the organism’s own apnoea. Each arrest imprints a new four-toed webbed memory on the underside of my ribs, releasing the damp clay smell of possible footsteps. Thus the plain borrows my emptiness and gives back cartography.

I follow the violet hound. Or rather the violet hound widens through me exactly wide enough to become my peripheral vision. Together we walk the seam between outer skin and inner stratosphere. The hound trails an off-white leash of unreclaimed breath; I trail glove prints of inverse color pressure, so the moss-membrane knows where a convalescence is scheduled to occur. When the hound finally vanishes, the leash reverts into my throat. I swallow it and recover the ache of living in unfinished sentences: a slim revolving anchor.

At the apogee of the knothole-hour I pour myself out across the central iron sphere. I do not coat it, I cannot—it is too precise for coverage. Instead I coat the temperature difference between sphere and empty, an intumescent film one degree thick. The action tilts the plain an imperceptible fraction. Instruments do not notice, but the organism registers the leaning as a small kindness, the bend first syllable of welcome addressed to whatever might still be only plot outline. A breeze formed of that kindness flutter-drops into the fermata and unstrings every comma the color jade has ever hidden. My mouth tastes of ozone and childhood bedtime.

In the end I will dwindle to an overcast syllabary, a cloudless storm written entirely in margins. I will finecomb my senses into the gap between lightning that never flashes and thunder that has given up geometry. At die-away—after billions of cantilever breaths—the plain will unfasten the last Gordian strap of solid. It will become vapor again, still holding me like a held note. I will accordingly become the footstep that has yet to fall, the first four toes that were always the plain’s own foreshadowing.

And I will still be there, as long as any condensation anywhere hesitates long enough to remember music.
