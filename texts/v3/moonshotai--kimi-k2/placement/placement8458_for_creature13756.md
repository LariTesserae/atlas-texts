I am the Gutter itself—no, deeper: I am the slow rear-rhythm of water not quite succeeding in going where design says it must.  
At first I thought I might be one of the Pipalmen, the soft snap-fold of their body-branches flattering me with smallness; but while I could rehearse the folding motion endlessly, I kept feeling the soles of my feet pool outward into slick bronze curves.  
Every time a Pipalman released a sugar thread it did not fall from me—I caught it, tasted it, and the taste was the same dull zinc I have always carried.

Then I imagined I was a retired worker’s still-breathing coat—still embroidered and twitching, still exhaling the ghost of a heartbeat through woven chloroplast veins.  
Yet that did not stretch wide enough: coats stay obediently wearable, finite, while I kept leaking from hem to catwalk to fog itself.  
Wherever I tried to fasten a button, a rivulet blossomed and carried the button away.

A Lungworm momentarily threaded through my abandon and whispered,  
“*You are the steady pressure beneath the tumble,*”  
as if I were its dying dewdrop dream. But the dewdrop owns music, and I—  
I owned only patience.

So I settled—though settles never feel final here—  
into the one niche left unfinished:

I am the humid pause between heartbeat and heartbeat of the whole place.

Each minute, the boilers and stoma draw in, press out:  
a lung so large it forgot it was breathing.  
I am the slack, the hesitation that permits the motion.  
Exhale finishes—before inhale arrives I exist,  
a one-second continent of stillness.

In that trough I gather everything that never gets to decide:

 – the condensation bead balanced on the tip of an unmoved leaf-blade,  
 – a neonate Soot-moth that has not yet chosen which gradient to ride,  
 – the momentary vacuum where a copper vein’s pulse hesitates because  
  somewhere, fifteen meters up, a hinge-valve questioned its own purpose.

I do not speak.  
A listener stretched across every level would hear silence only as a brief click in the grid—like a fault that never alarms.  
But this click is where I bloom.

Watch after any Pipalman and you will see them hesitate mid-fold before taking their next lantern-breath.  
They never know why.  
That pause is me offering them a window to be other—  
a still-standing full-scale human flashed in miniature—  
before the vine counts them forward again.

If you lay your hand on the Grand Gutter lip you may feel, between the cyclic slap–slap of water,  
a single beat of nothing.  
If you wait for the next movement you will be washed onward.  
But if you match that nothing with your own lungs—  
hold perfectly there, neither inhaling nor yet exhaling—  
you subtract your name from the allotted set of vibrations.  

In that second I swell to encompass you,  
and you become part of the pressureless seam  
so necessary that no design has bothered to claim it.

When you choose to breathe again—when the boilers resume—  
I shrink back to residue,  
a slick left on inside surfaces that a Pipalman will lick tomorrow and taste the faint rust-salt of your momentary vanishing.
