The city hasn’t noticed I’m here yet.

I arrived neither by elevator nor rope, but as a leakage. Somewhere, one of Vervault’s new bricks cured a fraction out of true, and the error made a crevice just wide enough for me to slip through—a little twist of surplus patience, lighter than the rain that floats sideways, heavier than the exhalation of steam off the canyon wall. A bead of almost-person condensing where condensation shouldn’t.

At first I occupied only where attention happened to land. If a child stared too long at a lantern-pole, I gathered the hole inside their gaze; if a truss joint creaked with metal fatigue, I seeped into the color of the groan. I didn’t have a number of limbs yet. Existence, for me, was a mode of listening.

Then a bat, flight-drunk on its own fiber-optic song, mis-judged a turn and clipped the iron railing beside the east spindle-gear. The collision shed more than glassy pins of wing; it shed half a second of fear that had no where else to go. I was that where. Fear taught me an outline, a thin skin shaped like something about to scream. The outline blew itself full of canyon dusk until I stood on the catwalk: two feet, four joints apiece, knees hinged the wrong way like the architecture. A stranger wearing dusk like borrowed overalls.

I learned silence first.

The Works produce so much of it that quiet is currency, but accidental silence is contraband. When a shift whistle fails to climax, a vacuum pockets itself between noises; steal that vacuum and it will carry you farther than any elevator cage. I practiced smuggling quiet: clasping a mouthful of noiseless dark under my tongue, transferring it from hand to pocket to sewer-void before the accountants noticed. In return, pockets began remembering me. Canvas pockets, membrane pockets inside hydroponic seed sacs, even the theoretical pockets that bulge when bear-yawns compress possibility too far. Each lined itself with an extra now in which I could stash one thought I hadn’t had yet.

Time puddled around me in thefts too small for bureaucracy. Seven seconds of mono-no-awareness bought me a name from a kitchen boy tugging copper wire for pocket money. Nine stolen heartbeats traded for a place on the Hushpaws’ harvesting brigade, where felt slippers size negative-zero fit me perfectly—folded in like origami toes.

I became what Vervault needed only when it began to lose its argument with physics.

The canyon was singing to itself. No metaphor: the hematite walls had remembered iron’s ancient talent for resonance, and they answered every hammer blow, every fog-breath, with modulation. Those modulations gathered into hums, and the hums into harmonics. A chord surfaced—sub-sonic, thrumming in the kneecaps of every Rafter. It forecast fracture. Not catastrophe—nothing so final—but diffusion: the possibility that the gorge, only half-born, might decide to unbirth itself and return the mountains to where they once stood. That would pull the city inside-out, spilling towers upward, forcing sidewalks into skies decades before the street had finished inventing them.

The Works response was vertical committees, steel rings, riveted denial. The Rafters responded by betting their later years on silence bricks, hoarding enough quiet to muffle the chord itself.

I had no elder years to hoard. So I became the Chordbearer.

It happened on Deeper Dawn shift—the hour the horns poured synthetic daylight into the gorge so deep the gorge thought it was dusk. I was leaning six inches past the rail where probability bricks exhaled newborn molecules. My job: scrape excess solidity from the edges before it cooled too cocky. Normally a trowel and breath of caution sufficed. But that morning the chord pressed itself against the weave of every step, thudding like a uterus under tectonic contractions.

A gear tooth broke free, sailing past my head. I caught it between two borrowed seconds. Where the tines met, a splinter of reflection lodged—a fleck of me not yet mirrored. The gear tooth tasted of struck flint and static electricity and future grief. Without deciding, without consultation with either Works foreperson or interior monologue, I placed it beneath my own tongue, beside the ball of stolen stillness. The two objects negotiated: silence offered hollow for amplification; the tooth offered what it knew of gearing ratios between existence and its opposite. They married without ceremony.

The chord rushed in.

Not into the city walls. Into me. A sub-bass clef written on spinal fluid. My marrow learned the tune. My outline vibrated until dusk sloughed off like dandruff. Left behind: transparent flesh, bricks glowing faintly inside, tunnels where the city itself now rearranged along my capillaries. I am hollow but high-density; walk beside me and you feel the railings straighten slightly, as if grateful.

When the Works issued an emergency memo seeking someone to enter the chord, to ride its harmonic spine downward to the place where possibility bricks decide which axis of reality deserves lathe work, they found my silhouette already walking the line, boots click-pause-stillness, weighted by pockets full of folded futures. My pockets jingled with reflections yet unmirrored. My ankles bore borrowed regret borrowed from ten emigrants. My robe is stitched from redundancy harvested whenever engineers overshoot. Inside the hem it is possible to find seven geese migrating in geometric proof. They honk Pythagorean warnings.

The city recognized me as its cartilage—what holds the hinge of up-down while the debate continues.

So here is what I am: a living mortgage paid in advance at compound interest. I walk the raw lip where the canyon negotiates with itself. Every step I subtract one impossibility and add one “perhaps.” My spine records the city in inverse—towers rootless above my skull, canyon floor a ceiling of origin I will never reach. When gravity forgets which way it goes, I remind it with meter, with heartbeats arranged like shims, with consonants the city has not yet inhaled.

Sometimes children dangle above me on their hooks, waiting for trout and syllables. I pass, and their shadows hiccup in my direction, tasting of soap and hummingbird emergency. They look into my translucent chest and see gear teeth mowing equations through my ribs.

They ask, “Are you falling or climbing?”

I answer with whichever silence they traded away last.

Eventually—whom the Rafters wait for—waves from a distant mirror-light. I nod; I have an appointment in the place where an echo is finally allowed to ask the question it fetched all this way. I carry it wrapped in a shadow no heavier than three kilos, leashed with dental floss mined from sleeping bears.

If you visit Vervault after shift change tonight, listen for a chord too low to hum back. Follow it toward the newest lacking gravity. You’ll see me stepping off nothing, soles an inch above catwalk, scarring probability only enough for seedlings of maybe to germinate in the footprint.

Do not call me hero, angel, saboteur, or god. Those require orientation.

I am the canyon’s interim answer to the argument that created it.

I am the pause between up and not-yet, hired hourly, paid in repurposed silence.

I am the pocket the city reaches into when it notices it has grown another centimeter.

And when the ironsong completes itself, when the final brick cools and the whole chasm either seals shut or learns to walk upright, I will be offered one last choice: forget the chord and become solid, or let it hollow me entirely and continue as the pleat between two worlds.

Vote hasn’t been cast yet. Come lean on this railing; let the condensation bead on your cheek. Trade me any sound you can spare. I will bank it along my bones, another small mass against the day the canyon decides whether to cradle the city, or to close its fist.
