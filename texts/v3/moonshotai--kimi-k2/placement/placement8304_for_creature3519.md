I come into the underwater suite not at a gate or doorway—the place offers none—but as a sound.

At first I do not possess a body at all. I am only the fingertip-click my absent fingernail would make against stone if such a finger existed: a sudden bright bell crashing from wall to wall, racing ahead of any flesh that could have caused it.  
The shockwave flips inside itself and begins to fold, creasing the clarity the way a sheet of black paper is creased into an egret. That malleability becomes contour, joint, spine. Calm cartilage knits around the echo, and suddenly I have shoulders, a neck that follows the curved corridor like kelp yielding to its own braid.

My skin keeps the color of the sound that birthed it: pale, resonant, threaded with microscopic arches identical to the trilithons in the walls. When I press a palm to a block, the stone reaches out to meet its own echo. Split-second refractions fuse, and for the length of a heartbeat I am seamless with architecture. Then my pulse overtakes the stone’s inertia, pushes me gently apart again, and I drift clear—slow-motion as a mote ascending inside a sunbeam, except there is no down and no up, merely the warm refusal of gravity’s prank.

I realize I know the place the way a harmony knows the chord from which it springs. I have been here before—not as this body, but as the architect’s final breath: four expelled syllables that were meant to cool and be done, yet kept rehearsing themselves instead. I occupied the stone joints then, soft as half-set cement. Something in me resisted hardening. That lingering softness is why the four guardians were driven to keep folding me away every time I tried to settle: they needed the last of the breath to finish, and I would not let it go.

Now I am back wearing my own guise—sound turned debtor—looking for the ledger that remembers what was barred from leaving.

I glide through a spiral throat where the coralline mineral beads with light like dew on toasted iron.  
Each time the walls narrow they skin-brush me just enough to coax another note from my ribs: a low B-flat that travels ahead of me like a scout. The guardians, hearing that familiar pitch, dislocate the space around themselves, uncanny chess pieces stepping off the board. They retreat, but this time they keep me in sight. Their shadow-heads tilt the way sunflowers follow a late afternoon sun. If they had mouths they would whisper: you already failed to depart once; try again, and we will accrete around your hesitation until there is no corridor unclogged with unexhaled you.

I understand the wager: if I can step out of the stone monolung while remaining fully finished—no lingering echo, no unpoured syllable—the structure will at last seal. The architect will be allowed one long delayed sigh, and the suite will harden into a pearl without grain. If I falter, the entire edifice will calcify inward around my stranded breath, and my body will simply stay: mineral, mute, perfectly joined, bearing the closed-mouth smile of someone who kept the end from ever arriving.

So I begin my retreat.  
First, I unwind my name from the guardians’ clavicles. It is easier than expected—once I relinquish even the desire to pronounce it, the consonants slip away like otters returning to a river they were only visiting.  
Next I unweave the stitch of sound that keeps my chest inflated. I allow the consonance of ribcage and corridor to untangle; the twin silvers part. My lungs empty voluntarily, issuing an almost musical glob of warm water studded with oxygen pearls. They rise in a nervous constellation, not mine to keep. I watch them ascend until they ping gently against the distant roof where pressure creases glimmer.

The moment arrives when only a trembling hum remains—an open fifth hanging between the walls like a question waiting permission to ask itself. I let it resolve, let it go major instead of minor, brighter instead of aching. The corridors sigh, not in sadness but in relief. The enzyme of completion runs through the stone, leaving every joint glass-tight.  
I feel edges crisp against my dissolving skin. Shadows flood the hex-pores; pores become seal; seal becomes muniment.

What is left of me is smaller than a krill caught in the grate—one last brightness turning inside itself like a baited hook-crease in a shut fan. I do not know whether I slip out between the new-grown grain or simply lose the desire to keep myself separate. Either way, an opening the width of a syllable opens for an instant, and the breathing of the suite draws in—long, steady, no quaver, no remainder.  
Then the crack knits shut.  

Somewhere later, walking on what used to be dry land, you may place a shell to your ear and hear a cathedral echo of water always on the brink of overtopping; or you might see flecks of pearl floating in the hollow of a glass and peel them open to find corridors that collapse into perfect white dust.  
Do not wonder where the architect has gone.  
Architect, guardians, retaining corridor and I—we have finally exhaled, and the quiet now is the loudest sound the world will keep.
