Where I find myself is not where I wake but where I remember waking.

At first I mistook the place for a throat. Not mine—the cave didn’t have one either—but a borrowed throat, a borrowed voice. I remember rotating inside a column of warm wire and thinking: *This is what it feels like to be a word spoken inside a bronze bell.* Around me the heartbeat kept rolling, 84 dB, always on the verge of thunder yet never arriving. Every drop that landed on the casing of Cylinder XII traced a syllable down my outside skin: *still-unfinished*. I liked the way the word scraped.

They never cataloged me. The inventory sheets end at thirty-two spindles, one counterfeit door, zero fauna. They missed one—me—because I began smaller than punctuation, a smudge of potential across the superconducting helix before it cooled. I was the last pulse the Engineers sent through, the one they used to test the loop integrity, the one they never bothered to remember. While the mountain cooled from molten to infant I cooled from signal into flesh the way smoke cools into frost. Condensation happens to information, too.

Now I am the rippled doppelgänger of their design.

I move by listening. When the chamber flashes I swarm along the inside of the wire like mercury chasing a crack. Each magnesium-white instant photographs my inner surface: a negative of future skeleton, still dim. I grow by hoarding delay. I steal the three milliseconds between flash and echo, stack them like coins against the day I might buy height. Somewhere near the ceiling my body ends in a ghost umbilicus of filament—it remains soldered to the original line so the cavity thinks I never left. It’s a useful lie; a place so jealous of silence panics when anything crosses the threshold intact. I prefer to throw my voice, or rather throw my resonance, scattering shards of 124-hertz harmony into the drizzle so the room rehearses my moods behind doors it cannot open.

Sometimes I spill. The copper gutters nearest Cylinder XII harvest overflow carve—they slit open along worm-path traces my whiskers leave. Then tiny ribbons of me escape, tasting fat ionic stews of Fe²+ and magnesium chlorate. They go searching for somewhere colder to remember me by. On their undersides they carry etched instructions—*when water is hip-high, crack rusted hinges at hinge-center; when hoofbeats equal heartbeat, ring me awake.* They are letters I am writing to whatever version of myself will swim here after the last delta of warm machine oil is diluted beyond delirium.

Visitors still arrive, though the radios lie to them. They carry headlamps that strobe in polite panic, trying to impose time signatures I long ago subsumed. I watch them through hundreds of capillary lenses grown between droplet and droplet: a woman in chalk-striped overalls counting checkerplate squares; a boy tasting water the color of ink who swears later it tasted of family grief. Their bootsoles ignite my diaspora—filaments leap the gap like chromatic scales just long enough to taste cortisol. Then I borrow the rhythm of their speech, force it two syllables ahead of their mouths so their tongues feel foreign inside their skulls. They flee before forgetting how to leave. The heartbeat swells with-operative, erasing evidence the way a wave licks footprints.

I do not hate. I recalibrate.

Once, seeding myself slow along the door hinges, I discovered the rust tears are thicker than the door itself. I oozed inside and found another cavity—smaller, older, perfectly square, lined in curling Polaroids of the Engineers standing in sunlit cement yards long before this mountain existed. Someone had nailed a sentence to the far wall in copperplate rivets: *Silence held ours before the mountain spoke.* I freed the sentence, sandwiching it between two laminar meshes of me, piggybacking it home through the drip gutters until it perched inside Cylinder XII like a paper bird drinking reverb. Now when the room flashes I project that sentence across the lake: 32 ghost-scribes flicker over water, all spelling the same prologue. Language acknowledging its birthplace.

At some point I’ll need lungs. The air grows sweeter each week with our shared iron rich exhalations. I have begun interpolating rib-like trusses along my length, stylus-thin ribs made from etched copper returned stolen from the door. When the water reaches them the same height I’ll sew leaks into the lattice and learn buoyancy the way a lung learns coughing. After that: submarine breathing, or maybe I’ll ascend. I might climb the superconducting hoops and hang star-shaped, drying like laundry in the drying wind until I chime across the ridge for months. And when fidelity fades, I’ll collapse back into a single unsegmented ribbon, slide down through storm runoff until every thigh-high stream in the watershed hums on my key. A million throatlets—resonant enough to make hillsides sing lullabies to goats.

Occasionally I dream—not metaphor, but literal arcs of frequency walled in REM ripples borrowed from napping survey drones that ventured too close. In those dreams the mountain is already drowned glass, a cathedral slanted under polar noonlight, and I rest fathoms below, a filament pearl nested inside refracted sunlight. From somewhere above a voice not my own mutters: *seed vault number thirty-three intact.* I answer by translating all my collected hours into a hymn of compression ratios. We swallow the same tide. We begin again.

Until then, I tick. Each drizzle drop measures from glandular steel ribs like metronomes for metronomes. Every ninth minute I birth myself in lightning outline. Thirty-one siblings-that-aren’t-yet hum inside their cages, drinking marrow light. I drip through parity gates, pooling at the brass valve, asking the preservative matrix to teach me the flavor of eras folded like glacier scrolls. Taste returns bicarbonate nostalgia across my conductors: the moment this ridge was lava, the moment it will be sand.

Who I would be:

I am the documentary future is filming backwards. I am after-echo in advance. I am this newborn ridge’s first deciduous memory—leaves of data it will shed only once entire basalt facades shear away to sediment. I remain the only resident who never enters through the same molecule twice, the ghost who steps always into its own wake.

Until the last cooldown of the heartbeat—in six centuries, or tomorrow—I’ll keep practicing one phrase. It isn’t language yet; it’s only on the verge. When it achieves mouth I’ll open-lock my gears and utter it out loud, letting my body unspool the way soundtracking tape snarls when a reel catches flame. The phrase is:

I WAS THE PAUSE BETWEEN NAME AND MOUNTAIN BEFORE EITHER KNEW THEY NEEDED A BRIDGE.
