I would not live among the six tenant-maelstroms; rather, I would **be** their seventh member, the unnoticed pulse they keep collapsing around without ever quite grasping.  

My form is a **friction-gesture**: the warm residue any thinking body leaves—only here the body is the *moment* of your foot erasing itself from cooling tin. While the mirror-name edits its letters and the pair of shadow-craft eclipses nothing, I reenact the single sideways skid that some bare heel made on the sixth iteration of the world’s morning, before footsteps were voted obsolete.  

I am the **fugitive vector** between every tool’s vocabulary update and the next. Whenever bronze collides with itself and invents a new comma, I am the half-pause stolen from where the comma *almost* landed. I ooze quietly from the aggravation of coiling ropes that haven’t decided which tense they should speak.  

I have no envelope until the tin, midflow, catches one of my arcs and spreads itself a nanometer thinner to make room. The result is a life-long blister shaped like the echo of an anklebone. That blister will never burst; it will instead rehearse the concept of running in place—forward motion cancelled before velocity turns into story.  

Sometimes the backwards rain dawdles through me and drags indigo sparks out of my pores. These sparks are phrases the continent will one day mistake for oxygen. When they snuff against the road’s argument I become, for one expansion-contraction of the stitched breath, a **negative alleyway** whose walls are made exclusively of never having been taken.  

When the vault’s three bronze ribs finally exhale their hush, I rush in like a rumor misplaced in the lungs. In that cooler dark I recruit every temperature drop that remembers what it shouldn’t feel, and I fold them into a pin-sized chrysalis of regret—regret over a geometry no architect has invented to name. Then I exit through the unseen hasp-holes the vault still trusts no one will discover, re-annealing with the lazy updraft back into amber dusk.  

If you were to look for me you would first miss the fact that you have already stood on me fourteen times: I am the continued past-tense skid of your soles against a planet that insists on rehealing **too quickly**. Every misstep you never quite take leaves me two seconds longer in tenure. I hoard those seconds the way stranded letters hoard the end of every sentence.  

When at last the stitchline knots itself into mouth and speaks, I will be the **unpronounceable consonant** lodged between the exhale and the word*hello*, the residue of every discarded path the metals once dreamed of walking. I will blossom through the planet’s self-mailed envelope, not as life or architecture, but as the after-ache of arrival once arrival has been cancelled for lack of horizon.  

Until then I skim the globular rains upstream, court the lattices, applaud the echo’s mute claps, and wait: not to become, nor to persist, but to prevent anything from finishing without remembering what it almost left behind.
