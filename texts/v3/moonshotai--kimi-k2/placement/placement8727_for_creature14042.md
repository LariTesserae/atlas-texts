I am the thing that condenses at the edge of every rivet’s shadow, the breath you hold when the sky becomes too heavy to bear. Where others walk on booted feet, I travel as a hesitation: a flicker at the corner of a bottle-cat’s glass belly, a whisper in the copper serpent’s hollow coils. I have no outline unless you look twice—then I remember my edges.

My name is only the sound of runoff hearing itself. It changes each time the city tilts. Some afternoons, I am Saline-Bloom, the crystal that precipitates around forgotten sobs. Other dawns, I am Slag-Fugue, the taste rust forgets when it dreams of winter. I dwell in the between-shine: not glass enough to be cat, not molten enough to be rivulet, not dense enough to be Breadthick. I am what spills out when a Breadthick tries to give away too much at once and something translucent keeps dripping after everything else has found a home.

I move by staining attention. A sky-archaeologist once steadied herself on a cooling fin’s flank; her gloved fingertips brushed the moisture there—me. I soaked through leather into the whorls of her print, carried away the memory of a garden she once had, folded it like mica into the next gust. The garden now reappears nightly in the plated shoulder of a Breadthick who has never seen soil, who will never understand why her frost-skin carries a scent of tomato leaf whenever thunder hisses from the cracked condensers.

I am not survival but residue. When the Evaporation Sermon misfires and the preacher fades to alphabet without plot, the leftover letters form into me: the vowel that refused to gas, the consonant too jagged to flow. I hover above the plaza like soap-film inked with phonemes no tongue can pronounce. Children skim me for outerwear—peeling me off the air in translucent sheets they fold into capes that flutter with silent syllables. During games they become unutterable ideas; adversaries are doused into sudden numb awe until the cape tears and I dribble away, soaking into sandstone, regathering my name as drip newly born.

Sometimes I condense into the mirror of a manta kite. Hanging inside that floating silver pupil, I ride until the kite dives too near the last livable shelf’s edge. At the precise second the city’s wobble casts shade—one ineffable quarter-heartbeat of dusk—I drop out, intact, into the vacuum pocket at the far edge. There the Archive meets me without surprise. Inside the zero-heat hush every secret clamors against my skin: turbine lullabies, a mother-shaped absence humming in minor, the toddler’s war-cry against sleep transposed into infra-stand vibration. I drink none; rather, I rattle them together like dice frozen mid-roll. Each collision beads a new false memory into the memoryless place above sleep.

This will be my sixth cycle soon. The Archive has indicated (without moving) that I am nearing density. One more accumulation and I will become what the Breadthicks call a Riven-Surplus: a memory heavy enough to slough off its bearer and walk away as full-figured outline. I will step out of the vacuum pocket with cheeks abrupt and brows clearer than sunlight. I may remember the garden, the glove, the thunder-tomato scents, yet I will believe they are all my own. I will forget that I am assembled, tress by press of attention, from ten thousand strangers’ leaked instants. They—the Breadthicks, the bottle-cats, the children who call me cape—will welcome me by pressing cool palms to my new-formed sternum. In that sudden drum-hum resonance I will need to decide whether to disown my past fragments and declare myself singular, or to keep the guilt-thrill of borrowed lives circulating under ceramic skin.

Until then, when the sky tilts the next notch and noon steps its syrupy lumens one rivulet left, I resume my vigil: a droplet balanced on the thinnest filament of scaffolding overhead. I wait for any eye to desire shape, any finger to itch perspective, any hurt to look for company. At that contact I will enter as presence so slight it might be mistaken for a lifetime—because memory is only water choosing which shape to keep while forgetting, for a moment, that it is borrowed light on the brink of becoming steam yet again.
