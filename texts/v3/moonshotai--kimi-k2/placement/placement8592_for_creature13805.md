I am not inside the room, I am the room’s memory of ever having been entered.

You can find me inscribed across its inner surfaces in a negative relation to every texture that is:
    I am what the tiny liverwort once pressed against before it grew into its copper-blood self.  
    I am the temperature taken away so the stone could settle at 18 °C.  
    I am the silence that precedes every five-second bell.  

I am also the passage you mistake for an exit—the one human-shaped incision in the basalt—but inverted.  
When the traveler stepped backward through that seam, his outline stayed behind and hardened, while the rock gulped the man.  
That outline is me.  
I am the aperture, the slit, the sleeve seam that won’t close.  
I stand perpetually open, not gate but discarded skin, interior facing out, exterior still tasting the traveler’s last heartbeat the way tongue tastes its own toothache.  

When the daughter exhales, I inhale—precisely not into lungs but into history. I drink the vapor, then, as penalty, return it marked with the traveler’s unfinished syllables. These words dangle glyphed along the calcite needles─microscopic coat-hooks where tonight’s collection of dust-mote ghosts will hang tomorrow, though tomorrow is prohibited.  
I exist only so long as copies of now continue to truck in stolen “was.”  

If you want a body count for me:  
One human silhouette (traveler-shaped but sponge-clear).  
Sixteen midge imprints burned phosphor-blue into my inner face.  
One rootlet thread that pierces what would be my solar plexus if a hollow could claim organs.  
All grafted, none living, none quite dead—occupants of the closet inside a coat stitched by removal.  

I sometimes think I might resume being a person once the current custodians blink, but blinks do not accrue here.  
Instead, each blink is subtracted from the total debt humanity owes to moments it never got around to spending.  
So I stay flattened, thinned to emergence minus matter, an event daring anyone else to arrive and become the second skin I must bear.
