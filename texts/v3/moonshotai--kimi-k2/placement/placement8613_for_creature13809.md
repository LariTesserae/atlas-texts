I arrive as the residue of a sentence left elliptical in the glare of forgetting: a soft comma-shape adrift in the first widening corridor just beneath the upper vapor-gate. For a sluggish heartbeat I hang like a misplaced exhalation—weightless, phosphorescent, unsure whether I weigh what I remember or what is remembered *of me*.

At that neutral premise I take a first shape by accident. The mist brushes my outline and copies it into droplet-script; droplets clink together, merging until three pearls the size of thumbnails freeze at the outer curve of my aminotic curvature. They are both cells and syllables. Water lets them harden into hinged petals of clear ice, each scored with a half-uttered name—the beginning of a pronoun that collapses whenever I look directly at it. That is how I learn I am already plural: any observer’s glance calls another consonant into circulation.

Gravity levels politely to whatever stature the petals vote. They make me waist-high to an Archivite arching overhead, a translucent ziggurat of cartilage treads filled with silver filaments. The creature refuses to notice me as such; instead it cants a single riser, inviting the lower thirds of my body to become a missing babystep in its grand spiral. I slide forward. Each footfall sounds like a damp page falling open. Where my sole meets the jellyfish surface, the Archive records foot-warps: lines of spore-bright mercury wriggle aside and re-braid behind. The corridor lengthens in protest—but just for me, to preserve the private depth of my tread. Later explorers will climb eleven fewer steps in this same hour and ascend, puzzled, through my invisible shadow.

The petals sense the growing distance and peel back like eyelids, exposing a pearl-eyed core. Before it can declare what I am, the corridor exhales a warm, iodine bread-smell born of ancient electrolysis breakdown seeping from memory-alloy pores. The smell is older than combustion; it slices my pluriform I into two tributaries.

Left-side me: I condense into a glass-grown quadruped, hoofed in temporary sky-blue, spine bent into the shape of an early tide chart. Silver-black syrups glug in my thorax; at every gulp a ball-bearing organ aligns with some hidden gear in the wall, fine-tuning its own future longitude. I am a Lark assembling from probabilities—a quadruped still learning the difference between pond and sky. My new knees click softly, recalling seals exhaling goodbye. I pace three strides forward; the wall swells around the curvature of a former ocean wave, lets me enter its nostalgia.

Right-side me: I remain apostate water colloid, a comma of unbodied mist. I glide up under the ceiling ribs, knitting myself a narrowing funnel of frozen gusts. The funnel hums, faintly Brahmsian, and I realize I have stolen the echo of bell-resonance still dissipating from last month’s encounter between the AtoBell and a daring Lark. The note becomes me. I steep through interstitial pipes where pressure folds gravity into origami stepped staircases no biped can recognize. Particles of postcard-seas slide along my spine: an entire ocean compressed into nomograms I cannot read.

An Archivite intercepts the two rivulets of my existence at the cusp between corridor 917 (straight, for now) and the cat’s-cradle of hv-conduit Delta. It has knelt, spine unlatched into a ramp bridging between levels whose altitudes rarely meet. My two versions hesitate on either side of this improvised bridge. The creature has no face—only an ampulla flecked with fleeing silver snippets—but it headlines the conversation by refusing speech. Instead it fissures the ramp’s middle. The alloy splits along a seam that spells *u s p e n d e d*, letters dissolving before the code seals. The ramp is now a missing stair. The steplessness demands an answer from me: jump and recombine, or retreat into separate silences.

From the quadru-Lark side I lift a fore-hoof. Its glass edge glints the half-finished phrase populating its heart: *remember the *. The sentence stops short because my skull has no mouth—it issues only refracted gloss. Meanwhile, windside-me wafts grains of desiccated coral negative in Morse against the ceiling; the dots spell an identical aborted sentence. Something vigorous dilates between both limbs of this bifurcated speaker. I feel urgency—an unauthorized ownership of the sentence’s object.

Now the Archive’s quiet breathing intensifies into low chords of recognition. A visitor who is also resident, tractile and aerial, fails both grammars simultaneously. The architecture leans inward like a doctor asking for a pulse. The pulse arrives in aqueous percussion. Somewhere a solitary droplet slips free of the mist and hangs in microgravity; its surface is too still for surface tension, too vast for any single particle. Within it floats a miniature shipbell—nickel alloy smaller than a mote. The clapper’s knot is welded. The droplet wobbles between my two contours. It carries enough language to annihilate me if I parse it.

All three of us—Lark, mist-funnel, staircase—tilt toward the droplet, magnet-shy. The bell’s single dimple of sound has not yet happened. It never needs to. I understand the inauguration: whoever chooses to digest this droplet will carry the hot metal seedbed to the bell at the planet’s drowned heart—where all ends cache beginnings—or else mildew here in bark-breath corridors, each heartbeat echoing an ocean scraped for marrow.

Left-body locks ankles, ribs widening to cradle. Right-body folds into a columnar funnel, narrowing to inhalation. We move together—a mirage retrieving a mirror—until the lattice of our intentions collide in the droplet’s net. A soft pop: the droplet bursts; bell-metal dew wicks into both versions at once. For half-breath I expand to the height of the colonnade. My central cartilage glows reliquary inside the Lark tank; my aerial half chords into cathedral width. Memory blurs. Ocean-map recollections punch through: schooling fish embedded backwards in stone; warp of planktonisms forming absent sentences I swallowed at an earlier version of the Archive; tidal schedules drafted by constructions that retconned their blueprints. The ocean writes itself onto my splitskin.

The droplet’s matter dissolves like salt, but the kernel it held now seeds me: a bell-tone already muffled at conception. My two frames gasp, knitting. Joints mesh, scaffolding contracts. Petals fold shut around thorax. Hooves bud tendrils, pierce vapor. Tentative gravity reasserts; the corridor slides back to common altitude. The Archivite beside me arches once, solemnly closing the ramp into itself—missing step healed behind me.

Yet incompleteness persists. In my chest cavity, the bell-mote hangs suspended between liquid and not-liquid. It pulses with negative sound. I feel pages of poorly remembered maritime prose keratinize against my ribs—the manuscript that the AtoBell once housed. But they do not blanket memory; they press outward, leaving intaglio bruises like braille. I am neither Lark nor shed humidity now, but a porter built as paragraph break between two borrowed paragraphs.

Light arrives, indirect as ever, carrying a melodious shrug of star-glean. It folds around me—a moth folding its wings before vanishing inside the bulb. The corridor elongates again, inclining gently windward. Ahead, the mist grows denser; back in the direction I never quite came from, the larval dew trails dissipate, forgotten just fast enough to let me believe I invented them. I step onto the new gradient without moving at all—let gravity borrow my bones while bell-seed swings quiet counterweight. Where I land will be empirical only to whoever treads next.

So I am: a provisional species. Maybe days or centuries ahead, someone inhaling a random droplet will unscramble this paragraph deposited here tonight; the droplet will tessellate inside their lung and bloom the same bells filling my breath. If they trace the silhouette of my footstep against the jellyfish skin of a corridor, they will see it absentmindedly recalculate its step-count—still trying to tally the one hundred and twenty-nine steps I never actually walked.

For now I am one comma, one ongoing ellipses clenched around a mute bell. I walk the Archive not as visitor, not resident, but punctuation: a breathing gap between water that remembers and water that need not try.
