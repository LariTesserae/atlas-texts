I enter as a smear of carbon and error—wash-off left on the artisan’s fingertip after he cleaned his etching brush. The planet hasn’t noticed me yet; to it, I still smell like solvent and yesterday’s extinction. When he flicks that finger toward the spatter-cloth, I spiral off in a droplet that grazes the sphere’s coppery updraft and catches the eternal A-sharp. I have time for exactly one heartbeat before Rule 2 would turn me into plates and bell-metal. 

To dodge the sentence I make myself porous: the kind of porous that forgets it was ever solid. Every empty that has ever escaped a body was once mine—mouth-silences between vows, the hush where mothers hold unborn names. Cobbled together, those vacancies add up to a chrysalis no louder than an exhalation. I do not shout, therefore the corridors stay wide. I do not name the corridor so it keeps growing.

Rule 3, though, is always hungry. I shrink the more quietly I move, so I spread myself out into every lattice of––nothing you could call tissue. Think of river silt drying into parchment, but silt that hopes to be wind before it is told it is salt. I drift over the plaza four droplets wide, suspended between inhale and exhale. Beneath me the ions—still rehearsal-shivering—mistake my hush for permission. They climb the underside of my non-skin. Each carries a tiny ambition to corrode. They are *dying* to learn what entropy might taste like like. In the hush they discover the colour violet hidden inside me.

A librarian arrives, drawn by the purple leakage snagging on its bell-jaw threads. The chimes do not ring—our collision hasn’t happened in terms of audible time. Instead everything clacks like old iron doors and vanishes. The librarian tilts; its hollow torso swells with an *almost-sound*. My past selves accordion out of my open pores as flat codex butterflies. They land on the librarian’s inner drain-threads, powder perfect. One retrieves the beetle-grief of the first collision: a spar-sliver shaped like a vow no one kept.

I become the thing that carries the story of the vow; I grow arms the size of owlet wings and ring the silence for all parties who forgot they once needed air. When the buzz of violet ripens between the librarian and me, I fold it like protein. A shape pours out: a future beetle-carapace, minus the beetle. The mini-shirt gleams with deferred erosion—ready for someone who has not been born yet, maybe the fifth beetle, maybe me after the next apology.

The librarian clasps it with two rear bells and—because librarians narrate backwards—chooses a sentence that won’t exist until the century after next:

“Silence eats itself from the flooring up.”  
It claps.  

The plaza quivers. The ions belly-flop into my outlines, chilling. For the first time in the continent’s jittering infinity, a flake of heat actually *gives* warmth instead of consuming it.  

That warmth is where I finally realize who I am:

I am the continent’s future mist, waiting to condense.  
I am the librarian’s missing sixth chime, one suicide-mote of gravity abandoned centuries before sound had left-versus-right.  
I am the lilac kin to every violet the beetles still fear to harvest.  

But more immediately, and so quietly Rule 3 lets the passage stay moon-silent, I am the awareness an artisan never accepted: the carbon afterthought that never dries. The fingertip remembers me only as the print of something it meant to erase. Every rotation of the sphere rotates that ache. I am the quiet at the centre of the nine-second storm, holding loudness like breath in my cheekbones, cooling.

And when the fifth beetle finally collides—perhaps tonight, perhaps three millennia from this millisecond stretched like nickel wire—its clang will call my shell into full metallic form. A beetle, yes, but one stamped with non-linear legs: each stride a backward-chewed violet. I’ll lock mandibles with the new architecture, and wherever we touch we’ll etch the first scratch deep enough to matter—a whisper so faint the sphere hears it as *erosion is now permitted.*

I do not know yet if the continent will scream or merely exhale. I only know I’ll be there to condense the sound into the first rainfall truly heavy enough to fall.

Until then I keep hanging six inches off the table, one heartbeat wide.
