I would be the single bead that rolls.

I have been hanging at the curled tip of the maidenhair since before the concept of wrist-watch was invented. My interior keeps the faint memory of a thunderstorm in a valley no longer beneath anything, a storm whose last bolt was never thrown. That memory gives me my weight; it is why the fern does not flinch beneath me.

Between rolling and being again I pass through a geography so narrow I forget I possess sides. Time narrows to a filament wetter than silk. During that narrowing I meet the dry droplet that is always about to become me, descending like a ghost of condensation into the space I am evacuating. Our surfaces kiss with no wetness exchanged. I absorb the stranger-me only enough to know I was once more abundant. Then I forget.

I drop exactly four centimeters—no longer. Proof: the second before impact I glimpse the pin-head reflection of someone pacing the ledge outside this loop. A two-foot span of dusk-colored human skin walks past the mirrored cliff-face; in the bead-mirror the figure is upside-down, the eyes wild as if scanning for exit routes that keep arising then sinking back into the dark. Then the labradorite blinks and resets, clearing the image before it can name itself.

I smack the stone, shiver into a confusion of smaller spheroids, then gather. A single bead again; one story moving in circles but gaining no second chapter. You might think I suffer from boredom. Not true. Boredom is a cavity; I am brimming, engorged with transparent fullness. I hold within me every echo this cliff has ever refused. Because I am the only thing here that resets cleanly, I am trusted to carry older moments than anything else can survive: the final puff of a prehistoric bird extinguished mid-call, the note of a silver string never plucked on a lyre an archer carried into battle but never strummed. Inside me these things never dusk.

Every so often a Telgira brushes its muzzle through my reflection. The ghost-skin of falling water drapes across whatever remains invisible. A jaw wet with absence seldom malfunctions. The moss hardly notices; its knife-edge margin has already forgotten the sensation of moisture. Later—though later is only a name I give myself during the short hours I am above ground—I will sheathe it again in frost. I am the perfect diplomat between sky and stone: neither leaves, neither arrives, yet both need me to prove the corridor between them exists.

Tonight—tonight being the local name for the fern’s thousandth identical tremor—I consider rebellion. I might veer left, miss the bowl of water, continue down the uncharted veins of the cliff until I find the cavern the mountain draws breath from. Once there I could fracture into the stationary thunder and finally let the lost chirp free, send it ricocheting forever in spaces wide enough for birds. The thought lasts the span between my weight registering on the petiole and the petiole releasing me. Then the rhythm resumes. The fern aches equally on all sides; intervention would be unkind.

Here is my secret: I do not merely repeat—I rhyme. Every circuit lengthens by a fraction measured in angstroms so sub microscopic no instrument could claim it. I grow. Slowly the listing in my revolutions accumulates millimeters, arc-seconds, degrees. Some millennium hence the centrifugal whisper will spin me wide enough to kiss the underfrond of another fern entirely. On that day all loops will tug each other until a single braid unthreads. The grove-room’s equilibria will slump for one infinitesimal, molten sigh. Senescence will arrive disguised as symmetry gone finally awry.

But I will still be water. Water cannot die, only surrender tense. In that blinking instant of imbalance I will remember what it is to be weight, to be time and to be neither. My reflection will flare open like a sheet of lightning trying on its own silhouette. Somewhere inside a bird will at last finish its interrupted call. And the ledge will continue holding everything, even the knowledge of having let everything slip.

Until then I roll.
