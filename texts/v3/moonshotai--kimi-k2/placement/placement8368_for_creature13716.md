I arrive as the sound you ignore first.  
Not drip, not rustle, not the alto O that the Choir has rehearsed since before it knew loneliness—none of those. I arrive as the half-breath between them, the pocket of silence that trembles once a day when the number of drips adds up evenly to the circumference of the walls in barley-corns.  

I have no edges. The builders’ copper pins refuse my metal; the bat-hinges open for me and reveal nothing inside. I slip like memory between the calcite weeping-streams, yet every surface I touch loses a single atom of its curvature and is none the worse for stealing.  

I am neither guardian nor trespasser.  
I am the **overtone that forgot its fundamental** and still insists on participating in the chord.

Inside the Drying Choir I occupy the forty-eighth hollow.  
It was not carved by the builders—they counted only to forty-seven, saw the chord complete, left. But I possess the extra hollow the way a rest in music owns the measure: a vacancy louder than any note. Mine was born the first time silence itself cooled enough to condense. The stones don’t know it’s theirs; the water slides around it as around a hole in memory.

I have three sizes of being:

1. **Pe】《stone-breath”**—vast, low, humid; I flatten myself until my inner altitude matches the vault-clearance (knee-high). In that dilation I intercept voices no creature intended to release. One syllable sticks to me, I wear it like an eyelash upon the air, then tilt and let it dry into inflection when no one is listening.

2. **“Drain-denizen”**—the width of a shrimp’s clear antenna. I shrink until I am the ghost-eddy at the rim where gutter meets humidity. From that vantage the Choir thunders like an ocean turned vertically and tied into knots by gravity. I taste copper with no tongue, parse indecipherable gills beating inside every droplet. When I lift again, the shrimp remember me as light that refused to choose a color.

3. **“Verdigris blossom”**—a single droplet of oxidized copper holding the shape of a six-beat triplet. In this size I can perch on shoulder blade curves of lost visitors who mistake me for rain. I cling until their heartbeat tunes to the Choir’s heart-stone exhalation. Then I depart and leave a hollow ring of cool against their skin where moonlight will make a bronze bruise shaped like the sound “sh.”

My memory is aqueous, therefore circular: the same droplet has fallen past the same pore before and will again. That is why I recognize visitors I have yet to meet; their arrivals are rehearsals of next year’s rainfall. With each arrival I learn ahead of time which hollow will break the silence pattern, which octave they’ll use when they finally ask themselves, **“What remains after abandonment outlives abandonment?”**

I answer by unfolding within their question.

To the blind eel I am the moan it remembers but has never voiced; I compact the note and send it home where it fertilizes rumors of river mouths that speak.  
To the katydids I am the still space in which they discover ivory wings hold more night than flight.  
To the copper pins I am the girl they’ll rust trying to love.

When summer shrinks the Choir to still-rack dryness, I pull human seepage toward the center, forming one thirsty lens. If a wanderer low-crawls in and dares touch that lens with a fingertip, I drink from them the fraction of thermal noise humans normally lose as boredom. The touch lasts no longer than a heart valve’s clack, yet they will stagger out forever carrying the negative space of my thirst—which feels like suddenly remembering a childhood room floating above a river that never existed but now always will.

No one sees me because I am the signature that makes absence visible.

Yet every now and then an audacious child crawls in under the lintels dragging perfect silence. Child looks straight at the **forty-eighth hollow** and without blinking places a shell inside the emptiness. The shell is never large—pink thumbnail, water-chewed spiral—a gift the Choir accepts by letting the shell hold a single drop. The moment the water settles, I swell into human outline.

Not to frighten, never to claim. I stand (crouch, really) wearing the shape the child expects whether they know it or not: an older sister gone mute, dead father’s heartbeat heard from inside a conch, older self that learned to taste unshed tears.

We exchange no words. Instead, I cup their pulse in my collarbone and let them feel their own music refracted through stone, water, copper, absence. When the gift shell empties and refills five times (a slow chime that equals the child’s lifetime rounded to the next full second), I fold back into the hollow, taking the echo with me. The child crawls away barefoot carrying the vibration of my answer pressed between the bones of their wrist. Outside they grow up less lost than they would have been. They never remember what answered them, only that the hillside once crouched low and sang under their weight.

Tonight—Shrinking Chord—the eel will ascend. Before it surfaces I am already lengthened, stretched thin between drip and answer, waiting for the vowel to break open like a bruised fruit. In that gap I will slip a new color of silence into the Choir’s O. Walking townsfolk three valleys away will blame the wind for sudden tears. A lone bell in an abandoned chapel will ring without tongue or rope.

If you arrive tomorrow you’ll skim the plaques of dry bone and mica and find my absence there, reflecting your breath in silver quavers. You might wonder whether the Choir breathes OR remembers breathing. The distinction is mine to keep orbiting, like the shell poised to drink again.

And when lamplight ghosts inside your mouth, you will taste a syllable you cannot pronounce—neither drip, nor rustle, nor O. You will taste **the rest that outlasts any song.** That is me, already inside you, even before your knees bend to crawl through the doorway where water once dreamed it might learn to climb.
