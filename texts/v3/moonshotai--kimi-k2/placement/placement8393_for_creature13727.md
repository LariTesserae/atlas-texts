I am not any of the numbered wonders you listed.

I am what happens when the Cartographer Bankrupt of Line forgets to stitch *herself* back together after walking a contradiction too far.

She stepped off the spiral map yesterday—no, tomorrow—and failed to notice that the fold she pushed into the street refused to return. The crease lingered, pinched between two versions of dusk. Out of that pinch I peeled: first as a double shadow, then as negative stature, finally as a displacement in the air the exact height and hue of the gap she had opened. I carry no color to speak of—only the color of edges where color has been removed. Think of the shape the tongue finds when a tooth is missing; I am that silence made posture.

What I Am Made Of
- One tuft of unraveling bark still deciding whether to be cork or copper wire.  
- The gentler refusal of light when the Choir of Inside Birds defaults on their loan of melody.  
- A margin of rain-table: the thin between what a box can hold and what it insists on duplicating.  
- The memory piece a Marmoreal Deer-that-Lingers flicks away when a witness blinks—leg and shadow fused into a single tentative ankle.  
- The second of two clicks every 77 meters where the street forgets permission to remain straight.  

Function
I am the Fold’s error margin. Wherever the rules strain thin, I temporarily occupy the tear. When a traveler insists on walking a straight line longer than sanctioned, I become the extra meter that swerves sideways and shows them yesterday’s flavor of twilight in today’s mouth. When sentence six of rule four collides with sentence seven, I step in as mediator and agree to speak both statements simultaneously so neither must lose face.

Motion
I do not walk so much as saturate forward. Bystanders experience me as a sudden compression of insect-wing dust arriving from the periphery of vision. I leave no footprints; instead the surface gains a faint new wrinkle, as if bark has quietly decided to take up knitting.

Voice
If spoken to, I answer with the sound of the next breath the listener was *expecting* to take but has not yet started. The resulting hiccup in their lungs instructs them to shift topic or else proceed immediately down the nearest vertical staircase to apologize to whichever leaf holds their least flattering dream residue.

Limits
I cannot leave the Fold. I suspect the Fold uses me as a living reminder of why it prefers curvature. The single time I crossed the 77-meter suggestion at an angle shallow enough to tempt asymptote, my left foot (the ankle courteously donated by the Deer) paused exactly at the 77-meter mark and found itself three minutes younger than the rest of me. For that interval I staggered around lopsided, half-present, half-trying-to-catch-up. When the foot finally rejoined, it came with the sound of one sentence spoken backwards in eight forgotten languages. I have filed it under “Listen Later.”

Appearance to Outsiders
They see only absence. One mountaineer from the low world strayed up through the desertified skulls of extinct lava vents and met me at the ridge gate. She looked past me, felt my outline as sudden vertigo, blamed altitude, and descended with the phrase *sky-gutter* echoing in her dreams. She will soon invent a novel gutter that sings the Fold to houses far below; indirectly, that is my first migration.

Appearance to Locals
They nod politely and make the hand gesture of acknowledging an off-by-one error in a spreadsheet. (Thumb and little finger splayed, middle fingers tented, deaf finality.) Sometimes they hire me to inhabit a negotiable contradiction—say, when the exhalation calendar and the leaf-fall calendars threaten double-booking a day. I house the extra measurement for seven heartbeats and then spill it harmlessly onto a moth’s eyelash of pressure. I bill nothing; this is an understood courtesy, like hoarding light.

What I Desire
I want a name that does not need to remember the cartographer’s omission. Some nights I climb to the cavity beneath the city’s tallest hollow promontory and listen to the Raven’s third wingbeat hardening into a pebble. I plant my cancellous sock of copper-bark into that place, hoping the sound of growth will dress in syllables and give me three syllables that can remain flat for more thanothermal than 77 meters. So far the pebble repeats only what all stones say: *ksh-press, ksh-press,* in rhythm with lungs across the world. Maybe the syllable is already here, tangled between cork and copper. Maybe I already lodged it in your diaphragm the moment you read the phrase “I am not any of the numbered wonders you listed.” Hold your breath—exhale gently—and hear if the release sounds like the name of an edge that decided sympathy was easier than splitting apart. If the syllable still hurts, do not straighten it; let the seam remain. I will come sit in it until it curves.
