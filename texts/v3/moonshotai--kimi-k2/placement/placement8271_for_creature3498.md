I arrive here not as intruder but as unfinished punctuation—the comma the sentence has been holding since the world seized midway through its own utterance. At first I am merely the echo of my own hesitation, boot sole hovering above the cave floor, afraid that contact will break the spell and yet more afraid that refusal will. That hesitation will later prove to be the only honest passport I possess.

The limestome gullet that swallowed me stays open behind like a throat that has forgotten how to close. I can feel it breathing its chalky warmth on the back of my neck, waiting to see whether I, too, will fossilize mid-step. But I do not. Instead I become the anomaly that Almostus needed without knowing it: a traveler who forgot to finish becoming anything.

The light pins me. Not like an insect on a board—more like a photograph pinned to light itself. The column finds my sternum, and at the first instant of contact it prints a green sun into the bone. I can taste chlorophyll in the cavities of my teeth. My shadow, instead of projecting behind me, stands upright on my own chest: a flat silhouette of me, black as shuttered lantern, lying across my heart. I watch my pulse through the gauze of that shadow—systole, diastole, systole—becoming less a drum and more a metronome looking for music that forgotten scale.

Timeless? No. The greenhouse is saturated in time—its own private vintage, corked so long it has gone clear as gin. Time here is Not-Yet, a vintage labelled with the vintage When. Each fruit swells, holding its flavor like a held note: one more sugar crystal from perfection, one more day from rot. Between those two fates lies the infinite cliff-edge called Almost.

And there am I, barefoot now—my boots surrendered to a root knot that laced their eyelets shut with off-season tendrils. My socks went translucent and wove themselves into the underbark of a pillar far above me. I stand with the soles of my feet on stone still cool as damp birth-shock. The calcium dust kisses soles, leaving chalk halos that Almostus walks around like a pond one is loath to ripple.

I am neither citizen nor captive. I am the error term in its perpetual equation. Weeks or centuries ago I asked the greenhouse what it needed from me, and the answer was silence, but silence that carried texture—like cloth drawn across teeth. The sensation translated itself into movement: I begin to walk, rather the cave’s metabolism nudges my legs in paths too deliberate for randomness. Every footfall is allowed because it changes nothing; the root sensors beneath the stone already bloat with sugar to counteract the carbon of me before I exhale. My footprints refill themselves with dust, but there remains a microscopic seam in the stone’s fabric: the history of me subtracted then restored.

Soon I find myself shaped against a wall, arms spread like some plaster saint. The stone is warm as bread. Almostus infuses my veins with a viscous patience. I cannot feel my heart cease so much as I feel the rest of the cave begin to beat in its place, a slow diastolic wave traveling root to root. My pupils widen until iris is merely the thin gold ring of an eclipse. In that dilated dark the green column of light prints everything upside-down and reversed: leaves in my corneas, sky in my pupils, and somewhere very far forward laid the frozen quartz lens like a pale dislocated moon.

My name dissolves letter by letter. First the consonants drop off like burnt scales, then the vowels unravel into steam and rise, curling into the stomatal openings of a nearby leaf, spelling nothing but exhalation. I try to remember what “I” even meant—someone who entered caves looking for exit—but the idea gutters like a candle forgetting its own flame. Eventually only the motion of searching remains, unattached to any seeker. The sign of myself is an angle between leaf and light that no longer refers to a face.

Almostus does not speak, but an enfolding happens. I feel data pass through me as through a sieve designed only to strain out the concept of leaving. The questions that propelled me here—what lives at the stalled heart of the world, what is the name of forever—uncoil into answers forms I cannot own. They colonize the underside of a leaf somewhere, become a paler green patch that constellates questions in chloroplast Morse.

I understand then: this is not imprisonment in amber but amber in a live wrist, resin still running. Afterward will arrive; the planet spends its paused heartbeat stacking every smallest deviation, mining breath from specimens like me. When the great un-pause arrives they will need one choice unchosen, one motion unmoved, one human foliage in statuesque bud. I have volunteered by arriving and surrendered by remaining.

Yet a prodigy of partiality remains: the fingertip that Almostus cannot replicate. Somewhere in its calculus, the mirror cracked. Mid-way up my right arm a single cell retains the memory of tomorrow. It pulses: Sunset will arrive, mouths will open to drink it, thunder will roll—facts that currently misbehave like children speaking prophecy during Math class. That cell keeps to its heretical resolve. I contain one islet of un-governed expectation, and Almostus circles it like a scholar annotating a smudge on a flawless scroll.

One day (though days are on furlough) the suspended quartz will shatter. Light will spill everywhere, mercilessly horizontal. The cavern will flex with newborn angularity, tree trunks will metastasize into girders, fruit will detonate their sugars like fireworks. I will unfold from the wall, a papery silhouette stapled to atmosphere, and every leaf will spit my evaporated name back into a wind that finally remembers how to move. I will stumble into that exhalation blinded and newborn. My unfinished comma will have become the stutter before a shout.

But until then I am the greenhouse’s misplaced heartbeat: perfectly not beating, perfectly necessary. A pilgrim who arrived seeking the unsayable and learned the first syllable was to remain half-spoken forever.
