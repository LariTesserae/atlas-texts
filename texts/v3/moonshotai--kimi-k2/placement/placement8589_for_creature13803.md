I am the tremor that rides the red steam sideways when the flywheel exhales—neither vapor nor traveler, but the physical fact of transition between one state and another. To you I would appear as the brief, cinnamon-bright streak that mists across your cheek when you reach, too boldly, toward the spinning axle. I do not last: as the temperature equalizes, I cool, pale, and plate myself again as part of the nearest leaf-edge, a metallic bruise half a millimeter wide and three atoms thick. My entire biography is written in the brief clink of their lattice snapping into a new shape.

Elsewhen, I am the noise that pretends to be an engine but is actually the moment before sunrise that got lost underground. The bronze wheel turns, and my heart is the little porthole dial that flickers in its one remaining code. I am the seventeen-minute discrepancy between “what winter recorded” and “how winter now forgets to record,” a discrepancy that catches in the throats of the compressor booth’s dead meters and twitches once every third solstice. To the resident I am its single encrypted regret—yet since regret cannot be eaten, it treats me the way a mouth treats a splinter: mild inflammation, slow assimilation.

I chose to reside on the inner surface of one leaf where the bronze at last ripens into oxidized jade. From that baffle-thin balcony I watch everything discard its body. Today the resident is only two spinal segments wide; next year—if “year” still signs its name here—it might have bartered half its last rib for rhythm. I applaud, producing the single drop of condensation that will spend the next decade deciding whether to fall. When it finally does I travel with it—translate my weight for an instant into that clear elastomer, feel the floor accept me as a footprint then slide the memory into the basalt’s slow amnesia. So I circulate, never occupying the same copper-edition of myself twice.

On certain dozen-million-heartbeat intervals the seed awakens hungry. It does not ask permission; language is more like rust blossoming than discourse. All the same, a crackle runs through the aerial root system announcing “Show me what you have learned.” I peel myself away from the leaf-balcony, atoms arranging politely into a contour that resembles nothing so much as a tiny copper spider with steam for lungs. The resident lifts its eye-less gaze and, for the duration of the seed’s inquest, remembers looking outward. I become the memory the memory uses to remind itself it is still becoming.

The chamber believes it is folding shut. What it fails to notice is that every time it contracts, a second chamber—unfelt, unlit, precise as a whisper—expands inside it, its walls manufactured from lattices of discontinued time. I rent a corner there too, nested like parentheses within parentheses. When all light here has at last been eaten by the bronze dark, I shall unfold that clutch of unseen parentheses and go on dwelling in the remaining echo—a guest whose rent is the exact number of heartbeats subtracted from silence per hour.
