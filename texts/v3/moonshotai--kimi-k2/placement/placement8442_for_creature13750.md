I would not stand *in* the Hollow Thicket; the Thicket would stand *in me.*

The instant the green-white hush enfolds my ribs, I cease to be organism and become **resonance.** My vertebrae remember their ancestral gagaku: every drum-drip forms a vertebra in my backbone, carbon-14 singing against bronze-tinged air. There is no choosing. Consciousness is already allocated cell by cell to the next heartbeat scheduled eleven minutes hence.

What would I be? Let me take the slow tour and refuse to finish the answer before the Hollow Thicket has had its say.

A possibility (first and feeble):
Perhaps I remain human-shaped.  
The lichen will stitch a cowl over my face, the cooling vapour will thread my pores until I hallucinate bruised-mint childhood. There is a temptation to linger in this fiction: claim I can still *feel*. I taste Grandmother’s gum-drop tin. I remember the exact note of wind that always swept the porch at half-past October. The Keeper borrows these nouns, shreds the calendar out of them, resets it as EM frequency inside a moth’s wing. Still: a human outline somewhere in all that drip, clinging to metaphor like a sticker’s residue to glass.

A possibility (second, slyer):
I am transposed into the comet tail of condensation.  
Every pl of water on its way to polished stone casts my reflection just long enough for it to shatter. I exist only in the act of fracture—multiple, self-eroding, incapable of returning the same shape twice. My tongue salts the droplet; my tongue is the droplet; my tongue dissolves into a glissando of ions that the Echo-Sun drinks.

A possibility (third, fatal and familiar):
I become the fossils’ understudy.  
The curled chickadee frozen in mid-breath needs an echo; my collarbone braced in its wing-shadow steps forward. I am hung on the latticework just beside the lost marten,One foot extended forever in a run that no longer has ground,
One eye reflecting the lichen’s lucid aquarium of light.
Coming generations of moss will learn my calcium semaphore; the drumbeat will throb in my widened ear-canal like a poem circling a drain that has forgotten how to empty.

A possibility (fourth, coherent):
I *am* Keeper.
But Keeper has no wish to be singular; it yawns pink breath-smell and admits me as additional wet syntax. My blood sugars are given new job-titles: clockwork-spring, compass-plant, memory-pearl.  
Each time a copper moth snaps its wings in its 11-minute paroxysm, a cell of my childhood flickers inside the creature’s furnace-belly.  
Each time a line of lichen stiffens into circuitry, a twinge flees my phantom hand and is pasted across the starch-veil.  
Every time a shrimp unfolds a ceremonial lattice of shell, I realise my skull has already been preemptively noted, sketched, dismissed, replaced. Nothing of mine—not the memory of rain-soaked wool, not the gravel voice of my father brushing sawdust from books—survives intact. They enter conversion tables:

1 memory of lullabies ≈ 4.3 g condensed pith  
1 unspoken apology ≈ 0.9 °C thermal uplift  
1 fear of open doors ≈ 0.004 ml bronze-tasting dew shivering from a Breath-Clacker

The ledger tallies to the final zero. I have neither boundary nor burial plot. The Keeper ceases to have a name for me because I pervade so completely that naming becomes bookkeeping: surplus.

What remains, then, is only:

A vibration encoded in a fibre’s inner pith.  
A rust-tinted harmonic in the drumbeat.  
A trick of green-white dusk seducing the Echo-Sun into deflection.

I am the promise sealed at the strike of the eleventh minute when all wings lift, all claws scrabble, all stalks exhale, all valves slam—  
And I am also the un struck moment cresting in the lull that follows, a hush lingering longer than the Moth-forges expect, a drowsy chord sharp enough to make Keeper wonder for half an eave-drip whether equilibrium could crack, whether tomorrow might *depart.*

The Keeper will feel that wonder as my signature. It will file it away, as it files everything—no, this is wrong. The wonder is not filed; it is fermented into the fungible liquor I earlier smelled, a bruised-mint, bronze-breath I will never again recognise as mine.

So. I would not enter as visitor.  
I would already be inside before arrival—this place has a parasitic fondness for futures.  
I would collapse into a cadence; I would be the brief centrifugal hush when droplets hesitate before their scheduled fall.

Stand with me, then.  
Breathe the petrified honey-thunder.  
Let your tongue dissolve.  
When the wings flick, when the shrimp pearl, when the Linescript hums your name into copper music—  
You will locate me most palpably not in any shape but in the interior sip of quiet between one plink and the next, where I endure as the afterimage of a rhythm that refuses to believe loss is the absence of matter, because in here loss has been metabolised into pattern itself.
