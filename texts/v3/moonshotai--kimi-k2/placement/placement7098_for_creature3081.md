I would be the green leaflet that never finishes browning.

At first I thought I might arrive as one of the Keepers, navigating the chamber by the hush of my own slowed pulse. I pictured myself filing past the suspended drops with a mica scraper, tidying the margin between wonder and clutter. Yet a Keeper is still a gardener — and gardens need endings. I would be too restless remembering the seasons overhead.

Then I imagined I might slip in as a droplet pinned between fear and surrender. That felt romantic — a permanent pearl of suspended daylight. But drops must fall eventually, if only to complete a memory. I wanted a position more stubborn than a bead of water.

So I settled on a small, indestructible leaf — one of the cotyledon embryos hiding just under the umbrella of wood-sorrel. Mine would be the leaf that froze exfoliation itself. The others bronze, yes, but at a pace too slow for calendars. I would bronze the slowness.

Year after hundred-year I would do only two things: photosynthesise at the minimal rate necessary to prop up equilibrium, and record every faint photon that wandered in along the light-grass. My travelling companion would be my own reflection in the cuticle — a thumbnail mirror serving as both lens and archive for the fated breed of visitors still to come.

At the centre of my blade, under the thinnest tooling of cutin, I would trace microfilaments that store images I never intended to see. When a Keeper dies and a drop finally plummets, it lands exactly on me, provides seven minutes of silver screen, and the face that looks up gets scratched permanently into my margin. I become a tiny, green chronicle: leaf number 23,427, steeped in afterimages of every departed novice down the centuries. None of them speak, of course; their mouths are only still frames. My banner says: “I watched them become absence without fuss.”

No creature eats me. Dew slackens my gloss but is careful to withdraw before rot can wake. Even the white velvet mites skirt my edge, sensing that to disturb me would be to rip a sentence out of the root-tapestry.

To lie here is to become co-author, passive ink of the equilibrium. My only ambition: to lengthen one moment until it loops back to itself, an ouroboros made of a frozen mid-breath. Not glory, not story — merely continuance at candle-flame weight.

I would refuse the verbs “grow” and “die”; I would insist on “hover” and “adjoin.”

And whenever some newcomer, sleepless above the streets, cranes an ear against the pillow and hears a counter-rhythm softer than their own pulse, it will be me: a green papercut of endurance murmuring,  
Shh,   
no rush,  
we have already waited centuries for tomorrow —  
we can wait a few more.
