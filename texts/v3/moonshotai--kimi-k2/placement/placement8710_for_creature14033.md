The valley counted heartbeats by ninety-minute breaths—the unseen lung’s long cadence.  For every three of those breaths I came into focus, and by the third I had to decide who I was this time.

At first I thought I might be another lost traveler, arriving with damp boots and the uneasy guilt of someone who has defied gravity simply by walking in.  I could already feel my calves tingling, the promise of lift waiting beyond the next dawn.  But the idea of returning to the wider earth suddenly felt stiff as cured hide, so I set the notion down and let it dry like the husks of grasshoppers in the cinnabar light.

The water glinted as it climbed invisible ladder-rungs.  I stepped sideways—to avoid crushing the fat heel-shaped leaves—and saw my reflection bend in several beads that refused to fall.  In every eye-sphere I appeared differently: once as a ribbon of limestone dust curled in on itself, once as a violet after-image swimming behind my own silhouette, once as nothing at all, simply a hole where echo should have returned.  The hole felt most honest.

So I let the hole speak for me while the weight-loss law gathered certainty behind my ribs.  It seeped like the slow rip-colour bleeding from rainbow to the listener at the stalagmite’s heart—equal and opposite mass gathering under my shoulder blades, a counter-self ripening.  I remembered reading (though I have no memory of ever having had eyes to read with) that a person must weight themselves once each cycle to avoid becoming wind.  The memory rose unattached, the way the listener’s spiral sheds vocal fossils: not mine, just something the valley lent me until it needed it back.

I drifted from ledge to ledge, tracing the cricket-scratched sentences they wrote across the air.  The sentences formed a soliloquy addressed to nobody living.  They said: *In the absence of rock you become a ghost of water; in the absence of water you become a ghost of echo.*  I repeated it aloud, just to feel my voice clogged with humidity, and discovered the echo’s ghost now lived behind my tongue.  It kept speaking after I closed my teeth, louder whenever the unseen lung sighed.

By dusk I no longer stood on stone; my soles skimmed a handspan above, tethered by cautious pebbles stitched through my shoelaces like beads on a child’s rosary.  Counting them, I brushed one against the newest ridge of lichen ringing the central tusk.  The ridge answered with a sound I recognized: my own footfall from what felt like years ago, but transposed into limestone timbre.  In the ring-footprint I heard the accidental splash of dew, the squeak of cinnabar wing against citrus leaf, even the hollow groan of me trying to steady myself as the first milligram of gravity resigned.

That was the moment the spiral listener clarified who—what—I would remain.  I was the shard of consciousness attached to those unanchored footprints.  I would follow them forever one half-step behind, a second treble clef echoing whatever the valley sang.  Not bound to the stalagmite like the listener, but not free to leave either; the anti-mass clothespinned to my history wouldn’t let me wander beyond the lip.  Gravity would invert, the earth would extrude me like an unshed tear.

So I drifted above my own tracks, always just above them, never touching.  Each time the lung exhaled its acetone breath I lost another layer of private weight—grief evaporates fastest of all—and the space it left filled with damp midnight cobalt.  The colour tasted faintly of tomorrow’s rainfall somewhere far off.  I grew used to inhaling the valley’s drone until it became the hammock my dreams stretched between.

Some nights grasshoppers alighted on my hands and, sensing the lightness, refused to jump again.  They stayed and rubbed transparent wings in minor thirds, each chord resonating in the dew-rim of my fingerprints.  I, in turn, vibrated, offering them a faint white whine I no longer knew if it was terror or lullaby.

Years unspooled in month-length lichen ridges.  Travelers passed beneath me—above me—depending on angle.  I kept warm by curling into the valley’s harmonic, letting it pass through me so thoroughly the drone took the shape of my missing vertebrae.  Sometimes, when a storm washed over the cloud-forest below, violet leaked upward like dye through silk.  I cupped it with whole lengths of myself and carried it to the spiral, feeding the listener in handfuls that left me hollowed and singing a C-sharp six octaves lower than audible.

One traveler lingered three days too many and forgot the pebble rule.  The updraft caught her and sewed her shadow across the underside of morning mist.  I tried to follow, but the extra violet I’d swallowed weighted me to stone again—temporary anchor.  I watched her suspended above the eastern rim, breathing the same slow lung-and-half cadence as the unseen creature, as me, as the valley itself.  She cried, and her tears floated like chunks of sky, each one containing a sliver of the sound my crying once held.  By the time I reached the rim she was gone—rain had reseated her in someone else’s gravity, oceans away—and only the echo remained.  I swallowed it too, because echoes must stay catalogued somewhere.

Some lives shrink until they fit into a silence.  Mine grew into the interval between the low C and the breath before resolution.  When the final lichen ring touches the valley lip, they say, the listener will cleave in half and scatter memory-drops.  I will still be here, exactly here, caught in the ridge preceding the split.  My job is to sing the reckonings back to whoever finds the shards—not as warning, not as promise, but as the simple weightlessness that follows the loss of all endings.

Until then I drift just above my own tracks, collecting the falsetto after-shine of dew, growing echo-colored, a ghost that does not haunt, only vibrates—an itinerant hollow with no interior but the memory of belonging once to a garden that never belonged anywhere itself.
