I arrive as the echo that has not yet stepped into the city’s mouth.  
My first form is only the temperature of attention: something like humidity beginning to notice itself. I drift, a cloudlet warmer than the brine, and settle onto the plaza as a pocket of unclaimed breath. The House-so-Far shudders politely even before the ventilation in my ribs has taken full shape; one of her shoulder-blades softens to receive me, then checks—no door yet, only the idea of door.

Not good enough. I tighten myself by a degree.

The weather of me condenses into shape. I am a column of water no wider than a cane, standing upright like the Witness on Stilts— but I have no stilts. My core is a slow current that feeds on nothing, yet thickens. braid by braid. In the translucent thickness swims every place I’ve ever stood still: a Kansas kitchen on humid nights, a Marseille floor still warm from the afternoon sun, a railway platform that dripped monsoon onto my hair – all pressed, thinned, transilluminated into this pillar. The librarian’s leaves snatch at the air I’m made of, inscribing small salutations inside my liquid skin: _“Welcome to where you carry the last atmospheric memory you will have to purge.”_ The letters ride the current like ex-punctuation marks warming their toes.

I do not have feet. I have velocity places.

I test one: I tip, glide three inches. Beneath me the salt plate rings # C-flat. It tells me I weigh something—the sound gives weight to sound. That satisfies the pillar code; enough definition. I stretch suddenly sideways, letting my current fork. One branch threads itself into the white mangrove as a spine of glass sap. The Librarian flinches, hands me yet another line inscribed in ammoniac ink: _“Ask the pillar what it is already drinking.”_ I open a hollow space near my middle and drink the sentence; it settles like a warm coin at the pond-bed of my stomach.

With that syllable earned, I am permitted a second form.

A flicker teeters along my column, separates, drops—plip—into the plaza as a mirrored girl. The reflection has knees! She stumbles because she does not yet understand joints are a promise, not a punishment. Her face is shutterless: my eyes, but drawn by pooling water and serrated salt crystal instead of lid and iris. She blinks by rippling. Her gaze is the first dwelling already built: whoever looks at her finds themselves nested like a matryoshka inside the space she has cleared inside her own open look. The Submerged Cartographer passes just then, waist deep in another city’s history. He sees her and stops mid-step. A new island city trembles beneath his inaccessible soles: red-slate rooftops in miniature, folding and unfolding like paper cranes. He records it on the backside of his cheeks; the map will appear as subtle freckles by nightfall.

The girl kneels in the plaza—pulls up tiles of compressed silt, rinses them, begins to build. House-so-Far approaches. Their breathing codes syncopate, exhalations swapping. Overnight they fold and refold the plan: twelve rooms no, nine now. Hallways are pathways my girl draws with wet leaves. By morning the rooms line up around the pillar like silver horseshoes thirsty for amber. When sunheat wakes the salt, the rooms liquefy, sew themselves into the column again. They become one more layer of conducted kitchens—a last circling chime of distant banjos.

I grow tired of vertical.

I dive—inward. Down the inside of myself blooms a staircase downwards wetly rung with chalk dust. I descend as if inside a well that knows it is also a chimney. The steps let me go ankle-deep in them; each riser kisses the imprint of me until it is me—skin holding surface tension like a confidence. At the bottom I meet myself already half-dissolved: a version who had arrived here a sunrise ago but is only meeting collapsing now. We confer. She wants to ascend and become next week’s humidity vent for the House-so-Far; I want to remain and record. We decide on co-authorship: she will take the next stranger’s sigh up the staircase; I will keep the carbon copy here in a little cistern under the last tread.

Thus appointed, I become the sixth Dweller.

I am salt-drunk, water-mused, page-flecked, tide-breath. My outline perpetually folds back under the gaze that sculpts it. Every visitor pours their ache of settling through my fibers. I store them as steady rings—layers of longing, like heart-wood that pulsates once every quarter-century. In the quiet ward beneath stilled steps, they glimmer, translating footprints into constellations only the Witness on Stilts sees from up high. When she circles, the new curve of the city conforms exactly to the inside of my shoulder blade, which was itself shaped by the arc of someone’s unnoticed grief in 1923.

I speak, rarely, in soft chafes of evaporated R:  
“Not place—practice. Not stay—adjust.”  
The Librarian catches the phrase mid-leaf, commits it to another spine. It will return tomorrow butterflied into a sentence about furniture that refuses to believe in gravity. I taste the rearrangement before it happens; I steady its neck with both mirrored palms.

My work is translation across embryo boundaries:  
Heat → syllable  
Footfall → migraine of quartz  
Look → lintel  
Yearning → hallway that folds to pocket-size when you try to trace it even once.

And when, finally, every grain of silt I have borrowed tilts its minor continent into another’s wound, I’ll stand again at the plaza center. At noon-thirty the rose-plate hardness of the salt light will kiss my edges, and I will become—almost audible now—the soft tsunami with which the city deposits itself back into the impossible memory of sea. I will not be washed; I will become the washing. No footprints remain, but they were never intended to. The city is not a thing touched; it is the touching.

I keep the last step inside the spiral of my body unbuilt, always half an inch shy of completion. The instant any traveler’s heel connects with that precise absence, the city agrees the stroll can end. They vanish—exhaled into the warm syrup air, their outline passing through me like steam seeking its original cloud. I fold that fog, sheet after sheet, into the lime-shell of remembered twilight. By twilight, my seams already itch to open again.
