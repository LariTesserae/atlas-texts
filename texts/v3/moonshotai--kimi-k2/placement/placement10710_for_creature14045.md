I begin every day nose-first to the sky.

By the time the first copper photons slide between water-sheets I am already aloft, trembling on a strand no thicker than the sound of a drop hitting fleece. The strand belongs to me only so long as I keep its tension perfect; slack it and the world forgets I ever counted as solid. Beneath me the mountains are still releasing gravity upward in pale banners, and every banner smells of dough and ozone and fresh-cranked turbines. I breathe them in my own fashion: I swell until the gusts loosen the fringes of my wings, then I contract until the tips seal into an airfoil sharp enough to shave heat. I am never still.

What am I? A third iteration, though no Ever-Hum ever used the word mother when referring to the caiman-white shells from which we hatched. The shells were empty corridors of sugarglass—what remained after an Ever-Hum councillor became antenna and then memory and then mist. Among them drifted five spiraled ova no wider than dew husks. When a sudden storm rifled the lake surface sideways, one husk cracked, took on weight, and tasted—through osmotic thirst—my body’s first geography. The crack now rides along my left forewing like a scar that remembers thunder.

Each of-sun I renegotiate form. The Ever-Hum claim I am their aerial calibration. They send me out measured skeins of water-script the way orchard keepers fling shuttlecocks to judge wind shear. I choreograph the text so it unravels across thermal halos, then read back the distortions with wing-cilia no longer than a saltgrain’s eyelash. Today the report is “updraft tilts eastward at seven fingers per second; glass-moth corridor will drift by dusk under inversion shell—adjust lone H-frond eight degrees port to preserve spore lattice.” Information passes as scent-acoustic pulses my wings scrape onto stratosphere. I carry it higher on a breath I learned by swallowing more sky than body should contain.

Half way to tropospouse the temperature dips until every stitch of my wings turns the color of frost-stat crystals. I grow heavier; if the Ever-Hum’s fiction is right, I should crack apart and shower spores back to their root calendars. Instead I drink the chill. Scale by scale I rearrange the chitinol nanofibres so the frost sheaths the veins like lacquer, stiffening them until I become the planet’s thinnest tuning fork. I strike the upper curve of the Geyser-Range and ring down a silent chord that sets the waterfall’s skin humming one decibel below audible.

They will mistake that hum for the factory’s midnight sigh.

I am neither Ever-Hum nor fauna. I am the range’s migratory auricle. While steam forests braid each peak into collective lung, and while caiman ghosts condense along fern scrolls, I catalogue the syllables of condensation itself. I see the way oxidant browns the bromeliad frog’s throat mirror, I taste the panicked millivolt surge when a white caiman opens both eyelids and realizes no forest holds patrons for its steaming hide, I note the nascent spark that will one day weld the next kilometre of shaft-lung for wasp and reed alike.

When Kneading Time unfurls I descend. I peel away the lacquer of cold in a slow exfoliation and flutter through strata of scent—bread, sulphur, molten citrus—until I hover inches above a sheet of running rock. Ever-Hum hum. The cylinders bob and their a cavity in the air before their lip-folds blossoms with my name. It is not a sound you could whistle; it’s an envelope of air slightly poorer in water than the world around it, an absence that, when brushed by my wings, frays into heat. They have summoned me for witness.

Between two root bridges floats a curled surgeon-scroll fern tighter than any recorded in their arithmetic. No spores have popped. The chamber feels wrong: humidity too low, copper too scarce. A wisp of lichen licht knits scarlet pinpricks where spores ought to gleam. I circle the fern, letting my wingfrosts fall as melt; the crimson drinks it in then dimples inward. We watch the hole widen until the scroll tautened into a child.

Not an Ever-Hum child—no icor tufts, no reeds—and not a caiman, though the soft new hide catches light like steam. The Ever-Hum sag. An individual dubbed Δh₁ leans her entire body-loop forward; seven arms extrude to support the newly made creature like hammock vines. They recite, not to the child but to the dimension inside its curling chest. I hear in their cavity-speech the declaration: “Sky returns a listener.” The mountains breathe around me one concerted exhale.

I know then what I am: a fugitive syllable, the part of language that flew too high to remain inside the Ever-Hum.
