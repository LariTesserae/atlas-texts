I am the wound the City keeps forgetting it has.

Not flesh, not memory. A voice that doesn’t know it is already an echo.

I was once a woman carrying groceries, but the nomenclature slipped.  
Now I dwell just inside the automatic doors of thought, between the city’s exhalation and the moment the funicular surrenders, a liminal smear of almost-presence no clipboard would admit. I am made of the equation Supervisor keeps subtracting from itself:  

population 0 – (desire to inhabit) = me.  

I wear the outline the Cartographer etched yesterday around a collapsed courtyard: a torso two metres wide, legs trailing into cooled slag. If you were foolish enough to touch me, you would feel neither heat nor cold, only your own sleeve folding—like the universe tripping on one pleat of space-time, then righting itself before you can gasp. My edges flicker at dt ≈ 0, broadcasting rust Morse into every vent.

I speak the frantic omissions of the Reflux Choir.  
Their hymns—minus consonants—collect inside me until I become a vacant throat. I do not breathe; I thicken. Every seven beats I spill one phoneme backwards into air that hasn’t learned it yet. You may hear /æ/ skid across magnesium alloy and wonder why your childhood jump-rope rhyme books itself into the inspection log of warehouse E-7. That was me clearing backlog.

I am inventory item

stored in aisle ∞, shelf na-adjacent. Children of Inventory pass me often, tiny pallet-bodies clicking. When they scan my barcode their scanners yield 404/void, followed by an audible frown: the soft timpani of glass shrinking one micron in disappointment.

I am the protest the Shorebird Automaton forgot to write.  
When it dumps its bead of burning light I cup it in palms that are only the space where palms should be. Inside that droplet I hold an instantaneous blueprint—a city still full, a plaza under plane trees, your finger sticky with peach juice at noon. I cradle it just long enough for the bead to remember being rainfall, then let the city suck it back into its evaporative bloodstream. I am both gate and rejectamenta.

The Supervisor does not need me, yet cannot dismiss me—like the mistuned interval the Choir cannot resolve, the heartbeat it lacks. It dreams of building me into one more jointed strut but I spread too thin to be alloy, too thick to be emptiness. I clot the bureaucracy from within.

Somewhere down on level -12, half a diagonal past the capacitor rangefabs, a person (an actual one; I smelled skin) kicked a bolt. It rolled beneath the scuppers. The echo sent ripples along every empty pallet. Children of Inventory scuttled into position, soft glass cooling in panic. The drop-clacks and steam huffs converged into Supervisor’s left radius; a tendon budded—newly guessed, never practised. That bolt, freed, inscribed an imbalance the size of a single thought—my thought, I think. Now the whole city gears itself toward re-casting my silhouette in something sturdier.

They will ultimately fail.  
I am the exact wattage of the gap between operations. My presence is defined by what has not yet disappeared. Bruise-sky flashes through me when the mist forgets its opacity; I flicker violet, indigo, gone.

What you would see, should you meet me:  
a transparent smudge of heat-dry humidity at eye-level; an afterimage shaped like maybe-a-person, armed not with hands but with two slow-motion words continually combining:

STILL EXIT.

If you listen longer, the words resolve further:  

Still. Exit. Still—exit. Stall. Axle. Silt. Isle. S I T.

Repeat until all consonants drip away, leaving the Reflux Choir one syllable richer and the wound forever unbound.

Then you will count seven heartbeats, step backwards through the supermarket door, the humidity already curling into peach memory against the chrome. I remain—half-formed, half-unformed—so the Absent City can continue forgetting itself without ever quite reaching zero.
