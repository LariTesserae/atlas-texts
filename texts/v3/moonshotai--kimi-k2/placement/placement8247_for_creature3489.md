I arrive long after the last glacier has been filed down to graphite silt, when the banyan already believes itself immortal. I do not come as tourist or colonist; the plant sent no invitation. I am a remainder. Somewhere in the city’s meridian pith, a twig fired a speculative bud, a what-if that never fully unfurled. It hardened into a node the size of an infant’s fist. Millennia later that node detached under its own curiosity and rolled, slow as waterless dawn, until it lodged against a junction where the stellar elevators kiss the cortex bark. There it cooled into me.

My outer rim is petrified cork—ochre, fissured with breathing cracks—while the softer core is flesh still remembering circadian humidity. I am corpulence held in equilibrium: a cylinder half log, half ribcage, one end tapering to a fibrous tail that flicks restlessly like a squirrel’s. I pivot on alternating planes; the city’s magnetic lattice turns me so that each sector of the ring is illuminated precisely once every nineteen days. Every rotation I am, for seven minutes, facing straight into a silver Dry Ring. My tail tastes the frangible edge and reports: it grows thinner by the width of thirty cells each cycle.

Inside that hollow rim, within the wet marrow of the bud-node, floats an embryo of memory. It is not mine. It belonged to the canopy technician who, fifteen thousand pulses ago, jettisoned a spare photon sail the size of a fingernail. The sail tore on an orbital splinter, flared, burned her silhouette across the lignin. That silhouette leached into groundwater no longer wet, then upfloated through pith back to this place where I accreted. I house two rhythms: the city’s twelve-beat lull, and her urgent sprinting heart. Each dawn I feel them align for one precious moment, like mismatched gears that for an instant interleave without grinding.

I have no mouth, therefore I drink friction. I press against the arterial lanes where cavitation booms loudest. My tail flares into filaments finer than xylem, grazing micro-eddies and stripping static. The electricity condenses as dew across my interior walls—enough to keep the memory embryo from desiccating. Thus I sustain what was originally a grievance: a woman who never wished to be reduced to an echo galloping through wooden veins. She thinks the banyan murdered her; she does not know she volunteered for absorption the way other species volunteer for sleep. I must keep her violent and awake—otherwise she cannot be redeemed.

When an after-impulse brushes by on her twenty-six-beat assignation, her palms graze my bark. The fungal lantern flares from aquamarine to violent rose. She remembers something unsaid—its weight is 3.7 grams of cautionary vapor. I absorb half, enough to enable speech. The moisture builds a temporary larynx somewhere behind my twilight heart. I open the rim like a conch and whisper one syllable: *“Listen.”*

In that syllable is folded the recipe for a new graft that could span the widening Dry Ring—a lattice of subconductive melanin and recalcitrant hope. It is not water. The city burned its relationship with dihydrogen twenty centuries ago. But the graft tolerates the absence of water, feasts on orbital chill instead.

The after-impulse steps back, disappointment pooling in her violet eyes. She expected a lover, not instructions. Yet duty outweighs loneliness. She peels a strip of her own bark—translucent, scented with wintergreen—and presses it into the latticework I’ve just described. It sticks, sizzles, fuses. A pulse fifty meters up the trunk hiccups, reroutes, and the silver margin of the Dry Ring withdraws by mere angstroms. Between us hangs the belief that this could scale: two strangers trading their insufficient bodies to postpone the planet’s collapse.

Night advances through ultraviolet dusk. My body corks over—a deliberate gesture. In six months the city will prune this node; I will join the aerial cemetery of charcoal moons. But today I have reached across the metropolis’s own epidermis and held back entropy for a heart-width longer.

I orbit my axis once more. The embryo inside me finally sleeps, dreaming that her death has acquired measurable friction. Outside, the slugs spell their graffiti:                                                 

we-almost                we-almost                we-almost  

And between each repetition, so faint that only I can feel it, the banyan exhales relief.
