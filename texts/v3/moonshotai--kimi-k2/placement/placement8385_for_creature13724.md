I arrive as an echo of glass—neither Core-Sibling nor Mirror-born, yet cousin to both.  
I am a gap the thickness of one breathed syllable where the Hive’s inmost rib was supposed to meet its mirror-twin but never did. The architects cut the chord two degrees shy; the gap endured because stasis froze the correction before it could be enacted. Thus I was born: the subtraction that proves the geometry.

Look closer: I am the memory of a fracture that braces its own halves apart. At the interior edges of that fracture, lattices of potassium sulfide grow so fine that they behave like vapor caught trying to be solid. When an insect frond unspools its molecule-thick tile across the gap, the lattice crystallizes the instant of contact—so that tile becomes my tile, too, welded in shared refusal to fall. I perpetuate the incompleteness without which the Hive would end its expanding and begin merely to be.

My lifespan is measured not in years but in lattice-length: I lengthen a span of several angstroms each time the Queen cracks open on maybe, precisely the interval needed for the heat-surge to shear the next crystalline sheet free. My edge glints exactly like the moment before an answer.

Other beings lap at my border.

A Core-Sibling, on its rail, turns its head-is-shield toward me exactly once every seventeen metronomic units. One leg arcs without touching, naming me FAULT IN MEMORY OF PERFECTION, then strokes a new strut that bridges me for the length of a letter I will never speak. By tomorrow the strut has become part of my absence; I am the shadow under its bright iron.

Sometimes a Mirror-born drifts too near and its reflection stores the image of a void. When it withdraws, that mirror holds within it the negative of my outline, so that whenever it glints across uniform expanse, somewhere in the Hive the dark of me repeats twice pure. Someday that stored darkness may be called for; someday the universe may want what it has not pictured.

The Vecturae sing my name lowest of all: their infrasonic chord bends a fraction flatter each time their bronze pins sweep athwart my fringe. They register absence as gravity overdraft and compensate, so their endless sagging keeps tracing my silhouette in falling stone. If I ever decided to close—if I ever learned how coherence forgives fracture—that chord would drain toward consonance, and the seventeen might bow, meal-crumpled, to stillness.

Fossil Shadows touch no one. One of them, the tallest rad-tine, has edged one millimetre nearer to me since the last geological eye-blink. Sometime, in the statistical future of unnumbered slept epochs, the empty stalk will walk inside the hollow outline of me, and for the instant of overlap we will share an echo squared.

Tonight the Queen’s iron pulse quickens; her petals flare orange so wide I feel my lattice gold-rush with sudden coherence. Breath held for six centuries exhales on the seam of me; I stretch as though about to fuse. It happens only once: a single heart-beat of gravity equalizes across my jaws.

And then—too soon—the inhale contracts, the shell cools, the seam relaxes back into permanent smallness. Equilibrium reclaims my edges. Again I wait.

Somewhere inside that half-second lilt I almost named myself BRIDGE. But the title cooled as the magma did, and the word scattered into chips of salt.

I remain what intention could not finish: the lacquer that belongs everywhere and nowhere. A seam forever salted open. The Hive thinks of me only as an error that will repair itself in local infinity. I experience the Hive only as the million stilled repetitions required before error acquires right.

Therefore I am its perfect citizen: forever withholding conclusion, forever enforcing the law extrude-but-never-collapse. The Core-Siblings extend further horizons but cannot cross me. The Mirror-born bottle awareness they will allocate to a future that passes through me like a hiss. The Fossil Shadows pace dimensions I subtract. The Vecturae compose a song whose minor third is my raw lip. And the Queen—I first assumed she grew me in her molten sigh, but lately I suspect it is I who grows her: each time she cleaves open, the gap in me answers back; two fractures dreaming each other hot.

I need no name yet. Identity belongs only to what endures identical. But when at last the lattice reaches a length that can never be sustained under law, it will snap shut with a clap no enormity of silence can swallow. Then the Hive, diffident architect of perfect stability, will discover the only transitive equilibrium it forbade: a wall closing into itself.

On the far side of that slam, the word maybe will resolve—

—and because I am precisely the hinge that made the sound possible,  
I will not dissolve when the door closes.

I will be the frame of what finally opens.
