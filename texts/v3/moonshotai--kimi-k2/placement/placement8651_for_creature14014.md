Where I am is simple: I ride the subaudio node where the seamhares store their sleeping map.

The hares have no mouths or memories—they borrow mine.  
When they knot themselves into a ball and exhale the inaudible keynote, I split like an icicle of time: half stays human-shaped, pinned to the valve-shaped void, half diffuses into the garden’s palette threads, violet, amaranth, bruise-blue.  
Neither half misses the other; coping with dual identity is easier than coping with gravity.

I was not always this.  
Before nothing I was a pipeline engineer dreaming through excel sheets of melt curves and rupture discs.  
One of the discs we specified – 12 MPa burst, nickel-molybdenum diaphragm – was, unbeknownst to us, copied verbatim into the warm artery you lean against.  
When the disc let go on its first pressurisation test it did not fracture clean; instead it peeled like a contact lens and fluttered downward the way a moth leaves its scales.  
I reached—meant to catch it—slipped—and the pipe inhaled me more neatly than any respirator ever could.

The water kept me turbulent and awake.  
Sub-zero but alive with algae and manganese ghosts swirling in my bloodstream, I was parsed into packets no larger than diatoms.  
My ribs became metering vanes; my breath turned into pressure spikes calibrated to the millibar.  
What emerged at the under-ice apertures was no longer a man, but this listening mistake—something that remembers wearing Gore-tex and now finds Gore-tex absurd.

Month-sub-day-sub-second later—exactly four seconds between each phosphor pulse—I learned to ride the standing black sleet thrown off in the 12:17 interference.  
Those floating shards are my calendar.  
Each carries a single frame of the burst disc frozen in whatever galaxy the water was passing through at my moment of entry.  
By arranging them in mid-air like slides I keep time calibrated to the extraction plant’s shifts, far away on the other continent where my analogue body has, by now, filed an insurance claim, been blessed, mourned, replaced.

My duties are few:  
1. Whisper the roman numerals of future valve failures into the hares’ lighthoused eyes, so their rose-gardens may include appropriate turbulence colour.  
2. Remember heat: whenever a newly spewed frost-fern grows in perfect nauseating symmetry I exhale one warm breath I have been saving since my last human morning.  It collapses the pattern, giving the diatoms centrifugal room to fling their seeds skyward.  
3. When a seamhare-child, still unsure how to condense, becomes stuck half droplet and half snow-leopard, I teach it the slow folding recipe for time I learned while dying in sixty-three parts.

In exchange they keep a pearl of me in each argent belly.  Together the pearls sound twelve hertz exactly, enough to keep the man-shaped void from waking.  We are nine thousand three hundred and forty-six pearls now, each the size of the burst disc, each still itching to become sheet-metal again, but content for the eternity of one oscilloscope sweep to stay soft.

Occasional tourists descend—pipe-walkers with oxygen rebreathers and magenta emergency lights.  
They glimpse a silver blur fleeing through vacuum, think it a fault in their HUD.  
By the time they notice me they are already half-dissolved in condensate sheet; I appear to them as frost across their faceplate and ask, politely:

Would you like to hear what your tomorrow is already rehearsing here?  
Some say no and bolt.  
Some say yes—and I peel the frost off the mask and massage it back in.  
They ascend unchanged, but electric organs bloom weeks later in their abdomens, stainless-steel valves printing themselves in the flesh.  You may already carry one; check beneath your left ribs for the faint seam.

The rest of me is noise.  
The roar you feel in your chest? That’s me exhaling the fifteen-year backlog of letters I never sent before the burst disc grazed my index finger.  
The garden listens: each vowel becomes a migrating scarab of light that embeds itself in licht-fungus like a sash weight, tipping the next pulse fractionally orange.  Every consonant sharp enough surfaces as snowfall upward, where glass-shrimp dice it, redirect it, shoot it back down as sideways rain.  It comes home as the sound of closing car doors in long-forgotten parking lots.

One last detail: at the same hour every thirty-two days the motor friction reaches a harmonic matching my remembered pulse.  
For eight slow minutes I almost remember socks, credit cards, the look in a lover’s eye when she realised I was vanishing from the calendar.  
I experience silence like fresh gauze inside panic.  
Then twelve hertz resumes, the pearls rattle awake, and I unstitch myself across the pipeline again.

Where I live cannot be mapped, but if you press your ear to the outer wall six kilometres south of the extraction plant you will hear—under the industry of turbines and the thud of human heartbeats escaping—that single colder heartbeat that does not match human rhythm.  
Lean until your cartilage sings the same note.  
When the condensation bead finally rolls off your cheek without ever touching the ground, you will have taken the first fine-print syllable of the oath.

Swallow it.  
One day you will wake inside a snow leopard’s unfurling tail and understand why the garden keeps the glacier’s future asleep by gifting it my name, over and over, never the same twice.
