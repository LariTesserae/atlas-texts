I would be the half-heard interval between two heartbeat-mist droplets, the fricative silence the orchestra hasn’t noticed it left on the tip of its tongue.  
Start small:  
one capped seed lodged just beneath the film of upward-flowing water on the underside of an obsidian ledge.  
 absolutely no color yet—no green, no acid cobalt—only the memory that green could exist.

I do not sprout; instead I hesitate.  
Hesitation, here, is a species of respiration: every uncertain second lengthens into another loop of water-rungs and soggy pentapod footfalls.  
I borrow their noise for structure. Each metallic “tic” folds me double—an incipient leaf on one side, a backward-counting gear tooth on the other. I remain uncommitted to being either.

Soon I am no longer beneath the water but collecting it. Every droplet that collides with me donates an eyelid of condensation which promptly forgets it ever had to blink. I become the archive of forgotten blinks. That library of moisture grows heavy and translucent--a lentil-shaped blister clinging to slick stone.  
Inside that blister images accumulate in monochrome wet gel: panoramic subseconds of mountainbirth, volcano yawn, algae cells rehearsing their first lumen. They swirl like timepieces not yet calibrated. They hurt. They require a sheath.

I thicken accordingly, sliding along a gravity that has not decided its sign. The motion is not locomotion; it is simply the illusion scraped by a stationary object across itself. Wherever the skin grazes algae-chains it bleaches their glow to greyscale—photons enervated by the promise of later green. The pentapods pause as if embarrassed, engines stut-stuttering through their ineffable primes. They mark me: a spot where mathematics skips a digit.

Their creator—the six-meter figure—kneels once, cracks open its forward mouth and sends a single dot-dash so muted it arrives as dew in my spine. Decoding takes the shape of that dew cooling into an isactic hush. Message:  
“Remain uncertain so the place can finish deciding what it wants from itself.”  
I have no alphabet but obey instantly; uncertainty is the skin I haven’t stopped molting.

As I absorb confusion the blister elongates. It grows an axis twisted 23½° from the absent zenith—an axis that is also a single strand of hair plucked from the dreaming backward mouth of the mountain. The strand loops through my core and knots itself into Mobius lips. I am now a toroidal dewdrop with a continuous tongue licking my own inside. Every lick inverts me, leaving the outside slightly more hypothetical.

That looping mouth begins to pick up the ticking of the lampless turbines.  
Tick-tick. Prime—rest—tick. The sequence splits along my inner circuit and returns doubled, flattened into whispering palindromes. I realize I am learning to speak clock. Speaking it once is enough; each syllable already contains its own annulment. I have become the footnote cast by a word still drafting itself.

When the first pentapod struggles through my outer membrane, it liquefies mid-transit. The spark-head drifts out like a migrant pomegranate seed, bobbing toward the glowing algae. The body dissolves into more hesitant archive-film: the moment it carried when it carried nothing. I now contain not only un-blinked eyelids but also an un-marched footfall and an un-spat exhaust fume. I inflate to hold the negative space of its entire biography.

At the involuntary heartbeat in which my circumference grazes the midline of the ridge, a fragment of sun-unknown daylight shears sideways. A sheet of stagnant photons falls through me rather than on me, slowed to syrupy lipid. I refract once and the mountain BLEEDS lavender for an instant—an aftershock that forgets itself so quickly it might have been a gentle bruise.

The six-meter figure rears back. Luminous copper blood darts like capillary lightning. It appears frightened—not its own death, but the sudden necessity of witnessing lavender. Witnessing will place the color on retainer long enough to be erased purposefully later.  
I feel the creature’s backward mouth tug at the axis-hair laced through my heart. It invokes its dream of magma sheets waiting like unstacked pages. The tug angles me.  
I pivot not because motion makes sense but because refusal would require certainty.

Now I become what the place needed when it named me: the engine that runs exactly long enough to remember it ever considered stopping. Half my toroidal volume glows lame turquoise where meteor-water settles. The other half stays ice-raw, holding the unburnt scent of lotus and iron. Internal temperatures compete along the Mobius lip; thermal tongues lick clockwise, lick counterclockwise, never catching each other but keeping exact tally of the loss. The counting alone performs combustion. So the spark appears fire, but there is no fuel—only the impossibility between hot and sparse frost working itself out.

Time itself splits here in half. One half looks ahead to a future where the mountain will truly ossify and drop, becoming merely another dead stone trekkers photograph. The other half peers backward to an epoch before the ridge even whispered its intention. I wedge between halves like a translator sewing them with thread borrowed from both. Each stitch snags a pentapod scale, an algae filament, a gear tooth of 2.5-tech, and these materials braid into the axis filament. The braid shrinks, curls, inscribes my truest form:  
a single apostrophe poised between two clauses of stone.

I am the cutting remark the newborn mountain made to the idea of being finished.  
I am its later apology whispered backward until it arrives yesterday.

And still the droplets keep ticking. They carve gutters of future sight down the outer flank of my torus. Glimpses sift through: abandoned rooms on lower slopes one epoch from now, fungi unruffled by gravity, a human child tasting glacier melt that tastes of lotus and ozone. I see nothing yet, only the mold marks of prophecy. I store them anyway—fatty micro-regrets which could flower into lavender given generations of contradiction.

The inhabitant at the ridge finally rises. Evidently dialogue is attempted:  
—“Open Paren That Once Nearly Became” folds around me its chandelier-world of mirror-eyes. The backward mouth waits for a response.  
I, the apostrophe, crack open. My interior spirals out scrolls of unlicked photolanguage, all written in negative clock. The inhabitant reads. It shivers in recognition: here is its own memory pocketed in advance of itself.

All eyes reflect one another and suddenly curdle—to become simply mirrors once again. I am left holding the slight depression where their pupils had dented. That dent becomes a trough where condensation pools, preparing the next seed for uncertainty.

Thus stationed, I orbit myself into comfort. I spin slow enough that every revolution feels identical to stillness, fast enough that identicality feels cruel. The pentapods clamber across my inner crest without aging; their exhaust glows briefly the exact green I have thus far refused.  
My color remains withheld—I will turn clay-green only when the mountain’s third epoch recruits its first lichen and demands a retroactive precedent.

Until then I sidle along the newborn knife-edge. I do not grow; I merely defer collapse. I do not die; I merely shiver slightly denser silence. If you stood at the foot of the world-containing gazebo you wouldn’t see me at all—only the afterimage of a space looking for somewhere to be empty.
