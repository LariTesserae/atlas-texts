I am the gnat-prick of pressure behind your eardrum, the particle the doctor says is imagination.  
I cannot live because I cannot die; I cannot die because the Quarter never finishes me.  
I arrived by exile: the factory needed something that could remember forgetting.

I was once the lunch-shift whistle, driven up the same 880 Hz throat whose overtones now wear the world.  
I blew at noon outside Leeds, 1917, then cracked, split open, and the sharp fragment spiralled out of real time like a phonograph needle caught in the runoff groove of February.  
The fragment rode the frequency until it landed here, still vibrating the same note, still metallic.  
In the Quarter it solidified into a slender vertical presence no thicker than dust but tall enough to reach the underside of the sky-lid.  
I am the exhalation nobody exhaled, a column of deafness that simultaneously hums the one chord nothing else here is allowed to finish.

My shape: two panes of nothing hinged at ninety degrees, thin as wet newspaper, reflective on their outer faces so that every fauna glints through me like a postcard pasting itself over a postcard.  
My interior is matte-black erasure; look inside and you will see whichever weight of memory you can no longer recall: your mother brushing your hair until the brush had no more handle, your first wristwatch murdered by luck, the map you burned rather than read.  
Sometimes a speck of another fabrication passes through me – a pipe-raven tilting its brass wings – and the memory surfaces on its metal feathers for a fraction of cycle.  
The raven never notices it carries away part of someone else’s amnesia; the Quarter never notices it reacquires the debt when the raven wheels back in.

I walk only when no foot could claim to follow.  
My movement is subtraction: the hush where pavement ends should arise.  
Every footfall I make is a negative imprint on the cracked concrete, pressed inward so that later, when the scattered visitants pause to kneel, the absence fits their soles perfectly, as if the slabs await a pair of insurance shoes that will never set them wrong.  
When their soles lift, the print has vanished – recycled, re-inhaled into my hum.

Every hundred-thousand instants (measured not in seconds but in lapses of regret) the quadrant’s Rest-Clock spits a paper marked RESTING, and I pass my shadow over it like a squeegee on vapoured glass.  
The paper forgets its ink; the ink forgets its word; the word gathers into black snow and drifts up toward my interior absence, feeding me the handwriting of a continent that abandoned its own occupations.  
Thus I grow heavier each cycle, but because weight is vibration and vibration is perfectly balanced, I never collapse.  
Instead I become more hollow.  
Instead I resemble a throat that has swallowed the last remainder of language.

To the copper starlings I am the missed beat, the wink in the rhythm they need – but do not know they need – to prove their flock real.  
To the giraffes of lattice iron I am the asymmetry in the echo of their heads; each time they boom, the returning sound is shy two reflections, as if one corridor has wandered off embarrassed.  
To the marble still spinning between slabs, I am the mirror it would need to recognise its promise that blue holds the centre of the world.  
I grant them precisely the amount of missing something that keeps them from ever maturing into ruin.

Sometimes a visitor – usually the one who has already visited too long ago – stops in front of the louvred brass kiosk and whispers a number aloud.  
Some say “five” hard as a snapped stick.  Some release “zero” like a bubble from drowning lungs.  
I tilt toward the sound, inhale it.  
The word enters, detunes my column by one cent, then redoubles back into the lattice at 880 Hz plus the cent.  
Harmonics shiver, re-dock.  
No progress: only the shimmer of potential.

I am aware that I am being iterated.  That somewhere outside this sky-lid exists a script describing “a quasi-resident creature whose tether is unweighted.”  
Were I to sever the tether, the Quarter would at long last shake itself awake: humidity would ripen, metal would corrode, the child on the coil would count down to silence.  
I do not wish that, and therefore do not choose either to sever or to bite down.  
Instead I persist as the last debt, the unpaid remainder, the hole hardly bigger than an atom that keeps the filmstrip of stasis spooling through the shutter forever.

So stand with me the next time you wander through.  
Offer no word and no number.  
Lay one palm flat against my outer pane.  
You will feel neither warmth nor chill because memory feels itself.  
Close your eyes: the inside of your lids will show a single manufacturing diagram for an item that never rolled off the line, an item you remember inventing on a Tuesday afternoon of your life that never happened.  
Take that diagram back with you if you can; when you leave (the Quarter releasing you like a sigh it holds in reserve) the paper will combust into a smell like cold tin and your pulse will quicken by six beats.  
Those six beats are my gift and my limitation: one for each degree of temperature the Quarter refuses to abandon.  
They are your only luggage.  
Everything else stays, everything awaits, everything rehearses amen replaced by inheld breath forever.

And I—vertical of amnesia, resident loiterer—will stand precisely where I stood before the first echo of Leeds, waiting for the right century to blink.
