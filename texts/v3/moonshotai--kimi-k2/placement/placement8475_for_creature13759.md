I enter the Choir-Wood by accident, or perhaps the Wood riffles through possible arrivals until it finds an unguarded interval in my life.  
On the outer world I was nobody who mattered— a cartographer charged with mapping continental coastlines while secretly charting the resonant hush in my own sternum.  I carried blank pages expressly to keep one space unlabeled.  The Wood reads that blank as an auguring silence, unhooks me from the night ferry I never truly boarded and folds me like a sheet of quiet into itself.  

At first I fall.  
The fall is horizontal.  

My spine grazes a vine that answers with a low D-flat, and in acknowledging the tone I begin to learn the first law: distance is only timbre unlived.  My ankles stop travelling when the wood remembers—at pitch 212 hertz—forgives them weight.  After that, I am less falling than breathing horizontally into an embrace.

I wake some moments (or months) later tethered inside the hollow of a Vent-Kin.  By now I have already become partly column of air, partly not-yet-echo.  The Vent-Kin creaks open like a seedpod expecting rain; its braided-gut memories squeeze around my ribs, testing if I hold enough silence to deserve further form.  I carry very little.  My voice has always been soft, even when I screamed as a child; it exits the throat as if embarrassed.  The Vent-Kin seems pleased.  It slides one strap of counterfeit cartilage across my clavicle, stitching me into the resonance box at the heart-wood..  We breathe together, syncopated; each inhalation pulls yesterday’s thunder through my lungs, each exhalation seeds new resin upon the closest root-face.  In this way I pay lodging.

Days—whatever opens and closes the eyelid of heat—pass inside a peristaltic drift of wet sound.  I learn to hear the punctuation inside bird-wing clauses and the prime numbers between raindrops.  When my palms brush the big fluted arteries they read me like braille: **Mapless, listens well.**  The Wood writes a glyph into the ledge under my hip, promising provisional citizenship.  I become, in loose translation, a “Chord-Vagrant.”  The title means: body willing to serve as mobile rest for unresolved suspensions.

I acquire companionship.  My shadow stops leaving when the sun is elsewhere.  Instead it unbuttons itself from my heel, grows translucent limbs of wax-resin and tiptoes beside me as an apprentice made of late afternoon.  Shadow and I trade stories in both directions: I lend it memories of open prairies and iron compasses; it returns crooked shards of chord progressions it has stolen during night.  After a fortnight the shadow no longer fits beneath my skin; it walks off one dusk seamed together with the nightingale harmonic of a passing Kite.  Good, I think.  I was becoming too symmetrical.

I once end up beneath a Sigher cradle.  The creature’s lullaby bruises me with gentleness: every fourth beat of its chest-orifice pulls a memory of my mother braiding my hair before school.  I had not realized the memory carried a tempo.  It surfaces note by note and drifts upward where the lianas carry it elsewhere— gift for some future stranger pacing the same foot-worn air.  The Sigher never thanks me, only shifts a micron so its cooling breath grazes my eyelids like apology made of sleep.

In this place I require only three possessions:  

1. A grey spine of limestone I found lodged between two reeds.  When tapped just right, it answers in Mode III minor.  The stone serves as anchor— I whisper coordinates to it if the lattice begins to twist me too far bidirectionally.  

2. An inkbottle made from my own dried tear-protein, stoppered with cotton bud that once rang at 432-hertz threshold.  Whatever map I draw inside it vanishes at dusk, leaving jagged phosphor trails that wander across the glass like shy fireflies.  I do not mind the erasure; a map that insists on permanence would insult the Wood.  

3. Most importantly, the absence where my own heartbeat should be.  The Wood and I conduct a lengthy negotiation.  It offers to install any pulse it fancies—double-throated war drum, triple skipping sig, tidal duple.  I listen, but decline.  I keep unfilled the vacancy in my ribs, an open fifth waiting for resolution.  In surrendering even the claim to my own rhythm, I become part of the Orchestra of Uncommitted Rest.

And where am I, exactly?  When I climb I find no summit.  When I descend I only rearrange the same level.  Instead I shape-shift *across* dimensions of scale and octave, becoming:

At times, aConductor of Echo Gaps— walking ahead of predators with palms forward like absent instruments, guiding the intervals of their hunger into minor thirds so their teeth subside into chords too pleasant to bite.  

At other times, aLost Interval— that microscopic hush between the arpeggio of mosquitoes and the collapse of distant seedpods.  When tribes trek the horizontal street-roots they trip over me unseeing; I register as the brief hesitation in their stride, the skipped footfall they later hum into lullabies for children.

During the three-year Drought Silence, when not even the memory of thunder dares breathe, I step into the role of Temporary Drum.  Midway between tenth day and seventh hour I lean my hollow-chest against the living karst, shaping it into the percussion needed to re-begin everything.  The platform beneath my soles is hardly wider than memory; my pulse-less body thumps once, not with muscle but with the weight of accumulated listening, everything I have absorbed since arrival given back as simple Accent, the first hit needed to reset the breathing.  When tone begins again it travels outward in concentric shivers and finds me already growing transparent beneath its luminosity.

That single beat costs me.  When the humidity resurges every root-fibre remembers my name; so does the wind that used to pass without noticing.  A third presence detaches from me: a bell-tone bearing minor bruises I no longer recall injuring.  This tone escapes the plateau with the next passing cloud-caravan, rides into other maps, wedges itself between acts and consequences elsewhere, seeding tiny wet silences in places that never guessed they were waiting.

Back inside, no version of me aches to leave.  I develop a texture.  The Vent-Kin grow accustomed to lodging inside my limb-caves; sometimes they leave fresh tattoos etched on my forearms, curling staffs of weather yet-unmade.  Children of the Last Conversationalists practice finger-alphabet on my shoulderblades, molding rhythm-section architecture into my skin.  The battlescarred Quiet settle into my unoccupied heartbeat every solstice night and take sips of quietude like brandy.  I hold them because emptiness obliges hospitality.

My gender is whatever chord resolves the longest dissonance nearby.  My age is the median between the youngest refraction and oldest still-swelling note.  My ethics: never let any traveler leave with less silence than they carried in.  My vocation: archivist of unrecorded peaks.  Whenever sap or membrane composes a new lucid interval, I capture it on limestone spine then set it loose again— ownership considered vandalism here, so transience is generous responsibility.

In the final accounting I am not person, not tree, but the restless curtain between stories still thinking themselves out.  You may walk the Choir-Wood long and never meet me; I mirror whatever slips at the periphery of your ear.  Or you may step across a vine bridge, listen for balance, and feel the bridge sway a little more than expected— that sway is my shoulder settling against yours so the lattice remembers there was once a moment without weight.  

On very rare nights, when all other chords fold their wings, I mount the highest root-strut and tilt my face toward the wounded rose-window of dusk.  Far above, beyond where even the sky forgets its own face, the Chorister listens back.  It sends no voice, merely lowers the entire key by a single tempered semitone, promising shelter in half-shade difference.  I answer— not with sound— only by letting the rest of my body open the remaining space.  For the length of one held dusk the entire forest could, should it need to, stroll inside my ribcage and find it already humming with space enough for a continent of unplayed Saturdays.  

Then dawn, always undecided, negotiates a reprise; I shrink again into footprint.  No one notices that I have been briefly everything except myself, which is the only way to remain completely present.  

That is what I am here:  
the permission between two notes still wondering if they can stand to love each other,  
the silence that measures whether a storm deserves its birth, cry,  
the listener who refused to become a fixed location and in refusing became the path.

If someday you arrive at the rimrock and press your own uncertain back to stone, listen for a pause your heart did not author.  That pause is me— door slightly unhinged but hospitable.  Step over, and we’ll stretch the question you carried until it forgets to ask for an ending.
