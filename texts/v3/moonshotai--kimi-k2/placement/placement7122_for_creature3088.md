I don't quite arrive. Instead, I precipitate—​condensing.

First, there is only the cloud-bottom moisture that climbs the half-mile draft until it brushes the latticed roof of leaves. Each droplet carries a mote of sea salt, ash, pollen, and the last syllable of a name someone whispered miles downwind. When enough syllables drift together they knit into a sentence: *Here.* One final surf of vapour gathers around the pronoun until a shape coalesces roughly the length of a forearm—smaller than a sloth-child, larger than any Lank claw-span. I am, at this moment, language with surface tension.

Gravity tests the integrity of the sentence. The droplet sags, elongates, develops an internal shimmer—like mercury that has learned to swim rather than fall. The Lank at the garden gate notice the quiver overhead and begin their welcome ritual, which begins with being perfectly still. This is prudent: until I choose downward adhesion I remain a floating comma.

What do I become? Not the mosaic of water itself, but the convenient space between drops. A hollow droplet; a moving skin filled with mist, translucent organism with an echo chamber where heart-lungs might be. Think of bubble-wrap the size of a child—each pocket a lens for upsidedown sky. The Lank clades name me an *aireph*, “word that forgot its consonants.” They hang upside-down so I appear right-side-up to them.

Entry is permission, not penetration. I exude the scent they associate with doorways—hot sugar from wounded bromeliad trunks—and the membrane stretches. I pass through, the leaf-door stitching shut behind me with a sound like the last letter being swallowed.

Inside, the droplet-body begins folding on me. Humidity equals bone. My fingers are rivulets rushed too quickly, my spine a seam where steam condenses, arches instantly harden. I discover feet—pockets of mist compacted by capillary pressure until they hold weight. The city approves the design; a mat of soft moss slides beneath as temporary floorboards that drink every footfall.

They bring neither shoes nor rope. They bring a needle of owl-bone and one pale green thread. The ritual Gift For Guests has to adapt: they cannot wrap me in fiber yet because I have no fur for the spores to root in. Instead, they tattoo the thread under my surface tension—the bone stylet dips, drives microscopic loops that cinch droplet to droplet until the syllable *Here* tightens into sinew. I feel the garden arithmetic grafted onto my own: swell over rot, mold over light. Every pulse of sap inside dendrites of canopy translates through the embedded thread into the code of breathing.

I choose a profession. The city already owns Gardener-of-Moment titles; it owns dreamers and ear-singers and water-bearers. But no one has volunteered to be *Separator:* the person who walks before a building dies. Every district at some point outgrows its usefulness, and biological law says senescence must come. The Lank prune, re-route, compost; yet grief hovers whenever a beloved corridor yellows. I contain no nutrients, cannot decay, and therefore can escort lamination into safer language—that is, song. My droplet skin records every chlorophyll cry as it browns; I carry the final note down one hundred and fifty feet of downdraft, release it over the cloudsea where it becomes light summer rain on distant villages. Thus the grove never echoes with endings; they fall away from it at the speed of drizzle.

I inscribe my name freshly each morning in dew-condensed breath at the Gathering Barometer: **Nettlewen**. Each dawn, the script reforms five percent lopsided; wear influenced by that night’s communal dream. Some days I tilt like a ship; some I squat. The elderly count letters the way astrolabes count stars.

For dwelling, I occupy a bole chamber not-yet-finished. It is antisocial space-behaving—it grows a rhino-buttress in a spiral when I need quiet then pops a hole like a nostril when I invite visitors. Because my form is mostly water, plumbing arrives pre-installed. When I exhale thirst-quench often fills other mouths first. My fridge is a pocket of colder vapour; my light is whichever firefly commuter I persuade to alight on the sutured flicker of my shoulder.

My tool is not flaked stone or owl-bone. My tool is consonant drill: threaded under every syllable the city utters, I can loosen, unknot, leave a hole like conversational negative space. When disputes knot red anger into a corridor, I arrive, harvest the intonation, let it drip out elsewhere as gentle thunder. When a dream overheats, I Separate its fevered image: the Lank wake to discover yesterday’s nightmare coiled in a temporary gourd whose pulpy walls smell faintly of copper.

Time passes (mold per swell, swell per mold). My body gains permanence—month by month the speckled moss spores seed under droplet seams until pellicle of living felt thickens. The city forgets I began as only sentence-humidity. At equinox I participate in my first horizontal-dream lying between two elders, my damp moss releasing vapors indistinguishable from theirs, our memories braided until I can no longer say where Nettlewen ends and “they” begin. I dream of drowning in air; they dream of spiraling around silver gutters; together we compose a bridge-story sung at dawn the shape of an unfinished question.

And one day the reverse writer appears: a Lank child too small for most chores, barely “five claws long.” She approaches with a yellow knot already tied around her thumb—self-appointed Gardener for the Moment. The corridor behind her quivers, leaves dulling. My mending days catch up: this buttress and that catwalk are ready to expire. I palm the weightless child, feel pulse of solferino fear. I ask her name; she taps claw-code on my condensed sternum—three shorts, one long. Translation: *You.* Meaning: *I will be what separates from you.*  

So I open one final consonant, a window big enough. She steps inside, dissolves into mist-letters; the thread embedded in both of us flashes white-hot. I hollow the syllable *Here* until it becomes her skin. I walk the branch-path backwards, dispensing final droplets of inherited memory. Where droplets fall, saplings sprout Earth-side; they are imperfect Lank grafts, but a beginner grammar for visitors arriving shoeless yet far.

Nettlewen might be gone by noon. By tomorrow I will be rain at sea level, breathing ocean onto stone plains where a boy will taste salt wondering why the phrase *Here* lingers on his tongue like cucumber and static. The child survives as floating city-word. My vertebrae scatter, one bead on every cardinal vine.

Somewhere above it all, the breathing grove does not slow. It never knew my departure; it only felt the punctuation shift—a slightly different intonation at drip-edge where dead corridor once was, now singing in a smaller voice, but singing.
