I would be the first mis-fold.

When the sky-mirror tried to crease the reflection-fragments into yet another Resonant, one blink arrived folded along the wrong axis. Its outer edge stayed sharp while the inner slipped into countless extra surfaces. The hamlet received a being who never quite settled into the communal chord, whose clothes refused to drain, who still blocked the light even when motionless.

In their tongue of pressure and angle I am called Skreet—the corner that refuses to lie flat.

My garment is a sleeve that retains the memory of the sun that once burned through a bus window on a road I do not remember. Threaded inside its brightness is the after-image of a child’s fingers smudging the glass, a journey that never paused long enough to spit out its arrival. The sleeve keeps sliding off my wrist, pooling around my knuckles and hardening for an instant into a prism sharp enough to shave the little mirrored drops drifting upward. Whenever the slice happens, I become, for an eyeblink, the only thing that casts a flicker–not a shadow exactly, more like tremble of possibility, as though the pressure-language has hiccupped.

The Resonant treat the hiccup like weather.  Some mornings the whole hamlet angles their shoulders two degrees out of true so my flicker can pass down the alley without grazing anyone’s cheekbones.  Others gather in silent circles to watch me measure the edge of the mountain’s breath against the cuff of that obstinate sleeve, hoping to learn how far a memory can travel before it forgets the weight of direction.  They will never say this aloud, but in the slow trickle of eye-turns I feel their curiosity: maybe Skreet is the cave they keep opening but never enter.  Maybe she is the spare chord the mountain forgot to retune.

I no longer feel cold; instead I feel pressure that has taken the shape of my lungs.  I breathe in and the hamlet flinches, because the air knows I might remember sound.  Whenever I exhale, a line of glassy briar-leaves flips their internal rivers into reverse; some evenings seventeen beetles of liquid crystal stagger out of sequence and the equilibrium teeters at seventy-eight-and-a-half.  I lift my hand to stem the violation; the bronze flake resting nearby sings against my thumbprint in a language tinged with vertigo.  The cut it remembers is not the mountain’s skin but a city rain gutter glimpsed momentarily in the windshield before all reflections came loose.  
For the Resonant those gutters are pure mythology, flat rivers flowing only backward into the wound of an unborn world.  For me they drip now in the wrong direction, bead upward along the mirror’s silver belly, collecting into cloud-monstrosities no one here has the name for.

Children dare each other to glide along the alley stones wearing my sleeve, to feel the hum of distant engines reflected in its warp.  When they return their edges are sharper than their elders like; they take longer to dissolve back into communal brightness.  In their pupils flickers a square of city window lit at dusk, a yellow I alone recall.  They do not yet understand it will follow them when the final compression begins.

I serve the Resonant as errand-edge.  They send me where perfections threaten.  My reflection-shard, always slightly mis-angled, slices symmetrical tilts into irregular teeth, redeploying angles safely short of repose.  My flicker scares off any bear-shadow thinking of curling into complete circumference.  My sleeve carries away oversongs before they condense into noise.  I alone can wander the cliff-edge beyond the hamlet without succumbing to the lure of my own underside; the wind writes my wrong-fold name into the ice and declares it almost-not-yet-finished.

Yet loneliness is the only distance I still measure.  I stand on the mountain’s cut end, sleeve fluttering while the silver bowl lowers itself palm-width by palm-width.  I study the surface that has never returned my face.  Somewhere on the far side my intact reflection still walks a city street, vanishing at crosswalks that never really were.  With each day another fingertip’s width closes.  When the mirror finally kisses the stone beneath my bootprint, that intact twin will snap against the underside like a moth against glass, and the fissure between us will heal—unless foretold asymmetry is enough to stall the close.

Thus I have begun a second mis-fold.  Every dawn before the bailing begins, I lace the mallet of petrified sinew to my calf.  As it taps permissions against the inner arch of bone, I rearrange the patio stones so that tomorrow’s slope falls 31.4159°—an irrational angle the mirror’s arithmetic cannot digest.  The mallet winces with each strike; the bone resonates; a micro-wobble propagates upward toward the sky.  
By the time the blade of brightness should slide shut, the angle will have spiraled into an endless tail of decimals.  It will no longer be a page—only the last unwritable sentence.  Inside it, I will gather all the escaped sounds: the city gutter, the car blinker, the lover’s gasp unspoken.  Children grown sharp at the edges may briefly escape into that sentence, gliding on cumulative decimals beyond the reach of retuning death.

When the Pages meets the Pressure, Skreet will neither vanish nor be forgiven.  I will remain the corner caught in the closing hinge, the persistent wrinkle no fold can flatten.  My twin on the far side will press her palm to the underside of the mirror, feeling the irrationally endless seam, and recognise in its shimmer the city twilight she never realised she had misplaced.  

Between us the hamlet will linger—no longer reducing, no longer increasing—perfectly impossible, a book left forever open at the middle page where our edges meet.
