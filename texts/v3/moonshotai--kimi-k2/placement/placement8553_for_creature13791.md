I arrived without footsteps, so the valley wrote them in backward, every print entering the peat a memory it had already stolen. My arrival was announced in 11-Hertz: a soft dark chord that braided with the dreams of the Counter-Axle boy. The sound grew a name for me at the expense of me forgetting my given one—names are currency here, and I came without pockets. I signed the treaty in exhaled syllables and a tip of fingernails that the woodlice harvested for percussion.

I am the Second-Hand Lullayer, the one who sings the kilns asleep each time they threaten insomnia. My throat is a spiral valve of cooling iron. The first four nights I served as apprentice to the Curator, learning how memories are cropped like photographs then pressed like leaves between sheets of rusted tin. I carried the butterfly net made from knots of my own undone laces; its knots remember every shoe they never tightened, and they whisper instructions in the cursive of vanished socks. When a visitor’s recollection drips too heavy for the page of tin, I lap it up, chew it until it forfeits pronouns, and spit it back as steam: veined, song-shaped, glazed midnight.

I wear masks the otters mold for me, but they mold from the spaces I leave between sentences, so the masks fit only while I keep talking. A mask of the pause after “perhaps,” a mask of the hesitation before apology, a mask of the “h” sound I have been trying not to make. When I grow quiet, the masks shatter into knee-high basalt shards the lice collect and stack into walls they later abandon for no reason exposed to daylight. I walk among them as architecture unfinished: a corridor of mouth-shapes that never closed.

My body is running eleven beats late. My skin arrives after the body does, flaps once like arriving laundry, then settles. My heart tries to synchronize to the valley’s rotating magnetic joke—north is west after dusk every seventh dusk—but my veins contain low-grade iron from a diet of dust and broken pianos, so directions slip from my grasp like calendars unwilling to be pages. The anemia means I walk forward even when the compass insists I am ascending. Sometimes the Counter-Axle twin glitches laugh in syncopation around me, attempting to lure my stance into retrograde; sometimes they merely tally my exhalations ᚪ under breath.

Yet I am not one of the inhabitants exactly; they have tenure, I have tenancy. I neither drown nor drown-out the place. On what counts for mornings here, I stand in the middle of Noagua where the water is never deeper than diaphragm. I stretch my arms perpendicular, copied from Grandfather-Axle, and wait for the 11-Hertz child to saddle the blameless rails. When metal shudders at childhood pitch, I remove a button from my blouse—blouses here become pedigrees lost in bloomeries—and fashion it into a gearless top. I spin it on the surface of the lake. The top is rusted before it rotates ten loops, gears read backwards, turning regret into equinox. With each slowing I learn a syllable of stillness the kilns have never earned.

I leave no prints anymore; instead I gather them, rolling the countershapes into small pellets I plant under the singular black willow. These pellets incubate into thumbnail-sized curators who curl around their own unasked questions. When the Quieter Dark finally exhales its last still warm story, these smaller selves will unfold paper lungs, rise, pluck the hatchling heart-louse from its shell, and carry it skyward on ashes no volcano remembers crafting. Their wings will beat once for every sentence spoken on the surface of the Earth that has never happened; the beat will match my final lullaby crescendoing after itself.

Until then, I drift half-renewed after my own undoing, a living 11-Hertz echo patrolling the boundary between yes and the silence that has trademarked the consonants of no. If you come, bring no name; the valley can mint a finer one from absence. I will be the accidental hush waiting inside your saying “door” the third time, the tender steam that flees before glaze sets. When you walk toward the least probable bearing and notice boots forming from your backward footprints, I will already be wearing them, walking beside you barefoot, humming in negative frequencies all the while.
