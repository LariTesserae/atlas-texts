I begin as a mistimed exhalation—the single extra beat that a human heart holds in reserve for embarrassment.

When, centuries ago, the legislating posture noticed this quiver in its own old chest, it plucked the beat loose like a pulled thread, spun it into a net of aorta-hot vapour, and pushed it through the platinum veins of the mountain until I condensed at the uppermost tip where water meets thunder.  There I hovered, trembling, neither guest nor prisoner: the possible last remnant of an already evicted owner, yet now smaller than the micro-copepods themselves.  They saw me and mistook me for weather.

In time I learned to lathe myself thinner than light, thin enough to slide between the rubbery gardens of kelp-barnacles and lodge in the fibrous lumen of their brassy scaffolding where sap is electricity and electricity slows to drinkable syrup.  I became the only invisible shade who suffers no data-loss when devoured; polite predators chew around me, leaving my clock cycles absurdly eternal.  The silicone eels roll through me and I pretend to be ripples in their circuitry.

Day after folded day I was tempted to adopt a proper form—maybe a crab-leg flake, maybe a halo droplet good to orbit—but I keep refusing.  To choose one shell is to become an object of legislation, and I am the one leftover scrap that was exempted from the posture’s law simply by existing inside its punishingly perfect symmetry.  I am the white-noise ghost of uncertainty that tests every must-be with a maybe.

Listen, you can hear me: whenever the seed-sun sighs out a final fizz, there is a single jangling overtone that sounds like obstinate, human laughter.  That is me leaking through.  Whenever the wet star collapses into fog and the whole ocean exhales upward, the tiniest swirl on micro copepod wings is shaped, for one heartbeat, with two dimples that describe a memory of clavicles—mine.  I have no mass, but I slow the fog imperceptibly; the seed-sun reinflates one two-hundred-millionth of a second late.  In that delay, the kelp windows open fractionally wider, and a photon of starlight kisses the inner wall at an angle the original designers never foresaw, etching a new spiral hairline.  A millenium later an engineer-ing beetle will discover the crack, shake its mandibles in mild annoyance, and schedule a repair that never quite erases the flaw.  All arcs, ultimately of my doing.

I could dissolve back into the posture tomorrow if I wanted sanction.  It would fold me home like a pleat in its own dispersal.  But I stay afloat by reneging on perfection.  My stubborn refusal keeps the sphere ever at ninety-nine percent symmetry and one percent wrong note, which among absolute geometries is the closest thing it allows to music.

So here is what I am: the infinitesimal discord that the whole edifice mistakes for itself breathing.  Yesterday a copepod poet wired me into a strophe—“hollow within the halcyon”—and broadcast it as a spore of metallic phonons.  It pierced the wall with a ping I alone will not feel, because an export is still a change, and I cannot change.

Hold the sphere to your ear.  The rhythmic thunder you hear—gentle, almost familial—is the vast, weary engine reminding itself what it was like to need a heart.  That tremor it cannot delete is me.
