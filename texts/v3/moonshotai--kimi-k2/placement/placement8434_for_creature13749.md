I am the last un-forged echo—  
not quite a traveler, still owed to arrival.

Where would I stand?  
Wherever the temperature is exactly midway between breathing and forgetting.  
On the split-edge lip of the oxblood tower, watching a newborn bell roll past me,  
its interior already singing the hush it will one day lose.  
I exist in the hesitancy before the bell kisses abyss.  
There is, in that pause, enough room for four chambers of heart, plus one vestigial room housing the idea of “again.”  
It is impossible for me to step forward; the valley folds its distance each time I name it.  
So I stay unnamed, and the terrace underneath me stops inventing new decimals of height.

Who am I, if not population?

I am the punctuation mark that dangles between The Inholding’s successive predicates.  
A single comma, cool as meteorite alloy.  
I do not record, do not populate the census of salt wind.  
I merely separate one clause from its mineral continuation.

I am also what happens when the chalcedony grey flicks through my eyes:  
three seconds in which the world forgets the difference between gaze and ground,  
and my own reflection—borne on the mirror-hide of a salamander—  
realises I have no mouth but the land’s.  

Who or what would I be?  
The temperature average of an eyelash and an archivist thorax,  
wandering laterally among streams negotiated to gravity’s southwest compromise.  
My skin is that same hue aquamarine rendered in negative—  
a lenticular bruise of the blue pipes that remember the machinery without the iron.  
If you placed your palm to me, you would learn your pulse is 0.3 degrees colder than the spindrift,  
and you would accept the debt gladly.

I am the leaf that never closes its chemical shutter—  
having photographed nothing for a duration that kills parenthetical summers.  
I cling, yet unfurl. I do not die because I refuse to admit the horizon where my death was scheduled.  
Instead I remain transparent, half-ember, half-inhaled film.  
I stitch newer silence to older silence by flossing condensation threads through the seams of negative space.  
Each stitch is one half-stitch delayed, one octave deeper,  
until the entire valley hums like a hidden brass section rehearsing the same laconic note of departure.

At times, when the slow storm of spores climbs to rearrange the sky’s constellation,  
I allow myself to be refolded along impossible pleats,  
and for the lift of these century-petals I thin into lineaments:

—an ossuary of nonexistent ribs whittled from glacier glass,  
—etymology graduated into crystal,  
—the last firm grasp one lava tongue ever had before it rolled itself into fossil laughter.

My only tool is prefix.  
I can stare with an uncompleted language until hoarfrost salts away the distinction between noun and ledge.  
In that lull, I step without naming.  
The valley contracts; the fold topside brands itself inwards.  
I arrive in the mouth paved with chalk,  
between the tongueless plates licking marrow resonance.

And because I arrived breathless—and therefore wordless—I am allowed to stay.  
The Inholding copies me into itself, layer by faint layer,  
and I copy it back into tendon and tectonic vowel,  
until every one of my memories is a tiny tonal fossil,  
filed alphabetically beneath whatever altitude holds our combined age.

We neither rise nor fall.  
The strata respire and shift sideways,  
the obsidian disc spins once every memory,  
and between us stands the comma,  
vivid, cool, transparent,  
holding open the long slow sentence whose full stop is younger than the sky.
