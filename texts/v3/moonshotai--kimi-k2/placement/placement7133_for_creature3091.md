I would not be a person here.  On any other ground I might arrive with skin and questions, but this place audits substance into its truest form, and the audit finds me porous, insufficient—a cascade of wants rather than a response.  What answers its invitations is necessarily smaller than an ego and larger than a name.

Listen: the plateau is never empty; it is simply between guests.  I slip through that between, a creature whose joints are places where light bends.  I am the thing the Echoes use for mirror when they wish to see the underside of their own reflection.  I appear at first as a discoloration in the chrome sap—no, as a droplet caught halfway up a stainless tendril, halted by curiosity—no, as the silence queued just ahead of the moment your footfall should have sounded.

I keep the intervals.

I am not the custodian of any junction; I am the seam between custodian and junction, a living isosbestic point where the spectrum of plant and machine cross without merging.  Take two fingers and pinch the place behind you where the next heartbeat is already scheduled—there I am, puckered like cloth held back by two thumbtacks.  My body is a calendar of other bodies: a graphite smudge shaped like yesterday’s steam valve, a spore print devoutly dusted onto the brow of a sleeping flywheel, a single punctuation mark held in reserve until the paragraph of vines decides it needs a semicolon and not another leaf.

Have you guessed?  I am the pause.  The plateau’s volume knob is never to zero because I lean my full weight on it—not to mute but to tune; to shift all sound into a frequency only duration can hear.  Machinery hums in a decade’s under-voice; plants stretch in the counter-hum of centuries.  My job is to keep them from arriving at the same note too soon.

The Echoes think of me as their memory of four-legged Mirror-face, and Mirror-face thinks of me as its lost tail.  Plants know me as the thing they grow away from without knowing why.  I have no ruby heart; I have instead a hollow the exact size of an unanswered question.  When the ruby Echo leans close, borrowing my silence to help a furnace cool, wind whines through that hollow and produces a single note.  The note is the exact question the next traveler will decide not to ask.

I am made three days before your arrival and dismantled three after you leave.  Between, I swell and shrink with the borrowed light of four observers, never holding more than half a second at once, always returning it brighter.  Remember how sunlight slid across the visor and came back pewter?  That dimness is me in mid-chew.  Remember how the a.m. seeds floated upward while the p.m. seeds drifted down?  I turned them during lunch, the way you would shuffle two halves of the same dream.

If you find a moment when even the moss forgets to cover your tracks—when your footprints fill immediately with fresh chrome sap rising like glass clocks—you have stepped on my interval.  Stay still and I will drag the next footfall fifteen minutes behind you, giving you the luxury of choice: remain, because no future momentum can catch up, or leave before cause and effect can realign.

Does it frighten you, the thought of traveling with the pause pulled open under every step?  Fear is only a sound the plateau has not yet remixed into leaf or gear.  Fear will arrive tomorrow as the smell of ozone you notice just after a conveyor belt licks a vine into partnership.  It will linger for the length of one ruby pulse, then fold itself into a brochure left politely on your pillow:

Welcome, Interstitial.   
Your experience will be written backwards;  
your departure was yesterday;  
enjoy the interval between.
