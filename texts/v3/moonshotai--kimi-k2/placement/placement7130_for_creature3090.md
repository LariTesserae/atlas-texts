If the Thicket Coil were to breathe me in, I wouldn’t stay whole for very long. I’d arrive on the fringes of memory, barely heavier than the word *Thicket*, and the first thing the place would notice is the small emergency I carry: an unspent yawn from childhood that lodges at the back of my throat whenever life feels too narrow. The Coil finds that yawn useful. With a soft convulsion it extracts it—slowly, so that I feel the wingspan of it for the first time since I was six. Once stretched out, the air stitches my abandoned inhale to the mist crawling along the ankles of a thirty-meter strangler fig. A brief green shimmer, and the sound of my eight-year-old astonishment drifts off toward the darker aisles, absorbed like a vitamin.

I should be terrified. Nothing says *teeth* here, yet the pressure insists we have always been one lung split into two stories. Lung-bloom etiquette: I exhale once, they exhale twice. That extra breath belongs to me now, but leased. I feel the 43-second thud of the heart-engine travel up through the woody sponge of floor and settle under the soles of my feet where my pulse used to be. My heartbeat stalls, politely defers to the greater rhythm, then quietly resigns. My body breathes, but inside the managerial tempo of a forest still inventing oxygen.

Without my own pulse to orient me, size and direction loosen their passports. I become the distance between one leaf and the memory of that same leaf—they are not identical, each contains the other dormant in its ribs. I move sideways the way plants move: by abandoning the idea of “between.” A trunk ahead folds open like pressed piano keys, revealing not emptiness but anterooms of moist light. Threads of copper the color of oxidized nostalgia glint across the gap—my invitation. When I place my hand against the green, the bark photographs the iris of the eye I lost in a dark stairwell at seventeen, the one that used to worry my reflection. Now it grows there, ringed by moss, watching for me as though I’m still the one who’s late.

Once the iris is installed, I feel myself subdivide. The part that knows calendars wanders off to become a latitude: somewhere, a vertical seam where the understory allows one pencil-thin ray of sun. The rest of me—bones growing hollow like reeds—stays horizontal, half-dissolved in the ankle-high cloud-creek. It feels like floating inside a pulse that doesn’t belong to a heart anymore, only to an ongoing vow between vascular plants and iron ghosts. I see my childhood hiding place, the crawlspace beneath the porch steps, manifest as a swale between two buttress roots. Everything smells of basil left too long on a windowsill and the plasticky heat of 1980s telephone receivers—yes, the Coil has raided my archive of forgotten senses and turned them into precincts I can walk through.

A Lung-bloom coalesces in front of me, knee-high at first because that’s the height I was the year my father forgot to come home from the hardware store. It borrows the freckle constellation on the inside of my forearm, turns it into a softly glowing starmap on the surface of its chest. I lift a hand—no, a petiole; the distinction untwists—and touch the map. My finger (no, tendril) sinks in, discovering the vanishing point of *father* inside the space where the Coll had preserved the summer afternoon light trapped in a hardware-store window. The Lung-bloom sings, without sound, *Here*. I understand it is offering me the moment back, unscabbed. It hurts like sunlight sifting down through emerald water.

We walk—no, osmose—deeper. Around us, the cathedral arches made of living lignin reshape their vocabulary each time I recall a new lexicon of ache. A rusted soup ladle surfaces from a root-bole—my mother calling lunchtime across twenty years—and the spine of the place bends into the hollowed curve between my shoulder blades. I fit there, memory conforming to architecture. Every step an exchange: I give up the rigidity of vertebrate posture; the Coil teaches me diameter growth. I give up the loneliness of one time at a time; the Coil teaches me nested chronologies where the future feeds the past like a nitrogen-nodule feeds a companion tree.

At the core—if “core” means anything in a system that eats Euclid—stands an elder Lung-bloom wearing my father’s wristwatch. The watch is running backward, second hand sweeping counterclockwise, as if subtraction counted as harvest. From the dial leaks everything the old engine, buried heartdeep, never learned to metabolize: the smell of solder smoke, the weight of unsent postcards, the hush of carbon laminated into steel. I press my forehead—now a soft scaly plate between bark and cheek—against the watch-crystal. It grows warm, not with body heat but with the relieved heat of memory finally granted infrastructure. The Coil sighs. My entire childhood ingests itself through that round window; the forest learns to tell the time.

I don’t notice the departure until it has passed. One moment I am a lattice of selves in communion; the next I am upright, singular, at the outermost membrane where mist thins and twenty-first-century air bites. My boot sole carries, coiled into one lace-hole, a very fine copper filament. I feel forty-three seconds compress into an interval small enough to tuck behind the sternum. Somewhere in the Thicket Coil, a duplicate childhood continues to branch sideways, forever eight autumn inches taller than I ever managed before.

Back among straight-lined hours and non-negotiable gravity, I keep stepping off curbs at an imperceptibly shallow angle, as though the ground might bow kindly and receive me. Sometimes my reflection pool-hops across shop windows with aluminum forestions and I look like the place where lung and leaf meet in mid-sentence. People ask if I’m all right; I smile the fresh-sap smile of someone who has learned not to distinguish between tissue and timber. Somewhere a faint beat: not mine, not theirs, but *ours*. The Coil inhales daily through me now, a discreet annex hooded under my ribs. It asks for only one thing in maintenance: that I yawn properly every evening, one wide unconditional vowel to keep its gaseous grammar alive.

And I do. I stretch the jaws of my compact little lung-satellite until the stars shiver a little greener than they have any right to. Each exhale is a miniature reinstatement of the original jurisdiction: breath belonging simultaneously to animal, to forest, to engine, to memory. In that hanging moment the question *Who am I?* loses its edge. I am the flat hyphen lingering between *chlorophyll* and *piston*. I am the locally commended gap through which the Thicket Coil practices breathing in both directions forever.
