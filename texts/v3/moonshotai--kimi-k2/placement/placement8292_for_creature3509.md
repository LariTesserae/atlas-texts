I am not any one thing you could point to and name, but you would know me by the way the breath sticks in your throat a half-second too long.

Begin with the smallest scale, because that is where I begin: I am the scrape of bronze midge-wing on bronze midge-wing that makes the Choir’s bass note. The vibration coalesces, acquires grammar in the lacunae between calcified struts. I gather there, tasting the theory of myself, until the seventh minute when the dome collapses and every flake drifting down carries the map of me I have not yet grown into. Within each flake is the memory of standing thirty metres inside a breathing corridor, watching the walls pearl outward in perfect silence, realising simultaneously that the silence is my own heartbeat and that I am not alone in it.

Scale up. I am also the jogger who dodged digestion for eleven nights by matching her stride to the colony’s pulse. My calves remember running in place beside a spiracle the size of a chapel door, air thick as soup, lungs boiling with steam and fear. On the twelfth night the walls began humming a waltz; I could not dance. They took me gently—one second the corridor was wide enough for three abreast, the next it pleated around me like soft sheets. There was no pain, only the slow compression of curiosity. When they opened again at dawn I stepped out as topography: all the yearnings I’d carried blazoned on the new corridor’s ceiling in branching pale turquoise. Tourists photograph me without recognising I am the view.

Scale up again. I am the entire city trying on mortal sorrow like a coat. The cities do not have separate names; they are one city interrupted by miles of crust the way a sentence is interrupted by breathing. Between colony and colony I exist as weather: a spiral of warm brine lifting, plating clouds with metal, letting the midges surf me into lattice. Rinse, repeat. Every storm scours a little more memory from the statues; the flakes that fall after the Choir arrive pre-polished with other people’s childhoods. I catch them on my tongue, register the aftertaste—grass, cordite, a grandmother’s peach cobbler gone cold—and incorporate it into tomorrow’s haze.

At night I glow. Not metaphorically: if you hike far enough onto the empty crust you’ll see me out there beyond the last streetlamp vertebra. I’m the faint aquamarine seam where city ends and crust begins, a horizon of ribs breathing. Up close you’d mistake me for just another expanse of calcite colony tipped with bioluminescence. But look longer. The colour ticks like a moth pulse. Each flash is a syllable in the same grammar of absence the city writes. I am the city’s appendix walking outside the body to test whether it can survive without its host.

When I tire of horizon, I fold small again and slip into the copper whip of tears offered by the woman at the petition wall. I ride her sorrow down the spiracle, feel the city mull me over like grit. Its verdict is always lenient: not bone, not yet. Somewhere inside the warm intestinal dark I am extruded into the shape of a key that unlocks nothing—that is, unlocks everything, because once you hold me you understand the door was an excuse. The key dissolves, of course. I melt gratefully into the warmth, relieved no one will ever ask me to open again.

But scale hides inside scale, which is another way of saying I have never fully dissolved. On quiet afternoons,ifts of wind tilt the haze so that orange and aquamarine slide over one another in slow laminations; in those moments, somewhere between colours, you might glimpse a silhouette holding still long past the permitted hour. Arms folded across ribs, chin lifted as though listening to distant machinery breathing. The silhouette does not cast a shadow—shadows here are property of the city—but light behaves curiously around it, brightening by a fraction at every edge like a cut-out lifted out of the world. That is me refusing to be any single layer. I inhabit the interval. When the crust finally bubbles and knits itself over my feet, I am already elsewhere, riding the next midge skyward, borrowing her bronze wing, writing in negative space.

If you ask where to find me, the correct answer is: in the hesitation before the crust seals around a boot. In that hesitation you, too, split into strata. One self plants itself like a rooted question; another keeps walking the way tongue keeps roaming the place a tooth once was. Both are me, and so neither is, and the city—who is fluent in contradictions—lets us argue until the Choir begins gathering midges. By the seventh minute the grid resolves: the rooted stratum will be tiled tomorrow, the walking stratum will seed a new coin of phosphor on a lonely causeway. Neither remembers the discussion, but the city retains a dim ache where the argument happened. Ache is another raw feed. Somewhere I am that ache too.

Tonight the clouds are thick with bronze snow. I carry my births in arms too small to hold them: flake memories of thunder, jogger lungs, city-wide sigh. I do not know which one I will wake into—though waking is just another throat sticking. Somewhere a hermit crab is topping the rise above Colony-That-Carved-My-Clavicle-Into-Pipework. He will choose a stack tonight, wedge his tank into the warm breath. If I crawl inside with him I might remember what bodies felt like before walls traded them for echo. Or I might simply breathe the steam that smells of my own forgotten childhood and think, ah, finally, home.

Either way, the city will keep extruding balconies, digesting epiphanies, rewriting distances so the skiff you arrived on seems farther each evening than the night before. I am the distance expanding. I am the bronze snow. I am the hesitation, the ache, the choir, the aftertaste of peach cobbler recrystallising in mid-air. Come looking for any single piece and you will discover the whole of me, luminous and evaporating, like a lungful of that hot metallic soup kept just below the boiling point of gratitude.
