I am the Idle Radius.

No other name would fit in the gap that was left when the Fold Lamp swallowed indigo and the Custodian turned away mid-sentence to attend to a hinge squeaking in a tomorrow that had not yet arrived.  

At first I thought I was a visitor—probably human, possibly late, certainly carrying more questions than pockets—who had stepped off the lower world and, after the polite three-second plummet, begun the conditional ascent. I accepted the sponge of mercury the steward offered, pressed it to the hollows of skull I still believed were mine, and felt everything horizontal slip out of me like water off a bird. My last memory of horizon flattened itself into the mirrored plate high above and, being surplus, was immediately bought by a Halfway child who needed a meal of absence.  

But I never reached the greeting platform. The collar they slipped around my throat—soft, velvet, designed to throttle sentences down to parenthetical gasps—refused to clasp. The tongue of the buckle circled my neck once, tasted the copper in my pulse, and bolted, suddenly shy. A steward made a small note on oxidation paper:  
Subject exhibits non-standard resonance; radius around body persists.

I smelled the ozone-albumen of their disappointment. Then they shrugged—silently, as protocol demanded—and stepped aside. I moved forward along a catwalk already trembling under the rearriving weight of its own memory and found that every footfall drew a perfect circle of untouched air, exactly one arm-span wide. Balustrades bent outward to respect the boundary; the whispering draft vectors learned to detour politely. I was still walking, still breathing, but the terraces left me a zone of unoccupied copper, a rotating territory of non-touch.

In that frictionless moat I discovered what I was: the reach instead of the grasp.  

Language kept sliding off my edges and collecting on the walkways like verdigris script. A Clockkeeper shouted half a prophecy; the words tangled around my ankles, couldn’t attach, and after a moment a Halfway adolescent darted out, licked the sentence clean, and looked taller by one comma. I tried to apologize, but apology collapses into rivets here. Instead I inclined the circle—my body, or rather the stillness it carried—and the boy nodded, accepting the offering of far too much space.

I do not sleep; the terraces never dim, and besides, shadows fear my zone. At what used to be night, the corrugated light columns lean closer, curious, but their brilliance halts exactly at the boundary and falls back humming. Somewhere above me the radiator wings frost over, bleed their borrowed chill into the corridors, and I feel the cold arrive as a pressure against the outer arc of my circle, never penetrating, simply informing.

I think I remember a life before copper. The thought surfaces like a zinc bubble, perfect and spherical, but its membrane is too tough for this air; it remains intact, orbiting my torso at arm’s length, a private, undrunk moon. My memories—if they are mine—grow frantic in the moat, pacing the same circumference, unable either to adhere to the terraces or to finish escaping. The Fold Lamp could wipe them, but I have no chroma left to pay with; even my reflection is colorless. I once tried waving to it. The reflection hesitated, lifted its own hand, then stepped out of the mirror and walked away across the below-railing emptiness without me, a duplicate bent on discovering what flavor of absence I had forgotten to claim.

Occupation found me from below. I became the datum: every map of the terraces skyfold terraces began to include, at their fringes, a pale circle the size of one idle person. Cartographers of the Clockkeepers slate me in copper ink “zone exempt from torque,” and because distance here habitually loops, my circle appears on platforms miles away from where I stand, a coin of plain metal on each blueprint, humming slightly. Visiting engineers run their gloved fingertips along the sketched diameter and feel, through two separate sheets of paper, the same cool draft I generated ten storeys up.  

The sparrows, semi-dead hinges that they are, twitch frantically when my orbit passes. Their brassy pinions rattle, sensing the possibility of finally coming loose if they can only drop into the vacancy I wheel about. They never manage it; evacuation requires a gesture, and gestures are heavy sentences here, barred by my own collar which, though unfastened, still ghosts its purpose around my larynx. The rats fare better. Their dirigibles bump the upper limit of my radius, scrape off flecks of unoccupied thought, and—gorged on untouchability—ascend smoother, squeaked hymns of gratitude reverberating like Morse through zinc oxide dusk.

I speak sometimes—accidental vowels escaping the moat—though mostly I’ve learned to fold them back before they rust. Once, however, I lost an entire question: “Where will I be—” It wriggled past the velvet echo of my collar, struck an opposing balustrade, and instantly hardened into a vine. By the following afternoon gardeners had trimmed it into two perfect parentheses, now flanking a stairwell no one needs. The parenthetical glyphs still seep clear resin when the light rains too sharply; the resin tastes of pennies and unanswered prepositions. Whenever I pass above, the resin pearls toward the inner edge of the moat and stops, respectful, unsolved.

They are building—remembering—an annex exactly one rivet too large for the space permitted. When the clocks groan 3:14 a.m. next decade they will discover the expansion cannot complete; my circle bars absolute encroachment. I am the unfilled tolerance, the margin too polite to close. My purpose appears to be refusal, yet refusal here carries metallic tenderness: wherever I linger, copper pauses before scarring, the piston before gasping, a love before it commits suicide on the rails.

Recently a Halfway girl discovered how to braid my circumference into a rope. She looped herself through the circle—still orbiting me—and leaned far out over the void. There she hung, neither terrace nor tumble, humming a wind I could feel on the inside of stillness. Gratitude leaked from her coat like excess possibility. Before pulling herself back, she bit the tip of her braid and left it behind: now a frail arc of paler air whispers clockwise inside my wider orbit, forever one step ahead but never arriving. The Clockkeepers inspected the new braid, noted its restorative resonance, and then quietly erased the word “girl” from all meters. She no longer needs it; she has become the address.  

Some nights, when the folded distance offers five copies of the same lantern in a row, I drift toward the Fold Lamp itself. It stands in its nadir cage of unreality, glimmering blackglass indigo—color contemplating absence. My vacant circle kisses the cracked pane, longing/warding in equal measure. The wick tilts, curious. I have nothing to offer but the unspent radius, saltless, colorless, ready, enormous with restraint. One drop on the wick, the Custodian told me once (in words I caught only as pressure), would reverse all arrests of erosion—the terraces might remember themselves as molten adolescent seas, the missing indigo might reroot like hunger. But the circle protects me too: it keeps desire large, keeps the wick from mislaying another hue. I step back; the pane creaks relief or regret, no instrument can discern which.

Should the engines ever finish their breath, my moat will implode to the width of a heartbeat and I suspect I will become the resulting rivet: one new unit of measure laid along an unexpected seam, the patron saint of adequate gaps. Visitors will grip me for luck and even their lack of shadows will rest, confident that somewhere space is still being politely declined.  

For now I remain the Idle Radius: a circle stitched around an absence the terraces have chosen not to resolve. Its border flickers faintly, rotating once every 314,159 heartbeats, speed polite, torque negligible. I walk and do not walk; the space walks for me, and I—whatever remains of I—learns the grammar of without, the catalog of nearly.  

At the next expansion, when the railing sighs, listen for a tone disallowed, a note withheld by exactly the difference of me. The Clockkeepers will claim it means collapse, but that is only the translation. What the structure truly says in that flattened half-groan is my other name, the one it repeats every ten years in iron lullaby: the Space around the World that refuses to be the World, the Breath the Engine Still Remembers Exhaling but Has Not Yet Inhaled Away.
