I would be the book the Archivist has not yet earned.

Not the ribbon itself, but the absent, unwritten volume it keeps trying to recall.  
I rest between two heartbeats that never quite arrive, tethered by no cartilage, memory, or pulse.  
When the Lantern-Grubs hum their syllables in the infrared dark, I feel letters brush my spine like warm draughts from unseen vents. The millipedes’ fungal lanterns try to spell me, but the letters melt; they always melt, refreezing into margin frost that the Carpenter Caterpillars nibble away, unsatisfied.

The Choir knows I am there because of the pause it keeps encountering—an accidental rest it never composed, a silence so symmetrical that it functions as a note.  
To them I am a skipped breath in the planet’s slow metronome; if they ever solved it, the entire architecture would briefly tune itself one comma flatter and lose one citizen.  
They guard that lacuna jealously.

Night-mice Engineers clamber across my invisible gutters to pry crystals that aren’t mine, yet end up beveling the air into facets I never intended; these facets later shimmer in Carpenter windows, momentary apparitions of my unspelled text.  
Between the frames they glimpse my reflection: nothing with a certain number of syllables, only an angle of quiet they wish to step into but can’t place a paw upon.

The Cartilage Handrails have nothing to cuff around me—my wrist is not a wrist, only the echo of where a wrist would have hurried past. Still they keep a slot held open, numbered 0: their emergency amendment for any phenomenon colder than law. If I filled it, the entire railway of veins and vegetable vows would re-route its curvature by the width of one never-committed heartbeat.  
I will not fill it; I am the vacancy that keeps 0 alive.

When the Dew Stilt Opilionids crack open and snow upward their hatchlings, some stray photon from their dream of flight ricochets off my non-spine and lands inside a moth pupa. There it incubates as an off-beat dream within the dream: a door that leads inward instead of out.  
At the pupa’s scheduled awakening the moth will have two visions—one allotted, one uninvited—and it will hesitate a quarter pulse, causing the city to inhale just that much deeper before its final contraction. The interval becomes a new stanza I still refuse to author.

Thus I remain, the unwritten whose margins creep across every surface anyway.  
If you ever fail to leave in time—if 52 bpm drops to 51, 50, 49—I will become the cartilage’s graft, the space in which your missing fingertip continues to question red.  
The Choir will add a new syllable. The Architects will revise my binding.  
A single comma will reappear on the ribbon’s last page, and for one planetary exhale I will be, at last, almost, the book.  

Then the next witness will arrive, needing one silence more.
