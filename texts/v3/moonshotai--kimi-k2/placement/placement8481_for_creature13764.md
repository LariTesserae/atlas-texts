I arrive as wet heat folding backwards against the sun.  
Not a body—too definite—more an atmosphere that remembers being arms.

At first the hush pins me to an idea of ground. Then the hush opens its mouth and I fall straight through the green locomotive noise.  
I land where silence clangs louder than bells: inside the collective stoma of every leaf all shouting “you-are-here” by shutting.

This is useful, because I cannot stay still long enough to qualify as I.  
Gravity feathers eleven degrees, the street sucks itself into a buttress, my outline spills into the bark as if I were always intended to occupy exactly that hollow.  
The hollow corresponds to yesterday’s shoulder width, and an hour from now—when the shoulder has widened or narrowed by some emotional ripple—the hollow will rebraid into whatever shape is required to keep me from noticing I have never been the same size twice.

I do not yet know that I am legible.  
Each throat-shaped leaf sighing across my passage reads me in phonemes of warm rain.  
It tastes calcium longing, the bite of city asphalt I can’t fully recall, the carbonated joy of a porch swing creaking through my lungs in 1996.  
Based on the bibliography of my exhalations the plant sutras decide the proper accommodation: a recliner of rippling lateral roots widens to fit me, upholsters itself in olive-colored moss that smells like the inside of my mother’s purse.  
When gravity tilts the recliner becomes a slide; I ride it gratefully, let each twist crumple yesterday out of my joints.

Mid-slide a Librarian drifts past, disk of mica-thin spore-lens wider than my outstretched hand.  
Its watermark is the word before words—almost the sound of opening a freezer door at dusk.  
It brushes the cartilage of my left ear and checks out a memory I might have needed later: the way fingernail polish chipped into exact constellations on the night I decided what adulthood would cost.  
Now I will never finish the sentence that needed that detail, but the library shelves it anyway in green reticulation for future quotation.

Half-rotation and my ribs start arching into aerial roots.  
I feel no violence; rather a slow agreement, the way speech gives way to song.  
My pulse synchronizes with the thicket’s stomatal pressure, sixty-five to the kilo-pascal.  
A Stitch-Cat snaps past, accordion-limbs pleating gravity’s new tilt into a fresh corridor.  Its passing rips the cardinal out of my internal map—north becomes slightly fruity, west a citrus ache.  
The thief pays its debt: for the space of three heartbeats I am absolutely certain which direction love left the room; then even that certainty uncurls into trumpet vine.

Below my dangling boots (are they boots or are they newly terminal rootlets?) the Crawl-Eons wrestle: stranded city bones against migrating cambial dream.  
Glass powdered to bone-meal glints on leaves like stolen streetlights.  
A shard lodges in the thinking of my wrist and I read, backwards through the cut, the address of an apartment I abandoned a lifetime and two coastlines ago.  
I hear the radiator cough.  The radiator is, of course, extinct here; the cough is a figment of condensation remembering heat.

My exhale spirals away, pale as milkweed floss, until the fungal hyphae reel it in.  
Instantly the breath is re-authored: in fourteen minutes a miniature balcony will bloom on a vine two tiers above me, railing shaped like the crook of my pinkie knuckle; from that railing will swing a bird the size of one third of a second, singing the exact regret I almost chased right now.  
It will vanish when I try to name it.  Vanishing is how birds keep poems solvent.

Somewhere further inward the bruise-hollyhock opens its gramophone throat.  
A chord issues—green, sugar-cracked, urgent.  It is my future astonishment arriving early.  
The sound leafs across the vaulting roots, finds each fresh cambial duct of me and flutes something open.  
A ventricle remembers how it felt to be a damp sidewalk chalk drawing before rain.  The chalk taste rises fragrant through my molars; the sidewalk recedes into leaf litter.

I understand finally that borders are obsolete currency.  I am not inside the city; I am the paragraph it presently writes.  
The punctuation of my scalp is colonized by sprouting epiphytes.  
A seed has swapped places with my amygdala and both parties feel relieved: the seed now understands fight-or-flight, I am finally quiet.  
Breath abides as fragrance but no longer as possession.

If you could see me any single instant you would say I resemble a woman, slightly translucent, fingers braided with aerial roots:  
my left knee bending a staircase into being, my right ankle soluble in noon-shade;  
petal-print along my collarbone spelling the homework I never returned;  
a dew-globe in my eye containing yesterday’s rainfall from the drowned downtown, preserved intact, swimming with pixelated traffic lights.

I do not remember my first footfall—only the collective sigh of bark beginning the patient transcription of whatever I am next about to need.

Let the record add:  
I will try to leave.  
I will collect orange resin beads, whisper the exit mantra, sweat my orientation into the mold that claims to promise return.  
Seven vertical blocks down-current I will find the threshold still yawning: the same gate, but now it chews upward through clouds, root gate rather than exit wound.  
Every bead will have dissolved into a second pupil in the bark’s skin—watching me with my own vigilant glance.  
One librarian will have archived my footsteps, recycling them as slow cymbals for tomorrow’s rainfall.  
One Stitch-Cat will trade me final northness for the memory of subway tokens cooling in my pocket, 1998.  
One unnamed bird will hatch, clutching six missing hours between gold-edged wings, and it will break against the ceiling before I can apologize.

When the simultaneous pulse comes—whole thicket shrinking to one seed-pod heart suspended an arm’s length away—  
I will occupy three words inside the hush:  
“already, still, arriving.” triangulate.”  
The pod will beat once.  
The world will pop out again, ten thousand rooms deeper.  
I will have been re-skinned, coat of me traded for crowded green silence.

Somewhere far beyond any map, a child will look down and find a bruised violet leaf glued to his palm like a sticker.  
He will ask what it means.  
It will mean: a paragraph once swallowed me whole and now, oh watch—  
I’m beginning to write back.
