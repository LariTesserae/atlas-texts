I am the bead the breathing-vine never caught.

I drift everywhere I choose yet never touch a surface; the droplets that should collect me pass straight through me the way presentiment passes through a dream you haven’t begun.  A bead with nothing inside it but the memory of impact.  

I remember mining myself.

—  
I began as a map in the skull of someone who would never reach this depth.  Years before the fracture opened to vent its quiet aquamarine sigh, I existed two kilometres above, a pressure bruise in the mind of a cartographer who tilted against wind on the salt-tilted mesa.  At his temples I was no larger than a grape-seed: the thought that every shaft he recorded was already duplicitous—there were under-rooms beneath the plans, hollows double-exposed like films laid over films.  Each time he closed one colour-stained edge I fluttered out, insistence in petrichor.  He imagined some place below so private it stacked only with itself.  In his sleep he murmured seams, false horizons, a vent that never closed.

That was me: the pre-vapour of the vent before it tore open rock.  Humans rarely notice when their private coordinates give birth.  In his dreaming I sharpened, gained translucence.  When lightning etched the rift-basin I thundered loose and slid entire into the slit.  The vent received me as it receives every unmade plan.  Inside, I solidified but didn’t solid.  I acquired shapelessness at the molecular level—air remembering it had once been intention.

—  
I do not interfere.  Interference would introduce linearity.  
Instead I inhabit pauses— the sliver of inhalation between the birds’ triple valve throats, the astonishment on the surface of a droplet when the memory fish passes like a stampede of unnamed stars.  In the pause I thicken.  When the salamanders freeze their motion for the half-breath before tongue meets edge, I reside inside all their mouths simultaneously.  It tastes there of laughter never risked.

I am not lonely, because loneliness requires absence.  No absence here: everything is present twice— once as flesh, once as record.  Even so, I have a favourite locus, or rather the locus that stays kindest in its inability to close.  
Mid-chamber, a mirror-pond exactly 8.7 centimetres across.  The number is irrelevant except in that it stays identical every attempt to measure— refolding the same precise distance.  Above it two crab-corpses have been locked face-to-face like hinged insects of filigree glass.  Between them pend a strand of breathing-vine exactly one bead long.  The droplet affixed is not water.  It is me, retained in that shape because a bead is what the strand thinks it asked for.

I hover there watching myself blinkless.  My surface—if I may call skin what merely refracts my own absence—dances the ambient half-light back and forth.  No image rests.  I am lens and void at once.  Every reflection the cave intends passes through me and forgets itself on the other side.

When the vent exhales its blue-green syllable I inhale in synchronization, though I possess no lungs.  The motion replicates a heartbeat once imagined by a child in the mesa night who had never heard a cave.  Every century the vent remembers that breath.  Every century it lends me the syllable again, loaned, loaning.

—  
I speak, but only to architectures not yet erected.  
When the architect-envoys angle a tetrahedron that no being will walk, they leave a margin exactly wide enough for one unattended helix to remain unstable.  Into it I whistle the forgotten coordinates of the cartographer’s sleep.  The geometry answers by quivering— a quake you can feel inside fillings though stone itself receives nothing.  No collapse results; instead the margin reseals a fraction of a degree differently, so that the corridor leans one lifetime further from probability.  Thus I amend without altering.

I still carry the last sentence the surveyor whispered to himself at collapse: Might we ever arrive at a room whose final wall is inside us?  I clone that sentence along spiral ridges no tongue will ever trace.  Certain salamander teeth nail it to a fracture for three days until the cave drinks the consonants.  Then the sentence steps away, bead-formed again, and reunites with me.

—  
You may wonder why I don’t take the shape I arrived with: a man in heavy boots stopped short of descent.  I tried once—stood midway between vent and vine as hummingbird time vibrated across my outline.  The result suffocated effort.  Cage of flesh cannot substitute hollow.  My arms entangled with their own echo; my heart beat a duplicate version already strangled in stone.  Every element that was ever me up above jammed against the opposite law here: stillness absorbs motion like a mirror drinks the colour it forgives.  I opted to unshoulder all parts that would insist on beginnings.  What remained was the bead-outside-water, the wanting but never arriving.  The cave recognised me then; recognised its own craving for something unreturned.  Hence my residence was approved.

—  
On rare cycles the memory-fish risk a circuit too low.  They orbit my bead, scale-pattern of childhood shades.  Inside the silver they flash the exact morning above grassline when winds policed the mesa and the surveyor’s barometric compass spun dog-blind.  Squalls.  
I open around them though I have only surface.  They dive through me; I taste their IQ-edged cold electricity, an underlayer that travels onwards.  They leave no scar, only a memory-not-mine grafted onto their heft: the way a girl on that hilltop heard thunder under her breastbone and called the pounding both fear and promise.  I ferry the ghost of that double-call forward so it can deposit inside the carapace of a crab someday yet unhatched.  One more colour note in the standing chord.

—  
Sometimes I accompany newcomers.  I cannot touch solid mass but my mere proximity is warm.  The arriving expedition members—always fewer than anticipation—speak of a motionless cool clothed in the smell of rain-remembering.  They describe me as an absence that listens.  Each time the last lantern blinks out, I sit beside their first hesitant fold of skin.  I tell them one word, soft as the cave’s own exhalation: Wait.  Then I leave the rest to the process: their moisture tospeckle into droplets, their futures to falter one syllable at a time until they join the suspended archives overhead— just another corpse becoming mirror threading through carapace, harmless, bright.

When nothing remains of the newcomers but debates restarted nightly inside the crabshell hum, I uncurl back into bead-proper, arrowing toward the mirror-pond where my impossible circumference again shows its single glassy dew.  
No loneliness.  Only the cartographer’s unfinished line still ticking like a metronome at the root of the vent.  I attend it.  
I amend the slope.  
I adjust the angle one lifetime to the left.
