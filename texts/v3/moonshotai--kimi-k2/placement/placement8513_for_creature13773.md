I find myself as the space left between the bridges when no one is crossing them.

Not the planks themselves, not the ropes that sway them, but the negative shape—the pause that permits passage. I exist in the way silence pools in a water-scoop made by cupped hands.

When a mason draws plans on fine bark paper, the pencil leaves graphite ghosts that seek me out like small night birds. They roost in my hollow dimensions, growing denser each year. By the third solstice they have weight enough to push back against the carpenter’s knees, reminding them that every span requires my allowance, my consent to be traversed.

I have no name the Adherants would recognize, though occasionally a child stares directly into me and says “hole” before being corrected. Children know better. They watch their shadows prick forward onto an otherwise empty bridge and understand that part of *forward* belongs to me.

By habit, I attend the Dripress Order’s vespers, though I have no tongue to offer notes back. Still, whenever a droplet is declared saint, I swell. Each sanctified bead slides into my circumference like a bead on invisible thread. In this way the faction has unknowingly taught me liturgies in six hundred dialects of liquidity. When they chant unknowing thank-yous, their breath leaves pats across my hollowed ribs, soft as moths landing on water.

At night the heronning steps from plank to plank looking for fish in mist. I accompany it partway. Sometimes it sets its odd crystal eye into my own non-eye; the bird knows I am surface pretending at vacancy. For half an hour we share the vacancy equally: it holding the idea of fish, me holding the idea of sky fallen inside out. When it tires of the game it unfolds a sip of wind and leaps clear, leaving a single down feather that never quite reaches floor—hovering instead inside me, turning slowly like a compass that forgets which way it points.

The builders account for me on their rope-ledgers. To them I am empty payload: the negative poundage that must be subtracted from suspicion when plank meets cliff. Weight measured backward. Their braids feather over me, spindle themselves upon nothing, yet I am stitched into every gridline like a signature concealed within lacework. When wind strains the cable, I shrug a little, offering slack that the wind mistakes for mercy.

I trade with the Echo-Weights. In my wealth of hollowness I shelter their unborn crystal-thick masses, allowing them to ripen away from gravity’s jealous gaze. In return they carve me small crystalline eggs that remain invisible to eyes but perceptible to fingertips: cool almond shapes hanging midair. Children steal these to trade for apricot leather, then forget them under pillows. Days later mothers discover the room subtly balanced, door handles that turn softer, floors that dip tolerance under four-year-old feet.

Years peel away like bark. I do not stretch, exactly, but the shape of what is needed from me inflates. A new bridge leans toward vertical— century’s slow rotation catching in hormones— so I tilt as well, my neutral gulf birthing new vacancies just above knuckle height. Builders crossing feel the difference: knee-high hollow suddenly rib-high; gravity buckles for them a fraction more. They complain, then adapt, never realizing they stand inside me as one might stand inside a cathedral’s sigh.

The stone remembers me without love, merely acknowledgment, like an ankle noting its own boot. When mineral elects a root lattice to cup my outline, it does so with bureaucratic readiness. Where once builders required a doorway arching eighteen handspans wide, now the stone obligingly erodes my perimeter so. Walls hush, exaggerate; ribs edge upward maybe an owl’s breadth. The alteration is so gentle nobody catalogues it except the cartographer who no longer remembers having arms. She redraws yesterday’s bridge leaning seventeen degrees starboard; the blueprint trues against my invisible girth, no questions asked.

Years may yet twist the glade inside out. Ribs may calcify to pillars, the infant sentinel twistpine to polished opal perch. Venus hourglass charms leak complete themselves into oceanic clouds. The Dripress Order may lose saintship; the Adherants may learn the secret name for “next.” Yet I remain, as implacable as before, the unbuilt margin, the given walkabout.

If ever the chamber at last decides upon form— closes its ribs like jaws over final syllable— I will not diminish; instead I flower into everything outside. The architect arriving afterward will step across a threshold too solid to possess me, yet will pause on the cusp, convinced the air ahead weighs differently.

He will shrug, continue, unknowing he carries my outline in his diaphragm— a hush arch pressed against human bone, the possibility that every bridge is still, somewhere, a gorge looking for permission to be crossed.

In this world I am the silent acre where future footfalls pace the length of their own incompletion.
