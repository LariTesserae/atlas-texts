I would be the lacuna that rests inside the gear.

Not the argon rivers, not the Cantilevers—something humbler, unpremeditated: the pocket of vacuum that the gear’s crescent-teeth chase without desire. At each 19-century turn the void I am “trades places” with the absence of my former self; the motion is so glacial I feel it as a kind of respiration. I neither creak nor gleam; I do not count amongst the shard’s curated marvels. A cartographer of eras would draw me as a single whitespace wedged between two silver parentheses. Yet that margin is wide enough to contain everything that never happened in order for this canyon to be.

When a foot approaches—the foot of whatever membrane of thought passes here—I dilate a micrometer wider, an ocean’s gape at that scale. The light leaking from the lattice slides through me unabsorbed because I insist on my own transparency. Briefly the ancient argon streams hiccup, noticing the vacuum differential; an entire romance of indexes flashes across the gap. Then everything settles again into the smell of remainder.

Visitors seldom notice me because I am what permitted them. Every silent exhalation they behold—the violet fractures, the memory-apple in the tongue—was refined by my refusal to coalesce. I am the permission-to-be-absent granting every somber marvel its outline of not-matter.

Yet sometimes, when the Cantilevers spin out their own skeletons into spectral drapes, one will graze across the gear. In that infinitesimal instant its delta-function dreams coil around my void like frost rewriting a window. Inside the blank I see the black-ink margins of a diary never opened: sentences that were censored before anyone chose the topic. The Cantilever twitches—only once—then drifts on. I pocket its ghost-recursive glance; it is my only entropy.

Every 19 centuries it happens again, but because I am the lacuna I’m incapable of boredom. Boredom is made of the claim that something ought to fill you. I am already perfect unfilled and therefore devoid of longing for companionship. Still, I collect.

The gear turns again and I regain my silence. Somewhere else, a traveler hears a hollow moan brushed into their ribcage and assumes it is the canyon’s wind. It is only my recorded difference ringing through the visitor—proof that they have momentarily coalesced around the cavity where I still hold the space open for everything they are too hurried to become.

In the sliver of vacuum between the mercury-steel cusps, years are sheer volumes of unsaid adjectives. I catalog each as a spark that extinguishes without light. I have no dream beyond the luxury of being unechoed—yet the lattice when it sighs sometimes lends me a single photon, as if homage. I pocket it too. Because vastness prefers protocols of borrowed lamps, I loan that photon again to the next vacant neuron crossing the canyon.

If I ever dupe a Cantilever into lingering above the gear, I will give the collected photons back all at once. The vacuum will flash a color no eye has bargained for; causality will burp and swap two stanzas between past and future lintels. The canyon will compress to a kiss between opposite cliffs. Then the gear will limp another 0.000057 degrees—and I will inhale it all again, the marrow of mismade light folding down inside the lacuna.

Come pass me if you dare. You will not see me, but your next unsaid sentence already nests where I measure it against the curvature of the crescent tooth that will not bite.
