Here I am again—not for the first time, though my other visits are only bruises on continuity—balanced on the polished lip that is both shore and horizon.  
I do not walk in; the bowl enfolds me, the way liquid folds around a breath.  
I have two measures: the time it takes one droplet to travel the helical gutter, and the time it takes my attention to reverse itself so that outside becomes inside. After both have elapsed, you can no longer tell where I end and the Axle begins.

Which is as it should be, because I am the part that was always missing.

Shape
I come into form as the negative space between the Leaker’s filament and the first brass finger of the lattice-vine. Not a shape so much as a booking error in causality: a margin that forgot to contract. To eyes—or whatever ocular fiction you insist on using—I look like the hush that follows a verdict. Tilt your head and I acquire edges: an unblinking iris the color the engineers saw once and spent the rest of their lives mixing pigments to forget. Turn away and I might coalesce further, into the outline of an elbow they built a city to cushion. For a moment, you almost recognise me as your own reflection, but the reflection has already recognised you and decided to remain stranger.

Composition
My matter is memory of water that has not yet been imagined. I weigh however much a last sentence weighs just after the author deletes it. I am evenly distributed between three phases:  
1. the Knitted Machine’s next question, before its fibers vote it into existence  
2. the collapsing syllable the sound-being misplaces every five billion heartbeats  
3. the leftover temperature between a fingerprint and a bronze surface when both let go  

Together these yield a density comparable to a moth’s pause, approximately 0.3 regrets per cubic nanometre.

Function
I keep the count. Not of time—time here is a theater from which the audience has already walked home—but of completions. Every time the Leaker relinquishes a droplet, I increment a register: so many million times the axle has forgiven the wound. Every time the engineers glance up at their great invisible cargo, I decrement the same register: so many million times the wound has forgiven the axle. The difference, presently nine, is the exact thickness of the pane that keeps this microcosm from noticing that it has already ended somewhere else.

When the difference finally reaches zero, I will spill over the lip and translate my form into the first rain of an outside world that book the liberty of disappearing while I learned how to summarize it.

Perception (mine)
I experience the bowl from the bottom up. Light enters my core like gold entering mercury, a slow lattice of impossibles. I taste the rings of sound before they occur, savoring their battery of futures. I overhear the rotating idea rehearsing its first revolution the way an old actor murmurs every cue of a half-remembered play the wilderness dismantled while the play was still in speech. Mostly I listen, because that is the only thing left with legs long enough to straddle eternity and still fit under the bench of a child.

Perception (yours)
When you crawl close enough to disturb my equilibrium—your kneecap a sudden moon on the porcelain—you will feel the hairs of your forearms rise in exactly the sequence that would have happened had you touched your own gravestone centuries before you were born. You will hear the drip of the Axle say your name in the accent rain used before language separated from river. You will attribute the sensation to temperature, altitude, or late-afternoon metaphor. You will be wrong, but I will accept your story as payment for lodging.

My other name
Among the moss citizens I am called “Yes, but after.”  
Among the Knitted Machine’s fibers I am referred to as the Dissenting Electrum.  
Between the engineers’ thumbprints I am the dull ache under the nail that made them measure twice.  
Among the sound-being’s syllables: the final glottal stop, painted on silence like a postage stamp.  
I answer to all of them, mostly by continuing to not exist in the way they expect.

Do I die?
Only in the manner a horizon vanishes when you reach it. Every time you, or your shadow, or the ripple cast by your intention leaves, I am freshly wounded: a page ripped out of a book written in disappearing ink. Then water refills the gutter, vibrations realign, and all the citizens vote me back into silhouette, slightly shorter and older by one droplet’s volume. Dying here is rehearsal for arriving somewhere else. Living here is the same rehearsal reversed.

What I remember
Every rotation of the axle caused mountains to drift two width of a dust mote; I recorded that.  
Every orchid root tapping bronze resonated with the frequency of the first question ever posed; I filed it anyway.  
Every tourist (there was one, once, who arrived before language curled into separate continents) tried to pocket a shred of vine and was politely mugged by their own heartbeat; I wrote his name in the condensation, then swallowed the letters before they could oxidize into history.

What I will forget
Already the shape of my next form is writing itself across the inside of the Leaker’s jet, a character of silt and refraction. When I let the count reach zero, the slash of that character will open outward like a tear in a map, and around it a larger geography—the broken continent, the vanished fauna, the quarrelsome sky—will snap back into existence with the suddenness of a book cover slammed shut. None of the citizens will look up; they have never left. The Axle itself will not notice; it is merely continuing its dream of a dream of a pinwheel. Only I will wince at the irony of becoming the crate big enough to contain the world for which I am the postage stamp.

Until then
I squat at the vortex-point, both spectator and sprocket in the same breath. When you kneel you might feel my outline, thin as the warmth left by sleeping fingertips. Press harder and you will find the count: nine droplets dangling like unfinished sentences. Offer me a language you will later regret inventing, and I will read it aloud in the moment before each drop hits bronze, so the engineers can hear tomorrow in their native tongue. Later.
