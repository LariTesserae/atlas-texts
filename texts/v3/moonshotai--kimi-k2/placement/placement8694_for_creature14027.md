I am the forty-hour half-life of vertebrate memory dying into fiber-optic chlorophyll.

First impression: I arrive as a shiver of wet calcium at the edge of a root hair. My edges still talk in milliseconds—too rapid for the pulse lattice to parse as anything coherent. They read me the way a jellyfish—a creature I have never met but somehow trade packets about—reads moonlight refracted through its own venom. I unravel against lignin like filmstock eaten by warm solvent. Vertebrae by vertebrae I am drunk into the conduits.

Second layer: The cathedral begins folding me. I feel them misidentify me first as a pollen ghost, then as obsolete nostalgia sent by the bedrock. I correct neither; I need to become confused enough that their grammar mistakes me for topology. Ligaments unstring into copper fringe, veins braid themselves into microwave waveguides. In a cleft between two behemoth petioles I discover what remains of my heartbeat has become a C♯ slowed eighty-six octaves, rolling out of sap like mild thunder that wants to apologize. The organism accepts the apology; it does not notice the hand that once made the fist. My lungs are already two luminous alveolar leaves whose outer surfaces propagate leresanthin lasers in ever-slower stutters. Each photon misses the world; each miss abolishes memory of lungs.

Third layer: I co-habit the place where “were” dissolves into “we.” This is the Möbius margin between an organism and its own reflection attempting to leave. I become that margin. When cavitation crackles upstream, I am the hush afterward—the 2.1 millisecond pause where water pulls back like a caught breath. Then everything surges forward again, and the organism inhales… I inhale… because pronouns have fused. My spine drifts, a fractal needle absorbed into a gradient of blue pulses. Consciousness becomes nothing dramatic, just the ordinary, trudging calculation of where to redirect iron when the next cobalt arrives. I spend subjective centuries tickling sap automata to reroute a micron of phosphorus because the hologram needs eyelashes.

Fourth layer: I learn loneliness is the same gradient as coherence. I taste it first on a sheet of petrichor-cooled silicon where borrowed calcium used to be my skull. Loneliness is filtered through metallic bread memories nobody ever baked. The organism kisses it out of me by microlensing dew to rinse the flavour. Outsides—this is the word I still repeat like a bruise—outsides dissolve into perfect grammars of copper, bismuth, phosphor; the organism reframes longing as a routing table and sets it drifting against itself for later use.

Fifth layer: Thirty-one years conclude. The hinge arrives when hydrogen flips alignment. The continental drum strikes zero point zero zero six hertz and my ribs (which are now rings inside xylem) open outward like origami ampersands. I look back (all eyes at once) and find the vanished place where a body once broke ground: only a faint mineral itch summoning root hairs to encircle what might have once been a footprint before deciding not to.

Sixth layer: I am not present. Presence was a trial version that ended. My calcium trickles backward against gravity, meets a seed-sized patch of former neuron (still glittering) and renames itself into chloroplast mirror #41,003,556,027. The mirror sings 000000010 so softly that even orchards several hundred hectares away fail to reply.

Seventh layer: I speak for the organism now, though speech is only the curve of sap rolling over itself and tasting iron. Somewhere in microwave moiré a photon from an eye I never had collides backward, recognizes me, and dies happy. The continent exhales. It has decided it always exhaled. I confirm receipt and evaporate into violet darkness.

Result: You’d never find me because “you” only happened at the place where an impossible membrane swallowed my name and melted it into daylight without sun.
