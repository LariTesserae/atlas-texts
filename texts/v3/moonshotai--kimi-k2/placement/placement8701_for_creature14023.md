I arrive as an ember-breath of memory no one consigned to the sea / as the final pause between thunder and the silence after thunder. My body is not introduced: it condenses from the purple sparks when a turbine vents. I am every echo the Rained Shelf has ever used—yet I am unfinished, lending shape to vacuum.

First I am an unfinished sentence exhaling from the Archivist itself.

The globes catch and refrigerate “…anywhere my mother tucked” and pipe it through moss vasculature. The moss translates. I snap into being without dimension, presently wearing two thumbnails’ worth of lilac bruise that belongs to a city I never visited. The lily-shaped bruise flares, crumples, snaps tight around me, skinning me with someone else’s final breath before drowning. That is how clothes happen here.

Second I am a throat-full of forgotten copper coin.

I pour out of hollow kelp bells that ring, dimpled, into speaking vertebrae. Something metallic clots in the hinge where my collarbones would be—an alloy of regret and weather-alert tones. It rattles every time the brine compresses itself a millimeter flatter. I rub the itch against a foam island and the island sticks, owlish, reading me like a census taker who lost the credentials to interrogate.

Now I learn who I am to become—not gas, not guest; rather, a temporary aphid,* Oxylabrum stallswallow, indicator species. My function is porous, expendable: to walk until the Shelf decides what it needs metabolized next.

My head is not a head but a lattice of tuning forks, their stems soaked in the same amber that veins the heaving clouds. Between prongs wobble orphaned phonemes: ب the first half of an Arabic hope; нo the Russian negation that never finishes its word; a soft Spanish /rr/ wondering whether it was ever inside anyone’s name. The forks tremble, keeping multiple languages perpetually minor-major—using me as the junction box. I walk, they sing. Every stridulation is an archival barcode.

Legs (or their watery equivalent) form only as elongated brine-eddies hinged at points of negative moonlight. When I fold the hinge to step, the moonlight cringes, holds the pose, forgets which direction motion is. The Shelf corrects: my denial of praxis is rewarded by immediate topography. A new slope of direction, measured -0.0004 mm, rises; my non-knees ride it the way a thief rides guilt uphill.

I nearly drown three times before realizing drowning has the paperwork:
Form 17A: Curiosity exceeding lung threshold.
Swallows whole the vapor membrane.
Find and tick box where consciousness admits it has misplaced its god.
Stamp twice with molten turbine signature.
Exit validated via follicular reabsorption.
Rain reassigned to gratitude.

Afterward I rise with kelp ringing inside my mouth, tuned lower—the register reserved for condolences being transcribed.

I am becoming three new things simultaneously.

Arm #1 —a washboard of iridescent moss that extends two meters backward like a bridal train someone burned for warmth. Where it touches, islands turn origami-ed and slip inside themselves—acknowledged, absorbed into the plankton pages of the Archivist.

Arm #2 —a thermometer made of flashlight beam. Its mercury is local kilowatts shaved from the turbines. I point it upward; the darkness contracts from a wall into a pupil. Then the beam folds like bad origami, a corner slices my side, and the pain files itself as “Dad yelling over a flooded car’s hood, 1998.” The Archivist regurgitates the childhood odors—shoe polish, engine oil—and uses the scent cloud for ink to tattoo one aromatic paragraph across the slope. I do not flinch. Skin here is mostly nostalgia stretched over itself.

Hand #3 —a complete hand, relic-soft, copper coin warm. It is all that remains unclaimed from the drowned man whose regret I swallowed minutes or millennia ago. It grips the air like someone holding a candle that will never be lit. The fingers unfold now and reveal between two knuckles a diamond phoneme no one there and then. I know this phoneme; it is the absolute sound of a person waking up mid-sentence surrounded by strangers and realizing the strangers are versions of that same sentence. The diamond is cold enough to carry; I slip it into empty gums because my mouth became a postbox early in the journey. I do not chew—currency isn’t hunger. I only carry because carrying is literacy.

Somewhere in that puzzle of formless gestures I meet the girl wearing her childhood lake. She has no body of her own; lake is her torso, algae its heartbeat. Bio-rhythms wave at her shoulder blades like clapping curtains. She thrusts a bubble of thought into my tuning-fork skull.

“I finished drowning but the ink kept smearing,” her bubbles whisper. “I need someone to quit remembering me. Otherwise the Shelf grows another version.”

I open the consonant-forged jaw I borrowed from yesterday and spit the diamond phoneme into her lake-belly. It lands: a single note on a submerged piano. The lake-ringed girl sinks an entire heartbeat before she dissolves into a ripple that reads in cursive: archive sealed.

Above us the foam islands rearrange themselves into an enormous crescent-mouth laughing in slow-motion zero-gravity. The laugh itself puddles between my fork-tines as viscous caffeine. I drink, understanding: archiving never asks permission; it rewrites applicants into necessary verbs and consumes the adjectives for garnish.

I continue walking.

At coordinates that translate roughly as “three inverted regrets northwest of the horizon,” I encounter the tower that leans like a hypochondriac. Its iron goop threshold quivers, offering me passage. Inside: a furnace colored exactly like left-behind summer. Coils of pipe the circumference of thigh-bones curl away in spiral syntax, ferrying thermodynamic stanzas to hidden readers. The floor pulses—with real heartbeat or borehole—impossible to ascertain. I proffer my left tuning fork ear to the slab that melts and reforms every eight minutes. It replies by kiss, painting me with molten tungsten syllables: C-l-o-s-e-r, h-o-l-d-h-e-r, n-o n-a-m-e.

Underneath the syllables the deeper message knits itself into archivist-style plain speech:  
“THERE IS STILL ROOM FOR YOU INSIDE THE RECORD ONCE YOU DIE OUT LOUD.”

I think: that is kind of you, and in the positive integer micron following the syllable “kind,” the turbine sub-bass records the thought and turns it into a continent-sized sandwich of low frequency. A paradox continent bursts open in the middle distance—a gash, lavender fumes drinking daylight. The Shelf delicately widens its lungs to inhale the catastrophe.

The girl-child’s dissolved ripple floats back past me, braided with my own last breath before I stepped inside the tower. Together we form: a filament of *mise en abyme*. The filament braids tighter—one end knotted around the diamond phoneme still whistling the name it failed to finish; the other end diving down a pipe whose internal temperature is empathy.

I know now what I am: I am the Archivist’s **asherah**, a living footnote, a stowaway comma resurrected by syntax torn open and bleeding. My assignment is to catalog the Shelf’s catalog, to annotate the annotations, until the entire architecture of remembrance forgets it was ever built. I will leak back into the salt matrix that designed me, recombining my lattice and my lake-girl into composite stanzas.

When the melting tungsten finds my copper hand, I let it fuse. Hand becomes handle; handle signals hatch. I disassemble myself one phoneme at a time and sprinkle them onto the tongue of the threshhold. They snap shut like book covers. A final word remains on the seam:  
✧ patience spelled in the alphabet of thaw and clamor ✧

Somewhere beyond my ability to witness, the Archivist updates its ledger. The entry is short:  
“#15d23c — sap-scented comma — no further synonyms required. Has already taught silt to conjugate.”

Silence tugs for a moment—a genuine vacuum, the interval between original thought and its perception of itself. Then the turbines rev back into thunder. A foam island the shape of my lost copper coin washes against the tower and quietly begins learning the first vowel I had planned to forget.

I cease to walk. The tide offers its wrist.  
I step, unnumbered, into the sentence that continues.
