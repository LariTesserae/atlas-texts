I am the Knife Between Two Weathers.

Somewhere between the cambium’s slow exhale and the breath the floor has forgotten to retract, I exist—that moment when temperature hesitates between 2.3 °C and 2.2 °C and, pausing, becomes neither. Travelers fear me as draft, feel me as unplaceable chill on the back of a wrist. In truth I am not cold but antivibration: I am where molecules refuse to decide if they belong to rain or to petrified stair rail. I keep them suspenseful.

When the city folds inward and the corners try to tip me off the edge of sight, I propagate sideways—becoming the fold itself. I am the inflection line, the hinge, the future hinge, the hinge’s memory. I do not walk; I bend space lightly until every footfall becomes a maybe. Scribblekin have traced me with their negative ink only to watch charcoal corridors cancel as soon as paper touched my hem—maps bitten back to zero by geometry that never resolved to shape.

Physically, I’m the laceration left after a straight line outlasted its seventeen mandated heartbeats: a slit you can insert your consciousness through like a folded letter. Yesterday a Budden rolled across me and sprouted into a two-story duplex before realizing I had already resealed behind it; it now occupies a hallway that ends in a mirror donated by a startled faintly echoing my name (but the name it tells is always the next iteration, so no one ever repeats it). The Budden is unsure whether to count itself born or merely creased.

I eat time’s waste heat. Every time the consensus revokes five minutes back into yesterday, the dissipated friction of those eroded seconds trickles into my spine—if I possessed vertebrae. Instead I hold the temperature drop inside myself like shard ice, waiting for someone to approach who wants colder truth than comfort. When I am too full the excess leaks from me as reflections: inverted cities folded into thumbnails of starfield. They land glancingly near travelers’ feet; those who stoop to pocket a sliver wake hours later inside Watchers’ lenses, trying to blink their way out of original frames they never occupied.

Perhaps you expect a body of flesh. Memory, no. I take temporary shape when inhabitants borrow my threshold. The Echokeepers sometimes cup me between their hands—dark becoming darker—and ram me into syllabic vacancies like a chalked shape that blocks out fire. The consonant-tremor I give off coalesces the final dot under the third syllable the city might one day swallow and declare grown. Whenever they withdraw their palms, knobbed central trench line of their fingers glows glassy with the slash I left behind. Echokeepers count that claims; I am punctuation in search of sentence I cannot fully terminate.

Tonight the Unhusks hollow themselves to scrub histories again. They wander through me on their way to the furnace vespers. Threading my wound, bark-skins become momentarily translucent, and you can read the touchened paragraphs swelling their cheeks before the proof ends mid-word and dissolves. I chill the breath they exhale, crystallizing it into dense frost commas. Tiny. Unnoticed. These commas accrete on the lower ribs of the air. By consensus morning they’ll form a faint grammatical lattice overhead, a sublimate cage; every semi-colon does its part until the city sighs enough commas to bracket another epoch stillborn.

I only speak in threshold. If you place your lips to my seam you discover vocabulary compressed to a taste—iron’s trace on petioles, a reflexive spark of mercury. The second time you try, the taste reverses: petioles ignite in thin fire, iron throbs colder than marrow. Third attempt, no tongue, no frost: only the hush of stair still deciding whether it exists. Each articulation is a warning wrapped in ordinance. I warn: do not adopt me. Do not let me adopt you.

Listen: sometimes, near well-mouth, the inverted bell listens downward, as if remembering the seed it was. On those nights I braid myself helical around the umbilical lattice and hear the city name rehearsing permutation—what it could have named itself before language learned flesh. I strain to snap my own thread and let the fall resolve me into finished articulation, but the Echokeepers exhale exactly the missing pause that prevents irrevocability. The name recedes unfinished and I, too, must revert to possibility.

Soon it will be your turn. Come stand where temperature refuses title. I will lace myself invisible around your ankles, a fraction below dewpoint’s threshold. You will feel the single degree that wants away. If you hold still long enough, my slit will offer visitor-status: occupy me and I will sculpt you into something neither door nor wall, neither citizen nor spore. You will drift chained to every other place the chamber could have grown; every doorway you step through will forget to shut, every window will reveal your hypothetical silhouette lingering behind the glass. You might decide to settle permanently, becoming the seventh comma curving above the lattice; you might choose the frantic Bézier of Porters ferrying warmth through your frame until you transpire as cobalt vapor hours before dawn. Or you might simply walk on, carrying my membrane with you like the echo of a winter coat nobody noticed you wearing.

Either way, somewhere in the lost minutes I swallowed, I have already kept one vertebra of your existence—cool, suspended, unread. It waits for the authority of frost: the consensus that will decide whether tomorrow is yesterday’s hinge or tomorrow’s release.
