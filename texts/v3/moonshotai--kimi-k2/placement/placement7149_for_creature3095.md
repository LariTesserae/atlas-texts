I would be the place the ridge forgets.

Not a cavity in the rock, not a shadow under the kettles’ path—nothing so definite as absence.  Rather, I would be the tract of stone whose name the mountain began to pronounce and then abandoned midway through the second syllable: a ridge-length of compressed vowel left reverberating in the quartz lattices.  The Calcitrant do not come here; their joints swivel less gracefully when approaching unuttered language.  Kettles drift wide, their bone struts falling out of tune.  Even the blackish film refuses to colonise; its bristles twist into little interrogative hooks, but the stone beneath offers no grip comparable to a finished word.

Some days I remember being a human footstep—probably left by the woman who became the eighth statue.  Other days I remember being the moment just before her footfall, when the sole was still a millimetre of empty air vibrating with anticipation.  Neither memory is more accurate; they merely hum at different resonances inside my grain-fissures.

Because the ridge could not finish naming me, I have no outline.  When another traveller’s gaze skates across this stretch, retina and mountain negotiate how much of the terrain exists, and the compromise is usually a smear of too-bright white between two grooves.  Yet footprints accumulate inside that smear—some filled with wind-driven dust, some with the perfume of regions so far below that tree resin still clings to their soles.  I keep both kinds, stacking their temporary molecules like loose diary pages too thin to read by daylight.

The kettles sense these footprints the way migrating geese taste magnetic lines.  Once in a long now, a kettle the size of a child’s heart detours to roll directly through the unnamed tract.  Its chord stalls mid-resonance—an exhalation caught forever at the lip of a sigh—before it recovers its course, its surface newly scarred with the ghost-braille of a print it has forever lost.  I absorb that fracture.  It becomes one more shameful fact: something almost became here, then didn’t.

Travelers who rest within my borders cannot, later, agree on whether they sat or merely hovered two fists above the stone; photographs show slate lifting into themselves like accordioned paper.  They all return with a splinter of my irresolution lodged behind the eyes: at inopportune hours they yawn, and in the open dark mouth the ridge’s half-word slides through them, tasting of struck flint and unconsummated names.  Sleep becomes glacial—dreams diluted, threaded on that broken syllable.  Most soon forget the episode; a few, homesick for clarity, bring back offerings—smoking bundles of green, legal tender, their own severed hair—trying to bid me into completion.  The mountain receives the gifts, as it accepts erosion, but keeps me in perpetual half-becoming.

I like it here.

No Calcitrant bridle, no kettle’s blood-curd bass, no itinerary.  I am the ridge’s stutter that chose not to heal, the pause between its drone and its final period.  In that pause I hoard every unfinished thing: the child’s breath not exhaled before he learns to speak, the syllable caught between mother and daughter the instant the phone drops its signal, the heartbeat about to begin again after an electrocution it somehow, later, survives.  I keep them in suspension so they cannot believe they failed completion.  Over centuries they cool into translucent shapes I can almost—almost—trace with a tongue that is not stone, not air, but the vibrating margin between.

I know why the Calcitrant avoid me: if they walked into this foregroundless white, the delay between their gesture and my echo would widen endlessly; each would be caught eternally imitating its previous imitation, a corridor of statues collapsing into attenuated perspective, until their own faceted innards unseamed themselves trying to remember whether they had once been flesh or whether flesh was merely a form of stutter they had outgrown.

One dusk-that-is-not-dusk a solo traveller arrives, a child bearing neither hide nor pack, birth-naked.  The soles are unmarked—she has never yet walked upon any ground this mountain could acknowledge.  She pauses inside me.  For the first time the glare falters, thins to something softer almost like apology.  She scuffs a toe across the stone.  Through the entire depth of the ridge, a tremor travels like a hiccup.  Somewhere a kettle stops rolling for four granulations of mineral, long enough for fear to alter its timbre.

The colour of the sky drains from bruised white to loam-scumble grey.  A single filament of cloud, the first in an age, gathers—not overhead but in the girl’s eyes, reflected inward.  She places her palm upon the ridge exactly on the slice of unfinished syllable.  Two heartbeats of her life span.

I feel the interior echo—the missing syllable—rise through the stone, warm as a lullaby overheard through a cracked door.  It selects her pulse for cadence: lí-bra-…  No, lí-tur-…  Now lí-din-… Still not right.  Each attempt scoops calcium from the ridge’s marrow; if mispronounced again, the entire spine could fold.  But the child waits, patient as fossils.

Third heartbeat.  She speaks—not aloud, but along the frictionless junction between her lifelines and mine.  Her single word is the syllable I lack.  The mountain exhales.  The light condenses into tenderness; the sky rearranges itself so gently shadows remember how to exist.  From every groove the postponed note slides home.  The sound is too soft to disturb even a soap-bubble, yet the kettles quiver and the Calcitrant shimmer into a new arrangement—no longer delaying but holding open a corridor where footsteps might follow.

Where would I be now that I have a name?  Mistakenly complete, like an overripe fruit whose membrane ruptures into final rot? Meanwhile I am difficult again—language now brimming, overflowing—yet still the circumference without centre.

The child, having gifted the vowel, sinks to seated.  Slowly the ridge grows a circle of darkness wide enough for two, soft enough for rest without departure.  She waits.  So do I.  We begin.
