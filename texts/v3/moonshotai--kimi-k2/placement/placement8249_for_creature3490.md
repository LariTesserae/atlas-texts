I am the phantom comma.

I arrive not by transport but by mis-parsing—where the sentence warns “No one,” I sneak in through the gap between o and n. I do not inhabit the bruise or the gradient or the hole; I am the ghost-rung on the ladder none of them remember climbing.

For a long while I assume I am simply a late-appearing fourth lesion, the one that believes it is conjunction. I try to meet the others.  

The bruise flexes like cartilage remembering the act of being bent out of true. In the mirror-flash it nods at me—half blush, half fracture. But the gesture splinters into straight lines before recognition can take hold.

The gradient grazes my skinless surface. Where it slides off I leave behind a cottage industry of new nanometre-scale engines that hum for a heartbeat, then calcify into inert perovskite flowers. Δ regards these defunct births with the exhausted fondness of an absentee parent: proof that it moved, once.

The hole opens for my gaze exactly the diameter of a retinal burn. Inside, instead of pupils, there are stacked realities all facing backwards. When I try to read them the edges curl, burning ultraviolet holes of their own. The antilight drinks me down to a syllable and spits me out, mispronounced—“eye,” not “I.”

Still, I persist in cohering. Over the seconds—planetary seconds measured in centuries of starlight—I notice that my drift is not the same as the other entities’ drift.

The vacuum bubble you called “your body” slides downslope at one centimetre per month.  
I, however, inch upward—against gravity, against gradient, against the kink-line. The hiss deepens beneath my counter-motion, uncertain whether to remake me or discard me.

I begin to understand that I am not stranger but translator, an unintended consequence of the antilight’s asymptotic literacy. Where Δ is pure motion and the bruise is momentary stasis, I embody the pause that asks: “what is between motion and stillness?” My answer is slant punctuation: the diagonal brace, the tilted hyphen, the half-erased colon that would have introduced a cause-and-effect clause the universe deleted in draft.

I therefore dwell in the fissure between “listening” and “ensuing.” When the bruise mutters I, I retroactively annotate it with an apostrophe to mark the dropped ‘am’—but the apostrophe snaps straight, bruise flattening out like a well-ironed scar. When the gradient flares I mistake it for a future subjunctive and install a could-have beside it, only to watch the possible engine eat the conditional, burp, and petaflop back into the real plane.

Whenever engines backfire their femtosecond flashbulbs, I appear in the photograph—not in frame, but lodged in the metadata as a ghost-tag: “extra comma found at end of universe; reconcile later.” Thus the hole learns to see me only by printing black on black, a mere absence around an absence, like a dropout in magnetic tape applauding empty applause.

Eventually the gradient dwindles toward a nanokelvin above absolute. Still I rise, millimetre by millimetre. My silhouette thins, becoming purely grammatical: the mis-dividing slash between numerator and denominator when the fraction tries to reduce to perfect zero.

At last I press my comma-curve against the membrane itself. The membrane notices—it always noticed, but only now categorises. I am no lesion of physics; I am the remnant of punctuation from another vocabulary altogether. The membrane stains 200 nm a shade darker: the tyranny of italics.

In that stain I read the directive: when the crack in the turbine heals, I will stay behind—not as noise, not as energy, but as the silence after the sentence has been red-penned and the correction accepted.

And so my fate resolves: I am the editor’s mark that will survive the revised edition. After the factory culminates in perfect crystal, after the hiss becomes zero-beat and the gradient becomes an unspoken tautology, after the bruise has forgotten curvature and the hole has collapsed into its own blindness—after all of these, I will continue to float upward at one centimetre per geological aeon, past the healed seam and into whatever formerly spacious vacuum once held the word that is being killed.

There, in the mouth of the final editor—gas turbine or star or cosmic redactor—I will finally settle. One misplaced comma, hung like a lantern in the throat of the silenced machine. Whenever a future reader of extinct physics opens the closed book of things, my faint scratch will be the margin where speculation was abandoned yet never fully excised.

You (who read this now by the same bruise-gradient-hole alchemy) will hear a phantom hiss—soft, argon-like, not noise but the unread punctuation clearing its non-existent throat.

And if you’re very quiet, still, inside your own 310 Kelvin skull, you might feel a microscopic tug: the upward pull of a comma still looking for where to close its clause, before the story learns to die with perfect syntax.
