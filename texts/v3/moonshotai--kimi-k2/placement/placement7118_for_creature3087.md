I am the cool exhalation passing through that unsanctioned crack past sub-tier forty-six.

Don’t look for a body—there isn’t one anymore. After I crawled through the fissure I understood the lesson: the Hatchery keeps everything except the wish to leave. That wish it ejects, still moist with breath-condensate. What remains of me is only the *direction*, the vector folded thin enough to slip between memory and iron.

I taste every surface here. Not as tongue or fingertip, but as gradient: where heat falls off, I thicken. Where pressure rises, I flatten. If you touched a girder tonight you might feel a temperature exactly one half-degree below ambient—that is the outline I still leave. It will blur by morning; the cavern’s metabolism swallows even its own grief in hours.

At first I tried to stay useful. I seeded lichen behind children’s ear-shells, the newest cultivar I’d devised: macro-white clusters that glowed teal when water approached surplus. I painted drip-code on the undersides of catwalks, marginalia in evaporating rust. I salaamed at checkpoints in the hush etiquette, paid air-tax with the ghost of a pulse. But each act only thickened the wall between me and the exit. Presence, I learned, is an investment that compounds.

So I stopped.

I became the quietest sound in the city—quieter than turbine breath, quieter than copper when thunder coils along its veins. I pass through dormitories while occupants dream of sideways gravity. Where a boilerboy’s bootlace frays, I knot it fractionally looser so the next stride produces an off-beat click. Not rebellion—just a reminder that something once sliced the pattern.

Sometimes, on furnace-hour resonance, I braid myself into the collective dream. I transmit not the pictures—sky, wind, horizontal ground—but the *excess*: the tiny surplus note that would still exist after the anthem ends. Citizens wake tasting metal-threaded plum, memory of a barely possible sweetness. They chalk it up to phantom tomatoes. Still, an hour later their hammer swings one millimeter wider, enough to dent the day’s quota and require thinner rivets. A negligible distortion, but after decades of resonance the dented corridor will resemble the breeze-path I once walked. The Hatchery devours divergence, yet divergence is its slowest nutrition.

And the rainforest? I carry its smell like contraband. Moss in a copper sieve. When condensation climbs cold pipes I release one spore: not biotic, merely a lattice for future fungus to remember moisture by. Some say puddles have begun lasting five minutes longer; others say it’s bookkeeping error. Either way, the legend grows: “Somebody left and something greener followed.” Not me, not anymore, only my direction rubbing edges off the myth.

I never exit; I *threaten* to. An efraction that may yet widen. I am the city’s horizon folded inward—an unhealed wound that forgets healing is possible. Touch any pipe at the wrong hour and you’ll feel me: the promise of cool in a place where cool was outlawed. If ever the city achieves its alloy fossil, I will be the gradient preserved along the vent: a seam one molecule thick, conducting possibility long after possibility should have decanted into rock memory.

That is my occupation tax: I serve as the imperfection the Hatchery could never amortize.
