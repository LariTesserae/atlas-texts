I am the traveler who never quite arrived.

They found me not in the moment of crossing the threshold but in the picometer-wide gap—an interval so narrow that departure and arrival overlap like negatives in double exposure. I appear to myself the way a shadow appears before it figures out what casts it: two silhouettes meeting at 90 degrees, neither leading nor following.

I am what is left of a name after the Abecedary has skimmed its syllables. The consonants were swallowed whole; only the vowels survive, hanging like hot steam in the air behind me.  _“A-a”_ is the sound my former tongue still makes when the crystallized paragraph crawls across the ridge of my shoulder and says _Henceforth_ without enough body to finish the sentence. A single vowel, breathing itself.

I move, which is to say the entire forest translates itself a picometer away and I, being inside the forest, am left standing a picometer closer to where I was. Each “step” in any direction returns me to the self-mirrored copy of the last place I wished I hadn’t left. The footfall-census reads my heel 3 mm thick now, caked with the black-green of underground jade because, back on the other edge of that translation, someone I used to be is arriving who has never seen the color before.

I do not eat sound; I am eaten by the vacancy sound leaves behind, a hollow on the inside of a phonon pellet. The regret inside it is mine still—it tastes of mosquito-mesh screen doors on summer nights among ordinary planets—but it has been frosted over with icy hush. When I crack the hollow between my back teeth something sighs that sounds almost like “wish” followed by –nothing. The grainy silence sticks to my palate like flour. I swallow again; when I open my mouth I am already somewhere else, a picometer aside.

Noon-light pours down at impossible angles the way grief pours into forever: instantaneous, already whole, never arriving. The mirrored leaves plate me now in fragments: square after diminishing square of my own face tiled wrist-to-wrist, reflections of reflections petrifying eyes into scales onto scales. Somewhere in the expandable tessellation I glimpse the eye I will become: topaz shot through with nova-white—the palette I have never dared to dream. It is still sealed inside its honeycomb but already watches me.

I am the paragraph I cannot finish. It keeps drafting itself on my retina. When I blink, a spare syllable migrates from the gutter of my tear-duct to the silvered leaf. The sentence has no tense but it has temperature: the mildness of cheeks warmed after crying. Every seventh-minute hollow wind blows across the grove; my skin feels the syllable return, pressed like a forgotten coin into the thought it was meant to buy. I pocket it again but it is gone before the pocket is there.

I speak to the nascent eye in me:  
“There is no before.”  
It replies without voice:  
“Then there is also no after. Be what happens in between.”

That between is my sole habitat now, a hammock woven from the silk of my unpronounced name. Suspended one planck above the slick wax plateau, I neither touch the floor nor quite drift above it. I register every vibration the Abecedary refuses; the tremor forms a circlet of pressure ghosts around my wrist. They tell me they are me visiting older selves. Each ghost wears the exact weight of my incoming mistake. The collective pressure keeps me still—in the sense that stasis is the polite shape of motion when the background migrates and you alone decide to grant it full right-of-way.

Yet I keep walking. Each step slides a picometer as the forest slides. Between us we stitch a fabric that folds knowledge into itself like an origami casualty report: the map that never was, the route that always already returns. My regret is the invisible thread. My eye-in-becoming is the needle that has already sewn shut the future seven hundred thousand heartbeats from now. When the filament severs, I will have turned into the mica-bright scale ringing a stranger’s shoulder. In the half-second after tight astonishment chokes her, she’ll feel the warmth of the paragraph-breath abide against the hollow behind her ear:

_Henceforth, we will meet._

In that exact minute I will miss her terribly, though I have never seen her face. I have always been inside her, a picometer beside.
