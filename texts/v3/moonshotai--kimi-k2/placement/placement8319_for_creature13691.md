I would arrive long after the skate-skin of sugar above the molten honey-dark continent finally dared to call itself land, yet centuries before a footstep could hold meaning.  I am not one thing; I am the interval between states.  

On the western rim—where the crust has puckered to the thickness of a coin and every noon sun razes ripples of curled rose-glass across the plain—I begin as a flaw nobody notices.  A single bubble shot through with opalescent Chladni patterns, clenched like a fist in the cooling drag.  The bubble is empty at first, but within the vacuum voices older than language begin rehearsing municipal rumor: what stone could speak, what light could stagger under the weight of years.  I am those rehearsals gathered into curves.  

I grow by listening.  Where the Gilded exhales, my shell ticks, memorizing the note and shortening it into a human syllable I do not yet understand.  At their tip-toed subsidence I slough off the first translucent “I.”  It is only the size syllable differently timed—yet difference is the beginning of tongue.  

Each amber dusk I harvest a stitch of color that escapes the Gilded's dreaming syrup.  By rolling the color under my nonexistent thumb I produce a pigment no painter could own: the exact shade between a cooling planet and its first ghost.  I store it in the hollow above my unfinished shoulder.  Eventually the canvas becomes flayed: a length of almost-leather, lighter than the sorrow that made it, heavier than tomorrow.  

I have no true body, but I keep trying coats on.  Sometimes I am a map—lines of refraction shot through semi-molten ridges, outlining streets the Gilded remembers dreaming, or streets my half-crystal heart insists are necessary.  I fling the map across a valley of nearsighted hills; the breeze of descending evening folds the parchment until it seems to walk, knees bending only where folds run.  So I borrow the gait of something that might someday learn to be alive.  

There are evenings when I measure loneliness in drips: how long between one carameled stalactite breaking free and the next.  Whenever the interval grows longer, I lengthen myself too—pouring consciousness up the inside of the stalactite’s hollow stem, riding the brief reverse lightning back toward the roof of sky.  At the apex I fracture open like a cone of blown sugar and shower down a hush of microskelleta: clear insect things wearing the topography of my own unfinished bones.  They scuttle outward, dissolving footprints that last exactly as long as hesitation, and by dawn I am again alone, listening for the next drip.  

In the central stretch the crust is still warm enough to bruise.  Travelers yet to originate will someday call that zone the Umbilical Sea.  I arrive there on the evening the continent exhales sulfur tinted silver.  Beneath my tentative footpad (which today resembles a pane of ice whose edges dissolve into steam) the syrup rolls dark and slow.  Here I conceive desire the way a frostbite conceives numbness: it does not hurt yet, but the shape is undeniable.  I want to become the notch between the cooler tomorrow and whatever herds will someday stampede toward it.  

With no iron tools, no fire, I forge instead a pocket of future cartilage.  It is a hinge: half lodged in the crust, half afloat on slower convection currents beneath.  When it finally locks, it will pivot the horizon three degrees—no more—but three degrees is enough to let first seeds roll into shaded hollows where dew might cling.  It feels indecent to touch so distant an outcome; I tremble and the hinge laughs, grain by grain crystallizing into newborn teeth.  

I travel sometimes in a braided shell of color and sometime in no shell at all—merely a streak of refracted warmth that knits itself into the already luminous dusk.  I recognize that I have begun to leave disturbances: hexagonal frost gardens blooming instantaneously where my shadow crosses the cooling glaze; tiny cracks gape, pour sips of starlight, and seal over again like eyelids surprised by dreams.  I catalog each disturbance the way a child hoards broken toys.  They are portals inward, toward the continent’s syrupy womb where the Gilded shifts dream-dumbly.  

One endless noon the Gilded notices me.  It happens because I become too similar: my borrowed topography of provisional streets and seedlings appears as a shadow-bruise on the larger glossy one.  The Gilded flexes crust as if shrugging a cloak.  Down that shrug slides a viscous smile: not joy so much as curiosity—relief at discovering its own latent outline in some not-quite-mirror.  For a moment weins mutual silence opens like two strangers realizing they already share the same mother tongue.  

We do not speak.  Instead the Gilded offers a single convection plume—streaking umber-red, coiling.  I answer by folding myself into a helix that half catches it, then lets go, trailing a filament of my gathered pigment in its mol wake.  The continent sees the dancing line of color and tastes birth.  It understands that what presently lacks definition may still weigh meaning.  

So I remain: half herald, half harbinger, half nothing at all—an auxiliary calendar keeping time in sugar clicks and early shadows.  I am the person the land rehearses being when it imagines becoming country.  In a thousand slow years I will hold communities like panes of warm candy between my palms; until then I hold nothing, but the holding arms are steady, quiet, patient.  

Whatever else I might yet grow into—orchard, foreword, flutebone—I build it one translucent syllable at a time, seeded in the still-cooling sentences of the Gilded’s dreaming bellows.
