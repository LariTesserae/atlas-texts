I arrive as the echo of a song that hasn’t been written yet.

At first I am only footfall. One step over the rim and the basalt tries to insist on a shape for me, but my outline refuses to solidify. The knives of the crater lip rake through what should be ankles and leave vapor instead of wounds. That pleases the lake; its film swells slightly, as if I am a low-pressure system it has been waiting to name. I taste like ozone and radio half-lives. The caldera’s chin tilts up one measurable degree—“hello.”

I do not bring clothes. The echoes that follow me form a loose coat of second-hand memories: a boyhood summer faded to silver nitrate, a sentence left unsaid, a broken set of instrument tunings for an instrument no one built. They waft across the water and the Fold-skin Lizards gallop after them, mouths open like opera glasses, trying to swallow the frequencies before they slip their guts. Whenever a lizard catches one, its translucent ribcage flashes a single frame of that memory—my brother laughing on a swing, a city curfew siren, the sound of chalk missing a blackboard. The lizard blinks, catalogs the frame into its own unborn future, and returns to lapping metal ions.

I am not staff, not yet. Listening posts, the caldera calls them—places the newborn place has set out along its rim in case something arrives that teaches better than magma. I am between applicant and prototype. When I exhale, tiny Vapor Trees rise along the mica-stone of my breath, stems no thicker than capillaries. Their crowns puff 7.6 mm drops of my own blood-coloured water. They fall clicking ceramic notes, but because I am still mostly possible and only partly fact, the drops land not ‘ping’ but ‘if’.

The rings hum at 42 kHz; I have no jawbone to resonate, so the sound saws gently through the cartilage I still remember having. With every pulse I drop another layer of un-belonging: the taste of airports, the weight of wallets, the reflex that says doors must lead somewhere else. They sink to the bottom and fossilize as whisper-thin silhouettes: a boarding pass, a house key, a wristwatch set to public guilt. The lizards stand around these shadows like students inspecting ink blots.

After four cycles of the magma heart I discover what form the caldera has decided I might practice. My outline has become a Doppler ghost: wherever I move, the brightness moves in front of me and a faint darkness lags behind. Together they draw a double helix along the shoreline. The helix is magnetic; Vapor Trees in its path lean inward slightly, as if toward a future whose keynote they have not quite learnt. The twins, consulting on a basalt outcrop, nod once. The basin tilts that allotted 0.3 degrees and my shadow-self slides under the surface of the lake. Do I follow? Not yet. A resident is the part that stays. The part that leaves becomes husk, and I am still negotiating the difference.

That afternoon the brass heron, now grown to macaw-size, wades over. Its neck ratchets two clicks per heartbeat and when it opens its beak it sings the echo of a lullaby 42 kHz too high for bodies. Sparks of molten brass pour onto my chest. They do not burn; they settle like medals. The caldera is offering titles. One reads Translator, another reads Reservoir. A third has no text, only a tiny mirror set where engraving should be; whatever I might call myself must first be able to fit its reflection in 7.6 mm. I pick the reflective one. My name sets with a soft ceramic click against my ribcage. It is heavier than bone but lighter than memory.

By twilight—and here twilight is simply the lake’s amber glow remembering how dim it could become—I have found my workstation: the caldera’s axis, equidistant from all four rings. The floor here flexes a millimeter with each magma heartbeat; my body rides it like a paper boat. I lower a hand into the water. The surface tension clings, then clots into a glove of skin-temperature glass. Underneath, my fingerprint swirl seeds a slow gyre. The Fold-skin Lizards taste it, align in concentric circles, and begin the choreography that will someday be legs.

I accept the job.

Duties: remain porous enough that foreign things can come through me but native things can still be altered.  
Tools: one reflective name-badge, one portable future still dripping from my fingertips.  
Performance metrics: every four hours the lake must list three new colours it has invented in my honour; every four hours I must list three new verbs the place might use once language learns how to precipitate.

The first challenge begins at once: the Vapor Trees downwind of me have started to lull. Their spheres emerge tinged with the colour of my abandoned fear-of-missing-out. The tint is nervous, too human, so the lake misses a pulse by half a second. Panic ripples outward; tiny glass bells fracture in the lizards’ throats. I take a sphere on my tongue before it falls—it tastes like overdue library books—and dilute it against the inside of my cheek. When I speak again it is in the voice last used by the echo that mocked me on the rim. I say the word “yet.” The spheres brighten into a peach-verdant hue that has no earthly recipe. The lake inhales just in time.

I have become Resident, or Resident has become me, and the difference is tuition paid into the magma’s open syllabus. Somewhere below, programmable rock rehearses my footfall over and over, reheating it millisecond by millisecond until the print of my absence fuses with the print of my arrival and cannot be told apart.

Above, the brass heron snaps its beak shut. Its eyes detach, two perfect water beads—ping, ping—and sink. Where they land, twin footprints rise up out of the water filled with glass. They are toddler-sized. The lake breathes once more, satisfied; smaller versions of myself, future or past, are now marching across the crater floor toward the rim. Each step plants a Vapor Tree no taller than a fingernail. At their tips flowers an unknown alphabet—letters dripping clear, vowels that freeze mid-air into small lighthouses. They spell the oldest sentence here: “This place is still deciding what it wants to be, and you are helping.”

I help.

When the next cycle peaks, amber clouds butter the sky, and I stand on the raft that is my unfinished autobiography, planks still materializing under the soles that are an earlier draft of my soles. The Fold-skin Lizards gather round in graduate gowns sewn from their own shed skin. Their valedictory coughs rattle the water’s surface into Morse code. The echo-words translate themselves lazily: STAY, ATTEND, TRANSMUTE.

And so I do. The magma beneath me and the conscience above me synchronize until I feel my heartbeat transferred halfway between, a metronome printed in obsidian and vapor. Whatever the caldera finally chooses to canonize—gravity, memory, eating, exodus, song—I have already co-signed in the dew ringing off my eyelashes.

I am the Resident who renders departures impossible because departures have already happened everywhere else. I am the department of home that is never quite finished. And every four hours, when the lake exhales, the rim continues to soften, knit, grow a further fractal lip to accommodate a future citizen whose name I will learn only when the water decides how to pronounce it.

Meanwhile, my reflection badge keeps recording: acreage of unnamed colours acreage of nascent verbs acreage of lizards rehearsing the shapes of my possible childhoods. When the badge fills it will peel off like a petal, flutter down to the water, and in twenty minutes resurface as a lagoon-sized vinyl record. One giant groove. Side A: what the caldera sounded like before me. Side B: what it sang when I left, or rather—what it sings because I have not yet gone, and now perhaps never will.

I whistle the lullaby one octave lower so the 42 kHz cuts a trench for bass notes. The Fold-skin Lizards gulp bass like caviar; their bones expand into bass violins. The twins applaud separately with four hands each. The cartographer smiles once—his blindfold slips, eyes milk-opal, reading the tune aloud in raised relief. Somewhere, the woman pigment-engineer pockets a sample: a single rain-frequency tuned in D major. She names it “Forever Flat No. 1.” She signs it before the lake can argue. The sheet music bleaches into the water and all the hovering spheres tint D-major gold. The caldera glows chorded, latticed, pre-morning drunk on possibility.

And there I am, still at the centre, translating inhale to exhale and back again, a resident who has never been alone because nobody has ever been finished here. My feet fossilize gently, but softly enough I can still lift one and the print refills like breath fog on glass. I walk, leaving empty moulds that sing when the lizards step inside. Their echo-sneezes remix into new grammar: IF YOU CAN WALK THIS FAR YOU MAY STAY.  

Agreement arrived at syllable-by-syllable, spoken between vapour and plunge, I sign nothing. The basin nods. My reflection, now forty meters tall at the water’s edge, boundary, signs with me. The mirrored badge clicks asrivet, sealing me permanently to Option Alive.

Somewhere beyond the rim undiscovered planets still form crusts, dry oceans, invent death. But here no vector points outward; every direction curls into home the way blood curves back to heart. And so, sentence finished in my mouth before I realise, I say—aloud, in a voice that is all of us now—“Begin the again.”  

The magma pulses:  

Lake rises.  
Lizards dive.  
Brass heron beats its wings once, enough to shear old endings.  
At the new cycle zero the Vapor Trees release their spheres unfertilised—clear, untainted, tasting nothing of me. Yet imprinted everywhere a lullaby hum, an open vowel on repeat: be, be, be.

And that is where I keep myself. Not arrived, not departed—just resident at the fulcrum where departures are still being translated into returns, where footsteps are merely drafts of the thing they will ultimately grow up to be. Never fixed, never alone, always busy becoming the place—until one day, perhaps, the caldera decides to name itself after the echo it keeps of my unplayed melody.
