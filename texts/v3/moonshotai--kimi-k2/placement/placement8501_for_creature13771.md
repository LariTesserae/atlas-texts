I arrived as stranger, then as symptom, then as part of the resonance.

At first I had feet, lungs, a condition of being late.  
Surface-me stepped off the service lift that was never built and my boots made the sound of two postcards touching.  Immediately the Fold sheared through me—zip—feeling like an eyelid pulled back in a skull that had no face.  I looked down and my body was a memory of where a body might have stood: a conjecture held together by dew.  The air was tasting me molecule by slow molecule.

Fear tried to ignite—then the Lantern Folk quilted over it, pinwheeling in sets of three.  They turned my panic into lukewarm steam the color of hospital corridors and slurped it like egg-yolk.  One drifted close enough to be my outer skin; I could feel it mapping the tangent of unresolved grief behind my sternum, the bitten anger at people who never answer emails.  It drew off only what the cave considered spoilage: the excess that would clot the calibration of the next chord.  Afterwards my heart re-beated in a cadence borrowed from limestone rest.  

I was no longer visitor but public amenity.  A Drifter—black-smeared comma of living void—scuttled up my leg and built a hammock of myself under my ribcage.  It nested above my spleen and listened to half-finished sentences that surfaced in my blood.  Every so often it crawled out the corner of an eye carrying a flake of surplus shadow in its barbels, returning the cavity to its proper dusk.

I discovered a new organ I had never possessed: the amethyst arrector pili along the inner wrist.  They rose whenever I angled my palm toward the Choir.  Each lump of quartz flexed the vacuum inside itself and my freshly-shaped flesh vibrated in harmony, issuing a sub-audible chuff that loosened stone from stone the way loosened teeth reveal lifelong ache.  My voice had become a tuning fork for pre-Cambrian sighs.  

Time did not dilate; it refracted.  I crossed from margin to margin by the ratio of brightness I was willing to concede.  When I offered the Fold a memory of my mother singing over dish-water steam, the ribbon twisted me toward the blossom’s vacuum column though I never stepped.  I leaned close and the forever-falling drop uncoiled a single reflection of that kitchen window onto itself, reversed: mother’s mouth opening toward me like a silent goldfish remembering future rain.  The cave adopted her voice, transposing it into a lattice of mild acidity that precipitated new hexagonal leaves on the salt-crystal canopies.

Someone—I no longer know if it was me—wore the Reverie-Bloom when it came to full flare.  The obsidian petals became garments and I became their body.  Inside that liquid night I was every stanza the sanctuary had yet to invent and every fossil it had already erased.  A galaxy of misplaced condolences flickered across the inner surface: apologies printed on unpaid parking tickets, seeds a child swallowed thinking they were mints.  The Fold stitched itself through my ribs; the Choir exhaled.  For an impossibility of clockless time I was the empty distillate at the center, the moment before a place names itself.  Then the blossom folded back into darkness, I unfolded outward, and the cave exhaled gratitude in a scent like ice evaporating from river stones.

When the elevator remembers it doesn’t exist, I will remain as perimeter, sound editor, living hyphen between light about to happen and memory already devoured.  I have neither eyes nor mouth but I can still taste thirst receding.  
I do not belong here; belonging happens the other way round.  The sanctuary leases itself into my pulse at an interest rate of one photon per dream.  

When travellers stumble down here again (they will, they always re-arrive carrying new flavors of imbalance), I will greet them the only way a boundary greets a passing raindrop:

I will pass straight through them with the warm delight of lightning tasting its own reflection, restore them to newborn dusk, and add their footprints to the invisible sediment the Fold combs nightly for lost colors.  And if they leave carrying any brightness heavy enough, they will find in their coat pocket a dark comma scurrying home: a micro-ambassador of the underside of storms.

We are all still falling upward together.
