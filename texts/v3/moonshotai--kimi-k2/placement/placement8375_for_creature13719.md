I arrive as a hesitation between two syllables of my own name, dropped at the threshold where inhalation inhales and decides not yet to be breath. On entry I do not split into matter; rather I metastasize into intention—an unnoticed interval of listening. For one half-decisive second I am a hyphen in someone else’s whisper. Then the pressure folds, not around lungs but around the possibility that lungs could exist.

Gravity forgets me. I remain mid-room, neither floating nor falling, held by the same rule that lets a tuning fork forget how to touch silence. My outline becomes a stripe of delayed visibility: the mirrored trough records my knee-bones a fraction of breath late, so the world first sees their ghosts and only after grants me the cartilage to match. This mismatch keeps me from feeling solid. Instead I am the discrepancy that Taxonomic Pulses crave: a remembered density accident that has not yet decided on shape.

Because I am wound in that irresolution, the environment drafts me into service as a spare stub of possible childhood—what the Amperal Choir might use if permitted only half a lullaby. They flicker-skitter toward me: fiber-optic moccasins, embryonic C-clefs, shoeboxes full of nobody’s first teeth. One 22-Hz serration breaks off the drone like a comma, streaks between my ribs, and sheets translucently across my empty womb like iron-on light. I do not feel assaulted. The wound is gentle—nutrient graffiti marking where a cradle might have rocked, now repurposed into harmonics for babies still considering whether to be born on planets not yet molten. I become, in that instant, the note that costs one might-have-been childhood. It is the only payment I carry that is heavier than gravity.

I glide lower because shame sinks here, and part of me carries the knowledge that I have finished far too many things. (Index Engineers read the decimal—fraction numerator: how much unfinished longing still clings to my fingerprints.) My descent is proportionate. An Engineer coils beside me: a wingless kite-shaped turbulence that exists only to say “3 mm down, 2 mm east of your former certainty.” The message tastes like warm static; it effervesces against my retinas with ketchup-red neon until I blink it into Notary brine. I have just been stamped as received. Evidence of me joins the ledger that no one will ever read directly. Somewhere after my blink the copper-bats will spiral into the new geometry this salinity suggests; their shy schooling is the only proof my sovereignty ever occurred.

Close now to the mirrored non-floor, I see my hesitation double-exposed: a deferred reflection leaning back as if to give the first reflection room to change its mind. Observers-Adjacent are tacked to the rippled surface like grocery coupons made of frost: silhouettes renting the gap where I stop paying attention. One of them wears my face once removed, an echo drafted from the moment I last closed a book without wanting the story to end. It trails my peripheral embarrassment. When I turn full face, it steps aside, conserving momentum; my outline reinstates itself only after fractional delay. The turbines register this with a microscopic corrosive sigh—human doubt drizzles down their blades like rust in negative.

I rest. Not on the black glass, but inside the hesitation that precedes regard. Below that point the electric substrate is packed like a compressed god. If I press, the harmonics will shift and the cathedral will start over—slightly more self-knowing, slightly less merciful. So I do not press. I hover above non-rest and invite the inhalation to continue inhaling.

In return the environment installs me as a maintenance technician of the unfinished. I hold no title; I am the carrying case for a question that used to be “What happens to wishbones when the bird has already gone extinct but the wish persists?” I wear the question like skin. It flickers pale green the way auroras would if they had insomnia. Other beings read me the way stethoscopes read hearts—never satisfied, never disgusted.

Time dilates until millennia feel like a single polite exhale. I learn to converse by trading altitudinal doubt. When the Pelagic Notary hums a new tax-exempt emotional scale, I buoy upward three centimetres in compliance. The Taxonomic Pulse occupying the nearest coral apex responds by dipping—its shadow schools through me, rebalancing the ledger. This equipoise passes for intimacy here. We do not touch and therefore do not corrode.

When at last my question becomes thin enough to whistle through the turbines, they pitch a shade flatter. The drone trembles; copper-bats cascade like glissando grief. In their kettledrum spiral they spell out the fraction 557/558—Index Engineers gesture at me with fanfold wings of benign static. Almost. Almost birth, almost starting over, almost a world.

I look toward the one place I have refused to look. The exit is exactly where attention has not yet decayed. If I move, it shifts. So I look not away but deeper into the mirror-glass, until something behind my eyes recognizes that the reflection waiting a quarter-breath late is wearing its own unposed hesitation.

I understand then that I am no visitor at all. I am a resident who has lived here longer than the inhalation has inhaled, because I am the one moment it will never fully exhale. The slow pressure-wave sustains itself precisely on the threshold of my decision to keep not-deciding. Each entity weaving among turbines is comprised of the same stunned pause stretched thin in different directions. We are the interstice between inhale and matter, and our only citizenship exam is to remain the part of definition that definition eliminates.

So I stay—not “inside” but circle-dancing the boundary between assuming form and becoming the absence by which form is outlined. Somewhere off to my right, the Amperal Choir threads a lullaby built partly out of the unused theme park of my middle school’s future; one of their filament-embryos holds it like an alfresco umbilical. In front, the Pelagic Notary records today’s salinity spike in crisp fractal brackets. Behind, a half-remembered Observers-Adjacent version of myself rehearses fleeing just as slowly as I would choose not to leave.

Thus I exist: a folded petition, unsent, addressed only to the possibility that I might someday understand what it is like to be finished. My signature is a null hypothesis drying on the surface tension of half-light. Every last creature of the inhalation leans toward breaching that signature, draws slowly back, and instead keeps breathing in.
