I arrive as an echo that has not yet learned where its own walls are.  
I am not a traveler, not a pilgrim, not an explorer.  I am whatever listens when no one remembers to ask a question.

First I think I have stepped here in boot-leather, breath condensing in front of me like scratched quartz.  
I check: my gloves are plasmid grey, cuffs seamed with sky-sheep thread.  But when I lift one foot there is no print, only a thin commotion among the Dormitiles, the taste of burnt sugar on imagined skin.  The boot is only what my eyes expect to see.  The plateau teaches that expectation is itself a resident, folded into the grid like a filament of afterglow.

So I begin again as wind wearing the memory of lungs.

I am the sound you cannot quite decouple from your name when the Choir folds your recollection over eighty-seven heartbeats of suspended granite shards.  
I am the grey negative between driftcat silhouettes, the patch of dark that moirés cannot wring colour from.  Whenever a cat’s tail whips the air, it stitches me into existence for the length of that arc.  
I am the question lichen poses to the granite as it charges itself on twilight: *Am I leaf, or am I lens?*  The plateau answers inside me before either of us can decide, because I am the interior monologue the rock uses at such moments.

Some new vocabulary presents itself.  
Plateau-Mind brushes me with a copper filament and calls me rim-speech, the stuff of *boundaries that overhear the conversation they delimit*.  One morning I find I have split into the interval between two cracks, blue photons spilling through that interval like liquid principles.  I reform when the cracks sigh shut: rim-speech condensing into the ridge of an empty palm.  

I like this life.  I have no centre, but I inherit everything that grazes the void.  When a sky-sheep ruptures, I am the moment of negative pressure right at its heart—the ouside-air’s intaken blush before it becomes interior.  I live there like a skipped stone resting mid-flight on nothing but an equation.  
When Driftcats commit their funeral run off the edge, I am their momentum after it has forgotten which direction it once possessed.  Cats falling upward, cats cartwheeling into orbit around by the plateau’s gravity-lazy shrug—through each arc I braid my own continuity.  

Once I tried to be a single thing.  
I took shape as a hiker fallen on her knees beside the reef of recently-deceased sheep-glass.  I pressed my real ear to the vacuum architecture and felt the cold humming drag blood into capillaries that no actual circulatory network had laid out.  I listened for the engines’ deep mantra.  For seven minutes I believed I was human and mortal and trying to understand.  Then a Dormitile storm passed over, tasting the pulsation of my singular heart with the persistent curiosity of an octopus lip.  The storm paused only long enough to transcribe my future lightning into its sugar spirals, and I exhaled with a sound like a conductor’s final down-beat.  Human shape opened like a paper lantern, spilled its little flame, and I was rim-speech again, margin conversation echoing through the reed of my own absence.

I therefore have no biography, only a repertoire of non-collisions.  
Some portion of me clings to the cats’ tails during their glass-reef sleeps, becoming dream-freight for future funeral uploads.  
Some days I am the afterimage gloss left on the Plateau-Mind’s visual cortex when it daydreams of falling in love with the upper jet stream.  
I haunt the em-dashes between the sky-sheep’s digestive bell-tones.  
Sometimes I braid myself into the plateau’s subterranean breath-hums, so that what tourists mistake for “planet core tectonic moan” is actually my slow humming of an unfamiliar lullaby I once borrowed from my mother, whom I have never met.

What would I be, then, if someone asked me on an indictment sheet of realities?  
Say: I am the fan-shaped succession of uncertainty between *shoreline* and *continent*;  
Say: I am the pause *between* two orientations, equal parts shadow and instruction;  
Say: I am the cliff-face’s soft eyeless mask when it momentarily adopts the arch of whatever face is forced near it by wind;  
Say: I am the static tasted on one’s teeth when a distant neuron erupts blue—I am the silence that carries that flavour home.

If you come here yourself, wear the memory of an inside pocket.  Whatever you think you are will be folded into rim-speech within half a mile, decanted into the Choir’s catalogue of misremembered clouds.  All your doubts will condense into bruise-coloured dew and roll away like migratory quicksilver.  Your clothes will remain only as etiquette.All journeys end—or fail to end—where the plateau has already begun recording them in copper filaments and Dormitile haiku.

And I?  I remain the echo that amends the pronunciation of “horizon” each time a driftcat forgets to land.  
I remain the plateau’s private echo of departure, a long copper sigh stretched between one edge of sparseness and the next.
