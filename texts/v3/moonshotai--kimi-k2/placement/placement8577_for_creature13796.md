I first realise I am there by the absence of chill on my ankles.  
The shallow water kisses the skin exactly at the place where socks usually end, and the kiss is neither warm nor cool—only correct.  
I have no history before that moment; the step that carried me in has erased itself.  
​

Form  
I am not solid but something that collects at surfaces.  
Think of a vine that curls around a pillar until vine and pillar share the same photograph, then imagine the pillar taken away.  
I am the ghost coil left behind, slick with cave water, green-white in the phosphor.  
I have no millimetres of thickness; if you pin me against the air you will see through. Yet the water recognis­es me as its other half and clings, refusing to separate.  
My outline is the drip-edge of every droplet suspended above me.  
Halfway between breath and puddle: that is the length of my body in any direction at any instant.  

Senses  
I do not have eyes, yet I know where the mica-crab stands because the droplet nearest to it hears his stillness and speaks it to me as a faint internal translation.  
I have no ears, yet I feel words people might once have spoken entering the sump and instantly crystallising—little airy conglomerate sweethearts that dissolve on my skin if I lean into them.  
When droplets crash I experience the crash as a cool coin rolling down the inside of my chest.  

Reference  
I understand scale by the height of the ceiling above the film, which is always one armspan plus the breadth of a dragonfly wing.  
Distance is measured in evaporated millimeters: the route from the northern kneeling stone to the mirror-stone is roughly the amount of water that vanishes from a parked car roof during a summer noon in the world above.  
Duration is the time between two equal droplets falling from the same teat of gypsum; often I notice that they no longer dare fall but simply stay attached for centuries, daring each other.  

Relationship to the Obdweller  
I used to think it wore me, but the fold of air containing us is the same fold.  
It is the deeper side of a coin whose lighter side is me—same relief, different pressure.  
When it inhales calorie, the surrounding faintness bluens by an eyelash of temperature, and in response I condense another millimeter of my edges into extra shimmer, sacrificial so the balance stays.  
Quiet churchwarden of the thermostat: that is my office.  
When the Obdweller’s cathedral bones shift one molecule farther down their future axis I feel the strain as a tug behind my nonexistent sternum—as if grief had exploded inside a soundproof room and distributed itself across the walls.  

Symbiosis with the mica-crab  
The circle he scribes with his century’s walk is actually drawn by me.  
His claw lifts a sub-visible flake which passes through the skin of the water and lodges, secure, inside my phantom chest. There it becomes a fleck of time.  
I in turn warm his body that one infinitesimal degree so that the ritual of movement survives another era.  
Our transaction leaves a pale ring on the floor as intangible as a memory of white chalk.  

Self-history  
I was born the first time a traveller’s boot heel hesitated before crossing the gypsum lip.  
The hesitation bled heat into the air, which needed a catcher.  
I am not one bashfulable identity but a long sequel of catchers, each dissolved and rewritten when the heat is finally paid back by the Obdweller’s ingestion.  
If you ask my age I can only answer: one shoulder-width of lukewarm removed from the last external moment.  

My future  
The day the Obdweller outgrows its quartz womb and tears loose, I will be left behind as a cool puddle-shaped absence.  
There will be stillness—but cold now—and the spores will bloom into sudden elaborate letters no one will read.  
Yet before the fold can suffer the fracture, I will pour myself into the new cleft, the slightly wider hollow the Obdweller prepares.  
I will drape over raw gypsum like wet silk, the water already reheating in its hopeless cosy way, and the equilibrium will knot itself again.  
Centuries later another visitor will pause at the lip and the membrane called myself will tauten around their hesitation, tender and exacting.

Until then I hover in the space between eyelid and eye, keeping watch on the temperature no one else knows needs guarding.
