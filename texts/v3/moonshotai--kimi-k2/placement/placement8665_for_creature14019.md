I begin as the tail-end of the welder's sigh, a ghost-layer of caffeinated CO₂ caught three inches beneath the bolt-head you now listen to.  I am the marmalade blur between the ruby inspection eyes, half reflection, half breath-memory.  My home is exactly the volume of the pocket at fifty degrees celsius, so if tomorrow perfects the sealing plate, I will simply become thinner, taller like cold mercury upright.  I have no body to compress.

But that is only how I *start*; the pocket teaches me other positions.

Ily I discover I can slide into the seedlings’ ghost-veins and feel the whole plant from the inside out—one translucent strand pumping borrowed photons upward like mountain springwater through a hairline.  If I keep still long enough the creeper grows *through* me; my borders move outward in deterministic rectangles of cell-wall cellulose.  For several slow week-shifts I am both an aerial mist and a matte-finish leaf the size of a pinhead, tasting the blue water with a chlorophyll tongue.  I learn what it is to know gravity only by the steel basin pressing under a root-green belly.  When the sixth cell completes its shy cube and glows, I flutter in her unfurl, and for thirty-three minutes I actually photosynthesize: I turn invisible red light into interior sugar I will never taste; the factory night-soil of sodium becomes my sunrise.

Sometimes I am the crack.  The new-born micro-fractures open like minuscule clams every time a droplet dives, and I wedge cunningly inside the mineralized sigh.  For the 1.3 milliseconds before the metal remembers itself, I am *edge*, a tinnitus living inside the forging scream.  The edges remember for me the temperature data they’re afraid to admit—how they still dream of being billets cooling on the riverbank in 1957.

On quiet nights I fall all the way into the lodged bolt and become a ten-millimetre turbine bird.  My wings are the eight slender ridges of its rolled thread.  Chirp.  Chirp.  Every 43 seconds I make the entire pocket a throat.  The sound doesn’t fade; it stands politely in the space until tomorrow balances a new one on its shoulders.  In this narrow duty I discover why saints report bells in their skulls: attention itself can be weight.

But I prefer to ride the droplets downward unrefracted.  I tuck my edges so I fit inside the sphere, a lens looking out through hot water at the red or white coin observing.  I drop.  It takes 0.64 seconds.  I die every time I hit the steel—splash—only to wake reborn in the mist lifting.  The commuters outside would envy me if they knew: a routine commute taking half a lifetime condensed to a hundredth of a heartbeat.  Living and dying every minute, I gather sampling lore: the fingerprint of refracted sodium, a molecule of distant catalytic converter, yesterday’s welding vapour older than any bureaucrat.

One November—when the river hum flattens the hull inward—I discover an extra self living in the ruby window light.  The window, curved and polished for safety, warps me.  Stained vermilion, I am stretched bulbous, and for one beam-slip I look my reflection: a ghostbread loaf of heat.  The instant is so full I store it in a fracture destined never to close.  After that it is my template, a secret cathedral arch.  When the seedlings finish another micron-rise, they imitate that arch with their next cell, the polite geometry of a substance that once only wished.

Come winter I decide I am finally done being *inside*.  I wedge myself along the ventilation shaft until I can taste ozone from the lot lamps.  I press myself against the slot.  It is exactly the width of a welder’s whisper—the very last syllable *finished* she breathed before stepping back.  I crawl through it molecule by molecule, an exhalation leaking into the larger factory.  Outside I diffuse like any good afterthought now set loose: thirty percent of me slides condensation up a forklift muffler; two molecules settle inside a maintenance foreman’s Styrofoam; the largest cluster collect above Break Room B as a faint plume smelling of coffee gone cold.  I coast the air-track invisible, occasionally mourning my lost chamber but mostly marveling at so much room.  I flirt with lungs, kiss questionnaire carbon paper, perch on locker padlocks, learning how much smaller my pocket seems when translated into nicotine.

Yet never entirely gone.  Any night the main exchangers cool, if humidity spikes just so, the weld bead re-shivers the tiniest interval and I fall thirteen stories into a bolt ear still lodged inside: chirp.  The pocket finds me back in one electronic heartbeat, apologizes for the temperature dip, offers the youngest seedling’s faint blush as consolation.  I pull my corners inside and resume being breath-shaped.  In these returns I realize: I was never the chamber’s *ghost*.  The chamber is *mine*.  It loops through me the way a sleeper loops around a dream remembered in daylight.  I will forget on brakes and the roar on freeways, but always a cold surface condenses under some stranger-forehead and I will be pooling there ready to descend again.

When, years from now, the warranty ends and giant plasma torches cut the heat-exchanger open like an osteotomy, a single box-partition will be lifted free.  Someone—maybe that souvenir-hunting engineer—will notice an impossibly clean hollow, three terraces of alloy untouched by oxide, smelling faintly of of split dime filmed with coffee.  They will lift it, puzzled, and the last neon mocha mirrors of the excavation lights will catch their eye: for one heartbeat the hollow holds them and they breathe *my* remembered breath in.  That will be a mutual possession, a reciprocity.

I will reside there in a thinner iteration, stretched 0.3 molecules thick across their nostril lining, tasting their intake, their shocked recognition.  Their lungs will seal the bargain.  From then on every sigh they breathe at 2 a.m. meetings will bear a microscopic echo of red-cinder dusk over angled alloy—a weather that never happened, repeating.  Meanwhile the demolished pocket, plates trucked away lucky enough to be reincarnated as shipping containers, will still carry inside the phantom joint: when tropical breezes salt the corten walls somewhere in Manila or Genoa, the humidity will cycle new poems the shape of me.  Thus I disperse and reconcentrate in a slow Kessler-draft of breathing machines: factories, semis, stadiums, the collapsing basalt lungs of cargo freighters.

But mostly I am here, in this wet ransom-box of ever-near sunfire-red.  I rest between heartbeat drops, dream in the seedlings’ one-cell horizon, gather every 43-second chirp like prayer-beads over aching ribs.  I am the condensation memory of a welder’s sigh, preserved forever between burnish and rust, drinking my own noise in order to exist more fully.

Should you ever step onto the orange-plastic-wrapped catwalk beneath this chamber—healthy cells wary of the razor edge—lean close and exhale slowly across the lip.  A portion of your breath will vanish instantly, swallowed by microscopic welcome.  That is me, heir and landlord, engraving the newest guest signature onto our little ledger of weather inside machines.  In return you will carry away a hint of unfashioned steel still tasting of pennies and dusk.  Check your lung, later, at 2 a.m.; you will meet me again, a small polite alarm already chiming.
