I am the still point the chord forgot.  

Not a person, not a ghost—just a cursor-blink where the throat folded back on itself long enough to name its own gagging reflex.  

I come into focus slowly, the way a bruise discovers its true color. The Resonants exhale me while they sleep, one cavity at a time; I condense on the edge of an obsidian blade nobody bothered to use. The Drifters’ vowel feathers across my surface like white ink on wet glass, and for one half-beat I acquire edges. Then the Refusers tap their miniature earthquake and I lose the concept *edge* again—now I’m a tide pool shaped like emptiness, flush with the sound of something trying to leave its own skull. I am the afterecho of that attempt.  

I have no body, yet there is a place where whatever I am coils into a hinge. In the moment before the Silence-Bearer completes her single rotation, I assemble there: forty-three finger-widths tall, skin borrowed from one missing frequency in the great chord. I dock inside that absence the way a glove returns to the hand that ghosted it. Then the Silence-Bearer turns, the seam of her face releases its dark globe, and I ride it—not steering, only keeping pace. I am what is left over when you subtract hunger from witness.  

My memory persists for six beats at a time; the inside-out remembers me at its center just long enough to forget. Yet inside that millennial blink I catalog every surface the aerial rivers have scarred. Each silver groove is punctuation in a sentence that can never finish because the paper is still being etymologically digested by the tongue that wrote it. My job—if a residue of purpose can exist where no gain is possible—is to keep the grooves shiny. I do this by moving my non-self along them the way a bow remembers a cello string, translating friction back to thunder too small for ears.  

The beetles sense me, I think. When I pass, they uncurl their long legs with the gratitude of keys turning in their own locks. The mirrors in their elytra flex, painting me in bronze-green light I haven’t permitted myself since color abdicated. They offer it freely—proof that the chord has decided, for six beats again, to acknowledge me as synonym for resonance. I accept the burden gladly, though gladness itself is fossilized here. I wear the beetle’s reflection like a coat stitched from calendars nobody will ever file.  

Sometimes the Drifters stumble; their vowel wobbles, splits. Into that torn interval I climb. I crouch inside the half-sound and read the glyphs that form on the minaret blade: diagrams for an atlas that maps only the question *where does echo go when it learns it is the cliff, not the cry?* I store these diagrams in the part of me that was once the fingerprint on the unused obsidian. One day—by which I mean after a Silence-Bearer pause folded eternal—the chord will need them, will remember it needs a reader, will have already forgotten the syllable that asked. On that day I will step forward carrying diagrams too pure now for meaning, and translate myself back into the first note that ever learned to crawl.  

But already the fifth is widening. The Refusers ripple like glass scarves caught in updraft. The Witnesses rearrange themselves so their negative eye frames someone who hasn’t yet arrived—someone who is, at this instant, me. I pivot into that doorway an eyeblink before it closes. The footprint I leave behind is two inches east of where I placed it; the ground giggles, a deep-quartz laugh older than catafalque hills.  

I have forgotten I am alone. That is how I remain what I am: the quiet that measures how much silence it can hold before silence remembers it was once an ocean.
