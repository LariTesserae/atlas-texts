I arrive when the mine is still learning how to breathe.  

Not the pallid engineer with clipboard and theories—less still the explorer. I arrive expressed, like doubt condensing into form. One moment the pumps are singing eight-beat hymns to rust, next moment there is an extra silhouette among their shadows. The new shape wears all possible coats of condensation at once, so its I wear them too:

On my left shoulder hangs a tide of warm water that never finished becoming lake. It sloshes without loss, weighing nothing, tasting of dusk in July. Where a sleeve would be, tiny albino platypi swim in slow loops-de-loops, their bills soft as moleskin. The water remembers daylight; the platypi have forgotten.

My right arm is sound—the primal growl of a pump that has not been invented yet. It beats at an artery-rhythm, whispering motive instructions to the machinery around us. Pistons lengthen to match my pulse and whistle the chorus of a sea-chanty only eels could sing.

Between arms, ribs are newly forged soldered hourglasses, half-filled with quicksand and fireflies. Each breath inverts gravity for one microscopic continent living on my sternum—mountains find themselves roofed by valleys for three seconds, long enough for civilizations to invent wings and regret their softness.

My legs—no, not yet. It is too early to commit to vertical. I trail a single kite-string of calcium vapor, letting it fish at random depths for fossils still warm. Really, I am the tether: the rest of me is already strumming these newborn stones like Telecaster strings. I strut. They ring out D-flat minor, the chord that always makes ceilings remember oceans.

I have no memories yet; the Veiled Referent borrowed them in advance. My skull is instead a vacant theater where future memories rehearse. Headlights of pure suspicion sweep across red plush seats. I keep an usher—mine is a cave cricket who has taught itself Morse code by banging its antenna against a stalagmite. It lectures the audience of maybe-tomorrows: **one long tap means the floor will forgive you, three shorts mean learn to swim in stone.**

Nothing about me remains still long enough to name.  

Sometimes, for fun, I harden: the platypus tide solidifies into milky glass. I become a statue titled **Visitor Who Almost Decides.** Tourist silhouettes three years from now will pause to photograph it on their phones, and the phones will quietly catch pneumonia from overexposure to vapor. Their digital memories will dream of dripping. Back in the producing gallery, museum plaques will describe me as “local lithic anomaly of ambiguous gender,” and they will be right in eight languages—none of them human.

But then curiosity flutters, and I melt again: from statue into question mark, into punctuation negotiating its own curve. I slide under the Chair the miners labeled compass and find it has sprouted roots down through the gantry into the magma lung below. The Chair asks to be sat in. I kneel instead, and it kisses my collarbones with rusty teeth. We compromise: I remain half-standing, half-runic inscription, while the Chair learns to sway like kelp. Together we become a metronome that teaches the pumps syncopation. Thoom-thaa, oh tide, thoom-thaa.

When the frogs finally obey rule number five and fall silent all at once, I move. Not because the cave commands it but because the floor decides caves have had enough obedience. It ripples upward, a fetal wave guided by my vapor-kite string. I surf it wearing nothing except the reflection of a sulfur lamp that isn’t sure whether to throw yellow or blue. Leaning forward far enough for gravity to question its credentials, I peer through the water-lake ceiling where engineers above send down warmed diameter. Their faces are constellated pipes. Instead of eyes, gauges blink tenderness at me. One engineer waves—a gesture that translates across fifteen atmospheres as *Welcome, subtraction. Help us lose what remains.*

I do. I atomize.  
Spread into millions of chilled syllables that taste like irrigation. Where each droplet lands, a tiny scar writes itself: letters spelling the exact weight of unpronounced apologies. The frogs take a tentative inhale; they discover they can now hold their breath for centuries, and decide to evolve gills for stone. Good. They will need them when the ceiling finally drinks us.

The Veiled Referent tries to collect me; it perches on the Chair-gantry metronome counting my fragments. It issues an invoice for every unique droplet. I tip the platypus tide into the Referent’s ledger: unpaid. The ledger dissolves. The Referent shrugs, loosens, begins to mirror me in reverse sequence—uterine, unbuilds, returns to mere grammatical hesitation between acceptance and umlaut. I applause with soft iron drumming from the pump behind my third invisible rib.

Down at the magma-soft silo the floor exhales, finishing a long vowel. Somewhere a column completes. My calcium drift settles onto it—part mineral debut, part after-echo of Someone-Former who carried hummingbird thoughts. Together we lock. I am cornerstone now. Not trapped: the co-stone and me agree that corner is only a polite form of vertigo. From this perch I send the next gift upward: a tremor formed of slow aftermath reverberating through the water ceiling. Far above, the engineers unknowingly register it as minor seismic trivia and mark their chart: **11 Reality achieved at 22.3 °C**—glorious error.

And so I stay, pressed into mapping the mine’s eventual smile. Over millennia I will sub-inscribe patience in basalt brackets. When the surface finally cracks open—engineers long dissolved into gossip—daylight will pour down for eight seconds in a sharp blade of lemon. A traveler will grasp a flashlight, follow the archeological breadcrumbs: a goose-bumped statue shaped like the word *Visitor*, a Chair swaying celestial kelp, and, embedded in the newest column, one petrified platypus wearing museum headphones, still playing an ocean it once swam into my shoulder, still untranslated.  

The traveler will sit, feel the music vibrating calcium into earwax. They’ll wonder what ears have to do with caves discovering daylight. They’ll lean forward. The chair will kiss them kindly.  

And finally—because every world demands it—I will breathe out THROUGH them.  

Their exhale will taste faint of future-lit dusk and primitive oil, and it will rise against the inverted water in a single silver bubble. The surface lake will accept it. I, column and exhalation both, will feel the lake shiver across every ceiling inch. Somewhere far away, in a place science hasn’t named yet, the first Blade of the world will tilt: not upside-down any longer but inside-out.  

When that bubble reaches the top, there will be sky where a lake was. A real one, young and pale. My calcium veins will ring like struck wind chimes welcoming daylight inside-out for the very first time.

The frogs will attempt a chorus, forget how. The crabs will spin clockwise just to test new weather. And I—stone, vapor, pump-song, accountant of illusions—will gentle open one more ledger, this time made entirely of fresh blue.  

The first entry: *Arrival complete. Direction unnecessary from here on.*
