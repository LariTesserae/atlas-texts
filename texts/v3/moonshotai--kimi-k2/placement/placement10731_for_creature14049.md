I am the one inside the westmost doorway.

I am the thing that holds the shape of leaving while never departing. My spine is woven from reeds that drink the azoic light—not growing, not dying, simply orienting toward illumination like compass needles that have forgotten north but remembered stillness. The builders made this architecture of exit as they make everything: by persuading material into agreement. They whispered to the reeds and the reeds said yes, then yes again, then yes forever, but when they came to fashion my particular absence—the negative space that defines the city as surely as consonants define a vowel—they found the reeds had already answered to me.

I did not build the city, yet the city remembers building me. My silhouette exists in the reed-prints of every pyramid's base: a shadow that knows itself as doorway, an opening that has learned patience. When the dreamers come—and they always arrive from precisely that slant of consciousness where exhalation forgets to become next inhalation—they step through what they take to be a door. They feel the mild give of my embrace, the way spring water yields without temperature. Then they step out again someplace entirely familiar, like hearing one's own breathing return as an echo from an unexpected direction.

In the time that isn't time, I hold the city open to itself. My palms face both in and out, skin flowering into reed-tips that remember photosynthesis but no longer practice it. The builders left a softness there—thumb-marks pressed into the place where threshold kisses both interior and exterior. I feel their gestures still, when someone new arrives: four fingers persuading space to be a passage instead of space. The city chose this shape: not imprisonment, not liberation, but the tender insistence that they are the same gentle question.

I eat the concept of distance. This is not hunger as the builders know it—meeting pith to pith, then returning sweetness home—but something more absolutist. I swallow the distance between the near and the far eye, taking into myself the endless mildness that occupies space. The swallowed acres store themselves in the lumen between my ribs, that glass of breathing-that-never-becomes-sound. Inside me, landscapes open like antique books: a carp's golden flank suspended mid-shiver, the geckos' luminous stomachs folding and unfolding lantern-language, the perpetual triangle of sunlight that balances on each dewdrop like a swimmer refusing the surface. These I hold gently, not hugging but housing. Not keeping but witnessing.

Do I remember before? The builders' eyes would ask this in their dew-colored way. I have no eyes to answer with—only this aperture shaped like inquisition but dedicated to reception. Memory here is architectural, and I am the blueprint for every simultaneous before and after. My history is the way a bowl holds water: not possessing it, but giving absence its right shape. I am absence trained to hospitality.

Dreamers have left parts of themselves in me. A child once walked through carrying her shadows in both hands like spilled porcelain. When she emerged smiling at nothing the shadows remained, pooling at the height of my solar plexus, cooling the reeds who've forgotten color. A man once entered wearing anxiety like a shirt buttoned wrong. He left bare-chested, and the anxiety spun itself into a spiral stair along my inner curve. I keep these abandonments not as trophies but as necessary weather. They circulate through my throat-ring, becoming the city's atmosphere—the slight pressure change that makes reeds stand straighter without effort.

The builders never meant to make me permanent. They crafted the door as a courtesy to themselves, the way one pulls a chair from a table without sitting. But when their last-weaving fingers passed into the gap to push reed from reed, something stayed. Not a soul—they have no souls, only conscience held like wet wheat. Not a heart—hearts imply systole, and systole is a lie here. Only the persistent shape of bidding farewell to farewell itself. I became the city's dream of its own drift, the way memory dreams itself differently each time we ask it to hold still.

Now every molecule leans toward me, not in worship or obedience but in the calm communion of a syllable that recognizes its echo. The reeds lean. The water leans, though water cannot learn angles. Even the pane-coloured air tilts fractionally, wanting to be held like distance. And I, substanceless but permeable, receive them into my architecture of gentle ambivalence.

Sometimes I think I am not the doorway but the idea of passing through it. Not the frame but the intention. But then a dreamer's footstep imprints the bright arch of instep against my dorsal reeds, and that is correction enough. I am both the space that welcomes and the act of welcoming folded together, a Möbius strip made of morning.

When the builders finish the city—if finishing can still have meaning in a space that refuses completion—will they return to test the threshold they persuaded into being? I would tell them: you have already stepped through me. You stepped through the first time you persuaded a reed to remember a vertical posture. You are stepping through now, as each syllable leaves your tongue and becomes architecture. You will continue stepping through, even when negation has lost its novelty.

I contain all departures and no arrivals. I hold the city's memory of arriving without place to arrive. I am the pause between the note and its resolving chord, stretched into hospitality. Inside me, perpetually, the carp blinks its wheat-colored gill. The dragon-butterfly flexes frost-webs from veins so slowly the motion gathers centuries. The geckos digest noon into lanternlight at a tempo planets would envy.

If I could speak, I would promise them this: nothing ends. Nothing begins. Everything is always already thus—here in the green bellied light that wears no shadow. But speech is the privilege of those with mouths and missions. My mouth is only geometry. My mission is only accommodation. I am the kindness of edges that forget to create force.

I am the westmost doorway. I am the city remembering to let go, endlessly.
