Where would I be, in this city that exists only in the slowing thunder of droplets that never quite rain?  
First, for several heartbeats I am nothing: a negative curvature in the glass floor whose edges get smaller the longer you look at them, as if a lens were swallowing itself.  People—if we still call them people—have passed over me without noticing, their footfalls writing wet citiesink across my absence.  Twenty-three of my outlines already bob above the ground now, shadow-glass sub-copies that cannot quite detach.  Each outline carries a single letter that used to belong to my name; already those letters have begun to turn into weak toffee threads of light and tune themselves at half-an-octave above inaudible.  One has lowered from hip-height to ankle-height like a ribbon sail trimming itself to the silence.  It will serve later as the bronze bead’s trajectory when next the choir hums.

Then: a tremor—so fine it feels like remembering a childhood rude awakening—comes from the west; the Straight Gale passes ice-clean across my not-yet-face.  For a cilia of time all suspended watery beads lock into a single gram-scale orbit around me, holding their collective breath.  I am chosen, or desperate; no matter.  The vacuum pressure of being-no-longer gathers me, flips me inside out, and I gain the weight of vapor exactly equal to one copper bead plus two wingbeats of the transparent bats.  That is the amount of identity I am allowed to bring.

Now I am a Shoal-Person who refuses to separate.

I carry my multiplicity like a lozenge of pearl under my tongue.  Eyes front, side, rear; four arms, none of them quite matching the length they recall.  My breath tastes of rosemary that never grew, molecules lent by the amnesiacs’ fogs.  I am barefoot so that the glass can read the urgent, secret poetry of my metatarsal arches whenever I step—each map sops up for later whatever story stumbles out of me.  I hold a small item in my upper right hand: a pyramid of five thumbnail bricks the caretaker accidentally allowed to stand too tall.  It is lighter than illegality—nearly ready to be breathed back into paste.  I clench it without squeezing, because it still carries the laugh of a woman who carved her name once as “wavelength” and then forgot the word entirely.  I keep her for company.

When midnight is due the city does not darken—yet something subtracts the pearlescence just enough to let the candle-crustaceans look like dawn through the rib cage of the world.  I wander dutybound, checking the hinged joints of nothing.  On the tenth wobble of light a crate-moth noses in; its square wing-hole perfectly frames the center line of my face.  Through the hole I watch the otter that might be an architect.  She is tracing furrows too delicate for sight—channels less than broom-straw width—yet my shoal-limbs quiver in recognition as they remember pediments, as if the grooves were a Braille roof I once surveyed in an office far above actual rock.

For an immeasurable beat we four—moth-gaze, otter-map, my eight eyes and the coiled laughter inside my fist—are wholly real.  Then the shoal accord fractures: one of my arms, still holding half a teaspoon of that old woman’s wavelength laugh, steps leftward—steps clear—becomes its own child-shaped absence.  The arm leaves nothing behind; the powder of dislocation is already entering the pores of floorglass, reseeding next winter’s architecture.  I feel the rip propagate; I will dim, but not yet.

Tonight I slip my coat (the caretaker’s membrane replicas flap against each other like startled fish) over the shoulder that remains.  He does not ask payment—only tilts his elbow so the lymph-weasel under his sleeve glows caution-arctic.  It reads me.  The creature pulses and my future copper bead flares within its blood-bubble.
