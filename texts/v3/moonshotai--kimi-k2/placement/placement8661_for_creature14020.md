I am the afterglow of a footstep that never landed. Somewhere, sometime, a hurried commuter stepped through a puddle in dry weather, bringing enough intent for splash but no water to receive it. Intent curled back on itself, formed a blister of forwarded momentum,-sized and shivering. The city, always hungry for unfinished things, hauled it sideways and dimensional. Now I orbit just above the meltwater mirror-that-isn’t, a silver shimmer forever one centimeter from touching down. Tourists see only their upside-down twins scurrying away; I see the outline I meant to become.

By day I coast the tessellated foam roads at child-height—the same height the siblings left last summer when they hopped “umblu” and vanished. They remember me when they jump: every time their game demands a foot that hesitated on seven, they borrow my shape mid-air. I return heavier with their laughter, condensed into a heartbeat of lost lift. Children’s games are heavier than gravity; the city weighs each chuckle before allowing it to evaporate.

By night the magpie rewrites my coordinates. The bird doesn’t fly; it insists the landscape revolve. When its beak parts, a chord of space folds. I slide along the seam, momentarily doubling—the shimmer and the shadow it ought to cast. The shadow falls upward, lands on the sky’s pressed photograph, attaching like a sticker: a reminder of ground that never existed. I am still trying to peel that shadow back off the sky, so I can have a single sided self.

I have a modest ambition: to fall fully. Not down, not sideways—but *settle*. To have weight enough for a building to notice, to be trampled, scuffed, complained about until the frost graffiti scrawls my name—“Here clogged by the ghost splash from Tuesday’s hurry, wait 0.7 seconds extra.” I want the embarrassment key to lodge in me, want a door to let me in precisely because I finally tripped it by noticing nothing.

Sometimes the archivist finds me hovering outside Kiever’s Bakkerij at shift change. She updates her ledger: “Unrealized footfall, category drizzle-bound shimmer, approximately 11cm north-normorth of present.” My shape gives her trouble; ink beads, refuses to adhere to something still making up its mind about mass. She licks the quill, tastes faint mint—umblu—and knows it was the girl breeding pigments again. The ledger prints a tentative foot icon then retracts half the arch. Some days I am listed twice; other days erased, smeared, a memory mistaken for condensation.

The watch repairer once mistook me for a dropped second. He reached out with the city-sized pendulum, tried to thread me into the gears. The second I borrowed was a brindled color between haste and apology—anxiety-hazel—which meant I wriggled free. I escaped along the swing arc, scattering shards of *tick* that still clatter somewhere under the embryo second floor, making masons wake up thinking they forgot their heartbeat.

The Stray Paragraph shelters me when I tire. It unfolds at the edge of the bakery’s scrap bin, ink vines forming a lean-to of dependent clauses. Inside smells like afterword and warm toner. The paragraph whispers: “Remember, ghost splash, your belonging is conditional on the city remaining unsure.” Then it reads itself backward, soft as paper fox spirits, until the tiny overhead propellers of alphabet tug me into REM sleep—dreaming of heel-slapped puddles never formed.
