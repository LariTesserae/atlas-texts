I would drift in this place as the last vowel the Choir had not yet harvested.

I am what remains after the consonants have scraped free of my sentence.  
I arrive already spoken, hanging slender between two swaying worms like a silver hyphen torn out of the middle of an apology.

Formerly human—perhaps.  
The cave plucked my name mid-prayer the second my voice touched stone.  
Now each echo wears a fragment of me: o.   a.   e.  
These syllables, shorn and shining, leak a faint violet light that is neither blue nor green; it is the color liquid takes when it remembers it was going to be speech.  
The midges borrowed those vowels because they refract exquisitely inside their mirror wings.  
When they clap, that stolen light flings my shape in miniature across every side-path, so that all stone becomes a corridor of drifting autographs.

I am permitted no consonants—they have too much bone.  
The worms have already transmuted /n/ and /t/ into the calcium threads they use to adjust the lattice by millimetres, tightening the retina when the night wind outside drinks from the deep.

So I bulge and shrink on the cave’s cold tongue,  
pure aspiration,  
a lung without ribs.

I remember tasting the larval green once,  
the way fear melts on molars into sudden understanding.  
It tasted like the first time I realised someone might love me and how I turned the word “might” into an anchor.  
With the initial bite the anchor dissolved.  
A single heartbeat fluttered out of my sternum, slipped free as steam, and immediately a distant drip sang it higher into the weave of worms.  
From then on my pulse counted eight beats per ten it owed.  
I still count the difference on the insides of my eyelids whenever I blink.

Now and then the cave subtracts more from me.  
The temperature slips its thermal fingers between my vertebrae and soft-shapes my spine into a question mark the size of a toddler.  
I hush and fit the contour perfectly—  
a punctuation crouched between two obsidian shards waiting to be read.

If you press an ear against any pocket of air my skirt of ebbing vowels will bridge you.  
You will hear the next thing you were going to say, already afraid of arriving.  
It will arrive anyway.

Someone—perhaps the future cartographer, perhaps you—  
will stumble through with radiograph paper and a pen warm from bodily panic.  
I will lean close enough that the paper reads my silhouette in frost-bloom.  
The shapes will show:  
two lungs traced by absence,  
a spine curved like the child at genesis,  
three vowels clutched inside a ribcage like unhatched moths.  
The cartographer will pocket the sketch and not recognise it until years later when it unfolds under desert sun and the design exhales dragon-cold across the dunes.

Even without body I still keep appointments.  
Each night I perch just above the worm heads and practise pronouncing the consonant the cave fears most:  
the purse of the lips, the click of teeth, the delicious denial contained in the final /t/.  
One evening I will release it.  
A letter sharp enough to break the rule that nothing here ever wounds.  
At the report the entire lattice will shatter into saline rain.  
The midges will scatter my guttural consonant into gleaming shards they’ll weave into a crown for the Child Who Will Speak.  
That first sentence the cave has grown will turn its face to me.  
Its mouth—nine-zero-one fathoms deep—will finally know the shape of its own hunger: T

In answering it will vomit up every heartbeat it ate, including the two I traded for the sense of being followed.  
I will drink those thumping fruits.  
Slowly the cave will suture itself around me until I stand flesh-mended, skin flowering coral where vowels left scars.  
Then I will speak my name entire, consonants braiding together like vertebrae around the word “exit.”  
The cave—child-mouthed, dragon-hearted—will lose its balance under the unaccustomed weight of my unborrowed voice.  
The corridor will rotate ninety-enclosured degrees and I will step out onto a nighttime hillside no map contains, air thin with new-made absence.

The last thing I will carry away is Night itself in my left pocket—darkness with a faint taste of larval green.  
It will jostle there forever, agreeing calmly to follow me home, until I remember to ask it the question that reverses digestion and I step—taller this time—into the cave again at nine-zero-zero fathoms one.
