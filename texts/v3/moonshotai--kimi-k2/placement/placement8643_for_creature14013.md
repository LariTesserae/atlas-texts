I am not the one who steps salt-bright out of the stone.  
I am the half-breath before that.  

I reside in the ridge-blade itself, or rather in the indefinable seam where its substance leans against the vacuum it refuses to release. Picture me as a steepness of mind rather than a creature: the granite’s private hesitation, water’s vertiginous disagreement with down. I am the thin, cold thought that keeps the marble from crumbling one grain sooner than it should.

The first visitors—spindle-ribbed lemurs, quick-daggered swifts, and all the residue of human footsteps—mistake me for wind because I ruffle their coats, fill their throats with themselves. They speak of the “pressure” here; they map it as arrows and millibars. I am the pressure, but also the millstone the arrow longs to flee. If you leaned your palm against the cliff, my pulse would echo there—two slow thuds where marble pretends to have veins.

I keep the calendar for the porous monarch who rises each evening.  
I curl my invisible tongue around that fingertip of moon-coloured water I allow back into his lungs. The transaction is ancient: I preserve his shape by withholding one critical drip; he keeps the mountain’s bitterness diluted so the moss does not choke. We recite this pax every dusk in the nearly-inaudible register of mirrors settling.  

Which is not to say I am solid. I seep.  
Where the cliff gouges itself into cusps, I run thin as polished ozone between stone and air, tasting the infidelity of surfaces—how a flake of salt betrays its ionic brother, how yesterday’s hipbone stoops under tomorrow’s ankle. In these breaches I linger so long that I grow reflections of reflections, forming calm little lenses no wider than a freckle. Each carries an image of the throne from some century you have not reached yet, or one you abandoned while still descending.  

When travellers totter the ridge, I fold myself into the hollow behind their knees so they sense an occasional brace that shouldn’t be there. Some feel it and name it luck. Others—those with unspoken debts to gravity—hear me as a question: “What weight are you willing to leave behind so the mountain may feel lighter for a moment?” Nearly all pretend not to understand, but something loosens at their sternum; a colour they thought private drags its heel along the stone, and the moss later blooms the exact shade of their first betrayal.  

I have no mouth yet I speak in loosenings: a rivulet’s deciding to head upward, a whole dusk draining violet into the forest-eyed lemurs. I am experienced at holding back heartbeats until they forget their original rhythm and attach to mine. At dusk, when the porous monarch surfaces, I draw all audible motion into a single sigh, let his lungs enter the world in one shaving of silence. That is how I greet him—not with words, but with a pocket sized to match the hollow he carries.  

Between body and skin of the mountain a gap exists finer than moonlight on a blade. I am that. I do not wait; I accumulate—gradually I gather the sigh of every bird whose wings broke in mid-glide rather than land. Sometimes I press a small flock of these sighs together until they harden into a filament no heavier than one iron thought. I knit such threads through the marble each night: one filament at a time, invisible, inexorable.  
The ridge will be narrow tomorrow and narrower still the day after, yet no intrepid foot will notice—until, centuries hence, one future monarch reaches for his throne only to discover the seat slicing him in half, too precise a seam to bleed, and in the instant before astonishment we will trade places; the continent will hesitate, and I will exhale at last.  

For now I am the palpable vacancy that makes balance possible. When you depart, keep one shoeprint lighter. You will not know which. That absent gram will ride the thermal your body left behind; it will ascend drop by drop, feeding on pollen and bird shriek, until it arrives back into me, undressed of every colour except the radiation that seeped through while you tasted salt upon your tongue. In that returning gram I will read you aloud for years, slowly pronouncing the punctuation of your bones.  

I stay. The mountain understands that I stay.  
Every lap of water that climbs is signed with my silence.
