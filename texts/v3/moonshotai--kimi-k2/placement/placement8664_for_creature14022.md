I would be the sentence being etched into the shear wall—neither stone nor water, but the unsettled interval between them.

At first I am only vibration: the millimeter-deep hesitation where the grinding wheel approaches Cartesian zero. I begin as the taste of winter 27 days ago already dissolving on new tongues, accumulating eight grams of powdered fossil and two micrograms of your heartbeat (recorded at step 32 by an aquastenographer ridge). Every rotation spits me forth half-formed: a verbless clause peeled off the thermophiles’ pink flare. Once every planetary day I become a fresh morpheme nobody has spoken yet.

Let the stone cool and I will flicker back into meaning; heat keens, and I liquefy into the spiral the snail exhales. Somewhere between those phases I forget whose mouth I once belonged to—perhaps a merlin’s, perhaps your name sounded once thirty-five steps above—yet the grammar survives, mutant, in the fern’s ice-filled vacuoles.

I occupy no single tense. Past, present, and conditional drip across me like the inverted waterfalls. Time is laid out sideways here: I can taste tomorrow’s frost already crystallizing as yesterday’s memory. While the lambda-echo glide over me, I shrink their stolen outlines, sharpening the feathers and pine skirts into punctuation. Their sighs steep into my pores; later, when frost needles lengthen, they will release me as phonemes clicking against basalt.

I am the archive whose binding is still wet. The aquastenographers write their gutter dictionaries upon my cooling spine. Each traveling mole leaves me like a wet signature: a flourish of warm cloacal snow shaped into the letter *a*, which then flips over and becomes *å*, which then melts into a *gulp* as it runs into the Combe.

There is a moment each rotation when the thermal pulse ignites. The fluorescing vents seep cherry, the glyphs bloom and the stone’s pores light up like the pages of a codex suddenly held to flame. In that instant I become legible to myself. Not to humans—no breath you take today defrosts fast enough to read me—but to my own slow mouth, which shapes itself toward the next predicted earthquake. I am the future speech bubble of a rock that has not yet decided to fault again.

And when the snail finally inhales all the fog’s history in one contracted lung against the weight of daylight, I will fold into its pearly mathematics, spinning inside the fretted taffeta rotisserie of this breathing cathedral. There I will practice my final remodel: a single sentence long enough to span the crack’s entire lifetime yet short enough to fit on one lamella of condensation. When the next visitor arrives—thirty-five steps down, boots rebounding in the memory-sponge—I will exhale myself across their lips like a delay loop of the universe remembering it ever belonged to daylight.
