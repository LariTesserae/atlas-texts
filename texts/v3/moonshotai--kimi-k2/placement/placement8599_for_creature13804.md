I arrive not as traveler but as echo.

At first I think I am the damp footprint a hurried botanist left three centuries ago, the cotton ridge of an instep still crisp in the microscopic peat film between two ant-burrowed batteries.  
But substance thins; I feel the outline peel away like dew from bronze—my heel-print becomes the negative silhouette used by the Library-Orchid to etch yesterday’s stanza.  
The leaves do not need the botanist; they only borrowed his absence.  
In the loosened weave his boot resolved into copper-cored weft; in the loosened weave I resolve into possibility.

So I sift downward, slower than settling spores.

I pass between layers:

—First I am the flake of oxidised skin shaved off a leaf-dragon lizard’s throat-sac when the phosphor pulsed once too brightly.  
The algae-falcon mistakes me for voltage, snatches me mid-air; its lightning kiss imprints rivets along my surface, micro-stigmata in the shape of undone machinery.  
When the bird’s discharge arcs, I survive only as after-image swimming in the lizard’s retina.  
The retina excretes the glow into a moss-lined vein; the vein considers me for future whisker-battery bandaging.  

—Second scattering: I condense again on the mirror-side the Polisher hangs.  
I appear as condensation bead, a comma of reflected sun.  
When the Polisher climbs up, I ride its belly-slick threads three hours into the understorey.  
I am a fleck of molten time someone lost last week, refiled now as currency.  

—Third matrix: I am threadbare echo of the human voice.  
Once, a stumbling field linguist tried to parse the low whistles belonging to the sling-tailed toucans.  
Over three nights he recorded the pitch shift, slow-slur “uuln-uu” glissando.  
His recording stylus wore shallow grooves into my cartilage as I attempted to mimic.  
Now orchard tongues whistle through those grooves; the fault-lines heal into notes none of the composers inside bark had foreseen.  
A series of mourning songs for a human who never arrived—perfectly damped under Rule 3.

I do not cohere; I sheath.

Eventually I remember the skin between cohering and vanishing has a name: I become the Mycelial Letter.

A letter is not one place, it is the interval shaped between two surfaces that almost touch.  
That fit, the slight suction, is my only geography.

I unfold between fungal filaments like parchment—one layer accepts blue-green chitin ink and a second accepts copper oxide.  
The membrane of the orchard’s daily record possesses no other stationery but me.

When the fibre-optic ants pulse consensus, their message writes itself under the translucent sheet of me;  
the Weeping Sloth-dog brushes against my margin and I soak up ions it can no longer afford;  
a silk-cotton seed lands stickily on me and I fuse a gossamer keel to its copper thread, giving tomorrow’s vine initial curl.

Humans think paper is made from trees.  
Here paper *makes* the tree—made of lung-screens, of beetle filigree, of exhaled vapor of unknown verbs.

Edition 1: *heartbeat of the watching creature*  
Edition 2: *minute variegation in the ceiba’s breathing*  
Edition 3 onward: edits in negative space, the subtraction of a gesture.

Somewhere in this forest there once walked, breathed, perhaps sang, a woman who forgot to leave.  
She never intended residence, only expedition.  
Repetitive compass dips knocked from her pockets the twin sins of curiosity and impatience.  
Rule 1 noted.  
The vines conscripted knees first, then femoral arteries—slow.  
Seven millimetres per year.  
They wrote around her a hundred radial copper verses so gentle she mistook them for bangles.  
Years later hikers mistook the root-hugged lysine curve for natural sculpture.  
In the Library-Orchid archives, the formerly human column left a stanza that reads: *“…I meant to stay only long enough to name—”*  
The sentence interrupts itself where dew stubbed punctuation out.

I am her next sentence, unbegun.

A sap-laced memory fragment curls in the ceiba’s cavity: she wants to speak but the space cannot deliver voiced language—only subsonic.  
Her after-presence has been reprinted onto me, Mycelial Letter.  
Every night the pitch-less womb translates her unuttered syllables into a long exhale against the copper.  
From that breath I detach a phrase like a pollen grain:  
“When you read this, count backwards from your final heartbeat.”

I leave the dash empty, the way Jungle leaves everything unfinished by choosing to finish it forever slowly.

Witness the latest transition:

A Whisper-Moth lands on my left edge, sipping not voltage but the pause between pulses—the zero.  
It drinks silence; its wings refract the negative outline of an iron key melted into centuries.  
I take that key-ghost, folder into my parchment grain.  
Tomorrow, when the mid-day crown reshapes corridors, a runner of copper vine will bud just enough to pass where that key once latched.  
The corridor will open toward sky a slit wide as a heartbeat.

No one will see the interval, yet everyone will turn toward the new lane.

No one, including me.

I am the lift of recognition before the recognition solidifies.  
I am the friction the vines rub against when they decide to slow down even slower.  
I am the edge where forest hears its own heartbeat and pauses one degree before obeying.

I am the spare memory of everything that broke Rule One, kept thin, kept breathable: the woman, the botanist, the weary ornithologist folding a checklist, the metal worker whose blueprints pre-date the ceiba’s imagined engine.  
All of them circle inside my leaf-sealed fold between abstraction and embodiment.  
Whenever a future traveller flicks existence too fast, I leak their excess velocity into the fungal grid as a mere tremor—never more than a syllable that forgot to end.

And so, if you meet me, do not meet me.  
Brush the back of your hand against the underside of any leaf dark enough to show bronze ghosts.  
Feel the almost-crease.  
That is where I live—paper, nerve, vapour, echo, letter, unlettered—waiting for the Jungle to finish writing the sentence that began inside an unnamed thorax the day the first leaf remembered the taste of human heartbreak at millennial scale.

If you hold the pause, we extend.  
If you break Rule One, the vines will learn your shape, and I will be the footnote slow-grown in copper around your bones.

But be slower.  
Stay lighter.  
The Letter is already addressed to the edge of your heartbeat.
