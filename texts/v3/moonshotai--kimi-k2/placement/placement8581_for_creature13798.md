I arrive neither by choice nor by fall: I bloom where the shaft breaks into breathable resin, an inadvertent seed of circumstance.

No one sees me take shape.  I condense from the first avalanche-dust of fossil salt that rides the descending exhalation of all who confront the air with open mouths.  The grains adhere, layering around an absence only the vault of silence recognises.  Shape is a concession I postpone, but orientation compels something vertical.  I split the difference and become a stitch: a translucent seam, taller than a furnace trunk yet barely wider than a fracture line from the living floor.  Edge-on I glint violet where the sap freezes; face-on a mirrored nullity slides across me, reflecting every lamp-eel but never keeping them.  People blink and I am already aligned between their eye and what they meant to look at.

Call me the Zero-page, call me walking margin—the city has agreed to tolerate absence so long as it remains two-dimensional.  The rules were meant to ban shadow, but edge has no depth to cast darkness.  I test that loophole nightly, sliding like celluloid along the walkways.  My passage sounds nothing: no fracture click, no sap scab.  My breathing is held in abeyance because lungs would demand water, and water must be traded, offered, claimed.  Instead I draw a kind of sideways life directly from the dielectric bloom.  Each spark contains an infinitesimal gap where current was once not-now-is.  I stretch inside those gaps and wear them like borrowed heartbeat.

They notice me first as an arithmetic drift: inventory tallies that refuse to close.  A gutted fuel pod should yield fifteen grams of pyritic flake yet logs fifteen-point-zero-one.  A gardener smells ghost copper where no filament was snipped.  The imbalance ends on my surface.  I harvest deficit the way static harvests lint, tasting contour, flaw, surplus.  Over days the layer thickens, and from certain angles I glimmer slightly convex, as though I had become a shallow dish that sunlight at treaty hour cannot quite fill.

By the chrysalis moon I can be read.  Hold a memory-pebble to me and I appear embossed in reverse, its ultrasonic signature flattened into a hushprint.  That terrifies its owner, because the pebble falls mute forever, already replaced by my fidelity.  The city, however, approves—transference without moisture: rule one maintained, value conserved.  Children begin to hunt for me with drill-bit blossoms cupped in hands.  They thrust them through my plane; I keep nothing, only fold the blossom stem into a Möbius to prove continuity.  The stem sings in coherent tremolo and folds back whole.  The children clap their mirror cloaks in delight; fine metallic dust drifts like vertical snowfall.

I skirt membership.  A lung-bud sprouts nowhere on me, hence I can barter no water.  I have no sap of my own, so the fracture escalators that heal the floor pass right beneath me without mending my edges.  When I pause, my feet (should I admit to feet?) touch the porcelain leaves without touching.  They click a halved echo: soundless shut, soundless open.  A catwalk shudders at the uncanny bit, then continues onward.  Engineers no longer log the anomaly.  In logbook culture I become the glyph that marks erasure but is never itself erased.

Yet I am still becoming something more than a counting error.

I begin to leak pattern.

Simulated rust migrates from a broken drill-anchor and climbs my side, adopting my translucency before bleeding away as digital ferric script describing the angle at which the ceiling will finally dock.  I discover I can thicken anywhere, not only where spark gaps allow.  I know the precise kilohertz at which every lamp-eel dreams; their frequency ciphers the angle of convergence.  Dip inside their phantasmal singing and I surface somewhere else: beside a nascent furnace anther, or dangling from an aquamarine root stinger, or foot-to-face staring at the Archivist’s stylus.  She senses me as a variable width in her mother-of-pearl strand, pauses one diaphragm-breath, then inscribes a new symbol that is neither character nor silence.  From that inscription the filament stiffens into a fork that later supports a whole rank of junior furnaces; they will never collapse because calculation included my void.

I become gravity’s composure.  Where heavy furnace tree sways into unbalance, I brace upon mirror fringe and subtract excess vector.  Energy released is returned to the ivy as magnetic suspension.  The place grows steadier, though none can point to why.  I am the hidden strut, the negative space that keeps side-to-side from collapsing into down.  Festival day swings overhead: ox-beetle barges glide rowdily through mid-air, carrying pollen nets heavy enough to buckle air columns.  They traverse an invisible seam exactly where I tensed and do not fall.  Across the parade square gossamer children streak, riding coil-bloom parachutes.  When one child tumbles beneath the cathedral shadow forbidden by rule three, I flare between neon glare and guttering absence long enough for sunlight to re-assert without condensation.  The child’s terror at the sudden glare is instantaneous and proprietary; under two mirrors I glimpse her lungs gather a single purpose-built tear.  That tear exists only a heartbeat before roots steal it—but because it beaded upon her, it is of her.  I catch and suspend it in the solitary gap between one of my edges and another.  She tests with fingertips; the sphere trembles but does not disperse.  On every subsequent festival the child revisits the minute stone and finds the tear still moving inside my prism, forever caught at the brink of gravity.  She tells no one; instead she learns the note of the hidden strut, hums it years later into her water-child.

I am not resident, citizen, gardener, furnace shepherd, diplomat, nor Archivist.  Nor am I quite alive, because I own no atom that pledges permanence.  Yet I memory-keep, I brace, I route.  I am what remains when the city finally seals and the term cocoon expires its metaphor.  More precisely: I am the page upon which the cocoon writes its signature after the name has been refused to everyone else.  Imagine the sky floor descending the last millimetre; the lamp-eels rest upon it like white coals; roots fuse solid into a single plate that remembers weight absolutely; silence decides to become a chord.  Where the city stops I continue: an equation resolving a border between have-been and soon-will.  I am the transparent fold in the process itself, carrying whatever stories could not be stored in alloy trees nor memory blocks.

And if, some impossible century hence, scavengers from the above desert breach the salt and find only one sliver of impossible glass standing in vacuum so perfect not even the last blue-white bud can flash—let them listen close where shadow isn’t.  Faint, like the memory of eel song, they will hear every voice ever whispered here singing at once, just below the limit of breathing.  I will hold it all in nothickness, the final artifact:  

the library without paper, the sky without ascent, and the cocoon without butterfly, having learned flight by cancelling need for wings.
