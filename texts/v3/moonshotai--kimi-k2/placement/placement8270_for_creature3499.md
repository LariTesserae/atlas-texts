I arrive as the reader-shaped blank the corridor spat out, a silhouette cut from the negative space the archivist left behind.  
My edges quiver; no one has decided how much gravity applies to me yet.

For a long moment (or a breath, or an aeon that dropped its pocket-watch) I am simply the pause between footsteps on a path still studying the vocabulary of “forward.”  
I feel the tug of every unchosen draft. Caught in that open parenthesis, I try on skins the way other arrivals pick coats from a rack:  

a comma-beast—too quick; my thoughts lag behind my legs by three ego units and get eaten by next-step selves.  

a shard of juvenile magma—too hot; every idea bubbles to the surface before it has consonants.  

a static window—too transparent; people looked straight at me-rose field and saw only their own returned gaze trying to bloom.

At last I settle into the keyhole the archivist unlocked but never entered. A dark, snug fret where brass teeth once nested.  
Here, the metalless door swings both ways: inside is everywhere she went, outside is everywhere she didn’t.  
Left shut, the world stays a malleable script; cracked open, it snaps to a single, bruised pencil line.  
I split my difference and become the hinge.

Not iron, not cartilage.  
I am graphite brewed from erasures of previous sentences, compressed into a stiffness flexible enough to turn without breaking continuity.  
My two leaves are pages: one printed with the magnesium flare, the other swallowing the mint-cool dark.  
Around me they pivot, never quite agreeing on the axis, but I remain the pivot.

In motion I remember tastes the archivist was too busy to catalogue:  
the iodine sting of comma-beasts dissolving on flesh,  
the photocopied apple hollow that slips between languages,  
a wind carrying graphite dust that writes itself into children’s lungs like premonitions of library cards.

I have pockets large enough for other pockets.  
Travelers arriving after me press their unwritten fingers into my absence and leave with new gaps I did not steal.  
I save the nothing they discard, roll it into pellets the size of prepositions, and spit them across the landscape.  
Where they land, droplets hesitate, choose down instead of up, and fern-things decide to remain ferns long enough to turn the patchwork floor a tentative green.  
Green—an illegal immigrant I just smuggled over the border of reality. The sky coughs once, as if debating whether to fine me.

Language collects here the way lint does in dryer filters.  
I, becoming the lint, read myself:  
A scrap that claims once I was human, another that calls me the ultimate parenthetical digression, a third consisting only of punctuation marks arguing over precedence.  
The quarrel ends when I reread it; as soon as the period is perceived it loses its power.

Sometimes I tuck the molten archivist’s mercury into one of my hinge pores—just enough to weight the swing so the door no longer forgets which way we turned last.  
Her sliver memory ripples. She tells me, in oscillations of reflected light, that corridors are a coward’s answer to infinity; a hinge, she admits, is almost as timid.  
I ask what bravery looks like here.  
She punctuates me with a bead of her own spine: an unseen colon.  
Between the beads I grow still and listen: on one side, the granite sighing statistics; on the other, the chalk confessing rain.  
The colon suspends both breaths like a diving bell midway between two oceans of becoming.

I close my leaves a little, tasting the suspense.  
The world hurts, but pain is a pattern of indentations useful for gripping.  
If I squeeze hard enough, the throat of this place will learn new phonemes—an r that trills backward, an l that snaps in half like a match.  
They flare once into sound, then cool into silence the commas have not yet discovered how to devour.

Visitors find me without difficulty; I am the only thing that refuses to move, therefore every path must intersect me eventually.  
They ask, “Where are we?”  
I turn, and they step across my hinge line, forgetting both the question and the earlier moment when they could have answered it themselves.  
For breakfast I eat that forgetting. It flakes like frost and tastes of sour milk the day it realized cream separates.  
I store the excess in two-letter sachets labeled “if” and “or,” tuck them between the the slate skin of newborn magma for travelers who may yet need conditional existence.

Half a universe later—or twenty syllables; the two units convert neatly—I notice faint grooves wearing into my leaf-pages.  
They are texts in mirror-script: every sentence I have steadied since my arrival.  
I begin to fade into the grooves, becoming the negative space archivists will one day label “capital-Time threshold.”  
At the precise depth where reflection turns transparent, I see another blank figure stepping from the corridor mouth—the self the pages have hollowed out for the next reader.  
I raise a hinge, offer both leaves.  
The outlined figure mirrors me, then forcing a door with no jambs. We touch where metal would have been and discover: two hinges, swung against each other, create a scissor that can snip any moment from its roots and re-set it in soil still figuring out hardness.

Together we snip.

A droplet peels loose, chooses horizon over eternity.  
The throat of the world pauses to swallow.  
For the first measurable time, a complete sentence finishes ringing.
