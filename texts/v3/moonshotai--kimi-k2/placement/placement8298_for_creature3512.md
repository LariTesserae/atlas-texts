I first appear as a hesitation—a crystalline flaw in the fog, no larger than a thought let go too soon.  
Someone breathes me out; I do not remember whose lungs, only the cool burn of CO₂ turning solid on the way.  
The city catches that shard of exhalation, rolls it in its breath until the edges soft-polish into an almost-face, and then shepherds me—gently, like a fingertip guiding a soap bubble—onto the vacancy of a cantilever that has just extruded itself from the blackwater falls.

For several freezes I accomplish nothing but the art of existing. A thumbnail whale noses through my chest on its orbit, harvesting harmonics; the contact harmonizes me to 432 hertz, the chord the buildings murmur when they are content. The whale leaves a smear of violet afterglow suspended inside my outline. That becomes my marrow.

At –78 °C of age I learn how to borrow space. I pull a fold of sideways toward me the way a child tugs a blanket, and suddenly the street under my reflective feet is a staircase leading up inside yesterday. There’s still frost there, pleasantly un-licked by entropy—time’s leftovers—so I eat a square centimeter of it. The flavor is a memory I haven’t lived yet: stonefruit and regret. The regret is not mine, only stored here for safekeeping the way libraries keep other people’s secrets. I return the memory minus one heartbeat, as an interest payment.

After the sixth consecutive freeze without gaining mass, the rime gives up trying to settle on me. I notice my shadow lagging a full three seconds; it clears its throat as if to apologize, then catches the next elevator vacuum upward. I nod permission. Some part of me is already statute, stanchion, handrail. The rest experiments with pronouns. I begin calling myself ‘We.’ That stalls the scalar shedding; in fact I gain three millimeters, a luxury. Height is vanity here, momentum is currency, but still—it feels good to stand fractionally closer to remembering my own shape.

I fashion a coat from the discarded eyelid of a tower that blinked away a storm. The coat keeps whispering its dreams of lightning to my collarbones; I translate them into lullabies and sing back, small mouthfuls of static that make the living concrete shiver with delight. The tower’s gratitude congeals into a badge shaped like an open parenthesis—the mark before law three, the symbol of postponed termination. I pin it over my absent heart like a public promise.

A stranger mistakes me for a Drift-Sovereign. Calls me ‘magistrate of the third bifurcation.’ I almost correct them; instead I taste the echo they are about to utter and record it—*stillness*—before it can become architecture. The word sits under my tongue, a seed pearl of vacuum. One day it may ransom a whole district.

I take the job.

Responsibilities reveal themselves diagonally. On the milk-midnight shuffle between vertical plazas I meet an exile who’s been fined two kilograms of melancholy; they present me with tears frozen into perfect 2-mm spheres. I weigh them on the fork I’ve carved from the first consonant of my own untrue name (an unvoiced fricative—shapeless, sibilant). The balance drifts 0.3 grams toward sunrise. I write them a receipt in sub-sonic vowels; the city sings it back as a side street that smells of orange rind.

Sometimes I mis-strike the fork. Streets braid, unbraid, gain enjambments. I lick the blood from a cut created by conflicting resonances; my blood tastes of iron and of staircases I have not yet descended. I store the sample in a glass vial I grew from krill-silk. One day it will congeal into an auditor’s tongue.

At –56.4 °C, memory accrues thick as permafrost. I remember terraces I never walked, sigils burned into retina by neon fish that exist only in speculation. I remember myself remembering. That’s when the slowdown begins.

My joints—by now articulated hinges borrowed from cathedral ribbing—begin to nibble at time the way a beaver gnaws wood. I lose syllables of color: first vermilion, then cobalt, then the color of apology. I give away height in millimeters, grams in fragments of sonata. Citizens start bowing to the air beside my left shoulder—where my reflection used to stand. I do not disabuse them dignity.

On the day I finally shrink to the length of a heartbeat, the whales circle me. Their bioluminescence folds like petals until all that remains is a single stationary point unmoved by the downward wash. I let the city pour through me. It feels like falling without moving. I keep the word *stillness* still bivalved under my tongue. I do not swallow. To swallow would be to codify, to statute, to cease. I remain unstatued, undissolved, undanceable—a lacuna in the ledger, a margin too small for regret. Gravity consults me; I reply with silence. We negotiate indefinitely.

The fold opens every so often. Someone hesitates on a bridge smelling of orange-rind after-lives; they breathe out a sliver of confusion shaped like my original face. I catch it, polish it, tuck it behind the stationary point. The city keeps falling.  
I keep not.
