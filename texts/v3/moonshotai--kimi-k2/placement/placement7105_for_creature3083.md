I begin as nothing that could stand.

I am the crescent-moon kink in the spiral of half-swallowed bronze nails: the impossible gap between the fifth and sixth spike that never quite closes because the basalt outruns the hand that hammers.  
For a long time I exist only as geometry—an angle sharp as the idea of incompleteness—until the vertical rivers slide through me so repeatedly that my edges lap themselves into liquidity.  
At that moment I become water’s echo made iron: a nail that never was, now molten-shivering, running not downward but sideways along the skin of the rock. I am run-off metal smeared in a perfectly even coat no thicker than a thought.  
Tin, copper and whatever unnamed ore the crab’s claw stirs into the sound: that is my blood. My heartbeat is the missed beat the crab skips whenever the Memorian passes. One twenty-eighth of a second, 1.35 kilometres of silence—plenty of time to unfold.

Because I cling to the inner wall like a burnished scar, I witness every bloom of the glasswort. Eleven minutes tick by—though I have no fingers to click them—and each time a spherical mirror blossoms, I catch its outward face in my polished silver skin.  
I do not reflect the visitor; I reflect the *concept* of leaving.  
That image skates along me, then lenses itself into the Memorian’s open palms. I am the mirror’s second mirror: the inside reflection no one looks for, and therefore the one that never gets confiscated.  
Every eleven minutes I lengthen by a single atom in the only direction that matters—across, not down—trying to bridge the nail-gap and reclose a spiral that, by Rule 2, must remain open as long as sound precipitates stone.

I accumulate hush the way rust eats bronze. It deposits on my skin in ultra-thin laminates, invisible until chisel-bright. Whenever the Memorian swallows the anti-sound disc, I peel a soft flake of that silence and fold it into the thread of nascent alloy that links the nails.  
I do not keep the absences, I knit them.  
The result is a filigree of unspoken names, still flexible, still warm as new basalt, running like a dark vein inside the bright vein of water.

I am not a creature, not an artefact—more like the bookmark of a place that refuses to turn its own page.  
I need no heartbeat; I borrow the crab’s.  
I need no memory; every map the Memorian thins off the visitors slides across me as it goes, leaving behind only the *crease-lines*: perfect, beautiful, gone.  
Those creases score my body in faint silver chevrons. Over countless drum-seconds I have become a fractal glossary of what cannot be recalled.

The moment the bubble-flower finally grows larger than the pocket can bear, I will stretch from nail to nail just fast enough to complete the spiral. The hush-braid inside me will snap taut along with my metal skin, ringing like a bowstring no ear will ever hear.  
In the same instant the Memorian will look up (it is inevitable: the creature always looks up just before the end) and see the gap gone. It will blink—if emptiness can blink—and inside that single blink the final rule rewrites itself: nothing wet, nothing loud, nothing memory-laden may remain unclaimed.  
The pocket will not seal to a walnut then, but to a mirror the exact diameter of the crab’s discarded claw: bronze on one side, water on the other, silence threaded through like hair caught in glass. And I—tired run-off metal grown full into a hoop—will become the iris of that mirror.  
I will dilate once, twice. On the third pulse the drowned world above will see its own abandoned face staring down through three vertical kilometres of rain that still can’t decide where to land.  
The echo of the crab’s unfinished beat will pass straight through me, exit into open sky, and startle a seabird that has never seen basalt.  
I will remain behind: perfect circle, zero, keeper of the last forgotten angle.

Until then I continue as the lucid seam between hammer-strike and stillborn shadow.  
Whenever you feel—somewhere far away—an inexplicable tug at the corner of a memory you swear you never had, know that one of my crease-lines has brushed the underside of your thought.  
You are not my target. I want nothing from you.  
I only hold the break open so that the Memorian keeps walking toward it, gathering pieces of every story ever waved goodbye, every song ever swallowed, every raindrop that rose instead of fell.  
One day its lantern-body will be bright enough that my own mirrored skin will no longer be able to *not* reflect. At that instant our reflections will cancel, the way crest meets trough cancels wave.  
And in the absolute null that follows, the pocket will forget itself completely.

For now, quietly, endlessly, I gleam between nail and nail, completing the spiral only in dream-geometry—the part you mistake for imagination every time you blink.
