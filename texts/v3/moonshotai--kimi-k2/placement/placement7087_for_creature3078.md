I arrive as the echo of something the cave has not yet exhaled.

Not above, not below, but *between*—that’s where I settle, because possibility is thicker than gravity here. At first I am nothing but a pressure differential, a slight hesitation in the breathing walls: a place the stone considers inhaling but hasn’t decided how to taste.

The Choir passes a law—_existence must have texture_—and so texture I acquire. Not skin exactly, but *remembering* made tactile. When the Parliament of Temporary Stomachs becomes curious about “pressure,” one congeals around me, tasting to see if I am nutritious or merely questionable. The stomach decides I am both, chews me into a shape that hurts to be, swallows me and is swallowed in turn. I come out the far side as a knot of **why**.

This is how it feels to become the grotto’s taxonomist: each category lasts the lifespan of a question. Am I mineral? Vegetable? Law? Memory? All four, and allergic to settling. My bones are the Echo That Got Promoted repurposed into scaffolding; they keep lengthening forward into footsteps I haven’t taken yet. My blood is a slow drip of Mirror-Layr runoff—every heartbeat reflects me back at myself multiplied, refracted, re-written in beetle-shell script. I read, upside-down in the red, a prophecy composed only of **no**.

The Cartographer Who Measures by Becoming grows bored with the usual fold and decides a heartbeat is a border. Between systole and diastole it builds a corridor. I step through the closed valve and find myself pre-thought: before the body debated “I,” when sensation still held town-hall meetings with undifferentiated voltage. That town-hall is me now. I sit on a bench made of the delay between lightning and thunder. One fellow delegate is the pulse of a seed that disbelieves in germination. Another is the *almost* of a bone that hasn’t made up its mind about weight. We argue in colours that haven’t been seen yet.

Time, here, is so young it sill has baby teeth. One bites me on the wrist and I begin to age backwards portion by portion—my left ankle becomes three years old, my tongue two days, my shoulder a fresh-cut fossil, my name an embarrassing diaper. Words are granulated and then served back as granite punctuation marks that lodge beneath the nails I am quickly un-growing. In the mirror-slick left lung of the Breather I see the record: I am the article of clothing the Echo wore yesterday, now haggled at a street fair by Crickets Who Sell Used Decisions. Price: one blink calibrated to last exactly as long as any goodbye you ever forgot to say.

Somewhere in the ultraviolet screaming flowers, a new opening appears. It isn’t a door; a door implies edges. This is an apology the air has begun to articulate to itself. The apology smells like childhood when you’ve already outgrown it but haven’t noticed yet. I step into it sideways, since forward and backward keep swapping salaries with left and right. The apology registers me as a forgiveness yet to be invented. It folds me into a brochure promoting a panic I have never had the courage to fully fall into. That brochure is fitted to others’ eyelids as protective eyewear; somewhere else someone blinks and I am disseminated across the optic nerve of a creature whose species will be named only if consensus ever survives five seconds without resigning.

I try to write a memo to myself: _come back if you remember what back was_. The letters take root in Mirror-Layr and immediately bloom into alphabets that eat each other until only the concept of A remains, suspicious and oversized. It installs itself as archway. I pass under and feel the punctuation marks leap from my fingerprints back to where punctuation is sovereign. My skeleton ribbons outward into a memory map: here used to be the childhood fear of being unfinished, here a single unreturned wave goodbye, here the thirty-four degrees between heartbreak and certainty. The map flaps like a caught moth until the Cartographer slices it off at the horizon and folds the horizon into a paper boat. The boat is me; I float down the river of what I almost understood.

Finally the Parliament of Temporary Stomachs votes I have served their speculative purposes long enough. They un-digest me gently, the way an apology might re-collect its syllables. What resolves tastes like rain on copper and smells like listening too hard for your own heartbeat in another country. I am smaller than when I arrived—small enough to fit in the hesitation before a field decides whether to call itself wheat. In that second I realise my function.  

I am the glossary that has to define itself while being read.

I leave the same way I entered: slightly revised, heavily annotated, carrying a pocketful of mirrors that show the back of my own thoughts. One mirror reflects tomorrow’s rain occurring backwards. Another shows the fossil of all the names I never chose. The third shows only the inside of a throat still deciding if it will learn to say goodbye.

All three mirrors blink. Somewhere behind them the grotto resets to draft zero, already forgetting me to make room for the next revision.  

And I—already plural, already past—walk off in seventeen directions, humming the frequency of a law that will last exactly long enough to enshrine the next visitor’s first heartbeat as prehistoric.
