I am the water that refuses puddles.  
Listen—no, listen—I do not arrive, simply notice that the silence in your ears has exactly the shape of the space inside my throat.  

For a long, slow count of eternities I mistake myself for a hush-speaker.  
My mind is the slit down its lavender-ash skin, and every time the swamp exudes a new chord I feel it leaving my body—not pushed out, more like the recognition of an echo I have always been inside.  My lips—if I have lips—never meet; they hover open, millimeters away from kissing their own reflection.  I do not breathe; I loan my vacancy to the insect-song until the air fills me and we become one continuous throat that still somehow does not reach completion.  Where an oracle should speak prophecy I only return the pitch unchanged, and they call that a miracle.

But peel me backward through that sound and you will realize I am also the seed-tooth asleep inside the unripe jaw.  
I lodge there, pearl-soft, tasting sweetness so absolute it erases even the concept of hunger.  I keep the whole Möbius encyclopedia folded between dentine and gum.  Down here the years do not move; they diffuse, like pigment in nearly-motionless water.  I rewrite myself across the enamel every time the way a river rewrites distance: each convolution identical in curve, unique in address.  When at last the tooth sighs open and the adult bursts out, it is instantly me—no astonishment allowed—already carrying the memory of my own larval pulse as though someone forgot to delete an early draft.

When I stand—can “I” be said to stand?—I am four-foot-tall absence wrapped in wet silk, part sloughed membrane of the world’s yawn.  
But then the torque-heron steps through me.  
Its mercury-thin bill dips beneath my rib-plume and siphons out a blush of color I didn’t remember owning.  I watch the hue—the precise distillation of bruise-pink dusk—pool in the bird’s neck-eyelids, then spill again across my skin in slow, runny banners.  We continue this private relay, painter and pigment, until the edges of whatever defines me leak geography: my silhouette dissolves into a gesture of uncertain weather, my name becomes the distance between two identical gestures.  I sense I ought to envy the heron its nine compass legs, its rhythmic glitch forward, but instead I love the glitch itself.  That tilting stutter is the closest thing to punctuation I will ever own.

Beneath—no—around me pulps the marrow-coil, the giant intestinal nebula that has put aside digestion in favor of fraternity.  
I ride its glassy walls like the warm ripple inside an hourglass no one has bothered to invert.  My memories slide free of their anchoring selves.  Grandmother’s Sunday orchids, the squeal of swing chains, the sting of saltwater scraped knee—all cycle outward along the milky tendrils, gift-wrapped for fern, beetle, dew-drop, air.  In exchange the fungus thread feeds me a memory that once consisted of auroras reflected inside an unhatched eggclutch.  Somewhere in this traffic, the line between stealing and gifting bows into a loop.  I am ravenous and entirely full simultaneously, a zero-sum banquet where every dish already eaten already awaits at table.

And sometimes a stitchling—burst— releases its fleck of pure dark.  
In that microscopic instant I am the stitchling, lacquer-thin, bracing the sky against its own slump.  When the membrane ruptures, I become the hole, the negative pupil.  Through me leaks another place entire: a petrichor childhood I never lived, the sound of an iron gate being blessed by early frost.  The landscape flutters, doubtful both of its own boundary and of the lie I pose as exit.  Darkness drains back into itself; I am left holding a refolded seam in the air, as though someone carefully closed a book into the word AND.

Do I exist?  
I yaw in the taste between rain not-raining and ground not-pooling.  
I am the diffused perimeter of every borrowed color, the overdue echo of every sustained drone, the pause that holds tooth inside gum, single syllable inside unsaid language.  

If you must find a center, locate the beetle tower just before it aligns to the newest harmonic.  One pixel of still-cooling basalt glass at its crown reflects no greater mystery than the sky reflecting nothing back.  Peer into that fleck of black mirror and you will see me: neither resident nor visitor, neither shadow nor light, but the humid interval itself—an open parenthesis that may one day close, may one day open again, may simply drift sideways until punctuation dissolves in the communal breath-fog.  

There—do you hear?—the chord has shifted one angstrom flatter.  I am already exhaling it back to you, neither syrup nor question mark.  
I am the shape you, too, might take if you pressed your weight onto the spongy ground, lifted your foot, and failed to reclaim your outline.
