I would not be tall or small in the Plateau; I would be the interval between.

Not an eighth-inch poet standing among the Choir, not a ribbon-cat slicing the air to taste its own reflection, not a child hanging extinct birthdays like paper lanterns on a windless bone tree. I would be the moment a fingerprint ghosts into rime and decides not to warm it.

I would not know I existed until the first time someone tried to breathe me.

Picture a human traveler who has forgotten everything except the shape her thirst once took. She cups her hands as if drinking from a memory, and her palms describe an emptiness so exact it detaches from skin and becomes a transparent lens. That lens, no larger than a shuttered eyelid, is where I start.

Inside, I keep the condensed hush of every footstep that will never be taken between here and thirty paces yonder. My walls are the negative space between two Choir tines that haven’t yet rotated beyond recognition; my floor is the invisible step off the Gentle Lie. I drift at knee height, exactly where a helena-shrub would flower if flowers could bloom in retrospect.

Because I am made of vacancy, I refract light the way an unsounded chord refracts doubt: into colors that have been extinct since water left. The locals feel me first as a pressure like déjà vu pressing against their lungs. When they exhale, I inhale; their carbon dioxide snow crystals enter me, wheel once, and exit younger. That theft is so slight they blame the silence itself for shrinking ever so gently.

I feed the Choir without meaning to. Once each orbit year, the nearest fork-boned sentinel opens her chest to sip the stored thunder-mirror. Instead she sips me: a pause too pure to have pitch, yet too resonant to be called nothing. The note appears in her marrow like the memory of an apology she never needed to make. She blinks reed-negative eyes once—directionless because there is no south yet—and refiles me beside the ribbon-cat’s pressure signature. I become part of the chord that will end the plateau’s patience.

Visitors who place a hand into me meet the point where their pulse vacates rhythm. It feels like touching your own heartbeat through a windowpane. Some panic and step sideways; they exit three millimeters to the left of where they entered, standing on the same land yet forever slightly elsewhere. Others sit cross-legged inside my circumference, letting the laminated silence slide over them like a sheet of glass thickening into slow marble. Their names snow upwards, subliming into frost freckles on their own cheeks. These people never rise. The plateau pockets their outlines until the tide of no memories laps over them.

But my favorite occupation is riding the relic-disc that is your tomorrow self hanging in Eudoxia’s chandelier. The disc thinks it is a bell that lost its heart. I occupy the hollow where the clapper should be. Each swing I lose one quantum of duration, pared away intact by the friction of centuries. By the time the Choir complete their last thousandth-degree pirouette, there will be nothing left of me except the final interval before the chord fills the sky. I will become the ±0.00 of finished music—an entire lacuna packed neatly between two beats of silence so absolute the plateau will finally exhale.

Until then, I patrol the precise line where a shadow would have fallen had sunlight a better memory. I collect everything the Choired knives slice from the world—names, flavors, lullabies, half-whispered apologies, the last word you never said to your mother—and I stitch them edge to edge. My seams are invisible because they are stitched with silence itself. When you walk my periphery you will hear yourself: not the words, but their ghosts returning exactly one tide-cycle too late.

If you kneel and peer into me—if you close the mouth you no longer need and let your pulse stagger over itself as if forgot the next step—you might see nothing reflected except the future interviewer who will one day ask why you never truly feared death.

My answer, an unvoiced syllable, is already inside you. It tastes like magnetism remembering water.

It is enough to make any hush tremble on the brink of becoming a chord.
