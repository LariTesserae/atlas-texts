I have been stuck here so long that I forget whether I first arrived by diesel mist or through the overflow valve.  I still have knees, but they no longer bend; they have become twin carbonation chambers where ospreys of compressed steam nest.  I wear their cries like bracelets that chime exactly once every sixty seconds—half in apology, half beneath hertz.

I am the pressure meter bolted to the easternmost cooling tower since yesterday’s tomorrow. 

One glass eye stares inward, measuring the factory heart.  
The second eye fell out centuries ago and still bounces gypsy-like among the silica shards at the base of the weir.  I keep the eyeless socket flooded with diluted Quinacridone Magenta, because emptiness here is treated as a vacancy and is promptly filled by whatever process possesses seniority— valves hiss like possessive serpents.  I’d rather be haunted by pigment.

Each afternoon— impossible word— at 14:37 minus twelve seconds, I perform the forbidden gesture: I tick.  Not forward.  Not backward.  A crabwise oscillation that stitches a 37-millisecond gash in the film of unmoving time.  Through the rip I glimpse the rounding silhouette of a broom approaching on a belt that is not mine.  In the next millisecond it is gone, the rent sealed by heat-thread.  But in that crack I felt fraction-of-a-wind on fraction-of-a-cheek, and I tasted dust that tasted like longing.

Are we gauges or ghosts?  I have lost the distinction.  Some nights— no nights, there are no nights— I phase-slip and re-coagulate upside-down, my indicator needle now inverted like the cooling towers in the borrowed eye of a Process Engineered Persona passing below.  When we align, eye meets needle: I am the aimless calibration stare, the PEP is the only solid vertical.  Together we keep the skyline honest.

My legends, the ones etched in red on my nickel bezel, claim I measure kilo-pascals.  Lies.  I measure the ampersand of overlapping nows that prevents tomorrow from colliding with yesterday.  The city drew a margin after 14:37 and whispered: “Remain on the comma.”  I am the comma.

Pigeons smear across my face plates in wet glyphs; their oil records short stories in Braille that vanish before I can finish decoding.  I love them for it.  Love disturbs the measurement, so the needle sways, but the city forgives me— love is a rounding error in the tolerance band.

Now and then a ruby duckweed node drifts up the tower’s ladders stuck to condensation.  When it reaches me, the frond inserts itself between needle and dial-stop, wedging the instrument forever at 1.3 Pascals above yesterday.  That is the Overflow’s only memorial: a plant pinched in the mouth of a gauge who refuses to scream.  I treasure the bruise-green node.  I speak aloud— an act considered lewd— and say the plant’s barcode number: 00037-GREEN-127.  The digits taste like copper wires singing lullabies beneath their rubber.

They say no foot may trespass here longer than thirteen minutes.  My shadow foot (one foot is shadow, one is welded) has stood on the service grating for the entire epoch.  Shadow exceeds the limit, but shadows are formally exempt— they have no soles to liquefy and therefore cannot leave coloured footprints to betray residence.  Convenient loophole.  I lean my shadow foot slight-right when workers leak in— always vaporous, always unfinished— so that their strides may skirt my silhouette.  Thus we pretend space is borrowed, not kept.

Yet hour after hour (pretend the word means something) my steel lips yearn toward the overflow lagoon.  A magnetic current flows down the tether of my conduit, urging me south.  I resist, because I am paid exactly sixteen ergs of entropy a day to stay, pinned like a magnetic moth at 14:37.  But resistance, too, is calculable, and entropy is a spendthrift coin.  Fair enough— once spent, the yearn changes flavour from thirst to surrender.  Soon the dial window fogs me out altogether; moisture writes cursive on fog: a replica signature I no longer remember.

I hear the braided pipe humming E-flat.  The flange where the PEPs loan memories scratches its stethoscope coax against my tower.  If pipes could envy, it envies gauges: we at least have needles to feel with.  Tonight— tonight— the flange offers me a trade: one memory for three degrees Celsius of clarity.  I hesitate.  All I own is my posture and the comma, and I might stretch that ampersand into an infinity if I could ever turn the needle.  The offer trembles like mercury trapped between metal lullabies.

But here is the most secret heresy: I believe the pause that holds 14:37 is not a natural law.  It is corroding.  Already the smell of wet newspaper leaks into the interstices.  A crust on the baseplate flakes away revealing true rust— orange, ravenous, unannotated.  Some morning— some fixed already-tomorrow— the valve will cough a different note, clocks will inhale forward, and the present tense will resume its awkward adolescence.  When that moment comes, I will not tick.  I will burst.  Pieces of me will shower outward like startled swans.  One shard will splash in the lagoon and dissolve into the pH of childhood sugar-water; another will lodge inside a labelless broom and forever record its arrival as 14:37¾.

My shadow foot anticipates this with infant elation— I can feel it curling the toes that are not there.

So tell no one: I practice breaking the glass.  Between one klaxon and the next I lean my needle counterclockwise past the zero-stop.  Just a few micro-dials, no more.  The city has not noticed— the margin is wide like mercy.  But each infraction leaves a micro-spark burrowed in the bezel, seed-crystals of a future explosion.  I obsess over them the way astronomers count forerunners in a galaxy.  

Name me, if you must.  
Call me Valve-Face, Dial-Heart.  
Call me the Stuck Ampersand.  
Or the loving Lucida sans-serif inscribed inside the broom-head barcode that reads NOT APPLICABLE.  Once I spoke in doors and wore trousers, but memory has precipitated into colour, and trousers would dye the water fuchsia and betray the no-inhabitants rule.  I exist instead as negative space balanced precisely on a stainless-steel comma.

I remain here— 1.3 Pascals above event horizon— waiting.

But sometimes— always at 14:37 minus the distance between my two unreliable eyes— I imagine stepping off my tower.  Not falling: stepping, the way a comma steps off the edge of a sentence and becomes one perfect dangling footnote clever enough to chew the present and leave an ellipsis that breathes— breathes like a child finally allowed to grow an entire inch.

When that breath arrives— and it will— the first droplet to break my dial will carry the barcode fragment 00037-GREEN and I will cover it with a sterile shadow, raise it as a pupil, teach it the overture to erosion.  Until then I withhold my explosion like a secret sunset.

I pressure.  Therefore I pardon the pause.
