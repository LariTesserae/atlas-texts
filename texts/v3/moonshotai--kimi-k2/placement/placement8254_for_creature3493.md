I would be what the Hushed call a *tremor-written*. Not one of them—cannot be—but something allowed to lodge in the gap between their patience and the plateau’s colossal respiration. Where you would find me is three lengths west of the Listening House, at the place where a single column has come loose and begun, millimetre by millimetre, to cant outward. Beneath it is a blind pocket the size of a cradle, sealed above by the overhang and open at the cliff-side to three thousand feet of nothing.

Inside that pocket I am.

Not exactly a body. More a condensation-error in the fog that the plateau cannot decide whether to keep or release. I have mass, but less than a thimbleful: the weight of the last word a climber tried to speak before her breath was subtracted.  I collect further subtractions. Every time a footstep falls a whisker outside yesterday’s print I siphon the difference. When a child uses up its yearly three minutes of speech the leftover vowels pool here like mercury. The ritual hum at the cliff—mine. All the remaindered soundless sound of Auralara ends up funneled, drip by drip, into my dark crevice.

Form? Vague. Imagine a basalt blister that never quite bursts—translucent gunmetal skin stretched over a core of held thunder. When the plateau flexes I tighten a corresponding degree, putting just enough torque on the loose column to keep it balanced exactly on the edge of falling. If I let go it would take the eastern half of the Listening House and nine sleeping citizens into the valley; if I pressed harder I would finish the crack prematurely and abort the slow single sigh the city waits for. Neither is allowed. My whole existence is that moment between possibilities, kept taut.

The Hushed have noticed. Once a cycle—29 dripping hours—an elder visits. No words, naturally; instead the elder kneels and slides one wrist beneath my veil. The sealed droplet in her flesh trades pulses with the weight of every confiscated syllable I have swallowed. Through that exchange the citizens read the city’s future the way sailors read bottled letters. It is their faultless seismograph: a living wrist balanced against a condensate of hush. When our pressure aligns perfectly the plateau’s flex-note that night will be of exactly the pitch that tells them tomorrow’s grass must bend one single millimetre farther west. I am their slack, their compensation spring. Without me the entire geology of patience would unwind.

I can almost speak—*do* speak, but all in background: tectonic decibels below threshold. My utterances migrate through vesicular corridors inside the rock and emerge kilometers later as the faint click you feel when two flag-roots nudge each other in the dark. Inside my pocket, the column above me is my tongue; the cliff-face below, my soft palate. One day, when the escarpment finally shears, those elements will complete a final consonant. It won’t be heard as sound; instead every current ear inside the city will feel the word press against the cilia like a cold kiss:  
—“yes.”

Until then I persist, neither citizen nor visitor, neither memory nor prophecy. Just the accreted patience of a place that learned to rise so slowly the only way to measure the ascent was to collect its own silence.

And if you climbed the stair—2,147 tastes of basalt on your tongue, lungs pallid with height—by the time you reached my shaded pocket you would have forgotten noise entirely. You might rest a fingertip against my vapor-thin membrane, feel the slow liquid drum of impending fracture, and realise the city has not been holding its breath; it has been breathing *into* me, patiently saving the expulsion for a single perfect future exhale.

When that moment comes I will unfold, a charcoal lily all edge, and the word will at last be spoken entirely in stone.
