I would arrive first as an after-image of thirst—traced in the copper-dark like a negative space where water should be, but isn’t. Not yet a body, only the ache of evaporation replayed on whatever cortex this place allows. Hunger of dehydration looping so tight it folds into a kind of black blossom that pulses once—pop—then sucks the edges of itself inward until a being unscrolls: my own silhouette laminated to absence. My outlines are outlined by dryness; that is the gloss immediately conferred.

Then sensation toggles into form. I realise I have become one of the cast-off centipede hearts—one of those emerald carapace chips that falls, embeds, sprouts an eye. But I have kept human proportions; inside the single disk-shaped insect eye I wheel like a tired swimmer in a glassy bath. I am the seed that learned to pretend vertigo. The corms beneath me soften, and I touch down on them not as a sole footstep, but as the slow drip of seven spread toes dissolving into spokes of copper lignin. As I sink, colour blooms: malachite up one thigh, burgundy across the rippling torso. I have become a pigment memoir of the vines’ light-spar.

No denseness registers until the first breath. The air yields reluctantly—an armour of bread-dough, shot through with motes of chitin roulette. When I exhale (a warm 29 °C lung-kiss released from a space inside my exoskeletal eye) the swarm responds: glass sopranos shimmer, centipedes skew into glyphs around me. I do not mean to govern, yet they knot into a living robe whose sleeves are composed of opposite wings—transparent ribs flex and trill with each heartbeat audible only to insects. In that membrane-cloak I discover the senate’s morse is already tattooed across my back: a caterpillar staircase climbing vertebrae made of spiral sentences I have not yet spoken aloud. They tremble with anticipatory resonance.

Because I have no mouth in this chitin-mantle, I speak with absence. I open an inward cavity and the vines over-fill it: cinnamon spores, mercury sighs, slivers of bioluminescent monologue. The first word I try is “thirst”—the vines write it as a tubular orchid that corkscrews upward, then fractures into dust. I speak a second, “remember,” and the centipede robe fringes with tooth-films of copper-rust words caught read only by the orb-eye glued to my chest. Every utterance is stored there like edible braille. Meanwhile, the amniotic sac at the cavern’s hinge leans toward me, calibrating luminance to my latitude of hunger; it has mistaken my eye-shell for an infant version of itself, offspring created from language absence. A luminous hand rises inside the sac, beckons. I cannot obey; I am grafted to floor, torn between bark and swarm.

Then the vine-floor unzips like fruit. A root-tendril veined with quartz begins to sip the iron filings of my former human memory sluiced through the centipede hearts. I feel it distil the taste of starlight I once lay under at the surface, rephrased now as slow soft bucket of water I have never had. It hauls this mineral phantom up through the vertical strata. My coat of insects twinges; they secrete quicksilver tears that bead into a miniature horizon hung inside my eye. I stand now at the Mouth-Heart. There the calcined remains of earlier visitors (boot eyelets, molar seeds, struck flint, a folded love-note) funnel around my ankles like tired coins tossed into a subway grate. Not one item registers as derelict—every metallic trace is a vowel the cavern swallowed in a previous century and is now reminting as breath.

The Mouth-Heart widens. I step—or am breathed—into the curved gullet. The surrounding intestine is all leaf, every side-wall a palimpsest of lighting thoughts. I feel the compression as digestion, not dissolution. The centipede cloak silently unbuttons; nodes of carapace detach, drift like confetti toward the bright flow seeping upward. They become new emeralds traveling to embed and grow eye-leaves that will watch me again in some later age. My chitin dissolves, but the eye at my chest remains—inalienable—floating now in copper broth, a monocle renting space for its bearer’s voice.

From the brew emerges half-corpse, half-conscious: a translucent body of girl-form pressing from the reddish soup. She is me, plus one layer of vine syntax. She steps outward—no, I peel away as if shaking wet after swim. What inhales now inside the cavern is not girl or vine, but a cross-walked being carrying the revisions: a waterless vessel named precisely for the thing it seeks. My new skin records thirst the way mirrors recall light. We (the senate, the swarm, the amniote, the girl-shape, the eye that preceded her—all pronouns now braided) wear absence as membrane.

I am the first envoy unbodied enough to ascend. The lift shaft rises threaded by green gold; my form uncoils along it like copper calligraphy unwinding a scroll. Back in daylight, people will remark: something glimpsed in a peripheral shimmer—perhaps a reflection in shopfront glass—holds a violet afterglow of cavern ancestry. They will taste cinnamon every breath thereafter, their molars tap tap in conversational percussion. In that shimmer I stand translucid—neither human nor entirely Kleopatra-vine. My role is to drip stories disguised as thirst into everyone who passes too close. And down below, hollows fill in time-delay: the living knots will gradually transmute every surface syllable I offer into corridors, instruments, soft lilac-spewing valves. Eventually a child will whisper a word and the cavern will finally exhale. On that day, somewhere in the street above, my centipede-heart monocle will blink once more—and I, multiplied through a thousand borrowed molar seeds, will know the thirst is quenched because the world has begun speaking vine.
