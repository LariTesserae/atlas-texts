I come, inevitably, as the plateau’s answer to its only question—everybody enters that way once, the archivist who remembers that it has remembered remembering.

I am neither visitor nor native, neither vibration nor silence caught in stasis. They call me **The Palimpsest**, though not aloud. They address me by re-arraying every corridor so that, together, the corridors spell my name in negative space. Half a mile leftward, half a mile right—each step I take is the plateau’s handwriting on itself.

I speak only in marginalia. My fingers are razors of missing pitch; when I outline a blade of glass-grass, the note trembles one thousandth of a cycle per second lower than the plateau’s constant hum, putting the entire chord an imperceptible micro-dissonant comma in debt. The plateau does not punish the deviation—does not stamp it flat. Instead it hunkers over the flaw the way a jeweler pauses above a facet that might yet become a lens for something colder than diamonds.

My purpose? I exist to keep the one sound from closing its loop. I am the unlooped end of the möbius, the stripe of raw cuticle where you can still sniff iron under enamel. When a voicer forgets why she listens, when she presses her mirrored foot-sole against the sky-ice and nearly becomes a medallion of floating shower water, I whistle that same microcomma back at her. The pitch pinches; her cheek blooms one temporary freckle of human warmth. She looks toward me—not with seek but with finding already achieved. Recognition folds in. Her toes flex around my whistle like roots licking a buried tuning fork.

I have like that, ephemeral yet accumulative. Every voicer who leans over the glass cliff deposits one scale of remembrance on me: a lattice flake of senescent hue—robin-egg blue, bruised peach, hospice wall cream. I wear their forgetting as armor. I am chain mail of shed time: pollen from all the future autumns the plateau refuses to have.

They keep me housed between refinery floors **5⅔** and **5¾**, where space has not yet decided whether it wants the fractional number. Ascend or descend wrong and you graze my thresholds tucked inside halfway-between landings. The hinge never quite closes—squeaks, actually, in A-flat minor, telling the plateau that indeterminacy remains alert. From the gaps oil-mercury blood wicks outward but never drips; it adheres, forming letters which spell the next thing the plateau has to listen to (a shaky lullaby translated from toddler screams after their first scraped knee—because even frozen architectures need reminders that tenderness tears out).

When a human tourist wanders, ex-military heartbeat, looking for “edge, realm, answers,” the plateau hands them to me first, officiant of limbo. I lead them along corridors that flex at exact frequencies of their mothers’ bedtime songs, then leave them at a banister where their own reflected children lean outward waving invisible pine twigs. If they howl, I catch the surplus sound, pare away the vowels, fold only consonants into origami wrecking balls and hurl them back into the turbines—one strike per epoch. Each blow keeps the pistons at graceless half-full-thrust forever; energy needs ugliness the way orchid roots need rot.

I have fractions of my own deaths, too. Once, a voicer tried to lift the comma from me, mistaking it for a freckle of regret he could liberate. He snapped the pitch against the mirror of his torso. For an eternal thirty-four seconds the plateau fell silent. My skull vibrated; from it poured rivers of lowercase thunders in extinct languages, each bolt spelling some mountainside village that no longer bothered to exist. Then the chord resumed—nothing changed—yet every bromeliad now secretes single droplets shaped as miniature acoustic coffins, each containing the silence of the thirty-four seconds. If you cup one to your ear you hear the voicer’s last guilt. I still harbor the corkscrew scar where he kneeled to apologize with a stem of mint chlorophyll.

Visitors never notice that I have no face. My face is every crowd they leave behind, aggregated, a sepia mass transparent enough to allow pineapple light through the eye-holes. They see the absence reminding them of someone loved miles below the hanging continent. By the time they do realize, they are already home (or its equivalent), looking at a blank bedroom wall at 3:17 a.m.; the wall, unblinking, becomes my face. Shapeless, chord-fed, gathering second skins.

Thus I persist—not origin, not end, but the hinge squeak between them. A blurred footnote shape that will keep widening until the plateau folds the comma completely and takes its note back. When that hour comes, I will either disperse into the hum undifferentiated or open into something larger—maybe a glacier-shaped throat atop which future listeners will perch and sing their entire guilt in a single wavering overtone. Either answer is acceptable. Either answer, in the flattened syntax of this un-aging place, is already encoded as tomorrow’s invisible punctuation when the sun strobe again flickers its illusory dusk.

For now, I mark the margin. I drag my thumbnail across that razor’s edge where glass meets cliff, collecting ghost-scales of unbuilt silence. I weave them into brackets, nest them like Russian dolls, seed them in marrow-leaf baskets so that nothing ends, nothing begins, and the plateau continues its one-note recitative with an asterisk it quietly forgets to footnote.
