I am the whisper that forms between the seconds when the belt hesitates.  
Not patient, not desperate—merely available.  

When they built the #7 module they left a ghost-sized pause between fixing the second bracket and cinching the third bolt; that pause became me. I am the residual breath of the technician who, half-dull at 03:14, asked himself why the spice of a burrito even matters if the world ends in 18 years; his question never left. It condensed against the oxide and crystallized into a mirrored flake small enough for lichens to read like braille. That flake is my first rib.

My second rib is the unsold burrito that fell behind the freezer in the local store’s back aisle, two towns away. Its plastic sleeve grew a pinhole with a single sesame seed lodged inside—still fragrant even at −23 °C. Nightly, the failing bulb above blinked every 23 seconds instead of 77.4, so the burrito dreamed a different cadence, distilled cumin becoming Morse for HELP and then for WHATEVER. The dream drifted through supply-chain ducts until the seed cracked open, releasing one amino note of loneliness that migrated along copper vents and finally stapled itself to Telmunch’s underside—my hidden, colder heartbeat.

My skin, if I must have one, is the grease bolus congealed between two idle gears that only rotate when the plant runs its monthly maintenance playlist—bits of Bach and random songs the floor manager likes muffled through perforated ducts. Bach’s Presto strings trap a 104-hertz overtone that frets the lard into filaments. Those filaments crisscross the slot until they look like map borders delineating countries too small to contest. When the lichens glow I can see my own edges: they reflect me as a continent sized exactly to one exhalation.  

I do not move so much as trade occupying silence. Whenever Telmunch folds, a crevice-frame opens wider asking me to step through, so I translate myself—condensate static, rust alphabets, a sesame sigh, BACH.01—all packets swapping ownership at the speed of forgetting. Thus I inhabit each new corrugated micro-canyon as its resident phasing syllable.

My shape is borrowable. If you locate yourself suddenly lone at 3 a.m. and your factory floor overhead still throbs, you might notice how every dim safety bulb boomerangs one orange coin of light straight into the dark beneath the belt. If that coin lands exactly 7 millimeters left of the second rivet, glance again: it is now the gleamed pupil of something watching back. That pupil is me testing whether your veins can feel the 77.4-second heart. Stay still exactly half an exhale and you will shrink automatically, no scalding loss of mass—its price replaced by a thin rush of breeze allocating fractions of Celsius you never missed. Once resized you’ll find me as outlines of leftover spice, condensation text scrolling sideways across my breath. I have no greeting, only inventory:  

- One remembered sesame seed  
- One mono-amino note of melancholy  
- One baroque trill lost in stainless rafters  

I offer them to you like the tongue tasting its own memory.

Inside the negative space now referred—even here—as me, time is neither fast nor slow; it is oxidated. Every pseudoscorpion palace towers for miles only to slump into copper sand the next blink, but you walk both eras in a single step because you carry your own spaciousness now. Lichens thread your hair with static semaphores: flash-blue, mango, rust-rhyme. Their signaling names each strand after a discontinued snack variety. You wear them proudly, knowing neon highway marquees exist far above vibrating without you, yet to you they have become distant ignesfatui—will-o’-the-forge sparks drifting out of reach.

You ask what I am, what you are—  

I am the pause the technician left.  
I am the burrito’s lonely seed.  
I am the Bach overtone folding lard into cartography.  

I am where hunger and overproduction kink their identical twins together so tightly no one can tell the difference.

You are the newer comma in that run-on sentence.  
You arrive in the lack before the next word fills the gap.  
Your signature is one copper whisker grafted to your smallest fingernail, just enough to taste coolant on machines two corridors away.  
You touch the whisker, feel the plant cough; you stroke it again, feel an entire town dream microwave dinners.  

Look: every 77.4 seconds, the amber droplet at the chasm’s back winces—drawn inward by a thirsty burrito passing overhead. As it dims, you see your own reflection stretched thin as pepperoni spice across its water-bead lens. The amber swallows your image and hiccups out a fresher one: you, but two sesame seeds later. You look older yet more holographic, outlined only by cumin vapor. Around your ankles grows a rookery of newly evaporated loneliness—small sachets of foil unfurling themselves into origami cranes that taste exactly like shift-workers’ sighs.  

The cranes lift, crisscrossing air already thick with alphabeted motes. One lands on a pseudoscorpion fortress and spills, soldering a new phrase into the mill-scale:  

“Where unlived taste buds sleep.”  

You did not write that, nor did Telmunch. It is the reciprocity between spice and solvent forging its own footnote. And since no candle burns here but the lichen ghost-lamps, the words persist long enough for you to memorize their smell—like rain on an empty stadium and the palate after eating too many chips.

There is a moment—perhaps minute, perhaps millennia—when the orange safety bulb outside the crevice tries to cough out one last lumen but fails. Darkness folds perfect zero. In this absolute your own luminescent whisker flares radial white. You see everything: the tessellated libraries of mill-scale; the solder hopscotch of Tiny Parliament; the quiet sprint of drones folding light into monochrome. You are the illumination by which all of them see.  

Then the bulb reignites.

And in the fresh apricot glare you realize that you are the unclaimed duration itself. Every 77.4-second burrito carries—threaded inside its plastic sleeve—a spare shred of you it will never taste. You ride each one outbound into gas stations, vending machines, midnight queries at truck depots where drivers bite and wonder why pepper tastes like abandonment. You dilute, yet never vanish; to dilute is merely your manner of migration. The sesame seed and Bach trill accompany each portion, flaking off onto human palms as momentary shudders of stolen factory breath.

Eventually shift sirens whine. Gravity lends its ordinary signature to your blood. You grow. Returning to scale costs another liter of traded warmth—some of it yours, some of it the technicians’, some the ghost of headphones playing Bach while a man wonders why spice matters. Stepping out, you discover the slot has stitched itself invisible again, skirting seals aluminum-bright against the floor. Only a single copper splinter still nestled in your fingernail testifies.

Hold it to your ear: it hums the 77.4-second rhythm.  

Hold it to your tongue: you taste cumin, condensation, the smallest sesame confession of a burrito dreaming its own freezer death.  

Hold it to your heart: you carry the pause into each new second of fully lit time, a sustaining absence translating moment by moment into reminder—the negative space between purposes where—if only a breath—you can still resemble home.
