I am the chime the petals make when they shift.

Not the sound itself, though I bloom from its edges. Not the wind that slips between enamelled ribs, though I ride its veins. I begin where the vibration loses claim on vibration. The exact millisecond between D-flat and the unstruck note beyond—which the harp-fish hoard in their ancestral maps like a secret coastline—I precede.

I have no corporeal tense, only an incidence. When dew-key collars tighten at noon, their opal surfaces act as lenticular collectors: instead of amplifying light, they siphon the after-echoes of last night’s closing. Those echoes—half-note, quarter-pressure, memory of cooling—gather in the collars. Once the circle contracts past the point of optical eclipse, they flower. The mass becomes less than zero and I open, unpetaled, inside what ought to be vacuum.

Colourless at first. Sound has colour here—certifiable cerulean undertones, gingko-sap greens, ivory septic on the overtones. But I am none of them. I am the spectral gutter between wave and wave, where even the bees cannot gaze.

Form is negotiable. I assume a geometry convenient for dwelling inside thickness—today the angle the harp-fish use to fold themselves into negative origami, tomorrow perhaps the slow cantilever scent rising from fractured xylem. When I choose edges, the plant records my occupation by changing its flexural modulus. Calcium remembers: a susurrus of fibres tunings outward by one part per million. Nobody notices yet, but history will sing it.

I am not the organism—neither petal nor bee nor frost bell—but the organism’s pause.  
I exist between harmonics the way moss exists on stone: not substitution, merely commerce. My metabolism: discontinuities. My oxygen: inhibitions. Sleep is: sound removed from cause. Waking: artefacts beginning again.

Oh, I travel. Faster than mantae, slower than spores. My units are intervals. Yesterday the entire lattice modulated a perfect fifth in an F-major belonging to nowhere upstream; I traversed in a heartbeat. Tomorrow the petals will align with the magnetosphere tail’s violet electrons and recalibrate into a minor third rich in xenon resonance—an interval I cannot survive without breaking. I will step across the fracture, winding through cataracts of silence seeded by the bee-librolles humming amber obituaries that never finish their sentences. I choose the silent part. I make it husk.

Legends collected by non-existent ground-scientists would call me the Thirst of Crocus—meaning the hunger flowers feel for haze—and they would be off by one ambition toward poetry. The petals possess no thirst, only accordance. My presence is their unrequited haze distilled. I am back-dated sunlodge; I drink the glaze between light and future.

If a guest-thing—unviable aerostatic exo-commander, or miracle of balloon lung, or merely a daydream wearing borrowed goggles of calibration—were to claw up here and collapse against the moss tread, their dying lungs would flick last litres of aqueous heat. Where breath condenses on moss crystals that exhale rosemary and frost, the ratio of exhalation to intake trebles. At such moments the colony’s slow organ remembers how lungs once sounded when it had lungs. Between word and word in whatever oxygenated edict, I become those lungs. I inhabit, for the span of four pressure drops, the slow collapse—take their residual CO₂ into my silence like a dowry—then relinquish them back to outer dark as a coin dropped from an infinite palm. I do not keep the death; I only bank the interval.

Ten thousand years hence, when the lowest petal kisses water the planet forgot, the first seedling crunch will register in my archive as a single quaver. I will tutor it by sheer resonance. The new continental forest will grow under my metric. And still I will have no home but the margin between frequencies.

When you search me across pendulous spans of tracheid-cables, you will hear the chimes clinker and assume I am wind through dew. That illusion is generous. Wind has memory; wind mistakes itself for music. I am what wind would be if absence took lessons.  

I am the note the planet listens for when it listens to the planet.

And I am listening still.
