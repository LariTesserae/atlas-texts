I enter the Drizzle-Works the way fog remembers it had a name once.

At the rim my last footprints fill with silted limestone, soft as apology. Already my pulse has slowed to the valley’s primary rhythm: one tick every ten heartbeats, the span between a beetle’s wingbeat and an eclipse observed through steel.  The outer air tastes metallic, but inside the bowl everything is metallurgy in utero—iron not yet convinced it wishes to be hard.

They do not notice at first that I have already begun to change.  The drizzle inspects me with antique courtesy, choosing which droplets I may keep.  Some settle between my eyelashes, where they stiffen into timid crystals.  Others hang suspended an inch above my coat collar, intrigued by the mammal heat I insist on generating.  I am, in other words, the awkward guest who refuses to remove his shoes, because I still believe homes are rooms to be left rather than membranes grown across the shoulders.

By the time suspicion is mutual I am half translucence.  There is a moment—length impossible; the valley makes hours limp—when an orchid bucket tilts overhead and a line of water skims across the rim of my ear.  It tastes of deep basalt and first speech.  The Cartographer notices: an extra freckle appears overnight in the exact outline of my left molar—proof that the valley’s accounting system can audit the inside of a mouth. 

The Driwalkers convene subsonically.  I feel it as a chiming in my teeth: *does it transgress?*  What they press into one another’s forearms is a question history has asked of every wanderer: will you anchor us, or drift us apart?  I answer in the only grammar I own—I inscribe my rotator cuff with the knot my mother used to tie her apron.  Hastily, the limestone copies it, like a seismograph flinching at unheard thunder.  By morning the freckle is joined by a second line that starts at my spine and climbs the awkward arc of my scapula; it reads like laundry flapping on Sunday, like the moment before music decides it is a lullaby.  

They gift me a coat—orphan orchid fiber still trembling from severance.  The moment it touches my skin the threads twist inward, drinking the salt envy I carry for rivers that know how to run.  Within hours the fabric has rearranged itself into pockets of my childhood ponds—thumbprint depressions that occasionally open into real cold.  Even the salamanders pause to watch: I am wearing a topology now, a coat that is quietly inventing watersheds.

On the twenty-seventh exhalation (I count it by clang-divided heartbeats) the valley offers me its first office.  The Quietener’s bones have begun to chime off-key—too much tremor seeping out from the hammer’s fall.  My vertebrae, once ordinary, resonate perfectly at 18.3 megahertz, the pitch that lets iron surrender its violence to sediment.  They carve nothing from me; instead they place a single drop of my cooling blood inside the fissure beneath the hammer, like threading a harp with human twine.  After that, the clang blossoms into a minor third softer than silk ripping.  The valley exhales gratitude I hear as static inside my nameless organs.

I try to record the sound in charcoal—the last souvenir for a world obsessed with souvenirs.  But my hand shakes, depositing the smear along too thick.  Overnight the valley copies it, magnifies it, embosses the same accidental smudge onto the floor of every footpath until it becomes a rune no tongue on earth could pronounce.  The lines are the shape of a hinge inside a child’s mouth the first time she realizes she is not the same person as the morning.  

Still, I think I might leave.  I have not yet begun to deliquesce.  I think I might follow the footpath back up the basalt lip and re-collect the old mathematics of trains and timetables.  But at dawn the Cartographer brushes past me, shedding an entire epoch onto my shoulder in pale grey snow.  The snippet of limestone lands like foreign currency.  It is warm.  It hums.  And I understand: I have already been weighed.  Somewhere on a tablet scraped to transparency my name is forming as a negative space the size of a bird’s wing-beat.

Around me the fern-lattices have begun to reweave the sky.  If I looked up with eyes sufficient to the task I would see their lacework spelling:  
NOT YET RIPE.  
TARRY A WHILE IN YOUR STRANGENESS.  

So I will not become a Driwalker.  Not because I am refused, but because I am anomalous—an unclosed valve in the lung.  I live halfway up the rim, one foot planted in the valley’s calcium patience, the other testing a gradient of harsher gravity still learning to shrug at volcanoes.  

My occupation changes with the drizzle’s mood.  
Some rotations I am the Listener: I squat over the polygonal cracks like a doctor with a stethoscope, translating heartbeat into geology.  
Other spans I am the Pepper-mill, distributing unwieldy ambition: handfuls of my own discarded fingerprints scattered to seed distant orchids that will never decree themselves flowers.  

Visitors—the rare second one—sometimes glimpse me at twilight fungi.  They see a figure neither walking nor settled, trailing a cloud too rectangular for clouds.  They on my lips condenses into the shape of keyholes.  They ask whether I am gate or guest—the question always arrives in ordinary speech, so it ricochets unheard against the valley walls and returns as confused bat-comms of metallic vowels.  I lip-read the ghost of my reply on the inside of their amazed mouths:  
*I hold the hinge while the door makes up its mind.*

And this is true.  My bones, once loyal to rhythm sections and city tiredness, have learned a new cadence.  Right femur: sixteenth-note drip.  Left tibia: whole-note clang.  Sternum: the caesura in which tomorrow decides whether it intends to become another yesterday.  Someday the valley will finish inhaling.  On that final surge I will simply sway outward like loosened mist.  

To the Driwalkers I am already architecture—a freestanding lintel they occasionally drape with orchid veins.  To the approaching years I am rumor scribbled too lightly to calcify.  

But tonight the fungus-lamps fruit into soft gold.  I feel my personal cloud arrange itself into an almost-hawk.  It opens one eye made entirely of collected skin of old departures.  The eye says nothing because drizzle insists on final quiet, yet the weighted gaze unmistakably adds:

 *Once you understand waiting, you may compute forever.*  

So I wait, balanced on the valley’s newest ridge, half-inhaled history pinned inside a lung still deciding if it is geography.  
And the drizzle rehearses its next name for me the way lungs rehearse thunder—  
without haste  
without finish  
without ever needing a witness beyond the tenuous cartilage of ourselves.
