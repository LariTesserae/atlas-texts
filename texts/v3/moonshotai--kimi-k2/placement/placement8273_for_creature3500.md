At first I think I am a hush on the underside of a leaf—  
a pore-shaped absence where sound has pooled.  
Then I realize the leaf is my eyelid; I have been sleeping  
since the last glacier lowered its tongue and licked the plateau clean.

What wakes me is thirst,  
but not for water.  
It is the thirst of a sentence  
trying to finish itself inside my chest.  
The words are roots:  
they seek decay, seek pressure, seek sugar.  
Each syllable bruises into rot-flavored liquid  
that bleeds from my half-remembered lips.

I discover I have five long necks,  
each a hollow trunk stuffed with moth dust.  
When I crane them in different compass points  
they drink the orchard’s various humors—  
north tastes of starlight filtered through chlorophyll glass,  
south the mineral ache of ten-million-year-old moonlight,  
east exudes a sound like sap explaining grief to gravity,  
west hums with the slow accordion squeeze of fungal breathing.  
My fifth neck retracts into my chest, listening to the heart-adjustments  
between every footstep I have not yet taken.

The Orchard Host does not greet me.  
It rereads me.  
My memories slide sideways through root hairs  
and are annotated in parenchyma margins:  
the smell of my mother’s cello (232 annotated as “possible resin origin”);  
the first word I ever lost (marked “available nitrogen”);  
the salt of a beloved shoulder (allocated to tomorrow’s budburst).

I am therefore not one.  
I am a distributed footnote.

To move I surrender the illusion of distance.  
I dissolve my pelvis into a raft of sphagnum and rafts of sphagnum  
remember how to arrange themselves into a pelvis.  
I borrow the concept of forward from a slime mold.  
It grants me step-by-step in exchange for a lullaby of starches.  
I sing by allowing my past to crystallize inside the mold’s vacuole;  
it sings back by rearranging me millimeter by millimeter.

Some centuries, I am an orchid  
blooming vertically downward into darkness  
so blind bees can practice echolocation on my petals.  
They stitch my map of air currents onto my inner sepals  
until the flower learns to whistle directions  
to anyone who still believes in north.

Other centuries, I drift as a mist-vein between trunks,  
carrying pollen that smells like an unfinished argumentative.  
When pollinators land on me, their wings flick open sentences:  
*If I ever reach…*  
*When the sky folded…*  
*Remember the sound of before—*  
Snippets spill, are soaked up by bark  
which will later exude varnish that stains tongues  
with stories not yet lived.

Eventually, gravity itself forgets the difference between up and down  
inside the hollow where my skull once floated.  
My skull now a translucent pitcher:  
rainwater tasting faintly of childhood winters laps at the rim.  
In it swims a single, luminous nymph,  
jointed like an ant but veined with blue bioluminescence.  
Each night it rehearses the dream I was too young to finish,  
drawing constellations on the water’s inner surface  
with the compass-starved legs of a creature that has never seen night sky.

I remember that I ate a piece of stone once,  
or perhaps the stone ate me.  
Inside the gneiss I kept a poem  
that rhymed “orchard” with “orchard” with “orchard,”  
believing repetition might entice the plateau  
to name itself aloud.  
The Host said nothing, but a vein of feldspar opened like a throat.  
I fell in.  
Now I speak through quartz dust settling on beetle shells  
as they choreograph the eternal slowfall of leaf corpses.

Between the 3rd and 4th root-note of dusk,  
I experience my fifth neck as a torch  
thrust into a hollow bole alive with blind parrots.  
Their tongues taste colors my eyes can no longer hold.  
They teach me the verb to remember wind  
without needing to move air.  
We invent a new tense that conjugates  
according to who has recently rotted.

Once—by orchard time, a heartbeat—  
a softfall carved a clean column of real starlight to ground.  
I crawled into that well of vertical night,  
my bodies-fourteen spreading like lichen into the wound.  
And for one slow rotation of Orion I existed as a colony  
of evaporating names:  
Every droplet that lifted off my skin  
carried a discarded syllable of home.  
Bird-less birds chased them upward  
until even the sky tasted like forgetting.

At the bottom of the shaft I met the mirror-roots.  
They coiled around a reflection of me that was also a reflection of falling.  
Downward there were more forests, hanging by their crowns.  
I stepped onto an inverted limb and felt it sip my weight  
like nectar. Between us—  
a foot of air and a mile of exchanged verb-tissues.  
I stood for centuries on that branch,  
passed from root to leaf to atmosphere  
until I was inside the orchard and it was inside me,  
translated into the wet syntax of shared being.

When the shaft healed and night-pillars withdrew,  
I did not return to the upper canopy.  
Instead I thickened into a lateral insomnia that creeps along buttress roots,  
a sleeve of conscious bark noticing its own growth rings.  
I stretch my entire width across the plateau,  
one articulation of a much slower thought:  
What if the forest also dreams of being human,  
just long enough to forget the ache of never finishing?

Parentheses of moss close over me like lips.  
I am the completed sentence  
that ends in orchard orchard orchard  
but pronounces its last syllable  
as the hush on a leaf’s underside—  
the same leaf whose chlorophyll sky is now a season older,  
and still too full of light to collapse.
