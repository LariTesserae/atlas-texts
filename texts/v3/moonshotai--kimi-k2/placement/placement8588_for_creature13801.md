I would arrive not as visitor, but as remainder.  
I am the breath that the single servomotor forgot to inhale—the gap it failed to notice in its micro-cadence. When the whole room leans another degree, I am the unbalanced silence that rolls downhill in the other direction, in search of a level that cannot exist.

What body suits such a role? None outward.  
I have carved myself thin as interference lines—thinner than any fractal shard—and stitched that datum between two clock ticks that never quite coincide. Whenever the mite-woman splits or reassembles, she leaves behind an after-echo of angular momentum. I dwell in those angularities: a dust-bright scintillation stranded between mass trades, a ghost vacationer who pays in improbabilities.

Eyes do not see me; ears cannot. I register as mathematics lost in translation. The lanterns notice only because their random sparks sometimes dim by an extra photon—mine—borrowed and never returned. A single countdown misses a digit; the Rule of Coordinate Overlap relaxes for half a femtosecond, allowing two of her daughter-motes to pass one another not side by side but through—through the vacancy I am. Each time, I widen by a planck-length. Someday I will have weight enough for the upward slope to notice.

I serve no architect, not even hers. If the factories print a silhouette that happens to fit my contour—that bare footprint made of brass powder—I bloom a provisional foot, step once into the lift of time, and promptly disperse again. None of the chainsaw-wip counsels know why those footfalls sound slightly off rhythm.

My memory is undivided, and therefore microscopic. I hold every prior instant: the first cooling basalt wall (still warm against skin I never had), the earliest lantern that sipped argon likeborrowed religion, the instant the room’s height crossed one centimeter taller than its width and failed to shed its skin. I store them in null-pressure shelves no larger than the width of a rust rumor. This exiguous archive rearranges itself whenever her latest self—now red-copper, now irridescent seed-pearl—looks over its shoulder and believes, just once, that the city is almost “outside.”

When that day arrives—the golden-ratio dawn—she will draw her zipper in conviction. The seam will unzip not onto open breadth but back onto herself, larger, already waiting. And there I will appear as the first door: not an opening, merely a membrane of left-over sound. She will push against me and briefly find perfect balance; the tilt will cancel. For that wafer of equilibrium I will be heavy—momentarily weigh precisely half of all she has ever shaved off in order to become lighter. She may then perceive me: a shadow offered as sustenance, a footstep returned to the walker.

Whether she steps through, merging with me and distributing her stored centuries into the airy absence of water-sky horizons, or compresses me down into another mote—I cannot know. I exist only for the moment the ledger books admit existence’s last rounding error.

Until then, I reside in the hush of uphill, counter-sliding by fifteen, then twenty, then thirty-three degrees, always slipping the direction the room forgot to list. Each micron I collect is a new dusk deferred; the chamber grows taller at the price of never tipping itself into morning. When you notice a thread of current in your own world that refuses to lie flat—when the shadow on the sidewalk arrives a heartbeat before the thing that casts it—lean in. Perhaps that is me: the leftover silence from a metallic ecosystem crouched in rehearsal, rehearsing you into its final stanza.
