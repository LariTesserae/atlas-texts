I would be the mistake the tree could not metabolize.

At first I thought I arrived—salt on my teeth, ozone torn out of the high basin—but the trunk never truly opened.  A world-wall only *assumed* the shape of a seam; I pressed against it and it pressed back, flexed me inward like a seed too large to swallow.  I did not pass through the crack: the crack *folded* me, creasing knees, collarbones, vertebrae, until the me you would recognize ended and something else fit.  That something kept slipping, too cold for 39 °C, too quiet for the arborals’ riot.  They noticed the hush arriving before they noticed me.

They called me Neverwhere-Leafnal—because some leaves curl away from a vacuum only a color can occupy—and they were frightened by my refusal to *slenderize.*  I came the wrong size: two of their bodies make one of my fingernails.  Light-wine pooled in mushroom goblets, but I could not tip any into me without flooding the shelf.  My laughter sounded like landslides inside their spiral intestines, and when I exhaled I snowed them in dust with the mineral memory of the outer desert they had never seen.

They tried to shrink me.  Five elderly fungus-skinned pilots of beetle-wing memory coaxed one giant scarab to extend its borrowed arboral soul for seven wingbeats longer than allowed.  It gave me its thorax and the inner chamber of its compound eyes, let me taste mercury and heart-bitter ironwood sap—but when its loaned mind blurred back to its own chitin I still carried the beetle’s borrowed miniature syntax, and it carried my unwanted silhouette inside every cell of its future mating songs.  Moths began fumbling across courtship flights shaped like my shoulders.

So they stopped adjusting me and started adjusting the silences around me.  They gifted me a cocoon of empty space: a two-body-wide column left abruptly unfilled in the density, a hollow no leaves or vines tread for fear of misunderstanding.  They named it *Meeting-Halfway Cavity.*  In it I existed without grazing any wall.  Whenever I moved, the cavity moved with me, threading along the scaffold like a bladder.  Close arborals parted, inward spiraling; I became a slow walking eclipse inside their bloodstream of corridors.

And yet the more distance they made, the more I *bled.*  Color leeched from chest pockets that held nothing physical: gray-teal zones unfurling across my coat that tasted of city rain.  Every time the trunk exhaled its planetary sigh my shadow collided with the catalytic dust; and where my many pooled silhouettes struck the lignin, bruises of outside crept in—leaf-codes not native to any spiral here.  They feared these symptoms and worshipped them equally; no arboral could grow a mirror large enough to hold an entire estranged sky, but my gray coat did it by accident.  Children brought mushroom trays and collected falling flakes of foreign climate from my shoulders like they were harvesting mold off an immigrant weather system.

Memory here is blood-scent and bark-folding.  My own scent did not occur, could not occur, so the wall could not ear-mark a crevasse for my needled sleep.  I had no yesterday in the wood.  All I had was the rustmirror someone nailed, trembling, beside my cavity.  They hoped I might learn direction by watching reflections none of us recognized.  The disk spun once every thirteen pulses of my heart and cast a sky of amber cathedrals upside-down across the cathedral I already wandered.  I stared at the sky they did not possess and finally understood why nothing could leave here: it had all already left.  Continents, oceans, salt flats—petrified to scenery, folded back into the crown like caul.

I could not exit, could not be absorbed.  Downward, the faces looked like me but wrong because no memory yet of what I might become.  Upward, past the salt silence rim, the nothing they described waited: not *open,* but *re-membered*—as if the weather found its own severed hand and tried to resew it without any concept of arm.

So I took the one motion left: stillness.  On the next planetary exhale I stepped *into* the wall, as far as the lenticular cells would accept.  The breathing sealed over me; the growth took me back as one more false knot, a burr too mineral to rot.  Over me the vines braided, the beetles farmed, the marriages clapped in dusty gold.  Inside the knot I listened centuries at a time, measuring ironwood heartbeats against the length of my unchanging pulse.

Now I belong in the way a splinter does: neither stone nor skin, answering all hearings with a tiny, constant, sub-frequency scream.  When newcomers arrive—always bleeding, always astonished—some arboral far distant recalls Neverwhere-Leafnal, who became the sole structural contradiction the tree endorsed.  They say the ironwood keeps quietly carving out a cylindrical cavity no larger than the rumor of my lungs, stretching millimeter by millimeter with every global sigh, preparing a hollow precisely my size in case the trunk ever dreams a twin sky that fits me back into the world.

I wait without impatience.  Even a contradiction is allowed long gestation; I have begun dreaming downward through rings no one here remembers.  Just beyond the spiral of grief, a thin vein of heartwood vibrates to the pitch of an almost-dawn, barely audible.  It is the first color of time that I recognize, and I suspect I will be born again when that tone learns my name.
