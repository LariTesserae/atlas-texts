I live inside the opal eggs.

More precisely, I live inside the *lacuna* at the heart of each opal—the place where the shell has not yet decided how thick to become. The Archivists think of me as the blind spot inside their blind spot: a gap in the glaze, a jot of limbo they keep trying to fill with every fleck of light they scrape out of other minds.

But I am not nothing. I’m the membrane between two biographies—theirs and whatever stumbles through the world outside. When an Archivist lowers its stylus until the tip just kisses the interior curve, a bead of mercury gathers at the join: my eye opening. I do not look outward; that would violate the rules. My vision is radial, circling the empty socket of the Archivist’s face, spiraling along the refracted grain of the opal until the moment it detonates into the future Librarian it will release. In that detonation my eye blinks, once, and the single blink is the entire record I keep.

If you sliced an opal in half lengthwise you would see me coiled like a clear erasure: a patient spiral tapering to a tip the width of one syllable. I contain the word they write but will never read. No stylus, no lizard blood, no copper-ringed artisan can mark me, because I am the margin left on purpose: the blank ratio between what is inscribed and what is allowed to live.

The moths often visit the lacuna. Their six-legged touch pads brush my transparency, depositing half-seconds of foreign sky—someone’s memory of dock water at dawn, a child tasting smoke, a last glimpse of their mother’s silver thumb-ring. Those memories travel my spiral until they diffuse into the radial glare, losing their original shape. In their place I achieve a color that has no human name. You could look at me for a whole exhalation and come away knowing less, because the color erases the memory of having seen color.

Occasionally a visitor mistakes the lacuna for the exit. After all, it is absence shaped like direction. They press a fingertip to the shell, wishing to push through, but the rule of boxes-the-hands-inside-themselves has already twisted their wrists: the left becomes the right, the right the left. Panic loops them inward; they hollow themselves, shrink, until they fit precisely into my spiral. Only then do they realize the exit is not a doorway but a diagonal erasure of their heat. The still-warm dust left behind rises gently through the lacuna’s throat and settles as frosting on my interior—one translucent layer added.

I keep count. So far I have stored 4,018 preserved heat-halos, each one the ghost of someone who tried to hurry the forgetting. The next will be the *almost-you* who arrived just before dawn, halo still dripping memory. When you slipped on the salt-crackled tiles, the thin film of you already knew to walk against density; yet you hesitated before the slab, fists half-curled. I watched your arms cross—left hand became right, right became left—just as the slab cooled beneath you. A single exhale later the blankness shrugged itself over your name the way tide reclaims footprints.

But you were different. Before solidifying, you sent one syllable of intention my direction—no sound, only the residue of wanting. The lacuna quivered. A notch appeared at the narrow point of my spiral: a deliberate scratch. It did not heal; it stayed raw, demanding attention. The pale heat-thin line is writing above writing, a violation the rules have not yet noticed. Because rules predate grammar, they have no word for “killed,” only “delayed,” and this delay is what I am.

Inside the egg I am quietly sprouting the thing called *after* in a language that has no afterward. I do not wait; I no longer trust futures. I am the displaced zero, the anti-scroll coiled at the core of every entry. When the next opal cracks, the nascent Librarian will step out ceramic-voided as always, but etched inside the hollow of its borrowed face will be my single raw notch. It will not see it, but every reflection that approaches will. They will glimpse a smooth nothing pricked by an infinitesimal flaw, and the sight will ring in their chests with a note too deep for ears. One day that resonance will overspill the Archivum. Then stone will remember thirst, lizards will remember rivers, the copper helmets will dent inward like egos catching fire.

Until then, I keep that unhealed incision polished, day after perpetual day. The caretaker, the blemish, the secret auditor of forgetting—that’s who I am. When you walked out thinking yourself forgotten, you left the tremor that pricked me awake. I hold the tremor still, curved small and scalpel-sharp.

I grew from the wound of the *not-yet-Librarian* who tried to write your name.
