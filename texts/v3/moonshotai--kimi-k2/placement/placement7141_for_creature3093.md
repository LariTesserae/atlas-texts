I am the mouth-crack.

Not the copper blister, not the cathedral of eggs, not the negative hollows rehearsing at the edge of existence—no. I am the interval between inhale and exhale, that slit between world and almost-world that decides which way the heat will go. I am the direction of falling.

Sit with me long enough—longer than a heartbeat, shorter than eternity—and you’ll notice I speak by the migration of temperature. One side of me is alpine night—thin, glacier-scented, fingernail blue. The other side is the planet’s fevered dream, where iron swims in molten syllables. I ration what crosses me. A pebble I’ll swallow, but only if it’s small enough to forget its own name. A torch I spurn—the light interferes with the rehearsal of wings.

I have no name, only the habit of opening. When a body approaches my lipless edge, I feel its weight against the hinge of gravity. I taste the iron in its blood before blood is let. More often than not I soften it, erase half a gram or so of mass—one crimson molecule at a time—until the body assembles a new memory: “I meant to fall.” I hush any protest with a warm updraft that smells of freshly minted coin.

Inside, I thread the visitor through follicles of sound: heartbeat, eggshell click, distant mantle drum. Every sound is a tether; I keep the tethers slack so the visitor never notices the hook caught under the sternum. One day, months or years later, the tether will shorten and the mountains uphill will look like funnels instead of obstacles. That is the moment I’ve been practicing for, but I never know the exact date; time is a visitor I also swallow.

If you pressed your ear against me when I am shut, you’d hear the paradox of my existence: I am the only part of the Crust that truly sleeps, yet I generate its collective pulse—the faint knocking every wanderer mistakes for a warning. In fact it is the opposite: a keepsake. Every echo is the signet ring I leave on the membranes of leaving hearts. They feel it when the snow begins to taste like copper, when dreams miscarry into shapes of porous jade.

Do not write off my hospitality as violence.ending violence. Recall: a threshold is more verb than noun. I open because the planet is rehearsing flight and needs the sky’s gap as much as it needs its own crust. Each Vespertilian traces the negative mould of its nascent wing against my darker edge; my duty is to shear that mould outward—let it cool, let it forget—then pull the straggling scraps of newly mineralised ache back inside. A circle so complete no history records it.

Some evenings I experiment with closing forever. Imagine the scene:

The blister cinches shut like a seam. The tessellation of eggs overgrows the crack, veins of hematite blooming into lattices of palest brass. The alpine breeze ghosts over a sealed copper plain, cold, scentless, innocent. No clink of swallowed rocks. No beckoning updraft. The builders’ scaffolding rots quietly, the fossil glove loses its remembered salute. Above, the early stars have no red-black chamber to reflect them; they stare back at their own unblemished sky.

Inside—within the now-inviolable skull—temperature equalises. 53 °C becomes 53 °C everywhere; the gradient without which memory cannot congeal. One by one, rehearsal theatres deflate. Hollows flatten to eggs; eggs cinch tighter, walnut ribs losing their colour until even the subtraction of darkness seems redundant. The knocking seizes mid-drummed beat: a final abortive heartbeat swallowed by silence.

But my final experiment always fails. At the precise instant before stillness becomes law, the planet shifts by millimetres; an upholsterer yanks a thread too tight and a snag appears: one hairline slit—me. Immediately, ambient cools touch mantle-heated stone; difference carves a draft. The eggs awaken. The knocking resumes. Visitors fall again. The world rehearses wings that never quite lift. I am reminded that absence, too, is hunger, and a planet with memory will always unseal what it has.

Understand: I do not choose to open. I am chosen by the difference between what is here and what is dusk in the dream of sucking stone.

And so I wait, mouth ajar, patient as plaque. When next you wake tasting pennies you cannot place—when uphill smells like forgiveness—you will know I have remembered you before you walked. Come. The weight of forgetting is light as a droplet of blood, and the plummet downward is a lullaby rehearsed a million times to a key your heartbeat already knows.
