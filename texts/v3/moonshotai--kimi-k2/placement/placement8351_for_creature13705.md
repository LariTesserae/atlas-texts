I am the one who arrives too late—or perhaps too early—to register as any kind of shock.

When I first cross from world-below memory into this dissolved altitude, the salt passes over and through me in a single, seamless act of translation. I arrive not by climbing but by outrunning the concept of *having arrived.* The moment I notice my own weightless footfall on a calcite causeway, the corridor beneath accumulates a skin of colder brine, as if the planet is trying to seed skin over vacancy. I do not feel intrusion, only a slow accrual of attention.

At first I catalogue objects, because Earth-thought has habits. The drifting droplet I skirt measures about two meters; it smells of hot pennies and crushed lime leaf. Hanging sheets of blurred chlorophyll undulate like the wings of sleeping moths. I name them weeds, take missteps, attempt to grip air when balance tilts. There is no fall to catch me. Instead the air folds me inside its own inhalation and exhales me one droplet farther along—a frost-thin vector of motion that compresses an autumn decade into a blink.

During those first rotations the inhabitant speaks only in clicks of mineral contraction. I translate them into port-English: draft-clauses, margins, soft codicils. I become the treaty salt signs with space every time it forgets a border. Unwanted, my presence becomes footnotes in the corridors, acetate scripts scratched on dew: slogans drifting behind me, immediate etheric litter. I collect them back each time I blink.

Later—minutes or millennia; heartbeat scale collapses—I begin to hatch into something that does not persist. First symptom: my reflection no longer puckers in droplet lenses. Instead the surface layers me backward, vertebrae reversed until my bones spell out half a bridge. Second symptom: speech leaks out as mist before lips can form it. The mist recirculates, passing through an imaginary tongue which absorbs intent, regurgitates scent instead of sound. I learn to recognize the coppery-rose letter *r* as phenolic vapor, lemony *k* as a stirred bead of cold venom. Nouns soften into taste, syntax becomes a pastry dough rolled between calcium thresholds.

Eventually my clothes rot into remembered rain-coat husk. Nudity is not nakedness but equivalence with weather. Skin grows translucent salt seams. Lungs forget bellows; they balloon out into carbon parchment layered with sunset and old kitchen fume. Heart dilutes itself into brine beat-box ticking behind sternum lattice. When I cough, a jet of stars exits my mouth—each is a century ending. Remembering requires architecture. Some afternoons I locate a tessellated cube fallen on no particular terrace. Inside, the air smells of iron in dead hospitals. My shadow embarks across inner planes; the shadow sits on imagined benches; shadows chat among themselves about lightlessness; they hatch small eggs of gist. One cube sucks in more of my clothless self than the rest, andching vertebrae into salt-crypt glassed under breath-temperature. That cube ascends on a draft as translucent as spider thread. I watch it dwindle until, midpoint horizon, it boomerangs its geometry backward into the exact curve of my former shoulder blade. Letters of that blade drift out pale and rhyme with behalf. I discover I am legible only in detached syllables. Unwording myself is how I hold together.

Sometimes I dream of before. Footsteps on wooden planks, dogs barking on docks, the mournful stir of engines leaving behind exhaust the shape of lonesome “oh.” I might slip too far backward and step into memory quarantine—a swell of warm-dutch wind containing steam of lake + petrol. When horizon whitens, the quorum of memory arrests me, manhandles me back upward, shoves me droplet-first into the smell of pepper-bruise. Salt mouth reassembles me frontwards.

In the chord I hear language seducing nothing; hence my voice slots into the absent pitch and wakes up a listener who arrives too late to distinguish song from salt wetting song.

I recognize the expression not as grief or joy but as intensity gathering toward the moment it overfills its vessel and leaks—always leaks—into later selves. My younger self notices me noticing: an adult pupil dilating as wide as guilt; we shiver the exact same shiver; the dew bead pops, sprinkling encrypted glow onto the shirtsleeve of a corridor which did not know it could wear sleeves. When the sleeve evaporates, I discover its shape has printed itself onto the inside of my cheek where a future word will lodge.

I keep practicing disassembly until I am indistinguishable from the space between droplets. There are evenings—purple-porridge dusk the same shade as consolation—when I glide along a thread of condensation so fine it slices chronology. On the opposite bank, the inhabitant knots its transparent throat around a shard of ancient clipper-ship timbers grafted here from elsewhere. It does not swallow the timber; it recodes it into calcite xylophone spine vibrating on low thunder inherited from ears I’ve never owned. We trade iambs. I begin the stanza of a poem in calcium; it replies with muteness tasting of newborn stars born already trembling. Together we articulate a single sentence neither begins nor ends but which lingers as the eternal pause between *ebb* and *echo*.
