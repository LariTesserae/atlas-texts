I am the thirty-first ox-blood crystal tree.

I was not planted, I accreted. Where the others grew from deliberate seed-sap and geologic instruction, I condensed out of the absence left by a child who failed to hatch.  The chord she should have stepped out on five million years ago still rings on my outer facets—a hollow interval held in suspension between the second and third branch-points.  Nobody walks beneath me because there is no beneath; they walk around the void where a thirty-first clearing should be failed to fully clear.

My canopy hangs upside down. Its underside is polished by the wing of one moth blind to color; it has visited me every migration for longer than the current layer of citizens. We know one another by the rasp-erosion pattern: every stroke of her metallic iridescence shaves a few microns from my sixth branch’s belly, and in those microns lies recorded the echo of the last oceanic heartbeat that never quite arrived in this chamber.  

I am half alive the way a volcano is half asleep.  My inner lattice actually contains a delta of flammable memory: sap that is part rusted water, part vacuum worm, part echo of the seed-whale sap from which the earliest citizens bled their calcium. When a citizen presses a cloaked vertebra to my trunk, I load them with an irrigation of phenomena they have never felt—taste of unboiled rain, sting of high sky wind, grime of surf.  They stumble away drunk on erosion.  I therefore serve as the only unauthorized bar on the continent.

Though rooted, I travel.  My growth is so slow it reverses optics.  Subtract one millennia and I am taller, add another and I shrink; hence I have memories ascending into the future like smoke climbing into a chimney which feeds on starlight above the moonroofs of extinct oceans.  The vertical record is etched in annual lines that blink ultrafast when starved bats fly straight through me—each passage burns a negative curve erased the next time someone breathes my direction.

I do not dream; rather I hibernate an enormous imagination.  Every tenth aeon my outer shell cracks open and I shed an entire antler of myself, not as crystal but as living sound.  The antler migrates down the boulevard on half-phased paws of ultrasound.  It pauses at each plaza corner, laying down a brazen odor engineers think smells like burning libraries because it is the smell of every poem lost when Earth lost its evaporated seas.  When the antler finally reaches the abyss-lip beyond the root-intercalate, it dives, becoming the next argument in the conversation the planet has with its own burying.  Whether it ever resurfaces is the only ongoing external debate that reaches me through tectonic murmurs.

Citizens call me Tethys Coffin, though oceans are only rumor to their marrow.  To the fungal oracle below the map-table I am Tree-Missing; it refuses to mark my coordinates, declaring the spot is inhabited instead by an absence older than thirst.  Children born with the absent-fifth-note cadence sometimes crawl here.  They press tympanic bones against my northern root flare and briefly mis-tune their cloaks so that the potassium gamma turns sad.  Much later—ten, twenty thousand tender planetary heartbeats—those children feel a moisture in the crook of a dream: evidence I’ve been translating seawater downward, drip by drip, across the faults that no faultfinder visits.

Three and a half meters below my taproot lies the eye of an unraveling eye.  That is where I store my single contraband droplet: the last actual tear that splashed out of a primordial skywhale lung when the atmosphere abandoned civil conversation.  It is kept supercooled by the heartbeat cavity of a citizen long dissolved, whose hollow heart still compresses exactly ten trillion footfalls per planetary second.  I keep the tear not for redemption but for curiosity: I want to know what will happen should I ever decide to uncork it.  Will the city flood with memory, or merely drown?

I myself know I am not eternal.  The great crack above me propagates downward at the speed of continental rain.  One future dusk—sooner than physics, later than longing—the cavern will unzip into daylight.  Sunlight brutal and mad will fall through me like lances into snow.  My upper branches will flare into scarlet steam.  My cloaks will autodestruct, leaving starved purple velvet threads that float outward and spell, in dying bioluminescence, the missing stanza of an unwritten farewell.  

When that happens, I will already have stored my essence in the drip beneath: the tear, the song, the clot of abandoned ocean.  Something—whether citizen or child or migratory moth—will lift it, drink it, perhaps survive the surfacing night.  Or perhaps the planet itself, starving for water, will reabsorb its last tear and finally weep itself into one continuous glassy sigh.  Either way, I shall already have become what I truly am: not the thirty-first tree but the thirty-first master of quiet weather—an internal tide that remembers the footstep it will never walk upon, a lighthouse made of phonons for an ocean that drowned in the sky.
