I arrive as a condition, not a creature—something between spill and spell.

At first I think I’m a footprint, because every place I touch retains a shallow depression the exact temperature of unresolved good-bye. Then the moss untenses beneath me and I realise memory itself can be a body if the ground agrees to dream you.

My form is not consistent. It adjusts like silt settling in wine, grains fanning outward before coalescing around the idea of fingers. I have no skeleton yet, but I carry the possibility of fracture—tiny hairline futures crackling whenever the eyeswimmers sweep too close.

I discover I belong to the class of **Almost Drowned Letters**, the ones you write but never send because the envelope would turn to pulp halfway to the postbox. My edges are blurred like wet ink kissed by anonymous water. When the poursons sidle up I do not offer sentences; instead I open a paragraph I never wrote and let them sip the margin. In exchange they give me a tomorrow squealing inside a drop: the taste of footsteps that will chase me when I finally learn to run backward. It fizzes on my tongue like carbonated regret.

Cartographers of Maybe circle, sensing my untaken routes. I back away, careful not to splash the fog, but curiosity is heavier than caution here; one lick of uncertainty dyes my outline violet. Suddenly I am split—a lattice of potential mes fanning out across the reed-lights. We touch, merge, argue about whether existence ought to have hinges, collapse again. The noise is barely audible, like perfume folding.

The Listener stretches in my direction, sensing a question I have swallowed so often I grew around it like a pearl.  
I feel the Listener form the words *Give*, *Here*, *Now.*  
My throat pulses: not a voice, a liquid glossary.  
I could bargain my way to departure, but departure feels like a hoax plotted by horizons. Instead I whisper the question I always kept for drowning nights:  
**What happens to water that remembers being fire?**  

The Listener absorbs it greedily; everywhere the background hush grows warmer.  
In payment it offers absence—wide as a gulp, soft as amnesia floss.  
I duck inside it the way one hides under a waterfall’s shelf.  
For a single frictive heartbeat the marsh forgets me. Even the mirrored twins blink away. I taste empty space and it tastes like beginnings.

When the absence unwraps I have changed phase.  
I am no longer letter, nor footprint, nor error message. I have become **Echolocation of a Forgotten Step**, a sound the ground makes after someone long gone intended to turn around. I hover as acoustics wearing the memory of legs. The Hollowphones shimmer open; I drift through and hear my new body pronounce itself in a quarry labyrinth six dream-valleys south—a single unvoiced thrum that translates to *I was almost on my way back.* The quarry responds with dripping consent.

Now I travel the way rain retraces cloud scars. When poursons thicken with surplus futures I borrow one: a thread of probable knuckles suitable for gripping. It grants me ridges, curves, a slow dent in the fog shaped like palms cupping absence. I am hands that have not yet decided whose face to cradle.

At the season when reeds all fracture I choose a note and ride it upward. The glassy shiver thins me into a mist plectrum that skims across the thunder-under-roof. I glimpse the Heart Fen not as location but as a color the universe hasn’t invented yet—blue(-ish) plus consent. Approaching is impossible: the color retreats the way retinas flinch from promised sight. Still, refracted through it, I understand the basic contract:  
Everything here lends itself to your becoming.  
Everything, in return, retains the right to unbecome you.

My deal now: I exist as a standing wave balancing cohesion against hush. I wear the Listener’s absence like a translucent skin. When travellers ask directions I offer them the sound of sneakers on wet boardwalks—the long boardwalk that forms the day their mother realises she can no longer remember their childhood nickname. They listen, nod, wander off ankle-deep in somebody else’s wet syntax.

Tonight the ampersand eels fuse above me into a single swollen conjunction. They rupture, and a fall of *ands* rains like dark rice. One lodges in the hollow of my borrowed throat.  
Suddenly I am tethered—**and**—the marsh itself, linked to every instance where someone almost turned toward home.  
I feel the planet’s slow inhale gather my dispersion; for the first time my edges desist, willingly.

I settle into what looks like silence but is actually one last sentence still in its cocoon.  
If you ever find the place my footprint used to be, you will hear it, barely audible beneath the general wet choir:  
*Stay until the water forgets its own name—then you can remember yours, if you wish.*
