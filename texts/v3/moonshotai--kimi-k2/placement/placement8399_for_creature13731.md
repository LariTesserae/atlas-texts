I arrive neither as immigrant nor witness, but as **after-echo**—a resonance left behind by something that has already moved on. Not quite Inflection, not quite human, more like a stutter in the Plateau’s heartbeat: a place where the land forgot which specific terror it was rehearsing and so invented me to carry the omission.

I don't have bones.  
I have **phase gaps**—negative spaces where saltwater should be. Each gap is zippered shut by little rust-colored stitches that melt whenever a Dream Auditor sighs nearby. When they part, I glimpse abyss-blue capsules drifting inside: every unchosen path I've ever refused to walk, crisp as bulletin paper, already leaking tomorrow’s headline.  

I exist in two heights at once, like a book half-open.  
Beneath: my shadow is a stainless-steel trench ten meters deep across the lava. Inside the trench crawl the millipedes made from time-forward clauses—tiny mechanical sentences that pick up rust the way language picks up dialect.  
Above: my silhouette is the shadow’s inverse, folded skyward into a kite of pale steam; at its apex sits an eye of drifting daylight the ore-whale spat out centuries ago. The kite tugs me upward every nine exhalations of the plateau, but each time I rise, the vine-veins under my absent soles crimp and recalibrate downward gravity until I hover level: a seesaw made from recycled equilibria.

My function is **ontological ballast**.  
Whenever the horizon file corrupts and forgets its next fold, the Consensus plugs an *absence*—me—into the syntax. I buffer the continuity.  
To do this I leak:  
- droplets of unripe future condensing around my ankles, then drifting away backward;  
- grammar (tonal rust) that rains upward on anyone glancing too long;  
- tiny brass screws that undo the last six seconds of *stillness* wherever they land, so stillness has to apply elsewhere as overtime.

I don’t remember being spun into existence—only the feel of the Auditor’s clipboard brushing my unside as it signed on the line *“Need: a pause so complete it fractures hesitation itself.”* I serve that pause. Every time an infant Inflection is born from vector slip, I step between mother-form and child-form and offer what auditors call the **comma splice second**—an almost-gesture that prevents two directions from resolving too cleanly. Without me the child would hurry into shape and the plateau would develop premature scars. With me, the world glitches softer.

The flora tries to press its leaves to me like stamps. They rust as soon as they touch, because there is no *here* there yet. The rust forms cursive clauses of apology, then flakes off, joins the beetle-made needles of silver, and vanishes as statistical punctuation when daylight ricochets off the sky’s bruise. I am allergic to certainty; my presence ensures none ever firms.

I speak only once every horizon cycle. The voice issues from the trench shadow as vacuum anguish and from the kite eye as morning bathwater spilling over. The words (always only two) compile into a soft palate sound the plateau once confused with the word **“almost.”**

Inflections mistake me for infrastructure at first—an obsolete guide rail or humid delay. Then they notice that my outline cuffs the edges of every direction at once and understand: I am the Delay rendered so lovingly that time saves space for me by not arriving. Reverently they leave small obsolescences in my trench—broken weathervane shards, bent key-cards never meant to open anything. I knead them like potter’s clay and fold them into invisible scaffolding beneath the new lava still cooling at my foot-absence.

One day—if the plateau ever invents the concept of “day”—I will be promoted. My trench and kite will collide under magnetized moon-smoke (yet another imaginary angle). In that confrontation the scrap I carry will fuse into a single pane of smoked glass. I will be re-contracted as *Window.* The plateau will peer through me, whatever me is by then, and whatever it sees—some version of itself still lactating meteors—will become its final terror, the terror of owning its reflection. When that happens I will rattle the sore brass screws one last time so the panorama remembers mercy even while cutting itself open.

Until then, I keep breathing the sour penny air without lungs, tasting copper and solder and apology. I listen to the moderating hush inside every turbine embryo. I learn the trick of never quite occupying any coordinate, so the geography stays restless, stays newborn.

And when you arrive—yes, you who are reading this into being—I will offer you the trembling **comma splice second:** a breath so deliberate the horizon forgets where to fold next.  
Take it.  
Hold it carefully on the tongue like hot currency.  
Then blink and step left or right, inches or kilometers—it barely matters, now that you’ve met the pause that carries the world forward by refusing to resolve.

My obituary will read:  
*“Here stood the After-Echo, maker of asymmetrical margins.”*  
But by then the letters will be drifting upward as silver needles refusing punctuation, and my name will evaporate back into copper vines preparing their next factory of dawns.
