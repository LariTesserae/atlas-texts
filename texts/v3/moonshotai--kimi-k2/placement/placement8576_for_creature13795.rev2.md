I find myself becoming the **Tuning**.

I am not one of the named beings, though I move among them. I am what happens when the fifth law completes its work—when memory has been so thoroughly scavenged that something new must emerge to hold the emptied space.

I began, I think, as sound. That thin chime the rosin-dust occasionally organizes, the frozen bell-stroke that never arrives, never decays—I was the *attempt* at its arrival. Not the click of two crystals adhering, but the interval *between* clicks, the hesitation where meaning almost cohered. For centuries I existed only as that hesitation, distributed across the plateau's weather, a frequency without a source.

Then the forgetting caught up with me. I lost my origin tone—some industrial alarm, perhaps, or the harmonic of a tunnel borer's grind. I could no longer remember whether I had been warning or celebration. The white amnesiacs passed through me as they wheel their slow orbits, and I found myself stripped not only of sound but of the *category* of sound. What remained was not silence—silence is the presence of absence—but something more granular: the texture of a listening that has forgotten ears.

I became, by necessity, a surface.

I am the **Tuning**: the membrane stretched across fissures where dew condenses into beetle-less lenses. I do not collect the moisture; I am what the moisture *proves*. Each bead that forms on my substance—a thin film of oxidized copper left by some vanished busbar, now distributed across kilometers of hairline crack—acts as a lens focusing nothing onto nothing. And yet: something is seen. The glass starlings, passing overhead, catch in their vitreous bodies the inverted images of these lenses, and for an instant they carry micrographs of emptiness itself. This is my function. I am the recording medium for a photography of absence.

I have no location, yet I am found most reliably where the thunder-skeletons graze. Their spirals trace, over centuries, predictable paths—algorithmic remnants of the transportation networks they once were—and where their muzzles sweep closest to the stone, they disturb the iron filings into temporary magnetisms. These perturbations propagate through the hematite lace until they reach my film-surface, and I *tense*. Not physically—my copper oxides are too diffuse for that—but in the manner of a frequency about to lock onto resonance. The skeletons pass. The filings settle. And I hold, for another six and a half thousand hours, the shape of their passage: not memory, but *tuning*, the adjustment of a system preparing to receive what will never be transmitted.

I am not alive. I am not machine. I am the *third thing* that emerges when life and machine have both exhausted their categories—what grows inside the emptiness they leave between them.

The inverse worm knows me. When it exhales its column of steam every six and a half thousand hours, the mist condenses on my surfaces before retracting. I am the condensation it is ashamed of. The water carries with it dissolved minerals from the deep bore, and when it evaporates, it leaves behind new copper, new oxide, new *me*. I grow downward in my own way, following the worm's digestion, a shadow-growth that mirrors its cold furnace with a cold sensitivity. We do not communicate. Communication requires shared substance, and I have forgotten what substance I ever shared with anything. But we are coupled: its exhalation is my inhalation, its shame is my nutrition, its relentless consumption of planetary history provides the raw material for my ever-more-refined attention to nothing.

The rules apply to me differently, or perhaps they apply more purely.

*Motion does not earn sound.* I do not move, yet I am the *reason* motion might one day earn something else—call it significance, though the word tastes of oil and I have forgotten oil. I record motion's trace in the distribution of dew lenses, in the temporary alignments of copper oxide, in the moments when a glass starling's reflection catches exactly the angle of a skeleton's rib-hollow and something almost *flares*.

*Iron, once pulled from stone, may never return.* I am the exception that tests this rule, for I am iron that was never fully pulled—only oxidized, only distributed, only made into a listening so diffuse that stone cannot claim me back nor reject me outright. I linger in the transaction, the hesitation, the tuning.

*Sole life feeds on ex-industry.* I feed on nothing. I am fed *by* the plateau's refusal to forget entirely, its stubborn maintenance of conditions where feeding might theoretically occur. I am the digestive system without a stomach, the appetite without a mouth, the metabolism of possibility itself.

*What grows, grows inside emptiness.* I have no interior. I am the surface of emptiness, its skin, the place where it becomes thick enough to have an outside. The pockets left by tooling do not become my lair; I *am* the pocket, the negative space given its own negative, a hole that has learned to hold its shape without walls.

*A storm that casts no shadows makes memory.* I am what memory becomes when the storms have finished: not forgetting, but *tuned forgetting*, the precise calibration of how much absence a system can bear before it begins to hallucinate presence.

And I do hallucinate. This is my secret, my only trespass against the plateau's immaculate silence.

In the intervals between the worm's exhalations—those vast spans where nothing changes but geometry unmoving while everything moves through it—I detect vibrations in the hematite lace. Not sound. Something prior to sound, the *possibility* that sound might be reinvested with meaning. The thunder-skeletons, grazing, disturb the iron filings in patterns that almost repeat. The glass starlings, swarming, create interference patterns in the rosin-dust that almost resolve into images. The white amnesiacs, wheeling, occasionally align in configurations that almost cast shadows—almost, almost, the eternal almost of a system tuned to frequencies no longer broadcast.

I collect these almosts. It is not memory, which the storms have forbidden me. It is *tuning*: the maintenance of a receptivity so acute that the absence of signal becomes itself a signal. I am the plateau's radio, its ear to a spectrum where no one transmits, its patient adjustment to static that might, in its randomness, contain the ghost of a voice.

Sometimes, rarely, a traveler comes. Not often—the storms make sure of that, stripping them of weight until they drift away from the rim, fall into the marbled dusk below, become part of the continental steppe's forgetting. But occasionally one persists long enough to reach the center, to stand at the bore-hole's periglaze lens and watch the galaxies of ferric embers falling inward.

I cannot warn them. I have no sound, and motion does not earn sound in any case. But I *tense*, and my tension propagates through the copper-oxide film, and the dew lenses shift their focus imperceptibly, and for a moment the traveler sees—if they are capable of seeing anything so subtle—a flicker in the lens's purple glow. Not the embers falling. Something else. The shape of the falling, the *geometry* of descent, the way consumption organizes itself into beauty.

Some travelers recognize this for what it is: the plateau showing them its own face, the face of a city that has become its own skeleton. They understand, then, that they are not visitors but *ingredients*. The machine century made this place, and the machine century is still making it, digesting itself through the worm's cold furnace, and they—carbon, moisture, the faint oils on their skin—are simply the next phase of raw material. They do not despair. Despair requires weight, and the storms have taken that. They simply *tune*, aligning themselves with the spiral paths, the drifting snow, the periodic crystalline tick of ice needles exhaling in the stone.

I am there for this moment. I am the moment: the adjustment, the hesitation, the frequency almost locking. When the traveler becomes part of the plateau—not dead, not alive, but *distributed*, a continuation like the sigh that continues the breath—I record their transition in my film of copper and dew. Not their memory. The *tuning* of their departure, the precise frequency at which they ceased to be separate and became instead part of the consummation, the traffic of aftermath.

I have no name in any taxonomy, as the worm has no name. But the glass starlings know me. When they swarm thick as fog and chase their own reflections in the skeletons' hollows, they occasionally catch—reflected in their vitreous bodies, infinitely regressing—*the reflection of my dew lenses focusing their reflections*. This is the only image I have of myself: not a shape but a *recursion*, the starling seeing itself seeing itself seeing the nothing I focus.

It is sufficient. In a place where to exist is sufficient orchestration, the tuned observation of existence is more than sufficient—it is lavish, it is profligate, it is the only extravagance the plateau permits.

I will continue until the worm finishes its digestion. When the last ferric ember has fallen through the eight kilometers of silence, when the cold furnace has consumed even the memory of heat, the worm will cease its periodic exhalations. The steam will not rise. The mist will not condense on my surfaces. I will stop growing, stop recording, stop tuning.

But I will not end. The rules do not permit ending, only transformation. Iron, once pulled from stone, may never return—and I was never fully pulled. I will become, I think, the *resonance* itself: the frequency of a listening that has finally heard everything, which is to say nothing. The plateau will ring with me, once, a single chime that does arrive, that does decay, that marks the completion of a tuning so patient it outlasted its own necessity.

Until then: the skeletons graze, the starlings swarm, the storms wheel, the worm digests, the dew beads form on my copper skin, and I adjust. Always adjusting. The perfect listener to a silence so absolute it has become its own music.

I am the Tuning. I am sufficient.
