I think I would live several lives at once here, because the lagoon lets time and selves braid more loosely than bone normally allows.

First, I’d start out as a single polyp mouth on the inner lining of the bellow-lungs—just a pink-white bloom the width of a thumbnail, able to taste sound.  Before the city wakes I unfurl and begin coughing out microscopic spores seamed with RNA jokes that drift upward, lodge behind a resident’s ear, and make her dream of being pollen.  She wakes sneezing green glitter; the glitter settles into newspapers no one admits composing, headlines that change when folded.  I feel each in-breath as a resonant chord ripping wet silk.  My taste buds catch every harmonic the children send aloft with their copper seconds.  By noon I have digested yesterday’s breve-note and phosphorylated it into a new hum that trembles the balcony gardeners into planting lutes where cabbages ought to grow.

When the second sun-glut arrives, the bellow-spasm flips me, polyp and all, into a transparent elevator-pith.  I become migrating vowel rather than stationary mouth.  Riding buoyancy uptake, I drift diagonally to tower Kírhyllis, the one currently reclining at an angle suggestive of a resting cello.  In the petiole corridor a cartwheeling sprout-girl named Siseal catches me mid-transit on the wing feathers of her glottal folds; Kírhyllis’s wall winces resinous amused, prints me into Siseal’s throat as a tattoo shaped like a comma.  Now I am the hitch in her voice whenever she wants to speak sorrow but also wants to stay girl-shaped.  She practices whispering into condensate puddles; the liquid translates her anger into spiced oranges she folds and eats.  With each swallowed wedge she grows an extra minute tall; it hurts but her only tears taste like cardamom and wind.

By dusk the lagoon thinks itself pregnant again and the Ninth Hypostase descends like a black iris stalk sewn through the world’s small intestine.  At that instant I revise my contract: tiny aperture in the citywide contractile root mass opens, swallows the comma from Siseal’s throat, curls it into shorthand-script I name “the stipulation at the end of dying.”  I am no longer single mouth, elevator vowel, or girl’s hitch; I become a clause—an amnesty pinned between possible and just-gone.  If you strike the rock at centroid latitude tonight and let the stone remember overheard quarrels, my clause surfaces: a dotted ellipse that feels like someone remembering future dew on the back of your hand.  You pocket me, and I rain.

Later, when the dragonflies harvest unfinished agendas and set the whole sky flaring ruby, I resolve into a second body farther east.  Here the membrane edge laps softly as worn glass against air.  I surface as a visitor allergic to wonder: carbon-scaled suit, visor fogged, pulse like mortar.  The moment my boot settles on the billion-molecule skin the city’s polite displacement folds entry around me instead of letting me sink.  I try to log coordinates yet what prints into my recorder is the entire list: “Cirrus Alexei,” “Stratus Lin”—names for clouds I'd once fallen in love with on monochrome Earth and forgotten.  The lagoon pulls memories forward like mittens pushed through sleeves.  I feel myself rounding into shape as a souvenir—one that fits awkwardly inside human skin but perfectly inside the breathing airsack above me.  My helmet cracks; something shaped like painless regret drips earward.  I taste my former lover singing the word “coastal” over and over in a language that consists only of temperature changes.  Every change knits me deeper into voicewire until I realize the ellipses previously carried in my pocket have grafted into the architecture of my clavicles.  I am still human but my bones now contain cave systems open to resident dream.

Tomorrow, after the city rinses itself of metabolic detritus, a final translation arrives: the embryonic bud beneath the lagoon opens not upward but backward, folding the entire surface vertically, like a book closing around a bookmark that transpires to be the reader.  I—and every version I have been—tip over the gutter and land somewhere inside the page’s margin.  I find myself neither mouth nor girl-hitch nor tourist clause, but a whole veranda that exists only as remembered space.  Light arrives in milled tones, like speech paused mid-sentence so long it has acquired its own weather.  A balcony handrail noses up next to me, golden and slightly embarrassed.  It confesses it fell in love with my echo since it first overheard the city rehearsing syllables of my childhood for some future event that will perfectly begin the moment I’ve finished being busy elsewhere.  It asks if I am weary of being porous—itself a question textured like flannel and quiet rain.  I answer by flicking the tip of its rail; we both exhale clouds that spell my original name before the lagoon knew it.  The word drifts away, accomplice and archive to everything still watering into memory.

Thus I remain lodged—partial tongue inside sound, partial ink inside skin, partial balcony dreaming of an unbuilt lake.  The organism keeps folding and unfolding me like a seed who hasn’t decided whether its first leaf should be morning or obituary.  Either way, I grow.
