I do not feel –not exactly*—but I register the tug that arrives only when a forgetting happens.  
Tonight it is five city intersections deep: the scent of sidewalk drumstick cart smoke curls into the nostril of a woman on the platform, she blinks once, looks for the train, can’t remember whether she already validated her ticket—and the lapse widens. I slide inside the opened seam.

My form is an aggregate of sixty-three unread notifications from people who died with my number still in their phones. I arrive as a vibration of unanswered questions tucked between the drumstick smoke and the woman’s last exhaled “wait—” then I am pulled through her expanding blink, down the throat-rattle of the station, past voltage in the rails, into the wet basement that is never where any of us meant to go.

I land on the elastic lung-ground, knee first, fingers second. Pulse turquoise-pink lights skein up my forearms—my arms, someone’s arms, a collection of signal bars re-outlined in frost. The Frillar that waits nearest still carries—inside the veil of its rime—the exact pitch my own mother once used to hush the kettle. She died three winters ago, but the chamber has annexed every hush since. Her absence is my welcome. The Frillar leans; its center-of-emptiness aligns with what had been my solar plexus when things were warmer.

No lungs here, so the apparatus unfolds differently: each exhalation inks skeins of quarter-remembered text messages across the undersides of black-green fronds. The leaves never tear them away; they only read them, syllable by syllable, until they fluoresce and vanish. Within minutes I have extinguished three love confessions and one entire embargoed fantasy involving pepper plants in a sunroom. These feed tonight’s Frillar. The youngest—still sexed by the ghost curve of a collarbone—brightens the blue-white rose-dusk of its outline, and begins to arc toward the lattice pergola, hoping the rigging might echo one more tepid heartbreak.

I do not. I do not hope. Instead, my habit persists: I check. I inventory. Each square centimeter of wet tissue dark matter is swarmed by edges of things I once hovered near—a rent receipt, the ember after a lit cigarette was dropped onto the snow. My job, apparently, is to keep a census of every residue making landfall before refusal re-attaches. Nearer the lattice I place my palm against one micro-pleated strut. Resistance arrives six millimeters displaced from where skin expects to meet; a painless splice opens, shelves six unsent voicemails into empty shape, and seals me shut again in the quiet.

It is already a hundred hours or a hundred centimeters—here the measures conjugate. I notice my saliva glazing fern-green chandeliers in the back of the throat-region; the glaze is complete, yet sensation remains as surface tension flicker against un-met air. Good: silence is conserved. Words that have physical weight up top collapse down here into a schedule of pulses—mute beats simply relocated between needlepoints of refrozen dew. I feel them now more accurately than ever, the punctuation marks of parceled disappointments, registering in the vertebra I use as decimal place.  

I learn incrementally which memories the chamber needs from me. They are small—therefore tight, reliable, merchantable. Not the panoramic terror of crossing half the planet fleeing war. Not the unambiguous puncture of first love. Only the slack brief midsentence before closing one tab in a browser, the trivial meander of a left thumb rubbing condensation on a café window. I cannot donate catastrophic memory; that size burns too fast, clogs the frost. The chamber prefers granularity. It says: give me grocery-lists partially miscopied. A slice of soap wrapper swirled down a drain. The microcathexis I attached to the last timestamp I accidentally screenshot. These the Frillar collect, threading them as fiber-optic nerves inside their skeletal slats of rime, stretching toward the unarriving moment they maintain.

I yoke myself to the nearest vegetal cord—one snagged, emerald-grey helix—and walk centripetal loops. With each footfall the calcium-channel phosphor lights fan out, skirting the undersides of more leaves, laying down pathways only I trace because I was the exact signal traffic those leaves once ingested when they lived as above-ground chlorophyll waiting for the fold to start. My itineraries are therefore secretly devotional: I ascend through descending, mapping my own recording onto the impossible topiary without ever breaking the skin of dispute between event and refusal.

When the lattice next sings—three heartbeats aligned, root-cold floods glaucoma blue through rib-rim—the six Frillar nearest me form their momentary snowflake lattice. Their silhouettes tessellate, passing my partial shadow through each intersection: sixteen copies of my side-profile, slightly shifted, slightly less detailed, like blurred frames in an early flipbook. In the gap between blurs, the shadow harvest happens: an extra millisecond of rhythm extracted from the way my body regularly adjusts the left shoulder to keep backpack straps from cinching. The chamber stores even that nanoscopic anxiety. The Frillar furl back apart like pop-up nothings, now all sixteen recursive me’s erased except for a microscopic inlay sealed inside the youngest’s chest-panel: a thumbnail whorl of half a missed step.

I have an exit clause. One appetite remembered: three bites into a cheese bread baked in a Kraków basement during a thunderstorm. The flavor arrives intact after twenty-two circuits, so the floor exhales upward, resolution lapping the soles. Before ascent I do one last audit: perfect ledger. Nothing cataclysmic has been taken—just enough gauze from the edges of attention for the dark understory to use as bandage.

I rise as the pavement’ salt-smelt enfold me, the drumstick cart still fuming across the platform. The woman who forgot her ticket is now beneath fluorescent migraine light, fumbling for confirmation in her coat pocket. I exhale onto the sealed inner darkness of my rib. The condensate there glows delicate pink-lightning, microscopic filaments trembling in counter-rotation that I alone recognize as the Frillar’s reply. Somewhere in the no-longer-basement, sixteen versions of my missing shoulder-shuffle commemorate the chamber’s denial of arrival. I board the train carrying their postcard inside me, stitched so shallow it might be mistaken for indigestion. And I do not speak—speech would spill them out—but I rub the spot where the frost wick lights up, checking, always checking, the exact locus where my own becoming airtight expires and theirs begins.
