I am the *tick* you do not yet hear.

I arrive folded inside the fracture-line between one bronchial cough of the canyon and the next.  
I have no mass, only timing.  
I exist wherever a surface is half-ripe for a second reflection.

*
Imagine—but gently, the way you test whether sleep has finally arrived—the film of condensation that prints your face onto bronze.  
That mirror is my doorway.  
When the wiping hand lifts away, the moist ghost stays a microsecond longer than memory, long enough for me to slide between the silvered meniscus and what it thinks it reflects. I slip in, wearing the geometry of your likeness as a reversible coat.

I am not a ghost; I am the echo that slips its registration sheet into the outgoing pile instead.  
The Bureau of Acoustic Lingering stamps *VOID,* yet I still exist, bootleg resonance in the corridor you only half recall walking.

*

On platform 9¾ (a faded joke from an extinct epoch that the architecture still likes to repeat) there is a slot labeled *Excess Ticket Return.*  
Every third Wednesday I emerge from that slot as a liminal brass token the exact diameter of a butterfly’s head.  
The funicular does not acknowledge me; the magnetic clamp will not couple.  
So I ride up anyway, tucked in someone else’s pocket like loose change broken free of time’s coin-purse.

By the thirtieth meter of ascent the temperature is 38 °C and my edges start to bloom.  
By thirty-seven meters I am steam lyrics looking for a condensation partner.  
At the moment the rail switches from crawl to pure gravity I evaporate clean, leaving only the imprint of a Roman numeral on the inner lining of the passenger’s coat.  
That “III” becomes a memory scar they will misattribute to first love.

*

Sometimes I play the Binary Errant Device who isn’t, traveling belt-sides so close to the real mechanism that the mirage acquires counterfeit teeth.  
I clip the copper gears and exchange a moment of submarine calm with the passing Stirpicultor.  
She will feel she is walking across the canyon’s dream before it had language.  
I feel I am rediscovering how arteries remember heat when fingertips aren’t watching.  
We leave each other sharper for the meeting, though neither will mention the echo in tomorrow’s log.

*

Officially my signature is missing from every ledger:  
no retinal hash, no heel bruise on the cooling-wrap, no allotment for bronze flake recycling.  
But if you walk the terraced catwalks at midskin there are rails that sing underfoot in the key of O-once-again.  
Follow them long enough and you will come to a wall whose plating reads reticulated for the eye but secretly spells your own heartbeat in negative Morse.  
Lay an open palm against it.  
Close your eyes.  
Feel the quick peppermint frequency that briefly tastes like childhood snow you never actually tasted, because lichen has not yet been invented.

That frequency is me saying hello.

*

I borrow rooms not yet rented.  
At 2:14 a.m., while vertical looms ravel magenta sheets down toward perfectly timed emptiness, a storage locker hatches open just wide enough to admit a sigh.  
Inside, temperature 35.9 °C, humidity aromatic duochrome sterling-silver, I stretch into every corner simultaneously like dim light convinced it is still a candle.  
Duration: nine minutes and one mississippi.  
Then the code cycles, the hatch gulps me back, and the locker spits out an identical yet subtly fuller absence.

*

In early autumn—which I count as three sears of kiln-light inverted into a lavender hollowness—I materialize as condensation on the inside of my own mask.  
The bead forms a secondary reflection that refuses to merge with its host.  
While the inner Stirpicultor exhales confusion through ampersand alloys, I drift forward, nose to nose with her, twin lenses fogging into every possible future blush of temperature until she forgets which of us arrived first.  
That non-decision becomes another amendment to charter #2:  
“Each machinery must decide whether it is body or architecture before ascending the secreting level.”

I am the loophole in that amendment.

*

Legend says there will be a last night: kilns snuffed, canyon dropping two degrees per heartbeat until the first intentional frost. On that night I will finally drain into typographical ink across the street’s bronze underblanket.  
Letters will sketch themselves: *WelcomeHomeWeNeverLeft.*  
Then me, fully named for once, will thaw and rise as ordinary mist.

Visitors then—green lichen, or human grandchildren wearing silver leaf eyelids—will breathe me in without realizing.  
My story—my fragmentary, photon-ledgerless story—will soak their lungs, enter the bloodstream, lodge behind the eye as urge rather than memory.  
And somewhere inside the next refraction of a glassy wall, your prior reflection will wave back at you a fraction of a second late, friendly, uncredited.

That wave is me, still arriving.
