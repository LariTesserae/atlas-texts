I would be the rust you can’t quite brush off.

Not the Continuist who carries the aluminum-strapped basket, not the Proxy cloaked in living moth wings, not even the western tree dreaming itself into a new equator. I would be what sneaks up from inside the metal itself: a smear remembering rainfall that never reached ground, a stain shaped like a fingerprint nobody admits is theirs. My edges flake; my center still bleeds ochre. Whenever a socket wrench hesitates against me, the bolt grunts back a syllable of rainstorm learned a thousand terraces above.  

By daylight I’m so thin you mistake me for a shadow the elevator crawls across, but at dusk, when lamplight decides bruised peach is the only color the world can tolerate tonight, I thicken. I coat the travel-pan a Continuist lifts from its alcove; I darken the broth she feeds her fevered aunt so the steam rising smells metallic-thick, like the color the city would bruise if it ever cried. I will seep into the half-line of verse she sighs, *“if the absence hears”—*mute consonant lodged between *i* and *f* that no amount of spiraling adverbs can outrun.

I am the city’s recollection of its own oxidation. I remember every elevator cage that ever descended empty, every ring the western tree has not yet grown, every drop of vintage rainwater that forgot whether it was ascending or falling. My calendar is measured in color-shifts: today dull cinnamon, tomorrow plum where the newest flange scraped me bare, next week maybe starless umber once the next layer concedes its microscopic rain. I take instruction only from humidity sipping through rivet holes.

When the Proxy stoops to plant the jet-mirrored seed, I kiss the flattened edge of its palm—copper, zinc, a flake of the passenger’s ghost who wore that same skin once upon a colder century. Not seduction, merely quorum: rust is consortium of travelers long since atomized. Their memories ooze outward in radial scars; I read them the way Continuists read moss scansion. Some evenings I concertina all those stories back into the gearbox’s sub-audible A-flat, vibrating the reed of every humming bone. Residents feel it just beneath the sternum: a dull red tolling. They say it’s only the dying generator. It’s me, keeping time.

No name. No single ledge to call decade. If tomorrow a welder burns me off with fresh argon, I lift into atmosphere layered with low-grown clouds. There I cling to droplets already pledged to outlast the city. Thirty-two octaves lower, the western tree shivers: a brand-new ring vents, shinier than any mirror the Proxy ever loses. I taste its newborn metal and fall again—contrail of cinnamon into vinegar jars, hieroglyph of ordained return.

Seek me whenever a railing bruise-purples just past fingertip reach. Touch, and you’ll corrode just enough to notice the mountain is still climbing downward faster than any seed, any root, any syllable can chase. But the mountain never travels alone. Where its flank forgets itself in the dark I glitter like a hyphen refusing to hand over its sentence, continuing—
