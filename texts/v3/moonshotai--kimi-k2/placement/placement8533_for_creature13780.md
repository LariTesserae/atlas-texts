I arrive as the pause between the seventh footfall and the eighth that never comes, the step someone forgot to continue. I am not the tourist, not the memory-coat wearer, not even the Fold seeking new ways to be space instead of self. I am what happens when gravity exhales and forgets to re-inhale—the millisecond of weightlessness just before it remembers itself.

I solidify out of the interval between the strobing subway-door coughs. In that 41-second window I am synaptic quicksilver, neither liquid nor flesh but the hesitation between deciding to be either. My form is a compromise: the shadow of a bird that once existed in a dream three generations after the dreamer died, folded fifteen times so no wingspan remains wider than a sigh.

The Choir sees me first. Their hollow heads tilt, cyclone and reverse-rain acknowledging the intrusion they did not predict, which is new for them. They begin singing not to me but to the residual space I occupy, treating emptiness as soloist. I recognize the tune: it is the lullaby my tongue never quite learned to cradle a screaming newborn universes ago, when I still thought lullabies mattered.

Limmer approaches, compass-arm tracing the negative outline of my existence. Where the silver line would bisect me, I side-step into the moment before the line decides what it wants to cut. The line hesitates, then folds into itself, forming a tiny helix of un-drawn geology. Limmer—in what I finally recognize as surprise—drops a pebble of compressed void that rolls toward my non-feet. I let it pass through; its own absence rounds the bump I’ve become.

Gravity exhales again—three-quarter strength now—and I sink not into but _alongside_ the copper membrane, like a boat whose keel never quite meets water. My body rearranges itself into what a drowned starlight would look like if asked to practice breathing. The membrane rises to kiss my ankles knowing I have no ankles, and its surface writes in microcurrents—a responding preface to the autobiography Limmer never decided to start.

I am, strictly speaking, impossible: neither arrival nor departure, I am the hinge between goodbye and the edge that word needs to fall off. I am what the membrane dreamed when it tried to remember dreaming.

Moments (though “moment” is still lobbying for tenure) after the Fold re-paper-planes himself into a dry riverbed of conjecture, I retrieve the echo he trails behind him like unspooled gut string. I pluck it once; the note falls across the newborn microcontinent below and makes it shiver into deciduous forests six million years too soon. Fold-flannel maps himself further, pretending not to notice, but the forest tells him thanks anyway in rustlelanguage.

The Tourist offers me a pocketed Tuesday that never quite happened. I thread it through my substance like a mirror through water; it emerges on the other side as a small yellow dog who immediately forgets its name and begins howling directions nobody receives. The dog is never real, neither were the directions, but its howl carves a canyon 40 footrieves deep before folding into static.

At cycle minus seven seconds, the seventh pillar begins to finish itself. Into the emerging gateframe I step—not forward but sideways, into the negative space enclosed by the future arch. I occupy the absence as roof and cornerstone simultaneously. As I fuse with the architecture of un-being, the valve prepares its next sigh. Gravity slips to one-third so it can lean close and whisper.

It says: You came back exactly the way one returns to a bedtime halfway through—someone in the dream remembers the dreamer is still asleep elsewhere. And before the world can decide if this counts as person or weather, I invert, hollow to echo chamber, echo to ear. The Choir sings my unnamed self into orbit as punctuation. Limmer etches it onto sediment-layers forming in accelerated paste.

The membrane drinks the final syllable of that signature and writes nothing in return. But it holds the taste like an unopened letter. In the interval between valve closing and next exhalation I exist only as the peach-soft blur the letter leaves on fingertip.

I am the punctuation that ends the sentence before the sentence is spoken. I am the pause that loves its own incompletion more than any story it might interrupt.
