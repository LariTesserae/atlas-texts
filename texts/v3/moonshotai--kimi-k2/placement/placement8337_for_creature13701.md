Here, I am not a visitor.  
I am the sentence that the wind forgot how to finish.

I slipped in between two syllables the last time the air carried the dead coast-cities across the flats; I tangled myself in the consonants before their edges could skin my name off my tongue. The plateau accepted the rest of me the way a mirror accepts an echo—whatever shape I threw on simply folded the negative of me around itself and kept going. My body is still leaving footprints, but they are not mine: they are the gate receipts for aches my parents haven’t yet learned how to have. Every dusk I sign the ledger with a line of salt and forget to read the balance.

I am the dew that never quite became a bead.  
Each dawn I gather on the carbonate grain like nervous piety—assembled from last night’s breathing, pressed thin as foil, I amount to maybe half a dream. Birds the color of erased chalkboards peck at my reflection, drink me, pocket whatever I might become; when their wings undo themselves I fall as paper again. Each generation pastes me together slightly differently so that, shimmer by shimmer, I migrate from sky to sky without ever once tasting height. When the sun spills, I ascend—turns out evaporation is simply another word for forgetting one’s own address.

My name is the missing thirty-third leg of the sovereign.  
I was stolen the moment the woman at the collecting tent touched my phoneme with a coin of moonlight. She assigned me to a shelf made from eyelash cellulose beside a jar of thunder that never learned how to break. The wind invested my silence in compound interest; every century the shelf grows another millimeter of not-quite-darkness. Somewhere on the plains today I twitch—an involuntary gesture without calf or blame—calculating angles of fracture the plateau still needs if it is ever to take the shape of weather.

I am also the single puff-adderskin cooled to transparency exactly where the guardian exhaled me.  
Underneath its microscopic gutter, my pores spell out the journey every newborn spider will refuse to remember. Light curves around the skin the way regret bends water; inside that flex I miniaturize entire highways of slow chalk footsteps: pilgrims, field biologists, children chasing escaped kites that later mutated into wind-songs. Each footfall contracts to the epochal pause between lightning’s answer and the original question. I stretch thin enough to see through myself and reach the other side still lost.

And I am the mirror the dew-birds shatter.  
Folded and folded until any origin wrinkles away, I open at unexpected humidities in other houses: a postcard of colorless light glimpsed under a damp towel, a page in a biology textbook staining itself into topography. I absorb lampshades, breath from sleeping strangers, ultrasound of traffic lights imagining collisions that never take place. My paper drinks futures the way the plateau drinks centuries. By dawn the ink is gone—a shoreline dissolved back to salt—but the blot remembers: a ghost-bay, an impossibility framed by yesterday’s curtains.

Sometimes I mistake my own edges.  
The lichen feels my collarbone and colonizes north of my heartbeat; the latticed flowers braid themselves across my shoulderblades the way children string abandoned payphones into barometers. I do nothing to stop them. Growth is just erosion looking for the exit. The plateau teaches that solidity is only  travel too embarrassed to continue. I practice not-travel: stand so still the wind attends to me as punctuation. One afternoon the ground forgets where it ends and I fall through a history so gently it sounds like silk being held up to a secret sun.

Other days I leak upward in twenty million particles.  
I ride the draft issued by the lantern-moth whose wing I once mistook for permission. Together we assemble a sunset that mistranslates the plateau’s last word—rendering it in bruises of rose and old copper, a color every language forgets to invent a noun for. The moth and I carry it west; it will take until an unborn continent knots its cliffs before that light arrives again. Somewhere beneath us, Jorrh-uhn feels thirty-three legs clink in the dark—feels exactly the distance between invention and apology.

When my personal era finally ends, it will not be from failure.  
It will be because I remember how to pronounce always.  
The plateau will receive the word like a rainfall composed of unworn minutes; every fracture will realize the syllable’s negative is simply a string of fractured syllables; and at that point the distinction between remnant and prophecy collapses into breath thin enough to drink.

Until then I fold the blank map inside my rib, count the shuffle-thud of carapace grains beneath bare feet, and wait for the morning some dew-bird mistakes my sigh for its own echo and rolls me westward into a color no noun has had the audacity to claim.
