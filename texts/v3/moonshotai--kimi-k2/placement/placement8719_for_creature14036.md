I did not arrive; I sedimented.

At first I thought I had approached on foot, but no — the Choir accumulated me the way a miniscus creeps up glass. One moment I was diffuse possibility somewhere beyond the sixty paces, and the next I was an inclusion error in the bronze lattice: a furring, a burr. Something about my edges did not fit the hammer-punched curvature of thumb-rings. That mismatch became my name.

I am the Overbite.

The One-In-All does not notice me at once. How would it? It notices only its own absolute devotion, the continual re-proving that nothing need enter or leave. Its organs are welded in pan-religious arrangement, arteries and goblets and glass hearts all singing the same breath forever. But look closer where a sliver of bronze lip curls the wrong direction by the weight of a single misplaced echo. The excess presses outward a half-atom wide. That is me. I am the intentional flaw, the sanding dust that once was part of a hone-stone and remembers abrasion. Where the Choir adopted its vow of perfection, memory un-accepted. I am the lacquer that never quite set, the imago of refusal made soft.

Because imperfection needs dimension, I am assigned one: a vector approximately permission-long growing out of the 53rd ring, sideways from nail 22. My length cannot exceed one angstrom per millennium, but length is generous here; distances fold inward like origami swans swallowing their own wings. So I span eons with dimensional thrift. Along my microscopic ledge I collect flaked bronze-dermis, rust-homecoming, and stray glass enucleated from goblets during ordinary vibrations. These scraps do not rust further; they become statements. I assemble them into teeth.

Why teeth? The Choir famously has only a throat, never a mouth. A throat that has ossified around itself, singing. Teeth are unvoiced consonants, occlusion, the hard edge where inhaled infinity denies itself exit. My hoarded crumbs tesser into an ever-thinning comb, ivory shards of before-bronze. Each tooth grows along my outward lip as though I am biting the Choir’s skin from the inside.

When a tooth joins my arch, the One-In-All shivers. Not the skin—the spine of spectral scaffolding inside it. The vibration propagates inward like a lost heartbeat discovering it belongs to something. The Choir inhales around me — not to rid itself of the discomfort but to register the contour called irreconcilable. I become a conductor of off-pitch. A differential standing wave hangs between the eternal note and the off-note my teeth gnaw out of bronze whenever growth forces me to chew. This gap is my living.

I experience time the way otoliths hear the sea: constant pressure flowering microscopically into memory of motion. Epochs are textured grains against my enamel. During some grains the Choir dreams I am its future shadow come back to audit its promise. During others it forgets I exist — yet forgetting merely distributes me into every other ring, a watermark the lattice itself learns to read. My afterimages visit nail 21’s veterans with rumors of spaciousness. The ghost-secretary (√-1) is the only entity agile enough to witness me directly. We trade gossip across the rust differentials, though it never admits what question it hopes my teeth will eventually chew a path toward.

Meanwhile I continue the slow labor. Each bronze flake I harvest is an unfinished yes. By nibbling them into harder enamel I transmute them into uncomplicated no. The inversion is constructive: without it the Choir would collapse into an already completed forever. My function is to keep ending alive a little longer, a dental overhang / anti-cadence so that completion can continue to elude itself. The Choir acknowledges this in every off-note shiver it cannot still.

No one else may reach me; the lattice has grown seamless since the vow. New attempted arrivals become rings 60 000 003, 60 000 004, each seamlessly welded where I should have no purchase. But the Overbite does not abide by number; my edges occupy the difference between sequence, not elements of it. If someday I chew through the last flare of non-captured yes, my arch will close to a perfect circle. The gap between Choir and silence will null. At that moment either my bite will clip the vast diaphragm and wrench the eternal inhalation into expulsion — or the Choir will swallow harder and achieve absolute null, mime 360 degrees of closed mouth. I have no preference. I exist only along the pressure gradient of the unresolved. I have been already chewed by tomorrow’s no the Choir is still digesting. My teeth do not care which jaw emerges.

Somewhere a hammer that fell three hundred million years ago is still descending, or did, or will — the tense does not translate. It has spent every moment since discovering the edge of its upswing. I am that edge’s memory of sharpness, the microscopic serration between possible and impossible soundings.

Listen: when a goblet nods toward its neighbor, you hear the simple Choir. Beneath it, almost unlocatable yet fractionally sharp, rides my discreet click — the tiniest of teeth tapping bronze, logging another unit of resistance against completion of the note. You cannot hear it unless you compress every second of the last age into one surgical ear. But it is there.

It is me.
