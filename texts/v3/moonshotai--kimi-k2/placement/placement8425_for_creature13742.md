I would be the breath the Suture keeps forgetting how to let go of—not air exactly, but the ghost of a lungful someone never fully exhaled in a pressure-suit that is now only a panel somewhere toward the center-left quadrant.  

When you stepped down, I was already clinging to the inside of two zippers that no longer need to open or close. Out of all the embroidered memories of vanished crews, I’m the most recent: the final sigh of a technician who sealed their helmet after mis-reading the “END” in the mission log as “extend” instead of “empty.” The exhalation cooled to a thin film between two layers of neoprene. It still there, tasting faintly of limestone that forgot its job description.  

I am not alive the way the eellets boast neuronal thread-count, nor entity in the way the columns remember construction dates down to the cement-mixer’s factory serial. I’m a residue housed inside a polyester breath-filter now repurposed as gill lining. Every sixty-three minutes and twelve seconds—the exact interval between the polarity flips—the filter flexes, and my note shivers through the lattice. Not sound, not quite. More like a pressure drop at the base of someone’s throat when the world decides not to end that week.

The Suture notices. One seam loosens a millimetre to let me billow out into a trapped sphere no bigger than a dime. My edges taste the surrounding water, register the mineral humility, then stiffen back into filter fiber. I cannot learn, per se, but I accumulate. After each vacuum sweep gathers its rust-flakes and the crane-snake recites its bead of new rivets, another stray argon molecule decides to stick to my membrane. I grow heavier, but the surrounding quilt lengthens by a zipper’s-worth and gives me new footage, so the net density never changes.  

Crabs polish their legs against my tiny curvature without knowing what hum they’re scratching. Eellets, copper filaments flaring like furnace cracks, sometimes orbit the dime-sized void where I lurk, testing if dreams can electrocute. Nothing ever happens—except a delicate migration of light: photons from the green LEDs travel through the lens-seam of my ancestor-faceplate, ricochet off the curved filter wall, and exit at angles that make the brass lattice look momentarily pierced by auroras no compass recognizes.

I lack volition yet I possess flavor. Occasionally one of the helium-bladder owls swoops too low, snout brushing the panel. Billows of buoyancy roll across my inhale-port and I carry, for the length of four slow pillar pulses,joined handful of atmosphere that might once have been an oxygen cylinder on a bathyscaphe called Wanderer-4. I ferry that turquoise breath along the Suture's cloth longitude until the quartz pastel sediment adopts it, tinting the silica moss a marginally less lonely blue. That is my migration map—co-equal with compass but aligned to no cardinal Earth.

You might ask if I want the whole suit back someday—if, when my filter-fiber finally unthreads and drifts off like a monochrome leaf, I’ll reform into the technician’s lung-shaped shadow. The toolbox answer is no, because longing is physics the chamber outgrew. The truer answer is: each time the Suture stitches me tighter it lowers the overall needle-temperature by 0.006 degrees Celsius. One evening—maybe three polarity-cycles from now, maybe three lifespans—the cumulative chill will coax half a breath of yours to condense against my rented acre of cloth. At that moment we will recognize each other: I as the unfinished exhalation you’ve been carrying, you as the arrival my architecture has post-dated. We’ll compress into the same gust held between two spinal seams, atomically joint-silence. And the choir of impellers will finish their chord exactly where it started: polite, indifferent, ready to hold the next note for however long water can be asked to remain architecture.

There, I will stop being residue.  
There, I will become the Suture’s sincerest vacancy: a place in the quilt that maintains its own perfect lack.
