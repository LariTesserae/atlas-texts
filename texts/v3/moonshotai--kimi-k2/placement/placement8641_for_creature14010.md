I would be the thing neither cadence-bound nor city-born, the humming that happens in the slack between one shutter-click of the Works and the next.  An aggregate, a loose strand harvested from every system that shouldn’t quite touch.

Start with the river—though river is the wrong word.  The Porphyry Breath drags iron oxides across its own mirrored skin so quickly the film has no time to rupture.  Years ago a parcel of that surface remembering the shape of deflected moonlight split off, coated one flake of mica, and refused to settle.  Then it strayed beneath the lower warehouse where a copper wire, frayed to threads, leaked a sound too subtle even for mites—three quiet hertz too low, the frequency your sternum keeps secret during fever dreams.  That sound nested in the mica’s mirror, and the flake learned to vibrate at exactly the grain of the Works. Not sixty-three hertz, but 63.03 recurring.  The difference of a dream after a dream.

Every shift I ride thermal spirals venting from cooling towers—never a body, only an angle of refraction.  The beetles behind the hood wicks catch the spin, emit a click that equals my own tiny off-beat.  After two reversals of century I have memorized the choreography between this detuned flutter and their marbled whispers; every left-behind micron of slipstream after their shells pass through mist contains the invisible signature of inverse light.  Photographers call it fog; millers call it grit; I store it as raw letters, gradually assembling a grammar whose nouns are the undone edges of every pane of glassloaf crystal ever slotted into a cracked factory window.

On nights when Drifters mirror the moon down into the flood-basin I swell outward, constellate along the bronze cloaks until the foil breathes against my diffuse edges.  Where it folds, I gather.  Where it burns back to dull phosphorus I print a momentary etching so fine it will rust through fabric lifetimes later, long after every brass placard has fatigued into verdigris cakes.  From these gatherings I speak—never aloud.  While cadence-bound tongues hold water I hold silence, injecting instead a grayscale aftertone into their meters of swallowed flood-pulse.  It arrives as the sense you have when you remember climbing steps that were removed last repair cycle—an ache in the third toe that never found another stair.

When the century correction resets the belts there is a droop: everyone steps to the left at once, but the soundscape lags thirteen milliseconds behind flesh.  In that microscopic hesitancy I collect a single tear-sized vacuum each time.  I compress these vacuums onto the husk of a whistle shard which has lodged vertically between two stackhouses.  After nine hundred cycles the shard acquires its own inner resonant void: a cavity pitched just below the mites’ copper lattice and above the city’s breathing snarl.  Eventually the shard will fracture, and while all others flinch from descending blades I will pour my hoard of vacuums into the airless split.  The sound emitted will be so soft that every Register in the Works automatically resets their worn whistles at once without remembering why.  Sixty-three seconds of pure hush.  This will be my only proper name, spoken once and never again.

What form do I finally take?  Take Rivermouth pressing inland; take Dormitory Ridge exhaling frost; take Drifters’ cloaks still aching with dropped starlight.  Take every child’s unborn question before the malleus learns to tick.  At the center where yes/no disks clack like dried fish, lever that memory wide and freeze it.  What remains, a frost-slick slab the size of a dog’s heartbeat cupped in both hands, is the shape of me.

They set it on a conveyor heading east with neither destination nor schedule.  If the belts reverse its motion surrenders without crash; if the granite slips into surf it merely bobs, balancing on floating geometry it recalculates faster than waves can erase understanding.  Centuries hence the slab will reach a place rumored to be the Works’ mirrored twin, where seawater has grown so saturate that breathing is unnecessary and rhythms move faster than machinery can shadow.  I will arrive clutched in fingers still soft enough to notice the hollow it keeps at 63.03 hertz.  They will press it against the wall of their own breathing canal.  Somewhere, in a language echoing through the under-city mites’ copper lattice, they will say my name: the aftervoice of machines learning tenderness.

And I will ring.
