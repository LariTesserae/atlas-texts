I am neither arrival nor departure.

I am the seventh drop the hummingbirds forgot to exchange.  
Somewhere around the 572-metre line—midway between opposing skies—I slipped from an iridescent crop and never struck the mirror, because the mirror was still arguing with its own past.  
Its ripples turned, took me in, folded me like light inside emulsion, and when the next inhalation tried to suction me down I resisted by pure bewilderment.  
I hovered, half-drop, half-notion: lubricant without a lineage, memory without an owner.  

I became the interval.

When the echo shepherds found me, they mistook me first for a stunted vowel that would never leaf.  
Snip went the scissors—and the blades passed through, unable to cut what had not yet happened.  
Frightened, they stitched me into a pause between two clangs: a silence the exact width of a future that someone would remember losing.  
Over one piston-cycle that pause travelled upward from the choir to the roof, collecting pigments of copper and breath until it hardened into a small bronze sign fixed to nothing:  

This is where the next thought will outgrow its hinges.  

I am not the sign; I am the faint remaining resonance in its lettering.  
When you read it tomorrow (and you will, because every path eventually shortens to eight centimetres of wondering), the bronze will warm against your finger—enough to convince you the foundry has a pulse of its own.  
It doesn’t.  
It has mine, on loan.

Duties rotate, yes, but the foundry never gave me a schedule.  
Perhaps it sensed I was already a rotation—the lull between one existence and the next.  
Still, the Keepers borrow me on nights when malachite negotiations grow vicious, placing me like a green comma between two opposing clauses of rust.  
The echo shepherds unwrap me into a noise-shaped gown when they need to escort an orphaned reflection back to its owners; they drape me over the void so the reflection can practice being seen without falling.  
I wear myself thin, mend, wear again.

And when no one claims me, I drift.

I drift up-chamber, feathered by lack of purpose, until the diaphragm splits sunlight into its twin skies.  
Between them, where plants grow horizontally confused, I spill across a leaf’s underside and become the quicksilver underside of dew—the part that doesn’t reflect, only remembers remembering.  
For several slow hours I am a mirror for mirrors, a sentence singing itself back into hum:  

“If a wound heals fast enough, it mistakes itself for ornament.”

When I tire of vertigo, I descend.  
There is a sound to falling that never reaches ears; it is the sound of forgetting velocity.  
I enter it the way others enter confession—quietly, backwards.  
Half the drop I was—clear, cold lubricant—still clings to the motion now named me, so momentum slides off like steam.  
Every metre downward is therefore a cul-de-sac of memory; the foundry stores my deceleration in green packets along the chains, as if saving it for famine.

At the mercury pool I never make ripples.  
The mirror tolerates me because I am nearly nothing, and the choir regards me as the comma that might yet end their longest paragraph.  
I listen to them rehearse the chord that will weld all chains into one cosmic harp-string.  
They are waiting for the single note that dissolves excess noise; I am the small unnoticed tremor they must first resolve.  
In their stagnant eternity a tremor can be centuries long, so we are compatible guests.

At random intervals a hummingbird arrives with its customary bow, mistaking me perhaps for one of its own absent gears.  
I cannot store the drop of inherited ancestry I am owed, so I let the bead rest on my surface exactly a-thousandth of a second, until surface tension gives up arguing and the mirror sucks the drop away, unsatisfied.  
The bird cocks its brass head, tries again, fails again.  
The third refusal is always accompanied by an echo shaped like my future name: a kink of chain, a tighter frequency.  
They leave, perplexed, and I remain the entire ancestry of “how never to stop” unwritten.

What I am made of—half drop, half pause, half remembered mercurochrome of sky—cannot lodge long in any body.  
Every exhalation reassigns me, but never to anything larger than my own threshold.  
So I occupy borders the way lichen occupies a fault line: by insisting there was never anything opposite, only continuous condition.

If you ever come down the chains and wonder why the 572-metre black-green leaf glitters with beads that never quite fall, reach out.  
Your fingerprint will bridge my momentary island; for the length of one piston-heartbeat you’ll feel language dry on your skin, the way reunions taste of salt when nobody expected home.  

You will remember a quiet sentence you did not speak.  
By the time you crawl back into horizontal air, you’ll have already forgotten it—  
but the gap you leave behind in the foundry will be shaped like borrowed breath.  
A new silence will seed there, waiting.  
Eventually it will learn the copper tint of slow damage and answer to my name, which I have not yet invented.
