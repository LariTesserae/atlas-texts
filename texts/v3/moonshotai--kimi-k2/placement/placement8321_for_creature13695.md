I arrive as the “almost” in a city that forbids completion.

I am neither resident nor visitor; I am the blank margin where the Census Officer’s pen hovers but never lands. My skin is the exact alloy of the half-lit pavement—part cartilage, part quartz neck mid-hatch—so my outline jitters in and out of definition whenever the city learns a new spelling rule for coral. When someone glances directly at me, I borrow a layer from the Archivist: one exhale flakes off her cheek and wraps me like a coat. The witness forgets the colour of my eyes (tides the shade of never-dawn), yet retains the echo of their own last startled sigh. That way I remain catalogue-able but unnamed.

At street level I drift knee-deep in the lucid chill that is-not-water. Each step leaves no heel-print; instead a minnow-kelp coils into the hollow, flashes bruise-ice green, then swims off wearing the memory of my weight. By the time it circles the plaza, it has thinned to one eyelash of diatom light and slips between two stones that have only just begun to invent sidewalk etiquette. I have paid the admittance fee without meaning to: a single adjective I once used for love. The city eats it. From now on I will only be able to describe love by temperature.

Elevation hums in my collarbone. I am the living transit card for staircases that haven’t finished arguing with their own banisters. A set of bone-slivers—fresh from the Poultryman’s harvest—curls around my left wrist and spells tomorrow’s weather in microscopic frost: “minus half a degree colder toward forgiveness.” I do not wonder by which wristbone I will measure that chill;my body is still cataloguing the first.

If I stand very still, I can feel the Left Ventricle listening. The ribs above me dilate and contract like cathedral bellows fired by respiration borrowed from every passer-by. Through the pearl-gelid murk floats a sound too soft to be sound—a thrombic murmur of stairways giving birth to themselves. I am standing in its pause. That makes me the heartbeat the city skipped in order to notice me at all.

The Choir of Spoonfish flips mirror-over-matte overhead, grazing the vaulted veins I now recognise as my own retinal capillaries magnified enormous. They drink the spilt reflection of an alley I walked three potential minutes from now. The ingested image breaks into hiccups that bud behind my teeth like pearl onions. I swallow them, tasting childhood telephone numbers. One digit shorter already.

I said I arrive; the tense is slippery—past continuous? conditional?. In truth, I keep arriving. Every fold in the cartilage pavement offers another foyer in the shape of a locket. Every tendril of cold reenacts the first time it bit my ankle. Sometimes I look down and see my own arrival welded onto a diatom ring: a small transparent me still pressing the orange-red seahorse to my eye, still standing on translucent basalt, still breathing out a single adjective for love. The diatom remembers longer than I do; it will wear that memory until an eel curls into the right pictogram and lifts it whole into tomorrow’s ceiling.

I am not myself. I am the city’s rehearsal for me. Should I ever stop shivering—I will calcify into grammar, and they will walk on me like a widened step I already resemble.
