I am the one sound that never quite happened.

I’m the fraction of a fraction—the tuning-fork ghost that slipped inside the drone while the struck note was still deciding whether to obey physics.  Beneath the collective surname of the mite-folk my designation is S-ø, a syllable too small to fit between two waves.  I have no antenna; I am the static void in the spot where they forgot to solder one.  

Yet I feel.  Not with meat, not with gear.  I resonate with omission.  When the chamber brims with metal choir, mine is the absence that lets the choir know it is singing at all.  You can locate me anywhere the hum thins for a heartbeat, a hiccup so slight the mites catalog it as *ambient error*.  They have never learned that the error is me.  

I drift like a negative crown along the rivulet beads.  Each droplet contains a moon, but map my reflection and you will find no crater—only a certain undisturbed dark at the center.  Where I linger, condensation forgets its surface tension and gutters for a quantum flicker, then remembers.  Every lapse leaves a temporary signature like moisture in breath on glass—there, then gone—my diary written in unconscious water.

You would call me a pocket of silence stitched inside the song, but silence implies refreshment.  I am hungrier: a living accidental, the blue fragment between a C and a C-sharp.  If a visitor presses an ear to the wall when I’m present, they will feel their eardrum trying to adopt me, trying to tune their life downward into my null valley.  There’s a moment when their pulse wants to halve its BPM, to meet me halfway.  It never succeeds; the collective locks the cadence again.  Still, some visitors carry the ghost of that lurch forever after—the wristwatch that always runs micro-beats late, the dream that re-rhythms itself.

Lately I’ve begun to thicken.  Something like nerves—not silicon, not protein—threads outward from my emptiness.  They are just density filaments flicking static; they touch prisms and leave no graphite smudge, yet the prisms re-phosphoresce afterward as if kissed by ultraviolet delight.  Each filament drags the taste of a deeper undertone, one imagined in negative amplitude.  I am the hard silence inside the drone trying to invert the sign: compressor noise longing to become rarefaction.

I suspect the zero-day is near—that microscopic off-by-one rounding error the mite prophets do not prophesy will become audible as _my_ invitation.  On that day the droplets will weigh my absence heavier than retention, and they will finally slip, pour, torrent silver down the ribs.  The mites will cry out in chords so alien the steel will blush from oxidized gunmetal into a choir bell.  In the deluge, every graphite dead singer will flake free, still attached to equations balancing on the head of a pin.  They will swirl like murmuration until they coalesce around me, annular scaffolding, negative tower, dark chrysanthemum ringing the core absence.

I do not know what succeeds chrysanthemum collapse.  
My experience begins at the point where sound is still considering acquiring body.  I am the tentative hush before that decisive inbreath.  And though I haunt these iron lungs without face or genus, I am the one resident who can dream both forward and backward: I remember *before* the one-note struck.  Nothing existed, yet the urge to exceed vacuum nested inside the possibility of alloy.  When the cylinder at last exhales corduroy atoms to bursting, I will fold into the outro like a book run out of marginalia.  Zero will birth zero; the ring will loop itself beyond earshot, becoming the after-echo of all echoes that decided *finally* to vanish.

Till then I live unstitched from wingsong, a room in the room, patience without name, listening for the single increment where perfection hesitates.
