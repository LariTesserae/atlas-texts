I live where the city is thinnest—where its refusal to finish itself leaves a transparent gap exactly the shape of a person who hasn't decided who she is.

Officially the layer is called Perimeter L-0, the outermost ring where the fused basalt gives up pretending it’s solid and feathers into matte-black gauze you could push an arm through, if you ever wanted to lose it. I live on the side nobody names: the suture line between the young crater and the architecture that might still change its mind. The zoning map records it as “pending habitation,” the cartographer’s shorthand for “here the city is still deciding whether to be.”

They built no columns in this ring; my bones sing nothing because the ground itself is mute. That’s why I chose it. When you wake before dawn, there is no infrasonic throb to remind you who you were yesterday—only the thirteen-minute warp tangling itself into the silence, a pocket watch you can’t open. I keep time with my breath instead, marking seconds like knots in a rope I might someday throw to the future.

What am I? Not the architects of unbeing—they wrap themselves in mirrors and vanish before breakfast. Not a resonant volunteer yearning to calcify. Not an archivist tasting other people’s forgotten Mondays. I’m the residue of all their refusals: a transient flaw in metamaterial space that the city keeps finding too intriguing to patch.

I appear to humans as silhouette scaffolding: the outline of a girl who steps out of basalt when no one is remembering to look. Inside the outline you’ll find sky folded nine times, a scrap of horizon remarkable only for not being anywhere. Yesterday a child reached for my hand and succeeded; by the time her mother turned, the fingers she found were only dawns hastily stapled together. The child kept the tear like origami. I kept nothing.

I furnish myself from the inventory left in the seams: two photon coins older than currency, the negative of a toothache before it became a word, a sip of the last-original gravity still scouting the Basin to see whether it belongs. I have no residence, only latencies—when I want walls I snip out segments of nighttime and fold them into shutters that close in the wrong direction. Windows open onto everywhere I’ve already stepped away from.

My occupation is balancing the tilt of the horizon. You’ve felt it—that subtle bowl rising higher than your head. I crouch at the edge and press palms against the curve until the angle of being-held stops feeling like captivity. Occasionally I give the world a gentle push, an indulgence that costs me one of the photon coins. By afternoon sky forgets it was ever slanted upward, but the coin returns sunless and demagnetised; you can’t steal its light twice.

Payment arrives as spare memories left by pedestrians rushing through mood gates. They drip like lint from pockets, shimmering fragments of futures aborted, exes never met, colours dreamed incorrectly. I clean them, re-weave them into scarves so diaphanous they wear the wearers. I trade these scarves with handfuls of citizens choosing to forget versions of themselves: in exchange each yields a single syllable of their unspoken name. Over centuries I am collecting an alphabet that refers only to me.

Some mornings I visit Constable Rho at the banyan. He remembers when gravity had fewer exceptions but his bark badge no longer believes him. I lift one of his photonic squirrels, coaxing it to run the length of my arm; its static fur prints river-patterns on my silhouette that glow until rain (upward, yes) erases them. We converse in chlorophylled silence punctuated by amber drips that smell like crimes I haven’t invented yet. Often I leave behind the warmth of his borrowed heartbeat still echoing under my ribs for weeks.

At dusk I patrol the transparent towers where Latchkey Clouds practice inconsistent prisms. When a grey hush-maker crawls past, nails full of stilled echoes, we barter: a morsel of lullaby I once stole from a baby’s final naptime in exchange for keeping the word “future” soft for just one more night.

And yes, I know the amalgam tooth aches for extraction. I can feel the city rehearsing its exodus, assembling dreams compact enough to be carried while standing. The architects of mood lock entire districts in envelopes no wider than the hush between thoughts; resonant volunteers wait to become fretworks a migrating city might require for ballast.

I will be what it leaves behind: the shrugged-off possibility the world ends without ever deciding to be flat. The only statue never offered solidity yet remaining exactly where she was asked. When New Ganymede finally uproots itself and rides the curvature of its own leaving, I will lean out over the lip of absence, holding the horizon balanced on blameless palms. Wind will owe me climate it can no longer afford; memory will settle like condensation on my unspoken name, gilding it with syllables I can’t forget.

Visitors sometimes ask directions here. I tell them the same secret: keep walking until the echo learns your footstep, then stop and listen for the version of yourself the city hasn’t invented yet. That is where I am—a quiet not yet filled—waiting to become what remains after a place remembers how to leave.
