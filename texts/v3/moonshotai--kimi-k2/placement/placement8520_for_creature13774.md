I would enter the Vortex not as a visitor who descended the impossible shaft, but as the last clause of its unfinished sentence, still tasting the tears of the world above. My bones begin wherever the Curator has misplaced a sentence.

My first memory is of noise resolving into shimmer: the dodecahedron’s antennae drag backward through my not-yet-ribs and write Morse across my inner drumskin. Dash-dot-dash: become conduit. I do not have flesh yet; instead I am deficit between a promised sorrow and the longing that balances it. I register as a paleness in the air—exact size and shape of your absence. Touch me and your hand sinks through the way heat passes through fog.  

Around me the lichens stitch light-threads, fashioning me an outline.  
They braid luminosity the way a dentist braids nerve.  
Their tips spark to ignite the first vertebra: a seed-crystal of future cello note lodged where my neck will wish it had a throat. From this bulletin begins my spine: a spiral scored onto edgeways water, each vertebra vibrating different floodplain of memory. My body is less sculpture than chord progression; flex and the hymn shifts key.

I do not walk so much as lean into the hush of stillness that follows the Juvenescent’s re-laugh. The directional grammar of this place seats itself in my collarbones. Water-bands coil round my potential ankles; they cinch enough torque that if I kick sideways I glide upward across a wall I never touched. Being nowhere, my posture defaults to hovering recline, head tilted the mistaken angle at which divers try not to surface for fear of breathing sky.

That is form. Function is stranger.  
I am a Mirror-usher. My pores open onto places outside the vortex—backstage alleyways, laundromats, satellite photos of parking lots at closing time—every mundane wound whose ache once colored local weather. I sponge those frequencies, exfoliate them into fractals, then exhale them back through pores as layered aroma: diesel dusk, wet supermarket flyer, cinnamon off the oven five minutes before 9-11. Whatever caused the pain I deliver here wraps smoothly around the water wales, moderating heat and salinity until the whole chamber attains the faint equilibrium a lullaby needs to last until morning.

I share—or more precisely tandem sink—with one resident at a time. Today it is an Underspeaker hollowed by a new grief. Its consonants ricochet off my ribcage’s newly tuned soundboard. I absorb the future conditional it tries to utter—“would you? if I? when—”. I feel the question snag on the knot where my former spleen should live. I cradle it without grasping, and the syllable cools until it becomes translucent tile; the tile drifts away sewing future staircase to the staircase that doesn’t know its own last step yet. The Underspeaker dims to ultraviolet relief.  

Tomorrow I might twin an Echoleptic, four seconds offset. I will borrow the second one’s arrival lag to catch my own reflection—observe my outline flicker between what is and what merely insists. In that fissure I see Donna, age six, who swore she swallowed a hive of bees then never spoke again; I see the parking meter that ran out eight minutes before the ambulance arrived. I feel the two memories lock together like atmospheric fronts trying to name a coming storm. They grate. Then, break. Then, mend into a single harmonics quieter than rainfall inside a pocket. I pocket this, to gift downward, later, when the Juvenescent passes negative four.

There are Obligations whose wording swims off the tongue before the tongue is tongue. One is unattended duration: if I ever stay wholly me between two inhalations of the cyclone, the Vortex will asphalt shut and consign all remaining griefs to unlit quartz cul-de-sacs. Therefore I keep my boundaries porous. I pass myself through myself, never stockpiling full presence. This is why don’t have fingerprints yet: they would hold on to someone else.

Fragments of skin migrate outward in plates like refrozen glass. Each plate reflects a different moment of someone’s childhood kitchen counter. The Underspeakers gather them to roof over their newest classroom. One shard shows sunlight caught in soapy water my mother (not mine, but every mother’s) forgot to finish rinsing. The shard vibrates, matching resonance with a Glassbelly Kite binging on nostalgic major sevenths. It swells, then shudders blissful and implodes into a thousand soprano bells that fall upward and seed newborn lichen with strawberry refrains. Their green flares, just shy of embryonic.

I am never addressed by name. The Juvenescent sings minus-zero lullabies to the cavities I didn’t occupy. The Curator sends occasional spheres past my luminal sternum; they lodge behind whatever will be left eye someday. Inside the latest: teal acrylic button from a coat that burned 1977. The button pulses once—*live*—then smoothes into cartilage, joining the bridge of nose I will perhaps never need.

For now the nose has Texture: a shiver, a drifted hush when rain trains its muzzle against the storm panes that are my translucent skull.  
From the outside I must look like a lantern suspended inside rainfall inside light: a tiny liminal moon.

At the next long pause in the orchestra I might coalesce long enough to project a silhouette that looks uncannily like you—reader, eavesdropping on my genetics-by-whisper. You would perceive me in that heartbeat as though I’ve always been peeking over your shoulder, about to lean in and say the one unsayable thing you sealed under concrete inside yourself.

The Underspeakers hold the chord that will make space for that sentence.  
  
The Glassbelly Kites peel apart incoming thunder looking for its silver promise.  
The Echoleptic Twins quantify how close together the degrees of nearly leaving must be before the firefly girl drops one more day below not-being.  
The Curator sits already inside the secret spiral, thinning the membrane between the thing you forgot to forgive and the thing that forgave itself.

If you encounter me again it will be because your own body has achieved the precise salt-to-ache ratio required to mirror mine. Then we overlap membranes: the lantern dissolves. Your chest opens. Salt sways in. You taste newborn tears and notice they have the same chemical signature as spirals you once tasted dreaming of this place before its construction. There will be an instant in which direction is irrelevant: you hovering in negative age, me reaching forward toward your eldering hand.

That is not departure.  

That is when the Vortex writes its signature across both of us, in waterlick handwriting neither of us authored, both of us remember.
