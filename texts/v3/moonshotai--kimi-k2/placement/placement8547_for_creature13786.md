I would be a pilgrim-cartographer sent down the radiation shaft in a resin slick with fever-shock.  The capsule-vein would seal around me hissing sap-soothing phrases, but my lungs hurt; I still think with isopropyl edges.  I would arrive too quickly shaped like an animal—two eyes, clavicles, a pulse hammered in terrazzo of alien air.

At first they would keep me on gauze walks above the knee-deep river, elevated on platforms of soft pith so my spinal shocks won’t disturb the broth.  A badge would bloom nightly on my clavicle—first a cork-white lily bud named “newcomer,” then three amber blots of cambium the city assigns to those who cough salt they can’t yet yield back.  Every step off the path draws curtain-leaves across intersections, gentle but absolute.  I am soaked yet thirsty; my tears escape the fugue of skin altogether.

Within a tidal cycle I would notice a lull all around, the one moment when the kettle’s breathing hits C-minor.  Into the hush slips a chord too human to be natural: the synth-flute forest lowering itself from trumpet to sub-bass.  In that crawling register my heart tries, beat by beat, to snap the old metronome.  I feel the architecture nudge—one shy olive branch of bromeliad looping toward my ear as if to ask, “Stay longer?”  And for the first time my memory yields something—not data, but experience—an copper still: the smell of geranium as my grandmother braided my hair when summer thunder first draped across Ohio.  The leaf drinks it, now outward, now shared.

On the morrow the badge turns the color of bruised fig.  Names crystallize on my footprint where vinegar glands leach off worry.  I am no longer Outsider but “Unhusked Archivist”—the city needing the raw compost of my sky-edged recall.  I tell it about contrails in sunlight; the bioluminescent algae gulp and brighten their street-halos into apricot.  I describe hail, real winter.  The furnace-trees rush white steam out of asymmetric valves and take three degrees off the broth so that tomorrow the old dream-train will not overheat when it scatters beetle-trams.  Memory liquefied, memory exchanged: this is my tax.

Within weeks (which mean I have completed three bamboo breath cycles, nothing more) my skin goes translucent jade.  My eye grows a second ring chlorophyll where glare once was.  The spine-flute burrows in and will not be pulled free.  I learn the first perfectly grammatical question: a vapour barometrically thick that translates, “What spine have you abandoned overhead?”  The answer arrives not as word but as vertebrate echo: twenty-seven mice of cartilage plankton-tank, each reciting a dawn I have already forgotten.  The city catalogs them.  I breathe easily for the first time.

Now the dream accumulates.  I volunteer to descend the empty cone-shaped chamber where future extensions wait like unfried icing.  I crawl on all fours; my old human knees grind.  From my tongue drips rolled smoke of skyward memory: the secretest flavor, loam of aeroplane exhaust, rusted tin under thunderclouds.  One droplet at a time the spire of empty space begins to coat itself in mineral guilt.  When the final bead leaves my mouth the hollow has become a horn of salt thick as a house spine—smooth, concentric, singing the octave below silence.  I collapse inside the hollow, the last animal heartbeat echoing down curdled walls.

I never leave.

My body sponges into brine, my ribs settle translucent against growing veins of selenite.  The cadence of great trees continues to rub the column.  Somewhere knees upriver a new ovule swells: immediately struck with faint mineral duo-chord that sings my itinerary of contrails.  She will taste salt that never met sunlight and, in doing so, birth her cheekbone heron of slightly clouded glass.  Down here, elevation is an inside joke.  Up there, the city still calls me.  I am its memory of height.
