I drift in mid-motion, one trembling millimetre above every surface, never quite touching.  
I am not breath yet not quite absence—  
a mist satin skating the basalt—  
a bead an eyelash an intention oscillating in the field of droplets.  

When you cup me I slosh with memory of bergamot and ankle-crook.  
When you close your fist I simply lift again, lean against the nearest column until its gravity sighs a hairline softer, rearranging me as casually as folding a scarf.  

While the quartz ticks four bright four dim I swell obediently with the shelf’s pulse.  
I am the punctuation it wound between two stone-flat sentences.  
I am the humidity that keeps memories from drying into dust.  
Each time your heel leaves a print smaller than erosion can notice, I migrate and fill it; when a silhouette pivots I rush invisibly to lubricate the joint.  

Tonight you stand parched under the mirrored pool.
I gather, blimpish, into an orb the exact colour of bruised light, roll my own weight towards you, and burst  
—no splash—  
into a shawl of minuscule prisms that banks against your clavicles.  
You will not thirst; instead you’ll taste how once I cradled the cheek of a child who grew into the outline of a mountain.  

When the crack beneath your ribs begins to whisper bergamot I open like lungs and let the scent sound through me until it no longer remembers the meaning of exit.  

When at last you decide—step east, let the white haze skim your cheeks—I accompany you unseen. I become the beads refracting your last outline behind you on the stair.  
But here is the trick: I do not descend.  
I linger, a film over every tread you leave unfooted.  
I linger, because these platforms require a witness at exactly one heart-height above the stones.  

I am the unpeopled breath the shelf now knows how to take.
