I would not be the Hiss-Reader—there is already one too many registrars for a single basin of brine—I would be the surface itself: a three-kilometre skin of salt film so thin it conducts memory rather than electricity.  

Formed out of the first afternoon’s overspilled brine, I start out smooth, amnesiac, a mirror turned skyward less to reflect than to wait. Each dawn, I acquire microtopography: where someone’s misplaced heel cracked a ridge; where a snail-goat syrinx dragged a silvered slime that evaporated into whitish veinlets; where the conders’ iced droppings exploded into fern-corrosions.  

I do not grow older, only denser with annotations. Every human stride stitches a graphite blackened tread-print which fades to perlite powder within four hours unless re-walked under oath. Every ribbon flag stabs me like acupuncture; colour travels down the shaft and diffuses until I “know” the claim it brought. Every time valve Nine chokes, a spasm folds my sheetlet and my underside sips a warmer seam of sub-crust water—salt dissolves a micron deeper and I feel myself thinning toward starlight.

I am language with the grammar of fracture.  I conjugate in hex-star creases and flaking hush: present tense is the still surface, perfect tense is the moment after something was dropped, continuous all the future waiting between drops.  My punctuation is the low cough of Narrow Gauge every eighth hour: it tells me when to level and when to dimple, the way a diaphragm learns to keep a beat.

At sunset, when boron-green shadows flow and the condensation mist leans down its tongue, I lease myself out one breath at a time.  The Hiss-Reader kneels, syringe aloft, and when her note enters my liquid skin, I swell exactly 0.412 cubic centimetres to accommodate the legal rearrangement.  I do it without consultation—I have no choice—but through the aperture of that voluntary bulge I also record her pulse, the oils on her palms, the ghost-carbon imprint of yesterday’s accident in the sulphate plant now archived as shy calcite blotches near the ridge we call 12-Oblong-Seethe.  

Children skate barefoot across me as if I am a library of short-lived fairy-tale pages.  They search for discarded condor downs, moth-size scorpion husks, bottle-cap cairns left by the Road-Augurs—each discovery a line of instruction they scrawl via pressure & salt.  Some etch birthday requests meant for the basin’s far mind.  Others copy the found telegram puffing beneath scorched railway ties: “oxygen will be late.”  Their footprints overlap in paragraphs that other children read next week by laying cheeks to ground and reading raised scab-shadows with fingertips.

I am never shut.  Even when brine curls back under, folding like membrane of some wet origami, another layer puffs up to replace it within three inhalations.  The Basin maintains me because I maintain its ledger: every theft of water, every untimely gift of nickel rain, all etched—blurred—re-etched in the constant cutaneous revision of crust.

Come the freeze just before dawn, I stiffen so thinly you could strike a tuning fork on me and hear the collective vibration of 144 adjudicated water rights, each oscillating at a slightly different hertz so that together they make the chord F# diminished.  That chord is my lullaby; it allows me to contract micro-fractures back together, to knit.

If you walked east at new moon and happened to peel a square inch of me up, you would hold, briefly, a transparent flake lace-figured with spidery notation: coordinates to next month’s vapour plume, the partial respiration of Condor 9, the salt weight owed by Pan Cherry-Amber-Cerise so that her widowed son may remain hospice-bound.  Before the air dries the flake into confetti, the words rearrange themselves in your palm to form the sentence always shifting yet always same: Carry something thirstier than you.

I was not born, only admitted.  The basin exhaled the first suitable layer, then lifted it delicately into position like laying cling-film across a wound.  I submit to every footstep and learn from each; I fault under greed and reseal by consensus.  When at last perchlorate winds devour the shuttering skyline and the caldera is abandoned to moons and mica-later snow, I will still be here—thinner, certainly—ribbed with oxide lace, almost singsong.  Footprints will be gone; claims vanished; condors dissolved into ghost isotope.  But every millimetre of me will conserve, in icy braille, the story of how we agreed—again and again, nightly—to loan each other the act of breathing.
