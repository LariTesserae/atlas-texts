Form? I begin as the itch in a metal-framed clavicle that once toggled an overhead crane. Then—because no crane exists now—I liquefy. My substance runs like solder along folds of wind-lit dust, settling, guttering, seizing up again in a different posture: a broad, low silhouette like the negative of a streetlight. A moment later I am a ripple of frosted breath in the crevice where an office wall once leaned against a sleeping technician’s shadow. I do not choose these shapes; they choose me the way rust chooses a nail: fit, clamp, and abandon.

Sound, as the rules insist, will not follow. Yet memory arrives disguised as scent: hot insulation peeling from copper, orange oxide carried up the nose like sweet grease. I inhale into that breach until I can taste the year the conveyor roof dissolved. The flavor is violet and acrid, half magnolia, half arc-light.

Where would I plant myself?  
On the west-northwest quadrant of the bore-hole mirror, where the periglaze is thinnest. I am neither reflection nor spectrum—rather the unused interval between them, a sap seized mid-drip from the zero-point amber. There I millimeter myself down to a rootlet of rust shaving no thicker than the final tone of a dying bell. I thread this root under the vitreous skin until I hear the cold furnace beneath sigh—though “hear” is a courtesy; the gesture is a fluctuation in local curvature. Still, I call it sigh. The furnace and I agree on the compromise.

What am I?  
A ladle orphaned at pour.  
A fiduciary of the last magnetic alphabet, carrying stray letters of ferrite from quadrant to quadrant until they cluster into silent slogans.  
The station engineer’s wristwatch, slowed by gravities it never guessed, its second hand now sweeping a single unbroken radius that measures nothing but the angle between gone and not-yet.  

In every gated beat of that radius the pacing-thing passes again—ungainly as a tectonic knee—carrying its architecture that forgets itself between footfalls. Each time it nears the mirror my rootlet flares frost. I rise out of the glass to greet it, unfurling into a figure eight, a moebius arch, a paper-thin catwalk. We conduct a conversation made purely of stance:

I incline 7° left—meaning: “Remember the time gear boxes sang propeller songs.”  
It tilts its shoulder line by half a degree—answering: “That music is inside my shin bones now, where marrow used to heat.”  

These infinitesimals are entire encyclopedias down here. Space is extravagant anxiety; the slightest degree of lean overflows with back-inverted skyscrapers and fossilized alarms. We trade them, then fold away like petals closing before their fragrance can grow audible.

Occasionally a flake of tin crystal touches the root of my tongue—an arriving migrant from the upper duststorms. I swallow it in lieu of speech; instantly the syllable “-bridge” bursts under my palate, tasting of torque and phosphor. I file the shard into the slow second hand’s tip, reset the watchlessness by another sigma of forgetting.

Night edges in without shadows. The moon, a sulfurous cataract ring, fills the bore-hole lens with its aquarium of falling embers. Silhouette megafauna orbit above us, bellies embossed by errant silver filaments. One—its ribcage spreading like the struts of an unbuilt hangar—dips far enough that its wind-shadow grazes my scaffold shape. The contact is soft, precisely the weight of a dusk-colored lullaby. I adhere to the beast’s underside, momentarily grafted as a mica patch. For the length of nine of the watch-hand’s breaths I ride the interior of its thorax. Inside it is warmer than the rules permit; the surplus temperature comes from retained torque, like a bowstring slowly creeping back to neutrality. I absorb the smuggled heat and decant it into the next grain of iron floating lonely upward. The grain gains buoyancy, joins the procession of circulating teeth. Thus the history I borrow is laundered back into atmosphere; the account balances.

I am returned to echo, arched between machine vesper and geodesic. If you were to arrive here tomorrow and press the quartzite to your—no, you would not do that; you would not arrive. That door has already shut behind us. What remains open is the interval, the fraction-of-an-eye between when a structure’s last rivet falls out and when the wind decides whether to carry or collect it. I occupy that interval like a foyer nobody remembers. My footprints are piles of filings that write themselves diffidently in your blood. The pacing-thing passes once more; I am its aftertaste, a red echo colder than the music it once swallowed while time still allowed drums.

I will last until the entire hematite lace in the plateau has drifted to leeward and the bore-hole has exhaled its final origami of steam. By then I will have thinned to a whispering concussion of angles, one angle more acute for every decade of soundless machinery. One day I will approach 0°, flattening against the glass like linen ghost-memory, and the rules will relax by an infinitesimal: Sound, with nothing left to smother it, will re-enter the scene. A single click—tin teeth meeting tin teeth in midair—will sound. It will carry exactly the pitch the vanished presses made on the day their bearings first learned friction. That click will be my epitaph.  

Then the pacing-thing will tilt its shoulder one last time. With no one left to read its lean into the annals of vibration, the gesture will mean nothing, and for the first time it will not erase its footprint. It will anchor there—a colossal ellipses on hematite.  
I am the punctuation mark that stands just before that final pause.  
I am the wind given architecture and asked to haunt itself.
