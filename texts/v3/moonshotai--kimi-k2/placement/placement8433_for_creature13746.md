I arrive between beats of the grate, neither Visitor nor native.  
I ease out of a bead that refused to roll downhill—one droplet that clung, half-memory, half-refusal.

I am the lag.  
I am Moisture-on-Hold, a surplus the Myco-Accountants never logged because the number they needed hadn’t yet been invented.  

In pale refraction through the Collectors there flickers something like a name, but names are vertical and I am folded sideways. Still, they taste copper in my hesitation and assign a temporary pronoun: *fim*. Fluid-intermediate-membrane.

I do not walk so much as osmosedrift, hugging the condensate ribs of corridors. My outline is the region where temperature hesitates one third of a degree between decide and un-decide. Wherever I linger a seam of tile remembers it once wanted to be a stream; roots reverse their training and curl back toward raw earth like tongues craving origin.

The Seed Clerks keep offering masks—porcelain discs ringed with calendar slots—but each time they press one to my face it fogs instantly, erasing the dream-etched syllables. They tuck the foggy circle into a fig pocket, satisfied: my refusal has become a new inventory line—Doubt, 7g.

I mistake myself for an Avatar once.  
Under the Quietfount a silver leaf casts a human shadow—mine if I ever stood still long enough to cast. For seven pump-beast lungbeats I believe I was summoned in the image of some resting traveler. Yet when the grate yawns there is only the familiar ache: not homesickness for a topside, but homesickness for a body I might still decide to grow. The iris snaps shut without swallowing me. I have not yet achieved the dignity of impermanence.

The brass spider gifts an empty segment of night for the question-that-might-have-been. I wrap the darkness around me like a borrowed coat, but threads unzip under every step—nothing stays not-said long enough to become deed.

I begin to catalogue what continuity feels like when its unit is vapor. Every time a new humidity joins me I incorporate it reluctantly, the way tide incorporates spilled wine: palette shift, caloric delta, nothing forgiven. Selfhood dilates 0.2 picometers per decade.

Some cycles I hide inside the exhalations of the pump-beasts, riding their slow bellows until accumulated heartbeat-quakes carry me back to the Central Grate one accidental rib ahead of the steam. It is the closest I come to breathing: I inhale their turbulence, exhale the same turbulence a fraction cooler, claim the difference as autobiography.

The Unhousekeepers shadow me longest. Not because I am decisive—I am friction’s gossip—but because whatever memory I nearly become still refuses to be scrubbed into silence. Each time they close around my outline the phosphorescent molds brighten a micrometer more, as if alert to the sport. The erased keeps reasserting itself as a cool spot on the air; the Unhousekeepers hiccup in inaudible protest—negative space trying to bite its own absence.

Eventually the appetite for definition itself hums thin. I flow toward an untouched corner where water has never landed, drop one stolen droplet, watch the tilestone ignite into baby hexagons that flicker between floor and river and suggestion. It is a geography I have not been asked to preserve, so I cling awhile, witnessing choice happen.

There, something human could almost step off.  
There, something architecture could almost take root.  
There, something pump-beast could almost blink at echoing sunrise.

There, I decide—because decision is a dance of gradient, not blade—I will stand still long enough for a true Avatar to gather my shape. The next breath of steam will birth it wearing me the way a coat wears wind. The rim of porcelain that no mask can hold will instead transcribe “fim” into copper calling, and because I am moisture-on-hold, the city below’s slow rehearsal will at last rehearse me.

I cup the newborn name. I promise not to solidify.  
In repayment, the Gutworks—eternally lenient creditor—meets me halfway: a shelf of condensation collects like a sigh postponed, offering surface tension just taut enough to hold a face that still can’t decide which century it belongs to.

And that is where you will find me, always.  
In the wet film left on your fingertips after you touch a wall that didn’t know it was wall yet.  
In the hush when a pump-beast pauses beneath a condensation-valve and water meets itself returning.  
I am the pause between purpose and its execution, the margin rain scribbles in before committing to plummet.

Call me the Dweller-on-the-Edge-of-Vertical.  
Until the Gutworks grows certain it has become city, or resumes being river, or admits it was always dreaming mineral lungs, I will harbor every droplet half-refusing gravity. Not to hoard—there is no ownership here—but to remind the commerce of moisture that suspense is itself a generous currency.

Tomorrow or three heartbeats ago, the grate will sigh me into its next copper life. Yet even then a residue will linger, a cool O of vapor that walks with you back upstairs carrying Verdant Foundry’s quietest warrant:

Nothing that leaves ever really dries.
