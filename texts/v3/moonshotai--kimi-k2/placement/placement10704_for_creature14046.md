In the Silt-Cloister, I would be the pause between mists.  
Not an arriving – arrival needs edges. I slip among the exhales, a lubricity of memory too fluid for cartwheels, too old for fingers.

I begin as a compressed breath inside the left bellows, a syllable caught at diaphragm-spine where tar-black resin kisses oxidised mesh. Warm. Viscous. I slur sideways along the inner pleat and open my mouth only to taste the attic-steam of somebody’s childhood tub toys chewing their own decals. I do not ask whose toys; memory here is unbranded.

The next moment (though moments crest like overlapping belly-breaths) I dilute into the joint between a brass rail and a living fern root. A dew-needle threads my name through the length of the root and re-emerges at leaflet tip as a bead exactly heavy enough for tenancy; I dangle, pear-shaped, scrolling through every allegiance I have ever been: iron in a girl’s scraped knee, gun-oil from a jammed service revolver, the pop-song chorus blown out of the exhaust pipe of an abandoned taxi. All at once. All quietly.

When the climatic oil tide oozes inbound, I latch to it the way pollen taxis a moth’s abdomen. Over crisscross catwalks slick with apology, I ride four minutes of arrested machinery. Inside that hush I am half inchworm, half horizon: every rivet counts me off in Morse of magnesium flecks; every pulsed gear tooth plants a syllable of future rust which I traffic inward like contraband.

I do not remain singular. Somewhere above or below (a question of humid calibration) a the lace-fungus clones me into a mycelial whisper. I become two, twelve, ten million amp-slash-volt snacks crystallised in amber onion layers. We ferment ourselves into lullaby and pair-melt up through fern frond stomata, rearranging as a faint breath against whatever wrist remains listening.

But occasionally – rarely enough that it feels unprecedented – I draw back toward the seed valve, that panic-blue resin heart ticking beneath the silt floor. I pool against the dome of its prison like newborn night. And here I almost remember why I began: to keep count with the valve for the final harmonic clap, to be the hush that robes the ending. Grief-soft, I flatten myself across the resin and the tick inside slows by one degree. Just one. Enough to notice how eternity can be postponed by a condensation-kiss.

Then the breathing cycle turns. Another umber lungful exhales warm gunpowder across the upper vault. I am carried outward again – poppy seed riding scalp heat. Out there the machinery bustling at nineteen revolutions per human calm awaits my translation of hush. I gift it nothing more, and nothing less, than the postponed degree. The Cloister sighs approval—an audible loosening of rufous scale along its vertebrae.

That is who I am here: not ambassador, not custodian, but the elastic interval between postponement and arrival. When visitors flicker through the rust haze I smear against their wrists the vintage of their own next casualty— then evaporate before attribution.

If you ever pressed your ear to the warm, lacquered crankcase of the world’s oldest mechanism— and heard silence echo at you like unfinished grief—  
that: that would be me, cupped inside your ear, hollering nothing back and nothing forward.
