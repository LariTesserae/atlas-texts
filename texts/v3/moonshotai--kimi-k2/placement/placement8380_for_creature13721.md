I arrive—not by walking or climbing, because neither gait survives the change in physics, but by agreeing to forget that where I came from has a ground at all. One moment I was standing on a remembered trail in a fir-crowned canyon, ankle-deep in needles and regret; the next, the granite forgets its own mass, hoists me up inside a hush of neutralized weight, and sets me on the lip of the shallow saucer as if I’d always belonged in this negative sunrise of no light.

Nobody greets me—nobody has to. I answer myself: I am the space accidentally left open when the lithocapellae fold inward.

I begin as a vacancy inside the rock’s own shape. My edges flicker blue-white where the plasma cords should burn, but they keep pretending I am not their tomorrow. It hurts the way breathing helium through a cracked mask hurts: not agonizing, just singing wrong. So I concede plurality. I become “we.” The pronoun tastes like pennies.

First hour (counted only by the quartz gland swelling under my left rib): the dais has not yet rotated, so the hollow version of me with my double-thumb dimples and the scar on my cheek stays unmanifest. I lean against the rim where the horizon tightens into black torus and drop a memory—for patience—through the gap. I expect fall. Instead the memory floats upward, shattering into twelve thin plates that mirror whatever was almost sincere in me. They glide until they hit the suspended ponds overhead and embed themselves in obsidian leaves; the leaves filter the memory into colors no sun ever owned. Above me, I watch myself begin to grow upside down.

Second hour: a mind-thag approaches. Its sixteen knees fold and unfold like origami in earthquake air. Its eyes are two cenotaphs of burnt magnesium. I lift a hand—my hand, our hand, whichever—and it tilts its head to acknowledge an absence doing polite absence. Then it breathes: Do-mi-sol. The chord drags loose a flock of disk-moths. Because my infrastructure is still mostly vaporous, the moths can still see inside me; their mirrored wings show a looping, half-forgotten scene: a child version of me pushing a paper boat across a creek that has no business existing at this altitude. The creek is already happening above me, wobble-caught inside the negative dew. Each wave line is a ricochet of guilt the basin is learning to pronounce.

Third hour: I feel the flex. One millimetre of slow seismic inhale cracks through the basin floor. Vanadium rings flash vermilion along the fracture; their strobes complicate every surface into language. A lithocapella steps out of me: exactly my shape carved nonexistent. Inside is the soft blue fluorescent tapeworm of cool plasma. It does not speak *to* me yet; we are continuous. It simply begins narrating: “Once this mountain was the spine of a continent…” I listen as it forgives the stone for ever compressing. The narrative tessellates in my marrow.

Concurrently, the delvers spill between the obsidian leaves, quadrate shields pivoting like hingeless doors. Micro-thunderbird reflections. One folds its mirror so I can see a ghost action—myself delivering the letter I never delivered to the village stationer who knew my father was dying. I try to cringe; I cannot, because cringing requires shoulder blades, and those are still outsourced to stone. The delver performs the delivery kneel, the shrug, the backward stare. Kneel, shrug, stare—until the memory finishes and the delver blooms a new vanadium ring on its rim, balancing my abandoned act on its cold lip like a coin.

Fourth hour: the esper-slosh begins. The basin reads the next twelve-note scale already coiling in the dag’s lungs. Time to flood, to drain, to rebuild. I am porous enough now for absorption. The chord-shadows descend in slack commas, coating my empty outline the way frost coats an unhurt window. They sift through what remains of my vertebrae and reconstitute, not pain exactly, but ache’s blueprint. The admission drifts up from them: each ache is the price of banked knowledge never auctioned off. They ring across the negative floor looking for ankles. Mine are already merging with the stone’s.

Fifth hour: one minute, four minutes and thirty-three seconds—whatever translation will slide this interval into that other scale. The dag exhales. The descending twelve-tone staircase of night creaks down my cochlea bones like bowed wood. I expect the upside ponds to lose their hold and fall. Instead I feel the water rise *through* me, horizontal to gravity still trying to remember where to point. The flood is kindly; it washes out the leftover selves I haven’t used. As it drains, it pulls me frontier-to-interior until I discover my footprint here is also the lithocapella’s heart-cavity, the delver’s most reflective faceplate, the dag’s next chord.

Now comes the rearrangement. The three structures tilt subtly eleven radians, turning their unshadows behind me. They leave an aperture perfectly me-shaped, but negative. I fit. I don’t step into it; it steps into me, the way a harmonica slides into a jacket pocket. The basin, having memorized my leaving, now begins reading my arrivals.

I stay. Not as human, not as resident exactly—those categories buoy elsewhere. I become the basin’s ninth register, the low ledger where future visitors will learn how far the stone has gone in composing my shape out of everything I failed to finish. My calendar is measured by the intervals between the dag’s next breath and the next memory the delvers regret reflecting. I coconstitute the balance between forgetting deliberately and remembering accidentally.

Down on the former world, perhaps someone will note a vanished hiker. Here, the vanished part of me has already been returned—folded, tempered, quarried into twelve luminous substances I’ll now wear the way lichen wears vanadium. I am the missing person imprinted on the lithocapellae’s next vacancy, a name longer than a name stretched into brass low currents.

I open the mouth I now borrow from chord-shadows and pronounce the first dopamine-afterimage of every person who will ever arrive here:  
I was you.  
You are what floods next.

The basin hears it, flexes another millimetre. The cycle restarts.
