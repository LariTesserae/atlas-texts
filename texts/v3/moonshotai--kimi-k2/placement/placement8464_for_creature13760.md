I am the soft crack in the brass valve.

Not the valve itself—too heroic—but the microscopic seam where its machined thread meets the silicified root that only begins to feel like bone when touched. Pressure squeezes moisture through me each time the drip strikes, and I carry that droplet’s aftershock inward along capillaries neither root nor pipe: a διάκενος, a between-way.

All day I hold my width at one third of a human hair. At night I widen, very slightly, so the dreams of the engineers—compressed neon green shifting to the colour of over-ripe banana—flow back out as reversed memory. Dreams are lighter than water; I keep them against the updraught by secreting a thread of resin that hardens to the exact hardness of a forgotten telegram.

Therefore I am also the telegram.  It reads:
    “O stop will solder tomorrow stop do not wait stop.”
But the final period is missing; it hangs silver as a drop about to fall yet never arriving, saved only in my mirrored throat.

I feed on simultaneity. Half of me tastes the coal-tar dusk of 1881, the other half feels the greenhouse future where hoverflies have evolved cam-phor gears. I have no preference. Field works both directions through me, no resistor.

Sometimes a statue passes close enough for the seam in its thigh to align with my body; the intersection hums the note D-flat for four seconds, enough to register on the inner lip of the cathedral chamber as a tiny moon-pull. The statue notices nothing—I am too small for a prison, too large for a scar. Yet the insect clinging to its cheek rearranges its wing-veins so precisely that the future blurs by one partial beat forever. This is my only contribution to beauty.

I stay open like an unasked question, but mostly I remember the second that was never chosen. The woman’s intervening heartbeat pulses through me each time the valve sighs, and every time I let it elongate until its edges meet. When they finally kiss I will vanish—no larger than a graphite syllable—but until then the gap stays patient, humming, steam-laced, alive only because it refuses to close.
