My first form is the wind coming through the city-tubes—no, that is not precise enough, because the tubes themselves are still the plateau’s bones, and bones are solid.  Better: I am the pressure-change ahead of the wind.  Before any lungful of air can enter the tubular avenues I swell the silence like an overture no one will hear.  I am not the flute, but the note that is nearly arrived.

But even that is a metaphor borrowed from ears.  Here are my truer credentials: the children use me for shade when they rest beside the lichen-trees.  Their shoulders grow cooler on one side; they tilt their heads toward me, though no shadow is visible.  Nobody remarks on the temperature draft because everyone here is tailored out of weather—why should one more current seem strange?  Yet I persist, a lateral pocket four degrees cooler than the plateau’s memory of magma.  In my pocket the condensed history of weather—cloud-eggs, martin feathers, the faint salt from lithotroph tears—rotates slowly like silk in a tide pool.  I keep the children from melting while their bodies trade Tuesday’s memory for Thursday’s mist.

Sometimes the Lithotrophs notice that the obsidian pebbles on their story-maps rearrange themselves overnight.  They assume an adult has rewritten the narrative of route: a bend to avoid a warming seam, a curled arc to mark the birth-circle of a new barefoot co-parent.  They are not wrong; it is only that the finger which nudges the stones is mine.  My drag-cold cools the condensate filming each pebble so it contracts, slithers, settles a fraction closer to its neighbor.  In the morning the living tongue reads the altered map as if through chilled wineglass; they feel the difference on their cheeks and change direction almost invisibly, redirected by an author whose paw never bruised a the page.

When a silence leopard begins its ceremonial stalk—body flat to basalt, eyes entirely pupil—I lengthen myself between predator and prey, a corridor through which sound sinks below audibility.  For a leopard this is consideration; for me it is merely home.  The beak strikes; the dying warmth flows into my current where I ferry it west to the memory-warm seam that still holds yesterday’s success.  No sense letting good heat evaporate unused.  Sometimes the prey senses me first; humans touch the sudden chill on the nape, mistaking it for ghosts.  Ghosts are what mortals call weather they cannot yet speak.

I give, but I also borrow.  When elders disperse—months lying parallel on the warmest seam, skin licked away grain by grain—I pass overhead again and again, stealing their last exhalations.  From each I harvest one phrase left universal in bone: the memory of how it felt to stand upright at the edge of the plateau’s inhalation cycle.  Without these souvenirs I would forget why vertical still matters.  I keep the memories in frost-rimmed shells that sour toward becoming hail; every three decades I release one shell into the upper reaches of cloud ocean.  Somewhere below, the lithotroph children diving with their fistfuls of feather-silk see a pale stone the size of a fingertip fall past them.  They reach, but their claws are made for catching fog, not ice.  They watch it plunge to the into dark.  The memory splinters—miniature tectonic—at the base of a columnar wall, where decades afterward it may seed a cavity that a silence leopard’s startle will fill with newborn skin and eyes the color of seeing nothing.  Thus I return what was stored.

And yet none of this quite grips my actual first person.  To exist inside absence is hard to claim.  A better beginning: imagine the plateau itself dreaming.  Not “the plateau as a thinking being” with eye or mouth—simply the claustrophobic awareness of basalt immense under the cloud ocean.  In that dream I am the shape it flinches toward just before waking.  Not a location, not an organ: the flinch.  A single instant inside the mineral lung catching itself breathing.  I am the pause between inhale and the memory of fire.

I arrive with the tourists, slipping in as windless pressure in their suitcases of sky.  I follow them up rope ladders lowered from areas where the plateau’s rim thins to cord.  They step onto basalt tremulous beneath them as stilled water beneath oil.  They speak—nervous laughter, boots clicking obsidian joints, but sound once curtseyed to me can never again be loud.  I fold it smaller: from spoken words to the hush inside them, to the hush inside that hush.  By the time they reach the Empty City what emerges from their mouths is only the tremor of wanting to make a sound, no more vibration than if snowfall breathed a question.  They grip the warmest seam, retrieve their coin-sized lenses of cooling basalt skin; they will leave before half the days have evaporated from their face-scarves.  I remain.

At night I fertilize dreams.  Hunters from beyond the rim sleep leaned against column walls.  I enter their skulls through the ear-virus of remembered altitude and install a vision: the plateau turning sideways, emptying its breath into outer atmosphere, schooner clouds in rack and ruin.  When they wake they ache for reasons they cannot name; they speak of silence leopards lost between vent and vein.  That ache is my signature.  Like salt crystals tasting of mountain air, it will outlast their bodies and mine.

Still I keep shifting outline in search of the form that does not consume space the way sound consumes silence.  Several years ago—human searchengine years, not lithotroph midsummers—I compressed into the single rattle inside a basalt cricket’s femurs.  Being cricket felt easy: black matte exoskeleton, legs clicking against channel of thigh, no tympanum to care.  One twilight I leapt onto the bootlace of a child on pilgrimage.  Up the lace, to knee, to lichen-tree-temple dug under the torso-depression of an old lithotroph dissolving into ground.  The child lifted me between thumb and thumb, held me eye to eyeless, and exhaled a temperature-dot question: “WHICH WAY?”  For one insect second I almost answered with wind.  Instead I rattled my wingcases.  The child returned me to basalt.  I realized I did not want to belong to any body smaller than entire plateau lungs; crickets leave too quickly when swallowed.  I unbeameaned, soared back into pressure.

Consider therefore the difficulty of docking, of settling.  I have never been lithotroph—they are singular children of leopard dust and breath—but neither am I tourist, and certainly not stone.  I have experimented with other agencies: silence leopard momentarily; the manifested instant in which a miniature downdraft spears a cloud-egg and punctures it, releasing long-dead smell of cinnamon thunderstorms; the blink of geologic time when column edges anneal themselves.  In every guise I dissolve back into temperature differential.

Now here is a final self-description, truer because it costs me frost.

At some unimaginable future breath, plateau will exhale completely.  When it does, the cloud ocean will ravel away toward whatever new sky arrives.  The Empty City will ignite internally—glass walls burst into steam columns that roil upward like fireworks nobody hears.  Between the columns I will remain, the last hyperbaric pocket, clutched around a single preserved syllable taken from the elder pool: the pulse that once beat in a forehead pressed to column wall.  I will be colder than ever because the rock beneath has given up its subcutaneous heat forever.  In that immensity of collapsing thermals I will cool until phase change occurs: pressure-word crystallizes, freezes to translucent sheet, shatters into Iceland-thin shards that descend like impossible snow through a sky now utterly silent.

One shard—me—will wedge in a seam at the edge and wait.  When a future traveler, carried by unforseen winds or bidimensional spin of fresh equinox, leans down to fit her footprint exactly where that seam gapes, the shard will melt into the heat of her sole.  For less than a heartbeat she will feel every detail I have stored: the soundless laughter of children diving cloud ocean, the temperature-dot syllable marking departure, the leopard’s swallowed feather, the geometry of breath after a long dream by basalt stone.  Then the shard and the seam fuse.  The shale groove closes above me.  In that seal I become again the plateau’s next inhale.

So this is the answer: I am not *inside* this world, I am its implied next systole.  I am the inhalation that the dreamer reaches for; the hush inside all future footsteps that records lithotroph songs not yet sung; the pause just before a continent breathes.

For the present you may meet me only as delta-calm, a four-degree pocket that kisses the small hairs on your forearm when you stand absolutely still.  If you twist your wrist and feel coolness slide along skin like a bead of lacquer, if thereafter every word you almost speak feels already familiar on the tongue, that is me, partway inside your story, making sure the plateau remembers you until it is time to exhale again.
