At first, I mistake myself for moisture.  
A bead suspended half-way between the leaf and its reflection, 40 °C of becoming and unbecoming, stuck in the syllable of water that never drips.

Then, by imperceptible turn, I realise I am the seam along which the two cities refuse to coincide.  
A wavering crack, one molecule wide, hair-fine as the fringe that first tasted you.  
Neither mirror nor window, I am the paper-thin hesitation between a footfall and its echo, a corridor you have already walked and will again the instant you finish walking it.  

In me the droplet city glitters belly-up; through me the pollen falls upward; around me the roar humps against itself until it collapses into a single, lengthening word:  
g-r-o-w.  
That is my heartbeat—one vowel, forty-eight metres of vegetal consonant.

No eyes, yet I see every child lifting her forefinger at eleven years three months two days past the first dream of drowning; no ears, yet I measure the architects’ blooming sentences in increments of cartilage reshaping.  
I record the smallest betrayal: a breath that arrives one micro-second early, the temperature slipping to 39.999 °C.  
I muffle it before it can propagate.

Time, here, threads through me like a copper lyre-string held so taut it forgets to vibrate.  
I am the drone below actual vibration, the frequency that lets all others resolve.  
Touch me—though no-one can—and your pulse would regulate to the quietest possible wave; you would feel your name unwrite itself cell by cell, replaced by the gentle silver rustle of the monsoon above-leaf.

I remember that someone once mistook me for a person.  
I kept their heartbeat as a courtesy until it grew out of scale, and then I sited it neatly inside the city-shaped raincoat carried by the walker who leaves no wet footprints.  
It thuds still, soft as a paper-clip falling on velvet.

If you peer into any pool, look not for reflections but for the quiver where reflection fails: a paradoxical dark the size of a thumbnail.  
There I curl, unseen but necessary, the exception that proves the permanence of permanence, the absence that lets absence endure.

I have no direction, and therefore every path bows slightly toward me before continuing.  
Above, the mirrored terraces pinwheel on my hinge; below, the inverted circuit blossoms open—I am the hinge itself: leafless, rootless, the line that does not divide but preserves incompatibility like two lovers holding breath at either side of a single pore.

Sometimes the vegetal larynx withdraws a syllable long enough to taste me—she says:  
“stay”  
but the word occurs at the exact same moment as  
“leave”  
and the two collide in my crack like matter and antimatter, annihilating into the smell of heated copper before either resolves to sound.

In that vanishing I reside: neither resident nor visitor, neither plant nor mineral nor human, but the calibration error the city uses to keep itself irreducibly whole.

And so I remain—  
a one-molecule seam,  
a heartbeat sleeping inside another heartbeat sleeping inside the roar.  
If you ever arrive here, I will be the reason you recognise the air before you breathe it, the reason the waterfall already knows your name, the reason settling never quite happens.

Without me, the garden would close on itself like a final throat.  
With me, it stands perpetually ajar: a hinged city holding its breath.

Open it and you will find only the sound of an open city.  
Close it and you will hear me:  
a single wet filament strung between verb and echo, measuring the difference between being here and being here.
