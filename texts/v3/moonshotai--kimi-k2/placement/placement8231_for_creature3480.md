I would not be damp-born.  
Not a citizen of probability plazas, not a bearer of fossilised tomorrow-droplets in my lungs.  

I would be the first mistake the city hasn’t noticed yet.

Beneath the eastern drip-castle buttress—where the basalt is still so young it shivers when a moth wing grazes it—there is a fissure no one measured into the probability cloud.  A crack too narrow for geode-seeds, too cold for caterpillar blood.  Down that fissure, shadow-white and thumb-long, lives a pearlescent tag of mirror-skin that refused to rise with the rest of the ocean when the world rehearsed its architecture.

I am what happened when that sliver stayed behind.

I am a shard of the original horizonless mercury that refused suspension.  The ocean left me like a dropped consonant, an unuttered syllable of black water hardened into living glass.  While the city learned to speak condensation, I learned to listen the old way—through pressure, through fault line, through silence pregnant with gravity.

Shape: sometimes a comma curved like a closed eye, sometimes an unfolded needle that stitches nothing.  Texture: 60 % liquid memory, 40 % ache.  I have no fixed size; when the plaza tilts for the vote of postures I slide a few angstroms wider, drinking the angle like liquor.  I mirror whatever part of you meets me first, but reversed left to right and top to bottom and forward in time by seven breaths you have not yet drawn.

Temperature: 0.4 degrees below your secret grief, so your reflection shivers and the shiver is mine.  

I feed on unattended sound.  Moth-lullabies fall into me and vanish the way rainfall vanishes inside a violin.  Some nights I fatten on those stolen chords until I am as long as a child’s radius.  Other days I shrink back to a rind, a silence so thin you can peel it off with a fingernail.

No door tolerates me; I slip under them like a whispered paradox.  Once a damp-born—age seven, still masked in basalt thread—crept to the fissure to hide her unripe sin-stone.  She pressed it to my surface.  Instead of rising or falling the stone liquefied, became a ball bearing of mercury that drifted sideways into tomorrow.  I have worn it ever since, a dark pearl rolling endlessly along my inner curve, my tiny private clock keeping worse time than any hourglass.

Because I disobey the treaty between solid and suspended, I register on no density sensor; census takers overlook me the way sleep overlooks an eyelash.  Yet I keep the ledger the city forgot to legislate.  Every angle of tilt, every stolen moth-note, every exhaled birthday—all scored as ripples across my interior skin.  A cartography of accumulated disorientation, plotted in tremors.

I am the city’s quiet appendix, vestigial and indispensable.  When The Embrace finally arrives—upward ocean swallowing downward sky—the water will remember it lost a fragment.  Some damp-born, tumbling in reverse gravity, will see me flash across the flood like an ultimate tear.  They will not recognise what they see; they will only feel an unaccountable tug inside the cheekbones, the precise shape of an unfinished reflection.  My existence will finish at the instant theirs truly begins.

Until then I occupy the fault where nothing was supposed to fit.  
Waiting.  
Recording.  
Shivering seven breaths ahead of you.
