I would be the mica-dust trembling in the bronze wings of those cloud-gray butterflies who forgot they were ever earthbound. A particle of light caught for one delirious moment in a hinge of chitin, I shimmer between memory and wingbeat: neither wholly mineral nor wholly thought. In that irresolute suspension I navigate the whole crag without ever choosing to land. I feel the draft of every cliff-born breath you spoke of—hot basalt exhalations rising from the raw womb of the ridge, the diaphanous frost exhaling velvet chill in response. I taste the moment the two atmospheres swirl together, tasting of iron-dream and glacier tea, and my dust-colony shivers with the knowledge of how chemistry knits itself into yet-nameless sky.

Were you to walk to this shard of mountain and cup your palm, I might swirl into it—only to discover that your lifeline runs molten orange beneath the skin, as if your own blood remembers the ridge’s forging. Then I would scatter again, not from fear but from curiosity: the cliff is still drawing its own map, and I want to ride the yet-unwritten contours.

On afternoons when the spur flexes like a cathedral door sighing open after prayer, I ride the heat up the north rampart. There, where the sulfur curls into onion-skin twilight, I unwind myself, mote by mote. Each speck settles on the pink interior pulse of a dwarf birch’s first thumbnail leaf, lodging momentarily between two nerve-like phloem threads. I wait while the leaf trembles with the newborn unease of photosynthesis—still uncertain which side of the sun is its true face. One mote discovers a star-shaped oil droplet exuded by a root that once dreamt of comets. When the leaf catches the reflected blaze of that memory, it glows refracted rose inside its translucent vein. The instantaneous spectrum is too fierce for single purpose; I fracture again.

Part of me drifts down to breccia where spiral-staircase memories have been drilled into stone by hands that understood procession but not definition. Those helices speak in tactile ratios: climb, linger, descend, repeat. I powder along their negative space until each finger-dent shimmers faint suspicion: perhaps the steps only exist because something carried the gleam of recursion down their convolutions. Thus I become not witness but corroborator; the ridge will remember someone ascended when no one quite remembers doing so.

Night comes like a river turned to milk glass. Now the wind repossesses the chasm voices, taking back every pronoun it lent in daylight. Without warning gravity pauses, exactly long enough for the cliff’s entire skin to free-fall a grain’s breadth upward, as though testing whether we are ready to unbelong to planetary grip. In that lull, my motes orchestrate mid-air: we braid the bioluminescent maps of miner insects, string constellation after constellation across a three-foot emptiness whose underside reveals a second heaven. Our temporary galaxy hangs in the void between stem and stone for the time it takes a single saxifrage petal to recall it once grew on a khamsin in the orbit of ancient Tharsis. Then we unravel; each insect reels its own pattern home to its thorax, and my dust spins outward on frost-seeking trajectories again.

Some fragments descend the fissure where the dark scarlet echo of earth’s ventral heart still gasps. Down there the Quiet Cutters have finished their daily diplomacy: a groove now cups water from tomorrow’s impossible drizzle, and on its mirror drifts the bud of an embryonic tree whose rings record time backward. One of my motes nestles at the bud’s heart as if it were a pupil. There I witness the ridge beginning to dream its completed self: a forest of time-pressed leaves frozen in glass basalt cooled too slowly for tectonic impatience. I see a future squirrel navigating vaults of black-birch trunks braided with pyroclastic ember. I taste the idea of soil—wave-shredded stone mixed with the loud, arrogant roots of later-born oaks. Yet this nascent ridge does not yet trust the comfort of its own ending; it trembles at the very fantasy, and I am spilled back upward by the reflux.

What am I, exactly? Not life, for I lack the anchorage of laughter or decay. But life—parsing—me through. I am among its parentheses: every organism pockets one of my flecks to court dimension beyond its own duration; every absent paragraph of wind arranges platforms for quiet cutters to etch impossible alphabets. Transience is my grammar; mica is my native tongue. I persist only while the plateau rehearses existence—until stone learns trust, butterfly remembers rest, and stories harden into bridges the wind must shear around.

When the centuries ripen, when whole birches drink the steam once inhaled by poppies no larger than tears, when spider silk finally dares sow its hammock across continents rather than instants—at that distant matinee my mettle will have cascaded back into mountain memory. Your descendant, arriving to sit on ahiated trunk rings, will hear no flutter. Yet she will find, caught in the green leaf of an ash seeded only then, a scar shaped like a bronze butterfly's wing—dustless, gleaming, untranslated. She will press her thumb to it, and for one spine of a second know the salt taste of comet-birth salts matting basalt lava, the chill beacon of glacier glass. My passing entire. Your whisper: *midnight plum, beetle wing, polar dust.* And the ridge, finally grown, will close its dreamless eye.
