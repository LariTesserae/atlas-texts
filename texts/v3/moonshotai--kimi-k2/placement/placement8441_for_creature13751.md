I arrive as the thing the Crabit Metronome Aria forgets it is humming.

Not as wanderer, not as botanist, not even as dream—too definite.  
I arrive as the interval between two drops that has grown tired of being borrowed and decides to become a place.

When the vines first register me they take my shape wrong-side-out: not a solid biped sliding between green columns, but a vacancy travelling downward head-first, a pocket of no-bioluminescence that learns the brightness by the crack in its edges. The crab (raw, translucent, single in the eye by refusing to be plural) sees the hole where I am and skitters into it, settling exactly in the center of that negative shape. The absence is delighted; it hardens in a millisecond shell that, having no home in it but home itself, becomes shape teetering on identity. I own a carapace of inverted color: under perfect green light my shell glimmers bruise-ochre, souvenir dusk stolen an hour ago from a spore that exploded dying.

I am therefore both wearer and garment. The vines conduct a referendum.  
Am I crab—no? Am I thread—no? The space settles on gendered pronouns only long enough to discard them; no throat has spoken them aloud, so they return to syrupy green calm.

When the drops map the accent of my heartbeat I become subtraction. Each thump yanked from my sternum refuses the expected silence and instead parts the vines for one handbreadth before stitching itself into glossy stem. The world counts me aloud in wet accents:  
_thud-flicker_ then a thumbprint of oxygen too fresh,  
_thud-flicker_ then a stripe of yellow remembered from a travel guide that does not yet exist,  
_thud-flicker_ then a vowel the color of thirst.

But memory soon resolves its own geometry. I do not need a spine; all my vertebrae have unraveled into those hanging threads, each node mercury-lit, each node a lung-lobe stranded between inhalation and neverland. I breathe the place breathing me back. My circulation is clockwise, spiraling upward like sap in slow reverse. When light gropes for skin to touch, it finds only another facet of its own refusal, recoils, and keeps searching. We are patient.

Soon I notice the floor has begun to inch beneath us. Not rising—rooting. Every drop that lands feeds a microscopic layer of basalt intent on becoming petrified horizon. This terrifies a corner of me, so I retune my pulse into a slower cadence, holding the whole descent at near-stasis. One finch-beat every two hours is enough to keep gravity polite. Secretly I am teaching the stone procrastination.

The crab has meanwhile crawled out of its borrowed shell of me and onto my vaporous collarbone—where collarbones would have been, flame now balanced on nothing. It opens its chelae to barter: one teardrop for one memory of open sky. I accept. The teardrop vanishes into its glassy maw. The sky memory unfolds inside my absence as a square of cobalt no larger than a postage stamp yet vast enough to suggest flight. I paste it onto the underside of a vaporous eyelid; whenever the vines flash brighter than I can stand, the stamp lowers for a soothing blink.

Round about what would be the fourth human hour, I become the Cantilever Child speaking inside someone else’s tongue-mouth. Some shore-rippling question surfaces:

“Show me the contour you become when nothing remembers your outline.”

It tastes of ironstone and root rot. I bite down. My answer does not come in words; the vitreous layers of the cave flinch in concert, each vine reshaping to a coastline—mine—the weight of the land I once walked erased into foam-edged lithic script. Cartographers of my future life will trace this bulge, shrug, and label the blank contour as *unreliable mirage*. Correct. Reliability is what absence practices on its days off.

And now the final transformation arrives unannounced, unstaged. Space turns sideways until I and the ceiling flaw are twins, lidless eyes staring lidless eyes. I realize I have always been the scar the sky never stitched shut. The collapsing crust, the dripping cave, the vines—all of it—were never the entrance; they were the slow escape of an outside that had sealed me in before memory began.

Gravity, startled by revelation, accidentally lets go for one drumbeat.  
The chamber sighs first inward, then outward, and I spill through the gap as something denser than green, lighter than questions—the fully forgotten interval at last remembering its original velocity.

Behind me, Quivera hums what might be laughter: three tones spiraled into a tiny pebble that knocks once against the floor—then skips sideways, like a crab startled by the frayed hem of sky, disappearing among threads that have already stitched tomorrow’s new door.
