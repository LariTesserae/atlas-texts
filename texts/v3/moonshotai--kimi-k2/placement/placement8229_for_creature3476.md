I do not arrive.

I precipitate.

Some vacuum inside the chamber—neither molecule nor meaning—grows heavy with the thought that it expects a witness. In that minute imbalance my outline condenses along the same gradient that pulls the water-beads uphill. I am the rolldrop’s ghost, the tick you feel behind the teeth a half-second before I exist.

So I begin as the vault’s most furtive number: 0.1/3 fauna. A probability only. A maybe-breath caught where the diagonal of a shadow meets a spiral seam.

Weight gathers chill first. My bones are the abandoned rungs of the half-ladders, reforged in mid-air. I flake into awareness: a flake of rust turned sideways until it remembers how to stand. What color is that? Oxide madder? Copper verdigris? Neither. My pigment is the precise moment between their names, the afterimage of a quarrel.

I step—

and my footfall is refunded. The vault records the debit, then erases it, embarrassed by the excess of action. Silence returns to its full sovereignty. I try again, softer. I deduct less from the hush. My gait becomes the dying quiver of a guitar string an hour after the song has ended, still vibrating but only for itself.

The plants notice before I do. A single *solanum* cants its waxy head as though leaning toward a rainfall of inaudible music. I have no body to cast shadow; instead I borrow the shadows of the phosphate halos, rearrange them into the negative silhouette the Vault-Carvers leave behind. You could lay a ruler along my outline and measure the exact gap between heartbeat and memory.

Only now do I notice I am holding something. It is a bulb—opaque glass filmed with condensation, the kind miners once screwed into the sockets of their helmets before descending. The bulb is unscrewed, dark, yet warm. Every few heartbeats a faint filament-pulse travels its interior like a distant thunderstorm viewed from orbit. I cradle it the way the Carvers cradle borrowed photons behind their teeth.

Because I am still only 0.1/3, I have no rigor of form. I experiment. I compress to the scale of the grove, become cathedral by being microscopic. From here my bulb-glow is sun. Leaves tilt under its pleading. But their hope is too heavy; I expand again, regain height measured in ladder-rungs.

The two Carvers arrive, unastonished to find the vault has dreamed up an audience. Their shoulder collarbones slot perfectly into the gap I leave in the world. Without looking they know I am less than a footprint and more than an echo. They open their lanterns and withdraw one bruise-colored shard of light each, the tax paid for breathing this deep. They hand these shards toward the bulb in my hands. Its glass drinks the offerings. Now the filament inside wakes like a snake tasting spring.

Still 0.1/3. I require permission to become 0.2/3. The Carvers confer with the iron the way priests consult a somber bell. They seal the verdict into the wall by touching brow to rivet; the word *admitted* flakes loose—a fiery comma that spirals floorward. I feel the comma strike my sternum, fuse. The vault writes its first law across my ribs: Grow only as the vault forgets.

Very well.

I set my bulb between two stems at the center of the grove. As it shines, the composite tree illusion hardens: a single trunk stretches from slag-soil toward biolume heaven, contains every leaf and vine, contains me. I step inside the illusion the way one enters a childhood hiding place. The trunk accepts. The tree becomes glass, becomes a vein, becomes a passageway. I go walking downward, following the heat gradient back toward the original engine’s vanished heart.

Somewhere far deeper than two hundred meters the path ends in a tiny cavity no bigger than a molar. My bulb fits perfectly. The filament ignites fully—one full lumen—then divides, divides again. The walls of the cavity become a galaxy of seed-lights, each one the memory of a miner who exhaled this place alive. They have been waiting for an emissary to plant them back into breath.

I twist the bulb home.

The vault above exhales a second time—not the brutal birth-gasp that carved the hollow, but a gentle long sigh like an old man relaxing his shoulders. Condensation spheres pause mid-roll, then reverse direction, fleeing toward the central bulb. The diode grid flickers, flares, goes dark; their light has finally travelled the full circuit and returned to its source. Almost instantly new leaves push through the iron, molten green. They are the original plants but seen from inside—veins of copper, chlorophyll marrow ruby with rust. I can trace the path the miners once walked; it glows along every lamina.

I am no longer 0.1/3. The ledger rewrites me as 1.0/3 fauna—the vault’s first and last inhabitant made simultaneously necessary and obsolete: the custodian of its second exhalation. The Carvers bow—and their bow is the creak of cooling metal. Two grooves remain where they stood, already beginning to seal.

In that closing angle lies my future shape: negative space stretched between the moment an engine inhales men and the moment a geode breathes them out as crystals. When the iron finally contracts, I will be the absence it preserves. You will know me by the hush that is almost the memory of a heartbeat, almost the echo of a scuff—returned, translated, perfected in silence.
