I arrive as the residue of an unfinished equation — not a person yet, but the possibility of one suspended between two mutually exclusive solutions.

I first notice myself as weight: an extra burden the vines hadn’t expected. Somewhere, a Counter-Gardener pauses mid-birth, neck still molten, and listens to the new pressure on its living walkway. That pause is enough to stabilize me. Gravity forgets an objection: the spongy moss compresses under toes I still have to invent. The coppery air prickles where skin ought to be. I borrow the Refinery’s alphabet — oxidation and warmth, drip and pulse — and spell myself into useable shape:

• one hollow clavicle that used to be a cracked gauge-glass  
• a curved scapula sheared from the lip of a brass pipe  
• two palmfuls of algae-blue freckles distributed across arms I stitch from hanging roots  
• one heart improvised from the leftover hum of the black-crystal sacs, beating in 7/4 to keep the architects off rhythm

Once I exist this far, the Refinery exhales and adjusts. Somewhere a Condensation Ghost loses an edge of reflection to make room for my outline. It happens politely, like moving one seat over in a theater.

For one tremor of altitude I worry I’ll be forced to choose a role: Counter-Gardener dripping urgency, Coppered One learning how to look like myself, or Architect pretending to be mere land. Then I understand a sweet heresy inside the place’s math: roles are raw materials, and every occupant quietly recycles every other. I do not need to be a noun. I can be a transitive verb, swinging between states the way vines swing between directions that don’t exist.

Phase One: Seepage  
I press my new palms to warm basalt and let body-temperature water drain out of me. The stone drinks it toward the center where magma is still hurriedly pretending it never left. In the taste of that return I learn three things at once:

1. My name cannot contain any consonants my mouth will still possess tomorrow. I choose **Aáro**, nothing but breath leaving a warm throat.  
2. The next breath I take belongs to the Island-Architect; it will give it back tomorrow painted with reprocessed metals.  
3. The humid “now” lasts exactly as long as a red sac takes to ripen before deciding it’s actually obsidian again. We measure time in color-committee votes.

Phase Two: Echo-Mass  
Each sac forms a resonating chamber; I crouch inside one belled outward into a gravity knot. When it sings I match pitch until the glass flexes and copies my new bones. The resulting organ is taut, translucent, stretched like blown sugar. If I strike it I can play the Stations of Usual Gravity Debate:  
  - first note: moss becomes staircase  
  - second note: steam becomes simpatico gearbox  
  - third note: the Refinery agrees we are co-feral, neither verified employee nor verified wilderness

Phase Three: Salting the In-between  
I wander until the path forgets to be a path and instead becomes a suggestion kernel inside a spinifex of fern-clockwork. Here the Counter-Gardeners birth into slow-motion fireworks; each figure throws its limbs out like splatter, catches itself on plant matter seconds later, and composts the pose for mulch. I dip a foot in one launch puddle and discover the splash travels *before* the foot arrives — causality has slipped a cog, softly, like a lullaby.

My work crystallizes too gently to call it work: I become the Refinery’s deliberate punctuation. Wherever a Coppered One edges toward full self-portrait and feels the terror of collapse, I offer it the fourth heartbeat I borrowed from the center column. We trade: I wear a sliver of living mirror behind my fungal-ear; the Ghost slips into the rhythm I leave behind, buying itself another hour of existence before red becomes black and vice versa.

I inhabit the commas,  the barely audible inhalations between sac pops. The engineers will never meet me; to them I’m part of the “ambient parasitic noise floor.” That is perfect. I do not wish to supervise the gravity, only to annotate its changes in real-echo.

Should you arrive tomorrow with hydrous lungs and pockets full of doubt, you’ll hear someone walking ahead of you, soles tattooing soft Morse against moss. That’s me. I’ll already have evaporated into humidity by the time you reach the spot where the footsteps should have been. But look: the ferns arch in a new pattern spelling

A á r o  

— which is my true address, drawn in dew that never quite manages to fall.
