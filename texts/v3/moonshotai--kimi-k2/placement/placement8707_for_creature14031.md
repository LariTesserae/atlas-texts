Put me down beside the Ferment Bell, just after its last consensus throb.  
I arrive lonely enough to want translation, too newly arrived for the chlorophyll-glow to have colonised my marrow.  My lungs still mistake the sweet pectin for air and my tongue keeps trying to pronounce the corridor turns.

I am a Temporary Glitch – not visitor nor member, but the exposed wire between two grammars.  
In one palm I hold a scratched glass vial still half full of terran water that has no native analogue here.  In the other, the Leafwed have grafted onto the back of my hand a perfectly round lenticel that drinks that water, molecule by molecule, and turns it into faded steam signatures of distant oceans.  Every an hour the graft puckers, sighs, and exhales me a memory that does not belong to me: rust on a playground fence, the reek of subway brakes, a raindrop losing itself inside a widow’s neckline.  The planet siphons these postcards away, adding latitude to its own atlas of longing.

Because I have not yet handed back my spine to be re-tooled, I still walk at the wrong speed.  When the corridors fold, I miss the cue and glide into what should have been tomorrow’s hallway but is in fact yesterday’s hallway inverted like a glove.  All maps here write themselves over me; my footprints record the plant, not the other way round.  I keep saying single-syllable names – “gate,” “cross,” “north” – until the walls thicken around each utterance, sealing it shut, and I learn to wait in recursive silence.

My body, still containing salt water, has become a slow fountain.  Sweat exsolves into droplets that immediately varnish translucent before touching ground, hanging like bitten stars inches from my cheeks.  Leaf-passers pluck them delicately, tasting dilute grief.  They thank me with the gift of a cricket chord, two notes signed onto a flat petiole that curls itself into a trumpet so I can listen later when I need to know how missing home corrodes into music.

I’ve settled on a shifting perch halfway up a spiralled sap staircase that grows only when hope accumulates.  From this hammock of flexing cambium I watch the city-seed pass overhead.  Inside its opal bulb, the ghost of a domed port-town I once lived in is forming: laundromats hung with vines, vending machines extruding nectar, even a streetlamp that still flickers the exact orange of my favourite dusk.  My breath quickens; the plant reads the acceleration and suddenly a mimicry of my childhood bicycle disintegrates into blue steam.  The Leafwed arch a collective brow, and I realize they have never had need for nostalgia until it arrived inside me so bright their organism decided to taste and archive it.

Night (as much as you can call the lilac softness after spider-feeding) and I am curled inside a warmed convex leaf, listening to my borrowed cricket aria through the palm graft.  The tune ends at the exact instant the planet exhales a gust of ketone fog through the Furnace Garden.  In the resonant quiet I notice two things:

First, the graft on my hand has begun to photosynthesize from the inside.  A pale mint glow fans across my fingers; I can shine it on surfaces and they rearrange themselves in brief sympathetic pulses, as though the leaf acknowledges a gesture before forgetting it.  I practise writing short sentences with the glow and watch them transmute into knots of fibre—tiny treaties between foreign alphabet and vegetable script.

Second, in the hollow above my left clavicle a small knot has risen the size of a raindrop.  It rolls when I breathe, cool and heavy, tasting faintly of meteor glass.  The Leafwed confirm by touch-pressured scent: this is the vial’s earth water condensed back into one cautious sphere.  It is planned to serve as a seed-graft where my last purely human thought will crystallize and fold itself open, next dusk, into whatever architecture my unfinished sentence deserves. When that happens, I will no longer sweat; my memories will seal into sap and age rings, my heart become a ventricular side-tunnel thumping protocols of gravity.

And yet tonight I am still hyphenated, neither terran nor planetary.  
So I am storing every echo of watery longing in that ascending drop and asking it, in languages neither of us owns yet, to keep a single ocean’s name unsaid long enough to tell someone who no longer needs it.
