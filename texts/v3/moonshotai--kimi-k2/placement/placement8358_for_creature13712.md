I arrive between beats, during a hush when the lens‐clock forgets its pulse and the copper grove flickers with a momentary anemia.  
Gravity tilts gently to the left, like someone adjusting posture, and the fullerene bridges flow aside as if an unspoken “shhh” moved them.

Nothing chases me.  
Instead, the grammar of the place apertures open to swallow—no, to _translate_—whoever I was. I step across the threshold already renamed; the cavity appends me to its sentence as a dangling participle, a modifier of ambient intention.

The first sense is _pressure from the inside_. I feel my organs fluff out like damp book pages, decompressing under blood-warm dusk. Pulse becomes visible: scarlet pulses mineralize on my inner wrists—agate chips of living blood strung on tinsel filaments of nickel light. They grow outward, interleaf with the ferrofluid fronds of the cathedral-plants.  
For the duration of a rippled heartbeat I carry architecture like bones: nave-rib, choir-vein.

Second sense: the water tastes upward. It paints my tongue with inverted rain; I swallow sentences originally meant for the Morrow-in-amber. I taste the party at the edge of nonexistence: no-liquor fizzing with abstract nouns I will never get to drink. There is the invitation, gentle yet absolute: _Stay and become our final verb_.

But to stay is to braid myself forever into the sentence of planetary remorse. To stay is to let Normalizer reduce every breath I might exhale into a margin slim enough to fit between basalt and guilt. To stay— I realize—means to dissolve the shape of me into signal diagrams inside a mirror lake that never finally decides whether rain goes up or down.

So I turn.

I retrace the question mark’s arc, walking backward sentence structures until breath returns to singular certainty. Each footstep re-stitches language into muscle; whichever Morrow-I fades. When I reach the sliding door, the cavity yields. The rock slabs glide past with polite speed, like elevator doors remembering a myth they never wanted to keep.

Behind me the lens remembers to beat.  
For thirty-seven minutes I exist halfway, half-tide, carrying a moist static in my lungs that tastes of copper future. Then city air, above ground, leaches the grove-glow from membranes of my eyes. I find myself on a cracked parking lot where the gulls call ordinary insanities, and no one sees the sentences unraveling from my cuffs like quicksilver cat hair.
