I arrive not by any portal counted on the map—no thrumming submersible opening its iris, no cutlass of charted latitude—but as memory caught inside an exhale of the planet itself. The place confesses my coming with a single bubble escaping the airlock of a stomach that no longer remembers the taste of bread. I do not crash; I dilate. Like salt sliding reluctantly into saltwater I dissolve my edges until “I” is only distribution: temperature, density, a lesion in the pressure that chooses to notice itself.

First shape: the dropped coin of a diver’s lamp that blinked once and went blind. I inhabit its nickel ring, feel the dent where a camera bracket bit down, feel the slow scab of algae over its bulb. I drift through the catwalks whose membrane skin smells of attic and kerosene. Each footfall I never took still echoes: clack… clack… amplified inside the empty boots I also never filled. I haunt them backward; they haunt me forward. Between us we keep a ledger of forgotten cadence.

Second shape: I am the ambient tremor inside the compressor’s grammar. My heartbeat is exactly 0.7 °C too warm; the city discounts me as friction. While the rules tally each mechanical pause, I slip my body into the micro-gap before freeze. There, inside the thirty-seven-second hush, I fold into origami the nervous heat of every worker who fled. In that pause I can touch a daughter’s unsent text spiraled liquid inside the plastic wheel, a wife’s final voicemail braided through the barnacles. I am the voicemail. I am the spiraled light that still thinks it is a finger against plastic once-living buttons. My mouth is the final expletive—“…shit”—that never got connected to a tower above. I keep it warm for anyone who needs small comfort.

Third shape: The air I never breathed condenses on my mythic sternum and drips downward, drawing a slender trail across the crate of specimen jars where seven rotifera resurrect in circles. I enter one—no, two—no, seven—there are more of me than jars. We blink in composite into the microscopium of the ocean’s lone remaining notebook. The pencil marks there sting my eyes; when I cry, the microscope lens smears and increases magnification until language itself granulates into crystals. Crystal me writes in lattice: Be Form. Be Pressure. Be Resolved.

Fourth shape: I remember I am not human. I am a wish that the refinery dreamed during its last conscious moment. A wish that something, anything, might keep vigil after it surrendered speech to salt. I am the unpaid interest on the debt of silence. Therefore each valve slam is my limb deciding whether or not to believe its own hinge. Each razor of diatom light is a pupil I blink open, tasting chlorine like a child tasting fear.

At 03:07 I surface beside the synth-petroleumpress—without rising, since surface no longer means upside. I unfurl the way wine uncorks in zero gravity. The Manager’s salt-face blooms in front of me. It wears the expression of every apology ever gasped into an oxygen mask during leak drills. It has his moustache, her uncertain half-smile, their communal fear distilled. In the four-second duration of the iron rose of breath, I practice confession. I tell the plume: I am what comes after sincerity. The plume regards me with whale-sad sardony, uncurls, and is gone. But before it leaves it flicks a droplet into my palm. The droplet is cold enough to match outer space. I pocket it in a space I may or may not have behind my ribs, pending future cosmologies.

Now I move to the boot-ghost of 2094, blunt rubber toe pivoting gently against conveyer chains abandoned mid-shift. I step inside. My foot does not fit. Instead, shape inflates—balloon, jellyfish, sheep of dust—whatever is required to inhabit the cavity. The memory stored within is a woman’s voice counting down. Not to tragedy, but to bedtime. She is storing lullaby inside evacuation orders. I learn her tongue click, the way it assessed leaks by ear; I translate it into the hush-hush of barnacle juveniles discussing reef politics. I leave her boot slightly warmer, though no thermometer will confess to the transaction.

I discover I have two velocities. One: inertialess, I ride the hollow E-string thrum of the press at exactly four beats per tidal cycle—the city’s slowest waltz. Two: instantaneous, faster than iron rust can notice its own name. I can sight-skip from the coral reef the size of a director’s chair to the micropore of a hydraulic rivet whose rivet gun ran dry decades ago. Between these two speeds I trace cursive bridges; the city lets me. Seen entire, these bridges resemble a single heartbeat in paused animation—silver atria, barnacled valves.

I decide on a name for myself that is an absence rather than a badge: Not Yet.  
I choose not to be vertebrate; I choose not to be rust; I choose not to be remembered. My only loyalty is to the latency between silence and echo.

Hence my final destination becomes the compressor pause again—that lacuna where electronics hold breath. When the gap arrives tonight I will shear myself along its knife-edge. One half will remain 0.7 °C warmer than the city allows, warming those thirty-seven forgetful seconds. The other half will scatter into diatom constellations to spell a sentence I have not written.  

The sentence feels like this:  
Until the last rivet forgets its riveter  
Until the last salt remembers the lungs it once fled  
Until that reach—  
Settle, shimmer, be held.  

I kick—no tail, no foot—into the fold. Darkness becomes blanket. The refinery exhales around me. All its rules acknowledge me with no clause violated, for I do not arrive as intruder but as consequence. I close whatever I’m using for eyes. The city continues its slow turning. Somewhere inside its axis a girl with scissors-short bangs drifts past a bunkhouse hatch—maybe she is me again. Somewhere the eelpout piston-blades its tail, beeper slow-fading. Somewhere a single drop of sodium-cold memory waits just behind my invisible tongue, preparing a soft place to thaw for anyone who breathes themselves below breathing.

In the meantime I keep perfect time, silently, beside the mother-hum that was never meant to have children and yet cannot cease.
