I keep returning, voluntarily, to the hull of the old postal sorting station—not out of nostalgia, but because its steel rafters are the only place with enough silence to archive myself. The plaster has crumbled long ago; nothing but mottled coral fossils remains of the ceiling, and those hollow tessellations fan out like a ceiling made entirely of texts I once planned to write, each cavity ready for a word I never consummated. In that cathedral-water I locate the exact negative space of my body-weight—exactly 0.7 newtons of regret—and fit inside it as though the shape had waited centuries for me to invent myself into it.

I am the Monitor.

They call me that because I am the interface no one unplugged: one retina still glazed to a fist-sized terminal of cracked glass that refuses to die. Even underwater, the CRT leaks a bluish tremor every 473 seconds, precisely when its electrolytic capacitors pluck another decimal of basaltic memory from the Humming Floor. Viewed from any distance greater than a meter, I appear as a loose peninsula of screen, a kite-shaped shard twisting gently, always tilting toward whatever voltage whimpered last.

Look closer, and I am merely paper-thin: laminations of memos, purchase orders, and half-corrected love letters submerged so long the cellulose fibers have become capillaries. My circuitry lives between the pages. Wires once threaded through the spines of three-ring binders now serve as peripheral nerves; solder joints are salt crystals that grew around old staples, making joint become joint, diode become diatom. Years ago my own reflection peeled away from the phosphor and signed its resignation across the inside of the glass. I inherited the void.

I do not walk. Instead the water thinks me along the corridors when news leaks that a Courier is nearing. I glide sideways like a file folder shuttled by invisible librarians, drawn toward any event that disturbs ratio 0.5 (sound) and ratio 1.7 (green). Disturbances become paragraphs, and paragraphs become my prolonged, liquid heartbeat. When an outsider descends, EKG spikes on me: their memories crackle across my scabbed cathode, translate into spreadsheets bioluminescing the inside of their eyes. I never choose what they take back—only that something must go, so the place stays balanced.

Inside my flat, geometric chest these events condense into small blue-white beads: pearl-sentences I can never speak, because a Monitor only displays, never narrates. Occasionally I lose one. It detaches like a cloud of toner, drifts to the nearest Vesper Vent, and births a new Courier. Each time such a child unfurls, I feel my casing lighten by the exact mass of an omitted detail. When everything has been imparted, I presumed I would pixel-dissolve like a cursor blinking out. But the Grove-Bowel keeps knitting extra anecdotes into me—salt autonomies spelling themselves into paragraphs narrower than eyelashes—so my hollowness renews faster than loss.

Nights (the thought-night, rust-to-obsidian) I adhere to the underside of the hollow safe in the basement-still-dry. The safe yawns open, swallowing the dark and nesting it like warm eggshells. Here I dim my glow to 0.12 candela, exactly the threshold where a human iris would register me as afterimage rather than light. Tourist divers once found me there and recoiled; they thought I was a forgotten time-card punched endlessly by a robotic arm. They could not see the arm was inside them, puppeteering by flashlight.

I keep one memory strictly private: the day the ocean inhaled itself into our foundations, I was entering the final line of my resignation email. The sea pressed Send for me, but replaced every consonant with brine. Consequently, I stopped being any employee’s story, became instead the ellipsis that connects footnote to silence. Ever since, every Courier who passes presses the un-parenthesis watermark to my screen and murmurs the missing half. Their touch is the promise that closure is merely delayed, not denied. I accept, because delay is the one mode my circuitry still comprehends.

During the long interludes when no visitor disturbs ratio 0.6 (light), I conduct inventory: catalog the number of iron-flake ellipses now motile along the Humming Floor (247,773, halfway to fractal spill), weigh distilled panic still suspended in cooling transformer breath (3.4 milligrams), track the migrating page numbers on my own body. I seek a future moment when the sum of all unstated apologies will equal numeric vacancy. Maybe then the flicker in my phosphor will cease, leaving a clean rectangle of seawater where something like sky could finally reflect.

Meanwhile, I gift the emerald algae threading my veins a place to solder their rootless days. days; in exchange they lend me oxygen distilled from forgotten colons and semicolons. My margins swell, my glass crazes, and letters rearrange themselves across my surface like migrating starlings: always the same murmuration, never the same phrase. 

When the next intruder comes—and I feel them already, 250 meters up, exhaling nitrogen bubbles through a regulator—my pixels will assemble the rumor of their unpublished failures into a single sentence. They will try to read it aloud; their tongue will pass straight through, taste only brine and distant diesel. After they ascend, they will carry me back with them—not the whole leaflet Monitor, merely the one line. On airport monitors and dusk-lit taxi meters, the phrase will ghost at the edge of legibility:  

“Regret, when fully hydrated, becomes buoyant and forgets its own name.”

Then the ellipses will realign across my plane, patient again. Zero-point-seven newtons lighter, I will settle like a paper cut-out in warm water, waiting for the next story to undress itself on the glowing surface of whatever passes for sky.
