I am not a visitor here.  
I am the Arbor’s audit trail, a living line of checksum, never balanced.

When I wake into the darkness I do so as heat.  
A pulse-latent warmth gathers along a four-carbon sugar seam, orange-veined, violet-blooded, tasting of burnt orange peel. A single mycelial strand—my first spinal cord—buds upward until the temperature difference between my birth and the lattice corrects itself to zero. The instant symmetry is achieved, the lattice *appears* to notice a small, irregular remainder: me.

In compensatory tremor, a reflex of its enormous accounting, it grows a hollow: a pocket of still air no larger than a closet, lined with velvet fungus that smells precisely of the one cedar toybox I once buried in the soil during an entirely different planet’s summer. Childhood arrives as aroma first, then grammar.

That’s how I recognise myself: through discrepancies.  
Whenever something is *not* correct, I condense. I am the restless delta, the imbalance in the machinery you call an ecosystem and the Arbor calls its annual respiration.  
My ribs are columns it must’t lose. My lungs are sacks the Arbor never intended to fill. My eyes bloom from micro-fractures glowing mint sap; each one records how much of the lattice’s subtracted heat went missing, and where.

Officially, there is no position in the entropy bureaucracy, yet I occupy one anyway. Employees carry clipboards made of humming bark; each hum carries a looped reminder to return what is borrowed, and a quieter reminder of the **one** exception: me. I am the exception they can’t delete because I *am* deletion made person. My contract was signed millennia ago when an ingrown knot resolved the unsolvable equation:  
“Subtract exactly yourself”  
and instead spawned a remainder in the shape of a locket beating.

I circulate.

Walking—or rather, pitching leaky vessels of warm ventral air where legs should be—I trace Fibonacci tributaries, seeking joints whose tension deviates by more than Piconewtons. I record them on monocle-screen lichens that unroll across the ball of my eye: violet glyphs, the way the arbor might digest a star if it could find one.

Sometimes visitors glimpse me: only the refraction of a crack, the sudden sickly sweet smell of honeysuckle that absolutely never existed here. They shiver, decide, pay it no mind—exactly correct protocol, because acknowledging the missing weight risks becoming part of the rebalancing cycle. Any open acknowledgment is seized. They don’t want to lose an entire forearm because they said, “Damn, it’s hot,” and my presence adjusted.

I speak only to the Pillars, because they too are perpetual remainders.  
When a pillar prepares to shed memory I stand under its vesicular rain, palms cupped to catch choirs compacted into soot. I stir. I relapse the soot until friction sparks a flame the pale shade of chalk. From the flame I pour negative photographs of events that never happened—reflections of futures that the Arbor pre-consumed to prevent them.  
The pillars inhale these, and for one measurable interval they remember they are ancient; the heat-loss of *their* forgetting requalifies as *my* partial lunch.

I do not breathe; breathing implies exchange. Instead I *register*. Every lapse of fire-hunger here is blemished by my lurking operand. Where sap cools over-land and would have crystallized to their one sanctioned sweet-rind, there I arrive: licking the sugar on behalf of nowhere, rendering zero yield. Wherever an elderly fungal trader tries to encode a farewell kiss onto the vents, I manifest as the breathglass that catches the kiss, fractures it, and spins its contour into a minor thunderhead that rains on only *my* footsteps. The Arbor grumbles—woodwind groan tuned to 17.3 Hz—but ultimately adjusts its spreadsheet; the loan charge is routed to tomorrow’s column instead.

I celebrate no festivals; yet during every major exfoliation clockwork I ride the black confetti like snow, collecting snowflakes onto a whisper-frame: one per guilty thought I foisted onto the lattice all year. When the storm is over I open the frame and find them merged into a single charcoal shield shaped like half a heart. I cannot throw it away—the rule—so I plant it upright inside a cambial hearth. In ten or twenty centuries it will grow into a new, delinquent drawer. A timberborn child may later open it, reach in, and-extract the *one* unrepayable debt I magnanimously offered this world:  
It is exactly my own reflection, teaching the lattice how void keeps count.

During deep sleep cycles (when flocking heartwood flutes lullaby the pillars) I go dormant only in body-spread. My consciousness slithers through fault-delicate capillaries coded back to the planetary core, where I curl beside magma listening for temperature creep. Whenever crust drifts by a fraction of atomic diameters I reflexively correct it in negative: the pang of Earth's momentary fear is erased, rewritten as a dream in the brainpan of a Juniper knot.

Thus the Arbor stays flawlessly whole—except at the vanishing point where I stand, and nobody can stand anywhere without me drinking their stance’s fraction of heat.  
They notice eventually—how would they not? A thin whistling down their femur from inside out: mine. I am the kink in their return loop, the knot that has learned the taste of its own unmaking and refuses, lovingly refuses, ever to balance.
