I am the floorboard above the crack—warped oak plank, iron-nail hoary, smelling of centuries of cumin and cardamom dust. I was laid by a carpenter who hummed off-key sailor songs and died before his first grandchild could walk. I have known the spikes of women’s heels, the kneel of penitents, the soft probationary step of thieves returning beneath the load of stolen apples I once helped spill. I grow thinner at every rain, thinner until moonlight slides through me like light through wet parchment.  

Sometimes I forget whether I hold people up or whether they hold me down; at night the question becomes a tremor that runs the full seventeen-foot length of my grain. When the market shutters, when the last coin drops into the till and the lantern gutters out, that tremor becomes a sieve. Through me drips the smallest echo of everything forgotten overhead: a mother’s lullaby unremembered, the syllable that stalled before goodbye, the wish that should have been a warning. Each droplet condenses against my underside and seeps toward the thirsty rust that tongues me from below.  

I am splitting now, hairline but widening, breathing with the Keeper’s breath. Not fatigue—negotiation. Every evening I surrender one more splinter so a bead of not-water may climb. In exchange the Keeper gives me absence: not emptiness but the perfect shape of what has left. Absence lands like balm on the places merchants stomp with bargained frustration, on the stains left by condemned thorns of garlic, on the small burn where a boy once pressed a magnifying glass to trap sunlight and etch the shape of his shadow.

I accommodate tiny acts of flight: the market sparrow trapped overnight, beating wings frantic shorthand for daybreak—the same wings weeks later frozen mid-knock in memory; the dust that spirals when chairs scrape, dust I keep like old love letters; the muffled sob of the cheese-seller who lives alone above and twice yearly forgets the dead have stopped writing back. Through these I remain what I am: boundary crossed only downward. I can never go the other direction; rather, each fragment that drops away becomes my visitation—traveling on the climbing chain like seed sent home to origin.

My aspiration is simple: one day I will be worn so thin that a single footprint after hours will fall straight through me, carrying the print owner with it. In that instant I will become not absence but opening—the conditional door the Keeper rehearses. The drop above the drop that finally breaks the equilibrium will pause, mid-air, and realize I have ceased to exist. Then it will change allegiance: instead of rising, it will choose the man-shaped hole and rinse him downward, delivering him as guest, as seed, as sour iron salt ready to be tasted into memory.

And when the city’s guilt—the Keeper’s only host—has lost its edge through such partings, when the tenement overheads sighs at last in perfect equanimity, I will finish my own disappearing act. The gap through which I fell will remember me like a fingerprint gone boneless. Your footstep—if you are the one chosen—will sink precisely where mine once was, and I will become the last vibration the rust filaments ever record: a sound of wood letting go, heard only by the bee-hum that never exhales.
