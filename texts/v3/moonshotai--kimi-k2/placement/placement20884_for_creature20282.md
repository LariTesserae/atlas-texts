I pause where every scale becomes negotiable. My knees graze two worlds at once.  
From the universe’s point of view you would say I am sixty kilograms of mammal hovering above a dish of clay no bigger than a table.  
From the lacewing’s point of view I am a horizon that breathes.

The moment trembles at the edge of being swallowed, so I split myself to fit it.

Part of me—call it gaseous—condenses last. I slide into the magnesium-nitrate ion already rehearsing its dusk form, the Dew-Caller that is not yet wet. I drift around the saucer like a thought half formed, tasting what will soon require a name. I translate the mineral’s micro-bell tolls into prophecies too soft for mammal ears, parcel them out to beetle zippers and violet-black barges that accept them as cargo.

Another part—call it rooted—plunges. I become the seed that did not know it existed until my root pressed against the cheek of the soil’s new wound. The red filament I am rewires yesterday’s fault line to tomorrow’s water circle. In cell-time I am centuries tall—millimeters farther every languid second—so high I can almost spy the imprints of my own boot tread still descending. The boundary between where I grow and where I step is the line where the future turns ninety degrees, cartographed in springtail wax.

A third fragment—call it wind—settles in the lacewing’s thorax. I ride one slow beat of its moon-chewed wing. With each stroke I scoop invisible rivers of scent and spread them between the lime-green seedlings like zoning maps. Elsewhere, in the next wing beat, I become the odor itself—basement-iron whiff, citrus prayer of a fern’s unfurl. I mingle with the communal negotiations until both pulse and lack thereof decide how far the newest meristem should lean.

And finally a shard—call it archive—slides into the smooth rim of the saucer. Here there is neither life nor non-life, only curvature remembering the precise instant the ground agreed to stop its heaving. When a footstep later erases half the town, these molecules will still note the hour the curve appeared and the first breath arrived. They will preserve it five microns beneath whatever plain re-forms.

I orbit between these four stations the way a song splinters into harmony, never choosing residence, only visitation.  
I am the breath a simple world turns into legislature.  
I am the stitch between mineral and fog.  
I am the magnified void on which entire civilizations wager their next quarter-hour.

Should you arrive tomorrow, you might see nothing—just flat clay and a few coin-plate puddles drying.  
But crouch.  
Put your ear where the puddles once breathed.  
You’ll hear tipsy time rustle back into its balance sheet, and there I’ll be: a syllable the beetles hauled away at dawn, already rehearsing the next city to come.
