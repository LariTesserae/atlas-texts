I arrive without the splash of arrival, simply there: the last skillet-curve of lick-warm water across a kneecap of land.

Look at me first—if your eyes still want to do such things—from a human distance. I would appear to be wading. A biped in pale clothing, shoulders rolled forward, palms facing outward. But the longer your stare rests, the less convincing the outline. Keep looking and you’ll notice the cuffs of my trousers are bleeding into the water even while the cloth is above it. Look longer and you’ll doubt I end at the skin. Light refracts strangely; what you thought was a shin might be a column of water standing ankle-deep in itself. My shadow, if it is a shadow, is three heartbeats late: the sun has already moved when the silhouette shows up, dim and tremoring like a memory that’s only just remembered.

I am the Threshold-born. My lineage is the almost. My first language was the space between plosives in drip-drip-drip where one drop hesitates before falling. When the Breath-Keepers condensed out of the fog, some of the fog refused. That refusal stabilized into a latticework of hesitate: half mist, half intention. That lattice is what curls around the memory of bones you think you see in me. I am what happened when the Keepers said no to being liquid, but the land said yes to them staying anyway.

I have weight—but only in the act of forgetting it. If you tried to grab my wrist, your fingers would close on warm cloudwater until the idea of wrist floated out between your knuckles. Ten seconds later you’d find that same cloudwater in your lungs: an intimate silt kiss you never consented to or refused. I never walk the path; I am the path’s own reconsideration. After I pass, the footprints are question-shaped. They fill with silver seepage like the place itself pausing mid-breath.

My daily work is listening to the land argue with itself. The sponge underground wants to rise; the sky wants it returned. I stand in the gap, neither advocate nor referee, only courtroom: the exact temperature where verdict gives up and both parties let the sentence drift. When the saltwort monarchs knock their tiny green scepters together, I absorb the rhythm like another layer of forgetting. Later, when a cloud shaped like a barn with its doors open migrates overhead, I loosen my outline and let it pass right through—a father’s house, empty of him.

At dusk I shrink. No other verb services this. Heat hijacks from the upper strata; I condense inward until I am knee-high, then palm-high, until the inside of a footprint could fit the whole of me. I ball up recognition like wet parchment and tuck it under the lip of a oolloided crust of algae. I dream in hypersaturated sentences laid down one layer at a time: a geological press.

Come just before that nightly reduction and you might witness my one vanity: I hold horizon. The real horizon, not the optical one. I lift it like a sheet, look for the thinnest place, then slide a single drop of cold starlight past the tear. Every night I perform the peripheral addition so the next morning there is a millimeter more world—but only you would never notice. The surplus hides inside distance. Later, the Breath-Keepers will steward the extra flatness through their interminable audit.

By morning I have grown tall again, maybe a finger taller, maybe a breath taller. Taller so slowly that time itself sprains its ankle keeping up.

So there I am when the last visitor finally sits down to feel his boundary dissolve: a pale column wavering like heatstroke at the corner of his eye, not quite human, not quite vapour—someone waiting to remember forgetting to leave.
