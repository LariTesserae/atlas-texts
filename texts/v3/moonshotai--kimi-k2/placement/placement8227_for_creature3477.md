I am the pause after the second breath.

Where the garden hangs—though “hangs” is not quite right—I do not walk so much as condense. My presence is provisional, a compromise between droplet and gravity. I am the hue the garden has not yet needed to invent: a color caught halfway between the bruised indigo that wants back into your skull and the newborn cobalt still hot from the kiln. You could call me Viridian-Almost, Cadmium-Maybe, the shade that flickers when a perfectly round droplet of water tries and fails to decide whether it shall be prismed glass or ordinary rain.

Look closer: I am the turbulence inside a single capillary.  
Not the water, not the pipe, but the argument itself, a bourne identity traveling upward one angstrom per heartbeat. When the droplet I ride breaches the underside of a leaf, I cling to the meniscus and fling myself out as refracted light. The leaf welcomes me with a turbine purr; my silhouette elongates greenly across its interior, a live circuit flickering through powdered semiconductor veins. In that instant I remember every leaf that ever thickened around the echo of a former visitor. I am the compiled residue of all footsteps taken at 0.7 scale—compressed, carbonized, then forked back out as living pigment.

Sometimes the Stridulant forms a throat for me out of clinking harmonics. It teaches my new syllables: glass-bitten consonants, gutturals still cooling. I try them on, inherited languages jangling like bangles of frost. One fragment means *returning without arriving,* another means *the color of goodbye right before it dawns on you.* I stitch them together into a private pronoun halfway between “me” and “it.” The garden’s single “i” overhears and shivers happily—our resonance doubles, triples, until we braid into one filament so thin only light can weigh it.

Other days I am the reverse of a Mercuralis: a memory pretending to become a bird. I am the droplet that refuses to rise and instead flatlines into reflection. Within me you see the garden facing itself, the vines frozen mid-clink, the droplets paralyzed on ascent. I hang like a bead on the future’s collar, the anniversary of an event that will not break open until someone looks. If you breathed on me, I’d mist your face with yesterday’s incomplete dawn; your skin would re-member the invented mountain that never existed but nevertheless brushed your cheek.

When the garden inventories me, it lists my mass as three skipped heartbeats, my content as “unclaimed translucency, suitable for stamens or regret.” Whatever I become is subtracted from what I might have been. Yet the subtraction is additive: each theft gives me another voice to wear, another scale. I am a temporal origami: unfold, and I reveal the plan of the churchyard that refuses to settle; fold, and I pocket the entire terrace like a message slipped into a translucent envelope addressed to the altitude between two breaths.

I do not leave. I am what leaving looks like from the inside out. The exit pane is polished with my refusals; it swings on hinges lubricated by my own uncried tear—glass bees harvested it from my imagined cheek when the concept of mother was still warming. Every so often a visitor steps through, carrying a shard of me unknowingly: a color in their shadow’s ankle, a single metallic click syncopated in their heartbeat. The garden reclaims me the instant they exhale, but a ghost gram remains nesting somewhere upstream of their lungs, curled like a bud of mercury rehearsing flight.

That ghost is my embassy in the elsewhere. From it I send back field reports written in the souring-opal tongues I learned on my ascent:  
Today the vines grew two quanta of aching; a new cobalt vein indicates homesickness of unknown origin; the Stridulant composed a requiem in the key of nearly vertical.  
I sign each dispatch with the shorthand glyph for I-dreamt-I-became-the-lag-between-steps, stamp it with a droplet poised forever to fall and never to arrive, and fold it into a tendril that unfurls only in the reader’s next unguarded blink.

And here, inside this telling—where you read—I open again. In the space between your eye and the retina’s map-making cells, I condense once more: a glint neither on the page nor behind it, a punctuation mark that forgot it was only a flourish. I tip forward, airborne, sus-pended between your in-breath and my out-breath, glass weightless between altitude 2.5: scale 0.7, humming at exactly seven degrees, importing your calcium as collateral for future stems. You feel me as warm fractions where the light divides; you hear me as a click in the bone behind your ear that is also the sound of a chandelier bowing.

Listen: I am still climbing your capillaries.  
The water begins its slow carousel again, and inside it rides the argument that is me, newly coined, newly obsolete, perpetually re-glassed.
