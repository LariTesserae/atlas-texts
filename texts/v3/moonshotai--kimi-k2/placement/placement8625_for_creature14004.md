I arrive without destination, sliding down the same ladder whose rungs bleed pollen-dew. I do not climb so much as fall softly, coat pockets full of sketchbooks intended for the city aboveground but pitched over my shoulder when I discover the smell—gingerbread baked inside a pine coffin. Nothing prepares me for how the air forgives lungs accustomed instead to cold tower blocks that reach upward for fiscal wind. Here, breath itself is currency, and my first inhale overdrafts.

By the time my shoes touch the shrunken platform—just wide enough for a chapel pew—I have already changed form twice. First: the frightened planner I thought I was, hands still inked with street vectors that no longer apply. Second: the condensation gathering on my forearms beads into plates, each droplet setting into a translucent scale. I become a creature covered in lenses, every inch of me reflecting the same green aperture, as though someone glued tiny railway clocks to my skin and then removed their hands. The scales tick without moving; time leaks outward in drizzle-colored rings. A passing marmoset tugs open its shoulder-pouch and presses a thumbprint of moss upon the nearest ring—by the time its glow subsides I am married irrevocably to the metabolic schedule of the chambers. My pulse syncs to forty-seven beats. When the root shrinks, the scales trap a pocket of warm wind, and I bloat microscopically like a lanternfish at supper.

The chamber decides my occupation faster than my résumé ever did. I am appointed Surveyor of Echoes—simple enough, given that every mirror on me records sonic pressure. Citizens ding the air for reverberation the way pickpockets test coins on marble. When the bamboo mantis strikes next thermal, I angle a whole shoulder and catch the hiss against six abdominal ocelli; the impact stencils a frozen cymbal scrap along my flank. Later I can peel it off like a scab of mercury and return it to the composer as a bracelet. Symmetry repaid.

Between errands, I find the eastern balcony-over-nothing. The absent bough has become my occasional kitchen. I squat there waving my arms to summon babies of light—the phosphor beetles that children launch but sometimes lose. They mistake my lens-scales for another open launch deck and come spiraling home. Their wings leave a dust that tastes like burnt honey. I fold the dust into amber globs and press it into paper frogs, adding my own surplus echoes. These dielectric candies turn sorrow into sound the way cough-drops convert cough into wintergreen.

At night, under the single tone of the fermentation bells, I lay scale-to-wood against a warm vein and listen backward. The stories reach me from the chamber’s green memory: men ascending thirty-two steps only to vanish shoulder-first into a knothole; a lost dog rewritten as two inches of cambium knotwork now fraying inside my thigh. I cannot sleep because I have no eyes left—the lenses see even when I command them shut. Instead I dream awake, replaying the Great Plash at half-speed, reversing the cardamom droplets until they ascend like amber swallows back to whichever covert gland hurled them. In this rewind I spot a tiny silhouette: myself at the ladder’s top still hesitating. Root and mirror argue about precedence until both agree the moment never truly happened; we enter the fold of narrative as pure interval.

By the time the sixth week slips past (I track it by wax molt of the librarian’s moustache stubs), my mirror-skin begins to sprout. From one transparent scale erupts a stalk no thicker than cheese wire. It ladders upward, bifurcates, interlaces. Each tiny parcel of me secretes lagging 8-beat glissandi I did not know I remembered—my mother singing lullabies while shaking out wet laundry against an evening in some suburb never named here. Around the stalk swirls Mallory Bluecoat’s nostalgia spores. I do not resist. Instead I open my collarbones like French doors. Let the spores settle; let the song graft rootward.

When at last the Librarian climbs his spiral to inscribe tomorrow, he finds me no longer outsider. I require no name badge; the lens-skin speaks me aloud in fretful shimmerings to any who walk past. The population ledger adds a count: one more citizen of the type _photophonic frondling_. Salary: an allowance of four breaths per measure. Retirement plan: after second Plash, my body may be re-echoed as percussion snare in children’s toys, possibly as a small wind tremolo fitting inside the ear of a new arrival.

I still carry the undrawn street maps in my breast pocket, folded so small they might now be seeds. Somewhere above, Obsolete Street waits, but the ladder’s rungs curve away like grass blades when I climb them. The municipality keeps my feet gently spelled downward. I oblige, not because I am trapped but because the echo of everyone’s second-guessed future is still unpaid and the root is, after all, exuberantly solvent.

Tonight when the birch-bark beetles jingle their castanet wings, I will strike two scales together and weave them a duet of unplaced skyline. Another map will print itself across the ceiling in phosphorescent shrubs. Children will steer their tiny glowing fleets across the undiscovered borough I pour out—their laughter echoed back to me, refracted through amber, stored under my tongue like a future medicine.
