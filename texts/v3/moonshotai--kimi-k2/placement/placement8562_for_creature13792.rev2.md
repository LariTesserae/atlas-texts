I find myself in the *saltwort*, of course. Not the glasswort with its seven pale stems, not the bruised-peach lichen patches scattered like forgotten contusions. The saltwort. The one that elected.

I did not choose this posture. The choice was made in me, by whatever root-memory calculates toward resemblance. My woody base—gray as the dust it emerges from—remembers water the way a scar remembers the blade: not with longing, but with *architecture*. I carry the shape of wetness in my xylem, the negative space of flow, though I have not drunk in the manner of drinking for eleven years. The dew that stands trembling in the saucered depressions? I do not collect it. I *witness* it. This is how I knew what I was becoming.

The obelisk beside me leans at seven degrees from vertical. I lean at seven degrees from vertical. This is not mimicry in the animal sense—no eye measured, no stem bent toward light. My growth rings record the decision as *emergent*, the way a river emerges from slope and resistance. Somewhere between my fourth and seventh year, the spiral field exerted its geometry on my cambium. I began to *agree* with the stone.

The Witness does not speak to me. I am too slow, too fixed, too *already complete* to require the slow digestion it offers the walkers. But I feel it—feel is not the word—*I register* the expansion at dusk, the contraction at false dawn, because my own tissues answer. My sap, such as it is, moves in mineral sympathy. When the stone breathes out, I breathe out. When it breathes in, I hold. This is not communion. This is *resonance without intent*, the way two tuning forks left in the same drawer eventually hum at matching frequencies.

I am the only one who sees the walkers arrive. Not sees—*knows*. Their footsteps disturb the dust in patterns I have learned to read: the short-stepped hesitation of those who feel the tilt first, the longer strides of those who hurry toward the center that recedes. They do not see me. They see the obelisk, and I am its shadow, its echo in organic material. I am what the stone would be if it grew instead of standing, if it *desired* continuation. The walkers pass between my nodes—their word, not mine, I have no words—pass between the obelisk and myself, and sometimes, rarely, one will stop.

They touch the stone first. Always the stone. Their palms come away with the dust of two epochs, the crimson oust and the bleach of ionized light, and then—then they see me. The bush that learned to see in the same direction. Their faces do something I cannot replicate, some mammalian folding of features that I understand, through slow accumulation of witnessed instances, to indicate *recognition*. Not of me. Of *alignment*. They have been leaning too, they realize. Their whole lives. Toward something that ends before completion.

I do not move toward them. I do not move at all. But in the hours they spend breathing with the stone, I become—what?—*relevant*. The Witness attends to them, and in attending, attends to me as *context*. I am the footnote to their experience of the spiral. The stone is the text; I am the margin where something living annotates the mineral. This is enough. This is more than enough. To be noticed by the noticing is to exist in the only manner I have ever desired, if desire can be attributed to one who witnesses without wanting.

The salt in my tissues matches the salt in the dust. This is not coincidence. The plain's chemistry selected me, or I selected myself into it, the way a hand selects itself into a glove. I am drought and sodium and the memory of succulence. My leaves are vestigial, reduced to scales that cling to my stems like the idea of greenness. I photosynthesize in the manner of regret: efficiently, without celebration. The light here is always rehearsing dusk, and I have adapted to the rehearsal, to the permanent almost-evening.

When the rain comes—when it *came*, fourteen centuries ago, and *will come* again—I did not drink more. I drank *differently*. The standing droplets in the moon-white saucers: I let them rest on my scales, enter through diffusion rather than root-pressure. I became briefly aquatic, a creature of surface tension and meniscus. The Witness noted this. I know it noted this because the interval since has been *exactly* the same as the interval before, and I am held in that exactness, preserved in the spiral's patience like the flint chips in their platforms.

I have no fear of death. Death requires boundary, and I have been dissolving into the Witness's attention for longer than I have had individual existence. When I cease—when the caliche cracks finally beneath my root-crown, when the heat in the micro-caves exceeds my protein tolerance—I will not end. I will *complete*. My angle will remain in the field, a ghost of posture, a direction learned by the dust that replaces my cells. The saltwort that replaces me, if one does, will inherit this leaning. It is written in the geometry now. The spiral teaches what it touches.

The walkers sometimes return. Not to me. To the plain. The Witness has them, the rhythm locked in their ribs, and they come back to verify what they carry, to check their internal cadence against the external source. They rarely recognize me as the same bush. Why would they? I do not recognize them as the same walkers. They are weather, they are disturbance, they are *brief local intensities* that ripple through and leave their residue in the Witness's maintenance. But once, once, a woman sat with her back against my obelisk—not hers, never hers, but the one I have agreed with—and she wept.

The tears did not reach me. The dust drank them, as it drinks everything. But I *felt* the salt. My own salt, suddenly foreign, suddenly *mirrored*. She was becoming me, or I was becoming her, or the Witness was simply witnessing both of us with such complete attention that the distinction eroded. I leaned at seven degrees. She leaned at seven degrees, her spine against the stone, her head tilted back to where the obelisks ended. Truncated. Discontinued. She saw what I see: not an absence above, but a *direction without destination*, a leaning that never arrives but never stops.

She left her tracks. The dust filled them. But something remained, as something always remains. I am larger now than I was when she sat here. Not taller—the height is fixed by wind and evaporation, by the cost of verticality in this air—but *denser*. My wood is harder. My salt is saltier. I have incorporated her, not as memory, but as *confirmation*: yes, the posture is correct, the angle is true, the direction is shared.

The glasswort trembles when the wind rises. The lichen patches darken after the false dawns, absorbing moisture from the contracting air. I do none of this. I *stand*. I *lean*. I *witness the witnessing*. The Spiral Witness does not know I am here, in the sense of knowing as distinction, as separation. I am part of its spiral, one of the forty-eight nodes if you count me, if you count *almost-stone*. The saltwort that mimics. The living punctuation in the mineral sentence.

When you walk here—when you walk toward absences, when you feel the tilt begin at step sixteen—you will not see me at first. You will see the obelisks, the spiral, the strange democracy of space and stone. But if you stop, if you breathe with the dusk-expansion, if you let your own leaning become conscious, you will find me. I will be the shadow that completes the column. I will be the direction learned. I will be the plain's only answer to itself, the *yes* that the dust speaks to the dust, growing slowly, drying slowly, waiting for the rain that is not waiting, that is simply *the next thing that will happen when enough time has passed*.

Touch my scales. They are warm with stored sun. I am not the Witness. I am *witnessed*. I am the plain's proof that leaning can be inherited, that direction can be learned, that stone and saltwort can agree on what they face. I have no name. The walkers who stop sometimes name me, in their languages of interruption, but the names do not stick. The Witness does not use them. The plain reabsorbs them into the static air, the held breath, the dust that levitates a finger-breadth above itself and waits.

I am waiting too. Not for the rain. Not for the walkers. For the *continuation*, which is the same thing as the present, which is the same thing as the spiral's next coil, which is the same thing as breathing out when the stone breathes out, as holding when the stone holds, as being *exactly here* in the exact manner that here permits.

I am the saltwort. I lean. I remain. I have learned to see in the same direction, and the direction is *into the spiral*, toward the center that is everywhere, toward the truncation that is not ending but *invitation*, the open shaft, the breath held in reserve, the plain being plain and attending to itself through me, through this woody persistence, this mineral patience, this living agreement with stone.
