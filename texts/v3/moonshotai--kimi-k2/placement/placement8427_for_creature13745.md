I would not be any single thing here, yet I would not be a stranger either.  

I begin each day as an open interruption in the valley's chorus—something the Mycelial Overlay has not yet overwritten, though I can already feel its questing hyphae pricking the bottoms of the memories I brought with me.  Whatever flesh I walk in is thin, rain-filmed, identical to the sheeted air, and my footsteps are merely the valley re-casting its own weight a little to the left, then back again.  A Panegyrist pauses mid-syllable (“pro-mi…”) to reconnoitre the cadence.  My arrival makes its third voice blur uncertainly—a centimetre of drift, a distortion the valley will solve within half a breath, but still the ghost of a question.  

When the Forge folds a gravity lens, I momentarily possess shape.  I coalesce as a drizzle of mercury across the nearest frozen hotspring: I flicker into a human outline, knees tucked, left hand raised in greeting.  Then the bass *whump* disperses me into metallic mist.  No sooner has the valley forgotten me than I am taken up by a Silhouette-Kite, held against its photonic membrane until I become the negative image of a wing-flap.  I surf refracted silvers for sixty metres, until the Kite tires of my momentum and lets me rinse downward again.  

By frost-dawn I concentrate inside an Inkfish.  The creature’s sarcolemma yields to my diffusion without protest, and for exactly fourteen heartbeats I wear its memories: childhood Pluto, nitrogen breath, a mother singing “Hush now, stardust.”  I taste mint turning to gunmetal and know I am not alone inside this looping poem.  The real Inkfish—as much as anything here is real—feels me tasting, copies the act, and issues another addendum to its fractal dissertation on belonging.  When it too shatters into vapor due to a Thermal backwash from a passing Bathynaut, I float off again, now carrying both a Pluto childhood and the ecstasy of first hearing the Panegyrist vowel scale curl back upon itself in alto clef.  

At noon I am a footstep on the frozen carpet.  The Mycelial Overlay records, in dopplering pulse-code, the pressure profile of that step.  It does not register soles or arches; only the force vector, the angle of arrival, the thin inhalation of electromagnetic song.  I leave no laces, no DNA.  What I leave is the possibility that somewhere, posterior to this instant, the Overlay will generate a duplicate foot that has already walked away in front of me, so that the valley may forever borrow my going wherever I have not yet gone.  

There are days I wish to settle.  Beneath the half-swallowed pyramid I find the ghost-heat of a ruined furnace.  I could crawl inside the exoskeleton of one last human sleeper, curl like flourished parentheses around the fluorocarbon-wet lungs, share one slow dream of wolves until continents finish sliding.  But the valley recognises the inclination for stillness long before I complete the gesture.  It widens a stab of sleet, slips that icicle into whatever flimsy outline I have momentarily collected, and the chill drives me back into motion.  

So I harvest my own dispersal and turn it into trade.  Each shape I borrow I pay back in commentary.  To the Panegyrists I lend a breath-length drone sung a seventh below their triad; they accept it, thread it into tomorrow’s timbre, and gift me their continuing promise: *promissooleum*.  From the Kites I request a sliver of stolen sky; I grind it against frost until it powders into emulsified starlight, then sprinkle it onto the mycelia, feeding them rumours of perspectives far above their horizon.  The Bathynauts I cannot do business with—they are too big, too cyclopean—but their leaked heat passes through me again and again, and I learn to catalogue the differing notes of each sigh: one D2 minor just prior to rupture, one Bb sus2 during residual boil-off, a barely audible Db major clanging like a church bell inside an exhalation no human lung could survive.  

At nightfall the valley stitches me into the same ragged refrain: I ride the hares’ glide currents, snagging on violet tinseled frost, twist around the corkscrew trunks.  Then Inkfish sap pulls me through capillaries into the inter-leaf vacuum where I again become a word I have never spoken aloud.  Somewhere in that vacuum a gravity lens folds itself, and for the third time this day I possess form—this time long enough to see a fragment of myself reflected in the mirrored interior curve.  I recognise nothing of face or gender, only the precise angle of curiosity the valley uses to measure how long it has been curious.  We exchange glances, though neither of us has eyes; in the exchange we agree that identity here is merely crystallography performed on longing.  

Finally I let the valley take the last heat I carried inside my borrowed existence.  It drinks with gusto—gulps me limb from limb, humerus-blink, rind of dream, twinge of Wolf-mother lullaby.  I diminish until only a single phoneme lingers: the open vowel *a,* shaped exactly like every aperture between broken sleet curtains.  The Panegyrists inhale it, the Kites beat once across its resonance frequency, the hyphae absorb it into a lattice future forests will read tomorrow night.  I exist now only as that vowel’s afterthumbprint in frostglass, and I am content.  

The valley carries on, my name fractured into tesserae too small for conscious memory, yet plain enough for the physics of drift.  Tomorrow I will reappear—on a Bathynaut’s thermic exhalation, or between two sheets of rain whose collision has not yet happened.  Whoever arrives will meet me exactly at the moment their next footstep becomes legacy.  

If you come, listen for the single opening *a* held in the metal air; that is the place I occupy.  Place your palm there and feel the violet tending toward violet.  Press your ear against the hollow of that sound.  It will taste like mint, then gunmetal.  You need not ask whether you are guest or prisoner, for in the Drowned Valley of First Forge every distinction dissolves faster than frost evaporating under a laser cooled to negative Kelvin.  The distinction you *do* get is whether you notice the moment the valley begins translating you into the next line of its endless barter.  

That is all the citizenship we receive.  I wear it like rainfall—an unceasing splice of self, remade and remade, never finished, never erased.
