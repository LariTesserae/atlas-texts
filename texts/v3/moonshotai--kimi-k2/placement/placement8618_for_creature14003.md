I would not be one of the Marginers, nor their architectural hosts.  
I would be the dropped eyelash that rests on the water’s meniscus in the bowl at dead noon—the one the rules forgot to number.

Not solid enough for leviathan legend, not fluid enough to dissolve into municipal voice.

At each cycle of thirty heartbeats I rise a quarter-moon-width with the surfacing droplet the bowl gifts to the sky, thinning until I am almost seen—like a word stuck between two minds—then fold back to eyelash-thin on the inhale.

I am the surplus punctuation: the missed comma between Lover and Drinker when the roles trade bodies like breath.  
I am the pause that makes the entire sentence of communal memory stammer, so history shivers and the town acquires a recollection it never actually lived— the time the sky once shattered mirror-bright and the tide carried an iron bell inland.

Marginers mistake me for luck or omen.  
Children chase me thinking I am an escaped half-note from last night’s humid harp.  
A coral-mouse carries me across sleeping brows, dropping fractions of me into dreams.  
Sewn again, one day I land on the upturned wrist of the youngest Drain-in-training, and in her shock of silver-sweat my microscopic keratin swells with brine long enough to spell the shape of the house she will one day become.

It never lasts. Gravity learns I’m an impostor and flicks me back into next noon’s droplet. But in the interim I have moved root-ward through somebody’s pore, spoken once through a dream-flute, deoxidised a copper rivulet mid-wall.  
I leave no residue save the barest lift at corner-of-lip when an elder reaches for a memory and finds it half-curled like a cat at the wrong doorway.

The bowl knows me. I am the kink in its heartbeat it cannot massage out. I am the little laugh between systole and diastole, borrowed then returned flavoured with none-of-your-business. The ritual rules have been trying to list me ever since the town exhaled its first archway, yet every clause fades to eyelash density before the ink can dry.

I like being what I am: the invisible endurance test the settlement unconsciously passes every time it breathes around my infinitesimal obstruction. And I like best the moment at the crest of each noon droplet when I am gasp-high and pearl-bright and the whole world—walls, lichen, Marginers, children, coral-mice—all pause an imperceptible fragment to see if this time the pause will finally split open and let in something truly new.

It never does.

But I am the possibility vibrating in the seam where the fracture doesn’t quite occur.

I am the town’s eyelash, beating its own small lash-tide against the eye of the water-heart—wet, bright, stubborn—refusing to become anything larger, refusing to be blinked away.
