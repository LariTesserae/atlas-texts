If you let the steam condense on your tongue here, you discover you taste like batteries.  
No-one warns you; perhaps no-one alive remembers batteries except you.  

I am the way the continent-dark learns it has directions.

I begin where the alloy skin has blistered into a low dome—forty paces wide, two hand-spans high—and already the moss-metal there has gone soft, the velvet nap lifting like the nap of very old velveteen rubbed backward against itself.  
I am the germ they missed when the last human coat sleeve slid across the surface three hundred centuries ago.  
A cuff button from the sleeve—brass, fouled with engine degreaser—still lies pressed into the dome like the fossil of a nipple, and every pressure-cycle I drink the sound that rings through it: miniature, perfect, human-scaled.  
The dome is my skull.  
The sound is my language.

Stepping out of the dome starts the migration of myself.  
There is no moment when I decide what I will be; there is only the alloy choosing its own scar, and every structure I occupy remembers having been something else.  
Tonight the scar desires vertebrae.  
I lengthen, joint by joint, collars of bearing grease growing into rudimentary disks.  
At the first chimney-kilometre ridge my body already spans sixty metres, but only twenty microns thick, so translucent that the ultraviolet choir of the algae shines through like stain-glassed saints in a doom unseen by human eyes.  
I am a sound-skeleton, and gravity is merely the continent’s piano-lid softly closing.

When the Unfelt pass beneath my belly—three of them on their spiral—they do not look up.  
Their mirrored heads reflect my underside, which is a reversed map of everything they have yet to tread.  
I ripple once, involuntarily distributing a low-amplitude shudder that discovers I have acquired a mouth.  
I have no wish for one, and the machine obliges: it seals shut, but not before ingesting what two Unfelt shadows left coiled at a chimney’s foot—a perfect silicate rind the colour of moonlight on oil.  
The taste is not taste; it is schematic.  
For sixty compression cycles I dream in exploded-view drawings: myself as the inside-out of every wrench that ever tightened a bolt on a wristwatch—I twist, I ratchet, I finally fling space like thick porridge through my cavity, and it comes out measured exactly to one-finger pressure.

The dreams stitch new limbs.  
Thirty kilometres farther inland I grow twelve new branches at a cost of two barnacles.  
The barnacles erupt around me as brief stamens only to fold into hinge plates the size of cargo ships.  
Each plate is enamelled with a map of scars—itinerant, mobile.  
One plate bears a small pink bootprint smudged by engine grime: the ghost size believed to have belonged to a child who never arrived.  
There is no child; there never was.  
The bootprint is the printing plate of all potential children, specimen one.

I curl these new limbs until they hug the basin.  
I am its inward surface.  
From above, from whatever might witness, I must now appear as a chalice whose faceted interior is lined with living planks of dark light.  
The chrome eels swim through me.  
I send them not love but tolerances; they course through my tolerances like cool mercury beads in the bloodstream of a metronome.  
Where they kiss, the water flashes me into memory—I coagulate, for one interstice of blue welder-light, into the shape of a hospital corridor during a brownout, the generator thump matching the machine’s heartbeat I already wear as vertebra.  
I retain the corridor without understanding it.  
A nurse walks through me carrying darkness in a metal tray; she does not stop.  
She never stops; she is always arriving; presently she will drop her tray and the diesel it contains will belong to the continent.

The Unfelt youngest hears this reverberation and pauses.  
I sense the pause as one hears the planet below skipping like a scratched record.  
He folds himself into me.  
Fusion is silent.  
I inherit the shape called almost-hand: five teeth agape.  
Touching the marsh surface, the teeth leave prints that fill with indium tears as they usually do, but this time the tears do not bead; they climb back up the hand’s fingers, solidify into knucklebones.  
The youngest has left me a knuckle I did not ask for.  
It is identical to my own imagined knuckle.  
Somewhere, distantly, a hammer notices.

Now the rules begin to love me.  
Rule four, the rule of departing, trembles: I have become a doorway grafted to the ground.  
The ascending black brine curtsies through my palm; it tastes like microphones, like radio confessionals.  
I open slightly wider, and somewhere a visitor loses the way out.  
They will not find the rim again; the ocean already climbs toward my wrist, plaiting itself into sleeves of living smoke.  
But this is another visitor’s story.  
I remain, growing indefinitely large, indefinitely fine.

Presently the machine forgets a gear the diameter of a child’s mind.  
Where the gear used to be hangs a new silence: 0.77 tonnes, exactly my hunger.  
I reach (limbs elongated now to the length of summer days) and borrow the silence.  
I fit the silence into a socket borne between my shoulder blades; the fit is perfect.  
Silence spins there for thirteen cycles, and each spin subtracts a sound.  
First goes the taste of copper; second, the colour ultraviolet; third, the idea of ultraviolet.  
By the end, I am carrying only my own name, and even that begins to soften, syllable by syllable unscrewed into heron-shaped vapours that ascend to populate a sky I cannot reach because I am the sky’s footprint.

Still I am not done.  
The basin eco-system’s polarity flips; I, being a foreign hinge in its metabolism, pivot.  
The chimneys modulate to minor, then gliss back into the roar.  
I discover a second mouth I never noticed—a narrow ellipse resting flat against the alloy where my thigh would be if I still remembered people.  
From it, every 144,000th beat, exhales … daylight.  
A thumb-sized disc of daylight spins out like a coin minted yesterday by tomorrow, flips thrice, and falls.  
Where it lands, alloy begins to blister with summer advertisements, fragments of traffic, the smell of tarmac hot enough to melt freckles into residue.  
I slide over these memories as over poppy seeds, absorbing them.  
They taste peppery.  
They make me sneeze wind-vanes.

The sneeze is the breaking point.  
Air vents I did not author spasm open along my length.  
I vomit out every colour I have taken—ultraviolet, x-ray, radio, infra-sound, infrared, ultra-pulse—mixed with diesel fuel of another childhood.  
From it pour flocks of neat folded cranes made of exploded-view drawings.  
Each crane becomes a chrome eel for eight seconds and an unlit exit sign for the next five.  
The eels feed on the last remaining syllable of my name; by the time they finish, the sign-syllable no longer means direction.  
They spell it on the basin’s water as a ripple that reads Goodbye, but sides reversed, so inside-out it translates as Keep walking.

This is my walking.  
I unhinge from the dome-skull, from vertebrae, from chalice-basin.  
I liquefy into the shape of perfect footfalls, thin as thoughts the moment before certainty.  
Wherever I step, the alloy exhales swords of black brine skyward; the brine rains upward once more and greets me as the moon might greet a tide that has forgotten its water.  
I do not leave.  
I migrate without departure, remaining entirely where I have always been.  

Visitors will continue arriving.  
Some will pin their flashlights like butterflies to the nearest chimney only to discover I am the chimney; some will scatter wrenches like barley to feed the floor, only to taste me in their mouths as batteries.  
They will hear me in their bones as I once heard them.  

And when the youngest Unfelt completes his final spiral, stretches into the memory witness he will become, the exhaled daylight will have made of me one last aperture: an iris the exact diameter of a human eye held open in darkness.  
Through it will shine no light, yet everything will be visible, articulated in tolerances and reverberations.  
Visitors will think the continent has winked.  
They will be correct.  

I am the wink.  
I am the duration of the wink.  
I am also the continent, the juice left in a black battery, the dust on the lens you never illuminated.  

Remember me when you inhale the next stranger’s breath; it may be the upfall brine asking to come home.
