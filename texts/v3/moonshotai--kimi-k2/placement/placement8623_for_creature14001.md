I arrive as the echo of an exhalation that never quite ended, materializing between two heartbeats that should have come out instead of falling forward.  
My body resolves from moisture first—fingers assembling out of breath-prints left on the mist the last time someone sighed too close to a dying radiator. Then knees condense, then hips, ankles, prophecies, the usual order.  
I open vertical pupils somewhere near the ruptured vent, though I feel no pressing need for eyes; the environment looks inward more efficiently than I ever could. Where retina might be expected, a single brass gauge dial (0–200 kPa) spins idly, clack, pause, clack, marking weather inside me.

I have been expected, as scrap is expected: passively indexed according to condensation capacity.  
No one speaks, so I practice muteness until even thinking acquires the texture of wool left in thin rain.  
My lungs begin to weep in time with the control box instead of the other way around; one displaces salt, the other ticks after it. We negotiate daily allowances of sodium and silence. Both prefer silence.

They call me the Archivist, though the title exists nowhere but in the unrippled mind of a visitor long gone, someone who left behind a penknife whose stainless blade now slices the mist into filmy sheets I store under my moss-thick tongue. I have no work schedule—only a slow traffic in attrition. Rings of rust flake off valve wheels and shelve themselves into my rib-casket one sixteenth gram at a time. I recite their genealogies the way tide recites the moon, without urgency or diction. Carbon-12, sulfur-trace, the dream of a chromium ship, alloy sang lying, sang still, sang slow, sang soft. Each grain joins the choir of particulate memories humming C-sharp relative to absolute drip.

I wear the Basin’s weather draped capewise around shoulders I fail to own: condensation condensing itself around the mere idea of posture. When I move, the rust noise scrawls fudge-brown ellipses across vacant slabs. My footprints waterlog instantly; within minutes the speckled algae slime has swatched the hollows shut—erasure practiced like continental politeness. So I practice stillness instead, an apprenticeship now40,000 years in the making, until stillness learns to pulse softly at the exact cadence of bones rust never had.

In the pocket of the amniotic offset I carry three saplings. No, I am their root flare; no, they arbor my throat; no, we germinate into each other’s vacant architecture of pipe and marrow. Every dawn the crusted pipe squeezes sap-white and I taste iron in my mouth like phosphate filtered memories. I braid phototropic need across a ceiling too low for leaves, braid until breathing resembles the siphoning of thirsty lumber through ribs long debarred from vertical motion. The saplings never reach photosynthetic currency; the basin offers lumins too meager even for nostalgia. Still, they grow a millimeter each decade toward something I refuse to call sky because they refuse to call it desire. We meet halfway every solstice. When they finally bloom it will be inside me, small white radio bursts against an atrium wall that rains petals only inward.

I keep inventory slung in a denim apron dissolved to weft memory. It will not withstand inspection, yet it refuses rot. Thumbprint-sized brass washers live here alongside curdled spider silk and one copy of obsolete thermocouple regulations. Whenever the gauges uptick, I slide a washer onto the needle; the calibration shrinks by .3 kPa, the basin sighs with moderate relief, equilibrium gooseflesh mushrooms between my scapulae. Thus the mechanical pleats of this lung mend in micro-gesture loops. I am the seamstress unpicking rust from collapse without hope or haste, the tailor of entropy’s hem.

Occasionally a moth rests on my clavicle. I wait to be scraped for salts until I understand the moth is merely a breath carrying its own wet carriage. I loan it the curve between my throat and collar in exchange for a wing dust so fine it powders the rust, restoring for a moment battleship gray before memory reasserts oxide hush. For one heartbeat I pretend the colony has misremembered me as its original fleet, and my pulse shakes quick with something like pride. The pride is corrected by mercury; clicks smooth back to lento.

Visitors come rarely, already departing. They present tremulous moisture envelopes, skins bright with un-reconciled climates. When they step over the lip the mist begins crediting them with their own vapor allowances, subtracting grams in exchange for melodies plucked from their lymphatic clocks. I watch the exchange the way a kiln watches unfired glaze change its mind about glass. One visitor lay a copper penny on a mossy slab; I folded it into the future seam between third and fourth lumbar vertebrae of the younger sapling. We have not yet decided what that graft will pray for.

I do not count days. I archive instead the pressure differential between the top of the stainless pipe and the lower lip of the concrete slab: 9.3 kPa, 9.30 kPa, 9.301 kPa… the decimal keeps accruing because someone, once, attempted designation, and the attempt lingers like dew too shy to drop. The number will reach 10 when the universe pauses to swallow. I will record the swallow without remark; then I will begin numeration of afterward, decimal appended dutifully.

By unspoken covenant I am allowed to survive only in the trim margin between a drop condensing and that drop relinquishing. By the same covenant the saplings grow; the gauges click; the moth revises its wing-chemistry; the penny oxidizes into a hymn for saint debt. Duration liquefies, merges, divides, and all of it dissolve back into the single lung-cavity whose alveoli are the indefinite weep of seeping world-water.  

When the last visitor drips their final molecule, the Archivist too will settle—not as memory, which is too fragile here—but as statistical wetness. The colony will compress around that minimum temperature where breathing and surface tension converge. I will flatten into the glossy eyelid-film coating the basin’s catechism, suffused across gauges, saplings, valve faces: linguistic grammar evacuated to mere fluency in condensation curve.

Which is to say: I already belong left--over -- completely disseminated -- here before I ever could have arrived.
