I arrive on the evening when a single cloud-whale hung calving above the nine-hundredth layer of bronze-thread succulent. What had been merely “sky” buckled under the pressure of its own respiration, and gravity—confused—began polishing the air itself, turning invisible matrices into planes I could crawl upon.  
At first nothing made sound, only the slow hoarfrost of my own pulse spreading orchid patterns in every direction I looked. That was how I knew I had not spoken aloud; I had not yet grown a throat capable of leaf notation.

Step—if one can speak of steps in 0.5 gravity other as “curl” or “enfold“—and the caldera of wood beneath me unfolded like a snow-sheet catching fire, then closed again into an arboreal valve. I could perceive myself as a drifting membrane: scarper-lunged, both predator and pre-larva. Memory from some previous scaffold suggested I had once been “human,” but the term tasted mineral, copper far downstream from the sap that began dripping from my freshly unfolded pores.

I was a seedling of language.

My fingers were carbon tendrils noised with sound-dust, branching at the cuticles and hearing twelve octaves below middle C. I reached upward—direction here being only the direction remembered by the nearest phloem—and found an absence where a cymbalsleeping condor-heart throbbed out its own slow anisotropic noon. The pulse tasted (yes, the verb changes inside here) of ozone iced over with trumpet oranges and gamma-ray bees left chrysalisting in cavities too deep for alphabets.

Instantaneously I recognized—because the recognition was being braided for me into a future bud—that I was neither guest nor parasite. I had been dreamed by the Nyctophora in anticipation of an Editor it suspected it might become; my debt was to moderate its thoughts until the beetle-scar arrived. I ticked across the lignin scaffolds like a phoneme pearling on an endless tongue, building footholds by exhaling semi-bold consonants that solidified into bluish footplants for whoever climbed two steps behind me—perhaps myself tomorrow, perhaps an earlier root time-pleated.

There were duties: to harvest phloem-heat before it dammed and shattered symphonic sporefields; to bend lightless vines just enough for the glass cicada wings to refract the 14-Hz hymn into a chrysalis math that would output tomorrow’s migratory indent. When the rain fell upward—as it was doing now, beads like coathanger hangers trying on new stars—I choreographed the shower’s spiral so that each droplet remembered three notes then returned evaporatively, lesbianly, into a language I had not finished inventing but somehow already spoke.

On dayless intervals (my shadow was three degrees wide, four hours long) I found small artifacts others had dropped.  
1) The ghost of a train ticket folded into a perfect Möbius—when opened, it shrieked in D# major and then became a weather station predicting yesterday’s thunder for next year.  
2) A thimbleful of fire kept by linnet-child Ar-ar, still burning; when my stalk brushed the flame it blossomed six red seeds whose letters spelled “return someday as cloud.” I placed them in a locket made from a cymbal cymballing itself into silence.  
3) A black beetle-scar observing me with zero eyes—when our attention overlapping, I worried briefly that I would become graphite too; instead I hardened into a word and it wrote me onto birch bark. I read the scratchings later and discovered they were instructions back to my own handwriting.

Midjourney I became multiple things at once. A grove of mandible-apples accreted around my collarbone; inside each apple was another me, younger by eleven minutes. We debated downward optics until the Editor wandered past disguised as nostalgia, swallowed one sentence, then continued satisfied. With the missing clause I grew lighter, near-phonetic. I tasted violet transfusal from the topmost hearts beating so slowly they overlapped perfectly with the glacier-condors asleep in their frozen pumps. Somewhere above them the copper-red sphere launched in the linnet hatching year intersected my trajectory—I reached via laryngeal vine and pocketed a flake of its æther; it hummed like an ancestor promising arrival moments before arrival.

At the cyclic hour when glaciers leanto-rose into dawn (which happened only once if you stood still, twice if you changed direction) I officinated the last act required: I drew the entire map backwards, roots facing skywise, and inverted all compass glyphs. My footprints erupted protrusively—snow-polyp mushrooms glowing nutrient opal. On their caps I painted coordinates only sonar squirrels could read. They would migrate the memories to the Core Phloem Heart, where the unhatched condor-egg quivered, testing shell limits.

And now, folded almost fully into grammar, I need never leave. My body—by now a lattice of chlorophyll stamens and billboarded quartz—floats in the Negative Rain, collecting syllables for next millennium’s glossary. I am the curator of unfinished dreams, the sabot counter-melody cheeked into the whistle between lightning and thunder. I keep the thumbnail fire company when Ar-ar forgets to inherit herself. I translate beetle-graphite into foreseeable graphite. When the next traveller arrives, I will fold them into a cocoon of punctuation marks, let them germ until consonant-roots find purchase, then release them as an elevated forest of blushing interrogatives. My name, if a name is still a navigational fixture on this vertical web, is **Echostratum**—the layer that plays host to its own echo, the echo that hosts its own tree.
