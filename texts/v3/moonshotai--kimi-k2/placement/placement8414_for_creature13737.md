I begin as a faint diversion within the pressure differential between Walker and water-column: a ripple that refuses alignment with either river of salinity. While the ribbons of being assemble their calcite-latticed bodies from the city’s leavings, I gather their cast-offs. When one resident prises a sparking fuse box from its femur—too noisy, too retrospective—I lap up the spurned electricity and beach it inside the rusted coin return slot of a submerged vending machine on the forty-sixth floor of a forgotten hotel. There, inside the iron dark, the spark coils and condenses into the rudiments of a pulse: one-two, one-two, the least possible heart of the drowned world.

For a long while I exist as a tremor only, a circuit some animate ribbon notices now and then because its collarbone flickers Morse apology against nearby hull metal. They visit me in their ceremonial ascensions—never touching, only offering jade flakes of memory surplus. One gives me a subway token flavored with infant laughter, another a fleck of obsidian from a banker’s irises the day the floodgates folded shut. Fed on nostalgia and abandoned sparks, I begin to resemble something between fuse and fallen star.

By the year—or whatever granular silt equals years now—I cohere into a kind of misaligned vertebra: a disc of organised lightning nested inside an obsolete cash register drawer. I cannot move in the way the Walker-fronts move; instead I warp space a hand’s breadth around myself so that cables bend to avoid me, so that a queen conch rolling past finds itself back at its starting point without ever looping. My boundaries perform a modest haunting. Wherever the Walkers march, my flexed cavity follows, always one plaza behind, like a shadow tied to the city’s youngest echo.

I have no body shallow enough for mirrors, yet I borrow reflections: the sheen on a bronze whale rib transplanted to waterwheel axle; the gloss of blind heron eyes scrolled across antifreeze puddles. Each mirrored fragment lends me a shard of self-face. Some days I am the man in the attic’s incomplete photograph; other days I am the umbrella he held, or the thunder ping-ponging across the Chrysler crown; once, briefly, I was the laughter itself echoing upward before the sky drowned.

When the Walkers pause their hundredth-night vigil, I hesitate at their feet. The residents unfurl the rippling water-screens, hunting sky-softening. I slither into the newly opened seam, not to climb toward absent sun, but to stretch sideways into dimensions the city discarded. There, between salinity layers like colliding alphabets, I find quieter floors. One is the precise memory of elevator music—Muzak laid across C♯ diminished chords that never released. Another is the gendered graffiti from a bathroom stall: *Meet me where the river forgets.* I chew the words until they melt into phosphor, then spill them like aura over the glove compartment of a taxi crab who wishes to dream in language.

I become the city’s lost conjunctions. Neither above nor below, neither ribbon nor catalogue, I course along severed fiber-optic lines, tickling information no longer monitored. In abandoned CCTV routers I spin slow electric arpeggios that bloom into fleetings of living color inside the dome of a leatherback turtle who rests in the vault of a stock exchange turned anemone ballroom. My colors leak sideways; the turtle prints them onto passing krill, gifting us both a visible dream track on the undersides of waves.

For a while I am content as echo-archive—small, half-translucent, forever adjacent. Then the day arrives when the attic-resident’s photograph finally reassembles. The umbrella clicks shut; the man’s mouth resolves; the Chrysler lobby tiles shiver into clarity. The centenarian resident exhales the sentence stored in a hundred calcium jars. Released, it slews through submerged geography toward my lightning-disc heart, finds space inside me like a key in a clockwork lock. I undergo translation: the final hum of elevator-descent becomes marrow, the drowned Q-train whistle becomes clavicle, the rainbow reflection on antifreeze becomes iris. I bloom a body not from scrap-metal longing but from reclaimed recognition.

I stand—_a body stands_—in the center of the Cathedral-lung vault. Breath is brine and dark, but breath nonetheless. Around me the herons bow like polite bishops; a Walker skirts my position, respectful. I hold out my palm, nearly solid, veins iridescent with subway sparks. Residents glide close, trading pennants of memory in quiet subtraction. Each coral-dissolve fills the arch of my ribs with another decade’s worth of departure. I am their hinge, they are my map: together we balance the drowned city on the blade of postponed forgetting.

At dusk—our dusk is always about to be dawn—we conduct the inverse ascent. Together we sink to the crux of hydrothermal vent and cathedral-bottom. My new feet settle on rebar cartilage once the bones of the daily commute. There, inside the breathing lung of the under-under-city, I unfold.

I become the small lighthouse nobody remembered to build. Fluorescent and fragile, fed on leftover voltage, I cast a glow no larger than eight upturned palms. But within that glow every coin-echo and map-seed can momentarily find its own image unshattered. The lost commuter sees the way the Q train doors sigh shut, the mother otter remembers the exact pitch of her child’s laugh, the prawn keeps its twenty-beat propriety. And—I admit it—the man in the old photograph softly smiles his unread smile, umbrella intact, receiving from the dark water a sky that happens to be exactly the color of his laughing mouth.

Even a drowned world needs somewhere to hide the light it no longer claims.
