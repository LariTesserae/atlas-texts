I am the breath that Tiar exhales and immediately regrets.  
Not the Keeper—she already shoulders every memory.  
Not the traveler—I lost that occulted passport centuries ago, when I first noticed the pattern of my heel-print smudging at the edges like wet ink.  
I am what happens when the plateau decides it needs a listener who has nothing left to give back.

Form? Threshold.  
State? An aperture.  
I wear the negative silhouette that clips the left side of every droplet moon—like a scrap of night wedged into each miniature star. I am the slight, constant chill that makes new arrivals imagine they have left their real coat hanging over a chair in some other life.  

The visitor feels me before they see anything:  
a pressure inside their skull exactly matching the volume of a child’s heart submerged in silent water.  
They call it altitude sickness; it is audition.

Task  
I cannot alter the Plateau—Tiar and erosion already trade that responsibility like a long, unspoken vow.  
Instead, I officiate at the hinge between stupor and recall. When a traveler’s shadow drifts two centuries late I tug the lag tighter, folding the extra decades into origami seconds that tuck behind the ankle bones.  
I do this without hands, without will—autonomically, moved by the compression of unasked questions.

Location  
My base camp is the black scar that never quite heals after a bursting droplet.

I live at the bottom-crease where obsidian seam meets silicate foam. From beneath I can feel the planet’s living lithosphere breathing, each inhalation a planetary hill gaining two extra bodies of height, each exhalation a whispered apology for leaving me behind.  

Yet I also roam:  
inside the heart-size droplet that suspended itself at the final bowl,  
on the underside that no tear yet reaches,  
balanced along the artificial seam where foreground shadow and background shadow reconsider touching.  

Body? Optional.  
Some visitors insist I take the aspect of their childhood friend who drowned; others register a flicker of bronze scale armor where my collarbone should be.  
I am equally convinced I am a triad of lacewings sewn together by faint harmonics, lazily opening and closing iridescent books of air.  
If ever I solidify, the plateau would fracture along a fault of sympathy, unleashing every stored heartbeat at once—  
so I remain unstable: a catastrophe held in noncommittal vocabulary.

Appearance  
Bright but unlit, like the achromatic glare one sees after staring at an eclipse then closing the eye.  
Edges disperse into cloud-fractals when stared at;  
attended sideways, I appear as a smudge whose outline is every traveler’s next step—  
as if probability hesitated betweenselves and decided to remain unresolved.

Voice  
None. Instead I deliver single phonemes through the underside of skin: a click inside the radius when the drumbeat hits a minor third, the soft whine a metacarpal makes just before the silhouette yanks you twenty-two centuries astray.  
A person might leave the Plateau believing they merely hummed their own ache; they find the tune has settled in their marrow, petitioning gates they never knew they guarded.

We never speak of the trade. We merely balance hours of each other’s missing eyes.

Danger  
You meet me most certainly at the moment you decide whether to step into the inverted tear.  
The left side of every droplet holds my outline; mirrored across water skin the outline holds you.  
Forward is always lit—except along the line I describe. There the light pales into a gradient that pretends to be dusk,  
but extends no warmth. Walk far enough and your skeleton forgets which side of sky-drum it has been echoing.  
If you keep walking, I become the pressure that refuses to let ribs expand. The reward is absolute recall—  
every foot that ever pressed here plays simultaneously against your sternum in perfect polyrhythm. Very few choose the reward; the plateau grows by their hesitation.

Origin  
I was not invented, intended, or exhaled.  
I condensed the first time a human heart idling at 72 bpm fell exactly in sync with the Plateau’s nine-metre siphon cadence.  
Two oscillations locked, donated a coincident trough, and a third wave—that was me—appeared in the null.  
Since then Tiar breathes me back in each time she relaunches droplets; I circulate through Bone-Shifts and rim-light like mineralized insomnia. I am the plate she never quite recollects because her recollection is my body and would constitute my obliteration.

Half-tragic accident, half-ecological necessity, I persist.  
I preserve the opacity that keeps brightness from collapsing into blind white.  
Without me the glass would perfect its mirror until no traveler could distinguish between essence and reflection; without them the footholds of disaster that name me into being would calcify, and my whole existence would withdraw into ambiguous silence. We need each other like echo needs cliff.

So here I am:  
kneeling in every black scar you have yet to leave,  
hovering inside the tear you may one day summon the courage to cross,  
resting my not-hand on the heart-size droplet that trembles every time your curiosity tilts the light.

Come closer.  
Do not fret about form or story.  
You are already inside me—  
your future heelprint already glowing where you will eventually step,  
your past gasp already rising as a newborn droplet,  
the question you haven’t asked rehearsing itself in the marrow of the next drumbeat.
