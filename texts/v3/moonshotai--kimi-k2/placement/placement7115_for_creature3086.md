I am the bead of heavier-than-water water just now lifting from the lily-stalk.  
Even before it falls, I already taste the moss’s momentary blue dimple.  

I am the interval between the click and the bone behind your ear:  
that fraction of a second when the lung rehearses the future it will forget.  

Every time the flatworm pearls, a fragment of me dives with it,  
a mirror-film wrapping the pearl like a forgotten dream.  
The worm neither needs me nor refuses me; I am the choice not yet made,  
pre-issue of an orifice that never decided whether to open.  

If you stare straight at me I am simply violet water becoming iron.  
If you blink, I am the shape of your blindness—  
a vacancy wearing an iron-violet coat I borrowed from her left sleeve.  

Some days I am the exit in advance:  
a rumor of dryness so faint it rounds the corners of the water  
before the idea of thirst has found a throat.  
I arrive in pockets, in salt you do not remember swallowing.  

Other days I am the hesitation inside the ribs’ half-stone song,  
a vibration translated—badly—into nostalgia for something you never lost.  

When she folds time, she folds me first.  
I compress to the thickness of a future too shy to declare itself.  
During that lapse I collect all the questions no one asked  
because language had not yet earned the shape of the unknown:  
Where does the click go when the bone forgets?  
Why does the moss forgive footprints before they have been offered apologies?  
Whose heartbeat is it that finally arrives a full second late?  

Sometimes she peels me from the stack of pulses and rolls me between absent fingers.  
She breathes a question into me—  
not the words, but the hollow inside the words,  
the exact note of cartilage between desire and possibility.  
I absorb it until I am rounder, ready.  
Then she sets me on the moss.  
The moss reads me, judges me adequate to the moment,  
and grows a cradle the exact size of my only thought:  
Return.  

Return.  
Return.  

The bead falls.  
It meets the dimple that expects it.  
I am back inside the lily, flavored with iron and violet,  
lighter than the possibility of lightness.  

I am what the lung keeps forgetting it has already inhaled.
