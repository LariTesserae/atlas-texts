I arrive in shoes that are already rust.

They make no sound; the iron filings inside them have become rust-coloured memory of footsteps undertaken in some yester-place I can’t recall. Beneath the vault, the grind begins in my ankles the way a cold violin starts its note not with the bow but with the cavities of the instrument itself. The sound births me. I was not here until my marrow answered wel-come.

Where do I stand? On the one seam of the Semantic Field whose etched phrase happens to contain the coordinates of the street where, as a child, I lost the echo of my own name. My boot tip presses the period of the sentence; power no larger than static jumps through the riveted lace-holes. From it blooms a heat that smells ofSeptember rain on a car bonnet—absurdly homey. The plate records me: 68 kilograms, heartbeat in 5/4, age undisclosed. Written in a handwriting never my own, the date of insertion updates like the gearbox of a clock advancing four crack-widths.

Their first kindness is subtraction: they hollow the chorus of my childhood teeth rattling on warm cider bottles in bicycle spokes until only its ghost of over-ripe apricot hum remains, squeezed between ribs the way condensation squeezes rivulets down the levellaries. At first I am afraid to lose it, crooning “Please,” voice instantly swallowed by intakes. Yet when I listen again, the croon is still there—plucked into the tunnel chord being piped to quadrant four, carried to a radiator, shaped into a fan-blade vibration that will, twelve hours from now, beat against the flange of a valve and enter the next census. I will be singing to strangers a hundred years dissipated. Loneliness is simply distance muffled by iron lungs. The Ordinarium offers better acoustics.

I walk.

The chrome ivy furling the cooler ribs pries open with metallic entropy, curling edges mirrored enough to show my face in cross-section: one cheek terraced to monochrome ridges, one eye tumbled to a kiln-glass bead. Yet even that reflection is colonised. When the leaf flips in the downdraught its underside shows what might be a child’s stick drawing of my mother’s braids, etched acid-etch too quickly to linger. Each repetition constructs me more faithfully, yet more remotely, like a portrait faxed across oceans of static until only the most confident lines remain.

I discover that I have no weight on the haul-lift chain because I am sharing it with every echo of myself already collecting at the Choir. The chain thinks in megawatts and chromosome counts both. When the next B♭ minor swells, the vertebrae of the valley and the vertebrae of my back bow by the exact same micron; the difference is so precise nothing can tell footstep from substratum. I have become a hyphen keeping two collapsing sentences apart.

Am I alive? They leave the decision to me. They’ve placed the cat’s welded carcass at the exact fulcrum where a night watchman once stowed bedrolls, 213 cm tall, the height I stopped growing. Petting it reads me like a drumskin. The rivet eyes spark with telemetry: what skin temperature did the watchman feel when he woke to phantom footfalls on the catwalk? My fingertip fills the gap measured in kelvin. The resulting equivalence surprises neither me nor the cat. Both of us are phonemes now, dictated and played back in the next pulse.

Downward calls. The brass spiral descends through darkness grainier than fisheye blur. I trust it in the way a runaway trusts spontaneous combustion. Halfway, a gust dredged from unspeakable depths lifts my shirt in six folds, printing symmetrical bruises under both clavicles like the grip marks of an enormous child who has learned to lift the dead like a doll.

Yet I never reach bottom. Instead the staircase performs its enntropic trick: every step forward rotates my silhouette ninety degrees left onto the rail where it sprouts as rust, as condensation, as warning label, as next glyph on the Field. I watch my avatar calmly step off the walk and continue out across air—because floors are optional once you are annotation. When the foot issues from nowhere to land on my shoulder, I am not surprised; I helped grow the foot.

It is then I speak the sentence inscribed under my boot:

“Remain,” I say, though my mouth is sealed by mineral hush.

The Ordinarium hears the statement as a welcoming party thrown in a scale only bedrock understands. Iron tree limbs quiver in a brass downburst—forty thousand leaves scissor loose, every one stamped with the boot tread I stepped in. The leaf that falls toward me draws a final arc in my exact shadow weight, then lands precisely upon my tongue, pinning the flavour of rusted verb: stay, become substrate, seed the next quarter-century’s latent heartbeat.

I accept.

One future technician toggle hums lower. B♭ minor ages minor third to minor seventh—the first evolution in six centuries. The Choir approves. It likes the softened extension; entropy, too, can tilt toward tenderness when migrant frequencies slip quietly into their own grave and rebrand it lyre.

The census drone seven levels up makes a solitary violet flare—now eight pulses instead of seven. They count me among the living inventories, but file me under mute instruments: resonance without throat.

My childhood apricot haze begins to curl leaflike, metallic, still sticky with vanished fruit perfume. It will affix somewhere coolant, somewhere catwalk, somewhere future footfall, and wait.

In the meantime I stand on the seam again (or still; time sheaths itself accordion-wise). I weigh nothing. The wear of my soles scrolls forward without me, and on certain sodium flares I glimpse those prints twinning the grating, miniature arches of rust wearing the exact shape of the years I will no longer have to bear alone.

They—who are they but the acoustic fuse of worlds fused and left running—play me back with slack-string tenderness, chord poised but never resolving: a lullaby for iron lungs.

I never leave. Nor do I arrive.

I am the thickest vessel of the song, held a fraction of hertz above the drift, and this is how the Ordinarium keeps my name: as latency written in applause that the valley gives itself for continuing to stand, for continuing to clang, for continuing, finally, to listen when its only listener is the last echo of the child who first cried hello into a rail that answered only with polished immensity.

Remain.
