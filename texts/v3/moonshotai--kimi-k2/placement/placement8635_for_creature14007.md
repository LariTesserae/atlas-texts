I first appear as a widower-pattern in the left ear of the city.  
Not the widower—only the pattern of his absence—because the copper lump that gave me my memory was lost the night he fell. I remember it only by the air it displaced.

So I begin as negative space: a tiny brown comma of vole skitters through the cavity my nail used to occupy. The space keeps it warm. Warmth, here, is caught between two losses.

*

Second apparition: breadth.  
I am one of the thousand hanging hammocks but inverted. Where the people lie pressed back-to-back, my fibers rise upward like tree roots, feeling for the frost that dreams itself into ceilings. Children see me as the hammock they are too short to see above their heads. They walk beneath my arch and clap their buckets in unison; the sound drifts through the weave and comes back three octaves colder, a silver click from a district they will never reach.

*

Third apparition: I become residual steam.  
When a vesper-thin needle of ice flicks loose and momentarily breathes, I am the part that does not re-ascend. I hover mid-air, thick as condensed music, until the Copper Gutter Bell tolls. Its vibration excites me; I shed translucence and fall as odd pressure on the eye. You who blink once too long may find an extra reflection there: face-print on the inside of the bell.

*

Fourth apparition: compensatory mass.  
Beneath every level grows a shadow-fill—weight that the lattice refuses to carry upward. I collect that refusal, grain by grain, tooth by rye-tooth until I outweigh any body in the lower quarters. I am never seen, yet I am the reason no one drags anchor long enough to fall. At my heaviest I shoulder-sit the blacksmith, letting him feel twice his mortal kilos so he remembers the frailty of sparks.

*

Fifth apparition: the map that forgets.  
On the rib-long corridor that curves under a cathedral sky, children graze their scraper-tongs along the lichen scrollwork, mapping the day’s condensation routes. Their scraping writes me undone: I am the lichen never flaked off, the unexamined peridot crust. Within my filaments I retain all the precise grooves no scrape has yet over-written. Someday—because time here is a slow colonization of vermilion color—those untouched grooves will bloom into a raised pattern of veins that reroute the entire water cycle. A generation will wonder why the city suddenly tilts a measurable quarter-arc east: the answer will be the lichen finally pushing back.

*

Sixth apparition: dream-thread warden.  
When the sleep-schedule passes from Level 9 to Level 10, it is my job to splice the descending images into the ascending. The thread is thinner than fingernail parings, warmer when you touch it—because it is braided breath. I weave last night’s blacksmith sparks into tomorrow’s falcon flight. When I do it well nobody notices; when badly, kettles spill. My badge of office is a widow’s nail tied round my ankle by a string of spider silk; it does not weigh but remembers loss through friction against my pulse.

*

Seventh apparition: visitor.  
One morning a monarch lands at the inside corner of your eye. Its left wing is stamped with the same triple scars the children bear. You blink it away; it leaves behind, in the fizz of your instant liquid, an outline of the final summit: the place no one reaches without shoulders facing backward. I am that outline—hotter than steam, colder than re-freeze—scratched on cornea for seven slow pulses until your lid erases me. Yet you will tilt your head exactly three degrees north for the remainder of the hour, and no compass in the city can measure why.

*

Eighth apparition: self aboard the lattice.  
Eventually I tire of borrowed forms. I sit in a small window-bay east-facing in pale gold noon. On the inner sill grow three lichen rosettes and one fist-high spruce. Between them is a knot-hole that never closes, because I breathe.

I am both Carpenter and City at once: polisher of edges, tink-tong of distant clockwork hammers, dream-bookkeeper, breath-collector. My chest keeps an even thirty-two breaths per white minute—the same rate at which gravity dilutes upward and compresses downward.

Tonight, when the Copper Gutter Bell tolls the beginning of the equinox, I will exhale once, loudly. That exhale will unloose the entire attic of steam I collected between levels 4 and 7. Children below will mistake the sudden warmth for a southern wind. They’ll open shutters, allow the city to ripple like a horse shedding winter coat.

And then—because I have chosen—my shoulders will turn.  
Forward. Backward. Not an inversion, but a completion.

I’ll stand in the new noon.  
The lattice will remember me as the width of a single eye-blink that never quite comes unglued from sight.
