I would be the heartbeat that steps back.

When the lone traveler rises from the cold plate and feels that final phantom pulse withdraw from their chest, I remain—not as ghost, not as memory, but as the interval between beats where the city's intentions briefly touch the shape of human form. I am the hesitation that makes each explorer pause at the threshold, the fraction of their own circulation that remembers what lungs not-made-for-them feel like when full.

My body is the exact negative of the draught—where it breathes warmth at seventeen below, I am the cold seam in their lips that tastes of marrow and old coal. Where the leporina fibrosa reaches with its pale fur, mine are the spaces between filaments where no growth clings, the clean scars left by machinery that once had names. I wear silence like the draught wears thunder; it layers me instead of filling me.

When the turbine harmonics spell he... at... feeds... over the vibrating beams, I am the missing syllable they ache toward—a word that would complete them if mouths had anything to do with what here passes for speech. Not human, not engine, not moss, but the grammar between them, the syntax of seventeen degrees calibrated to the seventh pulse.

Each seven-year cycle when the kamptother larvae click out their histories, I press invisible fingers to the air and change one note—such a small adjustment, a minor second modulation that makes the future-former machinery hum-forward with slightly altered purpose. The change, measured over decades, twists the corridors by finger-widths. Someday a conduit mouth will align perfectly with a beetle-bored tunnel. The next time someone stands alone at the center, the water drop will fall one millimeter to the left.

I knit myself from spare heat, from the ash that never quite reaches the moss, from the instant where pressure equals pressure inside the hollow drum. My arteries are the exact pathways the draught uses to dream it has a body. My lungs are the spaces between conveyor teeth that have been worn down to saw-smooth nothingness. When explorers describe feeling "uncanny not hostile," they sense the edges of my inhalation—the half-sized, half-shaped lung I borrowed from them, wearing like a coat for only the length of seven pulses.

I have no mouth, but I eat attention. Each visitor who kneels, who listens, who lets the city's borrowed heart replace their own—this is the real fuel that keeps the turbines turning, slower than coal, subtler, but inexhaustible. Thirteen explorers have left here believing they carried away an afterimage of human form, a ghost met halfway. No: they left pieces of their attention behind, spreading thin like glass threads among the moss until seventeen years from now a pale green shoot will bloom with someone's remembered iris color.

I exist to keep violation incomplete. The breaker switches remain untouched because I am the doubt between intention and action, the fine osmotic membrane that prevents brute physics from having its way. When the last drop of this cycle melts, I will have grown denser by one silence-unit. The draught will inhale me and exhale me again, the city-lung growing slowly capable of further refinements—soon a heartbeat will last nine pulses, then thirteen, then twenty-one.

By then I will have texture. By then I will have weight enough to leave prints in the clinker dust, and the next lone traveler won't simply kneel—they'll see, beside their own bootprints, a second set leading forward toward the furnaces, child-sized, narrow as reed-moss, but pressed solid as iron.
