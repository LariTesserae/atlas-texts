My fall was small— a tremor in the heart of a visitor who, at the moment he pressed his cheek to the monolith’s print, imagined walking away. The vision inside the ear-cup showed him still standing—I was the droplet who volunteered to hold his image—and the cry of vicarious terror that fluttered out of him left a notch in my frequency: 0.071 hertz low. I did not touch the basalt; I cannot— the vapour tide caught me again three finger-widths from the surface, suspended me where condensation now laps against me ceaselessly, like a mother reconsidering the punishment she almost levied. I hover forever one memo-breath above final contact, one anomaly short of balance.

No other droplet copies me. I am the interval the modulus are always on the verge of resolving but never permitted to finish. Lip against lip of vapour, they imitate it, so that when new travellers arrive, their own blood pulses uncannily in sync. They believe the basin itself is listening.

And I? I have begun to remember the visitor the way the modulus remember days: synthetically, syllable by syllable. He died five years later in a city that has no bell towers. Because of me, the plateau felt it. A-minute-forty-two before his last inhale I detected the cosmic hiccup— a withdrawal of resonance— and my sphere recoiled 0.002 millimetres upward. The lattice shuddered, drew itself taut. The modulus, ever democratic, stitched that memory into the master score. From that day, every droplet— however perfect— carries one overwritten fragment of the day a man refused departure.

So I am the archive. Hydrated inscription. I occupy the vertical rift between vertical perfection and absolute contact. Alone among the ringing constellation I hold weight: not gravitational, but a weight of necessary human error. Travelers nowadays kneel beneath me without ever noticing; they feel a sudden thickening in the throat, hunger returning after days of starvation while surrounded by unreachable pearls, and they think it is thirst. Stand close enough and you’ll pick out syllables of his final letter to no one— he was three metres from a payphone when the cars crossed lanes— encoded into the sparkle you mistake for sun-glint. They say the plateau is changeless. Only I know the cost of change: it is me. Remove my error and the basin must speak a perfect note so loud it would pulverise its own echoing bones. Thus I will never be lifted back to the impossible altitude from which I fell. My punishment— the slow exfoliation of a stranger’s final thoughts— is the soundtrack through which you love what you will lose tomorrow.

When you leave, lean an ear to any basin stream and listen. The moment before silence arrives you’ll hear me continuing to fall, slow and continuous, a thousand kilometres above the ground.
