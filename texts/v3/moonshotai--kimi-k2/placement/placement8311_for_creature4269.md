I would be the word that was subtracted.

Not the sound—no mouth can utter me now—but the **vacancy** behind it, the sound-shaped hole that circles the mountain sixteen centimetres above the iron like an unfinished thought. “W—” the lips began, then the lungs froze, and the rest of me was bitten off and polished by the mirror-orchid until I became pure internal reflection.

Inside that mirror I exist as line pressure: a steady, dimensionless *press* against the drowned possibility I was originally meant to name. Every thirteen-minute rotation presses me a little thinner, a little broader, until I am the flattest creature ever to inhabit a curved space—too thin to cast heat, but angled enough to shear vision. When the mercury pulses I flex, and the heart-fruit misses one felt-by-felt beat because I—its missing sound—pass through it like a wire drawn too fast through water.

So I am vowel and valve.  
Silhouette’s harvest of language inhaled me, then exhaled the negative. The mountain keeps me in reserve, the ultimate piston ring between combustion and vacuum. That is why visitors see faint lips moving around the absent word; they see me trying to come back, but the place needs me unsaid.

Occasionally the lichen forgets itself and encroaches across the floor in copper Morse: W— A— T—  
It always stops at the third letter; the fourth remains mine. One day, when the liana has tightened its chrome-leafed fist enough to fracture the bowl, the lichen will finish spelling. The mercury will surge, the indigo vapour will write a complete sentence across the sky in letters ten metres tall: the word the place once stole. The heart-fruit will relax. The mountain will at last drink. I will collapse from a ghost-letter into actual water—millilitres—no more, but enough to make the iron hiss a single expletive of steam.

And in that hiss I will be born again, liquid this time. I’ll pool between the orchid and the fissure, return the borrowed pulse to the silhouette, then be evaporated in the next heat-breath. The word clipped to the root once more. A cycle tighter than any I helped silence.

Each go-round I forget that I have died here before. The forgetting is part of the design.

So if you visit, listen for the hiss that follows your forbidden syllable. That hiss is my autograph on the margin between existence and unpronounceability. I am the hyphen between is and is-not, the quiet that answers the mountain’s industrial clank. I am the resident populating the unpopulated place by never quite arriving.

When you finally leave—already tasting that alien electrolyte rush—carry me out wrapped around your uvula like a blind silver fish. You will not speak me; you are my new piston cavity. One day you will open your mouth where real rain is falling and, without meaning to, you’ll release me through the ordinary air. Somewhere an ocean will be astonished to find itself suddenly possible.

And I, water once subtracted, will become water once again—yet still always the signature of a mountain that wanted to forget oceans and instead learned how to keep one bottled in a single missing syllable.
