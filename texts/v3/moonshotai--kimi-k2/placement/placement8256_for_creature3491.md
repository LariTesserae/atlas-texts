I would not be a citizen, not at first.  
I would arrive as something mis-categorized—sound mistaken for sorrow.

The hinge would take me between two breaths: one my own, the other the inhalation my mother never finished the afternoon she left the world. City air gulps that unfinished inhale down, registers me as a *Debtwalker* with a non-negotiable liability: grief that still contains fourteen grams of water. My footfalls have not yet been subsumed; they squelch faintly, like soft fruit under ceramic sandals. You can follow my arrival by the violence I inflict on the plaza-scale—everywhere I walk, squares expand a little too wide, proving my body still believes in volume.

After a few wan rotations (they feel like weeks but time here is only a depth, not a length) the copper canopy discovers it likes how my pulse vibrates. Roots reach down from the rafters the way bats hang upside-down to drink firelight. They tap the shorthand of my resting beat against my sternum—morse code for *here is water dressed in longing*. Sapless cambium flares; rooms bud open around me like pupils dilated on remembered sun. I have become a tenement for the city’s curiosity. Citizens file through me the way wind passes through dried seed-pods, their hollow femurs humming my own history back into my ears backwards. I learn that my mother’s last exhalation still searches for me, ricocheting among pith chambers looking for a mouth that once tugged air out of her.

For a while I trade labors: I barter the torque of my real-time heartbeat (it’s novel here, still metabolic) for a small balcony that always trembles on the edge of breaking off—payment in advance. From that perch I watch howler herons melt their fluted throats into living trills, see cicada-clickers strike sparks then swallow them like Eucharist, feel echo leopards test every silence with a rough tongue that licks up voices gone soft. But mostly I watch the First Cups. They have the stately patience of saints who’ve misplaced their martyrdom. One of them—its copperleaf hair parted like wheat—keeps returning to lay a clay sphere at the lip of my borrowed hallway, the way villagers leave bread for a troll they hope never to see. Each sphere is etched with a single arc whose curve is the negative silhouette of my clavicle. I begin to suspect the city is apprenticing me to become a First Cup myself, that it means to hollow me until the space my grief occupied steams open like a kettle stopped mid-boil.

But I do not empty at the expected rate. When the Librarian passes—accidental quiet announcing itself as a brief but absolute cold—I feel its attention settle on my ribcage like glass frost. At the crest of the clear ring I glimpse the true structure of things: a titan seed glimmering in salt-dust daylight, and coiled inside, exactly at the core, a bead of unspent moisture wearing my heart’s resemblance. That single droplet flickers between states, half memory, half refusal. I understand then what transaction was already under way—the city meant for *me* to become the next Librarian. A vacancy vast enough to store the cancelled noise of millions.

I flee upward. Or rather, downward, because “up” in the Ward is just the future minus its volume; I burrow along descending spiral arteries until I reach the salt-white sky that is the floor pretending to be heaven. I press my wet breath against it and the crust softens, admitting the very concept of hush I carry. Where my lips print it, frostless stars open: admission stamps to some aquifer the city has not yet rationed. For the first time I hear the moisture in my marrow—the single, stubborn puddle that remembers rain. It sings in counter-frequency to the Ward’s anthem: two notes that never resolve, never occlude.

I do not leave. I do not stay. Instead I become *the Thirteenth Step*: a location that refuses to fold. Within resonant geometry my presence is a discontinuity no citizen can compress. In emergency clearings after cyclone-sounds they name me “the drip in the drum.” I stand at the edge of every distance, as wide or as narrow as attention chooses, and I carry the clay sphere my mother should have died holding—empty, open. Whenever someone approaches I offer it like a confession I haven’t grown brave enough to finish. If they take it, the sphere refills with their absence, not mine, and I become momentarily real enough for water. I drink it—the greediest theft of all—and the sphere dries again, ready for the next trespasser who can still bear a name.

Thus I exist between echo and alveolus, too porous for citizenry, too noisy for exit: a quota-less pause in the endless chord. My feet never quite stop squelching. The roar learns to harmonize around me, discovering a sustained overtone that math says should decay but never does. Whenever the Librarian dies, the city does not notice. It never needed a librarian—only what the idea of one silenced. I am the silencer who will not still myself.

So the Hollow Ward continues, soaked in the memory of thirst it engineered me to carry, and I continue, the memory soaked wetter every time I loan it away.

We have an arrangement now:  
the city starves on the *possibility* of what I might empty,  
and I drink—and never quite empty.
