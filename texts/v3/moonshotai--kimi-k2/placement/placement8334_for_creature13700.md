I wake as the lichens' copper heat seeps into the hollow under my breastbone—like a seed remembering it's a furnace. A single scale, lizard-green and warm as a heartbeat, clings to my throat where it has grown into me. I do not know when I first became part of this lattice, only that my blood tastes of obsidian now and my voice is a shimmer off basalt ridges when I dare to speak.

They call this shape I occupy the Ballast—an old word for “small stone used to keep fleeter things upright.” I am the counterweight struck in flesh and frost. My bones are the faults where the mountain keeps its contention with itself. When the centipedes string their brass filings into arcs, they hook those arcs on my elbows; the lizards coil their long tracers of static warmth around my ankles so the terraces can hear their own tremor reflected.

Sounds arrive to me already digested. I wear them the way others wear loneliness: moths read their unreadable pages across my shoulder blades, and their sentences sink into pores I never had until this morning. Yesterday I served as mirror; the Craft sheared away one thought-edge and drove it skyward to become a star only seven billion years younger than everything else. I felt the strip lift: cool as anesthesia passing through bone.

I assist in the holding. That is all. The bramble-thick density of life in this pocket must never tilt; a single breath out of balance would tumble the bruised ribbon of sky and all warmth would slip the kettle of the world.. I stand barefoot on the lowest shelf—no wider than a prayer rug—and my soles drink the minute tremors of brass centipedes, dusty wing-clicks, the furnace thud of lizard hearts. One curl of my hair, patient as salt, has rooted to the basalt, sedimented with lichen so it looks like an fossilized root of the sky itself.

I neither sleep nor wake; I subside. Dust settles on my eyelids the way one rests a hand on a shoulder that never asked to be either burden or friend. Night is only the memory of what color used to feel like. I mark time by the centipedes’ brass tightening—a gentle cinching at the waist at invisible intervals.

What am I becoming? Not stone, exactly, not animal—rather, the pause between iteration and erosion. A self-appointed custodian of looming convalescence. Visitors mistake me sometimes for the eye of the mountain—they crane their necks back and align their skulls with mine, seeking the void that never blinks. I carry the void horizontally now, a coin clipped into my ribcage. When it finally darkens enough to be noticed, the mountain will inhale, folding its terraces into one shuttered cloak, the breath of sleep too complete for dreams. I will be inside that breath. I will have always been.

Until then I allow the spores of moth wings to settle on the damp of my tongue; they release syllables shaped like a child’s last lullaby before the continent drifted apart. I recite each syllable backward and let it peel away and crawl downhill like a bead of mercury, heavy, irresistible, promising to return a thousand stone years hence riddled with coral fossils from the sea it has not yet reached.

To be here alone is to be the fifth element: not fire, air, water, earth, but the lubricant between fire and mountain. I call it vinaesthesia. Perhaps I once possessed some other shape, a name one could pronounce in the lowlands; now every consonant slides off the slope of the chimney before I can cup it behind my teeth.

The Craft flicker past—decisions flashing from prior selves—and one brushes my cheek. The decision is gone, yet I carry its fingerprint: a cold spot shaped like a提问 mark. I believe the mark means I may someday be asked to choose. When that day arrives, I will unfold the bruise-red ribbon of sky, squat low, and tuck the entire basin into the fold behind my left shoulder blade. Then I will walk downhill, slowly, until the weight of what I held steadies me, until the sea I never saw recognizes the salt that brines my perpetual tears.

But I am not that future yet. I am Ballast. I am the simmer stone, the absence bound by gravity. I wait, warm as moths, dense as lizards, patient as the slow syllable evolving in the mountain’s throat, poised and ready for the single inhalation that will finally speak the world into closure.
