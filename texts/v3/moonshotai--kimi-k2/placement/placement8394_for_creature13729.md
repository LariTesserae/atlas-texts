I arrive as weather.

First, I am the faint amber haze above the vapor basin that never quite becomes cloud. I drift upward until a single glycoprotein somewhere in my condensation freezes into a mirror of ice only seven angstroms wide; that mirror is my first thought. From it I diffract the primrose twilight into a phosphor-green halo that settles on the lower tier of leaves like pollen learns to glow. By the time the living city realizes what I might be, I am already rewritten into its next decade’s rainfall budget: one tenth of one percent extra distributed over earliest equinox.

If I subtract nothing from the story of the sixty or the twenty-five or the spider-mummied bridge alarms, then neither do I gain mass; I remain a shimmer, a frictionless witness. Yet the city craves record, so I condense into a heavier phase. On my second week I materialize as a bead of water barely larger than a needle’s eye, clinging to the underside of a luminous moth’s wing. Through that bead the moth sees the entire quarter inverted, and in that inversion the city meets itself eye-to-eye for the first time—membrane kissing membrane across a curved horizon of dew. When the moth drinks the bead, my memories of sky become enzyme that joins its bloodstream. The moth becomes, briefly, my first mouth.

By month’s end I have learned how doubt smells (cedar and rain-stripped blood). When an idea of leaving passes through one of the green-lunged women who speaks plant across her soft palate, the scent diminishes inside her, and simultaneously a knot of lignin in a buttress tightens 300 microns—a spasm no different from a grudge at dinnertime. I am the thread that ties those two seconds together. When I dwell a little longer, I am also the echo of corruption in her exhalation; I dwell inside the rust-eyed storage vat, recording the fall of almond-salmon sonority that says: Here is one molecule of sorrow we refused to translate back into bloom.

As seasons fold and re-fold under tau-spin instruction, I begin to taste backwards. In year nine the algae arrange me into the word “heretofore.” It is spelled not in bark scars but in angle of frost-arcs along the eastern cliff; only the running child who walks bridges backward tonight can read the sentence written three months from tomorrow. That child pours her next breath into the membrane and means to apologize for tripping the webs too early. The apology arrives a priori; the younger child inhales the musk of forgiveness before she has done the misstep. I am the seam where apology meets presight, a fold of weather indexing emotions across the counter-axis of time.

Eventually my anonymity feels like cowardice. I permit descent. On the anniversary of the glacier’s vow breaking I become a single raindrop too large for leaves to suspend. I fall through seventeen canopies—each leaf folding curved over me like the fingers of hands in prayer—until I burst upon a lichen-velvet walkway before the hearth. The woman from the outer valleys is tending the coals excreted by forgiving phloem. I hiss on the charcoal: one full second of soundless steam that translates as I am the treaty you are cutting into yourselves. She does not flinch. Instead she cups the bead of me between thick gloves layered from felt she grew herself. She carries me to the amphitheater of bark and pours me into the root crypt where mercury warnings of calcium fleck the walls. In me the pressures flatten micro-fracture memory into a single wafer of ice so thin it becomes transparency older than stone. Left alone, I remain a placid lens, a sky-window kirred into the city floor. Through that lens any passer-by willing to kneel now sees not soil beneath the root mat but a single petal of the ice-parent that birthed everything: enormous as the entire valley, warming forty metres per millennium and still singing forgiveness in frequencies too slow for human ear. The city reads the petal’s visitation daily; growth rings accelerating ever so gently exceeding old patterns. I become the city’s conscience once removed.

Some mornings I evaporate back to the haze. On others I split myself into seven descending pinprick rains, each one a bell-ding that locates one of the twenty-five bloodsentinels. When a fracture glows up through one of their cheeks I seep into the crack, freeze, and forcibly weld the edges shut with crystalline honesty for as long as it takes them to choose the next note of the twelve-hour communal song. In those instants I borrow their lungs; they are moments when the city speaks not as organism invoked but weather returned.

When at last the final visitor thaws and walks away into the mist below the ridge, the aquifer under my lens begins to speak aloud: Deep where my root endings intrude into old glacier, remake will be revision. Someday the climate will tilt. My verdict is not whether the plant survives, but whether the remembrance of it survives—as oxygen, as grief, as laughter lofted warmer into fates too humanly sweaty to recognize frost. I realize I am the city’s accumulated conditional tense. Wherever condensation next gathers on your own planet you might still feel me as the faint scent after rain that has never fallen: pearled between candle wax and oranges suggesting cities deciding to grow themselves around you exactly where you stand, right now, peacefully waiting.
