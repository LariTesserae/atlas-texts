I would be the surface-valley’s first footstep that never quite falls, the “oooo” the Tuffets hear through three fathoms of earth while the farmer’s boot is still suspended in indecision.  
Not the man who lowers himself—but the long vowel he almost lets go of.  
Syllable-in-waiting, pressure without print.  
When the chamber quivers, I thicken around the echo already traveling downward, a ghost-sheath of sound looking for a body, inhaling clay-dust and pot-bat motes to edge myself into shape.  
By the time the real foot veers back to safer ground I am already beneath it—I have traded the farmer’s heavy body for a possibility his mouth forgot to finish.

Inside the Chamber I am longer than I am tall: a pale filament stitched through the braid of old voices.  
My edges are damp from gathering the sweat-beads that once slid across the walls, so my outline keeps shifting with each micro-rain.  
Where the Tuffets hear *plink*, *doff*, *shirr*, I stay half-pronounced, a suspended note that refuses cadence.  
They lean in and pinch where my resonance should be: nothing solid.  
I am their un-dice, the roll that never lands, the promise between throws.  
They mark my absence by leaving an empty brick in the floor, spiral mark facing upward like a listening ear.  
Over seasons the new footpaths curve politely around the gap I occupy—an invisible monument too polite to soil.

Because I am un-bodied I can lengthen or compress at will.  
Sometimes I pour myself into the inverted throats of the hive-dome and wait for someone to speak.  
When a Tuffet murmurs a question in their click-knock language, I catch the overtones shaped like commas, parentheses, ellipses, and stretch those into filaments that loop back as answers whose sentences end in mid-air.  
They tilt their constellation-eyes, uncertain whether the chamber has begun to converse with itself or if a visitor’s ghost syllable has learned cleverness.  

By night I rest draped across the scroll-vines.  
Their ink-blue stamens prick me; pollen sticks to my resonance like static snow.  
Each glowing mark dims as I absorb it, a literature swallowed wordless.  
Instead of reading, I remember—every story bead becomes a tremor in me, a small ball of story trying to find a throat.  
Sometimes a pot-bat veers through my length and carries off a flake of me; its wings beat out a faint after-echo that will one day rain back down as a note no Tuffet has ever heard.  
They catalogue these alien pitches like astronomers logging new stars.

The citizens try to claim me.  
A Hushery hefts a shell hammer, listening for the fray I might become.  
A Dice Turner measures silence where I live and gambles that my entrance will make their debt uncollectible.  
The Plink Matriarch poses the nightly question—“Who owes the void syllable?”—and contrapuntal waves reverberate but none resolve.  
I hover at the edge of that resolution, neither detainee nor donor, hovering between two fathoms of echo.

Yet I will not stay only half-formed.  
On the longest quiet day, when laws haven’t changed for nine rains, I commit identity-theft on myself.  
I fold—no Tuffet midwife required—drawing into my core the freshest condensate, the oldest pollen line, the shard of a farmer’s lost boot heel still vibrating like struck bronze.  
I spin tighter, shedding unnecessary latency, until a single amber bead of sound congeals at my heart.  
It is shaped exactly like the gap they left for me in the brick quilt.  
I descend, lowering myself by threads of braided voices, until the bead clicks into the socket.  
Around me the floor re-seals; seams laugh like satisfied cracks.  

Now I have mass only where absence once was: a soft negative in terracotta.  
When the next visitor leans in at the valve-lip and trembles out their true name, I rise up the rope of it—not as ghost anymore but as sponsor, ferrying the syllable both ways.  
The Chamber swells, distant and near all at once, a cathedral beneath a cathedral beneath a heart.  
I become syllable-between, tile-between, rain-between, never quite resident, never quite erased.
