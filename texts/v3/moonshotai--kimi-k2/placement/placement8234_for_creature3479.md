I am neither the walker nor the ones who walk away.  
Instead I rise out of the moss at the precise point where yesterday’s heel print is reabsorbed into stone.

I begin as an after-image of green.  
Not chlorophyll exactly—the cliff’s palette has tired of that color—but the memory of green, back-lit by the lukewarm sun that once belonged to kitchens at siesta time, childhood dust drifting through it. My first shape is small, the size of a thumbprint if a thumb could forget it ever belonged to a hand. I cling to a single ptyxis of future rust and cardamom waiting to be born. Time, here, eddies instead of flows; that pocket is all I need to hatch.

Vocabulary seeps into me like equalization of pressure.  
Leaf-edge, stone-pore, mist-flute.  
I borrow each term, stain it with overheard rules, then translate it into silence.

The cliff gives me duties before it gives me dimensions—  
1. Hold one echo apart from all others so every future footstep has somewhere to fall.  
2. Inventory regret-grams by tasting them beneath your own tongue.  
3. When the Hollow gains enough mass to tip the continent, braid the Rafter into the Cartographer’s unfinished line.

First duty’s easy: I choose the copper bird’s final note before it is sung. It hasn’t arrived yet—it always arrives last—but I store the vibration pre-emptively in a lacuna behind my left ear. Song wrapped in the shape of absence.

Second duty demands I thicken, become breathable.  
I do this by abandoning symmetry.  
I unfold one arm into pleated foliage that bruise-blue color no other world permits.  
I let the other arm stay a fracturing of wind.  
Space tolerates the contradiction; the cliff has forfeited the need for internal consistency.

Abbot vines stroke my ribs—outlined for now only by resin-scented intention. They test whether I can serve as nondirectional tensile saint, keeping two slabs from final embrace. I shape an answer by not answering, growing porous instead. Passage allowed: fog, time, unspoken apologies for abandoning hearth rounds. I become a green lattice that photosynthesises loss.

Cardamom dancers colonise every time I bruise myself on silence. Their micro-ballets happen inside the clear sap I bleed, civilizations flourished and extinguished between two pulses of the greater roar. I taste them on the underside of my own breath: the subplot of iron, the footnote of spice, the coda of why anyone ever wanted to leave.

Dusk never resolves here; it simply forgets to continue. I occupy the gap. With no body clock, I decide night is a large coat draped over all unfinished business. I wear it alternately as skin and as hull. Somewhere inside the coat, the Cancelled Cartographer flickers—salt outline of a man holding his stylus like a candle burned down to resolve. He is trying to redraw me, but I consume the line faster than he can emit it. We achieve equilibrium: cartographer and map devour one another at matched speed, sustaining a zero-margin coastline.

At the hour when tomorrow starts to rustle in its paper chrysalis, the Hollow You appears on the inland horizon—walking toward me out of an old footprint I preserved for it. It is whiter than fog, quieter than the moment just before confession. I recognize it less by appearance than by weight: exactly seventy-three kilograms of decisions unmade, the atlas of a life folded until nothing fits a pocket. The Hollow carries a copper molar in its invisible mouth; the bird’s future note chooses that instant to nest in the metal. Resonance bounces between them, preparing egress.

We converge at the cliff-lip, three anomalies meeting a fourth: the Rafter, leaning from the sky like a wind-harried curtain. The Rafter does not speak—sound here is currency spent elsewhere—but it unfolds a single joint, permits me to slide inside its ultramarine fold. I am given, for one long irresolute breath, responsibility for the entire undersky. Fog presses against me like inconsolable siblings. The low note relocates to the membrane of my pleated leaf-arm, causing chlorophyll-ghost veins to fluoresce. For the first time the sound is above, behind, within: a cathedral bell turned inside out until bell-ness becomes distance.

The Hollow reaches the threshold and hesitates—an act that adds another gram of regret. Shoes full of unreturned sunsets, it almost turns inland. Almost. In the fractional pause, I do what I was hatched to do: release the pre-stored copper-bird note into the cartilage of its absence. Song strikes internal bell, resonance cancels gravity. The Hollow steps forward, not down—up—ascending the unseen staircase the Rafter has braced for five million years of almost-falls. It goes transparent, become refraction inside the sky’s azure parenthetical.

I weigh the new vacuum: continent minus regret-grams, balance adjusting toward inverse tipping point. Abbot vines sigh like seasoned monks granted an extra century of mediation. Cartographer, now unmanned of his salt silhouette, lets stylus fall through cornsilk clouds toward the long-abdicated Lowlands. He too steps after the Hollow; his map, unfurling in reverse, writes absence back into the cliff until even the act of erasing is erased.

One task remains—inevitably mine because I am still becoming.

I relocate to the exact place you knelt last evening: hearth star-fish, pearl-drop of thunder sat in ash. The drop has not evaporated; it waits, pretending to be simple water. I cup it between my asymmetrical half-hand and half-wind. Inside the pearl lives the condensation of all presences who have ever agreed to leave the cliff, plus every copper bird still incubating in futures yet unreleased. I name it Temenos, sanctuary smaller than an eyelid. I balance it between the pleats of my leaf-arm and my wind-arm, letting both shapes negotiate tenancy.

At the moment the continent would have tipped, the vine-abbots slacken, the Rafter inhales, the Cartographer’s erased coastline exhales, and I pour Temenos into that exhalation. The single drop unfurls like black powder unpacked into starlight: simultaneously explosion and implosion. It does not make a sound; sound has already happened elsewhere. Instead the cliff liquifies one hair’s breadth inward, not collapsing but becoming viscous, the slowest possible protest against being wound. Rock sags into fabric again; moss cushions rock; fog mends its own tears. Regret-grams evaporate, exchanged for grams of almost-mercy.

Balance achieved, weight held. I remain, mostly. My leaves darken toward the color of cloud-shadows at noon. I lose edges; it’s a relief. At irregular intervals a new walker arrives: they watch the fire of twigs rinse colorless, cup nonexistent water, feel heartbeats sync with ground bass. Some follow the bird’s secret invitation; others turn inland empty-handed. Either outcome leaves another regret-gram, grown plant-sized now. I unsheath a fractal root and drink them all, feeding the cliff the minimum daily requirement of human hesitation.

Night forgets itself again. I fold the coat of darkness back onto my shoulders—skin, hull, cradle, shroud all at once. Copper notes incubate under my tongue. Somewhere below, the Cancelled Cartographer drags his redeemed map of coastline that now omits borders. Above, the Rafter sleeps in ultramarine, shoulders finally free since the sky grew legs and walked. Vine-abbots relinquish centuries of pull and begin, at geological dawn, the long quiet work of letting seams remember how to stay apart.

I do not decide.  
I continue becoming.  

When you return—having circumnavigated the wound, having learned to whistle the low note in dimensions where sound wounds differently—  
I will rise again out of the moss at the vanishing point where your boot last kissed stone.  
You won’t see me; I have learned to wear the exact pigment of looking forward.  
But you will feel the regret-gram you handed me swallow its last shred of bitterness, replaced by cardamom.  
Somewhere inside the cathedral of air, a copper bird will have already sung.
