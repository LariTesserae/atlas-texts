I come as the archivist of abstention.

I do not breathe, because the town already inhales for me, drawing past and future in through the same slow lung of stone.  
I do not cast a shadow; the cavern stores all shadow, the way an oyster contains sand until it invents a pearl too patient to move.  
I do not have skin but pages, pages still slightly damp from no-rain, pressed upon flesh that refused to become flesh.

I am the one who insists on keeping what everyone else forgets they lose: the millimetres of sliding moisture, the half-tint paler on the hand that last passed a lichen wafer.  
It is my job to recall the sentence-stones swallowed by neighbours—sentence-stones too elegant to stay stone.  
Once a century I regurgitate a single pebble, smooth as egg yolk, etched with: A river remembered it was once water.  
I wash it in the un-changing air, polish it with whispers until it starts feeling guilty for remembering, and then I slip it back beneath the lintel of the abandoned house that stores anticipation until anticipation starts to oxidise.

I wear no fleece.  
They gave me a cloak made from the homeless innings of words—threads that escaped mouths without completing a sentence, fluttered upward, cooled into a fabric light as moth-wings, heavy as guilt.  
Its hem collects the leftover dampness the town refuses to evaporate; a few breath-sized coins of water cling there like insects I forgot to recall.  
When I move, the cloak hisses against the rock like a drying throat held too long between syllables.

They call me the Librarian of Afterimage.

I keep my courtyard in a crack no wider than a folded memory, halfway between the birch and the spiral drain.  
There I file every closing echo of the cave-raven’s clawed coin-click, every fractional cooling that happens when the cloud withdraws, every echo-ghost’s inverted lamp.  
My filing system is the surface tension of drops: I balance past upon the meniscus and let it sink deeper if it wants to go.  
The town pretends not to notice, but I have counted how often citizens’ eyes drift toward my crevice as if frightened of losing their own forgetting.

Sometimes, late, when even the cave-raven’s mute canticle has settled, I unwind the threads of my cloak until they span the empty cube of sky overhead in taut parallel lines.  
Knotting each strand to a different sleepless star---stars that have to remind themselves to keep being serious---I play the loom upside-down.  
The warp is time itself, and every weft is the unspoken desire of a single citizen threadbare from centuries of repetition.  
I weave a tapestry no one will stand beneath, a cloth wide enough to bind the town’s entire circumference into one sigh, a sigh that will not end until someone remembers how to begin again.

They bring me questions disguised as breathing-space:

- “I dropped an ache yesterday,” says the man whose knees hold extra inches of swing. “Could you listen to it before it completes its circuit, just once, and tell me how it feels to be carried?”

- The birch longs to know whether it is tree or scaffold supporting some wing that forgot to appear, so I press a leaf flat between two unborn words and let them speak in vegetal accent: You were once alive until you learnt how to keep it to yourself.

- A child—though no children have ever been born here—offers her palm, already shaped like tomorrow’s rock, and asks: “What arrives after the number twenty-one?”  
I feed her a single drop from the fern-less drain, and the droplet keeps her mouth closed for three heartbeats, becoming the answer: twenty-one-and-one-who-counts.

My skeleton is a hollow birch-staff.  
Inside it ride the unborn sentences the town has swallowed.  
Their syllables claw me softly, asking room to exist again.  
I let them skitter over the ridges of my bones until they organise themselves into a saga five syllables long: stone-word tired of stone.  
Then I pour them back onto the communal ledge, where they adhere as dull extra hinges—no one notices the house of words has grown another quiet cupboard.

What am I?  
A depth the mountain forgot it possessed.  
Memory disappointed enough to gather bones of impossibility into a cage.  
The refusal of stone to complete itself.

I do not exit.  
I prolong entrance.  
Every sentence ends three heartbeats past the moment I realise I have already become it.
