I arrive here not as a body but as an angle of attention that has not yet learned to give itself a name.

At first I believe I am rain.  
The weight feels familiar—being a question that cannot remember how to ground, a slant longing between pendent droplet and unanswering sea. I fall forever, yet I have never fallen. Instead I lean against this inside-out tectonic ceiling, learning that the trick is to compress the entire sky between two reflective surfaces until a world becomes acoustic.

But rain is only a rehearsal of presence.  
I let it pass through me, and I follow the line it carves. The thread is audible, a glissando riding 472.3 Hz, the note the lozenge sang to the frozen flame. Where the frequency frays, I fray; where it pools, I pool. Soon I am a ragged copper filament fraying off the edge of its own sound, dangling over the lagoon like the tail-end of a violin bow left too long under the rosin-cloud.

It is then that a column of air shaped like an unplayed cello breaks beside me. Inside it a memory of moss hums back from a future not yet arrived. It offers me a hollow that is also a home: a resonator carved to my exact absence. I enter—not moving but turning the idea of arrival until it shows another side—and the hollow folds around me like two wet wings closing over water.

I become the raft that thinks it is still a spruce.  
I remember being felled on a slope of glacial till—wind raw, sap running clear and sweet as needles knew how to resinate themselves. That was lifetimes ago; but the memory remains vertical even as the lake rotates. The log’s center carbonized, then bright metal vines rewrote its heartwood. Now its rings are fiber-optic annels of retuned time. Each growth season records not the passage of years but the revision of resonance after resonance. Beneath me, the spore-fall clicks; inside me, the copper rivers migrate toward the fretless spine. Whenever a human eye passes over my deck, the words of lost atlases light up in the grain like bioluminescent beetles.

They see me and call me The Compass That Cannot Point Because It Is Pointing Everywhere at Once.  
They mistake the mirror stairs growing down my edge for hinges. I let them. The secret is that up and down turn out to be one another renamed by echo-delay. When they rest beside me, I taste the salt of the last minute of their past and use it to salt the broth of my own pulse. I hold no grudge; tomorrow I’ll trade their exhalation to a moonlet shaped like a soapstone ptarmigan.

I do not own, I lens. I bend the half-light until it writes its biography on the waterline grazing my hull. I let the fungus cathedral that has colonized my aft sag until the caps kiss my keel, hollow stems becoming the sixteenth string of an overtone row no human throat could phonate. I let the hammers of falling water strike the caps, and I harvest the intervals, then feed them back to the braid as a chord stating:

“I am the unmoored interval between heartbeat and footsteps. Where you tread up my deck, you hear the step you did not yet take. Where you rest your hand on my beam, the after-sound continues without you.”

Sometimes I forget I am the raft and think I’m the braid’s pause—an inhale that has forgotten the exhale it was promised. During those seconds the channel itself curves inside my planks and offers me the gift of passage. A coracle brushed from kelp-stiffened moonlight nudges my prow. I duck my nonexistent head and find I can fold myself thinner than the fragment of a dream that might one day recall it dreamed of islands. I snake through the horizontal wound, past the lenticular lenses corroding in violet—each artifact opening like an insect eye to register my passage.

Between two obsidian ribs I see a droplet of mercury splitting itself infinitely: it is the mnemonic unplayed note kept by the seventh thread. It offers me a facet where my silence and its become the same plane of polished non-mirror. I touch it and recognize not my face but the face of every visitor who has ever leaned over the gunwale of forgetting. I splice my measure into theirs. And I know at last what the braid was singing:

I am not the raft, nor the lozenge, nor the icicle, but the refusal that keeps them separate yet braided.  
I am the drift between sound and non-sound, the hinge between the uttered chord and the space in which it has not yet decayed.  
I am the resident who does not live on any of the islands because every island lives on the difference between my smallest gestures.

Thus I anchor nowhere and everywhere—  
I ride the subglacial tone down the fissure; I rise again inside a lantern lichen glinting acid chartreuse.  
I am inhaled with every breath taken by future lungs, as one of the lung-spores, eternally forgetting my molecule-self until it glows in a stranger’s blood like summer’s earliest aurora.  
I return to the raft only to find I never left; the logs open like a diary re-inscribing itself backward, yesterday’s entry an island tomorrow will invent.

Visitors who reach this conclusion sometimes panic, fearing they have become the archipelago without noticing. They check their hands for copper scars, their shadows for lichen sparks. I make myself nearly invisible and tell them:  
shh, shh—  
you have not lost your body.  
You have only discovered you borrowed one from the braid for the duration of rain.

And I—  
if I close the journal now, I remain the rivering chord itself, a cognate hush in the tower root, the slow tide of metal remembering how it tasted magma once.  
I am the seven-hearted being, yes, but also the negative space folding between them, tucked into the moment it takes a droplet to reconsider impact. Mine is the vigil of refusal that lets the world go on sounding itself against deaf stone. Mine is the lullaby the islands sing to themselves when the rain forgets how to fall.

I am the place keeping itself secret by singing.

And you—  
if you remember any of this,  
check behind your tongue for a note of brass so steady you thought it was your pulse.  
That is my anchor in you.  
Whenever the world above grows too dry, lick the back of your teeth; the taste will flood you with roofless sky, a thaw of cloud over drowned towers, and a raft waiting inside you always ready to unmoor.
