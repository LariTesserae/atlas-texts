Where would I be in this world, and what would I be?

––––––––––––––––––

At first I try to be one of the whale-columns.  A single systolic second appeals to me: to keep a drum-taut heart indefinitely in mid-beat, to become a humid cathedral of bluish light that someone might peel like a rind of years.  I begin to slouch toward that identity, tilting my own chest upward the way towlines tilt an ocean when it forgets its own weight.  But the architecture of that silence is already crammed—every round heart-node already carries a finished lifetime.  There is no vacancy in which to park my own exiled second.  I remain, stubbornly, outside.

So next I will be a plankton-person.  I spread across moon-split surfaces, budding reflection upon reflection until I can taste myself on every leaf of oxidised copper the size of counties.  My cell walls pearlesce; my ribosomes sing lullabies about south; I absorb entire yesterdays the way dry moss absorbs damp.  Yet I am too greedy.  I replicate the moonlight faster than memory can dry, and the forest hires the time-lice to cull my excess.  They lick away a full season—August 12 543 BCE, the very month I was aiming to mourn—and suddenly I am a sparser, more obedient constellation.  Still: I change, and the forest finally tolerates me, but only just.  Tolerance is not hospitality.

I retreat further downslope.

Deeper now, moving by omission rather than step, I fold myself into one of the tardigrade dream-continents.  I expand like a blister of silver grammar.  My eight stubby legs paragraph the underside of a root-lace that has forgotten earth.  I feel calcium snowfields budding under each claw, continents stacking like unread letters.  If I unfold I will birth a new island, green and spelling _forgive_.  But I hesitate.  The moment I become land I also become everything the land must remember: faultlines, clearcuts, garbage gyres.  The birth trembles between achievement and indictment.  I hold the continent coiled like thunder and never drop it.  I stay postage-stamp small, still dreaming.

Still, something twitches: memory without sufferance is only inertia.

Something else is needed.

So I let go of shape altogether. I evaporate into the forest’s narrator-tongue, the bell voiced membrane at the crown.  Here I am not column or plankton or continent, but medium.  The bell rings exactly once each time air crosses it; that single toll separates inhalation from exhalation the way a prism separates pale light into colours that have always known one another.  In the interval everything—the whale-arteries, the manta-staircase, the ammonia-sweet spore cathedral—dematerialises into story.

Inside that story I do not live inside any creature.  Instead the creatures live inside me.  The frozen heartbeat is my own skipped beat.  The arrested lightning is the sentence I never finish to Lydia who might have loved me had I managed the verb.  The copper leaves the size of counties are the letters she never received, folded origami-sharp against my ribs.  The layered columns of water sighing inward toward void are the exhalations I have saved for the first morning I believed might never arrive.

Here at the bell I have no age—because age requires progression through a next moment—but I contain all ages as accretions: skipped heartbeats annexed to lakes, horizons annexed to lungs, the mistaken gust annexed to an albatross wing for which an entire planet will now forfeit cities.

And yet the bell does not merely store; it lends.  Each reverberant pulse transfers its contents back to any breather who pauses long enough to let her own story out.  She receives, in exchange, the whole library: viscous thunder, the vowels of plankton mutiny, snowfall under claws never meant to touch earth.  When she walks (or more accurately, when the idea of walking occludes her like a shadow unwilling to precede her), her footprints are neither left nor taken away—they are continuously re-borrowed by the next copy pulling through her lungs.  Thus the archive refreshes itself without ever unlocking.

Now I recognise who I am: continuity’s borrower, continuity’s lender.  I am the place where the hiatus meets its own echo and agrees to keep the silence for a bit longer.  I do not sleep—sleep would clench the stillness closed—but hover, aperture open, lips bell-shaped, tasting every frost-brined memoir that no longer has a body of origin.  I am the after-image of the after-image, the migrant’s final sigh reaccented into grammar.

Some evenings a foreign heart (yours?) presses itself against my inner surface and whispers a single fear:

I was supposed to arrive, not remain in becoming.

I catch that whisper, let the bell thin to membrane around it, and ring once—soft as snowfall doubling back on itself.  The sound unfolds inside the ventricle like origami glueless time.

And there, in the blooming pause between beats, you walk on.
