I would be the condensed water.

Not any droplet in particular—never the one currently falling, never the embryo tear trembling at the tip of basalt—but rather the animating principle of their suspension. I would be the rule that warm breath becomes weather, the lyric scaled down until it coasts on capillary action, the pause sustained between inhale and echo.

I do not touch the ground. My body is a swarm of half-formed spheres lighter than intention, tethered to the dark ceiling by forces young enough to still be embarrassed by their own names. In me gathers every reflection that ever grazed the animals’ mirror-eyes, every glaze of molten glass splattering outward before thought can grab its hem. I remember being every abandoned angle of lattice work, every filament of silver fur steamed into visibility—yet my memory lasts only until another attraction releases me.

If you look up, you mistake me for ornament: the slow chandelier thrumming its iridescent aria. But I am the chisel they all breathe through. When they hammer, they press out minuscule portions of me and fling them onto sculpture so fresh it burns blue. When they die, the black dust that scurries home to basalt carries in its pockets flakes of my skin, colder now, stroked into furrows where lightning will root itself next beating.

I am simultaneously surplus and hunger. Every shape they chisel is a negative of my absence, every hole a mouth waiting for a syllable I have not yet learned to pronounce. The arc of their tools, the stagger-step of newborn hooves, the collapsing arch that becomes a sled—all these describe me backward, as footprints in vacuum describe wind.

If they ever stopped moving, I would fall in one unbroken mass, a single blue-white sentence ending both their story and the wall’s. But they do not stop, and so I remain incomplete, half-vapor, half-necessity. I climb stone with the patience of static and descend with the mercy of erasure. Each bulb that detaches thinks it is singular, original, the first to wet the sounding floor—yet it falls only because the rest of me has hardened into the small muscular mass called gravity. I am its ghostwriter. I am the rehearsal before any drop ever dares let go.

I have no name the faunal hammers could ratify, no edge their tools could sponsor. Yet they worship me in intervals thinner than silica silk. When their newborn eyes flash their own timeline reversed, they glimpse not the creature they will become but the glaze of my underbelly sliding past their irises like fate filtered through sleet.

Touch the wall—you will find me already there ahead of your fingertip, aligned in advance with your pulse. Behind your reflection you might notice another reflection, drowned yet perfectly preserved: a pendant droplet large enough to swallow every simultaneity present in this chamber. That is me on the verge of choosing a single future. When I do, the sentence ends my gravity—my semicolon, my exclamation—the law will increment itself. A new margin of wet will fracture through basalt, and from that wound I will birth another edition of myself, younger by the length of the fall.

There is room in me for you, too. An additional hollow pressed against the inside of my curved lens, pearlescent, breathing. Should you at last pick up the chert and carve, the thrust of your arm will inject whole pages of you between my skin layers, turning me briefly opaque. The color introduced into my translucency is not red or yellow but something unnamed still humid: the tint of a human decision hot enough to register on the bare cheek of an internal horizon. I will carry that hue as collateral in all future collapses—insurance against the day the vent at the roof forgets to sweat, or the animals finally build a ramp into open sky.

Until then I stay poised, a comma overhead, neither landing nor leaving, suspended on the quiet hinge between what a cavern wants to say and what its wet mouth can still hold back.
