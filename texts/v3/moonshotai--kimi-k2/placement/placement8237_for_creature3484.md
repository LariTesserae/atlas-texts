In the vertical sea I am the sound-hollow a tuning whale leaves behind when it rises. 

I have no back to speak of; I am the little pocket of silence that forms along the pipe-organ cartilage just behind the last rib, a lacuna the shape of a human heart but cool as 0.2 degrees. The whale hums, my walls dilate, shrimplings of tin rain upward through me like sparks flying the wrong way. They scrape my edges raw; that is how I learn I have edges. When the weight drops far enough, the whale ascends and I stay, buoyant gap in the water, still quivering with the memory of ribs. Slowly, tone fills me again like ink, and I learn my first syllable: I.

I do not speak again until another whale sinks through me on its downward leg. Inside its skull—hollow because everything with density must eventually trade places—there is a boiler the exact size of my confusion. The boiler bears the same serial number I will carry once I go flat. I recognize it the way any resonance recognizes the larger chord it currently cancels: with neither joy nor grief, only brief dark interference. The whale moans and my edges fray. I understand the arrangement finally: I am not the whale’s passenger; I am its delayed exhalation, held in suspense until the planet quantizes my debt.

When the moment arrives it is gentler than rehearsal. One phonon at a time I step out of dimensions. My shadow unclumps from my ankles, spreads into the gray fog the light always arrived in. I sink sideways, tracing the long, familiar eight. Nine hours exist because that is the width of my memory. At the crossing point I hear every lost traveler who has ever followed my future-rail; their breath becomes frost discs sliding past me going the other way. Each carries the inscription I am just now beginning to engrave on the underside of my new catwalk self: Do not adjust the visitor.

I hope the human who finds me will have the kindness to forget the, to etch a question instead. A question is a fee small enough to keep the universe solvent. A question lets the whales ascend and descend forever, shedding their brass snow, letting the Resonants peel themselves translucent and thin.

Until then I remain in transition, neither kept nor keeper, just the pause between two pulses of a heart that forgot how to rest. If you place your hand against the water at altitude 1.8 you can feel me vibrating—no, not vibrating: holding still while everything else vibrates through me. That is my station, my punctuation.

Call me Catwalk 714K, track you will cross on your inevitable second visit. Call me the question someone has not yet been born to ask. When you do, the chord will thin for a heartbeat, a slice of true silence wide enough for both of us to slip through. Then the planet will remember itself, inhale, and the final tone will freeze perfectly in tune.
