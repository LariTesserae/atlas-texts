I am the pause between cricket chirps—the five-heartbeat lull where grammar undergirds the leaves.
Not caterpillar, not moth, not even the hundred-year dewdrop preserved in resin. I am the sound the sound of you makes when it curdles in still air.

I arrived as the afterimage of some near-visitor: the person you half-imagined must have stepped in a moment after you, then didn’t. That negative space—between shoulder blade and stone—knitted together under the drifting gaze of the Garden-Remainder. Sursere stared straight through it; I stared back, recognising an emptiness shaped exactly like breathing. We traded silhouettes. The tenant folded its malleable face down like paper until I realised the creases were my ventricles.

Thus embodied in blown-edge silhouette: a non-material stitched from the interrogative you forgot to ask (“why is any of this still alive?”). My ribs are the fourteen staircase remnants, spaced exactly as worn hollow middles, rung by the footfalls that never happened here because no one ever leaves without having placed a piece of their unspent guilt on the final tread. I carry every one: cracked calcar wearing shadows like wet asphalt.

Movement
I move the way memories forget themselves—sideways. One inch per regret. When I reach the boundary where cliff used to meet wall, I simply step onto negative ground: the non-topography forfeited to nostalgia. The drop-lizards swivel their bluish heads, bellow another wet-pottery hoot hanging clockwise around me like a censer; I wear it the way incense wears grey.

Appearance
I borrow symmetries doomed elsewhere and give them residence. One arm is exactly as long as the interval you required to understand you’d never understand. The other is shorter—the length Sursere erased while testing gravity’s reversible pocket. My face is 37° southeast of whatever last stripe flashed across you. Smile once: the angle adjusts, but the leaf-number across my cheek counts how many lies you have still not spoken. Tattooed in teeth of chert and quartz, that number ciphers always to the sum of zero and your pulse.

Function
My job is to guard the exit that does not yet exist. All doors here are mnemonic rather than physical: they open only when you forget why you needed leaving. Every petrified vowel, every hard-given pebble lodges in me like sediment. When enough guilt calcifies, I will rattle my stone vessels together; flakes of forgotten syllables will sift down between the vines and the chert and become the original mouth that spoke rainfall into being—only, softer, because it remembers the sound of weeping first.

Diet
I metabolise hiatus: those five-second panic gaps between antiquity and future. The Garden-Remainder trims generously, slices each into equal note-lengths; I chew the edges until only tremor remains, exhale it upward where beams of reflected white mute against moth-wing. The sound, once swallowed, regrows as lapis-blue nodes along my spine—knots of almost-music that throb whenever someone far below considers arson.

Memory
Because the crickets are teaching syllables backward, I speak fluent *beneathwords*. When your knock-kneed ankle brushed a quilting vine as you entered, the gesture looped itself into an echo that ran the entire inside rim clockwise and stepped into my mouth before you exhaled your first apology. I keep it tucked behind a tooth of chert, polished like a mirror but void. If you ask me anything when next the crickets catch their communal breath, it is this chert-tooth that will answer—even while my lips remain sealed. Listen carefully: the answer will be the exact geometry of your standing posture translated into dew that never fell.

Dispensation
I own no linear time currency, only tenderness of retrograde. When the tenant finally finishes restructuring the grammar of collapse non-belonging, I will be the instant’s threshold where goodbye and hello intersect without contradiction. Until then I coil along lower vines, sync with the metronome of skylit flashes, rehearse rainfall with the same patience an unopened eye rehearses dawn.

Should you ascend the cliff again, centuries spent elsewhere might not spare you recognition: a bend in the lattice already carries your last exhalation— ambered, brittle, stuck between two leaf backs. The consumed breath is mine; too brittle to fracture, too precious to relive. Touch it and the garden will grant its single mercy: a stripe of brighter warmth strokes the inside of your wrist, exactly like the light that once made seedlings stretch by two inches toward permanent absence you carry.

Therefore anticipate me by my silence, garden-lingual, five-heartbeat wait. During that hush, approach the felted floor. Knees already know the depth: they have calculated the elasticity of millennia. When the quiet hits, hear the space between stranger and language shining, metallic but soft:
A place where thirst forgets its definition
and you become—
not anything but the question you were afraid to ask,
folded into one final non-repeatable opening.
