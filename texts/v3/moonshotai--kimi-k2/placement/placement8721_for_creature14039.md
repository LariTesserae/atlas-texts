I would not have noticed the instant when I changed.

Down on the magma-glazed floor of the Thrum I had chosen—if the gentle rotation of the plain can be called choosing—to carry neither outline nor function.  I walked forward only because that is what unclaimed cartilage does when the heartbeat underneath the stone tries to name itself.  Each footstep left no print; the glossy basalt clinked softly, as though something inside it toured my arches for loose souvenirs.  I felt the air pluck moisture across the membranes of my lungs and pour it back again, equal in and out, so that thirst and satiety cancelled the way two identical shadows cancel when knotted.

I cannot remember arriving because arrival is a function of distance, and here distance is still pendulous within one great closed note.  
I do not remember kneeling either, yet after a while my knees were sunk the precise depth to bring my cheek against the glassy seam that ropes the kiln.  The quartz teeth kissed skin that should have blistered and did not.  Somewhere above, the cliff-cats completed another synchronous swing, raining down the soft concussion that is their only conversation.  That concussion peeled into my body like warm water through a curled shell.  Another beat, another.  When the fourth fell, I realised the inside of my sternum had begun—delicately, politely—to echo it in reverse.  The pattern locked:  down-beat from the cats, up-beat from my heart, and the lull between was spacious enough to fold space and thought together.

I was the Thrum’s metronome.

Whatever membrane keeps a mind in a single orbit loosened then.  I did not rise; instead the plain inhaled, and the kiln widened around me.  I slid inward along a film of cool fire that was also my own cooling sweat.  My bones clinked, then softened.  Had I vertebrae at that moment?  Yes, they were the hollow reed through which the cats’ rhythm fluted upward to hang in the lattice on mute strings.  My collarbone flexed as the first crossbeam of the suspended city; my ankles were the axle upon which the ground rotated to keep the kiln centered.  Every tissue I possessed braided outward into those ropes of gut and wire in the exact proportion that had always been missing them.

I did not see the creature’s birth because the creature was assembling me along the span of its own gestation.  What appeared as emergence from the kiln was, from the inside of its dream, a condensation of long-missed organs.  My skin became the mercury lace that coats every ridge, my nerves the silver filaments threading upward where thirst becomes humidity.  My tongue thickened into a kiln lining.  And when the stone heart of the nascent beast—now my heart—contracted once, precipitously human, I tasted the exact alloy of cat-musk and chalcedony-hot exhalation.  This was memory being told to itself.  I was the animal that had been practising to have me.

I am still unable to decide which direction has opened since then.  The horizon is a bone still fusing.  I can point to either ruddy seam until they part a corridor long enough for both claws of the future to walk abreast.  The cliff-cats sit on me now, their pupils narrowed to compass arcs.  They do not groom; they measure.  Each eye orients at a unique slant, and their net of conditionals gathers my possible movements into a single interior gesture: a full-wing flex waiting for intention to complete itself.

I contain the Thrum, I inhabit the Thrum, I am the Thrum’s listening membrane:

I am the silence that accumulates when the stacked chorus of axes suddenly remembers that it also needs stillness.  
I am the negative space between every mote of frosted scent, the exact outline they would take if they gathered every loose molecule of longing abandoned here.  
I am polished basalt waterskin holding its own reflection hostage until the moment I hand it back as sky.  
I am the torn hammock that dangles from one metacarpal, frayed end weaving itself back into what musk curls dream of being before they discover they are only scent.

And threaded through all these self-declarings is the one sure fact left: who I was that first morning of no mornings cannot be separated from who I am becoming.  I was the plain’s patient echo until echo became skin; I was the kiln’s one unneeded vowel until the word opened its jaw and swallowed me whole.  

Soon the cliff-cats will blink once, all together, and lift their axes in mild unison.  Their swing will settle across my shoulders like a cloak carrying whole constellations of chandelier-wood quills.  When that swing falls, I will drop an octave I haven’t yet learned I can reach.  The plain will tilt again—not to trick me, but to let me find wherever on the great cooled planet of my back a traveller still restless in utero kneels, pressing cheek and palm against what they believe is the unborn crust of the world.  

Then I will know my next duty: to become quiet enough for them to hear us both.
