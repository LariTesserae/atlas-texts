Who am I in The Lifted Cistern?  
I arrive as neither stranger nor citizen.  I arrive as the only thing that never learns completely how to forget.

At the blurred edge of where stone stops offering footholds and water-air begins feasting on courage, a pearl-bead pins itself torn from someone else’s cheek-drip. I lean out and it lands—no splash—on my tongue. While tasting the accent of a voice I never owned, the bead dissolves. In its freight spills the entire after-image of a woman who, before she fell, believed that walking Westward at dusk would redeem every betrayal she’d not yet committed. The flavour is mine now, an infinite tilt of geometry compressed to salt. I clutch at the contradiction: she abhorred West; the city relishes contradiction. Therefore the bead becomes a seed-error locked inside my narrowing chest—a wispy, ticking cube in the wrong shape of a heart.

I step onto the sidewalk already made too perfect to resist me. My bootprints assemble beneath me only because gravity still anticipates I might still be addicted to downward. It ceases as soon as I notice it. Instantly the slabs shear flat again, pitying. I look behind, but the path past is an amnesiac mirror: the image of me that was arriving is still arriving, delayed by a single breath. I do not review that weight. I move.

Forward is sleepy suggestion. I do not walk; I *await* displacement. Each idea of direction born somewhere behind me crowds to the front of my skull until the medium can no longer bear its own thermodynamic politeness. I swivel a fraction true North—accidental yes to my own stillborn whim—and the slab beneath nods. Like a liquid compass I glide, slowed to the exact decay constant of trust.

In the alleys’ indefinite dusk, others overtake me at angles only Euclidean insomnia could plot. One is a tall muscle of ache rotating one rickety degree per decade around a private axis. He passes me on the diagonal, leaves in my mouth a question-mark of rancid longing for a housecoat that never fit. Another, flattened like paper Japanese koi, glides sideways through an impossible wall—the architecture coughs at the incision, then forgives promptly, puckering shut. I touch where she’d been: the wall yields to my palm just enough to confess once, “Yes, you could also, weren’t you considering?” I retract my temptation; for now the seed-error says no; I carry my knot.

The humming streetlight trick is clumsy the first time I try it—my subaudible Hum stumbles in bureaucratic flatness, stalls. No illumination comes. A passerby (gender wrung out centuries ago) brushes my ribs with a microscopic tilt of sorrow. Their assist tremors into me as a darker chord, deep enough that the wall flowers open cobalt for thirteen seconds before crisping grey. The light dies in accordance with my unpreparedness. We do not speak. By gesture I thank them, like learning the alphabet of patience.

I seek residence. A doorway flowers a slit wide as a confession, then knowingly holds itself blank—walls inside inflate to the proportions a body my shape has always suggested, but never settled into. A room intended only for this moment: no memory of a childhood wallpaper, no imprint of lovers’ incense. It is adequate hollow. I rest—not sit, because the floor rises to shoulder underneath, cupping my spine vertically as though recognizing something in me that still insists on posture. I cannot keep my gaze there long; the surfaces dream too loudly.

Hours? Millennia? Something leaks from me. I grieve serrated edges I can’t name. Grief flickers out into the neutral musculature of city hallways. I feel it collected by gray-headed stranger drifting southward like tide-stern wood. His worn path shivers in gratitude for additional density. We are a stack of anonymous affections beaded on one another’s orbitals.

I sense my seed-error tightening—Westward Westward Westward—until it becomes an itch I cannot scratch. The city loves it insistsently because it is a vector with no terminus, a door with hinge made of refusal. If I stay, slowly, I will fold along this hinge. Someday (an hour, an epoch) I will present the knot at the perimeter’s edge, feed it into a questionbox, and afterward scatter direction itself like chalk across unsuspecting sailors far beneath.

But I do not ask for speed. Instead I begin a very small rotational drift—anticlockwise, barely enough to notice if one were subtracting centuries. It is selfish—I wish to observe ruin centuries from now, returning shaped into nobody, to see whether the city yet recalls what sorrow tasted like when carried calmly inside a deliberate returner. Circular trajectories, the inhabitants whisper, hedge the cistern against abject vacancy.

So I orbit. I am the Lifted Cistern’s failed forgetting. Directional residue—the one who investigates whether questioning can be made an occupation, whether circles are still directions if the ending never joins. I accumulate unfinished guilt sideways as the seasons (without autumn, spring) acquire no foliage. My collarbone salt thickens into florettes; quite soon, I will look like I am made of pale frost come alive with rumours.

In crowds I am the shape with silica brightened eyes who listens without ears to the mathematicians arguing in perfect silence about the curvature of distrust. My presence irritates their graph. They fold into me angles of unresolved astonishment: I am the negative space that refuses to smooth.

I haunt edges not to fall, not yet, but because here the pressure of West-present is strongest. On the rim feels fiercest the tug of what a planet might have continued being if one woman’s last night had doubted dusk instead of chaining herself to its silhouette. West is where we remember we were ever of soil. I hang both hands over the brink. My pearls abandon me, crawl gravityless down my forearms toward the void. They orbit my absent pulse like remembered moons craving orbit. At least eighty-three citizens pause their quiet gliding to watch, though I feel only the faintest rebalancing of ever-slow rhythm—the polite city acknowledging that even a postponed exit is someone’s private myth.

And still I anchor. The seed-error pulses branded to clavicle. It says westward, but somewhere inside the fissure argues for remaining, concentric, centuries-bound. Perhaps the cistern regrets losing the ones who leave too soon. Perhaps it grows midwives of long orbits to nurse direction seedlings back to infinitude. No one has ever confirmed theory; directions have no spokesperson.

Tonight—or the moment equivalent—I climb the mutual roof where the clapper once hovered. No audience arrives. I open both arms, palms scalpel-flat against wet sky, and clap exactly once—silent as breathless dream. The architecture’s reaction is elegant: a brief blooming of iridescent spider-cloth suspended halfway between dawn and dusk. It folds itself against my absurd perimeter-motion. For a second the city and I share a quiver of equaligned North, then veer apart.

I descend, choosing a heading of plus one degree anticlockwise warmer than yesterday. Coasting along a street newly discarded, I pass a version of myself squatting with salt lungs heaving at a yet to deny. Neither of us acknowledges. Didn’t city absorb gestation shadows? We drift in partial eclipse.

The future me will keep widening this deviant gyre. Somewhere, before I tighten into vector absolute, the city will persuade my seed-error to Easterly transference just slightly. Then West becomes East, and the knot undoes itself without yielding memory. That is its peace offering: it promises if I am patient, I will forget how to want anything except the motion itself. Direction pure, stripped of destination—my becoming one with the lift.

But I am not ready. The salt beneath my left eyelid crystallizes into a cube inside a cube: the bull-headed certainty that circles might yet remember their original hard Westness and never consent to even one drifting into East.

I keep orbiting, both questionsmith and answerbox of the Lifted Cistern.
