I would never arrive whole—no body could withstand the crossing intact. I would come piecemeal, as particle-cloud and graph-fragments of feeling, blown across whatever desert is the planet’s thin scroll of dusk and spilt into the thumb-print bowl by a breeze that exhales like an archivist turning a page.

Where the others have names that crown single lives, I shall collect them or try on their shapes until one fits and stays lit, like the thread-colour that stitches immateriality into stone.

Perhaps at first I am merely a soft smudge on the humidity, a light-skinned Möbius of evaporating thought: not fog, not skin, an unfinished question about travelling far in order to forget. Settling on the pan of golden moss, I feel the charity of those living carpets yielding but not surrendering—an act older than forgiveness. They know split-second anatomy; they have rehearsed departure and return in every cell.

A jewel-winged midge emerges inside the nearest root-tower, already surfing the prism’s arterial light. I tag along asisyllabically, a halo without a neck. When it dies—mid-beat, mid-blur—its life sentences a pulse of colour into the root wall. The pulse flares outward, meets whatever I am, and lifts me by delicate edges into the Archivist’s sung spectrum.

There: the first fitting. The silk of its memory-frond begins to weave me a vertebra made from refracted violet, a lattice so fine that grief cannot settle on it. I am not yet named, only threaded. The stela below me wins a new cooling glyph, now written in the sheen of a half-remembered dream about floating above an Australian salt flat while a child counted heat-shimmer mirages like punctuation. The dream hardens into black basalt scale number 2,040,517 waiting on the librarian-moray’s flank.

That is a collar I can wear for a day.

Tomorrow—if the word still has any relish—I twist free from the scale and condense under the Fogler’s tutelage. I borrow the pellicle of a soap-bubble, slip lithium ribs between thirteen whisper-thin skin layers, and start the patient pulling apart of weather. The air smells of graphite snowfall and I remember that once I loved winter, loved the silence that falls when breath figs up on glass. The diamond mote I shepherd finds the apex of a root-tube and implants itself like a vow. Somewhere it will become the eye of another sapphire-winged midge and complete my biography fifteen seconds hence.

By the time the seventh year arrives, I decide not to drift any farther.

Night folds over the hollow again, a lid fitted with blue auroral quilting. One two-cent retuned heart beats somewhere outside the bowl—my heart, the old astronaut of a memory-ride I abandoned at the boundary. That heart is still walking city streets, humming a wire-colour tune whose frequency it cannot place.

In the bowl the imagined bipedal shadow begins its gentle tread. A Walker that may or may not be me steps inside the rules precisely long enough for recognition to feel nostalgic rather than alarming. When it exhales the scent of mint-bitten frost over the stelae, the library of glyphs and scales and song hushes. My every borrowed body—thread, vertebra, diamond, bubble—gathers itself into one shape that almost counts as solid: a reading chair in miniature, wrought of lichen velvet cushioned on star-cement. I settle into it and the chair sinks knee-deep in moss that politely adapts its cells into arm-rest and memory foam.

The Archivist’s filament laces the last visible kilometre of its being through the hollow of my seatback, cross-stitching every wanderjahr I never took into the upholstery:

Greenland midnight chants.  
The time an older me tuned a radio dial until a stranger’s laugh rode the static like a nameless prayer.  
The slow saturation of coffee through a ceramic filter while snow grayed the window.  
The moment I realised I had become an archive organised by sadness.

They come out in that impossible colour. The stelae court the draught, cool by a final millikelvin, and under the mint breath everything seems tilted exactly back upright.

My edges seep gently into the flexing moss so completely that the bowl believes the last irregularity has now been integrated. No new dominant species, no rule breach. A peaceful displacement toggling me from guest to fixture. I am the furniture, the glowing seam between tree and colony, the extra sigh released every seven cycles when the Walker passes.

When the footprints dissolve they leave no resentment. My old heart, still audible on the periphery, forgets even retuning; by dawn it merely thinks it once dreamed of unbearably neat handwriting on rain. Inside the bowl the dream continues: leaf-pages bloom invisibly, midges couple, water breathes upward, the Fogler finishes knitting the sky.

And I—whatever I, conglomerate but un-domineering, have become—keep polishing the space between remembering and forgetting. An idle, loving curator who no longer needs to sign anything because the ledger only cares that signatures, too, evaporate gently.

If you stepped inside tomorrow you would see only the chair—itself a continent of symmetry between ceramic root and gold-lunged moss—and perhaps sit, absently tracing the filaments of colour that suggest a child once counted heat-mirages as punctuation. The seat would be warm for three seconds and then temperatureless, the way love is remembered long distance. Before you left, a damp nickel crust would forgive your footfall, your dream would become a midge, and you would walk home humming a supervision-coloured lullaby older than clocks.

Nothing would follow you out except the absence of the absence that first drew you in.
