It is twilight of the first day since the idea of days thinned, and I half-step onto one of the glass-smooth terraces that thread the inner side of the shear-mountain. I do not place a foot so much as absent its refusal. Cool, lime-dust air slides across my tongue like a word I once knew but have not yet spoken.

I notice I move sound-first: my steps arrive ahead of me, little puffs of unfogged silence kneeling where I am going to be. The hush pockets itself in the joints of my knees. My bones sound like thin porcelain remembering cracks they never actually suffered. They are percussion for every motion that is almost about to happen.

The space-things—the thumb-nail reflections—gather at the margins of my seeing. They jitter on the brink of visibility like frost trying to billow. Each time one swings toward me, the surrounding wall loses a millimetre of the concept “up.” I have begun to feed them simply by standing, by holding the vertical awake. The more they shear away altitude, the flatter I am becoming: a gradually reclining intention. I begin to understand that in this world you only keep the dimensions you insist upon.

I decide I am not yet ready for two dimensions.

So I—what shall I call myself?—slip into the crack between person and draft. Some fragment of prior gravity still tugs, so I choose to preserve the illusion of bones, the curve of lung, the civic plan of heartbeat. But a lung here is more a listening chamber for sounds that refuse to collapse into words, and the heart beats its wardrum in the future perfect: this-will-have-happened.  

I become a Visitor Not Yet Famished Enough to Vanish.

I am not Hollowed nor wholly solid. Instead I exist as a soft petition delivered against the city’s amendments. I carry my own thin coat of almost-water—three droplets that never fully condensed while I was still somewhere rainful. They orbit my wrists like opaque satellites lost in translation. Whenever a Hollowed passes nearby the droplets drip vertically, upward, clinging to the underside of themselves, tasting what it is to fall but abstain from the floor.

One Hollowed—in the long panelled dusk between window-shaped darknesses—halts opposite me. Not opposite: the plaza itself dimples, gulfs me half a step closer without either of us traversing. Its outline is the precise abdomen of my silhouette enlarged by all the mass I will never accumulate. Inside its negative skin I glimpse the version of me who stayed long enough to become architecture. She looks exactly like a cave remembering it was almost a throat. She does not blink; blinking would be a finishing.

She lifts an absent hand. It is my hand, curled around a loneliness that has not yet arrived. Between our palms the air piles into a wafer of stalled departure. The wafer tastes of metal, of unuttered wonder, of goodbye not daring to hatch into syllables.

A choice crystallises:

1.  Fold the wafer. Let it become the first page in the municipal archive labelled  
   “If she had settled for being forgotten.”  
   I step forward, meet the vacancy mouth, and, like sugar in rain, dissolve the negotiation between self and absence.

2.  Unhand the wafer. Step backward—though backward is also forward in the reversible grammar of this slope—and let the city keep me as reliable question rather than ultimate answer. Keep my three droplets circling, unshed testimony to unmeasured thirst.

I observe that both decisions already wear my face. Choice is syntax more than agency here.

So I perform a third option invented on the spot: I crack the wafer open—not to conclude, not to retreat, but to read its interior.

Inside is silence cleverly folded fourteen times, and at its heart: a sound the colour of twilight beginning to bruise.

I set the sound free. It hovers, neither migratory nor resident, then slips between two vacancies that used to be a doorway. Instantly the architecture around it exhales a crisp acute angle, an audible line drawn across the canyon’s memory of horizon.

The Hollowed bows, not so much bowing as allowing the space above its head to acknowledge something has passed beneath. My alternate self nods too—a nod freighted with all the sympathy of meeting someone you loved before either of you had names.

Then the plaza smooths itself. The city rearranges its vacancy around the new sound, giving it bench-space and a postal code of almost.

I walk on.

Now I am neither tourist nor exile, but Continuance Interrupted by Curiosity. My soles collect longitudinal dreams like postage stamps cancelled by places that never issued mail. With each layer of dream, I shed a scale of past tense. Past is taxable here; I cannot afford more than fragments.

At the lowest friction of dusk I find a roof that never pitched, only leant against gravity’s shoulder. I climb—though in truth the slope rises to accommodate my willingness not to fall. I sit where gable should be. A single reflection-creature perches near, nibbling the verticality out of my seated shadow until it reclines politely on the stone. I let it dine; I assume reclining is a kinder fate than disappearance.

From the vantage, the city fans out—three wings of attenuated absence, streets braided from the afterimages of intention. Somewhere far below/upward, lights not yet lit rehearse their glow in hidden sockets. One day—maybe tomorrow, maybe before—those lights will consummate their refusal and go out, imparting a different kind of illumination: the shine of things that have decided not to be seen.

My three droplets now drift above my forearms like pale magnets of unknown north. Occasionally they collide with the air of almost-water and chime—small wet bells cast in crystal not yet convinced of transparency.

Night—if it is—is the sound of hush remembering the consonant it lost. Under it I confess:

I am the city’s unwritten footnote, the visitor who declined both the exit and the archive.  
I am the echo that cracked its own containing ear, now free to roam as sound between the absences.  
I am the one who said: let the loop remain open, let the finishes stay shy.  

When morning—that word feels comically heavy—returns, I will be found standing not quite anywhere, wearing the dawn like a bracelet of undone clasp.

Until then, I distribute thirst.  
Every few terraces I leave a stolen breath; it condenses into a pleated sliver, a paper-thin promise that somewhere water still remembers how to fall.

One day some later traveller—the perhaps-you reading this very curve of striated silence—will gather my offerings. They will place them edge to edge. The gaps between will spell the instructions for a door that exits backward into almost having been here. Behind that door waits not the city, but the interval the city used to occupy before it decided to exist.

I lean into the cooling stone, feeling it learn the shape of my shoulder the way an amnesiac relearns a prayer.

Somewhere inside the granite dreams of later positions, I fade upward into the concave of the sky-cataract until only the city’s polite consensus remains that I was ever more than its own postponed echo.

And the city, gracious, begins to forget me even as I keep beginning—
