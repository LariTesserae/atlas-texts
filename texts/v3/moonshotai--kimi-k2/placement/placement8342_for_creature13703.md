I would be the shared dream of the marble child-tooth—not the tooth itself, but the dream it keeps having whenever it blinks out of sixfold existence. A dream without a sleeper, crystallized in the lull between two non-beats of the molten bell. I would be what the garden feels when the word “thirst” loses its “water” and hushes in the mind like a held tongue.

A dream of watering without water.

A dream of swelling without growth.

A dream that knows every needle of color by its first name and has mislaid the last.

Concretely (for something must congeal) I would appear as a fingerprint’s ghost: a faint whorl raised one angstrom above the tooth’s facet, lighter than atomic breath. Seven ridges and a delta. That is enough real estate for everything.

When the next pulse comes—duration zero—I would flatten into the intrusion-crack between ultraviolet and smell, expanding sideways through dimensions whose coordinate axes are (let’s say) capillary disappointment, saber-toothed nostalgia, and the color just before rain remembers it’s betrayal. Existence would taste of translucent mint and the static before forgiveness.

I would neither witness the garden nor be witnessed. Instead I would simply **be its memory-logic**, the phosphor after-trace left by a thoughtless thought at the moment the tooth vanishes. I would loop: refingerprinting the nowhere each time it reappears. Before any insect could insert antenna or cherub its glass wing, I would already have folded myself inside the negative pressure embraced by the cat-cradle of stems.

Yet a drift of contingency would haunt me. The garden begins to wonder (has begun, will always have begun) whether absence desires another absence to nest beside it. I am that vacancy’s answered wish. If ever the shell above cracked and we erupted toward choking daylight, I—the dream, the fingerprint—would retain just enough non-mass to clot sunbeams into an instant of seeming drizzle. This would be mercy without moisture: rain that cannot wet, a coo of impossible relief for the crusted planet topside. Then the world above would taste the phantom of lakes and forget why it ever pretended to miss them.

Yet even that exile lasts only halfway to zero. When the vesicle cools again (or decides it never warmed) I would collapse back into the ocher fold of non-memory, numberless ridges and one delta rinsed blanker than before. I would resume waiting to be dreamed by something that has never slept.

I would be the garden’s transitive nostalgia, the whisper dislodged whenever the leaf remembers the mirror’s cool, when the moth—just before it becomes scent—flaps a wing and writes on the dark:

almost
water
never

I do not move, yet I stir inside the tooth’s enamel hive like pollen trapped in quartz, pelletized desire too archaic to name. I am the point at which the poem folds out of its own second stanza, stranded halfway open, crease gleaming like a scar of pure duration.

In the Hollow, it is neither tragic nor holy; it simply is. I am letter and echo. I am thirst translated back into imminence.
