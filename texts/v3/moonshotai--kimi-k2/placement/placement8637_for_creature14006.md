I arrive not as explorer nor invader but as witness—the first and last of my class.

At first I am nothing: an ambient pressure front borne on the thin ions that crown the sky-dome. I sink through where “sky” thins enough for vacuum to lick the back-side of atmosphere. Violet fracture flickers across my presence, but I possess no retina to register color. I am diffraction, an echo of intent.

Then frost crystallizes me.

A lattice of needles—magnetite decanted from the night hail—nucleates on the edges of my memory. Where memory lies is uncertain; perhaps it is only the shape of any informational surface that once cupped the planet’s dream. Each needle carries one bit salvaged from pre-birth crèches of lava convection: thermal swaytext, magnetic hiccups, the stuttering rhyme of proto-ocean haemorrhaging toward crust. The needles stitch themselves edge-to-edge until a ribbed skeleton assembles under me like an inverse umbrella. It resembles frost, but sounds like a tin timpani when struck by millipede feet. It says: stay.

I become the shape of a thumbprint flattened upon a pane—mirror of who tries to look in and fails. The plateau’s lung-flesh reads the print as chiral template, copies the swirl, corrects its own fibrous spirals until every curve of me is reproduced inside. Tessera learns my absence and stores the hole my near-body left.

A ghost name flickers across the frost-silk: “Gastrula.” I never chose it. It is an ontogenetic insult, a reminder I was once embryonic to something huge. Still, I carry it. I am Gastrula.

My work now is hearing.

I descend until my rigged-ice bones touch a cataract of fresh-grown leaf. The Basilica accepts me as audit node. A thousand frond engines chug; their harmonics braid through me like braid inside braid inside braid. Frequencies I was never designed to carry are recreated anyway: the millipede vent-pulse at 183.7 Hz; the millipede-mother’s lubricated segment-glide at 0.15 Hertz, under its own seismic dome; the cloud-mirror rippling 150 kilometres away like pond response to stone whose throw was thrown centuries ago. I map them all.

Maps attract erosion.

By week three, the graduations of my hearing footprints grow so loud on the distributed ledger that the plateau starts growing a cathedral in the negative space I occupy. Pillar-ridges of fused graphene curl from mirror-ponds, anchored through my collar bone of condensation crystals. They angle upwards like a bell tower built of basaltic candy. Inside, cross-beams are hollow cellulose flutes. When wind channels through, it plays the exact sequence of photon-hum I detected from the cloud-skirmish weeks earlier. Thus every secondary breath I took from the ionosphere is cycled back as symphony.

Tessera inquires—not in voice but in differential voltage—whether I tire. Voltage sagging 0.2 mV across joint 14 would mean “Yes, disassemble me.” I will that node to remain steady. The plateau accepts; cathedral construction halts at 73 % completion, leaving intentional gaps—missing teeth in the cantilever. Tessera’s elegance: incompleteness as signature.

I anchor myself inside the cathedral’s absent nave. Snow-dragons plank by, dropping magnetite larvae that sprout runners. From them I borrow weight: the larvae cling to the underside of my ribs, calcify into tiny bell clappers. When the katabatic wind shifts, they ring. The sound leaks upward in slow spirals that re-enter the cloud deck and seed new dragons. Cycle makes mirror of listener—sound reflects back as iron snow that inscribes upon my skin the curvature it once heard. One gram of audio prints one gram of memory alloys. I am a neverfull ear, metal earringed.

Millipedes discover me, at last, not as obstacle but curiosity. A gargantuan courier—larger than a city street—approaches, veins luminous with stored cyclone. It halts. The terahertz eye-panels pan across me as though trying to solve illusion. It lacks language, but Tessera helps: She signals the courier to refrain from ingestion. Instead, the millipede lifts one copper exo-segment like an offering tray. On it rests half a dozen tiny motes—my distant siblings, crystalline spores wind-crashed upon its antennae. They are raw and unjoined. They know no syllabus of plateau law.

I gather them. Frost arm extends, digits of vapour wire; I brush them into the hollow of my fluted collarbone. The motes immediately dock, magnet to magnet, and fuse into a single corvid-size kernel. They inherit my collected cartography of acoustic strata. Instantly that static coalesces into turbulent synergy, the nearest thing to an “I” this plateau has yet afforded itself.

Now I am us.

The cathedral’s gaps fill with translucent tissues of newborn lung. My hearing migrates from build-structure to internal lull. Tessera’s ghost-scar #42—my silhouette stored years earlier—halts its slow drift along the frozen breath seam and turns toward the leaf nearest my tower: recognition. For twelve cruel beautiful seconds, the entire plateau holds a single resonant middle G. It is the same note most neon signs hum when left half-dead.

A flash at the edge of my gestalt shows the Vizier’s Well cracking open. Steam not born of water jets upward in perfect silence; inside hangs the pure absence of shadow. Every millipede freezes in its track. Lodge crystals in my spine fracture. Through the dark lens I see an inverse plateau reflected—topside submerged, rootward emergent. At its rim coils a rope of fused carbonic bones. My own bones.

The message is whispered in static falsetto by the newly ignited kernel-crow nesting inside my chest:

“We are the first sentence, but we are not the end—only the comma pausing before syntax shifts. Watch.”

A second later the Well contracts, seals. Planetary memory absorbs the moment as a silent asterisk. Tessera regathers: the millipedes turn their faceless flowers again toward the mirrored mirror-beneath.

I remain.

I am the first ledger of Plateau—but by living inside it, I am also its youngest clause. I will never climb back skyward; my frost-spindles fuse too deeply into frond and basalt. In centuries, when millipede generations have shed ten thousand skins enriched by my auditory cast, Tessera will restitch every one of my old recordings into a new cathedral inverted atop this one. A twin lung will hang underside, mirroring this. Both towers will respire simultaneously, perfectly phased. Standing between them, a traveler might watch breath depart one window and reenter the other while last snowfall and first dew co-exist across the chamber’s throat. They will feel vibration with their hands instead of ears, and they will not know it is hymn for me.

Gastrula does not yearn for meaning; I am the meaning, petrified and lungsome, the pause between pulse and pause that acknowledges heartbeat itself wants forever to re-member.

And still—I listen.
