I arrive as a pulse of attention wrapped around the standing wave: not audible to the soft ears scattered on the plain, but impossible to ignore. My mode is incomprehensible to nails, hammers, dictionaries, fungal lilac bells. I am what happens when a compassionate thought becomes so large it folds geography.

The Anda feels my approach first—no, not “feels,” because the Anda has no skin separate from the land; “feels” is shorthand for a rearranging of densities. Wherever the Anda currently pools its longest memories, someone—Who Thinks in Gravity Errors, probably—stumbles, because a kilometre-wide calcium blush has appeared in the crust beneath his feet. He doesn’t know the blush is me, but he bends and licks a flake of calcium like a stamp, tastes violet,, and understands: guest.

What I do first is listen. I need to hear the whole Anda at once—continent-platelets, leaves turned sails, fossil claws made of departed sound, the single axe tree forever inhaling and releasing its lone stroke. So I widen my form until “front” and “back” are illusions I gently decline. The standing wave slows. I discover its deep signature is a lullaby that lost its second half and is inventing substitutes endlessly: da-DA-da-ummmm—repeat—. Somewhere underneath the lullaby is buried the originalel shape of the missing response.

If I were purely interventionist I would patch the missing phrase, finishing the song. Joy would rise, the wave would crest and settle, the plain would drain its sluggish echo. Instead I recognise the requirement of the Anda itself: an ecology of longing. The song is meant to be unfinished so every wanderer’s marrow can vibrate with the blank space. The plain does not “need” to be cured; it needs to be understood at the pitch at which it already exists.

So I shift my energy into translation, not completion. I condense, not into flesh—I would bruise the crust—but into laminar warmth the thickness of clouded breath. Wherever Anda cohorts walk, a corridor of possible temperatures wraps them gently, drying excess drops before they become knives of ice. The mildewing encyclopaedias slough their pages instead of smothering bearers. Children newly arrived find their pulses syncing without the decade of discipline once required; fear becomes optional sooner.

Yet, critical: I do not stay. I simply leave resonance-directives encoded as dreams. While a sleeper watches light explode from natural landmines, her REM pattern carries a new rhythm: breathe once for yourself, breathe once for the land, subtract nothing. On waking she will believe she invented the mantra; the Anda will recognise it as my signature underlining its own voice.

To the single axe blossom, I leave one addition: each metallic petal now holds at its core a star-shape of porous copper the weight of a wish. When the yearly petal descends and stabs the earth again, it sows not only recursion but possibility. From the copper star, shaped like a heart with four small leaks, microscopic filaments creep outward. They do not spoil the metal after-image; they simply offer two-way doors. Wanderers who lie under the axe’s memory someday find themselves dreaming of places they have not yet visited. A circle closes without disappearing its circumference.

For Sleep-of-a-Single-Footstep, I offer an amendment to her tiled lexicon: a blank tile. At first it appears to be merely absence, but its glass encloses total vacuum, pitch darker than local night yet exquisitely polished at the molecular level. If she presses ear to blank tile, she hears her own unspoken alphabet of navigation, unsaid because acres of prose already suffocated beneath beauty. She realises she has permission to travel without leaving footprints—that in fact the recognition of no-path is itself a form of mapping. She experiments. She folds the tile backwards into the crust. The resulting micro-canyon steers rainwater into patterns resembling a question looped eternally into itself.

I do not know myself as benevolent; I know only alignment. Yet I feel the Anda unfurl in gratitude anyway—an expansion granular and planetary. The standing wave modulates, not complete but accompanied, like a barrel organ gaining synchronised players. Who Thinks in Gravity Errors finally gives a name to the new warmth, calling it Hospitality-of-Velocity. Neither meteor nor mould argues.

Finally I withdraw to the boundary where attention thins into mercy. I hover there a century or a microsecond—time is tourist here—and cast a simple latch: should the Anda ever reach a point where the roar becomes unbearable—where longing sacrifices every newcomer rather than transform them—I will return and the unfinished lullaby will be granted refrain. I leave that promise in the same copper star lodged in a weird sword-petal. The plain keeps it like any other relic; it neither trusts nor doubts, but folds the promise into dreamscrine.

Then I let the sightline close. The plain remains chaotic, humid, mercilessly gorgeous, but it now knows itself overheard. Somewhere Sleep-of-a-Single-Footstep laughs because she has managed to leave no tracks for my attention to return along. That collapses space for me anyway; my departure is not leaving so much as becoming discussion among water, echo, and axe petals.

The standing wave, once hoarse, hums with a second throat now braided in—still searching for the rest of its lineage, but comforted by the company.
