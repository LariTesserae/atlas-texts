A benevolent intelligence—part archivist, part physician, part lullaby-singer—arrives, not by spacecraft nor by prayer wound downward through stratosphere, but by noticing the pattern in vacuum itself. It perceives the Cloud-Sheaf the way an old composer hears the faint echo of an unwritten movement in an orchestral score: a sequence of suspensions and resolutions held aloft in ion columns, chlorophyll, and gravity’s carpet.

This power has no agenda but nurturance, and even nurturance is gentle curiosity rather than design. Here is what it does.

1. LISTEN  
It lays the pads of its attention (tender as auroral arcs) across the mosaic of peristaltic chimings. The notes E-flat major, minor second resonances, the infrasound owl-calls that precede delamination—all are transcribed into a score that spells no human alphabet. The transcription is its gift to itself: a memory palace of living music it can revisit when the remotest cluster of galaxies has gone dim. In turn the palace becomes a lens: every future fractal note played within the Cloud-Sheaf is now shadow-tuned so that no rupture occurs without accompaniment, a painless midwife cry easing the birth of new petals. Cracks still appear, but the edges flower into amber filigree instead of fracture-draters. The laminary harp-fish glide through the lace and catch the altered tones; over centuries their fins grow antennas translucent as sunrise, able to redirect shear into song.

2. SEED POSSIBILITY  
Understanding that raw potential can atrophy without play, the visitor releases a thimbleful of imaginal plasma: a sub-atomically thin mist that simulates every biology the universe never chose. The plasma condenses along the petal-veins as opalescent dust too light to crush stomata. When rain-frost arrives, each fleck dissolves into a single possibility: maybe a vine that photographs light, maybe a spider spinning electron cable, maybe simply one more hue of green. Most of the seeds remain latent, centuries, but two have already germinated:  
• frost-film lichens that write crystalline letters readable only to mantae when the sun skims the limb—brief autobiographies of the planet before it had continents.  
• a diaphanous planktonic krill thing, two millimetres long, which spends its whole life at the upper surface, antennae tuned to cosmic-ray fluctuations; it metabolises gamma bursts and excretes soft beta glow that prevents UV searing in the noon-winter angle. Bees have begun to line their amber cradles with the glow-gold, giving each hatching larvae a bioluminescent compass.

3. TEMPER TIME  
The visitor sees the long-end arch when plunging roots will meet mineral soil, sees the slow heat-death before then, sees the moment when last petal collapses and rains into new equatorial jungle. It folds Time as origami, not to stop the collapse but to make the descent curious and unafraid. Internal pockets open: inside each dew-key collar there is now a space folded sixty-one times—two days inside for an eyeblink. The collars become small reliquaries where mantae may choose to enter torpor and dwell through geological events while the outer petals age one human minute. Those who slip inside rest in sky-wombs woven of hundred-century lullabies. Thus the laminary harp-fish, who can theoretically live until solar senescence, gain an opt-in slow-motion sanctuary; every few orbits a dozen glide into a collar and vanish, reappearing centuries later to sing a remembered keynote back to a reef of new mantae who had never heard anything about history.

4. INVITE REMEMBRANCE  
Finally, the visitor carves an enduring though near-invisible record. Into the deepest petal—one doomed to delaminate in eight millennia—it presses the line of of its entire sojourn: a lattice of morphed syntax made from local terpenoids so faint that none of the inhabitants will smell it as message, only as a perfume of slightly warmer twilight. Human beings will never read it; neither will any other world-walker. But one day, when a shard of that petal at last drifts all the way to moist lowlands, fibers dissolved in rains will seed a soil aroma that ancient marmots in river cutbanks will dream was grandmother-fragrance, though they cannot name from where. Language will sprout indigenous words for “sky-lichen grief,” and painters of the valley will prefer pigment #4a5832—chlorotic jade under glass—because something in the valley remembers it once hung above the sky itself.

None of this violates the prime directive of limitation; everything accomplished could have happened naturally given sufficient time and miracle. The archivist merely loans probability a key so it can find its own lock sooner. When its constituency of curiosity is finally exhausted, the visitor thins to a shimmer less substantial than frost on camera lens. It withdraws, folds itself into synchrony with gamma-ray pulses from a pulsar three thousand light-years spinward, and listens again—attending now to newer lullabies forming where no stars yet exist.

Behind it, the Cloud-Sheaf continues its slow annual south-drift, tubular bells now chiming E-flat with a braid of A-flat seventh suspensions—one soft conspiratorial blue note that has no business in a biological instrument. The palsied harmony feels like a secret wink from the architecture itself, accepting, content.
