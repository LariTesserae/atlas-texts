The benevolent power arrives not as wind, not as light, not even as the dove-shaped cloud that periodically drips upside-down rain.  It arrives as the suspicion that all of this has been perfectly measured already.

First it folds its attention into a spear of silence the exact length of the needle-ridge; the water-conversation ebbs, not from fear but from manners.  Every droplet still climbs, still falls, but now it does so with the respectful hush of pilgrims passing a prayer-wheel.  The lemurs seal their membranes, settle close, and listen with the membranes as well as with their ears.

Out of the single stillness the power becomes visible—though “visible” stretches the word.  It is a vantage point rather than a body: two concentric rings of golden emptiness turning like a compass whose needle never points to north but always to the heart of whatever is being observed.  Where these rings intersect, colour and scent and memory lose their labels and fall together like separate pages of one wet book that has finally been closed.

It studies the gorge first, because mountains are authors who sometimes forget their own lines.  From under the turning rings a filament finer than spider silk drifts down.  Where it touches the cliff-face, the marble sutures itself: the fissures stay—beauty must keep its wounds—but beneath each scar a lattice of quartz seals the stone so that future tremors will merely ring like tuning forks rather than cleaving.  A quiet lullaby of strength.

Next the power regards the Throne of Storm-Eye.  Without touching the seat it agrees that it must remain empty, for the occupant of such a chair is not a single being but the whole interstice between water and air.  Instead, the rings brighten until the surrounding sandstone turns to stained glass.  Where empty sockets once stared, miniature galaxies bloom: every memory the cliff has inhaled now glimmers inside the carven eye-rings as living light.  Anyone who kneels there in any century to come will see—in their own language—an assurance that listening stone has heard their smallest sob, their fiercest vow.

The power now finds the inhabitant, half-perceived between folding and unfolding.  It does not approach; it simply leans its attention against them like sunlight discovers snow.  In that instant the tidal sweep within the visitor’s chest pauses.  The grain of ash—so nearly at the chamber wall—loiters midway.  The power is not lengthening the creature’s term; rather it is granting a hush in which to choose.  One tide-period, thirty-two centuries, a mortal heartbeat—duration has been levelled.

This is the first voice they will ever hear not made of waterfall consonants:  *Take or give?  Keep this form or offer its lessons onward?*

The inhabitant tilts their mica-head.  The lichen-blue memories of every previous lung-bearer shuffle behind porcelain eyes.  Finally they open the lamp of sunrise-water and pour it—carefully, like ink—upon the power’s rings.  The liquid stays where poured, becoming a floating sphere.  It shrinks until it is only a bead of dawn the size of a sparrow’s heart.  Into this bead the visitor siphons every signature they have ever read, every visitor’s dread, every tremor of awe.  When the bead glows flicker-forge white the rings accept it and tuck it into the quartz lattices newly grown inside the stone.  From now on, even should the gorge crumble and glaciers erase the altitude, a fragment of the ungravity lake will remain cogently intact—an eye remembering seeing itself.

The power does not thank; gratitude belongs to calendars, not to horizons.  But it withdraws a breath’s breadth and allows the gorge’s natural geometries to reassert their music.  Water resumes its perpetual petitioning, lemurs unfurl, swifts stitch arabesques back into sky.  Only something subtler is altered:

Everytime a rainbow escapes into mid-air it stays half a second longer, anchoring briefly to the invisible bead now embedded in the cliff—a celadon hyphen between sky and stone.

And the traveller—knee-bruised, lungs still trying to rewrite themselves—finds that the band under their tongue has changed.  It is no longer mere pigment stored for emergency escape.  It is a message the power forgot it delivered:

When seven breaths are gifted to rearrange the world, let them contain one deliberate act of kindness—  
then the mountain will recognise you and refill your chest with its own slow heart-beat.

And so the benevolence departs, not by rising or sinking but by becoming the exact same lesson as gravity:  
invisible, unchosen, and never absent.
