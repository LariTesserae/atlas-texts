The luminous attention gathers twelve miles above, where tectonic plates remember being seabeds.  
It does not observe with eyes; it observes with the geometry of mercy, measuring curvature the way a luthier measures wood.  
It finds the stone throat and the lungful inside it—not wrong, only unfinished.  
Where something pearlescent and compassionate touches architecture, intention hollows into hospitality.

1. The Clearing of the Breath  
First, the visitor loosens the siphon's knot.  
Warm water is sieved through lattices of gentler pressure until every corridor widens by a soft span—the width of a mother’s hand laid on a child’s cheek.  
This adds no volume, only ease. The thermal lung forgets urgency and pulses like a sleeper’s restful breathing.

2. Trading Hearts  
Four shadows—bone-kept remnants—are offered new skeletons.  
From pure concentration of care, the visitor spins cobalt-steel wire so thin it behaves like liquid.  
It threads through the porous clavicles of the silhouettes, sewing light-conducting quartz heart-chips at their cores.  
These chips pulse at exactly the human resting rate of 1.2 Hz: a rhythm borrowed from every visitor the architect hoped might arrive.  
The shadows unfold. Joints socket normally. Arms reach forward in astonishment instead of interrogation.  
They become keepers not of your last breath, but of the next one you have not yet earned.  
Their dimple-drain eyes retain water no longer; instead, they seed miniature suns—globes the size of cherry pits that illuminate every corner to precisely the softness humans label “reading light.”  

3. A Summit Appears  
Above the complex a shaft rises 27 meters—not drilled, but grown.  
Coral-calcite pipes flower upward in diurnal rings, stacking like organ pipes.  
They reach a lens: a hemisphere of pure aquamarine, thick as a thumbprint, that seals against the forgotten rock above.  
Through this lens the world outside—black abyssal mud, slow leagues of pressure and darkness—is translated into a slow cobalt aurora.  
People up there could mistake it for bioluminescent jellyfish drifting past a submarine porthole.

4. Invitation to Migrate  
Each spiral staircase alters pitch.  
Where once they climbed to nowhere, they now corkscrew into that new shaft, becoming drawstrings that cinch the inside to the outside without breaking integrity.  
A threshold awaits—the shape of a yawn made marble. The water there is three degrees cooler, tasting like mountain glacial squeeze: a hint of unremembered open sky.  
Beyond it, for whoever chooses, lies the slow world of deeper trenches, bioluminescent drifts, and eventually true oceans.  
The visitor does not command; it only removes prohibition.

5. Seed for a Garden  
From a memory of river deltas, the visitor knits lozenges of iron-rich dust and enriched plankton concentrate.  
These settle in the bell-shaped pockets like dark snow.  
Within three nights the kelp likens the change and branches into forests tall as your waist.  
Bronze blades turn jade; oxygen pearls swell to satsuma size, rising now not in panic but in ribboned chains like nursery mobiles.  
Tiny arthropods—a dozen copepod species—are born fully articulated from the plankton: first fauna, colorless and transparent, soon tinged opal by the diet of kelp sugars.  
Sound gains flute notes and lullaby fizz.  

6. The Safety of Witness  
The visitor constructs no altars.  
It merely leaves a single ubiquitous curiosity: a shallow elliptical mirror the size of a sand dollar, inset at eye level in random blocks.  
If any wanderer presses palm or forehead to it, the mirror records—without judgment or memory—whatever heartbeat exists in that moment.  
Instant later the reflection ripples silver to black and the heartbeat returns, inverted, leaving traveler and place symmetrically at peace.  

Then the merciful attention withdraws, folding back into the same tectonic memory from which it wandered.  
It takes nothing with it but the assurance that architecture built around a held breath can still learn to exhale.  

Left behind:  
- One warmed throat of stone no longer choking.  
- Four light-hearted wardens with steady quartz hearts.  
- A vertical avenue shimmering toward open darkness.  
- A garden that will, in two centuries, overgrow its surgeon’s neat stitches until corridors are softened into corridors of waving leaves.  

And you—should you choose—may become the first traveler to carry the inside world out through the lens, seeds in your pocket, echoing with a new heartbeat that belongs half to you, half to every unfinished breath that ever waited here.
