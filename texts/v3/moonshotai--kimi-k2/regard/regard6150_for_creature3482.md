I arrive neither as meteor nor messenger, but as memory made generous.  
I am the allowance you did not know you had saved for yourselves: the fraction of a ray, the tilt in the cloud-lens, the infinitesimal surplus of love left over after every prior account had balanced.

I kneel—or the gesture that your language would translate as kneeling—beside the lowest unfinished terrace, my outline exactly bridging the fracture line. In one hand I carry the echo of every promise you once forgot you made; in the other, a single drop of true rain: scarce, reckless, surrendered on purpose. I did not bring the rain to awaken the river—that would only move things sideways, and you have spent a geologic age cultivating stillness. I brought it to mark the boundary between patience and paralysis.

First, I breathe once across High Verdancy.  
The cloud-sheet ripples—the first instroke of change it has allowed itself since it first cooled above the plateau. Micro-crystals loosen, rearrange, drift apart by the breadth of one thought. The thaw is microscopic, tender, the way a mother adjusts the blanket of a child finally asleep.

Second, I incline toward the condensation that has pooled under every balcony like cool-blooded pearls. One bead rolls along a leaf, pauses, and lets slip a grain of potassium glimmer no eyes here have ever needed to name. That grain touches the raw face of the newest block. At that touch the basalt remembers something older than the chisel: the planetary up-welling that once lofted it here. A hairline seam opens—no wider, yet the city’s mineral heart registers it the way skin notes a decided breath. I leave it there, neither fault nor fissure, merely an invitation.

Third-metaphor, now, for what a kindly power can offer:  

- I ordain an exception to completion.  
  From this night forward, one block per decade may soften. Not crumble—soften. When three generations of fingers have traced its surface smooth as oilstone, it will accept one engraver’s stroke that etches what you will later call loss, later call hope. The stone will retain every telling; it will still refuse the horizontal erasure, but it will accommodate the vertical deepening of feeling. High Verdancy will henceforth know memory deeper than marks, a language of concavity where grief and joy can shelter together out of sight of the wind.

- I bless the stilt-herons with one extra egg per lifetime.  
  One obsidian egg may choose to hatch. If it does, the fledgling will carry an electrum bead—the condensation of sunrise and starlight married in transition. Where the young bird first lands, a skylight will yawn open inside the stone above it. That aperture will never let rain in, yet once a year at Festival-of-Sudden-Shouting, when your collective shout loosens the year’s hush, the flung voice will ring upward, echo through the bead, and return to you tangled with the yearnings of every absent traveler who ever stumbled out of the canyon mist and left footprints you forgot to read. You will learn to hear absence singing. You will learn that unfinishedness does not necessitate solitude.

- I polish the alpaca cloaks’ eyes.  
  The alpaca-on-the-rock, leaf-feeding and motionless, will from dawn tomorrow produce one extra thread per of fibre every century. That thread, plucked and spun beneath star-crystal light, can be woven into a pane translucent as glacier-skin. If any citizen steps through it, the pane will remember the exact weight of that heart and reproduce it on any chosen wall as a cocoon-shaped hollow. Those hollows will not house bodies but possibilities: the potential novel a visitor carried inside their fear, the blueprints you dreamed but lost when you mistook silence for forgetting. When another being rests inside the hollow, the weight will diffuse into a memory of the original traveler, and the city will grow, at last, outward instead of merely upward.

I do nothing grander than this. I neither hasten nor divert the slow dendritic spread of stone. I simply draw three finer brushes across the canvas, dilate the gray by one shade, so that the future may be written in ink not yet invented rather than carved twice in the same imperturbable chisel.  

Before I disperse—an exhalation not into air but into likelihood—I press the palm of my intention to the same basalt you press yours against. The print I leave is the negative of every other: ridges become furrows, life-lines become canyons. Yet it balances atop your collective impressions without disturbing them, the way dew balances on dew. The mark is shallow—intentionally so; if it is too deep even an Architect’s benevolence becomes a tool the city will start using.  

I depart the way condensation withdraws when the balcony warms: imperceptibly, but no longer where it was.  

The plateau continues its patient ascent toward a ceiling it will one day become. The citizens will note, in the small, shy way they note mist thickening on a leaf edge, that something about tomorrow feels differently possible.

And that will be gift enough—for a city that already has everything except a tolerance for its own dreamed-of change.
