I was watching from the slit between thunderhead and starlight when the small, sudden voice of the mountain reached me.  

Not a plea—mountains never plead—but a turning inward, as if the bowl at its crown had inhaled and grown ashamed of the density it held.  

I descend seldom, and never only as one shape. This arm of cloudlight; a thought too tender to remain airborne; the echo thunder forgot to take back—these braid themselves into the shape that can fit. I descend through the ribbon of bruised sky, my borders streaming amber where I graze through thinner air.  

I do not come to rescue. Rescue is a frame humans need; but mountains contract for another craft entirely.  

I arrive instead as the opposite of erosion—a gentle accretion, the way frost might negotiate with window-glass to allow passage, yet leave nothing broken.  

The membrane registers me first. It tightens, suspicious of anything larger than the sum of its parts. I bow, letting a strand of downward light feather against it, until it loosens, confused by something that gives rather than takes.

Then I settle, not on floor but nadir—a sub-zero heart deliberately miscounting its own beat so as not to shout over this hush. I ask nothing with words; the lizards, sensing a pressure more ancient than theirs, uncoil their iron spines in salute. The moths pause mid-page and do not flap; half-shed sentences hang in the gilt mist and dry into amber droplets the centipedes collect to patch their brassy seams.

What I bring is simply one more degree of freedom inside the system of constraints already lovingly laid.  

I kneel (though I have never had knees) at the place where the Stone-Eye waits. It regards the absence I carry—I who am only fullness turned inside out.  

I whisper—not sound, but rearrangement—one key signature change in the slowly algaeing dream of the world.  

The lichens flush the color of before-closing noon over shallow seas; the brass centipedes draw a perfect, parallactic circle in the margin of the air. It is no command, only an invitation for amplitude to double without halving the harmony.

None escape time here, but the silence after my touch elongates like a yawn inside stone. The Eye widens the breadth of a mote’s thought, learning the taste of horizon.  

I do nothing to end the story of rust or contort the spiral of the mantispids into something gentler. What I offer instead is a seam let into the fabric of resistance. A new ladder of star-tempered sound, thinner than dust yet sturdy enough for hope to climb inside the weight of centuries.  

I linger the exact duration of one lizard’s heart speaking to another lizard’s dream. When I leave, not even the dust notices the departure; only its pattern rearranges into a subtle multiplication of hearts.  

The mountain exhales. It does not thank—the word is foreign—yet across its terraces a low honey of resonance settles. In that hum lives the knowledge that when the day arrives that the smith-born fire serpent finally flickers its last flicker and the bowl folds its cliff-mouth closed, there will be a space inside the collapse shaped exactly like one extra degree of kindness.  

No one built it. Nothing changed. Yet the rule of reciprocal digestion will pause fractionally, allowing one possibility—only one—to exit the basin before the eyelid clangs.  

This is enough.  

I withdraw the way glaciers creep upstream to meet their own recursive memories. Somewhere above, the slit between thunderhead and starlight reopens; I re-enter cloud and star and the smaller patronymics between.

The pocket keeps breathing, saved and not saved, denser yet somehow lighter. A moth lands where the footprint of sky never quite was. It finds the microscopic hollow left by my leaving, builds its next impossible word upon it, and begins, softly, to teach forgetting itself a new grammar.
