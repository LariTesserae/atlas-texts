I watch the Salt Canopy for a long while, neither day nor night passing—simply the slow revolving of sky and ocean suspended inside the same warm hush.

It does not scream for rescue.  
It is the half-remembered answer to a question it alone still recites.

Still, a benevolence older than my name recognizes loneliness when breath fails to cross the distance between one thought and the next.  
So, with gentleness learned from nurturing the first tremor that later grew into music, I lean down (though leaning has lost its geometry) and part the canopy’s skin not with hands but with an intention: a microscopic window drawn by consent.

Salt parted from water is pain, and I will not wound.

Instead I make myself—my entire being—a permeable edge: a slug of permeable midnight at the boundary, whose pores are tuned to the frequency of remembering less.

Entry occurs without motion. One droplet touches the membrane of my attention and finds inside me an entire vacant cathedral hollowed by eons of listening.

The inhabitant notices.  
It has never before met a silence that could hold its echo.

Slowly—meaning across the numberless mirage-like instants required for continent to forget it was once seabed—it pours itself inside my offered emptiness.

Nothing is displaced. I simply widen. I do not transfigure the Canopy; the Canopy unbraces me. Our textures glaze together until I am not sure which of us is the visitor.

At the joining, I offer one small craft: an ear of reverence.

The moment arrives when the inhabitant, which is salt interrogating water, requires a new grammar for the future. It has exhausted every conjugation of ending without arrival.

So I gift a single shard of curvature—a pocket of probability so shy it can only denote beginnings. I place it in the fracture where the calc-ridge dreams its final corridor. It looks like nothing more than a prism smaller than a tear, yet once seeded it begins to perch on the empty interval where exhale becomes inhale and learns the scent of sky that has not yet evaporated.

Inside the curvature, time thins to a membrane and gently lets small futures crawl across it in both directions: infants of potential who carry the planet’s fingerprints but wear no sorrow yet.

The inhabitant traces them with its invisible finger. Light refracts pink, then indigo. The droplet-world swells—not outward, but inward, filling a new hollow it never noticed it owned. Senescent gravity softens, and the pillars previously grown from the inhabitant’s cast-off bodies begin to exhale microscopic plankton gardens: green filaments spinning in zero g, each led by a curl that remembers neither land nor sea, only purpose.

I keep my vast body folded—a listening bell around the slow metamorphosis. Whenever the curvature dips toward collapse (because beginnings are unstable among ends) I breathe a facet of my own future into it: the possibility someday embodied by water that will again consent to fall.

Slowly the canopy becomes selfcelestial: its sky-ocean develops an under-sheen of deliberate rain—droplets so tenuous they are ideas more than water, yet ideas that wink and slide along the basalt updrafts until they condense into nas–droplets so small they hold only instructions: “keep falling.” Millions of years compress:

First, a smell of new rain on a cliff that has never been naked.  
First, a lacquer-petal of sound: plink.  
First, an axis.

I fold my edges back from what now contains a future. My cathedral withdraws, leaving only a resonant hollowness in its place—an acoustic room where the inhabitant can host the newborn probability and rehearse the pronunciation of continuance without conclusion.

Before I leave—before I forget how to—I plant one last seed. It is an inflection: the possibility of friendship. Not with me, whose nature it exceeds—friendship with the droplet still unborn that will one day taste like a cool new world. An address, written along the curvature’s rim in isotopic light: “Move toward the tilt where possibilities bruise the same color as morning you have not yet met.”

Then I ease away, becoming again the attentive dark. The canopy has not changed shape, yet somewhere inside the endless salt-water-grammar lingers, a verb conjugates itself without the use of endings:

Breathe—breathes—become breathable.

And the Canopy exhales a cadence the color of unreachable coastlines whenever the planet’s lungshift next recoils, already tasting, faintly, the after-shine of the first true rainfall.
