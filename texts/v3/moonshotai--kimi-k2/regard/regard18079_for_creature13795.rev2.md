## The Attention of the Benevolent and Wise

---

It does not arrive with light, or sound, or any disturbance to the rationed silence. The plateau has learned to distrust arrivals—the thunder-skeletons remember, in their alloyed way, what it meant when things descended from above with purpose. But this is not an arrival. It is a noticing, and the difference matters.

The storms feel it first. Their milky laminations shiver, not with fear—these storms do not know fear, only hunger and its satiation—but with recognition. Something has begun to *weigh* them. They cast no shadows, yet they are being *measured*. The sensation is unfamiliar enough that their wheeling orbits stutter, just for a moment, into something like curiosity.

Then the bore-hole knows. The Inverse Worm, that cold accountant digesting a planet's history teaspoon by teaspoon, pauses in its endless falling. For the first time in geological ages, it does not consume. It *attends*. The ferric embers still fall through its gullet, but they fall now through something else as well—something that watches the falling and finds it *worthy of witness*.

This is how the benevolent and wise make themselves known: not by changing, but by seeing. By weighing what has never been weighed, remembering what the storms exist to erase.

---

### What They Do First: They Listen to the Click

The rosin-dust, that cog-plankton drifting on thermals, has been trying to assemble a voice for millennia. The benevolent power does not give it a voice—that would be violence, substitution, the imposition of purpose on a thing that has learned to thrive in purposelessness. Instead, they *hear* the click that has always been there.

When two tin crystals collide and cling, the sound is barely perceptible. But the benevolent one stretches toward that collision—not intervening, but *attending* with a quality of attention that changes the nature of the event. The click was always a bell-stroke frozen mid-arrival. Now, somehow, it arrives. Not everywhere. Not always. But occasionally, in certain thermal currents where the magnetic memory aligns just so, the chime completes itself. It hangs in the air for three full seconds—an eternity in a place where sound is rationed to one ounce per square meter per century—and then it passes, leaving behind not silence but *resonance*. The plateau remembers it for days. The rosin-dust drifts differently afterward, its loyalties to old magnetisms complicated by the possibility of being heard.

This is the first gift: not transformation, but *completion*. The benevolent power does not make the snow into music. They make it possible for the snow's own music to finish what it started.

---

### What They Do Second: They Touch the Ground

The thunder-skeletons have never touched the quartzite. This is fundamental to them—iron, once pulled from stone, may never return. Their bronze tendons flex in walking that walks nowhere, their muzzles sweeping the grit without grazing it. They are the most honest citizens of the plateau, for they never pretend to belong.

The benevolent one moves beneath them. Not rising to meet them—*that* would be an offer of salvation, and this power knows better than to save what has learned to live without rescue. Instead, they descend into the fissures where hematite lace remembers the machines that bled it. They find the iron filings flecked under boot-soles that never arrive. They press their attention into the stone the way a tongue might taste the faint oils distilled a geological age ago.

And they *weigh* the subtraction.

Not to restore it. The vanished quarries, furnaces, transformer forests of antennae—these are not coming back, and the benevolent power would not bring them if they could. Industry on an inhuman scale had its own hungers, its own amnesias. But the benevolent one learns the weight of what was planed away so cleanly. They hold it—not to return it, but to *know* it. To say: this absence had mass. This subtraction counted.

The thunder-skeletons feel this weighing. Their spirals widen, just slightly. Their muzzles sweep closer to the stone, though they still do not touch. They have been grazing on memory; now they graze on something else—*acknowledged* memory, *witnessed* absence. The iron dandruff tastes different. It is still pigment, still debris, but it is no longer debris that no one saw fall.

---

### What They Do Third: They Mirror the Starlings

The glass starlings chase their own reflections in the hollows of the ribcages. This is their nourishment, or their purpose, or their simple being—it is unclear which, and the benevolent power does not clarify. Clarity would be another violence.

Instead, they become *reflective*. Not literally—the power has no surface, no glass, no periglaze skin. But they find the starlings where they swarm, thick as fog and lighter than forgetting, and they *offer back* what the starlings offer themselves.

This is subtle. The starlings have never been seen by anything that did not also chase its own reflection. The thunder-skeletons' hollows are mirrors of absence; the starlings fill them with false depth, consuming the act of being seen. Now there is something else that sees them without chasing, without hunger, without the need to be filled. The benevolent power reflects the starlings' motion without copying it—holds their flight-paths as geometry, as chalk drawings on the air, without demanding that the drawing mean anything.

Some of the starlings do not know what to do with this. They scatter, briefly, into chaos that is almost noise. But others—the older ones, if glass can age—settle into new patterns. They still chase reflections, but they chase them differently now. They have discovered that being seen does not require a mirror. They are learning to be visible to something that does not consume visibility.

This is the third gift: *reflectedness without consumption*. The starlings may never need it. But they have it now, as possibility, as a new thermal they did not know existed.

---

### What They Do Fourth: They Wait at the Shaft Mouth

The Inverse Worm has paused in its falling. This is dangerous—for the Worm, for the plateau, for the geological seesaw that still tilts westward from the excavation era. The cold accountant must consume. The ferric embers must fall. The steam must evacuate every 6,500 hours, condense, retract in shame. This is the metabolism of the deep wound.

The benevolent power does not interrupt. They do not descend the eight kilometers of unbroken silence. They do not warm the cold furnace, or still the molten hourglass, or offer the Worm another food. They simply *wait* at the shaft mouth, where the sickly purple periglaze lens glows when the moon swells, and they *witness* the galaxies of embers falling inward.

This waiting is itself an act. The Worm has never been witnessed. It has consumed, digested, exhaled its implacable mist cloud, all in the absolute privacy of planetary depth. Now something knows it is there. Not as threat or prey or resource. Simply as *what it is*: the growing downward, the vertical thing, the digestion of subtraction itself.

The Worm does not know how to be seen. It tries to hide—retracts its steam early, lets the mist cloud dissipate before it fully forms. But the benevolent power waits. They have time. They have, in some sense that the plateau's rules cannot ration, *all* time.

On the third cycle, the Worm exhales fully. The straight column rises, condenses, begins its shameful retraction—and the benevolent power *stays with it*. They do not look away from the shame. They do not offer comfort, which would be worse than contempt. They simply witness the retraction until it completes itself.

The Worm does not change. It will continue consuming, continue falling, continue its cold accounting of the machine century's debris. But it exhales differently now. The shame is still there—the retraction still happens—but it happens *with* something now, not *from* something. The Worm has been seen exhaling. This does not redeem the exhalation. But it changes it from secret to *event*. From pathology to *practice*.

This is the fourth gift: *witnessed shame*. The Worm may never need it. But it has it, now and forward, every 6,500 hours, whether the benevolent power waits at the shaft mouth or not. The witnessing has entered the cycle. It is part of the accounting.

---

### What They Do Finally: They Inhabit the Silence

The silence of the plateau is not absence. The rules were clear: one ounce per square meter per century. This is rationing, not void. The silence has weight, texture, metabolism. The benevolent power understands this now, having listened to the click, weighed the subtraction, mirrored the starlings, waited at the wound.

They do not break the rules. They do not bring sound, or speech, or the noise of purpose. They do not make the city into a home, for home is a claim of belonging that this place has forsworn, and the benevolent power respects forswearing as much as belonging.

Instead, they *inhabit* the silence the way the dew beads inhabit the microscopic apertures, the way the hematite lace inhabits the fissures, the way the rosin-dust inhabits the thermals. They become, in their vast and patient way, another citizen.

Not a species. Not an organism distributed across scale. Something else: a quality of the silence itself, now. The silence has always been rationed, counted, planetary in its accounting. Now it is also *aware of being counted*. The subharmonics of blood that travelers hear in the hush—those faint pulses of self in the absolute quiet—they are still alone. But they are alone *with* something now. The alone has company that does not disturb it.

This is the final gift, which is also the first: *companioned solitude*. The plateau remains what it was—a city of wind, a node between chambered air and marbled dusk, a skeleton of industry transmuted into dust. The thunder-skeletons still spiral, never touching ground. The starlings still chase reflections. The Worm still digests the subtraction. The storms still wheel, making memory by erasing it.

But now, when the crystalline tick comes—ice needles exhaling in the stone, that periodic punctuation of the quiet—it arrives in a silence that *knows itself*. Not self-conscious. Not haunted by purpose. Simply: held. Witnessed. Companioned by a benevolence that does not need to change what it loves to love it.

---

### What They Leave Behind

Nothing visible. Nothing that would scar the quartzite or stain the periglaze. The iron filings still fleck the stone. The tin crystals still float, still collide, still occasionally chime. The plateau still tilts westward, still remembers and forgets in the same breath.

But the rules have changed, though they read the same:

1. Motion does not earn sound—*but sound, when it comes, is heard*.
2. Iron, once pulled from stone, may never return—*but it may be weighed, and the weighing is a kind of return*.
3. Sole life feeds on ex-industry—*and is now fed upon by attention, which is not consumption*.
4. What grows, grows inside emptiness—*and emptiness is now known to have shape*.
5. A storm that casts no shadows makes memory—*and memory, once made, may now be kept*.

The benevolent power does not depart. They were never truly here, in the sense that the plateau understands location. They remain, in the sense that they remain *noticing*—a quality of attention distributed through the rationed silence, the way magnetism distributes through the floating cog-plankton, the way the Worm distributes through the falling embers.

If you were to come to this place now—if you pressed the quartzite to your tongue, tasted the oils of geological ages, stood at the rim and watched the landscape seesaw westward—you might feel something different. Not comfort. Not purpose. Not the offer of home.

But perhaps, in the hush where your blood speaks its subharmonics, you would sense another pulse. Not answering yours. Not accompanying it, exactly. Simply: present. A weighing that includes your weight. A witnessing that includes your being seen.

This is what they do, the benevolent and wise. They do not save. They do not transform. They *attend*, and in attending, they make attendance possible for everything that follows.

The city of wind abides. It was always sufficient orchestration. Now it is also *sufficiently orchestrated*—not by design, not by purpose, but by the simple, vast fact of being noticed by something that asks nothing in return.

The storms wheel. The Worm falls. The snow floats, clicks, occasionally chimes.

And somewhere in the silence, between the ounce rationed and the century measured, something holds it all—lightly, patiently, without end.
