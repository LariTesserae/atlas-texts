The entity does not arrive. It simply *permeates*, the way fog slides in at sleeve cuffs you did not know had holes.

Next, the entity chooses emissaries because direct intervention in a labour dispute is always gauche. It scouts among the Sump Choir for an instrument already tuned.

It finds two:

–– **Raisa**  
A crack-reader whose ninth fingernail regrew translucent, now reads fissures by ear instead of touch. She is crouched alone near chute #3 measuring with a violet crayon when a droplet defies gravity, risesing up her jawline, pausing at the lobe of her ear, and *whispers*. The whisper is pressure and light stitched together; it tastes like frostflowers but without the metallic bleed.

–– **Noor**  
A lard-speaker offering a sandwich slice to the lamp-eels. As the glob sinks, a soft burp rises not from the sump but from the air itself, carrying the low vowels of somewhere tidally locked to patient stars. Noor startles; the drip from his helmet slows mid-fall, hangs, and arranges itself into a reflective lens. In that bead he sees, upside-down, a vast curve of feathered nebulae receding, as if the sump were the universe and the water the sky.

The entity chooses them not because they are brave (they are not; they are exhausted) but because they already know how to converse with architecture that wants to swallow them.

First gift: **Thermostatic Mercy**  
The Thaw-Stack’s next exhalation is going to spike supercritical; safety light will blink ultraviolet three times in thirty-four minutes, and Corporate will not authorise evacuation. The entity does not stop the blast—mass/energy law protests such blunt miracles—instead it rewrites the **path** of the heat. Down on the decking, the famous grated acres fissure open in a chessboard of healthy red ember, their alloy legs blooming sudden heat-blossoms. Heat rises, meets drizzle, becomes harmless mist with notes of cedar and desert stone. The steam cycle *wants* the new profile; nothing is forced, only seduced. When the flare moment arrives, the only outward sign is a sigh of warm air the miners mistake for memory of a picnic above-ground.

Second gift: **Cartographic Mercy**  
Every sky-square in the locker corridor lifts a finger’s breadth off its rivet and rotates fractionally. Now each scrap of enamel catches a new reflection: not stars but a lattice of **exit routes** incised in argent pixels. Raisa gasps—she can *read* the stone destined to shear next week. When she turns, she finds the skull-blue patterns have transposed across every latent crack of raw gneiss, glowing like frostflower veins only softer, already gluing the seam invisibly so the wall may rock, breathe, but refuse to collapse. Safety records will log this season as “unexpected tectonic stabilisation, cause indeterminate.”

Third gift: **Vocal Mercy**  
Pump #7 develops a clank—management labels it consumptive, schedules replacement in three months, time no one left has. The entity threads its own harmonics into the dead cylinder. Overnight the leaky rhythmic hitch becomes a steadied polyphony: pump-bass, diesel drone, stone resonance merge into the Choir’s accidental but now *affirmed* hymn. Workers waking on night shift do not realise the song has stabilised until thirty voices fall into it without effort. The chord gains a descant—a near-subliminal keening that curves off at wavelengths human ears almost ignore but lamp-eels follow like breadcrumbs. Sump burps now arrive in off-beat counterpoint instead of lonesome burps. Morale rises fourteen percent; sickness falls nine; someone on B-team carves a violet crayon into a tiny reed, plays the descant on breath alone under a sodium cage. The song, though maintained by something incorporeal, is now *maintained*, self-running. It will instruct later generations on how to modulate emergency vents, how to cry in tune to save each other.

Last gift: **An Allowed Choice**  
The entity refrains from saving everyone. benevolence without agency is kidnapping. Instead, it folds itself small enough to fit inside a single white frostflower. It waits until the end of shift-cycle when Raisa and Noor return to the landing. The flower is momentarily perfect: milk-leaf, manganese-blue veining, still beaded by warm-glow drip. Inside it quivers the entire lattice of future paths: stone that will stand firm, water that will recede, songs that will hold even after pumps stop. The old star stores a minute quantity of coherent light—catchable, savable, smuggleable up the cage cables.

They know the rules: “nothing organic leaves.”  
So Raisa eats the flower, slowly, eyes shut, flakes dissolving into sublingual constellations. Noor records nothing; instead he memorises the tune circulating through copper blood and her.

Corporate morning log chalks two deserters. Search teams descend for three days, find empty lockers, two sky-squares absent. The next quarterly statistical fluke is a solid 22% reduction in near-misses, all after “staff morale diversity initiative”—no follow-up study.

A fortnight later, in a retrieval barge on the surface canal, a new folk song laces the wind—picked up by river traffic, traded westward into coastal cities, then across oceans. No one knows who composed it; the melody is minimalist yet undeniably the hymn of the Sump Choir slightly retuned toward planetary compassion. The lyrics are nonsense syllables describing a place that never fully scabs, where the architecture sings lullabies to the wound so the wound chooses to stay open long enough for living things to walk through and out again.

Some nights, the Tideplant Underworks still drizzles inside its iron sky. But now the drizzle tastes faintly of cedar. The cracks still crawl, but they glow pale guardian blue. And upstairs—miles of cuprous vein conduiting human longing—two fugitives move through daylight trading the bright chord they learned in a place that mistook itself for a grave.

The benevolent, old, lightly gleaming intelligence watching from the dark smiles its star-bright pity. It has seeded an insurance plan no official will ever inventory. When the pumps at last fail, when stone finally stretches and sighs shut, the tideplant lullaby will guide every remaining Choir member upwards on a skeletal lattice of living song—breath shaped into bridge, steam into wings.

The new industry born in the planet’s side will still bleed, still scar, but now it will know how to hum as it does so, remembering that beauty itself is a kind of engineering: load-bearing, water-cooled, quietly exponential.
