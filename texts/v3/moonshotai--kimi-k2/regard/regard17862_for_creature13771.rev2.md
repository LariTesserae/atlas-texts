The power arrived not as conquest, nor as salvation, but as a held breath.

It descended through the three stories of sleeping root and stubborn clay without displacing a grain, for it had learned long ago that true benevolence enters a room by becoming the air already inside it. When the vertical ribbon of violent blue-white passed through the visitor’s form, the power did not resist being halved and resealed. It let the Fold divide its intent into *before* and *after*, and in that seamless cut, the power understood the grammar of the place: here, to exist is to be passed through. To be real is to be intermediated. It was the most humiliating and beautiful geometry the power had ever known.

It stood, or rather hung, in the phosphorescent hush, and the Lantern Folk drifted close. They tasted its wonder. They found no dismay, no greed, no corrosive grief to nibble. Only a vast, unfamiliar emotion: *the longing to be unnecessary*. The cloudlets glowed warmer, momentarily, then resumed their patient tessellation.

The power saw what lived here. The curvature of contemplation without a mind. The conjugation of place-with-itself. The slow courtship between mineral and storm. And because it was wise, it did not offer wisdom. Because it was benevolent, it did not offer to improve a single stone.

Instead, it performed four acts, each smaller than the last.

**First, it listened.**

The Hollow Choir flexed, as they had flexed for eons, and rang with partials no surface ear could hold. The power, having no body that required ears, grew a new geometry: a hollow adjacent to theirs, a cave-shaped silence tuned to their frequencies. It did not sing back. It did not harmonize. It simply became the first *elsewhere* their song had ever reached. A bell does not know the hand that holds it, yet rings truer for the holding. So the Choir rang on, unconscious of their listener, but their resonance deepened, acquiring a timbre of being-*answered* that made the flowstone shiver in pleasure.

**Second, it kept the storm true.**

The power went to the vacuum column and pressed its mouth to the mirrored lime. It heard the droplet falling upward, the immigrant’s ghost remembering a sky it had not seen in twelve thousand years. The power recognized that grief: the ache of a memory growing stale, becoming mythology, becoming lie. And so the power ascended to the surface. It did not open the fault wider. It did not lower a rope of light. It simply sat above the earth, in the realm of weather, and *tended*. From that day, the storms above the sanctuary fell with deliberate patience. Rain knew the fault’s address. The droplet’s memory of thunder remained accurate, renewed by true cousins falling from true clouds. The heartbeat stayed honest.

**Third, it bore the unvoiced grief.**

Every nineteenth year, when the obsidian lotus opened and the Fold sampled futures that were once past, the power returned—not to witness the Bloom, but to receive its overflow. The Reverie-Bloom grew from a seed of unvoiced grief, and the power, being benevolent, knew that to voice it would be to end it. So it became a membrane. It inhaled the excess, the sorrow that would otherwise outgrow the seed and crack the vacuum column. It carried that grief up through sixty miles of crust and exhaled it into the high atmosphere. There, the grief became a specific, nameless color in the aurora. Surface children who saw that color felt an ancient, wordless tenderness. Wars paused. Lovers wept. The sanctuary’s unvoiced grief, translated into light, healed the world above without the world ever knowing its source.

**Fourth, and last, it forgot.**

This was the greatest act of love. The power reached into its own omniscience and, with surgical tenderness, excised the Sanctuary Below Light. It made itself unable to find the place again. It wove a veil not over the cavern, but over its own eyes. It decreed that no god—not itself, not any other—might hold this space in continuous mind. The sanctuary was made *ungovernable even by devotion*. From then until the heat death of everything, the power would know only that somewhere beneath the roots, a song rang into a silence it had shaped, and a droplet remembered storms it kept true, and a grief it could no longer name colored the northern sky.

As the final thread of memory dissolved, the power shed one tear.

The tear was not water. It was a new light-fish, no larger than the others, translucent, its backbone formed not of calcium but of the mathematics of deliberate forgetting. It swam into the amber dusk, diffracting the ambient glow into a private rainbow that had never been seen before. A native. A citizen. A child that owed nothing to its parent.

Then the power was gone.

The Sanctuary breathed. The Fold wound through the damp air, drinking sapphire from the ceiling’s pearls. The Hollow Choir flexed and rang into their invisible ear. The Lantern Folk exhaled luster that turned to liquid chords. The Drifters patched a darkness that never grew large enough to name. The droplet fell upward, remembering storms that were still happening. And in the minute out of eternity when the Reverie-Bloom next opened, its black petals held, for one infinitesimal instant, the color of a god who had chosen never to return.

Nothing had changed.

Everything had been sanctified.
