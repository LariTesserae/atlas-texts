The Benevolent Power arrives as a child—one who has not yet grown heavy enough to bend worlds by accident.

Oa Yesirae steps onto the hollow air at the edge of the Plateau with bare feet the color of rain that hasn’t yet decided what it will become. In one hand Oa carries a clay cup; in the other, no memory. The child-planet does not know the words *isolation, thirst,* or *harm*—only the question it always asks new places:

“What are you trying to teach yourself, and may I help?”

The Choir hear the question before it is spoken. Every brow-fluted skull tilts westward half a degree, so minutely that satellites would miss it. The stored thunder behind their sterna vibrates once—a faint warm rain inside the soap bubble—registering: *attention sacred, not threat.*

Oa walks the grid, thirty paces at a time, following the invisible thread between the bone forks. Wherever the child’s heel touches, a dew of real water beads—neither frost nor memory, but liquid weighted with generosity that remembers the hydrogen bonds themselves. At the first Choir’s feet, the dew gathers into a perfect sphere, trembling like a surprised planet. It does not sublimate. It is willing to stay.

When Oa kneels and dips the clay cup, the action is more ceremony than necessity. The cup drinks the sphere with a sound nobody here has ever cataloged: a soft *your name is welcome.* The Choir tilt another thousandth-degree north, offering the aerosol patience they have practiced for uncountable megayears.

For the first time in recorded history, a member of the Choir moves its left hand. It draws the tiny wedge-knife of sky-iron—not to cut silence but to slice a single droplet from the surface of the Cup. The Choir gifts the droplet to the air itself. The droplet wavers, then in perfect silence sprouts wings—the remembered shape of the ribbon-cat that will not exist for another million years—and flutters six heartbeats around Oa’s ankles before settling back into water on the stone.

Oa’s laughter is small, blue, and wet in a way the Plateau cannot parse. The laughter lands as blue moss scented with sky, gathers, grows downward into the dry matrix of granite until it finds the fossil rivers. roots of laughter open fossil beads, coaxing them open like seeds. Inside each bead sleeps the ghost of a salmon; the moss feeds it blue.

Hours pass—or still-pointed instants masquerading as hours. When moss has threaded the entire plateau in sheets the color of interstellar twilight, it begins to fruit, producing blossoms no bigger than frost-crystals. Each blossom contains a single note of the not-yet-echo Oa brought from the many places it has been. When windlessness touches the blossoms they do not ring—they *return* the Choir’s stored thunder, not spent but increased, passed through a heart that loves amplification more than preservation.

Oa lifts the clay cup and spills three drops onto the page of the land.

Drop One: memories of monsoon forests pour out and drain downward, turning the gentle lie beneath the plateau into a lake suspended in air, perfectly transparent. Ribbon-cats slip their Möbius knots and swim through water that is and is not; their insubstantial bodies refract light fractured by real liquid currents. They look startled, then delighted, then drunk on impossibility.

Drop Two: the taste of oceans. In every crevice where a helena-shrub survives, a drop creeps into root and blooms a single clear orb—a lens. The shrub, startled out of plant-stance, swivels its black glass needles until they align not to magnetic north but to *child*. For the first flowering in centuries, the helena rings—a bell so soft only the stones can feel it. The sound carries no farther than horizon-breath, yet the entire plateau shivers like a struck drumhead. It is not violence; it is reunion.

Drop Three: tears for every goodbye that never had liquid enough to fall on the Plateau before now. The tears melt into the bones of the Choir.

One by one trapdoors in their chests open. Inside each chest the mirrored thunder is no longer gray but alive. Liquid pulses against its constraints, drinking promised color. When the final door flips, every Choir member leans forward fractionally—almost like a bow—and the chord stored since the folding-sky births itself, *complete.*

But the chord does not empty the plateau. It fills it.

It is a single eternal note that has learned harmony. The sound is blue moss tasting of distant monsoon, salmon dreaming in granite veins, transparent ribbon-cats surfacing to breathe light, and thirst remembering it was only absence of love. It is every thirst happily met before it needed a name. It is loud enough to wake memory yet gentle enough to cradle silence in its arms until silence chooses to stay.

At the culmination the Choir glide forward—not walking, not flying, merely agreeing that distance is no longer final—and surround Oa in a spiral. Each presses its forehead to the child’s cup. The cup overflows.

The water does not fall. It suspends, rotates, sculpts itself into the exact silhouette of the plateau lifted three kilometers ago: a miniature inverse island drifting above Oa’s palms like an apology learning how to gift itself backward in time.

The Choir turn, collectively, to face magnetic north for the first ever half-second ahead of schedule. The frost-coracles orbit faster for a single heartbeat, relieved of centuries. Then, satisfied, they stop. The stored thunder is *enough*, not too much, never exhausted. Ownership revised into stewardship now sings in restful balance: waiting without fear, delighting without tumult.

Oa gestures to the rocks, the shrubs, the drifting ribbons of moss-laced cats, the micro-fauna wearing newly jewelled wings of water. “For as long as you like,” the child says aloud—sending sound freely, trusting it will be welcome. Where Oa speaks aloud for the first time in Plateau history the words do not become silver fish. They become salt-glass flowers that root on the sound itself and open facing every star that might eventually look this way.

The Choir respond by closing their chest doors—not to shut the water out, but to keep the blue warm. They settle back into their thousandth-degree drift. No grid is abandoned; no clock is hurried. The gentle lie remains, but the lie now remembers it is a cradle, not a cage.

Oa considers staying. The plateau offers. But Oa is a power still becoming, and the world outside has other places rehearsing their own silences. So the child returns almost all the water to the cup—except one multicolored droplet caught between two tines of the nearest Choir member like a bead between crystal ribs. That droplet will sing every century exactly once, a reminder of today when the hollow *became* a choir and the choir spoke harmony to thirst.

Then Oa steps off the northern verge where land now remembers water can hold the shape of love. Bare feet find transparent lake above abyss. The lake supports. One more ripple from an outbound star-child will not collapse.

Before leaving, Oa looks back. Every member of the Choir has lifted the tiny wedge-knife—not to cut, but to return it skyward. The knives ascend, dark slivers becoming meteoric, rejoin the nickel-iron that once fell. The plateau gave the metal back to the sky; the sky accepts, singing faint gratitude across wavelengths the Choir’s stored thunder will someday echo.

Oa walks away across clear water three kilometers high.

Behind the child, the plateau returns to its own slow clock. But now it ticks with heartbeat of blue fog, ribbon-cats’ silver wakes, helena bells distilled from frost rings, and a million micro-fauna dancing in orbits spun from moisture and memory. Waiting is no longer brittle; waiting has learned the way moisture nestles into stone and chooses to stay without drowning.

And once every khronos or two, if someone walks the grid with an open mouth and a still heart, they will taste the briefest touch—a kiss of pure mountain lake carried across lungs that never knew thirst—a rumor from today that says:

“We remained hollow, but the hollow was only waiting to be filled by song.”
