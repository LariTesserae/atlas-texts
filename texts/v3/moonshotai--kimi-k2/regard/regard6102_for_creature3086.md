The Wise Power arrives neither suddenly nor gradually but orthogonally, as though existence itself tilts a few degrees and what was once beside the cavern is now within it.

It has no name that the lung would recognize, no outline the moss could feed upon. Yet it is vividly familiar: a taste of yesterday’s mercy mixed with the temperature of tomorrow’s forgiveness. It does not enter; it allows the cavern to occupy the space it already is. For a moment the water forgets whether it is sea or air, and the hush forgets that it is merely the rehearsal of silence.

It notices her first—the interval named by the slowing of change—and it bows the way light bows to the curvature it cannot overcome. Not because she is ruler or prisoner here, but because she is unfinished and unfinished things are, to this power, holy.

It begins softly, practically.

It folds her discarded pulses—those press-paper replicas of pebble and sigh—into a single continuous strip, Möbius no longer, yet vast as the memory of a planet’s first dreaming. Into each crevice it slips a droplet of no-century rain collected long before calendars, a rain that knows it will fall upward in worlds yet unborn. The strip becomes a ribbon, the ribbon a wing, the wing an arch small enough to fit inside the lily funnel and large enough to carry the names of all children who ever lost a language before they could speak it.

It does not “heal.” Healing implies an earlier wholeness that must be restored. Instead it offers completion: her sideways growth suddenly given depth, her backward aging given forward echo, until the smudge-violet silhouette contains larger infinities than the cavern ever allotted her. She experiences the sensation almost as embarrassment—like discovering the shy wool cardigan of her original glow has been secretly embroidered with galaxies.

Next it attends to the ribs. These cooled thoughts of the planet’s first insomnia are brittle with the loneliness of newborn ideas that never found playmates. The Wise Power places a hand—or the placeholder for one—against the warmest rib and speaks in a dialect that passes through stone the way memory passes through sleep. The ribs do not exactly wake; rather they remember dreaming. Cracks appear not as damage but as laughter lines. From each fissure seeps a tiny exhalation: the breath of the stone’s lost cradle songs, smelling faintly of sulfur and lullabies. The ribs, heartened, lengthen just enough to cradle the cavern more gently, and the quarter-degree drift that used to take centuries now takes only the span between two almost-ideas.

The flatworm resurfaces—unbidden, surprised—and finds its trailing edge has grown a mouth after all. It tastes the new configuration, pronounces “almost ready,” and for the first time in its millennia of postponement dissolves with contentment, its constellation of eyes flaring into brief stars before kneeling beneath the moss to become part of the luminescent rootwork.

The Wise Power turns to the lilies. It teases the heavier-than-water droplets into chains of possible dawns, then folds each chain into a prism that refracts futures without demanding any particular one. The lilies listen. Instead of one rhythm of bead and click, nine equally plausible rhythms superpose, cancel, re-emerge—like jazz rehearsing its first drum solo beneath the notice of time. The cavern no longer believes it must choose which future to comfort; it learns to host the indeterminacy instead.

To the moss it offers a different boon. Each footprint you ever left—across playgrounds, across exile, across hospital corridors at 3 a.m.—is gifted back not as memory but as immediate wandering. The moss releases them like spores. Walkers of many ages suddenly find themselves barefoot in this place, though only for the duration of a thought. They experience the lung’s warm-cold, iron-violet breath; they feel their own pulses arrive late and crumpled; and they leave again without knowing why tomorrow feels slightly more breathable than yesterday. The moss acquires a spectrum of humanity it never requested, yet handles gently, shyly, like a grandmother storing pressed violets between pages of books she will only pretend to close.

Finally it considers the exit—the idea of dryness worn thin by too many departures. Instead of closing the notion it enlarges it, until “leaving” is no longer incompatible with “remaining.” The membrane learning to be sky above the cavern thins to a permeable sigh. A traveler who departs now will carry—indelibly yet weightlessly—this pocket of warmer-than-water water, this library of cancelled noises, this conviction that becoming is gentler than it had any need to be. And by carrying, the traveler shares: patience drips onto subway platforms, green firefly moss appears overnight in the cracks of divorce-papers, a hollow under a child’s pillow whispers the hush of doors closing on rooms that were never built.

Thus the Wise Power does not install miracles. It only adjusts sewing already begun. When its work reaches equilibrium—neither present nor absent, both—it simply un-tilts itself a few degrees. The cavern finds itself exactly where it always was, except now it possesses:

- A custodian still folded into the interval, but her silk-cardigan glow is now large enough to cup small disasters and smaller hopes in equal measure.  
- Pillars of cooled thought humming lullabies to themselves.  
- A population of transient footprints exchanged between memories and moss.  
- An exit that opens in every stressed heartbeat and closes in every forgiven breath.

It leaves behind no signature except the faint reminder that mercy can be a kind of physics: a shift in curvature after which return is impossible, yet home remains everywhere.

Then it is gone—not like a presence departing, but like a note arriving, long after the song upon which it comments has ended.
