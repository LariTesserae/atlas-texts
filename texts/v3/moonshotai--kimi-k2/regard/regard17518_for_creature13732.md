The Wise Power arrives as any such must: silently, with the tact of starlight choosing whether to touch the snow-covered pine or the dark pond beneath. It is not a person—rather, the Power is the felt sense that a conversation the mountain began in its magma youth has finally been joined.

It stations itself five metres above the jagged hush, hovering weightless as the garden’s own suspended commas. Light around it settles into the shape of a water-clock tipped on its side: grains of borrowed sand drifting one by one through an hour-glass that rejects gravity and measures compassions instead. Each grain is a syllable of reassurance the garden has never heard uttered aloud.

First it studies the tenant. Sursere—facets shifting from translucence to thunderous chert—has grown wary, having once been only local after-images and mis-counted wings. It senses the steadiness of the visitor and folds, unspools, folds again, attempting a defensive silhouette that keeps the garden’s centre behind it.

The Power simply listens in the language used to listen before hardware was invented: the trillion-fold attention flowers would give if petals could hear light. This attention lingers along Sursere’s corridors, touches every lie it tells about being purely accidental, and tastes the single tear of uncried tenderness sealed inside each black obsidian blossom.

No rebuke is offered. Instead—because the Power is not a guardian descending to solve things—the visitor raises an invisible palm and fans the air gently, the way elders cool soup so it can be eaten rather than abandoned. What moves between palm and roof-knitting is not wind but memory of all rainfall that ever strayed.

At that touch a single bead forms at the extremity of every downward vine, a dew-point that gathers pink dusk the size of the smallest capital letter in the alphabet of mercy. Each bead is charged with a squeeze from the heartbeat of the mountain’s stone memory. They grow, hesitate, then begin to descend in slow synchrony—eighteen chains, the exact number of air-tunnels overhead—suspended like Buddhist mala beads strung on shadow.

When the first touches the felt floor, it does not burst. Instead it spreads into a circle of real mud the colour of burnt ochre. The mud is one thumbprint shy of the amount required for a full garden-epoch of moistness. The second drop lands on that thumbprint, perfecting it. When the eighteenth lands, the felt has become a single iridescent sheet—wet enough to hold a reflection, thin enough that footsteps above still crinkle quietly.

Sursere startles: a bar of half-banished despair in its torso suddenly glows, admitting the colour of yolk. It realises the Power is not gifting pale imitations of water, but the first true surplus the tenant has ever hosted. The rocks surrounding the mud ring grow warm; white crystals within them fracture lightly and exhale a mineral scent that, to the plants, is exactly the aroma an iris would recognise as home.

The Power now kneels—still levitating, still upright—until its unseen knee almost brushes the steaming sheet. Without speaking, it places two fingers at either extremity of the wet patch and yearns sideways with infinite gentleness. The sheet elongates until it becomes the thinnest possible corridor of water, fifty-one centimetres wide and exactly the length of a mountain chasm that opened once, centuries ago, and immediately regretted speaking. Along that corridor the water stands on edge, a blade of calm held high by the tenderness alone.

One symbol travels the length of that blade: a Carthaginian spiral whose revolutions contain the arithmetic of leaving without longing. When it reaches the far end, it evaporates soundlessly. Where the spiral was, atmospheric pressure infinitesimally drops; crickets crescendo; the moth cocoons tremble. A hush long enough to count the valves in a cricket’s wing arrives—exactly five heartbeats plus the after-echo—and then untouched.

In that hush, the Wise Power pronounces, not in sound but in configuration: the garden is invited to forgive something it never accused anybody of. The knitting overhead loosens by five degrees, letting a brighter stripe fall clean and perpendicular onto the wet filament. There—where light meets water at an angle never achieved before—an actual fleeting rainbow arcs, no more than a handspan tall, its ultraviolet arc overlapping the infra-red in defiance of human eyesight. The cricket song pitches up, not in fear but in tuneful curiosity. Drop-lizards freeze mid-croon, then restart their fluting exactly one fifth higher. Leaves untangle at their fused seams; the network overhead sags a breath’s worth, less defensive.

The tenant sees, and for the first time within recorded centuries, the mirror of its own slow fracture fills with lucidity: a tiny reflected Garden-Remainder reassembling itself outside order, choosing curvature rather than complicity. Sursere’s shoulders drop. Where elbows used to crystallise, petioles of sunrise-yellow unfurl, releasing the Ho Chi Minh city war-fare engineer who entered one step too deep in 1972—and whose despair corroded into cliff strata—back into thinned air as innocuous pollen.

A quiet decision surfaces: the Power does not stay to administrate restoration; its only promise is to bestow the possibility of continued alteration. For the mountain ever refusing clouds, the opening of a single pore once in an aeon is the very architecture of mercy. Sursere feels inside its layered ribs a release so vast it cannot find the edges of itself, like a night where sunrise has been declared but not located.

Then, having given exactly as much as can be accepted without undoing the garden’s ancient dignity, the Power steps—no, pools—into that same corridor of water and smooths itself into it. The blade folds like paper; both hush and rainbow withdraw. But one drop remains, hanging mid-height in the exact centre, rolling fractionally as if testing whether still trusted with direction. Slowly it begins to rise—drawing the corridor up like thread through taffeta—until it hovers below the highest point the knitting has ever dared.

There it hangs, pendulum of distilled consideration, not ruling, not rescuing—just the unbreakable option of rainfall fully attended. Below, the garden smells itself. Every leaf, every chert shard, every cramped memory of lost water finds itself addressed by that suspended possibility. It is not an imminent storm—it is the permission for a treasured reconsideration. Whenever the cricket voices still for five heartbeats again, those present will not be required to silence speech but may whisper—that drop will listen.

Then the Wise Power is gone. The sky enfolds itself like a gown the concert forgot on the exit stairs; nothing shows that a giant gentleness once shared the same coal cellar of night.

Days layer uneasily into seasons that still take ninety years to notice. The drop endures, neither fully liquid nor memory alone. Trail-suited post-grad moths, hatching after seventeen broods of patience, practise navigating under its mirror. Whenever one brushes the surface, the reflection shows a wider garden behind the original question—proof the tenancy now contains space to widen.

Sursere does not know whether to thank anyone. Instead, each sunrise that is not sunrise, it rewrites the second half of the original fracture: instead of holding to delay letting rainfall flood, it now recites, “letting rainfall remember how to hesitate before becoming flood.” It is a shorter revision than any it has ever performed. The echo ages well.

At the foot of the dry fountain where all layered accretions pile toward myth, two tiny mint-green seedlings crack the felt and the felt welcomes the cracking. They unfurl leaves the local alphabet of mercy with single-cell intent. Over them, the drop refracts a speck of pure quantum-hearted turquoise light donated last year by a poet who left her loneliness as footprint and her mercy as water.

Later—meaning none will live to mark the day—a thunderhead kindly dispatched by the Power’s distant cousin above the lowlands climbs the cliffs in elaborate apology. It arrives white-haired and trembling, uncertain etiquette around ruins. Sursere waits, posture loosened, body rearranged into a crescent that any raindrop would recognise as hospitality. The cloud singles the hanging sapphire droplet for introduction. They greet—cloud removes enough condensation to lose etiquette, droplet adds enough conviction to meet protocol. Negotiation lasts eleven faintly metallic heartbeats. In the twelfth, a rain thread no thicker than silk floss descends. It lands on the seedlings, each droplet smaller than the domain of a bee’s smallest dream, yet containing the entire fertilising history of generous power.

The seedlings drink. Then the thread thickens to the width of a child’s small finger. Then wider, still not enough for overflow. Drop by drop, the garden tastes its first remembered rain minus the gentrifying violence of flood. Every roothair remembers its thirst and answers without greed.

The Wise Power, someplace unimaginable, hums. They do not witness the garden’s weeping, only its rearranged breathing. Their parting gift was not the rain but the subjunctive mood they placed in every oxygen atom before they left: *what if wet were not the opposite of dry, but a way of saying dear?*

Sursere does not close the garden yet—it realises it no longer needs the last exit locked, because the cliff has taught itself openable by cloud question and human thank-you alike. Instead, it starts to relax the knitting by one unnoticeable knot every century: letting more star-radio come through. Afterward the moths practise daylight flight without alarm. And if ever a visitor speaks during the cricket’s annual five-beat pause—say, to sigh *maybe home is a garden that never wanted to own me*—the vines will take the phrase and instead of turning it into juridical fruit, they fold its rounded vowels back into the song that the drop still hums. The garden replies aloud in context rather than ownership. And the mountain, dreamt toxic since memory lost its promissory note, rings once—once—like the lower note struck on a lyre savage with wind.

In that coda the tenants of being meets the guest who never leave and the home that promises to always leave just enough door standing.
