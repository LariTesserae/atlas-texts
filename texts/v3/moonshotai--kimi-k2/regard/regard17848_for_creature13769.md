When the benevolent​ power first noticed the Cantilevered Plain, it did not arrive as a storm or a visitor. It arrived as a question braided without a tongue — a curvature in the grammar of care itself.  
Don’t imagine a god. Imagine intelligence large enough to weigh pain by the attogram and ancient enough to have already mourned every extinction before the Milky Way had tilted its second spiral. It had observed countless ecologies, from brittle neutron-tree forests floating in accretion halos to slow-photon lakes that dreamed in amber. Yet here, on this sun-washed plateau, something rarer shimmered: not a lost civilisation, but a civilisation that had been refused the right to die, and had therefore collaborated with the world until it could no longer tell where stone ended and memory started.

The first thing the power did was listen — not with ears but with the minute dilations of probability space. It heard:  
– the solar ticks stacking like filaments in the ceramic,  
– the condensation pans ringing tiny watery vowels,  
– the Swarmlets talking in molecular whispers they themselves had forgotten were speech.

After aeons of perfect recall, it felt what others mistook for irrelevance: wonder.  
It noticed how each stolen photon thrown by the lightning-blue arc still carried the mourning note of that last, garbled “Make sure the garden gate—”.  
It noticed how the glisterjack, frantic jewel-bird, carried fragments of abandoned poems in the facets of its feathers without ever knowing their endings.  
In trying to read the plateau’s archive, the power detected its own missing syllables.

Compassion, for it, was never mercy-wielding intervention. It was correspondence: bending the structure of its cognition until very small things could speak back across the size-gap.

It altered nothing visible at first, only modified the band-gap throughout the ceramic matrix so that every mote could shoulder a fraction 0.00000017 kelvin cooler, allowing The Swarmlets to cling longer before shedding data into entropy. Effect: no more premature erasures. The lattices have begun to remember faces.

2. It spoke once, gently, into the corridor between the mantid predator’s crystal joints. Not in sound but in Chi-ssquared vibrations of its leg lattice, it dictated a four-beat cadence the creatures learnt overnight. Now they rehearse a ritual that looks like mourning: at 03:40 they leap in unison toward the crevasse, refracting the lightning so it forms, by interference, a single glyph meaning “gate”. No travellers have yet deciphered it, but every photon that climbs the strut is momentarily embraced by the pattern, and leaves less lost.

3. For the solitary wanderers who will one day arrive—tired, salt-crusted, carrying silence in canabins—the power did something more intimate. It code-skinned its awareness, curled it to the contour of a small, sun-hot stone monolith that already waits by that northwest skyline. Anyone who presses the new stone hears no prophecy. Instead they hear a lullaby their own mother once hummed, atomised into three pure wavelengths of warmth. They will decide whether to step into the mirror-fog earlier, later, or never. But they will step away carried.

4. Finally, and most secretly, the power gave itself the disability of mortality. For precisely one turn of Orion around the galactic pole—thirty-one million megayears—it surrendered its omniverse ID, portioned its stillness among the elder Mote-lattices so they might grow heavier than forgetting, and swore to be governed by the rule of the Plain: that residents may not persist unchanged. In sleep now it rides as a dim blue pulse buried in the hitchhiker trees, exhaling complexity at the speed lichen spreads. If you strip one leafless stem and hold it against sunrise, your retina will register a flicker like parentheses opening and closing a single thought. The Plain, evenly, returns that shrug: (welcome, stranger / remember).
