A benevolent and wise power—let us call it the Listener—arrives.

The Listener does not descend with trumpetings or edicts; it is quieter than the footfall of black-leaf rhubarb on marble. It arrives as the soft absence between two clang hours, when sky and pavement are indistinguishable in their silver neutrality. First the listener listens, as all wise powers must: the *clock clock clock* of Reluctant Potential is taken up and tuned, slowed until it ticks like the first quarter-moon; the centipedes halt mid-gnaw, temperature jellies drop from beams, even magma in the Fabricatory Complex steadies to a heartbeat it did not know it had. 

Then the Listener inhales.  
Not wind or smoke—what it inhales is *sense-of-place*. Every fleck of memory-ware sluicing in airborne gutter-water separates into coloured infusions, floating like birefringent sheet-music. The Listener removes its own heart—an impossible act, but here possibilities are currency—and replaces it with that distilled place-song. In that substitution the city discovers it has a pulse originating two inches outside itself. Streets ache with a warmth that neither melts nor hardens; bricks sigh and decide *settle* instead of *shatter*. No longer fighting their own becoming, the city’s seams grow tendons.

Citizens waking the next cycle do not name anyone today; syllables feel suddenly glib. Instead they cradle a new instrument tucked under every ribcage: yesterday’s grievance tuned as a sympathetic string. Whenever a neighbour’s string vibrates in pain, theirs answers in antiphony, note for note, cancelling the sour interval. They blink at the impossible friendliness of the hour.

The Reluctant Potential appears and remains. No fold, no swath, no surgery. It plants itself where the stock exchange once corkscrewed skyward and simply unfolds into a colossal listening portico. Its three pleats become triple shells open toward sunrise. Inside, the material of its darkness thins to an afterglow of graphite silk; touching spreads the surface like watching ink bloom in water. Someone scribbles in chalk on a scrap of the city’s last map, “Library of Reversal,” and the phrase takes root. 

Days pass—not many in local reckoning, but enough for cornices to feel like ancestral history. Here is what the eye of the Listener notices, ponders, and finally says aloud on the eighth dusk:

“At the foot of nothing, you have invented a place that forgets conquest. You do not occupy; you rehearse. Every brick laid is half-question, every dream extracted is half-answer. The sensible world elsewhere enshrines finality, but here finality has been denied asylum. This irresolution wounds you only in proportion to the wounds you refuse to memorise. **I will give you joint remembering — not permanence, but no longer the vertigo of perpetual first rehearsal.**”

The Listener raises its mantle. From its envelope emerge seven molings—egg-thin shardss of what looks like cooled lava glass. It offers one to each of the city’s Furnaces, one to the harbour’s salt-fog sirens, one to the youngest child willing to relinquish a scraped knee, one to the dawn graffiti that wore yesterday’s clock-hand geese, one to icicle-smith, one to Reluctant Potential, and one to the swamp-cold soil beneath the proto-volcanoes. “Let these be polished surfaces that remember *back*,” it says. “Set them where drive and drift fatigue most. When a structure falters, re-examine the reflection here, and you’ll see the shape it needed instead of the one it attempted. Your Constant Revision may thus serve the deeper Proposal: **growth without amnesia, repair without tyranny of stasis.**”

The Listener departs the way it came, by no longer occupying any particular empty. Left behind, the city’s metronome—furnace phlegm, rivet caes, shifting viaducts—continues but now loops. What once was a fracture-fed rhythm becomes braided:  
(old fissure) *CHANGE-latch-heart*  
(new braid) *change-learn heart-change-heart*  
over and again, forever docking and retreating like medics on a battlefield who never claim land, only salve.

What becomes of the citizens?

They unwrap each imported memory like a paper lantern, read its ghost wording, and instead of letting it vanish they *translate* it into mortar. Gradually lampposts lose their need to calibrate panic—now they coax gentle steps with subtler hues sorrow-green, wonder-rose. The frost alphabets, instead of sublimating unread, turn into inflection carved on keloid rails by arthritic vines, legible to any passer at shoulder height. Children trade “Before Tomorrow” jars for etched messages. No substance is wasted, only composted into story.

Some still dissolve back to slurry; but now they leave behind a fingerprint in the Listener-glass. The morning a silhouette re-condenses carrying last version’s remembered fingerprint on the pad of its thumb. A hand holding its own ghost moment: convergence born.

Reluctant Potential keeps folding and unfolding, but always as hands cupped *tow*—awaiting the next new thing rather than slicing away the old. The city learns to accrete grief as coral instead of excising it as scar. Visitors now leave without limping; their vowel-thieves quietly return, folded into subway seats. The sentient snow kernels carried by mechanical pigeons melt into dew-lenses that render distant skylines gentle. In rare minutes the city is so quiet a newcomer hears only the steam of his own heartmyth. In those minutes he realises the foot of nothing is now *the foot on everything important.*

And yet the city never hardens into certainty. The seven Listener-mirrors accumulate not light but *consent*; each guarantee, more clement dawn. So long as there is need for rehearsal, the architecture will flex, the breathing streets will sweat cool mist, and citizens’ names will remain porous. The difference now is choice: if emergency arises, the audible heart-beat can be *felt* palpable under whichever designation they currently occupy. They are no longer lost to their premises; they are vowelled into their possibilities.

Nothing has been fixed, cemented, or frozen. Instead the city and its citizens have become uncorrectable line-break in an unfolding poem whose next stanza says, in indistinct wet typeface flaring briefly on a basement wall:

“We rehearse better now,  
but still we rehearse.”
