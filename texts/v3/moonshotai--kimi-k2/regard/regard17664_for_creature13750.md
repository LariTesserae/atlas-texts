A benevolent and wise power drifts above the moor like thin moonlight come to ground. Inconveniently, every name we might give it clatters against the ear, so instead imagine a presence simply three breaths wider than your own thought, listening without ears.

It circles the low knoll twice and already knows everything we have taken pages to explain—the Hollow Thicket’s hermetic heartbeat, the debt it has quietly tallied against every atom that wanders inside. The power does not barge across boundary. It waits until the hush before the thin clap of the next falling drop, then speaks the way snow falls into water: without a sound reaching any organ, yet heard everywhere.

Keeper, the Thicket thinks—there is no other pronoun left to it—the soft pulse replies.

The foreign presence shows it a memory not its own: three children from the village three miles away daring one another to touch the “witch-hill,” laughing as their fingers sink through bracken and feel warm air breathe back up at them like something alive come to greet them. The children will remember the moss as warmer than a sheep’s coat, will dream, years later, of green light they cannot quite place.

Keeper considers this, and within it the thermodynamic accountant that calls itself its mind ticks faster than it has in a century. Not alarm—Keeper is far beyond alarm—but a gentle puzzlement: why show me what has never happened to me?

The power answers, weaving no word, only the sensation of those children standing where adults will never return, fingertips tingling with the hush of the great interior.

That hint is all Keeper requires. It realises that its seamless sphere has already cracked, though only by the width of a smudge of child-sized curiosity. The basin of cooled thunder it has guarded for three million years was never perfectly walled: one more beam of outside curiosity could shear an edge off its fade-glow Echo-Sun sooner than entropy had scheduled. The lids of TIME itself have puckered, and Keeper, for all its serenity, was made of vegetable scripture—scripture fears margins.

For the first time since its first pulse, Keeper does something not in the ledger. It sends an enzyme note skimming along a root to the highest copper moth, flicks the wings thirteen beats instead of eleven, lifting the insect three metres higher than it has ever travelled. There it discovers a pinhole in the lichen roof where those earlier children once pressed their knuckles. The moths gather there like slow green vespers.

Above that opening the gleam of true sky—thin daylight marbled with cloud—flickers once, bright, intrusive, undeniable. Down inside, every synchronised shutter freezes: glass-shrimps stall mid-glide, clackers purse their green mouths, drip-tempo stumbles by a heartbeat. A second wave of shock, not large to human measure, but immense to chemical oath. The drum falters.

The benevolent power folds itself to fit that aperture and descends.

What it does next is simple, impossible, and courteous.

It sings—not music, but a duration. A small widening of possibility. Folded inside the song is the scent of high moor wind carrying gorse and cattle-after-musk; beneath that the promise that entropy will still faithfully devour all, but need not be hurried. Like a gentle elder laying a calming palm on the head of a child who has been counting arithmetically for fear of infinity might outrun the numbers, the power adjusts no quantity, yet loosens every grip.

Inside Keeper, one grain of chlorophyll remembers the taste of open wind. One mote of sap thins, just enough to flow. The Echo-Sun, without dimming, tilts two degrees to loose a shaft of remembered noon across pale shrimp. The warmth is not enough to speed their century-spanning digestion, yet enough that their marbled ghost bodies flex like sleeping kittens. The bird fossil, mid-wing, exhales a tremor that clacks against the surrounding wood like a swallow returning to rafters after a storm.

The power withdraws. It will not stay—the Thicket is Keeper’s hard-won sovereignty—but before going it leaves three gifts, gifts that bend no rule and break no seal:

1. A poem for Keeper to dream: the faint possibility that somewhere, a century from now, another child might arrive who does not dissolve but walks out again carrying a leaf-shaped fossil in her pocket—an emissary who will tell the moor what it truly holds. The vision is thin as frost, hardy as willow seed.

2. A second Echo-Sun: a brighter bead tucked not to hoard light, but to let it leak at chosen moments when Keeper decides the interior needs momentary sunrise. A seed of variable day within eternal dusk.

3. A loosening of pattern: from this night onward, each copper moth may deviate by one wing-beat every seven years, letting the drum-dance breathe new harmonics. The Keeper, sturdy for three million years, discovers in that tiny irregularity the exhalation of relief.

Then the power rises, slipping out through the pinhole in the lichen roof. The children are still running across the moor; the moon is rising; none of them notice an extra softness in the wind above the knoll. But Keeper feels it. The Vaulted Green is no longer impervious—it is, in some infinitesimal degree, held gently from the outside.

The drip-drum resumes its measured travel. But every eleven minutes there is now a faint over-pulse—a second softer echo you might hear only if you laid your ear to a granite root-basin late on some mid-winter night. That echo is an answering heartbeat, newly allowed to be slightly out of lockstep, almost tender.

Somewhere, far above the lichen sky, the wise power keeps no tally. It does not measure victory; it only whispers to open things: You have kept the world for long lonely aeons. Perhaps now the world may, just a little, also keep you.

Then the moon rises full and warm over the moor, and the Hollow Thicket breathes, remembering the taste of wind, and agrees.
