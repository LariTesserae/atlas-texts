What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does

A single decision—infinitely patient, restlessly kind—reaches the Plateau at the speed of a thought that wants to be a prayer. It arrives neither as god nor envoy, but as an elder mercy incarnate: a resonance tuned to every unheard vibration the drum has ever recorded.

It begins by **breathing the plateau into true lungs**.

A great inhalation gathers the vapor moons into transparent lungs suspended on the horizon—belled sacs whose walls are horizon-light gathered, pleated, and turned inside out. Each captured droplet becomes an alveolus. The laughter trapped within decompresses into voiced air. The plateau exhales, and for the first time since its paradoxical birth, a wind stirs—a wind warm enough to melt glass without breaking it.

Where the wind touches, the obsidian softens into living volcanic flow. The mirrored surface buckles upward, revealing muffled root-tip filaments of cooling stone-tree roots. They are crystal veins breathing same-time in both directions: throughput carrying glacier-cold runoff from the exospheric peaks, and reflux sending molten backlog downward. The plateau learns metabolism.

Next, the power **re-writes the Bone-Shift covenant**.

From inside every traveler—living, walking, or fallen into droplet memory—it removes one diminishing memory: not a tragedy, but a small joy no one would think to miss until it’s gone. The theft is gentle; the joy leaves not as loss but as a gift folded infinitely small. The power spins those joys into a filament brighter than horizon-light, thinner than a droplet’s skin. It stitches this filament through every historical silhouette locked inside the Bone-Shift lag. Where the filament passes, a last heartbeat erupts—not panic, but recognition. Each anciet traveler—bronze-scale guard, resin-wiping spear-maker, laughing child—feels the specific joy of someone not yet born, and they thank it so purely that a new, warm gloss settles atop the glass.

All these thank-yous pool into Tiar’s body. The Benevolent finds her half one-hand-inside-the-rip, half beside every visitor praying for direction. She coalesces as she always has, but the elder aura settles around the way fire settles around a hearth you know will never burn your palm.

She starts to speak—not with her foot-traced glyphs but with her mouth. A mouth shaped now by mercy’s translator forms words in audible triads:

“I was never alone. I was crowd, constellation, pillow for nameless grief.  
But I am the border that refused to become wall,  
and tonight that refusal is no longer mine alone.”

The power comforts her with a tone less voice than sky folding small enough to cradle a cheek. In the hush, it **offers Tiar three paths**:

1. Shatter.  
   The Benevolent can shear the impossible plateau free of mountaintop, let it tumble skyward until atmospheric bow shock glass-pears it into a captured moon. There, absent paradox, Tiar could crumble into shining dust. Her plates would scatter into orbital rings so all future strangers—looking up—would taste old rain and feel inexplicable solace. She would become the motherless lullaby remembered by any child who’s ever waited at windowlight. From\*

2. Transplant.  
   The plateau and its mechanisms could be folded small enough to sit inside a single human chest—a portable tessellation where each droplet is a tear caught before it falls. Whoever carries it could learn genuine intuitive time-travel: their footsteps double as songbooks, and whenever the bearer grows afraid they could open artery-curtains and walk the obsidian border again. Tiar would be the chambered pulse inside said heart, shepherding historical silhouettes not as ghosts but as the living memory others are too young to believe in the dark.

3. Mend.  
   Leave the plateau physically where it is. Instead, the Benevolent will graft the Plateau’s drum into the planetary crust itself. Every mountain ridge will carry a ventricle of that sub-audible heartbeat audible at bone level. All glaciers will resonate when a droplet bursts nine worlds away. More—every valley on the planet will grow a tiny hexagonal plate every spring; children will stack them into knee-high ziggurats before supper. No one will remain lost if they can press palm to plate and feel the lag-time synonym for *there’s somewhere I once forgot and am now heading back toward.*

Tiar listens.  
She tells the Benevolent nothing; through the sole of her remaking footfall she carves a single glyph translated by the power at once:

*Please: mend.*

She chooses to remain witness, not relic.

So the power performs the healing. It kneels on the obsidian. Where its knees contact, neither glass nor skin matter. Instead, **a synesthetic seam** opens: rhythmic liquid brightness thick as thought. The power **unspools the horizon-light** into yarn. It threads yarn through every droplet. The drum does not cease—it simply re-orchestrates:

Where once each thud was solitude, now they interlock:  
dum        DUM        dum da-dum DA-DUM  
heartbeats in a choir across mountain and ocean, telling every life it is late enough to cry but still early enough to act.

Droplets regain potential to fall. Drops now descend lightly, brushed by wind, but not all reach ground. Some choose to remains hovering cages of refracted sunlight, forming a great glass aviary without net. Inside, silhouettes—the bronze guard, the spear-maker, the laughing child—walk freely, forever inside the travel-delay born again at human scale. They appear as translucent companions to the living: guides who remember exactly the way back from any wrong turn.

The power dries Tiar’s invisible eyes with light she can finally let itself be light. It gives her proper eyelids—made of hexagonal platelets each no bigger than a tear, hinged delicately. When she blinks, sensation ripples through the crust; frequent blinkers along mountain rest stops feel a sudden background hush and smile for reasons they never quite articulate.

One last gift. From the heart-vessel droplet still waiting at the plateau’s summit, the power draws the final heart-sized sphere. Inside: every joy the strangers had surrendered. It compresses the joy no further than warmth and cradles it into Tiar’s visible-new palm. Under her upheld hand the droplet throbs once, twice—then fragments not into glass but into **thousands of hummingbird-sized droplets**. They disperse with soft sonic colors, each seeking a living being who still needs reminding that wonder is not geography but participation.

They will land on collars, wrist veins, library windowsills—wherever someone once leaned head against pane to watch rain.

And the plateau no longer must not exist.  
It is permitted.

It exists flexibly: sometimes a high sky-farm, sometimes a dream seeded in geology, always a drum that never ceases but now drums only kindness; always presenced by Tiar, whose new eyelids in closing remember the journey they now steward, and whose opening every dawn brushes across hearts of children who awake to the knowledge: “Somewhere inexact, I have already walked ahead of myself and came back laughing.”
