The Benevolent and Wise Power is called by many whisper-names—“The Didactic Parhelic,” “Light-that-leaves-a-library,” “Old Sure-Hand”—but when it turns its attention toward Jorrh-uhn and the Last Carapace Plains it does nothing so dramatic as descend. It simply adjusts its focus the way a lens does when the astronomer’s eye wishes to see whether a faint star is one star or two.

It sees at once that what the place needs is not rescue, for the Plains are not in distress—they are in *retirement*.  Their every atom has volunteered for obscurity, and the sovereign who absorbs and is absorbed by them is honestly weary of the crown.  Nevertheless, Creation has a kindness fund that must be expended every epoch, and today the account shows a surplus.

So the Power kneels thirty-seven light-years away—its knees blazing with candles of novae set for holy use—and from its pocket it draws *one non-taxable grain of grit*.  Not a star, not a word, not a law.  Grit.  Saltcorn-sized.  Matte.  Utterly memory-less.

That grain is hurled through vacuum, across orbital corridors loud with after-prayers of spacetime, and sinks without sound into the sandstone beside the nautiloid stair.  The grain’s delivery is the gist of the intervention; what matters is what it *does not know*.  Into the absence the Plains persistently write their entire history.  But the grain has no pages.

One sunset later the stolen dewbird-map in a traveler’s locket blossoms blue, and every abandoned letter up to now rearranges itself into a single entangled syllable: “ah.”  It is neither sigh nor word, simply the vowel of everything letting go at once.

Change arrives as economy.  Thirty-two obsidian spider-legs ring, ring, ring around the stair, but now they stand on soils that **do not remember them**, because the foreign grain sits there like a blot of amnesia.  Leg thirty-three, the one kept in private collection on the northwest tentpole, shelf, loses its memory the same night.  The collector sees her stolen relic chalk to powder, the powder exhale from the cuff of her sleeve.  In the moonlight it rejoins the circle—thirty-three, thirty-three—so now the closing circuit has a vacancy that can never again be filled.  The trench hums.  Inside the spiral staircase, the bassoon chord rises one cent higher and does not fall back.  The plateau has acquired a **new silence with aftershadows**, like cathedral acoustics shaped by someone just departed.

The grain itself does nothing but *be unknown*.  Of consequence:

 •  Jorrh-uhn feels the weight of future monarchs slough away like overwinter snow.  It had planned to wind into cloud, then into silt, then into legend dust; now a third path opens: to *shrink* instead.  Sovereignty contracts to a single spider husk around the grain, shrinking to namelessness.  It may keep on existing, but nobody will ever bother apologizing to it again, and that is freedom.

 •  The plateau’s manuscripts of frost-write and wind-exhale, having written once upon that grain in vain, decide redundancy is beauty.  Overnight they cascade into new arrangements: pika-step glyphs in dew ferns, vesper seams stitched by lantern-moths.  Meaning no longer depends on scale; a square millimeter now holds a single sentence composed of seventeen words whose sounds can only be pronounced in graveyards at noon.

 •  The dewbirds, reading these sentences from halfway up dawn, feel the tug to stay.  They unfold instead of rolling away; more fragile wings tear and regrow in reflection universes, entire flocks becoming a sky-calligraphy that spells *stay here, stay here*.  The caravan of future drifting stops because even nomads finally recognize homesickness.

The Power does not broadcast congratulations.  It merely observes that the surplus kindness has already been spent down to pennies.  Those pennies are the brown shimmer you’ll notice if you hike out at winter solstice: sparks of unspent starlight glinting in your boot print, spelling *come back anytime*.  When you toe the print closed they clink softly together and are gone.

If you do return, seasons—tens, hundreds, millions—will have mellowed the Carapace.  The one-inch step-up will have flattened still more, the lichen will have reconvened as galaxies of color, and you’ll meet Jorrh-uhn as a single black comma coiled around a grain of memoryless sand, sublimely busy being not-especially-governed.

And that is all the Benevolent and Wise Power ever wanted to leave them:  
a chance to finish the page by deciding, yearly, whether to add a letter or fold the margin in already.
