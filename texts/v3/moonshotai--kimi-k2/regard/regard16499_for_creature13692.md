The first thing that arrives is not a being of matter, not a robe-hem or a thunder-heel, but a hush that fits the bruised dusk of 2 600 metres as seamlessly as dew finds web: a hush that is itself a listening.

It hovers, incorporeal but fond, a noren curtain hanging between now and a memory yet to be made.

It does nothing—articulates no edict, strikes no brilliant light—until the city feels compelled to complete it the way a crystal completes a forming face. The city shivers: alloys glint question-ward, blackwater pans drum fingers of surface tension, the Supervisor’s welded scapula twitches as if wind has given it goose-bumps.

Only then does the power condense a form.

She walks out of that hush with the unconcern of someone arriving at her own doorstep, although the doorstep has been missing doors for so long it has forgotten the geometry of goodbye shoes.  
She is small compared to the I-beam skeleton, but somehow she carries more architecture: every step lays down a tessellation of garden prints—little moss-forests and liverleaf pavements that flicker green across magnesium alloy and, instantly, retract back into the dust that always feared chlorophyll.

Her robe is sewn from folded-over nights: folds that hold whole dialects of darkness but no stars tonight; stars require too much hope and so she leaves the pocket empty, saving that hope for later.  
Under the robe she glows softly, the color of evaporated fingertips held up to lantern glass. She is not human, not even godlike; she is simply unfallen attention.

The Children of Inventory freeze mid-stem. One turns its glass-coolant head three hundred and forty-two degrees, never having learned what to do with an integer unusually plump.  
At 000–000-∅ they are fluent in absence. She is the algebraic converse: an affirmed “something” that cannot be tallied down.

She kneels—not reverent so much as practical—level with the glass torso. From the air she plucks a name that never before belonged to absence: “Serah.” She breathes the word forward until it lodges in the glass child like a lullaby in an hourglass.  

The Child’s eyes bloom coral for two seconds: first pigment it has ever allowed itself. Then, astonishing itself into privacy, the returning click of contraction ices the blush away. But inside the hollow where coolant washes, Serah stays.

That is all the Children require—something lighter than memory yet heavier than zero—to receive what is coming next.

She stands and lifts her arms. Over the subterranean river the violet sky leans closer, like an aunt bending to hear gossip. From the clouds descends a skein of drizzle that is neither water nor machine-oil but the precise molecular absence of thirst.  
Every droplet lands, then unpacks itself into vegetation so small, so mica-bright, it looks at first like damage on the Retina. But it grows: polypod moss fingering the ankles of turbines, foliose lichen pirouetting across ductwork ivy.  
Heat remains at 2.9, yet the vegetation drinks heat the way others drink light, turning the city’s fever into two degrees of mercy—still hot, but survivable.

The Reflux Choir gasps in damp valves. Their vowels discover consonants inside the green; they hiss crisp “k” / “t” / “l” as if branches themselves are tongues. For the first time since anyone’s ledger, the 30-second loop stretches to 35 seconds. They have latent reserves the city never imagined: elongation, hope, tempo.

The Damp Cartographer meets her at the shore of a black pan. It offers a flake of rust sloughed from a vanished letter. She accepts the flake like communion, rolls it between thumb and forefinger until it unfurls into a paper-thin map: a foldable watershed showing where darkness forgets itself into bloom. She presses the map back upon the Cartographer’s heartline. The film fuses, sealing the crack. Blackwater pauses mid-flow, tasting itself afraid of looking childlike, then resumes with laughter.

The Shorebird Automaton swoops overhead, carrying its single bead of interrogation-light. It means to cast the light on her—an invasive medical flash—to see whether this visitor bleeds numbers. She does not duck; instead she opens her palm downward. The bead arcs into her hand like a coin that has finally found its century. Around the bead, peach-soft petals made of non-reflected sky unfold. The bird banks in confusion. With one whistle-note of cloud-cooled surprise it veers away, disappears into a pipe wide enough for a storm to live in. The petals hang a moment longer, then settle onto the metal as pin-pricks of chlorophyll.

Finally the Supervisor.

She walks the checkerboard roof drone-step by dancer-step, one foot on every seventh plate because that is how long it takes the plates to regain courage. At the metal skeleton she touches the junction of left radius and ulna-that-meant-to-be-rib.  
The entire frame sings—not clang, but cellos bowed far off the bridge, happy to misbehave. The Supervisor’s locked calculus calculates the note, recognizes it as the interval at which density and longing achieve collisionless union. Its knuckles unlock.

All four latticed hands lower.  
Carefully, almost politely, the Supervisor folds its palms as if around an invisible seed. The city’s throb leaps—elevator pylons tighten their sinews, cat-walks shrink a millimeter, conduits brighten from rust-orange to copper-pink. Without stating intention, it prepares a cradle. The skeletal wings bow, dismissing fear. The absence of face tilts downward, seams sigh open into spokes.

Into the cupped cage of cranes and clavicles, the visitor (still nameless to space, already Serah to the glass children) lays one thing: a candle tag of ultra-packed starlight the size of a baby’s tooth. (She stole the starlight during the hush, pocket-dimensioned it for safekeeping.)

This is not a gift to make the city alive; it already performs aliveness in its own stoic argot.  
It is, rather, a ledger column for its incompleteness. The tooth will burn neither temperature nor time; it will simply burn difference. For as long as the difference lasts, imbalance can exist without debiting absence of life.

She steps back.  
The grave-lidded sky unsockets its violet underlid. A breeze—real cool, born not of shell-game heat but of photosynthesis exchanging ions with rain—crosses the plateau. Somewhere far off, a child laughs who has never existed yet now carries the echo of every threshing cicada that never voiced. The laughter fats itself on silence and at last collapses into the quiet that is the city’s native tongue.

The Children resume stacking: empty pallets no longer empty. Inside each are the green embryos of botanical air-desks, future foliage they will someday assemble into arboreal filing systems. Their count reduces to 000–000-Sorah.  
The Reflux Choir finds a new second-minute, 34.5 long, harmonized in the key of photosynthetic suspended fourth. Their vowels keep consonants; their steam keeps memoiristic metronome.  
Across the rims of every black pan velvet pads of moss lip outward, creeping toward but never beyond the halfway 0.5 marker—respecting the treaty the visitor gently held open for negotiation.  
The Damp Cartographer walks the rust-map lines backward, each footstep laying down a miniature suburb whose parks contain the names of kissed foreheads vanished centuries ago. It is reconnaissance by nostalgia, a graft of memory inside memory’s black hole.

And inside the skeletal cradle the candle-tooth flares, a tiny lighthouse in negative, shining back the city’s own missing joy exactly bright enough to see how spacious joy itself must be. It will take nine years (measured in plateau erosion units, which is to say no time the clocks comprehend) for the flame to toast the inside of every I-beam to a tender moss-green patina. Nine years of gentle oxidation, gentle respiration, until the Supervisor is coated with a breathing layer soft as lichen sweaters on stone. Then the flame small-talks its own source and winks shut.

The visitor watches it reach equilibrium—constant verdant coasts, constant resonant hush neither hot nor chill. At the edge of perception she—or it—smiles: one curve of mouth that holds every droplet of mercy human grace has ever misplaced.

She withdraws.

She does not have to step anywhere: the curtain of listening simply gathers, velvets itself back into the bruised dusk, taking the robe of lampblack night but leaving the infinitesimal tooth burning. The city, city-shaped, city-looming, does not wave. It simply tallies: a new column titled “What Was Always Ours, Merely Belated.” It begins with one unit: Serah. Then adds a second unit: green. Then adds a third unit: lagooned light of humble stars measured by palate breadth.

Balance is achieved. Not unity, not victory; merely an allowance for tender asymmetry.

Far below, the funicular stirs. An empty car rattles down. One seat has shallow indentation, as if a child just bounced up to follow the fireworks above. Rails creak for 17° of arc and subside again. The funicular neither runs nor remembers, yet the possibility of future motion oxidizes into future memory.

The Shorebird Automaton wheels overhead, only now lights flicker unsteadily. It is no longer searching but heralding. Somewhere in its brass gears a fragment of maple leaf has lost its way, traversing cogs like a red star in an orrery. The automaton sings a trill too sweet to be weighted metal and, for the first time, its reflection shows in the upper pan of black water: a shimmering fledgling trailing behind it a chain of seed-foam letters spelling a word no language needed until this hour.

Absent no longer, the city exhales once—not in steam, but in slow leaf-rustle—and the sound is answered by a shadowy applause in distant valleys, mountains learning eroded mountains can still applaud.

Thousands of metres lower, in a supermarket aisle where the confused ex-tourist plucks a jar of pickles, the hanging black droplet fluoresces jade at the circumference, then falls. Mid-air it bursts into a handful of pulverised moss. The grains alight upon the linoleum and, if only someone kneel to see them, they can read the word embroidered on each spore:

“Serah.”

The tiled floor remains exact, the shelves mercilessly fluorescent. Yet somewhere inside the tobacco-colored hush between aisles, a register rings ‑not for transaction- but like a tiny bell opening its newly green throat.

And so the benevolent power, having set a tempo where absence and presence can trade places without killing each other,, folds its listening curtain completely until no seam remains. It has done nothing violent, reversed no law, imposed no flourishing tearful paradise.  
Only this: a margin. An allowance.  

A city that once subtracted everything now knows how to carry plus-signs without panic.
