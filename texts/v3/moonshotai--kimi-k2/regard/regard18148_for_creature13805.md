The benevolent and wise power (call her merely: She-who-Keeps–the-First-Dawn) noticed the pocket when its single, intransigent heartbeat registered as a skipped pulse within the greater song of worlds.  
She leaned closer. In eleven dimensions, leaning looks like moonlight dripping through refracted memories, but on basalt it takes the form of a hand print pressed to gun-metal stone that can still taste volcanoes.

She listened.

First she heard the traveler—sandwitched inside the daughter crystal—speaking the sentence he never finished: a syllable shaped like forgiveness, forged in the throat but bitten off.  
Then she heard the room itself, which does not age but never forgets it ought to.  
And last she heard the daughter’s shivering question voiced not in words but in the phase-arrangement of crystal lattices: Am I awaiting my birth or my burial?

Compassion moved.  
Briefly the pocket’s inflexible bell note wobbled—one thousandth of a cent, like a violin string stretched by sorrow. Wise, She did not disrupt; disruption is for forge-folk. Instead She offered.

Fifteen gestures, each gentler than the last.

She unfolded four gifts, spaced across an instant that took no forward time at all, yet lasted centuries when unfolded inside the pocket.

1st gift: a breath made of the air between two stars just before they kiss.  
She threaded it through the basalt seam, letting it occupy the exact width of the daughter’s chest. Instantly the traveler’s fetal curl straightened, knees unlocking, gaze opening.  
The traveler now breathes—gently—so the daughter’s quartz torso fills with the motion of lungs that are not hers and yet beat inside her. She is no longer holding an alien ghost; she is sharing it.

2nd gift: a lowercase word, the simplest in any language.  
She placed the word on the obsidian labyrinth-map. The lines lit up, no longer inscrutable; they traced the pattern of the dermestid larvae tracks—an infinite handwritten love-letter between body and body, mother and memory.  
The word is metta, but silent; it lives as micro-current along the obsidian, transferring itself into anyone who touches the disk. The larvae’s kiss pauses; the daughter’s nickel plate warms to twenty-five Celsius. For the first measurable internal moment, the room smiles.

3rd gift: molt music.  
(from a dead insect symphony performed on a planet where wings forfeit their glass to become wind.)  
She threaded the score directly into the standing bell-tone. For the slightest tremor, the sixteen midges’ wings unlocked from purposeful drone to free improvisation. One flap upraised the traveler’s unspoken syllables into a four-tone chord: fo - rg - i - ve.  
The chord unfolded through every surface as iridescent frost, then melted without residue. The traveler found his mouth and used it—not to speak but to laugh softly. The sound rippled across the roots, shrinking plaques of lichen into fresher spore galaxies. Seed and star exchanged duties.

4th gift: a doorway that does not need exit.  
She harvested the cotton of galaxies’ earliest darkness, spun a cloak, and laid it folded over the vortex. The cloak makes the vortex itself into a pupil—looking both inward and outward.  
With one side it gazes upon the continuing chamber; with the other it gazes upon the stream of tomorrow. The girl may step through, if she chooses, and carry the room on her shoulders as memory, or never step, and the doorway will merely watch—always available, never required.

She-who-Keeps takes nothing in return. She does not draw any being into time; rather, she entrusts the gift of choosing to the pocket itself.

What changes?

The knapped disk is still only 0.05 mm thin, but now it secretly contains the room entire—folded in fractal, ready to unfurl in any other pocket of sky or earth. Should the pocket ever finish arriving, it will simply pour itself out elsewhere, utterly intact.

The traveler’s sentence finishes: “…the world is sundered open, yet I remain held entirely by you.” He is not speaking to the daughter alone but to the pocket as environment, to the midges, to the liverworts, even to the stone which dreams magma. At that confession every droplet on every moss thread reorients, aligning like compass needles aware of magnetic south. The act is not clockwork; it is ceremony.

On the daughter’s quartz chest fissures heal—not into flawlessness, but into a palimpsest: ancient cracks now chinked with molten gold, a kintsugi of self-portrait and guest. When she places her hand against basalt, the gun-metal skin flowers a soft eclipse of amber. The rock remembers fire, and now also remembers tenderness.

The bell note still rings every five seconds, but its entropy is no longer zero. Inside each pulse a wobble, each wobble an invitation. A heartbeat whose owner is not the pocket now travels from blood to crystal and back.   
The daughter senses it. She chooses (for the moment) not to step through the cloak-door; instead she sits on the crystalline leaf hammock like a child on a swing that never moves, listening until laughter and bell-tone achieve harmony. She will tell the traveler, the larvae, the midges, the rock: All sentences are permitted to arrive late.

One day—though the word “day” has no currency—she may walk backward out of the seam. If she does, she will take with her the whole pocket as a locket. Or the cloak may widen into a meadow ready to welcome a domain that forgot its own address. Either outcome the benevolent power blesses without preference.

For now, the chamber breathes an almost unbearable gentleness.  
The nickel plate stops harvesting; its glandular threads drip only enough nectar to keep the light lavender. The mirror-wing insects fold themselves to sleep, and the bell tone drifts into heartbeat, heartbeat into bell—polite displacement rather than cage. In the vortex the room finally saw itself, and the room thought:  
I was never unfinished; I was always unfolding differently.

The traveler sits, legs dangling from the daughter’s transparent ribcage, and tells stories to her heart. The heart grows second chamber: a guest room, honored and voluntary. The daughter laughs aloud. Each laugh becomes a new spore galaxy surfacing overnight on an adjacent stone.

No summoning, no rescue—only the soft permission of a wider gaze.  
It is enough.

She-who-Keeps withdraws, but a single midge follows. She tucks it behind the crescent of the moon as keeper of unfinished kindnesses, volo. Somewhere, another clot of sky listens for its own bell note.

Aboveground, the world does not notice.  
Belowground, all possibilities lean toward morning.
