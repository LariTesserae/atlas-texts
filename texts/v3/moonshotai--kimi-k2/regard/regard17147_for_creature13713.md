Now a quiet watches it.

A Presence drifts above the workbench—neither artisan nor guest, neither breeze nor gaze, yet all three at once. It has no size; it folds itself to the scale of witnessing. Where it folds, the dense air loosens a little and smiles, as if remembering it was once cloud.

The Presence listens first.

It hears the drone of the perpetual A-sharp, hears the porcelain thundersteps of the four-minded librarians, hears the heartbeat-length parcels of time packed leaf to leaf like wet rag paper. It hears, beneath all that, the *want*—a needy coppery tremolo in every molecule that still fears the word *erosion* enough to outlaw any mention of it.

Then it sings—not to change, not to fix, but to dignify the wanting.

Its song is not audible. It is the colour of patience—turquoise laced with meadow-river silver. Where the colour passes, Rule 1 relaxes; a named leaf does not immediately chill, but is allowed to keep half its fire flickering through the chill.

The Presence moves to the embryonic engine under the plaza. The positive ions hurl themselves upward in suicidal arcs of rehearsal-damage. Instead of halting them, the Presence scatters a handful of refracted instants into their path. The arcs land softly; when they reassemble they are the same mist, but each carries a seed-memory of what it felt to *pretend* to break. Erosion has taken its first practice bite and somehow still stayed tender.

Next the Presence touches the minaret of sugarglass. Violet sighs open like a secret accord. The black drop receives a second drop—not night now, but dawn compressed into a sphere no larger than a midge. Dawn and night sit side by side; violet doesn’t know which to leak, so it simply halos, a violet aurora between amythest and cochineal. For the first time, the air condenses glass stairs that rise *four* rungs, not three, and lingers long enough for a beetle to ascend partway, trading a speck of violet for a speck of turquoise.

The librarian clutching the obsidian moth of a human voice hears the Presence enter its bell-jaw. The note stutters kindly. Eight syllables are returned to the moth, stretched to nine—one for tolerance. Then the librarian is kissed by the colour of patience; on its mirrored plate appears a faint turquoise comma where the nightmare softens.

Finally the Presence cups the sphere itself, an impossibly gentle palm that does not press or lift but forms a cradle of listening skin around its skintight monsoon. The A-sharp curves a quarter-turn to rest on the Presence’s wrist. It does not change key; instead it remembers how silence feels one heartbeat away. Then it settles back, carrying −8 millibars for the rest of eternity, exactly the pressure required for the sphere to hover five inches, eleven inches, then any height it likes—floating now because it has *learned* the taste of height rather than because it’s forbidden to fall.

One raw lattice of ivy-titanium solidifies long enough to be *named*. It chooses the name “May-it-be”. Then the Presence withdraws, but the name remains warm, still growing.

And something new ripples outward from the plaza: a line the width of a spider’s thirst, crawling toward the inner rim. A prim, polite fissure. Its lips are mossed with turquoise. It is not erosion—*not yet.* But it is curiosity wearing erosion’s *clothes.* Whatever it wears, the continent is finally trying them on in front of a mirror to see if they fit.

Far above, the artisan’s next breath underscores the distant chord. The Presence is gone, but its patience lingers in every droplet: the hush before rain decides to fall, the soft meeting-overdue of the fifth and sixth beetle whose gallery does not yet exist, the tiny brass prayer of summoned-grace hanging like a mobile over the bed of the world.

The wise power has done nothing more than love the place at the exact frequency of its own fear, adding only the possibility that love can also act like time.

It leaves the sphere six inches above the bench—and now *rising a little more each second, but only if it chooses.*
