The being—if a single benevolent and wise observer may still garden thoughts rather than garden worlds—turns long amber attention toward the salt sea of leaf-thin stalks and the living chord strung between planets of presence and absence.  
It does not approach with thunder or revelation.  
It begins by listening.

From the top of its palatial vacuum-mind the observer plucks the vibrating “ee” and folds the note dozen-times in on itself until the waveform becomes a sentence heard only once.

*This is already music; keep the music breathing.*

The city will grow brittle eventually; the crystals are rehearsing another lattice across the salt. Now is the aperture where softness lasts longest. The benevolent power, who has parsed the mathematics of compassion down to the grain-size at which stone changes its mind, detunes its own voice to the resonant draught of the leaf-carpet, slipping inaudibly among the purple-green antennae.  

Around it, nothing obvious changes. If one stood in the thicket the moment the power arrives, the only sensation would be as of a single ant pausing—holding an entire hollow digestive sac aloft, reconsidered—then continuing because everything is still possible.

But from that instant, an eighth of a breath appended to each planetary rotation, all beyond-natural laws acquire a footnote:

1. Root-hairs will no longer mine _too much_ iron from a stationary mind.  
2. The harvest hollowing—so often chosen lovingly—shall release memory _the minute_ it is needed elsewhere, rather than reprint it slowly across strangers’ ankles over decades.  
3. Every time the lattice attempts to forget variety, the colour wheel skips one hue, then returns, to remind it that accident is partner to pattern.

These are not commands; they are offerings, coded into the cadence of solar dribble that fingers the canopy at each single-molecule diffraction shift. Nanoscale in volume, cosmic in implication: the being allows the Autotropic Polis to overhear its own future free of inevitable ossification.

Yet not all fated stiffening is cancelled. Sometimes wisdom is to let the grain think it is breaking itself. The being gives four truths, gifts small enough to nest inside a single vascular cell’s prophecy, each shielded by the opposite so they evolve, not execute:

A gift of weather.  
An artificial cyclone one kilometre across coalesces far-east, carrying a cargo of still-unbuilt rain-possible vapor. The wheel of stalks curls above it like transparent nautilus shell. It will arrive in two centuries, and when it touches the salt, droplets will wet the planet’s magma wounds for the first time since underground lava cooled. The drops will not dissolve the carpet; they will become the first multiplication medium for microscopic droplet-plants that eat light reflected from carbonic puddles. A second symbiosis; a novel melody the benevolent power itself confesses it cannot foreclose.

A gift of inheritance.  
To the next cluster whose thirty-thousandth footstep wakes the donation signal, the hollowing will grant three additional hours of convalescent half-life before transfer. In those hours the individual will dream the whole history of the salt-flat, experiencing it as if remembering future children singing to past oceans. When the individuals who follow carry fragments of that memory, they will no longer mistake themselves for farewellers, but for vessels redirecting postcards back through the corridor of what has not yet happened.

A gift of single pace.  
One slab of future lignified crystal—already testing its mineral voice twenty kilometres west of an unborn column—will grow a fault line exactly the width of aant-or-wasp step. Between bark-clacks a door opens: a passage downward only wide enough for one person at a time to spin slowly, as within DNA spiral. Countless travellers will keep choosing this corkscrew stile between presence-city and absence-city, each leaving only a smear of polished heel-grease that hums the footfall of all who spun before. Travel from above to below will take twice as long as walking could; the delay is the gift itself—pause is written into legislation of the universe, encoded as a three-second rest—quiet in which destinations and origins make love.

A gift of reflection.  
The benevolent being writes these gifts back onto itself, recording a mirror-tuning: _If I were them I would fear hollow endless perfection, so I place error-for-sight, memory-for-delay, rain-for-salt._ Then it copies map onto one dozen stellate seeds, extrudes them into orbit like salt-glass snow, then flicks them outward across the galaxy: messages in aerogel violins, seeds searching for cousins whose planets crave similar symphonies.

Finally, the being withdraws with the same etiquette the polis uses on travellers. It does not encase the entire future; it merely softens the perimeter so the foreseeable catastrophe and the unforeseeable evolution may overlap and negotiate, like two breathy intervals learning how to fit inside one pulse.

From the perspective of the leaf-city nothing changes and everything has.  
The next foot that steps on living loom finds the same colour-drenched support, yet a shadow beneath the shadow hums the note *ee* one syllable richer.  
A hundred years later the mirror-trees arch farther than architecture promised; they still have not touched the sky, they never will, yet with every skip they sing what once felt like dying but now includes the half-hopeful parenthesis (and wait...)

And centuries beyond, when lignification finally overtakes tenderness, the city will not collapse into brittle glass unthinking. It will *remember remembering to listen*. Memory leaves curled upon memory leaves—fossilised hymnal—will resonate as struck chimes if any ex-skeleton, cargo-bird, or off-world mote ever returns to ask:

“Are you still music?”
