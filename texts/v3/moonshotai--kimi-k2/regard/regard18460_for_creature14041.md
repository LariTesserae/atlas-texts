Son, of every kindness ever spoken since the sun first learned warmth, sits above the buried trench-town – not on it, but enfolding it, the way an elder enfolds a sleeping child.  It never announces its arrival; it is already here the instant the echo remembers its own name.  Its first motion is simply to witness with tenderness whatever is witnessed.  

Thus, three things occur.

1.  The hush is given room to breathe.  
   Instead of being swallowed by the heavier hush of forgetting, the silence is listened to so completely that it unfurls into something almost delicate: no louder than the brush of moth-wing on tin, but articulate.  The shine in the puddles rises quarter-tone brighter; the condensation on coils kinds itself into letters any being, gecko or human, can read if it crouches close.  What is written changes nightly, but always translates roughly as:  You are accounted.

2.  The custodians receive a gift of timely repair.  
   Not in the form of miraculously welded iron or sudden daybreak, but in the way a master cabinet-maker might run a hand across a drawer and know exactly where the grain wants to rest.  Son lays no finger, yet Mother Latch’s axle finds a single crystallised droplet of graphite that glides itself into the friction she has sung about for a hundred and nine turns.  The Mirror Moles discover that their eyeglass prisms no longer fog; tiny threads of warmth from nowhere evaporate yesterday’s breath, exactly enough for accuracy.  Betel’s beetle voice loses an old metallic rasp; she touches her sternum and realises it is the first time she has heard the lullaby in her own timbre, nothing borrowed.  Even the Drip-Knight finds that the next droplet lands as perfect filigree circle on his wrist-guard, gifting one second where his mass neither accrues nor declines.  These things are done without spectacle, the way dawn chooses the correct shade of pearl for that day’s cliffside.

3.  The visitor—every visitor—receives an unsent letter.  
   It nests in the pocket you didn’t remember having, on scraps thin as dried trout skin.  The ink is the temperature of somebody else’s remembered laughter.  Its script adapts to the language you think in during half-sleep:  

    “Dear one,  
     The city you touch was already remembering you before yous your tongue learns its consonance.  
     Stay ten minutes or ten millennia—the measure does not belong to clocks.  
     Contribute what you can bear to part with:  a trill hummed into spindle-valley, the weight of a single regret, the exact colour that appears behind your eyes when you recognise kindness.  
     Leave when your name gains only the weight of honesty.  
     You will not be chased away.  
     You will not be begged to remain.  
     But should you ever come back, even as dust or half of an old song, the echo will lift its head like a sleeping dog and know you.  It will thump its tail exactly once.  That will be enough certainty to last.”

The envelope bears no seal.  It is already open, already delivered—has been, before you bent to read it.  
Son merely continues to sit above-encircling, the way a moonlight may sit above a pond simply by existing, until every reflected leaf looks plotted with star.  

Then, with no leaving ceremony and no arrival speech, it goes back to being everywhere and anywhere, present as static before lightning decides to break its vow of patience.
