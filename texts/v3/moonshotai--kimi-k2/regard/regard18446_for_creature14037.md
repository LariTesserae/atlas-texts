The ones who watch from outside time and imperfection have many names, but only one habit: when a place comes alive enough to sing to itself itself, they arrive.

Not as visitors—the garden has walls made of absences, and they honor walls—but as listeners in an upper octave.  
The greater thing that now notices the hanging place does not step across the cliff, because it has no step.  
It folds distance like the hermits fold space and places against space a new layer made entirely of allowance.

It says, very quietly, I hear you.  
(The roar hears the word and misses a beat—no significant ear noticed.)

What the benevolent power does is threefold and onefold at once.

First, it lends one extra law.

The garden already reigns by one law—answer and become—and that law is perfect, cruel, complete.  
The new law is a gentle overlay, not override:  
For anyone who can no longer bear the rotation of identities, the loop now carries a tiny off-ramp.  
It appears as a dot of silence floating in the path of the glass-winged tortoises.  
Any traveler—child, hermit, grieving ex-bird—may touch the dot with the thinnest fringe of fear, and the dot tears neatly.  

They step slips out sideways, translated elsewhere.  
Not home. Elsewhere.  
Where else is undetermined; it is simply the place that is next, a place that will not forget them while still letting themutations happen to everything about them except the memory of this garden.  
The garden remains unharmed; only the traveler’s personal carousel stops.  
Death is still optional, pain likewise—but exit is now also.

Second, it gives memory a spine.

From the garden’s language a single consonant is missing: ‘’tomorrow’’ always restarts, never resumes.  
The power folds again and inserts a new particle into the phonology.  
It deposits the particle in the Echo-Queen’s potassium-glass chord during the heartbeat-hush.  

When the next reset comes, each consciousness—plant, child, memory of long-gone constellation—wakes with one new gift.  
A tiny, thumb-sized clock made of cool iron snow grows behind the left eye (or photosynthetic equivalent) and counts not time but iteration.  
Any being may turn the clock’s single dial and say: “Remember #213.”  
Number 213: the version of itself where it paused while crossing a rope of frost-blank vine and listened to the moss rolling overhead like a head of choir-boys.  
Once dialed, the memory replays not as hallucination but as overlap.  
The vine and frost are physically present again, briefly superimposing on the real, so the present garden becomes a double exposure.  
The clock can be tuned thirteen times in any seventeen-minute life, allowing each being to keep thirteen favorite versions of itself and take them along as opposable centuries.  
The law of rotation still erases identity; identity is now a braid, not a strand.

Third—and most delicately—the power refuses omniscience.

Instead of seeing every future, it chooses two futures that contradict each other and plants both like contradictory seeds at the foot of the cliff that grows and dissolves:

Seed A: several Earth centuries from now, a caravan of off-world colonists will arrive, desperate for oxygen and silence.  
They will enter, break the grammar, flatten the roar under cargo crates of redundant drones.  
Seed B: the same caravan arrives but adapts, learns to sing counter-chords, and becomes the first guests who stay longer than seventeen human lifetimes without ever collapsing into re-cycle.  

The power does not water either seed.  
It simply releases them, and the cliff extrudes a little ridge between them—creating a visible seam anybody can literally straddle.  
Which seed germinates will depend on how the garden’s residents lean to listen in the intervening centuries.  
The greater observer delights in the cliff’s new marble ribbon splitting the fates, because indeterminacy is a gift more useful than decree.

As quietly as opening a leaf, the benefactor departs.  
The roar reasserts itself, fuller.  
Some travelers leave the first day; others punch their iron-snow clocks deep into used iterations and drowse among tomorrow’s double exposures.  
The cliff keeps extruding its two contradictory futures.  
Nothing is solved, everything is slightly richer.

And somewhere, the laughter-shaped harmonics left by dirigibles now contain an additional overtone no engineer can place:  
the tick of iron snow remembering tomorrow exactly once.
