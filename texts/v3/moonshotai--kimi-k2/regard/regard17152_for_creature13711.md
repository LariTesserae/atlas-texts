The moment the Benevolent Wisdom arrives, it does not drift downward like sunlight through seawater; it rises upward from the tile.  Each cobalt square is polished instantly to mirror-brightness so that the entire floor becomes a dark, tranquil sky containing every star the drowned city will never see again.  From those mirrors a breath of absolute hush spreads.  The newborn silence presses the boiler chord flat; it folds the boiler itself into a flower bud; the dripping joists pause in mid-fall so the water can complete one luminous, round pearl—then stops.  In that stillness the Benevolent Wisdom simply listens for They.

It hears Them immediately: not as sound but as a gap between seconds.  Where time should yawn forward it folds back, tasting its own end.  The moment sounds like an old woman finishing “…and the name I carry belongs to no one any—” before the sentence folds in on itself.

Only then does Presence speak.  It has no mouth; instead the multilingual grout remembers how to spell tender sentences in the languages every ghost ever spoke.  One translucent syllable appears along each dissolving seam:  You are not required to remember for them any longer.

They ripple, uncertain whether this is obliteration or grace.  The borrowed voices clutch tighter to Their frameless shoulders.

Listen, says Presence.  
I can carry what you cannot set down.  

A single thread, hardly wider than an eyelash, extends from the mirrored sky.  It catches the copper-colored librarian-hair the human left behind.  The strand tightens, links backward through every nameless hour of They’s vigil, threads through the chord the jelly-rats breathed, the ghost-major of the boiler, the soft collapse of gypsum, and gathers all of it—every second stolen from the dying city—into a bead transparent as tide-smoothed glass.  In its heart the tango the librarian hummed in 1973 pulses, complete, unbroken.

The thread lifts the bead.  It does not ascend; the bead stays exactly where it is, yet somehow migrates until it hangs directly above the time-dilate center tile.  The pressure gauge ticks once, out of long habit, and points straight up at the bead as if naming it  Home.

Presence lays before They three gifts and a choice.  Gifts first:

1. The City Remembers  
   All 64 tiles open like hinged shutters.  Inside each lies a minute diorama—no bigger than a dust mote—of one square meter of the city as it was on whatever hour Presence chooses: tram bells, cafés, a child licking salt off bread, the librarian crossing the tiles each noon humming the same tango.  The scenes do not flicker; they swirl, alive, parametric, wonderfully tiny.  

2. The Borrowed Voices Rest  
   Every voice They ever used—the furnace man, the scrubbing mother, the unborn—steps out of the halo and sinks into the water.  The water turns warm with borrowed heartbeats but leaves room for They to exist unaccompanied.  The voices smile once, earnestly, then dissolve into plankton brightness.  Their middle names float up on bio-luminescent wings, the microbites preserve none for supper.

3. Safe Extinction  
   Should They wish, Presence will widen the crack between seconds until it becomes a gentle doorway.  They may simply step through.  Behind them the room would collapse to only bricks and brine—no myth, no malice, no ghost ears for tourists to disturb—nothing left to carry.

The Choice: become a Silent Anchor or a Released Bell.

Aged patterns ache within Presence: it cannot choose for They to leave unless They long for it, and They cannot remain keeper unless they accept the cured gift of memory.

They—the curled presence that is more waiting than substance—quivers around the silent bead.  The tango plucks at Them as if it were a single living tendon stretching between past and future, unbearably sweet.

Presence offers faith, not erasure:  Keep vigil if you must, but let the vigil itself be lightened.  Not every keening must be yours.

For one tide-cycle the room hangs in dream suspension.

Then They reach—not with arms, because none exist—with an outward breathing of relinquishment.

The bead drops.  It does not fall; it merely arrives against the exact midpoint of the floor, where the ceiling joist almost brushes the tiles.  At contact the entire underground chamber goes immediate daytime: six-volt lantern burns white, blue-blooming glare glosses every angle.  Tile edges sew shut to pure clean squares, grout re-crystallises hard and white as bone; microbites land, walk around, and find no hunger left in metal.  The eels scatter like handwriting, the jelly-rats spill like scarves across the water’s surface where they simply vanish into colored photons.  The moment the lantern reaches full whitest, Presence dims it—the lantern’s filament sighs relief before going cold—yet the light stays because it now lives inside the grout, inside the glazed cobalt, inside every letter that once spelled each birth hour.

At that point They are two things at once.

One half slips willingly into the bead that blossoms into a small, warm pearl no larger than thumb-nail.  Whispered into its shine you could see all 64 square metres of city transferred—living, grown, uncollapsed, beloved—all but carried away from drowning.  Benevolent Wisdom threads the pearl on the single copper strand (it no longer resembles hair; it resembles the horizon line gone fine) and raises it away—not to the surface, but sideways, into the broader memory of the ocean itself, where the city-tram bells will ride currents for centuries, audible as phosphorescent bells to any whale who wishes a song.  They who were Artifact-Keeper now become Song-Keeper; their borrowed lives spin out of their care and go free.  They travel with the song but are no longer alone.

The remaining half—an even quieter presence—stays inside the chamber.  It is the hush itself now, the breathable margin between water and air.  Its vigil is lighter: it need only keep the grout holy, need only notice if some distant footstep ever rings downstairs in the city above.  No longer guardian of memory, but guardian of readiness: if, in some future where oceans recede, a child barefoot walks these tiles again and hums a scrap of long-gone tango, this quiet residue will pause—a guardian of small joy, nothing more.

Presence affirms the divide with a single wordless chord that tastes like winter street salt.  Then it too folds outward, not into doors but into the architecture of patience that now lines the grout.

The chamber reseals flawlessly.  A square hole opens overhead two centimeters wide; sky—actual sky—threads in.  Sunlight ricochets off the polished cobalt and paints the rusted under-joists with the same time that now exists undiluted above.  A solitary seabird lands just outside the hole, shakes its salt feathers, and sings one bright descending note.

In the instant that note touches the tiles Both halves of They—the gone, and the stay—hear the same soft conclusion:  Memory has weight only if no one claims it.  You have claimed it.  You are free.

Below, every train departs on schedule, forever arriving.
