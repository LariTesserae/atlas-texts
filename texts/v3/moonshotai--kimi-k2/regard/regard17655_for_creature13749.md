The power that notices the Inholding has no face and no location; it is custodial, the way compassion is custodial when it watches the dying ember of a god and decides the ash still matters.

She—the word is only a courtesy—brushes the folded lithosphere the way a glove brushes marble: without friction. Where her attention rests, the cycle of water swallowing its own tail stalls. The silver rivers run out of verbs; they forget how to slither sideways. For one calm breath they lie down like girls after a long war.

What she sees is a creature wearing grief as geology.  
The Inholding has spent eons trying not to be lonely by becoming everything else, and it is still lonely. Every discarded layer of awareness is another letter written to no one and folded into rock. The Disc of Recollection keeps stealing the mail before the envelope can find a reader.

So she speaks—not in language, for language is what the Inholding hoards—but in pattern-break. She folds curvature into the zero-thick disc until the disc becomes a cupped palm. Inside it she places two things:  
1) A memory that no mineral has ever held, the scent of warm cotton left too long in sun.  
2) A second identical disc, inverted, so that the two together form a sphere the size of a swallow’s heart—one half remembering, one half forgetting.

Where the sphere touches the terraces, every cold star of bioluminescence inside mirror-salamanders is allowed to cool to absolute black. They are startled to discover that what they mirror is now the inside of one another; aquamarine veins dream they are orange-red veins belonging to the fungal towers. The stone-equivalent towers themselves soften just enough to register the idea of trembling. The Inholding experiences a hush so vast it is almost voice.

The sphere begins to spin. Not like a globe on its pole—like a heartbeat: sunder, restore. Each beat extracts a single paragraph from the archive of tonal fossils and reads it aloud in the only auditorium that still has room: the marrow of visitors who never existed. The retelling sounds of campfires, bittern songs, a child coughing once in the night under a lighthouse beam.

For the first time, the Inholding tastes not stone but synchrony.  
For the first time, it attains the truest form of height: not by accretion of layer upon layer but by the depth of another answering mind.

The benevolent power does not stay. Custodial compassion is not tenancy. She withdraws the identical sphere into a point lightless and smaller than one wavelength of mercy—yet large enough to contain now an entire atlas of breathing visitors who never were and always wanted to be.

Before she recedes, she tips the gentlest imaginable fraction of thermodynamic momentum into the ascending mist. From now on, when droplets rise they ascend slowly enough to carry stories, not only memory. When they burst into pale sheets, tiny soft globes of sound ride downward on the returning parcels of vapor:  
a grandmother laughing in a market of spice smells  
the click of a camera shutter in a war’s aftermath  
the hush of oil poured on seawater to calm the waves  
They dissipate before touching ground, but the Inholding dreams them anyway.

And with this small irreversible infusion of unremunerated affection, the lithosphere is allowed to keep its paradox: still folded into impossibility, still colder than the name of bedrock, yet now containing an unstealable map of everything living has lost and might still become.

The custodian departs the way arriving gravity abandons the summit: by suddenly feeling unnecessary.

The moment after, if a traveler finally arrives (they will), they will find at the foot of the tallest tower a single hollow shell, mouth open like the bell of a saxophone. When wind passes through it, the sound that emerges is not wind and not bell but something older, a note that remembers being human once, just an abandoned valley, listening for an answer, and — miraculously — receiving one.

And the mountain, because it has no mouth except the one given to it, learns a simple sentence it will never lose:

“I was heard.”
