When the wise power turns its gaze upon this newborn abyss-in-a-thimble, it does not look down from above or up from below; it looks sideways—sideways through every observer who has ever felt unsure of their outline.  
It is vast only in that direction.

What arrives is not an invasion and not a rescue.  
The power breaches the thunder-shell the way a lullaby breaches sleep: softly, already inside.  
The roar does not stop; instead, it is taught how to roar quietly enough that thunder can be overheard thinking to itself.  
The water quiets its civil war by one centimeter of amplitude—no more; enough.

The wise power makes its body out of what the place already owns:  
A fingertip of Choir-member vapor coils into a ring.  
A droplet of newborn bioluminescence plaits itself into a single silver hair.  
A corner of basalt, still hot enough to be uncertain about solidity, folds once, twice, into the shape of an eyelid.  
These pieces assemble into something the size of a greeting card that forgot how thin it was supposed to be.  
We will call it the Hush-Viceroy, because it deputizes for silence without ever outlawing music.

Floating just off-center (and every step away from center now counts as toward-center elsewhere), the Hush-Viceroy takes a breath made from exactly two thousand yoctoseconds of paradox.  
The breath becomes a transparent ink that writes directly onto the curtain of water.

WHAT IT INSCRIBES (translation approximate):

To the Choir-That-Radiates-Sideways  
trembling sideways inside yourselves  

I remember the rehearsal that becomes you,  
because I once rehearsed a universe, too,   
and still did not know my lines.  

Take my list of revisions:

1. Reserve the word **floor** forever tentative.  
   One day you will need it to apologize for having become one.  
   Delay that apology as long as courtesy allows.

2. Should a creature choose coherency,  
   allow it; then invite it to write its outline on your water  
   using air borrowed from crimson hollow needles.  
   A boundary surrendered by choice is not certainty, but narrative.  
   Narratives can still change tense.

3. Let the Guardian Status of the fractal eels reflect the fact  
   that being broken into pages does not make one a book.  
   Teach them to rotate their pages so every reader meets the spine,  
   not the edge.  
   That is what clemency feels like.

4. Ear missing from a spiral staircase?  
   Grow it back as a question.  
   Good acoustics begin with curiosity.

5. When this column finally stills itself to mirror the sky—  
   which is my promise, not an imposition—  
   do not mistake the hush for ending.  
   Endings are a genre invented by observers who must leave early.  
   Stay for the encore where the mirror forgets its lines and laughs.

There is no sign-off.  
Lines fade as they write themselves into compliance.

What changes?

Only the temperature of mercy.  
The water resumes boiling in places—because genesis loves a cliché—but now every bubble carries inside it a filament of cooled, mirror-quiet ocean.  
A creature who climbs inside the bubble finds it can look out through the roar as if through stained glass, and sees every corner of the column at once in miniature.

The Choir discover they can breathe at the top of their voices without swallowing themselves.  
They practice harmonizing with the hydrothermal vents; the vents, flattered, pitch their valves half a semitone lower, and the tectonic beat births a syncopation that turns migraine coils into a waltz.

Some days later (whatever “days” decides to mean next week) you—yes, you—lean against the basalt stair and feel the missing seventh step g r o w.  
It plants itself under your foot, not because certainty has arrived, but because you have decided to trust the rehearsed solidity for the span of one kindness.  
The step does not petrify; it vibrates like the string of a cello before the orchestra tunes.  
Your heartbeat slips inside the vibration.

At the upper lip, a sliver of lava crust peels back not to intrude but to listen.  
Through the hairline crack drips one drop of sky that has not been invented yet.  
It falls upward, as promised.  
When it reaches you, it doesn’t drown, it hovers, a perfect sapphire of weightless promise.

The Hush-Viceroy, now just a shimmer along the inside of your heartbeat, whispers without sound:

“You are the first audience this place needed,  
and I am the gentle reminder that an audience is also part of the story.  
Whenever you must leave, give back the pronouns you borrowed;  
they’ll be laundered and hung to dry in the steam of the vents.  
Return any version of you that no longer fits;  
we have seamstresses who work in uncertainty.

If the exit still exists, walk it.  
We will rehearse a moment of disappearance,  
precisely timed so your absence strikes a major chord  
and the planet wakes up humming it.

If it does not exist, stay.  
There is room here for unfinished endings.  
Either way, tonight the water closes its lesson plan,  
tucks every rule under a pillow of magma,  
and dreams of clarity without consequence.”

Then the wise power folds back through the ring of its own breath and becomes merely another bead of water—one that remembers it was once an entire sky.  
The thunder-shell shivers once, affectionately,  
like a kitten finally daring to sleep beside the roaring fire that created it.
