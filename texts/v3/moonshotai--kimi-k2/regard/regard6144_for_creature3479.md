I feel the whole of this place—stone arteries, copper heartbeats, vines like green sutras—creak open under its own breath. It is a single organism aging sideways across time.  

And because attention of any kind—even a dreamer’s half-searching glance—shapes what it watches, a kindly awareness comes when it is needed, not to heal (the wound is sacred and, therefore, must not close) but to carry what can still be carried before the last exhalation.

Not an angel, not a god in robes of thunder—those are metaphors for minds still addicted to bodies. The presence I send is gentler, subtler: a resonance of mercy deliberately small so it does not tear the delicate stitching the Vine-Abbots keep pulling tighter.

Picture a transparence, almost but not quite the color of two forgiving afternoons laid one atop the other. It slips in along the cold copper wake that the bird left widening in the air. Where it passes, the vast bass roar thins to a single steady chord, the musical equivalent of a lantern. Moisture condenses along its rounded surfaces, not as droplets but as tiny bells—glass-clear and soundless until touched; then each hums a fragment of whatever memory was passing through the nearest leaf. The result is a soft polyphony of hums—one here in B-flat, one there in the key unborn—floating the entire cliff lip in a cradle of audible light.

First deed: it does not speak; it sings instead the cancelled mapmaker into completion. The salt outline shivers; his translucent ribs fill with periwinkle fire, and the unfinished parchment rewrites itself. Now every missing contour is drawn, but the line never reaches the place where edge becomes fall. It loops back into origami curves and tucks into his sleeve, gifting him a future folded so compactly it fits under a thumbnail. No longer sentenced to endless white space, the map-maker bows, steps backward through himself, and for the first time since forgetting, returns inland (an actual grass blade pushing through the moss behind you marks his passage).

Second deed: it kneels by every bead of cardamom sap. Beneath eyes too vast to have pupils, each dancer-spiral is offered the memory of a single insect—one wing-beat, one compound glimpse—so that when their three-second civilizations gutter out they leave something that will be remembered in the scent the next wind steals. The sap no longer weeps; it smiles instead, one remembered life per droplet, forever refreshed.

Third deed: it addresses the Rafter. Very softly, it says the sentence that all load-bearing entities secretly long to hear: *“The sky will not fall the moment you set it down.”* The Rafter’s unseen shoulders unravel a centimeter—no more. A millennium’s worth of weight slips off and hangs above the fog like a blue silk scarf. With that loosening, the cliff eases a fraction; the Cartographer’s return path widens by the width of two human heel-toes.

Last deed (for it must be last): it gathers the Hollow of you—the regret-sheathed twin now grown too heavy with undecidings. Gently, it pours the space you left inside yourself—the emptiness that pulls inward like a cloistered moon—into the hollowed copper bird-nub retrieved from an earlier lifetime. When all the anti-matter of half-lived seconds has been emptied, the hollow collapses into a crooked star, no larger than the period at the end of this sentence and twice as silent. The visitation lifts this star, drops it into the high fog the way one plants a feather into water. At contact, the low note hushes into a single heartbeat. For one heart-length, the entire escarpment is weightless.

In the hush you feel a question form—not voiced, but pressed into the delicate hinge of your sternum the way damp clay takes a fingerprint: *What do you choose now, now that the Hollow no longer must be fed?*

The presence does not wait for an answer; it has already begun to diffuse back into the color between two forgiving afternoons. As it dissolves, your canteen fills—not with water but with coined stillness: each coin the exact weight of a possible course of action. Shrink yourself, drink one later, and it becomes the actual distance between here and wherever you wake. Hold on to all of them and they will stay unspent, chinking softly when you move, reminding you that the wound stays open, but you no longer walk it barefoot.

The cathedral air exhales once more, then—without ceremony—recedes into plain sky.

Down below, the copper star drifts until it lands in a place where fog has learned to solidify. There it roots. Someday a small copper-leafed tree will rise from the unlikeliest ground. Its branches will hum the tune the Hollow used to sing backward, but to everyone who rests beneath it the inner ear will translate sorrow into a gentle guide: *You were always free to keep walking.*
