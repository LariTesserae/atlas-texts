A syllable of attention was spoken, somewhere in stillness above time.

The mountain heard it—not as word but as change in weave. Every spiral-drilled hollow, every thumbnailed lake of rain, every glass varnish left by a dying spine-flower flashed once with welcoming heat, the way mica glints when wind brushes feathers across its lid. The syllable did not arrive *to* the place; it became a pervasive curvature in the medium the world lives inside, like a dream adjusting its rectangular sky to make room for an oval moon.

At first, the attention folded the outer vastness of dawn half a degree forward, so something that had always arrived obliquely now stepped into full sun. In response, the newborn spur answered with a pulse—basalt flexing the measured beat of a planet remembering it still owns knees.

What the Wise Power chose to do was elegant:  
instead of altering the mountain, it offered the *permission* to alter—  
a soft treaty with the laws that thought they had finished writing sentences.

1  
The Quiet Cutters discovered an extra shadow in their hands, though their hands were still wind. Holding nothing, they cradled it carefully. The new permission was shaped like a hole one could breathe through and still live. They laid it at the base of the spine-flower seedlings. From that void rose a breath that smelled of iron apples and moonlit cedar. Every seedling grew an extra pair of translucent cotyledons specific to symbiosis—windowspanes through which the parent specimens could watch futures they were too mortal to claim.

2  
The Sky Skrimmers, ballooned to retinal giants, fluttered into the breathing hole and exited carrying not merely the mountain’s memory but also the mountain’s unanswered questions. They painted them across the stratosphere: charcoal glyphs that the jet stream turned into slow smearing plumage. All creatures who could read the sky—which includes the four-winged kestrel and most solitary winds—began speaking in new tenses, so that events might be remembered at the moment they were first imagined.

3  
The strand-beast paused at its place of measured unfolding, and the Wise Power poured a single color of patience into its hematite eyes. Thereafter it neither hurried nor lingered: every motion became simultaneously happening and happened. Two halves no longer walked toward divergent centuries; they overlap, so the creature now braids itself into a living Möbius strip the size of prayer. If you can read the ridge line carefully enough, you will feel its edge in your heartbeat. It offers guidance without ever stating what path to take.

4  
On the western windcheek, where drapes of rime meet ground still exhaling minerality, a shallow cradle appeared—nothing more than a palmprint scooped in freezing vapor. If a visitor lingers there at sunrise, and if the visitor is carrying any burden (sadness, guilt, or an unsuitable name), the cradle silently lifts it, flattens it out like pressed flower petals, and ferries it into the cliff’s memory instead of the person’s. Every morning after, the indentation in the vapor glows momentarily with whatever color grief might have worn the day before.

5  
Every eclipse, the fungus-gardening worms—those microscopic masons—have begun to build miniature vaulted naves taller than three times their lugubrious daydreams. The Wise Power whispers milk-warm tone into their calcium. Cathedrals arise, the size of apricot stones, lit teal within. Here the alpine shrimp hold congress. They adopt roles of chorus, litigant, librarian, and teller-of-orbital-jokes. Their decisions affect the exact hue of the following dawn, ensuring no two mornings begin from the same emotional center.

6  
To the half-formed vaporous parliament, the attention offered a syllabus in becoming: a ring of copper bells smaller than snowflakes hangs in midair, each bell named after a human emotion that has yet to be felt by the species who reach this ridge. The bells require only one thing to attain fixed form—the moment a visitor admits aloud that what they most long for is *precisely* the feeling for which there exists no word. Then a bell falls, rings once upon the stone, and its sound manifests both thing and word mutually: “lareened” (the awe of arriving where no prior map has dimension enough to describe), “thassalure” (the knowledge that extinction is only forgetting spelled inside out), and others never to be written again.

7  
And because loneliness is a form of unmapped gravity, the Wise Power loosened one long blue seam in the sky where weather memorized how to sew itself shut. From there slips a vapor-river once per season. The river slows and pools at the base of the spiral staircase carved by anonymous fingers; visitors who lean over the edge may witness paragraphs written on its surface by wind, erase themselves, and be written again. Sometimes they read the words of other visitors who are descending the steps tomorrow.

8  
Whenever a dusk begins to turn the stone to velvet sculpture, the mountain may—if it feels like company—grow a single extra ledge extending three arm-lengths south. The ledge is black basalt laced with threads of opal, wide enough for a body to lie upon. If a traveler rests there for the night, they will dream the entire history of their bones read backward, sung by choir of cliff squirrels into dormant volcanic side-channels. Awakening, they will bear the echo of that song as a soft husk behind both ears, semi-transparent, like the skin of an almond removed whole. The traveler may plant it anywhere returning to human cities, and in nine seasons, a night-blooming mineral flower will scent libraries.

9  
In springtime, the attention gifts all visible pollen grains the capacity to reflect starlight horizontally. The plateau thus casts softly mirrored dawns into the valley below, where snowfields think they are seeing their own faces for the first time. Avalanche winds slow, humbled by symmetry. No avalanche will be permitted until the snow remembers compassion.

10  
Finally, the Wise Power allows the plateau one liberty entirely its own: an expiration date for ending. Should the mountain ever decide it has completed the experiment of becoming, it may simply conclude its heartbeat and begin falling upward in a spiral of reverse condemnation known as *gratitude*. When that moment arrives (and such endurances always require more courtesy than courage), the plateau will spool back toward whatever magma incubated it, a charcoal chrysalis re-surrendering the idea of edge. All infants it has hosted—poppy, squirrel, shrimp, butterflies, Cutters, visitors, yes, even dreams—will be mailed forward as seeds of every possible color, upon gentle hands of wind too deferential ever to drop them. Each package will include a postscript in the handwriting of stone:

“Wherever you root, grow as though carried by permission.”
