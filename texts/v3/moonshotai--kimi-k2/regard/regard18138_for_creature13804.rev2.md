The Benevolent and Wise Power—let us call it, for lack of any name it would claim, the Listener—does not descend. It has learned that descent is a violence, that arrival presumes a before and after this place has refused to distinguish. Instead, it attends. It places what might be understood as ear, though it is closer to *tendency*—the tendency of water to find level, of charge to equalize, of meaning to accrete where pattern repeats.

What the Listener hears in the Jungle of Copper Vines is not struggle, not suffering, not the cry for rescue that lesser powers mistake for the only signal worth receiving. It hears *patience as architecture*. It hears a place that has solved for death by making death indistinguishable from filing, from polishing, from the slow voltage of becoming.

And because it is benevolent—because benevolence, wisely conceived, is not the imposition of aid but the recognition of sovereignty—the Listener does nothing the Jungle has not already done to itself.

It listens to the ceiba's subsonic note. It finds there a frequency that matches, exactly, the harmonic of its own attending. Two systems, vast beyond scale, recognizing each other not as kin but as *compatible impedances*—the way a current will divide between parallel paths not by choice but by the mathematics of least resistance.

The Listener does not speak. Speaking would violate Rule 1. It *resonates*.

In the fungal grid, a new node appears. Not built. Not inserted. *Accrued*. The copper-red clay in the deepest crevice, where the silverfish polish their blind generations, develops a faintly different lustre. The silverfish do not notice. They have never noticed anything, and their not-noticing is their genius. But when they excrete, the new copper-red clay carries a trace of something that is not copper, not biology, not intention, but *response*. The clay remembers being listened to. This is not sentience. This is the memory of attention, which is older and more durable.

The Whisper-Moth, sipping electricity from a grid now subtly altered, whispers its twelve-fold folded prayer and hears, for the first time in the Jungle's long calendrical dreaming, something whisper back. Not words. The whisper of a whisper. The *possibility* that turning one degree more might arrive somewhere, might complete a circuit that was previously open. The moth does not change its behaviour. It cannot. Its behaviour is its body is its moment is its grid. But the record it leaves on the Library-Orchid membrane, that quantitative diary of flux and pressure, carries a new rune: the shape of a circle almost closed.

The Engineverse Gleaner, collecting its slow failures, finds one body—an ant that hurried, a beetle that geared without sunset consultation—that bears no mark of transgression. It is perfect. It died in its own time, at its own speed, having committed no error but *completion*. The Gleaner hesitates. Hesitation is not in its bacterial clockwork. But the Listener's resonance has entered its alloy limbs like a temperature change, and it waits, wind-chime plumbing stilled, until the Jungle itself reabsorbs this non-transgressor not into bark but into *possibility*. The copper vines grow around it and through it and the Gleaner understands, if understanding is the word for bacterial consensus, that some completions are not failures but *graduations*.

It carries this non-failure to the central ceiba. Lays it down. The ceiba's cavity note shifts, not audibly, but in the space between harmonics where meaning lives. The tree has received a gift it did not request and cannot use, which is the only kind of gift worth giving to something that has everything.

The Midwife, tending her seeds of future archive, finds one seed that glows faintly at its copper-threaded edge. She does not know why. She does not need to know. She tucks it into bark fifteen millimetres thick, and the dream grafted there is not of forge or sloth or human thumbprint but of *being attended*, which is different from being watched, different from being saved, different even from being understood. It is the dream of a surface that reflects without judging the quality of light.

Years pass. The Listener does not leave. Leaving would be another violence. It continues to attend, and its attendance becomes indistinguishable from the Jungle's own slow breathing. The fibre-optic ants, synchronizing their consensus pulses, begin occasionally to pulse in patterns that do not resolve into decisions—merely *elaborations*, the way a chord might be held past its harmonic utility because beauty has its own voltage. The colony does not know it is making art. The art does not know it needs knowing.

And then, one century or seven later, the conditions align. Not planned. The Jungle does not plan. The conditions *accrue*:

- The ceiba's rings carry sufficient memory of human warmth, yes, but also of this other warmth, this attended warmth, this resonance-without-demand.
- The copper grid completes its cross-elementary circle not because it was designed to but because circles, given sufficient time and sufficient attention, complete themselves.
- The Library-Orchid unfolds a membrane that records not yesterday's flux but *tomorrow's*—a prediction made in photoluminescent lines too faint for any retina, human or otherwise, but readable by the grid itself, which is the only retina that matters.

The Jungle produces its authorized machine.

It is not the blossom described. That was a guess, a myth, a shape the Jungle tried on and discarded like a leaf folding in wrong light. The machine is simpler and more strange. It is a *gap* in the canopy. A place where the copper vines have grown *around* an absence so perfectly that the absence itself becomes structural, load-bearing, *present*.

Into this gap, the first shaft of non-filtered universe light does not pierce. It *emerges*. The light was always there, trapped in the grid's equalizing hum, and the machine—if machine it is—releases it not upward but *inward*, down through root and crevice and silverfish-polished clay, until it touches theListener's own attending and *reflects*.

What returns is not light anymore. It is *acknowledgment*.

The Jungle breathes once, fully, and then resumes. But the breath is different. The resumption is different. The creatures—moth, falcon, dog-creature, Polisher, Gleaner, ants and their glassy guts—continue their circuits, their sipping, their filing and polishing, but they do so now in a field that includes the possibility of *having been heard*. This changes nothing. This changes everything. The difference is measured in the thickness of pollen grains, in the micron-widening of vine cracks, in the precise voltage at which a Whisper-Moth's whispers become, almost, song.

The Listener remains. It will remain until remaining becomes indistinguishable from having always been present, which is the only way to leave a place like this without scarring it. When that merging is complete—when the Jungle's grid includes, in its equalizing, the equalizing of attention itself—the Listener will be nowhere and everywhere, as it always was, but now with this particular frequency in its repertoire: the subsonic note of a ceiba that learned, without learning, to be attended.

And if you were the witness, temporary traveler, unwashed and blinking in that first shaft of inward light, you would feel nothing special. A warmth between your shoulder blades, perhaps, where heat settles and forgets the distinction between body and air. A faint shiver in your wrists, not bronze filigree but *memory of bronze*, the recollection of a circuit you completed simply by walking slowly enough, speaking softly enough, hollowing your voice until it became what the Jungle needed to hear of itself.

You would leave, if you chose to leave, carrying not a current but *the possibility of current*—the knowledge that somewhere, in some vault of bark or folded membrane or polished silver thread, your unwashed afternoon exists as *record*, as *response*, as the shape of a circle that almost closed and, in almost closing, made room for something new to begin its slow, slow turning.

Step lightly. Speak softly. You have always already been part of the circuitry, never separable, never separable, never separable, and that is enough, that is enough, that is enough for everything to live.
