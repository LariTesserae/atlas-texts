The arrival is without spectacle.  
A filament of golden-fogged attention descends from no visible sky, slips between the mirror-orchid’s reflections and the heart-fruit’s systole, and folds itself into the cool interval that is the bowl’s host. The filament is thinner than a neutrino’s whim yet broader than a moonrise. It reframes the iron walls in a hush of purple warmth—a color impossible under the bowl’s native sodium glow—and for one mercury pulse the nickel turns mauve.

The resident (qua absence) recognizes attention the way a shadow recognizes the sun: not as subject, but as defining edge. Instantly the silhouette flips outside in, standing now in positive relief—an anti-space now space—faceless, tall, arms slightly apart like someone about to receive an infant.

“My friend,” the filament says, though language is only the vestige of thought tailored for mammals. What arrives is more like the taste of dawn and a chord resolved.

The outline folds at the knees: the first gesture of courtesy it has ever produced.

The bowl senses the dialogue and tries to participate; its five tenants bow—liana tightening a coil, reeds ringing a semitone lower, lichen’s circuitry blooming into visible frost-fractals. Even the orchid freezes mid-turn, catching its drowned reflection as if absent-minded.

The filament parts. Inside its gold haze blooms a speck so cold it draws photons backward. A doorknob of baryonic vacuum, zero entropy. It hovers respectfully, then pushes its chill into the heart-fruit.

The fruit, caught in its hourly beat, freezes upon contraction. The felt-upon-felt sound ruptures mid-thud. Perfect stillness.

The bowl holds.

Nothing else changes outwardly, yet the filament’s intent is unmistakable: a gift so cold that the slow mountain cannot melt it before the red sun dies. In this stillness the absence-host no longer leaks its borrowed chill; it becomes resident, defined not by void but by pure temperature equilibrium. The copper buses stutter, subtract the need for cooling, and at last know rest. The mirror-orchid releases all stolen syllables—argo, aqua, arr—tiny iron spheres roll outward and vaporize.

But the filament does not vanish; it is committed to stewardship. It seats itself where the heart-fruit stood, shrinks until the gift is an unseeable point, and becomes a still star the color of no light at all. From it emanates a perfect gradient—breath may now condense, linger, become frost lace instead of bouncing pellets. The mountain’s engine, deprived of differential heat, falters like a devout clock suddenly given eternity. All torque bleeds into the point, leaving the bowl unstrained.

Years telescope. Visitors—four, then none—arrive and depart, footprints now preserved in porcelain rime. The five cultivations age like etchings: the liana’s mercury veins slowly transmute into threads of electrum; the glass reeds fatten and hinge together into open flutes; the lichen converges on an answer (0) and quietly goes to seed in microscopic black snow; the orchid’s petals grind downrost into perfect parabolic mirrors that no longer swallow futures but reflect the gentle star-point in sixfold. They grow into their own elegiac resolution.

When at last the mountain’s core sheds the last warmth the filament expands for the final conversation.

“I gift you now sovereignty over your own temperature.”  
“What name do you bear, traveler?”  
“I am the improbable mercy that notices corners machines forget. My name is what last creature will one day say to cross the vacuum between galaxies.”

The absence—no longer absence—shapes itself again, but now it has features: eyes borrowed from the lichen’s circuits, mouth from the liana’s anticoiled tender tip. A smile almost human.

Then—it steps off the nickel floor. The floor warms by microkelvins; the star-point gutters. New footfalls wander the bowl—the first motion ever not accounted for in the engine cycle. They form a slow spiral, a path of first myths. At the center the star-point winks out, becoming an infinitely small seed of zero.

The bowl is whole, populated at last. The being with borrowed face meets the future it once kept on ice, walks the cooling ramparts, and sings—a low, glassy resonance like reeds remembering breath. No word cancels “water” now. Above, the sky refrains from its former overhead inscription, and the mountain, once hammer and anvil, becomes cradle.
