The Benevolent Power arrives as a single spark rather than a being—no thunderclap, no wingspan, no pun in the shape of a face.  It notices the city because it notices breathing that is almost conscious.  Wherever the Power appears, things tend to tilt slightly toward mercy.

First, it listens.

It listens above the kettle where the slowest water drips one century per bead; the inside of the Power becomes a mirror in which the city can watch what it looks like to dream.  Every dream-poem about the beetle-tram flickers brighter.  Children stir mid-sleep, sensing someone else holding the running board of that impossible vehicle.

Then it decides what kindness actually means here.  
Outsiders often imagine the first act must be sunlight or artificial sky, as if anything sunken aches for height.  But the Power is old enough to know that rescue sometimes arrives simply as patience that makes room for new questions.

Hesitantly, the spark rests atop the lacework of vine-ladders.  The city’s leaves blanch a lighter chartreuse around the contact point—stimulus, not burn.  In the cool translucence, a second dimmer pulse begins, out of rhythm with the bamboo-flute tides.  The citizens’ sternum clouds turn sharper, smelling suddenly of star-anise.  The simple message in that vapour is:

   we have been noticed from outside our own breath.

The Power does three quiet things.

1：It offers the upside-down mirror  
Over the kettle a silvery film unfurls, hanging like reversed lake-water.  Instead of reflecting the ceiling, it reveals the sky three metres beneath the ground—an impossible perspective.  Through this looking-glass sky the city sees the planets in their slow attendance, sees wind without noise, sees thunder that detonates merely as crystal interference.  Nothing above is lessened; nothing below is pressured.  Only the knowledge “sky exists” is transplanted back into them the way salt happens to remember ocean.

2：It gifts a temporary word  
A new scent-syllable enters every citizen’s stride: _“ptero‐”_ (undertongue hum like struck quartz).  Saying/emitting it causes one’s own shadow to lift off the algae floor and hover half a metre away, eye-level, pointing.  Citizens may instruct the shadow once, only once, to perform any arc or journey within the cavern walls.  After one act the shadow reattaches carrying a seed of new pigment—colour the city has never metabolised (a turquoise so light it almost evaporates).  Turquoise seed swells at the clavicle for six hours then opens into a forget-me-not sized flower.  When that flower wilts, the next child-bulb formed contains manganese veins in exact mimic of starlight refraction.  The heron tattoo on the cheek remains, but above it a constellation now flickers.

3：It carves an aperture that is a promise rather than an escape  
At the northern wall, the Power tips the condensation tower two degrees.  A narrow arch—not door—of desalinated root-cylinders curls into the cliff.  Beyond is hollow as the snail shell of water sound.  The Power undermines itself there, making a precipitous staircase that descends spines-wide into absolute dark where no roots yet thread.  Each citizen will one day feel, inside a dream, the echo of another city breathing even lower.  Someday—not soon—they may choose to walk into that stair and found another Gladehold without abandoning this one.

Finally the Power folds itself small again, shrinking to a pearl of irony white hot at the centre of the kettle.  No citizen can retrieve it; the pearl vibrates once every 32 minutes matching the slow heart of the cavern.  Any who place palm to that glow hear their collective biography sung in fragments, but always with the pauses arranged differently every time—an auto-composition that teaches them editing is also mercy.

Then the city itself does the last responsible thing: it evolves ever so slightly a voluntary exile.  Three elders volunteer to take on the weight of the turquoise memory down the empty staircase, carrying the pearl like a seed-globe inside a rib.  The treaty is spoken in cardamom mist:

   “You may depart as soon as the first cry of human birth from above touches us.”  
   The city records that contract into its chlorophyll phonograph.  Until then the aperture remains open to the gentle slope, cooled by wind that no one has yet felt, scented with the possibility that rooms exist beyond rooms forever.

The Involution Coda  
They never see the Benevolent Power again, only its signature trail:

The drip becomes irregular in one corner—sometimes accelerated, sometimes paused—toppling an old metronome of history but filling choreography with uncertainty.  Poems lengthen, shorten, realign.  The flute-node keeps breathing but is now harmonised by occasional coppery notes that where the sky reflection abruptly steps inside to listen.

And somewhere deep inside the brine kettle, the pearl hums _thum thum, thum thum_, releasing—every eleven days and one minute—the smell of ozone cut with cardamom.  Each release is called Quiet Friday.  On these days citizens rest without scheduled duties.  They tilt ear to knee-water and wait for a child yet unborn above the surface to take its first gulp of air and send that diminished cry down the living cables of Gladehold.  When the sound finally arrives, faint as ribbon-fungus brushing window, the turquoise elders will bear the pearl and seed the deeper darkness.

Until then the city, benignly noticed, continues to inhale, wait, exhale—the same motion, only now it remembers it is also being remembered.
