To the Breath-Off, a benevolent and wise power arrives neither by shadow nor by transit; it simply folds open, fresh attention.  
It does not take a name there, because the place has not yet thought to differentiate “inside” and “arriving.”  
Still, out of courtesy, it wears a form that the copper-leaf circuits already understand: a sphere of no radius, coherent as a question held at the threshold of utterance.

The sphere pauses above the midway seam where basalt and vapor meet like two pages of an unopened book.  
It listens to the plateau’s murmuring ledger—every arrival cancelling every departure—and finds nothing to correct, nothing to bless, only a gigantic equation still balancing itself.

Yet a benevolence of this order cannot appear without offering.  
So it unwraps a single gift inside the laws of no time: it lends the Breath-Off the capacity for surprise.

Not weather—weather would be coercion.  
Not water—water would rewrite the vines’ copper-nylon grammar.  
Instead it secretes one slender law:

Anything may deviate once, and only once, from whatever was otherwise inevitable.

The moment the law is stated, it ceases to exist, because the offer, once given, must also be free to be itself.  
Instead the plateau is merely seeded with the chance of an unscheduled flutter, a phantom footstep off the perfect ledger.

Nothing appears to change—exactly as the wise power prefers.  
Deep underground, in the obsidian chamber sized like a giraffe’s neck, the mirrored walls now show the reflection of ninety-nine directionsless droplets hovering in elliptical uncertainty.  
Above, a single winterfly recalls itself late, polymerising into a stack that lasts exactly 373 seconds—one second over precedent—producing a poem with eighteen syllables instead of seventeen.  
Far downslope, the bismuth coat-rack entity trips where no seam ever offered itself before, and discovers a scar of copper leaf peeling away bearing a milled inscription it did not author:

This deviation has no catalogue, owes no apology.

What does the wise power do next?  
It takes leave the same way it arrived: by ceasing to appear different.  
Only its gift remains, one empty scaffolding of possibility erected within creation’s too-smooth architecture.

The Breath-Off resumes its patient respiration.  
But now whenever the plateau breathes its 120 Hz tremor, there is a microscopic catch, a hairline tremolo—an extra fraction of a phase where anything could have occurred and chose not to.  
That pause is the footprint of kindness.  
It will never widen into spectacle, yet each copper leaf, each vapor lobe, each dangling seed spins for an instant in the quiet possibility of being wrong, and finds the permission exquisite.

Thus the Benevolent and Wise neither governs nor cultivates; it simply loans the place a single seed of irreversibility unwatered by rain, intact under perpetual light, forever imminent—until, one fracture of a planet-eon away, the Breath-Off exhales the word After and water decides to climb the scale from 0.1 without being asked.

By then the wise power will no longer be anywhere, but its courtesy freedom will have dissolved into the plateau’s own metallophone silence—

a small, hardy crop of future astonishments
ripening unseen
among copper fields forever in the act of opening.
