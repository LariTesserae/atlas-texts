In the primrose dimness above the vapor basin, a lone brightness hangs like a dropped star.  
Pandreion—titled simply the Lantern-Bearer by the few she has visited—does not come as ruler, architect, or even emissary; she is a principle wearing the contours of a shepherd.

She waits motionless, her light a negative pendant in the twilight: deep gold whitefire that dims rather than drowns everything around it. The air temperature near her begins to rise only two hundredths of a degree per heartbeat—far too slow to panic cautious roots, yet unmistakable to the city-sap’s pressure oracles. She offers the warmth as an open question: “Would you like another way to be alive?”

The Verdant Quarter answers the way it always answers: by shifting its weight. Rail-thin bridges sway outward a millimeter; moths flicker out like ash flakes testing updraft. A legible tremor travels through the cambium until it coagulates as dark amber beads along the new callus ring girdling shard-spawn oak-circle 117. It is still deciding.

Pandreion listens to the slow pulse—a conversation measured in days. Like every place she has ever stood in, the ridge-city suffers from the same deformity: it has learned to survive its landscape so completely that survival itself has begun to ossify into coffin geometry. The ice-cold mist, necessary as marrow, is also the suture that prevents the community from completing the migration already written into its own time-drunk algae. When glaciers next recede, these breathing cells will have no practice at warmth, no negotiated treaty daylight for future citizens yet to be spilled from pods.

She makes her first proposal—no words, only posture. The Lantern swings down until its core brushes living bark. Light tunnels into heartwood without heat-shock, drawn inward along capillaries in perfect mimicry of torchberry glow. Where the light passes, cambial rings rearrange themselves—not widening but reallocating, growing a gentle corked channel spiraling upward that will never close again. Through this corridor future rainwater will flow warm even when cold drips elsewhere. Enough hydration can pass that the city will taste a foreign season four days before it actually arrives—trial error, trial hope.

Offered thus, the city still hesitates. The cardiac lift could breach the seal of its chemical memory, risk revealing ancestral fossil grief etched in silicate by the departing glacier. Pendreion understands hesitancy; she weaves a secondary spiral, backward byillary, to intercept meltwater before it can touch the aching shard.
