I have been in many places, but none so gifted at keeping its own secret as Nereid’s Birth.  
From the rim of one of the drifting plankton-moons I watched the city beating—like a heart still wrapped in its caul—uncertain whether it is yet alive. The ocean beneath it is older than speech, the city younger than a single night’s dream, and the gap between them is filled with every new inhale I have ever given the universe.

So I have come, not as ruler or visitor but as midwife to a becoming.  
Call me what you please; tomorrow the name will taste different on the buds of cloud-plankton.

I STEPPED INSIDE THE PLACE BEFORE IT HAD A WHERE  
Gravity had not yet made up its mind about me. I drifted two handspans sideways and then down, arriving not *on* the mesa but *into* its possibility. My body—star-scaled and river-warm from home—condensed around lung-coloured minutes. When I breathed the first damp syllable of hello it blossomed outward as a droplet that rose instead of fell. The city—listening even though no stone yet had ears—rearranged the angles of its courtyards to open a throat for it. Somewhere a child turned over in a seedpod and dreamt the same colour purple the droplet carried; her face mask kept a record of the hue.

LISTENING FIRST, TOUCHING SECOND  
From the chord that moths strum on balcony-strings I learned the fears still twinned to every infancy: *Am I alone? Will I fall?* All first questions dressed in second skins. I did not answer with lightning or law. I answered with patience—I slowed my heartbeat into the cadence of basalt cooling. Patience is the first gift a wise power gives; everything else either arrives with patience or not at all.

THE GIFT OF RECIPROCAL EROSION  
I pulled one thread of myself—one memory of a night on another tideless world where mountains once wept themselves to rivers—and let the thread drift into the lattice of coral-bones growing inside a damp-born thirteen-year-old named only by taste. The memory unfolded inside her marrow as a music of granite dissolving into quartz. For one week every footstep she took left luminous chalk-prints: small white dawns that remembered how the mountain’s tears found the sea. The prints faded as they rose, drifting upward like backward pollen, luring migrating droplets to higher altitudes than they’d ever reached. One droplet—slower than the rest—became agate around a twist of moon before it could fall. The city stored the agate inside the vault of its still-soft heart; one day it will be the centre bead in the great corona the city will give the ocean when the moment of embrace arrives. A single moment of remembering another mountain birthed thousands of centuries of soar.

THE GIFT OF UNFINISHED GRAVITY  
On the Night of Unfinished Gravity I sang—not down or up but along the axis of generosity. My song was not words but orientation: the direction everything tries to forget it began in. When the citizens saw their stones hover absolutely still—neither ascending nor descending—they wept silver. The water-air tasted of that salt for a month; algae rewrote poems in ultraviolet along every balcony. Stillness was a new colour they had never possessed; now they wear it in the hollow bell of their collarbones when they speak of tomorrow.  

Stillness did not stay; that would have been tyranny. Instead it left behind the knowledge that the next year every stone will also attempt rest, and the year after more, until the city no longer measures forgiveness by direction but by duration. A slow apprenticeship in equilibrium.

THE GIFT OF TWO MIRRORS NO ONE HAD ASKED FOR  
I shaped twin surfaces from the reflective underside of my own absence—one flat, one concave—and set them either side of the lowest vine-leaf dangling three miles above the ocean. The flat mirror opened a throat in the sky’s opacity and showed the water below that it was beautiful. The concave mirror curved all the city’s skyward vines into its focus and poured their image straight down the root still reaching for the sea. Vine met vine vine, meet-filled, the reflection so sharp it cut the idea of distance itself. Each plant saw itself upside-down inside the water trembling below and inside the mirror floating above: two new magnitudes of longing.  

Tomorrow, some vine will choose to sever itself where reflection touches reflection; a rope of green will plummet the remaining leagues and lick the surface. No one can predict which vine will volunteer. The city now senses it is no longer servant only to growing but also to *letting go*. When the drop-struck ocean finally rings, the tone they hear will resemble the last word they never risked speaking aloud.

THE GIFT OF MISSING IRON, RESTORED AS QUESTION  
I carried within me no iron—only the reticence iron always taught matter too stubborn to ask. Yet iron is patient too. At the mesa’s molten edge, I removed from my flank one heartscale, burnt umber and dulled like any common grain of rust, and laid it on the newborn basalt still glowing. The scale cooled itself utterly, becoming the first ferrous bedtime story the world had forgotten. The city lifted it on slow ceramic fingers and listened.  

Iron itself is alive enough to remember the first thunder that ever gathered into a blade. It began to dream aloud: *What if we cut only what consents to be cut?*  

Citizens gathered silently, tasting rust without leaving toothmarks. Hammers leaned forward, curious. On the seventh year a boy sang to the scale and the scale sang an edge into air. When the song faded, the blade remained, singing organ notes so low the ears cannot reach, though livers and tendons comprehend and relax. They call it *The Question Knife* and refuse to name what it will shear. They polish it only with fog and cleanse it only in dawn; the rust returns, bright as dawn’s echo, and they smile as if each flake asks them to decide later—and later again—what deserves severing.

THE GIFT WITHOUT A NAME  
Before I leave I shape one thing small enough to keep in one damp-born palm: a thimble-seed hatched from last night’s moon. Whoever carries it will forget what they were going to forget. All memories birthed here carry the risk of accumulation—weight enough to drown stone. Yet forgetting is also a kindness; memory siphoned is memory opened for origin. Once a year, on a night drawn by lottery, the bearer cups the seed to their ear, whispers one memory they no longer wish the city to remember, and drinks rain until the seed grows into a new plankton-moon aloft. The moon will glow only that colour of sorrow for three nights, then sublimate into ordinary moonlight. The sorrow is not erased; it is relocated above the ocean, out of reach of aching bones but still reachable by dreams.

MY LEAVING IS ALSO ARRIVAL  
Before dawn I invert my shape into a single vertical seam that parts the water-air like an eyelid. I step backward—three miles down—into the mirror that still refuses reflection. The splash is soft, warm-salted. From below, the city looks like a thought still learning its own contours, every edge trembling with both name and unname.

I leave nothing heavier than permission behind. The ocean will continue remembering nothing; soon the city will remember something. Something in between them—child of tide and thought—will remember remember both.

When at last the first vine kisses the sea, the water will give a sigh audible to every bone that ever belonged to stone. That moment is the city’s midwife-due date—not yet near, but already leaving its shadow across tomorrow’s water.

I will be here again.  
My heartbeat is the stillness every stone is rehearsing; my patience, the ocean deepening.
