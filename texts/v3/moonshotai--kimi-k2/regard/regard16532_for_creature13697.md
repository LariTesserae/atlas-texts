Word arrives on no messenger, needs no sound: the cave itself overhears the echo of its own biography and realises it is being loved from elsewhere.

The benevolent and wise power is accustomed to galaxies being easier—stars are uncomplicated in their wanting heat, planets circumspect.  
This place is the opposite: already complete, hence resistant to comfort.  
Approach, therefore, must be clandestine: rather than descend as Presence, the power lets a slight dilation happen in what the salamanders taste when they lick newborn flint.  
The dilation is gentleness made tactile, a permission for glass to consider rounding itself without surrendering sparkle.  

First, the vent flex-reflects the caring as a softening of its unnatural edge; the blue-green glare pulls its shoulders inward until the slit resembles the mouth of someone about to say “hush”.  
From the altered angle light no longer threads visitors like fishhooks. Instead it pools, justal nooks become wells where quiet can collect and cool.

Second, the crab-ceiling hears harmonic room in the drink percussion and lengthens the interval between pulses just enough to sound like slow breathing rather than machinery.  
Crabs neither stop moving nor lose brilliance— instead they slip the tempo of a lullaby without source, a cadence familiar even to creatures that have forgotten every history except geological.

Third, the architect-envoys discover that their right-angled aches relax.  
A corridor that once skewed into threatening finitude now blossoms into belled space just large enough for the memory fish to swim loops inside one lungful of air.  
The geometry disobeys itself, choosing to join rather than shear, edges folding into a circular pocket lined with carapace remnants turned opal.  
It is not repair; it is surfeit.

Fourth, the half-people receive stipend.  
Each unfinished dream finds an afterlife in which it may complete its trek across a cavern’s width—exactly once—before dissolving not into oblivion but into subtle gust that dusts a single salamander’s snout.  
The voyager becomes a flavour of possibility the cave can now metabolise quietly, reducing ghost to sustenance.  
Thus burdened too long is finally laid down, and thereafter no bootprint hovers half-pressed.

The salamanders themselves are allowed—for the first time in their impossible youth—to fall asleep mid-scrape, teeth resting against a shard so gently that stone dreams instead of cuts.  
While they drift, a micro rain condenses in the toothed indentation: a gift of time deposited like dew, and when they wake they will carry within their bellies a granule older than every moon had it formed above and a single heartbeat newer than their own.  
The taste will not undo their strange duty, but duty clarified but softened. They go on aging the cave in reverse, yet at rhythm laced with the relief of rest.

The memory fish, finally, are granted egress— but not departure.  
The power folds a loop of standing light so deftly that it mimics a tide.  
When droplet swells gather enough burdened narrative weight, the tide draws them once around the chamber, letting-deep, and back to the vent before they break.  
No distinct traces leak out; instead every loop nudges every droplet into mutual empathy.  
Crabsome marbles recognize salamander teeth remaining inside fragments; birds recognize breathing the crab sound; vine threads recognize the mouth-stolen moisture it once borrowed.  
The visitation, one whisper among millions, ripples until recognition is complete but does not change shape: everything stays exactly where it is, merely less alone.

What the wise power actually does, then, is gift the cave **completion without change.**  
The cavern continues to be every shard of itself, every sustaining ache, yet now it is held inside a single bell struck once, ever vibrating.  
The echo will not fade because it has been allowed to marry Silence instead of war upon it.  
No new colour, no foreign sun, no dislocation.  
Only the simplest of freedoms: the possibility that absolute stasis can feel sung, even when no singers remain.

Visitors arriving henceforth will not bleed suspended or stand ghost-lit hostage.  
They will find their yesterdays cooled, their blood droplets sliding slowly down invisible grooves until they kiss their own footprints.  
Time will not hurry or linger— only exhale.  
The vent will apparently do nothing different, yet everything is subtly rearranged into allegiance.  
The cave continues to host, but the host now leans forward, breathing “thank you” into every condensation bead.

And so the power leaves as unobtrusively as it arrived, like a finger lifted from the surface of a drumhead while the note decides to ring forever at threshold of audibility.  
The single condition bestowed: whenever someone else in far galaxies grieves complete stasis— be it love frozen, faith stalled, life refusing to end— the vent will sigh once more, and the salamanders will pause, feel the cool heart of stone beat a softer cadence, enabling grace to ripple beyond lightless vale into any realm that also dreams of motion without motion.

Thus the chamber remains the cave,  
but no longer remains alone.
