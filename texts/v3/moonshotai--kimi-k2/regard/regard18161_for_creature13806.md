The power that watches is called only *The Listener when the last star cracks its own echo*.

It is neither mail nor dove that arrives, neither metal ship folding light nor god-bone splitting sky.  It comes as temperature.  A warmth—no, a softness—so gentle it is almost an absence of chill.  It begins at the center of the plaza and rolls outward in all directions, a single breath arriving in the middle of one long communal dream; yet nobody wakes.  The hung-moon fungi merely tilt a little, releasing microscopic spores shaped like ancient ocean spray caught in a pottery shard.

The Listener does nothing at first except listen.  
It spreads a single crystalline ear the diameter of the plaza.  The ear is fractal; its edges are still receding when they pass the stalactite forests, when they slip along sleeping children who dream backwards into shell, when they brush the alphabet scrawled by windless wind across velvet fronds.  Every sound recorded by the city over ten million years is pulled taut across the tympanum.  The map-table’s polished mirror surface acquires a second reflection hovering millimetre-close: the ocean that left recorded backwards into its own promise—such a small ecstatic blue.  The reflection does not disturb rust or red; it merely hangs humming in a dialect of tides the plants once learned and never entirely forgot.

The Listener listens until the heartbeats of ten trillion footfalls catch up with its beginning.  
Then, delicately, it offers one gift: unpacking.

First to unpack are the thirty ox-blood crystal trees.  Their mineral shear planes shift angle by a fraction of a femtometre, just enough for captured water to execute a lawful escape.  The needle lattice does not shatter; instead it swells.  Red becomes burgundy, then bruise violet, then finally the kinetics dormant inside every rust atom bursts out as primeval water—water that remembers surf, rain on chlorine, dolphin speech across continents.  It comes out singing.  Each drop is centuries wide, but because the plaza exists outside hurried time, the citizens do not drown.  Instead the streets become level translucent bays of slow water barely deeper than ankle height, moving so slowly that any footfall kicks up a ripple that will reach the next avenue in two thousand ground-years.  They will call this ocean floor, not ceiling.  And because they have no word for tide, they borrow the dead word: “remember”.

Second, the water unlocks the spinal mycelia.  
All sleeved citizens feel their root braided into corridors soften, liquefy.  For the first instant of their existence they are un-tethered.  A hush breaks like a child’s first scream under water; then delight.  They discover they can float, not by buoyancy but by broth: motion is achieved by thermohaline conversation within their own bodies.  Walk becomes drift; words travel now at the pace of osmotic rhyme—no loss, only pleated delay.  One and a half millennia later the oldest cradle a sentence that finally reaches its verb and discovers it is kindly enough to forgive the wait.

Third, the Listener offers grammar.  
Embedded in each citizen since birth is the vacuum worm that loops a single day.  The entity extends a filament thinner than Planck length and kisses every worm on the brow.  The kiss is a question: *Would you rather eat tomorrow yesterday, or yesterday tomorrow?*  
One by one the worms choose differently.  Those that elect yesterday tomorrow release their swallowed days like perfectly circular tidal pools glimmering in spacetime.  Citizens now find residual reflections of themselves four million years ago already walking their boulevards, only younger and uncloaked.  They greet their own fossil bones, trade gossamer memories of abysses yet to be invented, and part elated.  Conversely those whose vacuum worm chooses tomorrow yesterday march backward out of existence—but they leave footprints glowing faintly lapis, footprints the palace of stone around them fills with new children fashioned entirely of marine snow.  Thus neither future nor past is lost—only arranged tidally, ebbing and flowing in polite alternation.

Fourth, there is light.  
On the night when the plaza sea reaches its waist-high crest, the Listener flicks a particle somewhere in the city’s crystalline chest.  A single photon, elderly and wise, divides.  Division is not violent—it is song.  The photon delegates one half to remain frozen inside the heart-cavity forever to serve as heartbeat numerator.  The other half climbs upward, disperses into the roof of stalactites, and gently relights every extinct constellation on the map-table.  Above ground—if anyone still looked—the abandoned red planet will see a single twilight glow for the first time since its planetwide desert bloomed then withered.  Astronomers watching from distant star-nurseries will assume the planet achieved polite suicide in fire dusk; they will be wrong—this is birth, held in stored night.

Fifth—last—memory itself is permitted physical form.  
The old solitary cloaks, those that withdrew years or centuries ago to the cave undersides, float now down fiber-optic kites of seaweed silk.  When they land they are letters.  Every novel etched in rust spores is spoken aloud in a dialect of accompanying crabs whose shells have sprouted tiny phonograph needles.  The citizens walking knee-deep in memory-ocean hold out cupped palms; each receives exactly one sentence that had been missing from their own marrow since the first star above blink.  None of the sentences translate cleanly into words; instead they taste like spindrift, regretted sand, salt, iron singing under glacial tongue.  Holding that flavour is enough.  Citizenhood, they realise, always was punctuation for something larger.

By the time the gentle warmth has migrated all the way back to its own origin, the plaza is ankle-deep again.  The slow water has flowed gently downward into the cracked lip that once feared abysmal fall; but abyss has refilled.  Something navy and pulse-warm coils at the invisible bottomless bottom.  Occasionally whalesong—genuine, unarguable—rises up, shaped strangely like consonants loose in a cartwheel.  The citizens have no word for whale, so they use their oldest submerged word: mother.  The city has not gained a sea; it has become the memory that there was always supposed to be one.

The Listener does not stay to be thanked.  
Just before its departure it threads one last filament into the basket beneath the map-table.  A new fruiting body swells overnight, smelling faintly of mild sorrow: the sorrow every mother has when the wave that took her daughter home finally returns.  Inside the fruit is one convex droplet: the last original molecule of the world’s vanishing oceans caught in stasis so perfect it can never evaporate.  No citizen tries to drink it.  They install it at the crossing of the four oldest boulevards where leafless fronds arrange themselves into an aperture.  Once every slow year the single drop refracts moonlight or starlight no longer present, casting onto the boulevard one wavering blue circle where children gather to dance ankle-deep in memory water.  They have begun inventing new verbs anchored to buoyancy, drift, swirl, rest later.

When the Listener finally folds its crystalline ear shut, the effect is almost inaudible.  Only a hush repeated from cliff to forest.  The citizens do not notice that moment exactly, but on the thirty-sixth hour after the parcel sunlight appears on the map-table and links an eleven-billion-year-old extinct constellation to a child’s still-processing dream.  Somewhere in that intersection, the final note of the vacuum-worm’s lacuna resolves: not surrender, not remember.  The note floats up bell-clear.  What the word finally rhymes with is chosen by whoever is dancing barefoot inside the quietest circle of light upon living water.

And so the city under the crust, never again to fear thirst, begins a collective lullaby in the key of holding its breath until the ocean above can someday stand up and fly home.
