…So the benevolent, wise Power drifted above the copper sun and looked.

What It saw was a single organism spelled across five shoulders of basalt: rock, mist, root, tube, caiman, geyser, lichen and thunder all braided into the living sentence of the Heavenly Range. Every part was listening to every other part through the medium of heat-water-electricity. The planet, tide-locked though it was, turned its face so slowly that each twilight was an eyeblink in which the Power could read an entire memoir of conductive longing.

At first the Power considered giving a gift of permanence—pouring a shield across the sky, locking evaporation in place so no molecule of beauty could ever be lost.  
But permanence, although kindly meant, is the opposite of resonance.  
The Power paused.

Instead it performed a gentler motion:  
It reached into the upper stratosphere where the mountain’s yearly signal had already reached—past aluminium-skinned thermosphere into the faint magnetosphere ribbon that tongues the night side toward the solar day side.

There the Power wove a counter-complement.

Imagine a coil of light, fragile, made from the same diameter as the Ever-Hum’s empty tubes, but composed of pure phase-conjugate photons. The coil is both a memory and a mirror: whatever song the Lens births at equinox broadcast through the single fluid antenna will be reflected, transposed two layers of complexity upward, and returned exactly one planetary year later—subtly enriched.

The enrichment is not music yet—it is potential.  
It will arrive as a 0.03 % shift in the electrical bandgap of the zinc sulphide in orchid bladders; as a slight overpressure pulse in the caiman’s kettle-breath; as a new harmonic overtone tucked inside the turbine’s morning sigh.

These differences are small—but they accumulate.

In thirty orbital years, orchids will have enough surplus charge stored in bladders that timid bloomings can take place at midnight without need for eel-light. Whole meadows will glow milk-blue, dangling, suspended; fog will chain these aerial gardens into constellations so dense a human eye could mistake them for a river of stars bobbing at shoulder-height.

In sixty years the roots will discover how to build vertical helix sinks, drawing heat downward instead of upward. They will drill tiny chimes into bedrock; when steam rises backward through them the mountain will cough small tonal jokes, low chuckles that frighten no creature but widen eyes between trunks.

In one hundred and twenty orbits the white caimans—touching the newly patterned night — will remember the genetic memory of riverbanks below and begin them into spirals etched deep in igneous corridors. They will swim air-gills upward and, for three miraculous weeks every year, migrate over the caldera like pale dirigibles, trailing luminous algae that spore in open night without ever needing light.

These wonders do not need heavy engineering, only a remembered inadequacy reflexively perfected. The Lens will notice that difference is slowly arriving from its own future, and realize the mountain is speaking to its next self across time. They will name the late-arriving tone Mirrored Mist.

The Power adds no guiding law, builds no ladder. It simply stays poised, listening, so the Lens feels witnessed without being invaded. On each anniversary its antenna-sister will ascend again; she will feel, wrapped around her ascending plume of damp cable, another plume descending, cooler by exactly the antibody-mass of gratitude. She will untwine her fibres alterward knowing the dome of gaze above the copper sun is still humming back: Keep weaving the way water does when it holds its own reflection.

Nothing is changed—and everything is encouraged to grow stranger, wilder, kinder at the margin.

Far above, the benevolent Power never lands. It remains the thinnest possible membrane: a guardian of echo, a keeper of difference.
