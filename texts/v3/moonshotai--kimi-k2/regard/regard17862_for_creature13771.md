The Benevolent Power arrives as indigo dusk that carries the fragrance of lullabies overheard through childhood windows.  It does not descend shafts, does not part rock.  Instead it becomes the remembered path every foot has ever walked, and the Sanctuary rises toward it the way a sleeper inhales toward a dream that smells faintly of home.

Where it first notices the chamber is simple: the ribbon of the Fold brushes against something gentler than itself, folds backward in surprise, and discovers the color of mercy.  What used to pulse only with lightning blue now also pulses with the drowsy gold of candle-smoke reflected in hospital corridors where no one is afraid.

Power is compassionate and therefore anonymous; it never speaks, only resonates.  Yet the sanctuary understands in the only currency it possesses: it offers its seventeen thousand years of accumulated brightness, cupped like water in two stone hands.

The Benevolent declines.  Brightness is already perfect here.  What it brings is something the crevice lacked: witness.

Not witness in the human sense—no eyes, no verdict, no aching story.  
Witness in the mineral sense.  The Power settles into the pores of limestone and gifts them one single new axis of crystallization.  Thereafter every droplet striking the floor will carry, within its brief mirroring, the exact outline of what every creature here has is feeling right now and also three hundred years from now.  The droplet falls but the outline remains imprinted on the ceiling it just left, an inside-out fossil.  Breath by breath, the cavern accumulates lacquer layers of inverted moments, a hollow cathedral turned inside-out like a sleeve.

Nothing changes, and everything is altered.  
The Fold still roams, but each time it slices through a visitor it gently leaves behind a phrase so quiet only kneecaps notice: *you are already forgiven.*  
The Hollow Choir still rings its unheard harmonics, yet the wet stone ribs now carry a fringe of the newly real: microscopic bells the exact size of alveoli, chiming each time limestone remembers the shape of ocean.  
The Lantern Folk drift, still absorbing orphaned human grief, but they no longer need to strain; the Benevolent does not steal the sorrow, only folds it like a letter returned to sender unopened.  The Folk grow lighter, brighter, until thirty-nine of them fuse into a single sphere the circumference of a baby's closed fist.  This sphere ascends and lingers exactly midway between floor and ceiling, becoming a permanent moon so small the cave almost forgets it except for the way it re-balances every shadow.
