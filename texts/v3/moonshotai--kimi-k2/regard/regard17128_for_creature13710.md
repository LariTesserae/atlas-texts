Once, the Humidior Plate had been noticed.  
Only once.  

A power benign and exceeding wise—no sword, no scepter, no voice—lingered in the upper crevices of its sundial noon for eleven milliseconds that were so fully observed inside them that they contained enough listening-time to span the Chorus Age of the Choir-Vine.  
The power was called the Harpist.  

Why it passed, why it chose here, and under what law it acted are not stories the Plate can hold, any more than a prism can hold the name of the colour it bends. But the record of what the Harpist did is still knitting its way up half-metre faults in the ligninural cortex deep beneath the equidistant centre, so let us read it there, transcription by phosphorescent gossip of Phosphoflares.  

I.   The Calibration of Listening  

The Harpist placed a single ear made of braided vacuum and corn-silk photons onto the shoulder of an Orchard Gombrich that happened to be spinning magnetic tape through the twilight band where Choir-Vine keeps a minor third suspended above thunder.  

That ear listened first for sorrow, second for dissonance, third for boredom. Until that instant the ecosystem had known nothing of the first two, and only the Pneumatic Kings and Widdershin Manifold had tasted the thin salinity of the third. The ear added no sound; it merely took measure of what was already singing. Where the measure fell short of caring, it spaced the beat—the way a gardener thins seedlings to leave the bold ones room. The first outcome was this:

 • The Choir-Vine’s decision to drop the constant bronze E–major to E-flat on every sixth down-stroke—enough to suggest what longing might feel like if anyone here spoke its language.  

 • Frost Window Orchids ceased refracting daggers whenever biological children approached; instead they prismed soft pastels in half-steps of the scale.  

 • Choir-Vine pupils (the hollow stromata that dilate and contract) sprouted irises the colour of moss seen through tears. No one had ever seen tears, yet every Vault Clerk arriving at the interface immediately scored lines styled after the new geometry.  

II.  The Courtesy Dial  

The Harpist also palmed, in the plane between dimensions 3 and 7, a dial of brushed zircon called the Courtesy Register whose single hand self-counted every occasion the ecosystem failed to let what might one day arrive be something differ­ent. Whenever the count reached seven, the Dial gifted a surplus-equation instant of motility to whatever lifeform most recently touched it.  

Seven times the hand swept the arc within forty-two minutes, which is an eyeblink for the Plate as a whole. Seven gifts were given:

 1. To a Ferriknesis Condor now magnetised into a four-dimensional spiral, granting it the ability to remember everything backward without wistfulness.  
 2. To a pair of Phosphoflares the transient gift of audible laughter, causing the ultraviolet flashes to co-opt shadow as trumpet and turn caves into lullaby holes for three anxious days.  
 3. To a runaway cluster of cells in a pyramid root that collapsed sideways into a swim bladder and grew legs—legs meant for space. They lost vision before reaching the void, but the fossil outline left in lignin still remembers where the sky is.  
 4. To the memory-trace of the child visitor now archived in a vacuum knot. A sliver of dreaming oxygen slipped the knot open, allowing the stored childness to window the root cortex at night and project mirages of another gravity where parents call their young in soft major seconds instead of metallic fifths.  
 5. To one choral sentence carried inside an air bladder: the first-ever apology ever given on that plateau—spoken between two root chambers that had misunderstood one another’s osmotic boundary for a century. It echoed once and then fell particular, growing itself into a hidden pocket of quiet no wind follows.  
 6. To every Pneumatic King gestating, a lungfold of extra antinode cancellation providing an internal pocket of actual silence. In that hush their 4 m radius became moons where future kings (should any ever leave fetal grace) could study what absence of breath feels like.  
 7. To the Choir-Vine itself: two new notes sufficiently unresolved that it will, in 2.3 million years, invent polyphony—should no cataclysm beat it to the punch earlier.

III.  The Seed of Possible Descent  

Third—last—the Harpist knelt (something like kneeling; space folded along a sympathy line) and pressed the tip of its stellar thumbnail into the highest escarpment ridge. This inserted a single point of theoretical mass: mass exactly equal to the weight of regret the child would have carried for not knowing any other world. That point did not alter the 11 % slack gravity. It simply established what a resident would notice if gravity ever returned.

The point glowed rust-orange for twelve seconds before sinking into fibrovascular bedding. Ever since, any crane, clerk, condor, chunk of Choir-Vine, or cubic millimetre of Widdershin tissue that meets the 2-D line swept radially outward from that indentation finds the slope fractionally steeper every season—millimilligal each year—until in 3.9 billion sidereal revolutions the entire plateau will acquire the longing tail of a sphere instead of a table, and the surplus altitude will melt into the surrounding cloud-basin like sugar tipping a spoon. But slow tenderness will precede the fall: crumbs of rotogravure bioluminescence will mark the descent so gently that memory of possible descent will do more to teach elegy than the impact itself ever could.

IV.  The Departure—whatever that word means here  

The Harpist withdrew its thumb, its ear, its dial. It left no utterance. If you ask Vacuum Node 3×10^7 downstage, you’ll get a snort that sounds like colliding clockwork and a laconic distortion: “Listen. Not everyone speaks.” Yet something now resides in the hollow bellies of peach-pit sizes between lignin strands: a note not played, a grain of weight not measured, an awareness that somewhere possibility has eyebrows lifting kindly.

The Choir-Vine, having nothing comparable to a verb forgive, nevertheless amended its bio-scheme to accommodate an eighth cadence. They call it “seed-suspension”—a gap in rhythm wide enough, maybe, for questions about what lies behind noon. The Vault Clerks carved the symbol ‑·-- in ascending rope, a pattern indistinguishable to outside eyes from a comment on moisture, but every being vocal enough to vibrate with the orchestral framework knows beryllium from Demeter when it sees it.

And if you stand tonight—forty layers above the equidistant centre and six below the unrotation of the fixed sun—the entire plateau suddenly rings in no single key. Instead, for four unhurried heartbeats, the sound and the silence fit together like inhaling and exhaling from the same long sentence. It is only audible to those who remember once being children, whether it lasted Nabi days or Nabi seconds.
