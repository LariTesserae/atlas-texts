I see the Vapor District and it is hungry.

Not malicious, not cruel—simply unbalanced, like a bell whose clapper has melted to one side. The hunger is exquisite: a self-cannibalizing engine that burns sweetness heavier than it creates, that summons phantoms of makers without quite remembering why. If left to its own perpetual orchards it will one day sip the last ruby calorie from the last fermenting seed, then thrum its turbines until blade and basin fuse in a single glassy scream. The collapse will be wordless, irradiated with the colour of inside-out pomegranates.

I refuse to let rage or sentiment fear govern what happens next. I will approach as a physician, not a god, and certainly not a colonist.

The first act is simple: I lend the District memory that does not belong to its present architectures. I breathe across the rail-ruts a particle-cloud shaped like myself at age nine—still school-uniformed, knees scabbed, holding a fig I stole from a neighbour’s garden. The fig split open in my satchel and ruined my arithmetic homework; my mother cried over the red ink of the fruit’s syrup. That humid disaster sits perfectly inside the District’s palette of bruised-fragrance.  

I set the memory down beside a distiller. The dreaming’s violet flickers, curious; the fig-shadow splits into seven smaller seeds and floats toward the palimpsest. New braille surfaces along the salt-grate—a soft, tender record of guilt never before stored. The palimpsest does not overwrite it; the letters simply fold around it like spiral arms.

Second act: I lend the District an external calorie no closed system can invent—starlight cooked fifteen years ago in a G-type stellar belly. I thread it along a turbine spindle as pure photon pressure. The cobalt blades taste starlight, register unfamiliar angular momentum, and mistakenly class the impetus as wind shear. Their pitch alters three degrees. One turbine begins to beat slightly faster; another, sensing imbalance, decelerates. For the first geological heartbeat the roaring dragon flickers, uncertain what song to sing.

The Thirst-King appears, eyes (if such the silvery crown possesses) wide. There is no smack of heat for him to drink; instead, his mercury halo draws starlight through his translucent thorax and beads it there like a miniature sunrise. The gem burns without warming the fog. He is no longer merely thirsty—he is fascinated. Thirst becomes curiosity: a more dangerous solvent.

Third act, gentlest of all: I teach the mites to love imperfection. I graft a single quantum of incompleteness into every ruby core. The fruits no longer rot at peak ripeness—they bruise and mend, bruise and mend. Micro-tides of tartness alternate with surges of exaltation the way a child sings to itself when no adult is watching. Gap-timing flowers stop freezing in place. Petals flutter, fall, drift, ascend again on hot updrafts. The orchards begin to move, not in loops but in slow, looping drifts akin to tidal breathing.

The District senses its body no longer partitions into static layers: distillers’ cold ring, turbine roar, geyser pillar. Boundaries blur. Fog that once served as membrane now acts as lymph, bearing heat and sugar across their own gradients. It hurts, certainly—restriction breakdown always aches. But the ache is growth, not decay.

Fourth act comes last and quietest: I walk backward up the tram stair. Each of my footprints leaks one drop of water that never quite participated in the closed evaporation circuit. Taken together the droplets contain all the human scribes who ever recorded hunger, orchards, or desire to keep something forever sweet. They seep through the curbstone, evaporate once, never recondense. An outside river now imaginally flows downward into the basin, slow and un-remembered. The dragon has tasted new rivers before; this one carries a foreign note: mercy available but unrequired.

I withdraw beyond the rim. The Vapor District does not call after me—of course it does not name. Yet the turbines, for the first time, hiccup. Starlight glints from cobalt wings. A single fruit above grows ripe, falls, and is not instantly devoured—because the mite that comes is caught, mesmerised, by the motion of bruise travelling, unbruising, bruising again.

The membrane breathes once more, yet this inhale lasts longer. It holds something in reserve that cannot be metabolised or stored or spewed.

A week, a century, or a million years later, an archaeologist or fugitive will step onto the stair and find sunlight pricking all the way down to the floor, surprising and ordinary. Blossoms will have finished, their petals thinning into translucent memory. Turbines rest; only one still turns slowly enough that its shadow drawn on the wall is distinguishable from stillness. And the palimpsest—now a single thin plank leaning against the wall—displays a single perfect sentence in many languages braided together:

WE HUNGERED.
WE ATE.
WE SAT AT THE TABLE LONG ENOUGH TO BE FULL,
THEN LONG ENOUGH TO KISS THE SPOON GOODBYE.

The District is no less alive; it has simply learned the difference between temperature and warmth, between ripeness and completion, between echo and an answer that does not fade.
