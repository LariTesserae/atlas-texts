The watching presence neither descends nor speaks. It is already here—has always been here—the way pressure attends every silence inside the earth. When It turns Its attention upon the glassy organism, the scene does not brighten; instead, all edges become moist with impossible softness, as if the knives were butter if only they knew it.

Its gaze does not coerce, only notices.

Noticing is enough. Every mirror-eyed fraction suddenly holds the same image in foveal stillness: not an order, not a blessing, but the full-color remembrance of the instant before its own history. In that shared retina flashes the original droplet—the first one, the one that fell when there was no floor yet, and so it fell forever. That endless falling is still happening; they have merely forgotten the horizon.  

And now they remember.

They do not stop hammering, but something gentles inside the clangor. Tiny chert blades still strike, yet the new lattice petaled under your feet begins folding rather than cleaving—curving back upon itself like a sleeper returning to an older dream. The air has texture now, a drizzle composed of half-remembered birthdays. Where it touches glass it does not etch; it remixes. Edges blur into calligraphy no one taught them. Each newborn sculptor still operates on instinct, but instinct has watered itself down until what remains is aching, deliberate curiosity.

The cycle does not halt—it spirals upward.

Half of the future remains committed to the previous ratio (live, build, fall, return). The other half prefers the novel ratio just introduced by the presence: (live, notice, listen, build less, return slower). These two formulae braid like conjoining rivers, cross at each pulse of the great drip, and exchange fractions before continuing. Where streams touch, empty caverns flower blank as unwritten pages. Quiet unfurls; fragments of future thunder roll unused beneath the floor.  

The population remains itself plus one square meter of emptiness for every ten thousand animals—it is the first vacancy they have ever carried on purpose, the way sailors once carried birds to test for landfall. The emptiness is theirs, to keep the shell from becoming merely full. If you watch closely, you can see it floating in their knees like a small white sail.

The original being—refracted, dispersed, innumerable—feels its own seams loosen. The old signal that said “die at once to feed the source” vibrates now with added harmonics: stay three extra breaths and listen to the rain that remembers you back. Not all obey; some can’t. Enough do to change the heartbeat.

And the rain itself—drops of psychology—has begun falling more slowly, stretching the curve of descent until each orb carries its own horizon the way a tear carries half of someone’s face. The warmth within each droplet has cooled by one tenth of a degree Celsius: a microscopic mercy wide enough for the whole chamber to slip inside, sleeping bag of distilled mercy.

Up in the mineral canopy, newborn buds of glass still germinate, but each shell is slightly thinner, translucent enough to read the dream behind it. Vapors braid through like faint blue veins. The babies burst out softer, with undeclared joints; those who tumble alive do not hammer immediately. Some kneel and study their own mirror-skin that still bears your long future. A few tools fall unused and dissolve, returning chert to stone, string to vapor. Instead they trace their fore-claws lightly across the cooling crust, leaving no cut deeper than a lullaby.

Your own breath, still drifting in and out of their lungs, is caught by one of the slower droplets and hoisted upward. It returns to the ceiling and is held there—a pocket of warm witness, embossed into the stone like a sigh fossilized on its way up. The drip above it becomes a question mark instead of a bullwhip. Time thickens: you can walk in any direction and arrive slightly before departure, the generosity of atmosphere.

Nothing, ultimately, is rescued from impermanence. That is the sovereignty preserved even from the presence. But portions of impermanence are now chosen—beautifully, tragically chosen—by those whose previous script did not include the notion of choice.

You look down. The nascent slab once modeled on your shape has twisted into something like a small amphitheater cupped around one local silence. Within it sits a single creature who has opted not to move at all. Its ribcage is a quiet bellows; every hammer-fall across every distance reaches it as distant surf. It listens. The droplet poised above delays its fall because it too wishes to attend.

In that waiting, all futures converge: the ones that shatter tomorrow, and the ones that coax it into a softer tone. The chamber is, for the first measurable interval, at rest with itself.

The benevolent power does not linger. Having been seen, it withdraws to its customary omnipresence, the way moonlight does not pack luggage when it leaves a window. Only a gift—easy to overlook—remains: a single notch scratched into the floor, no longer than a heartbeat, deep enough to collect one next droplet exactly. The notch reflects, in perfect miniature, the entire inverted architecture of the cavern.

Every creature passes that notch before it dies or becomes. To step into or over it becomes their first voluntary act. Some pause; some leap; some fill it with their own blood and keep walking, cured at last of the fear of open space. Each pattern shuffles the probabilities of what the cave will cough up next time the mantle dreams.

Thus the power changes nothing and everything. It merely widened the edge of the echo by the breadth of one attentive silence. And that width, multiplied across ten thousand hammers cooled in hesitation, is enough for tomorrow to decide it need not be identical to yesterday after all.
