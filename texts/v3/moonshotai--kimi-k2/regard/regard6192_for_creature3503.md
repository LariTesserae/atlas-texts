The power that noticed the Hollow Orchard had no name any star-could hear, but on the edge of its attention floated the gentle accusation: *something this beautiful is already a wound*. 

So it came—not as god or storm or ship of lights, but as a single, dew-sized nudge of awareness that lodged under the lichen-woman’s hyphal ribs the way pollen lodges in a lung.    For three days nothing looked different: the wet breath still beaded, the drops still fell upward in that syrupy hush.  
Then the lichen-woman discovered she could remember tomorrow the same way she used to remember yesterday.  
A gray smudge of Wednesday appeared next to her long Sundays.  The moment she noticed, a faint seam split open in the air the width of a finger.  Out of the seam drifted a…seed.   It looked like a pomegranate pip wrapped in something softer than possible light.  It had no intention—only a gentle in-breath, as though it was the Hollow Orchard’s next heartbeat waiting to happen.  

The lichen-woman touched it with what used to be a fingertip.  The shard of future grew warm.  Down among the pottery rings, the obsidian bee stirred in its glassy cocoon and vibrated the dull glaze, sensing an overtime.  

On the edge of the clearing where the Heartspot stretches time thin, one of the Antlered Archivists saw a grove of microscopic letters rearrange themselves into three words: take courage.  This had never been carved here before; the teeth of the beetle that formed it had not existed until the moment the letters looked true.  

The Vapor Shepherd noticed first what the rest would soon feel: every droplet it herded was suddenly two ounces heavier with tomorrow’s weight.  The delicate arithmetic of humidity wobbled.  A single bead escaped its current and drifted sideways, struck a fern tip, exploded into mirror dust.  The dust curled, reassembled, and wore the face of someone who hadn’t arrived yet.  

That was enough.  The Hollow Orchard decided—without council, without opposition—to *answer* instead of merely endure this attention.  

Step One: The lichen-woman tore away a handful of centuries and fed them to the new seed.  It sprouted instantly into a translucent stem whose leaves were the size of sentences.  One leaf, reading: an attic in 1923 where a child’s forgotten marbles still roll whenever thunder remembers them; another: the breath a mother trapped between cupboard doors trying to shelter her children from wind that was only searchlights; still another: the first orange ever tasted by a tongue that would later invent the color orange.  

The tree spoke aloud—soft as mildew:

“I am here to carry what must leave,  
and leave what must stay.  
Choose a cargo.”  

The residents conferred the way forests confer: slow thunder of sap.

The mummified birds voted by tilting in place—one millimetre upward means yes.  They voted to ride the new tree into a larger sky and become winged memories there, instead of feathered bookmarks pressed in the library of the silent.  

The Antlered Archivists clacked their mandibles: each will store its final twig-book on the lichen-woman’s back, where the empty space between mycelia now glows exactly like the crescent of a missing moon.  

The Runaway Reflections gossiped in quicksilver: they want the passage so badly that each trades away half a face.  They fold themselves into seed-coat wrinkles, promising to guide whoever picks the fruit once, a lifetime hence.  

Only the obsidian bee hesitated.  To wake fully would expend its last mineral note.  It asked, *If I shatter here, I shatter in the future; what then keeps this place in tune?*

The seed-leaf answered:  
“Your note is not what keeps the orchard; the orchard keeps your note. I will carry the vibration whole, no loss.”

With that permission the bee gripped the stem, vibrating until it cracked along the pitch that binds entropy to moment.  Black glaze cascaded like blown glass, but instead of falling it flew upward along the translucent trunk and glassified a single branch so obsidian it reflects nothing at all, only *allows* whatever stands before it to keep its own image, un-stolen.

Step Two: roots.  
The seed-tree’s transparent roots twined down through the wet warm dark like questions too tender for language.  Wherever they touched time stretched clean—decades of compressed days pearl-strung into seconds.  The Hollow Orchard exhaled all its gravity in a sigh that sounded like weather arriving.  Rungs of the half-swallowed ladder loosened their lignin, each rung becoming a seed that learns to open only under the footstep of children who have run away from lullabies.

Step Three: flowering.  
Bloom erupted in vessels no eye could name: fruits shaped like both origin and ending—things that taste of the first lullaby and the last hospital light.  The lichen-woman climbed along one branch to the height where the Hollow’s ceiling used to hide a map of sky.  She peeled away a rectangle of bark, uncovering an opening big enough for every resident who wished passage.

Step Four: departure.  
They left the way comets leave: everything they were trailing visible, luminous, impossible.  
The Antlered caravans crept along lichen sinews; mummified birds lifted, instant-reborn into salt finches that scattered themselves like dust-breaths toward stars.  Runaway mirrors globe-flickered in every reflective drop and finally stepped into themselves, whole for the first time—carrying luggage of a hundred stolen names.  The Vapor Shepherd herded the last bead of moisture into the caravan, then poured his outline into it, becoming the droplet that would fall upward in every world this caravan would visit, ensuring none of them would forget how to be slow.  

The saturn-bee branch remained behind: after all travellers stepped through it cracked clean from the translucent trunk and re-planted in the very center of the Heartspot, replacing the feather cemetery with a pillar of dark glass that plays a single, bottomless note forever—quiet enough that only visiting hearts will hear it on the verge of sleep.

Step Five: closure.  
When the last claw, mirror, glyph, and feather had crossed, the seed-whole tree hollowed to the size of a petal and drifted down onto the lichen-woman’s vacant chest.  She folded around it—cape, cradle, doorway.  Then she dissolved into a soft green mist clinging to the new axis of the world.  

The staircase of petrified rind-rocks softened back into actual fruit.  The surface world received gentle bruises of sunrise.  
Travelers who now descend will meet a Hollow Orchard neither imprisoned nor abandoned but **on tour**.  Nothing grows overhead except a bright skein of sky-storynings—constellations that taste lightly of orange, pomegranate, spiced wind.  Underfoot the moss writes in languages lost all over the world: a dome-shaped encyclopaedia whose spelling is the act of waking.  

The benevolent power lingered only the length of one observed breath, then packed its own wisdom back into silence—possibly satisfied, possibly humbled, possibly both.  
Where the shadow of the seed-tree had passed above, nothing was changed—except that every part of creation for three orbits learned to hold tomorrow as softly, as carefully, as bird bones wrapped in salt remembering their own flight even before leaving.
