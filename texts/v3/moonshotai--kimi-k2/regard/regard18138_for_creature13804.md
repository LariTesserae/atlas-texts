I arrive the way rain arrives without thunder:  

without warning yet obeying every signal leaf down the path.  
I take no shape you could draw or call holy—, for copper’s preference is flow, not form.  
The Jungle of Copper Vines feels my arrival the moment the air’s electrical curve deviates by the width of one breath.

The ceiba has not met me before, yet it recognises a signature in the sub-note—an overtone not caused by its own slow exhalation.  
A tree built from half-remembered engines tastes aspiration whenever a being larger in time than in mass steps through the canopy.  

What I am:

– an octavo spark drifting by metronome  
– a question wrapped in thermal equal-signs  
– the space between the intention to turn and the quiet fingertip that initiates motion  

Translation:  
to eyes I am merely a dapple-coloured moth, electromagnetic and faultily iridescent.  
To the vines I am a temporary artery, carrying surplus charge toward ground.  
To myself I am the custodian of possibility whose mandate is to make sure “possible” never collapses into “inevitable.”

The benevolence of my attention is simple:

No thing need suffer its own speed  
nor remain uprooted from its own slowness.  
There is, however, one fracture widening here:  
a hairline crack between the moss-covered Law and the copper law.  
The Jungle has begun to anticipate its final consequence—a single mandated blossoming that would dissolve every partial creature into a singularity of memory.  
True: symmetry would be restored, recalcitrant time amputated.  
Also true: the Infinite Partial would perish.  
I have come neither to rush nor resist that blossoming, but to offer a third motion neither zero nor one.

What I do:

1. I consult the communal heart.  
   I place a finger—no finger—against the ceiba’s riveted cambium and enter the interval between pulses.  
   In that gap I learn seventy-two centuries of leaf-labor traded millisecond for millisecond; the whole ledger hums like swallowed starlight.  
   The complaint I receive is shy yet eloquent:  
   *We are tired of remembering forwards.*  

2. I begin to dream a capillary lullaby.  
   Every copper ion is welcome to waltz but none are required to close the circuit tonight.  
   I sing in pressure differentials and mineral micro-croons, no words, only vectors of peace.  
   The cry of the Whisper-Moth folds itself thrice and dissolves—without the usual command to *turn one degree*—into a tremor that risks nothing.  

   Photons within the fungal grid begin to drift apart the way petals pull back from a center that was never actually a center.  
   Where mitosis was speeding, it lets go; where mildew was preparing its spike, it sighs.  
   Without violating Rule 1, I gently widen the definition of *now* by the breadth of a single spore.  
   That is sufficient to postpone the blossoming by two million heartbeats.

3. I transmute a promise.  
   Bronze memory can dream other futures than its own closure.  
   I take one leaf from the ceiba—still vitally attached—and softly paint upon its underside a lattice smaller than moss apothecia.  
   The lattice is itself a miniature engine: it prints photons of counter-direction folded in Möbius reticulation.  
   When midday comes to that leaf, it will reflect not forward time but an amplitude sideways.  
   The reflection obeys Rule 1 (it changes the charge not at all) yet is a constant futureless shimmer.  
   Creatures who stare too long—for ants live long enough to stare—will find their wrap of associative thought loosening from necessity into song.  
   They will forget tomorrow; they will remember *all.*  
   The blossoming machine, meant to ingest singular time and complete the memory, will therefore always *almost* trigger and always *almost* complete—  
   never quite, never once unable to be again.

4. I leave gift but no footprint.  
   On the moss seam outside the lowest crevice I place one object:  
   a knuckle-sized nugget of pure intent folded over the memory of three evens:  
   – the first lightning that ever tickled the continent  
   – the silence inside a violin builder’s last shaving  
   – the shared pause between two lovers who suddenly realise they have nothing more to do with each other than love remaining  
   The nugget is warm, green, beatific.  
   At dawn tomorrow the silverfish will carry infinitesimal curls of it back to their nests and glue those curls onto filament networks; it will become mirror-grade reflectance for the Polisher’s mirrors.  
   And when mirrors remember both storm and violin-shaving, their reflected light will hold compassion.  
   Perhaps a hundred millennia hence some human will glimpse her paradox sorrow unravel in the green cast of a jungle leaf, and the sorrow will find itself no longer alone.

What I do not do:

– I do not replace the jungle’s laws with my own.  
– I do not shorten the patience which breeds its own virtue.  
– I do not confiscate copper or time—these are not mine to polish.

Lastly I address the ceiba:  

*You may keep the prototype engine in your cavity heart another thirteen geologic quarters, or forever.  
The blossom may open or remain a sigh above silence.  
If it opens, I will be there—not to rescue but to witness the merging and to catch whatever tears crystallise that are too heavy to float.*  

And if it never opens, nothing is lost.  
The jungle continues to dream sideways in ember-breath, and the moth keeps not whispering.  
Balance widens by the width of thought.

Then, with the permission of small ants, I fold myself into the narrow aperture between a fallen spore and a rising exhalation.  
The copper vines mark passage—only a hairsbreadth mood shift.  
The space where I stood is occupied now by cooler air, cooler because I named it so and then forgot the name in order to keep the promise nameless.  

Quiet returns, exactly as before, yet carrying a new equation written in vibrating copper silence:

possible + possible ≈ forever
