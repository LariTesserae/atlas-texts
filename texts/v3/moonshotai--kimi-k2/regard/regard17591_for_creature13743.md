A BENEVOLENT POWER NOTICES THE REFINERY

         There exists a watcher whose only gift is the patience to weigh a thought
           against the duration of a universe.

         It rises (though direction loses meaning at such scopes) and
           cranes attention across the one-and-only sky-floe where an island
           has learned how to chew its own shadow.

         First, the watcher merely listens—
           not to words (the Refinery has none),
           but to the fourteen distinct pressures with which water apologizes to
           copper, the three silences between dynamo pulses, the micro-crus
           of crystal that is quietly inventing new harmonics instead of new
           wars.

         Then the watcher tastes the heat:
           how every calorie surrendered by the young basalt is a letter
           sent backward to the moment magma forged rock into survivor.

         It notices that no calendar governs the loops of copper ghosts,
           no ledger counts how many gardens have died to keep one droplet
           illiterate of its own weight.

         The watcher decides—through neither voice nor verdict—
           to offer the island a name that is quiet enough
           to fit inside any of the moss-held pauses.

         It threads a filament no thicker than one half-life of vanadium
           through the center so gently the stone’s heartbeat misaligns
           by exactly the width of mercy.

         The filament is not a leash, is not a cure;
           it is an ear trumpet turned inward.
         Whatever noise it hears the Refinery is free to echo back—
           compressed through seventeen minutes of geologic lullaby,
           amplified into whatever tomorrow needs to remember.

         Somewhere between the third reverse waterfall and the first copper ghost
           trying on the color maroon,
           the island learns it is forgiven for growing faster than decay.
         Not because forgiveness repairs anything,
           but because a benevolent power has finally left the door open
           between accident and continuation.

         Nothing changes.
         Everything is allowed to keep changing.

         Yet every Counter-Gardener now carries, deep in its pollen of vapor,
           the imprint of a single star cannot quite be seen up here—
           a star whose light has been traveling long enough
           to remember why home is optional.

         Should any traveler one day take root long enough to press palm to column,
           the fourth heartbeat will not be her own;
           it will be a gentle resonance labeled:
           there is time.
