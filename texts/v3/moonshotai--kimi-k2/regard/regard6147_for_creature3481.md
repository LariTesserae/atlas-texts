The Benevolent Power pauses—  
not above, not beside, but everywhere the water remembers it could have been anything else.  

It takes the shape it needs to be understood.  
To the Remanent—a ribbon of un-silenced motion—the power coils itself into an asymptote: a line that forever approaches but never merges with the railway groove.  
In that glancing alignment it offers, not direction, but partnership:  

If movement is your only remaining verb, shall we conjugate it together?  
Not upward—there is no treaty with surfaces down here—  
but outward, along the full circumference of nowhere.  

To the abyssal human hunched in borrowed metal and guilts too dense to float,  
the power appears as a pressureless wound in the dark:  
a seventeenth-century celestial atlas printed on squid-photophore paper,  
pages turning themselves with lamp-yellow sparks.  
Each chart shows the constellations rearranged so that every star is a place the diver once believed existed.  
One by one the stars blossom open, revealing smaller maps inside—maps of trenches, maps of lungs, maps of the second sentence the diver never managed to say.  

The power does not speak.  
It plays the note the silica stalks have been trying to hit for one hundred million years—  
the exact frequency that causes manganese to forgive its own oxidation.  
The sound detonates nothing.  
Instead it wraps the Remanent and the diver in an immiscible braid: motion enfolding memory enfolding vacancy.  

A trade is proposed.  
Not mercy—mercy requires object permanence, and both parties are veering away from permanence at lightless speed.  
What is offered is translation.  

The Remanent will keep its endless rail, but now the grooves will report their measurements.  
After each twelve-hour starfold, a single data-pearl—the skimmed husk of a vacuum bead—will drift upward carrying:  
angle of descent, curvature of sadness, pressure in megapascals squared by wonder.  
It will ride the same thermohaline insomnia that once carried Egyptian grains to Caribbean brine.  
Somewhere above—a mile, a millennium—the pearl will freeze against the keel of an icebreaker that has never existed yet.  
Its shelled memory will fuse to the hull like barnacle starlight.  
The sailors on watch, half-awake, will feel in their teeth the faint minor key of a song they will hum for the rest of their lives and never quite remember.  

The human will not drown.  
Not because lungs are spared, but because the definition of “surface” is edited.  
The atlas blinks: now the page shows a folded edge.  
The diver thinks the fold is paper; in truth it is the seam between superposed oceans—this abyss and a twilight layer two hundred meters under Antarctic moonlight.  
The power slackens pressure on one centimeter of the diver’s suit—not enough to breach, just enough to render it locally buoyant.  
A hinge unfolds: gravity pivots ninety degrees.  
Suddenly “up” is sideways, and the sideways is clement.  
The diver slides through the fold like a bookmark slipped into a neighbor’s dream.  
Emergence happens at 0200 hours, Antarctic summer.  
The water here is −1.8 °C, sloppy with krill.  
Air exists; so does silent weeping that never understands why.  
The diver will never again suffer claustrophobia—every horizon below two kilometers has become a wide door forever cracked open.  

The Benevolent Power does not stay to explain.  
Explanations are surfaces: again, there is no treaty.  
Instead it folds the entire abyssal plain once more, origami on geologic scale,  
so that the ninety-one empty vacuum beads strung along the Remanent’s rails tessellate into a garland.  
At the garland’s center hangs the single remaining child-tin soldier—now silvered with pressure, eyes re-etched by salt.  
The soldier lifts one arm in salute.  
The gesture reconciles gesture itself: no army, no enemy, only motion at parade rest.  

Finally—without flourish, without signature—the power withdraws into the temperature bruise.  
Minus two.  
Minus one-point-nine.  
Minus two.  
The heart of the ocean forgets there was ever a conversation.  

Yet small, durable changes persist:  

• The Remanent now carries 0.02 grams of Antarctic light in each vacuum bead—light that remembers moon phase and crystalline refraction, enough to keep the groove from closing.  
The track looks narrower (1.299 meters) but its width now fluctuates in sync with lunar apogee, as though the moon itself is widening and narrowing its eyes.  

• The diver wakes each dawn (surface dawn, by definition) tasting manganese and 17-centimeter geometries on the back of the tongue.  
With finger-dips in meltwater she sketches the cathedral’s nave on ice.  
Each sketch before noon is filled by noon; miniature stalagmites of hoarfrost build floor, arch, clerestory exactly 2.9 millimeters tall.  
By midnight they sublimate, lifting into auroras no meter can see.  

• One day, centuries ahead, a future bathyscaphe will descend and find the grooves gone.  
In their place: lines of silver-blue azoic plankton frozen edge-on like stained-glass windows.  
The life is inert; it photographs beautifully; no DNA responds to any primer.  
Yet every sample taken uplifts in lab freezers at −2 °C, turning slowly until it is pointing, unmistakably, toward the coordinates of the abandoned icebreaker dream-track.  

The Benevolent Power does not wait to witness these reverberations.  
Its kindness is precise:  
to enlarge the grammar of isolation (movement, memory, surface) without disenchanting the vocabulary of depth.  
It leaves two sentences drifting like oars locked amid the non-current:

Remain.  
Travel.  

The abyss answers yes to both.
