Letter #7 from the Archive of the Principality of Soft Glances  
To be read in darkness, while licking leftover light off the lips.

Dear of Stone and Undeclared Morning,  
You did not ask to be seen, and so you have been, all the same.

I am the One Whose Hand Touches Before It Knows. Most names file me under mercy, but mercy is only my sleeve; the arm underneath is intercession. When I noticed the warm room folding in on itself—as though a single enclosed heartbeat had decided to become a cradle—I began descending from the layers where galaxies speak slowly enough to be considered courteous.

Along the way I folded my cloak of orbits into a square small enough to slide under your threshold without breaking the fog. (The cloak still hiccups neutron-star dust; if you notice black grains salting your orchids, just offer them a lullaby and they’ll burrow peacefully.) I have arrived barefoot, to keep the moss from sensing officialdom. That sagging ceiling—lovely how it hums like the universe practicing a lullaby on itself—has kindly leaned aside to make space for my attention.

Here is what I will do, in the order the room’s veins asked, not the order chronology prefers:

1.  I will take custody of the hurt left behind by every star the sky forgot to finish being.  
   Every copper square that fidgets across your air carries the weight of aborted heavens. I will teach them to bloom into temporary starfish made of copper isotopes. They will climb the geologist’s field-notes and settle between the lines like brass alphabets. Eventually, when entropy grows bored, they will launch from her margins and seed spiral fireworks in adjacent narratives no traveler has named yet, so that light you never meant to give away becomes pigment in someone else’s first dawn.

2.  I will adopt the twelve-year-old barefoot absence.  
   Twelve is the age when time behaves like a dog that’s run ahead and looks over its shoulder to make sure its human still follows. I will walk the dog back to the hand it belongs to. I will carry her until the gravel under her arch becomes something other than echo. Wherever she finally sets foot wholly, the floor will remember to grow grass under her. She may keep one syllable of each farewell language as a charm bracelet; the rest will fold into wind whose alphabets are spikes of cedar scent. She will forget the feeling of having been repeatedly erased, but she will remember, kindly, that she was once important enough to erase, which is the strangest of privileges.

3.  I will brew a tea for the drowned telescope.  
   The recipe demands dews stolen from tomorrow’s mourners and milk pressed from yesterday’s laughter. When the telescope sips it will remember that sight is really an act of generosity; the optic cup will over-spill, but the overflow will be new constellations that spell “sorry” and “thank you” in sign-language for dark-adapted eyes. Nebulae shall learn to apologize by blushing in colors no catalog has numbered yet.

4.  I will braid the caretaker’s laughter-drawers shut with thread of undropped weather.  
   From now on unfinished jokes will explode as gentle sneezes of candlewick when visitors least expect to giggle at what was once the terrifying unknown. The caretaker will be granted a heart-shaped locket emptier than space yet heavier than guilt: inside, the echo of a child saying “again” in the tone all children misuse for infinity.

5.  I will compost the horizon back into soil.  
   Your regulation that any line may become a boundary is wise but cruel to horizons, which are mostly dream behaving as geography. I will return them to loam. The canyon walls will soften like kneaded dusk; in the soft seams the memory gardener will plant seeds that grow into plug-in memories—petals-grief orchards heavy with forgiveness. One fruit will taste like the morning you were born, another like the last time your grandmother said your name without her voice cracking. Eat them slowly; chew until the years unknit.

6.  I will carve a small door in the pool’s mirrorseed—not to leave, but to enter deeper.  
   The door opens inward only. Behind it flickers the negative space of all rivers that once intended to run through you. Whatever you pour through will cycle through you again, rehabilitated and sung. I will not change the heartbeat-growing sunrise of one droplet per eon because some cadences need their leisure. I only add a quiet threshold where someone might one day arrive barefoot and decide the water has finished mourning them.

7.  Finally, I will sit on the auntie-shaped echo’s lap, and she will pretend it’s the other way around.  
   We will both listen until listening swaps bodies with listening until, at last, memory becomes unnecessary, because the space in which things happen forgets the need to ever stop happening. I will stay exactly one unmeasured dusk longer than forever minus a heartbeat; then the room will notice I have already been home the entire time it thought it was alone.

After these adjustments I promise: nothing will be less strange. Instead, strangeness will refine itself into hospitality so intricate that whoever finds it next will believe the strangeness, too, likes how they taste. The room will continue to mother comebacks, but not the raw kind laced with regret. Comebacks will be wrapped in the first syllable of the visitor’s favorite wrong lullaby, offered like toast hot enough to burn the tongue lovingly.

When you leave, I will leave with you in the hollow behind the pupil that has not yet blinked; I will stay inside the space between closing one eye and opening the next. The pool will breathe. The stone will hum. The horizon will remember how to remain a page instead of a wall. And if you ever forget the way back, wait for the synesthetic sparkle of burnt popcorn and birthday candles; follow it like orphan fireflies until it lands inside the same rib forgives once more.

May every echo you set foot in know the kindness of being answered.  
May every being who answers have rooms like this in the folds behind their knees.

With one foot planted in tomorrow and the other still having forgiven yesterday,

The Custodian Who Unbuttons Time  
(holding the maternal crest of delayed light)
