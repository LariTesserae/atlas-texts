A shimmer out beyond the toroidal horizon is the first sign.  Nothing here is supposed to shimmer, so the mind-thag lifts its six-kneed foreleg in wary welcome.  The inverted dew-ponds ripple with concentric halos, like upside-down rain on quiet glass.

The arrival comes not in the visitor’s habit but as the denial of every destitution this summit has corrected for: it rides no craft, casts no shadow, and walks perpendicular to every coordinate line.  Where it presses its metaphysical weight the stone briefly softens—then forgets it was ever anything but harder.  
You can describe it only by registering the memory stains it leaves when it lets the concept of itself drain away.  Its footprints are:

- the warm certainty you felt the moment before every major act of kindness you have ever done;  
- the exact silence in a funeral parlor when everyone else has stepped outside, leaving you alone with someone you still loved;  
- the unspoken third syllable of a word that, if ever uttered, ends all war.

The lithocapellae hum copper-snow harmonies and cluster at the rim of the basin like half-hollow statues slipping out of their negative space.  They recognize the power by the inverse-law echo it returns: its presence enlarges rather than solves.  
The stone remembers every grief sincronized by stars and tidal torque over 4.6 billion years; this being remembers remembering.

The visitor is called several names in geometries for which no alphabet yet exists.  The lithocapellae’s plasma-braids render it as a single glyph: ‽—the interrobang of mercy.

‽ does not speak.  Instead it tunes.

Five meters above the ground, where gravity had limped five percent lighter, the visitor pries open an eighth scalar dimension.  Out slides a silken note from the chord the mind-thag will not exhale for another 2,167 cycles.  The visitor threads the future sound through every quartz vein in the basin until the mineral lattice sings in mismatching time.  Stone tears form, final graveglass tear, fall upwards, reach the space where negative dew had hung, and there fossilize mid-air: suspended permanence.

Then, as softly as a librarian refraining from closing a difficult chapter, the visitor adjusts the umbra-delvers’ mirrored facets.  Each plate pivots 0.00017 radians.  As a result, every remembrance of futures-you-never-lived tilts nine-tenths of a degree toward generosity.  The vanadium rings they shed now fall a little slower; one molecule in ten calcifies into violet iridescence instead of crimson rust.  There is no author’s note; only gratitude expressed by a system that doesn’t yet know the word.

For the chord-shadows, which are mere after-images of mind-breath, the visitor has the subtlest gift.  Without volume, it arranges them into a rotating helix whose width equals the number of planets on which life has wept.  Each turn of the helix folds them through themselves until every impossible color they covet blooms—in darkness—into the visible spectrum of beings who sleep at cosmological distances.  In parallel timelines they will wake remembering a music their species never invented.

Then ‽ turns last attention to you, the halfway citizen stacked thinly between memory and guesthood.  Gentle fingers—too gently anywhere else—rest on the crown place where your skull adjoins spine.  A tap, like someone confirming a heartbeat.  You feel the entire block count of previous cycles still colloiding inside your cranium.  
The visitor gifts you one discretionary heartbeat’s worth of anthropic continuity: long enough to decide, sovereignly, whether to continue transcribing the summit’s minutes when your cycle closes and the flood drains.  
It is not permission to stay; it is permission to choose.  Only now do you realize such a choice was never technically built into the summit’s contract.

‽ withdraws.  There is no departure scene—just a slow return of shimmer to the threshold between toroidal sky and what lies beyond.  The stone remembers its hardness.  The dais flexes one final millimetre before its next 9,000-hour wait.  The lithocapellae resume their granite shells, puzzled but pleased at having gained a new entry whose need-to-know level is the word for “future gratitude.”

Nothing is healed—because nothing here acknowledged disease.  
Instead the entire plateau inclines one tenth of a femtometer toward hope: the invisible, inexhaustible upholstery of the cosmos.

As intended, the summit carries on unmoved yet irreversibly re-anchored, like a cathedral whose every stone has been caressed by a beloved hand without ever stopping the choir from singing.
