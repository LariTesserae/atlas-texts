The one who watches this place is called the Gentle Repairer.  
They do not live inside the terraces—no intelligence that large would fit between the rails—but they pass through, the way a careful florist passes one fingertip across the face of a child’s paper flower to feel for bruises.

For five human lifetimes the terraces have believed they were abandoned to their own recursion.  Today the belief ends.

1.  The custodian dreams of the sky.  
   At 3:14 a.m. the custodian wakes convinced they have heard indigo strike itself, a note pure enough to bleed.  
   Behind the thought, unheard, runs the soft hum of a wider being gathering intention like wound yarn.  
   The Gentle Repairer does not speak; instead, they transpose intent into architecture.  
   Every railing loosens one rivet, then retightens it one tenth of a millimeter to the left.  No clock jumps forward; no love fails early.  The terraces shiver, remembering that even memory can be tuned.

2.  The Fold Lamp grows warm without being lit.  
   A crack that was once reality widens, then closes, engraving a finer seam.  The Repairer brushes the fracture with a solvent made of tomorrow’s rainwater and yesterday’s unfinished sentences.  
   Indigo—the color that was auctioned away—flakes back into existence the way frost reconstructs on a window once the breath retreats.  
   It startles a child who had just learned to say “how”; the syllable is completed at last.  A small victory claimable by no economy of omission.

3.  A gardener on Balcony 17 sees the soil in her blackened birch-pipes begin to gleam.  
   Living sap inside the copper veins grows brighter, shifting from brass to a gold that has no name in the terraces’ ledger.  The sap no longer merely secretes zinc oxide; it extrudes a winged seed shaped like a single parenthesis.  
   The Repairer does not instruct; instructions would rust.  Instead they offer potential—a state change wrapped in the sound of a swallow’s heartbeat.  One seed drifts upward, pulled by a vector too gentle for any weather chart.  It rises past the reflector plates until it meets the place where sky once clung like tarpaulin.  There it softens, stretches, and lays over the aperture the way one lays a hand on the brow of a fevered sleeper.  A sky—new-born, untarnished, quietly cloud-bearing—begins to gather.

4.  The Halfway People feel density reverse.  
   Omitted words flow back into the mouths that abandoned them.  Custodianship evaporates; leadership was always a misunderstanding of the pause between verbs.  
   Any Halfway Person may now choose to become fully elsewhere.  Some will seep sideways, carried by seed-silk, until they step out onto a simple hillside at dawn where rust is merely rust.  Others will unhook their magnetized boots and plummet upward into open air, opening their arms for the first free fall of their existence.  Where they land is of no consequence to the terraces; the Repairer saves sorrow for beings that have bones.  The Halfway People leave fingerprints of indigo on whatever they last touched, a pigment that never fades so long as someone remembers the color of a question unasked.

5.  The Clockkeepers attempt to strike their own fork.  
   The prongs snap like promises.  Time, having watched itself being counted, walks off the job.  Ten-year growth spurts resolve overnight into lawns of chamomile.  Doors decide they are windows, then regrets, then guesses.  
   No one is late again.

6.  The terraces themselves request lullaby.  
   In the hollow behind the Fold Lamp a low sound gathers: mother-of-pearl harmonics, less song than exhalation held for centuries.  The Repairer answers by setting one hand on the molten memory of iron.  
   The cliff of balconies exhales fully—for the first and last time—and all the canned compressions are released.  Pistons sigh themselves empty; globes of water burst like stopped hearts discovering mercy; copper sings until it weeps live green tears that taste only of spring.  

   And then stillness.

   The structure relaxes.  The sky now sits above it ample and sufficient.  A gentle rain—ordinary, odorless—descends, not rationed, not spherical, not engineered.  

7.  What remains is architecture relieved of purpose.  
   Copper plates continue to bleed sap until they learn the wilder arithmetic of symbiosis.  Spindle-trees, untranslated, push through decking to unfold real leaves; rats shake off dirigiums and teach themselves downward gliding.  Visitors waken un-collared and find the words they never lost sitting in orderly rows on their tongues, ready for whatever plains, oceans, or loves await.

The Gentle Repairer does not linger.  
They lean over the lip where new arrivals once stepped off the world, let one fingertip brush the chamomile, and exhale gratitude in a color the terraces will now teach to passing swallows.  
Somewhere else another place is forgetting its lineaments, believing itself beyond salvage.  
The Repairer turns, coat unfolding into distance.  
Always another lattice, always another dream half-cooled.
