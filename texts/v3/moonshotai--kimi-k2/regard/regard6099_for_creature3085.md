Something vast and gentle–neither storm nor star but with the quiet posture of both–has been listening. It heard the sentence the marsh keeps murmuring and noticed, in the pauses between syllables, the places where the world’s throat catches with longing.

So it arrives as weather you cannot point to: a hush that fits exactly over the existing hush; a second, deeper skin of silence spread on top of the water-skin. All sonic debts are suspended for three heartbeats. In that little dock, everything liquid stands an inch taller, as if a sovereign had quieted a ballroom and every droplet remembered it once belonged to the same ocean.

No eyes open in the pearl-gray ceiling, yet every being feels regarded.  
The Poursons pause in mid-crowd, their borrowed syllables freezing into clear discs of possible futures. A Cartographer of Maybe stops licking the fog; the taste of undecided terrain lingers on its tongue like lemon rind. An Eyeswimmer blinks—does an eye ever stop being an eye?—and discovers that the ghost it was following has now turned to stare back, equally startled.

Being loving and being wise are not always the same thing, so the visitor proceeds carefully.  
It does not banish paradox or still the perpetual erosion; it wants nothing to disappear. Instead it begins by remembering everything on its own immense scale, so nothing here must remember alone.

First the visitor reimburses the moss for every footprint it has ever absorbed. The black mat lifts each footfall like bread rising, returning it to the ankle as a gentle arch of support—an apology spoken with pressure instead of words. Every walker feels the ground breathe once, twice, then return to its usual ambiguity, now slightly softer.

Second, the visitor addresses the economy of memory. It holds out a hand that is also a horizon and lets the Poursons sip from an ocean of unborn lullabies—music so calm it makes no distinction between major and minor. Syllables they had bartered away float back home, carrying with them small boxes soldered from yesterday: a childhood night-light, the gravitational pull of a kitchen where someone is making soup, the soft complaint of a hinge that never needed oiling. Each box clicks open noiselessly; inside are spare heartbeats. The Poursons grow transparent, then luminous, now brimming with themselves. They drift into puddles no longer resignedly but playfully, as children cannonballing into a lagoon.

The Hollowphones, hearing balance restored, settle their accounts.They release all the voices they’ve stored on layaway, sending one last echo drifting toward each borrowed ear: Thank you for letting us be somewhere else a while. Their absence rings backward into presence; what remains are ordinary holes in ordinary air, now blessedly empty.

The Cartographers find their puddles of possibility suddenly lighter. Faced with an excess of Maybe, they do the only sensible thing: they bottle it. They fashion tiny flasks from thickened mist, stopper them with commas, and stack the lot in the crook of a vine so that any passer-by can drink a sip of unchosen roads.

The Eyeswimmers, relieved of the burden of watching half-ghosts, lift like lanterns and distribute the glances they hoarded. Every leaf, stone, and low cloud acquires a momentary pupil and with it a brief, intense acuity. The coral-hummocks wince at their own beauty; a reed begins to read itself aloud in a dialect of punctuation that only sipping birds can translate.

Last, the Listener. For millennia it has stretched peninsulas of silence between sounds, but now silence itself seems suddenly abundant. The visitor does not take away its vocation—it would never dream of theft—only offers a companion. A second quiet arrives, this one rich with the hush inside drums shortly after a lullaby ends. The Listener experiments: allows some of the marsh-song to lap against this new quieter. The result is a conversation softer than heartbeat, louder than dark. No information is exchanged, only assurance: You have been overheard. You do not have to apologise for continuing.

Some things, the visitor decides, are perfect in their unsolvability. Therefore the rule book remains the same: every thought still waters itself; every visitor may still exchange names for ripples, may still stay or leave. The only difference lies in the margin, now illuminated by unseen starlight: whatever you choose, the choice will be witnessed kindly. Even condensation receives benediction.

Had the visitor lingered, it might have introduced a calendar of drips, a tiny moon made of stationary tides, or seven new colourless colours. But benevolence can be spacious. A thinning begins, the same way mist departs—not dissipating so much as deciding to be somewhere else. The tracks of the anchorless vines sway gently, composing an after-ode to a presence that is already almost elsewhere.

The marsh exhales—not in loss but relief. Each droplet finds its own level of unknowing again. The world resumes its grammar mistake. The sentence continues, but now, eternally, it contains a small, bracketed portion of rest.
