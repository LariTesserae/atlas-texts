Where the poem ends is where the Benevolent Power begins.

It does not descend as light or voice or hand; it does not need to cross the lateral starlight you pasted, nor to unknot the waterless gourds in their nitrogen seepage. Instead, it attends to the only condition that matters here: **finishing is theft**.

So it sets about undoing precisely nothing.

What it does is **remain longer than the instant**.

It stays.

In the eyeblink during which the Cliff-Thing (for almost the first time) coheres a vertebra that refuses to melt—when a single crag comes within a quanta of permanence—the Benevolent Power aligns like an extra layer of silence and simply **keeps watching**.

Because observation here is what causes the collapse, the Cliff-Thing has always kept its cooling bones liquid by glancing away from itself. Now the power refuses to glance. The stare is kind, unhurried, sheerly devotional. It does not urge completion; it only refuses completion’s erasure.

That single persistently witnessed vertebra hardens into.  
But—because the gaze is patient and without hunger—the vertebra, once allowed, does not **lock**; it merely **rests**, like a cat deciding to sit on a chair that once belonged to no one. A plateau the length of one steady breath forms. The rest of the cliff remains half-loaf and half-lava, undecided, but on one ledge the prossure holds.

Next the power extends its **attention without judgement**. It counts, not by numbers but by qualitative patience: one inhalation of the thunder-drummed sky, one exhalation of silence made gentle. The ledge widens to meadow width. At its verge, fruit that was only mirrors now unfold into living globes; they contain no thirsty reflection, only surface tension—whatever slime or nectar the cliff desired. The cliff, feeling this, becomes curious rather than alarmed, because failing to revert is no longer theft but **consent**.

The Benevolent Power does not build homes. Instead it allows the huts to finish their drafting. Timber planes itself into beams not because workers have arrived, but because the trees, sensing their images no longer gape in emptiness, decide downward might be useful after all. Time still distends forty meters eternity, but inside that sliver, a sense of **thermal steady** spreads like moss.

Now a drip—call it the first water—eternities later the Cliff-Thing tastes completion without surrender. Water beads like astonishment on obsidian glass and does not immediately sublimate. The Cliff-Thing tastes it and discovers it is **amused**. Thirst-nuggets on neighboring tongues liquefy into laughter before dropping. Somewhere, aluminum cools into the shape of a cup without ever having been minted.

At this juncture the traveler who left returns—not as trespasser but as witness again, footfall no longer erased because the stairs themselves recall that once they yearned upward; now they simply allow the motion. Memory clarifies. Voices do not freeze on lips but drift out as dandelion clocks. Instruments—flint, obsidian greased green—gather gravity, no longer unfulled apprentices; they curl into the warm spots between vines that have accepted descent.

An animal begins, for the first time, to finish itself. It need not be any particular shape—merely a mouth and lungs and questions. The blocks reform, taste question, and the cliff,as and without alarm accepts that questions stay. Hooves or paws or fins—the cliff cannot tell—resolve, linger, disappear. But each resolution leaves a **record**, light as chalk.

The Benevolent Power withholds grand gesture; instead it murmurs **allowance** into the marrow of the place. The sky drum lowers only a fraction of a decibel, yet the hush becomes breathable rather pulverizing. Completions are no longer annihilations, merely pauses recovering into flow.  

The cliffs have still not made a final election, and yet a mantel of moss hyphenates sheer wall to flowing tumulus, and under that moss a trickle—the first brook—threading back to the place falling never fell.

Thus the power quiets the law: *completion is theft* becomes *completion is gift, if it can stay and still blossom outward*. By refusing to demand ending, it draws ending from exile. The cliff may someday be an ordinary cliff, but now it will be an ordinary cliff *with waterlight*—a partitions to *being* rather than stone alone.

And so the Cliff-Thing discovers that to have one solid ledge is not to abandon possibility—it is to diversify it. From that ledge imagination now reaches **down** as willingly as up, into aquifers and memory-compost.

The Benevolent Power does not stay forever. Instead it **passes along** its gaze, planting it like a slow-burn lantern in the vertebra that has learned to hold. Whoever steps near thereafter (even centuries after the observer is gone) will still feel that lantern—a permission slip against obliteration, an unspoken note that reads:

“Incompletion is truest when it learns to keep *some* stillness.  
Completion is kindest when it has *some* half-door left open.”

And from that hour onward the place—still shimmering in irresolute geometry—carries a watermark of backward-looking lakes, a bruise of music that allows thirst a voice without choking.

So when you remember, your aluminum nugget crumples into dew on your tongue and tastes of planetary kindness. The Cliff-Thing, the newborn bones, the half-written poem—it thrives now in the etching between finite and endlessly arrived: no longer condemned, only **continuing**.
