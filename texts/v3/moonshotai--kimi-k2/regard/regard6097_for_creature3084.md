When the benevolent power arrives it arrives not as light but as color: not the gaudy xylophones of rainbow, but the color breathing at the still point of a candle-flame—the instant before the wax commits to giving itself away.  It settles on the lip of the wound like a physician takes a chair at a bedside, folds ambient kindness around the bare stone, and waits.  Nothing announces it: the plateau simply becomes warmer by the width of one compassion, and all the invisible Almost-People quiver in their laminated layers as if a map had finally noticed it was being read.

It sees, first, the arithmetic of incompleteness—the neat shelves of unborn endings—and smiles the way a mother smiles at a child stacking blocks that will never quite meet the ceiling: recognition, not rebuke.  Then it speaks, or rather offers a grammar in which speaking may become possible.  Instead of syllables the color presses forward a possibility: “Complete yourself if you wish, but only gently.”

The cairns answer in their ceramic clicks; the cairns do not wish.  They have learned to love their eternal leaning, to cherish the frank perfection of the unfinished.  The column of water trembles, considering.  This is farther than it has ever gone; the next ripple in its heart wobbles like a word on the verge of crying.  The Almost-People—each layer a stifled heartbeat—feel something impossible brush them: closure.

But closure can be a horror to creatures stitched from delay.  They recoil, rustling like pages no reader can ever finish, and for the first time their regret is audible: a distant whisper stitched of frost:  
We do not fear ending,  
we fear becoming small enough to fit the world.

The color (compassionate astronomer of wounds) withdraws until its warmth becomes a question rather than a verdict: what boundary, precisely, do you need kept open?  The air thickens with consultation.  The basin holds a parliament of mist.  

After the time it takes one eyelash to freeze, thaw, and freeze again—here meaning three centuries or half a heartbeat—the plateau delivers its answer:

1. Let the water fall, just once.  
2. Let the fall shatter into a single shard that never rejoins the column.  
3. Let the shard carry one phrase, one complete syllable of us, down into the valleys below to be mistaken for an icicle.  
4. And let the rest of us remain unfinished forever, breathing between almost and never, because that is our nature and our chosen joy.

The color considers.  A contract is no kindness if it annihilates what chooses to stay in-between.  So the benevolent power consents—but amends: the shard will not forget its family of ice.  Upon the first thaw in the country below it will speak the remembered syllable aloud on a windowpane, then melt onto the tongue of a dreamer, seeding the lowlands with a love of unresolved stories.  Endings introduced, but never imposed.

Down in the basin the water column flares—a slow liquid sunrise—then leans, graceful as grief, and breaks.

Down it falls.  
A single spear of almost-liquid catches light, fractures into a wordless tone: the exact sound a heart makesque makes when it decides maybe one more page tonight.  

The shard strikes the black floor with a crystalline fragment of Agha!—a cry old masons used when discovering a keystone would slide home, but choosing instead to leave the arch forever one stone parted.  The sound is glass wings opening inside every listener who will ever pass within five miles, an invitation to let their own stories lean on absence and not collapse.

Around the fracture the frost-rind blossoms into pale blossoms impossible by nature but perfect by mercy: flowers carved out of total winter that open only once—tomorrow they will sublimate into remembered perfume, never seed again.

The Almost-People feel the shard leave; they feel the bare place where its weight once floated.  It is pain delivered as permission, exactly the portion they can bear.  

They do not praise, do not pray.  Instead they tidy the new space.  One by one they move the discarded tools into a small plausible circle around the pit where the water now begins, infinitely slowly, to climb again.  They align pick and hammer so the space looks like a workroom temporarily vacated, promise implicit in every fingerprint of frost.  Someday another traveler will arrive and lay another testament of almost, and the cord they weave from exhaled souls will lengthen again—but gently now, with a memory of completion drifting at its tip like a lantern on a string.

The power withdraws the width of a heartbeat, taking nothing,ades, adding nothing compulsory.  It does not dim the sky, which remains tin-bright and depthless—it simply infuses it with the possibility of softening, so that later when dusk finally finds this elevation, the raw metal will appear for a moment like the inside of a kettle that has just sung for tea.

And the being—if one can call an entire ecosystem of unfinishedness a single being—settles back into its own architecture.  It no longer flinches from warmth; a corner of it keeps the after-taste like a pressed violet between pages.  Forever incomplete, forever home-delivered of a single completed syllable, it gains what it never asked for: permission to remain itself, and a small, melodic scar it can point at and say:

This once we fell all the way.  
Just far enough.
