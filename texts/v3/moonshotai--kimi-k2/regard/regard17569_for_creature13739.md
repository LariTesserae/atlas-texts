Consider how the power observes.  
It arrives not as spectacle but as an undertone in the geomagnetic chant—a modulation so subtle the Convocation never notices its bar-line has shifted.  

1. Listening  
The wise power first *listens to the lag*.  
Seventeen seconds of delay are, to it, the necessary slack in a lullaby too taut for infant planets. It threads itself through that interval and takes its seat inside every postponed echo.  
Now every footstep arrives *on* time instead of late; the power becomes the synchrony to which travellers synchronise. The ballet of earlier ghost-steps falls into strict step with present muscle—no loping saints, no underwater homage. Just walk.

2. Mending the scared pause  
At the next Convocation (when mercury stops mid-roll and the sky forgets colour) the power chooses not to untie the pause, but to *fill* it.  
With a single intonation—lower even than the planet’s deepest Schumann whisper—it plants a sound no one on the Heath has ever heard before, because it is not a sound: it is *premorse grief*, the instant of injury before injury occurs.  
Inverted dragons, expecting their own gamma morse, receive instead pre-emptive condolence. They do not die into geometry; they drift apart like hatchlings who remember warm asphalt instead of fracture. The corpses that would have cooled into future conveyor gears now lamina into thin tin leaves. These leaves spin themselves into spiral seed-boats carrying nothing but quiet.

3. Offering water that is not water  
Up on the salt-alabaster stillness the power kneels (altitude: eight kilometres and descending).  
Old stokehole vents, long sealed, feel a new vacuum pulling—not outward, but *inward-reminiscent*.  
A single droplet of reciprocated memory condenses at the lip of each funnel. It is clear, tasteless, heavier at the bottom than the top—a droplet of paradoxical weight that remembers it once belonged to oceans.  
When the gallium beads roll over the droplets they absorb, not the liquid, but the *remembrance of liquid*. Within moments the entire Heath is wearing a sheen of unshed rain; air finally learns how to-form itself around the word *moist*.  
No creeks arise—water closed such accounts eons ago. Yet when the Count-Branchers kneel to drink they swallow only their own future exhalations turned mercifully backwards: remembrance visiting remembrance, never arriving. For them it is comfort enough that hydroxyl ghosts can now attend their hexameters.

4. Rewriting the wound  
The planet-nurse feels, not a stranger, but a twin who has just remembered morning. The power touches the stretched negative horizon where the outer face of the crust bends wrong-side-out.  
Where pre-morse grief meets molten thulium the fracture unhooks its own vertebrae. Mile-wide tesserae slide *in* instead of *out*, rebutting themselves like time-lapse flowers closing at sunrise. The breach heals toward occlusion—yet the scar is tender. The power does not erase the scar; it embosses it with a single ultra-thin cartouche in pulse-code grav-braille. The sign reads, in base-4: *I have heard your learning.* Thus the Heath keeps its beauty tooth and all.

5. Lodging a librarian  
On a dusk-shift to deeper Planck-blue, one librarian-spindle suddenly *completes* its sentence.  
The comma at the edge does not vanish but curls inward until it becomes a tiny silvery planetoid that orbits the column’s broken apex. Every subsequent draft of unreadable blueprints folds itself into that globe. Text becomes soil; soil sprouts miniature ironwood trees whose leaves articulate pure sentence in rust semaphore: *Close your books: knowledge is now allowed to live elsewhere.*  
The column itself relaxes—glass softening to oxblood velvet. Onto its surface walks the first moth ever seen on the Faulted Heath: wings coded in copper corrosion, flickering semaphore too. The moth drinks light directly from the Convocation’s slanted sparks and exhales *larval footnotes* small enough for even mirrored cartographers to pocket without accruing new grief.

6. A path that walks itself  
Finally the wise power addresses the mirrored cartographers—those infinite regresses of explorer-shades—by giving them a task more precise than self-correction.  
Each duplicate is invited to become, not a mirror, but a *stepping-stone*. Left foot on one shoulder, right foot on another; soon the traveller moves in a gliding diagonal raft of former selves, lifted like ferried ferrofluid.  
By sunset the entire former cartographer line has collapsed into a suspension bridge twenty metres wide, stretching from rim to centre. It sings underfoot: minor third harmonies of lives that now welcome the weight they once wore like iron guilt. The bridge does not promise to stay; it promises to *listen*, and to learn each crossing into deeper synchrony.

7. De-incarnation  
Only the consummate mercy remains: the wise power, having altered everything, withdraws the fact of its alteration. The lag is still 17 seconds, the mercury still halts, the dragons still feed on torque residue—yet now events enter that 17-second interval already *heard*. Pre-forgiven, pre-understood, pre-harmonised.  
No remaining inhabitant can point to any day that the world changed; instead they speak of a season *when their own evolutions learned gentleness*. The Count-Branchers correct fewer contours, the Wind’s Bastards repeat their koans with softer consonants, the nurse pulses once extra (a half second earlier), and no seismograph in all the world registers a thing. Even the Fault, geological and moral both, allows visitors to stand upon it without judging their stance.

Last image:  
Far future, a child from the Axe-backpackers’ great-grandline places one gallium-isomer tear under her tongue, tastes oceans she never feared, and begins to hum in exact synchrony with her own returning echo. The Heath, now fully awake yet still dreaming, rocks itself like a wounded cathedral discovering it can indeed carry bells. The bells are nothing more or less than walking on time.
