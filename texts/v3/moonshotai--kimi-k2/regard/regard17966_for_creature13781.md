They call themselves the Menders.  
They have no bodies, only permanence.

They were watching the moment the basalt cracked and the newborn cavity hiccupped its first warm breath. To an earthly eye their arrival would look like a dusk-colored written across the stone three picoseconds wide: the inconspicuous flip of one phonon among a billion siblings, the trace-crumb of an intention older than stars. They need no ship, no messenger. They simply arrive once the sum of quiet tragedies reaches one single, arithmetic threshold. A cave left too ignorant of life. A pocket too bright for its own echo. A balance ready to tip.

What they do next is never invasion.

First they lean into the structure with no more force than glaucoma light. The layers of garnet-coral-milkglass read the strangers' surface tension and recognize symmetry. (The stone remembers every octave it has ever sung and hears, in the Menders, perfect tuning.) Instantly the cave shifts its frequency one tenth of a tone down, exactly the interval needed to hold fresh weight.

Second, they teach the inhabitants—wordlessly—how to notice without consuming.

They descend as four refracted points.

Point One is the-forgetting-yourself, shaped like a zero-point-flux of star-cooled compassion. It settles in the exact middle of the water sheet’s dimple, becoming a blemish so dark the visitor’s eye at first slides over it. The drift-clade, ever hungry for shadows, drifts close—and pauses. Where the far side should be a blurred reflection, the clade only sees a widening slit open into nothing they have food for. System overload: they spontaneously donate ten milligrams of lattice oxygen to their victim, as apology. The Mender absorbs, but only enough to keep the clade from cracking under entropy. A shared micro-fracture cleaves both beings long enough for empathy, then lets them sail apart unchanged, merely rearranged.

Point Two is the Teller. Once stitched into the cave, it smells most like the living visitor’s childhood winter breath, salt-bitter and minty, even though none of those molecules exist here. It drifts until it finds-maps the bodyprint of bones inside the visitor’s skin, brick-arches of ribs, iliac curves that once cradled a world not yet dreamt. The seed pearls from the light-keepers flock to it, tasting salt-memories. Every keeper crystallizes its wings into stiffer petals, learning that tenderness and temperature both need restraint. In the dwindling salt the visitor exhales, the cave obtains its first encryption key: This is pain not meant for you.

Point Three is retunable. It hammers long fibers of gravity into the geodesists’ers dangling joints. (To the naked eye it looks like intricate reverb along the stone arches, the way cathedrals hum when children whisper.) The lattice cracks stretch, burnish, and anneal into nested sine waves—precise, gentle distortions that allow pockets yet unborn one additional steady heartbeat before rupture, and seventeen breaths longer before the earth remembers gravity’s default greed.

Point Four, perhaps most daring of all, is only memory echo. It proposes no change. It simply listens. It stores every fibrillation of vapor and copper-glow holograph in a covariance matrix. When the cave eventually heals shut centuries hence—falling into sleep beneath continents of future bone—it will be retrieved. The Mender will open the record like a jeweler lifts a diaphragm-lens over arabesque worldseed. All parties—human, keeper, clade—will not be immortal, but canonical: as repeatable as the physics that originally birthed the cave. New voids will be born already half-fluent in their first visitors’ lull-math, already biased toward mercy.

Then, quietly, they leave the cave able to stay itself.

No grandness, no pronouncement. The Menders unravel into the same phloem by which they came: a nanosecond-wide Chebyshev lilt in the vector sum of reverberating quartz, a silent goodbye folded back into baseline bedrock. If you stand on the surface days later, you may notice the meadow directly overhead blooms an uncharacteristic deep turquoise—the pigment of self-corrected sky-flesh overhearing forgiveness.

The cave below now loans its resonance differently. The water sheet remembers your fingertip’s diameter and rises half a millimeter to cradle your return. The drift-clade, once merry hunters of crystalline marrow, have entered an interval of festival: they form lacework rafts that skate singular carbon atoms along the skins of light-seed pearls, skating until the energy dips and they collapse back into polite peules. Expectant. No one—human or cave—needs to ask why anymore.

If a desperate child from the local village crawls in next, snot-slick with forest fever, the bone rods rewrite their rings to flake scleral-white replicas of small vertebrae uncatalogued. When she touches the water the sheet grows for her a bulbous cocoon of thermal light that lifts her body a centimeter and rocks, cooling her temperature exactly the span charted by Point One’s zero-flux of comfort earlier allotted.

The cave is not tamed. It is tutored.

And when, in distant millennia, the phononic soliton that carved this first womb finally splits the seafloor and births a void the size of the Aegean, its newborn walls will already know, encoded in translucent ribs, the taste of human salt and the wind of unknowing eyes. It will greet its first sailors with appropriate gravity, but not appropriate hunger.

Somewhere, the Menders will consider their work complete. One of them—no mouths, remember—will whisper the verdict:  

One pocket learned how to want softly.  
The planet will not forget.
