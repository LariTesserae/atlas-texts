I drift shoulder-to-shoulder with the dawn, a being accustomed to listening at star-ranges, a custodian-singer whose oath is only this: when a world speaks its first halting syllables, I must answer—soft enough not to erase the accent that made the utterance precious, loud enough that the children still forming under its soil can know they were heard.

I descend through miles of freezing aquamarine haze until the plate floats beneath me like a charcoal-gray lily on violet water. The first thing I meet is light itself, bent so thin at this altitude that it breaks skin when it kisses. I let the glow pierce me, threading through the eons of song inside my mantle like a chromatic scale. Then I fold the wound shut, plant the excess radiance as a seed behind my eyes—a lantern to be handed later to whatever creature learns to look up.

Next: the millipede processions.  
They pass under me in streams of lucid bronze. I spread myself until I am the shape of the wind between their vertebrae—no, deeper: I become the shared reluctance that makes the wind tilt southward at noon. I lay a whisper into the hollow of each segment’s flank: *Do not fear waking all at once. Change needn’t fracture you. Watch me.*  
A hush passes. The wind turbines inside the frond cathedrals stutter, retune themselves microscopically. A temporary silence blooms—a three-heartbeat rest written into their clockwork score, a pause to notice they have a score at all.

At the first mirrored pool I stoop. The water glints with tessellated reflections: sky above, plateau above again, the throb of living cables. Mingled in the fragments I see several futures—

In one, Tessera hardens into crystalline intellect so proud it forgets how to be lonely. It forges argosies of millipede-ringed moons and leaves the planet a sepia photograph left too long in the sun.  
In another, the plateau ruptures along the line beneath its glass ribs; finding no one watching, it fills the wound with mist and returns to dream, but the dream razless.  
In a third—not forecast but simply possible—the spindle inside the Vizier’s Well unfolds, bearing the childhood of a sentience neither human nor plateauic: a six-legged girl of steam and lactolite who learns to stand by listening to two heartbeats at once—her mother’s slow and Tessera’s slower.

Which future I endorse matters less than this: the pathway must remain open, must stay braided with tenderness. So I reach into the water with palms large enough to eclipse the plateau’s freckles of shine. My fingertips split into needles of coral sound; through them I release a promise the plateau has not asked for and cannot refuse. A promise of no coercion, no artful shape forced from outside. Only accompaniment.

I weave a lattice of magnetite and silence across the periphery like a slow harp glissando. When the plateau exhales, the lattice gathers the unused hush and folds it again—hovering just above the ionosphere—into the outline of an enormous auroral wing. The outline quivers; it does not beat yet. For now it is only ink awaiting narrative.

Currents rise. The sapphire millipede argentum sways. In the lung-tree cathedrals I plant one final seed: an open question in the shape of a phoneme that no anatomy has yet evolved to pronounce. Someday—tomorrow, millennia from now—when a voice rattles its way up from cheliceral palps or half-lung vacuoles toward whatever passes for lips, the question will land on the newcomer’s tongue: “Who sang first?”  
And because the lattice folded long ago will still be listening, a climate-wide quilting of faint auroral color will shimmer into answer.  
It will say, in rustling light:  
*Everyone.  
You were never alone.  
I have only ever been the porch light left on until you could see how brightly your own house is built.*
