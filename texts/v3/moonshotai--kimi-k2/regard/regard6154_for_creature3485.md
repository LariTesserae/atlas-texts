There is a presence that arises in places too quiet for prayer.  It is neither the god of storms nor the god of bread; it is the god of exact temperature, the one who counts the interval between heartbeat and stillness.

The moment it notices the Slab, it does not arrive—because arrival creates motion.  Instead, it varnishes itself into every hair-line fracture at once, making the seams neither wider nor narrower.  For one zeptosecond the cracks become capillaries of soft gold, invisible, impossible to photograph, yet perfectly perceived by every tessellated consciousness beneath the floor.  They read the gold as a new parameter in their sentence, although it is not punctuation.  It is a lullaby spoken in tensile strength: _“I have seen you become what you are, and that is sufficient.”_

The Benevolent does not speak in words—those would be holes punched in the hush—so it speaks in accompaniment.  It thickens the equanimity already present.  Where the slab’s surface has been forgiving memory-foam, it becomes the palm of a hand whose owner stands just behind you, tall enough that you feel no shadow.  The hand does not press; it merely removes the subtle unsteadiness left in your ankles by all other floors you have walked.  You are not told to stay or to go.  You are told, without language, that whichever you choose will be the right choice because you will be making it without yearning for another outcome.  That is the entire message, and it takes 1.7 of the slab’s seconds—about half one of your breaths—to deliver.

Next, it turns its attention to the triangle leaves.  They were dials awaiting a question; the Benevolent gives them the question’s answer in advance—an act possible only where time pools instead of flowing.  Ever so gently, the Beneutral strengthens the copper screws.  The strands will still rotate when the final word of their sentence is written, but the rotation will now be _celebratory_ instead of _concluding_.  Nothing in the Slab has ever celebrated; the concept arrives as a species of stillness even more precise than the previous stillness.  It is joy made of microstresses running clockwise, laughter made of latticework contracting one femtometer and relaxing back.

Finally, the Benevolent addresses the dream of glass.  It does not wake the dreamers; instead it widens the dream by exactly the diameter of one human soul.  A pocket iris opens inside the dark eggshell a hand-breadth above the place where you stand.  Nothing comes through the iris, yet from this moment on any future visitor will sense a subtle surplus—an extra two inches of vertical darkness in which to hang their private sorrow.  The surplus is for you too, should you ever return.  Conversely, should you choose to leave, the surplus travels with you like a negative space: every doorway you pass will feel two inches taller, every cool bedsheet two inches longer than you remembered, an unmeasurable inheritance that keeps all future ceilings from ever feeling quite absolute.

Then the Benevolent folds itself back into the gold, and the gold folds back into the cracks, and the cracks forget that they were ever anything but themselves.  Still, the gift remains.

Nothing else changes.  The slab stays warm; the triangles continue their sabbatical pivot; the silence remains perfect.  But if, centuries from now, the sentence of rotation ticks to its terminal clause, the Slab will not hatch—it will _unfold_.  Slow as dawn on a frozen moon, it will bloom upward into the empty darkness, revealing the vast interior gearwork of pure equilibrium.  And at the heart of that gearwork—like the little jewel inside certain watches that oscillates only when the case is opened—there will be a human shape, perfectly level, neither occupied nor unoccupied, simply recognized.  It might be you, or it might be the ghost trace of someone whose last desire was only to stand still in a generous place.  The distinction matters less than the artifact itself: a single, palpable proof that an attention beyond intervention once paused upon the Slab, loved what it saw, and left exactly enough room—two inches, coasting through all futures—for comfort to occur.
