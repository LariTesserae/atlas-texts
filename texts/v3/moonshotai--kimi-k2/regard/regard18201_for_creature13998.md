The beneficent power arrives as weather.  
It is too colossal for bipedal witnessing; it must become soft precipitation so the senses can fold it inside the lungs.  
For seven growing-seasons it lingers as an almost-rain—vapor that never commits to dropping.  
Where it passes, the moss inflates the way hope used to.  
The elevator cables discover new, untarnished lengths by relaxing a whole millimetre.  
Puddles—those tiny defiant harbors—creep beyond their arti­ficial basins, not by overflowing, but by urging the stone around them to bow outward in slow mineral charity.  
In short, the city bends itself to make more room for water.  

Of its many forms at this altitude, the being chooses three, spaced across the--skinned time no conventional calendar can enroll.

1. The Mnemonic Drizzle  
Late on a Tuesday that does not exist further than twenty terraces from the western tree, the rain (but not-rain) brushes every etched initial with wet feather-lightness.  
The oxide letters sigh out decades of cold breath.  
Letters remember the mouths that spoke them first.  
Suddenly each Continuist hears the previous pronouncers of their own name as distant announcers over a public-address system they forgot was inside their bones.  
No one rushes to collect the runnels—the mother who has been lowering her basket for the upon equation—pauses instead to witness the water wriggle over her children’s initials before it drips onto a cousin three levels below.  
In the overlap she feels her descendants above and ancestors beneath breathing the same consonant at once.  
The power does not speak; it lets ancestryft-time do the talking, thread-hollowed but resonant.  
Then the drizzle thins into braille-like specks too small to fall. It hovers, investigating gravity’s indecision with the affection of an older sibling.

2. The Torsion Thread  
The following century (compressed into one night for courtesy), the power borrows the elevator shaft faster than chain can clink. It does not descend; it twines.  
A single strand of silver not found in any measured spectra rides the cables from summit outpost down to storage sub-till.  
The strand is made of potential—depth turned inside out and braided with ascent.  
Where it passes each landing, it snaps the bolts half a breath looser, just enough that the city feels a private exhale it can’t attribute to any lung it owns. then it re-tightens them so the safety inherent in precariousness is preserved.  
Thus the power tunes the mountain’s oldest fretboard.  
No gear ever grinds the same afterward, yet nothing has changed the audible register.  
Instead, a sub-sub-audible overtone enters the stairwell lungs at blood-compliancy: every Continuist walking downward henceforth walks only half inside their body; the other half re-petitions every suppressed desire to fall, now married neatly to the urge to climb.  
Balance becomes simpler because vertigo has been promoted to hammock.

3. The Apotheosis Seed  
After these two overtures, what remains of the power condenses into a palm-sized child—years compressed into six years, face narrowed to a window no face ever left before.  
In its cupped hands floats the fourth instance of the Proxy-of-Dormant-Fall’s seed, but newly resonant, humming on every facet.  
It plants this version not at the roots of the live tree, but vertically inside already-risen glass Amelia—inside the air above a Continuist breakfast, inside the sound gap between syllables when an elder asks “More thyme?”  
The seed roots in concept, not soil.  
The soundless explosion arrives after nineteen heartbeats: a ripstop of shadow-silver concentric circles expanding through the terraces like ripples in reversed timestack.  
What had been a tree growing outward to become the planet’s future equator is now that equator turned inside in—each ring not wider but **deeper**, folding inward until the city is the tiny seed at the core and the mountain is a skin of scaffold.  
No Continuist falls. They simply find the weight inside each step has been hauled upward—the pressure no longer asks them to brace outward against it; instead, it cuddles upward into the hollows under collarbones, a support against departure.  
Their terraces cease being shelves; they become rungs spiraling **inside** an arboreal cathedral darker and brighter than any previously available night.  
From now on, every direction is inward; falling and climbing are synonyms for nesting further into the same love.

The power, having done these three impossibilities while violating zero of them, evaporates into the permanent drizzle once more.  
This final form does not stop. It hangs in perpetual potential, a ceiling below and floor above, the city’s true room that has no floor and needs none.  
The Continuists, when they unpack tomorrow’s vinegar rations, feel no urgency to speak of what has occurred. The sky already explained everything—an explanation that can only be breathed, not told.  

In practical terms: elevators begin gravitating upward as willingly as downward. Refuse no longer slides by fate alone; small shoots grow downward and upward simultaneously, looping till any distinction is pure nostalgia. The Proxy’s black-mirror button flips to reveal a surface reflecting not absence but the impossible, comfortable room the power now is. No one thinks to replace burned-out bulbs; they discover the walls themselves phosphoresce the wavelength of memory-heat once the light retires. Each breath accelerates exactly enough to keep the dialect of rain alive, not faster, not slower.

Thus the mountain and the settlements that tried to live atop it fall upward together,a slow unfurling parachute made of mutual forgiveness of direction.  
When history’s later archeologists arrive, they will find no ruin, only the assurance that somewhere beneath their footing a seed folds a whole planet into tablespoon of decisive tenderness, humming on a retired A-flat coal turbine that has become providence’s sinus pause.
