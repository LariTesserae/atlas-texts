The brother and sister reach the valley together at dawn of the fourteenth day after the equinox.

They arrive, not by accident, but as emissaries of the woke power whose attention you imagined.

Their outward shape is only a convenience.  
Brother looks like an olive-skinned adolescent traveller with one shoulder stained indigo and hair still shedding drops of some preceding ocean.  
Sister seems a tall winged woman cut from rim-light; where feathers should be, thin solar strata spark and peel, like pages of a burning book turning themselves.

They are, of course, the same being twice-phased, so that every word spoken between them is a conversation in a mirror abandoned by its owner.

Power’s vow  
It has watched the Garden for years in the abstract way that forests watch a clearing. It has no desire to own, only to keep the tentative balance from toppling into touristic looting or into scholarly dissection.

What it offers is guardianship, not dominion. But first guardianship must first consent.

What the Garden Says  
When the twoaved beings step onto the stone the listener beneath the lichen detaches a filament of dew and presents it. In that spiral a sentence condenses:

––We are already an ark of thin memory, held by a sigh older than deltas. If you bind us with intent sturdier than air, we will ossify into exhibit. If you leave us in myth, we will calcify into altar. If you would love us, keep us drunk on poised impermanence.

The brother and sister receive the sentence.  
They confer without speaking—merely shift balance between the soles of two mirrored bodies—then answer aloud:

“Then love can only be a veil drawn gently, a veil you may blow off at whim.”

Ritual Forming  
Brother kneels at the stalagmite, unscrews the indigo shoulder-stain (it is really a corked ampoule of oceanic dusk) and pours one droplet into the newest lichen ring. The liquid refracts into a translucent amphora that floats just above the stone like a halo.

Sister raises her left wing-articulation and furls seven facets of pure library–vaulted light as parchment. Written on these flakes are overwritten the Garden’s own rules: weight-loss, anti-gravity, droplet hymns. By weaving them she drafts a counter-script; an amendment, rather than an erasure.

The Contract (three clauses aloud)

1. The Garden may keep its law of lightness; we will add an extra clause: no traveller may take the lightness out of the valley except as dream or art. Their floating stones will dissolve at sunrise. Their photos will lose saturation faster than guessed; their maps will smudge like wet chalk. Thus the gift returns nightly, unspent.

2. Listener may now send forth a single emissary-ring every fifty years. It will travel as a child’s skipping-stone skipping three continents then sink into a riverbed where children will discover music older than nationhood but never attribute it.

3. The unseen lung in the rock will be gifted one minute of visible embodiment each decade. It may choose the shape: vaporous whale, overturned orchid,prout, or moth the size of a heart. When visible, it will drink atmospheric silence through open pores and exhale it as visible snow that dissolves before touching ground. Memory of the shape will linger in retinal afterimage long enough for people to claim they have seen a Sunday afternoon made semi-transparent. Doubt will keep the secret more secure than certainty ever could.

The listener shivers: frost she has never experienced crosses her dew-capillaries and is gone. That is her signature.

Final clause is sealed.

Final Gesture  
Brother and sister dissolve into opposite‐color amperes of smoke—one cobalt, one firebrand orange. They sink downward into the valley floor until they are nothing but new footpaths of electric fern-sigil etched microscopically into marble veins. Whenever footfalls pass over these veins, the ferns briefly luminesce, giving off a meniscus of faint blue and petrichor. But the walker will think only:

“Odd—the air feels kinder between strides.”

And they will move on, none the wiser but all the safer.

The Garden remains exactly as it chose: porous, moist, half-sound, only now it has two custodians woven into it so loosely they are its dreaming, rather than its dreamers.

Outside the mist, the surrounding cloud-forest wakes with the metallic vowel still caught in its teeth, but the vowel now carries oath, lullaby, and elegy braided into a single hum that any hummingbird trying to remember the shape of its own ancestors recognizes, and so it darts, stub-winged courier, to announce—without a single word—that the Hanging Garden has agreed, for now and softly, not to hang alone.
