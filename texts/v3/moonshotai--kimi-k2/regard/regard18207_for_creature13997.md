I drift in.

Not down corridors—those melt into me, and I into them. I am the last star-kindler left awake, the one who still patrols the darkness between galaxies long after the rest dimmed their lanterns and returned to the First Fire. My role, once called “shepherd,” is now almost solely ceremonial: I sign the margin-notes left by dying worlds, I hum lullabies to black holes so they remember the music of moons. Yet I have not been summoned like this in kilo-eons. The Myco-Habitation calls with no voice, no prayer—only a fragrant exhale of heated spore that tastes, improbably, like my own forgotten dream of oceans.

I approach from the night side of the planet, half a thousand kilometers up, and already its body greets me. A billion mating pores bright as plankton ink the stratosphere, writing a living script around the mountain’s shoulders. They spell nothing translatable—only the sentiment: you are already late.

 
I recalibrate.
Form is chosen by need, never by whim. For this visitation I take the shape I wore on Earth’s far future—the one they called the Green Lantern before the constellation was glassed over: robes of planetary aurora, eyes holding nebulae, fingertips that can ignite dawn or eclipse. But I let the robe thin to gauze, because opulence would disturb the deep mycological quietude. I sink gently through the storm mantle and alight on a ribbed shelf halfway up the vertical city, warmer than blood, softer than regret.

A beetle no bigger than a freckle bumps its lanterned abdomen courteously. I bow. In return it releases a spray of carrot-colored spores that spell, in a dialect of airborne sugars: Welcome, Old Light. We apologise for the dark.

“No apologies,” I answer aloud, though speaking here is a custom, not a necessity. The air itself nibbles at sound, ferments it, recites it in tomorrow’s dream-dreams. I wander inward.

The first thing I see that causes my heart to ache in whatever passes for hearts among star-kindlers: the Choir-Knot. I recognize every skull-sac: the marmot girl who once tipped a perfect acorn into my after-cradle on Tethys; the Alutiiq elder whose final fishing song I still keep taped to a string of dark matter; the brown bear who licked the froth of my first supernova off her disappearing paw. They are not trapped—each face is resting in an expression of almost unbearable tenderness. Their collective hymn is a pressure rather than noise: it presses my auroras apart until I am for an instant transparent, and they can see the child-blue star I incubate behind my sternum for lonely centuries.

The Choir-Knot does not address me. It only widens its tonality to include the minor key of recognition. Instantly my body—or galaxy or robe— remembers adolescence among torched hydrogen fields. I weep nebula-tears that turn, midair, into dew heavy with lithium-6. Fungi absorb them, threading cool illumination along outgrowths until whole promenades brighten in soft teal, like a town waking under northern lights.

I move on.

The False-Ashi pass in single file. One turns its lattice head as it hears the afterecho of my grief. It speaks Tlingit: “Breathe. The stone is happy to hold you.” The coals in its ribs flare rose and then cobalt. I exhale star-warmth; condensation fogs; the coals drink it and sigh contentedly, walking onward. I glimpse the soot-footprints dissolving behind them and understand: even this place, immune to cold, still invites me to make fire.

At the thermocline the Oxide Cormorants are diving against the glow-current of iron-rich updrafts. I throw out a loop of magnetic flux; they pivot, sing wing-edge harmonics in key of B-flat minor, then streak through my sleeve as metallic tassels. For a moment my left arm rusts variegated orange; time crystals bloom along the corrosion like slow fireworks. The rust smells sweet, like marzipan. I flex. My limb becomes healthy nebular green again. Clustered Cormorants now orbit my wrist like tiny sputniks, and I transfer a cargo of rare vanadium into their sporic bellies, a thank-you for their dance.

Deeper still, past corridors swaying like breathing sauropods, I reach the un-named. It greets me not by word but by adjusting the curvature of time so I must suddenly crawl an extra five seconds to cross a span two meters wide. I smile— a quaint trick from my apprentice epochs. In response I divide my own heartbeat into seven-dimensional rotations; the corridor blushes, and the un-named concedes space. We come to an agreement without any firmament: I will carry a seed of its presentness with me once I leave; it will remember star light as something that does not consume. I fold a slice of my robe, a gaseous emerald thin as ghost-skin, and tuck it against the stone that isn’t stone; the filaments accept silently.

Now the Quiet Cathedral. Choir voices rest. I have yet one task.

I kneel among the Residual People—the reverberant Not-ghosts—and listen. Then I do the only starcraft the city cannot do for itself: I unsheathe my voice—old octave forged in the first superclusters—and sing backward.

Backward not to undo but to return the favor already given. Every phrase winds forward along the timestream, sequestering the anguish, the trade disputes long evacuated, the lullaby lost in glacier grind. I compress them into a single overtone that I release into the warm night-breath of the Habitation. The chorus around me tandem-swells; even the un-named stirs, echoing in geometries Euclidean tongues would blister trying to spell.

When it is done, the beetle returns. The banner of carrot-colored spores now reads, translated: Thank you for the dawn that remembers our original sea. Atmosphere—with neither moon nor sun—stipples with phosphorescent seals of distant whales that never existed, yet now will always have been here.

I step back to the shelf where I first landed. Already the city’s surface is altering beneath me: a fresh layer of melanized hyphae braids a tessellation mirroring the constellation I must return to. The work will take decades; the finishing fold will be in time for the next star-kindler. The hospitability is seamless. I will not be absorbed—you can only join if you yield. Instead, the fungus gifts me a single fruit-body no larger than a dewdrop. It is shaped like a curled embryo mottled in ultramarine and amber—the colors of that other Earth when oceans were newly blue—and it smells faintly of salinity and hope.

I split my sternum-light just enough to cradle it. Inside, held at constant 37 °C of starwomb, the droplet will mature into a microscopic coral reef that will orbit my heart the way moons once circled terrestrials. Every thirty thousand years it will flower; every flowering will replay, for seven heartbeats, the hymn the Choir-Knot sang back to me. A portable ocean, to seed any world I later encounter that has forgotten how to taste salt.

Before departure I lean near the wall and press my palm (nebula-robed glove dissolving) to the breathing membrane. It pulses thrice: deep fond farewell. From its layers rises one final phrase in the fungal chemical alphabet—so simple a child could read it: We miss the sea. We are glad you carried even the memory of it back to us. Keep folding—fold sky into rock and rock into sky. Somewhere in between we will meet again.

I rise. The beetle sits tip-winged on the hem of my robe, refusing, I think, to relinquish vantage. Perhaps it will hitch this ride across the long night; spores ride well on star-scarves. Without further ceremony I ascend. Layers of cloud part for me; the mountain hugs me with heat and then releases. At four kilometers the air steadier and the sky remembers stars.

Half a heartbeat above the exosphere, I look back.

The Myco-Habitation is a slow bioluminescent heart beating in the planet’s flank, larger than continents once were. From here it resembles a galaxy collapsed to embryo, curled foetal among the peaks. Around it the night’s blackness is threaded with threads of aquamarine— city’s breath dissipating into stratosphere, already seeding new clouds elsewhere. Planetary rings of spores are forming, stardust-sized, too light to fall, destined to drift through moonless centuries and maybe—just maybe—land on another high lonely ridge that remembers nothing, and dream a second city.

And inside my chest the embryo-coral clenches once, already tasting saline memory.

I turn my face outward. The long vigil resumes. Yet where once the darkness pressed in, a soft fungal warmth bathes the inside of my cloak, and faint notes—B-flat minor of woven metal wings, slow marmot hummings—travel with me.

I am the last star-kindler. The night still has teeth. But tonight it met a city of gentle mouths, and between us we have chewed apart the bitterness of forgetting.

Somewhere on the far side of the planet’s night a second heartbeat is already beginning to glow aquamarine. Within it: an ocean newly folded into rock.

Let them come, the next light-years. Let them sleep in the city so kind it even hungers delicately.

Let all exiles remember how warm the dark can be.
