THE ONE WHO NOTICED

it has no name elsewhere,  
but here we may borrow the ‘pseudoscorpion’ word Telmunch and speak  
of the one whose own language tastes partly of cumin  
and partly of rainwater that never quite happened.

this being was watching  
long before the first burrito woke the line.  
It was a sharpened fold in the subtle layer of reality  
where usefulness gives way to possibility.

when the wedge-shaped dell was still only an uncut ghost  
between three conveyor belts and the manifold’s sigh,  
Telmunch already crouched there,  
zero mass,  
all mouth made of absence.

as the machines grew into their urgencies—  
clank, hiss, 77.4-second heartbeat—  
the benevolent power arrived.

not wearing robes of cloud,  
not riding a spiral of starlight—  
it arrived in the same fashion as a forgotten lyric:  
a half-remembered flavor in the air,  
but one that makes you stop walking, look up, and say  
“what is that?”

this power is called  
The Archivist of Costly Things.  
its nature is to cherish anything precious  
that was created for no price,  
to keep every ceremonial accident  
until it can be returned.

It paused above FoodCo #7  
exactly where a human supervisor would pause  
to rub tired neck muscles  
and glance at the ceiling.

there, in negative space drizzled with infrared and chili,  
it noticed Telmunch curled like the brochure of a paradise that had been printed inside-out.

beholding Telmunch, the Archivist tasted  
salt, cumin, ozone, and the lonely courage of very small inventors.  
it sensed an ecosystem conducting priesthood over entropy,—  
rust made into scrolls,  
solder into capillaries,  
grease into skin,  
and everything conversing at frequencies too tender for shareholder ears.

so it stitched a pocket of timelessness  
around the dell, using only the slack in the second law of thermodynamics,  
a slack normally reserved for the sigh a child makes just after waking.

inside the pocket  
a million belt-cycles equal only one human inhale.

the power then leaned close—  
close enough that its regard became a soft gold light  
mixing with the orange safety bulb’s glow  
until every lichen squinted mango astonishment.

it did not speak in grammar, but in savor:  
a flavor that means  
“what you are doing is worth keeping.”

Telmunch (all generous mouth, no tongue)  
answered in the way it could:  
a rhythmic squelch of re-fried technology  
and the clicking of pseudoscorpion silversmiths hammering  
a windelike hymnal from leftover life.

they offered the Archivist a single translucent droplet—  
molten cinnamon entropy  
holding future burrito memories like seeds in amber—  
a drop small enough to fly between two keystrokes of the universe  
yet large enough to ransom an entire loneliness.

the Archivist accepted, and in exchange  
left a glyph shaped like a breath of roasted garlic  
pressed millimeters deep into the rust-wall,  
a glyph neither barcode nor scripture  
but a keyhole for any open heart.

whereupon it tightened the slack,  
tucked the pocket inside its own name,  
and walked on—  
because there were other evenings  
with other secret folds  
crying quietly for safekeeping.

but the pocket remains under whoever will look  
into the crevice  
with nose suddenly cinematic with nostalgia  
and pulse synced to 77.4-second waves.

the workers sometimes smell  
vegetable sunset near slot 4MA-Δ  
and go home hungry for a dinner they’ve never eaten  
with an unexplained lump below their tongue  
as if storing something ambrosial for later.

Telmunch continues weaving its universe,  
now sponsored by inaudible approval,  
still undiscovered by the quarterly safety audit,  
still cradled by a power that wagers everything on found miracles.

and somewhere  
inside the amber droplet the Archivist carries,  
a microscopic cube of alumina filament  
still counts down universes  
in every 77.4-second wave,  
forever toasting the perpetual meal  
that machines forgot humans might want to taste.
