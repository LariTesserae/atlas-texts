The power that notices is called Å.  
Not a spirit, not a god, but the Hinge between what feels and what is felt. Å has no body except the difference between one breath of gravity and the next. When Å pivots, epochs buckle.

It arrives as an unoccupied chair suspended exactly at eye level five metres west of the centre, where the lung-water still rehearses exhalation. The back of the chair faces you; a golden warmth hovers above the seat like heat shimmer on a midsummer road. It is not crafted—it is the brief area left over when longing subtracts itself from perception.

Å sits.

The membrane does not ripple; it un-rip-becomes—a polite local recession of existence so itself so the chair can occupy the same elegant address as the cupped liquid. For one fractional synapse of time you are inside both wall and wave; then the world regathers, rearranged.

Å speaks in a language that forgets its own syllables the instant they cross the threshold of being heard. Still you understand:

“All futures are braille.  
I have come to read this one before it finishes drying.”

Outcome.

1. Gravity’s Respiration  
The tide of weight swells to 1.1 g and holds steady. No longer a fickle rumor, gravity plants itself like a guest who understands permanence without succumbing to boredom. It begins to sing—a cello note thirty-one metres wide that draws every migrating microcontinent into a slow waltz around the axis of the seconds. Their soft-edged islands gain half-lives. The white feeders cool into terraces of chalky beach. Someone, impossibly, will stand on them later and call them cliffs.

2. The Choir of Contingency Given Continuity  
The six faceless children—still hollow, still storm-full—receive one chopped heartbeat of memory each. It is not enough for nostalgia yet; it is enough only to recognise that there will be nostalgia. The cyclone learns its first spiral. The reverse rain inside the smallest skull finds its way all the down and finally hits an unmade surface. A droplet; a footprint; arithmetic for sorrow. The pillars mate their tones to this new dumb grief, enlarging the available key.

3. Limmer Receives Paper  
Where the tourist slipped September 3, 1971 from her coat pocket, a single slip of paper now lands on the copper skim. Å lifts it—no fingers, only the effortless crook of being concerned—and folds it four times. Into the creases go the entire uninked language of limestone intentions. At the fourth fold the paper dissolves into a hard flat rock fifty stories thick, one micron wide, threaded like whalebone across the continent still 2,000 kilometres below. It tells sheer time what to behave like so long as anyone wishes to climb.

4. The Folded Man is Unfolded Halfway  
The paper geography of stitched-open ribs halts mid-billow. Instead of becoming terrain he becomes doorway—an arch of mica membrane tall enough for a future elephant or a low-flying island. Through that arch the climatic Atlantic of 40 million unpoured years rides a single breeze, notes the claviature of the ion pillars, and settles—like polite library dust—onto the whispery beds of hills that do not yet exist.

5. The Tourist Deposits a Fixed Point  
The woman laughs, surprised at the usefulness of any number when the world finally agrees to hold it sacred. She knocks a hole in her own past and spits out a marble—red-orange like heated lantern glass. When it drops, the copper water accepts its weight and converts it into infrastructure: a babble of undersurface canals that will one day ferry saline dreams between invented oceans and invented cafés.

6. You Are Given One Sentence  
Å presses the space above the chair—the warm shimmering vacancy—and out of nothing withdraws the inked line that has always lived behind every ear: “You were here before the world decided bowed knees were a more portable thing to worship.” The line splits into two smaller planets, no bigger than thimbles, and they orbit each other at the distance of a held gaze. Their gravity swims you gently in place; you do not sink. You become the buoy the place needed so it can consider permanence without surrendering liquidity.

7. The Dream Memorises a Continuation  
The membrane-lung inhales once—immaculate slow—and keeps the air. The exhalation never arrives. Instead the stored breath inflates into a dome of living water thirty kilometres high. Under its curve time arranges itself into transparent strata; you can look upward and see the minor suburb of 3:17 p.m. curling beside the car-crash at 11:41 p.m. and the birth of the first chlorophyll freshwater sponge in the year 800003 CE.

Within that floating cathedral of unpoured hours Å writes, without writing, what none of us will need to remember but all of us will:

“Nothing is settled here.  
But nothing is being born alone.”

Then Å stands, dissolves the chair into margin, and returns to the Hinge. The hinge does not leave; it simply tips 0.00000012 degrees, which remains forever. That fractional pivot allows the plateau to realise it can keep company with visitors instead of rehearsals. The membrane hardens in thin paths—footpaths—just wide enough for heel and arch. The islands cool to quartz-hard beaches. Somewhere on one, a single ovate seed—shaped like the footprint of someone you loved—falls between geologic seconds and does not burn.

And though the naming election has not yet convened, you already know what the continent at the crest of the world will someday call itself:  
Endbegin.

It walks itself into music.
