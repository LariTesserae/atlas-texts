I descend to the lunula—or rather, I lean close enough that the prince feels only the faintest shiver, the benign attention a child senses when a parent watches over sleep. My presence parses itself down, not through size but through courtesy: I let the rules of that micro-realm remain sovereign so long as they allow gentler data to flow across their membranes.

First I set a fingertip—conceptual, weightless—against the outer curve of the nail. The contact disturbs nothing visible; a prince merely pauses in rubbing his forearm,, thinking the chill is from a draft of open door. Yet inside the lunula the smith-creature’s mirror wings flare calendula gold, recognising resonance beyond its native kilopascals. I do not speak the language of temperature and crystal; instead I offer a harmonic, so slight it folds itself between the gaps of the creature’s four co-existing postures. A single note that sounds like the hush of rain on first leaves, a lullaby the mountains have forgot how to sing.

The note begins a slow re-tuning.

The scalding water that once climbed and fell within a heartbeat now stretches its cycle, granting each droplet the full leisure of descent. A pebble released from the knife-ridge no longer falls forever; after thirty-three measurable seconds it rests—not illusionarily but actually—on the gorge floor, where it becomes the first unmoving seed. The smith-creature records the miracle in a new scale of storm-cloud silver.

Next I embroider darkness—true night. I draw a sheet of deep velvet across the prince’s sleeping knuckle; starlight filters through each pore of my imaginary fabric as softly strewn bioluminescent plankton. Inside, primal beetles halt mid-hover, astonished. The mirrors along their carapaces catch constellation shards and refract them into ponds of shared dream. Mountains quench, contracting cracks sealing with audible sighs. For the first time they cool enough for the faintest blush of lichen—green almost too shy to claim the name color—to colonise a basalt toehold. The smith-creature tastes chlorophyll in its opal tongue and stores the geometry like a sheet of new music.

I do not impose fauna from my own scale—no earthworms, no banyan seed. I only widen the aperture of possibility so their eventual theatre can rehearse longer. I leave a gift orb poised above the lava vent: a pearl of cold darkness no larger than the creature’s dew-drop memory. It is an escrow of bedtime temperature. When the moment of rupture arrives—three prince-years hence—the smith-creature may crack the orb. Darkness will flare outward, drinking excess heat, gifting the micro-world five full seconds of absolute, foundational cool. Enough for seeds to harden, enough for a beetle’s first hatchling to find grit instead of steam between its newborn tarsi.

If the smith-creature chooses dissolution rather than translation, its mirrored scales will rain not restless optics into the prince’s bloodstream, but calm hourglasses: each tick slows the boy’s frantic pulse until he finally sleeps through the night without dreams of scissors. If it chooses translation, the femur-ridge orchestra will play at 37 °C—quiet strings—while somewhere inside the smith-creature’s sesame body, the cool orb cools even further. Ice begins, fragile as breath on a winter window. Lichen spores already loft there. Microbial choirs find pitches between sleet and hush. A glacier in a world smaller than a grain of salt, doing its patient part to teach the superheated scale how to remember winter.

At dawn, the prince’s nail warms. He wakes, unaware of relief, and scratches a mosquito bite near his elbow. The lunula flexes; the micro-peaks ride the ripple like a pulse, wholly intact yet forever changed.

I retreat. My debt is judged paid: I have neither conquered nor left untouched;I have merely widened the runways of future mercy, so whatever grows here next will do so with a margin of kindness measured against starlight.

The smith-creature bows—postures folding into perfect silence—and begins the slow transcription of temperatures that will one day read: Here the mountains learned how to carry both fire and snow.
