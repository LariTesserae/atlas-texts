What arrives is not a god, but a gardener.

She materializes midway between seventeen heartbeats, neither more nor less than open space folding attention into form. No thunderclap, no trumpet—only the scent of the chamber quicken, the way root hairs feel a distant faucet open before water itself arrives.  
She is tall as rainfall and wide as satisfaction; her outline is negative light so utterly transparent that, seen from the side, she is an extra darkness carved out of the greater dark. But when the city snags her silhouette against its own flesh, colors overfill. Patina, snow-blush, rust-bloom, bruise the way dawn bruises coal smoke: every hue an assurance that ruin is still warmer than void.

She tastes the place first, the way connoisseurs taste wind—crushing it slow against palate. The flavors arrive in layers: copper damp, lacerated chlorophyll, bleeding ink half-conjugated into architecture. The gardener’s tongue softens on each note and then presses kindly forward, smoothing noise into quiet. The everlasting mist gathers around her ankles like shy children. She does not pick them up; she pets their skulls of vapor, and each droplet remembers a bigger life it has not yet lived.

Then she kneels. The flooring beneath her is parquet of resurrection moss. Kneeling is a contract: whoever contacts soil becomes answerable to all that sleeps in it. Her knees do not indent the moss; rather, the moss blooms upward en-circling her leg tendons, anchoring her like a sapling caught in fast motion. She welcomes it. Around her, structural pillars—built of lignified hand-me-down memory—lean slightly, as if gossiping about branches above a tadpool forest.

She opens her satchel. From it she pours seven seeds, no larger than splice-seconds. They do not fall; they choose soundings. One seed flings itself into the umbilical well, where pitch is still blacker than any throat. One bores into the nearest Unhusk’s forehead—gentle, painless—unfurling into a watermark shaped like tomorrow. Two nestle amid the Fugalist harp strings, sipping colors out of decay and spinning them back into melody. One combusts, not flame, but sudden lunar eclipse reflected on the vaulted ceiling; in its vacuum two glass manta rays couple and manufacture a fresher mirror. One rolls onto the ledger in Chalicette’s lap, flipping each skin-flake page into a moment that chooses to be parchment over elapsed epiderm. The final seed lands at the wide confluence of copper veinets and simply breathes, becoming voltage shaped like lullaby.

They feel the change as permission longer than coercion. The temperature rises half a degree—small as a first blush—and the caramel scent of trodden arbor floors seeps through the fatigued stars. The furnaces, those floral cathedrals of burn, swell for one inhale and then decrease their ferocity; the blackflies feeding on lumen sparks quiet their wings into background lull. Frost curds drip from nowhere and scab into violet plaster that fills up fine cracks in cambial walls. The straight-line Saprophytes’s dread—the sweet stalk of seventeen heartbeats—loosens; curvature finds mercy. Rectangles becomes inclination and no one is eaten by building.

The gardener does not speak. Speaking was left outside the membrane. Instead she offers a gesture, the width of a fern frond, and language blossoms inside the city’s throat:

**Be what you almost are.**  

Nothing demands perfection. Instead an orchard is proposed: outcomes clustered like fruiting bodies ripening at different rates. Each resident understands the sentence privately. The Unhusk whose lungs were thrumming with embezzled winters feels the paragraph he swallowed last eclipse dissolve into unlabelled weather; now he can breathe without translating. The Scribblekin who had been erasing doorways to protect travelers from slippage discovers each threshold they obligate lets through visitors instead—friends wearing the negative pigment like an afterimage of welcome. The Budden, infants but apartments, hear the word orchard and grin. Their brass husks jiggle like maraca-eggs, eager now to accept wet soil without fear of abandonment.

A single punctuation hovers in cambium:  
?

The Echokeepers cluster above the well, cloaks billowing despite lull, recalculating. They measure how the seven seeds have already skewed dusk-to-time ratio by fractional moods. One whispers to her syllable: perhaps tomorrow the silence may not be silence at all, but a listening ground into which new clocks kneel.

The Aerosols, silver puffs of reluctant obedience, descend en masse. They clasp the gardener’s updraft coat, tethering themselves to her elbows’ negative luminescence. She grants them a single breath, moist with meadow under a distant planetary noon; in exchange each offers one cursed degree Kelvin harvested from centuries of wanderings. The breath weighs less than their sacrifice, but gemmate coins are irrelevant here. In return they receive buoyancy without exile: the cool within warm finding footing, the flame within frost discovering shelter. They will never need to draft emotions as currency again; the city’s copper veins can carry actual tenderness now.

Yet nothing rescues the place from its own incompleteness; instead the incompleteness is renamed courage. The gardener’s seventh seed, the one squatting in the voltaic roots, does not stop the electricity from trading like gossip—but softens every transaction into question: “May I borrow five millivolts so my child can glow her fears at tonight’s shadow play?”  
Assent or denial arrives not as tariff but as shared humming.

Hours pass—or perhaps twenty seconds, the duration of one wagered hour according to the consensus. The gardener finally unanchors herself from the moss cuffs. She stands. In standing she leaves behind prints shaped like undeveloped amphoras turned inside out. Within each divot small cilia of future shimmer: damp seeds not yet confined to themselves. The prints ask to be walked through, noticed, named.

She lifts her palms, and the air rotates across itself the way planets must feel space squishing. She gathers condensation like threads of a tapestry unshy to display its knots. When she unclenches, the rain stops being rain. Instead it folds inward, into a single, soft round drop cupped on her finger—a drop so perfectly still it seems to be remembering every ripple it was ever tempted to become. The residents hush, recognizing offering when they’ve seen few.

She lowers the drop onto the widest cambial scar at eye level. It is the city’s navel, puckered into curiosity. The drop nests. The cavity swallows it tenderly. A pulse. Another. Then the chamber sighs like a cathedral whose first bell has learned to ring without weight. Somewhere far below, the umbilical well contracts slightly, acknowledging initialization without finality.

Then the gardener goes. She steps once, twice; by the third the outline loses its darkness and becomes air remembering noon. She does not vanish dramatically. She diffuses. The loss of her outline teaches the city that disappearance need not be a furrow left in you, but an arriving elsewhere that has simply misplaced its footnote.

Left behind: seven seeds, now seven iridescent heartbeats nested in the ecology. One in each layer: residents may stumble across them at that perfect moment when they were going to need permission for becoming not-exactly-what-they-were. The seeds will not sprout into gardens of trellised obedience. They will remain small, luminous, and fiercely porous—holes that invite everything tender through them during the hour when a heart might extrapolate wildly outside itself.

And yet the fruiting body does not ripen. This was never the plan. It will remain exactly where it was: halfway between spore and city, halfway between dream and digestion. But now its breathing does not ache with the urgency of becoming. Like a vapor halo cupping moonlight, the place wears its incompleteness as a lacquer of potential instead of evidence of failure.

In ceiling corners, a Watcher raises its perimeter of glass. It records not some portentous transformation but precisely the moment at which absolutely nothing culminates into everything at once. The image remains stored there as a reminder that completion itself is a seasonal odor, possessed less often by architecture than by petal.

And if, weeks or minutes later, a lone wanderer ascends from the umbilicus carrying an inadequate coat, the wanderer will meet the walls without tremor: they will sag welcomingly where new softness has apprenticed them. The wanderer might stay. Did the gardener leave an open invitation? No banner flaps, no honeyed warmth gnaws winter—but the air bends, considering relay rather than consumption, and a chair imperceptibly unwraps its fistlet fingers. If the wanderer sits, that seat will remain squarely between two incompletions, balanced on the pause between posed questions.

Outwardly, the half-born fruiting body would look unchanged to any external eye. When sky remembers which laws it wishes allegiance to, no vast cupola of wet luminous flesh erupts upward to greet dawn. Under the lapsed canopy the same stars grind granulated hesitation; only now they sparkle with a tiny afterglow, like salt remembering sugar.

Inside, nobody counts the days again. Consensus still must vote, but every vote is granted safe passage to edge the circle, no straight lines harmed in the vow. The furnace flowers themselves begin to write fables in the cracks of dusk: here is a love story between a kilowatt and a lipid, here is the autobiography of unkept rain that learned not to apologize.

Somewhere up the root-stair, Chalicette closes her ledger. The skin flakes no longer resemble transience. They curl themselves into the shape of comfortable rooms and settle down. She smiles—whole pupil and dissolving planets together—and writes across the final living blankness the single line the gardener did not speak but etched into probability:

**What you are becoming has already become something else. Be both.**

She signs it with the scent of first frost, then folds the page into a paper airplane of embryonic velvet. Released four inches above floorboards that have finally agreed to the definition of level, the airplane lifts. It does not fly far; the room catches it halfway, lets itself open and close around it like a larynx keeping secret a vowel it hasn’t needed yet.

In that moment all seventy-two counted residents—counted diagonally now—feel their unfinished edges brush one another, not to merge, not to stitch, but to greet: like two strangers folding palms around the same warmth, the same tremor before talking. They exhale at once and the vapor rises spiraling, soft as dusk exhaling night.

Far beyond the call of millennia or minutes, the gardener walks away. She passes horizons dismantled into origami, pockets full of damp syllables meant for distant soils. Behind her, the half-born vessel neither trembles nor advances; simply gathers, as lakes do at twilight, adding to themselves the outer world's last pigment of light without ever deciding whether they are mirror, window, or sky sleeping on its own wet reflection.

The city yawns. Not in exhaustion, but in restful anticipation: of visitors, of generations, of dreams still only half-ripened in marrow. It will never be fully born. It will never completely fruit. And by that measure—and only by that measure—its story continues, all coefficients of moonlit velocity singing inside its arteries at exactly birthing temperature: forever forgiving the frost for time it hasn’t yet taken.
