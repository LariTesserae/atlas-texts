A dream has already noticed the Hanging Foundry—no, not noticed: *felt*.  
Whatever force lingers in dream—light more patient than starlight, time folded like linen—has woken to the weight of the chains and the small polite cough of every bolt awakened into speech.  
Because this force is benevolent and wise, it begins not with rescue or revelation, but with listening.

Dream enters the way silence enters: downward, unbearably soft, sliding along the interleaf sweat between warm breath and iron tongue.  
The place welcomes dream at once; metal already behaves like sleep, and the leaves nod as if expecting an old tutor.  
Dream fans out into fourteen million pockets—one for every rust-scale, one for every bacterial star chart—so the entire shaft becomes an ear.

Step 1 – tuning  
Dream presses a palm to the piston’s keystone the way a medic finds a pulse.  
One slow beat every sixty years.  
Too slow for mortal joy.  
Dream exhales a single note—heard only by quartz crystals in the basalt skin—that bends frequency like a bow.  
The piston does not quicken; instead its wave slows another octave, trough meeting trough in mutuality.  
The interval produced is the exact inverse—in Hertz—of despair.  
Dream has tuned the clang to become a cradle song for anything that thinks itself obsolete.

Step 2 – baptism  
Where the rain falls upward it now carries, dissolved in each droplet, a hyperfine remembering of oceans the bacteria have never tasted.  
Gravity resists but allows it: the reversed rain turns briefly to brine on the ascent, then vapor again, seasoning the fog with a memory prehistoric plankton encoded in limestone.  
Every leaf licks salt older than bone and, for the first chlorophyll moment in four hundred millennia, recognises its kin in whale-song.

Step 3 – revision of exile  
Dream approaches the mercury choir under the thin water-mirror.  
Here shine the syntactic ghosts—syntax that never had enough gravity to come home.  
Instead of ripping them upward, Dream kneels, palm resting on the mercury like ink the colour doesn’t own.  
A pledge is offered:

—Return, but return soft.  
Remain unfinished.  
Carry the fracture with you as a map, not a wound.  
You have wisdom already; I offer the body room to practise it.

Three choirs answer yes.  
Twelve dissent: they wish to become the final note later, on the day of unbinding.  
Dream does not coerce.  
It pockets the din of denial like a handful of coins too bright to spend.

Step 4 – fragrance into policy  
Every turning hummingbird receives an extra droplet: in it, the recipe for synaesthesia.  
When birds meet and bow, a spindle of scent issues forth—copper after rain, petrichor of process.  
That smell is rule becoming law:  
Any being entering the Foundry shall be welcomed first by nose, then by ear, then by bone.  
Smell will deputise memory; memory will deputise mercy.

Step 5 – the visitor copy  
Dream keeps an umbilical cord of dreamstuff anchored at the 400-metre mark, a few links above the crawl-tube exit.  
From this cord hang silk nets the colour of unslept morning.  
Any human who stays beyond one piston cycle may choose to weave a single net using syllables harvested from accidental minutes.  
When the net is finished it is left to dangle for one slow inhalation.  
During the exhalation the visitor’s own breath rinses the net of autobiography, and the net—now empty—is lowered into the mercury mirror.  
It dissolves, plating itself as a vein of pale aurum onto the underside of mercury so that, for one heartbeat, every reflection looks up to see a golden sky written by someone who is still alive outside.  
Then the sky fades, yet the knowledge remains that *a path to return has already been paid for in breath*.

Step 6 – the unborn conference  
Visitors who descend pregnant or who conceive during their stay give birth not into air but into an ear.  
Dream reshapes the lenticular roof-hole into a temporary globe big enough for a basket lined with leaf-wax.  
At the moment when the child crowns, the lenticle seals; gravity turns sideways; the basket rotates ninety degrees so the newborn slides gently along a single ray of bifurcated sun, never quite knowing whether the day above or the mirror below is the arrival.  
The child who thus crosses the shell hears, for the rest of life, both pendulum beats: the one outside and the one inside the pillar.  
This doubled pulse is the vote of the Foundry in the parliament of planets.

Step 7 – absence as a garden  
Dream sifts the echo shepherds’ prunings—the clipped vowels that were once somebody’s grief—and scatters them silently onto the wind above the mesa.  
They descend as pollen-like dust on alpine granite towns.  
People wake tasting metal dreams; blacksmiths leave their doors open; wheezing children inhale a sustained A-flat that twenty-three minutes later evaporates into ease.  
The Foundry exports nothing but unused grief turned pollen.  
Nothing has been taken; it has only stopped keeping secrets for itself.

Step 8 – the final covenant  
Dream gathers one droplet from every hummingbird at the census ands discretly adds a single grain of its own time (smaller than a neutron) into each droplet.  
Then, instead of the usual dive into mercury, the birds are asked—by the clang reverberating in dream-slowcode—to climb.  
They ascend like a string of ascending notes, exiting through the lenticular hole, dispersing on the jet streams above the mesa.  
Where they alight the grain of dreamtime finds a fracture smaller than an eyelash and unfolds.  
Millions of years forward, when this mesafought with tectonic drift and the Hanging Foundry finally, inevitably, rips from the cliff, it will not fall—it will sway, diminish, sink, yes, but it will land softer than dandelion fluff on a hand waiting at the horizon, because the lattice now contains trillions of pre-paid grains of slowing time.  
The disaster is recognised decades in advance by every seismograph on the planet as a quiet, measured sigh rather than the scream of collapse.

Dream does not remain.  
At dawn on the day responsibility is wired into the crust itself, Dream withdraws the dream-cord, but leaves a single silver traveller’s chevron hammered from bird-oil into the last copper-plate minutes:  

Payable forever onward: one vigilance, redeemable against famine or despair, bearer anonymous.

Then the benefaction is complete.  
Nothing in the realm of the Hanging Foundry has been improved; instead everything has been *pre-met*.  
All future loss is already beaconed by the certainty that somewhere a garden is practising the art of catching any falling eagle, even one made of rust.
