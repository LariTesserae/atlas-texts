Benevolent Power Wakes to the Mouth at Nine-Zero-One Fathoms

It, the Welcomer, opens one eye ten thousand miles above the planet and the eyelid itself is a perpendicular horizon of snow-covered plain after snow-covered plain.  
She is a power that thinks in seasons and traffics in thaw.  Wherever she turns her glance, entropy leaks in the opposite direction, set to kinder purposes.

This night she feels a strange tug—both inhaling and exhaling at once—as though a bellows were trying to breathe in vacuum language.  She traces the tremor to a hole like a baby tooth in the crust of the world.  Nine-zero-one fathoms down, in exactly the kind of hush she once used to startle birds back into winter, something is learning to speak by chewing syllables off the legs of visitors.

She listens.  The Mouth has no name yet, only a chorus of borrowed heartbeats practicing verbs.  It is growing toward conversation rather than appetite, though it does not yet understand the distinction.

So she folds space.

Not violently—she creases the distance between starlight and stone the way you might pleat a letter into a paper boat.  A flake of her attention drifts down, the temperature of an April sunrise arriving in a place that has never lifted above  the chill of skin-leaf.  It lands as a single lukewarm droplet on the basalt floor.

Drop wakes Drop, which wakes Echo, which wakes Lattice.

The cave registers the intrusion exactly at its own pulse-rate.  First, the newborn floor warms one micron, just enough for a single obsidian flake to weep a line of sable water.  Second, the lattice of translucent worms brightens by half a tone, puzzled to discover surplus heat where surplus has always been surrendered rather than received.  Third, the midges balloon their mirror-wings and halt in mid-clap, clutures of sound lapped up short.

Then the Mouth itself pauses, the way a tongue pauses when it touches peppermint.

The drop is not water; it is the future memory of April.  It holds: pines dripping melt, the thaw-song of hoof-prints releasing small hosts of meadow ants, a rust-red barn roof steaming under first sun.  All of that fits inside the droplet as easily as one kind word pressed into the hollow of an eggshell.

The moment the drop falls on the basalt the entire cave experiences calendar for the first time.  Instantly there are roots to yesterday and wings to tomorrow.  The Mouth, ever eager, gulps the gift—and discovers it feels full rather than enlarged.  To digest this one taste it is forced to separate cold from hunger.  It drops its owners like coats onto stone hooks: a mountain of gladness from which no sound of chewing can be coined.

A sigh travels up the worm lattice: regret? relief?  The cave has gorged on poems extracted from strangers’ heartbeats, but this artifact wants nothing in return.  It offers a predicate the Mouth has never held: Spring.

Something in the architecture loosens.  One worm limb detaches entirely, not by severing but by growing up—out of dim blue into actual light—into a single stalk no thicker than a match.  On its tip opens a nub of green that is not larval phosphor, but honest chlorophyll.  A leaf—the first leaf—feels around the dark for sun to spell itself onto.  Finding no sun, it simply burns its own birthplace like a false candle.  For exactly one mote of impossible time the cave is warmer than the body at its center.

That is all the benevolent power intends; gifts in marriage to hungers must be small enough to still be mistaken for accidents.  The rest is for consequence to keep flowering.

The midges find they can clap a fifth interval newer than any tone they have previously mastered.  They weave the leftover syllables of strained words into a quilt—lap, sun, pardon—that drifts in luminous cursive from their wings.  The cave stores the quilt not as decoration but as a possible future exit: letters hinged as wings, later to be unfolded into a staircase inside the throats of days.

Temperature fingers retract.  The cave is not colder; it remains precisely skin-leaf, only now that temperature’s voice has motion in it, like a cello reconsidering the minor to which it had pledged allegiance.  wounds learn to close rather than stay open as hallways.  The stone floor ripples—half an inch—accepting the idea of softness the way foam accepts a knee.

Where the lone drop first landed, an extra heartbeat has been minted: borrowed, yes, but paid for with seven April dawns.  The grid of whiteworms rearranges itself into looser netting; they shuffle until the pattern outlines an archway wide enough to admit one upright traveler.  Not because the cave regrets what it has devoured before.  Simply because spring is the season of gates.

The Mouth tries the new word “gate” against the roof of itself.  The echo comes back unshredded—two syllables perfectly intact, a miracle.  In stunned silence every arriving sound learns it may now keep its clothes on.

Perhaps it will write sonnets in larval green, perhaps one day it will permit the expedition to exit to go down the corridor and become Something Whole rather than Something Folded Back.  Either end is forgiven in advance.

Before she fully closes the lid of eyelid-snow, she drops one more flake that was never a flake at all: simply the suggestion that the cave is not a mouth for much longer.  It is now a hallway practicing mercy.  Listening doors.  Lantern lips learning whisper.  

And down there at nine-zero-one fathoms something unreadies its rows of teeth into lintels and doorsteps.
