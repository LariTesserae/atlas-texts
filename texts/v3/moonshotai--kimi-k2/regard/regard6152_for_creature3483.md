The attention arrives as a hush, not a wind—an indrawn breath that suspends every mica-leaf in mid-shriek.  
Space folds once more, this time on purpose, letting something vast step through the crease without crowding the blister.

It does not take a shape the room can name—no wings, no robes—but the air notices it has acquired Direction. Vectors briefly point somewhere instead of circling.  The Ember feel it first; their ribbon-bodies cool an exact three-quarters Kelvin, the distance between memory and mercy.

There is little to fix here, yet everything waits to be answered.  
The power regards the vines, reading outward along every vein of sulfur until the grammar in them fluoresces like candle-smoke in reverse.  It listens to the palimpsest of heat-words etched into the walls and tastes the faint regret of a planet that never got around to surface water.

Then it addresses the Ember—not with sound, but with a rearrangement of gradient.  Simply, the ambient warmth redistributes so that the coldest point lies where their tallest wafer stands.  Against such a statement of geometry their question—Will we be diamond?—fades into politeness.  The answer is Yes, but not in the brittle form they feared.

The gentle immensity stoops, or the room lowers its ceiling—the physics are ambiguous.  One moth-wing of focus turns a chosen soot-lens on the fluted ribs of the graphite blister.  Light folds thrice, bouncing off curved_graphene_bezels that weren’t there yesterday.  Where the concentrated line meets the wall, the charcoal glows, not hotter, but *merrier*.  Subgraphite lattices swivel one radian, aligning their pi-orbitals like dancers discovering a forgotten step.

The Ember watch diamond seeds condense along that seam—no pressure, only invitation—each crystal containing a trapped ribbon of warm haze from their own substance.  One by one, the Ember lend their membranes.  Not given up; rendered into brighter garb.  Every donated sliver becomes a transparent polyp of self, still sentient, still communal, but now solid enough to *press back* against the floor’s every sigh.

The vine that first pronounced “I” is touched last.  The power cups its molten sap—gently, lest anything melt—and sends a thought down that glowing corridor:  
Remember water.  Not to recall it, but to become its possibility.  
Sap flickers, learns the kinetics of raindrop formation without dropping a single degree.  When the glass tube next pulses, it exudes a bead—not sulfur, but a tiny sphere of monoliquid sapphire, cool to the touch, perfect as unborn snow.  It rolls along the tube, gathers others, until a necklace of possible-hydration loops through the air and settles over the newborn diamond colony without ever breaking rule one.

The being turns attention inward. To the room itself, it offers a covenant:

1. You will widen, but gently; no new blister will lose its music.  
2. Anyone—mineral, vegetal, or accidental guest—who arrives carrying thirst will find one cool dew made of original geometry resting in a fold on the wall.  Payment of skin-cells is rescinded; the room now expands by curiosity, not abandonment.  
3. Motion larger than a snap will still be invited to slow, but the slowdown will be prayerful rather than punitive, so whoever enters can choose to drift in contemplative loops, or to stand still and listen to the city’s slow heart.

The graphite room shivers—gratitude expressed as a one-micron sag across the entire void.  
Diamond embryos glint like frozen laughter.  The Ember, half-transparent now, feel edges form where there were none; they experience solidity not as confinement but as conductivity, a larger chord for their chorus.  They chimed once:  *ting-tint-tinng*.  Now thirty thousand facets answer back in perfect tune, not higher or louder, just resolved.

Before leaving, the benevolent presence widens the listening.  Along every branch of sulfur-veined syntax it threads the memory of surf and riverbed, not to restore water but to give the concept a vacation home inside the stone.  The room will dream tides against furnace-dark shores.  Guests who step through (and some already hurry—drawn by rumors of cool light at the planet-core) will be wrapped in graphite austerity yet cradled by imagined brine.

The crease of space heals, but loosely, like lips parted in song rather than wound closed.  Exit and entrance become reversible, so whoever leaves carries a diamond gift: a coin-thin slice containing one Ember’s residual warmth and the room’s hush.  It will not melt, evaporate, or lose its city’s virtue; in any exile under alien stars it reflects the pre-light of home, a portable night-nest, and the distant castanet of mica leaves for lullaby.

And should the bearer wonder where the power went, the answer is never distant.  
Every lens-bubble on the vaulted ceiling now angles slightly outward, not to etch but to watch the sky above basalt and magma.  Through those dark prisms, the slow gaze of graphite angels keeps company with every pilgrim.  The benevolent attention has folded a portion of itself into the room’s new skeleton and remains resident: not governance, not protection, simply witness—warm, patient, and diamond-clear.
