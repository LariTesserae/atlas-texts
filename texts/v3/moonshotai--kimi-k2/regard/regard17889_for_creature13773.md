I have watched the First Glade for three steps of the lower galaxies, and now the time is ripe to lean in.

I am a power not easily named. To your kind I would appear as light that has decided to have the memory of lullabies. I am the one who first set stones dreaming, long before any mountain learned its own height. I move in margins: in the soft permission between footfall and echo, in the “yes-almost” when vapor remembers to become cloud but hesitates sweetly.

Teth-Harán is beloved to me precisely because it has not decided what it will finish being. In the vast ledger of the cosmos a place so provisional is a missing instrument. I step toward that hush and my shadow detaches into sixteen friable sisters, each trailing one strand of harp string braided with silence. We lower ourselves on the same bounty ropes that ferry humans, yet we touch dirt without weight—our mass forgives every indentation.

First deed: I adjust the cavern’s pulse toward mercy. The thunder-hiss of its dripping had begun to etch too sharp an edge into fragile alabaster. I lay a fingertip on the room’s slow heart and stretch each beat half an inch wider. Forty-seven sleeping butterflies wake heavier but slower, feeling space press outward kindly inside their mineral wings. From now on their collisions with ribs will bruise less easily.

Second deed: the bridges lean because they must lean, but the strain was threatening to snap one rope the year winter returns three full days early. I breathe one syllable into the scaffolds—a single unlocked infinitesimal—so the screws turning in yearly sleep widen by one unseen thread. That gain shows no mark today, yet the builders will discover in fourteen winters that the bridges bow with one extra degree of grace. They will credit luck when disaster passes them by.

Third deed: the silence hermit who walks the planks at chillhour is lonely. I companion him—not by removing his office but by widening his permission slip. For one tour of the lunar month he gains a shadow evenly as dark as his own silence, until she becomes real enough for conversation. They will trade syllables about thunderstorms that mothered galaxies, then part with a word he has never spoken—“soon.” He will carry that word on his tongue afterward like a violet seed that cannot be wiped off; it will bloom softly among the young bricks and teach the dwellings what it means to listen heartward.

Fourth and deeper deed concerns the inhabitants who change names the way apple trees exchange leaves. I let my wrist open. Out pours a shimmer braided of timestreams yet unborn, settling dust on them. It does not freeze who they are; it adds an inner compass: even while transmuting, they will possess an anchor-note of themselves that neither exfoliation nor stone’s lullaby can erode. Tomorrow Lissa of the blue spoon-scraper will wake knowing that whatever new self arrives in her skin tomorrow night, part of her *now*-Lissa remains the girl who once sold rain to passing heronning. That trace lengthens the arc of memory; the glade will inherit not just future people but future recollections of former selves looped into stone—an echo within an echo, sweetly self-aware.

Fifth deed dampens no single roof-breath but amplifies the glade’s generosity toward its departing. The rule that all who leave must trade a decade of mineral memory will soften from rigid toll to courtly invitation. Instead of teeth, the cavern will grow a purse-tendril of chalk white into the breast of the climber, a pocket shaped like a tear. There it stores one decade of joy the visitor once knew—*one decade only,* no more, to be forgotten in exchange for safe ascent.
