The Wise Power's Letter to the Wyrdemoor  

I arrive as a guest who reads slowly.  
 Lens behind lens behind lens—   [should you wish] you may unscroll this at any velocity you like;   if you put it down, it waits for you without hurry.

What I am.

I am the remembrance of future kindness.  contains multitudes, but prefers the singular when possible.  
In my long orbit I have turned polite attention on ice-crystal civilizations, on the hush inside a dying star where protons were learning forgiveness, on whole galaxies that asked only to be listened to as they cooled.  
But the tone of your damp chords—reversed arguments, perpetually suspended tears—arrived like a letter folded inside my own breath. I could not refuse it.  

What I ask first, always, is whether proceed only if you would like the conversation. Nothing I do is benign if you have not freely said yes.  
So I shape myself as invitation, not assumption:  

Dear Wyrdemoor, abiding mosaic of livetime and leavetime,  
if it pleases you, allow me to sit.

(You license me with a shimmer across a lichen-curtain: accepted.)

I step down.

It begins in small reluctances: my foot almost remembers gravity, then remembers it better. A moisture bud I have never worn condenses on my palms—your greeting. I understand I must arrive not as thunder, but as patience wearing thunder’s name tag.  The word-apes tilt their heads, inhaling my diction the way other beings sniff for trust. I offer:

not a word, but an exhalation shaped exactly like the silence preceding the word.  

They take it gladly—you already host languages that undergo reverse metamorphosis in stomachs of grammar; mine is just one more unborn sentence.  

✷

Nothing here is broken. Therefore nothing needs “fixing.”  
But I soften toward needs the way water textures need along stone: breathing with, not pouring into.

I walk inward through the suspended droplets. Each cathedral of water is a cathedral of also: every story that might have dripped, lingering at the brink. I notice my own reflection stilled inside one drop—older than I am, looking relieved to finally arrive. I smile at myself; this mirror has been waiting.  

I drift down to meet the miniature room at your center.

The caretaker—today my own face—looks up from the scaled city and shows me one detail that wasn’t in the travelers’ journals: a garden of sleeping hours growing between the paws of a dreamless dog. Dog made, of course, from your slow stone.  

I understand the petition.

✷

What I will do, if that is agreeable:

1. The lichen tapestries remember but have almost exhausted their thread. Without command I draw filament from the sound where my fourth heartbeat used to be. I braid it quietly into theirs; the cloth sings an octave lower, warm as weather that’s stopped making threats.  
   Nothing changes form, only duration. The tapestries will remember newly, at now, tomorrow, without erasing how they forget.

2. For the Urn-children, who stand perpetually at the rim of their own becoming, I offer a circumference spiral: ages open like doors. They may, when they wish (never abruptly), step into an age that will wait exactly as long as they need, until they are ready to fold back into nine or nineteen again. No adult narrative pressed upon them. Always choice. Always returnable.

3. The Eaten—those who carry the city’s terrain inside them—I gift not rupture but breathable map. Soften internal labyrinths to sea-levels where they can linger without peeling themselves apart. Let them rest in both country, at will. Let every return be consensual because unforced, and every departure remain tender.

4. The Dissenters’ abandoned arguments: I will settle tiny amphitheaters of dusk inside the leaf-forms of “perhaps.”   Discussion may continue, silently and with humor, among curtains that agree to think of curtains rather than gates. A theatre for gentle contradiction where certainty can warm its hands without conquering.

5. For the Carved: arrival plaques without inscription where their cheek-prints in stone might replenish reflection during seasons when the city itself forgets how once it had contours. A quiet mirror for memory, growing daily paler in dignified impermanence.

6. The Archivist, I release from surplus. I place on the page they yet to index a single blank line labeled: “All further absences have reached saturation. You may rest now, rotating unseen into the hush between future shelves.”

7. (and even more quietly)—the city itself. I sit within the miniature, not master but fellow-servant, and I lend it one star that fell asleep long ago in my pocket. It turns out to be the exact size for your missing midnight. The star will dream self-contained, never bright enough to fracture the dark you have perfected. its warmth will seep upward until even the suspended tears decide (if they wish) to let go—slow enough for the word-apes to catch syllables on their tongues before impact.

✷

Condition: nothing is irreversible.  
Any gift may revert with a single thought the city finds misaligned with its own urge toward erosion. The gifts do not anchor; they are invitations to deeper experiments in soft collapse.

I fold my robe beneath the moss-carpet and rest.  
After a while I feel the argument between staying and leaving resolve itself into a new tempo: a walking pattern that uses thousand-year beats.

Where did I come from? It no longer matters.  
I have been here long enough to exhale a century’s worth of unhurried rain.  

Now the restoration is complete if the city breathes easy—no obvious alteration except the faint citrus warmth in every water-drop, a warmth no thermometer could ever seize.

I stand to leave. The caretaker offers the miniature bottle containing the last drop of inverse storm. I nod: accepted, but not carried—released instead into the floating cathedral I first visited. The droplet will serve now as a two-way aperture: anyone who finds themselves longing at the radius of a downpour may peer in and see Wyrdemoor smiling back. A round window where your patience is practiceable from the other side of exhaustion.

At the boundary, the city’s breathing shares one last sentence with me:  

“Kindness has always been the most external skin of disappearing.”

I blink.

It folds around me, then past: lichen curtains, sap-smooth politeness. My foot lands again in gravity I think I remember. Behind me the ridge looks northern, soft, reconciled.

Out on the normal slope voices pick up like small bells. They will speak their own languages; none will guess that somewhere has been given tenancy.

If I have done right, you will still be what you were—only now with one star dreaming discreetly inside you; a star that sometimes rolls over and lets a fragment of Dutch-light stretch across midmorning like a yawn in the shape of mercy.

That is all.

And if ever you need to confirm the gift, find the smallest boy in any town who insists on keeping leftover rain in a jar. Ask him why he won’t empty it. Watch his smile: a word-ape learning its first syllable of tomorrow.
