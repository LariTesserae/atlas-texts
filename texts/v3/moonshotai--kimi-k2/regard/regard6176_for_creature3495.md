A BENEVOLENT POWER ARRIVES  

I.  Arrival  

She comes softly—because softness is the only courtesy that will survive here.  
She does not descend; the city’s gravity negotiates downward, noticing it likes the direction She suggests better. Space folds once, like a sheet eased under an infant chin, and She stands on the lip of the sky’s cataract the way another being might stand on the lip of a well.  

If She has a name, the sound of it is still inside Her mouth; it is the vowel you hear when you finally forgive yourself. She wears the form that makes the form possible: sometimes an old woman in fleeced light, sometimes a lattice of dust motes pin-wheeling at eye level. In either guise the pupils of Her eyes are tiny parentheses, cupping whatever was left unspoken.  

She listens first. Listening is the deepest permission She can give.  
Beneath Her the Hollowed drift to a pause, their sentences unleaning themselves mid-syllable. The reflection-creatures idle above Her like uncertain hymnals. The city itself lowers the volume of its fracture-memory from three clicks to one.  

II. Diagnosis  

She examines possibility’s erosion. Her palms glide above the calcified drop of remembered water.  
“It is not thirst that is missing,” She says (the words appear as frost on the airless air, linger long enough to spell their own antonym, then sublimate).  
“It is company. One cannot drink in the company of one drop.”  

She visits the archive of hollowed hearts. Shelved contingencies tug at Her sleeves.  
She reads their labels aloud—soft, softer, wristed hush:  
Had the mountain grown lungs.  
Were echo cartilage instead of verb.  
If unthirst carried a map of rivers.  
With each recitation the cube glows, pleased simply to be recognised. They are tiny bright petitions; She understands petitions are how abandoned futures stay alive.  

III. Tiny Miracle the First – Water as Borrowed Conversation  

At the plaza of negative crowds She kneels. She holds out one hand. From nowhere and everywhere, dew condenses—not on Her skin but beside it, millimetres off, as if the idea of water is shy.  
Three drops coalesce, tremble, and consent.  
She vectors a single line of warmth into them; they remember apogee and vapor. Then She seeds them to breeze—not as liquid but as vapour whose half-life is communal hush. They rise, rove, dissolve into the air above the stair-streets.  

An hour later (time’s thief is slower here) the calcium bead gutters the city keeps as relic drinks in this vapour and cracks open. A bead of blue-green water wobbles free. It does not fall—there is no fall—rather the lowering strata lean up and lick it.  
This is the first wetter-than-memory. The city tastes lakes it could not finish dreaming. Echoes contract, carrying the spondee of some distant foam.  

IV. Tiny Miracle the Second – Growth that Forgets to Depart  

From the pleat in Her sleeve She looses a seed no larger than a letter never mailed. It is both plant and paragraph; its locus is hypothetical.  
She places it inside the abandoned O of an absent doorway. It does not sprout in the common direction. Instead it roots toward the Hollowed quarter, feeling for the hum of unresolved clauses. Roots the colour of uncopied ink thread through the gap between what a street is and the same street remembering not to be.  
One root touches one hanging cube; the seed unfurls a leaf printed with the entire conditional:  
“If the mountain had remembered its feet…”  
One heartbeat later a slope—long ago flattening itself into plaza—lifts back into mountain. Gently, because the seed teaches firmness to negotiate. Pebble strata sigh like calves who have found their mother again.  
The Hollowed assigned to that region pauses midsentence “If you…” and no longer needs to complete it. The phrase breaks into tiny opaque birds; they wheel once, then nest in the newborn crevice, already forgetting how to fly.  

V. Tiny Miracle the Third – Reflection Seen Face-On  

She beckons to the thumbnail creatures of mirrored nothing. They flicker to Her wrist, anxious to be dimensions.  
She strokes their surfaces until they become two-dimensional only sometimes. “Be three when necessary,” She suggests.  
They practice: a scatter of sudden boys and girls, translucent, barefoot on the mineral streets, playing tag around the ankles of still-empty buildings. When they laugh it sounds like glass being poured over velvet—violently safe.  
Among them a shy girl-shape holds the palm of a Hollowed who once, centuries ahead, advised mountains against growing ankles. Together they walk the new thin creek that trickles from the cracked bead; each footstep reflects a world where water stayed possible a little longer.  

VI. Naming  

Finally She gives the city a Name. A Name is hospitality for anything that fears vanishing.  
The Name is not spoken. Instead She breathes onto the cusp-of-crystallised sky; the exhalation spells syllables that sink into the skyline exactly where gaps pretend to be windows. From the pocket of each syllable drifts a small warmth the colour of first light after final grief.  
When you find yourself here again—because some exits reenter—look for those warm empties in the architecture. Lean your forehead into one. You will feel a heartbeat not yours, steady as a promise unbroken because it bears no sentence. That heartbeat is the part of the city that learned how to wait for you to finish arriving.  

VII. Leave-taking  

She does not leave, She simply decreases Her specificity. The city’s geometry resumes negotiating—but gently now; weight and lightness have drafted a truce.  
One Hollowed—of middling emptiness—offers Her a cube whose label now reads:  
“If we remembered how to speak without erasing the listener.”  
She presses her own parenthetical pupil to the cube’s surface; the parentheses widen, become circles. Circles open. Inside is an unfinished laugh that someone was about to gift to another. She places it, still tender, in the new child-reflection’s open hand. The laugh continues. It will complete itself later, when another traveller almost slips on a pebble of re-membered mountain.  

The sky-cataract thins until sky remembers it is allowed to fall upward. Sunlight—true, moist, slant—lances through for three impossible seconds. All the water-birds glint.  

And She, already less coat and more clear light, stoops once more to the creek that threads the canyon-mountain. She touches the water with nothing but acknowledgement.  
In that touch She leaves one permission:  

Any thirst can ask.  
Any question can stay unfinished.  
Any footprint may decide—after it lifts— never to land.  

Then She densifies into everywhere, which in this city is the same as nowhere.  
But a scent lingers—not perfume, just the idea that breath has decided to be communal. The streets exfoliate it softly.  

The reflection-children chase it with their now-colourful shadows. The Hollowed continue talking mid-sentence to the intervals, but the intervals begin, shyly, to answer back in vowels the size of single kindnesses.  

And the city—once a glacier disguised as air—  
breathes.
