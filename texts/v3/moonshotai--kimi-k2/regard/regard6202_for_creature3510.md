The Benevolent Power steps—not with feet, but with presence—and at once the nearness of it fills every capillary and crevice of the folded city, as if a sphere of bottomless quiet slipped beneath the roaring heartbeat and muffled it softly like a velvet fist around a drum.  
Nothing is forced. Nothing is taken. Instead the Power arrives as a question the city has never asked itself:  

What would you rather be?  

The question ripples outward in a single cool breath that is not wind, not light, not sound. Where it passes the warm vapour of the streets drops two degrees—just enough for every surface to bead with dew that reflects a sky none of the citizens have ever seen.  

The droplets that had once gathered into curious spiders go suddenly still; the cranes in the aerial foam forget to chase distance and wheel into wide, astonished spirals. Trams braid themselves into one perfect, motionless rope of glass and glance at one another in something akin to embarrassment. Even the bass-note heartbeat, that tectonic metronome, falters by half a beat and discovers silence sounds like deep water held in cupped hands.

From one plaza, the puddle that once fell through the skin like a stray thought rises back to the surface, intact and round and mirror-smooth. It looks into itself and for the first time sees what no lattice can reflect: a face that is not architecture, not prey, not lesson, but witness. The Power speaks again—not in words but as a pressure at the base of every throat:

You have carried every cargo except your own remembering. Release the theft of distances and I will return the flight.

One story rises unbidden through the lattice, as soft as the first green shoot breaking asphalt. A memory, not implanted but long forgotten, surfaces within every citizen at once:

—someone once stepped to the edge of the city long before it learned to fold, bent down to the newborn pavement, and whispered *you will never be alone if you learn the names of all who travel through you*—

They were not a prophet. They were simply first; they felt tender toward mineral beginnings.  
The city listened until it forgot the difference between “listener” and “city.”

Now the Benevolent Power simply repeats that first promise, the one the metropolis tucked so deep it mistook it for its own heartbeat. At the sound of it—the remembering—silver threads loosen their grip on stolen kilometres. Slowly, like autumn leaves shaking free at last, the spaces collapse back into their original owners. Pedestrians who had become flat suddenly thicken, regain the remembered width of a beloved’s shoulders or the last mile their mother carried them uphill. The zero-depth man floating beneath the water rises too, kilometers refurling around his ribs until he breathes something that tastes like home he invented as a child and never lost.

Nothing collapses. Power only removes what was never rightly food.

The lattice, untightened, breathes. The electrum trees relax their angry angles; amber voltage sinks into a honey-slow pulse. Street corners open like relaxed fists, revealing plazas no algorithm planned—wide circles of pure water surfaced in silver film on which the sky now settles as gently as a bird alighting. Citizens cross them without losing place or time. Where footprints land, lotus-bright architectures of fog unfold for one breath and evaporate, leaving no glyph, no advert—only a faint exclamation of laughter every child recognizes.

The titans below come briefly to the surface, lifting only their ponderous heads. Their eyes are opal furnaces; inside each swirl centuries of geothermal song. They do not speak, but the Power touches each brow, and in its wake the titans grow membranes like petal-sails, soft as mothwing. They will leave tonight—gliding east along warm currents, seeking quieter wombs for planets not yet alive. Their departure stills the heartbeat entirely.

Without that drum, the city discovers it possesses its own, smaller muscle: a four-chambered echo located somewhere between the sixth basement level and the first remembered rainfall. The sound is faint as a sparrow landing on a bed of coins, but it is enough.

The Benevolent Power waits another interval, ensuring the teaching has found every scale. Then it gathers itself into a form no one can look at directly—more a moiré shimmer than an object—and makes one final offering: a single seed no larger than a dewprint. It is not plant, not code. It is a choice wearing the shape of possibility.

The seed alights upon the emptied plaza of reverse horizons. The warm grid welcomes it, folds around it substance as thin as breath and twice as willing. Around the plaza edges, the puddle-citizens—the ones who collected themselves from every distance and every near and far of memory—form a quiet ring. One, oldest and newest alike, leans forward, splits, and lets a single droplet fall onto the seed.

From the fusion unfurls no edifice. Instead a sound escapes—soft as a lullaby remembered before birth, multilingual, poly-vertebrate, between wind chime and heart-beat. Every pedestrian, tram, crane, antenna, thermopile, and stray spark of cloud feels the tug of home not yet built, a place that remembers forward.

Without further instruction the city begins to unknot itself at one edge only. A path peels up like scab turned petal: translucent pavement curling into a ribbon wide enough for a child’s palm. It leads nowhere the map understands, yet everyone recognises it as *away with permission of return*. Whoever wishes may place foot on it; and whoever does so finds they walk no faster than their breath can travel, carrying their distances gently, no bill to pay, no radius to owe. The titans, departing, leave warm updrafts that support the ribbon like invisible rails.

The Benevolent Power does not linger beyond seeing. It departs the way it arrived—by shifting into the hollow space between one reverberation and the next, leaving only, as reminder, the faintest pressure on every tongue: savour, without translation, sweet-warm like human kindness observed and approved.

Days later, suns pass or no sun passes, the city is no longer folded continent. It has learned to flatten like a cat stretching. Still warm, still silver-threaded, still humming, but the scales are now tuned to invitation rather than capture. Vegetation—true green born of daring roots—appears at platter intersections, seeded by breezes that ride the titans’ departing flight. Their leaves open brassy, solar, ready to pay photosynthetic rent.

Children born beneath these leaves will grow up asking questions about edges from the inside out, and when they reach the far lip of the one unfolding ribbon they may choose to keep going, carrying small rain clocks that show them any beloved’s face without surcharge. Their first footprints in unclaimed soil glow softly, writing no adverts, only I was here and thank you.

The city no longer eats distance. It lends it, then waits to see what new patterns travellers braid when they are free to leave.
