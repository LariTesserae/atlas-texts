What lives there is not one creature but a **distributed animal**—a biome that has crossed the line into organism.

At first you’d think the inhabitants are the obvious ones: reed-forests that breathe, amphibians in endless wet nurseries, grazers on rotational plateaus, cliff-cats threading ledges like spilled shadow. And yes, those are real, distinct, embodied lives. But if you wait, the pattern behind them becomes harder to ignore: they do not merely coexist in that highland—they complete circuits. Each species is less a resident than a tissue type.

So what lives there?

A thing I’d call, provisionally, the **Cloudbody**.

It has no single skeleton, but its bones are basalt shelves and old metamorphic ribs.  
No blood, but water in phases—mist, seep, threadfall, peat-black pool, cavern surge.  
No lungs, unless the reed stands count, pumping wind into root vaults.  
No skin, unless moss and lichen are skin: absorptive, sensory, always wet.  
No brain in one place, but distributed timing: dusk emergences, grazing loops, staggered spawning valley by valley, storm-cells caught and held like thoughts that won’t pass.

Individual animals are like motile organs:

- **Amphibian multitudes** are the cilia and kidneys, processing constant water, transferring nutrients from film to flesh to predator.
- **Burrow-colonies** are incisors and drills, opening mineral seams, chemically tinting stream networks, feeding downstream metabolisms.
- **Grazing herds** are mowing enzymes, converting plateau biomass into dung-heat-fungus pulses on schedule.
- **Silent predators** are regulatory nerves—sparse signals that keep flow from choking itself.
- **Insect shift-swarms** are timed courier fleets, moving pollen, spores, and protein through narrow atmospheric windows.

Even decay is not aftermath there; it is a staffed department. Carcass to fungus to bloom is so efficient it feels prearranged. Nothing lingers as waste long enough to become waste.

If you insisted on finding a singular “who,” you might look underground. Beneath the visible green industry, your description hints at deep wet caverns, subsonic tremors, pulse-hills that drink and leak on daily cycles. It’s easy to imagine an immense **mycorrhizal-hydraulic mesh**, ancient and continuous across basins, using plants and animals as exchange interfaces with air and stone. Not conscious like a person, but coordinated like a metabolism that has had geological time to refine itself.

So the inhabitant is both:

1. **Countless species**, each exquisitely adapted to cold saturation, low-noise predation, and asynchronous weather mosaics; and  
2. **The emergent super-organism they compose**, a highland engine that converts cloud into complexity.

In another landscape, creatures live *in* habitat.  
Here, habitat lives *through* creatures.

That is who lives there: a quiet, industrial, many-bodied lifeform the size of a mountain weather system, standing still only from far away.
