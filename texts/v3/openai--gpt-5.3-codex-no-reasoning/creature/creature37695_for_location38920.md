What lives there is not a single creature, but a **civic organism**—a many-bodied resident made of workers, machines, roots, flocks, molds, rails, and rules.

If you insist on a “who,” then it is something like this:

A people of **listeners and tenders**, more than builders in the old sense. They don’t construct so much as tune. They grow braces instead of bolting them. They herd scavenger-birds through grease trenches with coded whistles. They read wall texture with their palms in passing, the way others read signage. Their children learn gradients before letters: warm means brood, resin-smooth means safe, sponge-moss means hidden water, brittle crust means don’t breathe deep.

They are never alone, because their nearest neighbors are always multispecies:  
- boiler-lizards blinking on manifold heat,  
- railline ambush predators feeling through their feet,  
- leaf-gliders dropping from the overhead dark,  
- clipped-frond grazers reshaping every root wall nightly,  
- insect weather—pollinator clouds that track body heat like weather fronts track pressure.

And beneath/within all of them, the **infrastructure-biome** lives too: algae in ductwork responding to pressure pulses, vine columns carrying both load and current, condensers “milking” rain on schedule, pumps that must never go quiet unless you want the water to turn on everyone.

So the true inhabitant is a **treaty intelligence**:  
an old industrial district taught, over generations, to breathe like an ecosystem—  
and an ecosystem taught, over generations, to keep shift time.

It has habits. It has memory. It has taboos that are really survival algorithms spoken aloud:
make noise at crossings, don’t cut blind vines, leave the old roots, respect warm zones, keep the pumps alive.

Its temperament is humid, practical, and alert.  
Its language is vibration, smell, texture, and routine.  
Its body is patched steel under bark, brick under moss, tendon-vines around rebar.  
Its metabolism is circulation: water, freight, spores, heat, rumor, predators, maintenance crews.

What lives there, in the end, is **co-dependence made permanent**—a borough-sized being with many throats and no single face, endlessly repairing the terms of its own existence.
