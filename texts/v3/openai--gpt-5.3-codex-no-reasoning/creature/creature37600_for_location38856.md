What lives there is not one creature but a **way of being alive**—a civilization of wet breath, soft ground, and continuous appetite.

If I had to name its dominant resident, I’d call it a **matriarchal marsh-jungle intelligence**: not a single mind, but a distributed one made of roots, spores, larvae, and lungs. Every trunk buttress, every floating root-mat, every film on the tea-dark pools is part of it. It “thinks” by circulation: water paths remembered by roots, nutrient pulses timed by decay, insect emergences answered by frogs, frogs answered by snakes, snakes answered by birds, and all of it folded back into mud before anything can become separate for long.

### Its body
- **Skin:** leaf-layers, moss curtains, condensation films.
- **Bones:** almost none—just broad, half-drowned stones and the temporary scaffolding of roots.
- **Blood:** warm tannin water moving slowly through channels too shallow to command attention.
- **Breath:** humidity itself, exhaled by millions of surfaces.
- **Memory:** stored in living architecture—where roots bind banks, where mats thicken, where shade sustains hatchings.

### Its temperament
It is **crowded but not frantic**, loud but never dramatic.  
It does not do events; it does persistence.  
Predation happens, birth happens, rot happens, bloom happens—simultaneously, continually, without ceremony.

There are larger animals, yes—heavy, reed-parting, mud-sighing presences—but even they feel like temporary gestures made by the greater resident. They pass through; the saturation remains.

### Its face, if seen directly
You’d see:
- frog eggs and fungus sharing the same fallen branch,
- fish dimpling black surfaces under suspended insect swarms,
- lizards threading root lattices beside crabs nosing silt,
- birds flashing a meter at a time through green dusk,
- blossoms like sudden signal-flares, then gone back into leaf-shadow.

Nothing rules alone. Dominance belongs to **overlap**.

### Its strange gift
Most newborn landscapes are raw, sparse, unstable.  
This one is newborn and already **full**—as if succession skipped ahead and then locked into a humid equilibrium. It lives in a permanent present tense: no clear season of scarcity, no clean pause, no silence long enough for emptiness to form.

So what lives there?

A billion specific organisms, certainly.  
But more truly: a **self-maintaining abundance**—a young world that learned, immediately, how to stay whole by never leaving any space unused.
