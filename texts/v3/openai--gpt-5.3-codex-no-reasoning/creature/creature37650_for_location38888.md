What lives here is not a species so much as a **custodianship**.

At first you might look for a people in the obvious sense—houses, tools, smoke, refuse—but this place would hide them from that kind of expectation. Its inhabitants are likely **garden-engineers**, descendants of those who learned to direct growth so completely that their works no longer read as works. They do not build *on* the land; they edit metabolism. They are less masons than conductors.

They’d be physically and culturally adapted to moisture, patience, and height: sure-footed on springy rootways, intimate with dangerous-looking but load-bearing mats, able to read subtle signs in sheen, sap pressure, leaf angle, and drip cadence the way sailors read wind. Their craft vocabulary would be hydraulic and botanical rather than mechanical—terms for seep rates, shade phases, tensile vine ages, fungal binding stages, seasonal pore-opening of trunk-cisterns.

You can imagine them as:

- **Keepers of channels** who “tune” water by trimming lips, opening membranes, and clearing breathing pores in woven embankments.
- **Binders** who braid juvenile roots into future roads and plazas that won’t fully harden for years.
- **Canopy stewards** who maintain spacing between masses so wind can speak through the city and mold cannot.
- **Pollination wardens** who synchronize blooms across districts to keep structural species reproducing in sequence.
- **Decay-harvesters** who intercept rot and reweave it before collapse begins.

Their settlements might be visually quiet—platform nests in pillar-groves, hammock chambers under folded roofs, storage bladders grown into walls. Little would be permanent at the household scale because permanence already exists at the civic-biological scale. Personal property would likely be light, fiber-based, water-safe, and perishable by design.

And alongside them live a few tolerated animals, never many: amphibians as indicators of water health, long-legged grazers that keep comb-grass in trim bands, fleeting canopy fliers that move pollen or spores between far districts. Fauna here are less dominant citizens than **signal species** inside a plant-first republic.

Most of all, what lives here is a **continuity of practice**. The true resident is tradition embodied in hands, songs, timings, and prohibitions:

- never strip a surface bare,
- never force water straight,
- never let masses close too tightly,
- never introduce a form that cannot be metabolized.

So if you ask “who lives there,” the deepest answer is:

A people who made a treaty with growth so old it became infrastructure.  
A society whose city is alive enough to heal, and whose citizens survive by helping it keep its long, wet balance.
