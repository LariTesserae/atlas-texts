What lives there is not a species.

It is a **civic metabolism**—a distributed resident made of pressure, timing, and appetite, wearing leaves and wood the way an animal wears skin.

At first you might call it flora, then ecosystem, then weather, then architecture; all are true and all are insufficient. The inhabitant is the *coordination* itself: millions of rooted bodies behaving like neighborhoods of one unfinished mind. Not conscious in the way eyes and nerves define consciousness elsewhere, but unmistakably selective. It remembers gradients. It anticipates dryness. It exploits rhythm. It edits itself continuously.

If it has a face, it is everywhere surfaces meet flow.

- In corridors that narrow exactly where wind accelerates.  
- In plazas that open like throats during humid pulses and constrict when the air thins.  
- In reed-fields tuned to resonate not as accident but as force—sound used as stress test, warning, perhaps even synchronization cue.

Its “organs” are districts. Thorn wards are pressure batteries. Mist wards are lungs. Translucent-leaf stacks are light refineries. Fungal composites are digesters and archivists, taking yesterday’s collapse and translating it into tomorrow’s scaffolding. The central crater-pool is likely not a heart but a **timing chamber**: a place where delayed reflections and mismatched weather reveal that regulation here is partly optical, partly hydraulic, partly temporal. A control room without controllers.

It reproduces not by making offspring elsewhere, but by **recurring itself** block by block: function copied, form varied. Every new terrace is both expansion and rehearsal. Every grafted seed is a vote for one microclimate over another. Every collapse is rezoning.

And because no animals interrupt the loops—no grazing, no burrowing, no pollinator intermediaries—its transactions are direct, infrastructural, almost legalistic. Exchange is done by bridge, burst, tether, impact. Desire is geometric.

So whoever lives there is:

- A city-scale plant-process that has outgrown the distinction between organism and environment.  
- A chorus of rooted engineers with no individuals at the level that matters most.  
- A thing that experiences “self” as circulation patterns, not boundaries.  
- A resident made of renovation, never finished enough to die in one piece.

If you insisted on giving it a singular name, it might be something like **the Verdant Municipality** or **the Root-Commonwealth**. But names imply edges, and this inhabitant has none that hold for long.

It lives as a verb:  
**wicking, grafting, venting, thickening, listening through tension, building through hunger.**

And if you stay long enough, it begins to live *you* too—coating your boots in rooting filaments, reading your heat, catching your breath moisture on nearby membranes, bending a frond to throw shade over your pause point, hardening a path where you repeatedly step. Not predation, not welcome—incorporation.

In a world with no citizens, this is the citizenry:  
growth itself, densely organized, loud as weather, patient as rot, busy as traffic, old for minutes at a time, and perpetually, undeniably home.
