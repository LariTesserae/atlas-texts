What lives there is not a single beast but a **civilization of appetites**—ancient, decentralized, and tireless.

If you insist on one emblem creature, it would be something like a **termite-keeper**: not an individual species name, but a role embodied many times over. Blind, pale workers in their millions, mandibled engineers of heat and gas exchange, raising mud towers that breathe better than lungs. Around them, ant nations draft roads into the soil crust; beetle guilds clock in at carrion; fungi lace the underworld in white arithmetic. The “inhabitant” is this total machine of life, where each body is a component and no component understands the whole.

Among vertebrates, the residents are practical rather than spectacular:  
- low-slung grazers and rooting omnivores that open paths by repetition,  
- small cats and snakes that read those paths like ledgers,  
- long-legged pool-waders that arrive with seasonal water and vanish when it shrinks,  
- monitor-bodied opportunists that eat almost anything not defended by speed or numbers.  

But even these are secondary to the true majority: **arthropods and microbes**. They are the labor force, transport network, demolition crew, sanitation system, and reproductive engine. They turn leaf to soil, corpse to swarm, dusk to airborne grain.

So who lives there?

- **Tunnelers** that breathe the ground.  
- **Weavers** that knot shrub to tree to reed into continuous architecture.  
- **Harvesters** that never stop counting calories.  
- **Ambushers** that trust edges, shadow, and timing.  
- **Processors**—fungal, bacterial, larval—who inherit everything that falls.  

Temperament of the inhabitants: efficient, heat-wise, conservative with motion, explosive with response. Most of them do not waste sound. Most of them do not waste light. Nearly all of them live by schedule—rain schedule, dusk schedule, hatch schedule, rot schedule.

In story terms, the thing that lives there is a **many-bodied intelligence of rhythm**.  
Not a king. Not a monster.  
A plain-wide metabolism wearing a billion small forms.
