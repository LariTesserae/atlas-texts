What lives there is not one creature but a **pressure system of life**—a civilization of mouths, roots, wings, and listening.

If I had to name the primary resident of that high country, I’d call it **the Chorus-Flood**: a biome that behaves like an animal.

It breathes by mist and condensate.  
It feeds by movement.  
It remembers by paths worn into vine and stone.  

Everything else inside it—grazers, climbers, burrowers, hunters, scavengers, people—is a specialized organ in that larger body.

---

The most characteristic “inhabitant” might be something like a **six-limbed escarpment runner**: part goat, part gecko, part antelope in behavior rather than appearance. Not huge, but innumerable. It moves in flowing files along near-vertical plant scaffolds, using paired forelimbs for grip and the rear set for springing between mats. Its hide is matte and dust-pale on top, moss-dark below, so from a distance a whole herd looks like shadow passing over stone. It doesn’t own territory in fixed blocks; it owns **timing windows**—dawn ledges, post-rain gullies, noon overhang shade.

Its predators are fast, social, and acoustically clever: **vine-cut packers** that hunt by steering panic into bottlenecks rather than pure pursuit. They read echo the way sailors read wind. One barks high, another low, and the herd turns exactly where the hunters need it to.

Above them live the cliff colonies—dense nations of nesting fliers whose wingbeats are part weather, part thunder. Below, in the softer saddles, burrow swarms pulse out at dusk in synchronized emergence so tight it resembles a single organism surfacing.

And everywhere between: insects in impossible variety, tuned to transient water. Some hatch in a single afternoon pool, mate by evening, and leave eggs sealed in mud for months. Some sing only when hidden seep-lines are near, so their calls are maps. Some farm fungus in peat pockets beneath root mats, making the ground itself a layered pantry for anything that knows where to dig.

---

Humans live there too, but as a **minor, highly adaptive species** among majors. The successful person in this place is less conqueror than conductor: someone who can read calls, wind, damp, and migration intervals with minute precision. Their villages are almost camouflage—woven into living growth, quiet when predators move, noisy only when noise is safe. Their greatest technology is **protocol**: when to pass, when to wait, when to leave offerings for scavengers, when not to be noticed at all.

So what lives there?

- Old stone, as foundation memory.  
- Plant architecture, as skin and scaffolding.  
- Animal multitudes, as metabolism.  
- Human tact, as a small but intelligent rhythm within the larger beat.  

In short: **a finished mountain inhabited by unfinished life**, endlessly revising itself in sound and motion.
