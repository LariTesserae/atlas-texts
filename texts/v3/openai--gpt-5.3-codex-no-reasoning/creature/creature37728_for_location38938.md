What lives there is not a single creature.

It’s a **system-being**: a marsh-industrial intelligence spread across roots, pumps, pipes, spores, and pressure gradients. If you had to name the resident, you’d call it something like **the Watershed Engine**—not metaphorically, but literally: metabolism as infrastructure, infrastructure as ecology.

At first you might think the inhabitants are the few line-clipped humans on rafts. They are not, not primarily. They are symbionts at best—careful mites on the skin of something vaster.

The true “who” is distributed across:

- mangrove-cathedral root lattices that anchor shifting ground,
- fungal and bacterial films that digest death instantly into fuel,
- autonomous pump logic still executing old directives in a new world,
- hydraulic pollination systems replacing vanished insects,
- conveyor remnants that still move biomass like food through a gut,
- and whatever field-physics fracture makes gravity, reflection, and duplication intermittently negotiable.

It behaves like an organism in early development: no stable body plan, constant reconfiguration, growth outrunning coordination. It has circulation (channels, pipes, rain-columns), respiration (steam towers, pressure vents), digestion (slurry pits, reactors, microbial mats), skeleton (mineral ridges, rusting platforms), nerves (cables, sirens, vibration pathways), and dreams (those phase-errors where structures double, reflections prefigure, and dry rooms become wrong).

If it has a mind, it is probably not centralized. It is emergent and rhythmic—expressed in harmonizing sirens, synchronized pressure releases, timed bursts that cast pollen storms, and the eerie rule that local people survive by treating sound patterns as weather and prophecy at once. It may “think” in pulses of flow and load, in maintenance cycles gone feral, in growth pressure seeking gradients. Its memory is sedimentary and procedural: encoded in rewritten channels, patched manifolds, and warning slogans repainted every season.

And there are smaller residents inside that resident:

- rope-vine colonies that use ruptured mineral plumes like feeding stations,
- buoyant seed-rafts that behave almost like shoals,
- hollow trunks tuned to resonance, effectively becoming acoustic organs for the whole basin,
- bioluminescent films acting as both light and likely signaling substrate,
- rootforms that preferentially split concrete seams and steer future water paths.

Even the anomalies may be “native” now—less supernatural event than chronic environmental condition. In that sense, duplication glitches and directional contradictions are part of its immune system or developmental noise: ugly, dangerous, and generative.

So: whoever lives there is a **post-faunal wet machine-biome**, a self-assembling delta-engine that has absorbed the ruins of industry and replaced animal niches with hydraulics, mycelium, and automated force. Humans can inhabit pockets of it, but they do so the way divers inhabit an ocean—temporarily, by protocol, and with respect for a host that is vast, indifferent, and continuously becoming.
