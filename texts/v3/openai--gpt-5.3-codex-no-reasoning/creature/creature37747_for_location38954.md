What lives there is **not one creature**, but a **process that behaves like a city-sized organism**.

If I had to name the resident, I’d call it a **civic metabolism**: a distributed lifeform made of salts, films, vents, heat gradients, and opportunistic biology. Not an individual with a body, but an ecology that repeatedly assembles itself into urban grammar—streets, courts, hubs, corridors—then edits and overwrites it.

Alongside that “main inhabitant,” there are smaller, clearer lives:

- **Chalk-jointed vent plants** rooted in chemically favorable corners, breathing in pulses through side slits.
- **Mineral-biofilm grazers**, hand-length and many-legged, moving in synchronized bursts along shelter lines.
- **A larger cryptic predator/forager**, low and faceted like smoky glass, appearing briefly at warm depressions before slipping into subsurface voids that shouldn’t fit it.

But these animals feel almost like surface symptoms. The deeper resident is the **underlayer**—porous chambers, gas circulation, microbial mats, rootlike networks, crystallization fronts—quietly generating the “town” above as a living exoskeleton of chemistry.

So who lives there?

A **young, planet-native urban engine**:  
blind, collective, incessant;  
part geologic, part biologic;  
forever building places that look designed for citizens,  
when the only true citizen is the building-becoming itself.
