What lives there is not one creature but a **composite resident**: a plateau-wide organism made of hoof, wing, rust, pipe, spore, and timing.

If you had to name its primary inhabitant, it might be something like a **ridge-runner guild**—animals and systems that survive by reading pulses: rain pulses, machine pulses, fog pulses, heat pulses. Nothing in that basin lives continuously in abundance; everything lives in **intervals**. So the true “who” of the place is a network of specialists that wake, move, feed, and hide on a clock written jointly by weather and infrastructure.

At ground level, the most characteristic life is probably a lean, dust-colored **ungulate**—small herds that drift between mineral licks, runoff lines, and old service roads. They are followed (or anticipated) by dusk predators that use the same engineered corridors. Around them, quicker citizens do the real turnover: carrion beetles that can materialize in minutes, burrowers that aerate scree edges, snakes that “hear” through vibration in buried metal and concrete. In wet bursts, dormant frogs erupt from mud capsules like seeds germinating after fire. In dry stretches, microbial films lie apparently dead, then green-black and active after a single fog-heavy dawn.

Above them all, the aerial residents stitch the basin together: scavenger kites circling thermal columns off black walls; swifts nesting in vent shafts and pump-house throats, turning abandoned industry into cliff habitat. Reptiles claim exhaust warmth; succulents claim condenser drip; moth larvae claim condensation mesh. Even lichens and mosses claim oxidation, making a skin over relic steel.

So what lives there?

- **Pulse-adapted animals** (migratory, opportunistic, heat-smart)  
- **Water-harvesting plants** rooted in fracture, shadow, and steel  
- **Microbial crusts and mineral-wet communities** that appear and vanish with moisture  
- **Half-domesticated machine ecologies**—still-running systems that accidentally irrigate, warm, channel, and shelter  
- **Human crews**, transient but influential, maintaining boundaries rather than dominating the whole

In a more poetic sense, the resident is a **truce intelligence**: life that has learned not to erase industry, and industry old enough to behave like terrain. The basin is inhabited by things that don’t seek permanence in individuals, only in pattern. Each species is partial. The full inhabitant is the pattern itself.
