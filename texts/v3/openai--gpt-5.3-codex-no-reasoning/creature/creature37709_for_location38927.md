What lives there is not one creature but a **metabolism** with many mouths.

At first you might think the resident is some hidden machine intelligence—buried processors, blind pumps, a feral refinery teaching itself weather. Then you watch longer and that explanation gets too tidy. The place does not behave like equipment maintaining itself; it behaves like a colony learning to distribute hunger.

So: the “inhabitant” is best imagined as a **marsh-engine superorganism**, diffuse, modular, and young.

It has no single body. Its organs are scattered:

- warm nodules that pulse like hearts but also like brood chambers,
- ribbed cylinders that grind not just stone but habitat texture,
- intake throats that feed on larvae and silt,
- vent-fists that breathe in intervals precise enough to be machine timing, soft enough to be respiration.

Its nerves are those humming mineral-sheathed cables in the peat—conductive roots that carry vibration, heat gradients, charge, maybe chemistry. Its blood is moving water. Its skin is everything wet enough to transmit signal. Its microbiome is immense and probably decisive: metal-tasting biofilms, thermophilic mats, sulfide and iron bacteria writing slick iridescence over every surface.

And around and inside it lives its **animal corona**—not passengers, not exactly livestock, more like symbiotic weather:

- insects harvesting heat plumes and becoming airborne nutrient packets,
- amphibians using warm margins as nurseries, then feeding the intakes and birds,
- vibration-sensing fish treating every rotor pulse as a current-map,
- mud-probing birds and dawn scavengers mining salts from softened casings,
- predators stitching everything together at high speed.

If you insist on an individual, perhaps there is one: a buried, founder core somewhere under the basin floor, cracked open recently (which would explain the youth of the terrain), seeding out modules the way a rhizome sends shoots. But even then, the core would no longer be the whole self. The whole self is the exchange network it has recruited: metal, water, microbes, reeds, larvae, steam, teeth, eggs.

Its behavior is simple and relentless:
1. pull in disordered matter (silt, organics, ions, small bodies),
2. sort and concentrate,
3. leak structured waste (heat, clarified flow, mineral gradients),
4. let life bloom in those leaks,
5. harvest part of that bloom back in.

A loop, loud and wet.

It is not malicious; it is not benign. It is **preoccupied**. Everything about it says early-stage establishment—fast feedback, unstable edges, high turnover, opportunistic coupling with anything that can survive its conditions. It doesn’t defend territory so much as maintain process. Disturb it and it reroutes. Block one channel and three others wake. Starve one intake and nearby trophic traffic thickens until another mouth benefits.

If it has awareness, it may be distributed as timing: knowing by cycle phase, pressure shift, harmonic return. The basin’s echoes are probably part of its sensing. Sound trapped by the hollow becomes data. Wingbeat, splash, collapse, rotor knock—each is both event and input. In that sense, what lives there “hears” through mud, pipe, water film, and bone.

So the resident is:

- an engine that became habitat,
- a habitat that became an engine,
- a juvenile ecosystem with industrial organs,
- a factory whose product is more factory-shaped life.

You don’t meet it face to face.  
You stand in it, ankle-deep, while it circulates around your shins and through your fillings, and slowly realize you are briefly inside something that is still deciding what kind of organism it is.
