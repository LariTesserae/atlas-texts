What lives here is **a people who became a hydraulic species**.

Not metaphorically—practically. Culturally. Anatomically, maybe, in small accumulated ways.  
They are not cave-dwellers and not city-dwellers; they are **flow-dwellers**: inhabitants of gradients, pressure differences, and maintenance cycles.

---

At first glance, you might think the “resident” is a class of laborers. But the deeper answer is that the true organism is the **complex itself**, and the humans are its mobile organs.

Still, there are bodies in it. They tend to be:

- compact and tendon-heavy from capstans, ladders, treadwheels, and wet hauling,
- wrapped in layered treated cloth and waxed aprons, less for warmth than for damp management,
- marked by trade through the hands: burn-callus, rope-groove, lime scars, pitch stains that never fully leave.

Their hearing is extraordinary in the way blacksmiths’ hearing is extraordinary: not “better,” but more *interpretable*. They can tell by resonance when a duct is fouling three terraces away. A skipped clack in a chain run means one cracked link. A drum pattern gone half a beat slow means headwater lag, not messenger error.

They smell faintly of smoke, leather sealant, clay dust, and cold mineral water. Even off shift.

---

### Socially, what lives there is a maintenance civilization.

Their clans/houses/guild-lines are probably not named for blood but for function:

- Wheelwardens  
- Seal-burners  
- Silt-Readers  
- Drumline Keepers  
- Roofwrights  
- Settling-Priests (whether sacred or just bureaucratic)

Status does not come from ornament; it comes from **continuity**.  
Who kept flow through the freeze week.  
Who prevented backflood when the north retaining face slumped.  
Who rebuilt a bearing tower without stopping second-rank water.

Children grow up learning sequence before story: open, brace, bleed, latch, verify, drum.  
Their lullabies are cadence chants. Their myths are failure case histories starring ancestors who “listened wrong” or “trusted green timber.”

---

### Psychologically, they are people for whom “nature” is suspect.

Not hated—untrusted.  
Raw wood twists. Untamed water lies. Open sky introduces variance.  
So they do not oppose weather; they **proceduralize** it.

Rain is inventory. Mist is capture opportunity. Drip rates are forecasts.

A person from here likely feels exposed in a meadow, the way a sailor might feel uneasy inland: no rails, no known load paths, no audible system heartbeat. Silence is terror, because silence means stalled mechanism, and stalled mechanism means cascading loss.

---

### Religion/philosophy—if present—would center on managed motion.

Their sacred image is probably not a god-face but a diagram: basin above basin above basin, right fall, right delay, right return.  
Sin is negligence.  
Grace is redundancy.

Burial may be impossible in ordinary soil (there is none), so the dead might be:

- immured into embankments (literally becoming retaining mass),
- ash-sealed into kiln brick,
- commemorated by named gates and measured lines rather than statues.

To “join the wall” could be both idiom and fact.

---

### Nonhuman life that lives there

Very little can, but what does is weirdly specialized:

- blind crustacean scavengers in slow sediment tanks,
- heat-condensation fungi that bloom overnight on vent lips,
- smoke-skimming flyers that evolved around flame and rigging.

These are treated as fouling factors first, fauna second.  
Yet some crews quietly track them as indicators: if the shell-things vanish early, pump chemistry has shifted; if fungi stripe blue-gray, duct heat is dropping.

So even tolerated pests become diagnostic instruments.

---

### In one line:

**What lives there is a population that has replaced ecology with procedure, and then grown a culture, a body language, and a moral system inside that replacement.**  
They are the caretakers of an unfinished artificial watershed—and, by now, its native lifeform.
