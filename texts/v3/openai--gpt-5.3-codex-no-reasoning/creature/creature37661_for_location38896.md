What lives there is a **mountain people of maintenance**—and everything that chose to evolve alongside them.

Not conquerors, not pristine ecologists, not pure machinists.  
They are **keepers of continuity** in a place where gravity, rust, roots, and plumbing are all political.

They’re the kind of civilization that would have dozens of words for *drip rates* and *load paths*. Children learn to read with signage stencils and moss patterns both. Adults can glance at a stain down a wall and tell you which cistern is overfilling three levels up. Their elders remember eras by retrofit style: “before the ceramic relining,” “after the twin-lift ordinance,” “during the vine quarantine years.”

---

The dominant “inhabitant,” if you zoom out, is actually a **metabolism**:

- crews patching spalls at dawn,
- goats trimming rooftop firebreaks by afternoon,
- market lifts trading algae sheets for machine oil and seedstock,
- night-shift pollinators tending shaft-orchards with hand lamps,
- bat colonies doing pest control better than any grid ever could.

So yes, people live there—but also procedures, species, and infrastructures that have become inseparable from culture. A pump has a caretaker lineage. A stair-ravine has a shrine at one landing and a tool cache at the next. A root-mat is treated like civic fabric.

---

If you asked, “Who are they?” I’d describe them as:

## 1) The Vertical Guilds
Not aristocrats of blood, but of responsibility.

- **Hydrologists / Weirwrights** who tune flow between stormburst and drought-hour.
- **Scaffold masons** who can tell which “temporary” steel has become structural ancestry.
- **Root trainers** who braid banyan and conduit instead of forcing either to yield.
- **Lift shepherds** who keep cargo and people moving through altitude climates.
- **Aerary keepers** tending wind-harps, vent crowns, and upper-belt weather stations.

Their status comes from reliability across decades. Failure isn’t shame—it’s investigation, adaptation, documentation.

## 2) The Seam-Folk
Residents of in-between spaces: catwalk courts, stair-shadow markets, cistern alcoves.

They are nimble social connectors—vendors, repair brokers, gossip-runners, roost tenders, animal handlers. They know shortcut ecologies (“swifts are nesting there, take the warm duct bypass”). They can route you by smell, humidity, and fan tone as much as by map.

## 3) The Belt Communities
Altitude makes culture.

- **Lower belts**: denser, louder, wetter-air; machine adjacency, big markets, amphibian wetlands.
- **Middle belts**: orchard shafts, family terraces, schools in old turbine galleries.
- **Upper belts**: wind-cut, bright, sparse canopies, long sightlines, antenna monasteries, cloud catchers.

Migration between belts is common but meaningful—like changing biomes and professions at once.

---

And nonhuman life isn’t backdrop; it is citizen-adjacent.

- **Swifts, kites, bats** are treated as air-traffic partners.
- **Goats** are municipal coworkers with ownership tags and route rights.
- **Lizards and insects** are micro-indicators—if they move, systems are shifting.
- **Fish, eels, amphibians** are living water-quality sensors as much as food chains.
- **Vines, mosses, grasses, orchard trees** are engineered companions, scheduled and pruned like transit.

In this place, “pest” is a temporary category pending better design.

---

Temperament-wise, whoever lives there is practical, ceremonial, and patient.

Practical: because everything can fail.  
Ceremonial: because maintenance repeated becomes ritual.  
Patient: because true change is measured in patina bands and root thickness.

They celebrate first rains not for romance, but for successful capture rates.  
They hold festivals when a century-old lift spine gets sleeved without shutdown.  
They mourn collapses by naming replacement beams after the lost section, not by pretending damage never happened.

---

So what lives there?

A **stratified civic ecosystem**: humans, animals, plants, machines, and waterworks bound into one long-lived agreement. A people who treat redundancy as grace, aging as data, and repair as identity.

In another world they might be called industrial.  
In this world they are simply **native to the built mountain**.
