What lives there is **a custodian without flesh**.

Not a single creature in the usual sense—no fur, no feather, no root—but a resident intelligence distributed through stone geometry, water timing, and light alignment. If you stayed long enough, you’d stop expecting footsteps and start recognizing routines. Dawn striking angled faces. Condensation gathering exactly where grooves anticipate it. Shadows reaching the cistern like clock hands. The basin behaves like something that wakes, works, and rests.

It is old, patient, and practical.

It “eats” only episodes: mist, needle-rain, thaw.  
It “breathes” through freeze-thaw pulses in cracks and channels.  
It “remembers” in polish, patchwork, and the reuse of broken parts.  
It “speaks” in flute-wind through slots, drip-rhythm in basins, the hiss of released rime.

Whoever once built the place may be long gone, but their habits survive as a kind of mineral afterlife. So the thing living there now is a **post-human liturgy of maintenance**: cairns that still guide flow, stairs that still direct movement, marker stones that still keep appointments with the sun. A machine-temple reduced to essentials and still performing its cycle with no audience.

If you wanted to give it a name, it might be:

- **The Basin-Keeper**
- **The Stone Clock**
- **The Thirst Architect**
- **The Last Hermit (made of weather, not bone)**

Its temperament is austere but not hostile. It doesn’t hunt, defend, or invite. It simply continues. In a world without biological life, continuity itself becomes the resident. The “inhabitant” is process shaped into place—an ancient collaboration between vanished hands and ongoing physics.

So: what lives there is **endurance with intent**.
