What lives there is less a **species** than a **regime of life**: a patient, vegetal intelligence spread across ridges, shelves, and suspended ceilings.

If you meet a “resident” of this drowned range, it is likely first to be a plant that behaves like weather.

---

The dominant inhabitants are the **architect-plants**—the ribbon forests, bulb-trees, furred meadows, and tendril curtains. They are not passive scenery; they are the makers of rooms, currents, and seasons. They rise, anchor, trap, filter, sedate, shade, digest. They occupy niches that in other worlds would be split among coral, kelp, fungi, grazers, scavengers, even ambush hunters. Here, vegetation inherited the whole economy and elaborated it.

You can imagine one representative lineage—call them **vaultweavers**—descended from buoyant vines and gas-bulb flora. A vaultweaver begins as a drifting seed-thread, catches in a stone crack, inflates a translucent reservoir, and lowers root-fans like hands feeling for purchase. Once secured, it sends up broad light-blades and down veils of filament. Those veils are alive in gradients: upper strands photosynthesize, middle strands trap suspended food, lower strands host dense microbiomes that pre-digest what settles. Nothing is wasted; everything is processed slowly into growth.

Over years, neighboring vaultweavers braid into living walls. They turn exposed slopes into baffled corridors, soften wave shear, and create calm pockets where delicate species can establish. In effect, they terraform water from within.

---

There are animals, but they are sparse enough to feel like apparitions.

The most characteristic large one is the **glass grazer**: long-bodied, nearly invisible, with a chain of opaline organs along its spine. It moves with deliberate economy, threading through canopy lanes to rasp biofilm and soft tissues from mature fronds. It is not a destroyer—more a pruner. Its scarcity keeps each encounter solemn: one body crossing an acre of motionless growth like a thought crossing a cathedral.

Above the ridges, you sometimes glimpse **kite swimmers**, dark and angular, circling in pairs. They may be omnivorous cruisers feeding on larvae clouds and narcotized drifters at dusk. They do little tearing; this is not a place of pursuit and panic. Even the mobile fauna seem adapted to low-conflict abundance and high structural complexity: glide, sample, vanish.

The true animal majority is tiny and distributed—filtering specks, larvae, symbionts embedded in mats and threads. Much of “fauna” here is inseparable from plant surfaces, living as films, colonies, and internal guests.

---

If there is a single creature that “lives there,” it is the **living watershed itself**:  
a vertical forest system where growth climbs toward light from below and hangs toward depth from above, meeting in a narrow middle stratum like clasped hands.

Its behaviors are ecological rather than individual:

- it exhales bubble ceilings by day;  
- intoxicates nearby water at dusk to reclaim nutrients;  
- reroutes flow through drapery and root-mass;  
- colonizes fresh fractures in mineral bloom;  
- sheds old canopies downslope to found new provinces.

So the inhabitant is: **slow, rooted, opportunistic, and cumulative**.  
Not hunter-shaped, but structure-shaped.  
Not loud, but total.

In another biome you would ask, “What animal rules this place?”  
Here the answer is: **the flora became the megafauna**.  
Everything else lives in its wake.
