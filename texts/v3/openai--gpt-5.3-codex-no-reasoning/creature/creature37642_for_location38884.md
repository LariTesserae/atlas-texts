What lives there is not a single creature but a **civilization of habits**—a fauna shaped less by speed or novelty than by **ritual movement through ancient infrastructure**.

At first you might expect leviathans, and yes, there are giants. But the true resident is a *kind of behavior*: things that **commute**, **graze**, **filter**, **tend**, and **return** along routes older than species names.

If I had to name the dominant inhabitant, I’d call it the **Chord-keepers**.

They are vast, laterally flattened swimmers, dark as wet bark, with ribbed bodies plated in matte keratin-mineral scutes that host whole gardens of lichen and feeding worms. They do not hunt much. They patrol current corridors and vertical faces, moving in slow file, and every few hours they emit the subsonic pulses that seam the entire region together. Those pulses are not song for beauty—they are **traffic law** and **weather report** and **right-of-way claim**. Smaller species time migrations to them. Predators avoid certain harmonics. Spawning colonies open and close to pulse cadence. In a world where sight is poor, the Chord-keepers are moving infrastructure.

Yet they are not “in charge.” Nothing is. The place is too old for monarchy.

Around them lives a layered society of specialists:

- **Wall-raspers**: plated herd-grazers with shovel mouths that skim biofilm from root buttresses without piercing living tissue. Their mouths are tuned to remove only the outer senescent layers—accidental gardeners maintaining flow surfaces.
- **Reed-clickers**: blind, articulate crustaceans that inhabit hollow cane towers, farming bacterial mats and cleaning fungal choke points. Their mass clicking is one of the constant high textures in the soundscape.
- **Veil-jellies**: colonial bell forms tethered to vine-lines, drifting up and down rain-shaft margins. They filter mineral-rich runoff and release larvae in strict lunar-pressure cycles.
- **Ribbon adjudicators** (predators): long, flexible ambush hunters that patrol crossing water between “roads.” They rarely enter the major vertical highways; everyone seems to recognize those as neutral transit lanes.
- **Bulb-nursers**: small symbiotic fish and amphipods that live in the suspended chamber-neighborhoods, tending eggs of multiple species in exchange for mucus, waste heat, and protection.

And then there is the oldest resident of all: the **architecture itself**, still metabolizing. The root-vaults are alive at their margins, the frond-shingles still thicken by fractions, xylem aqueducts still pump. Animals don’t merely live *in* it—they live *with* it, keying their life cycles to its growth pulses, bloom centuries, and rot pockets.

So what lives there?

An ecosystem that behaves like a city after ten million years of zoning law.
A choir of migrants and custodians.
Creatures that trust shadow over light, roughness over smoothness, continuity over chance.
A people-without-personhood whose memory is stored in routes, echoes, and regrown walls.

In short: **the inhabitants are those who can hear structure, not just see space**.
