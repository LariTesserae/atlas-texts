What lives here is not an animal at all, but a **colony-being**: a slow intelligence distributed across roots, films, pipes, and heat gradients.

At first you’d call it just vegetation reclaiming an abandoned works. Then you notice the patterning is too consistent to be accidental.

It lives in patches, because this world only allows patchwork life. In one corner it is a mat of emerald moss drinking from sweating insulation; in another it is pale reed-clusters threaded into basalt cracks; elsewhere it is bioluminescent algae skimming the margins of warm pools. These are not separate species so much as separate organs. The vines are its tendons. The moss is its lung. The fungal films are its brief sensory skin. The algae are its night-vision.

It “moves” by succession.  
Not step to step—season to season, year to year. A pipe begins to condensate; spores gather there; a biofilm thickens; roots follow the wet line; mineral crusts trap more moisture; eventually a new green island appears where there was only rust. That is how it extends a limb.

It feeds on three currencies:

- **Water** in pulses, hoarded wherever geometry permits.  
- **Heat** vented from below, used as a metabolic subsidy.  
- **Trace metals and salts** leached from concrete, steel, and dust.

So the old infrastructure is not just habitat. It is diet, scaffold, and memory. The plant-body learns the layout by growing along it. Pipe routes become root routes. Cable trays become condensation combs. Grate shadows become leaf venation templates. Industry leaves a script; biology keeps writing in the same hand.

If it has behavior, it is this: **it invests in reliability**.  
Where a vent has steamed for years, growth is dense and layered. Where a lamp flickers predictably, phototropic fans orient and stay. Where moisture fails twice, it retreats and seals itself in wax and spore. It does not gamble on the basin. It audits it.

There may be a single dominant organism—or many that have converged into functionally one network. Either way, individuality has blurred. Reproduction is mostly cloning, budding, fragmenting, re-rooting. Genetic novelty arrives, but slowly, and is accepted only where microclimate permits. Evolution here is less a branching tree than a set of edits made to an engineering diagram.

Its timescale is infrastructural.  
In an hour, nothing.  
In a year, a valve disappears under stalks.  
In five, a plinth becomes a moss altar.  
In twenty, the basin is still recognizably industrial, but every line has been softened into living utility.

And if there is a “creature” in the singular, it is the basin itself as metabolized system:  
a warm, dim, semi-artificial wetland mind, built from leftovers, thinking in condensation cycles, remembering through root placement, and patiently converting maintenance architecture into anatomy.
