What lives there is something that stopped being a single species long ago.

At first you might call them **people**, because there are dwellings, corridors, permissions, memorials, schedules, laws. But if you keep looking, “people” dissolves into a wider organism: a **civic flora** and its stewards, or perhaps a flora that became its own stewards.

The inhabitants are likely a layered society of:

- **Enclosed sapients** (human-derived or otherwise) who live in pods, husk-doors, cambium rooms, shaded arterial interiors.
- **Phyto-mechanical bodies** that move in the open—pruners, pollinators, inspectors, couriers—some remotely inhabited, some autonomous, some legally personlike.
- **Root-intelligences** distributed through the filament substrate: not individuals in the animal sense, but jurisdictions, guild-minds, orchard councils, slow constitutional processes encoded in growth.
- **Contract species**—semi-wild engineered plants and remnant native flyers—protected, licensed, folded into ritual and utility alike.

They are adapted to dryness not by resisting it, but by **withdrawing wetness inward**. Their bodies—biological, mechanical, institutional—are sealed, docking, rationed, exchange-based. Contact is deliberate. Even reproduction seems civic: permitted bloom, synchronized release, negotiated gene-flow. Desire itself may be calendared against heat load and ion traffic.

If you met one up close, it might wear a photosynthetic mantle and carry tools that look like petals. Its face might be hidden behind translucent membranes that regulate humidity and identity in the same gesture. It might speak very softly, because loud sound is a kind of vandalism there. Or it might not “speak” at all in air—perhaps it presses a wrist-vein to a wall-vein, and the wall translates.

They likely think in multiple tempos. Fast for transit corridors, slow for grove law, glacial for continental redesign. A child might learn perspective correction the way we learn manners: legal coordinates before visual ones. “Morning” might be a zoning condition, not a time. “Neighborhood” might be defined by shared transpiration cycles.

Culturally, they seem like a civilization that made peace with maintenance. Not utopian, not wild, but **devotional about continuity**. They honor obsolete machines by grafting them into trunks. They leave nectar for the last flyers. They grow shade as public infrastructure with almost liturgical precision. Their tenderness is infrastructural.

So: who lives there?

A **dry-planet canopy polity**—part gardener, part regulator, part descendant, part tool.  
Citizens who may be vines in one district, cloaked walkers in another, and voting algorithms in the root-net below both.  
A people whose oldest ancestor is probably not an organism, but a decision:  
to survive aridity by turning the whole continent into a living factory,  
and then to stay long enough that the factory learned to be gentle.
