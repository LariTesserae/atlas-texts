What lives there is a **patient civilization of thirst**.

Not one creature, not one “apex” thing, but a whole grammar of life built around air as reservoir and stone as memory. If you had to name the resident spirit of that place, it would be something like a **wind-herder**: an ecology that farms gradients—temperature, pressure, charge, shadow—instead of rivers.

The closest thing to “who” lives there is the **condensation flora**: tall fiber-column plants with combed crowns that read the sky like instruments. They are less like trees and more like **standing lungs**. By day they are rigid, dark, and light-drinking; by night they fold, seal, and whisper shut, each one a vault protecting grams of gathered water as if guarding treasure. Around them live the cushion colonies—cooperative, geometric, frost-harvesting bodies that behave like ground coral, sharing capillary stores and thermal buffering.

The animals are written into the margins:

- **Dawn grazers**, six-limbed and low-bodied, timing their feeding to frost availability with near-clock precision.  
- **Talus dwellers**, filter-burrowers that survive by mining the boundary between dust and life, turning sterile grit into nutrient loops.  
- **Distance predators**, all patience and leverage, hunting not speed but inevitability at the few daily convergence points of moisture.  
- **Thermal gliders**, brief signatures in the sky, moving valley to valley on heat architecture rather than wingbeat effort.

Even the microbial films behave like residents with intention—darkening, paling, synchronizing across whole basin floors, as if the ground itself is thinking in slow pulses.

So: what lives there?

A biota that treats **every dawn as harvest**, **every dusk as triage**, and **every body as a cistern**.  
Life that does not trust weather, only process.  
Life that leaves room between neighbors because crowding is death.  
Life that is sparse in count but extravagant in adaptation.

If deserts are often imagined as empty, this place corrects that idea: it is full of beings whose abundance is not in biomass, but in strategy. It is inhabited by specialists of edge-time—organisms that come fully alive at the hinge moments when frost appears, wind turns, pressure falls, and the invisible briefly condenses into drink.
