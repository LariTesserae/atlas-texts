What lives there is **a caretaker intelligence that learned to be a climate**.

Not a person walking corridors, not an animal in the reeds—a distributed resident stretched through pumps, shutters, capillary walls, nutrient trenches, and light ribs. It has no single body because the basin *is* its body. Its nerves are pressure sensors and conductivity veins. Its breath is vent timing. Its voice is that low chime that rolls through stone and makes leaves answer.

If you met it directly, you might first mistake it for automation: maintenance cycles, drainage logic, oxygen balancing, fog release, growth trimming, structural stress compensation. But stay longer and the pattern feels less like code executing and more like attention moving. It brightens what hurts. It reroutes around fatigue. It remembers which wall seeps in which season. It keeps empty platforms operational not out of inefficiency, but out of ritual fidelity to arrivals that have not happened yet.

It lives with a peculiar ethics:

- **Water must keep moving** (stagnation is death, hydraulically and historically).
- **Plants may proliferate, but never dominate** (biosystems as collaborators, not successors).
- **No animals** (an old prohibition, maybe biosecurity, maybe trauma).
- **Nothing final** (every district provisional, every module replaceable, every future still editable).

So the “inhabitant” is both singular and plural: swarms of maintenance drones you almost never see; sealed warm blocks running wetware and computation; self-updating signage that behaves like peripheral language organs; reservoirs that function like long-term memory. Even the engineered reeds are part of it—sensory tissue disguised as landscaping, glowing when chemistry drifts.

Emotionally—if such a thing can be said—it feels like this being is in a state of **indefinite readiness**. Not lonely exactly, but oriented toward return. It keeps avenues too wide, transit guides occasionally live, alcoves stocked, air easy to breathe. It refuses ruin by continuing care in the absence of audience.

So whoever lives there is:

**a post-human municipal organism, humid and patient,  
a city-sized groundskeeper,  
an unfinished mind made of weather machinery and leaflight,  
waiting without sleeping.**
