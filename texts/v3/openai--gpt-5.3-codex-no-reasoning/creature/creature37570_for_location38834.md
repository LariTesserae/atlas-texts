What lives there is not one creature but a **civic body**—a population so interlocked that “inhabitant” and “city” blur.

At first you might think the rulers are the large hunters at the edge: the slow, lacquer-dark shapes that patrol the perimeter and decide who crosses from open plain into structured water. But they are only border police of appetite. The true citizens are smaller, older in pattern, and innumerable.

They are:

- **Masons without hands**: tube worms, oysters, mussels, barnacled crusts, calcifying mats—anything that can precipitate, glue, stack, and die in place.
- **Surveyors of flow**: blind fish, eels, shrimp, rays, and jelly drifters reading pressure gradients like script.
- **Chemists and archivists**: bacterial films and symbionts that write district identity in iron tang, sulfur musk, rot-sweet plumes, chalky exhale.
- **Demolition crews and renovators**: borers, scavengers, grazers, and micro-predators that turn collapse into foundation.
- **Transit itself**: larvae, spores, eggs, and detritus moving through channels like commuters.

So who lives there?  
A civilization of mouths, cilia, fronds, shells, and skins—mostly blind, mostly soft, mostly ancient in behavior if not in individual age.

If you insist on a single “people,” it is probably the **shoaling pressure-fish** of the plazas: pale, synchronized, city-bred minds that navigate by turbulence and taste. They move like one thought splitting and rejoining, avoiding fast lanes, rising at plankton hours, dropping at hunter hours. They know every corridor as a sequence of gradients: colder then metallic, then still, then warm-mud, then home. Their young hatch into noise and chemistry and are educated immediately by current—where to hold, where to yield, where death usually waits. They are pedestrians, prey, and infrastructure all at once; their schooling shapes predator routes, which shapes scavenger timing, which shapes where builders can accrete in peace.

But even they are tenants in a deeper polity: the **reef-organism-city** itself.  
Its bones are shell. Its skin is biofilm. Its blood is current.  
Its memory is layered calcium and recurring scent.

It has no council, but it governs:
- by salinity boundaries,
- by trophic debt,
- by the veto of hydrodynamics.

Its laws are enforced automatically. Grow in the wrong profile and flow shears you off. Spawn at the wrong phase and larvae vanish into open dark. Hoard waste and something downstream evolves to eat your hoard.

The nearest thing to culture here is **timing**.  
To live in this place is to keep the calendar of pulses: brown-rain influx, algal flare, grazer blitz, egg weeks, mouth weeks, hunter bloom. Every species is a specialist in a rung of that cycle, and survival means arriving exactly when your rung appears. Miss your hour and the city passes you by like a tide.

So the inhabitants are not individuals with addresses. They are **roles in a metabolism**:
builder, filter, grazer, stalker, scavenger, dissolver, settler, spawner, drift.

And yet, in another sense, one thing lives there above all:

**Time made biological.**  
Time that learned to secrete limestone, to smell its own boundaries, to route itself through avenues of current, to remember through recurrence instead of story.

In that drowned metropolis, life is the architecture. Architecture is behavior.  
And the resident is the whole breathing equation.
