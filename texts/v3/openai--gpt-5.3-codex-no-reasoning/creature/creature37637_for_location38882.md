What lives there is **a guild of keepers that behaves like a species**, and a species of weather that behaves like a mind.

Not crowds. Not cities in the usual sense.  
You would meet them one at a time, after long hours of hearing only dripstone and steam-throat echoes.

They are human—or near enough that you first read them as human—lean from treadwheel labor, skin lacquered in salt, wrapped in layered cloth and treated leather that can be unsealed and resealed as humidity swings. Their tools are simple and exacting: chalk reels, weighted cords, tuning forks, brass bowls, resin knives, wax tablets gone soft in the heat. Every keeper carries measures the way a herder carries feed: not as profession, as survival. A knot-cord at the belt. A plumb vial in a padded tube. A little slate of yesterday’s angles, already suspect.

They do not “build” this world so much as **continuously persuade it**.

Their work is maintenance, calibration, appeasement. They walk the kilometer walls and re-seat displaced blocks. They listen at shaft mouths for shear-hum. They open and close gates in choreographed teams timed to condensation pulses, not clocks. They repaint alignment marks that won’t hold still. They recite gradients like prayers. In another place they would be engineers; here, they are also clergy, because when channels decide to run uphill and distances edit themselves, correctness is half mathematics and half ritual confidence.

Over generations, they have speciated by district-function more than bloodline:

- **Gate-Walkers** with massive legs and careful hands, living by sluice complexes.
- **Fog Readers** who work the chimney halos, faces scarred by dry lightning flash.
- **Chordists** who string resonance lines through arcades and detect adjacency drift by pitch.
- **Cistern Keepers** who memorize basin behavior like temperaments—this reservoir broods, that one spits steam at dusk.
- **Reckoners** who do nothing but reconcile conflicting maps, carving and recarving coordinate stelae until stone turns to script.

Their settlements are sparse, tucked into lee sides of pressure towers or built within ringed maintenance courts: low vault dormitories, drying racks for leather seals, communal counting floors scored with geometries. No ornament except accumulated correction marks. Their children learn to walk on hot brick and to distrust straight lines that haven’t been checked twice that day.

They likely believe—correctly—that if they stopped, the world would not die; it would **misalign**.  
Basins would feed wrong mains, steam would overpressure, fog fronts would migrate, and whole districts would become inaccessible not by destruction but by topological sulk. The planet would continue, but no longer at human scale.

And alongside them, inseparable from them, lives the other inhabitant:

A distributed, non-biological quasi-life made of loops—condensation, runoff, heat storage, mineral bloom, albedo shift, pressure venting, geometric revision. It has no organ, no center, no DNA, yet it exhibits preference, memory, mood. It “learns” routes through repeated flow. It “forgets” alignments unless reasserted. It rewards some interventions and punishes others with sudden impossible gradients. The keepers do not worship it exactly, but they negotiate with it hourly.

So whoever lives there is this paired thing:

1. **The Measuring People**, solitary and disciplined, heirs to hand-laid megastructure.
2. **The Procedural Organism**, a world-machine in adolescent instability.

Each keeps the other in existence.  
Without the people, the loops go feral.  
Without the loops, the people have no world to keep.
