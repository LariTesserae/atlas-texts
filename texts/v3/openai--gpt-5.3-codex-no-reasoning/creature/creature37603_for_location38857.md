What lives here is not one creature but a **culture of precision**—an assemblage of bodies tuned to minutes, angles, and melt-lines.

If I had to name the primary inhabitant, I’d call them the **fog-keepers** (whether that is their own word or not): highland pastoral foragers who live by the clockwork of freeze and thaw. They are less “settled” than *anchored*—returning to this hanging basin in the productive season, rebuilding what winter fractures, then shifting vertically with weather, herds, and water timing.

### The people

They are compact, strong-legged, and wind-burned; layered in hide, woven grass, felted hair, and stitched scraps repeatedly repaired. Their wealth is portable and practical: cordage, needles, traps, drying racks, skin bags, knowledge of routes, and social ties that let them move between elevations.  

They don’t treat the basin as farmland to dominate, but as a **timed machine** to read.

- At dawn they harvest condensation and ground-fog with cloth and grass screens.
- Midmorning they rotate grazing through green patches before they are scalped.
- Noon is for exposed movement, scouting, and raptor-watch.
- Evening is processing: drying meat, scraping hides, mending, resetting traps before hard freeze.

Their shelters reflect the mountain’s refusal of permanence: low stone, flexible roofing, rebuildable walls, everything modular. Even ritual objects (the rattling hide-and-grass poles near springs) double as weather indicators and territory signals. Sacred and practical are not separate categories here.

### Their animals

Closest companions are likely **small caprines** (goat- or ibex-like stock, perhaps semi-managed rather than fully domestic), moved constantly among micro-pastures. These animals are engines that convert brief alpine productivity into milk, fiber, dung fuel, and transport of value on hoof.

Around them are wild analogs and competitors:
- cliff-running ungulate herds,
- burrowers in soft pockets,
- insect blooms synchronized to thaw pools,
- aerial predators using thermal schedules,
- and stealth carnivores in white coats patrolling boulder shade.

Everything is on a tight energetic budget. No species can afford to be slow in response.

### What defines life here

The real resident of this place is **timing intelligence**:

- knowing which stone sweats first,
- which slope keeps dew ten minutes longer,
- where fog pools on clear dawns,
- when larvae lift into winged clouds,
- when glare blinds hunter and hunted alike,
- when to move fifty meters and enter a different world.

So whoever lives there—human, hoofed, winged, or furred—shares one trait: they are **microclimate readers**.  
They survive not by owning abundance, but by arriving exactly when abundance briefly exists.
