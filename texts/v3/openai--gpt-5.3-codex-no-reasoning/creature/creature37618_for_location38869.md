What lives there is **a caretaker species that learned to disappear into process**.

Not gone—just diffused.

At first you expect obvious residents: villages, roads, smoke, voices. Instead you find a basin run by timing, gradients, and restraint. That usually means one of two things: abandonment, or mastery. This place is the second. So the “who” that lives here is likely a **small population of long-term hydrologists-ecologists-engineers** (whether human-descended or something adjacent), whose greatest achievement is that their presence no longer reads as occupation. It reads as climate.

They live in intervals rather than crowds.  
In service niches cut into lee slopes.  
In shaded cavities near maintenance spines.  
In temporary camps that leave almost no trace except a repaired seam, a recalibrated condenser, a changed mineral profile in next season’s runoff.

Their settlements are probably not central and visible because visibility would be wasteful in this ecology. Instead: **distributed habitations**, thermally buried, water-frugal, acoustically soft. They travel at dawn, dusk, and night, when glare is lower and condensation events are active. They follow infrastructure routes the way older pastoralists followed river margins—except their “herds” are moisture fronts, pollinator blooms, grazer migration windows, and soil recharge cycles.

Culturally, they would value:

- **Patience over throughput**
- **Repair over replacement**
- **Calibration over control**
- **Silence as a working medium**

Their tools are likely field-sensitive, low-noise, and modular; their aesthetics matte, mineral, anti-glare. They probably mark ownership weakly but stewardship strongly—hence glyph plates and sparse signage. They don’t dominate wildlife corridors; they negotiate with them. They don’t “irrigate” in the old sense; they **compose temporary water**.

So yes, many creatures live there—grazers, burrowers, raptors, insects in explosive wet microcycles.  
But the deeper answer is that the basin is inhabited by a **custodial intelligence** that chose to become background: a people (or lineage of systems acting on their behalf) whose daily life is to keep abundance patchy, mobile, and resilient in a dry high world.

In other words, what lives there is not a city, not a machine, not wilderness alone.

It is a **quiet covenant** between stone, weather, animals, and a sparse civilization mature enough to let the land keep center stage.
