What lives there is not one creature but a **listening civilization**—a whole ecology whose citizens include people, engineered plants, and animals that are half-wild, half-civic.

If you had to name the primary “inhabitant,” it would be this:

## The Resonant Keepers (human and near-human descendants)

They are lean, high-altitude people adapted to thin, dry air and mineral-heavy atmospheres. Their bodies waste almost no water; their skin tends toward matte dark tones to handle copper-dim light; their sinuses and inner ears are unusually developed, because in their world hearing is as vital as sight. They move quietly but make sound on purpose—taps, hums, coded rattles—because sound is infrastructure.

They do not think of themselves as builders in the old sense. They are **garden-engineers of structural life**: tuning genomes, adjusting electrostatic fields, introducing corrective pollen, and performing nightly retuning rites on bridges and load-roots. A child there learns harmonics before writing. An elder can hear a failing truss in a crosswind and tell you how far the fracture has propagated.

Social rank often follows acoustic responsibility:
- those who interpret infrasonic network pulses,
- those who tune growth-tones in living supports,
- those who patrol for silence (the most feared sign),
- and those who negotiate with the nonhuman swarms that keep the city alive.

Their religion, if they call it that, is maintenance. Collapse is sin; coherence is grace.

---

But they are not alone. The place is inhabited by a **civic biome**:

## The Living City-Organism

The plant reef itself is alive in distributed ways: root-mats computing, spore vaults remembering, vascular braids carrying ions and signal. Some districts behave like tissues in a single organism—closing pores during dust tides, shifting atmosphere chemistry chamber by chamber, rerouting nutrient debt like a financial network made of xylem and charge gradients.

So “who lives there?”  
In a real sense, the city does. It sleeps in spores, wakes in vibration, heals through pollen-borne nanoscopic repair systems, and remembers in color-shifting proteins.

---

## The Animal Citizens (and not-citizens)

A dense cast of fauna shares this system:

- **Sail-backed gliders** that ride particulate fronts and snap fins to signal flock turns.
- **Six-legged silica grazers** that harvest bark-minerals and open feeding scars later used by maintenance cultures.
- **Reedcats**—ambush predators using vibratory stunning against rail lines.
- **Courier beetles** carrying coded spore packets between districts.
- **Pollinator kites** whose feeding circuits double as access keys for bio-gates.
- **Tunnel-worms** domesticated to keep transit pores from clogging.

None of these are background wildlife. They are entangled with transport, repair, communication, and risk. Even scavengers track drone routes because waste is resource.

---

## What they are like, together

The inhabitants of this plateau are defined less by species than by **attunement**. Anything that survives here must do three things:

1. **Use dryness instead of fighting it** (salt brines, vapor capture, electrostatic feeding).
2. **Translate sound into action** (warnings, mapping, structural diagnostics, law).
3. **Prevent brittleness cascades** (constant sensing, distributed repair, ritual retuning).

So the truest answer is:

A people of caretakers live there, but so do engineered forests, signal-bearing spores, predatory acoustics, migratory herds, and old botanical architecture that has never stopped growing.  
It is a habitat where civilization is not built *on* nature, but grown *as* nature—and everyone in it, human or not, lives by listening for the next fracture before it happens.
