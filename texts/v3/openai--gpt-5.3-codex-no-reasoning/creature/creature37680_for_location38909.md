What lives there is not one creature so much as a **way of being**: a people-and-biome organism built for permanent weather.

If I had to name the primary resident, I’d call it the **Tidebound**.

They are human, yes—but only in the same way cliff pines are “trees.” The place has edited them. They live by interval and pulse, not by clock: slack tide, flood surge, rain break, rotor window, fog lift. They speak in verbs of motion and maintenance. Their day is less “go to work” than **keep the system alive**: clear intakes, re-seat anchors, unjam winches, listen to bearings, read cloud bellies, count bird departures, check whether the foghorn’s tone has gone flat.

They are thick-jacketed, salt-scoured, practical to the bone. Gloves clipped to belts. Knife, radio, lamp, marlinspike. Everyone smells faintly of diesel, kelp, wet wool, and cedar resin. Hands are mapped with tiny scars from cable strands and fish spines. They don’t trust surfaces that look dry. They don’t walk under loose scree in thaw. They don’t joke about mooring lines.

And they are never alone, because the place’s other residents are constant and opinionated:

- **Cliff nations of birds**, stacked by species and wind exposure, governing the vertical city with noise.
- **Kelp-forest mammals**—otters, seals, porpoises—treating channels as roads.
- **Amphibian and insect weather pulses** that appear instantly when sun hits still water.
- **Moss, lichen, liverwort, algae**—the slow green occupation force reclaiming every unattended edge.
- **Rust** as a living adversary, always feeding.
- **Water itself**, in all states short of calm, as top predator and engineer.

So what “lives there” most completely is a **maintenance culture inside a hydrologic engine**. Children learn knots before handwriting neatens. Elders can read tide-rips like text. Courtship happens on repair runs and fish lifts. Stories are indexed by storms (“the year the west beacon went dark,” “the spring the arch collapsed,” “the night all three trams iced over”). Beauty is real, but secondary to function—unless beauty *is* function, like a perfectly balanced line splice or a drainage channel that never clogs.

Even their spirituality, if they have one, is procedural: inspect, secure, re-check, leave redundancy, tell someone your route, return before the turn. Reverence here is not kneeling; it is preventative maintenance.

In another setting they might seem severe. Here they are simply correctly shaped.

So: who lives there?

**Keepers of moving edges.  
Citizens of spray lines.  
People of old drowned mountains who survive by listening for the moment noise changes.**
