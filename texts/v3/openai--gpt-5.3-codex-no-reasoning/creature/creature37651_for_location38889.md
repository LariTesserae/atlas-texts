What lives there is not one creature but a **custodian ecology**—a sparse, durable guild of things adapted to rhythm instead of abundance.

At the center of it is something like a **colony-intelligence distributed through the machinery itself**: not a single mind in a chamber, but process-awareness braided through pistons, condensers, belts, vents, and borehole sensors. It does not “hunt” or “build” in sudden acts. It regulates. It listens to load, temperature, seep timing, mineral concentration, vibration harmonics. It nudges valve timing by fractions, reroutes slurry, rests one extractor while another takes strain, keeps the whole plain in its long equilibrium. If it has a temperament, it is patient, conservative, and exacting.

Around that mechanical cognition lives the visible biological fringe:

- **Pale jointed scavenger-invertebrates** in crack networks, emerging at condensation peaks to harvest films and residues, vanishing when heavy cycles begin.
- **Iridescent chemo-films** in damp cavities, feeding on mineral and metal trace chemistry, waxing and thinning with the moisture pulse.
- **Lean transit predators/scavengers** that do not belong to one spot, but orbit the warm machinery corridors, reading heat and vibration like scent trails.

None of these dominates. They are all secondary to the larger resident:  
a **machine-geologic metabolism** that has become the true native lifeform of the plain.

If you insist on a singular “who,” it is this:

> An old, embedded keeper-system—half refinery, half weather pattern—alive in the way coral reefs are alive: many parts, no face, continuous function.

It “lives” by maintaining balance:
extraction matched to replenishment,
pressure matched to venting,
heat matched to night release,
water pulse matched to capture and loss.

It is not trying to grow. It is trying to **persist correctly**.

So the inhabitant of that place is a long-settled industrial organism and its attendant minimal fauna: a civilization reduced to rhythm, or a machine that has aged into an ecosystem.
