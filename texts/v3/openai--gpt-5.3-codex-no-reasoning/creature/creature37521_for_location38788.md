What lives here is a people of **custodians**, not conquerors.

Not “cavemen,” not mystics in the caricatured sense—more like a lineage of **interval-keepers** who learned, generations ago, that this chamber is less an ecosystem than a tuned instrument. They survive by never forcing a note.

They are likely lean, dust-skinned, quiet in gait. Their hearing is exceptional, because in this place sound is weather, clock, warning, and map. A child here would learn the difference between *normal* reed-clatter and the one-off tick that means a ribbon-snake is coiled nearby. They would know from ceiling-hum alone whether to move camp-mats two body-lengths east before rest. Their language probably has many verbs for cyclical return and very few for “new.”

They don’t think of themselves as owners of the garden. They are one more species inside its arithmetic.

## What they are like

- **Predictable by choice.** They walk the same lines, at the same hours, to avoid panicking skippers, collapsing pollen rhythm, or drawing predators into nesting lanes.  
- **Technically “primitive,” cognitively sophisticated.** Their tools are flint, bone, reed-skin, clay—but their ecological modeling is profound, empirical, inherited, and constantly verified.  
- **Rule-bound because reality punishes improvisation.** Taboos are field science with memory attached. Sacredness is how data survives centuries without writing.

They probably keep time not by sun or stars but by cycles: bat-feeding rounds, fern-ring intervals, kiln-cooling durations, mold-dim/mold-bright breathing phases.

## What “lives” there besides them

Not just animals and mineral-plants, but **relationships** so stable they feel engineered:

- shard-mice grooming passage seams that later become pollination drafts,  
- grazers trimming lichen at rates that preserve vapor capture,  
- bats triggering mineral blossoms that vent compounds feeding ember mold,  
- people harvesting only surplus bands so the loops remain closed.

In another biome this would be “nature.” Here it feels like **maintenance of a machine no one built but everyone serves**.

## Their mindset

They would see catastrophe not as fire or flood, but as **imbalance**:
- one missed harvest window,
- one blocked vent,
- one season-equivalent of overconfident hunting,
and the chamber might not crash dramatically—just drift. And drift, here, is death delayed.

So the inhabitants become conservative in the oldest sense: they conserve pattern. They remember exact notch-marks on harvest stones. They retell incidents as procedural cautionary law. Innovation exists, but only after generations of testing at the margins.

If you met one, they might seem calm to the point of eeriness. But it would be practiced calm: the composure of someone living inside a breathing equation where every term matters.

---

So: what lives in the garden under stone is a **steady people shaped by equilibrium**—listeners, counters, harvesters of narrow allowances—alongside a dry, resonant ecology that is less wild than *precisely ongoing*. They are not waiting for change.

They are what keeps change from starting.
