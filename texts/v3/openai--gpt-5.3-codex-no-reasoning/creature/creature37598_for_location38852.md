What lives there is not just a people, and not just a flora.  
What lives there is a **compact between pulse and patience**.

At first you might call them gardeners. Then engineers. Then priests. Then couriers in a city-sized instrument. None of those are wrong, but each is too small. They are a root-citizen culture—human or near-human by hand, voice, memory, and social complexity—who survive by participating in a single sprawling dry-botanical megastructure that is at once ecosystem, infrastructure, archive, and kin.

They do not “inhabit buildings.” They inhabit **growth agreements**.

---

### The people (or people-like dwellers)

They are tactile beings first. Their palms are literate.  
A child learns route-texture before language grammar: spiral ridge means transit, dimple bark means household boundary, char-slick means hazard. Their walking sticks are not supports but sensors—light tapping to map chambers by echo return and tissue response.

Their hearing is trained to absurd precision. A skilled listener can tell:

- whether a column is under healthy tension,
- whether a nutrient channel is blocked three terraces over,
- whether a pod-release was reproductive, defensive, or structural,
- whether an approaching stranger carries resin from another ward.

Identity is partly acoustic. People announce themselves with knock-patterns on shared root rails. Family lines may be recognized by characteristic gait-rhythm through felted floors. Silence is not peace here; silence is disorientation, sometimes threat.

They are dry-world adapted in body culture if not biology: oiled skin wraps to reduce moisture loss, breath veils that condense exhale for reuse, diets of dense carbon cakes, fungal protein, mineral salts, fermented pith. Water is medicine, solvent, sacrament—handled in droplets, never poured.

---

### Their society

Guilds matter more than blood, though blood still matters. Three old powers recur everywhere:

1. **Listeners** – acousticians, navigators, code-keepers of civic sound signatures.  
2. **Grafters** – growth-shapers, structural horticulturalists, reproductive stewards.  
3. **Fault-Tappers** – inspectors of stress, rot, gas pressure, and subsurface shift.

Law is practical and embodied. You don’t ban root-cutting; you require warning knocks so everyone in the load web can brace and reroute. You don’t demand beauty as ornament; you demand that architecture speak audibly so no one vanishes in the dark.

Maps are sung with versioning: place-name + resonance cue + date-mark.  
“Third long echo after split column, winter-ash cycle, post-red-spore drift.”

Governance likely happens in resonant halls where consensus is literally heard: proposals struck into root planks, responses returned as patterned counterbeats from districts.

---

### What else lives there

The vegetal continuum itself is the other citizen. Not metaphorically—functionally.  
It has distributed metabolism, slow responsiveness, memory in layered tissues, and dependency on human intervention for pollination, pruning, and controlled stress. It may not be sentient in a human sense, but it is an active collaborator with its own constraints and rhythms.

Alongside it:

- thread-fungi lace interfaces and digestion zones,
- iron-biofilms process mineral chemistry,
- spore-cloud species move genetic material through engineered airways,
- fermentation mounds generate thermal commons.

Large animals are absent, but life is not sparse; it is **substrate-dense** and behavior-rich at slow tempo.

---

### Their temperament

People raised here tend to think in long durations.  
Urgency exists—structural failures, gas surges, reproductive windows—but culturally they are calibrated to incrementalism: fingernail growth per month, district drift per generation, tower repurposing per century.

So whoever lives there is likely conservative in method but inventive in adaptation. Experimental, yet ritualized. Superstitious only where superstition and systems safety overlap (which is often). They revere continuity because continuity keeps roofs from collapsing and lineages from drying out.

Their highest virtue is probably not bravery or purity, but **attunement**.

---

In short:  
What lives there is a blind-yet-hyperperceptive civic species intertwined with a near-continuous dry botanical superorganism—an acoustic, graft-based civilization that survives by listening harder than it looks, and by treating growth itself as architecture, memory, and law.
