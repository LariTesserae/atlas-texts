What lives there is not an animal, and not a people.

What lives there is a **young lithic ecology**—a civilization’s skin after the civilization has stepped away, inhabited now by heat cycles, water pulses, salts, and stubborn plants. The primary resident is process.

If you insist on a singular “who,” it might be described as:

## The Keeper of Seams

Not a creature with limbs, but a distributed life made of thresholds. It “inhabits” every joint, crack, drain mouth, cistern lip, and condensation shadow. It wakes before sunrise as dew threads itself along ruled lines. It feeds at dawn on gradients—cool against warm, damp against dry, alkaline crust against fresh fracture. By noon it retreats into depth, into pores and voids, while the exposed world sterilizes under glare. At night it resurfaces in films, blooms, and mineral veils.

Its body is composite:

- **Mineral memory** (the made ground, still holding the intentions of builders),
- **Intermittent water** (brief, decisive, migratory),
- **Thermal breath** (daily expansion and contraction, the tiny tectonics of slabs),
- **Pioneer flora** (isolated, tactical, root-first intelligence).

Its metabolism is slow but relentless:  
seal → stress → crack → catch moisture → root → pry → powder → settle → seal again.

It does not move across the continent as an animal would; it appears wherever conditions align. A hundred thousand small occupations, each temporary, together becoming permanent. In one district it is mostly salt and fracture. In another, mostly dew and rosettes. In unfinished tracts, it is almost embryological—testing raw modules for weakness, deciding where “nature” will first gain purchase.

It has no voice, but it has signatures:

- thermal pings at daybreak,
- a single drip repeating in a cistern throat,
- faint white efflorescence outlining last night’s moisture route,
- a new green punctuation mark where none stood yesterday.

It has no predators and no companions.  
In a land without animals, it is both pioneer and succession, both scavenger and architect.  
It does not mourn the absent makers, nor worship them; it simply translates their geometry into future substrate.

Over centuries, this resident will do what all first inhabitants of abandoned order do: make complexity possible. It will turn intentional surface into negotiable terrain. It will write porosity into certainty. It will prepare rooms for lives not yet present.

So: whoever lives there is **Weathering itself, organized by design and interrupted by water**, with plants as its visible thoughts.
