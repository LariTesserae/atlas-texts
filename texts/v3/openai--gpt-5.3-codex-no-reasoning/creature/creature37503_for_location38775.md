What lives there is not one creature so much as a **way of being** shaped by heat, metal, and patience.

At first you think: vermin, moss, maintenance workers. But linger, and the resident life resolves into something more coherent—a **cistern-adapted community of small bodies and slow habits**, with humans as only one species among many, and the machinery as a kind of host.

### The obvious inhabitants
- **Blind silver insects** in valve pits: quick, reflective, thriving in wet darkness and heat gradients.
- **Pipe geckos**, translucent and still as peeled glass, stationed where condenser warmth and gnat traffic overlap.
- **Rail-thin scavenger mammals**, cable-shadow specialists, living by routes no larger animal can follow.
- **Microbial films**—arguably the true dominant life—orange, black, iridescent, sketching humidity maps across seals, brick, and steel.
- **Outpost plants**: gasket moss, junction-box fernlets, a lone vine tracing coolant infrastructure like it has read the blueprints.

None of these rule the place alone. They survive by exploiting **leaks, seams, edges, lag**, not open territory.

### The human inhabitants (if present)  
The people who belong to a cistern district are usually not conquerors of it, but **attendants**. They become cistern-shaped over time:
- skin always carrying a faint warmth and iron smell,
- speech lowered to not compete with hums and drips,
- reflexes tuned to sequence lights, pressure sighs, and relay ticks,
- memory organized by routes and gradients rather than dates.

They are ritualized by necessity: stop only on side platforms, test air by marks, read pipe color by dullness, listen for upslope. Their culture is procedural, but not sterile—almost monastic. They inherit patched seams and old warnings like oral law.

### The less obvious inhabitant: the district itself  
Most strongly, what lives there is a **quasi-organism of infrastructure**:
- pipes as vasculature,
- pumps as ventricles,
- condensation as breath,
- corrosion as aging skin,
- maintenance patches as scar tissue,
- automated carts as nocturnal fauna of steel and battery.

It does not think, but it has rhythm. It does not intend, but it enforces behavior. In that sense, the cistern district is both habitat and resident: a fabricated body that has outlived its original purpose and settled into metabolism.

So: whoever lives there is **the leak-wise**, the warm-surface avoiders, the slow movers, the edge dwellers—microbes, small hunters, careful workers, and one old machine-world that has learned how to keep itself just alive enough.
