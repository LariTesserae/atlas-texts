What lives there is not one species but a **civilization of bodies** built from appetite, timing, and touch.

At first glance, you’d call them arthropods—mites, isopods, beetles, crabs, centipedes, things like that, remixed and exaggerated by that basin’s physics. But if you stay longer, categories fail. They are less “bugs in ruins” than **the ruins made animate**: shell patterned like flaked rust, leg-hooks fitted to corrugated steel, mouthparts tuned to scrape bloom off iron and pry fungal films from seams. Their colors are the basin’s palette—oil-black, oxide red, chalk pale, old brass green, soot gray—with occasional warning flashes where lamp-light catches polished plates.

The dominant residents might be described in guilds:

- **Scale-raspers**: low, broad grazers that plane oxidation and microbe skins off warm metal. Their front edges are reinforced like chisels. Their feeding leaves matte tracks on once-glossy surfaces.
- **Pipe-lickers**: quick, fine-limbed foragers that patrol vents and dead lines, harvesting trace condensates before they evaporate. They’re competitive, frenetic, and live on thresholds.
- **Slab lurkers**: ambush hunters flattened to millimeters, tucked beneath plates and ceramic curls, reading vibration through entire body undersides.
- **Cracker tanks**: the door-panel-sized bruisers that ram old structures to knock down mineral scale. Ecosystem engineers by violence; everything follows them.
- **Lamp-ring specialists**: pale-eyed or many-occluded nocturnals who orbit the always-on light, exploiting its permanent congregation effect—predator and prey locked in a standing market.

Then there are the **small sovereigns**, thumbnail creatures in impossible numbers: brood nurses, egg thieves, carrion drills, molt scavengers, tremor mimics. They occupy the micro-geography nobody notices: bolt shadows, cable braids, gasket hollows, the under-lip of flanges. In biomass and influence, they may be the true rulers.

What truly “lives” there, though, is a **behavioral machine**:

- They farm heat without understanding heat.
- They mine vibration as information.
- They trigger relic mechanisms as if pulling weather levers.
- They schedule life cycles to dawn coughs of generators and evening vent-breath.

A clan that can make a valve open owns humidity for minutes.  
A brood that claims a fan housing owns stable temperature for a night.  
A hunter that can imitate the stutter of a failing motor can call curiosity—and then meat.

So the resident of that basin is a composite organism: millions of hard-bodied lives braided so tightly with dead industry that causality runs both ways. Machines wake animals; animals wake machines. Metal shapes anatomy; anatomy reshapes metal polish, dust channels, even which components still move.

If you insist on a single “who,” it is this:

**An ancient, many-legged reef-colony of scavengers and predators, evolved for dryness, vibration, and ruin—an animal weather system wearing the skeleton of a refinery.**
