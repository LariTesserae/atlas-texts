What lives there is not one thing but a *design principle made flesh*.

At first you might think the dominant life would be huge—some leviathan built to rival the cataracts. But the true sovereign of that world is **distributed life**: bodies and colonies and swarms tuned to flow gradients so precisely that individuality blurs into hydraulics. Creatures there don’t simply inhabit current; they are shaped as if current were their skeleton.

If there is a single emblematic resident, it is something like a **fallline listener**—a long, ribbon-muscled predator/scavenger that rides the lip of the great drops. Not fast in bursts, but tireless, with lateral pressure organs running flank to flank like instrument strings. Eyes are many but secondary, recessed and shielded; hearing in air is useless in that roar, so it “hears” through stone-coupled vibration and water shear. It feeds where turbulence shreds and stuns: grazers, tower-fragments, carrion flushed from upstream lanes. It rarely “hunts” in a chase sense; it stations itself exactly where flow geometry does the work.

Beneath and between those hunters, the most numerous visible animals are probably **glass grazers**: fist-sized, transparent, ciliated discs or wedges that skim centimeters above basalt. In calmer worlds, grazers browse plants. Here they browse *films*—iron bacteria, sulfur skins, mineral-fed mats so thin they are almost a color shift. Their swarms move in phase, like muscle fibers contracting. Predators read those synchronized ripples from far away.

Anchored in fracture lines are the **filter towers** you mentioned: colonial animals that behave like living infrastructure. Imagine chimneys of cartilage lattice and shell-flake, each with thousands of intake pores and contractile throats. They grow where particulate density, oxygenation, and anchoring stress all meet narrow tolerances. Entire tower fields become reef-analogs in a world without reefs—nursery, shelter, ambush corridor, detritus trap.

And then there are the **industrial opportunists**: species that have moved into heat leaks, vibration halos, and corrosion bloom around old machinery. Idle intake mouths become den networks. Sacrificial anodes become feeding grounds for metal-metabolizing symbionts. Beacon flashes attract plankton analogues, which attract grazers, which attract hunters—an accidental food web scaffolded by abandoned engineering.

If you ask what *person* lives there—what intelligence might arise in such a place—it would likely be something profoundly non-visual and non-ornamental. A being that maps the world as pressure topology, not landscape. It would navigate by memorized roar signatures and standing-wave harmonics. Its architecture (if it builds) would look like baffles, anchors, mufflers, and flow-gates rather than walls. Its language might be tactile, percussive, or transmitted through conductive frames because airborne sound is mostly futile. Its mythology, if any, would not center on seasons or stars but on **stable gradients**: this seam, that drop, this warm effluent, that lethal resonance zone.

In short: what lives there are creatures—and perhaps minds—selected for **endurance over novelty**, **position over time**, **sensing over seeing**, and **cooperation with force rather than resistance to it**.

A loud world does not produce silence-loving life.  
A nearly unchanging world does not produce haste.  
A shallow, endless flood over raw stone produces beings that are patient, pressure-literate, and exact.
