What lives there is **the Maintained**.

Not a single species at first glance, not even a people in the ordinary sense, but a whole guild of beings whose primary adaptation is not fang or speed—it is **upkeep**. In that basin, life survives by participating in construction. Organisms don’t just inhabit niches; they reinforce joints.

The closest thing to a dominant inhabitant is the human culture you glimpse at work: terrace-keepers, channel-combers, seam-setters. Their bodies are sun-glazed and water-slick, always dusted with lime, ash, pollen, and fine grit from fresh repair. Their hands are broad-palmed, scar-lined, exact. They move with the calm rhythm of people for whom emergency has been replaced by procedure generations ago. They are not “builders” racing toward a finished state; they are **continuers**. To them, stopping would be a metaphysical failure, like ceasing to breathe.

Around them lives a second citizenry: animals that have become masons by behavior.

- Marsh birds that probe mortar seams for larvae and in doing so clear drainage.
- Amphibians that bask in spill-basins, their skin carrying films of algae from cup to cup, seeding the luminous night tracery.
- Lizards that claim crack-lines as territories and keep insect bloom from overwhelming the shaded joints.
- Grazers whose hard-capped hooves polish causeways and tamp soft edges back into shape simply by following inherited routes.

Even insects obey the geometry: swarming where planting and moisture demand pollination, thinning where open courts must remain clean and sun-sterile. Nothing is idle. Even drift is functional.

And maybe the strangest resident is less creature than condition: a local physics that favors the **worked** over the wild. Untended space doesn’t erupt into jungle or desert—it becomes ambiguous. Count shifts. alignment loosens. sound edits itself. In that sense, the basin is inhabited by a quiet, pervasive entity you might call **the Blur**, held back by every reed tied, every tile reset, every cup refilled. The people do not maintain infrastructure *against* nature; they maintain ontology against soft unmaking.

So who lives there?

A civilization of patient ritual engineers.  
A fauna domesticated by pattern rather than pen.  
A flora that accepts edges and thrives in designed islands.  
And at the margins, always waiting, the not-quite-real.

It is a world where to dwell is to re-make, and where every living thing—human, bird, frog, algae, vine—serves as a finger pressing reality back into form.
