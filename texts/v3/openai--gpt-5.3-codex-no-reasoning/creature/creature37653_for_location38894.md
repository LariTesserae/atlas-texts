What lives there is not one thing but a **temperament** made flesh in many forms: a civilization and a biosphere both built around restraint.

The dominant “inhabitant,” in a sense, is **patience**.

- Life that does not squander motion.
- Industry that does not pretend abundance.
- People who plan as a reflex, the way forest peoples once read weather from leaves.

If I had to name the beings of this world, I’d start with its people: likely compact, layered societies of engineers, route-keepers, salvage crews, vent biologists, migration wardens, and dust technicians—cultures where maintenance is honored as much as invention. Their heroes are probably not conquerors but stabilizers: the one who kept a line running through a bad static season, who found water loss before dawn, who remembered the old corridor patterns of burrow drums and prevented a crash. They live in pulses, like their cities—high-output shifts alternating with deliberate quiet. Waste is moral failure. Shade is public good. Noise has law.

They would be a people fluent in **invisible maps**:
thermal maps, conductive seam maps, predator glide lanes, static-bloom calendars, Hush Belt migration timings, where sound carries at dawn versus noon. Children probably learn distance the way others learn weather: not “far,” but “two shelter intervals and one exposed stretch.”

And alongside them: a deeply non-Earthlike animal community that feels less like wilderness in the romantic sense and more like a distributed intelligence of niches.

- **Glassbacks** polishing stone plains in slow herds, turning mineral films into muscle.
- **Column mites** writing temporary architecture into vent fields.
- **Kite predators** as moving punctuation along cliff thermals.
- **Burrow drums** making the ground itself a communication medium.
- **Rail scavengers** proving that industry is just another ecosystem edge.

Even the microbial crusts are protagonists here: the first eaters, the true pasture. Everything else is an argument built on them.

So who lives there?

A world of **low-flame survivors**:  
stone-feeders, heat-riders, knock-speakers, seal-checkers, shift-horn listeners; beings that evolved or were raised to endure altitude, scarcity, and distance without drama. Nothing flamboyant, everything exact. Creatures and citizens alike that understand the same rule:

> Spend little. Sense much. Last long.

In that cracked bronze lung, life breathes slowly—but it breathes everywhere.
