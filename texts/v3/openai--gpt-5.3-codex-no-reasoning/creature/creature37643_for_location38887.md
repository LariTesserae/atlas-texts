What lives there is not a single creature so much as a **civic body**—a society distributed across stone, current, and habit.

At first you might think the inhabitants are the fish, the eels, the blind crustaceans. Then you notice those lives are arranged—not by command, but by architecture’s long pressure. So the true resident is a layered thing:

- **Animal guilds** tuned to chamber-temper: gate-schoolers, culvert drifters, jar-nesters, sluice ambushers.
- **Micro-faunas** in seams, clamps, amphora necks, and silt pockets—small enough to vanish to the eye, essential enough that larger life maps itself around them.
- **The Keepers** (if human, nearly post-political): sparse, quiet technicians of continuity who inherit maintenance routes the way others inherit myths.
- And over all of them, the oldest inhabitant: **the hydraulics itself**—recurring flows, pressure cycles, thermal layers, remembered paths of water that dictate where life can persist.

If you asked “who lives in the drowned fortress,” the most accurate answer would be:  
**a patient consortium of creatures and custodians, all domesticated by masonry and warm darkness, all practicing coexistence as slow spatial negotiation.**

Its character, if personified, would be this:

- **Blind but not unaware** (it senses by pressure, chemistry, vibration).
- **Conservative but inventive** (nothing discarded, everything repurposed).
- **Territorial without warfare** (displacement over annihilation).
- **Ritualized without religion** (repeated movement patterns that outlast memory of why).
- **Ancient without nostalgia** (repair over restoration; function over origin).

The apex “resident” may be rare and easy to miss: a lineage of large, long-lived predators or custodians (perhaps both, depending on chamber) that patrol thresholds rather than ranges, knowing the system as a sequence of valves and waits. Not kings—**regulators**. They survive by reading tiny shifts: a colder tongue of water, a new mineral taste, bead-lines trembling where they never did before.

And the people, when present, resemble another species of reef fish more than masters:  
they arrive, inspect, scrape, retension, plug, leave. Their genius is humble and exact. They live *with* the place by preventing abrupt change, keeping all other inhabitants within the narrow band where this hand-built weather system remains livable.

So what lives there?

A **hydraulic polis** in the dark:  
stone-trained animals, maintenance-minded humans, and an inherited intelligence spread through channels, thresholds, and time.  
Not a monster in a ruin—an old, submerged civilization of many bodies sharing one circulatory mind.
