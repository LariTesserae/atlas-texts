What lives there is not a single creature but a **civic organism**—a population so interlocked with masonry, mineral, and current that “resident” and “infrastructure” are the same role.

At first you might think the primary inhabitants are the obvious animals: the blind fish, transparent shrimp, belt-flat eels, patient snails, the sudden silver hunters, the ray-bodied negotiators of impossible doorways. But if you stay long enough, the picture shifts. The true inhabitant is a **cold-water consortium**: a distributed intelligence of filterers, grazers, ambush bodies, scavengers, biofilm, and precipitating stone, each one writing rules the others must read.

It lives like this:

- **In the seams**: shrimp colonies and worm threads occupy balustrades and rebar lattices as if those were purpose-built tenements.
- **On the skins**: every wall has a living epidermis—films, mats, eggs, grazing trails, larval anchor points.
- **In the timings**: fish and predators don’t merely move through corridors; they keep appointments with tide, slack water, sediment weather.
- **Inside larger bodies**: some animals are walking blocks of real estate, carrying polyps, parasites, cleaners, nurseries.
- **With the stone itself**: calcium curtains and soft precipitate are not backdrop but active zoning policy—opening passages, narrowing others, creating seasonal neighborhoods.

If you asked for the “citizen,” you might find one emblematic being: a pale, long-jawed hunter that has gone half-architectural in behavior. It knows the breathing doorways. It waits where outgoing pulse pushes prey into a choke point. It allows cleaner fish to maintain it in exchange for protection. Eggs of smaller species are safe for a time in the eddies along its resting groove. Even fear around it has become organized, ritualized, spatial.

But that creature is only a ministry, not the state.

The state is this: a **reef-city larva** in cold suspension, perpetually unfinished, where life has inherited a human geometry and converted it into ecology. No mayor, no blueprint, yet there is planning: oxygen districts, predation corridors, nursery alcoves, waste pathways, collapse zones, luminous wayfinding. The laws are pressure gradients and hunger budgets. The archives are snail trails, polished bone, layered calcite, recurring migration tracks.

So whoever lives there is a **many-bodied metropolis**—quiet, tactical, and devoutly adaptive.  
Not abandoned people. Not feral ruins.  
A new kind of resident: **urban life after humans, learning to breathe through stone.**
