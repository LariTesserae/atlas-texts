What lives there is not a single creature but a **caste of water-forged animality**—a civilization without minds, an ecology that behaves like a machine.

If you had to name its “resident,” it would be something like this:

## The Column-Bound

A collective of species built for pressure-tier life, each body less an individual story than a moving part in a hydraulic system. They are creatures of seam and edge: jawed wedges, fan-limbed sifters, ring-muscle drillers, shell-plated anchors, and ribbon scavengers. None dominate alone. Together they occupy the basin the way weather occupies sky.

They are:

- **Articulated, mineral-facing, and unsentimental**
- **Slow in baseline, sudden in strike**
- **Obsessed with surfaces**—every ledge is grazed, guarded, seeded, stripped
- **Acoustically fluent**—they read vibration the way land animals read light
- **Layer-loyal**—pressure is their caste law

### Their body plan logic
Most of them look engineered because selection here rewards repeatable modules:

- Interlocking plates instead of soft flanks  
- Valve mouths and ratcheting throats  
- Cilia arranged in comb-banks, not fuzzy halos  
- Limbs that grip glassy basalt like pitons  
- Bioluminescence as a side-effect, not spectacle

Even predators are economical: broad-jawed lurkers that conserve effort, spend it once, and reset.

### Their behavior
The “who” of this place acts in cycles:

- Grazers harvest films in lanes that refill on chemical schedules.
- Filter ranks open and close in sequence like sluice gates.
- Spiral-chain rotors physically turn current through mineral throats.
- Scavengers audit fracture zones after every rockfall.
- Larvae ascend and descend on clockwork pressure tides.

No kings. No architects. But there is coordination by collision, by current, by inherited rhythm.  
Contact is constant; exchange is unavoidable.

### Their temperament, if one can call it that
Not aggressive, not peaceful—**procedural**.  
Life here is conducted as throughput: mineral to microbe to grazer to hunter to detritus to mineral again. Hunger is not frantic; it is scheduled. Violence is brief and exact. Waiting is the dominant act.

---

So what lives there?

A **living engine-population**: densely packed animal lineages that have replaced the ecological role forests play elsewhere. They are the moving infrastructure of a cliff-chamber underwater—its pumps, teeth, screens, conveyors, alarms, and recyclers—bound together by flow, pressure, and noise into one continuous, industrial metabolism.
