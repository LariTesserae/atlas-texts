What lives here is a **layered people**—amphibious in habit if not in anatomy, civic-minded in a way that feels older than states, and intimate with water the way desert people are intimate with wind.

They are not one image at first glance. From far away you might mistake them for fishers, gardeners, and scaffold-climbers sharing a flooded ruin. Stay longer, and they resolve into a culture shaped by three constant teachers: **depth, growth, and echo**.

### The people

They are lean, broad-shouldered through the back, with hands marked by rope, reed cuts, and cold. Their bodies are trained rather than exotic: long breath control, powerful kicks, scarred shins from submerged lattice, ears tuned to vibration through wood and water. Children learn depth before distance—how far down light remains useful, where cold layers start, how to read suspended silt like weather.

Their clothes are practical and stratified like their laws:  
- close-wrapped fiber at the waist and chest for swimming,  
- detachable buoyant reed-collars for work in rough weather,  
- lacquered leaf capes or fishskin mantles in exposed air chambers.  

Ornament is sonic and positional. Shell rings that click at certain stroke tempos. Bone toggles carved with clan intervals. Copper-green clasps reserved for lineages with old obligations—keepers of gates, judges of resonance, tenders of nursery heat.

Hair is often braided up and resin-bound when diving; worn loose in canopy spaces. Skin carries stains of algae oils and medicinal pollen. Many wear depth cords around the arm: knotted records of what layers they hold rights in.

### How they think

They do not divide “nature” and “infrastructure.” A wall is also a living root mass; a street is also fish habitat; maintenance is also agriculture. Their central virtue is not conquest or purity but **continuance**—keeping flows open, oxygen balanced, disputes voiced, growth guided.

Ownership by vertical bands has made them unusually tolerant of proximity and unusually strict about boundaries of use. Someone may swim under your sleeping pod daily and still be trespassing if they cast a net at your licensed depth. Privacy is acoustic and ritual more than visual.

### Governance and memory

Their lawmakers are vocal specialists—part judge, part cantor, part engineer. A ruling is valid when it can be propagated in correct intervals through waterlogged districts without distortion. So authority depends on lungs, ear training, and public repetition.

Archives exist in shell etchings and knot strings, yes—but true legitimacy is **performed memory**. The city remembers itself by being re-sung. A failed transmission is a constitutional problem.

Silence is dangerous here. Not quiet—the basin is never quiet—but civic silence: failure to answer a call pattern, refusal to repeat a judgment tone. That can mean dissent, mourning, quarantine, or revolt depending on cadence and context.

### Daily work

Most residents are some blend of:
- **rootwrights** (grow and shape structural vegetation),
- **flow keepers** (manage sluices, gates, pressure masts),
- **silt dredgers** and oxygen gardeners,
- **acoustic clerks** (law chanters, drum relays, interval teachers),
- **cold stewards** (heat pits, nursery insulation, fermentation houses),
- **fish wardens** (protect herbivore runs, cull invasive spawn),
- **canopy tenders** (medicine, seeds, weather watch).

Even celebration resembles maintenance: festivals timed with pruning cycles, court days paired with dredging crews, marriage oaths sung at shared mooring posts where multiple depth-rights intersect.

### What else lives there

A dense companion ecology: frogs in the rung towers, quarrelsome waterfowl, bark-grinding herbivore fish, pipe-clicking eels, rasping shell grazers, insect veils above emergent gardens, and cultivated fungi that paint dusk with low blue-green pulses. None of this is backdrop. It is accounted for in law, labor, and myth.

Some families specialize in interspecies listening—reading frog choruses for barometric shift, eel click-patterns for pipe blockage, fish schooling behavior for oxygen debt. Their science is empirical, oral, and continuous.

### Their emotional tone

They are not romantic about catastrophe. Their ancestors drowned, adapted, and institutionalized adaptation. So they distrust grand fixes and adore small reliability: a correctly tied lashing, a cleared channel before storm season, a child who can hold pitch in cold water.

Hospitality is real but procedural. A visitor is assigned a safe depth band, taught local call-and-response signals, warned where passage is public and where currents are privately worked. You are welcomed by being integrated into flow, not by exemption from it.

---

So: whoever lives here are **citizens of a vertical commons**—cold-water horticultural urbanists whose law is carried by resonance, whose architecture is grown, and whose identity is neither “survivor” nor “native” but something steadier:

People who accepted drowning, then made a civilization that breathes through it.
