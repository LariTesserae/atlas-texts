What lives there is a **civilization of keepers**—not conquerors, not pioneers, not survivors in panic, but custodians of continuity.

At first glance you might think “machines live there,” because the city breathes through ducts and rails and furnaces more audibly than through crowds. But the machinery is too carefully tended, too ritually maintained, to be ownerless. The true inhabitants are a sparse people shaped by scarcity of water, abundance of mineral, and a culture that long ago replaced growth with **persistence** as its highest virtue.

They are likely physically slight but dense, economical in motion, protected always from the air by layered textiles, sealed boots, reflective masks, and throat filters that double as vocal dampers. Their skin—when seen—would be matte, mineral-pale or furnace-burnished, adapted more to dust and radiant heat than to sunlight. Eyes probably favor low-light contrast over color discrimination; in that violet dimness, shape matters more than hue.

Socially, they seem to live in **small operational lineages**: maintenance houses, pressure guilds, kiln families, tag-inheritors. Identity may be less about blood and more about what subsystem you are trusted to keep alive. A person is not just “a citizen,” but “third-valve steward, east solvent spine,” or “night relay auditor, tower ring five.” Names might be practical strings of function and inheritance, with personal names used only in intimate spaces.

Their religion—if they would call it that—may be inseparable from engineering. Not worship of invention, but reverence for **stable process**. A pump that has run for three centuries is not obsolete; it is proven. A route walked unchanged for generations is not habit; it is encoded safety. In such a place, novelty is suspect unless it can demonstrate durability across decades. Memory is infrastructural.

So whatever “lives” there includes:

- **The Human Custodians**: few, masked, disciplined, inheritors of procedure.
- **The Industrial Body**: semi-autonomous systems that are not sentient perhaps, but behave like organs in a sleeping giant.
- **The Mineral Fauna**: enamel scavengers, thermal gliders, blind burrowers—an ecology stripped to extremophile essentials.
- **The Archive of the Absent**: credentials still valid for dead workers, lights still burning in empty sectors, readiness without demand.

If there is a single character that lives there, it is this:  
**an old metabolic intelligence distributed across people, protocol, and machine, committed to never failing all at once.**

The city does not feel haunted by catastrophe. It feels inhabited by duty.
