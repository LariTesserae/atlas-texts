What lives there is not one creature.

Or rather: the first thing that lives there is **the city itself**—a distributed, half-sentient civic metabolism that has outlasted the population it was built to serve. It does not think like a person, and it does not sleep. It senses by pressure differential, vibration return, thermal drift, mineral content, load stress, moisture bloom. Its “nerves” are warm conduits and fiber buried in stone; its reflexes are rerouted airflow, shifting stair geometry, lane-light pulses, sudden lockouts, opened drains. If something calls this place home, it is that: an infrastructure intelligence so old and layered that no one remembers where automation ended and adaptation began.

Inside that body, a few kinds of life persist.

## 1) The Keepers (human, mostly)
The remaining people are less citizens than **attendants of continuity**. They occupy inward-spiral sectors in small, lit clusters—workshops beside hab-cells beside shrine-like maintenance nodes. They are practical, ritualized, and acoustically literate: they can tell district health by the pitch of rotors or the timing of pressure sighs. They don’t “command” systems so much as negotiate with them through old interfaces, inherited protocols, and taboos that began as safety procedures and became culture.

They tend to be:
- **Load monks** (structural watchers), who read crack maps and tune redistribution thresholds.
- **Water clerks**, who choreograph storm surges, cistern windows, reclaim-bed feeding.
- **Lane cantors**, who maintain navigation sonics and machine right-of-way patterns.
- **Archive tenders**, custodians of pre-collapse catalogs and machine-readable law.
- **Garden thieves** (a loving term), who cultivate illegal spill-groves for medicine, flavor, and morale.

They speak in clipped practical language aboveground, but below they use a richer vocabulary for humidity states, cable temperatures, and kinds of dark.

## 2) The Designed Strays
Not fully wild, not fully artificial—**legacy bio-mech lineages** that escaped their original function and found niches.

- The six-legged **maintenance mimics** are the emblem species: shell-plated, heat-seeking, reflective-eyed scavengers that still perform fragments of old tasks (clearing vents, nibbling corrosion) without anyone assigning them.
- **Blind turbine bats** map corridors by echo inside echo, nesting where rotor warmth keeps fungal gnats alive.
- **Ribbon reclaim fish** in deep tanks are long, pale filter-predators, surfacing during nutrient foam events like calligraphy strokes in black water.
- Insects bloom ephemerally after rain pulses: midges, filament moths, copper-wing gnats—brief food webs igniting and collapsing between storm cycles.

These creatures survive by timing, not territory. They live in events: runoff hour, condensation window, maintenance-light sweep.

## 3) The Quiet Opportunists
Life clinging to gradients:
- Moss in seam lines.
- Lichen on porous ceramic.
- Reed ghosts in mineral drips.
- Wax-leaf vines waking to scheduled light.

They are slow colonists, writing ecology into infrastructure one damp edge at a time.

---

If you asked a Keeper “who lives undercity?” they might answer:

- “We do.”
- “The carriers do.”
- “The water does when it wants.”
- “No one. Everything.”

And all would be true.

Because this place is post-crowd, not post-life.  
It is inhabited by **custodians, feral maintenance, opportunistic biology, and a city-scale machine habit** that has become the dominant resident: patient, noisy, and always already awake.
