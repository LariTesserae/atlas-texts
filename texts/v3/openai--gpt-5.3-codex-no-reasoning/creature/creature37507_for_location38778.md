What lives there is **a people before architecture**—a clever, social omnivore that has not yet decided to become a builder, because the basin itself already functions like a ready-made settlement.

Not one creature alone, but a **coalition species** at the edge of culture: think packs, bands, and rival lineages of ground-running tool-users whose intelligence is obvious in patterns, not monuments. They are the kind of inhabitants you notice first by repeated behavior:

- the same bone ring used to crack marrow season after season  
- the same stone clusters at crossing points where hands have chosen weight and shape  
- the same trampled arcs around pools, mounds, and lookout ridges  
- the same call-and-response warnings that ripple across species lines

They live in a place where visibility is high and cover is low, so their minds evolved for **coordination, memory, and timing** rather than secrecy. They are not hiding from the basin’s abundance—they are participating in it.

---

Physically, they likely look built for this open, damp-cool floor:

- long lower limbs for steady trotting over mixed mud, clay crust, and stone  
- dexterous forehands for turning rocks, probing seep edges, stripping sedge roots, opening carcasses  
- broad sensory attention: eyes for distance, ears for layered calls, noses for wet-ground chemistry  
- skin/fur/feather adapted to chill shade and sudden wet, not deep cold or blazing heat

They’re generalists in diet because the basin is generalist in opportunity: insects in swarms, eggs and nestlings, amphibians at temporary pools, carrion, small prey flushed by ungulate traffic, roots and shoots from wet pockets, occasional hunted meat shared by rank.

---

Their intelligence appears as **ecological literacy**.

They “read” the basin minute by minute:

- which depression will hold water longest after rain  
- where hoof traffic will compress new paths  
- where predators will stage on shelf margins  
- where insects hatch thickest at dusk  
- when to wait, when to move, when to gather in numbers

No permanent houses because permanence is in the pattern itself.  
No walls because the basin’s ring and sightlines already define neighborhood and frontier.  
No fields because water is intermittent and mobility beats ownership.  

Their culture is made of **routes, sounds, and repeated places**.

---

You would know their society by sound before sight. Distinct calls for:

- aerial threat  
- cursorial predator movement  
- fresh seep opening  
- carcass claim  
- crossing permission at crowded pool margins  
- juvenile recall at nightfall

In a habitat where “every niche is filled,” intelligence is less about dominating and more about **negotiating density**. These inhabitants are diplomatic opportunists: they steal, trade access through tolerance, shadow herds, follow burrowers, and scavenge after larger kills. Conflict is common, annihilation rare. The basin rewards those who can re-enter the flow tomorrow.

---

So: who lives there?

**A proto-civil people of the open ground**—highly vocal, tool-selecting, path-making, socially stratified but lightly rooted.  
They are not failed builders; they are successful non-builders.  
In a raw, young, half-sunken world of damp pockets and constant traffic, they have become the one thing the basin most favors:

**behavior that persists more strongly than structures do.**
