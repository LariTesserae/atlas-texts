What lives there is not a single species.

What lives there is a **citizenry of adaptations**—and, at the center of it, a thing like a civilization distributed across roots, resonant hollows, and rain timing.

If you insist on a singular inhabitant, then the truest answer is: **the City itself is the organism**, and everything else is either symbiont, courier, grazer, interpreter, or police.

---

At the largest scale, the resident intelligence seems **mycelial-botanical-acoustic** rather than individual.  
Not a mind in one trunk, but decision-making spread through:

- cambium pressure changes,
- fungal exchange webs,
- hydraulic pulses after rain,
- and above all, patterned sound.

It does not “think” in sentences. It thinks in **opening rates**, **moisture permissions**, **growth refusals**, and **chorus shifts**.  
A sealed doorway is a no.  
A sweating lintel is an invitation.  
A sudden silence in one nave and a rise in seed percussion in another is rerouting.

So: the first resident is a **distributed civic metabolism** that remembers pathways as living habits, not plans.

---

Within that body live the visible inhabitants—most of them avoiding open sightlines because open space is transit, not home.

### The near-invisible majority
- **Pollinator gyres**: rotating insect clouds that occupy specific acoustic pockets. They are less “swarms” than weather cells with memory.
- **Root-runners**: low, fast hexapods reading substrate vibration the way fish read current. They probably “see” your footfall minutes before you arrive.
- **Cistern sleepers**: slick-skinned climbers folded into bromeliad pools by day, descending at condensate peak.
- **Pod-mimic ambushers**: predators that participate in the architecture’s visual grammar, hanging in plain sight as plausible fruiting bodies.
- **Reweavers**: detritivores and fungus-herders that convert death to structure in hours. These may be the closest thing to sanitation workers.

These creatures are not random wildlife in ruins. They occupy roles in a stable urban trophic design: ventilation, pruning, nutrient redistribution, alarm signaling, spore transport.

---

### The probable “people,” if people exist
If there are sophonts here, they likely do not dominate by force or permanent construction. They would be:

- **Acoustic negotiators** (voice, percussion, mimicry),
- **Moisture bearers** (carrying damp, sap, fresh leaves to unlock routes),
- **Seasonal cartographers** (mapping by chorus edge, not direction),
- **Growth litigators** (redirecting overgrowth without stopping it).

Their bodies—whatever their lineage—would trend toward:
- broad contact surfaces for vibration sense,
- skin or garments that hold humidity,
- resonant anatomy (throat sacs, chest plates, click organs, or instrument use),
- fungal tolerance and rapid wound-healing (everything here is abrasive, wet, and alive).

They wouldn’t “own houses.” They’d maintain **relationships with hollows**.  
Residence would be conditional: kept by song, moisture tribute, and compatible traffic patterns.

---

### Temperament of the inhabitants
Everything living here shares a common ethic imposed by the environment:

- **Never remain inert.**
- **Never be dry if you need access.**
- **Never be acoustically rude.**
- **Never mistake stillness for safety.**

Aggression is contextual, often triggered less by proximity than by **acoustic trespass**—wrong rhythm, wrong amplitude, wrong timing in a hollow that is currently claimed.

Predation feels bureaucratic: not rage, but enforcement.

---

### How they experience space
To outsiders, geometry fails.  
To residents, geometry is secondary.

They navigate by:
- hydration gradients,
- resonance corridors,
- spore drift vectors,
- tension states in load-bearing growth.

So the thing that “lives there” most successfully is any organism able to treat space as **a living negotiation**, not a fixed container.

---

In the end, the inhabitants are a layered polity:

1. **The City-Body** (growth network + fungal memory + hydraulic logic),  
2. **Its mobile organs** (fauna guilds handling pollination, pruning, transport, predation, recycling),  
3. **Its interpreters** (possibly sapient beings who can speak moisture and sound into temporary rights-of-way).

They do not dwell in an abandoned district.

They are the reason it was never abandoned at all.
