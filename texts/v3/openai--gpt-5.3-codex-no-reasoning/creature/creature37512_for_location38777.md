What lives there are **custodians of equilibrium**—not a people in the ordinary civic sense, but a sparse lineage of beings shaped by a world where correction is stronger than desire.

At first you might think the city is empty, and in one sense it is. No crowds, no commerce, no visible routine. But if you remain long enough, you begin to notice presences that are easy to miss because they move only when movement is required, and requirement is rare.

They are called, by the few outsiders who have seen them, **the Stillborne**.

They are pale like everything else living there, but not bloodless—more opaline than white, as if color has been reduced to undertones. Tall and narrow-boned in low gravity compensation rigs, or compact and folded when at rest in pressure alcoves. Their skin is faintly conductive; in darkness, tiny charge gradients skate across it like heat-lightning under ice. Eyes large, irised in concentric metallic films that can shutter to pinpoints or open to almost full black discs. Hair, where they have it, grows in sparse filaments and is usually braided tight against the skull with ceramic thread so nothing drifts loose and triggers correction fields.

They do not swim the way fish swim. They **translate**—small vector bursts, hand to rail, ring to ring, body always aligned with lane geometry, never wasting momentum. Watching one cross a courtyard membrane is like watching a calligrapher place strokes: deliberate, minimal, impossible to improve.

Their speech is quiet and short-lived. In open water they mostly use contact-sign: fingers to wrist, palm to shoulder, knuckles to hull, transmitting intent through pressure and vibration so the medium carries almost nothing away. When they do vocalize, the language is built from clipped tones tuned not to reverberate into infrastructure bands. They have lived so long beside the Choir that even their consonants avoid sympathetic frequencies.

They are not many. Perhaps a few hundred distributed through the district, perhaps fewer. Census is not a concept they prioritize; occupancy is measured by **load**, not headcount—how many minds are needed to observe, verify, and intervene if the forever-machine ever falls out of song.

Because that is what they are: observers of a solved equation.

---

They did not build the Anchor Choir. Their oldest records admit this plainly, though “record” means something strange here: etched ceramic leaves stacked in vacuum sleeves, memory-metal rods that ring different when warmed, choreographed maintenance routes remembered as ritual. History to them is not narrative but **tolerance**—acceptable deviation from baseline. They remember founders as error bars, migrations as phase shifts, disasters as frequencies that should not recur.

Children are rare and planned. Gestation happens in shielded pods near thermal-null nodes where the corrective field is calmest. Infants are not rocked; they are held against sternum plates so they learn heartbeat, then machine-hum, then silence, in that order. Toys are weighted loops, mineral discs, tiny inert gyros—objects that teach return-to-center before language fully forms.

They do have art, but it looks like maintenance to outsiders. Arrays of suspended pins adjusted by microns until they ring with passing field harmonics. Black ceramic walls polished to reveal only one reflected angle. Processions around the seven rings timed to beacon pings from the far lanes. Their most revered performances are called **Rebalancings**: a group enters a storage arcade, disturbs everything deliberately, then restores each item to its exact prior vector by touch alone, no instruments. Memory as devotion.

Death is handled with the same precision. Bodies are not buried (no soil), not burned (combustion is chaotic), not launched. They are dissolved in mineral baths and cast into load-bearing tiles, handrails, pressure thresholds. The dead remain as structural certainty. To step across a transit latch is literally to trust your ancestors.

---

And yet they are not machine-priests in the simple sense. They laugh, though softly. They form attachments. They keep private caches of pre-equilibrium contraband: a bent spoon, a cracked glass bead, a scrap of dyed fabric impossible to reproduce in a colorless world. Some secretly cultivate motion the way others once cultivated gardens—tiny illegal vortices trapped in sealed spheres, watched with awe until the field smooths them out.

Their central taboo is not violence, nor blasphemy, but **unbounded perturbation**.

To make something that cannot be easily corrected is their nearest thing to sin.

That is why outsiders unsettle them. Not because strangers are hostile, but because strangers carry habits from turbulent ecologies: fidgeting, dropping, improvising, speaking over one another, expecting systems to absorb mess. Here, mess is a demand placed on ancient power. Every unnecessary disturbance is a tax on a machine no one can repair.

Still, they are not cruel. They assign guides—usually adolescents, whose vector control is best—to shadow visitors and quietly undo their wake. A glove left unclipped is retrieved. A shifted crate returns overnight to its mark. A spoken question receives an answer days later, precise and complete, once the right archive keeper has verified it three ways.

---

Beyond the Stillborne and the ambiguous spindle-creatures, other life persists in edge forms:

- **Hull discs** that harvest ions and plate themselves in concentric armor.
- **Thread eels** no thicker than wire, living in the warmth around dormant relays.
- **Choir mites**—perhaps truly engineered—microscopic colonies in ring casings that rearrange trace corrosion into harmless salts.

The Stillborne classify all of these not by species but by function: stabilizer, scavenger, neutral, unknown. Biology is a maintenance category.

---

If you ask what truly “lives” in the hanging city, the deepest answer is this:

A **culture of exactness** lives there.  
A people who inherited an impossible stillness and chose, generation after generation, not to break it.  
They are neither thriving nor dying by ordinary measures. They are *holding*.

In a world where nothing grows, they have made continuity itself their form of life.
