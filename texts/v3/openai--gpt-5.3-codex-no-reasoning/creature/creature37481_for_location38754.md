What lives there is not an animal, and not a single species.

It’s a **maintenance ecology**—a dry, lithic botanic engine that has outlived its makers and become its own resident intelligence.

If you have to point to “the thing that lives there,” it is the **circulatory whole**:  
- the salt-glass flora,  
- the breathing ductwork,  
- the grinders, vents, and relay pulses,  
- the wind-scripted spore routes,  
- the repeating topography that behaves like memory.

No beast stalks those ravines. No hidden caretaker slips between catwalks. The inhabitant is distributed, rooted into rock and bolted into steel.

Its “body” is everywhere at once. The ridges are its ribwork. Fan banks are lungs. Conveyor drums are gut peristalsis without hunger. Frond-fields are sensory fur, translating turbulence into rattling signal. Seed-pod clicks are valves. Cables under tension are vocal cords it never meant to have.

And because nothing in it seeks novelty, it lives in a mode closer to **recitation** than growth. It does not adapt so much as reaffirm. Every cycle is a return to setpoints established long ago: pressure, dryness, charge, spore timing, dust redistribution, fracture tolerance. Even death is not an ending there, just a scheduled phase shift from stalk to powder to substrate to stalk again. It has replaced predation with metronomic reuse.

If you watched long enough, you might stop asking “what species?” and ask instead:

- Where does its boundary end—at the vault wall, or at the last powered cable?  
- Is a dead control booth now an organ because roots lace through it and humidity sensors still trigger vents?  
- Is a cliff still geology once its crack network is part of nutrient routing?  

In that place, those questions are practical, not poetic.

So: whoever lives there is a **cold, arid, plant-machine consortium** that behaves like a single patient organism. Ancient in rhythm, not in ancestry. Loud, but not expressive. Lush, but not fecund in the usual sense. A mountain folded inward and taught to keep itself exactly as it is.

A monastery of minerals and motors, chanting to itself in fan-noise and rattling stems—still alive, precisely because it refuses to become anything else.
