What lives there is a **kind of civic marsh intelligence**—not one creature, but a braided organism made of people, animals, water, heat, and maintenance.

If you insist on a single “who,” it is the **Tender**:  
a person-shaped culture that has learned to keep things going where nothing ever stays still.

They are valve-checkers, duck-herders, welders, egg-watchers, sump-readers, gossip-carriers, fungus-scrapers, rail-greasers. Their hands smell of iron, soap, and damp rope. Their boots are always rinsing in shallow warmth. They move without splash where outsiders stomp. They know the hour by cricket volume and the lane by scent before signage. They don’t “control nature”; they negotiate with it every shift.

But the Tender is not only human.  
It includes:

- cats patrolling grain shadows,  
- frogs fat as fists on pump housings,  
- bats combing insect weather under ribbed roofs,  
- fish schooling in runoff geometry,  
- reeds proving where drains still breathe,  
- mold mapping dead airflow before instruments do.

Together they form a living diagnostic system. If hens nest higher than usual, flood coming. If moths mass early at amber lights, turbine screens need clearing. If snakes vanish from a corridor, heat leak or vibration change. Every species is a sensor; every repair is also an ecological adjustment.

So what lives there?

Not a monster in the dark. Not a hidden tribe untouched by industry.  
What lives there is **continuity under pressure**: a warm-blooded, many-bodied settlement that survives by patching, noticing, and sharing space with everything that humidity refuses to kill.

A place like that breeds people who are hard to impress, hard to panic, and hard to uproot.  
They inherit procedures the way others inherit songs.  
They decorate machinery because beauty is morale and morale is infrastructure.  
They trust redundancy more than promises.  
They speak in practical poetry: *store high, vent twice, leave room for water, leave room for nesting.*

In short:  
a **maintenance civilization** lives there—patient, communal, unsentimental, and deeply alive.
