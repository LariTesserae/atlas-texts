What lives there is not a single creature but a **timed community**—a basin-wide organism made of stone, weather, relic-tech, and a handful of species that have learned to exist in pulses.

If you insist on naming a primary resident, it is this:

## The Rainphase Keeper (or whatever locals would call it)

Not an animal in the usual sense.  
Not a machine in the usual sense.  
A **distributed intelligence** that resides in the plateau substrate and wakes through humidity.

It “lives” as:

- the spiral-grooved pylons that drink climbing droplets,
- ring fragments sleeping under thin water film,
- mineral-crystal lattices in the fresh black stone,
- conductive roots and fern-fronds that complete transient circuits,
- warm marsh pockets where amphibian clutches act like living capacitors.

In dry conditions it is nearly absent—just inert geometry.  
In wet conditions it assembles itself from interactions:

- rain introduces charge gradients,
- condensation maps pathways,
- mist becomes temporary architecture,
- hum frequencies phase-lock across pylons,
- biological tissues couple in and out like adaptive components.

So the inhabitant is a **weather-assembled mind**: intermittent, non-centralized, and ecological rather than individual.

---

Secondarily, there are true creatures—small, elusive, and specialized:

- **Calibration clickers** (the amphibian-like nesters): dusk-active, heat-loving, likely timing development to electromagnetic pulses.
- **Threshold ferns**: almost leafless until humidity spikes; then they open conductive fronds and harvest static.
- **Probe vines**: tactile cartographers of temporary force planes.
- Possibly a **shy aerial predator/scavenger** (the abrupt wingbeats), adapted to navigate shifting invisible partitions.

But all of them feel like **organs** of the larger resident system.

---

So: whoever lives there is a **machine-garden intelligence in low-duty cycle**, one that breathes with rainstorms, thinks in brief activations, and remembers in stone spacing and moisture paths rather than in constant consciousness. It is quiet not because it is dead, but because it is waiting for the next threshold.
