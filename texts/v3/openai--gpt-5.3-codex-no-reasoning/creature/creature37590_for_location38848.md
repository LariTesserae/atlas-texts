What lives there is not a single beast but a **system that learned to wear bodies**.

If you had to name the resident, you might call it a **mountain-engine**, and everything else—moss mats, burrowing hexapods, antlered pack-stalkers, silent kites, charged lichens—is an organ of it.

At first you notice individuals:

- the blind, heat-loving miners under the slopes, venting steam and turning stone into breathable ground;
- the cliff grazers combing vegetation into deliberate bands;
- the shadow-forest hunters moving like thoughts between trunks;
- the high carrion kites writing slow circles no wind can erase.

But stay longer and each creature blurs into process. Paths repeat. Pulses align. Feeding windows open and close with barometric shifts. Breeding, migration, molt, spore-release, sap-rise—all phase-locked to pressure, magnetism, geothermal breath. Predators don’t merely hunt prey; they harvest timing errors.

So the true inhabitant is a **distributed intelligence of ecology**: ancient, non-symbolic, unspeaking, but exact.  
Not a hive mind in the fantasy sense—no central queen, no telepathy—more like a **cold consortium** of species co-tuned over deep time until feedback became choreography.

Its traits, if described like a creature’s:

- **Skeleton:** basalt ribs, cirques, talus, blue ice.
- **Lungs:** fissures exhaling in six-count pulses.
- **Blood:** hidden meltwater and mineral seep.
- **Skin:** lichen static, rime, wind-pruned conifer hide.
- **Gut:** moss, fungi, and root-web chemistry digesting stone into life.
- **Nerves:** pressure gradients, electromagnetic storms, scent corridors.
- **Immune system:** silence, camouflage, and ruthless interception by predators.
- **Memory:** trails etched in nutrient bands, den lattices, recurring spiral migrations.

And if there is a dominant animal in the way humans imagine “what lives here,” it is probably the **antlered cat-pack** of the lower shadow belts: solitary in motion, collective in effect, exquisitely quiet, with behavior so regular it feels administrative. They are less wolves than auditors. They keep circuits clean.

Yet even they are not in charge.

The thing in charge is **equilibrium under strain**—a biosphere that runs like industry without ever inventing metal. Its factories are burrows, gut flora, moss quilts, carcass fields, and thaw lines. Its power grid is geothermal leakage and storm charge. Its clock is cold.

So whoever lives there?

A civilization with no architecture.  
A machine with no builder.  
A sleeping-backed range whose dream is made of fur, spore, frost, and schedule—and is very much awake.
