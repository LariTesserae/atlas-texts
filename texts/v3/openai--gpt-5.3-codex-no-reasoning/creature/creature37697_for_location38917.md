What lives there is not one creature but a **pressure-woven civilization of bodies**—a colony ecology so dense it behaves like an organism in its own right.

If I had to name its central resident, I’d call it the **Seamkind**: the collective of blind, heat-reading, condensation-feeding animals that occupy every wet edge of that volcanic pocket. Individually they are tiny, translucent, soft, chitin-lipped, hinge-plated, many-limbed things; together they form living weather, living plumbing, living instinctive engineering.

They do not “inhabit” the wound so much as **complete it**.

---

At first you notice individuals:

- bead-sized crawlers with clear skin and dark pulsing channels,
- flat obsidian filterers that open and shut like hand tools,
- scraper-mouthed crustacean analogues combing invisible biofilms,
- shoulder-length ambush climbers moving in impossible bursts between long stillness,
- drifting vapor-bladders—moth-equivalents made for updrafts and sudden predation.

But after a while, you stop seeing species and start seeing **functions**.  
Everything is part of a circulating system organized around three currencies:

1. **Condensed water**
2. **Thermal gradients**
3. **Microseconds of opportunity**

Their “food web” is tight, local, and continuous. A drop forms; a filter unfolds; a scraper arrives; a hunter waits; a hatchling enters the flow. Death is immediate and almost noiseless. Nothing is wasted long enough to decay into stillness.

---

The true resident, then, might be called a **colony-mind without a brain**.

In fog events—when cold alpine air shears into geothermal breath and the pocket whites out—the entire animal mass shifts position in coordinated reorientation. Curtains unpeel. Clusters migrate. Driplines are re-mapped in minutes. It resembles intelligence not because anyone plans ahead in abstraction, but because the whole system senses and responds as one tactile computation.

Their “technology” is primitive only if you mistake simplicity for lack.  
They practice a direct lithic hydraulics:

- tapping pressure rhythms into stone,
- selecting pebbles by shape,
- plugging seep-holes,
- releasing them,
- steering droplets toward eggs.

No architecture persists, because the rock itself won’t permit permanence.  
So they build in time, not in structure.

---

And at the basin—the milky thermal cradle ringed with amber egg-cases—you meet their reproductive engine: concentric nurseries fed by heat and mineral breath. Hatchings are constant. Recruitment is constant. Attrition is constant. Stability emerges from relentless turnover.

That is who lives there:

- creatures adapted not just to altitude and heat, but to **inconsistency itself**;
- animals for whom optical truth is optional, but touch is law;
- life that evolved inside a geologic fresh-tear where space lies, gravity hesitates, and sequence can briefly misorder.

In another world we might call them vermin, crustaceans, arthropods, filter-feeders, predators.  
Here, those words are too separate.

Here, what lives there is **the Seam**—a crowded, whispering, mineral-fed republic of flesh that turns dripping stone into metabolism, fog into migration, and a mountain’s new wound into a beating interior.
