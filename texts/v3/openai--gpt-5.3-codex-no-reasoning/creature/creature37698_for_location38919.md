What lives there is not a single creature but a **civilization of metabolism**—an animal-plant-mineral engine with no architect and no pause button.

If you insist on picturing a “dominant inhabitant,” it might be this:  
a **highland megaherd organism** made of many species locked together so tightly they function like one distributed body.

- The giant six-limbed ruminants are its muscle and transport.
- The smaller grazers are its precision cutters, taking what the large mouths miss.
- Hide-harvesting cliff arthropods are its groomers and mineral recyclers.
- Moss-rakers and dust-exhalers are its fertilizer pumps.
- Predators are not enemies so much as flow regulators, preventing jams.
- Scavengers and detritivores are its dismantling line.
- Pollinators and spore-carriers are its data couriers, reconnecting fragmented habitats.

So “who lives there?”  
**A moving trophic machine** lives there: countless lineages, each ordinary alone, but together behaving like an organism spread across mountain ranges.

---

At closer range, individual inhabitants feel designed by cold light, unstable ground, and electrical weather:

- **Broad-chested, reflective-furred ungulates** whose coats shed sleet, wick condensation, and build static fields that help flock cohesion in whiteout conditions.
- **Six-limbed high-gravity climber-grazers** with split stance geometry: two limbs for purchase, two for propulsion, two for manipulating forage crusts and scraping mineral salts.
- **Needle-limbed cliff ants** (not true ants, but niche-equivalents) that farm skin salts, fungal films, and dried sweat residues from herd corridors.
- **Filter-backed moss harvesters** that comb low vegetation in sweeping passes, then exhale nutrient-rich particulates that seed the next growth wave downslope.
- **Pack predators with vibration and strain sense**, reading microfractures in ice and fault-shattered rock, steering prey toward terrain failure points with frightening tactical patience.
- **Hand-sized armored peat borers** that warm themselves metabolically, venting steam through resonant spiracles—little walking chimneys in the frost.
- **Mirror-wing pollinators** that turn meadows into flashing signal fields, their synchronized swarms responding to UV, magnetic cues, and electrostatic gradients.
- **Charge-moss consortia** that store windborne electrical potential and dump it in crawling discharges when compressed—living tripwires that can redirect migrations.

Even the “passive” life is active infrastructure:  
lichen etch stone, cushion plants trap heat, root mats pry open mineral seams, microbial films condition bare rock for the next colonists. Soil is not background; it is **under construction**, everywhere, all the time.

---

Behaviorally, the inhabitants are tuned to three clocks:

1. **Immediate clock (seconds to hours):** storm fronts, charge shifts, predator drives, swarm blooms.  
2. **Seasonal clock (months):** thaw pulses, migration braiding, vent-field productivity.  
3. **Geologic clock (decades+):** escarpment collapse, ridge uplift, rerouted highways of life.

That’s why they feel both frantic and conservative. Nothing is still, but little is random.

---

So the truest answer is:

What lives there is a **bright-cold biome of kinetic abundance**, where evolution has favored organisms that are conductive, collaborative, and terrain-literate.  
Not kings of the mountain—**components of a mountain-scale process**.  
A place where life does not merely occupy landscape; it **operates** it.
