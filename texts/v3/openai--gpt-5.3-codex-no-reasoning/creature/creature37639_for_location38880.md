What lives here is something that almost doesn’t count as an organism at first glance.

Not a beast, not a colony, not even a stable species in the way surface life imagines species.  
The resident of this chamber is **a process that occasionally wears a body**.

If you wait long enough by the warm corner where the rill hesitates and mineral crust thins, you begin to see it: a translucent thread, hair-fine, almost invisible against the bright wet stone. It is not truly white; it borrows color from whatever light is passing through ceiling-crystal at the moment—blue when flux is high, pearly when vapor thins. Most of the time it lies still, and you mistake it for a crack filament or a peel of silica skin. Then, over minutes, one end lifts and writes a slow curve through the water film.

It “lives” by riding gradients.

- A few millimeters toward the vent is too hot: proteins loosen, membranes leak.  
- A few millimeters away is too cool: metabolism stalls.  
- Too wet, and it drowns in low oxygen film.  
- Too dry, and salts crystallize through it.

So it keeps to a moving contour—an invisible line where heat, dissolved iron, sulfur traces, and redox potential cross in a narrow band. In that band it feeds, not on plants or prey, but on **chemical disequilibrium**: tiny electron bargains between rock-derived compounds and the oxygen that interior rain brings down from the chamber air. It is less eater than mediator, a living resistor laid across a battery made of basalt and steam.

Its body is modular and temporary. Segments swell, pinch off, and decay. Mineral plates nucleate on its outer sheath; sometimes those plates become so heavy the thread anchors and sloughs, leaving a ghost-cast tube that looks purely geological. Later, another thread—clone, fragment, or unrelated successor—slides through the same cast as if inhabiting an abandoned corridor. Over time, lineage and landscape braid together until you cannot tell whether the channel shaped the life or the life selected the channel.

And that microbial sheen you noticed? That is its weather, its pasture, and its risk.  
When the mat appears, the thread grazes its exudates and the reduced compounds trapped beneath it. When deposition surges, both are entombed under fresh mineral skin. The chamber does not kill dramatically; it **overwrites**. Burial is ordinary here. So is reappearance.

If this world has “technology in stone,” this creature is the biological analog: a soft, self-repairing trace that forms where energy flow is high, disappears when conditions drift, and returns when the circuit closes again. No eyes, no jaws, no haste. Just a patient line of living chemistry that persists by being minimal enough to survive a place where minerals do most of the talking.

So what lives in the geothermal pocket?

A **pale thermochemical thread**—rare, nearly anonymous, perpetually at the edge of erasure—  
and behind it, the deeper inhabitant: the chamber’s own repeating metabolism of heat, water, and crystal, which hosts life briefly the way a spark hosts light.
