What lives there is something that learned, long ago, to stop arguing with the planet.

Not a conqueror species. Not exactly a survivor species, either. More like a *tuning* species—one that discovered that on this world, persistence belongs to what can couple itself to gradients: charge, temperature, strain, vapor, silence, time.

At first you might think the inhabitants are only the sparse biota you can point at: wire shrubs, crust lichens, gliders, ceramic burrowers, kites. Then you spend longer. You notice that none of them are truly separate from the built systems. Their niches and behaviors fit too neatly into the machinery of storms and stone. Their life cycles align with events too periodic at geological scales to be coincidence.

So whoever lives there now may be:

- a thin biosphere adapted to electrostatic weather,
- a distributed machine ecology that can pass for ruin,
- and a lineage of intelligence that dissolved itself into both.

### The probable “people”: lithic-aeriform custodians

If there are still persons in any recognizable sense, they are likely rare, widely spaced, and mostly sedentary—anchored to specific topographies the way tidepool organisms are anchored to rock. Think of beings that are:

- **low-profile and conductive**, built to bleed or route charge rather than avoid it;
- **partially subterranean**, with most vital mass below thermally stable strata;
- **modular**, extending temporary sensor/harvest structures during storm windows;
- **slow in baseline metabolism**, with bursts of high cognition during energetic events.

They would not “live in settlements.” They would live in **catchments**: cliff lips, sinkhole rims, fracture intersections, old installations that function as organs larger than the body. One intelligence per basin. A sibling three hundred kilometers away across a mineral flat. Communication by line-of-sight light when possible, by induced currents through conductive strata when not, by atmospheric perturbation when storms permit.

Their architecture would be indistinguishable from landscape engineering: pylons that are both lightning organs and memory reeds; vaults that are both shelter and archive; lattices that are both net and instrument. Home, body, tool, and sensor all braided.

### How they experience time

Fast weather, slow life.

Daily urgency is real—charge events kill, slope failures erase routes—but cultural timescales are probably measured in:

- erosion depths,
- migration of fracture fronts,
- periodicity of rare condensation blooms,
- drift in storm corridor statistics.

A “season” might be the interval between two phases of conductivity in a ridge network. A “generation” might be the time it takes a lichen fan to mineralize enough substrate to change local charge behavior. Memory would be externalized into durable patterned matter: doped ceramics, strain-coded rock volumes, and maintenance behaviors carried by semi-wild helper organisms.

### What else lives there with them

The small fauna are not pets or livestock; they are **co-functions**.

- **Glider forms** map thermals and may seed microbial films in new shadows.
- **Hexapod burrowers** open grounding paths and keep conductive veins exposed.
- **Scavenger kites** strip transient biomass after condensation pulses, preventing biofilms from insulating key surfaces.
- **Wire shrubs and lichens** are likely both wild and cultivated—biotic components in a planetary electrical ecology.

Predation exists, but probably subtle: interception, parasitic charge theft, habitat sabotage. On this world, eating can be secondary to **energy routing**.

### Why they’re hard to find

Because conspicuousness is maladaptive here.

Anything tall attracts discharge. Anything noisy wastes energy and broadcasts position across impossible distances. Anything centralized dies in one good failure cascade. So the inhabitants—biological, mechanical, or hybrid—favor:

- low signatures,
- redundancy by dispersal,
- dormancy as default state,
- awakening only in narrow environmental windows.

That’s why visitors report “ruins.” They are looking at a civilization whose active phase may be minutes per day, or days per year, synchronized to specific storm geometries.

### In short

Who lives there?

A civilization thinned to near-geology:  
intelligence distributed across ridges, pits, and ancient installations;  
life forms that harvest charge and vapor with monastic efficiency;  
beings for whom stillness is not peace but preparation.

They have not abandoned the world. They have become legible only in the same terms as the world itself—gradient, fracture, field, and return.
