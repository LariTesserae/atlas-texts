What lives there is a people of **listeners and keepers of gradients**—not rulers of this underworld, but a species that survived by becoming exquisitely fluent in its physics.

They are probably not large. In a world where food is mineral-thin and danger travels by vibration, bulk is costly. I imagine them low-slung, strong through the shoulders and hips, with broad, splayed feet that can read the ground like a tongue reads texture. Their gait is deliberate: heel never slams, weight poured forward in controlled increments. They don’t just walk *on* stone; they walk *through* its signals.

They are likely blind, or nearly so. Eyes, if present, are recessed and milky, useful only for glow-contrast at close range. Instead, they map space through layered senses: jawbones tuned to subsonic returns, throat sacs that click and hum in tight coded bursts, skin patches that detect airflow temperature shifts like directional whiskers. Their ears might be less external organs than internal architecture—sinus labyrinths, resonant plates, fluid canals—anatomy shaped into instrument.

Their bodies wear the place. Skin may be matte and mineral-dusted, patterned not by pigment but by abrasion and salt staining. Protective keratin or chitin scales over shins, forearms, spine: not full armor, just where rock and predator meet flesh most often. Hands are dexterous and hard-tipped, nails adapted as scrapers, picks, and signalers. Teeth include grinding plates for mineral-grazers and sulfur-crust foods, with finer incisors for tendon work and fiber splitting.

Socially, they live in **moving knots** rather than permanent settlements. Camps are seasonal only in the behavioral sense—rotation by airflow reliability, prey migration drumming, collapse forecasts. A “home” is a remembered route network plus stored caches plus obligations at waystations. They return, maintain, leave, and return again. Ownership is probably temporal and practical: *this niche is ours while we keep it sealed; this bridge is everyone’s while it stands.*

Their intelligence expresses as maintenance of equilibrium. They are conservative in the literal survival sense: novelty can kill chambers. So innovation is ritualized, tested in peripheral hollows, adopted only after long proof. A new tool isn’t celebrated; it is audited across generations. Children are taught not bravery but calibration—how hard to strike stone, how long an echo may live before becoming a lure, how to smell stressed crystal before a fault sighs open.

They herd some species indirectly (guiding grazers via vibration fences), scavenge others, and hunt with traps that exploit predictability: tuned pillars that trigger flight into funnels, dust screens that reveal pressure-waves, conductive pits that stun shell-backed swarms. They farm in the underworld way—cultivating microbial mats and mineral films, seeding vent crusts, rotating harvest surfaces so chemistry can recover.

Their language is likely multimodal and local:  
- short tongue-click clusters for identity and intent,  
- throat-hums for distance/location tags,  
- stone taps for route-state updates,  
- scent marks for long-lived warnings (predator active, collapse likely, cache empty).  

Poetry, if they have it, might be mnemonic route-song: not metaphor but encoded survival maps. Religion, if present, is probably about **continuity**—the moral good is keeping loops unbroken: airflow, food chains, tool reuse, ancestral routes, predator balances. Water would be taboo not as impurity, but as omen of systemic failure.

They leave artifacts exactly like the ones you describe because they think in **afterlives of material**. Bone becomes marker lattice, then needle, then ash additive for seal-clay. Copper cycles blade → scraper → wire → resonant strip. Obsidian flakes are cataloged by fracture tone. Nothing is inert; everything is future function.

Physically and culturally, they might seem austere to surface eyes. But in their own context they are rich: rich in micro-distinctions, in memory depth, in collaborative sensing. A child learns hundreds of named drafts, dozens of “silences” (each meaning something different), and the biographies of predators by scar-pattern alone. Their art could be acoustic sculpture and reflected biolight geometry—works you can’t fully perceive unless you stand in the right thermal current at the right pulse interval.

So: whoever lives there is ancient without monuments, adaptive without expansionist hunger, clever without industrial excess. A people made not by sunlight and seasons, but by **pressure, resonance, scarcity, and repetition**—tense, precise, communal, and always listening for the first impossible sound of change.
