What lives there is not a creature so much as a **system that learned to wear the shape of life**.

At first glance you’d call it flora: mats, plumes, tube-forests, needle choirs. But those are just the visible organs. The true inhabitant is a distributed metabolic fabric spread across terraces and basins—a planetwide dry-biochemical guild that blurs microbe, mineral process, and atmospheric engine. It has no single body, no center, no face. It lives as repeating districts of function.

If it can be said to have “individuals,” they are colonies like:

- **Light-drink mats** (matte black, velvet-dull): carbon-rich films that harvest harsh noon light and bind airborne trace compounds.
- **Amber pressure towers**: translucent tubular growths where gas gradients are cultivated the way trees on wet worlds cultivate sap flow.
- **Silver chime plumes**: conductive needle arrays that bleed static charge, align dust flux, and broadcast spores/flakes on wind-sheets.
- **Brittle fan canopies**: high, sacrificial structures that fracture intentionally, becoming seed-dust for downwind colonization.

None of these live alone for long. Each is a phase in a cycle: active membrane → rigid scaffold → conductive ruin → feedstock for the next growth front. On this world, evolution favored **phase-transition ecologies** over predator-prey ecologies. Nothing chases. Nothing hides. Fitness is about persistence through conversion.

So who lives there?

### 1) The Rooted Chemotrophs
Closest to the ground are dense films and low colonies that “eat” mineral potential: oxidation gradients, trace atmospheric gases, electrostatic differentials. They are closest analog to microbes, but many form macroscopic tissues of silica, metal salts, and carbon polymers. They anchor entire local food-web equivalents by fixing volatile compounds into stable, reusable matrices.

### 2) The Atmospheric Harvesters
The tall stalks and tube systems act like dry lungs and distillation columns. In lieu of water transport, they circulate non-water solvents, superheated gases, and charged particulates. They condense what little can be condensed from thin warm air, not for hydration but for reaction control.

### 3) The Electro-mineral Engineers
Some colonies are less “plants” than living field-shapers. They exploit planetary magnetism and electrostatic charge to sort dust, attract specific ions, and steer grain movement. The neat crescent drifts of iron and parallel belts of grit are partly geophysics, partly guided by these organisms’ conductive architectures.

### 4) The Lithogenic Successors
When a colony “dies,” it does not rot. It vitrifies, crystallizes, or collapses into graded particulate. Specialized successor films colonize those remains, catalyzing conversion from biostructure to mineral crust. This is decomposition translated into a dry industrial idiom: not decay, but **materials processing**.

---

If you insist on one name for the inhabitant, it might be:

## **The Quiet Foundry Biome**

A biosphere whose metabolism resembles automated manufacturing because both are, at core, controlled transformation of matter and energy. The vent pulses, crystal blooms, foam lattices, and magnetic sorting lanes are not separate from life; they are life’s extended physiology, coupled to geology so tightly that agency becomes hard to localize.

There may also be rare mobile entities—thin-shelled drifters, charged flake swarms, crawling crust-rafts—but if they exist, they are peripheral. The dominant resident is sessile, distributed, and patient.

It does not dream of forests, oceans, or beasts.  
It dreams in terraces, gradients, and repeating hexagons.  
It lives by classification: gas to film, film to scaffold, scaffold to shard, shard to dust, dust to growth again.  

On that high unfinished bone, the thing that lives there is **life after urgency**—an ecology that became indistinguishable from planet-scale process.
