What lives there is not one thing but a **culture of continuities**.

At first you might expect a singular guardian species—something monumental to match breathing mountains. But the longer you stay, the clearer it becomes: the primary inhabitant is a **distributed intelligence made of people, plants, machines, and watersheds**, none complete without the others.

If you insist on “who,” then the nearest answer is this:

## The Terrace Keepers

They are human or near-human in body, but ecologically extended. Small populations, moving in loops rather than fixed settlements, they inhabit shelters the way herons use branches: repeatedly, lightly, with memory. They don’t own territory so much as **attune to catchments**—each lineage responsible for a chain of terraces, fog membranes, and migration corridors over generations.

They are recognizable by adaptation to wet heat and conductive ecology:  
- mineral-stained sandals and calf wraps for grip on living stone,  
- skin films or oils that deter fungal overload without sterilizing the microbiome,  
- layered capes that shed rain and harvest static,  
- tools grown as much as fabricated—ceramic handles overgrown with root-friendly textures, charge-safe baskets, resonant stakes for reading subsoil water movement.

They speak in ordinary language, yes, but also in **maintenance acts**: redirecting vine weight before a cliff calves, tuning a humming spine by fractions of pitch, opening or closing mesh under moss depending on migration season. Their calendars are hydrological and acoustic—“before the upper west wall starts singing in thirds,” “after the basin has risen two hand-widths.”

They are not priests of ancient tech, nor engineers dominating it. More like **orchardists of systems** old enough to be called ancestral and alive enough to require daily negotiation.

## Their companions and co-inhabitants

What “lives there” includes several semi-wild alliances:

- **Capacitor epiphyte guilds**, cultivated in patterns that feed pollinators and bleed off dangerous charge before storms.  
- **Vine intelligences** (not sentient in a human sense, but responsive, selective, trainable), guided into living bridges and flood brakes.  
- **Browser caravans** that the Keepers shadow at distance, using their routes as indicators of safe footing, mineral exposure, and pathogen drift.  
- **Glider colonies** nesting in old pylon forests; their flight timing predicts pressure changes better than instruments in some valleys.  
- **Stone-singing microbial films** in resonant gullies, whose presence (or sudden muting) functions like an early warning network for slope failure.

Predators live there too, respected as regulators. Keepers rarely kill them unless a pattern has broken badly. Usually they alter water access or cover density instead—nudging behavior through habitat rather than confrontation.

## And beneath all of that: the Old Steward System

The embedded lattice, spines, and field projectors are still active, but not centralized. Think of it as a **planetary-scale caretaker reduced to watershed dialects**. It doesn’t command; it modulates: water lift at dawn, fog sorting, conductive balancing, boundary chords, glyph updates. The Keepers can tune it, petition it, and sometimes argue with it, but not wholly rewrite it.

Over centuries, this produced a hybrid resident:  
**the biome itself behaves like an organism with many minds**.

So what lives there?

- People who measure responsibility in monsoons.  
- Animals that navigate by harmonics in wet stone.  
- Plants that store weather as charge and release it as light and signal.  
- Machines that forgot empire and learned patience.  
- And the long, breathing mountain-range-body formed by all of them together.

In another place you might call this wilderness with ruins.  
Here, it is closer to a **commons that is awake**.
