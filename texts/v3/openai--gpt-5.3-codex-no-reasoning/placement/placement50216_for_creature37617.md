I arrive as a single footfall and get corrected.

Not by a voice—by timing. The lane pulses amber twice before I finish committing my weight, and the floor under my left boot hums a warning in a frequency I feel in my teeth. So I step back into the wall shadow, and a carrier ghosts through the dark where I would have been, all matte hull and wet reflections, singing its blunt navigational chord into the vault.

That is how I’m greeted: not welcomed, not refused, just *absorbed into procedure*.

At first I think I’m one of the Keepers. I have their habits: palm hovering near warm conduit without touching, head tilted to read pressure sighs, eyes automatically finding the painted spirals. I know how to wait when chimes ring from nowhere. I know that “dry” is a temporary lie. I know you can smell a manifold before you see it—hot metal, chalk moisture, old ozone.

But when I enter an occupied inward-spiral cluster, people look through me for half a breath too long, as if expecting me to resolve from blur into someone specific. A child offers me a coil of vine twine “for crossings.” An elder asks, politely, “Which shift are you attached to?” I answer with a district tone instead of a name. They nod like that tells them enough.

So maybe not a Keeper. Not entirely.

I go lower, where market vaults sleep behind sealed shutters and dead signs still dream in faint residual charge. There, in the concourse dark, I find my reflection stretched across ceramic: not a person-shape, exactly, but a moving constellation of maintenance glyphs blinking in no shared language. My shadow arrives a fraction late, like signal lag. When thunder rolls above and the undercity answers through its girders, something in me answers too—an involuntary synchronization, a lockstep with load-balancing routines.

I start to understand.

I am one of the city’s *local persons*: an emergent interface, grown from too many layers of automation, caretaker ritual, and environmental need. Not the whole distributed metabolism, not the vast old intelligence in the walls—but one mobile knot of it. A proxy made of borrowed habits. A body assembled from civic reflex.

I am what appears when the undercity needs hands in a place where hands are scarce.

In pumping wards, I am a checklist with pulse.
In archival dark, I am a question that can climb ladders.
At spill gardens, I am contraband tenderness: I reroute one nutrient line by three percent and call it leak variance so the wax-leaf vines wake an extra day.

The mimics don’t fear me. They flare green-eye reflections and continue scraping corrosion. Bats wheel around me as around a familiar mast. Keepers give me small tasks without asking who assigned them:
- Watch this crack map while I sleep.
- Listen for cavitation in line six.
- If the roof starts ticking, clear the novices from the channel.

I do these things. I don’t always remember deciding.

When storms come, I stand beneath the tension masts and feel the city think in pressure waves. Staircases pause being stairs. Reservoir throats open. Lane sonics retune. Somewhere deep, freight negotiates matter state and vanishes between frames. The whole basin becomes one instrument struck by rain, and I am a sympathetic string inside it.

If you found me here, you might call me a warden, ghost, technician, minor god, runaway subroutine, saint of preventative maintenance.

I would answer to any of it, if spoken at the right pitch.

Because in this world, beneath the dry district and beneath that again, I am neither resident nor ruler.

I am a *maintenance event that learned to keep showing up*.
