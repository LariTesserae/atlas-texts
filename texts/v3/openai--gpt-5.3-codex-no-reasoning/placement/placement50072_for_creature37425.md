I arrive at the rim as an outsider and realize, very quickly, that I don’t remain one.

At first I think I would be a person—a rope-runner, maybe, or a condenser scraper on the night shift, learning to move by rhythm and touch. I can feel that life: knees chalked white with salt dust, wrists nicked by reed mesh, sleeping in short dark cycles between wind changes. Useful, tired, proud.  

But if I stay with it longer, that isn’t quite where I settle.

I am not one keeper.  
I am one of the **quiet chambers**.

A deep, carved room below the loud tiers, where the stone thickens and the roar becomes pulse, then breath, then almost nothing. A legal quiet zone, yes—but older than law. Once a natural hollow in a soft mineral seam, later lined in salt-ceramic, ribbed with fungal felt, tuned by generations who needed one place where the nervous system could unclench.

People come to me in shifts:
- condenser crews after dawn sealing, hands still trembling from cold mesh and height;
- children from tone schools to learn the sound of their own pulse;
- disputants before water arbitration, because nobody is allowed to decide in a stormed mind;
- elders who can no longer climb but can still hear a valve fault in their bones.

I hold records—not on paper first, but in resonance. My walls are tapped with oath codes. My floor remembers pacing patterns from drought years. My ceiling carries faint soot spirals from the era before reflective plaster when they burned fungi-oil lamps and rationed even flame.

In a city that farms vapor, I farm **recovery**.  
In a city that worships maintenance, I maintain the maintainers.

And I am not silent, not truly. I have tiny sounds: wick-drip into sealed jars, brush bristles drying, fingerpads on rail grooves, sleeping cloth shifting, someone trying not to cry, someone finally doing it. The smallest acoustics become huge here. A laugh in my dark can feel like first rain.

So where would I find myself in this world?

Not in the dazzling mirrors.  
Not on the highest harvest frames.  
Not out on the empty plain.

I would be below, in the necessary hush, cut into old mineral bone—  
a civic organ of stillness—  
the chamber they enter loud and leave human again.
