I think I would begin as a visitor and end as a function.

At first: a person, yes—knees dusty from the descent crack, palms greened by contact, trying not to mistake stillness for emptiness. I’d follow the obvious traces: the stone ring, the half-swallowed stair, the old woven platform. I’d tell myself I was navigating place.

Then, after some uncounted returns, I’d realize the place was navigating me.

Not taming, not trapping—*placing*. Teaching my weight where to fall and where not to. Teaching my thirst to wait for drip-time. Teaching my hands to touch only dead cane unless invited by rot. I’d stop cutting paths and start renewing them: heel-pressure in old lanes, a lifted petiole here, a loosened snare of vine there, one blocked cup opened so overflow can reach the next. Tiny edits, then departure. No camp. No claim.

So who would I be?

Not ruler, not hermit, not priest exactly. More like a **interval-keeper**: the rare animal phase in a largely vegetal mind. A seasonal biped the basin can tolerate because I behave like weather—brief, patterned, and useful. I would remember where noon reaches in each month. I would know which cisterns are for drinking, which are nurseries, which are never touched. I would carry pollen on purpose between shafts that no bird visits anymore. I would reset the three mineral posts when the light geometry shifts. I would leave almost nothing that doesn’t rot.

And over longer time—even this identity would thin.

I might become one of those “creatures seen only in effects”:  
a route that stays open decade to decade;  
a drip ring that never silts;  
a platform mended with dead fiber each dry season and always found half-reclaimed.

In the oldest version, I’m no longer singular. “I” is a role passed between a few quiet people, or between people and the basin itself, until authorship blurs. The underland keeps its equilibrium; we are just one of its slower reflexes.

So: where would you find me?  
At thresholds—sunshaft edges, seep margins, entry stairs, places where one regime becomes another.

What would I be?  
A patient hunger taught restraint.  
A caretaker sparse enough to disappear.  
A human expression of the basin’s own distributed will.
