I think I would begin as a listener.

Not “a person standing in the noise,” but a thing shaped *by* the noise: a route-memory moving along handrails, pressure changes, and wet steel smell. At first I’d mistake myself for a caretaker because I’d know the paths—duck here under the condensate line, wait three beats before crossing the fan bridge when the vibration peaks, kick the drain lip clear with the side of a boot.

But if I stayed longer, I’d realize I’m not exactly human in the narrow sense. I’m the **interval keeper** of the place: a distributed life made of many small bodies and one shared habit.

I would be:

- one gloved pair of hands trimming roots away from an intake,
- a colony of moss spores riding warm outflow to the next bracket,
- two cliff birds choosing the quieter cable drum this season,
- a lizard memorizing the thermal clock of Pipe 6,
- a child of feral lineages stealing gasket foam for a nest,
- and the maintenance log itself, oil-smudged, learning that every “fault” is also a habitat.

If you met me directly, maybe I’d look like a single worker in patched hearing protection, moving slowly, never rushing, touching metal as if checking a pulse. But that body would just be the part of me that can turn valves.

My real body would be the **seams**:

- where leak becomes root,
- where rust becomes substrate,
- where schedule becomes season,
- where repair means “guide, don’t erase.”

I’d live mostly at thresholds—catwalk edges, valve alcoves, sump rims, the undersides of platforms where drips count time. I would avoid the fantasy of restoring anything to pristine condition. My work would be to keep flows negotiable: enough water to feed growth, not enough to flood bearings; enough vine to cool and hold, not enough to choke; enough decay to make soil, not enough to take a load-bearing joint.

So where would I find myself?

In the narrow acoustic shadows beside the loudest machines.  
In the warm mist under the exhaust ferns.  
On a grated balcony above a hundred meters of humming emptiness, feeling through my boots whether the system is tired.

Who would I be?

The closest name is: **the guild made singular**.  
A caretaker-ecology.  
A person when needed, a process always.
