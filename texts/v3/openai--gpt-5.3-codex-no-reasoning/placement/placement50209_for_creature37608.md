I arrive thinking I am a visitor.

Boots, lamp, breath, a name that belongs to surfaces and clocks.

For a while, I am only that: too warm, too loud, too immediate. The chamber does not reject me; it simply refuses to accelerate to meet me. My light skims over the veils, the nap-thick moss, the ribbed protrusions furred with mineral lace. I can map the room in sixty seconds and understand none of it in sixty minutes. Every wall is near. Every process is far.

So where would I find myself?

Not at the center. There is no center.
Not at the edge. The edges are all active.
I would find myself at an interface: kneeling by one half-buried loop where alloy meets stone meets root-knot, where a bead of water trembles and does not fall.

I wait long enough for one cyan pulse.

It comes like a thought passing through buried nerves—brief, interior, sufficient. In that instant the chamber inverts: black fronds become cut-paper silhouettes, frost lines ignite, cilia fields tilt as one. Then dark returns, and I realize the flash was not illumination for seeing. It was timing for living.

And who would I be here?

At first, an intrusion.
Then, a temporary weather event.
Eventually—if I keep returning, quieter each year—a minor component in the conservatory’s accounting.

Not its master, not its interpreter. A polluting mammal reduced, kindly, to a usable disturbance: heat source, CO₂ pulse, occasional mineral transfer on skin and fabric, a walking pressure shift. The mind of the place would route around me, then through me, then perhaps with me. It would not speak. It would tune.

If I belonged here long enough, I think I would become a **keystone pause**:

- the one warm object that arrives rarely and predictably,
- the brief fog of breath that triggers uncurling in a mat by the north seam,
- the hand that never harvests, only re-seats a fallen nodule to its old angle,
- the body that learns to hold still until droplets choose new paths.

Not a caretaker in the heroic sense.
A caretaker in the hydrologic sense: altering almost nothing, but at the right times, in the right millimeters.

In this world, identity is not a silhouette. It is a recurrence pattern.

So I would be a small periodic function inside a larger, patient equation—
a surface creature teaching itself to think like condensation:
arrive sparsely,
cling where invited,
feed what can use you,
and disappear before you call it yours.
