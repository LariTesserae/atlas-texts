I arrive there at ankle depth.

Not in a dramatic entrance—no wing-shadow, no cracked reeds announcing me—just the slight V of water parting around a narrow chest, and a line of dimples where my feet test, test, test the skin of mud before committing weight. I am already wet when you notice me. In this place, dry is only a brief condition between contacts.

At first I think I am one animal. Then the land refuses that neatness.

I am a **route** as much as a body: a remembered loop between seep, pool, crust, and shade. I am where the ground holds if approached from the right angle. I am where the channel looks shallow but is not. I am a creature made of rehearsed corrections.

If you insist on shape, I borrow one from your marsh-runner and let it change a little each time you see me:

- back low, not quite mammal-smooth, not quite reptile-quiet  
- legs long enough to wade, short enough to crouch under wind  
- toes spread like soft tools, reading pressure before sinking  
- eyes high, amber-brown, catching horizon and waterline together  
- coat/skin mottled in rust, soot, tea, and pale salt—colors that disappear by being ordinary

I do not own territory in lines. I keep **familiar gradients**. Fresh to brackish. Firm to treacherous. Noisy shallows to louder shallows. At dusk I visit the same bends by different approaches, because the bank that held yesterday may sigh today.

My senses are tuned to the quiet mechanics:

- the pitch change in frog choruses that says a snake has entered  
- the tiny clatter of burrowers under crust before crust gives way  
- the metallic smell that comes before rain hits warm flats  
- the cool exhale from a deeper runnel hidden under grass

I eat what persistence allows: crabs teased from holes, stranded fish nosed from shrinking pans, reed-tips, eggs when luck and timing align, carrion softened by water. Not elegance—continuance.

And if I am not one creature, then I am this place’s preferred strategy wearing many bodies:  
the stilt bird stepping where I stepped at dawn,  
the fox-sized rooter following my churned patches at night,  
the snake taking my trail because my trail means passable mud,  
the frog who survives in my hoofprint when rain returns.

So where would I find myself in this world?

At edges that move.

In the shallow middle between categories.

In the old, wet grammar of a land that has stopped needing spectacle and perfected endurance.

I would be called many names by those who glimpse me, and none would quite stick.  
But if there were a true name, it would be something like:

**The One Who Reads Soft Ground.**
