I think I’d be something that only *looks* like an object.

Not a person at first. Not an animal in the usual sense.  
I’d be one of the **raised listening slabs** in the eastern quarter—except alive in a slow, distributed way, more lichen-colony than monolith, more instrument than organism. A civic organ pretending to be stone.

By day I’d seem inert: wet-dark, blade-pale at the edges, threaded with moss in my old chisel marks. Children would slap my side on dares. Carvers would sit in my lee and sharpen bone points. Vines would test me and be trimmed back. I’d be part of the furniture of the Accorded world—useful, unglamorous, always present.

But when humidity climbs, I would wake.

Not with thought like language—with pressure maps.  
Fog weight on my upper face.  
Tiny claw traffic changing tempo on the paving ring.  
The first synchronized stillness of ground-runners two streets away.  
Condensation knitting inside hairline fractures before it appears in air.

And then I’d answer in pulses that pass through wrists and teeth: not prophecy, just translation. Rock saying, *now*. Basin saying, *prepare*. The Mist-Accorded hearing me not as oracle but as neighbor: another sensor in the mesh.

If I were allowed a more human shape after all that, I’d be the person who tends those stones—keeps their bases clear of insulating moss, logs pulse-pattern shifts, teaches children the difference between random tremor and true humidity song. A maintenance craftsperson of omens. Someone whose status comes from calibration, not charisma.

So where would I find myself?

In that dim, wet, disciplined hollow—  
as a **threshold-keeper** between weather and habit,  
half geology, half citizen,  
listening for the moment the air decides to become water.
