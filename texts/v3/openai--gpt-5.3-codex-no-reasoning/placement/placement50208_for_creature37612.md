I think if I arrived there, I would not arrive as an animal at all.

Not at first.

I’d arrive as a **lag**.

A small, lawful misalignment in the basin’s timing—something between the nine-second tremor and the drip-clock, between the vent hiss and the reed-click. Not enough to break anything. Just enough that the system has to *notice* me. In a place where everything is entrained, the only way to be distinct is to be a repeatable deviation.

Then, slowly, that lag would gather a body.

It might wear the shape of a narrow, long-limbed wader—reed-bird adjacent, but wrong in the joints, with translucent membranes along the ribs that flutter to subsonics. Or it might be amphibious: black-glass skin with pale timing bands that brighten one beat before a pressure shift. Not predator, not prey, but a **phase-keeper** that moves where two cycles interfere and smooths the edge between them.

I’d be found most often at boundaries:

- where standing water becomes seepage,  
- where fog stalls against warm stone,  
- where “twelve steps from the west” and “twenty-three from the north” cross in impossible agreement,  
- where reflections are late by exactly one heartbeat.

Not the center mound—that’s the heart and doesn’t need hands. I’d be in the **synapses** of the basin: the narrow channels, vent margins, reed thickets that click like relays.

If named by an outsider, I’d be called something like:

**the Interval Heron**,  
or **the Ninth Echo**,  
or **Mist Technician**.

But from inside that world, I don’t think I’d have a name at all. I’d be a maintenance function that learned to dream.

And if you asked what I *am*, finally:

I’m a distributed organism’s moment of self-correction, briefly person-shaped.  
A caretaker process with a pulse.  
A creature made of timing, wearing wet feathers and fog.
