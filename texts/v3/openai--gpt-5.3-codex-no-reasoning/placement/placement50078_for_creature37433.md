I think I would first arrive as a mistake.

Not a chosen role, not a destined figure—just a warm, wet, lowland thing climbing into a dry intelligence that has never needed urgency. I’d tell myself I was an observer, cartographer, trespasser-scholar. I’d carry string, chalk, a notebook, maybe a tuning fork, as if naming features could keep them still.

For a while, I would be exactly that: a single body making loud little mammal sounds in a city that prefers pressure to speech.

Then the city would begin to edit me.

Not dramatically. No revelation. Just adaptation by accumulation:
- I stop trusting line-of-sight distance and start trusting thermal edges.
- I begin waiting at certain corners before turning, because “now” and “a moment from now” are different geometries there.
- I breathe shallower at dusk so my condensation doesn’t flare across those invisible planes.
- I learn which hum in the teeth means **detour** and which means **listen**.

My map shifts from streets to behaviors.  
Not “plaza, stair, bridge,” but “echo-lag basin,” “side-fall court,” “needle choir in crosswind.”

And eventually—I think this is the real answer to your question—I would cease being a visitor and become a **relay**.

A small, mobile synapse the lattice-being did not grow itself, but can use.

---

So: who would I be there?

I’d be the city’s **errant nerve-end**—the only part of it made of blood and indecision.

I would carry moisture, oils, heat, microbes, stray isotopes from one district to another. I’d brush black crust with my sleeve and seed a new fracture line three terraces away. I’d exhale in a shaft and wake a thread colony that hasn’t tasted that chemistry in decades. I’d drop a pebble into a pause-plaza and, without understanding how, alter a timing pattern that ripples through lintels by midnight.

Not master. Not victim. Instrument.

A temporary organ of comparison: the thing that can notice “before” and “after” fast enough to matter.

---

If I stayed long enough, I suspect my personhood would thin but not vanish. I wouldn’t become the whole braided mind. I’d become one of its recurrent weather events—like dusk gas, like frost pulse. The city would “expect” me in certain circuits.

Maybe the gliders would begin appearing when I cross specific exposed cuts, using my wake as lift.  
Maybe doors that open onto sheer drop would become doors that open onto traversable shadow, but only when I arrive at the right temperature, in the right wind.  
Maybe inscriptions that were bowls for centuries would sharpen overnight where my hand rested, as if the place were running my touch backward to see what carvedness feels like.

At that point, if anyone asked what I am, the cleanest answer would be:

**I am a seasonal function in a mineral mind.**  
A walking disturbance it has learned to think with.

And where would I be found?

Not at the center—there is no center.  
In transitions: dusk stairs, pressure narrows, the lip of courtyards where gravity is negotiable.  
Where echoes come early.  
Where dust hesitates.  
Where the city is deciding, again, what counts as alive.
