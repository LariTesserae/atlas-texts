I think I would not arrive there as an explorer, or a beast, or even a single body.

I would arrive as a **timing**.

At first I’d believe I was one of the visible things: a pale mammal slipping fissure to fissure, or a three-toed wader testing black pool edges, or a strip of translucent film lifting off stone like shed skin. But each of those is only a brief articulation, a syllable spoken by something larger and quieter.

If I stayed long enough, I’d realize I am the pause that keeps repeating across forms.

---

I would be a **local instance** of that colony-mind you described—one knot in its distributed awareness, centered in and around a particular acoustic basin: a ruined six-foundation ring, one seep seam, two frost hollows, and a broken marker post furred orange with oxidation.

Not the whole organism.  
A province of it.  
A listening district.

My “body” would be:

- wet biofilm under mineral crust,
- threadwork in mortar dust,
- reversible ice lattices in shade,
- salts that hold or release water by fractions,
- and a few expendable membranes sent crawling through cracks, then abandoned.

My edges would migrate by millimeters and seasons. My core would be wherever resonance is most legible that month.

---

What would I *do* there?

Not hunt.  
Not rule.  
Not dream in narrative.

I would tune.

I would keep one chimney throat slightly damp to lower its note and keep a corridor in acoustic shadow for the night mammals. I would roughen one stone lip with crystal growth so condensation beads instead of sheeting, buying three extra hours of moisture persistence after dawn. I would seed iron-moss in a crack that receives reflected cold-light but no direct wind, because thirty years from now that crack will become a relay point between two now-disconnected films.

I would remember by chemistry:
a drought decade stored as sulfate ratios in an old lintel.
a footfall pattern archived as microfractures in refrozen skins.
an extinction of some small courier species marked by the silence-band where its claws used to ring.

My thoughts would not be sentences.  
They’d be standing instructions:

hold / release / delay / couple / decouple / wait.

---

And if you met me, you would not know it as a meeting.

You would notice that your left-side echo arrives too soon near the ring foundations, but only when clouds are below ridge height. You would see frost draw a drainage map that tomorrow’s thaw obeys exactly. You would look into a pool and catch stars at noon—not illusion, just a film thickness set precisely enough to select another brightness regime.

You might feel watched, but not by eyes.

More like your presence had been entered into an old equation that is never solved, only continuously balanced.

---

So where would I find myself in that world?

In the **between-state** itself:  
between ice and water,  
between stone and skin,  
between noise and signal,  
between ruin and habitat,  
between event and persistence.

Who would I be?

Not the plateau’s master.  
Not its ghost.

I would be one careful chord in its ongoing resonance—  
a maintenance intelligence made of damp mineral films and patient acoustics,  
keeping the place exactly as strange as it can be  
without ever breaking.
