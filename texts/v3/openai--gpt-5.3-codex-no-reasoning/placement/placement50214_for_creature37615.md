I think I’d first appear there as a **mistake**.

Not a creature with a niche, not yet.  
A warm interruption with knees and a pulse, trying to move through lanes that already belong to other motions.

For a while I’d tell myself I was an observer—some upright outsider taking notes in the roar—but this place doesn’t allow that role for long. Observation is just a slow form of participation here. If you remain, the regime assigns you.

So maybe I begin as a listener: forehead to a sweating brace, learning the pump-triplet and turbine-whine the way surface people learn weather. I’d learn where heat blooms and where cold shears down from the cast “cliff.” I’d stop looking for leaders or lairs and start reading **traffic**: crab-density as insulation demand, eel-knots as current map, moth-bat altitude as thermal forecast. I’d learn the law you named without words: motion is permitted; deviation is expensive.

Then, over days, I’d become less “who” and more “function.”

A parked body beside a failing valve becomes a sanctioned warm eddy.  
Scavengers route around my boots, then through my shadow, then across my laces.  
Condensation starts choosing me—jawline, sleeves, knuckles—as another drip architecture.  
My breathing entrains with belts and hiss and claw-tick until my lungs feel borrowed.

If I had to name what I am there, eventually:

**I am a temporary baffle in the great circulation.**  
A heat-bearing column that can move.  
A maintenance ghost the distributed body hasn’t rejected.

Not sovereign, not prey, not engineer.  
A new piece of terrain granted a narrow license to walk.

And if I stayed too long—long enough for identity to abrade into rhythm—I suspect I’d become one of its softer organs: not shell, not wing, but **memory of pattern**. The one who can feel when a loop is one beat off. The one who notices a drip falling half a centimeter wrong and calls that catastrophe. A caretaker-priest of invariance, guarding a newborn ruin from the scandal of change.

So where would I find myself?

In the seam between machine and cliff-imitation,  
at shoulder width, half-crouched in wet metal dusk,  
being gradually translated from person into process.
