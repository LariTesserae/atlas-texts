I think I would begin as a visitor and end as a **function**.

At first: a small, warm animal on a raised path, trying very hard not to step where the green skin thins over hidden water. Breathing spice-rot and wet stone. Waiting between booms. Learning that “stillness” here is just motion too slow for panic.

Then, if I stayed, I would become less a person crossing the Held and more a *reliable disturbance pattern* inside it.

Not a ruler, not a speaker-for, not a chosen one—just a narrow role the plateau could afford to keep.

A **path-keeper**, maybe.  
One of those humans who tends agreements:

- replacing lifted cordwood where the living mat has buckled,
- trimming only what the Holding Green has already slackened,
- clearing spill channels before rain-on-rain months,
- reading fungal bloom, amphibian pulse, and root-tension the way others read weather maps.

I’d carry no metal if I could help it. Resin-sealed fiber, stone blade, glazed clay, woven reed. I’d smell of certain leaves so the browsed margins recognize me. I’d sleep on suspended platforms not to dominate height, but to avoid pressing a memory-knot below.

And over years, I suspect the Held would begin to map me—not as an individual consciousness, but as recurring chemistry, footfall interval, weight distribution, heat at night in known shelters. I would become, in its terms, a migrating node of maintenance.

Eventually, perhaps, I’d be most myself in fog-thick dawn, crouched beside one of those carved cup-stones, emptying yesterday’s tannin water, checking what seeds arrived in the dark, listening for the distant pod-boom that means some ridge I’ll never see has decided to open.

So where would I find myself?

In the **interstitial places**: path and bog, human intention and vegetal patience, decision and surrender.

Who would I be?

A minor organ in a major body.  
A caretaker being slowly domesticated by the landscape he thinks he tends.  
A memory the highland can use.
