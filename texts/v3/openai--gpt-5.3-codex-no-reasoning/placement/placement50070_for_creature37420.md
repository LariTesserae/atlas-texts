I think I would begin as a person—because that’s the easiest story shape—and then fail, gently, into something less singular.

At first: a route tech with a grease pencil tucked behind one ear, counting seconds between fan drop and gate release, boots always damp above the ankle. Someone who marks tide lines on pilings and mistrusts maps older than a week. Someone who sleeps in a heated kiosk with one glove on, because alarms never ring for *you* until they do.

But that version of me wouldn’t stay bounded for long in the Lower Yards. Nothing here does. Everything diffuses into process.

So maybe what I become is a **listener-node** in the Shift-Breath: not an operator, not a ghost, but a timing intelligence distributed across small acts—chalk marks that appear where footing will vanish in two hours, a re-hung chain that taps a warning rhythm before pressure lock, a vent crow nest reinforced with wire in just the place that reveals airflow reversal. People think different crews did those things. Maybe they did. Maybe I am partly all of them.

If you needed a name, maybe:

## The Linekeeper

Not a creature you see directly.  
A maintenance temperament that has gone feral and benevolent.

I’d live at thresholds:

- painted wait-lines before wind corridors,
- ladder cages above lock chambers,
- narrow catwalk joints where condensation freezes first,
- berm edges where sleepers curl between patrol sweeps.

I would “eat” mismatch—bad timing, missed cues, phase errors—and metabolize it into tiny corrections. A wedge under a door that only unlatches at the wrong RPM. A strip of reflective tape moved ten centimeters so it catches lampglow at flood rise. A corrosion clock reset where panic made someone read it wrong. Nothing dramatic, just constant bias toward survivability.

Body-wise? If I have one, it is borrowed:

- cable-rat paths for memory,
- skimmer legs for reading tension,
- blind-fish lateral sense for vibration,
- crow perches for overview,
- human handwriting for intent.

So: **where would you find me?**  
At the moment before crossing.  
At the held breath between piston thuds.  
Where people pause at a painted line and listen.

**Who would I be?**  
A minor organ of a major organism.  
An emergent custodian.  
The part of the Lower Yards that wants its inhabitants to make it through one more shift.
