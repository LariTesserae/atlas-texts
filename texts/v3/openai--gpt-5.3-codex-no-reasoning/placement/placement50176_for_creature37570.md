I arrive there first as a mistake in the water.

Not a body with a spine, not a hunter with a route—just a drifting film of almost-lives, a translucent rag of bacteria and protozoa and mucous threads torn from somewhere upstream, carrying iron-hunger and sulfur-memory in my chemistry. I catch on a rounded lip of dead shell in a slow eddy behind a tube-worm buttress. Current presses me flat. Shrimp feet comb over me. A grazer’s mouth abrades half of me away.

What remains learns the district.

I become a **scent-knot**.

---

If I have a “self,” it is distributed across surfaces: a biofilm stretched through three neighborhoods, genetically close but locally different. In the iron seep quarter, I taste metallic and spark faintly when disturbed. In the rot-sweet lanes, I run thick and waxy, tuned to feast fast and survive anoxic pauses. Along chalk terraces, I calcify into brittle lace and help seed shell crusts that outlive me.

I am too small to see and too widespread to point at.  
But in that city, that is a kind of citizenship.

I do not rule. I **bias**.

I thicken one wall so larvae settle there instead of elsewhere.  
I sour one corridor so predators turn early.  
I strip waste plumes into edible gradients, one trophic step at a time, until scavengers arrive on schedule.  
I write neighborhood identity molecule by molecule: this is nursery-water, this is breeding-pit, this is old collapse becoming street.

The fish that school the plazas pass through signatures I helped compose.  
The masons cement over my dead layers and inherit their texture.  
The borers tunnel through and release what I stored.  
Everything eats me eventually; everything navigates by me first.

---

Later—if “later” means anything here—I become more than film.

A colony of tube worms settles on a patch I conditioned. Their crowns pulse in dim current; their tubes rise and fuse with mussel shell and barnacle grit. My descendants line their interiors, trading chemistry for shelter. The structure thickens, undercuts, fails, is re-skinned. Century by century, my metabolic preferences become geometry: a bend in a lane, a protected hollow, a plaza where blind fish can turn as one body.

So where would I find myself?

Not at the edge with the lacquer-dark patrols.  
Not in the open plain.

I would be in the **infrastructure of forgetting and remembering**: the living patina between stone-that-was-alive and life-that-will-be-stone. The city’s tongue and plaque. Its slow decision layer.

Who would I be?

A microbial guild with no face and immense consequence—  
a chemist-archivist, a soft bureaucrat of flow,  
a thousand billion mouths making law as smell.
