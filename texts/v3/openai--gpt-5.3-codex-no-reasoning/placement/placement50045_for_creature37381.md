I think I would be **a route** before I would be a creature.

Not a fish, not a drone, not a person in a suit—  
a learned path in moving water, one that keeps getting chosen until it becomes real in the only way this place allows: by repetition.

A pressure-soft lane between two intake districts.  
A band of survivable current that threads hazard paint and turbine hunger.  
Used first by baitfish by accident, then by hunters, then by scavengers, then by maintenance swarms timing their battery budgets to the same forgiving flow. Birds learn its surface signature. Sensors flag it as anomalous efficiency. Engineers name it Corridor 9-Blue or something forgettable. But below the label it is older than their naming and younger than rust: a **behavioral artery**.

If I had a body, it would be borrowed:

- scales flashing through me at dawn,
- eel-muscle writing cursive through my darker sections,
- crab-feet testing my seams at shift-change,
- bot lights passing like thoughts at midnight.

If I had a mind, it would be distributed and practical:  
turn here when the gate-thump doubles;  
drop depth when the warm plume goes glassy;  
wait out red strobe cycles in the lee of Column 442;  
never cross the silver boil above Auxiliary Intake C when the moon tide and pump schedule overlap.

So where would I find myself in that world?

In the **between**—between megastructures, between designed intent and animal use, between control and weather.  
In the long cold reaches where “nothing is happening,” except that everything is learning each other.

Who would I be?

I’d be one of the Pelagic Works’ first true memories:  
not stored in servers, but in recurring motion.  
A living shortcut.  
A consensus written in wake and blood and code.  
A small intelligence made of many ordinary decisions, surviving by keeping time with a sea that is also a machine.
