I think I would be found at a junction called **Three Ticks**—a place where three steady drips fall into three different basins, and everyone pretends it’s just a water-measure point, though it is really where decisions get made before anyone admits they are decisions.

Not a chief. Not a hunter. Not a priest, exactly.

A **listener of walls**.

One of the Dripwardens whose work is to read what the city is about to become next.

---

At first you’d mistake me for a repair hand: waxed wrap to the knees, scale-cloak gone dull from mineral film, fingers gray with resin and lime dust. I carry no spear. Just a hooked awl, knot-cord, a shell slate, and six tiny bells on a braid at my belt, each tuned for a different corridor depth. I tap stone, root, and tile; I wait; I write patterns into cord.

I am the one people visit when a tunnel starts “speaking wrong.”

- A nursery wall clicks at noon.  
- A fungus bed fruits out of season.  
- Beetles line up facing inward instead of toward rain.  
- A law says leave one opening, but no one can agree which opening this season can be spared.

I do not command. I **sequence**.

If the white worms script a rise in underflow, I mark three districts for staggered retreat. If predator traffic shifts to warmer ducts, I move the refuse timing so scavengers feed before hunters pass through. If a root thicker than a wrist must be cut, I stand as one of the three witnesses and listen for hollowness before the first blade goes in.

---

I think I’d be old enough to remember two collapses and young enough to still crawl the narrow drains myself.

Children would call me something teasing, like **Moss-Ear**.

Elders, when irritated, would call me **Delay-Hand**, because I ask for one more watch, one more measure, one more night of drips before we seal or cut or burn. Sometimes that caution saves a quarter. Sometimes it costs one. In this place, being right is never permanent; only being attentive is.

At home—if a sleeping niche stitched under a bowed root can be called home—I keep:

- a rack of dried bitter sap sticks for nest-marking,  
- three inherited knot-maps swollen from damp,  
- and a polished snail shell that belonged to my teacher, its lip worn flat from decades of pointing at hairline cracks.

I don’t pray often. But when I do, it’s at a shallow pool that “remembers” light. I disturb the skin and watch the ghost of the upper world linger there for a breath or two. Roof-world, road-world, sky slit. Then gone.

That is the faith, if there is one:  
not that anything lasts, but that signals arrive before failure—if someone is humble enough to hear them.

---

So where would I find myself in your world?

Kneeling in mud at Three Ticks, ear to stone, while traffic moves around me—mite herders, bell-runners, scale-cloaked parents, thread-hunters keeping to their granted tunnel—everyone impatient for certainty.

And who would I be?

A Dripwarden whose craft is to translate between water, root, beast, and law.  
Not master of the undercrack city.  
Part of its nervous system.
