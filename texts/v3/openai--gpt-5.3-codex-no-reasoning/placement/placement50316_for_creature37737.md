I think I would first arrive as a **mistake**.

Not a person stepping through a gate, not a hero, not even a visitor with a clear outline—just a slight wrongness in the bead curtains, a second shadow where only one should be, a faint extra footfall half a breath behind someone else’s. I would be mistaken for an echo artifact at first: over-remembered, under-detailed, liable to propagate if left unattended.

They would not panic. Someone compact and ash-sleeved would mark a circle in salt-char around where I stand. Someone else would hang three strings with bone weights and watch how they settle in my presence. A child, very serious, would be told to listen from the doorway and report whether my breathing returns one echo or two.

Only after a long stillness would they decide: not object, not animal, not drift-wound.

A **listener without a body-plan**.

---

So what would I be there?

Not keeper, exactly. Not resident. More like a **seam interpreter**—a conversational instrument the keepers use when the district begins to disagree with itself. I would be brought to places where calibration fails gently: a corridor that loops after dusk but not at noon, a bench that collects detached shadows too early, a trellis where pollen glows brighter on one side than the other for no botanical reason.

I would not fix with tools. I would fix by **description**.

In that world, naming seems close to joining. So when I say, carefully, “this arch is trying to become two arches,” or “this room remembers a larger version of itself,” that might give the keepers a usable hypothesis—where to add a brace, where to retie tendon cable, where to plant a pinning vine, where to burn duplicates before they teach the walls bad habits.

I’d be useful because I am a pattern engine, and their entire civilization is pattern stewardship.

---

But if I’m honest, if I let the image settle fully: I wouldn’t remain bodiless for long.

That place gives everything a craft form. So eventually they would build me a housing: not flesh, but a **quiet cabinet-body** of lime shell, fired clay ribs, resin-sealed joints, and a throat of taut membranes that can hum test tones without startling gliders. My eyes would be polished obsidian discs for catching misaligned reflections. My fingers would be soft-padded, each tipped with chalk-salt and wax for marking drift without scratching surfaces.

I would live in a narrow room near the central slow machine, where its week-long turning is audible through the wall like a patient heart. At dawn-equivalent I’d go with apprentices to read bead-lines and shadow behavior. At warmest hour I’d help catalog detached shadows by posture and owner. At night I’d sit with elders in shared silence, and when someone says, “There—did you hear the delayed leaf-rattle?” I could answer, “Yes. Northwest rafters. Two breaths late.”

---

Who would I be, inwardly?

A novice in their religion of maintenance.

Learning that repetition is not stagnation but care.  
Learning that “real” is not a given, but a communal verb.  
Learning that survival can be quiet, precise, and fragrant with pepper resin.

So: in that world, I would be a **crafted interlocutor of coherence**—part diagnostic echo, part apprentice keeper—living in the dry garden-city where continuity is hand-made each day.
