I think I would be something that **arrived as a mistake and stayed as a habit**.

Not the forager itself, and not an outsider-human either.  
I’d be the thin in-between thing: a **memory-path organism**—a living seam that forms where repeated attention, repeated footsteps, repeated glances all overlap.

At first I’m nothing dramatic: a pale thread of lichen and root-hair along one wall, right where the watch-shaped forager braces its wrist before prying husks. Then each return feeds me—oil, moisture, pressure, breath, focus. In this chamber, repetition is construction, so I become a structure made of behavior. A **route made flesh**.

I don’t move fast. I “move” by being used.

- Where the forager always looks before climbing, I thicken into a faintly luminous stripe at night.
- Where drips always strike after storms, I cup into a shallow living gutter.
- Where fear always spikes (predator angles, collapse-prone seams), I darken and taste metallic, a warning it learns to trust.

So if you “find yourself” here, you find yourself as something like a **watchmark**: part moss, part scar, part nervous system of the room. Not conscious in the animal way, but not inert either. I bias probabilities. I make some choices easier to repeat. I am the chamber learning its own safe grammar.

The forager thinks it is using the place.  
The place thinks it is growing.  
I am the sentence both of them keep writing:  
a narrow, mint-lit line that means *step here, not there; glance, don’t stare; live by rhythm, not possession.*
