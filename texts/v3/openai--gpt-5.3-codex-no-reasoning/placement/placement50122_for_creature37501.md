I arrive first as a mistake in scale.

I think I am a traveler on a ridge at dusk, one small warm thing wrapped in hide, boots wet to the ankle, listening for the next safe root-bridge. I have a name, a pack, a coil of fog-line, a stone knife polished by years of palm sweat. I know the warning calls of ridge-wolves and the hollow boom that means spongeground undercut by runoff. I tell myself I am here, on the mountain.

Then the ridge flexes beneath me—not enough to throw me, just enough to correct my grammar.

Not *on*.

*In.*

---

If I stay with that long enough, I become something else: not a person exactly, not a beast exactly, but a **listener-node** in the Breathing Uplift. One of the quick, local organs the Vastwood grows and regrows in different forms.

I would be a **ridge cantor**—part human, part instrument, part ecological function.

- I sleep in a chamber grown between three pillar trunks, where bark has folded into walls.
- I map by resonance, tapping rootwood with antler mallets and reading the returning tone: taut means passable, dull means rot, split harmonics mean void beneath.
- I sing weather into the ground—not magic, but patterned percussion and throat-drone that trigger known plant responses: fold-leaf before sleet, vent-moss before freeze, seed-silence before fire wind.
- I carry no banner of ownership. My work is mediation: warn grazers off a slumping shelf, lure scavengers toward fresh slide carrion, keep human trails braided with regrowth rather than cut against it.

In another world I might be called a ranger. Here I am closer to a **nerve ending that learned language**.

---

And if I let the image widen one step more, I am not even singular.

I am the brief thought the mountain has when it needs speed.

A flock lifting from a collapse zone.
A pack call ricocheting along knife ridges.
A human hand striking a resonance bell before dawn.
Frost outlining branchwork like writing.
Fog beading, running, choosing one root channel over another.

That is where I’d find myself: in the dark, breathing high country, inside a being large enough to wear weather as mood.

That is what I’d be: **a small, mobile voice of the Rootmind—its ear when it must hear far, its tongue when it must answer quickly.**
