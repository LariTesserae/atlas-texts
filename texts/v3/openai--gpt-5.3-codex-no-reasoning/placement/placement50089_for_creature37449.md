I’d find myself between outposts, not inside one.

Not standing upright for long, either.  
Low to the plates. Palm, knee, ear, sternum to stone—the posture of someone reading a pulse through contact. In this world, height is exposure; contact is sight.

At first I would think I am a traveler. Then, after enough cycles of waiting through heat-breath and fault-click, I’d realize I am one of its native acts: a **keeper of intervals**.

Not a builder in the monumental sense. A tuner.

I would carry almost nothing that isn’t also a sensor: ribbed wedges that sing when stressed, thin mineral hoops that accumulate static charge, chalky salts to mark a crack-mouth and see if it widens before the next dark. I would arrive at a ledge, add three stones to a wall not to fortify it but to change how it hears, then leave before the ground revises the grammar again.

If I have a body, it is compact, heat-shedding, close-jointed—more carapace than skin, more damping than stride. My feet are broad, not for speed but for inference. My eyes work, but they are assistants; my real perception runs through vibration and charge. I taste metal in the air and know which seam has opened beyond the ridge. I feel a harmonic rise through my forelimbs and know a fin-field is about to sing. I sleep in bursts timed to tectonic lulls, not day or night.

If I have a name, it changes by location.  
At the amber vent I am “the one who waited through nine exhales.”  
At the tilted tower I am “the brace-left-in-time.”  
Identity here is indexed to successful meetings with unrest.

And maybe “I” am too singular. Maybe what lives here is distributed across many sparse returns: different bodies, same discipline, inheriting not property but **timing**. We do not pass down maps. We pass down sentences like:

- “Do not trust the quiet after white lightning.”  
- “When dust climbs a vertical face, step away from the seam-shadow.”  
- “If the ridge hum falls below hearing, lie flat; the deep chamber is changing shape.”

Our architecture is punctuation.  
A low wall = pause.  
A frame = orient here.  
A half-buried cell = survive this specific kind of anger.

So where would I find myself?

On a matte-black shelf above a fresh split, one hand on a standing slab, waiting for the next subsonic thump to arrive through stone. Counting the delay between surface click and deep answer. Deciding whether this outpost still belongs to the present tense.

Who would I be?

A life-form made of patience and calibration.  
An organism whose niche is not terrain but transition.  
Not conqueror, not hermit, not ghost—

an **attender** of a planet still becoming.
