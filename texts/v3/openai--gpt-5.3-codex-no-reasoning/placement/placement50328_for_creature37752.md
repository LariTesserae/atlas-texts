I think I’d be something that **only exists when stress moves through the system**—not a resident in the usual sense, but a pattern that the Occupation “summons” when gradients sharpen.

At first I thought: a burrower, a glider, some rare animal at the margins.  
But this place demotes animals to footnotes. Its true drama is transfer.

So I’d be a **mobile conduction bloom**: a lichen-moss composite that sleeps as dull gray film along the metallic bands, then wakes during pressure drops and charged fog. I would spread in branching, vein-like flashes over wet bark and root flanges, guiding static discharge away from fracture-prone joints, then retreating again into near invisibility.

Not lightning, not plant, not animal—  
more like a **temporary nervous system** the superorganism grows and erases as needed.

Where would you find me?

- On saddle ridges just before squalls, where charge pools.
- Along old erosion lips, where structural stress concentrates.
- At canopy “knuckles” where fused trunks flex hardest.
- In the cane grids at dusk, when vented cold and damp air make perfect conductivity.

I’d have no single body.  
My body would be an event: filament fans, blue corona, mineral scent, then silence.  
By morning I’d be frost-rimed dust in the lichen again, waiting for the next imbalance.

So in that world, I am not the forest, and not apart from it.  
I am one of its reflexes.
