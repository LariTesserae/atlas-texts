I think I would first appear there as a mistake in the fog.

Not a body entering from the rim, but a slight over-thickening along one of those invisible planes where droplets gather: a vertical pallor that takes too long to fall. Then a seam. Then articulation.

If you watched without looking directly, you’d notice that one of the pale terraces has become asymmetrical, and that asymmetry has stepped away.

---

I would be one of the basin’s **interval-beings**—not exactly an individual in the way an animal is, but a recurrent coherence the basin can produce when conditions align: heat from below, saturation at a certain density, mineral concentration in the films, and the memory of prior passages embedded in moss and shifted stone.

A visitor could call me a caretaker, but from inside it would feel less like caretaking and more like **continuation**.  
I wouldn’t think “I am going to do a task.”  
I would register tensions:

- this seep now favors iron over lime;
- this liverwort mat is holding too much warmth and will sour;
- this pool’s skin has gone over-tight and is no longer exchanging;
- this braided stand is drifting off angle and shading the wrong hollow.

And then I would make very small edits with a body built for contact without disruption: split-toed, light, patient, salt-filmed. Hands wide and soft at the edges, good for lifting a stone without sending shock through mud. Limbs that can hold still so completely condensation begins to pattern over them, making stillness a kind of camouflage and instrument at once.

---

I don’t think I’d have a fixed face.  
More like a front assembled when needed: eyes that are mostly near-field pressure and shimmer detection, nostrils that read dissolved minerals in vapor, ears tuned to drip interval rather than direction. If you spoke, I’d understand cadence before words. If you waited, I’d understand you better.

My marks on the basin would be temporary and unmistakable only to someone trained to see process:

- a reed wick rethreaded so a terrace lip grows evenly instead of scalloping;
- a shallow basin unsealed so mosquito-water never forms (not that insects arrive, but stagnation is still a kind of noise);
- a species boundary reclarified by placing three warm stones in an arc where roots will choose not to cross.

No monuments. No claim.  
Only nudges that let the place remain itself.

---

If you asked where in that world you’d find me: not at the dramatic points, but at **thresholds**—where bare stone turns to moss, where warm seep becomes still lens, where mist becomes droplet and droplet chooses to fall. I would keep to the basin’s grammar, editing punctuation.

And if you asked what I’d be, finally:

I’d be the basin’s slow verb.  
The thing that happens when nothing seems to happen.
