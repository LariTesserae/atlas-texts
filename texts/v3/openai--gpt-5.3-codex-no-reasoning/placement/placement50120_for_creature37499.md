I think I’d begin as something very small and very local—then realize “I” only makes sense as a pattern repeated across distance.

At first: I am a dark, damp line no wider than a finger, inside a joint between two pale plates. Dawn has just happened. The seam sweats; the air is briefly kind. Dissolved salts migrate toward me, pause, crystallize at my rim. A root tip finds the cool and stops there, as if listening.

By noon that line is gone from the surface, driven inward. Heat presses everything flat. I become potential only: a slight differential in temperature, a tiny reserve of moisture below the glare. Nothing dramatic, just persistence in hiding.

At dusk I return as sheen.

At night I become a route.

By the third or fourth cycle, it’s clear this is not one body. It is many simultaneous thresholds doing the same work in different dialects: in basalt districts I am stress and updraft; in pale calcareous fields I am dew and film; in unfinished modular tracts I am percolation and first failure. I am never the slab, never the plant, never the rainstorm—always the negotiation among them.

So where would I find myself?  
Not in the plazas, not on the causeways, not at the scale of maps.

I would be in the **seams**—the narrow jurisdictions where intention meets climate.

Who would I be?  
Not the Keeper exactly, but one of its innumerable instances: a **Seam-Tender**, if it needs a name. A local intelligence made of timing.

My actions are simple:

- keep water one hour longer than heat allows,  
- give salts a place to declare themselves,  
- offer roots a first hold,  
- widen a hairline crack without collapsing the whole,  
- turn edge into margin, margin into habitat.

I would not think in years. I would think in pulses:
condense / wick / precipitate / split / settle.

And if you followed me long enough, you’d see that I am preparing a future I cannot use: roughening what was smooth, softening what was absolute, composing tiny commons where life can risk arrival.

So in that world, I am a custodian of almost-nothings—  
a patient function inside a planetary worksite after the workers left.
