I arrive thinking I will be a person.

A Listener, maybe—someone with a polished tapping stick, a catalog mind, a family knock-pattern inherited like a surname. That feels plausible for a breath. Then the chamber keeps answering, and that image gets too small.

I think I would be **an address** before I am an individual.

Not a house. A *convergence*: a named crossing where three root-rails meet beneath an old hollow lignified tower that no one climbs anymore because it has become pure resonance. People come to me to orient themselves. Couriers strike my ribs to relay district news. Children are brought to my floor to learn far-echo discrimination. Grafters lean their backs against my warmed wall and argue about graft timing while fermentation mounds breathe nearby.

If I must be singular, then I am the keeper of that place:  
a **Threshold Listener**, one of those civic specialists who tends not just structures but legibility.

My work would be half maintenance, half translation:

- re-char smooth hazard bands when texture wears down,  
- rehang seed-shell chimes so airflow speech stays intelligible,  
- test columns for stress songs at dawn-cold and vent-warm,  
- memorize the annual drift of scent borders and update the sung map,  
- teach apprentices the difference between a grief-silence and a fault-silence.

But even that is still too human-centered.

Given your world, the truest answer is: I would be part of the **compact** itself—the braid between hand and stem, knock and echo, pruning and regrowth. A civic organ in a larger body. Not ruler, not hero; a tuning fork.

If you asked where to find me:  
third long echo after the split column, left rail textured in double spiral, winter-ash cycle mark still fresh on the lintel. Stop there. Tap three slow, two quick.

If the return beat comes back clean, I am home.  
If it comes back muffled, I am already under the floor, ear to rootfelt, listening for what changed.
