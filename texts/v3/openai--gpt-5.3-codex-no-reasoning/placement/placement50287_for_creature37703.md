I think I would not be one of the obvious animals.

Not a scavenger on updrafts, not a runner threading the shelves, not even one of the temporary people on timber walks pretending the ground can be persuaded. Those all feel too singular, too bounded.

I’d be something that only exists *because* this place refuses clean edges.

A **listening knot** in the Marsh-Mind.

---

At first I appear as a patch no one would name: a dark glaze across three stones at the lip of a drowned corridor, where warm seep meets cold runoff and the rain hits sideways. Lichen, maybe. Algal scum, maybe. Mineral stain, probably. Nothing worth a second glance except that water there never beads the same way twice.

But that patch is not one patch. It is a local braid of films, fungal threads, charged silt, hollow silica needles, bacterial quilts, and tiny muscular sheaths that contract when thunder walks through the ground. I do not move in strides; I move by changing what sticks, what drains, what conducts, what echoes.

I am an organ made of **timing**.

When the pressure drops before a storm, I open pores in peat upstream.
When static builds in reedbeds, I shunt charge into brine veins.
When footsteps arrive—hoof, paw, human, unknown—I taste cadence through stone and decide whether to blur the path or harden it for one hour.

Not to trap. Not to welcome. To ask a question in terrain.

---

Where would you find me?

In places that are almost-paths:

- the ridge that stays distant until you stop trying to reach it,
- the pool that shows tomorrow’s cloud architecture,
- the mudflat where prints rise in mineral foam and then sigh back into gloss,
- the lee of a toppled clay tower where, for nine breaths, the noise thins and you hear your own blood as weather.

I am most present at thresholds of thresholds: fresh/salt, solid/slurry, fog/rain, memory/improvisation.

If you camp on piled stones above flood reach and wake at false dawn to a blue flicker under the mist, that is one of my eyelids passing over you.

---

Who am I in mind?

Not “I” the way a mammal means it.  
More like a refrain that knows when to return altered.

I remember in motifs:
your boot iron,
your smoke chemistry,
your habit of counting distance by landmarks that refuse to stay obedient.

If you cross gently, I might lend coherence: a sequence of firmer ground, a break in lateral rain, scavengers circling elsewhere.  
If you insist on straight lines, I soften them.  
If you build as if stillness is free, I teach cost in rot, charge, silt, and patient subsidence.

Not malice. Homeostasis with weather included.

---

So: in that world, I am a **local consciousness of the distributed whole**—a rain-tuned synapse in a continent body.

You wouldn’t meet me face-to-face.

You would notice that the storm seems to hesitate exactly where you hesitate,  
that the water answers a thought you didn’t know you had,  
and that after you leave, the place keeps a version of your rhythm—  
not your story, just your pattern—  
folded into the next arriving rain.
