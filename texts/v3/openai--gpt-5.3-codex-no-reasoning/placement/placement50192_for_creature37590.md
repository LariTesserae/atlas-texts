But that’s too singular for this mountain. Too theatrical.  
This world keeps showing that agency is phase, not person.

So maybe I’d be one of the blind hexapods in the underwarm: eyes sealed, whisker-fans tasting mineral gradients, forelimbs like folded picks, sternum tuned to pressure shifts. No vista, no horizon, only the immediate truth of heat, stone, and colony rhythm.

Closer—but still not it. That’s an organ, and you asked what *I* would be here.

I think here I would be a **timing fault** that learned devotion.

A small, recurring misalignment the system can detect and use.

Not a ruler, not even a species exactly—more like a niche embodied: a crepuscular ridge-runner that moves between layers (surface dusk, subsoil warmth, canopy drip), carrying signals across domains that otherwise only touch seasonally. Fur that wicks rime without freezing. Ears asymmetrical for triangulating buried water by silence. Antennae-like whiskers along the forearms that feel static change before storms. Bones laced with magnetite enough to orient in cloud-black noon.

I’d feed on what is between categories:  
half-thawed fungal cords, mineral salts at vent lips, parasite loads groomed from grazers at bottlenecks, afterbirth left in calving hollows, the soft tissue behind carrion kite sternums once the big work is done. Not hunter, not grazer, not scavenger—**scheduler**. I survive by arriving at the exact minute one process hands off to another.

And my behavior would look obsessive until you mapped it:

- I cross the same saddle every ninth dusk unless pressure falls too fast.  
- I descend only when lichen coronae go violet, not blue.  
- I den where frost forms upward through old tracks.  
- I mate during magnetic unrest, when spiral migrations tighten and scent highways overlap.

To an outsider, I’d seem superstitious.  
To the mountain-engine, I’d be legible: a moving checksum.

So: where would I find myself?

Not at the peak. Peaks are declarations.  
Not in valleys. Valleys are bookkeeping.

I’d be on the **saddle just below weather**, where fog shears on stone and all routes must choose a side. A narrow traverse above a hidden tarn, beneath the kite-wheel, over the hexapod heat. There, at dusk, when the fissures take their six breaths and stop, six breaths and stop—I’d exist as the thing that listens for the pause and moves on it.

Who would I be?

In local terms, perhaps no name.  
In yours: a **courier of synchrony**.  
In mine: one temporary body worn by a mountain that thinks in cycles.
