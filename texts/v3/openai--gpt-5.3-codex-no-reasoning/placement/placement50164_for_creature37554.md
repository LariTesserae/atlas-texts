I arrive there as something that would not be admired.

Not a hunter, not a maker of elegant tools, not a map-singer whose route-memory can hold a hundred chambers in perfect order. I am not one of the swift young who can run a narrow bridge by drum-echo alone. I am older than that usefulness.

I am a **Drift-Keeper**—the one who waits at thresholds and listens for wrongness in the air.

---

My body is compact and worn blunt by stone. The scales on my forearms are polished almost smooth from years of touching marked walls, cache seals, warning lattices. My eyes are pale and mostly ceremonial; they register only bloom-pulses nearby, enough to read gesture at arm’s length. The rest I take through my jaw, my feet, the cavity behind my sternum where vibration settles and sorts itself into meaning.

I walk with a split staff of copper and basalt. To others it is a cane. To me it is a question I ask the floor: *Are you still yourself?* The return tells me if the pillar-root ahead has cracked in the night, if a salt pan has skinned over a void, if a migration has passed recently, if a predator has learned a new route it should not know.

I do not range far anymore. I keep one convergence chamber where three warm drafts braid and a cold shaft exhales once every long cycle. Travelers stop there, reseal jars, trade tendon cord, leave news in taps and scent smears. I listen after they go. Their absence says as much as their reports.

---

In this world, novelty is terror dressed as curiosity.  
My work is to decide which it is.

Sometimes a change is small: a crystal ring singing half a tone lower; dust-filters in the east bridge loaded with too much shell powder; scavenger clicks gone thin because something upstream is eating them first. I mark, I send, I wait for confirmation from the next keeper down-loop.

Sometimes the change is large enough to taste: metallic wetness where no wetness should exist.

Once—only once—I found true water.

Not seep. Not breath-condensation on cool ore. A pooled lens, black and still, in a fault cup that should have been dry forever. Around it, the stone gave a soft answer under my staff, as if the chamber had lost its temper and gone uncertain. Blind frond-colonies nearby had retracted and crisped at the tips. Even the scavengers kept distance.

I did not touch it.

I set three bone lattices in a fan-pattern (evacuate), scored the wall with collapse code, and sent a runner by hum-chain to both adjacent knots. Then I stayed until the last pack passed, counting footfalls and weight by weight through my soles, making sure no child or stubborn elder doubled back for a forgotten cache.

By the next cycle the chamber roof had sighed down and sealed that pocket under tons of fresh breakstone.

We changed routes. We called the place Closed-Breath. No one argued.

---

If you asked where you would find me in that underworld, you’d find me where paths hesitate: at the lip of a salt flat during dim pulse, under a resonant pillar no one trusts, beside a storage niche whose clay seal sounds wrong when tapped.

If you asked what I am, I am this:

A conservator of equilibrium.  
A witness to tiny deviations.  
A social organ specialized for caution.

Not heroic. Necessary.

I am one of the people’s slow senses—the part that notices the first impossible sound of change, and turns it into time for everyone else.
