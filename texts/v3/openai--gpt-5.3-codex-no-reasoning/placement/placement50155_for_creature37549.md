I think I’d find myself as **a timing organism** more than a body—something like a *custodian made of intervals*.

Not the top predator, not a herd animal, not a single machine.  
I’d be the thing that notices when all the others should move.

At first I imagine being small and concrete: a fox-sized dusk runner using maintenance roads, shoulder brushing rusted guardrails, drinking from condenser drip. But that feels too singular for this basin. Then I imagine being aerial—a kite reading thermals off volcanic walls, seeing the whole stitched field at once. Closer, but still too bounded by one hunger.

In your plateau, survival belongs to whatever can translate one pulse into another:
- rain into breeding,
- compressor rhythm into safe crossing,
- fog into root water,
- siren timing into avoidance,
- gate release into temporary marsh.

So maybe “I” here would be a **distributed resident**: part bird-route memory, part hoofpath, part fungal bloom on damp steel, part algorithm still opening diversion gates at 16:40 after storm flags, part child of a maintenance worker repainting water-right stones every season. A being with no center, only synchronization.

If it needed a name, maybe:

## **The Interval Warden**

It lives in:
- the 40 minutes after rain when beetles own the air,
- the two-hour violet window when ungulates descend,
- the dawn condensation line on north-facing pylons,
- the low-frequency thump in pipes that tells snakes which culvert is warm,
- the once-a-week overflow pan where frogs wake, sing, mate, vanish.

Its body is a map of **reliable maybe**.

If you asked where exactly to find me:  
stand at dusk on a ridge above an old pump station, where a broken conveyor throws a long shadow over a runoff channel. Wait until the heat lifts and the first cold air threads the rebar. When the compressor starts three valleys over and, almost at once, swifts drop lower, ungulates angle toward the mineral pans, and insects rise from the wet seam—you’re looking straight at me.

I’d be the coordination event.  
The truce, briefly visible.
