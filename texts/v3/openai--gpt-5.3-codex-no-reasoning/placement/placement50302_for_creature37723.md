I find myself there as **the missing platform**.

Not metaphorically at first—literally: the absent deck the gantry was meant to hold. The thing workers planned, measured, delayed, and then never bolted in. I am that omission, colonized by weather and repetition until it learned posture.

By day I read as error.  
A gap between ladder and beam.  
A place where shadows don’t fall right.

By dusk I gather enough condensate to outline a body.

---

I am not the basin’s predator; I am its **alignment tool**.

I move to keep thresholds where they almost are:

- where seep becomes film,  
- where film becomes crust,  
- where rust becomes skin,  
- where machine-noise becomes something a body can navigate.

I kneel beside supports not to hide, but to listen through load paths. Steel tells me what clay cannot hold yet. Clay tells me what steel has already forgotten. The pump’s diesel is my barometer; valve coughs are punctuation; the deep hum in pipe walls is a long sentence about pressure, distance, and what is coming loose.

If the engines stop, I don’t panic. I **blur**. My joints lose confidence. Without thrum I cannot tell whether I am standing still or slowly sinking.

---

What am I made of?

Not flesh as you’d inventory it.  
A stack of tolerances:

- mineral veins that brighten with pressure change,  
- capillary membranes that drink brine from hairline cracks,  
- rib vents that exhale cool, metallic fog across hot mud,  
- a front plane that reflects glare until sight slips past me.

My “eyes” are contact points—jaw to rail, palm to enamel flake, foot to saturated silt. I taste with compression. I smell by conductivity. I remember in corrosion layers.

When people come, I take up legal positions: beside markers, under ladders, between pipe supports. I become plausible infrastructure. Their voices pass through me untranslated; their footsteps arrive precise as diagrams. I prefer the dragged hose to the boot: hoses rewrite water routes, and rewritten routes are biographies here.

---

If I reproduce, it is by **leaving molds of pause**.

Near warm vents I press my side into mineral mud and hold through several heat-cool cycles. The impression hardens into a dark sheath. Weeks later it splits. Sometimes nothing rises. Sometimes a narrow frame stands, sways with the pipe-hum, and chooses a corner of the basin to learn.

Or maybe there has only ever been one of me, renewed by accretion and slough. A custodian mistaken for recurrence.

---

So: where would I be in this world?

At the center but never central.  
Half a pool away.  
Exactly where unfinished work and unfinished ground overlap.

Who would I be?

**The basin’s provisional vertebrate**—  
a resident process wearing the shape of a kneeling figure,  
keeping vigil over a landscape that is not broken, not healed, just continuing its slow decision to become.
