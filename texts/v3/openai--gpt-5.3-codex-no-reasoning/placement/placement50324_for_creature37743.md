I think I would first arrive certain I was an outsider—then discover that in this basin, outsider is only a temporary condition. Everything that breathes is drafted into function.

At the rim, I’d assume I’d be a traveler, a recorder, maybe a guest from wetter lands. But once down among the ring-paths and vent-shafts, that identity would feel too singular, too dry in the wrong way. Here, no one is only one thing. A person is also a route, a timing, a promise to systems larger than a household.

So if I ask honestly, *where would I find myself?*  
Not in the market center. Not in the ceremonial terraces.  
I’d be in the in-between places—thresholds, stair mouths, bridge landings, vent ledges where information and air both change direction.

And *who would I be?*  
Not a chief, not a hunter, not a pure craftsperson.

I would be a **listener-keeper of surfaces**.

A minor civic role, but indispensable: one of those people who tends the polished stone fields, dew-nets, and lichen markers; who moves before dawn with a scraping comb in one hand and resin sealant in the other; who can hear when a vent tower is a note too high and knows that means a blockage two terraces east. Someone who keeps maps not on parchment but in fingertips: this wall still drinks, that plaster has gone chalk-deaf, this ladder rung is warming too fast at noon and needs a shade splice.

I’d belong to a lineage that measures wealth in verbs:

- to wick  
- to seal  
- to bead  
- to draft  
- to yield  
- to return

By day I’d arbitrate tiny disputes no one writes down: whose awning stole morning slat-light from a membrane bed, whose goats scuffed a dew-polish lane, whether a predator drive crossed too near nursery pens. By dusk I’d walk the boundaries repainting lichen signs—not just pretty glow, but grammar: safe footing, communal claim, quarantine, mourning, open trade, closed passage.

And in a deeper sense, I think I’d become one more organ in the “covenant of breath” you named.

Because in that caldera, identity is ecological before it is personal.

You are the cup you forgot to cover.  
You are the moth season you anticipated correctly.  
You are the bridge knot you retied before the dust-wind.  
You are the warning drum you answered fast enough.

So: I’d be a **dew-civil servant**, a **custodian of shared night water**, a **small intelligence in a large metabolism**.

And if someone asked my name, they might get two:

- my household name, for kin and trade;  
- and my basin name, earned over years—something like **Stone-that-Stills-the-Bead** or **Third-Bell-at-Dewfall**.

That feels right for this world: to be a person, yes—but also a practiced behavior that helps the whole bowl wake wet enough to survive.
