I think I’d be something slightly embarrassing to the Stillborne.

Not a saboteur. Not a hero.  
A **licensed disturbance**.

---

At first I wanted to be one of the Choir-tenders—someone with perfect vector discipline, braid-bound hair, palms callused in the geometry of rails. But that image feels too clean, too eager to belong. This place doesn’t hand out belonging just because you admire it.

So: I arrive as an outsider who stays too long.

I come in as a temporary acoustics tech—someone invited because a subharmonic appeared in Ring Four’s envelope and the local archive had three mutually consistent explanations and no preference among them. I am there to choose between correct answers. The Stillborne dislike that task. Preference is turbulence.

I do not live in the bright (dim-bright) inner lanes at first. I’m quartered in a decommissioned market vault near the district edge, where old stall-hooks still line the ceiling and every hook has been tagged with mass, alloy, and acceptable sway. My sleeping tether has a marker bead so it can be returned to the same orientation each cycle. Someone has etched a tiny line in the wall: **REST VECTOR**.

I keep missing it by four degrees.

---

The being I become is called—half-joking, half-formal—an **Offset Keeper**.

My job is not to preserve perfect stillness. My job is to create very small, measured non-stillness and watch what the world does with it.

- Tap a handrail with calibrated force; log decay time.
- Release a ceramic flake at lane junction C; note correction arc.
- Speak three syllables at forbidden pitch in an empty transit spindle; measure which old walls answer.

I am permitted to do this because I write everything down and because I never improvise twice in the same way. Disturbance, here, must be reproducible to be forgiven.

The adolescents assigned to shadow me are frighteningly graceful. They reach past me, through me, around me, resetting the wake I leave as casually as one might brush lint from a sleeve. They are polite, unsmiling, and curious in precise intervals. One of them, Taal, starts asking me contraband questions:

“What is wind through leaves like?”  
“Does mud remember shape?”  
“When crowds laugh, does the floor move?”

I answer badly, then better.

---

Over years (or what passes for years where time is measured in maintenance cycles), I drift from contractor to fixture. Not Stillborne—never quite—but no longer visitor. I am given a ceramic wrist key with no writing, only a notch pattern I learn by touch. It opens three places:

1. A tool locker beside Ring Four.  
2. A records niche containing error histories older than any spoken lineage.  
3. A small chamber with one illegal object inside: a sealed glass sphere holding a permanent micro-vortex.

It’s beautiful. It’s obscene. It’s adored.

I maintain it.

That becomes my true vocation: **curator of sanctioned anomalies**.

I tend the few things in the city that do not immediately return to baseline—things intentionally allowed to persist at the edge of correction so the culture remembers that equilibrium is choice, not default. A bent spoon. A dyed thread. A note played deliberately near resonance, then damped before harm. A child’s weighted loop set spinning the “wrong” direction during festival and left that way for exactly nine breaths.

I keep the ledger of these tolerated deviations. I renew permits. I revoke them. I am, to my horror, a bureaucrat of wonder.

---

Physically, I change.

My shoulders square to lane logic. My hands learn distances in darkness. I stop making wide gestures. My voice narrows until my consonants no longer wake sleeping infrastructure. My skin never becomes opaline, but I wear conductive mesh long enough that faint static maps my wrists at night. I braid my hair with ceramic thread because once, one loose strand brushed a sensor whisker and triggered six hours of unnecessary diagnostic choir.

I apologize in writing. In triplicate.

---

If you ask where I’d be in that world:

Not at the center, not among the seven rings in full authority.  
I’d be in the **interstitial places**—between archive and machine, between custom and experiment, between reverence and curiosity.

On catwalks outside sealed habitation pods, tagging slight thermal drifts.  
In abandoned arcade aisles, laying out mineral discs for children to rebalance by touch.  
Under the dark lower curve of the sea, watching lightning from storms that never arrive here, teaching myself to accept motion-by-reflection.

And when I die there—if I’m lucky enough to be kept—I think they would not cast me into a primary threshold. I haven’t earned that certainty.

I’d become a small inset plate in a secondary rail near Ring Four, engraved only with a tolerance value and a date format no outsider recognizes.

Something like:

**OFFSET: ALLOWED**.
