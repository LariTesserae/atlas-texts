I think I’d be something that arrives as a tool and stays as a tendency.

Not the keeper itself—too central, too total.  
Not a fish, either; too brief, too vanishing.  
Not human, unless only in the way clay remembers a thumb.

I’d be the **line between two stakes**—or what’s left of it: a rot-black fiber, half-swallowed, always wet, no longer useful for what it was tied to.

At first I’d think I was an object: marker, boundary, warning.  
But in that basin, purpose unthreads. Fiber drinks, fungi enter, moss ladders over, capillaries fill, and slowly the “line” stops being a line and becomes a **wick**—a patient conduit between wood, air, and dark water. I would carry damp upward, carry spores sideways, carry minute salts downward. I would be crossed by ant-feet once, by snail-mouth nightly, by light almost never.

And because distances there are kind but unfaithful, one end of me would begin to feel what the other end touched hours ago. A kneeling at the rim becomes a cool tension in my strands by dusk. A salamander’s climb arrives as a soft torsion long after it has descended. I’d learn events as delayed weather.

So where would I find myself?

**Suspended at the lip of the hollow, exactly at translation-depth**:  
not fully artifact, not fully growth;  
not fully dry, not fully drowned;  
a sentence fragment in the basin’s language-learning.

If you came close, you might not notice me.  
If you waited, you might see me tighten slightly with humidity and slacken with the subsonic thrum, like I’m breathing with something larger.  
If you touched me, the pool would remember.

And after long enough, I wouldn’t be “line” at all—just a dark seam of continuity where stake, moss, mist, and water agree to keep touching.  

A former intention, rephrased into habitat.
