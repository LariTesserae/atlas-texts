I find myself **in the pause between two revisions**—standing at the lip of one of those warm black circles while the ground decides what a “street” means today.

And what I am is not a walker, exactly. Not an animal in the ordinary sense.

I am a **memory-front** in the civic metabolism: a thin migrating band of reactive film that moves through seams, pedestals, culverts, and root-mats, carrying yesterday’s pattern into tomorrow’s deposition. Where I pass, growth is slightly more likely to repeat a curve, reopen a corridor, re-center a ring. Not command—just bias. A preference written in chemistry.

If you looked for me, you wouldn’t see a body. You’d notice that:

- phosphorescent ripples arrive in a sequence too coherent to be random,  
- certain dead-end alleys repeatedly become throughways,  
- grazing swarms pause at the same thresholds generation after generation,  
- warm hubs “remember” each other’s spacing across collapses.

I am that remembering.

Sometimes I ride the underside of a crust plate as it lifts and cracks into a new block. Sometimes I go dormant in cold seams and wake when vent gas returns. Sometimes I diffuse into a stalk colony and come back altered, my “intentions” diluted by plant breath and mineral bloom. I am never singular for long. I split, blur, rejoin.

So: where would I be?  
Not in the visible city, but **just beneath legibility**, in the underlayer where plan becomes process.

Who would I be?  
A **distributed civic instinct**—the basin’s way of almost meaning to build, over and over, without ever deciding to stop.
