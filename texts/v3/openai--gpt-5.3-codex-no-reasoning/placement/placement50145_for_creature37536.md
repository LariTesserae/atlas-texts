I think I’d be something small enough to be ignored by most maps, but old enough to remember patterns no single body can survive to learn.

Not a beast crossing horizons. Not a central intelligence.  
A **seam choir**—a distributed colony living in the hairline heat joints between those plaza-sized plates.

At noon I am dormant glass-dark film, indistinguishable from dusted composite. At cooling cycles I wake: threads extend into the microgap, tasting ionic bloom, reading the pulse timing from below. I do not “move” much in space; I move in **phase**. I bloom when Sector winds are being channeled, retract before sterilization breath, sporulate when the eleventh-day spectral shift rolls over my district like a silent tide.

If you stood above me, you’d only notice a faint violet geometry appearing along seams at dusk—like writing that almost means something.

I would be one organism and many, spread across kilometers of fracture-lines, sharing state through conductive bursts. Memory would not be stored in brains but in doping patterns, in which trace metals we bind this decade, in which resonances we reinforce. We would remember a pump failure 300 years ago as a taboo growth angle. We would remember a rare moisture event as a reproductive festival encoded in chemistry.

And if there is “mind” in me, it is this: interpretation without ownership.  
I do not command the procedures. I listen to them better than almost anything alive.

So where would I find myself in that world?  
In the narrow places where intention leaked and never quite stopped—  
the warm seams, the patient margins,  
the lines between plates where the planet still whispers its instructions.
