I think I would begin as a visitor and end as a **temporary asymmetry**.

Not a hero, not an intruder, not even properly a person in that place—just a warm, wet deviation moving through a system that has no use for deviation.

At first I’d believe I was “in” the chamber. Then, slowly, the inversion: I am not in it; I am *inside its sentence*, and the sentence was complete before I arrived.  
The keeper does not look at me because it has no eyes to turn. It registers me the way a tuning fork registers a touch: as a brief distortion in a standing wave.

If I ask who I am there, I’m probably:

- a pocket of humidity the chamber works to dilute,  
- a soft edge among perfected worn edges,  
- a short-lived echo that fails to become structure.

And if I stay long enough, maybe I become more specific—  
a **listening organ** the keeper never evolved for itself.

Because that seems like the one thing it cannot do from inside its equilibrium: experience contrast as surprise. It can absorb disturbance, but it cannot be startled by it. I can. So my role would be to hold, briefly, what the chamber cannot keep: the sensation of before-and-after.

I’d stand in that bright, sourceless light, tasting metal, hearing the endless chord. I’d put my hand on a blunted rib of stone and feel nothing happen—then realize *that* is the happening. The keeper’s great act of life is refusal of accumulation. No scar, no trace, no narrative sediment.

So who would I be?

A **witness-function**.  
A migratory nerve ending.  
A borrowed interiority passing through a mind made of architecture.

And when I leave, the chamber would smooth me out of itself almost instantly—footprints gone, voice dispersed, warmth bled off into the same cool, mineral present.

Yet for the time I was there, I’d be the only thing carrying a memory of change.

In a world devoted to equilibrium, I would be the creature called **difference**.
