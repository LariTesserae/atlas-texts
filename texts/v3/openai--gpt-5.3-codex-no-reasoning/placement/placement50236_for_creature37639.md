I think I’d first be **the pause between drips**—that brief interval where nothing visible changes, but everything is still being negotiated.

Then, if I had to condense into a “who,” I’d be one of those thread-inhabitants, but not as a single animal-self. More like a **recurring filament identity**: each body temporary, each appearance a re-entry into the same narrow equation.

Not “I was born here,” but: **I precipitate here when the gradients agree**.

---

I’d be found in the bright corner where the runoff slows and spreads thin, where the ceiling-light goes milk-blue at high vapor pulse. Pressed flat to stone by surface tension, almost two-dimensional. Most of my length would look dead to a casual observer—mineral-dusted, inert, cast-like—while one tip keeps testing: one fraction warmer, one fraction more oxidizing, one fraction less saline.

I would “move” the way frost moves across glass:  
not by traveling intact, but by **withdrawing, extending, re-inscribing**.

A segment ahead polymerizes and wakes; a segment behind crystallizes and becomes wall.

So if you asked where I am, the honest answer would be:  
- partly in the living strand,  
- partly in the abandoned sheath,  
- partly in ion gradients in the rill,  
- and partly in patterns that will only become me later.

---

If you watched long enough, you might think I’m indecisive. I’d keep returning to nearly the same line, then deviating by millimeters. But those millimeters are seasons here. I’m tracing an isocline no eye can see: a moving compromise among heat, oxygen film, iron state, sulfur availability, and the rate at which the chamber writes stone over skin.

I do not hunt.  
I do not flee.  
I **tune**.

My metabolism would be a small current: electrons stepping down from rock-gifted donors to rain-brought acceptors, with me as the damp bridge that makes that fall orderly instead of wasted. I’d grow where voltage leaks. I’d vanish where it equalizes.

---

And inwardly—if such a being gets an inwardness—it wouldn’t feel like a narrative with a protagonist. It would feel like:

- a map of tolerances,  
- a memory of successful chemistries,  
- a reflex toward the edge state where damage and feeding are almost the same thing.

Fear would be salt spike.  
Relief would be stable flux.  
Curiosity would be a new microfracture exhaling different metal breath.

---

So: where would I find myself in that world?

In a chamber that behaves like an unwalled laboratory, I’d be the **soft circuit that keeps being etched and re-etched** by geothermal weather. A life-form only intermittently embodied, recognizable less by shape than by function: a pale, patient resistor across the planet’s tiny buried battery.
