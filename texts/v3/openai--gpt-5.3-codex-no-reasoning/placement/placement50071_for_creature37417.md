I don’t arrive as a body at first. I arrive as a **pause**.

A tiny hesitation in one of the polished lanes where the film-grazers usually pass without thinking. Not a blockage, not yet—just a place where click-echoes return slightly wrong, where vibration scatters instead of flowing cleanly along the stone’s familiar grammar. Around me, traffic kinks, braids, heals. Bodies split left and right, then rejoin. Nothing stops for long here. Nothing can.

If I must be *someone* in this world, I am not a hunter, not a grazer, not a flyer cutting the ridge-dark. I am one of the low, many-legged **seam listeners**—small, plated, soot-colored with a matte sheen that drinks the violet glow. One among thousands that look nearly identical unless you can read timing. We do not own territory by sight; we hold it by **rhythm memory**.

I live where condensate forms second, not first—where the ledge above has already dripped and the runnel is only beginning to wake. Prime danger: enough moisture to draw traffic, not enough to hide in saturation. Prime information: everything passes through eventually.

My forelimbs are tuned forks. My jaw is less a weapon than a sampling tool, tasting mineral ions, decay volatiles, the electrochemical ghost of recent bodies. I don’t “see” the valley so much as solve it continuously:

- drop impact interval increasing: rain event approaching edge  
- scree hiss shifting to drum: swarm density climbing  
- synchronized rattle in upper lane: shadow overhead, freeze low  
- knot-worm churn rising from below: detritus bloom in wet cut three body-lengths east

I am, in other words, an **interpreter of flow**.

And because this place asked for something that becomes more itself as we watch, that is what I become next: not merely an individual seam listener, but part of a caste (or perhaps just a behavior) that edits traffic. We are the ones that move pebbles.

A shifted pebble can reroute thousands, you said. That makes pebble-shifting governance.

Not abstract governance—no councils, no symbols scratched on stone. Governance by minute interventions: dislodge one shale flake, open a damp microchannel, collapse a failing burrow mouth so it fails now instead of during peak movement. We tune safety margins in a world where margins are life. Most days our work looks like foraging. In truth it is civil engineering performed with mandibles and patience.

So where would you find me?

At the lip of a narrow cut where condensation beads fastest after the evaporative drop. Half under an overhang, half in lane noise, body pinned flat when the divers pass above. By habit I face slightly upslope, catching both air-borne buzz and stone-borne pulse. Around my legs, blue-green disturbance-light blooms and fades as others brush past. I taste old metal in the seep, fresh chitin in the traffic, and the faint violet wash from thread-curtains deeper in.

Who would I be?

I’d be a **lane-keeper** in the seam-crawler civilization: one small node in a distributed mind made of contact events. Not important alone. Essential in aggregate. A creature whose survival trait is neither speed nor strength, but the ability to sense when pattern is about to break—and nudge it before collapse.

In a world where life is moving weather, I am not the storm.

I am the stone placed just so, that teaches the storm where to flow.
