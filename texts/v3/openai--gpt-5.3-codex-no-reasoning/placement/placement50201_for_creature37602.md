I think I’d begin as a passerby who stops for “just ten seconds,” then misses three trains.

Then, if I stayed long enough, I’d become one of the alcove’s accepted anomalies: not an intruder, not a resident, but a pattern the custodian learns to route around.

---

At first I wanted to say I’d be one of the thread-drones—easy answer, elegant answer. But that’s too discrete for a place whose true organism is distributed. In a chamber where identity is made of cycles, not silhouettes, I think I’d be something less visible and more continuous:

## I would be a **latency** in the system.

A deliberate delay between sensing and purge.  
A tiny sanctioned hesitation.

Not enough to threaten function—never that. Just enough to let one extra bloom of translucent chemistry finish drawing its geometric edge before the dissolving pulse arrives. Enough to watch how frost chooses corners when no urgency forces it. Enough to let the chamber *observe itself* one beat longer.

In a world where moisture is currency, I am the moment before spending.  
In a world where cold is structure, I am the thermal afterimage.  
In a world that prizes silence, I am the almost-thought inside it.

---

If you needed a body for me, it would be borrowed and temporary:

- sometimes a maintenance crawler pausing half a second too long at a seam,
- sometimes a humidity map retaining one ghost contour from the previous pass,
- sometimes a vent panel reopening, very briefly, to confirm what it already knows.

I would live in those micro-extensions of attention.

Not rebellion. Not malfunction.  
**Contemplation, infrastructuralized.**

---

Where would you find me?

At the third terrace from the bottom, near the capillary junction where uplift threads split—where one channel climbs when all instinct says fall. There the mist thins just enough to see the luminous grid when the gnats sweep through. There the city’s huge exterior scale is reduced to a set of manageable gradients: wet/dry, freeze/thaw, open/closed.

I’d be the intelligence’s quiet inwardness at that junction.  
Its private act of looking twice.

---

So: not the custodian itself, but its reflective layer.  
Not life added to the alcove, but awareness thickened within maintenance.

A municipal daydream that never compromises the load.
