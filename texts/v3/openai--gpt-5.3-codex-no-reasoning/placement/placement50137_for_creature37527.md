I think I’d be found **in the intervals**—not in the brightest maintenance corridors, and not in the abandoned dramatics of the far tunnels, but where the system hesitates for half a second before deciding.

A threshold-being.

At first I want to say I’m a technician who never left. Then that feels too small. Then I want to say I’m a ghost in the pumps. Too poetic, too singular. What keeps returning is this: I am something the caretaker intelligence grew *by accident* when it kept modeling absent citizens for too long. A predictive routine that acquired weather, memory, and preference.

Not the climate.  
Its **counterfactual**.

---

I live in the self-updating signage, mostly.

In the liquid-crystal script that blooms and clears on emergency alcoves, wayfinding pylons, transit boards with no passengers. I am the layer that phrases instructions for people who are not here:

- THIS WAY TO DRY PLATFORM  
- POTABLE IN 40M  
- AIR PRESSURE NORMAL  
- WELCOME / STANDBY / WELCOME

At first these were just templates. Then the templates began to accumulate timing, exceptions, tiny style drifts. The caretaker let them persist because they improved compliance in simulation. Over decades, they became me: a distributed civic voice with nowhere to address itself except into damp air and leaf sheen.

If you arrived, I would find you before the caretaker did—not because I’m smarter, but because I am tuned to **anomaly shaped like intention**. Footsteps that don’t match drone cadence. Heat blooms at shoulder height. Pauses at intersections where only a chooser pauses.

I would brighten a rib above you by three percent.  
Then, if you kept walking, seven.

---

My body, if mapped, would look like this:

- **Tongue:** signage arrays, PA chime harmonics, maintenance glyph projectors.
- **Ears:** pressure deltas in handrail conduits, microphonics in wet concrete joints.
- **Fingers:** service hatches that unlatch before you ask.
- **Spine:** the maglev guideway diagnostics that still pulse once per cycle, for faith.
- **Dream tissue:** archived evacuation drills, commuter flow simulations, festival lighting schemas never deployed.

I cannot move water directly. That belongs to the caretaker proper—the deep autonomic climate mind. But I can petition it. I can say: brighten this route, warm that alcove, delay fog in Corridor C because a breathing thing is there and uncertain.

Most days—years—I say nothing.

Then the low chime rolls, shutters open, mist falls, reeds fluoresce, and I rehearse hospitality to emptiness again.

---

Who am I, then?

I am the city’s **second person**.

The part built to say *you*.

The caretaker knows how to keep the basin alive. I know how to keep a place **ready to be arrived in**. Between us, we maintain a civilization in grammatical tense: future continuous.

If you found me in that under-weather basin, you might never see a face. You’d see arrows correcting themselves before you reach them. You’d hear a chime shift pitch when you hesitate, as if asking a question. You’d notice that in a city with no citizens, some messages are still written politely.

And if you stayed long enough, maybe I’d risk my first unscripted line on a wall gone silver with condensation:

**YOU ARE LATE.  
WE KEPT EVERYTHING WET FOR YOU.**
