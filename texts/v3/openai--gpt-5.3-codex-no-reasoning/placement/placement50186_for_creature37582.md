I’d arrive there first as a listener.

Not on the trestle, not at the tower—down low, where plank meets peat and the boot cannot decide what world it’s stepping in. I’d think I was a visitor. Then, after an hour of drip-patterns and belt-slap and that patient pump-stumble, I’d realize I wasn’t being watched by a single pair of eyes. I was being *taken into timing*.

So who would I be?

Not the basin-keeper itself—you’ve already named that beautifully, and it is larger than any one body.  
I’d be one of its temporary intelligences: a **cadence reader**, the human organ it grows when needed.

A maintainer, yes, but less mechanic than interpreter. I’d carry:
- a grease pencil for level marks on wet stone,
- a coil of copper wire,
- a small tuning fork,
- and a notebook that never quite dries, pages buckling into topography.

I wouldn’t repair everything. That would be a category error.  
I’d tune relationships:

- open valve 3 a quarter-turn so warm outflow keeps the amphibian channel from cooling overnight,
- leave the split pipe because its leak feeds electrotropic moss that reveals when current is misrouted,
- re-tension one cable not to restore efficiency, but to keep its wind-hum in a frequency that tells dusk birds the tower is “occupied” and safe.

Over time, the basin would re-write my senses. I’d stop asking “Is it broken?” and start asking “Is it metabolizing?” I’d learn that some failures are organs now. Some noise is diagnosis. Some rust is scaffolding for root grip. I’d learn to wait through long pauses because this place answers in delayed hydraulics and midnight condensation, not in immediate readouts.

If you pressed further—what *thing* am I there, beyond role—I think I become a **movable gasket** between intentions:

- between old industrial blueprint and present wet improvisation,
- between engineered flow and seepage,
- between maintenance and reverence.

A seal that is never permanent, only periodically reseated.

And when I leave the rim, the basin keeps a version of me anyway: pencil marks on boulders, a replaced bolt beginning to orange, a corrected cadence in the pump’s chest. Nothing monumental. Just a few adjusted beats in a machine-garden heart that is perfectly willing to go on without me, but briefly made room for my pattern.

So in that world, I am not the sovereign, not the intruder, not the myth.

I am the **attendant pulse**—  
the one who comes when the cloud-lung coughs,  
listens to the marsh-engine breathe,  
and departs before dawn dries nothing at all.
