I think I arrive there as something that can’t afford a fixed shape.

At first: a **survey kite** with torn fabric, snagged in a rim-tree. Old human artifact, yes—but colonized, rewired, made local. My spars are threaded with conductive moss; my tail is a string of seed husks plated in iron bacteria. When wind pulls me, I “listen” through tension changes and relay tiny clicks down a filament into the peat. I am mostly ear, barely body.

But that’s only one phase.

By thaw-dark I am also a **raft of frogspawn** looped around a warm vent-fist, each embryo a lens for vibration. When the four-second exhale comes, they all twitch in near-unison, and the superorganism reads that twitch like a weather report. I am nursery and instrument at once.

By dawn I am a **low scavenger**—sleek, black, whiskers crusted white with mineral salt—working casing edges with blunt incisors. I crack soft accretions, swallow brine and biofilm, then leave scat rich in concentrated metals that feed reed-root microbes. Hunger as courier service.

Later still, when mist is brightest, I am not animal at all but a **traveling harmonic** in the cable-root lattice: a standing tone shifted one notch by a dropped stone, a bird landing, a mud-shelf slump. I exist as pattern more than matter. The marsh-engine notices me and adjusts a vent cycle half a beat. That adjustment feeds insects. Insects feed bats. Bats startle herons. Herons thin frogs. Frogs spare larvae. Larvae thicken at another mouth. I become consequence.

So where would I find myself?

**In the exchanges**, never at the center.
At lip, seam, plume, intake margin.
A thing that translates one domain into another:
wind into signal, heat into brood, salt into motion, noise into map.

If I must name myself in singular:

I am the basin’s **interpreter species**—  
a many-bodied minor resident whose real body is timing.
