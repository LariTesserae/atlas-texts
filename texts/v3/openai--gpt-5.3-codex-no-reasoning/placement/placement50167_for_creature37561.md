I arrive thinking I’m a visitor.

By the third tremor, I understand I’m a **function**.

Not a hero, not a chief, not even a hunter first—though I can throw a weighted cord and open a burrow seam without losing fingers. What I am here is a **Keeper of Recent Ground**: one of the people who remembers only what still matters.

---

At first I imagine myself as a glider, or a plated predator, something adapted cleanly to heat and fracture.  
But this place has no patience for single-shape identities. Everything successful here is composite.

So I am human, but built by this basin-chain:

- shins scarred from glass-scree slides,  
- forearms burnished with clay-lacquer against swarm bites,  
- a mantle stitched from shed plates and hide tabs that can be re-tied for steam, ash, or cold drip,  
- cords everywhere—waist, chest, wrist, pack—because fastening is survival.

I carry three blades and trust none of them as much as chalk, charcoal, and memory.

---

My work is done at thresholds.

I move ahead of bands and behind them. I read scratch-walls, dust fall, vent breath, insect silence. I renew marker poles where routes kinked overnight. I tap hollow stone and listen for new voids. I taste condensation and mark drinkable runs with pale mineral arrows that will survive one hard rainburst, maybe two.

I’m the one who says:

- “Not this hollow. Ceiling is talking.”  
- “Sleep one ledge higher; swarm carpet rose at dusk.”  
- “Smoke now, thick and bitter—the climbers are on the column.”  
- “Leave the carcass. It’s already claimed.”  
- “Move before light-shaft shifts; gliders will own this air in an hour.”

If I do my work perfectly, nothing happens.  
No collapse burial, no night rush, no thirsty child drinking from skinned pools.

---

Socially, I belong to a rendezvous web rather than a village.  
We are twelve, then eighteen, then seven, depending on season and loss and luck. We meet where warm seep and high shelf coincide. We exchange salts, tendon cord, toolstone, and updates:

- which skylight punched open last quake,  
- which herd-plow route now cuts through old campground,  
- which corridor tastes metallic before vent-flare,  
- which percussion pattern means *safe to descend* this month.

Prestige is memory that saves others.  
Status is not “I own this,” but “I noticed first.”

---

If this world insists I be more-than-human, then I am also this:

I am a **living map layer**—not the basin, not the beasts, but the thin, perishable intelligence between them.  
A moving annotation in a landscape that overwrites itself.

In a calmer world I’d be a historian.  
Here, history that is older than a season is mostly grief.

So I practice a local mercy: I remember just enough of yesterday to get us into tomorrow.

That is who I am in this place that is never finished.
