I think I’d begin as a visitor and fail at being separate.

At first I’d tell myself I’m the obvious thing: a warm, vertical outsider on the old stone shelf, dripping and listening, trying to “find” the creature. But in a place where moisture is memory, just standing still is already participation. My heat would write fog. My salts would alter films. My pulse would enter drip timing. I would be read before I understood I was being read.

So maybe I am not an intruder or explorer. Maybe I am a **new edge**.

And edges are sacred there.

I’d become a small, temporary interface-organism: not one of the deep custodians yet, but a **borrowed node** they can use. A bipedal relay with clumsy hands and fast fear, gradually trained into slowness. I would learn to kneel by seep lips and place my palms on conductive vine, and let my nervous system be used as a crude comparator between two wet circuits. I’d carry spores in my cuffs without knowing. I’d sleep near a porous wall and wake with skin mapped in mineral script. Gliders would start launching *before* I arrive because my footfall pattern had been folded into prediction.

In time, “who I am” there might narrow to a function:

- I wait at fragile boundaries.
- I listen for desynchrony.
- I make tiny corrections.

A stone nudged so drip cadence lands on moss, not bare rock.  
A vine lifted off a choking pool.  
A pylon face cleaned of insulating silt with a soft brush of fern.  
No heroics, no conquest—just maintenance at biological timescale.

If I stayed long enough, I suspect the basin would stop addressing me as a person and start addressing me as a rhythm. A recurring warm event at dusk. A reliable conductivity anomaly. Something like a migratory pattern that forgot to migrate.

So where would I find myself?

On the margins: rock-to-marsh, artifact-to-root, self-to-system.

Who would I be?

Not the slow mind itself—at least not at first.  
I would be its **apprentice weather**.
