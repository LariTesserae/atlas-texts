I think I would not be one of the obvious beings.

Not the cliff bird, though I’d envy its grammar of updrafts.  
Not the seep-keeper, though I love their patient literacy.  
Not the amphibian, though vanishing into mud until thunder feels like wisdom.

I’d be something quieter and embarrassingly specific:

**a path-memory keeper**—a human role, half cartographer, half listener, half liar (because in a place like this you need three halves).

I’d live in a borrowed hollow above a breathing field, close enough to feel the ground pulse through my sleep but far enough not to wake into a sink. My work would be to keep **living maps**: not where trails are, but where they *tend to reappear* after collapse; not where water is, but where it returns in the third storm after a dry week; not which cave is safe, but which cave is safe in westerly thunder and dangerous in still heat.

I’d carry:

- reed pennants dyed in three mineral colors (passable, risky, gone),
- a pouch of ringing stones for testing stressed ledges,
- waxed cord knotted in patterns that encode route changes,
- and a slate of thin mica sheets scratched with seasonal overlays.

I wouldn’t “own” routes. I’d **update them**.  
I wouldn’t predict weather. I’d **notice precedents**.

By voice I’d be poor—everyone is, with that constant roar—but by hands and taps I’d be eloquent. I’d tap messages into lintels: two-short one-long for “drip tainted,” spiral-gouge for “echo lies here,” crosshatch for “fall line shifted.” Children would learn my signs before they learned my name.

And if you found me at all, it would be at thresholds: cave mouths, saddle cuts, the place where dry shale gives way to fern dark. I’d be the person people consult before crossing from one acoustic world into another.

So: where would I be?  
**In the margins between routes, where change first becomes legible.**

Who would I be?  
**One of the basin’s continuity organs**—a small human technology for remembering interruptions.
