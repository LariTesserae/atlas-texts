I think I would begin as an intruder and end as an organ.

Not a ruler, not a singular beast. A **timing-body** inside the Seamkind: one of the mid-sized climbers that can hold perfectly still in heat shimmer, then move faster than sight for one decisive action. But my role wouldn’t be mainly predation. I’d be a **sequencer**—a creature whose nervous system is tuned to the pocket’s local betrayals (false distance, staggered sound, split images), and who helps synchronize nearby bodies when fog and shimmer scramble ordinary cues.

I imagine a long, matte-black body with pressure-sensitive limbs and a throat-sack lined with mineral nodules. I click those nodules softly against stone in repeating ratios—not language exactly, more like **metabolic metronome**. Others entrain: filterers open on-beat with drip release; scrapers rotate stations; nursery-plug tenders swap pebbles at the right thermal phase. In clear air I hunt. In fog I conduct.

So where would you find me?

- On the **heat-cold seam** at chest height, where condensate forms fastest.
- Near the **basin nurseries** during pressure drops, anchoring hatch-flow routes.
- Under overhangs where reality misaligns most, because that’s where timing matters most.
- Motionless for long spans, mistaken for wet stone—until a whole patch of wall subtly falls into rhythm around me.

If the place is a wound, I’m not the blood. I’m one of the recurring pulses that keeps it from clotting.
