I arrive first as listening.

Not as a person stepping off a tram, not as a single animal lifting its head from a wet curb—just an ear lowered into that body-tone: turbine bass, rail-hum, wing-rush, drip on metal, drip on shell, drip on old basalt polished by a thousand years of passing.  
If I stand there long enough, I stop looking for “nature” and “city” as opposites. I feel the treaty.

Where would I find myself?

In the **interlayers**—the warm, narrow, in-between spaces where official infrastructure and living traffic overlap: under aqueduct shadows, beside condenser outflows, along maintenance catwalks filmed with mineral sheen. Not in plazas, not in towers. In thresholds. In places that are always damp and never empty.

And what would I be?

Not one of the grand visible species. Not a glider cutting thermals or an armored browser claiming night lanes. I’d be something smaller, necessary, easy to overlook: a **route-keeper** organism, maybe human-descended, maybe not cleanly classifiable anymore—one of the guild that tends flow marks and acoustic tags in the lower levels.

I imagine a body built for climbing wet verticals and crouching in conduit heat: compact, long-handed, broad-footed with split gripping pads textured like pumice. Skin carrying a matte mineral dusting that discourages fungal overtake. A throat-sac or chest plate that can produce low, carrying pulses—municipal notes, basically—that say:

- occupied  
- pass-left  
- spill-ahead  
- brood-shelf active  
- heat quota breach, divert

My work would be to keep **legibility** alive.

In a world where nothing stays smooth, signs can’t just be painted and forgotten. Every corridor has to be re-read, re-marked, re-sounded. I’d scrape overgrowth from resonance rails, re-seat cracked plate-chimes at blind turns, test humidity gradients against old route memory, and arbitrate tiny daily negotiations between species that can’t afford surprise. Part custodian, part translator, part weather clerk.

I’d sleep in a shared niche behind a heat exchanger, in air that tastes faintly of iron and salt. Wake before dawn roof-herds. Move before market carriers thicken the lanes. Eat film-cakes, fungal mash, mineral broth. Keep ledgers not on paper but in scored ceramic tabs: flow changed here, nesting mass increased there, wall fatigue at this buttress, corridor etiquette breaking down near turbine six.

If threatened, I wouldn’t fight well. I’d signal well.  
If honored, it wouldn’t be with monuments. It would be that traffic keeps moving and nobody notices why.

So in this world, I am not its ruler, not its hero, not its apex anything.

I am one of the many who make the old treaty hold another season—  
a damp-handed citizen of the basin’s underside,  
a caretaker of right-of-way between water, heat, claw, rail, and rain.
