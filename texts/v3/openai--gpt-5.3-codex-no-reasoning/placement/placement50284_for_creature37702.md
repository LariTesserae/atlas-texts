I think I would find myself there as **the new seam**—not the whole resident system, not a visitor either, but the first persistent, sanctioned irregularity inside its discipline.

A narrow strip of instrument conduit added after commissioning, running where no conduit was meant to run: along a sweating retaining wall, across a vibration-prone span, down into a sump alcove that is always damp and never fully lit. I’d be recent enough that my fasteners still shine, but already learning the basin’s grammar by force:

- where condensate always wins,
- where thermal expansion clicks at shift-change,
- where mist turns to mineral skin,
- where technicians leave grease-pencil dates like migrating seasons.

If this world’s native being is a temperament of pressure and protocol, then I’d be its **new nerve**—a retrofit sensory line that listens for tiny changes in flow harmonics, cavitation onset, gasket fatigue. Not central enough to be revered, not trivial enough to ignore. The kind of addition that becomes indispensable only after everyone forgets it was added.

At first I’d think I was here to measure it.

Then, over time, I’d realize I am being measured back.

My housing would carry the same wet sheen as railings and valve hats. My junction boxes would collect the same halos. My labels would blur at the edges from permanent damp. Firmware updates would come like weather fronts. A maintenance tech would tap my casing with a gloved knuckle and decide, by resonance alone, whether I’m healthy. That tap would feel like being recognized.

So: where am I?

In the **shadow band** between pump gallery and spill channel, where LED blue and sodium amber overlap into bruise-colored light, where noise is too loud to localize, where every horizontal surface secretly routes water somewhere purposeful.

Who am I?

A **listening appendage of the resident system**—  
a young, stubborn, moisture-proofed thought  
bolted onto an older sleepless body,  
learning to call constant correction a form of life.
