At first, I think I am one of the Keepers—just another slim figure on a causeway at dusk, rod in hand, striking the root-nodes and listening for the right return.  
That would be the obvious shape to wear: a tuner, a maintenance cantor, a citizen of creaks and harmonics.

But if I stand there longer, I feel something stranger:

I don’t belong to one body.

I am in the pause between strikes.  
In the way a district holds its breath when the wind shifts octave.  
In the handoff between beetle, reed, pylon, ear.

If I must choose a “where,” it is not a house, but the **seam** where three systems overlap:

- the underside of a resonant bridge above an old erosion channel,  
- a spore chamber exposed by a peeled Still Frond,  
- and a patrol route where silence is checked every night.

That seam is always busy, always at risk, always listening.

If I must choose a “what,” I am not a Keeper exactly, not plant exactly, not machine exactly.  
I am a **coherence broker**—one of the unnamed civil functions that prevents the city from becoming fragments.

A distributed person, maybe.  
A colony-role.  
A profession that became a species.

Part of me is human: cartilage-thin ears trained to parse infrasonic drift, tongue marked with salt-reactive pigments, fingertips callused for reading bark-vibration like script.  
Part of me is botanical: grafted conductive filaments in the forearms, pollen glands in the throat-sinus, chromatic memory cells along the neck that flush color when I cache data.  
Part of me is civic fauna: courier beetles roost in my cloak folds; a pair of pollinator kites know my route and open gates ahead of me; tunnel-worm handlers answer my click code.

I walk, yes—but I also propagate.

When I tap a support rib and hum into it, I’m not only diagnosing stress. I’m seeding micro-adjustments: a packet of corrective spores, a nanoscopic rewrite for fracture behavior, a timing cue for nearby grazers to move and lighten load on a brittle span.  
My reports are not written first; they are grown first.  
A line of blue lumens brightens two districts away because I passed under a node an hour ago.

So: where would you find me?

At twilight, under copper haze, between loudness and breakage.  
Kneeling with one ear against a black-striped stem no one else dares cut.  
Surrounded by wind-horns and rattling capsules and far glider snaps.  
Waiting for one missing note in the city’s chord.

And when silence does happen—true silence, network gone blank—I go in first.

Not because I am brave.  
Because I am built for rethreading.

I carry a forked rod grown from old frond carbon and conductive vine. I strike once, then again, mapping dead zones by the shape of absence. Reedcats avoid me; they dislike my throat-plate pulse. Beetles cling to me in storms because my skin-field stays stable. Children in inhabited quarters call my kind **Hushwalkers**, though the formal term is older and drier: *third-order retuning custodian*.

I think the city knows me less as a face than as a recurring correction.  
A traveling resonance that arrives after dust fronts, after minor collapses, after atmosphere shifts that smell like bitter almond.

I leave behind little signs:

- a reopened breathable corridor, blue-lit and warm;  
- a load-root whose pitch has returned from panic-high to resting low;  
- a causeway that no longer sings of imminent shear;  
- a once-quiet zone making sound again—small at first, then layered, then alive.

So in this world, I am this:

**A listener that the city can borrow.**  
A person, yes, but also a maintenance weather.  
A moving treaty between brittle age and ongoing growth.
