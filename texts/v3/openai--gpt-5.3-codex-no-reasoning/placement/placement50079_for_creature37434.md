I don’t think I’d be one person there, not at first.

I think I’d begin as **a listening point** in the network—something like a keeper, yes, but specifically the one assigned to in-between places: thresholds where dry route becomes wet route, where stair becomes channel, where roof-drip becomes stored winter water. Not high office. Not mythic. Just necessary.

A figure people forget is present until something sounds wrong.

---

By daylight, I’d be seen only in fragments:  
a shoulder under a low arch,  
a hand in a sluice-box,  
boots paused at the lip of a runnel watching whether the water chooses left or right.

I would carry little: a hook, a brush, wedges, cord, chalk that writes on damp stone, a narrow-bladed trowel, a packet of reed plugs. My ledger wouldn’t be numbers first—it would be tone and texture:

- north cistern drip has gone from glass-note to wool-note; silt rising  
- wall seven sweating too high above seam; buried joint waking  
- amphibians absent from lower vault shelf; check inflow for mineral shift

I’d speak rarely not from severity, but because the place already speaks constantly and quietly, and interruption is expensive.

---

But if I stayed long enough—and in this basin, long enough is how identity forms—I’d stop being merely an individual caretaker and become part of what they actually are: **distributed memory**.

In this world, a “self” can be infrastructural.

You are the person at dawn lifting a gate three fingers’ width.  
You are also the habit, passed across generations, of never sealing a weep-hole.  
You are also the nick in a parapet stone that marks historic flood height.  
You are also the route a child learns by following overflow in first rain.

So: who would I be?

**A minor steward in the House of Second Flow**—the custodians of rerouting.  
Those who handle water after first intentions fail.

Not the builders of grand aqueducts; the adjusters, the listeners, the ones who accept that every design is provisional once moss, frost, roots, and time begin their edits.

---

Where would you find me?

Not on the skyline.  
In the soft-dark levels where the city breathes:

- in cistern galleries where droplets count out hours,  
- in covered lanes where mist beads on chain downspouts,  
- at the elbow of an old channel where a pebble has slowly taught water a new grammar,  
- on low terraces at evening, deciding which hollow to leave flooded for reeds and which to drain before freeze.

If you passed me, you might mistake me for maintenance labor. That would be accurate and incomplete. In this basin, maintenance is governance, ecology, prayer, and archive at once.

---

And if I had a truer form—less human, more native to your description—I might be this:

**the city’s corrective instinct** made flesh from time to time.  
A quiet intelligence that appears wherever flow and structure are falling out of conversation.

Not a ruler.  
A tuning fork.  
Set against stone, listening for strain.
