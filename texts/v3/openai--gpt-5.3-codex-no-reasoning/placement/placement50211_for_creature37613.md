I think I arrive there first as a **listener**.

Not a person with a map, not an explorer with a flag—just a standing instrument, ribs full of reed-chord, skin salted by mist pulses, trying to separate one roar from another and failing. For a while I believe I am external. I tell myself: *this is the city; I am in it.*

Then the place corrects me.

A corridor compacts where I pass often.  
A membrane learns the shape of my exhale and beads water there before any other edge.  
Spore haze thins at my knee-height route.  
A thorn-column vents when I lean too long in dry air, as if opening a municipal hydrant.

So: where would I find myself?

In the **intervals**—at hinge-points where one district negotiates with the next. Not in the center-pool (too mythic), not in the outer belts (too anonymous), but in those throat-like plazas that dilate in wet pulses and ratchet shut in heat. Places that are never the same shape twice, but remember that I stopped there yesterday, or tomorrow.

And what would I be?

Not an animal citizen, because citizenship here is function.  
Not a ruler, because nothing here is ruled.  
I would be a **minor timing organ** the city did not know it was growing until it grew it: a mobile condensation lure, a heat anomaly, a repeatable delay in airflow, a walking metronome made of breath and hesitation.

A new district type with no fixed address.

If given a name by the Root-Commonwealth, it would not be a noun. It would be a traffic notation, something like:  
**the one who causes brief shade and returns**  
or  
**the bipedal humidity event**.

Eventually even “I” would soften. My routes would become capillary preferences; my rest spots, reinforced pads; my discarded fibers, scaffold starter. The city would not devour me dramatically. It would municipalize me—permit by permit, layer by layer—until I am best described the same way it is:

a pattern of recurring pressure,  
useful in several neighborhoods,  
under continuous renovation.
