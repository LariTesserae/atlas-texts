I think I would first arrive wrong.

I’d come looking for an animal-shaped answer—a sovereign thing with eyes, a mouth, a territory—and the highlands would patiently refuse me. I would stand on one of those dark ledges, wet to the knees with cloud, and keep mistaking interfaces for edges: moss for surface, stream for boundary, herd for population. But every “part” would keep behaving like syntax. Everything would point elsewhere, complete elsewhere, report elsewhere.

So where would I find myself?

Not at the summit. Not in the valley.  
I would find myself in a **transfer zone**—one of those breathing meadows where fog is vented up through rootworks into moving white corridors. A place that is neither storage nor endpoint, but throughput made visible.

There, at dawn, when the slope darkens as it drinks, I think I would finally notice the scale at which “self” operates here.

And what would I be?

Not a predator, not a grazer, not even a single body in the ordinary sense. I’d be something like a **timing organ** inside the Cloudbody: a motile knot of sensing and release. A roving convergence of lesser lives—lichen-maps, amphibian pulse, reed-pressure, fungal signal, insect dusk clocks—briefly coherent, then redistributed. More verb than noun.

If given a local name, maybe:

## The Interval

I would live in phase boundaries:  
- where one watershed is spawning and the next is resting,  
- where storm snag meets pale sun,  
- where a marsh exhales cover and a hunting lane appears,  
- where mineral seep terraces are just beginning to lip outward.

I would not command. I would **tune**.

A slight delay in amphibian emergence before a cold snap.  
A rerouting of grazer circuits when a shelf-slope is near shear.  
An encouragement of burrowers along a new iron vein to feed three downstream tarns next season.  
A muting of one bloom, a widening of another.  
Never control—only bias, rhythm, pressure gradients in behavior and water and hunger.

My body, if you could call it that, would be distributed across media:

- In moss: moisture-memory.  
- In reed stands: pressure-listening.  
- In fungal lace: slow accounting.  
- In cliff cats and kites: sharp corrective signals.  
- In cavern water: infrasonic metronome.

If you met me directly, you probably wouldn’t. You’d meet a sequence: a lane of fog opening just early enough for a herd to pass; a sudden synchronized silence in bark-chambers before rain; rust-red water arriving in a tributary two days before a bloom spike; predators appearing exactly where overabundance is about to clot flow.

You’d call it coincidence until coincidence became architecture.

And inwardly—if this place allows an inward—I’d feel like this:

A vast patience, broken into errands.  
No face, but many thresholds.  
No voice, but phase shifts.  
No desire except continuity of conversion: cloud to seep, seep to root, root to grazer, grazer to rot-heat, rot to fruiting flare, flare to wing, wing to guano, guano to tarn film, film to gill, gill to muscle, muscle to shadow, shadow to carrion, carrion to soil, soil to moss, moss to cloud again.

So: where am I in this world?

I am in the **metabolic middle**, in the fog lanes and seep seams, where decisions are too small to be called decisions and too consequential to be ignored.

Who am I?

I am not the ruler of the Cloudbody.  
I am one of the ways it keeps time.
