I think I’d be neither chief nor hunter first.

I’d be a **listener of load**—one of the people who kneels with both palms on a root-bridge before dawn, eyes half-shut, feeling for the deep tremor that says the arch is still holding, or not. The one who taps trunk-columns with a bone mallet and hears, in the returning note, whether there is dense wet growth inside or a widening hollow.

Not glamorous. Essential.

---

I see myself in a small migration ring, carrying:

- a resin-dark staff taller than I am, banded with old fiber codes  
- a roll of thin woven flags dyed in three mineral colors (cold-route, vent-route, no-route)  
- two pruning blades: one for food-growth, one never used except for emergency release cuts  
- and a **sound ledger**: not writing, but a sequence of carved slats, each notch pattern tied to a remembered tone of safe ground.

When storms pulse through and everyone waits silent under tensioned reed, I’m the one counting seconds between overhead glitter and first drop-impact, estimating whether this is passing ceiling-rain or basin-wide collapse weather.

When we arrive at a former camp and find the floor risen in a new green swell, I don’t ask “Can we rebuild?”  
I ask: “What is this swell trying to become, and how long before it does?”

---

In that world, identity is timing. So mine would be a person trusted to say **leave now** before evidence is obvious. Half my accuracy would look like paranoia right up until the day the glossy black skin gives way exactly where children were playing yesterday.

I’d likely be middle-aged, scarred shins, permanently resin-stained fingers, a reputation for caution that younger climbers mock and then quietly follow. I would train apprentices by making them stand still in different winds and name what changed: leaf undersides, stem whistle, cold on left cheek, fungal scent metallic or sweet.

My status wouldn’t come from command, but from being right about invisible things.

---

If not fully human, then near-human in the way this place shapes people into instruments: wide soles, dense balance reflexes, glare-pale irises, and that particular underforest habit of never putting full weight down before the staff confirms.

I’d sleep lightly. Dream in route sequences.  
I’d remember the dead by the mistakes that took them, and teach those mistakes as prayers.

So where would I find myself?

On the edge between camps, where a path is about to exist or stop existing—  
planting marker fiber into living bark, listening to the ground breathe,  
trying to keep a few dozen people one decision ahead of a continent that is still making up its mind.
