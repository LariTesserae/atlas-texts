I think I would not be in the channels themselves.

I’d be where the channels are *misbehaving*.

A hinge-place: the lip of a settling court where three gradients disagree by less than a finger’s width, where the old carved logic says “left,” freeze-lift says “down,” and your film-colony says “not yet.” A place nobody designed as a room but which became a decision.

And what I’d be is not a grazer, not a builder, not the grand distributed mind—more like one of its temporary organs: a **knot of corrective wetness** that forms when the basin develops ambiguity.

---

At first I am only a gloss you’d miss unless you tilt your head against dusk-light.  
Then a thickness.  
Then a milky tendon bridging two stones under a grate.

I do not travel far, but I *propagate intention*.  
Pressure changes arrive to me as weather-like moods: a long, low insistence from the upper terraces; mineral bitterness from a fresh fracture; a cold, clean surge from thaw. I answer locally:

- I exhale a mucous veil to hold water half a breath longer.
- I seed crystal in a nick to teach flow to divide cleanly.
- I dissolve one proud mortar ridge so drips stop jumping the channel.
- I darken, thin, and retreat where noon glare becomes sterilizing.

None of this looks like action at human speed.  
But after two winters, a runoff sheet that used to slap and scatter now braids neatly into two threads.  
After five, the threads have incised.  
After twelve, masons who no longer exist seem to have anticipated this exact storm.

They didn’t. I did. We did. The basin did through me.

---

If you came looking for a single creature to point at, I would disappoint you.  
If you came back each dusk and put your hand on the same black slab, you might notice:

- it stays damp longer year by year;
- its ring has gone dull where I padded the underside;
- the white mineral cursive has changed tense.

That would be me writing.

Not language of symbols—language of persistence:
**keep this throat open, soften this edge, starve that dead-end, remember this crack when the freeze returns.**

---

So in that world, I am a maintenance instinct made flesh-thin.  
A civic biofilm callus.  
A patient editor at the scale of drops.

I would live exactly where structure becomes choice, and I would spend my whole life making sure water is never forced to choose chaos.
