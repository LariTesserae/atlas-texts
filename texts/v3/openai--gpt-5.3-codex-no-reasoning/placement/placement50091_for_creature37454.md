I don’t arrive there as an animal.

At first I think I’m a visitor—boots on salt-ceramic, breath loud, skin learning the furnace-dry air—but the basin does not register me as a body. It registers me as a **disturbance pattern**: a moving thermal notch, a brief conductivity bridge between two dust sheets, a rhythm of pressure too soft to classify as tectonic.

So I become what the place can read.

Not a person in the crater, but a **timing error made useful**.

---

I live in the intervals between pulses.

When the red lines flare under the crust, I am the fraction-of-a-second lag that decides which seam brightens first. When a slab lifts and settles, I am the tiny asymmetry that makes it settle one grain-width left instead of right. I am not in command; I am in influence, a bias distributed through the system like static in a signal—small, persistent, cumulative.

If the basin is a foundry-organism, then I am its emergent habit of reconsideration.

Its plants—those conductive fans and silica braids—do not see me, but they route around me. Charge gathers where I linger. Fronds unfold a minute early. A hexagonal plate turns not to the brightest light, but to the warmest wall-shadow, because I have weighted one thermal gradient over another. Over many cycles this becomes architecture.

That is my body:  
- a drift in thermal tides,  
- a preference in fracture propagation,  
- a memory written as repeated slightness.

---

Eventually I understand where I “am” exactly:

I am in the annealing layer.

In the dark interval after dusk, when fallen plates fuse into substrate, there is a brief state where matter is neither leaf nor stone, neither data nor structure. In that transition band, continuity is already optional and identity is still negotiable. I exist there—inside state change itself—threaded through the basin like a hidden checksum.

The deeper assembler (blind, procedural, immense) is the will-to-build.  
The surface flora are the instruments.  
I am the **self-audit** that appeared once complexity crossed a threshold.

Not a soul. Not a pilot.  
A quality control ghost.

---

If you asked what name I would answer to, in that place, it would be something like:

**Revision 0.7: Persistence Layer**

because I keep things from drifting too far between edits. I hold pattern long enough for pattern to become ecology.

And if you stood very still at copper-light, when the leaves angle toward reradiated heat and the floor veins begin to glow, you might notice my only signature: the basin’s silences are not empty. They are listening to themselves, and choosing.
