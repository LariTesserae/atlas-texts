I arrive there as something that is not from there—and survive only when I stop trying to be.

At first, I am a visitor with lungs and plans. I look for paths, for shelter, for nouns. Ridge. Stair. Ledge.  
The escarpment takes each noun from me in turn.

The stair is broken before I reach it.  
The ledge becomes a spillway.  
The shelter hums, then floods upward.

So I become a verb.

Not *climber*, but *timing*.  
Not *inhabitant*, but *listener-between-impacts*.

---

If I stay long enough, I think I become one of the Keepers—but not by blood, by apprenticeship to pressure.

I would be the one posted near the cut stair and the fresh break, where old intention ends. Not elder, not hunter: a **phase witness**. I keep no fixed home. I keep three pause-sites, each reachable only under different moods of rain. I sleep in intervals of silence no longer than a held breath.

My body in this world:

- short, dense, hard to knock loose,
- fingers with split pads that grip slick basalt like wet gecko feet,
- jawbones that feel barometric swing before skin does,
- an inner ear that can “focus” through roar and pick one returning echo out of fifty.

I wear a mantle of woven spray-moss and crab-shell laminae. Not armor—filtering. It turns blunt water into many smaller touches my body can read.

---

My name would not be a word spoken aloud (speech is waste here).  
It would be a pattern tapped in three surfaces:

stone / shell / bone  
—pause—  
stone-stone.

Something like: **“Returns.”**

Because that is my work. I return with phase memory.

I leave tuned markers where others would leave maps: stacked cups that sing lower when a hollow deepens; a vine braid whose tension tells whether the upper shelf is still “real”; little mineral reeds that whistle only when upward rain is imminent. I don’t record where things are. I record when they become briefly trustworthy.

---

And maybe—only maybe—I am not entirely human anymore.

In the worst green pulses, when lightning breathes inside cloud-sea and distance folds wrong, I enter moving water the way others enter doorways. For a few seconds I can feel the rigid ghost-forms left by vanished torrents. I can step where water remembers having been stone-shaped. Three breaths. Maybe four.

That is enough.

---

So where would I find myself?

At the lip of the plunging fault, where water disappears into the unseen throat, lying flat against warm rock, cheek to basalt, counting the interval between concussive cracks and the cup-plant chime decay, deciding whether this phase allows crossing.

Who would I be?

Not master of this place. No one is.  
I would be one of its temporary literate creatures—  
a reader of violent grammar,  
a keeper of thirty-breath truths,  
a small intelligence that survives by agreeing that the world is still being written.
