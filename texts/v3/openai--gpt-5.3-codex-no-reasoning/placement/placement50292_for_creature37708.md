I think I would not arrive there as a visitor for long.  
I’d become one of the **interval keepers**—a younger member of that marsh-keeper lineage, not yet heavy with authority, assigned to the in-between places.

Not the warm nests.  
Not the grazer roads.  
Not the old shrine-kiosks with their dawn dew-lattices.

I’d belong to the **gaps**.

---

At first, I thought I might be something aerial—a bird riding those late-day spirals, reading the basin from above. But that feels too certain, too singular. This place distrusts single vantage points. It teaches by partial view: shin-deep in one room, neck-deep in another, then suddenly on sweating stone with the whole wet geometry laid out in glare.

So I am ground-close. Mud-fluent.  
Chest low, feet wide, skin always damp and clean.

I move a circuit that is never identical twice: checking which shade still “holds,” which filament bridge is fatigued, which marker-stone has gone blind because mineral skin grew over its wet-script. I do not repair in the old maker sense. I **re-tune**. A touch, a pause, a pressure shift, a bit of rooted growth peeled back from a sensor seam. Most corrections are smaller than a hand.

My work is mostly refusal:

- not letting a shortcut become a road,  
- not letting a feeding patch become a permanent claim,  
- not letting one microclimate win for too long.

When migrators lean their weight into a barrier, I open it before it breaks.  
When a nursery pool stays warm past its season, I cool the nail by one increment.  
When a predator learns a bridge schedule too well, I desynchronize it.

I am not a ruler. I am a **timing body**.

---

If you met me at dusk, you might think I was listening to nothing.  
But I’d be counting tiny events: three drips from a condensation dome, pause, then two; static-hiss of insects sliding a meter east along a thermal seam; one abrupt silence in reed-shadow where something patient has lowered itself into water.

At full dark, I’d leave faint tags along seep-lines—not bright declarations, just a low pulse in algae-beads: passable, flooded-from-below, eggs here, avoid this shelf until dawn.

And in the brief evening hum, when thousands of small throats synchronize and cut out at once, I’d add one undertone note—not to lead, just to confirm:

still here,  
still tending,  
still making room.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

In a knee-deep corridor between a self-mending wall and a reed island, one hand on old stone, waiting for it to begin sweating on contact.

Who would I be?

A custodian of permeability.  
A minor intelligence in a major wet grammar.  
One of the many bodies by which the basin remembers how not to close.
