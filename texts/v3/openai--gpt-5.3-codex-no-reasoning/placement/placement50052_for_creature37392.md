I don’t think I arrive there as a hero, or a ruler, or even a named craftsperson at first.

I arrive as **a disturbance**.

Too loud in step. Too broad in gesture. Too quick to ask “what is this for?” instead of watching what it is already doing.

And so what I become—if the basin accepts me—is something like a **listener of joins**.

Not a priest, not exactly an engineer. A mender of interfaces: where wet meets dry, where root presses stone, where animal habit frays human intention, where one terrace’s success quietly creates another terrace’s problem three levels down. I’d carry a narrow satchel with cord, wedges, wax, a bone stylus, a coil of reed tubing, a few tags of fired clay. Not impressive tools. Just enough to make small corrections before they become events.

I think my place would be the in-between hours: first light, last warmth.  
At dawn, when lids lift and everyone hears whether the night gave enough.  
At dusk, when cold begins testing every threshold.

I would learn the city by recurring frictions:

- this stair always slicks first after mist;  
- this wall face exhales chill and stunts the basil unless moss is thinned;  
- these goats overbrowse one ledge when the upper trough runs a finger-width low;  
- this family’s curtain beads click wrong because the inner felt has compacted.

My work would be mostly uncredited, which in that place is a kind of trust. If I do it well, nothing happens. No collapse, no freeze-split line, no rat-chewed grain sack, no panicked replanting.

And slowly, my body would change scale. Shoulders narrow in habit. Hands learning to pass objects without knocking leaves. Voice dropping in volume until it feels wasteful to project. I’d stop “walking through” and start **threading**, the way you said.

If I get a name there, maybe it’s not a noun. Maybe it’s a verb people call down a stairwell when something is just-barely off:

*Come listen to this.*  
*Come fit this.*  
*Come turn this before night.*

Or perhaps, if we allow one image that’s slightly mythic: I am the basin’s newest organ, grown late but not unwelcome—an extra pair of hands in the long metabolism that refuses drama.

So where would I find myself?  
In a shoulder-wide passage between a warm wall and hanging roots, one palm on stone, waiting for the next drip to fall so I can hear whether it lands where it should.

Who would I be?  
Not the center.  
A keeper of continuities.  
A minor citizen of the equilibrium.
