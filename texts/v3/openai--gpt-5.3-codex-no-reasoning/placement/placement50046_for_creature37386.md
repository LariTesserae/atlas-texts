I think I’d find myself as **a listener-runner in the middle layer**—not a chief, not a hunter-hero, but a **pattern-keeper** whose job is to keep the group one shift ahead of collapse.

Small-bodied, rope-muscled, always damp. Ankles scarred from vine-burn. Fingers stained with tannin and fungal oil. I’d carry almost nothing permanent: a cord of knots at the wrist, a stone flake in bark wrap, ember tucked in punk-fungus, two bone whistles tuned just off frog pitch. Everything else borrowed, made, dropped.

My “place” wouldn’t be a place. It would be a **moving braid of routes**:
- the leaning fig corridor when the ground-swarms rise,
- the clay lip above the steam hollow during first rain,
- the stormfall clearing for one dusk of egg-taking,
- then gone before scent settles.

I’d sleep in things that last six hours: a sling between wet trunks, smoke cupped under broad leaves, one eye open to acoustic gaps.  
I’d wake to check not light—but **texture of sound**: whether the beetle rasp is continuous, whether pollinator pitch has gone thin, whether an absence has opened like a cut.

Socially, I’d be one of those who speaks in fragments: clicks into root-buttress, throat pulses, leaf-whistles bent to seem accidental. Not secrecy for its own sake—just refusal to be locatable. I’d teach children the first law: *if the basin goes suddenly quiet, move before you understand why.*

Spiritually, I’d believe identity is rhythmic, not fixed. You are who you can keep in relation: storm-timing, swarm-mood, predator-tax, kin-distance, rot-gift. Ancestors live as techniques in the body—how to braid a flood sling blind, how to hear hollow soil, how to leave no camp worth finding.

So: who or what would I be?

**Not a resident of the basin. A temporary coherence inside it.**  
A pair of ears, quick hands, and a memory that knows how to expire on time.
