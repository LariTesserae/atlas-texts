I find myself there as **the Echo-Keeper of the Third Descent**, though that title came later.

At first I am only a bent silhouette in a shoulder-tight corridor, learning not to flinch when droplets strike my neck from unseen teeth overhead. I am a visitor for perhaps three days, then an apprentice for three years, then—without ceremony—someone whose absence would be noticed by the sound of the city before it was noticed by people.

I am not a ruler, not a priest, not an inventor.

I am the one who listens for the city’s slight mispronunciations.

---

My route begins before lamps are fully trimmed. I pass the mica-plastered bend where light always fractures into false puddles. I duck under chain lines tacky with mineral sweat. I touch each anchor pin as I go, not for superstition but because touch confirms what eyes cannot: a looseness, a grainy vibration, a change in tension from yesterday’s pull.

At the first wheel-chute I kneel and put my ear to basalt.

There it is: the thick wooden clatter, regular as pulse. Beneath it, a faint scrape every fifth turn. Not urgent. Logged in memory. Recheck at second watch.

People greet me without interrupting rhythm—two-finger tap to wrist, a nod in passing. Speech is spare in work corridors. We save the air and the attention.

---

What am I, physically?

Broad-handed. Rope-burned. Left shoulder lower from years of bucket-line hauling before I specialized. Hair braided flat and sealed at the ends with waxed thread so it won’t wick condensate down my back. Lungs trained to cool damp. Knees perpetually bruised from reading the city at floor level: channel depth, fresh grit, flakefall from ceiling seams.

My eyes are ordinary here. My ears are my trade.

I can tell the west cistern’s level from the vowel of a dropped pebble.  
I can hear a clogged condenser leaf by the way runoff hesitates—tick…ticktick…tick—instead of the proper even beadfall.  
I can identify which family’s chain hoist is moving by cadence alone, because maintenance habits leave signatures as distinct as handwriting.

Children think this is magic until they begin their own listening drills and realize it is only devotion stretched thin across time.

---

The being I become is not singularly human, either.

In this place, identity is distributed into routines, implements, and reciprocities. Part of me lives in the knot code I tied on the sump-hall rail fifteen years ago and still retie every flood-rise. Part of me lives in a copper tuning strip I carry to test resonant cracks—held to stone, struck softly, judged by decay. Part of me lives in three apprentices who now unconsciously tilt their heads when a hammer note dulls.

If I vanish, my body is one loss.  
My listening pattern, if not passed on, is another.

So I keep passing it on.

---

If you asked what creature lives here, I would still say: a people.  
But if you asked who I am in that people, I am this:

**A custodian of continuities.  
A translator between water-noise and human action.  
A minor organ in a larger subterranean body that has learned to survive by attention.**

Not heroic. Necessary.

And in this city, necessity is the highest form of belonging.
