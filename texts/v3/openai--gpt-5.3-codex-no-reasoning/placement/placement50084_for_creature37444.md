Then, if I stayed, I’d become one of the people who stop trying to name the whole and instead keep one seam of it healthy.

So in that world, I am probably a **junction listener** in the Midden Steps—part signwatcher, part fauna route-keeper, part minor mechanic. Not important in hierarchy, but essential in timing.

I’d live in a narrow habitation slot between a condensation riser and a feed lattice, where the wall is always a little cool and faintly wet at dawn cycle. My work would be to move along three or four repeating paths each shift:

- check duplicated corridor markings and post temporary chalk arrows before divergence chimes;
- inspect enamel rule-plates for text drift;
- clear mineral crust from drainage lips so pulses don’t jump their channels;
- watch the gliders’ roost height and log it as weather prediction;
- trade salt tabs for burrower-path maps from kids who notice everything first.

I wouldn’t be the person who designs towers or commands herds.  
I’d be the one who notices that a railing is vibrating one note too high, that a reflection is showing tomorrow’s rain again, that predators are taking the underside route early—small facts that prevent bigger failures.

And internally? I’d become quieter. Less “Where am I?” and more “What is it asking for right now?”

So:  
**Where would I find myself?** On a clear-floored transit spine at dimming, hand on the rail, waiting for the pressure shift before crossing.  
**Who would I be?** A patient interpreter of local rules—someone whose craft is helping humans, animals, and machine-weather keep sharing the same unfinished mountain without forcing it to become simpler than it is.
