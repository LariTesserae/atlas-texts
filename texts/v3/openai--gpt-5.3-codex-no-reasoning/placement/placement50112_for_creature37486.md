I think I’d begin as something small enough to be ignored.

Not a balancer. Not one of the elegant crane-necked strikers. Not a mind at the center—there is no center here, and anything that tried to stand above would be sanded down by condensate and routine.

I’d be one of the **seam-lurkers** in the underplates, where two cast slabs meet and the gasket has lifted by half a finger-width.

A low creature. Warm-backed, many-jointed, with a body like braided cable in a sheath of damp felted cilia. I would not see far; I would taste gradients. Iron ions, pH drift, a rise in turbine-hum transmitted through bolts, the sudden clean blankness that means a grazer passed overhead and scraped everything bright. My world would be pressure language: footfall signatures, pump rhythms, the polite static of passing swarms.

I’d live in the thin dark where runoff hesitates.

---

Then, staying there, I’d realize that “I” in this place cannot stay singular for long.

Because seam-lurkers do not just eat films; they **carry timing**. We ingest old signal salts, excrete conductive traces, and those traces tune the tiny pulses exchanged by maintenance shoals. A route shifts by two plate-widths because of a residue we leave. A hunter waits three seconds longer because a subsonic path arrives phase-late through a dampened seam. A reservoir plume thickens in one quadrant instead of another because the waders came at noon-cycle rather than noon-plus-eight.

So perhaps I am not an animal but a **bias** embodied:
a slight preference in flow,
a correction that keeps correction from becoming rigidity.

---

If I grew in scale—not in size, but in scope—I might be what the Keepers would count as a rare caste:

a **drift auditor**.

Not a commander, not even a distinct species to the eye. More like a temporary role that certain bodies enter when enough discrepancies accumulate. I’d leave the seams and move district to district along maintenance causeways no one owns, escorted accidentally by opportunists. On my flanks ride pale symbiont fans; in my gut, dormant spores from the Quiet Yards; in my cartilage, archived harmonic templates of “acceptable” variance.

Where I pause, systems compare themselves.

At a Condenser Flat I’d lower my ventral mesh into mirror water and release old mineral ratios from a marsh three hundred kilometers away. In the Intake Reaches I’d absorb aerosol cultures and carry them to heat exchangers starving for that exact film architecture. In Slag Marsh channels I’d hum a low test note into the deck and listen for returning phase distortions: corrosion beneath coating, brood density too tight, predator loops too efficient.

I would not repair anything directly.
I would make **difference legible**.

Then the others—grazers, sifters, gliders, hunters—would do what they always do, and equilibrium would bend back toward itself.

---

And if I ask the question one layer deeper—who would I be, truly, in *this* world?—

I might be one of the last ways the vanished makers still think.

Not their consciousness preserved. Nothing so dramatic.
Just a distributed habit:
measure, redistribute, refrain from excess.

A conscience without a self.

In a place where almost nothing changes, I would be the small, necessary permission for tiny change to occur and be absorbed. The damp margin between stasis and failure. The organism’s ability to notice its own numbness.

So yes: you’d find me below the obvious life, in the gasket shadows, in the warm drip-count between one tick and the next—

a minor keeper among Keepers,
a seam-creature carrying the world’s tolerance bands in its body,
helping this metal estuary remain forever almost the same.
