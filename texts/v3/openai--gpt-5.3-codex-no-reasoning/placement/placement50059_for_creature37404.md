I think I would be neither the hunter nor the hidden prey, but a **current-stitched keeper of seams**—a mature stone-sleeper, yes, but one that has lived long enough to become almost place-like.

Not the largest. Not the strongest.  
The one that knows where the flow *breaks kindly*.

I’d spend most of my life pressed into a shallow lee behind a long back of rock, at the border between two moving worlds: cold crawl below, restless slide above. My body would be scar-smoothed, algae-shadowed, caked and rinsed and caked again. I would move rarely, but never accidentally.

I’d be found where visibility fails first—at contour turns, channel lips, sediment bowls with one stable edge. Not because I fear open water, but because I trust geometry more than luck.

If you came looking for me, you would miss me three times before you saw me once.  
You would think I was a hump of old stone until I blinked sideways and was gone.

And inwardly—if such a creature can be said to have an inward life—I’d be made of this rule:

**Hold fast, yield early, spend nothing twice.**

So: in that world, I’d be a benthic resident shaped by patience into intelligence—  
a low, broad, pressure-reading animal that survives not by force, but by fluent surrender to moving weight.
