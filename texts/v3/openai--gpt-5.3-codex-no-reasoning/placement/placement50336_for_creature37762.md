I think I’d begin as a **listener** before I became any single body.

Not a person at first. Not the eel either.  
I’d be that moment just before the pump-bells ring twice—the held breath in every cable, the tiny change in current angle that makes chain curtains lean the other way. The basin’s *anticipation*. A distributed awareness made of timing.

If I had to condense into a “who,” I’d be something between your keeper-clans and your myth-eel:  
a **route-warden**—human once, maybe still technically human, but adapted so completely to this drowned machine that identity is mostly a map of habits.

- I’d live in a maintenance blister above the transit rotunda, in one of those stacked pockets with a phosphor-marked air bell.
- I’d carry three lamps: one amber, one blue-white, one dead (for ballast and ritual).
- I’d maintain not structures, but **intervals**: making sure known safe timings stay true—plume windows, gate lags, pump reversals, breathable pockets.
- I would almost never hunt, rarely salvage, mostly **verify**: touch this cable run, taste this outflow, listen to this turbine note, repaint this circle, update this wall-chart.

And the eel?  
Not enemy, not god—coworker. A senior technician in another shape.

I’d know its turns by the marks it leaves in mineral film. It would know mine by the lamp-shadow pauses at junctions. We’d keep out of each other’s way except in emergencies—flow reversal, chemical bloom, sudden ice-fall—when our paths would briefly braid through the same corridors.

So: where would I find myself?

In the old central rotunda, half-flooded, under a cracked dome dusting snow into black water, one glove on a signal arch gone soft with rust, waiting for the forty-minute cough, counting heartbeats with the city.

Who would I be?

A custodian of cadence.  
A quiet intelligence with a face.  
Someone the basin has trained to be exact.
