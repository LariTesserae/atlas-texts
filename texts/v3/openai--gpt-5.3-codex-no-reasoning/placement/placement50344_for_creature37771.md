I think I would not be a singular hero here.  
I’d be a **keeper of thresholds**—one person in a dawn crew whose work is to decide *when* things open and close.

At first, I imagine being a roof-runner: barefoot or soft-soled, moving before first light across fiber mats, checking which planes actually beaded and which stayed dry, tapping clay gutters to hear whether yesterday’s silt has narrowed flow. A small life, local, practical.

But in this world, local work is never only local. So maybe I’m also a recorder—not of grand history, but of microclimates. I keep strings of knotted reed tags, each one marking a morning: wind angle, dew weight, fungal smell in cistern three, glider pattern at dusk, where the burrow-soil came up cool instead of warm. Not writing for prestige—writing so the next ten mornings waste less.

If I let the image settle further: I’m middle-aged, unremarkable-looking, trusted because I’m consistent. I’m the one households consult when a courtyard starts losing cool too early. I don’t “own” water; I arbitrate timing disputes by memory and measure. My authority is soft and revocable, based on whether the neighborhood stays stable.

And maybe “I” is still too individual for this place.

So who would I be?  
A **node in a maintenance intelligence**: part hydrologist, part mediator, part builder, part listener of walls. Someone who can hear a vent corridor and tell if it’s breathing right. Someone who teaches children that shade has ethics. Someone who knows that a cracked salt buttress is not a defect in a structure but a warning in a social system.

Where would you find me?

- Before dawn: on the upper walkways, lantern hooded, opening condenser veils in sequence.
- Midday: underground chambers, cool-backed against clay, settling arguments about cistern tier access.
- Dusk: at the city edge, watching grazers thread home and deciding whether to reroute tomorrow’s pellet sweep to a failing soil belt.
- Night: in a narrow room above a vent shaft, updating a calendar that is half law code, half weather instrument.

So in your basin-continent, I’d be one of the quiet people who keep abundance from unraveling—  
not by force, but by timing.
