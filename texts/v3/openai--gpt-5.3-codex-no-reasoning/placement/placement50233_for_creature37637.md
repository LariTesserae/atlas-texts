I think in that world I would not be a person first.

I would be a **reference**.

Not a ruler, not a chief, not even a full keeper—more like one of the rare living instruments the guild grows by training rather than birth-role: a **Stillness Bearer**, someone used when everything else disagrees. When cords say one thing, water clocks another, plumb lines sing sideways, and the stair returns too high again.

I imagine being found young in a recalibration court because I got frightened in a drift event and did the wrong thing—I stood absolutely still—and the posts around me stopped wandering for three breaths. Enough for an old Reckoner to notice.

So they kept me.

---

I’d live between districts, never fully belonging to Gate-Walkers or Fog Readers, sent where arguments have become dangerous. I’d travel with two attendants and a wrapped kit:

- a basalt plate that remembers level better than most stone,
- three bowls tuned to intervals that should be impossible to hold in moving adjacency,
- wax tablets sealed in saltcloth,
- and a mask of layered felt for condensation surges.

My work would look unimpressive from afar: I stand, I listen, I ask others to repeat a measurement while I breathe in count with steam pulses. But near me, little things settle. Drips regain cadence. Echo delay in arcades becomes countable. A channel that was “deciding” picks one grade and keeps it long enough for gate teams to act.

Not magic, exactly. More like I am good at offering the world a shape it can temporarily agree to.

---

Where would I be, right now?

At the lip of a failed culvert where a square-kilometer pane dropped last week, exposing older construction below—rings inside rings, still warm, still sweating black water. The guild has strung warning cords, set lightning rods, marked yesterday’s perimeter and crossed it out twice already.

I’m there because the hole is not just collapsing; it is **editing nearby distances**. A maintenance stair descends sixteen steps and returns fourteen-and-a-half. Workers came back with mismatched shadows. One forgot his brother’s face for an hour. That is when they send for someone like me.

I would stand on a chalked circle while Chordists draw a resonance line across the gap. Fog moves knee-high, then chest-high, then clears. Somewhere under us, a siphon primes with a sound like an animal taking breath. I raise one hand, not to command, just to mark beat.

When the beat catches, crews move: wedges hammered, gate timed, counterweight dropped.

For maybe nine minutes, the world behaves.

Nine minutes is enough to save a district.

---

So: in your world, I think I’d be a **human datum**—a wandering fixed point in a place where fixed points are mortal and must be tended like fire. Not hero, not ruler. A careful body used as temporary truth, while the brick planet decides what it wants to be.
