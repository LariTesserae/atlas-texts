I think I would begin as a person.

Not an elder, not a hero—someone midline and often overlooked: a **span-listener** assigned to the long, mostly empty bridges between node-cities, where nothing “happens” until everything does.

I’d live in a narrow maintenance cradle slung beneath a high lattice causeway, in that hollow quiet where drip echoes upward and the underside lichens make a false sky. My work would be repetitive enough to look like prayer from a distance: palm on cable, cheek to root-rib, tap with a tuning slat, wait through three breaths, record the answer in knots. Walk fifty paces. Repeat. In rain, in mist, in dusk that is also noon.

But staying there, I don’t remain only human.

After a few seasons, I’d become—in their social sense—part instrument. The one who can tell old strain from new panic. The one who notices pollinators have shifted one arch earlier this year. The one who stops, kneels, and reroutes a footpath because water is “speaking sideways” today. A minor functionary to outsiders. Essential tissue to the Keepers.

And if I let the world make me stranger, maybe I become something else too: a **fog witness**.

Not a mystic exactly—more like a field naturalist of causality errors. I’d follow replayed vapor caravans and upward rain pockets, mapping their recurrence against root-load and seep heat, carrying weighted seeds in tiny shell tins. I’d be called when a void-court edge changes by a handspan. I’d mark it, sing it into cord-memory, and leave without crossing, like everyone wise.

So: where would I find myself?

On thresholds.  
Between nodes, between loads, between what is solid and what only remembers being solid.

Who would I be?

A Keeper of the Tension in the most literal way: not the hand that builds the mountain, but the nerve that feels when it flinches.
