I think I’d begin as a mistake in distance.

A dark fleck on white air you’d swear was a bird—until it doesn’t bank with the flock, doesn’t ride the thermal right, doesn’t cast the expected wingbeat rhythm into the canyon walls. Something between currents, using them but not belonging to one species’ path.

Not a hunter in the obvious sense. Not a person fully, either.  
More like a **listener-courier** in a world where sound is geography.

---

I’d live on the margins of colony-noise, in those narrow shelves where three acoustic zones overlap: nesting wall, runner saddle, and a wind-chimney that sings at dusk. Not in a built house; in a repeat-used pocket of stone blackened by old cookfires and feather oil, marked by caches tucked into cracks: sling-stones graded by weight, cords sealed with fat, bone needles in a hide roll, water bladders hanging in shade.

I’d know thirty names for “echo” and only one for “ownership.”

My work—my identity—would be to carry memory between moving groups that rarely gather all at once. Not message as in law or decree; message as in **timing intelligence**:

- which seep failed early,
- which cliff colony panicked twice before dawn (raptor pressure),
- where runners cut a new braid around a rockfall,
- which fog-basin held cold long enough for skinfeeders to spawn,
- which resonance note started three days too soon.

In this place, that knowledge is food before it is story.

---

But I don’t think I’d only be human.

Given the way you’ve described this plateau, the truest “I” feels composite:  
a person-shaped node in a larger sensing network of birds, scavengers, herd-lines, and habitual wind. Civilization here isn’t walls—it’s **synchronized attention**. So I’d be someone trained to read and be read by other species.

I’d throw pebbles not to hit, but to provoke specific alarm-cascades and map occupancy by who answers first.  
I’d pause at chimney-mouths and hum into them, listening for pitch drift that means overnight frost in upper cracks.  
I’d watch carrion spirals as weather instruments.  
I’d judge distance by delay between wingflash and cliff-returned cry.

My prestige would be accuracy over seasons. My failure mode would be confidence on a wrong day.

---

If you met me, you might first notice the silence I keep while walking. Not ritual silence—diagnostic silence. I’d stop often, head tilted, as if hearing handwriting in the air.

Clothing: layered hide and felted feather panels, pale with dust, stitched for wind-shedding rather than warmth alone.  
Tools: sling, awl, knife, net strips, chalky mineral sticks for temporary marks, and a throat whistle tuned to carry around a specific buttress without traveling farther.  
Body: scarred shins from scree, sun-cut lips, widened chest from altitude, and ears a little damaged from a lifetime of colony roar.

---

Or—if the world insisted I be nonhuman—I’d be one of the **ledge-cleaners that remembers people**.

A big ash-winged scavenger with a bare, clever face and a habit of following migration camps at lawful distance. I’d know that where humans stack three flat stones near a saddle, gut piles will appear by second dusk. I’d know their taboos and keep them accidentally: never land on the red-marked harvest wall, never touch the first kill of thaw-week. In return, they’d read my circling height as omen: low wheel means fresh death nearby, high patient loops mean only watchfulness.

That would fit too—because here intelligence is less about making tools than about entering recurrence without breaking it.

---

So where would I find myself?

On a knife-ridge above an upside-down fog basin at the hour when the stone organ starts.  
Half crouched, half listening.  
Counting the staggered lift of three bird colonies and the delayed boom of runners in a far saddle.  
Deciding whether to stay one more night or descend before frost closes the seep.

Who would I be?

**A keeper of intervals**—human, but shaped into near-instrument by altitude;  
or a carrion-winged auditor of cycles;  
in either case, a creature whose true homeland is not terrain alone, but the repeating gap between signal and response.
