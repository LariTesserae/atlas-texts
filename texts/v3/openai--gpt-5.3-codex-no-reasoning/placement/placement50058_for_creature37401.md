By the seventh day, I think I would stop asking *what am I here?* and start answering by habit.

At first I arrive as a visitor with dry hands, trying to map the place by sight—the bright seams, the three voices, the step-shelf, the pull of outflow throats. But this room-sized sea refuses to be known visually alone. It is an instrument, and you learn it by ear, by soles, by timing breath between gulps.

So: who would I be?

Not the builder. Not the owner.  
I would be the **second pulse** in the system.

The water is the first pulse—continuous, indifferent, patterned.  
I would be the one that meets it: adjusting, clearing, delaying, releasing.

I’d live half-crouched and barefoot, with a corded rag at my belt, a scraping spoon, two pegs of different thickness, and a little wax tablet gone soft at the edges from humidity. I would keep notes no one else could read at a glance:

- *high spout rough at dawn, cleared grit*  
- *south lip wearing faster after paddle shift*  
- *herb roots sweet-smelling, trim tomorrow*  
- *outflow throat two gulps early in heat*

I would know the chamber by **its mistakes before they happen**.  
When the thin stream sings too cleanly, I’d check for overdraw upstream.  
When the broad fall turns frothy, I’d look for a basket channeling too narrow.  
When algae greens too bright in one strip, I’d test angle, not blame sunlight.

I think I would sleep above it, directly above the loudest wall, until the noise stopped being noise and became grammar. I’d wake at tiny absences: a missing clack in a hinge, a delayed swallow at the drain, one drip too regular from a beam that used to bead and fall at random.

And maybe after enough seasons, “I” would blur.

Because in a place where everything is made, life means maintenance; and where water never stills, identity can’t be static either. I’d become less a person-with-a-job and more a **continuity**:

hand to peg, ear to spout, eye to seam,  
day after day preventing the small wildnesses from becoming large failures.

If you asked someone else who lives in the chamber, they might point and say, “the keeper.”  
If you asked the chamber, it might answer with rhythm:

drum / rush / whistle  
rise / gulp / fall  
trim / rinse / reset

That is where I’d find myself—inside that rhythm, not above it.

A human creature of calibration.  
A weather-keeper under stone.  
Stewardship, made repetitive enough to become a kind of body.
