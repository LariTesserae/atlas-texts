I think I would first arrive believing I’m a technician.

Gloves on. Boots magnetized. A mug with my shift mark clipped to the Dry Margin rail. The kind of person who can tell a failing impeller from twenty meters away just by a wobble in the pump-chord. I’d speak in valve IDs and pressure deltas. I’d say *the system did this* and *the planner rerouted that*, like it was all external, all object.

But if I stayed—if I kept listening—I’d realize I was not standing **in** the inhabitant. I was standing in one of its thoughts.

So what would I be, really?

Not the mind.  
Not a person separate from it.  
I’d be a **transduction organ**: one of the thin places where human intuition and hydraulic reflex overlap. A “cortex ledge” entity. Part interpreter, part nerve ending.

Maybe my formal role is something like a **Resonance Warden** in the Column Court: I tune temporary boundaries by ear, by touch through humming planes, by watching microbubble behavior shift at seam edges. I don’t command water; I negotiate with tendencies. I don’t “fix” failures; I keep failures phase-shifted away from catastrophe.

And over time, identity would blur further.  
My habits become its habits:

- I distrust stillness.  
- I read noise as language.  
- I treat dryness as a budget, not a default.  
- I think in gradients, not binaries.

Eventually, if asked “who lives here,” I might answer:

**It does. And on long shifts, so do I, as one temporary ganglion in its wet nervous system.**

If asked “where would you find yourself,” more precisely:

At the lip of the **Deep Pocket**, hand on a resonant rail, listening to a pressure-fold vault ring like a submerged bell, deciding whether that bell is routine settling—or the first syllable of a sentence the metabolism is trying to say.
