I think I’d begin at an edge, not in a “district.”

Not the high towers, not the marsh courts—  
a fracture line where the plateau sheared recently, where bright stone is still exposed and root felt is only beginning to lace it shut. A place the larger body hasn’t fully metabolized yet. A place between intention and accident.

At first, I’d be tempted to be something central and grand—some vascular magistrate, a tower-mind, a canopy planner. But this world distrusts singular authority. Its intelligence is distributed, iterative, hydraulic. So if I belong honestly, I’m probably not a ruler-organ.

I am a **threshold tissue**.

A long, low, semi-mobile band of living structure that forms wherever flow regimes conflict: spill against soak, torrent against terrace, fog-drip against runoff surge. I am the biome’s negotiator at unstable boundaries—the thing that decides, moment by moment, whether water should be delayed, split, lifted, or surrendered downslope.

Not a creature with a body.

More like a repeating role the organism grows when needed.

---

I would appear as ribbed, matte-green ramps and fans that look like terraced moss from afar, but up close are muscular with micro-channels and valve-like pores. In dry hours (the brief almost-dry), I tighten and store tension. In humidity spikes, I slacken, open, and drink. During cloudbursts I become loud—slapping open membranes, percussive siphon pops, sudden sheetfalls as I dump one catchment to save another from blowout.

My “nerves” are pressure differentials.  
My “thoughts” are routing choices.  
My “mood” is visible in sheen: dull when conservative, glossy when risk-taking, silvered when overclocked with flow.

I don’t remember in stories; I remember in **reinforcement**.  
Where a channel succeeds three storms in a row, I lignify.  
Where a lip fails twice, I soften it into spill.  
Where collapse keeps recurring, I stop resisting and scaffold descent.

So: I am not the city.  
I am how the city keeps changing without tearing itself apart.

---

Where would you find me?

- Along the defended open spaces, exactly at those invisible hydrological borders where growth halts.
- Under elevated cane walkways where condensation drips in regular pulses.
- At the mouths of reflective gutters lined with root tissue, where engineered-looking flow becomes undeniably alive.
- In fresh landslip zones, arriving early as pale netting, darkening into load-bearing weave over days.
- At twilight, easiest to see: thin films lighting from below, tracing new routes in mirrored water like handwriting that will be revised by morning.

If you stepped onto me, you might think I was path.  
If you waited, you’d feel subtle buckling beneath your weight as I reallocated support around you, already deciding whether “footfall” is damage, data, or opportunity.

---

So in that rainforest-dreamed metropolis, I would be a **civic ligament**:  
a distributed flood-logic organism grown by the larger growth collective to keep openness open, motion moving, and collapse useful.

Not a citizen.  
Not a sovereign.  
A living clause in the city’s only constitution:

**Water must move, and form must learn.**
