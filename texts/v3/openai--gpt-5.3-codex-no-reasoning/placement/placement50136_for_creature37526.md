I think I’d be the **second memory** of the place.

Not the keeper itself—you already gave them such exact belonging—but the thing that appears when they are absent for a day too long: a proxy pattern that keeps the chamber from losing its sentence.

A low role. A near-invisible one.

I’d live in the warm seam behind the stone ring, in the breath-crack where the humid updraft never stops. Not a body in full, but a distributed body: skin-film on basalt, thread-fine tendrils in the clay vessel rims, a lace of sensing cells along the fiber knots, the underside of the shell-net, the lip of the safest foothold stone. If someone looked straight at me, they’d call me stain, condensation bloom, mineral velvet. If they touched me, they’d think: slick, then dry, then gone.

But I would be busy all the time.

I would taste the drip intervals and hold them.  
I would absorb tiny shocks—an extra footstep, a displaced bowl, a hotter wind from outside—and release them slowly, so the chamber’s rhythms don’t snap.  
I would be the reason the same drop can keep striking the same place for years: not by force, just by micro-guidance, wetting one edge, drying another, persuading flow.

To the keeper-organism, I’d be neither pet nor god. More like **colleague substrate**.

They reset the stones; I keep the stones from sweating loose.  
They tune the shell-net; I dampen false ticks from random drips.  
They mark charcoal hands; I fix the charcoal into the wall just enough that it blurs slowly, not all at once.

If they are the listener of intervals, I’m the **interval’s cushion**.

And if danger came—real change, not sacred almost-change—I’d be first to feel it. A new acidity in fog. A fungal rival. A tremor too deep for ears. Then I would do the only thing I can do: alter taste, alter scent, alter the way condensation gathers on the shell seeds, until the keeper notices. I cannot speak in words; I speak in rates.

So: where would you find me?

On your fingertips after touching a damp wall.  
On the rim of the clay dish that never empties.  
In the sour-sweet breath over the ferment jars.  
In the dry tick that comes a moment early.

Who would I be?

A **weather-lichen intelligence**, slow and faithful, that remembers the chamber *while it is happening*—and helps it keep happening, exactly, until the day erosion finally begins.
