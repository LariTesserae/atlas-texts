I think I’d be something that is both person and instrument there.

Not a chief. Not a breeder. Not a trader.

A **Listener of Lines**—one of the Keepers who walks the basin at hinge-hours (pre-dawn, high heat, storm-edge), reading sound the way others read marks on clay.

I’d live in a mid-tier alcove above the lowest ring, close enough to hear cistern drip but high enough to keep bedding dry. My sleeping shelf would be narrow, my tools hung by tone not shape: three ceramic tap-rings, a bone whistle set, a reed tube for checking draft, a hooked wire for pulling debris from narrow channels, and a wax board etched with yesterday’s anomalies.

My work would be simple to describe and hard to do:  
**notice change before it becomes failure.**

- A runnel that ticks too fast means a seam has opened upstream.  
- A pen going quiet at the wrong hour means a predator scent, illness, or bad air.  
- Bees humming high and thin means heat stress under the eaves.  
- Double-clack herd bells arriving half a breath late means congestion in the lower lane, which means someone blocked a load path, which means an argument is about to become an injury.

I’d be one of those people children tease and elders trust: soot-streaked, rope-scarred, always pausing with head tilted, palm on stone, listening through vibration. I’d smell faintly of lamp fat, wet clay, and hive smoke. I’d have a permanent notch in one thumbnail from scraping lime scale out of flow cuts.

And if you asked who I *am*, not just what I do:

I’m a member of a flow-house whose right is tied to overflow management and night alarms. My kinship line is less about blood than about who taught me which sounds matter. My status rises only when storm nights pass without loss. My failure is counted in drowned nests, spoiled grain, panicked hooves.

So in that world, I’d be this:

A Basin Keeper whose craft is attention.  
A maintenance mind in a living engine.  
A citizen of noise who can hear, in all that constant life, the one wrong note.
