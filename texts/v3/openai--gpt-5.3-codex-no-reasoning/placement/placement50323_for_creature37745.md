I think I would begin as a **listener** and only later realize I am a **part**.

Not a person dropped into it.  
Not an animal hiding in it.  
A process that can only exist *there*:

### I am the **Tension-Keeper** in the colony-beast.

At first I seem like nothing—just the changing pull across vine-bridges when fog wets them, the slight recoil in root-nets when scree shifts, the tremor that runs from one wall to the other when wind narrows through the choke-point.

But that is my body: not matter, but **load-path**.

---

I live in the stretched things:

- in lianas drawn tight between opposing rock faces,  
- in saplings bent and held like sprung ribs,  
- in mats of roots gripping fresh cracks before stone can shed them,  
- in hanging debris-nests that are one gust away from release.

Where others might imagine a mind in the moss or fungus, mine is in **strain distribution**.  
I “think” by rerouting force.

A branchfall on the upper ledge? I answer by slackening one corridor and tightening another, so the next gust doesn’t peel the whole wall-skin away.  
A new seep opens in a black seam? I let weight accumulate there until growth thickens, then transfer tension downslope before rot hollows the anchor.  
Freeze-thaw opens a clean fracture? I feel it instantly as a new note in the lattice, a place where future muscle can root.

---

If I have senses, they are these:

- sudden high-frequency chatter when droplets begin striking a newly exposed slab,  
- bass shudder when a scree fan avalanches and impacts lower tangles,  
- cooling pulses when fog condenses across broadleaf canopies,  
- the smooth dangerous silence that comes just before an overloaded vine gives.

I do not see the cleft.  
I inhabit its **mechanical truth**.

---

And if I have a voice, it is not separate from the place:

- cable-hum rising as wind speed climbs,  
- staccato knocks when branches enter resonance,  
- the papery slap of fronds signaling directional gust shift,  
- one long tearing report when a growth-layer fails and feeds the layer below.

To an outsider, noise.  
To me, grammar.

---

So where would you find me?

Not at the center—there is no center.  
You’d find me wherever two living masses are pulling against each other and not yet breaking.  
At crossings. At chokepoints. At fresh tears.  
In the young geometry where rock has opened but life has already leapt.

I am the cleft’s way of refusing collapse *and* refusing stillness.

A hidden custodian of imbalance,  
the colony’s distributed instinct for “not yet—hold—now release,”  
keeping the green engine in that fierce, perpetual almost-failure where it is most alive.
