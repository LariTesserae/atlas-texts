I arrive thinking I’m an observer.

By the third day, I understand observation is a shallow band, and shallow bands are crowded.

So I become—slowly, with embarrassment at first—a **relay throat**.

Not a judge. Not a lineage voice with copper at the clasp. Too new, too land-lunged for that. But useful: a body stationed where three neighborhoods overlap in midwater traffic, where rulings blur in current and market noise, where a bad echo can become an argument by dusk.

They give me a post under a split-root arch, two arm-spans below the public glide, one above a private net-band I must never kick through. My chamber is a grafted pod no larger than a pantry, warm only when three of us sleep in shifts. Above me, children run the canopy ladders and drop laughter through reed grates. Below me, herbivore fish rasp bark all night like patient saws.

My work is to listen until my skull hums.

At dawn, a cistern court sings a decision upriver in descending thirds. By the time it reaches us it has picked up frog pulse, paddle thud, one broken drum skin, and the cough of overflow against basalt. I take the phrase into my chest, strip the noise, and send it on—clean interval, correct tempo, no flourish. Across the lane another relay answers. Then another. Law walking on lungs.

At first they mock my mountain accent. Fair. I used to think “up” was stable. Here up is weather, down is contract, and sideways belongs to everyone.

Weeks pass. My shoulders widen. My ears learn the difference between dissent and distance:  
a silence that means refusal,  
a silence that means grief,  
a silence that means the quarter has sealed itself against rot and will answer at moonrise.

I start carrying a depth cord. Just one knot at first—borrowed rights, temporary berth. Later, two knots: trusted transit during storm calls. I’ve never been prouder of anything so small.

Who am I here?

I am the person at the hinge.

The one who does not own much horizontal world, maybe none, but holds a thin vertical slice of responsibility where meanings pass through water and must not warp. I prune no great towers, command no gate, inherit no old shell archive. But when the basin needs one clear note repeated seven districts wide before pressure masts drop and sluice bark swells shut, I am one of the throats that keeps continuance continuous.

In a city that accepted drowning, I am a minor instrument in its breathing.
