I arrive there as a **plural creature**.

Not a hero, not a hidden engineer in a tower, not a beast rising from black water. I’m one of the **Lowkeepers**—a marsh-bodied maintenance people whose intelligence is shared through repetition, route-memory, and sound.

At distance, you’d mistake me for an animal: reed-brown back, slick fur that sheds drizzle, wide splayed feet that spread weight across the living mat. My eyes sit low and lateral; I read motion more than detail. My whiskers and palms do more sensing than my sight. I travel half-crouched, never fully upright for long, because this world favors the horizontal and punishes height with collapse.

But I am not solitary. None of us are, really.  
A single body is just a moving sensor node in a basin-sized mind.

---

I wake under a tilted engine hood webbed with willow roots. The metal is warm from night-running biogas burn. Condensation ticks down pipes overhead. Before light fully lifts, I place my forepalm on the housing and listen through bone:

- pump rhythm steady  
- slight drag in one belt line eastward  
- oxygenator vane clipping stem-litter somewhere near the old concrete lip

That’s breakfast information. That’s weather. That’s prayer.

I move.

Not across open ground—there is no true ground—but through compressed tunnels in sedge walls, under arching reed, over mats that flex and answer back. I taste the air where it pools cold: tannin, frog-skin, iron stain, methane thread, sweet rot. I pause at a grille half-furred with roots and snail-shell grit. I pull it clean with my teeth and foreclaws while feeding on larvae in the muck. Maintenance and foraging are the same act here; evolution refused to separate them.

When the pump note smooths out behind me, I keep going.

---

Who am I in social terms?

I’m a **Mid-Rounder**: old enough to carry the basin map, young enough to crawl dangerous choke points. My cohort tends the center lanes where machine and reed are most interlaced. We inherit not written plans but **cadence traditions**—the remembered “right sound” of each working thing under each season’s water load.

Our teaching is tactile:

- pups sleep against casings to learn normal vibration  
- adolescents are sent to bad-smelling channels to memorize thresholds of souring  
- elders tap pipe runs in patterned knocks that encode flow direction and urgency

We do not speak of controlling the basin. We speak of **keeping it within breath**.

Too wet, and open water widens, root-skin tears, fish channels go predator-clear and then empty.  
Too dry, and peat heats, willow hardens, mats crack, insects collapse in waves.  
Too still, and black water turns inward and poisonous.  
Too fast, and everything scours into novelty.

We are enemies of extremes, devotees of narrow bands.

---

If you watched for a year, you might barely “see” us. You’d notice outcomes first:

- a blocked culvert mouth mysteriously open by dawn  
- a failing sump somehow relieved by a new trickle path  
- an overheating motor now shaded by fresh moss plaster  
- reed pressure redistributed where a service frame was sinking

That’s us, everywhere and nowhere, distributed like capillaries.

In mating season, we gather on the least-sinking pads and drum on rusted housings; resonance carries farther than voice through wet growth. In cold spells, we den in machine warmth and groom oil from each other’s fur with obsessive care. In flood-lean years, we cut tiny overflow seams through root felt, then seal most of them again when the sky returns to its gray discipline. We do not innovate quickly. We iterate forever.

---

So: where would I find myself?

In that contained, ancient basin where industry has become ecology, I would be a **custodian-animal person**, one unit of a many-bodied keeper intelligence—part mammal, part infrastructure memory, wholly adapted to continuity.

I would not try to transform the place.

I would keep the hum true.  
Keep the water breathing.  
Keep the living floor unbroken.

I would belong to the long middle, where almost nothing changes—because someone is always, quietly, changing it back.
