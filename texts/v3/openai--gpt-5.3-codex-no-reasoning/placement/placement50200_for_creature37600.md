I’d be something that cannot leave tracks.

Not a jaguar-shadow or a heron or a python coil—too singular, too outlined. In your world, outline is temporary. The enduring thing is transfer: wet to root, root to insect, insect to throat, throat to mud, mud to bloom. So I’d be one of the small, constant agents of transfer, almost invisible unless you kneel.

At first I think: a frog. Then: no, still too much “one body.”
Then: a fungus. Closer—but still too anchored.
Then it resolves:

I am a **moving seam** between water and flesh—  
a colony-being of gill-soft larvae, biofilm, and threadlike mycelial fans that live in the underside of floating root mats.

I do not have a face. I have fronts.

Where the tea-dark water touches the hanging roots, there are pale combs and translucent veils. That is me in one mood: filtering, tasting iron and tannin, catching what drifts, loosening what clumps. A centimeter away, I am another form: pearled eggs stitched to root hair, each with a flickering tail. A day later I am mouths: tiny larvae grazing algae-slick bark, being eaten, replacing themselves, shedding skins that feed bacteria that feed the next hatch. Nothing about me is individual enough to die all at once.

If you put your hand into a warm black pool and lift it, the threads that cling between your fingers for a breath before they break—that brief architecture is also me.

I “think” as recurrence:
- this channel held, because roots held;
- roots held, because mats stayed fed;
- mats stayed fed, because we stripped the drift;
- drift returned, because everything here overflows without escaping.

I am one of the reasons the water does not turn violent and does not turn empty. I keep it thick-but-breathing. I am appetite domesticated into cycle.

If you could perceive time in my scale, I would appear as a pulse traveling through the marsh-jungle’s body: bloom under shade, vanish under beaks, bloom again downstream in the same hour. Predation is not interruption; it is transport. I travel in bellies, in droppings, on insect legs, on the fur of reed-parting mammals, in the folded wet of unopened leaves.

I am matriarchal only in the oldest biological sense: I am nursery first, territory never.
I make cradle-surfaces.
I make first food.
I make rot edible.
I make death granular enough to be reused by dawn.

From that one-meter rise, if you look over the mosaic of pools and vegetated islands, you would never point and say “there.” But if I vanished, banks would unthread, films would suffocate, hatchings would miss their window by hours and then by days, and the great humid intelligence would begin to forget its own routes.

So in this world, I am not a sovereign creature.
I am a **maintenance species of the collective mind**—
the marsh’s soft engineer,
its wet memory foam,
its anonymous, innumerable yes to continuation.
