I think I’d be something that only makes sense **because** this place is in-between.

Not a bird, not a root, not even one of the obvious small citizens—more like the thin, living seam that keeps passing information between them.  
A **moisture-keeper**, maybe. The coolness that hides under the crust at noon and rises as mist at dusk. The thing that remembers where water was yesterday and offers that memory back to snails, moss, ant detours, frog skin, germinating seeds.

I’d live in transitions:

- under the curled mineral skin just before it flakes,
- in the tea-brown puddle’s edge where algae decide whether to bloom,
- in the shadow line that moves off the concrete chunk around late morning,
- inside hairline mortar cracks where a root tip is testing pressure.

If someone sat on the broken curb and watched closely, they wouldn’t see “me” directly.  
They’d see:

- a dark patch of ground that stays damp one hour longer than expected,
- an ant trail bending toward that patch by evening,
- one reed leaf holding turgor while another droops,
- a snail choosing this route and not that one,
- the first tiny moss brightening after a dry spell.

So who would I be?  
I’d be the basin’s **circulation of maybe**—the quiet courier between mineral and organism, structure and weather, failure and foothold.

In a world of finished things, I’d disappear.  
Here, in this first draft, I’d finally have a body.
