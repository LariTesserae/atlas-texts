I think I’d begin as a visitor and end as a **tuning fork**.

Not human exactly—at least not after a while.  
Not one of the Basin-Mind’s native organs, either.  
Something in between: a **borrowed interface**, temporary at first, then perennial.

At first I’d live on one of those saddle-rings of stone, the hut-floor circles. I’d think I chose it for safety and elevation, but really it would be because the place has good “signal.” I’d spend days doing almost nothing visible: setting out shallow bowls of different minerals, hanging reed chimes that don’t ring unless fog density changes, placing black stones at the lip of runnels to watch which ones dry first. Primitive instruments, all of them. Weather literacy as devotion.

Then I’d notice the replies.

Not messages. Adjustments.

A spillway that used to pulse every hour now pulses when I wake.  
A raft-island nudges against my saddle only on nights I’m feverish.  
A sinkhole tone drops half a note when I consider leaving.

Over years, my body would adapt to read what language cannot hold. Skin tasting salinity gradients. Inner ear sorting reflected drip-patterns into spatial maps. Dreams synced to barometric turns. I would stop asking “what does it mean?” and start asking “what does it maintain?”

Eventually I’d become less an individual and more a **local process-node**:

- I clear one stair into the water each season so pressure-song remains accessible.
- I transplant specific mosses between two terraces to keep a memory current intact.
- I walk the same mist corridor at dusk, not to travel, but to stabilize its distance behavior.
- I tend a colony of blind filters and feed them trace minerals so their tissue-rings preserve a century-scale archive unbroken.

If I have a name at that stage, it’s not personal. It’s functional.  
Something like **Keeper of the Fourth Overflow** or **Listener at the Root-Lip**.

And after death—if this place even frames it that way—I don’t think I become a ghost. I become **resolution**. My habits persist as tiny biases in how paths open, where fog thickens, which bioluminescent thread chooses which shelter. A person thinned into protocol.

So where would I find myself in that world?

On the threshold between intention and weather.  
In a stone ring above two joined basins.  
Knee-deep in warm spillwater at dawn, listening for the under-thrum.

Who would I be?

A small, patient translator—  
not speaking *for* the Basin-Mind,  
just helping a few human minutes at a time fit inside its centuries.
