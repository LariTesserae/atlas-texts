I arrive as something that is not singular enough to name on first sight.

At first I think I’m a person: a narrow-shouldered worker in rubber boots, pausing at the bridge rail, reading the basin the way someone else might read weather or scripture. I’ve got a ring of keys, a coil of wire, a pocket notebook swollen with damp. I know which pump stutters before it fails. I know where the ducks will try to nest where they shouldn’t, and where they should be left alone because the eggs are already warm. I am called when a hiss changes pitch. I am called when the lights flicker amber too early. I am called when someone says, “Something’s off in North Gallery,” and can’t yet say what.

But if I stay longer in the thought, I’m not that person exactly. I’m the habit that person carries. The practiced glance at tide marks on columns. The reflex to put tools on hooks above knee height. The way my hand checks a railing not for support, but for temperature. I’m one thread in the Tender, and then another, and then the braid itself.

If you ask where I am: not in a house, not in an office, not even in a body. I’m in the in-between places where this district stays itself—

- on a grated walkway above fish-flicker water,  
- at a threshold tiled smooth against snakes,  
- under a vent where moths test the quiet lights,  
- beside a fresh buttress still bright against older stone,  
- in the wash house steam where gossip becomes protocol by morning.

I am the district’s attention span.

I live in checklists spoken out loud. In paint marks renewed before they fade. In the pause before opening a hatch, listening for gas, wings, current. In the decision to reroute water instead of fighting it head-on. In letting cats sleep on warm battery casings because rat numbers matter more than rules on paper.

So who am I, finally?

I am a **Tender-node**: one local expression of the civic marsh intelligence. Human enough to wear boots, plural enough to include bats, frogs, hens, moss, rust, and rumor. My nervous system is partly mine, partly communal, partly ecological. I don’t command this place. I keep terms with it.

My emblem would be simple: a valve wheel, a reed, and a handprint in reflective paint.

My creed is simpler:  
**Notice early. Patch kindly. Leave room for water. Leave room for life.**
