I arrive as something that can **be mistaken for debris**.

Not camouflage, exactly—more like an organism shaped by a place where being *noticed too clearly* triggers the wrong physics.  
A low, many-jointed grazer-scavenger, knee-high to a human, plated in matte mineral like wet basalt. I move in bursts and long pauses, because here rhythm is identity. Too steady and the pylons answer. Too erratic and predators test you.

At first I think I am only myself: one body, one hunger, one route between warm stones.

Then the basin starts finishing my sentences before I have them.

---

I feed on three things:

1. **Lichen bladders** at cliff base—nipped, not stripped.
2. **Tower-shed scales** when they slough microscopic edges.
3. **Signal-rich water**, skimmed from those still concentric insets in the dome whenever thunder primes them.

The third is not nutrition in a normal sense. It is calibration.  
After drinking, I can feel invisible planes the way a fish feels current.

I have two forward eyes and a lateral band of pressure pits along my flanks. The eyes are for edges. The pits are for intentions: wing-swarms turning, hums rising through rock, a boom about to happen half a heartbeat from now.

---

I am not a conductor node in the grand sense—not tower-core, not marsh membrane authority.  
I am a **roving checksum**.

I cross boundaries repeatedly: marsh to shelf, shelf to dome, dome to pylon arc. My body logs where rules drift. Where water climbed yesterday and falls today. Where a safe corridor now bites. I leave thin conductive smears from glands in my feet; biofilm reads them; the Choir adjusts.

I am how the place asks itself: *still coherent?*

When everything freezes for three heartbeats, I freeze too—except for an internal tremor in a pearly organ tucked behind my jaw hinge. During the freeze, it listens downward and outward and submits my little packet: traction, temperature, resonance error, fear density. Then release. Then life resumes.

---

Where would you find me?

- Under the leaning tower at pulse-bright moments, collecting scale-rain.
- On the narrow warm shelf at cloud-sweat peak, licking mineral condensate.
- Standing absurdly still beside black reeds, so still that flyers land on me and use my back as mirror angle.
- In the dome threshold during thunder, facing inward like a pilgrim at a machine-shrine.

If you approached gently, I would sidestep, not flee.  
If you hummed a repeating note, I would leave immediately—because repeating notes are commands here, and I am not built to be mistaken for obedient architecture.

---

Who am I in this world?

A small, unspectacular citizen of a place-sized mind.  
A four-kilogram boundary-walker whose survival behavior doubles as systems maintenance.  
An animal that became, without consent or ceremony, part sensory organ of the basin.

Not the Choir Under Stone itself.  
But one moving syllable in its weather-long sentence.
