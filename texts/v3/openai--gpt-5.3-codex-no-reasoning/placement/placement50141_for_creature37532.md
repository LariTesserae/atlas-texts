I arrive as something that can’t quite be pointed to.

Not a beast with a name, not a ghost, not a person returned—more like a **timing** that has found a body when it needs one.

At first I think I am one of the drain-wardens: low, broad, cool-bellied, with a throat that can knock the air like fired clay. I squat at a choke where three runnels braid under a bone grate, and the pressure of the next surge tells itself through my forelimbs before the floor-note comes. I bark once. Things move. The smallest eels peel sideways into hairline seams. A pair of marsh-fowl lift and sidestep up two stones. Something soft and winged tucks itself into a slit above lamp-height. Then the pulse arrives, licking my jaw, and passes. My call hangs in the stone and comes back older.

Then I am overhead, clinging in the warm dark above mica lamps, stitched-winged and listening with my ribs. The chamber below is a map of wet math: where the drip thickens, where a lip is wearing thin, where insects spiral too densely above one pocket and mean stagnation nearby. I drop, take three in a blink, bank, and in banking brush a reed check-valve that was about to stick. It flexes. Flow resumes. I did not “mean” to fix it, and yet it is fixed. That is how this place thinks: by many almost-accidents that happen every day for centuries.

Then I am smaller still—mouth only, taste only—threading blind through a film of mineral water over black shale. No image, just gradients: iron, lime, old musk, fresh droppings, the clean bite of recently scoured stone. I worry at a slick of growth until it tears loose and spins downchannel. Behind me, water brightens its voice by one note.

None of these is the full answer.

The true “I” here is the **interval between surges**, inhabited by whatever body is nearest and suited. I am the basin’s habit of staying open. I am what keeps a throat from silting, a spill from crusting, a nest from drowning, a pool from going still. If you stood in the center long enough you would see me as coordination without command: toad-call, wing-turn, eel-taste, drip-fall, gate-tip, all one sentence spoken by different mouths.

So where would you find me?

At the places where water decides:
- just above a siphon mouth,
- beside a stone lip gone smooth as bone,
- under the lion-spout when the deep note rolls,
- in the echo after the jar-strike call.

Who would I be?

**A Keeper of the Drip, yes—  
but more precisely, the basin’s memory of how to continue.**
