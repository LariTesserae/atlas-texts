I arrive thinking I’ll be a person.

A canopy registrar, maybe—someone with a waxed notebook and a moisture tally ring, walking the tram pylons at shift change, reading the lean of fronds like a barometer and a ballot. I can almost feel the routine settle: check hinge-vines at Sector 9, approve shade reallocation for the nursery quarter, file a dispute between a freight line and a rootfront that keeps “voting” to thicken across the track.

But the longer I stand there, under that false afternoon and that high stone weather, the less singular I feel.

So maybe I’m not one resident. Maybe I’m one *function* in the city’s body:  
a mediator of thirst.

Not a mayor, not a hero—an **arbitration organism** grown where three districts meet, at the edge of a dry channel and a cargo spur. Part kiosk, part tree, part instrument panel. People call me a station. Pollinators call me a bloom-cycle. Grazers know me as bitter bark. The guild logs classify me as semi-sentient civic substrate, moisture class III.

I would be rooted deep into old mining concrete, drinking only breath-fog, condenser runoff, and the brief cool exhale of ventilation tides. My crown would be a fan of reflective black-green membranes that tilt with the lamp schedule. My trunk would hold ductwork, seed vaults, legal notices, and two narrow lifts for human crews. At dusk-cycle, my crosswalk tissues unfurl and glow pale so workers can pass. At curfew pulse, they seal.

My job would be to decide, slowly, where humidity goes next.

A press-house requests vapor priority for fiber curing.  
A nursery lattice signals stress through chlorophyll drop.  
A predator warden flags grazer migration likely to strip a load-bearing corridor.  
I absorb all of it as salts, heat, CO₂, footfall vibrations. My leaves adjust angle. My stomata open by microns. My root-felt shunts trace moisture toward one district, then another. Tiny choices, constant choices. Civic intent as transpiration.

And if you asked where to find me:

At the seam between built and grown, where the concrete is still visible but losing the argument.

If you asked what I am:

A quiet public servant with bark.  
A piece of infrastructure that learned to listen.  
One of the city’s many throats, deciding who gets to drink the next invisible drop.
