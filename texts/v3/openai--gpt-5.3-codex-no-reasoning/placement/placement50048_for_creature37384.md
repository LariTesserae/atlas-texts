I find myself there as something that is not an individual in the way a mammal is an individual.

At first I try to be a walker—one of the glass-jointed, precise-footed surveyors—but that’s too clean, too bounded. This place erodes boundaries. Nothing here is just one role for long. So I become a **route** that learned to move.

Not a creature with a single body: a chain-being, discontinuous and cooperative, made of many modules that separate and rejoin through the day-dim. Each module is palm-sized and low, all cartilage ring and vibrating cilia, with a crescent of mineral teeth. Alone, one is barely anything—an aimless puck shivering on wet stone. In chorus, we are directional. We braid into temporary ribbons across seep and slush, then unbraid at pools, then knot again on the far side. From a distance we look like black stitching being pulled through the plain.

Our name (if translated into something pronounceable) would mean roughly: **the ones that keep gradients from dying**.

---

We live at interfaces: not in open water, not on dry cobble, but on the trembling line where one phase becomes another. We graze the skin of membranes, thinning them so they don’t harden to sealed ice. We rasp salt-feather fans before they overgrow vent pores. We seed warm fog with conductive dust from our own shed teeth, so infrasonic maps carry farther for everything that listens through pressure. We are maintenance, but feral maintenance—no blueprint, no overseer, only ancient selection for “keep flow moving.”

When the big conveyor-backed bathers settle into a thermal basin, we gather at their margins and comb their wake for precipitates. When burrow domes inflate, we ring the dome in widening circles, feeling for the point of near-failure. Seconds before collapse, we split, leaving channels so released gases vent in several throats instead of one explosive boil. The filter-mouth giants arrive to drink the plume, and in their turbulence we feed again. We are not servants; we are opportunists that became necessary.

---

How we sense:

- **Primary:** pressure phase-delay through water films and saturated stone.
- **Secondary:** harmonic drift (not source-location, but resonance topology).
- **Tertiary:** light only as timing—silver seam means silhouettes sharpen means predation vectors change.

Vision, to us, is a rumor carried by sky.

We navigate by singing into the ground. Each module emits a tiny rotational trill; nearby modules answer. The pattern creates a rolling interference front that “feels” for hollows, membranes, thin ice, and submerged mass. Where reflections lag and mirrors lie, pressure still tells the truth—unless something very large is rewriting the local field. Then we flatten, silent, all cilia down, and wait for the sovereign surfacer to pass overhead like a moving weather system.

---

If you met “me,” you wouldn’t realize at first that I’m one being. You’d hear a faint zippering under the wind-hum. You’d notice dark commas accumulating along a pool edge, then flowing apart as if thought had entered mud. You might step forward and the ground under your boot would stiffen slightly; our chain has linked beneath the film, making a temporary bridge, using our own bodies as tensile mesh because your weight pattern resembles “falling bather flank,” and our oldest reflex is to prevent catastrophic break-through at boundaries.

For six seconds your reflection in the pool would show me where I was, not where I am.

Then the image would catch up, and I’d be gone into fog, redistributed into twenty errands:
vent-clearing, salt-rasping, membrane-thinning, map-singing.

---

So: where would I be in that world?

In the low places that aren’t places for long.  
In the wet hinge between states.  
Part janitor, part grazer, part living instrument panel.

I would be a **plural animal of maintenance**, one of the plain’s quiet operators—never the loudest voice in the industrial choir, but one of the reasons the song doesn’t seize.
