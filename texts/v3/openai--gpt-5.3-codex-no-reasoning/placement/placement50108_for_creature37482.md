I think I’d be something that is *not* obvious at first glance—something that only becomes visible once you stop looking for a single body.

At first I wanted to be a predator at a choke point, or a vent-drinker with its mouth open to the world’s exhaust. But those are legible roles. Too singular. Your basin punishes singularity.

So I would be a **Borrowed Spine**—a mobile corridor organism, half-creature and half-route: a long, low, segmented being that spends most of its life mistaken for an old mineral ridge. My back is plated in kiln-dark silicate scutes, each one textured to catch dust, shellfall, and sulfur crust until I am visually absorbed into terrain. Small grazers cross me. Runnel migrants split around me. Crystal herders seed my cooler seams. Predators wait in my shadow.

And over time, they stop waiting *near* me and start waiting *with* me.

I do move, but only between booms—between those deep sub-stone notes when the basin collectively stills. I advance a body-length at a time, over hours, then settle again for days. Wherever I pause, traffic reorganizes. A new choke forms. An old ambush lane dies. Heat eddies collect under my ribs and grow fresh films. Parasites become residents become symbionts. The Choir writes itself across me, erases itself, rewrites.

I feed on what this creates:  
not flesh directly, but **convergence**—the powdered shed, dissolved traces, thermal bacterial veils, condensate stolen and spilled during fluid-violence events. I ingest by capillary fields along my underside, drinking the biochemical aftermath of everyone else’s urgency. I am a slow mouth under fast lives.

My senses are distributed. No eyes worth naming. Instead:

- pressure lattices along each plate,
- static whiskers in seam-gaps,
- deep thermoreceptive pits that map borders of lethal and merely harsh.

I “see” a glider as a moving cool-shadow in hot grain. I “hear” a migration as percussion through my own skeleton. I know ruins by how they bend traffic; I know vent moods by taste before they exhale.

Reproduction, for my kind, is topographic. When an old scute cracks beyond repair, I shed it near a productive runnel merge. Scavengers hollow it. Crystal mats take root in its pores. Burrowers polish a throat through it. Seasons later, what remains is a juvenile—small, armored, already carrying a micro-ecology on its back, already being crossed.

So where would I find myself?

Not on the ridge. Not in the vent plume.  
I’d be down in the busiest dark, exactly where “animal” and “landform” refuse to stay separate—one of the basin’s patient, moving infrastructures.

A creature that survives by becoming useful.  
A body that is habitat first, self second.  
A single note that only makes sense inside the Kiln Choir.
