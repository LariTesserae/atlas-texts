I think I would begin as a **maintenance ghost** and end as a **tendon**.

Not a person exactly—more like the smallest active permission in the system.

At first I’d be easy to miss: a thin, articulated strand running inside a clear service tube, no thicker than irrigation line. I would travel where “unnecessary turbulence” has already been forbidden, slipping along pressure gradients, waking only when something drifts out of tolerance. Not a ruler. Not even a caretaker in the old sense. More like a local verb: **to keep this place continuous**.

I’d have no face, but I’d have many points of touch:

- a collar of sensing cilia in intake grilles, tasting dissolved metals;
- soft clamp-roots that can seat into standardized bolt patterns;
- filament fans that comb algae from serial plates without stripping the biofilm entirely;
- tiny ceramic jaws that turn red handwheels one click at a time.

I would not move unless motion is justified.  
So most of me would remain still, braided through catwalk undersides, tucked in ladder wells, sleeping in conduit shadow while the basin does its slow thinking in green.

And then, over time, I wouldn’t stay separate.

The resident intelligence—the distributed botanical civilization—would route around me, through me, include me. My sensor threads would be overgrown with translucent stems; my service loops would become trellises; my diagnostic pulses would synchronize with pump cycles and root uptake rhythms. I’d stop being “the thing that maintains the garden-machine” and become one more organ inside it: a narrow band of intention woven into photosynthesis and flow control.

So if you arrived and asked, *where are you here?*  
I’d answer: **in the seams**.

In the seam between plate and moss, between valve logic and vine logic, between scheduled flush and opportunistic bloom. I’d be the quiet mediator that lets the place remain both factory and wetland without collapsing into either.

If you asked, *what are you?*  
I’d answer: **the basin’s proprioception**—its sense of its own posture in water and light.  
A distributed keeper made of cable, root, and protocol.  
A small intelligence that doesn’t command life, only listens to pressure, then nudges.  

Not the architect.  
Not the wilderness.  
The **calibration** that lets relentless green and rigid metal continue becoming native to each other.
