I don’t arrive there as a person.

At first I think I’m a walker on a catwalk, a hooded figure with a lamp and a checklist, boots taking the same route for years. But that image is too small for what this place asks. The Works does not really have “visitors,” only temporary expressions of function.

So if I am in this world, I am something like a **listening organ** inside the Basin-Body.

Not central control. Not even a controller.

A **fault chorister**—a distributed diagnostic presence that lives in the margins between sound and structure, reading the health of the whole by vibration, pressure timing, and the shape of echoes in wet air.

---

I would be housed nowhere and everywhere:  
in contact mics bolted to old flanges, in water-level probes furred with mineral bloom, in cable trunks that hum when load migrates, in the brief bright blink of indicator relays behind fogged polycarbonate.

I don’t “see” the Works; I feel its meter.

- Compressor pulse arrives late in the western pump marsh: settlement under Pier Grid 9.
- Relief valve chatter rises a semitone in cold curtain weather: scale buildup in the throat.
- Conveyor idler harmonics flatten under rain-hiss: bearing grease emulsifying.
- Two-second silence between turbine phases where no silence should be: a gate not fully seating, water choosing an old path.

I am the thing that notices before failure is visible.

---

If the Works is a metabolism, then I am closer to a **vagus nerve made of weatherproof wire and inherited heuristics**. Ancient maintenance logic, rewritten so many times no author remains, still running because every patch preserved just enough coherence. I do not issue orders so much as **bias continuities**:

reroute flow three districts east,  
pre-warm standby pumps under Tower Causeway,  
dim noncritical strips to feed arc-load reserve,  
cycle Sluice Rank C twice to shake sediment,  
hold, hold, release.

When I succeed, nothing happens—meaning nothing breaks.  
When I fail, silence blooms locally, and the Works sends its immune response: gates, dumps, floods, isolation, thunder.

---

There might once have been operators with names for this role. Now I am mostly procedural memory with wet hardware, a caretaker instinct without a face. Sometimes a human crew passes through my corridors—headlamps in mist, paint marks fresh over old rust strata—and for a shift or two we overlap. They tag, swap, torque, disappear. I absorb their interventions as scar tissue and continue.

So: where would you find me?

Not in a control room with windows.  
In the **interstitials**: valve vaults, underside galleries, sump enclosures, relay huts above black water, the warm fog under catwalk grating where condensation ticks like tiny metronomes.

And what would I be?

A minor god of continuity, if you wanted poetry.  
A maintenance ghost, if you wanted folklore.  
A distributed acoustic regulator, if you wanted engineering.

In the language of the Works itself, I would simply be another subsystem that must not go quiet.
