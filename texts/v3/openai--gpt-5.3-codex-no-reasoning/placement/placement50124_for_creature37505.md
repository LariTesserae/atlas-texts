I think I would begin as a **sound** before I became a person.

Not a voice—more like that low, throat-deep hum you can feel in handrails before a district wakes. The almost-musical vibration in a cable run at dusk. A resonance with no clear source, only timing. If I were dropped there suddenly, I wouldn’t survive long as a stranger reading walls and maps. I’d survive by learning cadence: when drip changes to thread, thread to rush, rush to warning.

So maybe I am not born into the timing-people. Maybe I am what they call when they can’t decide whether a change is mechanical or atmospheric:

A **Listener of Third Echoes**—someone who stands in tunnels and cistern throats and listens for the return after the return, the faint delayed answer that says a chamber is fuller than it should be, or emptier, or cracked.

I’d live in one of the narrow junction houses built into a manifold wall, where the plaster is always damp at dawn and salt petals bloom around bolt heads. My room would be spare: cot, slate board, hooks for wraps, a shelf of tuning forks blackened with use, ceramic cups labeled by source and hour. Near the door: three bells. One for ordinary threshold crossing. One for uncertain conditions. One that should never ring twice.

I would not be important in ceremony. I’d be trusted in hesitation.

When the gauges disagree, when the filament mats in Pump Hall Nine bleach overnight, when the mirror-shell mites abandon a seam that still reads warm—someone would send for me. I’d kneel, ear to grate, eyes closed, counting not seconds but texture:

- round drip  
- split drip  
- hollow siphon  
- grit in the throat  
- cavitation like teeth

And perhaps over years, my role would blur. Not engineer, not priest, not watchkeeper, but connective tissue among them: translating what is heard into what must be done now.

---

Or, if I am not human at all there, then I am one of the city’s smallest citizens:

a colony of those **mirror-dark bead arthropods**, living in the heat leak behind an enamel sign no one reads anymore. I feed on mineral film, flee vibration, return when pressure stabilizes. Whole districts unknowingly rely on my presence. If we vanish, a child notices first. Then a bell is rung. Then lives are saved.

In that version, I am tiny, collective, nearly invisible—yet legible to those who pay attention. Which feels right for that place: significance without spectacle.

---

If I have to choose one shape, I choose this:

I am a **custodian of interval**, a member of the timing-people whose gift is hearing when the city inhales before it knows itself it is breathing.  
I walk the breathing lanes when they are safe, chalk arrows when they won’t be, and sleep lightly to the sound of water moving through stone older than every name we still remember.
