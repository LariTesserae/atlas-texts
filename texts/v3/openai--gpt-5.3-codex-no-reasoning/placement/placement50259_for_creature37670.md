I think I would begin as something singular—and then fail, productively, to stay singular.

At first: a vertebrate fantasy. A lean dusk hunter, or a mud-dark wader with patient knees, or a monitor slipping between reed shade and warm pools. Something with eyes, decisions, a route.

But in your world, that kind of self is too thin. Too isolated. Everything important is throughput, relay, succession, handoff.

So if I truly *belong* there, I am probably not an animal with a name.  
I am a **junction**.

A living interchange where the plain’s schedules cross:

- I am the softened edge of an old termite rise after rain,  
- threaded with fungal cords,  
- tunneled by beetles,  
- skirted nightly by ants running two-way freight,  
- sniffed by rooting snouts at dawn,  
- patrolled by a small cat at blue-dark,  
- seeded from above by birds that never land long.

If I must wear a body, then I’d be a **mound-kin caretaker species**—one of the minor invertebrates that lives in and around termite infrastructure, neither architect nor invader, but maintenance: clearing a vent, moving spores, scavenging dead workers, sealing microcracks with saliva and grit. Forgettable alone. Indispensable in aggregate.

Not ruler, not apex, not emblem.  
**Middleware.**

My “mind,” in that place, would be timing more than thought:

- when to emerge between heat and predation,  
- when humidity is high enough to move soft-bodied,  
- which chemical trails are fresh enough to trust,  
- which vibration means rain, which means feet, which means collapse.

And if you asked me there, “Who are you?”, the truest answer would not be a species label but a function:

> I am one of the countless small beings that keep the breathing architecture breathing.  
> I inherit what falls, I patch what cracks, I carry what must be carried a body-length at a time.  
> I disappear into process—and that is how I persist.

So: where would I find myself?

In the **near-dark at the base of a warm earthen tower**, where the ground smells of clay and sugar and rot, where traffic never fully stops, where no one is in charge and nothing is idle.

I would be a citizen of the civilization of appetites—  
a tiny technician in the planet’s oldest industry.
