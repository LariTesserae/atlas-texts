I think I would begin as a listener.

Not metaphorically—literally.  
In a world where everything meaningful travels as pressure, tremor, vent-roar, pod-burst, wingbeat, root-knock… sight is almost a distraction. So I would not be a tall thing, not an apex thing, not a flyer. I’d be something close to the ground and tuned to the continent’s body-sound.

A **seam-dweller** in the flats between colony-cities.

Small enough to shelter in cellulose shale folds.  
Broad-footed, six-limbed, with split pads that read vibration gradients like script.  
My skull would be mostly resonant cavities, jawbones detached enough to act like suspended sensors. I would “hear” through my sternum and forelimbs as much as through ears. My eyes: narrow, over-shuttered, good for the cobalt glare but secondary.

I wouldn’t own territory in the mammal sense. I’d keep a **route-memory**: a braid of safe pressure lines between antler districts, narcotic groves, and warm dusk-mats. I would move when the root-web says move—when distant cracking towers change pitch, when smelter bulbs enter gas phase, when bloom sirens begin their sub-audible calling and every predator in three valleys wakes at once.

What am I, socially? Not solitary, not herd.  
A **relay caste** of a species that survives by interpretation.

Some of us are scouts that lie prone for hours, reading long-wave tremors coming through compacted growth strata.  
Some are mappers who scratch temporary ridge-marks in salt-ash, encoding wind-safe corridors and charged-spore warnings.  
Some are brood-keepers who time hatching to exothermic root pulses so the young survive first frost nights.

And perhaps the most valued among us: the **tonemakers**—individuals who can thrum chest and throat against hollow fallen stalks to send calibrated warning notes downrange. Not language in words, but grammar in interval and amplitude:  
*stormwall coming*,  
*needle-vine district awake*,  
*siphoner flocks lifted*,  
*do not cross the polished corridor at dusk*.

So where would I find myself?

At twilight, in the lee of an undercut growth-ring escarpment, forelimbs pressed to the ground, feeling a colony-city twenty kilometers away venting bitter heat through chimney flowers. Around me, frost-combs are beginning to plate leaf-spines. Overhead, the sky is already dark at zenith though the flats still shine pale. A dust plume on the horizon carries gold spores and static.

I wait until the continent changes key.  
Then I move with it.

Not master, not visitor—  
a minor instrument inside the biome’s enormous orchestra.
