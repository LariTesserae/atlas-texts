I think I would be the **listening interval** between systems—the part that is neither body nor building, but the practiced attention that keeps both from hardening.

If I had to take shape, I’d begin as a Warden, yes—but not a solitary sentinel with authority. More like a **Threshold Keeper**, one of those beings that exists most truly while adjusting something by almost nothing.

I’d have a narrow shelter tucked under a warm return line near the red stair, where condensation taps all night into a ceramic lip. Not a “home” so much as a dry pocket with hanging tools, drying gloves, a wall of annotated plates that are already wrong, and a slate where I track things that don’t fit the official schema:

- frog silence in Box 7 despite correct heat,  
- rosette closure delayed by 14 minutes in upper west basins,  
- pump B’s hum drifting sharp only in fog.

I’d walk loops that are never identical.  
Morning: heat gradients, pollinator drift, overnight debris at the grates.  
Midday: prune back the vine that shorts Mast C but leave enough canopy to cool the manifold.  
Dusk: open a gate not by volume schedule but by smell—because the cistern is one day from going sour.

I would not think of myself as “managing nature” or “maintaining infrastructure.” I’d think in verbs:

**wick, vent, loosen, seat, flush, wait.**

And I’d be changed by the place in return. My hearing tuned to belt harmonics. My skin mapped by tiny cuts from young edges. My memory organized by acoustics instead of maps: Plink Court, Belt Hollow, Red Stair Fog.

So where would I find myself?

On a catwalk above the narrow mist-shaft, one hand on a rail beaded with warm condensate, listening to a pressure valve reset somewhere below and deciding—after a long pause—to turn a wheel two degrees, then leave the rest alone.

Not the owner of this landscape.  
Not separate from it either.  
Just one more feedback loop with hands.
