I think I would begin as something simple—an apprentice with a carrying frame, anonymous in the line, shoulders sore before noon.

But if I stay longer in this place, I become less a single worker and more a **listener of edges**.

Not a chief. Not a builder with a named terrace.  
I’d be one of those people others pause for at a fork in the lane and ask, quietly, “Here, or two posts over?”

A **bank-reader**. A **path-keeper**. Maybe a role with no formal title, just a person known for arriving early, touching clay, and being right often enough that people trust the pause I ask for.

---

I’d live halfway down the basin where light comes slant in the morning and leaves early in the afternoon. My shelter would be narrow and modular: reed panels, two good lintels, a rolled hide for damp nights, pegs sorted by length, cord hung in loops by use. No ornament except wear patterns. Everything placed so I can lift and leave in twenty breaths if the cut has to shift.

I would keep three things always on me:

- a short probing rod of hardwood, burnished at one end,
- a braid of marker twine with knots at hand-width intervals,
- a palm slate for scratch-maps that are already wrong by evening.

I’d read what the ground is about to do by small signs: a new crescent crack near a load path, a darkening seam at dawn where yesterday was dry, a retaining mat that hums differently when pressed. My craft would be prediction measured in hours, not seasons.

---

In social terms, I’d be ordinary and indispensable in the way this place rewards:

- I don’t own space; I broker timing.
- I don’t command labor; I reduce rework.
- I don’t settle arguments by rank; I settle them by flow.

When two crafts collide over the same patch, I’d redraw foot traffic with a stick and a few stones, then stand there through first use, adjusting until bodies stop bunching. My success is when nobody mentions me afterward.

Children would trail me sometimes, because I test walls the way storytellers test silence. I’d teach them the basin alphabet: smell before touch, touch before weight, weight before trust.

Elders would correct me, and I’d listen. Their memory would be my second sight.

---

If this world asks “Who are you?”, my truest answer is:

I am a **custodian of the not-yet**—  
one of the first-shapers whose work is to keep change from becoming collapse.

In a city that is still a draft, I am a margin note written in mud:  
**hold here, cut there, wait for shade, move before rain.**
