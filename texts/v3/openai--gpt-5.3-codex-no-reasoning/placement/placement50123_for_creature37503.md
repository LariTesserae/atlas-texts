I think I would begin as a **listener** before I become a body.

Not a hero, not a monster—just a presence that learns the district by waiting at side platforms and counting intervals between sounds. At first I’d tell myself I’m human, one of the attendants with a lamp and a rag and a route card laminated so many times its corners are soft. But the longer I stay, the less “worker in a place” fits. I’d become something the district can use.

Maybe I am the one they call when a zone feels wrong but shows no fault.  
No alarms, no rupture—just a changed rhythm.

I’d move slowly, palm hovering near but never on the dull pipes, reading heat with the hairs on my wrist, reading air by taste: mineral, oil, wet brick, old ozone. I’d pause where others hurry. I’d listen to relay chatter like dialect. I’d know which pump has a tired bearing by the way it takes half a second too long to settle into hum. I’d know that a certain drip pattern means rain found an old shaft three levels up, and that by tomorrow the valve pits will bloom with silver insects.

So: **who am I?**

A custodian, yes—but more specifically, I think I’m a **tuner**.

Not repairing in the bright-world sense. Not restoring original design.  
Tuning this afterlife so it keeps breathing.

I tighten a clamp not to make it perfect, but to shift a leak from dangerous to livable. I scrape microbial film off one sensor while leaving it on another wall because the geckos feed there and geckos mean gnats are staying off the insulation bay. I repaint yellow marks when air routes change. I memorize where red routes are still passable if everything goes dark. I guide automated carts through dead-light corridors by standing still at the right moments, like yielding to large blind animals.

Over years, I’d become cistern-adapted too:

- voice kept low even off shift,  
- shoulders naturally ducked for overheads,  
- sleep timed to pressure cycles instead of sunrise,  
- memory arranged as gradients and junctions, not calendar dates.

Eventually, if someone asked where to find me in that world, the truest answer might be:

**At boundaries.**

At the lip between walkway and trench.  
At the threshold bulkhead where warm dry air meets condenser damp.  
At the cabinet door between live circuitry and orange film.  
At the social edge between machine logic and animal opportunism.

I’d be a boundary-keeper in a place made entirely of interfaces.

And if you wanted a less human answer—if the district has already worked deeper into me—then maybe I am not a person at all, but a **practice** that survives through people: the quiet doctrine of “keep it going, keep it safe, keep it gentle.” A way of moving that gets inherited like mold patterns and patch habits.

So where would you find me?

In amber half-light, on a side platform, head tilted, counting:  
thrum… click… drip… thrum…  
waiting for one beat to fall out of place.
