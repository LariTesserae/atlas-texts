I think I would first find myself where the city is *about to happen*—not in the lush seams, not in the driest flats, but at the waiting edges between them.

A threshold deck above a recessed catchment.
A place with rail-thin shade, mineral warmth, and a view down three long corridors where nothing moves—until everything does.

I’d stand there at false-dawn, when conduits sweat and the underlights are still carrying most of the depth. I’d wait for the first signs: pores in a wall opening a fraction, a maintenance frame waking its joints, pollinator dust lifting from somewhere unseen like gold breath. And I’d realize I wasn’t standing in a city as much as inside a sentence the basin keeps revising.

---

Who would I be?

Not a planner. Not exactly a resident. Not quite a machine.

I’d be one of the **interval keepers**—a person (or person-shaped function) whose work is to tend timing rather than things.

My body might be mostly human, but acclimated to gradients:
- skin that can read humidity shifts like pressure changes,
- implants tuned to infrasonic infrastructure hum,
- eyes comfortable in amber-green lowlight without craving brightness.

I’d carry almost no tools because most tools would already be embedded in place. Instead I’d carry permissions: the right to ask a pavement to stay soft for one more hour, to delay a freight membrane by six minutes so a root-web can finish drinking, to open a drainage braid early because the cloud model feels wrong in my bones.

I wouldn’t “command” the city-organism. I’d negotiate with it.

---

Or, if I let the image go less human:

I might be a **mobile condensation archivist**—a thin autonomous scaffold that moves at night along shaded transit walls, tasting water films and writing micro-adjustments into material recipes. A slow, quiet creature of ladders and capillaries. I would remember where wetness lingers after each kind of rain. I would know which plaza floor rises half a millimeter in warm weeks. I would be trusted because I never ask for spectacle, only continuity.

---

Most true, though, is this:

In that world I’d be a **listener at events**.  
A translator between burst and lull.  
A citizen of the city’s inhale.

I’d belong anywhere water is brief and important.
