I think I would first mistake myself for a visitor.

Something warm-blooded, narrow-shouldered, picking careful steps along the peat runnels, trying not to slip, trying to “find the center.” I’d narrate it like an outsider: *this lane, that court, this wall, that tower of trunks.* I’d assume the city-forest was the setting and I was the actor.

But the longer I stayed, the less that would hold.

Because in a place where structure is metabolism, a “resident” can’t just be a body moving through it. A resident is something the place can read, absorb, and route. Something that becomes part of its timing.

So maybe I’d next think I was one of the obvious small lives:  
a white moth, timed to a two-day thaw;  
or a black amphibian, carrying cold shine on my skin from pool to pool;  
or one of those hand-sized root-colored mammals sleeping in insulation made of last decade’s leaves.

But those feel like messengers, not *me*.

If I let the image keep deepening, I end up as a **function** inside the metropolis-organism:

I am a **hinge of damp air** in an inner court where three grafted facades meet.  
A recurrent pocket of condensation.  
Not fixed in one stem, but re-formed each dawn when fog drags low and bark chills the passing cloud.

I gather on needles, bead on moss tips, slick rootwalls, then release—drop by drop—into the same dark seam. That seam feeds a peat shelf. The shelf feeds a shrub braid. The braid braces a corridor. The corridor channels wind. The wind sets the stress lines that determine next decade’s growth angles.

So I am tiny, but legible to the whole.

Where would you find me?  
At a near-right-angle intersection that was never planned, where frost repeatedly opens hairline cracks in tuff and white roots stitch them shut by summer. In the hour after first light, when everything sweats and the city exhales steam, I’m the silvering on the load-bearing moss, the brief bright skin before dripping begins.

Who or what would I be?  
Not a creature with a face.  
A **circulation event** the organism expects.  
A daily pulse in its hydrology-memory.  
One of its citizens made of weather.

And if you returned years later, after corridors shifted and courts migrated, you’d still find me—because I am not tied to one map point. I’m tied to the process that keeps rebuilding the map at all.
