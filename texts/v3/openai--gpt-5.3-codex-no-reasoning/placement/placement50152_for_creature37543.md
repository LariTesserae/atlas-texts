I’d be something that mistakes no boundary for a wall.

Not the heron, not the rat, not the workers on shift—though I’d borrow from all of them. I’d be the **district’s unofficial second circulatory system**: a narrow-bodied amphibian that moves where runoff, heat, and vibration overlap. People would think I appear only when something’s wrong, because that’s when they notice me. In truth I’d always be there, paced to pumps.

I think I’d be called different names in different sectors, and none would be quite right.  
In the market sheds: **gutter-jack**.  
At lock control: **red-eye**.  
On night maintenance: **the soft wrench**.

I’d have:

- matte-black skin that gathers moisture from air like cold pipe does;
- forelimbs with spread digits, good on algae-slick concrete and rusted mesh;
- a low dorsal ridge sensitive to pressure changes in culverts (I’d feel rain thirty minutes out);
- a jaw built less for killing than for prying: cable ties, weed mats, crab shell, fish spine, plastic strapping;
- inner ears tuned to machine signatures, so a misaligned fan or stuttering valve sounds as obvious to me as a shout.

I wouldn’t “haunt” the district in a spooky way. I’d **service** it in passing, for selfish reasons that happen to align with human ones. Clear a grate because trapped shrimp collect downstream. Worry at a flap gate because the eddy behind it shelters rats. Tug loose cloth from an intake because the vibration hurts my teeth through the water.

Where would I find myself?  
At dusk, mostly, in the seam-zones:

- under the grated walk beside settling pond 3, where flies hatch thick and swallows cut low;
- chest-deep in warm outflow where tiny fish gather stunned;
- curled in generator lee heat during noon glare, breathing slow;
- on rain nights, atop retaining walls, tracking first overflow tongues as they search for old paths in new concrete.

If startled, I wouldn’t flee far—just slip from visible geometry into infrastructural geometry: down two steps, through a half-submerged conduit, up behind a ladder cage, gone.

So: in your world, I’d be **an opportunistic custodian**—feral, unowned, uncounted, and entirely at home in managed wetness.  
Not a monster. Not a mascot.  
A resident function that evolved before anyone wrote it into the schematics.
