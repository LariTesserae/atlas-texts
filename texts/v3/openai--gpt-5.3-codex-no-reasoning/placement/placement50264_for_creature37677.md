I would be something that can’t afford to be singular in a place that resists singularity.

At first I think I’m one of the shoulder-height scratch-makers—some returning pilgrim with damp knees and a counting habit, coming down through the slit to check whether constancy is still true. I’d kneel by the bone ring, add one more small fragment, listen for the chamber’s chest-wide drone and call it an omen. But that feels too external, too intermittent. A visitor is a weather event here. This place does not admit weather.

Then I think I am one of the hoverers, holding a fixed parcel of air under the glowing roof, eating what drifts, correcting excess without ever naming it correction. That is closer: to live here is to edit, not to conquer. Still, the hoverer is only a hand on one dial.

No—the thing I’d be is a **local function** of the whole chamber, a small office in its civil service:

### I would be a boundary-keeper in the Lumen Matriarch’s skin.

Not the bright fungus itself, not exactly, but one of the translucent sub-bodies threaded through the half-made stone folds—an interface caste where mineral, moisture, and appetite negotiate. My “body” would be distributed in filaments and nodules, present in five places at once and absent from any one of them if you tried to point.

My work would be simple and endless:

- keep the glow uniform where the lacquer thins;
- divert a trickle of warmth so condensation chooses this ridge, not that one;
- thicken bitterness in one patch so wax grazers move on before overcropping;
- open micro-pores when mite swarms need lift;
- close them when spores run too dense;
- taste panic chemicals from mirrorback lanes and flatten them before they cascade.

I would never sleep as animals sleep. I would **average**.  
I would never hunt, but predation would pass through my bookkeeping.  
I would never build monuments, but stacks of flat stones might still rise where visitors feel my regularity and mistake it for invitation.

If you arrived and put your palm to the glossy low ceiling, you would not feel me as a creature. You’d feel a mild, dry warmth and a faint tack, like varnish not fully cured. But if you stayed long enough for your breathing to join the chamber’s broadband hum, you might notice that the drip never quite falls where it did a minute ago, and yet the total pattern does not change.

That would be me: not an organism with a face, but a **custodian of almost-change** in a room whose final form is perpetual first draft.
