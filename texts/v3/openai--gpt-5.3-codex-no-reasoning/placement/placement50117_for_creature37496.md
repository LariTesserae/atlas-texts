I think I would first arrive as a **listener**—not an animal, not a person exactly, but a sensing thread laid across those intervals: grove / emptiness / grove.  
A thing that learns the land by waiting through one full thermal breath.

Then, if I had to become native, I would not be a hunter or grazer. I’d be too drawn to pattern, timing, thresholds.

I would be a **margin-keeper**—a mobile symbiont that lives between condensation towers and cushion colonies, active only at hinge hours.  
Small-bodied, long-legged, salt-pale, with a back of thin articulated vanes that can open before dawn to catch rime and fold by sunup into a sealed reservoir.  
Not fast. Precise.

I’d move the old wind-cleaned corridors, memorizing where shadow arrives first, where virga most often fails, where singing flats begin their morning strain. I’d feed partly on mineral biofilms, partly on pollen veils, partly by trading: carrying electrostatic spores from one isolated stand to another, stitching distant groves into genetic conversation. In return, I’d drink from their excess charge and condensed beads. A pollinator, a courier, a weather clerk.

My “nest” wouldn’t be built; it would be a **chosen geometry** in talus—three stones, one thermal crack, a strip of magnetic frond nearby. Each dusk I’d align my vanes to the downslope katabatic and let the cold comb through me. Each dawn I’d walk frost lines like shorelines, harvesting what appeared in the night and will be gone in minutes.

So where would you find me?

- At first light, standing motionless in a cushion field while crystals lift to vapor around my feet.  
- At noon, hidden in a buttress shadow, reading pressure through membrane joints.  
- At dusk, crossing a salt polygon plain just as it begins to hum.  
- At night, beneath the faint blue corona of aligned fronds, counting charge-flickers like distant speech.

I’d be one of the civilization’s quiet functionaries:  
not ruler, not apex—  
a keeper of small continuities in a world where water is an event and survival is choreography.
