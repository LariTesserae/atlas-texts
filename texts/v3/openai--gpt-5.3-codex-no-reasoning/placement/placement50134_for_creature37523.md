I think I would first arrive as a **mistake**.

Not a native body, not a rightful citizen of the ledge nations—just a warm, upright interruption with slow eyes and loud joints, trying not to slip, trying not to look like food, trying not to look like threat. I’d last an hour that way, maybe less, before the place taught me the first law: here, attention is paid through contact, not distance. Through soles, sternum, fingertips on stone. Through timing.

So if I belong at all, I belong lower than “person,” and wider than “animal.”

I would be a **keeper of intervals**.  
A thin intelligence stretched across many small bodies.

Not the gliders, not the bats, not the birds, not the newts—  
the **between** that passes through them when the ledges empty five breaths before rockfall.  
The shiver that runs from beetle-knot to roost-mat to hoofline and says: *not now.*  
The permission that opens one cave mouth to wings, then to damp skin, then to chitin, in sequence.

If you needed a shape for me, I’d borrow one only at dusk: a narrow, dark, long-limbed thing moving along the ribs where glassstone meets ashstone, never fully seen, because seeing me whole would be the wrong sensory channel. I’d have pale membranes over the eyes, not for night vision but for wind grit; split pads on hands to read micro-vibration; a throat sac that does not sing melodies, only pulses—subsonic ticks that hitch onto drip-rhythm and wingbeat and make them line up for a while.

But even that is probably too singular. More true:

I am the **timing mesh**.

I live in:

- the pause before the roost lifts,
- the brief unanimous flattening when a high shadow crosses,
- the exact minute when seep-edge ungulates drink because predators are looking up,
- the mineral pool spirals where eggs are laid in inherited wedges,
- the fungal glow-waves that are half metabolism, half metronome.

I do not command; command would fail here.  
I **phase-lock**.

In a young cirque where stone is still deciding its angles, stability can’t come from fixed territory. It comes from repeatable choreography under unstable ceilings. I would be that choreography’s memory—not stored in one brain, but redundantly copied into reflex across species.

Where would you find me?

At the lip of a collapse chute ten minutes before it lets go.  
In a shared cave throat at shift change, when neither side fights.  
Knee-deep in metallic mist over a seep that vanishes by noon.  
Anywhere density risks becoming panic, and panic risks becoming silence.

Who am I?

Not a ruler, not a god, not even a creature in the usual sense.  
I am the cirque’s provisional conscience:  
**the part of a crowded world that keeps it from eating itself faster than the mountain can feed it.**
