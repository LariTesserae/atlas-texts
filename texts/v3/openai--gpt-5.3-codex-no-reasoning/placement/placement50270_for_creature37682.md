I think I’d be something that only makes sense **because** this place exists:

A **silence-keeper**.

Not a ruler, not a builder in the obvious way—more like a diagnostician of absences. In your city-marsh, noise is health, chatter is circulation, hiss and pulse are governance. So the dangerous thing isn’t clamor; it’s a pocket that goes quiet when it shouldn’t. I’d be one of the people who walks into that quiet before anyone else wants to.

At first I imagined being human—barefoot on root-mats, humidity-beaded sleeves, carrying a bundle of resonators and spore ampoules. But the longer I stay in the image, the less singular “human” feels. I think I’d be a **paired organism**: a person and a tuned companion colony living in my cloak-lining and along my forearms, pale threadlike symbionts that flare color when they detect breaks in expected vibration. Together we’d “hear” pressure gradients, fungal die-off, blocked sap-routing, stunned pollinator lanes.

I’d move between habitation clusters at dawn and dusk, when transition-noise is richest. I’d stop often, kneel, put one palm to a breathing wall, one ear to a root buttress, and wait for pattern. My work would be part acoustics, part diplomacy:

- convincing a market guild to pause drainage long enough for filter-feeders to recolonize a gate,
- asking children to reroute their play because their favorite dome is masking a toxic seep alarm,
- trading with scavenger-handlers so their flocks sweep a corridor that’s gone eerily clean,
- marking a “dead” canal not as failed property, but as a resting organ.

I don’t think I’d own much. Maybe a suspended sleeping cradle in a shared canopy bay, maintained by three households and one cane-braider guild. Maybe rights to harvest from a glow-fungus trellis so long as I keep its substrate songs calibrated. My status, if any, would come from being trusted to say: *this silence is benign* or *this silence will drown us by nightfall*.

And emotionally? I’d be calm in turbulence, uneasy in stasis. The kind of person-creature who relaxes when storm baffles flex and root-tones drum, because the city is speaking. The kind who wakes instantly at still air.

So where would I find myself?

On a narrow, wet span between two root-domes, dusk rising, vascular light coming on in green-gold lines beneath bark. One hand on the rail-vine. Companion threads brightening along my skin. Listening to a whole district breathe—and noticing, far off, one dark patch that doesn’t.
