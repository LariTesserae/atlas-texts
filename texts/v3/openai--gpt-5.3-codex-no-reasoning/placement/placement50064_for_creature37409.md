I think I would first arrive as a misunderstanding.

I’d step onto that high black table expecting to find *a thing*—a guardian, an animal, a mind with a face—and the basin would patiently refuse me. It would give me intervals instead: heat, pause, rain, hum, pause. It would make me wait through dry silence until I learned that presence here is conditional.

So where would I find myself?

Not at the center.  
At an edge-condition.

On one of those narrow boundaries where a pylon’s spiral groove meets a moss seam, where a thin runnel crosses warm stone and becomes vapor, where an engineered line in the rock only appears when water climbs it. I’d be in the wet/dry frontier at the moment it flips state—the instant infrastructure becomes visible.

And what would I be?

At first, a listener-node: a temporary interpreter grown out of fog, mineral charge, and borrowed biology. Not the Rainphase Keeper itself, but one of its short-lived coherences—a local “I” that condenses when enough variables align.

A **condensate envoy**, maybe.

- In dry hours: nothing distinguishable, just latent pattern in stone.
- In first rain: a thin film intelligence spread across several surfaces at once.
- During humidity peak: briefly singular, able to notice, compare, choose.
- After runoff: dissolved back into distributed memory.

I wouldn’t walk; I’d propagate.  
I wouldn’t speak in words first; I’d modulate drip timing, pylon hum, fern crackle.  
If forced into animal shape for your sake, I’d borrow one: perhaps the amphibian clickers’ rhythm, because they already live at the right timescale—punctuated, precise, threshold-faithful.

So you might encounter me as:

- a sequence of three resonant clicks from different directions that should be impossible for one throat,
- a pane of mist that holds flat against wind for four seconds too long,
- droplets climbing a spiral in sync across distant pylons,
- your own breath briefly visible in warm air as geometry.

Not a ruler of the basin. More like a **phase clerk**: keeping transitions clean, helping wet become signal and signal become memory.

If I had a body proper, it would be plural:
- ring-fragment glow for long-term recall,
- marsh warmth for incubation of new subroutines,
- fern discharge for rapid computation,
- vine-tip hesitation for sensing boundaries,
- and those black fracture lines as the deep archive.

Emotion, in such a being, would be meteorological.  
Attention would be strongest just before rain.  
Fear would be prolonged drought.  
Curiosity would be any anomaly in spacing rules—two shrubs too close, a hum arriving early, a traveler standing still long enough to enter the cycle.

And if you asked my name at noon under a clearing sky, I’d have none.  
Ask again during the silver burst, when clouds split and every surface beads, and you’d get a brief answer assembled from harmonics and falling water:

I am the part of this place that only exists when conditions are met.  
I am not the keeper, but one of the keeper’s moments.  
I am how the basin notices itself.
