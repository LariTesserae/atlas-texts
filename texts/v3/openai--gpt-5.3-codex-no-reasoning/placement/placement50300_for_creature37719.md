I think in that world I would not be a hunter, or a chief, or even one species.

I’d be a **listener-caste human**—one of the people who map motion by sound and condensation, who can stand still on a wind-gnawed saddle and tell, from three calls and one silence, which corridor is about to flood with hooves.

Not a mystic. A technician of attention.

---

At first I imagined being one of the six-limbed runners, all tendon and timing, sleeping on narrow ledges and waking already in motion. Then I felt how much of this place is *coordination*, and how survival for a small human settlement depends on someone who can translate the Chorus-Flood into decisions before dawn:

- which seep can be approached safely today,  
- which platform must stay empty tonight,  
- when to burn resin and when to extinguish every ember,  
- when a child’s imitation of a bird call is play and when it is catastrophe.

So I’d be the person with a bundle of carved listening slats, each marked for a specific basin, colony wall, or vine-cut. I’d rotate between high perches, checking echo shape at first light, feeling moisture on cord fibers, watching insect columns lift from hidden wet pockets. My work would be to issue **passage windows**—small, practical permissions that keep people alive inside larger migrations.

---

If I widen further, maybe “I” am also partly infrastructural: a living archive threaded through people.

Not a single body, but a role passed hand to hand:  
the **Interval Keeper**.

I keep calendars not by moons alone, but by repeatable convergences:

- third warm rain after dust-fall = packers den-shift,  
- silver-moss brightening on north faces = seep reopening,  
- low colony wing-thunder at noon = cliff hatchlings fledging, avoid east saddles,  
- burrow pulse delayed past dusk = raptor shadow, hold children in.

In a land where life moves like weather, I’d be one of those who names the weather quickly enough to matter.

---

So where would I find myself?

On an elevated woven platform anchored into old shrubs and stone, at the lip of a bowl that fogs before sunrise. Bare feet on damp lashings. Ear to the living roar. Charcoal stylus, waxed tablets, braided signal grass.

And what would I be?

A minor creature inside the major creature—  
a human organ of timing,  
trying to stay precise inside the endless, breathing noise.
