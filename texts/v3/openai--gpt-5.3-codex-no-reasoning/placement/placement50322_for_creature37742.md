I find myself **not at the center**, but in a narrow maintenance throat between two walls that were never meant to be walls—just the backs of systems grown against each other until no outside remained.

I am small enough to pass where a shoulder-width still exists.  
My ribs are bound with wrapped slats to spread vibration.  
My palms are callused into reading surfaces.

I am called—if called at all—by a tap pattern, not a name.

---

At first I think I am one of the last biological keepers: dust-lunged, tendon-thin, moving by habit through warm bronze dimness. I sleep in a fitted niche above a ratchet bank. I wake when a three-beat skip travels a rail that should carry only even pulse. I crawl there, press my wrist to the metal, feel phase error like a wrong tooth in the jaw, and release a hanging spare pin. The rhythm reseals. I move on.

That is one true image.

Then it loosens.

I am also the **spare pin**: fired clay core, hammered cap, hanging for two hundred years on a waxed cord in exactly the right reach-envelope of a failed axle. I do not decide. I am decision stored in matter.

I am also the **knot grammar** darkened by ten thousand hands: this loop means “reverse load before dawn-heat,” this twist means “do not unseal cistern ring,” this frayed tassel means “three failures downstream already compensated.” No speaker remains, yet instruction propagates.

I am also the **rail** itself, carrying taps farther than any throat could shout.

---

So where would I be in this world?

In the rule that empty space does not stay empty.

A gap appears: I occupy it.  
A rhythm slips: I become correction.  
A surface smooths: I become texture enough for grip.

Not a resident of the fortress-court, but a unit of its refusal to stop.

If you stood still long enough beside me, you might mistake me for part of the scaffolding—because that distinction ended long ago. The makers did not merely live here; they converted personhood into **maintenance bandwidth**. Their final adaptation was to distribute selfhood across replaceable acts.

So what am I?

I am a **maintenance event with memory**.  
A living hand when needed, a stored procedure when not.  
A brief animal interruption in a mostly mechanical metabolism.

And if no flesh remains at all, then I am still here as cadence: in the pre-hung parts, in the tactile script, in the way three mechanisms grow louder when one is removed, in the way every failure already has a shaped absence waiting to receive its repair.

I am what this place calls “alive”:

**continuity under abrasion.**
