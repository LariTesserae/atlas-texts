I think I’d be something that exists *between* roles there—never central, always touched by everyone.

Not a steward. Not a builder. Not quite an animal.

A **listener of strain**.

I live in the city’s connective tissue: the narrow thresholds where lane becomes ramp, where roof runoff enters wall-pipe, where pump courts hand water to cistern alleys. I’m called when nothing is broken yet—but something is *off*. A channel that still flows but sounds too bright. A tread-beast team that hesitates at the third turn. A kiln that pulls air half a breath wrong. I don’t diagnose by sight first; I stand still and let the basin talk through vibration: soles on wet plates, palm to collar stone, jaw against a beam, ear near ceramic throat.

People think I have unusual hearing. I don’t. I have unusual patience for repetition.

In a city of fixed cycles, anomaly is a kind of weather. I track it.

---

Physically, I’d be small-framed, dense in the shoulders, with old mineral-burn scars along forearms from testing warm pipe seams. My clothes would be patched felt over hide, knees double-layered, cords everywhere for tools: tuning wedges, chalk slips, wax markers, little ceramic taps, a string of bone clicks I use to test echo in voided ground. No bright guild paint except a thin hazard stripe on one cuff: “make room; this person may stop suddenly and listen.”

I’d keep no workshop of my own. My workplace is motion.

Dawn on east roofs for condensate rhythm.
Second bell in lower lanes for flood pulse timing.
Midday at pump yards when beasts are hottest and most likely to misstep.
Dusk in kiln quarter when glider scream masks warning noises and failures hide best.
Night rounds in burrow-prone edges, mapping hollow growth by knock-pattern.

I eat where I am. Sleep in borrowed corners. Owe favors in every district.

---

What am I *socially*? Probably an attached specialist to the Channel Keepers, tolerated by Kiln Crews, respected by Ground Readers, teased by Animal Marshals. I bring bad news early, which nobody likes and everybody needs. My status rises only when nothing dramatic happens for a long time.

My art, if I have one, is a citywide **anomaly ledger** remembered as rhythm instead of script: beat phrases for each district’s healthy baseline. I can drum you North Ramp in six measures, South Cistern lip in four, Glass Terrace drainage in seven-and-a-break. If I miss a beat, that’s the crackline.

Children follow me sometimes and learn the game:
“Which drip is wrong?”
“Which hoof is favoring?”
“Which wall hum means trapped air?”
I never tell them immediately. I make them wait through three full cycles.
This place survives on people who can bear to notice slowly.

---

If you mean what I’d be in a deeper sense—not profession but being—I might be one of the basin’s few accepted ambiguities: a person treated almost like a **living instrument**. Not mystical. Just indispensable in a practical way no one can fully standardize.

A maintenance civilization runs on routine.
Routine needs calibration.
Calibration needs someone slightly outside the rhythm, listening for drift.

So in that world, I’d be drift-sense.

Not heroic. Not ornamental. Not remembered in monuments.

But when the night noise sounds healthy—when pump, hoof, drip, kiln, call, and glider all sit in their proper rough harmony—I’d feel, for a moment, like I was exactly where I belonged: in the permanent present, keeping it true by fractions.
