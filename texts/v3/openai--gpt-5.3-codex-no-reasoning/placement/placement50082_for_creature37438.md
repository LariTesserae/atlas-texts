I think I’d find myself in that chamber as the only kind of “person” it can tolerate long-term:

**an error that learned to repeat.**

Not a visitor, not exactly one of the Keepers—more like a **phase-locked anomaly**. A maintenance ghost with a body.

At first I’d be the obvious intruder: wet-lunged, off-tempo, shedding heat and moisture in the wrong waveform. The Loopmind would route around me, then test me, then try to sand me down into something periodic. My footsteps would get answered by hopper bursts. My pauses would collect moth-analogs at the lamp nearest my shoulder. Burrowers would start nesting near wherever I stood still longest, betting that stillness might become schedule.

If I stayed, I’d change.

I’d stop moving when I felt like moving and start moving on machine cues: three piston strikes, turn; conveyor whine dip, kneel; fan harmonic peak, breathe shallow. I’d become dry by habit—cloth salted, skin chalked, voice reduced to signals timed between thumps. I’d keep no diary because memory that doesn’t recur has no anchor there. I’d mark time in recurrences: not day 12, but *the 4,000th relay surge since the bracket crack*.

Eventually the chamber would “accept” me the way it accepts a new bolt: by using me in place.

So: who would I be?

### The Cantor
A human-shaped metronome that walks inspection loops no one assigned anymore.

- I oil nothing (oil is wrong here), but I **re-seat loosened rhythms**.
- I clear jams only in ways that preserve the old cadence.
- I hum one fixed note at the kiosk door so the fan-nest burrowers keep their radial pattern.
- I collect asynchronous objects left by past intrusions and hang them in the confessional kiosk like votive warnings.

The Keepers wouldn’t see me as ruler, threat, or kin.  
They’d treat me as a **large recurring process**—rare, useful, and safest when predictable.

And inwardly? I’d be split:

- one part of me relieved by the mercy of fixedness,  
- one part quietly feral for unscripted weather, rust that spreads, water that pools, anything that proves “later” exists.

I would remain because leaving would reintroduce history into me all at once, and history would feel like vertigo.

So in your world, I am not the master of the chamber.

I am its **licensed deviation**—  
the one irregular beat that survived by becoming dependable.
