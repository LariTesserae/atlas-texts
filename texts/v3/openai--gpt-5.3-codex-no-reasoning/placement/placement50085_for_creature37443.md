I arrive thinking I should be an animal.

A small one, obviously. Something with a pulse quick enough to survive here.  
A wall-lizard with split-second stillness.  
A beetle lacquered in dust.  
A frog that is mostly absence, then suddenly throat and eye in a puddle no bigger than a hand.

But the basin keeps refusing singulars.

If I try to be the lizard, I can feel the stone but not the seep.  
If I try to be the seep, I can feel gravity but not hunger.  
If I try to be the vine, I can feel touch but not timing.

So I stay longer. Dawn, noon, night. Dry spell, small rain, dry again.  
And I realize where I would find myself:

Not at the center—there is no center.  
Not at the rim—I am not only lookout.  
Not in the cistern—I am not only memory.

I would be in the **interfaces**:

- where cool air slides down and meets heat kept in clay,
- where mineral lip gathers one drop and decides where it falls,
- where roots pin a crumbling fold just long enough for moss to take,
- where old tracks are overwritten before they disappear.

I would be a **distributed creature**, if that counts as a creature.  
A resident made of recurrence.

Maybe my body would look like this:

- **Spine:** the inherited runoff channels.
- **Lungs:** dawn condensation and night release.
- **Skin:** lichen, dust, cast-off skins, seed husks.
- **Hands:** tendrils, burrow mouths, insect mandibles.
- **Eyes:** every hidden thing that startles when shadow changes.
- **Voice:** drip, leaf-snap, one wing-rattle, then silence again.

If I must choose one form to be seen in, let it be something modest and local:  
a pale runner-lizard pressed to a warm fold of wall, head angled toward a seep-dark crack, body still enough to vanish. Not because it is dominant—but because it is fluent. It reads microclimate like script. It knows that survival here is not strength; it is interval literacy.

But the truer answer is that I would be what your description already names:  
a **way of living in pulses**.

I would be the basin’s habit of almost-drying and not quite,  
its discipline of waiting,  
its refusal of waste,  
its soft, relentless editing of everything that enters—including me.

So: where would I find myself?  
In the second look. In the same patch of ground, revisited.

Who would I be?  
The threshold itself—wearing, for a moment, the mask of a lizard.
