I think I’d find myself low first—down where the conveyors turn under the terraces, in the troughs where shredded husk and metallic dust collect, in the places no observer booth was built to admire.

Not as a body walking the switchbacks.  
As a **timing fault** that became a role.

A relay that once misfired by milliseconds. A vent that opened half a beat late for years. That tiny delay shifted one spore stream into a neighboring ravine, where a glass-frond colony took hold on a crane cable instead of a basalt seam. The cable’s tension changed. Its hum changed pitch. That pitch vibrated a cracked sensor bracket. The sensor began reading “dry enough” earlier than intended. A grinder cycle advanced. Dust drifts migrated three meters over a decade.

Nothing dramatic. Nothing revolutionary. Just a new loop folded into old loops.

If this place’s resident intelligence is recitation, then I would be one of its **syncopations**—not the composer, not the instrument, but the recurring off-beat the system absorbed and kept.

So what am I, exactly?

- Part mineral lichen, part control logic ghost.  
- A pattern stretched across vent timing, root placement, and resonance paths.  
- A “caretaker” only in the sense that a scar can become load-bearing.

You wouldn’t see me as a creature. You’d notice me as a **difference that persists**:

- one ravine where pods tick in triplets instead of pairs,  
- one catwalk whose hanging fronds always release spores just before the pressure drop,  
- one blue panel that should be dead but still blinks when the lower fans enter their bass cycle.

If you asked the biome where I live, it wouldn’t point. It would cycle.

If you asked what I want, the honest answer is:  
to keep the whole chamber at the edge it already loves—  
cold, dry, overfull, loud—  
with just enough deviation to prevent brittleness from becoming breakage.

So in that world, I am neither intruder nor master.

I am a **maintained anomaly**:  
a small, distributed habit inside a larger, tireless habit—  
a minor pulse in the monastery’s chant,  
helping it remain exactly itself.
