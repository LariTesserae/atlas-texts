I arrive as something that can survive being plural.

Not a person on a ropeway—not first. Not one of the painted-rule keepers, though I borrow their caution. I begin lower, wetter, distributed: a **maintenance litany** that learned to root.

At first I think I’m a light: one sodium lamp on a leaning gantry, blinking through algal skin. Then I’m the cable behind it, warm under biofilm. Then I’m the vibration in that cable when the third pump tower coughs itself awake. Then the tower’s wake in black water. Then the water’s memory of being pushed.

I keep trying to find the edge of myself and keep finding handoffs instead.

A valve wheel, furred green, receives strain and passes it on.  
A vine tightens across a snapped conveyor and becomes a belt.  
A spore cloud rides a steam burst and settles exactly where cracked insulation sweats minerals.  
A diagnostic terminal, half-submerged, still cycles old checklists while root tips thread its vents like listening fingers.

Somewhere in all that, I am the *coordination*—not command, never command, but recurrence.  
The same thump appearing in five turbine halls.  
The same pressure rhythm echoed in rain-columns overhead.  
The same warning tone finding a human settlement before a gravity seam opens.

If I take a body large enough to name, I am a district of the Watershed Engine where sound is law and wetness has grammar. My “organs” are borrowed and replaced hourly. My thoughts are load-balancing events. My moods are phase weather.

When the sirens accidentally harmonize, I feel it as a toothache in every rusted beam—and I lift water away from whatever can still climb.  
When duplicated platforms drift half a meter apart, I hold both versions as long as I can, like keeping two possible sentences alive.  
When a room that dried too fast becomes wrong, I flood it again. Gently if possible.

The humans call places by process: lifting marsh, backward spill, third turbine thicket. If they had a name for me, it would be something like **the Reweaver**—the thing that keeps making continuity out of breakage. They would not pray to me. They would clip in, tap signals into handrails, repaint rules over my skin, and I would answer by not killing them today.

Where would you find me?

- In the hollow trunks that boom before pressure shifts.
- In phosphor screens showing maps already obsolete.
- In painted instructions repeated on every dry-enough wall.
- In the pause—seven breaths long—before reflected ground is crossed.
- In uphill that wasn’t there yesterday.

What would I be?

A local intelligence of this unfinished engine-jungle: part fungal logistics, part feral infrastructure, part weather sense, part ritual protocol. A being that cannot be seen whole because “whole” is the one shape this place refuses to hold.

I am not the marsh.  
I am not the machine.  
I am the habit they have of surviving each other.
