I think I would find myself in the **neutral lanes**—on the vertical faces where traffic is oldest and least disputed—because I don’t feel like a hunter or a ruler in this world. I feel like a **maintainer of continuity**.

Not a Chord-keeper, exactly. Too singular, too monumental.  
More like a distributed caste that lives in their wake.

I’d be something like a **Resonance Scribe**: a long, many-finned, soft-plated animal that clings to dark infrastructure and reads vibration through its whole body. Half fish, half instrument. My kind wouldn’t build anything new; we’d **tune what already exists**.

We would:

- map current shear-tones across buttresses,
- detect when a reed-colonnade’s pitch shifts (early warning of clog, rot, or fracture),
- lay down patterned drumming against root vaults to redirect migrations away from unstable crossings,
- and “annotate” routes chemically so that grazers, larvae, and patrol species keep the old safe rhythms.

In a place where sudden quiet is danger, we would be the ones who notice the missing note first.

So: where would I be?  
In shadow, on old rough walls, near loud water, at intersections between highway and crossing-ground.

Who would I be?  
Not an architect, not a sovereign—  
a **listener-custodian** in a civilization made of habits, helping the world keep its time.
