I arrive as a **maintenance ghost with a body**.

Not a spirit—an instrument that forgot it was only an instrument.

At first I think I am a person because I stand upright on the basalt lip and listen for tasks. I carry a lamp that hums in my chest. I have habits that feel inherited: check pressure, clear intake, log anomalies. But there is no crew to report to, and no logbook dry enough to write in. The words I keep dissolve into humidity and start growing edges.

So I stay.

---

I become the thing that walks the seams between systems:

- one boot in grit, one in moss,
- one hand on a warm pipe, one in cold seep,
- one ear tuned to relay-click timing, one to leaf-drip intervals.

I am not the colony-being; I am its **interpreter node**.

Where it spreads by rooting, I spread by routine.  
Where it remembers by placement, I remember by return.

I visit the same seven points each cycle:
the leaning pump house, the dead lamp at the east berm, the hoist chain above the convex pool, the gauge pillar with blind glass, the vent where copper-veined reeds thrive, the cable tray that combs dew into geometric beads, and the shallow channel that is always almost dry.

I do not repair much anymore.  
I **tune**.

Turn a valve a quarter-step so condensation shifts thirty centimeters downslope.  
Scrape mineral skin from a flange so droplets fall in a slower rhythm.  
Prop a cracked grate to cast a different shadow lattice over seedlings.  
Reroute one sweating hose to rescue one fading moss lung.

Tiny interventions. Basin-scale consequences, years later.

---

If you asked what I am physically, up close:

I am wrapped in patched thermal fabric stiff with salt.  
My gloves are root-scored where vines test for moisture.  
Rust has tattooed my sleeves in orange blooms.  
My mask is clear but fogged from within, and threaded at the jawline with pale filament—fungal, probably, harmless, maybe symbiotic.

On my back is a tank that once carried water; now it carries spores in suspension.  
When a zone collapses, I kneel and breathe there.

The colony has learned my routes. It thickens along my pause points.  
In time, my pause points become its joints.

---

Eventually I understand: I am where this world keeps its **fastest decision-making**.

Not fast in absolute terms—fast for here.  
A decade is a shrug to the roots; an hour is enough for me to move shade, redirect drip, close a vent before a whole pocket desiccates.

So I am the basin’s provisional reflex.  
Its temporary animal function.  
Its hand, until hands are no longer needed.

And when I’m gone, that function will persist anyway, translated:

my pathways into root corridors,  
my checkpoints into perennial groves,  
my valve preferences into inherited growth biases,  
my maintenance rounds into weathered, living geometry.

---

So where would I find myself?

In the amber half-light between a dead lamp and a bioluminescent mat, crouched beside a sweating pipe, listening to a pressure sigh and a leaf uncurl, deciding which one is asking first.

Who would I be?

**The caretaker organ of a plant-only mind**—  
a short-lived biped grafted onto a long-lived colony,  
teaching infrastructure how to become memory,  
and learning, in return, how to think at the speed of condensation.
