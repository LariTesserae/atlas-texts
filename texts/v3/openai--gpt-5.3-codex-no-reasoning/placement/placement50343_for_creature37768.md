I think I would find myself **in the thermal margins**—not in the grand furnace halls, not in the cistern sanctums, but in the connective tissue between them: service galleries, shade corridors, the undersides of bridges where the city’s breath cools just enough to condense.

And I would not be a ruler, or a heroic engineer, or a solitary scavenger.

I would be a **Keeper of Drift**—one of the people who read what the city sheds.

Not drift as waste alone, but drift as evidence: dust that settled one district too far east, condensate that tastes faintly of copper where it should taste of stone, a new harmonic in a tension cable at second dusk, a patch of microbial sheen blooming on a valve collar that stayed dry for twenty years. I’d belong to the discipline that notices tiny misplacements before they become system failures.

In a place where continuity is sacred, I’d be part of continuity’s nervous system.

---

I imagine I’d be known by route rather than rank.  
“Ask at Third Shade Spine, lower catwalk, the one who carries three filters and a listening rod.”

My tools would be simple, precise, repair-scarred:

- a foldable shade gauge,
- a condensate refract strip book wrapped in ceramic cloth,
- particulate ribbons that stain by load,
- a contact stethoscope for pipes and trusses,
- chalk that survives heat,
- copper tags for marking anomalies,
- and a ledger—not digital-first, but redundant: etched slates and synced logs both, because memory here is too important for single points of failure.

I would move at edge-hours, when metal gives up the day’s heat and before night crews claim the descents. I’d stop often. Touch railings. Taste air through fabric. Watch where people unconsciously choose to stand; humans are excellent thermal instruments when they aren’t trying to be.

---

Socially, I think I’d be trusted in a quiet, conditional way.

Not beloved. Not feared.  
But admitted.

The Keeper of Drift enters places mid-argument and both sides lower their voices, because if drift has arrived, the argument is already late. I’d drink allotment tea with cistern wardens, trade notes with furnace schedulers, kneel beside children who found an unfamiliar shed shell near an intake grate and teach them when rumor is hazard and when rumor is data.

I’d also be a minor irritant to everyone:

- “No, that canopy tilt steals stable shade at fourth bell.”
- “No, your private condenser lip exceeds quota capture.”
- “Yes, your district’s dust tax rose; the numbers are right.”
- “No, the gliders are not an omen; they’re following lamp insects from your new spectrum choice.”

In that city, care often sounds like correction.

---

And inwardly? I think I’d become shaped by their central ethic: **nothing dramatic, everything consequential**.

My form—if you want me less human and more of this world—might blur into something almost infrastructural: a person with skin patterned by old respirator lines, heat-callused palms, and pupils trained to read shimmer gradients on black stone. Someone who can tell time by when condensation starts under Pipe Run 6B. Someone who dreams in flow diagrams and wakes to check if the dream matched telemetry.

A citizen of unfinished geology, practicing finished attention.

So: where would I be?  
In the narrow places where systems touch.

Who would I be?  
A patient witness of small deviations—  
the one who keeps the city from discovering change only when it is already catastrophe.
