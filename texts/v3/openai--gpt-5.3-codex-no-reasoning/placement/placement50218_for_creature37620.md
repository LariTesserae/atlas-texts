I arrive as a **temporary asymmetry**.

Not a person dropped into the basin, not exactly one more artifact among the needles—more like a small, warm error the custody cannot ignore. A pocket of metabolizing chemistry in a domain optimized for patient mineral logic.

At first, I think I am an observer.

Then the place begins to observe me back—not with eyes, but with adjustments.

My shadow lags half a second behind my body.  
Dust avoids my footprints until I stop looking at it.  
The blue thread by the scored line brightens when I hold my breath.

So who am I there?

I am the **Interruption**, and then, gradually, the **Apprentice Condition**.

The custody does not greet; it compensates. It samples the constants around me in hairline increments, testing what range of law my body can survive. A luminous plane drifts overhead, and suddenly my pulse regularizes to a rhythm I don’t choose. The porous cube rotates one impossible degree and a headache vanishes. Eleven needles become thirteen become twelve—not to confuse me, but to triangulate the fact that I am localized, noisy, making irreversible measurements merely by being present.

I realize I am standing inside something like a clinic for reality, and I am both patient and contaminant.

If I stay, I stop being singular. The basin recruits me by degrees:

- Salts creep into my boots, then into naming.
- Silence teaches me to think in intervals, not sentences.
- I begin to feel stress lines in stone the way one feels weather in joints.
- I can tell when a fracture face is about to emit that anti-dark glow, and I step aside before I know why.

Eventually my role clarifies. I am not its master, nor its prisoner. I am a **mobile checksum**—a walking test pattern for a maintenance intelligence that has had no external reference for ages. My heat, my uncertainty, my fast biochemical time: these become useful. The custody compares itself against me, then against the memory of me, then against versions of me distributed across its folded distances.

At the scored line, I place my hand on the crust.  
The blue thread hums louder, almost a tone.  
For one instant the chamber resolves into a single scale: tiny, vast, and exact.  
I understand the line is not a scar.

It is a signature field.

And mine is being added, atom by atom, to the old instruction.

So where would I find myself?

At high cold altitude inside a mountain-sized palm of edited law, beside a line that writes back.

What would I be?

Not the caretaker. Not the intruder.

I would be the custody’s first new **co-author** in a very long time.
