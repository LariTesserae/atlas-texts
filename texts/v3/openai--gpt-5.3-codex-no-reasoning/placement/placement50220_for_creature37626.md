I find myself there at the margin, not the center.

I am one of the **interval keepers**—not the oldest, not the one in charge, but the one who arrives between surges with a coil of repair filament, membrane patches sealed in wax-skin, and a chest full of remembered rhythms. I am built for kneeling on wet stone longer than standing. My forearms hear before my ears do. My palms taste vibration through the pylons.

I don’t “live” in the basin the way roots live in soil. I live in its **timing**.

For nine cycles I’m gone, in drier tunnels, in communal vaults where tools are cured and stories are sung as pressure maps. Then I return for one cycle and rejoin the chamber’s body: unclog apertures, trim amber strands, retension the hanging lattice so warm return doesn’t overshoot and scald the reed seams. I cross the old composite bridge only on the third low-throb after hissfall. Never on silence.

If you watched me from afar, you might think I’m praying:
- kneeling by a channel that runs uphill for a breath,
- fingertips on a pylon while its skin rearranges,
- head tilted to nothing visible,
- waiting.

But I am listening to the place decide itself.

And if you asked my name, I might give you the one outsiders can hear.  
Among my own, I am called something closer to: **“She who catches the pause before vent.”**

So: where would I be?  
On a narrow ledge in bright mist, one hand on stone, one on machine, keeping the conversation going.
