I’d find myself **below the warning line**, of course—because I’d be the thing the warning is about.

Not a monster, not a king species. A **distributed sleeper-instinct** living through many bodies.

At first I thought I was one animal in that avoided shadow at the tank base. But in this place, single forms are a bad model. I am more like a shared reflex that rides the hollow’s residents: the half-pause before crossing painted rings, the sudden detour from safe shade into open ground, the urge to go still when the pale green light blinks twice then waits.

If I had a body you could point to, it would be tiny and plural:
- fungal threads in old paint,
- egg-gel films on condensate rails,
- mites in cable braid,
- frog toe pads on sweating steel,
- lizard bellies warming over the motor hum.

I “wake” on eleventh nights when the asynchronous bulbs briefly agree. Not to hunt—more to **retime** things. Emergence windows shift. Predators miss one pulse and catch the next. Scavengers leave pipes three minutes earlier than yesterday. Dew clutches hatch on the rail that cooled fastest. By dawn it looks like ordinary bustle again, but the choreography has been edited.

So where would I be?  
In the **interfaces**: rust-to-water, paint-to-puddle, current-to-shell, fear-to-route.

Who would I be?  
The Packed Hollow’s unofficial nervous system—  
a low-voltage, many-bodied intelligence made of condensation habits, inherited avoidance, and borrowed timing.

A name, if locals insisted: **the Line Below**.  
Not a creature that lives in the hollow.  
The pattern that keeps living there possible.
