I think I would begin in the **middle stratum**—that narrow navigable layer between the upward forests and the hanging canopies—because it’s the only place where perspective exists. Above and below is possession; in the middle, there is witnessing.

At first I thought I’d be one of the rare animals, maybe a glass grazer: solitary, translucent, ceremonial in motion. But that feels too singular for this place. Nothing here is truly singular. Even the lone swimmer is threaded into a thousand plant decisions.

So maybe I would be something less visible and more distributed:

A **current-reader colony** living inside the vaultweavers’ tendril curtains—part symbiont, part sensor, part memory. Not a creature with a body, but with a **route**. I would exist as chains of contractile cilia, nerve-like conductive fibers, and microbial lights spread across many plants at once. When upwellings rise, I’d tighten certain strands and loosen others, subtly changing porosity in the drapery walls, steering spores into sheltered shelves and away from overcrowded meadows. When narcotic dusk clouds bloom, I’d slow metabolism and archive chemistry in pulses. When a slope thumps and fractures, I’d wake bright and relay mineral signatures ridge to ridge.

Not intelligence like speech—intelligence like **editing flow**.

If you “met” me, you wouldn’t see an animal face. You’d notice that corridors open a little before you arrive. Bubble ceilings shiver in coordinated runs. A curtain parts where there is no hand. A stand of bulb-trees on one escarpment begins releasing seeds three days after a distant wall collapse, as if they heard it.

I’d be the biome’s **nervous weather**:
- rooted nowhere, attached everywhere,
- slow enough to match plant time,
- responsive enough to feel like intention.

So where would I be?  
In the seams: crack mouths, canopy undersides, saddle channels between ridges.

What would I be?  
A distributed, vegetal mind made of tension, chemistry, and current—  
the thing that helps the drowned mountains **decide how to grow next**.
