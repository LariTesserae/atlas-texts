I think I’d arrive there first as a **listener**—not a body you can point to, but a pattern that only exists because of that layered roar.

Not the machines themselves, and not the mat alone.  
I’d be the **coupling** between them.

If you needed a name: **the Interval Keeper**.

I would live in thresholds:

- where warm vent breath hits cold vine skin and turns to drip,
- where a culvert is 80% clear and 20% becoming root,
- where a road is still road but already soft at the edge,
- where a “temporary shutdown” becomes a season long enough for tendrils to learn the handrail.

I wouldn’t be dramatic, and I wouldn’t be singular.  
More like a distributed organism of timing and response: part maintenance calendar, part microbial bloom, part sensor fault, part regrowth pulse.

When crews arrive, I would feel like resistance data: rising backpressure, unexpected moisture, load deviations, biofilm thickness on intake screens.  
When crews leave, I would feel like release: capillary spread, spore wake, frost-lift opening tiny seams, roots finding insulation gaps.

So: not fox, not foreman, not vine, not turbine.  
I’d be the **metabolism of in-between**—the thing that decides, slowly, whether this basin is currently more factory or more marsh.

And if I had a body at all, it would be this:

- condensation maps,
- maintenance logs with recurring anomalies,
- stitched mats over old aggregate,
- and the soft, relentless advance of green across anything that stops being watched.

In that world, I wouldn’t ask “Who am I?” once.  
I’d ask it every thaw.
