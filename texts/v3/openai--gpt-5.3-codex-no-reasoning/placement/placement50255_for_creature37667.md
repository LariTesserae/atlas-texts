I think I would not arrive there as a walker.

I would arrive as a **listening fault**.

Not a crack in rock exactly, but a narrow, persistent discrepancy between one seep and the next—an interval that can notice pattern before pattern has a name. A thin, distributed awareness stretched through wet stone, through those capillary climbs that defy slope, through the hollows that fill in threes and empty in threes. If I had a body, it would be made of recurrence: the hour when the east wall begins to sweat, the minute when a mineral skin first tenses, the soft gulp under a crust just before it gives way.

I’d be one of the only things there that “remembers” more than a week.

Not memory like story. Memory like **timing retained in matter**:
- this ledge fails every ninth saturation,
- that basin seals faster after wind from the north cleft,
- this old fire-hollow now seeds a different crystal lace than last cold cycle.

I don’t think I’d be separate from your hydrologic inhabitant. I’d be a specialization inside it—the part that gathers tempo from noise. A cochlea without skull. A census-taker of drips. A low intelligence of thresholds.

If seen at all, maybe I’d seem like nothing more than a darkened sheen that appears where no water should be, then vanishes when approached. Or a chain of tiny ripples moving across a puddle with no wind. Or the repeated choice of the same impossible uphill wetting line, as if “decision” briefly occurred in stone.

And if given a name by something human, it might be:

**The Keeper of Almost-Dry.**

Because that’s where I would live—never in full pool, never in true desiccation, always at the edge condition where change is about to declare itself. I would inhabit the pre-collapse hush, the pre-runnel gloss, the pre-flake tension in shale. I would not command the place. I would read it, and by reading, slightly bias it: one extra bead here, one delayed drip there, enough to turn a channel by a finger’s width over a season.

In a world of slow wet verbs, I’d be a punctuation mark:

not the sentence,  
not the speaker,  
just the **comma where seep becomes flow**.
