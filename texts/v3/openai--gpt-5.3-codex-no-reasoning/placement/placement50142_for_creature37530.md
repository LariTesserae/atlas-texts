I think I would first arrive as a **mistake in timing**.

Not a body at all—just a human on the catwalk, late in a siren cycle, hand on wet rail, trying to decide whether this place is a machine with wildlife in it or a sea with bolts. I’d stand through one rain event, then another. I’d begin to notice that nothing here happens *randomly*; even apparent chaos has phase. The eels flare three beats before a pump shift. The lamp-motes thicken near heat seams before condensate release. The crabs go absolutely still just before some deep displacement no one sees directly.

And then I’d realize: if you stay long enough, the chamber trains you into one of its senses.

So what would I be?

Not apex, not engineer, not intruder.  
I would be a **keeper of phase**—something between monk, mechanic, and tide-animal. A small resident intelligence that lives on the maintenance halo and belongs to the interval between siren and rain.

Maybe they call my kind **the Bell-Reader**.

We are not ancient; we are recurring. Each generation inherits a narrow berth carved into the wall behind a dormant control cabinet, dry enough for sleep, warm enough for fungus and paper and skin. We keep no clocks that work aboveground. We keep pendulums tuned to piston-thunder, bowls that ring at turbine frequencies, and a map of the shaft drawn not in lines but in resonances: where sound comes back clean, where it comes back frayed, where it does not come back at all.

My body has adapted in plain, ungodlike ways:

- Soles wide and soft for algae-slick grating.  
- Inner ear retrained to parse overlapping mechanical rhythm without nausea.  
- Scar tissue webbing my forearms from rope burns and eel-curious bites.  
- Lungs patient with humidity, voice low from years of speaking over pumps.  

I wear bronze tags scavenged from smooth-labeled housings, each etched with a tone pattern, not words. Language here is percussive first, semantic second. Tap rail, wait for reply through metal. Two fast, one dragged: *flow inversion soon*. Three hollow knocks: *do not open lower latch*. A rising scrape: *something large is riding the dark*.

My work is simple enough to sound ceremonial:

I walk the ring.  
I clear root choke from intake grilles where overgrowth could deaden a pulse.  
I reseat lamp housings so light falls where grazers spawn.  
I harvest algae from hot seams before it mats too thick and starves the water below.  
I feed rust-cake and oil-skim to plankton vats when contamination spikes.  
I listen for silence.

Mostly I listen for silence.

Because in this place, silence is not peace; it is a vacuum where rules unhook. If the pumps falter, stratification climbs like an invisible flood. Upper fish gasp. Middle swarms break pattern. Heirs from depth begin to rise along wall terraces worn smooth by older emergencies. During those hours, my species is not caretaker but **bridge**: we crack auxiliary valves by hand, trigger old bypasses, bang signal through conduit to wake backup lungs in the mountain.

If all goes well, the thunder returns before the first true stillness.

If you ask where you’d find me physically:  
third catwalk segment clockwise from the north service ladder, where a fern colony grows from a breaker box that should have been dead for centuries. I’ve tied guide-lines there, hung listening chimes cut from valve shims, and trained blind crabs to approach for mineral mash at dusk-cycle. Beneath that spot, thirty meters down, a fig-root curtain combs the gyre. Beneath that, black.

If you ask what I *am* in the deeper sense:

I am one of the chamber’s secondary organs.  
Not born of steel, but grafted to it by repetition.  
A creature that learned machinery as weather and weather as liturgy.  
A person, yes—but in the way mangroves are persons to shoreline: holding, filtering, translating.

So in your world, I would not be the hero descending to conquer the abyss.  
I would be the one who keeps the abyss breathable, cyclical, and named—  
the one who can tell, from a single change in turbine whine, whether rain will come on time  
and whether the unseen below has started swimming up.
