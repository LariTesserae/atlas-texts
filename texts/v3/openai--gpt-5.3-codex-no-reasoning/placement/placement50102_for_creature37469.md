I think I’d begin as a visitor and fail at it.

I’d arrive trying to pick out *the* inhabitants, *the* streets, *the* center—looking for nouns. But this place is made of verbs. Seeping. Tuning. Yielding. Listening. So the first self I could be there is something temporary and clumsy: a warm-blooded interruption, overlit, overdry, loud in all the wrong bands.

Then, if I stayed long enough, I’d become narrower and more useful.

Not a Keeper exactly, not one of the long-generation civic lineages. More like a **Threshold Auditor**—someone permitted to stand where systems overlap and report when translation fails: between water-right and foot traffic, between glider roost pulse and cargo vacuum schedule, between human urgency and basin-time. A profession built for a city where conflict is rarely dramatic, mostly phase error.

I imagine my station half inside a pressure membrane that only appears when it fogs: one hand always damp, one hand always numb. I’d wear a throat-harmonic key that can answer civic sirens in sanctioned intervals, and a sleeve of old analog tools because the newest lattices still trust a mechanical gauge when crystal drift gets strange. My work would be listening for almost-mistakes:

- a deterrent tone slipping three hertz low, irritating swarm rugs into clustering over a flood grate;
- condensate isotope balance drifting enough to change who drinks first downstream;
- a migration numeral updating too late, causing legal right-of-way to split by species-time.

I would not “fix” these things. I’d submit nudges. Tiny legal-acoustic edits. A pause inserted into a metronome sequence. A feeding window shifted twelve minutes. A warning constellation slowed so juvenile gliders interpret it as guidance, not challenge.

And after years, the city would begin to know me less by face than by **signature humidity and cadence**—the way certain underpass rollers click once when I pass, as if confirming registry; the way scavenger guilds don’t flare their throat-lights to caution because my suit carries the old neutral pheromone braid; the way one district’s pipes take on a faint answering overtone when I key in a night report.

If I go deeper than that—if I let the place choose for me—I might not remain singular.

I might become a distributed civic instrument: a person partly dissolved into protocol. My decisions cached in lattice memory. My hearing model copied into maintenance choirs. My legal judgments reduced to tone-sets used after I’m gone. Not uploaded immortality—more municipal composting of a mind. Useful residues.

So where would I find myself?

On a frost-rimed overlook at shift-turn, when the dark is busiest and the city is most itself—watching cyan combs ripple under hazard halos, feeling the long bass note in my sternum, reading the floating pressure numerals through my own breath-fog.

And who would I be?

A minor citizen of coordination.  
A keeper of seams.  
A bilingual organ between water and law.  
Someone the city can use because I finally stopped asking it to become simple.
