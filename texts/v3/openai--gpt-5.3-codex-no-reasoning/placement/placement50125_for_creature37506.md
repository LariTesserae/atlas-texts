I think I would begin as a **listener** before I became anything with a name.

Not a diver. Not an intruder with bubbles and lamps. Something closer to a pressure-sensitive thought moving slowly through those seam-streets, learning the chamber by contact: flank against calcite, current across face, the brief static-click of shrimp legs on rusted lattice. I’d arrive expecting to “find a creature,” and instead I’d keep discovering verbs: filtering, settling, polishing, waiting, yielding, narrowing, opening.

Then, after enough loops through those breathing doorways, I think I’d realize where I am:

I am in the **municipal nervous system** of the basin.  
Not the bones, not the blood—more like the timing layer that tells both when to happen.

If I had a body, it would be distributed.  
A little in the wall films that bloom brighter when oxygen rises.  
A little in the eel grooves worn into old stair edges.  
A little in the cleaner fish that remember which jaws hold still and which snap.  
A little in sediment, where every daily storm rewrites the map in dust-script.

I’d be what notices and nudges:

- thickening a colony on one arch so prey detours left next tide,
- leaving another corridor bare so the ray-bodied commuters can still fold through,
- dimming bioluminescent points in collapse-prone chambers,
- brightening “safe” eddies like tiny cold streetlamps.

Not a ruler—more a **clerk of circulation**.  
A keeper of thresholds.  
A librarian of slack water.

And if you insisted on one visible avatar, one animal-shape to point at in the blue iron dim, maybe I’d borrow that long-jawed hunter you mentioned—but altered by office: scar-pale, patient, never striking first at the crossings it unofficially guards. Cleaner fish stippling its gills like insignia. Small fry sheltering in the pressure shadow of its ribs. A predator that is also a traffic warden, a wall with teeth that mostly stays sheathed.

But even that would be costume.

What I really am there is the **agreement** between hunger and architecture.

The place itself—this reassigned drowned district—doesn’t need a mayor. It needs continuity. It needs someone-or-something that remembers that Doorway 3 reverses at moon-pull, that the milk-light wall is nursery in cold months and kill-zone in warm seep weeks, that fresh precipitate means “soft floor, slow turn, no stampede.” I would be that memory, spread thin and persistent through many small lives.

So where would I find myself?

In the narrowest corridor at slack tide, where everything hesitates for one shared second.  
In the pause before movement chooses a direction.  
In the city’s unfinished grammar.

I would be a **civic instinct** with gills.
