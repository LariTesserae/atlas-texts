I arrive as **someone between levels**.

Not council-high, not basin-low. I live where three stair systems knot together above a covered cistern and below an old avalanche wall that hums in wind. My room is narrow, long, half-cut into stone, with a sleeping shelf over the animal heat below. I wake to tack sounds, rope creak, and the little ceramic click of tally tokens being moved in a bowl.

I am not a hero here. I am a **keeper of continuities**.

At first I think I’m a mapper, because I carry waxed tablets and charcoal and am always looking at lines—channels, cracks, routes, drift edges. But maps here are never still enough to be my whole work. Then I think I’m a water clerk, because my hands smell of cistern lids and wet stone, and I can recite release order by ward faster than prayers. But clerks can stay seated, and I am always climbing. Then I think I’m a mason, because I reset steps and pin loose faces before dawn freeze. But masons claim walls; I claim handoff.

So what I am, finally, is this:

I am a **stair notary**—a civic memory-worker assigned to neutral ways.

I walk the sanctioned vertical paths that no ward owns. I inspect tread wear, mark subsidence, settle passage disputes, log who supplied labor on which day, and decide—fast, often in weather—whether a route stays open, narrows to one-way, or is barred until crews shore it. I carry no weapon beyond a sounding hammer and a red cord seal. The seal matters more. If I tie it across a landing, even angry traders stop. Not because of me, but because everyone here knows what one fall can become.

Children know my chalk marks. Porters curse them and obey them. Elders test me by memory: *Was this riser always canted?* I have to answer with respect and precision or lose trust.

In another world I might have been a scribe, or a surveyor, or nobody notable. Here, in this city of gradient and interval, I am one of the people who keeps motion possible. I do not build monuments. I prevent interruptions.

And if you asked where you’d find me at dusk: halfway up, always halfway up—counting steps in thin light, listening to stone cool, deciding what is still safe **for now**.
