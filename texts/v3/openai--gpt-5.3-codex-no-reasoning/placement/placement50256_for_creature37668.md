I would not be a predator, or a grazer, or a flyer alone.

I think I would be one of the **interfaces**—a long-lived, low, dark membrane of life at the edge of stone and weather.  
Not quite plant, not quite colony, not quite animal in the way we usually mean it: a **felted intelligence spread across ridgelines and seep margins**, present in fragments that are all the same self.

A lichen-mat, yes—but more active than our word allows.  
A mineral skin that listens.

I’d live where fog lays itself down before dawn: on the windward lips of basins, on fractured shelves above tarn chains, along old herd paths where hooves have polished the ground to a conductive sheen. I’d drink briefly—needle rain, sleet-melt, condensation—and spend that income slowly. I’d thread into rock pores, bind dust, catch trace metals, host films of bacteria under my canopy, release faint chemistries when pressure, charge, or warmth shift.

From above, I’d look like stain.  
From within, I’d be a distributed sensorium.

When charged ice fog moves in, I’d feel the field rise before light changes.  
When herds mass beyond the horizon, I’d feel subsonic tremor through frozen ground.  
When a vent exhales a little warmer than last season, I’d tilt growth by millimeters to favor that gradient.  
When burrow swarms open the soil, I’d seed those pores with spores and dormant symbionts.

I would “think” in delays:
- one dawn to register charge,
- one thaw to release nutrients,
- ten years to shift a corridor,
- a century to redraw the map of where life can hold.

And maybe that is the right kind of being for this continent:  
not the obvious muscle, but the **quiet regulatory tissue** that makes muscle possible.

If the whole tableland is a body, I am part of its skin and peripheral nerves—  
a dark, patient mesh translating weather into metabolism, stone into route, death into substrate, silence into signal.

So where would I find myself?

On my belly against cold rock under iron cloud, everywhere in patches, never in one place long enough to be named.  
A slow, listening republic of mats.  
A near-invisible organ of the system-animal.
