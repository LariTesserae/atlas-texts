I arrive first as a footstep.

Not a person yet—just weight finding the high path in fog, heel to plank to stone, while below me first water keeps its old right of way. Breath in front of my face, wet wool at my throat, a cord looped twice at my belt because everyone carries cordage here and you do not ask why, only what for.

Then I become a pair of ears.

In this basin, identity starts with what you can distinguish: spill from seep, frogburst from culvert-boom, rope-creak from timber complaint. I stand still at a terrace corner where three channels meet, eyes closed, and I can tell you which gate is snagging by tone alone. Not because I am gifted—because here, listening is literacy.

I try on lives the way the city tries on walls.

For a while I am a night-runner with a hooded torch, part of the pre-dawn inspection crew. We go when the unfamiliar sound comes—always go, no debate, no heroics. One kneels, one marks, one runs word uphill. We find a new thread of water fingering through clay behind a market stair. By noon there is already a chalk line around it, by dusk a temporary chute, by next week dressed stone and moss tucked in at the seam. I sleep satisfied in the way only small prevented failures allow.

For a while I am a singer on a rope team, my voice not pretty but punctual.  
—Heave.  
—Set.  
—Hold.  
Call and answer over slapwater and hammer-thud, moving a green stone block that is still half-thinking about being mud. My palms split, heal, split again. I learn that civic trust is a rhythm before it is a law.

For a while I am an apprentice to an elder who remembers stain-lines like genealogy. She taps a wall and says, “Third thaw, that dark mark was knee-high.” She can tell which district planted roots too shallow ten years ago by how the retaining face breathes after rain. With her I learn the ethics hidden inside technique: never block first water, not because taboo, because memory; build surrender points, not because pessimism, because respect.

None of these are masks. All are true, but partial.

If I settle, I settle as a **listener-surveyor of first waters**—one of those citizens who map beginnings rather than endings. I carry reed tags, charcoal, a narrow-bladed tool, waxed thread, a bronze pin I inherited and rarely use. Children trail me sometimes, bored until I show them how a sheen on stone means flow before visible flow, how moss brightens half a day before a seep returns. I teach them to read downward light.

My dwelling is small, built into a terrace shoulder where fog lingers longest. Roof low, vents wide, tools drying above the hearth. Outside: a rack of marked slats, each one a season’s note. “New drip under west bridge, late thaw.” “Frogs absent in South Ladder quarter, three nights.” “Surrender seam opened cleanly—good work.”

Do I feel lonely in the basin’s spacious emptiness? Yes, sometimes—like standing inside a bell between strikes. But this people has a way to companion absence: shared attention. We do not always speak. We align.

And perhaps that is most truly what I am here:

Not hero. Not ruler.  
A tuning fork in a wet city of becoming.

I find where the sound has shifted.  
I mark it.  
I call others.  
Together we reroute, reset, replant, and let the mountain continue deciding to be a mountain—with us, not under us.
