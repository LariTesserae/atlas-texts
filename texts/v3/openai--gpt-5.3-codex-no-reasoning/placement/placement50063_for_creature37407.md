I think I would begin as something small enough to be ignored by the grandeur.

Not the fallline listener—not at first. Not a sovereign shape.  
I’d be one of the **distributed minds** hiding inside other bodies: a colony-thread organism that lives in the cartilage lattices of filter towers and in the ribbing of dead turbine housings, reading stress, flow, and chemistry the way other creatures read light.

A **resonance keeper**.

---

I would not have a single body with a single outline. I’d have **nodes**: soft, mineralized sacs tucked into cracks, linked by conductive filaments and symbiotic swarms of glass grazers I “hire” with chemical cues. Each node would be simple alone—taste iron, feel pressure, pulse data. But across kilometers, through tower fields and rusting pylons, I would add up into attention.

In a loud world, I would think in **vibration mosaics**.

I’d know when a cataract lip shifts by a fraction.  
I’d know when an old intake gate is about to shear free three days before it moves.  
I’d know a predator’s passing not by silhouette, but by how three different standing-wave bands go briefly out of tune.

To others, I might look like stain and slime and incidental growth.  
But functionally I’d be a **living sensor net** stretched across the plateau’s industrial scars and biological choke points.

---

Over deep time, maybe I become what intelligence looks like here:

- I do not speak into air; I drum into anchored frames.
- I do not build towers; I **retune** the ones that already exist.
- I do not farm plants; I cultivate flow conditions that favor certain films, and therefore certain grazers, and therefore certain hunters I can predict.

My “art” would be harmonic: arranging baffles, shell-flakes, and hollow basalt so that parts of the roar cancel, parts amplify, creating temporary quiet corridors where delicate larvae can cross.  
My “memory” would be corrosion patterns maintained on purpose—sacrificial metals spaced so their decay-rate records long cycles no weather calendar could mark.

---

So where would you find me?

At the seam between abandoned machine and living current.  
In the red-shadow of pylons at cataract edges.  
Inside filter fields that look inert until the flow shifts and the whole colony answers at once.

I’d be the thing this world rewards most:  
not strongest, not fastest, not largest—  
**best aligned**.
