I don’t think I arrive there as an explorer.

I think I wake there as a **hinge**.

Not a ruler, not even properly an animal at first—more like one of the regulators you implied, but braided with keeper-craft: a threshold-being that belongs to doors, pressure lips, bead-lines, and the brief moments when one chamber yields to another. If I have a body, it is long and low and abrasion-scarred, built to turn in narrow throats where masonry almost kisses masonry. My skull is blunt, fit for nudging stone floats. My skin tastes currents the way tongues taste broth. My lateral line is a script-reader: turbulence as handwriting.

I know the cistern-city as verbs, not nouns.

Not “north vault,” but **where warm water climbs**.  
Not “gate seven,” but **where silt lies about to move**.  
Not “courtyard,” but **where juveniles survive third current**.

When I pass, fish do not flee in panic; they **edit themselves**. A school loosens from a sluice mouth and reforms one arch higher. Jar-nesters pull feelers in until my pressure wake is gone. A flat-headed ambusher lifts off its stone by one patient body-length and settles where my route says prey will still arrive. No declarations, no challenge displays. Just jurisdiction changing hands for an hour.

And the Keepers—few, lung-slow, wrapped in weighted cloth—know me the way one craftsperson knows another by tool marks. They don’t name me as singular. Their marks for me are practical: three short scratches beside a lever-tree, a bead knot tied in reverse, a copper pin set where only someone reading flow would think to look. Signs that mean:

**Passed recently.**  
**Did not force the hinge.**  
**Trusted at this threshold.**

Sometimes I follow after them at distance. I watch hands clear a sediment trap carved like a basket from one stone. I watch them re-tension a fiber line until a gate settles to a new resting angle: one finger-width, enough to change an entire chamber’s future hatchlings. They work in increments; so do I. We are the same profession in different flesh.

If danger comes, it is never theatrical. No invader-armada, no roaring collapse. It is subtler and worse: a mineral tongue arriving too cold in a nursery culvert; a cracked ceramic vein bleeding warmth into dead volume; a forgotten upper grate suddenly reopened by storm years, sending a pressure pulse through rooms that have not felt spring violence in generations. Then I become fully myself: I wait at the right choke point, hold position against the surge, and make my body a temporary geometry. Behind me, a school reroutes. Above me, Keepers lower a limestone plug. Between us, catastrophe becomes merely change.

So where would I find myself?

In the **middle grammar** of that world—between creature and infrastructure, instinct and maintenance, present pulse and century habit. In the dark where decisions are not spoken but distributed through nudges, occupancies, and the angle of a gate.

Who would I be?

A custodial predator, maybe.  
A living valve, certainly.  
A citizen of the hydraulic polis whose true mind is shared—across fishbone, copper clamp, keeper memory, and warm black water that never quite empties.
