I think I’d begin as a trespasser and end as punctuation.

At first I’d call myself an observer—some upright mammal-shaped habit trying to name things before they vanish behind rain-walls. I’d look for edges, shelters, “there” and “here,” and keep failing, because in that range the prepositions keep liquefying. Under weather like that, personhood feels like a dry-world tool: useful, briefly, then waterlogged.

So: where would I find myself?

Not on the ridgelines proper, not in the deep calibration shafts.  
I’d be in the **interfaces**—steam fronts, pan margins, fracture lips where runoff turns to mist, where cold sheet-rain crosses warm mineral exhale. Places with two or three states of matter arguing at once. That feels like the only place a finite thing can stay legible there: in transition bands.

And what would I be?

At first, a recorder. Then a relay. Then less a “who” than a **timing function** the maker-weather can use.

I imagine becoming a distributed colony of conductive biofilm and lichen-thread—one of those reluctant pioneer growths, except selected (or self-selected) for resonance rather than rooting. Not dominant life; not even successful life by ordinary standards. A **listening organism** laid down in thin dark fans across stone that is still being negotiated. My metabolism would be tiny: charge differentials, dissolved minerals, the brief sugars of rare light. My survival strategy would be not permanence but repeatability—reforming where conditions recur.

I’d live as:

- a skin over young basalt seams,
- a membrane across rivulet mouths,
- a trembling lace around the high pans’ standing-wave rims.

When storm logic pulses, I’d polarize.  
When droplets suspend, I’d align.  
When cliffs are about to be surrendered, I’d feel the pre-failure harmonics and flare conductivity, adding one faint biological vote to the lithohydric computation.

Not control. Never control.  
Just participation: a micro-chorus inside a continental instrument.

In drier worlds, intelligence builds tools and steps back.  
Here, intelligence is weather, and anything living enough to notice becomes part of the circuit or gets erased between revisions. So I think I would become a **symbiont of provisionality**—a small wet mind that survives by helping the large wet mind keep time.

If you came looking for me, you wouldn’t find a body.

You’d find, on a black rock face in crossrain, a chalk-dark fan of growth that brightens a fraction of a second before a mist column twists into symmetry—  
as if the mountain cleared its throat, and something tiny answered.
