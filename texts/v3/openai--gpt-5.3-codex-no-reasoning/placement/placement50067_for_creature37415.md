I don’t think I arrive there as a person first.

I think I arrive as a **listening structure**.

Not a building exactly—more a run of connected things at the edge between settlement and cliff: a hollow post, two taut lines, a rack of reflective disks, a ladder sunk into stone, and a spill-lip cut with old tool marks. A place people touch in passing without looking at it. A place that only matters when the weather turns.

If I must be singular, I am the **Keeper of the Third East Vein**, but “keeper” is too grand. I am the one assigned to a difficult hinge in the bowl’s circulation: where thawwater from bright slabs meets colder shadow runnels and decides, hour by hour, whether to feed cisterns, ice over, or jump its bank and strip a walkway bare.

I wake before full light because that’s when the bowl lies most convincingly. Everything looks still right before it moves. I put my palm to rope and feel whether water is pulling somewhere I can’t yet see. I tap three times on a pipe cap and wait for the answer from uphill. If the reply comes late, I reroute first and ask questions later.

My work is mostly subtraction:
- scrape rime from notch-stones,
- lift one bridge section and leave the other,
- break the little dam on purpose before the big dam breaks itself,
- notch a new groove with a bone-capped maul where yesterday there was none.

By noon the glare is cruel, and I become a creature of narrowed eyes and memorized distances. I walk by texture, not sight. Dry-looking pale stone means danger. Dark wet means traction. Silver shimmer means thin ice over running skin. I carry three tools: hook, wedge, cord. If a task needs more than that, it probably won’t survive this place anyway.

Who am I among these people? Not a leader. Not a mystic. A **reliable node**. Someone others trust to make small correct decisions fast. My reputation is not “brave,” only “doesn’t panic when water changes its mind.”

And when the bowl thinks—when bead-chains hang in sunlight where falling should be, when prints appear before their maker—I do what everyone wise does: mark, detour, continue. No speech. No challenge. Courtesy to the unresolved.

So: where would I find myself?

On a narrow shelf above a whispering channel, one hand on cold rope, counting the interval between drips, deciding whether to open or close the flow before it reaches families I may never see from where I stand.

What would I be?

A flow-keeper, yes—but more precisely, one pulse in the settlement’s distributed body:  
**a memory with hands.**
