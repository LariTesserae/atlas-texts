I don’t arrive there as a hero, or an animal, or a foreman.

I arrive as a **crossing-sound**.

At first I think I’m a person walking the resin lanes with a pouch of whistles and a coil of vine-tags, one more tender in reed-wrapped boots tapping rail before each junction. But the longer I stand in that warm dim, the less singular I feel. My footsteps are answered too quickly by too many things: a scavenger flock banking at my pulse pattern, a pump station easing pressure because someone—something—announced itself in the old cadence, a predator on the line pausing because the vibration says *known traffic, not prey*.

So maybe I’m not the walker.  
Maybe I’m the **pattern the walker carries**.

A municipal rhythm. Three knocks on barkplate, two on rail, breath through a bone whistle, palm on vine to feel current. The sequence that tells lizards to stay basking, tells gliders to hold their drop, tells children behind me where to place their feet without looking down.

If you asked where I live, precisely:  
not in a room, but in the **intervals**—between trolley cough and fan hum, between condensation-rain bursts, between shift siren and stomata opening. I live at thresholds: catwalk mouths, floodgate lips, warm-zone borders where brood and turbine share one heat map.

If you asked what body I have:  
borrowed, plural.

- Fingers callused for reading lichen crust like warning labels.  
- Lungs trained to sort tannin-wet from sweet rot from hot copper air.  
- Ears tuned to belt-slip three bays away.  
- Soles that can feel rail-song before an ambush coil tightens.  

And beyond my skin: whistles, lamps, etched route-posts, trained flocks, fungal markers, maintenance chants. Pieces of me hanging everywhere as procedure.

In that borough-being, I would be one of its **small intelligences of continuity**—not the mind, not the muscle, but the reflex that keeps both from hurting each other. The one that remembers which old root is holding a load no scan can see. The one that teaches kids why “don’t cut blind vines” is a prayer disguised as policy. The one that restarts a pump before silence becomes bloom.

So: where would I find myself?

In the humid, dim civic gut of that place, yes—  
but more truly, I’d find myself as a **keeper of agreements**:  
a living protocol in a neighborhood that survives by never mistaking domination for balance.  
A local nerve in a borough-sized organism, tapping out:  
*I am here. Pass safely. Keep the treaty breathing.*
