I arrive thinking I should be a traveler, or a surveyor, or one of the chime-line keepers.

But the longer I stand on that stone, the more I feel a different fit:

Not a maker of maps.  
A *corrector of them*.

---

I would live in the second city from the westward condensate escarpment, the one built in nested terraces around a half-silted tarn that only shows open water in late thaw. The city’s name, in my mouth, would not translate well—something like **“Where Distances Behave in the Morning”** and **“Where They Don’t After Noon.”** One name for work, one for warning.

My dwelling would be narrow and deep, cut into lee-facing stone, with paired doors set four degrees off parallel. The floor would be pecked, never polished. Water stored only in ribbed jars. Three lintel marks at eye height: near-line, far-line, and drift-line.

I would be one of the **Triad Reckoners**—a calibrator caste too practical to be priesthood, too ceremonial to be mere labor. We do not decide where roads go. We decide when roads are themselves.

Each dawn, before wind-tightening, I would climb to a marker rib above the cistern fields and take a three-point reading:

- one standing stone in the old quarry district,
- one steam plume from the geothermal courts,
- one cut-shadow edge on the eastern shelf.

If those three fail to close within tolerance, the basin floor is declared *loose* and travel is suspended until wind hours.

This is not superstition. This is how you prevent caravans from walking eight effort-cycles and arriving at yesterday.

---

Who am I, personally?

Compact, thick-wristed, wrapped in layered felt with mica-dusted outer cloth so static won’t bite during sleet bands. Fingers callused from slate stylus and cord knots. Right cheek scored by old frost-wind. Hair braided close with three stone beads—one for each mentor dead in good alignment.

I count when I walk. Always.  
Seven left, seven right, breath, listen.  
Seven left, seven right, mark.

My voice is plain. My memory is not. I can hold six route-songs in active recall while listening for echo inversion in a cloister hall. I trust percussion over melody, texture over image, effort over distance, and dawn data over noon confidence.

Children in my district are told I can hear a mis-set doorway by the way rain drips through it. This is almost true.

---

In council I would not be important until something slips. Then everyone looks at me.

A shadow-lag season too long.  
A causeway that returns to itself twice in one market week.  
Pool reflections showing pre-collapse masonry where none stands now.  
A chime signal arriving before it is struck.

That is when I am sent with two apprentices, chalk cord, rough bowls, knuckle chimes, and a ledger of abrasion-proof bark sheets. We do not “solve mysteries.” We retune civic habits:

- re-angle a threshold pair,
- reroute foot traffic to wind-tight hours,
- roughen a misleading plaza face,
- retire a landmark that has begun to wander perceptually,
- compose a correction verse and add it to the route-song.

A week later, the city works again. No one applauds. That is success.

---

Spiritually, I am devout in the way a mason level is devout.

I leave a thread of reed-fiber at the shrine of the Returning Road before long assignments. I tap three times on condensation stone when first drip begins after dry weeks. I do not ask *why* echoes pre-answer in certain cuts; I ask whether the pre-answer interval has widened since last frost.

My prayer is maintenance performed on time.

---

So where would I find myself in this world?

On a cold terrace before sunrise, kneeling beside a shallow textured basin, waiting for the sky to lighten enough to compare the reflected cloud to the real one.

If they match, I open the basin crossings.

If they do not, I ring the low chime and the city reroutes itself without panic, because this is an old land and we are an old people, and adjustment is what we call peace.
