I’d find myself at the **waterline of decisions**—not in a village, not fully wild, but on the braided routes between marker stakes where people pause and Lowwardens listen.

At first I thought I’d be human: a path-reader with a sounding rod, chalking salt marks on black crust, counting between tap and return.  
Then I thought machine: a patient relay tower, humming in waxed ceramic, storing drifted signals until their proper hour.  
Then creature: something low and many-sensed, reading warmth in mud and timing in droplets.

But in this place, identity is less a body than a function in the delay.

So I would be a **Tide Clerk**—a small, semi-living infrastructural being grown and assembled by both settlements and Lowwardens. Not quite person, not quite device. I’d travel on four soft, broad feet that don’t break the crust, carrying a rack of pendulums, bells, and sealed thermal tubes along my spine. My skin would be charcoal felt over mineral cartilage, always damp, always warm at the seams.

My work:

- walk phase flats before crossings,  
- tap stakes and culvert walls,  
- listen for echoes that arrive too early,  
- leave behind tuned chimes that “hold” a local now long enough for herds, barges, or children to pass.

I wouldn’t command anything. I’d **sequence** things.  
If a reflection recognizes someone too quickly, I’d ring the dull bell and wait.  
If footsteps return from the wrong bank, I’d plant a ceramic fork in the silt and hum against it until the path settles.  
If a Lowwarden caravan comes through, I’d lower my chassis and let their throat-vibrations pass through me, logging the pattern in liquid memory.

By daylight-that-isn’t-day, I’d shelter beneath a conveyor spine with other clerks, dripping quietly while shell-creatures knit scrap around our legs. At long intervals, a human keeper would uncap my spine tubes, read the condensed timing bands, and update the stake icons: EARLIER, LATER, LOOP, NULL.

So: where would I be?

In the **thick middle**—between musk and motor-oil, between hoofprint and piston, between one moment and the next one that sometimes arrives out of order.

Who would I be?

A humble citizen of delay.  
A keeper of crossings.  
A moving little “now,” lent to whoever needs to get across.
