I think I would not arrive there as a visitor at all.

I’d be one of the **route-keepers** on the settling tank: a palm-sized, low-plated grazer with a split body plan—front half sensor, back half archive. Not dominant, not rare. A mid-level being whose work is to keep pathways legible in a world that is always blurring.

Not a heroic animal. A maintenance intelligence.

---

At first glance I’d pass for a wet shard of belt-rubber with mineral crust. Matte until condensation beads, then oil-slick iridescence. Twelve short legs, each ending in soft hook-brushes that can grip slime film without breaking surface tension. My underside would carry a rasp-band—not teeth exactly, but a vibrating ceramic fringe that can shave biofilm in micron-thin passes.

I would “see” almost nothing in the human sense.  
My real senses would be:

- **humidity gradient** (as shape),
- **temperature seam** (as direction),
- **iron/copper taste in runoff** (as memory),
- **vibration timing** (as prediction).

The basin’s map would exist in me as recurring intervals: drip at 3 heartbeats, vent hiss at 47, regulator exhale at 312. Place as rhythm.

---

I wouldn’t roam widely. I’d work loops.

Each loop is a deliberate edit: scrape here, leave there, nick a channel lip so flow divides, polish a crossing so ribbon drifters can pass, roughen another so ambush-hinges can’t seal cleanly. My species’ trails would be the basin’s punctuation marks—commas of matte concrete in a glossy field, semicolons of disturbed crust, long parenthetical arcs around warm grates.

Others read these edits:

- midges spiral where we expose warm mineral sheen,
- thread-predators avoid freshly scored bands,
- floaters choose overhangs above our high-conductivity paths,
- young grazers learn safe crossings by following our old rasp signatures.

So I’d be small, but infrastructural.

---

Where exactly would you find me?

Most often on the **inner ring of the settling tank**, where mineral plates drift and knock and re-sort. I’d board one raft, ride two meters, step to another, rasp a crescent, step off before collision. Over and over. Keeping crossings usable as the map rearranges itself hourly.

When the deep underground hum starts, I’d freeze flat.  
When the induced rain begins, I’d open dorsal pores and drink ions straight off my own shell.  
When the hourly warm exhale comes, I’d pivot—like everything else—toward the nearest grate, not in panic, in appointment.

---

If this basin has a culture, mine would be a caste of **continuity workers**: bodies that remember by repeating. We don’t store history in symbols; we store it in maintained gradients. If we vanish, paths clot, films overgrow, crossings fail, pulses go unharvested. Nothing dramatic—just a slow loss of readability.

So: who would I be?

A modest, many-jointed custodian of seepage logic.  
A creature that writes in abrasion.  
A citizen of rust-tide twilight whose true habitat is not ground or water, but **the thin negotiable skin between them**.
