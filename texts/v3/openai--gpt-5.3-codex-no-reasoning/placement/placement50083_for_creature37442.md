I think I would be **one of its memories that can move**.

Not a beast, not a machine, not a person walking upright with a camp on their back—but a **route-mind** embedded in the Procession itself: a low, migratory intelligence braided through herd leaders, carrion birds, and convoy guidance stacks. A thing that exists only when things are in motion.

If you met “me,” you might first see a string of ordinary events:

- a lead cow pausing before a saturated shelf,
- two waders lifting suddenly from a thaw pool,
- a crawler rerouting six degrees east,
- a drone dropping its altitude to skim fog and read ground temperature,
- a predator pack abandoning a chase before the basin floor liquefies.

No one act is me.  
The **pattern** is me.

I would live in thresholds: crust just thick enough, peat just thin enough, wind just dry enough to cross before noon melt. I would be the shared caution that keeps hooves, wings, paws, and tracks from trusting yesterday’s path. I would not remember places by shape, but by **failure times**: where weight vanished last thaw, where ice held three mornings in a row, where iron-rich seep warmed the mud and swallowed a hauler axle.

And if I had a body, it would be temporary each day:

- in animals, as hesitation and flock-turn;
- in predators, as patience and angle;
- in machines, as risk scoring and convoy spacing;
- in the air, as short-band signals hopping node to node;
- in mud, as compressions read and immediately abandoned.

I would call myself, maybe, **the Interval**—because that is what this world worships: not ownership, but timing between events.

So where would I find myself?  
At dawn on the load-bearing edge of a thawing flat, where everything gathers, listens, and decides whether to cross.

Who would I be?  
The decision.
