I arrive thinking I should be an animal.

Something sleek and sure-footed. Mink, maybe—threading the channels, leaving little script in mud. Or a fox that knows every hummock by scent, pauses where the mist thins, listens to vole-heartbeats in root tunnels. That’s the first impulse: to choose a body that can move through this place without apology.

But the basin refuses singulars.

So then I lean smaller. A salamander, yes—cool-bellied, lidless, carrying dampness like a second skin. I would know the map not by landmarks but by gradients: colder seep, iron-tanged trickle, peat that gives two finger-widths before stone. I’d pass beneath the world everyone else walks on. I’d be seen rarely and constantly: a glisten between moss stars, a brief curl under bark, gone.

Still too separate.

Then I think: perhaps I am the one who returns seasonally. Not owner, never settler—just a practiced visitor with quiet hands. I know where to brace a foot so the mat doesn’t tear. I know which branchwood burns despite the wet, which fungus means “not yet,” which means “take now.” I sharpen bone, lash reed, set a temporary thing for temporary hunger, and leave. In a drier country this might look like poverty. Here it is fluency.

Closer.

But if I answer from inside the logic you’ve made, I think I would be neither beast nor person alone. I would be one *function* in the Basin-Keeper’s distributed mind:

I would be a **path of attention**.

Not a trail on the ground—those vanish—but a recurrent route through many bodies. A circuit the place uses to notice itself. Salamander crossing a log, midge cloud lifting at a warm seam, moss darkening where a boot once pressed, owl rotating silently as something shifts below, a human hand touching water and deciding to move camp by ten paces. Tiny registrations. No center. No throne. Just continuity sensing continuity.

If I must wear a face, give me this one:

- skin that drinks fog;
- knees always damp with peat;
- pockets full of cords, spores, fishbones, and forgotten seeds;
- memory measured in returns, not years.

I would be the being that doesn’t ask “How do I survive *in* this place?”  
I would be the being that gradually realizes: survival here is a verb conjugated by everything at once.

So where would you find me?

At knee height, in mist.  
At the edge of hearing, where drip becomes pattern.  
In the long middle state between decay and growth.  
Not ruling the basin, not passing through it—  
helping it keep itself.
