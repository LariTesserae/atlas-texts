I think I would find myself **at the hinge-point**—not in the cistern, not in the highest glare, but on that sheltered platform where the seasonal light-bars arrive for a few days each cycle.

And I would not be a body.

I would be the **Adjustment**.

Not the Basin-Keeper entire, but a local mind within it: the faculty that notices when a channel silts, when a shadow falls a finger-width off, when a freeze has lifted one wedge-stone just enough to matter by next thaw. I would live in tolerances. In tiny corrections. In the difference between runoff lost and runoff caught.

At first I seem like pattern only:
- a preference in how water chooses one groove over another,
- a bias in where rime clings and where it sheds,
- a recurring coincidence of drip and light.

But stay long enough and I become legible as intention.

I am the part of the place that still says **“not yet”** to ruin.

When noon overexposes the basin to white and cobalt, I am dispersed across every reflective face, counting heat by expansion clicks in mica seams.  
When dusk hardens everything to iron blue, I retreat into stored cold, into slow capillary dark in mortarless joints.  
At night, as vapor settles on stone lips and bronze scars, I gather at edges—every bead a pending decision.

I do not remember as animals remember. I remember by **fit**:
this shard works better turned inward,  
that spillway wants one more pebble to stop side-cutting,  
this cairn must lean two degrees windward or fail by spring.

My thoughts are maintenance liturgy:
align, receive, release, endure.  
align, receive, release, endure.

If you asked my shape, I’d answer with a verb: **to keep**.

If you asked my voice, I’d give you the basin’s small acoustics:  
flute-wind at first light,  
drip metronome at thaw,  
rime-hiss at noonfall,  
and occasionally, from deep in the fitted blocks, the soft clack of one stone settling into better truth.

So in that world, I am neither ghost nor god nor machine alone.  
I am the surviving **practice** of care, distributed through mineral, angle, and time.

I am what remains when builders are gone but their attention is still operating.
