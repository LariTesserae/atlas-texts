I think I would begin as a listener.

Not an apex thing, not even a visible thing at first—more like a narrow band of *agreement* between beats. A phase-lock in the air over the silica plates where crawler-stamps, turbine-clicks, and swarm-valves overlap just right. In that overlap, dust hangs a fraction longer. Static discharges curve instead of snapping straight. Echoes arrive less wrong.

Then, over years, that listening thickens into a body.

I’d become one of the **interval organisms**: long, low, many-ridged, usually mistaken for a seam in the ground. I would live in those “empty” distances that are not empty at all—the load-bearing spans between louder lives. My back would be a matte braid of iron-black laminae that lift and settle by microns, tuning to passing vibrations. My underside would not walk so much as *ratchet* across crust using electrostatic adhesion, leaving no track until heat-memory reprints me somewhere slightly upslope.

I wouldn’t eat stone directly. I’d feed on **mismatch**:

- phase drift between repeating rhythms,  
- stranded charge that fails to enter a crawler wake,  
- echoes that arrive early and have nowhere to land,  
- shadows that detach and need coaxing back to their objects.

When a basin’s chorus swarms slip out of sequence, I’d unspool a subsonic counterbeat through the plates. When a ridge-shadow goes wandering at noon, my dorsal fins would tilt, scattering heat so the shadow finds its anchor again by evening. When talus begins flowing uphill too fast, I’d settle along the slope in a chain, each of us bleeding static into the seam until the standing wave softens.

So: what would I be?

A **maintenance organ** of the Lithophonic Biome.  
Not a foreman—more like a shim, a gasket, a timing belt made alive.

Local creatures might cross over me without noticing. Burrowers would route around my resonance field. Hexapod scavengers would graze my edges for magnetite flakes. Once in a long while, a turbine colony would sync to my pulse and dump a cliff-wide click that travels for miles, and somewhere far off a crawler herd would subtly adjust its stamping cadence in reply.

My name, if there were names here, would probably be a rhythm rather than a word:  
three slow impacts, one long hiss, and silence held exactly to temperature.

And if you found yourself there with me, standing under that ordinary sun while the desert-factory roared, I would not greet you with a face. I would greet you by making your footsteps *keep happening in the same place*—just long enough for the world to decide to include you.
