It is a pocket below the surface, hidden in the way a single dark thought hides beneath a living root system. There is no great approach to it, no valley leading inward, no road or ruin marking the way. The land above gives little warning: a warm mat of vegetation, taut with growth, and a small failure in it where the ground seems to exhale. If you stand there, you are looking downward into an opening that may be no larger than a room, perhaps smaller, perhaps strangely larger depending on which leaf-surface you choose to trust. It is a contained place, a single feature embedded within the world, and yet once you are inside it, it behaves like a complete environment with its own weather, its own logic, and its own impossible light.

The first strange thing is the light. It is normal light. Not glow, not phosphorescence, not the sickly shimmer of a cavern. It has the color and crispness of ordinary daylight: white-gold, even, casting clean shadows from leaf edges and root fibers. It feels as though you are standing under an open sky, except the surface is above you, sealed with soil, roots, and plant tissue. There are no visible windows. There is no sun. The light simply belongs to the place, as if the pocket has borrowed the visual quality of afternoon from somewhere outside its walls. It falls evenly across the living surfaces and makes every vein, every thread of moisture, every pale animal skin look vivid and present.

It is below the surface, but it is not cold. The air is warm in a close, breathing way, around the temperature of skin or the shaded underside of a leaf in the middle of a humid day. This warmth keeps everything active. The place does not settle. It works, shifts, drinks, releases, cracks open, closes again. Nothing here has reached the quiet balance of an ancient landscape.
