## The Hollow Shelf

It is high enough that the sky stops looking like air. Below the shelf, a gray ocean of cloud presses itself into the world’s lower folds; above it, the atmosphere thins to a black dome where starlight is almost the only thing that remembers distance. The terrain is not a summit. It is a tableland, broad and open, laid across the upper shoulder of the world like the floor of a hall whose roof has been taken away. From one low horizon to another, the land holds itself with a kind of exhausted patience: terraces, basins, faulted lips of rock, dry channels, shallow frost flats, and dark pockets of meltwater that appear where the ground exhales heat.

The shelf extends over a region large enough for multiple climates, multiple ground textures, and multiple silences, yet it never feels endless. A vent field may sit a kilometer from a moss basin; a glassy basalt terrace may curve around a basin of iron-stained rime; a ridge of shattered shale may vanish into a flat so open that the eye finds no place to rest.
