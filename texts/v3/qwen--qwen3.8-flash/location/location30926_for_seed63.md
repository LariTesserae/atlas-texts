## The Salt-Vault

It sits at the top of the world like a wound that has healed into a room.

The outer plateau is high enough that the air feels thin and strange in the lungs, as though each breath must be pulled through a little dry fire. Beyond the rim, the wind has a hard, mineral taste and the sky is a dark lid of pressure, not a blue dome but a heavy black field shot through with faint orange seams, as if the world there were too hot to show itself clearly.

Then, in the middle of that high place, the ground opens.

Not into a valley. Not into a horizon. Into a single bounded feature: a collapsed vault the size of a cathedral nave, perhaps a hundred paces long and fifty wide, its floor sunk below the rim like a bowl. The mouth of it is narrow at first, a split of black stone that forces you down on hands and knees before the space suddenly expands overhead. Once inside, the outside world disappears entirely. You are no longer on a plateau or under a sky. You are inside a room that the mountain has built and forgotten.

The room is hot.

This is not the heat of sunlit stone. It comes from below, from the shallow pulse of the planet’s belly, and it is so dry that heat feels like pressure rather than atmosphere. There is no humidity to cling to skin. Sweat does not bead. The air scours. Every exhalation returns to you faintly scorched, carrying a taste of iron, sulfur, salt, and old ash. The heat does not move in gusts the way ordinary wind might. It rises in breaths, slow and tidal, as though the vault itself were sleeping with its mouth open.

It is also very dark.

The vault is sealed from true sky by a broken dome of salt and fused glass, and the only light that enters is the weak amber glow from heated rock and the occasional blue-green flicker of stressed crystals. There are no sun shafts, no moon pools, no clear horizon. Shapes are seen reluctantly: a pale ridge resolving into a pillar, a pillar resolving into a wall of growth, a wall resolving into a moving surface. The darkness is not empty. It is packed.
