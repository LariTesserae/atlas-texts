**The Verdant Salt**

It lies low. That is the first thing you notice, not because there are mountains to suggest otherwise, but because the horizon feels like the rim of something enormous and shallow, a basin folded into itself, extending far enough that you cannot trust distance in the ordinary way. The light is normal light—clear, broad, warm without glare—and under it the land looks for a moment like an ocean bed drained and abandoned, except that it has never held an ocean. There is no sea. No lake. No river. No rain. No standing water at all. Not a puddle gathers in the hollows. Not a droplet trembles on the leaves.

And yet it is lush.

This is the paradox the place carries so quietly that you forget it is a paradox. It is lush the way a packed settlement is dense, the way a reef is crowded, the way old wood is thick with life that has had long time
