**The Cold Loom**

From the rim, the place looks like a basin that once held a town. There are terraces and ridges, long parallel scars, rounded mounds, shallow channels, and open shelves of pale stone—except that none of it is made. There are no walls, no paths, no ruins, no broken things. There are no tools lying in the grass, no bones in the soil, no sign of hands. The entire valley-size region is simply itself, ancient and unowned, and every surface is alive.

The light is ordinary. That is part of the strangeness. A normal pale sun hangs high over a high, cold upland, and its light falls on a landscape that would seem more credible under violet skies or storm-black clouds. But here the sun is merely clear, thin, and indifferent. It warms the upper mats of vegetation for a few hours at a time, then withdraws, and the cold closes over the basin like a lid.

It is plant everywhere
