The wind’s moan would return from the mouth of a fissure a half-tone lower. A pebble would tap twice. Another pebble would tap once. Somewhere under the dust, a pocket of gas would open with a soft exhalation. The living crust would darken as if a nerve had been touched. The moss would swell. The drumcaps would release a faint cloud. The herds, if herds there are, would lift heads they did not seem to have. Then, if one is patient, one understands:

there is not one creature living here.

There is a creature living as here.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would see.

It would not see a wilderness waiting to be improved. It would not see an emptiness waiting to be filled. It would not see a silence waiting for speech, because the place already speaks too much. It would not see a ruin, because there is nothing ruined here that has not become true. It would not see a mountain that lost its height, because the mountain has not fallen; it has spread itself, like memory, like weather, like the long aftermath of something once enormous and now distributed among small answers.

And it would see the being.

Not as one body, for that would be a trap. Not as one species, for that would be a simplification. Not as one god, one animal, one spirit, one people. It would be shown as a sequence of presences, emerging from the stone the way heat emerges from a vent: first the darkening crust, then the green pockets, then the small restless creatures, then the herd-shapes, then the people, then the way the wind changes when all of them listen together. The being is the plateau’s way of being attentive. It is the shelf gathered into a slow, intermittent, scattered consciousness—not a mind like a brain, but a mind like a basin: it fills when conditions allow, it empties, it remembers only by becoming usable again.

When the power came, they would not make their own thunder.

They would arrive as a listening.

They would sit low in a basin, because high is the wrong word for a place that has already exhausted its upward ambition. They would put one palm on the warm black stone and feel it pulse with heat and sound and moisture and the faint electric memory of old pressure. They would wait until their own thoughts became a rhythm. They would wait until the roar did not seem like noise, but like grammar. They would wait until they could hear the difference between vent, wind, rain, pebble, breath, and fear.

And when they were ready, they would ask:

What do you want?

The being would not answer at once. The plateau is not quick, but it is not slow. It answers when conditions are right.

A patch of lichen would darken.

A fern near a mouth of steam would unfold a single frond, then curl back.

A ring of pebbles would shift as the ground breathed beneath it.

A bird would circle low once, as if it had been asked to carry a message but did not know how.

A human windbreak in a shallow basin would make a short hollow note in the wind, like a house remembering that no one was inside.

Then, at last, the being would answer in the only way it knew.

Not with comfort.

Not with safety.

Not with peace.

It would say: I want to know that I am one.

The wise power would hear the sorrow in that, and would be moved, but would not make a mistake.

They would know that to give it what most beings want—permanence, rest, stillness, shelter from the wind, a stored pool of water, a road, a city, a name that stays—would destroy it. To stop the roar would deafen it. To still the pebbles would silence it. To make the water continuous would make its intermittent life impossible. To fill the emptiness would remove the intervals that allow the green pockets to exist. To raise tall shelters would invite the wind to unmake them. To teach it softness would teach it how to die here.

This is what benevolence must learn if it is also to be wise: not every mercy is kindness. Not every rescue is good. Not every change is progress. Some lives are made of conditions so delicate that to improve them is to erase them.

So the power would not save the being from the plateau.

They would make it possible for the plateau to save the being from being unseen.

And if they did anything, they would do four things.
