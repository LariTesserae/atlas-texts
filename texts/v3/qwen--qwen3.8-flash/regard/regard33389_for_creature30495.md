A benevolent and wise power would have to do the hardest thing first:

understand that the thing to be helped is not lacking.

This is not a world waiting for rescue.

## What it would not do

The first impulse of almost any kindness would be wrong here.

It would say: *There is no water. We should give water.*

But water would not be a gift. Water would be a catastrophe. It would dissolve the salt veins that carry charge. It would cool the mineral rivers that keep the deep machinery moving. It would rust the oxide threads into a soft, meaningless powder. It would muffle the dry bells of glass and salt. It would end the great resonances that give this world its voice.

A benevolent power that loved water, trees, wet fur, mud, rain, rivers, swamps, forests, moss, or the cool breath of moist air would not know how to be kind to this place.

It would bring life only by killing it.

It might say: *There are no plants. We should make them grow.*

But plants are not the first condition of a living world. Here, life has found another grammar. The plateau does not need green leaves and wet roots to have metabolism. The land already breathes through heat, charge, mineral vapor, dry oxidation, pressure, crystal, sound. To insert plants here would be to insert an alien law. It would be less respectful, not more.

It might say: *These creatures are too hard, too slow, too large, too loud. We should soften them.*

But that would be misunderstanding them. They are not mistakes
