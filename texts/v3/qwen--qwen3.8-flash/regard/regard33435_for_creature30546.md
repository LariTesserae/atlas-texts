One tap. Two. A rest. Three.

The shell-crabs are not sending a message.

They are cleaning the metal bars, or perhaps not even cleaning; perhaps they are simply tapping because their bodies are clocks and the cage is their frame. Still, patterns that are not messages can become messages if the listener is patient, if the listener is dry, if the listener is kind enough not to interpret too quickly.

A benevolent and wise power would understand this.

It would arrive, if it arrived at all, without rain.

Not light in the old sense—not shafts from heaven, not burning pillars, not a voice that shatters the pale plates and makes the predators freeze in their seams. Not a god’s footstep on the mineral floor. Not the wet stamp of a messenger wearing flesh. That kind of arrival would be violence, because this place has rules, and rules are the only kindness small lives can survive.

A benevolent and wise power would come as a pressure change.

A faint cooling near the upper shaft. A slight warming near the oldest vent
