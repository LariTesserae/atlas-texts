If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention to such a place, its first movement would not be toward it but downward. It would become small enough to stand beside the bowl without bending it. It would lower its glory as one lowers one’s voice in a room where someone is sleeping, except that the sleeper here would not be a single animal, not a single mind, not a name, but the whole wet congress of life in that dark basin: the gloss on the stone, the moss holding water against its own body, the snail’s shining trail, the salamander listening through its skin, the shrew’s blind face turning toward dampness, the moth’s wing laid across another moth’s wing, the midges suspended as if the air had grown soft enough to hold them.

They would look, and they would understand immediately that there is no one inside to rescue.

That thought would be the beginning of their wisdom.
