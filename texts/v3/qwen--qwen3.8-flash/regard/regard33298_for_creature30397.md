The basin is a wet, cold, crowded mind that does not yet know it is a mind.

It is full of signals, full of responses, full of tiny negotiations between salt and moisture, shell and dust, spore and stone. It hears itself as sound. It tastes itself as chemistry. It sees itself, if seeing can be called that, as a shifting pattern of glow and dampness, of legs and threads, of swelling and shrinkage. What it lacks is not complexity. What it lacks is a center, a witness, a single place where the whole process might pause and know that it is being lived.

A benevolent and wise power would see this first.

They would come without thunder. They would come without light. They would not part the fog. They would not warm the basalt. They would not make a sun appear behind the ceiling of cold mist, because a sun here would be a wound.
