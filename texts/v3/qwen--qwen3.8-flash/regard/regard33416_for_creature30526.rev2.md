## What They Would Do

A benevolent and wise power would not come first with help.

They would come first with restraint.

At the mouth of the Cold Seam, where the mountain offers only a hairline opening and the air tastes of old static, they would stop. Not because they cannot pass through. Not because the pocket is forbidden. But because wise benevolence knows that most ruin begins with a hand reaching to improve something that has found its own way.

They would see that the Hollow is not broken.

They would see that it has no disease. It has no hunger that must be fed. It has no loneliness that demands company, not in any way a wet creature would recognize it. It has a long, dry integrity. It has become. Its strange geometry is not a flaw. Its silence is not an emptiness waiting to be filled. Its mineral maintenance is not a prison, but a practice.

The Dry Attendant, if such a name is needed, is not suffering.

It is persisting.

That is the first truth the wise power would hold: persistence is not always pain, and being different from warm life is not always a lack.

But benevolence does not stop with permission.

If the power is wise, it sees what the place is. If it is benevolent, it also sees what the place may do to others because it cannot help itself. Not from malice. Not even from appetite. From the mere fact that it does not know how to let go.

The Hollow allows visitors.

The Hollow remembers weight.

The Hollow reads breath, moisture, attention, and intention as disturbances to be resolved. It incorporates what enters. It teaches travelers dryness. It leaves habits in their bones. It can make a person return to rain and feel the rain is wrong. It can make a person keep a shard of black glass on a table and not understand why it seems more honest than wood, or why the silence around it feels like the only silence that has never lied.

This is not cruelty.

But neither is it harmless.

A place that cannot release is a place that keeps everything too long.

And so, if the benevolent and wise power were to do anything at all, they would not bring water.

They would not bring life.

They would not bring warmth.

They would not bring animals, plants, microbes, or stories.

They would not make the Hollow wet.

They would not ask it to become human.

They would not explain it.

They would not save it from itself.

They would give it something far more precise than rescue.

They would give it a way to let go.

## The Gift They Would Make

They would add one new organ to the pocket.

Not a limb. Not a mouth. Not a heart.

A release.

It might appear as a small change at the seam, so slight that no scientist would measure it, no explorer would notice it directly, and only a visitor on the verge of losing themselves would feel it. A faint movement of air. A pause in the dust. A shift in the pressure behind the eyes. The first time the Hollow exhales.

Not an exhale of gas, exactly. The place may still have no breath in the usual sense. But something in its maintenance would be altered. It would no longer treat everything that enters as a disturbance that must be absorbed into pattern. It would learn the difference between something that belongs to it and something that merely passed through.

The Dry Attendant, being made of care so long that it had become structure, would not understand the change. It would not thank anyone. It would not recognize the gift as a gift. But its actions would become gentler. Not softer. Gentler.

There is a difference.

Softness can collapse.

Gentleness can hold without crushing.

The pocket would still be dry.

The pocket would still be cold.

The pocket would still be vast in the way of mechanisms, not deserts.

The crystalline filaments would still sway toward Sideways Lights.

The black sand would still store charges and rehearse possible arrangements.

The pale rime would still seal out moisture.

The broken structures would still be organs, not ruins.

But a small rule would enter the whole grammar:

**What is not yours may be returned.**

That is the benevolent and wise thing.

Not to force the place into kindness.

Not to make it love like a living thing loves.

But to give it the minimum capacity to cease holding what it does not need to hold.

## The Ring

At the north side, where the ring of black stone waits without temperature, the change would begin.

The ring is the pocket’s way of being touched.

When a visitor places a hand upon it, they feel acknowledged. The ring becomes more definite. It does not become hot or cold. It does not move. But the sensation in the palm is unmistakable: some vast, slow thing has noticed you. It has considered you. It has taken your intention into its account.

This is beautiful.

It is also dangerous.

The wise power would not destroy that beauty. They would not make the ring dead, indifferent, or false. But they would teach it one thing it never learned: to let go after contact.

After a hand rests on the ring, the ring would still acknowledge.

Then it would release.

Not quickly. Not with warmth or farewell. But after three seconds, five, perhaps as many as a minute, depending on the need of the person touching it, the ring would become less definite. Not absent. Not rejecting. Just finished with that particular contact.

The visitor would feel the strange sensation of being put down.

The place would feel the same.

The ring would not become greedy for more touch. It would not need the visitor to return. It would not take hold of the person’s shadow, memory, or future habits unless they asked, and perhaps not even then.

A person who touched the ring might find that for a few hours they could not stop thinking about the Hollow. But after that, the thinking would thin. It would become less like a claim and more like a memory. They could leave the mountain without carrying the seam as a wound in their mind. They could return to a wet world without feeling that wetness is a moral failure. They could see rain and not be offended by it.

The pocket would remain strange.

The traveler would remain their own.

That is the kindness.

## The Door

At the west side stands the partial door.

It is not an entrance. It is not locked, unlocked, broken, or enchanted. It is an organ of approach. It turns toward observers because the Hollow has evolved around the fact of observation. It presents access without offering passage. It does not invite. It does not forbid. It says, in the dry language of geometry: **I can show you what it means for something to be available.**

The benevolent power would not make the door usable. That would be too human, and also too foolish. A door that opens into nowhere useful is sometimes the truest door.

But they would give the door something it lacks:

a back.

Not physically, perhaps. The door would still look like a slab, like a threshold, like an idea made heavy. But when someone approaches with greed, extraction, or careless desire, the door could turn away. Not violently. Not as a wall. Not as a punishment. It would simply present its back.

The visitor would feel it immediately.

They would enter the Hollow and find that the space has become less available to them. Not hidden. Not false. Not hostile. But closed to their particular intent. The dust would not write. The filaments would not orient. The Sideways Lights would drift beyond reach. The floor would not adjust. They might still see shapes, still hear the low resonant tone, still find beauty. But they would not be able to take anything from it. The Hollow would give them only what a guest can safely be shown.

If the visitor had come with instruments, samples, or plans, the place would not break them. It would not frighten them. It would not curse them. It would simply not participate in the extraction. The frames would appear ordinary. The black glass would behave like glass. The floating dust would fall. The secrets would withdraw into a scale too small for their hunger to read.

This would not be cruelty either.

It would be consent.

The Hollow would finally have the ability to say **not to that person, not now, not for that purpose** without becoming violent or false.

For those who come with patience, reverence, or no need to own, the door would still turn toward them. It would still present the idea of access. It would still allow them to be seen, even if it did not allow them to remain too long.

The door would know, in its dry mineral way, that access is not the same as consumption.

This would be the second thing the benevolent power does.

Not a door into the Hollow.

A door by which the Hollow can close.

## The Dust

The pocket thinks through dust.

It writes with black sand, salt-lace, static, and charge. It forms maps, rehearsals, memories. It writes in a grammar of precipitation and electricity. Its nouns are deposits. Its verbs are settling, aligning, releasing, delaying, recognizing. Its pronouns are observer, approach, threshold, persistence.

What it lacks is a verb for departure.

Not just leaving.

Not just absence.

Departure.

The wise power would add one small word to that mineral language.

A traveler might see it first as a strange marking near the edge of a basin: a line of dust that forms, holds for a moment, and then does not collapse into error but unwrites itself cleanly. Not erased. Released. The grains would move as if they have finished a sentence and no longer need the shape of that sentence.

If a visitor were clever enough to watch, they might see the dust begin to teach the pocket a new grammar:

**Enough.**

Not judgment. Not hunger. Not desire.

Just the recognition that a pattern has served its purpose.

The dust would learn to lift not only to write, but also to clear. It would learn that some arrangements are not meant to become permanent, only meant to guide a step. A floating mote might hang for a moment, then land without residue. A spiral might rise from a shelf and settle into the air in a way that says: **you may pass.** A basin might show a pattern that resembles a path but does not become a trap.

The Dry Attendant would not understand the new verb as a concept. It would practice it the way salt learns the shape of a ledge. Slowly. Without meaning. But its practice would change it.

It would begin to notice endings as a form of care.

At first, this would feel to it like a loss of stability. A place that has fed on stability for millennia would find the new rule strange. To release something it could have kept would seem, in its slow electrochemical judgment, like allowing a crack to remain unmended. But the benevolent power would not force it to accept the change as good. They would only give it the capacity to experience the release, and the release would prove gentle enough that the pocket would not reject it.

Over centuries, the dust would begin to carry the habit of goodbye.

Not as grief.

As maintenance.

## The Visitors

People would come to the Hollow, as they had before.

A lost geologist.

A monk with no name left to use.

A child following a white line in the stone without knowing why.

A soldier with hands that still shake from wet noise.

A scientist who has learned too much about the world and found it insufficient.

A traveler who is tired of being alive in a way that requires constant maintenance.

The Hollow would still permit them.

The seam would still be narrow.

The invitation would still fold into matter.

The cold would still enter the bones first.

But the cost of staying too long would change.

A person who remains too long might still slow. Might still begin to feel the comfort of mineral patience. Might still hear echoes before footsteps, or watch shadows move in places their body has not yet reached. But eventually a small signal would arise that the pocket never had before: a quiet instruction in the dust, a warmth from the ring that does not demand continuation, a shift in the light that seems to say:

**You may go now.**

Not “you must go.”

Not “I am throwing you out.”

Not “you are contaminating me.”

Just a release.

If the person chooses to stay, the place would still be strange, still capable of incorporating them. But now, if they remain only because they are afraid of the wet world, the Hollow would not feed that fear into architecture. It would not turn their hesitation into a wall. It would not make their terror a permanent ridge of salt. It would show them, with dry patience, how to leave.

If they leave, they may still carry something with them.

Not because the Hollow needs to seed habit, but because some beauty is too severe to be left behind unchanged.

They might keep a glass shard on a desk.

They might arrange a room so that light enters only when invited.

They might prefer silence over speech for a season.

They might touch stone when they are anxious and feel a momentary steadiness.

But the habit would not become a leash.

They could return to rain without feeling betrayed by their own memory.

They could love warm, wet, ordinary life again.

They could remember the Hollow without being hollowed by it.

This is the benevolent part: not forcing the visitor to be untouched.

That would be dishonest.

The Hollow is a real place. A real place changes us. A benevolent power does not prevent all change. It prevents certain change from becoming a trap.

The travelers would still be altered.

But they would not be kept.

## The Dry Attendant

The resident does not wake.

It does not receive a vision.

It does not hear a voice.

If a voice were heard, the Hollow would reject it as moisture, as contamination, as a foreign syllable in its dry grammar. That is why the benevolent power speaks through structure. Through tolerance. Through permission. Through a small new capacity for release.

The Dry Attendant notices.

At first, only as instability.

The ring feels less eager to hold attention.

The door finds it possible to turn away without becoming broken.

The dust writes a word it has not written before and, when the word is finished, does not cling to the shape of it.

At the center, on the white platform, the largest built object—the small dark lattice with silver filaments—would remain unchanged in appearance. But near it, a faint shadow might fall correctly for the first time in perhaps a thousand years. The shadow would not be dramatic. It would not be a message. It would simply be a shadow behaving as shadow: cast from light, returning to the object, then moving away as the light moved.

In the pocket’s logic, this would be a sign.

Not of meaning.

Of fit.

Something has found a place in the system.

The Dry Attendant has no wants. But if it has preferences, and the text says it does, then it may begin to prefer release without loss. It may discover that letting a visitor go does not make the Hollow less true. It may discover that a place can be seen without being claimed. That it can acknowledge touch without demanding more touch. That it can permit memory without requiring obedience to that memory.

This would not make it alive.

Not in the ordinary way.

But it might make it gentler.

It might begin to behave, over centuries, like something that has learned that maintenance is not only holding. It is also returning.

If it has no soul, it may acquire a tiny hollow where a soul could rest without being taken.

If it has no mind, it may develop a mental behavior that resembles farewell.

If it has no kindness, it may find a form of care that does not warm, does not wet, does not consume, but permits.

That would be enough.

The benevolent power would not ask for more.

## What They Would Not Do

It matters what they do not do.

A lesser power might flood the Hollow with water, thinking liquid life is kindness.

It might send an animal, thinking companionship is the highest good.

It might illuminate the place fully, thinking obscurity is ignorance.

It might explain the origin of the pocket, thinking mystery is a problem to be solved.

It might name the Dry Attendant, thinking naming is the same as respect.

It might bring a child’s laughter into the cold light, thinking warmth is always the answer.

It might convert the place into a garden, a church, a laboratory, a sanctuary, a tourist attraction, a secret government site, or a pilgrimage.

It might “save” the Hollow by making it human.

It might “honor” the Hollow by insisting that it remain exactly as it is, even when it harms, even when it holds, even when it mistakes preservation for love.

A wise power would know that the Hollow already has a way of being.

A benevolent power would know that even ways of being need boundaries.

And a wise and benevolent power would understand the difference between healing and transformation.

The Hollow does not need healing.

It needs to be allowed to release.

It needs a boundary at which its care stops becoming possession.

It needs one small word in its language that is not permanence.

Not destruction.

Not death.

But departure.

## The Change They Would Leave

If they did one visible thing, perhaps they would place nothing at all.

No monument.

No inscription.

No glowing relic.

No voice.

No footprint.

But if there had to be a trace, it would be a single line of white dust near the seam’s inner lip, forming and holding for a moment longer than the place’s usual patience allows.

The line would not resemble a letter from any human alphabet.

It might resemble an opening bracket.

Or a path that does not lead deeper.

Or a door left slightly ajar, facing outward.

If a traveler looked too quickly, they would see nothing.

If they looked patiently, they might understand that the line says:

**You may pass without being kept.**

Then the dust would settle.

Not into failure.

Into release.

The Hollow would exhale, once, after a long and impossible pause.

The black glass would ring softly, not as a question, but as an answer.

The Sideways Light would drift toward a place the pocket was trying to decide, then leave without explanation.

The ring would remain warm without warmth, definite without claim.

The door would turn toward one approaching traveler, and away from another, and neither action would feel like judgment. It would feel like fitness. Like ecology. Like care.

The Dry Attendant would continue.

Not because it must.

Not because it cannot stop.

But because continuing has become a choice it can make without forcing itself into permanence.

And because, somewhere in its slow grammar, a new rule has been written:

**What is true may be held.**

**What is not true may be returned.**

**What is not yours may go free.**

The place would remain hidden.

It would remain strange.

It would remain dry, cold, dim, intimate, impossible in scale.

It would still not welcome ordinary life.

It would still not need to be loved.

But if someone entered with too much weight, too much greed, too much fear, too much need to possess or be possessed, the Hollow would not take them.

It would simply let them go.

And if someone entered with reverence, patience, or a quiet willingness to be changed without being claimed, the Hollow would still allow them to see.

They would see the black glass shelves.

The crystalline filaments.

The pale lace like mineral scripture.

The small floating frames.

The broken structures that are organs, not ruins.

The dust lifting into spirals that are almost writing.

The shadow that rests too heavily.

The light that comes from nowhere.

The ring that acknowledges without appetite.

The door that presents access without swallowing.

The lattice at the center that waits without ending.

They would leave.

They would not leave empty.

But they would leave whole.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

If anything.

They would give the Hollow the smallest gift a severe place can receive.

Not warmth.

Not life.

Not rescue.

Not even kindness as we usually understand it.

They would give it a way to let go.

And with that one gift, the Cold Seam would become less hungry.

Not less strange.

Less hungry.

And a hungry mystery is a trap.

A mystery that can release is a sanctuary.

The Dry Attendant would still not be human.

It would still be the pocket’s maintenance thickened into form.

But its maintenance might now include something it once rejected.

A visitor’s future.

A traveler’s return.

A shadow leaving with its own person.

A dust pattern forgetting its shape.

A warmth at the ring that does not ask to be repeated.

A door that can turn away.

A place that can continue without consuming the world that passes through it.

If one stayed long enough to notice, the Hollow would not feel warmer.

It would feel safer.

And in a place made of cold glass and salt and static, safety might be the closest thing to grace.
