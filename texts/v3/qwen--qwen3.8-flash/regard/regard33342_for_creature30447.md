A wise and benevolent Power would approach not as light, but as patience.

It would come to the basin without thunder. Without wings large enough to cast a shadow over the whole pale floor. Without a voice that could be mistaken for weather. It would come as one comes to a sleeping creature whose sleep is too long for a human heart to follow: carefully, from below the line of urgency, with its attention laid flat upon the ground until the ground was willing to tolerate it.

If the Power were merely benevolent, it might have made the place more comforting. It might have sent rain more often. It might have softened the plates into soil. It might have shaded the plain with trees, filled the silence with birds, broken the horizontality with animals, mountains, rivers, villages, roads, names, songs. It might have tried to make the basin beautiful to those who could not understand its beauty.

If the Power were merely wise, it might have done nothing at all. It might have recognized that the plain was already a complete answer, already a mode of being with its own dignity, and that any addition would be an alteration, any alteration a violence. It might have said: leave it. It is not empty. It is merely not made for you.

But the Power was both benevolent and wise. Therefore it faced the hardest possibility: not how to change the place into something lovable, but how to care for it without making it false.

It would begin by learning.

For a long time, it would learn the basin’s language. Not the language of words, but the grammar of thermal expansion, the syntax of dew, the slow clause of a fungal thread binding dust to pore, the conditional of salt blooming after evaporation. It would learn how a plate becomes a plate by dying organisms becoming mineral, how moisture becomes structure, structure becomes dust, dust becomes seedbed, seedbed becomes cement, cement becomes surface, surface becomes home. It would learn that the plain had no need for drama. It had no need for hunger, chase, reproduction for spectacle, death for tragedy. Its story was not a story of conflict, but of assembly.

It would learn that the being there was not missing something. The being was not lonely, because loneliness was the shape of a mind that wanted another mind. The being was not incomplete, because completeness did not require a face. It was a distributed life, a whole basin doing the slow work of becoming itself. It had mouths without eating, joints without walking, memory without remembering, breath without lungs. It was not a poor animal. It was an animal made of process, and if the Power tried to call it poor, the Power would reveal its own poverty of imagination.

So the Power would not first make the plain into a place for animals. It would not make it into a place for gods. It would not make it into a place for its own attention.

It would do almost nothing.

But it would not do nothing entirely, because wisdom without tenderness is only a polished way of standing apart, and the Power was benevolent. Its benevolence would not demand to be thanked. It would not leave a temple, a sign, a commandment, a burning bush, a voice from the sky. It would not make the basin conscious of itself as a single being if that consciousness would be a burden. It would not make it love the Power. It would not make it pray.

Instead, it would protect the basin’s future from premature endings.

It would do this so quietly that the basin would never know it had happened.

Perhaps there was a star whose light, in another age, might have hardened too much. Perhaps there was a comet on a slow line that would one day strike the pale plate and shatter the first drafts of weather and life. Perhaps a poison wind from a distant sea was gathering itself, one season too much, enough to bleach the seams of their microbial ink. Perhaps a drought was building in the logic of clouds that would last long enough to seal the hidden moisture beneath the plates into a brittle deadness. Perhaps a young planet’s axis was wobbling too far toward the heat, enough to make the first weather too angry, too soon, too fast for a slow land to endure.

The Power would not shout at the comet. It would not command the wind. It would only adjust, with a hand like gravity and a heart like rain, a fraction of a degree, a whisper of pressure, a minor deflection of heat. It would keep the basin in the long middle of its becoming, where things can begin again after injury. It would give it time.

That would be the first act: not improvement, but preservation. Not shaping, but making room. The Power would say, in the language of causes: *Continue. You do not yet need a purpose.*

Then it would do the second thing, which was harder.

It would make a threshold.

Not a city. Not a garden. Not a creature. A condition in which the basin could later decide whether to become more than it was.

It would arrange a small edge at the rim of the plain, perhaps where the pale plates thinned into a shallow pan, where underground moisture lay closer to the surface, where fungal threads crossed through fine sand and calcium flour in a dense dark lace. There it would leave a stable pocket of warmth, not too hot, not too cool, where mineral-rich films could cycle slowly through wetness and dryness. There it would introduce a single chemical possibility, so slight it might not seem like a gift at all: a molecule that could contract. A cell that could carry a message faster than diffusion. A filament that could pull another filament by a hair’s width. A local nerve net, rudimentary, fragile, without intention.

It would not say, *Be an animal.* It would not say, *Be intelligent.* It would not say, *Be like me.* It would only leave the world with one more option, placed carefully, like a door left ajar in a field where no one was expected to pass through.

The basin might use it. The basin might ignore it. The new possibility might die out in a few seasons, too tender for the salt, too strange for the seam. If it died, the Power would not weep, because the Power had not commanded it to live. If it persisted, it would not be because the Power demanded persistence, but because the basin found it useful, or merely survivable.

The Power would know that to make a being able to move was to give it the capacity for hunger. To make a being able to feel was to make it capable of pain. To make a being able to see was to make it capable of loss.
