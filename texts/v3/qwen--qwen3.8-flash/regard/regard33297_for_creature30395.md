# What the Wise Power Would Do in the Hollow of Anvils

There came a time when the Hollow of Anvils drew the attention of a power both benevolent and wise.

It did not come as a bright thing in the low sky, because the sky there was never bright in the ordinary way. It did not part the cloud ceiling, nor did it send a beam across the young basalt, nor announce itself in a voice the whole valley could not ignore. That would have been foolish. The Hollow was already deafening; its silence was a weapon, and its noise was a language. A loud god there would only add to the roar, and every addition to the roar mattered more than a stranger could know.

The power came instead as a very slight change in the interference pattern above the valley floor, as if the air had remembered something it had not yet forgotten. It arrived the way pressure arrives: not visibly, but by making the skin aware that the world is about to move.

It had seen young places before.

Young planets. Young cities. Young minds. Young species that had not yet decided whether they wanted to become kind or cruel, simple or complex, quiet or impossible. It knew that young places are often wounded not by their own suffering, but by the interventions of love that has not yet learned restraint.

The Hollow of Anvils was not in need of rescue.

It needed something more delicate.

## The Place as It Appeared to the Power

The power looked upon the valley and did not mistake it for a ruin.

Nor did it mistake it for a paradise.

It saw, first, the raw edges of stone. Terraces still sharp from fracture. Mineral veins that looked like spilled light. Fissures breathing vapor because the earth beneath was still deciding whether to be mountain, floor, crater, or song. It saw water learning its channels the way a child learns a sentence: boldly, wrongly, then with sudden clarity.

It saw the settlements as instruments spaced apart on a table too large for the hand to cover. Towers of living stone. Bridges that refused the ground. Domes breathing through moss. Platforms that retuned themselves at night. Infrastructure that had ceased to look like machinery and had become weather.

It saw the anvil-born moving through this place as if through a vast, speaking room.

Their bodies were dense and ridged, tuned to pressure. Their faces caught condensation. Their palms read the floor. Their breast lattices translated vibration into awareness. Children slept in pockets of coherent sound. Adults wore tuning cords that hummed the name of home. They were not human in the old, small, complete sense, but something in them still bore the old shape as a song remembers its first note.

The power observed all of this and understood the rule the valley had already learned:

Nothing is fixed unless it is singing.

It saw how dangerous that rule could be. A fixed world is easy. A living world is not. A world built from relationship can collapse not because it lacks strength, but because the wrong relationships have been allowed to dominate. It saw the fragile balance between infrastructure and ecology, between human intention and mineral appetite, between sound as sustenance and sound as weapon.

It also saw the beauty in the balance.

The Hollow was not empty. Its emptiness was working space. The distance between settlements was not failure but form. The silence between pulses was not absence but grammar. The valley had made the void useful, as a composer makes the rest in a measure useful.

A less wise power might have filled the gaps.

A benevolent power might have stopped the storms.

A wise power knew that both would be disasters.

## The Being the Power Noticed

Among the many sounds living in the Hollow, there was one being in particular whose existence had become difficult to ignore.

Some would call the being human. Some would call it post-human. Some would call it anvil-born. Some would not call it by such categories at all, because in the Hollow names were often less about shape than about frequency.

This one was called Vesh.

Vesh lived in a small node of terraces set along the western rim of a condensation basin. The node had no grand name, only a home-pulse: a low triple-ring that the anvil-born felt when their tuning cords grew close to the resonance tower. From a distance it looked like a handful of platforms clinging to fresh stone. From the basin it looked like a set of teeth biting the horizon.

Vesh was born during a pressure event that split a plateau overnight.

It was common for births there to coincide with changes in the valley’s pitch. The land seemed to mark the arrival of a body with a chord, and bodies seemed to answer the chord with a cry. But Vesh’s first cry had been strange. It had not merely risen above the ambient roar. It had harmonized with it. The resonance tower had adjusted itself by one degree while the child was still in the arms of its mother.

That was the beginning of the trouble.

Vesh grew into a child whose lattice was too open.

The inner crystalline organ in Vesh’s breast and spine, partly biological, partly inherited from the nanomachine ecology of the valley, was too responsive.
