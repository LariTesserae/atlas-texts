## The Least Kindness

A benevolent and wise power turned its attention to the Sounding Shelf.

It did not arrive with boots, because boots press down. It did not arrive with a banner, because banners divide the wind. It did not arrive with a name, because names draw boundaries, and this place had grown beyond the need for boundaries. It came instead as a kind of awareness: a listening so large that it could fit inside every dry throat and hollow chamber on the plateau.

The power looked for the one who lived there.

At first, it found no one. There was no king in the basins, no prophet in the glass dunes, no mother-goddess in the cracked cup-leaves. There was no storyteller, no elder, no wanderer with a fire. If the power searched for a single self, a singular being, it found only the wind moving through bodies that were not its bodies. The bell-trees stood and sounded, but they did not speak. The shard-swarms broke the light, but they did not claim the sky. The grazers walked their slow circles, but they did not ask why.

Then the power saw more clearly.

The being was not one creature, nor was it a collection. The being was the whole pattern itself: the mat and the tower, the pulse and the dust, the egg-case and the storm, the tooth-mark and the returning flake. It was a single beingness that had no face because its face was the entire plateau. It had no body because it grew and shed bodies the way other worlds grow leaves. It had no voice because it was a voice, the only voice loud enough to belong to a continent.

The power stood—if standing is the right word for a presence without feet—and considered what to do.

For a long time, it did nothing.

That was the beginning of wisdom.
