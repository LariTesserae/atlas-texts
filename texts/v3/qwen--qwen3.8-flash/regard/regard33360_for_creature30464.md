**Do Not Finish Us**

A benevolent and wise power came to the Cold Hollow not because the place called, but because it did not call, and still made itself heard.

It was drawn first by the sound: the low, living roar beneath the rim, the click of many small legs on fresh basalt, the pop of salt lifting, the tremor of water that had not decided whether to be liquid, cloud, or crystal. Then it was drawn by the darkness that was not empty, by the air so crowded it seemed to have a skin, by the stone so young it had not yet learned to be gentle, by the life so small it had learned to occupy every surface without leaving a single made thing behind.

The power looked for the being who lived there.

It searched as one searches for a face in a crowd, but it found no face.

It looked for a nest, a burrow, a queen, a shepherd, a keeper of the hollow, an animal that could be pointed to and said: this one is the soul of the place.

There was none.

The shardlings shifted in their cracks. The glassworms pulsed in dark moisture. The soot-swarm hung beneath an overhang like ash that had learned to breathe. The dampwalkers tested the air with too many pale legs. The salt tessellates loosened across a dry wall into a thousand living tiles. The cisterns moved through a trembling pool with no eyes, finding their way by pressure and hunger. The moss waited in the dirt of other creatures’ endings. The lichens wrote maps of gray and rust on stone. The bacteria glowed faintly where something had died and returned.

No one of these was the being.

All of them together, in their slow and dense exchange, were the being.

The power understood that it had found not a creature in a landscape, but a landscape that had become capable of life by becoming a creature. The Hollow was not housing this being. The Hollow *was* this being. Its body was the crowd. Its breath was the fog. Its thoughts were the cycles. Its memory was the dust. Its hands were the legs, the mouths, the scraping edges, the borrowed flakes that were dropped again before they could become possessions.

And it was alive in a way the power recognized, even if the Hollow could not name it.

---

At first, the power thought it should save it.

That is what benevolence often does before wisdom arrives: it sees a small cold place full of small struggling lives, and it imagines comfort. It could warm a pool. It could widen a crack. It could bring food from below the mountain. It could shine a sunbeam into the darkest corner and teach the creatures there to follow it. It could choose one glassworm, one shardling, one cistern, and carry it gently somewhere safer, somewhere with soil, with green things, with the ordinary mercies of a less sharp world.

It could build a wall against the wind.

It could put a roof over the rim.

It could make a temple here, and the temple would be holy, because it would be dedicated to life too small for most gods to notice.

But the wise power held its hand.

It saw that every kindness of this kind would be a violence. A wall would change the evaporation. A roof would alter the fog. A sunbeam would teach the swarm a new law. A temple would become a monument. A monument would become a story. A story would become a use. A use would become a wound. To save the Cold Hollow by making it comfortable would be to unmake the very thing that made it alive.

Here was the paradox: the Hollow was not suffering because it was unbuilt. It was living because it was unbuilt.

Here was the second paradox: to protect its fragility, one must not make it permanent.

Here was the third: many small lives were dying here, yes, but not all dying is injustice. The shardlings cracked, the glassworms molted, the cisterns froze, the soot-swarm fell as dust, the salt tessellates scraped themselves thin, the moss withered, the stone broke. Death passed through every crack. But the Hollow did not die as a whole. The Hollow was not a creature that needed a rescue. The Hollow was a way of continuing.

The power looked for a long while.

Then it asked the Hollow a question.

It did not ask in words. Words were too large, too structured, too likely to become artifacts. It asked as moisture asks of stone: by contact, by gradient, by presence.

*What would you have?*

The Hollow did not answer as one voice.

The shardlings lifted their split antennae toward dampness.

The glassworms pulsed in a crevice too narrow for a human eye.

The soot-swarm rose in a cloud and then sank again.

The dampwalkers touched the wet wall and the dry wall and seemed to weigh the difference between them.

The salt tessellates tightened into a black plate, then loosened into a hundred plates.

A pool trembled.

A flake fell from the rim and struck the ground with a bright lonely chink.

The wind over the mountain mouthed a sound like a question no one could own.

And in all of that, the answer came:

*Do not finish us.*

*Let water hesitate.*

*Let tools be borrowed and returned.*

*Let no one make us into a meaning we cannot survive.*

*Let us be small and full and strange.*

*Let us crowd without a plan.*

*Let us live without leaving a monument.*

*Let us die in the cracks and be used in the cracks.*
