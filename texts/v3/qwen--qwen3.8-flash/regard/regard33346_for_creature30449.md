Their relationship with such creatures is not ownership. It is not even kinship as wet worlds understand kinship; kinship here is a question of field compatibility. A veil-hawk roosting on a basalt pole outside a dwelling is not a pet, not a servant, not a possession, but a living gauge: the bird knows by the charge in the house’s walls whether the family is balanced or frayed, and the family knows by the direction of the bird’s wings whether a grove to the east is beginning to lose its tuning. A grazer pressing its foot-pads to the ground is not being tended like livestock; it is completing a circuit. To keep such beings near is not to command them but to adjust oneself until their presence is possible. On the plateau, power appears first as calibration.
