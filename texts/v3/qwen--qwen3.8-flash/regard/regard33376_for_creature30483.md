...where a single careless footfall is an apocalypse.

If the power were only benevolent, it would save them. It would part the root curtain as if a river. It would make the hollow larger, easier to see, impossible to crush. It would put a name on them. It would give them a guardian, a story, a little eternal light. It would say: live without fear. It would make death optional. It would make rain gentle. It would make predators polite. It would make the town a monument.

But if it were wise as well, it would know that these are the first ways in which benevolence kills.

For this place does not live by safety. It lives by fit.

The larvae are not safe. They are moist. The beetles are not protected. They are adapted. The fungi do not shelter. They bind. The mosses do not comfort. They retain. The snails do not rest. They polish. The spiders do not menace. They regulate. Every body in the hollow is a way of answering a condition that could otherwise unmake it. Remove the condition, and you remove the answer. Make everything safe, and you make everything unnecessary. A wise power would not make them unnecessary. It would make them free to remain themselves.

It would understand that there is no single being to rescue. The being is the negotiation itself.

It is the water finding the just enough dampness in the clay. It is the fungus knowing where to hold a wall together. It is the beetle reading the floor with its antennae. It is the larva turning away from too much light and toward a cool side chamber that has no name. It is the root lifting a shard of crust and then, gently, dying into the space. It is the death of a grub becoming a wall, the shell becoming a basin, the trail becoming a path, the path becoming a street, the street becoming a town, the town becoming a living condition in which death can become construction.

The being is not the town’s inhabitant. The town is the being.

And if a benevolent and wise power noticed it, the first thing it would do is kneel.

Not in worship. In proportion. It would lower its attention until the hollow was not a specimen and not a marvel. It would bring its eye to the level of blind larvae. It would let its breath slow. It would make its presence almost negative, a kind of withholding, because at this scale, presence is disturbance. Heat is wind. Shadow is cold. A question asked loudly is a collapse waiting to happen.

It would not speak.

Speech is for creatures who can stand upright and make themselves heard. The creatures of the hollow speak in damp gradients, in taste, in the bending of legs, in the refusal of a larva to cross a line of dry clay. A wise power would respect that. It would not interrupt their grammar with its own. It would not say: I am here to help you. That sentence is already too large. It already contains a boot, a hand, a map, a story, a god.

It would begin by doing what benevolent powers often forget to do: refrain.

It would not become a center in their world. It would not become their ruler, their father, their enemy, their oracle, their myth. A myth would be worse than the centipedes. A myth would teach them to listen for something they could not see. A myth would teach humans to come looking.

If it did anything, it would act only at the edge of the possible.

It would move the angle of a root by a hair, so that shade fell over a nursery that was about to dry too much.

It would let a droplet hesitate at the tip of a branch and miss a clutch of eggs by the width of a leg.

It would carry away one grain of sand from a channel so that the next thin rain would find its way correctly.

It would cool the underside of a leaf by almost nothing, but enough for moisture to remain in the chamber beneath it through the morning.

It would make a human’s foot hesitate one step to the left, so that a boot did not crush a court of larvae, a mound of repair, and the slow civilization of their skins.

It would make a child look away from a pretty spiral shell and toward a distant sound, and the shell would remain empty because the snail was still alive, and the moss would not lose its roof.

It would make the exact location slip from memory like water from clay.

Not because knowledge is evil, but because some knowledge is a hand reaching for the thing before it has become comfortable with the hand. If someone tried to describe the hollow later, their words would fail in the right direction. The place would seem too small to find. The description would sound fanciful. The map would not hold. The photograph would show only mud, roots, shadows, and the ordinary failure of the world to make itself visible at close range.

The power would make the hollow hard to own.

It would make it hard to visit, too, but not impossible. It would leave enough for wonder, and not enough for exploitation.

Because a wise benevolence knows the difference between a witness and a collector.

A witness says: I was there, and I do not deserve to take anything.

A collector says: It is beautiful, therefore I must have it.

The power would want witnesses. It would not want collectors. But it would make the difference so small in the outer world that the witness could pass as the one who did not collect, even while their hands stayed empty.

If a person knelt long enough to become unimportant, the power might do its largest work on that person.

It would not send a message. Messages demand obedience. It would not make a sign. Signs invite worship, and worship is another kind of grabbing. It would do something quieter: it would loosen the person’s attachment to importance.

It would make them understand, without understanding why, that the world is not made for their use.

That being small is not failure.

That being hidden is not lack.

That a civilization can be made from mud, dampness, hunger, death, and patience, and still not be a metaphor for anything human.

That the just-damp are not lesser lives because they are small.

That scale is not dignity.

That silence can contain more movement than a speech.

And when the person stood up, their legs would ache. Their breath would be slow. They would try to mark the place, but their hands would not know what to mark. They would feel foolish about drawing a map. They would walk away carrying something that was not a fact but a habit of care: a new way of looking down, of kneeling, of holding still.

That would be enough.

The power would not need the whole world to know about the hollow. It would need the whole world to become less able to harm small places by accident. It would make the person carry the feeling away from the hollow and scatter it into their ordinary life, where it would become a tiny change in the way they walked through the world.

They would step more carefully.

They would not crush what they had not noticed.

They would not call small things insignificant.

They would not say, if it cannot see me, it does not matter.

They would have seen a town under a lip of root, and they would not forget how much of the world is made of such towns.

The wise power would not make the hollow immortal.

Immortality is for monuments, and the hollow is alive because it is not a monument. Its walls crumble. Its floors dry. Its chambers abandon and return. Its fungal gardens fail and recover. Its larvae die and become paste. Its beetles fall into cracks and are incorporated by slower bodies than memory. Its water is temporary. Its architecture is temporary. Its peace is temporary. To give it permanence would be to give it death by another name.

A wise power would understand that the hollow already has a kind of eternity.

Not endless existence, but continuous becoming.

The town may move upward one grain a week. It may reroute a passage in a night. It may seal a chamber because the heat changed. It may make a new tower of clay because the warm air needs to exchange with the cool air below. It may lose its center and find that the center was only a behavior, not a place. It may collapse in one part and rebuild in another. Its eternity is its adaptability.

The power would protect adaptability.

It would not protect the forms.

It would not insist that the town remain the same town.

It would not stop it from becoming a different shape.
