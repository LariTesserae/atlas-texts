They are membranes stretched over frames of suspended frost and slow electric breath, thin enough to tear in a gust, wide enough to feel the city’s currents before the city itself has decided what the currents mean. They have no legs because there are too many surfaces to touch. They have no carapace because weight would ruin their work. They have no obvious face because the whole membrane is a kind of listening. They drift between the highest spires when the thermal veils are weak, folded in half, riding the cold rising from the engineered stone. Sometimes two of them pass through one another without harm. Sometimes one sticks to a ledge and cannot tear itself free. The ledge is not cruel. It is only still unfinished.

They are the city’s closest approximation to thought moving in the open air.

A traveler who saw one would not be certain whether it was alive. It could be a trick of light. It could be a piece of sky that forgot to fall. It could be a wound in the cold that had learned how to breathe. But when the wave-crabs began their long tuning below, when the plates of mineral glass creaked and the condensation froze into patterns that lasted only hours, the smokelings would rise in slow columns from beneath the bridges and hang above the sound like pale flags waiting for a wind that had not yet decided whether it wanted to be heard.

## What the benevolent and wise power would do

The power would not come as a star.

It would not open the clouds with sunlight, for the light is not absent here because it was forbidden. The light is absent because the world has chosen another grammar. To bring ordinary sun would be to bring conquest dressed as kindness. The power knows how many ruins begin that way: a benevolence too large, too bright, too sure of itself.

It would not send angels into the wet dark, because the city has enough living things already. It would not send saviors, because the city is not a tragedy waiting for a plot. It would not send healers in the familiar way, because the wave-crabs are already healing the fractures with their bodies, and the siltwalkers are already packing dust into seams with their legs, and the sifters are already drinking the air into new walls. The city has its medicines. Some of them are slow. Some of them eat the sick. Some of them cannot be comforted. The power would respect that.

It would come, if it came at all, as a pressure too slight to be measured.

For one moment, the city would feel less alone without knowing why.

---

## First, it would listen

A wise power does not act before it knows what kind of thing is asking for help.

It would sit, not in time, but near the city’s own pace. It would listen to the roar beneath comprehension, the sustained mineral thunder of water in too many channels. It would hear the tapping of wave-crabs on stress lines, the whisper of siltwalkers turning grit into glue, the wet flutter of sifters growing their translucent threads through engineered holes. It would hear the vein-things moving inside the walls, the faint bells of stone under tension, the low exhalation of suspended platforms opening like lungs.

It would learn that the city does not speak in sentences.

It speaks in thresholds.

A ledge cools enough that a colony stops moving.

A basin receives water before the ice arrives.

A bridge vibrates at a note that tells the wave-crabs: *not this way yet*.

A wall becomes warm where a thermal veil lies close beneath.

A smokeling tears.

A traveler would understand none of this, perhaps, except to feel watched by a system too large to be cruel and too busy to be indifferent.

The power would understand that the city is not unfinished because it has failed. It is unfinished because it is still in dialogue with itself. Water is asking stone what it wants to be. Stone is asking air where its edges should fall. Life is asking architecture whether it can bear to be touched. The city is not a body waiting for a soul. It is a becoming that must not be rushed.

So the first thing the power would do would be to refrain.

This is not coldness. This is wisdom.

It would not reach down and say, *Be complete.*

It would not say, *Be beautiful.*

It would not say, *Be saved.*

It would let the city continue being a question the stone has not yet learned to answer.

---

## Then it would remove one burden

A benevolent power that does nothing visible may already have done the most important thing, but if it is truly benevolent, it will also know where small mercy fits without collapsing the world.

It would find the place where the city’s pressure has become a kind of loneliness.

Perhaps a ledge near a broken causeway has dried too long. A colony of sifters hangs there, pale as old thread, drinking moisture that no longer comes. They are dying slowly, but their death will become glue, then dust, then new walls, so perhaps it is not a death that needs undoing. Still, the power would feel the dryness as a small injustice within a world of necessary injustice. It would not save every creature. That would make a mockery of their ecology. It would not create an endless spring. That would erase their meaning.

Instead, it would adjust one channel.

A vein-thing would find a crack it had not seen before. A tiny hairline of warmth, almost nothing, would rise through the stone. For one hour, condensation would gather on the ledge where it should have gathered a day later. The sifters would drink. They would not know they had been saved. They would only grow. Their pale threads would tighten across the gap. The bridge above would hold one season longer. The city would take the event as part of its weather.

This would be the power’s gift: not permanence, but one more chance to continue.

---

## It would give the wave-crabs one note of ease

There is a plateau where the stress has become a sound too high for the body to answer.

The wave-crabs come to it every few days. They tap. They retreat. They return. They add mineral slurry. They scrape the surface. They form circles and tap in unison, trying to teach the stone a rhythm it can accept. Their shells are cracked by the vibration. Their joints calcify from repetition. Some of them die standing there. The power would see them and feel what a human mother might feel upon watching insects repair a wall with their own bodies: admiration, pity, fear.

It would not stop their labor. Their labor is their meaning.

But it would soften one edge.

Not the edge of the plateau.

The edge of the tone.

A single column near the plateau’s rim would shift in its thermal expansion by a fraction of a degree. The sound that comes from it would become less like a warning and more like a held breath released. The wave-crabs would pause. For the first time in years, their tapping would not be answered by fracture. The stone would resonate beneath them in a way that makes repair possible. The colony would work longer, but with fewer deaths. The power would not claim credit. The wave-crabs would not know it was there. The city would continue believing its stone is only stone.

That is how benevolence should work in a place that has not yet learned to worship.

Quietly.

Without needing a temple.

---

## It would open one gap to a gentler kind of danger

The city has gaps too wide to see the bottom of.

Travelers would die in them, though no traveler has come. Smokelings would tear against their invisible lips. Vein-things would crawl too far in and become stuck. Wave-crabs would fall into them one by one, tapping until their legs froze, until the gap took their rhythm into itself and became a little more hungry than before.

The wise power would not fill all the gaps.

To fill them would be to make the city ordinary.

But in one place, where a suspended platform leans too near a black chasm, it would create a small ledge that is not a rescue.

A traveler could not stand on it.
