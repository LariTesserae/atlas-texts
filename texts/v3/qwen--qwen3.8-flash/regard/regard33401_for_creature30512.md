There is a way a great kindness can enter a high place without making it smaller.

A benevolent and wise power would not come to the hollow as a thunderstorm of rescue, nor as a golden hand, nor as a voice loud enough to change the world. If it came like that, the hollow would cease to be what it was. The moss would lose its reason for learning water. The hare would lose its reason for learning silence. The fox would lose its reason for learning to listen. The lichens, patient as they are, would become unnecessary in a softened place, and their slow reading of basalt would stop. The emptiness would fill itself, and the emptiness would be gone, and then the being made of that emptiness—of edges, damp, light, and quiet—would also be gone.

So if a wise and benevolent power turned its attention to this place, it would come as if it had not come at all.
