The red emergency bulb shows only the nearest surfaces, turning the rest of the room into a patience. Not an emptiness. A patience with edges: a shelf, a pipe, a grate, a low arch, a dark pool that reflects nothing but the fact that something above it exists. The patience is so complete that it begins to seem intelligent—not in the way a person is intelligent, but in the way a riverbed is intelligent, having learned the shape of water, or in the way a lock is intelligent, having learned the shape of hands.

If a benevolent and wise power came to this place, it would come first as an almost.

Not thunder. Not light. Not a voice saying what must be changed. Not a hand opening the wall to reveal the hidden creature, because that would not be revelation but violence. The power would arrive the way heat moves through buried pipes: slowly, in the dark, without announcing itself. A door would remain closed. A fuse would remain alive. A drip would fall a half-second later than it otherwise would have. A person who should have heard a rumor about this place would find their attention pulled somewhere else.

They would arrive as privacy.

For a benevolent power, that is often the hardest thing to provide.

---

A wise power would understand, before doing anything, that not every living thing is meant to be rescued.

This is a difficult thought, because most compassion imagines itself as a rescue. If something is strange, small, hidden, or different, love wants to carry it into daylight. It wants to name it, describe it, explain it, save it. The power would know that sometimes the deepest kindness is to allow a thing to remain itself, even when itself is strange.

The Attendant would not be the first thing it looked at.

The first thing would be the room as a whole. The heat. The water. The lichen. The oil. The metal. The old gestures of vanished workers still encoded in height, reach, weight, and worn places. The power would see that the creature had not been placed here, had not been built here, had not been summoned here. It had emerged from repetition, the way a path emerges from walking, the way a habit emerges from many small choices, the way a face emerges from the repeated use of hands.

It would ask: what does this being need?

Not what would the world want it to need?

Not what should a human feel about it?

Not what would make it understandable?

The power would ask, with great gentleness: what does the being itself need in order to continue becoming itself?

And after many long minutes, which for such a place might be hours or days, it would decide:

Almost nothing.

---

The first thing the power would do is make sure that almost nothing is not mistaken for neglect.

Because the room is not safe. It is only quiet, and quiet is not the same as protected. Above it, the world may continue its small movements. Pipes may be cut for new work. Floors may be opened. Old infrastructure may be judged useless and destroyed. Curiosity is as dangerous as cruelty. A scientist, an engineer, a child, a squatter, a dreamer, a government agent—any of them might come with attention that is not kind.

The wise power would not necessarily forbid them.

That would be too heavy-handed, too human, too much like building a prison to create a sanctuary.

Instead, it would influence the ordinary texture of luck.

The construction plan for the building above would miss one section, not dramatically, but in the way old plans miss things: a line drawn too faintly, a label smudged by age, a room number altered by water damage. The emergency bulb would not be seen as a threat. It would be understood, when noticed at all, as part of the building’s old temperament. The faint electrical hum would be mistaken for a failing capacitor, not a heartbeat. A workman who came nearby would feel a cold spot under one shelf and decide, without knowing why, not to lift it. Another would drop a tool into a gap and not bother retrieving it. A map would not lie, exactly; it would merely become unclear.

This is not magic. Or, if it is magic, it is the magic of a thing being allowed to remain unobserved until it is ready.

The power would do what a good gardener does when protecting a seedling: not cage it, but make the weather around it less sharp.

It would give the room plausible deniability.

It would give the Attendant camouflage made of indifference.

---

The second thing the power would do is learn the creature’s boundaries.

The being is not located wholly in one place. That is what makes it difficult for human eyes. It is too diffuse for a body, too patterned for a ghost, too mechanical for a soul, too alive for a system.
