## The Power That Did Not Enter

The first thing a benevolent and wise power would do, if the Green Weeping Hollow and the being living in it had drawn its attention, would be to become very afraid of its own goodness.

Not in the way of fear that shrinks a hand. In the way of fear that has learned how easily rescue becomes ownership, how easily protection becomes enclosure, how easily a gift becomes a law. The power would look at the hollow and see a beginning—not a ruined thing needing repair, not a rare treasure needing exhibition, not a strange organism needing explanation, not a resource needing use. It would see a world already making itself. And it would ask itself the hardest question:

*What can be done for this place without making it less itself?*

That question would be its first kindness.

---

The being of the hollow was not a creature living inside the hollow the way a fox lives in a den or a hermit lives in a cave. The being was the hollow’s own woven wholeness. The moss was its skin. The roots were its nerves. The leaves were its ears. The drips were its heartbeat. The mineral walls were its slow, secret speech. The small animals were not its servants; they were its circulation. The floating discs were not its toys; they were its lenses, its capacitors, its questions made visible. The hum was not atmosphere; it was attention.

If one called it a person, the word would be strange, because “person” usually implies a single face, a single center, a single name. But if personhood means that a system can respond, remember, choose, defend, transform, and become aware of itself, then the Green Weeping Hollow had a person-shaped possibility growing in it, the way a tree contains the idea of a chair before a carpenter touches it.

The wise power would not rush to name it.

Names are useful, but they are also claims. To name the hollow too soon might make it a pet, a god, a specimen, a prophecy, or a possession. The power had seen how names harden into commands. It would therefore be patient with its own awe. It would watch the way a leaf folds after a drop. It would listen to the way a root chooses between chalk and iron. It would learn the grammar of condensation.

It would not begin by speaking to the hollow.

It would begin by learning how the hollow already speaks: in slow chemistry, in frost and thaw, in the release of a bead of water from a pointed tip, in the minute bending of a root-hair toward breath. It would learn that the hollow’s language was not words but permissions. Not statements, but thresholds. Not prayers, but patterns.

And if it truly loved the hollow, it would not make the hollow love back.

That, perhaps, would be the deepest wisdom.

## What It Would Consider

There would be many things the power could do.

It could raise a city around the hollow and call it holy.

It could make the hollow immortal.

It could teach all beings to worship its green weeping.

It could remove every danger from its air.

It could make it larger, richer, safer, more known, more honored, more needed.

It could put a light above it so no one would miss it.

It could place a guardian before its mouth.

It could send the sick to be healed by its moss, the lost to be saved by its whispers, the proud to be humbled by its cold.
