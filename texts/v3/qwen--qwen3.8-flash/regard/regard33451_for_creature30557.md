There are grinders, presses, roasting trays, and sealed baking pans. Food is made from dry solids and hot surfaces. The bacterial films that taste most like bread are scraped from cold walls in the late evening, when the metal contracts and the films loosen from the alloy. They are dried in warm chambers, ground between basalt millstones, mixed with calcium powder, rendered grub fat, and the salted protein dust left after beetle shells are boiled in oil. The paste is pressed into wafers, scored with a hot blade so the heat can reach the center, and cooked on salt-stones until the edges turn the color of old copper.

These are not delicious by the standards of wet worlds. They are dense, chalky, sometimes bitter, sometimes faintly smoky, often carrying the mineral tang of a world that has forgotten what it means to be green. But they keep the Braced alive.
