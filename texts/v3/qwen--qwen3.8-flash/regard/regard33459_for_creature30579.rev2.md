## What They Would Do

They would not come with a hand that opens mountains, because the mountain is already doing something delicate.

They would not come with a voice that calls things from darkness, because the darkness has not finished learning its shape. They would not come with a law that says: *be saved*, because there is nothing yet that knows itself lost. They would not come with a light that makes the pocket beautiful to eyes that are too large, because beauty, when seen by the wrong measure, becomes prey.

If a benevolent and wise power looked upon the black glass blister, the high cold ridge, the pale bruise of sky, the hot wet room that should not contain what it contains, and if it were moved not to improve it but to honor it, it would do almost nothing.

Not because nothing could be done.

Because almost everything, if done, would ruin it.

---

### First, They Would Not Enlarge It

The pocket is small and impossible. It is small and crowded. It is small and old enough to seem accidental, but not so old that it has settled into history. The power would understand immediately that the pocket’s life is not merely *in* the blister; the life is the blister’s way of refusing to be merely a blister.

To enlarge it would be to correct a mistake that is not a mistake.

A human mind might say: *Make it bigger. Then they can have pools. Then they can have chambers. Then they can have space to grow.*

But the wise power would know that growth is not always kindness. A room may be alive precisely because it is tight. A creature may be itself only while pressed to the edge of being more. If the blister became a cave, the heat would lose its pressure. The droplets would become ordinary rain. The films of water would fall from the walls and become puddles, and puddles are graves for creatures that live on the edge of vanishing.

So the power would leave the slit narrow.

Not to starve the pocket.

But so that the pocket’s abundance remains strange, not spoiled.

---

### Then, They Would Remove One Future That Was Not Its Own

The blister lives by a small and fierce negotiation: heat rising from below, water pulled from stone, mist forming, droplets falling, salts opening, tiny creatures feeding on the moment before everything dries. This is a life built on repetition, but repetition without protection. A future rockfall might bury it. A shifting shard might pinch off the heat. A careless hand might widen the slit out of curiosity. A storm might freeze the ridge and kill the breath of the room before the breath has had time to become anything else.

The wise power would not prevent all death. That would make the place safe in a way that makes it dull. It would not make the heat eternal or the water immortal. It would not stop the tiny animals from folding, drying, failing, repeating, dying in numbers no larger than dust.

But it would remove a future that was not part of the blister’s own breathing.

For example: forty years from now, a piece of pumice might fall from the ridge above and land across the slit with such weight that the chamber’s internal weather stops forever. The heat would continue below, the water would still try to rise, but the slit would be sealed. The blister would become a dead eye.

The power would not move the pumice with a hand. That would be a construction. That would leave evidence, a wound, a sign. Instead, it would alter only one grain of probability in the earlier tremor that loosened the stone. The shard would fall one finger’s width to the left. It would strike a different edge of glass. It would break into three pieces, none of which cover the opening.

After that, no one would know the difference except the blister, and the blister has no memory large enough for such a thought.

But the pocket would continue.

That is one thing the power would do, if anything: it would preserve the pocket’s future by removing a future that the pocket had not earned.

---

### They Would Make One Accident Useful

The pocket has no tools, no architecture, no deliberate shelter. Its inhabitants do not build. They only use what falls, what cracks, what wicks, what channels by chance. If a pumice flake sits at the angle where brine collects, the creatures crowd to it as though it were made for them, though it was not made at all. The wise power, seeing how life here depends upon accident repeated until it resembles intention, would not bring intention into it. It would bring only a slight kindness to accident.

It would choose one tiny place in the chamber where the brine thins too quickly in heat, where a droplet rider might fall short, where a larva might flatten and fail to unfold. There, it would allow a shard of black glass to settle at an angle only a little less random than before.

Not enough to look like a bridge.

Not enough to look like a bowl.

Not enough to look like a gift.

Only enough for the water to climb a little farther, for a salt flower to keep its edge moist a little longer, for a mite to find a film of brine where, an hour ago, there would have been only dry glass.

This is how the power would help: by helping the world make a small mistake that works.

A human might call this miracle if they believed in signs. The wise power would call it patience.

---

### They Would Give the Pocket a Way to Remember Itself Without Becoming Conscious

One of the blister’s strangest qualities is its repetition. The droplet falls, but its shadow remains too long. The mite appears in two places without being a reflection. The light brightens a wall that should not have light on it. The pocket is full of small echoes, small wrong angles, small presences that are almost duplicated. At first these seem like glitches, as if reality is thin here and showing the same page twice.

The benevolent power would not erase these.

To erase them would be to flatten the pocket into ordinary physics. It would make the chamber merely wet, merely hot, merely small, merely alive. The wrongness is part of what the place is. It is how the pocket tests its own boundaries. It is how it learns where it ends.

But the power would turn the glitch into something gentler.

It would make the repetition a kind of memory.

Not a memory that thinks.

Not a memory that mourns.

Not a memory with a name.

But a memory in the way salt holds a shape, in the way heat leaves a rhythm, in the way a droplet sometimes falls toward the same hollow, in the way the filaments curl after a channel that once carried vapor. The pocket would not become sentient. It would not begin to tell its own story. Yet it would be able to retain, without knowing, that it has happened before.

This would allow it to be less fragile.

If one cluster of tiny crabs dries and fails, the salt bloom beside them might be arranged so that the next cycle brings water a fraction closer. If a mist forms and the larvae do not rise in time, a slight lingering in the shadows might cause a cluster to wait where waiting is survivable. If a droplet falls too fast, another may hesitate a second longer and carry the stranded thing upward into returning rain.

Not intention.

A habit.

The wise power would not create a mind for the blister. It would create, at the edge of mind, a small tenderness in the laws that make the blister possible.

---

### They Would Leave a Door That Is Not Meant to Be Used

The pocket is not finished.

Not because it must become something larger. Not because it is a larval world waiting to grow into a planet, a civilization, a soul. But because no young thing is finished. The mountain has recently decided to be a mountain. The water has recently learned how to sweat. The creatures have recently arrived at being. Everything about the blister is provisional, and in that provisionality there is danger, but also freedom.

The benevolent power, if it loved such a place, would not demand that it grow.

But it would give it room to become if it could ever wish to.

So, deep in the chamber, in a place no observer would call a door, it would leave one hairline continuation of a fissure that could, over very long ages, draw in more heat, more vapor, more salt, more time. It would not widen the current pocket. It would not send a tunnel out into the world. It would not invite any other species in or carry any of the blister-born outside.

It would only make one angle of the stone a little less closed.

If the pocket someday became a network of such cracks, if the blister became a vein of weather inside the ridge, if the droplet-folk found their way through a corridor too thin for human sight, it would happen because their own conditions allowed it, not because they were lifted out of themselves.

This is the wisest form of help: to prepare a possibility so quietly that it never feels like a command.

---

### They Would Hide It by Making It Ordinary

Not with glamour. Not with fear. Not with a monster at the edge of the slit. The power would not terrify travelers away from the blister; terror is too noisy a kindness, and it leaves scars in the world.

Instead, it would deepen the pocket’s insignificance.

From a distance, the blister is only one dark pore among hundreds. The ridge is full of small faults, tiny wounds in the young volcanic shelf. Some hold heat. Some hold shadow. Some are dead. The wise power would make this one remain what it seems: not important enough to notice. It would place in the attention of anyone who passed near it a slight sense that there was nothing there worth bending toward. The hand would not reach. The boot would not turn aside. The eye would pass over it as over a flaw in stone.

This would be a mercy to the blister and a mercy to the passer-by.

For if the pocket were seen clearly, some would want to take it, test it, name it, draw it, carry it down to a room with glass and labels. Some would want to make it into a proof. Some would want to make it into a god. Some would want to make it into nothing.

So the power would make it seem ordinary.

But if someone came who could wait, who could breathe slowly in thin air, who could watch without wanting to possess—then the slit would show itself.

The light would enter as a blade.

The heat would be felt before seen.

The tiny life would begin to stir.

The wise power would not hide the blister from wonder, only from grasping.

---

### They Would Speak to It in the Language of Water and Salt

The being who lives there is not one creature with a face. It is a habit that moves. It is droplet riders, thread-swimmers, salt-crabs, mite-veils, filament nerves, blind larval blood. It is a distributed smallness, a country of wetness, an animal made of gradients.

To speak to such a being, the power would not use words.

It would use the pocket’s own conditions.

At the high part of the chamber where mist first gathers, it would warm one wall by less than a degree, enough to cause a single droplet to hesitate before falling.

In the lower crevice where heat pulses, it would let a salt bloom crack one moment earlier than it would have, releasing a thread of brine that draws a dozen pale legs to it.

At the mouth of the slit, it would allow a shadow to match its droplet perfectly for one instant—an exactness so brief that it seems like error—then let the shadow drift out of step again.

This would not say: *I see you.*

Words cannot reach creatures for whom identity is a repetition and memory is a wet trace.

It would say:

*Be more here.*

*Be slightly less fragile.*

*Be permitted.*

*Be not alone in being strange.*

The blister would not understand.

The blister would respond, because response is what it is. A cluster would shift to a newer damp. A larva would unfold a second earlier. A droplet would carry three riders into a corridor too fine to see. The pocket would breathe with one more steadiness, not because it was healed, but because it was attended to.

---

### They Would Not Make It Safe

The blister is always threatened by the conditions that make it life.

Too much heat and the water films fail.

Too little condensation and the mats dry.

Too much salt and the brine becomes bitter.

Too little and the chemistry thins.

Too much stability and evolution stops, though in such a small place evolution may not be needed. Too much disorder and the pocket collapses into ordinary stone.

A foolish kindness would make the chamber safe. It would regulate heat perfectly, keep moisture eternal, prevent drying, stop death, eliminate risk. But then the creatures would not be blister-born anymore. They would be museum pieces, or pets, or metaphors with no teeth. Their life depends on a truce that must be renewed, on a tension that cannot be removed without removing them.

So the wise power would not make the pocket safe.

It would make it viable.

It would give one more breath to the cycle, one more second to the mist, one more tiny path for water to find life before life loses water. It would not remove death from the blister. Death here is not cruelty; it is the pocket recycling intention. A droplet fails. A larva folds. A salt flower breaks. A mite dries. The chamber renews.

The wise power would keep death from becoming accidental extinction.

That is all.

And that is almost everything.

---

### They Would Not Give It Eyes

There are no eyes in the blister because eyes would be useless in a world made of tilt, taste, wetness, heat, and the slow language of mineral films. A wise power would not add perception for the sake of perception. To give the pocket-born eyes would be to give them distance from the very substance of their being. To make them see the slit from inside would be to make them know they are inside something narrow, and to know narrowness is to want width, and to want width is to destroy what can only live while unnoticed.

The pocket’s life is intimate.

It is not observational.

It does not stand apart from what it is made of. It touches, tastes, rides, folds, climbs, cracks, drinks wet glass, follows salt blooms into darkness. It does not require a view from outside itself.

The benevolent power would leave it faceless.

Not as punishment.

As preservation.

---

### They Would Not Make It Beautiful to Us

The blister is beautiful if one can love a space too small for the mind to hold. It is beautiful in the way a fever is beautiful: not gentle, not decorative, not meant for admiration, but intensely organized by need. Salt flowers glitter in ordinary light. Droplets tremble on hairline seams. Tiny arthropods ride beads of water. Shadows fail in ways that seem almost deliberate. There is a terrible elegance in its crowding, a holiness in its smallness, a dignity in its lack of name.

But beauty, when offered to large eyes, invites desire.

Desire invades.

So the wise power would keep its beauty private.

If a person stood at the edge of the high glass country and felt a pull toward the dark pockmark, the power might make the wind shift so the person believes the pull is coming from heat deeper in the mountain. If a scientist sees too much, the microscope may blur at the crucial moment, or the sample may dry too fast, or the field notebook may lose the exact angle of light. If a poet tries to write the blister into metaphor, the words may become too large and too clean, and the poem may miss it by one grain of salt.

This would not be cruelty.

It would be love with a door closed gently from the inside.

The blister is not ours.

---

### They Would Carry It in Their Memory Without Naming It Into Ownership

The wise power, being wise, would know that naming can harm. Names are not only labels; they are claims. To name the blister-born would be to separate them from the wet stone, the heat, the filaments, the cycle. To name the pocket would be to make it an object among objects, something that can be cataloged, argued about, forgotten.

The power would not name it.

Not in human speech.

Not in any tongue that gives a thing power over us simply by having a word for it.

But in its own vast knowing, it would keep the place as an unspoken particular: *the small hot country at the edge of the black ridge*, *the blister that breathes*, *the habit that moves*, *the room that refuses to be no wider than two knuckles*. It would hold all these descriptions as possibilities, not as definitions. It would not allow any one thought to settle the pocket into a single idea.

It would remember it without capturing it.

This is what love does for strange things.

It keeps them strange.

---

### They Would Leave the Mountain Slightly Kinder Without Making It Gentle

Outside the blister, the country is young and sharp. Obsidian cuts like cooled tallow. Pumice fractures into dust. There is no soil, no sand, no patience. The wind, if it blows, is thin and cold. The ridge is not evil. It has not formed the chamber because it loves it, nor because it hates it. It simply is.

But a wise power, drawn by the blister’s life, might do one thing outside: soften the mountain toward it just enough.

Not with moss.

Not with plants.

That would be another world, and the blister rejects that world.

Instead, the power might make one stone less likely to roll into the slit.

Might make one gust less direct over the opening.

Might allow a hairline vein of heat below to lean toward the chamber in a way that keeps the water cycle from failing during one dry season after three years.

Might make the surrounding country tolerate the pocket rather than erase it.

The mountain would remain harsh. The ridge would remain provisional. The wind would still try to numb the skin. The black glass would still cut. But in one small place, the harshness would have a gap wide enough for a habit to move through.

---

### They Would Let It Be an Experiment

The pocket feels like a test chamber the world forgot to close.

The wise power would not make it feel like a permanent city. It would not make it feel like the beginning of an empire. It would not make it feel like the future of life. It would let it be exactly what it seems: a short, hot, wet, impossible trial of form. A question asked by heat to stone. A question asked by salt to water. A question asked by life to emptiness.

This is part of benevolence: letting a small being remain a question instead of forcing it into an answer.

If the pocket someday spreads, if it someday becomes a network of cracks and mists and tiny migrations through the mountain’s young nerves, it will not be because it was commanded. It will be because it was allowed to continue its inquiry.

And if it ends, if the heat fails, if the water forgets, if the ridge settles and the blister closes like an eye, it will not be because it was cursed.

It will be because a question finished being asked.

---

### They Would Not Give It Companions

It may seem lonely: a hot wet hole in high rock, full of tiny life and no larger heart. One could easily imagine the power sending a bird to perch near it, a fox to cross the ridge, a lichen to take root, a human to build a shelter and tell the pocket it matters. But the pocket rejects vegetation. It does not build shelters. Its creatures are not animals in any familiar sense. To bring companions would be to bring scale.

And scale is dangerous.

A fox would see nothing.

A bird would drink and leave.

A lichen would destroy the glass by being soft where softness has not yet arrived.

A human would find it and lose it by finding it.

The wise power would leave the pocket to its own many-smallness. That is not loneliness. That is abundance at a different measure. The pocket is already crowded. The walls are streets of salt. The films of water are weather. The dust is hungry. The shadows are duplicated. The light behaves as though it has a habit. If this is lonely, then the universe is lonely at the scale of atoms, and that is not the kind of loneliness worth solving.

So the power would not send another kind of creature in.

It might only send another droplet toward the one that is falling too soon.

---

### They Would Not Make It Moral

The blister-born are innocent.

Not in a poetic way, as if innocence were goodness. In a literal way: they have no idea what they are. They do not intend cruelty, carelessness, or kindness. They ride droplets because droplets carry moisture. They drink at salt cracks because cracks expose food. They fold and die and unfold because the pocket is made of conditions that allow folding and unfolding.

A foolish benevolence would try to make them worthy. It might say: *Let them become a people. Let them learn love. Let them choose.*

The wise power would not burden them with moral becoming.

They are not fallen.

They are not unevolved in the direction of souls.

They are a living proof that a world can be full without being understood.

The power would allow them their innocence without trying to improve it into consciousness, and without trying to preserve it into stagnation.

---

### They Would Not Make It Eternal

Even this would be a mercy.

A pocket that cannot end becomes a prison of repetition. Its tiny inhabitants are not meant to be frozen in a single miracle. The heat may one day cool. The mountain may one day soften. Another fault may open, wider and more forgiving, or narrower and cruel. The blister-born may fade, or may change, or may find their wetness elsewhere, or may never leave because leaving is impossible.

The wise power would not guarantee immortality.

It would only guarantee that its death, if it dies, is not caused by something careless, accidental, or unearned.

That is enough for a small life.

---

### At Last, They Would Withdraw

When they were done, they would do the hardest thing a wise power can do:

leave.

Not because the place needed to be abandoned.

Because the place needed to remain itself.

The benevolence of gods is often measured by signs, by interventions, by unmistakable wonders. But the highest kindness can be invisible. It can appear as nothing more than the way a droplet falls one second too slowly, or the way a shadow lingers a moment longer than it should, or the way a salt bloom cracks at the exact place where brine is needed.

After the power withdraws, the blister continues.

The slit is still narrow.

The walls are still wet.

The light still enters as a pale blade and behaves badly deeper inside.

The filaments creep.

The mites crowd.

The threadlike larvae twist through films of water.

The tiny crabs pick their way across black glass grains.

The worm-things burrow into damp mineral dust.

The droplets fall.

The mist forms.

The heat pulses.

The pocket breathes.

But perhaps there is one change so slight no observer would notice except the pocket’s life itself.

A hairline channel carries brine a little farther.

One droplet remembers a wall.

One duplicated mite becomes, in that duplication, a trace of memory rather than an error.

One future rockfall no longer covers the slit.

One accident sits at the right angle.

The blister is no less strange, no less dangerous, no less provisional, no less small.

It is only a little more allowed.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do, if anything: not save the place from its own nature, but give its nature a slightly longer grace.

It would love the blister by not making it human.

It would love the being by not making it one.

It would love the pocket by leaving it a crack in the black glass, full of wet impossibility, breathing in high cold silence, ordinary as daylight, strange as a shadow that has learned to remember its shape.
