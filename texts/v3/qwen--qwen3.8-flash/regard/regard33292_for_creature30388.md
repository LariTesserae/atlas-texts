## What the Benevolent and Wise Power Would Do, If It Would Do Anything

A benevolent and wise power, looking upon this basin, would feel at once the most difficult fact of love: that to help a place, it must first not correct it.

It would not begin with light.

That would be the obvious mercy, and the wrong one. It would be obvious because land-creatures think that darkness is a problem to be solved, that cold is an enemy, that depth is a punishment, and that a world without a shore is a world waiting to be rescued. But the basin would not be rescued by a sun. A sun would bring air, and expectation, and shadows, and the strange tyranny of visibility. It would bring things that know how to want too much. It would bring the beginning of a shore, and the shore would bring the idea of a boundary, and the boundary would bring the idea of possession, and possession would bring ruin.

So the wise power would not make the water clear.

It would not give the basin a ceiling.

It would not build it a floor of dry land.

It would not call the pale shrimp beautiful in the way a terrestrial eye would call it beautiful, and it would not ask the blind fish to be less blind, because its blindness is not a defect; it is a translation. Its blindness is how it reads the world when the world refuses to be seen. Its translucent flesh is not nakedness. It is honesty: a body admitting that in this place, matter and water are nearly the same substance, and that to live is to remain briefly coherent inside a storm of particles.

The benevolent power would not save the being from death.

This, too, would be obvious. The being could not understand salvation in the way a saved creature from land might understand it, because the being does not live in a drama of individual destiny. It lives in a process of continuation. Its body is almost a rumor of form. Its death is not a fall but a redistribution. If the power lifted it out of the water, the power would not rescue it. The power would unmake it.

But a benevolent power would still look, and still feel, and still want to do something.

That wanting is what makes wisdom necessary.

Following its heart, the power would want to reach in with something like warmth. Not fire, for fire would mean oxygen and combustion, and combustion would mean air, and air would mean a violation of the basin’s integrity. It would want to give a small kindness: a pocket of ease, a softening of the strain, a place where the current paused for a body too fragile to argue with it.

Following its intuition, the power would know that any dramatic gift would become a wound. A hand reaching from above would not be seen as mercy. The basin has no above, no below, no surface that could function as heaven. It has gradients. It has pressure. It has density. It has chemistry. The power would have to act as the water acts: slowly, invisibly, without leaving a story.

Following its reason, the power would calculate that the only gift the basin could accept is the basin’s own nature, arranged so that one more possibility appears.

Therefore, if it did anything, it would do almost nothing.

And that almost-nothing would be enormous.

### The Being It Would Notice

The being, if the power chose one, would not be the most visible creature. There is no most visible creature. The being would likely be the colonial one: the low, mat-backed, mineral-crusted animal lying along the worn ridge where warmer water rises through a seam. It is not easily seen. It has no face, no eyes, no posture of intention. If an observer passed over it, they might mistake it for a stone with pores, a sedimentary plate, a slow outgrowth of calcium and bacterial film. But it breathes, or filters, or maintains a gradient. It hosts. It shelters. It is neither plant nor animal nor mineral in any simple sense. It is the basin’s attempt at a center without becoming a monument.

The power would see it not as a lonely soul in the dark, because loneliness is a concept for beings who have many other beings nearby. This being is rare because rarity is its condition. It does not suffer because it is alone. It suffers, if suffering is the right word, from interruption: from a silt plug, from an oxygen collapse, from a current that fails to bring food, from a cold seam that seals one of its channels, from a storm that buries its crown in too much matter.

The wise power would not ask it to become more than it is.

It would not give it a name.

It would not teach it to speak.

It would not say to the pale crustacean, “You are not alone.” That sentence would be meaningless, because the crustacean does not live in the world of alone. It lives in the world of gradients. It tastes the water. It reads the wake of another body as a chemical sentence. It does not need the comfort of a name. It needs the accuracy of a current.

So the power would approach the being through water.

It would approach not as a god descending from sky, because there is no sky here, and not as a spirit moving through matter, because spirit would be a category error. It would approach as a subtle permission.

A current would bend, not because the power commanded it, but because the power made a tiny allowance within the system. A layer of warmer water, rising from a deeper fissure already existing in the basin, would curve one fraction more toward the colony’s flank. A particle that would have struck and sealed one of the colony’s small openings would be carried instead past it, deposited a few centimeters away. A cloud of suspended silt that would have smothered the mat near its base would settle in a different hollow, where it could be colonized and used.

No one would see this as grace.

Grace would be too large a word.

The colony would not receive it. The colony would only continue, with one more opening free, one more filament extended, one more cluster of symbiotic cells receiving the faint chemical gift of minerals from the warm seam. A tube worm anchored nearby would extend its crown a little farther. A small grazed patch of microbial mat would recover before being torn away. A blind fish would pass close enough to sense the difference in pressure and perhaps hover a little longer near the current’s edge.

This is what the wise power would do, if it did anything: it would not save the being from the basin.

It would help the basin become a little more itself.

And in a place where life is a rare event, sometimes that is salvation enough.

### What It Would Not Create

It would not build a reef.

It would not create a cave with an air pocket, or a thermal oasis where life gathers in abundance, or a garden of chemosynthetic trees. It would not make a sheltered niche too perfect for the being, because perfection is a kind of trap. A niche that is too easy becomes a dependency. A body shaped by abundance forgets the lesson of scarcity. The power would know that to give the basin comfort from outside would be to remove the reason its life has its strange, fragile beauty.

The shrimp-like crustacean that reads the current with its antennae would not be given a brighter signal to follow, because its antennae are tuned to the faintness of the world. To make the signal stronger would be to distort its perception, like shining a lantern into the eye of an animal adapted to darkness. The worm in its tube would not be given food too abundant, because the tube’s crown must still be extended, must still tremble in the flow, must still risk breaking to gain what it needs. The blind fish would not be given eyes. Its eyes, if it has any, are not a punishment; they are a quiet withdrawal from a medium that does not offer images.

The wise power would resist the temptation to make this place more like a world that can be loved easily.

It would know that this world has its own form of love.

That love is not tenderness as a warm face. It is continuity. It is the patience of particle, water, mineral, and body exchanging without ceremony. The mat loves by becoming substrate. The tube worm loves by straining. The shrimp loves by tasting the turbulence. The colony loves by holding a gradient. The basin loves by not being finished, by not becoming a monument, by not allowing any creature to say, “I am permanent.”

If the power understood love, it would understand this.

It would not bring comfort as rescue.

It would bring possibility as maintenance.

### The Smallest Act of Goodness

Imagine a narrow failure.

Not a catastrophe. The basin is full of catastrophe, but not the loud kind. Catastrophe here is chemical. It is the moment when an opening fills with sediment and the colony cannot draw enough current through its chambers.
