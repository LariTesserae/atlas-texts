What the benevolent and wise power would do, if anything, would be this:

They would not enter.

To enter would be to bring the wrong kind of size. The Humming Hollow had spent long ages learning its intervals: thread to thread, ridge to ridge, pool to pool, seam to seam. It had become so careful in its spacing that even kindness, arriving too large, could be a blow. A hand that means only blessing can crush what is fragile simply by noticing it. So the first act of the wise one would be to remain at the rim, or above it, or outside the idea of rims at all, until it had learned, with great patience, what the hollow already required.

It would watch without looking. It would listen without adding its voice. It would know the place not by name but by pressure, not by form but by gradient. It would understand that the darkness was not emptiness waiting to be improved. The darkness was the condition of its life. If it brought light, it would bring eyes, or the need for eyes, or the memory of eyes that could never see. That would be cruelty dressed as mercy.

If the place needed anything from such a power, it would not need salvation. It would need permission not to be saved.

The power would do almost nothing. That almost nothing would be the shape of its care.

But “almost nothing” is not the same as no thing at all.

## First: It Would Make the Hollow Hard to Find

Not by hiding it. Hiding is a kind of cage.

It would instead make the hollow illegible to those who came looking to possess. The land around the bowl was already harsh, but a benevolent power could adjust the harshness so that it became a mercy rather than an obstacle. The wind at the rim would be slightly too cold for any foot to linger in the wrong way. The stones would not offer a place to camp. The soil would not smell of water to animals with thirst. The air above the mouth of the bowl would be a little too thick with mineral fog to seem inviting to insects, too strange for birds, too uninteresting to wandering humans.

If someone came anyway, the power would not harm them. It would not make them dizzy, or blind, or afraid. It would do something more gentle and more complete: it would make the hollow seem not to be there.

A person standing at the edge would feel a small, correct reluctance. They would think, without understanding why, *This is not a place for me.* They would look at the darkness and see not a secret, not a treasure, not an entrance to something beyond, but merely a shallow bowl of stone. Their mind would slide from it the way water slides from a waxy leaf. They would not feel watched. They would feel nothing at all, and that would be their freedom.

The power would also prevent the hollow from becoming a story. Not every story is a lie. Many stories are traps made of language. To say “There is a place that listens” is to summon feet that want to be heard. So the wise power would let no map take the hollow’s shape. If one was drawn from memory, the lines would not fit. If one was copied, it would copy itself into nonsense. If a traveler tried to return by the same road, the road would become a little too long, the landmarks a little too common, the wind a little too forgetful.

This would not be because the power feared the hollow.

It would be because it loved the hollow’s privacy.

## Second: It Would Not Make Them Eternal

A foolish benevolence always tries to remove death.

A wise one asks whether death is the same thing as ending.

In the Humming Hollow, death was not disappearance. It was redistribution. The old pool-listener’s membranes became listening tissue. The thread-bodies returned to mineral drift. The ridges digested what had been lost from the air and made it into structure, scent, vibration. The slow singers below added their calcium and silica to the stone until it was impossible to tell where one had lived and where the geology had begun.

If the power tried to stop this, the hollow would become a museum. Its patience would turn into stagnation. Its listening would lose the softness that comes from having parts that can fail. A body that cannot bruise cannot learn the value of care. A place that cannot lose things cannot learn the shape of its own dependence.

So the benevolent power would not save the hollow from death.

It would save it from being made immortal by another hand.

It would not let it be preserved in glass. It would not let it be taken into a laboratory and turned into data. It would not let it be worshipped into a symbol. It would allow it to age, to change, to lose parts, to become another arrangement of itself. It would allow the rim to collapse, if collapse came, as long as collapse was slow enough to be understood.

If the hollow eventually died, the wise power would do only one thing: it would make sure that some small part of its listening could go on, not as proof, not as prophecy, not as monument, but as a possibility elsewhere.

## Third: If a Note Was Becoming Too Tight, It Would Loosen the World by One Hair

For a long time, there was no need for action.

But the power, in its care, noticed a slow trouble in the deep singers.

One of them, an old rounded presence beneath a broad plate of stone, had grown into a configuration that was almost but not quite wrong. It was not sick. It was not angry. It had simply become too dense, too near to the chamber walls, too certain in its swelling. Its long pressure had begun to gather a frequency that the hollow could not fully disperse. The thread-drift above it thinned. The ridges near it closed a fraction more than usual. The black belts, sensing an approaching overfullness, began to vibrate in an anxious mode, comparing pressures as though trying to find a seam that had not yet opened.

If this pressure continued, the hollow would not be destroyed in the way a fire destroys a house.

It would be destroyed by becoming too beautiful for its own inhabitants.

A sharp, sustained note would rise through the moisture, through the spongy ridges, through the air full of threads. The pool-listeners would contract. The threads would blur into a dull roar. The intervals would fail. The place would lose what it had spent geological time learning: the spacing between one voice and another.

The wise power considered what to do.

It could have opened the plate with a crack of light, but that would have been a violation. It could have sent a voice into the bowl, but a voice is a predator when it is too loud. It could have placed a hand beneath the singer, but hands do not belong to such slow creatures except as accidents.

So it did what such a power does best: it changed one condition outside the bowl, so that inside the bowl, something could become possible.

Far above, it allowed a single cloud to cool slightly earlier than it would have. It let one rare rain gather without wind. It let one drop fall toward the rim and miss the bowl, strike a stone ledge outside the mouth, and slide inward in a thin, silent stream that was almost invisible. It carried one grain of mineral from a dried sea far away, a grain that had never known the hollow.

That grain entered the bowl.

It did not enter as treasure. It did not enter as gift. It entered as a foreign pressure.

The black moisture belts noticed it first. They leaned toward it with the caution of tongues tasting rain. They did not consume it, because it was not food. They compared it to their own gradients and found it not dangerous, not nourishing, not theirs. It sat in a gap between a ridge and an old pool-listener, where no living thing could claim it without disturbing the spacing.

For a long while, nothing happened.

Then the hollow made room for it.

Not because the hollow wanted to.

Because a system that listens will eventually be changed by what it cannot absorb.

The grain became a tuning mark. Not an object. Not a symbol. A point of comparison. Around it, the air learned one new interval. The ridges exhaled slightly differently. The threads brushed at a frequency they had never reached alone. The pool-listener near the gap widened one fringed lobe by the width of a hair.

The deep singer beneath the plate did not know why its pressure softened. It simply found, through the stone, a tiny new route by which its long swelling could be distributed. The tight note loosened. The hum returned to something more like breath than demand.

The hollow did not become different.

It became still more itself, with one new possibility added.

If anything, the wise power had introduced not order but a very small, very patient surprise.

## Fourth: It Would Give the Hollow One Memory of Outside

After the grain settled into its gap, the power did one more thing.

It did not do it for benefit. It did it because love, when wise, sometimes leaves a wound shaped like an opening.

The rain that had carried the grain left behind a thin cold film on a shelf of stone near the rim. As the hollow warmed from below, that film evaporated. It rose into the thread-drift as a moisture the bowl had never made. The threads brushed it and found their spacing disturbed for an instant—not badly, but enough to make the air carry a note that did not belong to the hollow’s own chemistry.

It was a note from outside.

It was not beautiful. It was not threatening. It was merely not internal.

The belts received it and passed it toward the pools. The pool-listeners heard it and, for the first time, did not only answer the body of the hollow. They answered an absence. Their fringes widened in a pattern that could be called, if one were careless with words, curiosity.

The ridges exhaled it.

The slow singers did not understand it, but their stone changed a little around it, as bone changes around a foreign shape.

Nothing dramatic occurred.

No creature stood up. No voice spoke. No darkness was illuminated. No being gained eyes.
