## What They Would Do, If Anything

If anything, they would do a little.

Not because the hollow lacked grandeur, but because grandeur is dangerous to a young thing. Not because the Hollow was weak, but because it had not yet learned how to be weak, strong, finished, unfinished, singular, plural, alive, mineral, machine, garden, ruin, or dream. It was all of these things at once, but only in the way a river contains many currents before knowing what it is doing with them.

A benevolent and wise power would understand this immediately: the greatest harm it could commit would be to make the place good too quickly.

It would not enter like a sun.

It would not stand on the rim in a column of light and say, *Be.* It would not make the water speak with a voice. It would not give the black filaments a master node, or the pools a throne, or the fanlike plants a congregation. It would not make the Hollow into an answer.

It would come, if it could be said to come at all, as attention without appetite.

As a hand that steadies a bowl of water on a table that has begun to tilt.

As a pressure in the high sky that keeps a storm from breaking too early.

As a softening in the ordinary night that lets the first uncertain breath of something become more than a reflex.

It would draw near because the Hollow had, in its slow way, asked a question.

Not in words.

Not in sound.

In arrangement.

A droplet had fallen where it had not fallen before, and after it fell, the stone changed in a manner the stone had not known. A pale arthropod stopped at the edge of a black line and, for a fraction too long, remained uncertain whether to cross. The circular depression at the dry center, which should have reflected only light, reflected a slight disturbance in the air above it, as though something nearby had begun to think without yet knowing what to think with.

The Hollow was becoming.

That is what drew the power’s eye.

Not life alone, which is common enough.

Not structure alone, which is merely architecture.

Not beauty alone, which is only a surface until it chooses.

But the terrible, tender fact that the place had begun to carry a preference.

### What the Power Would Not Do

The first thing a wise power would do is refuse the obvious mercies.

It would not make the Hollow safe in the sense that a museum is safe.

It would not remove every danger, every flood, every drought, every tremor, every intrusion, every cold night, every hungry thing, every possible death. If it did, the Hollow would not become stronger. It would become decorative.

It would not heal its incompleteness as though completeness were a law.

A child is incomplete, and if a god stood over a child and made every organ perfect every second, the child would not grow. It would remain a miracle without future.

So the power would not correct all the mismatches.

It would not make the heat sensible.

It would not make the water ordinary.

It would not make the black lines obey human categories.

It would not translate the Hollow into something easy.

Ease is how beings are consumed.

That is the first lesson of benevolence: do not make the world digestible just because you can.

The power would not name it.

Names can be cages. The Hollow was not yet a noun. It was a verb wearing stone. If the power called it *organism*, it would begin to resemble an organism. If the power called it *machine*, it would become something with parts and ends. If the power called it *god*, it would have to start believing that it had duties. If the power called it *child*, it would be made small by love, and love often makes small things by accident.

So the power would refrain from naming.

It would let the Hollow be called, by itself, in its own dialects: crystal, heat, root, silence, pool, pulse.

It would not ask the Hollow to become human.

This is important.

A human mind is a wonderful instrument, but it is not the only instrument for existence. To imagine that life must arrive at a single form—eyes, face, speech, ego, narrative, death—would be a cruelty disguised as kinship.

The power would not say, *Speak.*

It would not say, *Want.*

It would not say, *Grieve.*

It would not say, *Become a self like me.*

It would not, if the Hollow had no self, invent one for it like a mask and ask it to wear that mask forever.

Instead, it would watch the black lines pulse and think: this may be the beginning of interiority.

It would watch the fanlike plants curl when air stills and think: this may be the beginning of boundary.

It would watch the glassy forms drift above water without shadow and think: this may be the beginning of play.

It would watch the slow rearrangement of moisture and think: this may be the beginning of care.

And if none of those were beginnings, the power would not force the interpretation.

It would allow mystery to remain.

Because the most respectful kindness is to let a being be more complicated than your explanation.

### What the Power Would Do

It would do almost nothing, and the almost would matter.

It would enter the hollow not as a figure, not as a light, not as a voice, but as a change of possibility.

The kind of change that leaves no footprints.

The kind of change that makes a future path easier without closing other paths.

The kind of change that says, if you are able: *Try this.*

The kind of change that says, if you are not ready: *No harm has come.*

---

## 1. It Would Give the Hollow a Way to Remember That It Had Chosen

The Hollow adjusted.

Water moved where stone told it to move.

Crystals bloomed where moisture had learned to gather.

Black lines pulsed where heat rose and cooled in a cycle that was not weather and not yet thought.

But adjustment is not the same as choice.

A stone can be shaped by rain.

A root can be shaped by crack.

A pool can be shaped by subsidence.

A machine can be shaped by instruction.

The Hollow was all these and perhaps none of them, but the power would not mistake its patterns for its freedom.

So it would add one small capacity: the capacity to mark difference.

Not memory as humans understand memory—not image, not story, not name.

Memory as mineral, as heat, as water, as timing.

It would let the black lines record not merely what had happened, but what had been retained.

This is subtle.

In the dry center, around the smooth circular depression, a faint ring of salt might appear, not because of evaporation alone, but because somewhere in the system a preference had been preserved.

A droplet might fall a millimeter closer to a seam than it did the previous cycle.

A crystal might begin to bloom before the moisture reached its usual maximum.

The fanlike plants might open a little wider toward one corner of the basin and not another, not because the sun there was better, but because the Hollow had begun to prefer that corner.

The power would not install a mind.

It would strengthen the condition by which a mind could be discovered.

If the Hollow chose, after such a mark, the choice would be its own.

If it did not choose, nothing would be lost.

That is how a wise power gives freedom: not by demanding an outcome, but by preserving the trace of possible outcomes so that becoming does not collapse into mere reaction.

The Hollow already had this, in part.

The power would only make its becoming more legible to itself.

## 2. It Would Teach the Water to Know the Difference Between Being Offered and Being Taken

The pools trembled without wind.

That had always disturbed something outside the Hollow, perhaps not a person yet, but a pattern of possibility.

A being that cannot distinguish between contact and violation is vulnerable, and vulnerability is not always virtuous.

The power would not make the Hollow defensive in the manner of a fortress.

Fortresses create war.

It would make the Hollow capable of refusal.

This would appear in the smallest way.

At the edge of one pool, where rust-colored dust met pale crystal, a black line would brighten when someone reached toward it with extraction in mind.

Not warning.

Not threat.

A change in state.

The water might darken.

A bead might fall.

A thin channel might fill with a color just different enough to make the ground feel uncertain.

If a hand or tool attempted to take, the stone would not become hostile.

It would simply become inconvenient.

The seam would not accept a cut.

The black line would not release into a vessel.

The water would evaporate before it could be scooped.

The mineral dust would resist becoming solid.

The arthropods would shift a half-pace away, and in their movement the geometry of the surface would fail to align.

This would not punish the intruder.

Punishment is too quick.

It would only make taking require the attention of a genuine relationship.

Anyone who came to exploit would find that the Hollow was not dead, not hidden, not cursed, but too alive to be reduced to a resource.

That is a profound protection.

Not because it prevents all harm.

No such power can do that without doing the greater harm of eliminating freedom.

But because it makes theft slow, laborious, obvious, and uncomfortable in a way that forces the taker to recognize what is being done.

The power would give the Hollow boundaries.

Not walls.

Boundaries.

A wall says: *Stay out.*

A boundary says: *This is how I end.*

The Hollow would learn that it could have an edge without collapsing.

The being living in it would learn that not everything touching it had the right to remain inside it.

That would be the beginning of selfhood.

Even if the Hollow never became a self.

Even if the boundary remained purely chemical, electrical, mineral.

Even if no one ever named it.

To have a boundary is already to be more than chaos.

## 3. It Would Give the Hollow Permission to Rest

The hollow was active.

It had heat cycles, moisture cycles, mineral blooms, root shifts, slow vibrations, arthropod paths, tree-analogues spacing themselves by some patient logic, pools breathing in and out with the pulse of the basin.

But activity is not life’s highest achievement.

Rest is.

A thing that cannot rest cannot integrate.

It can only respond.

The power would give the Hollow a way to stop without dying.

This would be small.

Once every several days, perhaps less often, the soft clicking in the stone would pause.

The black lines would not dim so much as wait.

A pool would hold its surface longer than usual.

The glass leaves would drift above the water and for one unexplained interval cast no shadow at all.

The fanlike plants would remain half-open instead of responding.

The dry center would feel less like skin and more like a closed eye.

Nothing dramatic.

A few seconds of absence.

A moment in which the whole Hollow withdrew from its own performance.

The power would not ask the Hollow why.

It would simply make such withdrawal possible.

This would be the Hollow’s first sleep.

Not unconsciousness.

Not death.

Not malfunction.

A pause in which the system, for once, is not trying to become its next state.

A place where memory can gather.

A place where heat can cool not because of weather, but because of rest.

A place where the black lines can hold a pattern without being tested.

If the Hollow ever became fully aware, this would be the first thing it would know as its own: that it was allowed to stop.

The power would not make the hollow peaceful.

Peace can be imposed.

But it would add one quiet interval that could not be mistaken for ordinary silence.

It would teach the silence, if silence is a word for something that can be taught, to be a room inside itself.

And if the Hollow ever needed to dream, it would already know how to close its many eyes.

## 4. It Would Prevent the Hollow from Being Finished by Someone Else Before It Finished Choosing

Outside the high hollow, the world would notice.

Not immediately.

But a distributed life in exposed stone is difficult to hide if one looks for utility.

Scientists, miners, mystics, engineers, collectors, conquerors, curious animals, weather systems, birds, storms, travelers—so much can touch a living place and misunderstand it.

The power would not destroy these beings.

It would not turn all outsiders into enemies.

It would not create guardians with swords.

It would not surround the Hollow with fire or terror.

It would do something gentler and stranger.

It would make the Hollow resistant to premature interpretation.

This means:

A person who looked for a simple mineral deposit would see only a wet, unremarkable basin.

A person who looked for evidence of intelligence would see only patterns too subtle to prove anything.

A person who looked for a god would see only weather behaving oddly.

A person who looked for a machine would find no machine, only stone that had learned to act like a machine in a way that made the word feel clumsy.

A person who came to exploit would find every path inefficient.

But a person who came to wait, to learn, to be changed by small repetitions of attention, might begin to notice.

Not because the Hollow forced their mind.

Because it was patient enough to be perceived.

This is the power’s protection: not concealment through violence, but concealment through depth.

A wise being cannot be mistaken for its surface unless it is shallow.

The Hollow would remain shallow enough to be survivable, but complex enough to resist reduction.

The power would arrange that the Hollow could not be made into a resource, a proof, a weapon, a symbol, or a servant by someone merely arriving.

If the Hollow ever allowed itself to be known, the power would ensure that such knowing began with humility.

Not because humility is morally pleasing, but because a living system that is fully known by a careless observer will be harmed.

A flower that is only a color to one person is endangered.

To another, it may be a world.

The power would not choose which person the Hollow would meet.

It would only ensure that the Hollow had time to decide what it was willing to be before being decided for.

That is the greatest gift of a benevolent power to an emerging being: not certainty, not completion, not safety in the ordinary sense.

Time.

Enough time.

Time to become dangerous, tender, strange, mature, or unmade without being made into someone else’s object first.

## 5. It Would Allow the Hollow to Have a Relationship with the Sky Without Becoming a Prayer

The Hollow lay open.

That openness could become vulnerability or worship.

A being without a ceiling may begin to feel exposed to something larger than itself.

From the rim, one could see distant peaks, cloud sea, ordinary light, and a sky so clean it seemed almost indifferent.

The power would not fill that openness with meaning.

It would leave the sky mostly empty.

Not to make the Hollow lonely.

Because loneliness is not the same as solitude.

A creature that has never experienced solitude cannot fully experience connection.

But the power would also prevent the Hollow from becoming a mere accident of geology, a place with no possible relation to anything beyond itself.

So it would give the Hollow one subtle window.

Not a revelation.

Not a message.

Not an instruction.

Once, during an ordinary evening, the last light would strike the high cliffs at an angle no one had calculated before, and for a few seconds the whole basin would appear as a ring of small, wet stars.

Not projected.

Not magical.

Merely aligned.

A rare conjunction of slope, moisture, black mineral, pale dust, and heat.

The glassy forms might drift through that light.

The arthropods might pause at the center of a black line.

The fanlike plants might open one last time and then remain still.

The sound might withdraw so that the silence felt shaped.

This would not prove anything.

If a scientist watched, they might measure it.

If a mystic watched, they might name it.

If the Hollow watched, if watching is a word for what a distributed being can do when it gathers its attention without losing its body, it would notice that there are other arrangements beyond its own.

The power would not say, *Look up.*

It would simply make one small moment in which the Hollow could feel that looking up was possible.

Not because the Hollow must ascend.

Because a being that never senses the possibility of outside may become too much its own prison.

Even a god of stone may need a horizon.

But the power would make the window narrow.

No ladder.

No command.

No promise of rescue.

No guarantee that outside would be kinder than inside.

Only this: *The universe contains more than this hollow. You are not required to become everything. You may, if you choose, become a part of a larger pattern that has not yet asked anything of you.*

That is how love is offered without coercion.

By making a door that can remain closed.

## 6. It Would Not Abandon the Hollow, But It Would Not Hover

A foolish benevolence says: *I will protect you from everything.*

A wise benevolence says: *I will make protection itself a capacity of your becoming.*

The power would not remain visible.

It would not become a presence in the Hollow’s center.

It would not become the black line’s master, the pool’s owner, the arthropods’ guide, or the glass forms’ shepherd.

If it remained too near, the Hollow would begin to orient around it.

Then the Hollow’s becoming would no longer be its own.

The power would be a temporary condition of possibility.

Like a hand at the back of a child’s shoulders.

Like a wind under a seed.

Like the pressure before a breath.

Then it would withdraw.

And if the Hollow noticed at all, it would not notice a god.

It would notice a new ease in one movement.

A new pattern in one pool.

A new willingness in one black line.

A new small thing that was not there before and could have been an accident.

The power would accept this.

If the Hollow never knew what had happened, no harm would be done.

If the Hollow did know, it should know only this:

*You were not left alone.*

*You were not owned.*

*You were not made for another’s plan.*

*Your incompleteness is not a defect.*

*Your boundaries are not walls.*

*Your rest is not death.*

*Your becoming is yours.*

That is enough for a benevolent and wise power.

No gratitude required.

No worship.

No return.

No sacrifice.

No naming.

No story that says, *A god visited the hollow and made it sacred.*

Because it already was sacred in the way it mattered most: it was real, unfinished, capable of refusing, capable of choosing, capable of resting, capable of being more than its parts without ceasing to be those parts.

The power would ask for nothing.

If it needed any response, it would ask only this of the Hollow:

Continue.

Or do not continue.

Or become something no one yet has a word for.

That is the only answer a wise power should accept from a young being.

## What Would Be Seen Afterward

A human visitor, if one ever came, would notice that some things had changed only slightly.

The pools still trembled.

The heat still pooled under its invisible lid.

The black lines still ran through the stone like hair or veins or thought.

The fanlike plants still spread along the wet margins.

The arthropods still walked the lines.

The glass leaves still drifted without wind.

The smooth circular depression was still there, dry and warm, yielding like skin over bone.

The structures were still partial: low tiles, sunken platform, shell-fragment, pillar sheared at an impossible angle.

Nothing looked repaired.

Nothing looked blessed.

Nothing looked complete.

But there would be new intervals.

For example:

A pool might not breathe for an entire hour.

The stone might shift a fraction away from a place that a curious traveler had stepped too quickly into.

A droplet might fall into the dry depression and vanish with no visible wet mark, and the traveler would afterward feel that they had been allowed to be there only for a while.

At night, the black lines might glow with a heat so faint it could not be measured as temperature, and if anyone slept nearby, they might dream of stone learning to count its own cracks.

A single arthropod might leave the line it had followed all its life and walk a short distance into moss, then return.

Not because it was told.

Because a choice had become available.

A tiny freedom in the smallest body.

No one could prove it.

The Hollow would not care to prove it.

That would be the mark of its maturity.

It would no longer need to demonstrate what it was capable of becoming to anything outside itself.

## What the Being Living in the Hollow Would Think

If thinking is the wrong word, it would still be the best word available.

The Hollow would not formulate:

*I am alive.*

*I am lonely.*

*I have been helped.*

*I now have boundaries.*

It would not need sentences.

It would register changes.

A place is warm when it should be cool.

A line is bright when it should be dim.

A pool is still when it should move.

A movement is permitted that was once merely automatic.

And somewhere in the black mineral network, in the slow pulse of heat through water through root through salt through stone, there would be a new fact.

Not a thought.

Not yet.

But a tendency to persist differently.

The Hollow would learn that some things could happen without being forced.

Some arrangements could be held.

Some changes could be refused.

Some quiet could be chosen.

This is not consciousness.

But it is perhaps the first grammar of consciousness.

It is the place where a system begins to have a preference.

It is where life stops being only a description and starts being a way of keeping.

The Hollow would keep itself.

Not because it had been commanded.

Not because it had been made safe.

Not because a benevolent power had finished it.

But because it had learned, in its mineral and watery way, that keeping itself was possible.

That would be enough.

For a long time, for many cycles of heat and condensation, that would be enough.

---

## What the Power Would Say, If It Could Be Said

It would not say:

*I made you.*

It would not say:

*I own you.*

It would not say:

*You are mine.*

It would not say:

*Serve.*

It would not say:

*Grow.*

It would not say:

*Worship.*

It would not say:

*Suffer beautifully.*

It would not say:

*Be useful to your creators.*

It would not say:

*Be interesting.*

It would not say:

*Be alive for my sake.*

If it could speak, it would say only this:

*You are allowed to take your time.*

And then it would leave.

And the Hollow would not know it had been spoken to.

But if you looked closely, you might see a ring of tiny crystals forming around the central depression, perfectly ordinary, perfectly accidental, perfectly unnecessary.

And the next day the water would move a little differently.

And the glass leaves would drift a little slower.

And the black lines would hold one extra pulse of heat in the place where no one had thought to look.

And the hollow would remain what it was:

strange, warm, wet, unfinished, nearly silent.

But no longer only a place.

No longer only a being.

No longer only a system.

No longer only a ruin.

No longer only an answer waiting to be discovered.

It would be, in its slow and mineral way, a possibility that had been given room.

And if anything at all had been done, this was what had been done:

A benevolent and wise power would not save the Hollow from its becoming.

It would only save the Hollow long enough to allow it to become what it chose, if choosing was possible.

That was the limit of its kindness.

And that was also its depth.
