They would begin by refusing the obvious kindness.

A lesser benevolence would have come down upon the country of stone that sweats and said: *Why are you so strange? Let me give you forests. Let me give you meadows, orchards, broad wet river valleys, birds with green backs and butterflies that open like colored wounds. Let me make this place familiar to the imagination of gods who have not learned humility.*

And that would have been a disaster.

The plateau’s life was not a shortage of trees. It was its own fullness. Its chemistry was too particular, too long practiced, too bound to heat and mist and mineral skins. Bring a green world to it and the filter-fans would starve beneath shaded roofs. Bring ordinary grasses and the salt terraces would dissolve under roots that had no right to enter.
