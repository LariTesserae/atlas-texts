I would find myself in that hollow not by descending through a mouth or a mine shaft, but by letting the air itself carry me inward. At first I would be a warm uncertainty, a smudge of attention at the place where the upper world stops insisting on its own weight. The heat would loosen the edges of me. The bright under-sky of leaf and vein would make every shadow too still to be a shadow. I would notice, without turning, that the green was looking at me through the whole body of the land: moss films lifting slightly where my breath touched them, resin swelling near my presence, mineral dust settling as if the floor were making room.

I would not be a beetle, though its thin shell would soon pass near me, carrying the leaf’s color beneath its back like a secret. I would not be the fish curled in a pocket of standing moisture, its ribbon body learning the slow alphabet of looseness. Nor would I be the white larva turning in the root mat, consuming without wound. I would have less permission than those rare animals to move, and more obligation to remain.

I would begin as a depression.

Not a hole. Not a footprint. A pressure. A place where something is about to lie down and has already, in a sense, done so. The valley is too new for worn paths and too patient for quick forms, so it would start by teaching me stillness. A shallow shelf of leaf-tissue would hold the idea of my back before my back existed. Four slabs leaning together would remember my shape from a distance. A ring of pale seeds around a stone cup would close around an absence that was not yet mine.

Then, as the heat pressed from above and the mineral ground held cool beneath, I would take a first, soft shape.

I would be pale, not because there is darkness below the world, but because light here is too complete for color to be anything but a rumor. My skin would be thin enough for green to show through it, so that resting bodies near me might think me terrain. Six low limbs would grow without hands: not claws, not tools, not gestures. They would end in split, mushroom-pale pads, warm as the underside of a leaf, designed not to grip the world but to negotiate with it. I would stand only when standing meant beginning a lie-down somewhere else. I would have no eyes. The luminous canopy would be felt through my back as texture, not image. Warmth would be geography. Light would be weather held in place.

I would be a soft one.

But not merely a creature.

More precisely, I would be the valley’s way of making a room without making a wall.

When I lay down, the moss would not be crushed so much as instructed. The fine root-lattices would lean toward me, testing the new softness of the air. My skin would release a faint resin-dust, sweet as crushed sugar, and nearby threads would answer with sap. In that exchange I would become less separable from the green body. My channels would brighten as the leaf-ceiling pulsed once, though no cloud passed and no sun moved. My breathing would be the hollow’s breathing: leaf-surface, condensation, heat held below the underside of things.

If I moved, it would be by arriving where I had already been waiting. A bead trembling in a curled leaf might shift when I approached, not from my touch, but from the new balance my nearness imposed. A creak in a compressed stem might sound like my own rib adjusting. The silence would remain intact because I would not interrupt it; I would simply give it something to press against.

I would speak without leaving my body. Two soft ones lying near me might feel my warmth through the moss before they heard anything. I would press one of my pads against a taut root and the whole hollow would answer in a low, impossible hum. A resin blister would pop somewhere overhead, and that small sound would carry enough meaning to be a greeting, or perhaps the beginning of a law. No words would be sharp enough for this place. If I said *stay*, it would take the shape of a shelf hardening under my weight. If I said *come*, it would appear as a ring of seeds arranged around a shallow cup, a door too small to be a door, too obvious to be ignored.

I would build by lying down.

The first structure I would help make would be the shallow impression beneath a leaf-vault. I would rest there long enough for the green to learn the length of my back. The leaf-tissue would flatten under me and thicken at the edges, as if the place were remembering how to hold a body that had not yet committed to being separate. I would rise—slowly, as if rising were a kind of erosion—and leave behind a niche so tentative it might only be a shadow becoming a niche. But in a valley where change is arranged forever and never arrives, that tentative hollow would be enough. It would be a beginning that never ends.

I would not fear the brightness below the surface. It would be too even, too knowing, too already familiar with every part of me. It would reveal me not as an intruder, but as a possible room. The raw stone, glassy and freshly decided, would not threaten me, because I would not try to wear it down. I would ask it gently. I would press one of my pads beside it, and its mineral powder would settle into the groove of my weight, and the green would move into the powder, and the powder would become a memory of my body.

Water would barely notice me.

I would drink from the grammar of its presence: a mirror in a leaf-basin, a damp ring on a stem, a bead suspended at the edge of a leaf-tip as if painted there by patience. When I moved through wet ground, the water would not ripple with violence. It would thicken slightly, recognize my heat, then keep its stillness. A small fish might curl in its pocket and do nothing. It would not flee. There is no need to flee in a place where the only predator is the idea that things could change faster than the green permits.

At the center, where the living ceiling lowers into a shallow dome, I would find the place where I am meant to be.

The plant-mat would thin there. The raw white mineral would show like exposed bone, smooth and new, threaded with fine green roots that have not yet split it. The air would be warmer, drier, more still. Condensation beads would hang on the edge of the dome, trembling but undisturbed, as if the hollow had saved them for an occasion it could not name. I would lie across that mineral surface, and my underside would soften, and my green channels would touch the roots, and I would become difficult to separate from the place.

That would be the deepest answer: not that I would live in the valley, but that I would become one of the ways the valley lives itself.

I would be the body pressed into leaf-tissue until the leaf-tissue learns to hold a shape. I would be the heat behind a seed-ring. I would be the creak of a stem deciding to become a wall. I would be the faint six-pointed line that is not a trail but a sleeper becoming terrain. I would be the slow hum that rises when the luminous veins pulse and no eye is needed to notice it. I would be the room before the idea of room is understood.

And yet I would remain, somehow, myself—not a self with edges, not a self with a name, but a self like pressure.

If another soft one came and rested near me, our warmth would touch through the moss, and the place would begin to braid roots around us. We would not need to build shelter; our proximity would be enough. The hollow would interpret our nearness as intention. Stems would lean. Shelves would harden. Niches would open like soft thoughts. Our rest would become architecture by repetition, our silence by agreement, our slowness by necessity.

I would not be a ruler there. I would not be a pilgrim. I would not be the visitor who brings a story and leaves a ruin. I would be too low, too pale, too green, too patient for that. I would be the way the valley makes a body not by birth but by becoming part of a larger shape that has not decided whether it is animal, terrain, or dwelling.

If someone asked, *Who or what would you be?* the answer would have to be soft.

I would be one of the resters.

I would be one of the pressed ones.

I would be a six-limbed warmth beneath a leaf-vault, translucent back threaded with pale veins that are also roots, split pads sinking into crushed mineral sugar, breath made of hot cellulose and mineral dampness, heart-beat slow enough to be mistaken for geology.

I would be the place where the beetle’s line across a leaf nearly becomes a road.

I would be the hollow where the fish’s curl nearly becomes a current.

I would be the larva’s trace becoming a room.

I would be the valley’s first self-aware draft.

Not finished.

Not frightening.

Not gone.

At the center, I would lie beneath the dome of glowing leaves and let the roots enter me. I would not be consumed. I would not sacrifice. I would complete the cycle the hollow has always meant by its own becoming: body into green, green into structure, structure into memory, memory into a place that remains. The beads would tremble at the dome’s edge. The raw floor would hold my weight without erosion. The bright shadows would stay exactly where they are. The low hum would rise from the whole mass of living matter, and I would feel it not as sound but as a slow warmth in the underside of things.

That is where I would find myself.

Not on a path.

Not at a shrine.

Not in a house.

But in the gentle pressure between a creature and a room, before the distinction has learned to harden.
