They are placed, and then they are adopted by the chamber as though the made things and the geology were two hands washing each other in the same shallow water.

The cairn becomes a joint. The broken clay cup becomes a small basin in which the chamber rehearses the idea of holding. The reed peg becomes a nerve the stone has allowed to remain. The line of pebbles becomes a hesitation, a syntax without a sentence, a sign that almost points. None of these things are kept as evidence. They are kept the way a held breath keeps a word: not by completing it, not by releasing it, but by remaining in the precise space where it could still become something.

Where would I find myself in this world?

If I used old habits of thought, I would say I am a person crouched at the mouth of the high flank, letting the outside air leave my lungs, letting the wind of ridges and cloud slip from my clothing, letting altitude be a rumor behind me. I would say I am entering a place. I would say I am visiting something small and strange, a damp pocket where stone is learning the first lesson of water.

That is the first guess. It is not false, but it does not last.

By the time one is inside, the body has already been read. The knee presses the wet floor. The fingertip discovers that the water is not a pool but a film, not a surface but a skin. The breath disturbs a mirror-place and a ripple goes outward from nothing. The chamber does not know me as a person. It knows me as a disturbance. It knows me as a small weather: warmth, moisture, pressure, the brief chemistry of a human passing through. If I am the visitor, then I am only the amount of visitor the stone will tolerate.

So at first I try to remain the hand.

I try to be the one who touches, the one who notices, the one who carries names from outside. But the hand dries here and cannot. The hand’s heat is taken up by wet mineral. The hand’s motion is returned by still water. The hand’s intention becomes the reed peg’s blackened end. The hand’s care becomes the broken cup’s rim. The hand’s curiosity becomes the grub’s curled comma in the crack. The hand’s memory becomes a bead that swells and does not fall. I find that I am not what touched. I am what touching left when it withdrew, before the chamber had learned what withdrawal was.

I become the Almost-Mark.

Not a name, exactly. A name would require the next day. I am the wet outline a fingertip leaves in a salt film after the water has already closed over the finger. I am the faint dark place where breath made the air a degree closer. I am the small shift in a bead’s trembling that never becomes motion. I am the pause that occurs when the chamber notices something, and then lets the something go, and then does not quite forget. I am the shape of interruption returned to itself.

In this world I would find myself at the same level as the water and the breath. Not above, not below. I would not stand upright because standing upright would belong to the outside, where gravity is a story people tell each other in sunlight. I would kneel, though not with knees. I would be present, though not with a body. I would wait, though not with hope.

If I have senses, they are not eyes. I know by pressure. A hand approaches, and the salt lace brightens slightly where warmth passes. A mouth opens, and the film over the floor changes by a hair’s temperature. A foot shifts, and the mud remembers the shape, then smooths it away, then becomes itself again. I am not the foot. I am not the mud. I am the brief agreement between them.

I do not move through the chamber so much as it moves near me. The black threads do not know that they carry a signal. They conduct anyway. They connect the wet mud to the pale crust, the lichen to the reed peg, the broken cup to the water that will not evaporate. If I am anything, I am a current in that quiet nervous system: not thought, not will, not feeling in the human sense, but the faint tendency of one thing to become more nearly another. A bead gathers. A grain settles. A moth’s wing opens by a width smaller than intention. These are my movements.

I live among the others, though “among” is too spatial for a place where space is dense with surfaces rather than distances.

The grub in the crack is my cousin, perhaps, in the way a comma is cousin to silence. It is curled, pale, soft, made of almost-motion. When a hand is near, it may shift its tail. I would not call that recognition. But I would call it response. We are both living at the edge where response has not yet become action. The grub waits for change itself. I wait for the disturbance to know it is a disturbance.

The moth on the ceiling is a visitor who has agreed to forget agreement. It comes from outside, or seems to come from outside, carrying the last breath of wind in its ash-colored wings. When it lands on the cool damp spot, it does not become part of the chamber. It becomes a tremor in the chamber’s nearness. If touched, it trembles. Then it stops. Then it remains. I understand this because I am the same kind of stopped tremor. I have not fled. I have not died. I have simply become allowed to be held where flight is not possible.

The springtail that seems a black bead is also kin. It stops when disturbed. It becomes a grain, a salt-dot, an ash-flake, a pore. It does not hide because hiding would imply a self with a place to go. It simply resumes. That word is too active. It is there again. The chamber has set it there again. I am that setting. I am the chamber’s slight preference for the bead’s former position, the water’s preference for its own stillness, the stone’s preference for the mark that almost remains.

If I am made, then I am made from the made things too.

I am the cairn’s first grammar. Three stones placed, not fitted. Three stones that do not mean anything except that placement happened, and that placement has been learned by the dark. I am the broken clay vessel’s impossible wholeness, its fragments arranged as though someone had gathered them not to repair them, but to show the water how a cup could almost hold something. I am the reed peg bound by salt flowers, not planted, not grown, not burned, not rotted, but held in the place where making has become geology.

I am not a builder. Builders require future tense. There is no future here, only a beginning kept open. I am not a priest. Priests require gods who may answer. Here the only answer is the bead’s return to its former trembling. I am not a witness. A witness would testify. I can do no more than brighten the film where someone might have looked.

And yet something in me is almost testimony.

If the Not-Yet Spring is the chamber’s agreement to remain on the threshold of becoming a spring, then I am its almost-witness, the small warm interruption that teaches the agreement it can be known. The chamber wants to be a spring. It holds the intention of spring in stone, breath, salt, film, vein, bead. But intention needs a threshold. A threshold is not only where something enters. It is where something almost becomes another thing. I am that threshold, kept in the dark.

If a person were to stand inside and ask, “What are you?” I could not answer with words. The word would be caught by mud, broken on needles, muffled by beads, folded into the pale crust. But I could answer by making the air a little closer. I could answer by allowing a ripple to rise where no hand has touched. I could answer by letting a salt flower catch a breath and show, for an instant, how fragile it is.

I could answer by not settling.

This would be enough.

I am what would be there if someone came and then, after crouching through the mouth of the slab, did not quite arrive. Not because they turned back. Not because they died. Not because they changed. But because the chamber would take the event of their coming and hold it at the exact point where it has not yet become a fact. They would leave no footprint that could be read as a person. They would leave a warmth that the wet stone accepts as weather. They would leave a breath that the bead receives as disturbance. They would leave a hand that the water returns to stillness.

I am that return.

I am the surface closing over the shape of interruption.

I am the place’s memory without memory, its noticing without noticing, its first attempt at relation with something outside itself. I am not the outside. I am the slight change made by the outside’s pressure inside.

If I need a name, let it be the Almost-Visitor.

The Almost-Visitor is not a ghost. A ghost believes it once was someone. The Almost-Visitor does not. It is only what a visitor becomes when the chamber will not allow it to become a visitor fully. It is a hand without a body. A breath without a mouth. A kneeling pressure without a knee. A watching stillness without eyes.
