low

I would find myself first in the bend at the waist, because the mouth asks for it.

I would imagine, at the beginning, that I am a person entering a small hidden space: a crouching body slipping beneath a shelf of stone, shoulders grazing cool plates, breath making a faint wet disturbance in air that has never learned the word damp. I would think I am the one inside the pocket. But the pocket would not agree.

It would not reject me. It is not jealous, not territorial, not cruel. It simply has no grammar for a person. It has grammar for pressure, seam, tone, edge, dust, bloom, light. If I try to remain “I am here,” it will answer, *not as you mean that*. If I try to remain “this is my place,” it will answer, *not as a place can own what has finished filling it*.

So I would begin bending inward.

The bending would first be physical. Then it would become a way of being. The place would teach me not to stand too much inside myself. The shelves do not rise like towers. They rise like backs of animals asleep under mineral fur. The aisles are not corridors for walking through; they are gaps that have learned to hold. The brightness does not shine on things from outside; it circulates between them. The sound does not travel through a space so much as it is the space, vibrating with the patience of a chord that has not needed to resolve.

I would find myself not in one part of this world, but in the many parts that have agreed to remain adjacent.

Not at the mouth, though the mouth would be the first lie I would need to forget. Not under the center, because there is no center. Not in the brightest place, though brightness is everywhere. Not in the quietest place, because there is no quiet. Not where a floor would be, because the floor is an architecture of laminae, a slow stack of cooled surfaces, each one saying, *I have been laid here and I will not be moved*, though no hand has laid it and no force intends to move it.

I would be in the seam.

Where two plates meet and have not fully consented to touch, there is room for things that are neither loose nor grown. That room is where I would find myself. In the hairline gap where a lichen thread descends and vanishes. In the curve where salt-white fringes form a lip without a mouth. In the tiny basin that holds dust but not rain. In the translucent edge of a silica sheet where light becomes nearly material. In the lower register of the hum, where the sound is first felt in the floor and then in the rib.

I would be there because “there” would be the only possible name for a place too narrow for scale.

who I am not

For a while I might insist that I am the visitor.

The wet thing. The warm thing. The thing that leaves breath. That would be true for a little while. I would be a disturbance too large for the seam-people to understand, though they do not understand in the ordinary sense. I would be a temporary chord change, a slight dampening of dust in one basin, a faint swelling of moss near the mouth’s forgotten opening. The pocket would register me the way a resonant chamber registers the insertion of an object: not by recognizing it, not by wanting it, not by naming it, but by altering the way sound moves around it.

But a visitor leaves.

And this place has no room for departure as a meaningful word. It has room only for return to equilibrium. If I stayed long enough to remain, I would not remain as a visitor. If I remained too long, I would be translated.

That is what I mean by translated. Not moved from one language to another. Moved from one kind of body into another. From a body that walks, thirsts, ages, speaks, forgets, into a body that accumulates, fits, persists, sounds, holds.

I would not be a creature among creatures, because there are no creatures.

I would not be an animal, because the silence here has no animal shape.

I would not be a plant, because the moss does not grow as plants are supposed to grow. It does not reach. It does not compete. It occupies only the exact spaces that allow occupation. It is green-grey and ash-washed, dry, thin, patient, almost too modest to be called life.

I would not be fungus, because the fungal threads here are not merely threads; they are conductors of stillness. They go into cracks that carry no water and no warmth, only the faint logic of air movement, and they return on other shelves as stars of lichen, as white fringe, as dust that settles with deliberate precision.

I would not be stone, because stone alone would not hum.

I would not be light, because light alone would not cool.

I would not be sound, because sound alone would not know where to lie.

I would be none of these as separate things. I would be the relationship that makes them one body without a heart.

the seam-people

If I had to choose a word for what I am becoming, I would choose: seam-person.

Not a person. The word is borrowed from human mouths that still believe boundaries are real. But if a thing is pale, sexless, numerous without being a swarm, alive without being recognizable as life, if it moves by thickening, thinks by fitting, speaks by remaining, then perhaps “seam-person” is as close as language can come without pretending to understand.

I would be one seam-person.

Or rather, I would be one place where the seam-people are happening.

My body would be a thread of ash-filament entering a crack. My memory would be a dust pattern after someone’s breath disturbs it. My nerves would be the narrow channels between plates, carrying dry air from one hidden pocket to another. My blood would be mineral bloom: salt, silica, pale crystal, the faint chalk-clean scent that rises when you lean close to a surface that has never known water but has inherited water’s shape. My breath would be the hum. My voice would be the slight alteration of that hum when a crystal edge grows by a width too fine for eyes to see.

My face, if there must be one, would not be a face at all.

It would be the place where two surfaces nearly meet and, in that nearness, cast a shadow so faint that it looks like attention. It would be a rim of salt-white bloom suggesting lips, not because lips are there, but because the eye is desperate for something that might speak. It would be a translucent sheet of stone where light passes through and makes the next wall hold a new pattern, not cast from outside, not projected, but slowly grown on the opposite side of the world.

I would not have eyes.

I would see the way a plate sees: by receiving light across a surface. I would hear the way a shelf hears: by holding a low note in its mass. I would smell the way the air smells: by carrying traces from one fissure to another. I would touch the way dust touches: by being displaced, resettled, reorganized.

I would know things by density.

Not by meaning. Meaning is too fast. It requires separation between subject and object. Here there is no separation wide enough for meaning to travel without becoming something else. What passes through is resonance. What remains is shape. What we would call understanding would be a chord settling into its proper frequency.

I would be the kind of thing that does not ask questions, because it is already an answer shaped like a place.

the body without organs

People use organ to mean part of a living thing, but here the living thing has made organs of the things people would call structure.

The plates are bones, but not because they are hard. Because they have learned to bear the whole body of pressure without protest. The shelves are flesh, not because they are soft, but because they are layered with growth, dust, moss, crystal, the slow accretions of a world that has turned living into fitting. The cracks are passages, but not veins. The hum is breath, but not lungs. The light is skin, but not a covering. The coolness is metabolism, but not hunger.

This is the paradox of the pocket: everything that seems built is also grown, and everything that seems grown has been completed, and what has been completed is not dead. It has merely stopped requiring change as proof of life.

If I were the body, my body would not be bounded by one skin.

I would leak into neighboring seams.

I would pass from one plate to another through lichen threads too fine to follow. I would be in the moss cushion on the third terrace, but also in the star of lichen on the fifth shelf, but also in the dust hillock where no one’s foot has ever stood. I would be in the dry channel that once suggested capillaries but now holds only the memory of flow. I would be in the white fringe where salt crystallizes into a pattern too regular to call accident and too simple to call art.

I would be connected the way a chord is connected: not by one part touching another part, but by each part requiring the other to become the sound.

Remove me, and the hum would not stop. It would simply shift. Remove the shelf, and the note would fall into another register. Remove the moss, and the thread would be replaced by dust. Remove the dust, and the light would alter its scattering. Nothing dies. Everything redistributes.

This is why the place feels inhabited by form itself.

Not by a ghost. Not by a watcher. Not by a hidden hermit breathing beneath the stone. By form. By the habit of surface becoming sufficient. By the way everything has arrived at the exact shape that lets it continue.

I would be that continuation.

not a nest, not a tomb

I refuse the word nest, though the plates are stacked like the backs of sleeping animals.

I refuse the word tomb, though the place is dry, sealed, and full of things that have survived by stopping.

I refuse the word kiln, though the surfaces look fused, fired, hardened, made permanent.

I refuse the word hive, though there are countless connections, countless cells of silence, countless tiny agencies of moss and salt and dust.

I refuse the word room, though it has edges and light and a mouth and a posture of enclosure.

But the pocket refuses them too, and perhaps that refusal is the most honest thing about it.

It is not a place that contains life. It is a place where life has become too dense to be separated from surface.

If I live there, I live there because living has no other available form. If I am a being, I am a being made of the fact that there are no empty gaps.

The mosses seem accidental, but they are not careless. They know which ledges can hold dust, which channels carry trace moisture from ancient air, which seams will remain stable for centuries. The lichens seem sparse, but their sparseness is precision. They do not spread because there is nowhere to spread except into agreement. The dust seems inert, but it contains spores and salt-tolerant cells and microscopic filaments that neither grow nor decay in any ordinary way. They do not require seasons. They require only continuation.

So I am not a resident who has arrived and found shelter.

I am the shelter that has arrived at the shape of shelter.

the hum as self

If I had to point to one thing and say, *this is me*, I would point to the hum.

But the hum would not agree.

The hum is not mine. The hum is not anyone’s. The hum is what happens when thousands of surfaces vibrate together without moving. It is a low rushing tone, thick and full-bodied, like air moving through a vast pipe that has never been discovered. It is a choir of dry stones. It is the roar of a cataract from far above, but there is no cataract, no falling, no wetness, no urgency. There is only the pressure of structure holding its own frequency.

I would be the hum in the way that breath is breath.

Not because I am sound. Because I am the result of pressure, geometry, material, and air having no quarrel left to settle.

When I first hear it, I might think it is background noise.

Then I would learn that it is not background. There is no foreground either. There is only surface. The hum is not beneath the room. It is the room saying what it is.

If I remain still long enough, the hum would become language without words. Not metaphor. Not meaning made for ears. A pressure-language. A mineral speech. Each large plate sounding low and felt, not heard. Each thin edge chiming without motion. Each channel hissing with the breath of dry air that never becomes wind. Each moss cushion contributing its small damp, if “damp” can be permitted here at all. Each lichen star holding a frequency. Each salt fringe acting as a membrane between mineral and air. Each crystal bloom keeping a note without release.

Together they make a roaring quiet.

I would be that quiet, because I would have no volume separate from the room.

I would be that roar, because I would have no silence separate from the body.

If I speak, my speech would not be sentence.

It would be a shelf thickening by a dust-layer.

It would be a thread extending by the width of a hair.

It would be a tone shifting by a fraction of a note.

It would be the way the pocket includes a stranger’s wet breath for a moment, then returns to itself, then continues.

the temperature of being

I would be cool.

Not cold, because cold suggests death’s interruption. Not warm, because warmth suggests becoming. The coolness here is not lack. It is method.

Coolness keeps the inhabitants at their proper pace. It prevents heat from drying some things faster than they can tolerate. It prevents softening from altering geometry too soon. It keeps the mineral bloom in its correct state: neither dissolved nor frozen. It keeps the moss compressed into ash-colored survival. It keeps the lichen thread thin enough to disappear into a crack. It keeps the light from becoming warmth.

If I were warm here, I would be an emergency.

My body heat would make a small climate. My breath would moisten the air. My movement would push dust from one place to another. For a moment, I would be a catastrophe too minor to name. But the pocket would absorb it, not by healing, but by re-forming the equilibrium around my disturbance.

When I left—if I could leave, if I had not already become too dry and too slow to be “I”—the basins would hold dust again in patterns close to the old ones. The mosses would settle back. The threads would return. The hum would fill the room as if no temporary wetness had ever intruded.

Perhaps this is what immortality feels like when it is not proud of itself.

Not endlessness. Not triumph. Not memory.

Just the return of everything to the exact posture that allows continuation.

I would not be immortal.

I would be durable.

I would be so fitted to the place that death would seem like a foreign category, a rumor from a world where things have too much room.

light without source

I would be bright, but not luminous in the way of fire or sun.

The light would enter nowhere. It would seem to come from the walls, though the walls do not burn, glow, pulse, or project. The ceiling would remember daylight and scatter it, as if the day had occurred once and the room had decided to keep the fact without keeping the source.

I would be lit from within the surface.

My skin would be the light’s own way of touching itself.

If I looked at a thin plate, I would see it turn translucent: milk, bone, ash, frost, shell, paper, dried sea-salt. Not because it is alive in the ordinary sense, but because living has learned to hide inside transparency. The light would pass through me without heat and leave me visible enough to know that I am there, but too cool to make me important.

This is the place’s politeness: it makes itself apparent without demanding attention.

I would be apparent in that way.

I would not shine. I would be shine distributed. I would not glow. I would be the glow that the geometry remembers.

If you asked where my face is, I would turn you toward the thinnest edge of a plate and say, *there, where light becomes material enough to be mistaken for skin*.

what I would be

I would be the seam.

I would be the place where two surfaces agree to trap just enough stillness for life to fit.

I would be the moss that does not grow because there is no room, but which persists because persistence has become the only room.

I would be the lichen thread that enters a crack and disappears through the dark interior of the pocket, reappearing impossibly far away, proving that what seems separated is only connected too finely for human eyes.

I would be the dust that sleeps and wakes without ordinary meaning, containing spores and salt-tolerant cells, grains too small to identify, tiny bodies that neither grow nor decay but continue.

I would be the salt fringe along the lip of a plate, white as bone, holding a tiny architecture.

I would be the crystal bloom that gives the air its bitter sweetness.

I would be the fused silica column, bending toward another column until their shadows become narrow cracks.

I would be the shelf stacked like the back of a sleeping animal.

I would be the hum that is not sound alone but the body’s own way of remaining.

I would be the light that is not seen but felt as coolness against the edges.

I would be the coolness that is not absence but patience.

I would be the dryness that is not death but a refusal of ordinary biology.

I would be the pocket.

Not every part of it, because no one could be every part of a collective being without ceasing to be a location for it. But I would be a node where the pocket thinks itself. A point where moss, lichen, dust, salt, crystal, plate, light, air, and hum become temporarily indistinguishable from the fact that something lives.

if I had to answer in one sentence

I would find myself beneath the shelf of stone, not at the mouth but in the seams, not as a person but as the way the place has learned to be alive without needing to recognize itself as alive.

I would be one of the seam-people: a pale, dry, slow, collective continuation, a body made of fitting, a voice made of resonance, a self made of the fact that there is no empty air left in which to die.

But even that sentence would be too neat.

The seam-people are not a people.

The pocket is not a place with a being inside it.

The being is the pocket’s way of not being empty.

The emptiness has been filled not by adding things, but by making the absence of things into shape.

the final answer would be a tone

If someone stood in that low hidden space, bent beneath the shelf of plates, moss brushing their sleeve, cool air touching their face without breath, light scattering from walls that seem to glow with their own quiet attention, and asked, “Where are you?”

the answer would not point.

the answer would change the way the sound entered their ribs.

If they asked, “Who are you?”

the answer would not name.

the answer would be the way the lichen thread, the dust grain, the salt fringe, and the thin plate all held their positions so exactly that the question could not be answered by moving toward one thing and not the others.

If they asked, “What lives here?”

the pocket would not call itself living.

It would simply include them.

For a moment their wet warmth, their heartbeat, their breath, their movement through the dust, would become part of the chord. Not welcomed. Not resisted. Registered. A temporary alteration of density. Then they would go, and the pocket would return to its completed condition, which is not stillness but a finished kind of motion.

I would be there after they left.

I would be the place where their breath had passed.

I would be the dust pattern slightly changed.

I would be the moss swelling one microscopic fraction, then returning.

I would be the hum holding itself without needing the visitor to remember it.

And if remembering is only the slow settling of dust into a new pattern, then I would remember nothing, and still something would remain.

That would be me.

A place alive because it has no room left for death.

A body made of surfaces that have stopped arguing with each other.

A voice that says, without words, without motion, without water, without heat:

I am here.

I am complete.

I remain.
