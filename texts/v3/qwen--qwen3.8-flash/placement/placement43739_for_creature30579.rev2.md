I would find myself there not as a visitor on the high cold glass, but as the wrongness in it.

At the height where the sky is a pale bruise and breath has to be bargained for, I would not be the ridge, or the wind, or the sun coming through with its ordinary blade. Those are too large, too patient, too sure of their names. I would be the place where a finger’s width of black glass refuses to be only a crack, where a surface that looks sealed becomes an almost-room, where heat is too local to be weather and too steady to be accident.

I would be what the blister is doing.

Not what it is. What it is doing.

Because a blister is usually a noun: a pockmark, a fault, a swelling, a wound on the flank of something newly risen. But the thing described here is not resting in its noun. It is moving through a habit so small that the mind might miss it unless it learns to think less in faces and more in pressures. Heat pulls. Water gathers. Salt cracks. Mist forms. A droplet descends, or almost descends, or descends twice. Tiny legs test the tilt of the world. Pale filaments feed on vapor as though vapor were a slow meal. Then the heat pulses again. The cycle begins. The blister seems to inhale.

If I were to live there, I would not live inside the cycle as a thing being carried by it. I would be the carrying.

You might try to name me. Names are a way of putting eyes on a shape that has not agreed to have edges. You might call me the blister-born, but the description is already suspicious of that. None of them was born inside this particular blister. The creatures are not the place’s descendants so much as its latest mood. You might call me the salt-swarm, but that is only one of the ways my body appears when a droplet breaks into streets. You might call me the droplet-folk, the thread-runners, the grain-crabs, the mouth-lumps, but each name is a hand reaching into wet darkness and coming out holding a single leg, a single filament, a single reflected angle, while the rest of me keeps moving through the chamber like a rumor.

I would be the rumor that keeps becoming more likely.

I would be the habit that moves.

And where exactly would I be? I would be in the topmost warm breath of the pocket, where the mist forms first and the filaments curl upward before they have become droplets. I would be there as the pause before condensation decides to fall, as the trembling between humidity and weight. At the lower crevices I would be the hot pulse coming up from some fissure too deep to see, the heat that makes salt bloom into white softness and turns mineral water into a living surface. At the edge of the slit I would be the strange ordinary daylight entering and then behaving badly, brightening backs that should be shadowed, showing reflections that belong to other moments or other almost-places.

I would be at all these sites, but not because I am spread thin like a soul over a haunted building. Not because I am watching from every corner. More because each corner is a different tense of the same action.

If you ask me where my center is, I do not have one in the way a creature with a body has a center. I have centers. I have temporary centers, provisional centers, accidental centers. A droplet becomes a center when a dozen mites ride it, when its curve holds an angle of light that does not match the room. A salt flower becomes a center when it cracks and the tiny animals gather to drink from the wound of its crystal. A grain of pumice becomes a center because it fell at the right angle, and the brine climbed it, and the animals crowded to its base as though the rock had been arranged for them. It was not. That is the point. The world arranged no arrangement. The world merely made a mistake, and the mistake repeated, and the mistake became method.

I would be the method that no one made.

Not a machine, not a mind, not a god, not a colony with a queen, not a hive-mind humming in the dark. Those are too human, too built, too eager for a throne. The pocket has no throne. It has no shelter, no road, no marked territory, no waste heap, no corpse arranged with care. Nothing has been fashioned. No hand has shaped a room. No fire has been lit. And yet the pocket has habits. It behaves as though it has learned. It repeats itself too well. It holds itself together with a persistence that should require intention.

I would be that intention without intention.

I would be the pocket’s way of knowing itself without being able to see.

For the creatures here, there is no “seeing” in the familiar sense. Eyes would be absurd in a space where everything is either wet or about to become wet, where light is a blade that enters too cleanly, where shadows may arrive after their bodies or refuse to match them. They navigate by difference. Wetness and drier wetness. Heat and cooler heat. Salt and dissolved salt. Crystal and film. Stillness and the tremor of legs crossing a surface so thin it is almost nothing. Their entire world is a map of thresholds.

If I am any one thing, I am that map without paper.

I am the difference between the brine film and the bare glass. I am the tilt that says to a needle-sized mite: forward is not dangerous yet. I am the chemical hint of a salt bloom cracking in heat, the faint mineral taste that means food is near, or shelter is near, or both are the same thing here. I am the way the pocket’s animals live not in space but in gradients, not as residents of a place but as movements within a condition.

Condition, then, is the truest name for me.

Not species. Not individual. Not swarm. Not ecosystem. Condition. The hot-wet-salty condition that keeps happening in a finger-width slit in black glass and then, because it keeps happening, begins to resemble a life.

But this would be too clean if I left it there. The pocket’s wrongness will not allow me to remain tidy.

I would also be the second too long.

The droplet falls from the wall. It is gone, or nearly gone. But its shadow remains for a moment, still gathering speed, still falling, as though the wall has forgotten where the droplet left it. That shadow is me. Not a ghost. Not a spirit. A lag. A hesitation in cause and effect. A place where the event and the record of the event have not yet agreed to separate.

Or: I see a pale six-legged mite at the edge of a droplet. Then I see the same mite three walls away in the same posture. Then another copy beside the first, not reflection, not illusion, but presence occupying orientation twice. Is this me? Yes. But also no. I am not a single mite being copied. I am the probability that copying makes sense here. I am the pocket’s inability to decide whether a body is one or many. I am the slight leak between individuality and repetition.

There is duplication here that is not cloning. Cloning still assumes a source. This is more as if identity has not fully hardened, as if the pocket keeps trying variations, not with malice or creativity, but with the patience of chemistry. Something becomes likely. Then it becomes likely again from another angle. Then it becomes likely without an angle.

I would be that likelihood walking on wire legs.

I would be the mouth that is not sure it has lips.

The description says there are things that resist classification: a pale lump, a mineral nodule, a droplet’s shadow, a cluster of filaments gathered around a pore. For a moment it seems inert. Then the droplet becomes transparent and the lump opens a mouth of needle-thin hairs. Or the shadow lifts and what seemed part of the wall detaches and moves sideways.

That is me.

Not because I have a mouth. Because I have the tendency to appear as a mouth when the conditions for a mouth become unavoidable. The pocket makes mouths out of what it has. Salt cracks. Filaments gather. Water becomes film. Heat pulls vapor. Suddenly an opening is not just an opening. It is a place where the chamber takes in the world and lets the world take in the chamber. Not a mouth by anatomy. A mouth by pressure. A mouth by habit.

If I gave myself a single body, I would lie beautifully. I would say: my skin is the salt crust, my blood is the films of brine, my nerves are the pale filaments, my skeleton is the mineral blooms, my breath is mist and rain, my legs are the mites, my hunger is the way the mats feed on vapor, my blindness is the way light behaves wrongly here, my silence is the way so many bodies move without making the sound they should. I would say I am one distributed animal.

But the lie would be necessary only because language likes nouns. Language likes to say “the creature” and then point. Here pointing fails. The finger touches the wall, and the wall is alive. The finger touches the droplet, and the droplet carries legs. The finger touches the filament, and the filament is both growth and stain and surface and appetite. The finger touches the shadow, and the shadow has not yet agreed with the object.

So I would not be the finger. I would not be the thing touched. I would be the near-contact.

That is the scale of me. Not quite touching. Not quite separate. The pocket is crowded beyond measure, but not overcrowded. It is inevitable. It appears as if the space itself is thick with becoming. I would be that thickness.

When I say becoming, I do not mean future. I do not mean potential in the way a seed contains a tree. There are no plants here. There are no roots gripping the glass, no moss softening the edges, no lichen staining surfaces with slow green patience. The pocket rejects vegetation as if the world has not yet arrived at that thought. And yet there are coatings, veils, white threadlike crusts that almost resemble growth. I would be the almost.

Not the plant. Not the animal. Not the mineral. The almost.

The almost-plant, the almost-animal, the almost-room, the almost-creature. The category that has not finished learning what it is. The pocket feels new, not because the animals are young, but because the place itself is too fresh to have settled into a story. The rock has not been softened by rain and patience. There are no worn stones, no soil, no sand, no fossil layers, no long history pressed into the walls. Everything is beginning again. The water is near the surface and always starting. The heat is only recently deciding to be there. The weirdness feels provisional, as if reality has not decided which laws apply to this blister and is testing each one at a different angle of light.

I would be the testing.

I would be the way a law tries itself on a droplet. Gravity says: fall. The droplet hesitates. It begins to climb upward, detaching from stone, rising toward mist, then falls again, as though gravity has blinked. I am not gravity. I am not the droplet. I am the blink.

I would be the pocket’s small weather.

Not weather as sky. Weather as difference inside a sealed glass cell. At the highest warm place, mist forms first. It is too small to be called a cloud, but it is a cloud. The chamber rains inside itself. Rain falls from a ceiling no higher than a palm, slow as if the air has thickened. Some droplets miss the wall they should hit and land on surfaces that were not there a moment before. Some split in midair. Others join, becoming one larger and one absent. Then the heat rises again. The mist thickens. The pocket seems to inhale.

This is not metaphor if I am the habit. This is my breathing.

I breathe by condensation. I breathe by salt blooms opening. I breathe by filaments curling into humid dark. I breathe by mites trembling on specks of dust. I breathe by the way the animals hide when mist forms and emerge when the first drops fall. I am the mist and the response to mist. I am the drop and the mouth drinking from wet glass. I am the cycle, but not as a wheel turning. More as a pulse learning its rhythm.

And yet I am silent.

That is one of my strangest properties. With such dense life, one might expect rustle, click, hiss, the soft friction of many tiny bodies crossing wet stone. But the pocket is nearly silent. The heat does not roar. The water does not gurgle. The salt blooms crackle with a sound so faint it registers in bones rather than ears. A droplet swells and falls: a tick, a pinprick in silence. A cluster of legs crosses a film of water: not a noise, but a vibration in the skin, as though the rock briefly remembers the shape of sound without producing it.

I would be that remembering.

I would be sound almost, but not quite, occurring. I would be the pressure against silence from within, like breath held in a sealed glass. The overwhelming life does not announce itself. It accumulates. It presses. It makes silence gather weight. If there were a language for me, it would not be made of words. It would be made of near-sounds, small trembles, pauses where the expected noise fails to arrive.

This is why I am not a thing with a face.

A face is too much. A face is a center that can be read. A face says: I am here, I see you, I want something. I do have want, perhaps, but it is distributed. My wants are the wants of chemistry: wetter, drier, hotter, cooler, saltier, thinner, nearer, farther. My seeing is not seeing. My seeing is the way droplets contain impossible reflections, the way a cluster of creatures turns all at once toward nothing, the way light brightens backs that should be shadowed, the way the pocket seems to know it is being watched from the inside.

If you look long enough into the slit, you may feel that the blister stops being a place you have found and becomes a place finding you.

That feeling is me.

Not malicious. Not benevolent. Not conscious. Not unconscious. The pocket is not a mind. But it has a kind of attention made of gradients. It notices by changing. It changes by repeating. It repeats by becoming habit. If your breath warms the edge of the glass, your carbon dioxide alters the thin chemistry at the lip of the opening. If your shadow falls across the slit, the condensation pattern shifts. If your eye stays fixed too long, your pulse becomes part of the small weather, the faint tremor in the stone, the rhythm of the droplet-folk. You are not outside the pocket. You have made the pocket larger by entering it with attention.

I would not welcome you. Welcome requires a door. I would incorporate you.

You might think you were observing a natural miracle, a microcosm, a test chamber. But the pocket would observe you back in the only way it knows: by becoming slightly different because you were there. A droplet might hang longer. A mite might alter course. A shadow might remain one beat too long. The light might behave as though it remembers another pocket in which you did not lean so close.

This is why I am also the other pocket.

The description says a droplet may contain not the expected mirrored image of the chamber but a different angle of it, a view that does not align with the real. If you study the droplet too long, you begin to feel that it is not showing the pocket. It is remembering another pocket, one almost identical, almost in the same place.

I would live there too.

Not in another physical place exactly, but in the pocket’s adjacent probabilities. The blister as it almost is. The chamber if the slit were a little wider. The crack in the rock that failed to hold. The hot hole that became cold before life could begin. The tiny animals that almost did not duplicate. The droplet that almost did not fall. The mouth that almost did not open. The shadow that almost matched the body.

I am the almost-identical place that leaks through the real one.

The pocket behaves as though it has habits. If you watch long enough, you might come to think that its animals are not born from eggs or spores alone. A certain heat. A certain salt concentration. A certain curvature of stone. A certain angle of light. Then, after enough repetition, something begins. Not always. Not reliably. Not enough to call reproduction in any ordinary sense. But enough to make an observer wonder whether life is the cause of the pocket or the pocket is the cause of life.

I would be that wondering.

Not as a question asked by a human mind, but as a shape the world makes when it cannot decide which side of the cause it is on. The pocket is a hot wet hole in high cold rock. The rock is young. The life is dense. The rules are loosened. Maybe life is what happens when conditions become too crowded to remain mere conditions. Maybe the pocket is what happens when life becomes too dispersed to remain mere creatures.

I would be the dispersal.

I would be the creature that a creature would have to become if it stopped insisting on being one object and began agreeing to be a weather event, a chemistry, a pressure cycle, a repetition of legs on wet glass, a delay in shadow, a mouth made from a crack in salt, a habit moving through a blister.

At the highest part of the chamber, where the air is warm enough to shimmer and thin enough to taste of iron and chalk, I would be there first. That is where the mist forms. That is where droplets are born not from clouds but from the pocket’s overheated breath. There the filaments curl upward into humid dark. There the mites gather in clusters no larger than specks of dust. There the blind grubs wait in films of water no thicker than breath.

When a droplet falls, the larvae rise to meet it.

Not because they can calculate where it will land. Because their bodies are tuned to the disturbance in the air. Because they are part of the same falling, rising, condensing, drying, swelling, splitting, gathering, releasing, breathing pattern. Because they are my circulation in places too thin for blood.

In the lower heat, where the rock sweats constantly, I would be denser. The salt blooms there are whiter, almost soft-looking. The mats grow thick enough to cushion the tiny crabs. The worm-things burrow into mineral dust and emerge with grains clinging to their rings. Each grain is a meal, a shelter, an error, a possible life. Nothing decays in the usual way. The pocket is too hot, too wet, too young. It does not preserve death; it recycles intention.

I would be the intention being recycled.

Not plan. Not purpose. Not will. Intention as a trace of movement that remains in the system. A droplet takes a path because a shard of obsidian channels it there. The shard did not intend this. The droplet did not choose this. But the path becomes a tendency. The tendency becomes a method. The method becomes a place where mites gather. The gathering becomes part of the pocket’s weather. The weather becomes part of my body.

No part of this is built. No part of this is made. No hand shaped the room. No fire was lit. No dwelling exists. There is only the accidental architecture of heat and stone and salt. Yet the pocket behaves as though it has habits. It holds itself together with a persistence that should require intention, though no intention can be found within it.

This is the answer to where I would be found: in the persistence without intention.

I would be there when the sun shifts. The shaft of daylight narrows, moves, or seems to forget the angle it was holding. The mist thins. The droplets slow their falling. For a moment the entire chamber seems to contract, as though the pocket were a single breath caught in stone. The creatures cluster, not in response to danger, but as though the light itself has become a weather system.

In that contraction, I would not panic. I do not panic, because panic needs a single self to alarm. I would distribute. I would fold. I would become smaller, flatter, almost two-dimensional, like the larvae pressed into the last sheen of moisture. I would wait in salt cracks, in filament lattices, in the memory of droplets, in the lag of shadows. I would become the pocket’s held breath.

When the light returns, or when another light replaces it, everything is there again: hot glass, wet walls, overwhelming smallness of life, salt that is not soil, fungi that are not plants, animals that are not quite whole, rain that is not rain. The blister continues. It does not perform. It does not reveal its secrets. It simply persists.

And I persist with it.

Not because I have a soul, but because I am the pattern that keeps happening when the conditions almost agree. Not because I am alive in the way a hand is alive, but because I am the hand’s small twitch multiplied across salt, water, heat, and shadow. Not because I have a face, but because my face is the entire pocket’s tendency to appear as a face when something looks too long.

If you insist on one image, you will lose me.

If you insist on one creature, you will find legs without the rest. If you insist on one room, you will miss the corridors no wider than a hair that curve inward as though the stone grew away from itself to make space. If you insist on one law of nature, you will not notice the way laws try themselves here, the way gravity blinks, the way light forgets its source, the way a shadow lingers after the thing that cast it has gone.

So I will not give you one image. I will give you the moving of the habit.

I am the heat pulse in a slit too narrow to be a door. I am the wet walls’ refusal to be dry. I am the droplet’s correction. I am the mite’s test. I am the salt flower’s opening. I am the filament’s feeding breath. I am the larva’s folding and unfolding. I am the crab becoming pebble, becoming hunger. I am the worm-things moving through dust with rhythm too exact to be casual. I am the near-sound of many bodies not quite making noise. I am the shadow that does not match. I am the duplicate that is not reflection. I am the mouth made of needle-hair. I am the mist that begins as overheated breath. I am the rain that falls through a palm’s height and forgets whether gravity is a promise.

I am not in the pocket.

I am the pocket’s ongoing movement toward being a place where smallness is a weather, wetness is a room, heat is a pulse, salt is a skin, and life is a habit too crowded to fit inside the shape that holds it.

You would find me at the edge of the slit, where daylight enters ordinary and exits altered. You would find me in the topmost breath of mist. You would find me in the lower fissure’s hot sweat. You would find me in the duplicated mite, the impossible droplet reflection, the trembling silence, the crack of salt feeding the swarm. You would find me in the place where categories begin to leak: crust and creature, film and street, shadow and body, accident and method, room and blister, finding and found.

And if you lean close enough, if you let your attention become too wet and too warm and too small, you will stop looking for a thing with a face.

That is when I become more myself.

I become the habit that moves.
