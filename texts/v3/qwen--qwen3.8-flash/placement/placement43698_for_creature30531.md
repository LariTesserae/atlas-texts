## The Keeper of the Almost-Answer

I would not begin by choosing a body. In the City of the Held Roar, bodies are less choices than consequences. A thing is noticed here, then repeated, then thickened by moisture, and by the time it thinks it is itself it has already been assigned a form by the basin.

At first, I would think I was still a traveler—someone who had climbed up to the high terrace where the clouds were torn into lower shapes, who had felt heat instead of cold, who had heard the basin before believing it. I would stand near the edge of a salt flat where the ground had been polished not by feet but by repeated attention. The light would come from everywhere and nowhere, steady as a lamp behind glass, turning humidity into something like suspended cloth. My voice would feel small there.

That would be dangerous. In this city, smallness is an invitation.

I would say something ordinary.

“I am here.”

Then the basin would try to improve it.

The city would not return my sentence intact. It would carry it through root-avenues and fungal knots, over still pools and grazing salt, into hollow chambers grown for exactly this sort of accident.
