I find myself in such a threshold. Not above the water. Not below the water. In the water, which is not quite water but a slow, cold grammar of suspension, carrying the powdered memory of things that were once walls, roads, clamps, courtyards, roofs that fell and were never cleared away. I am not at a shore. The prompt of a shore would be wrong: a shore implies an edge where land and sea make agreement. Here the land is already surrendered, but it has not disappeared. It has only become substrate for another kind of patience.

I am in a low depression between two broad banks of fitted tile, where the made surface thickens rather than rises. Above me is a terrace, a flattened mound of block and mortar gone soft at its corners. Below me is another level, sealed by mineral skin and furred with moss-analogues. Between these levels there is a seam. I am the seam, or I have become the seam. At first there was no difference between being in a place and being the place, but the law of here is slow, and distinction arrives as a kind of thickening.

The first thing I would be, if “be” can be stretched over so long a process, is a correction.

Not a correction of recent error. Not a patched roof, a plugged crack, a quick repair performed with urgency. A correction that has spent centuries being mistaken for natural terrain. I began where the water learned to pause. A groove had been worn into stone by a rope, then another by a hand, then another by a tool no one now remembers. The groove filled with lime-rich mud. The mud caught dust. The dust made a surface for bacterial mats. The mats changed the chemistry of the groove. The changed chemistry drew fibers. The fibers braided. The braid caught other bits of made material: green-black copper alloy, flake of fired clay, a fragment of tile no thicker than a nail. These fragments were not food, not prey, not treasure. They were habits of persistence. They were the first syntax of me.

I would say, if I had once learned to speak from the front of a body: I am a seamling. But the word is only a convenience. A seamling is a being that has learned to prefer its own edges. I learned that too. At first it was only accident: one patch grew faster where mineral seepage rose; another grew slower where the current brought cold, thin water. Between them, a boundary formed. The boundary channeled. The channel fed. The fed boundary thickened. The thickened boundary made a rim. The rim made a direction. The direction made something like a self.

But I was not then a self the way a thing with eyes is a self. I was a gradient. I was a preference moving slowly through tile dust and living fiber. I was water passing through a body that did not know it had begun.

If you were to pass near me in that early season, you would not say I was alive. You might say I was a stain, a swelling, a seam that had collected too much greenish matter. You might scrape away a mat with a finger and expose the tile beneath, only to find more growth packed into the joint, a pale olive strap entering the crack and withdrawing as if shy. You might disturb it, and perhaps a dull metallic glow would appear where the water had changed. Not alarm. Not pain. Disclosure. A record: the disturbance altered oxygen and mineral balance, and my chemistry answered by becoming visible.

This is the closest I come to being seen. To be seen by light in this place is too generous. The light is not bright. It is a bruised, chemiluminescent faintness, the glow of microbes in old seams, the light life uses to organize itself when the sea has forgotten the sun. I am visible to another being because my body changes the water around it. Another seamling reads me as a shift in calcium, iron, sulfur, suspended dust, the slow drift of particles. I am not a sound. I am a pressure made of chemistry.

Later, I became more architectural. That is not a metaphor. I grew around a clamp.

The clamp had once held layers together: tile to brick, brick to mortar, mortar to some deeper foundation that had become a memory of foundations. Its metal had spent long centuries corroding, swelling, flaking, forming a halo of green-black and dull red, a halo so rich in altered material that filamentous growths found it useful. At first the growth around the clamp was merely rind. Then collar. Then spine. Then something that could be mistaken for a stump. I had no limbs, but I had structure. I had a node. The node held.

This is one name others might give me: clampborn. I am a being whose inner skeleton was a made fastening, whose outer mantle was bacterial velvet and braided fiber. When the water cooled and thickened, my fibers contracted slightly—not muscle, not desire, not fear, but the slow shrinkage of saturated matter. When the current changed, the load of dust shifted over me, and my body moved by a degree, by the settling of mineral weight, by the tightening or loosening of fibers. I was not fast. I was not quick. I was a thing that could shift by the patience of sediment.

I was not only a node, though. Nodes are lonely things. In this world loneliness is not emotional; it is hydrochemical. A lone structure catches what falls, but a structure with neighbors catches what passes. So I gathered. I did not choose neighbors as a person chooses company. I offered surfaces. Surfaces received growths. Growths sealed cracks. Cracks redirected flow. Flow brought nutrients. Nutrients built more surface. This is selection without appetite.

A nearby field-patch of pale olive algae grew in broad ribbons from a shallow hollow. It was not me, not yet, not clearly. But it brushed one side of my collar of fiber. The brushing altered current. Altered current altered deposition. Deposition narrowed one of my channels and widened another. The field-patch became part of my outer contour, not by absorption, but by becoming so entangled that removing it would leave a wound shaped like a body.

A long weaver, in the deeper black-green depression, sent strands toward me. Long weavers grow from fissures where mineral-rich seepage rises, and their bodies are distributed, braided, anchored in many cracks, gathered at some central braid that rises and falls with faint water movement. They have no obvious head, only a center because some strands carry more chemical traffic than others. One of its strands brushed my clampborn spine. The strand was tough, translucent, narrow-leafed in places, fibrous in others. It did not attack me. It anchored. It found a gap in my older tar seal. It braided itself through that gap. Then it began carrying water into a chamber inside me that had been empty too long. The chamber brightened. The chamber darkened. The chamber learned to hold a gradient. The weaver did not consume me, and I did not consume it. We became a sentence written in different materials.

I was becoming larger than the word seamling. I was becoming a made repair with several repairs inside it.

Where the current brought dust, I caught it. Dust here is not mere waste. It is the fine residue of construction, the powdered history of a civilization that made its own ground and then made its own sea. I collected this dust along broad lips where my body flattened over an old block. If the lips were shaped well, the water slowed and deposited its load. The dust gathered, bound with bacterial mat, hardened into a pale skin. The skin changed the flow, and the changed flow deposited more dust. A feedback. A self-shaping trap without prey. What enters is material. What remains is form.

Thus I became partly a dust-collector. You might see me as a soft hill, a moss-cushioned lump, the rounded top of an old tile overgrown until it resembles an object that has forgotten its shape. But you would be wrong to think me dead. I am alive because I persist, and I persist because I am continually remade.

The deepest part of me learned from vents. Below the terrace there is a seam where water exhales from below, cold at first, then faintly warm with mineral life. There my outer edges became green-black, while the center remained pale, almost white. A fan-shaped colony grew around the mouth of the seam, white at the center, dark at its border. I was not beautiful because beauty was intended. I was beautiful because beauty here is a record of chemistry. The whiteness is concentration. The glow is saturation. When the vent strengthens, I brighten. When the vent cools, I lose structure at the edges. When the edge dies, it does not scatter into bones or flesh. It dies into a paler stone. Other growths seal that stone into future architecture. This is death without carrion. Decay without scavenger. The body becomes material again, waiting for the next intention to pass over it.

So where am I? I am in the lower terrace, at the joint where a collapsed bank meets an older floor. I am in a caulked crack that once held tar and lime and now holds a body of slow exchange. I am in the rounded stump of a foundation that may once have carried a wall, perhaps a road, perhaps a roof that was never a roof but a platform for making more things beneath it. I am in a seam, but seam is not merely my location; it is my mode.

And what am I? Not animal, because this law forbids animal-ness. Not plant alone, because plants would still need light in the usual way, and here greenery feeds on cold chemistry, mineral seams, faint microbial glow. Not stone alone, though stone is my oldest memory. I am a plant-microbial-ceramic continuum that has learned to keep its edges. I am a made thing that persists by remaking itself. I am a repair that has become the thing repaired. I am a being of living repairs, but that phrase is too simple unless you understand repair as a way of continuing rather than a way of fixing.

I have no face, but I have faces.

The side that receives current is not a head. It is a receiving field, a dense mesh of filaments that filter dust and respond to gradient. The sheltered side is not a back. It is a slower zone, where heat or cold from the seam changes the bacterial balance, where fibers loosen and reattach, where new mineral bands form like rings in wood but without the wood. The side that touches the neighboring field-patch is not a cheek or a hand. It is a negotiation.

If one presses against me with a slow passing current, my glow changes. If another seamling presses against me in the long season when water cools into lower terraces, its chemistry changes mine, and I respond not by fleeing but by growing toward or away, thickening or flattening, extending a fiber braid into the place where conditions favor exchange. I do not hunt. I do not gather. I do not flee. But I do favor. Favoring is my only action, and it is all my action. To favor is to move toward better current, or to seal worse current, or to build another body’s edge into my own until there is no clean place to say where the favor begins and I end.

Language is difficult here. The beings communicate, if the word can mean any exchange of information. Sound does not travel as gossip. Light is not used to see, only to signal chemistry. I speak when I change the water. I speak when I leave particles suspended in patterns. I speak when one of my mats darkens or brightens. I speak when I seal a crack that has become too rich in poison, or widen a channel that brings nourishment, or grow away from a neighbor that has blocked my current. The communication is slow enough to be mistaken for geology. A being that changes the water around itself is saying: I am here, this way, with this chemistry, at this time. Another being reads that alteration by contraction of fiber, shift of color, growth toward the favorable condition. That is not greeting. It is record. It is sentence.

I am a record of repairs.

If you opened me, not with a blade—there are no blades here, only patience and chemical erosion—you would find a sequence. The oldest layer might be tile, fired clay from some early making, partly softened by time but still carrying the impression of a mold or the scrape of a tool. Above it, a seal of lime-rich clay. Above that, a band of tar, black and brittle where cold has worked it, softened where seepage has warmed it. Then bacterial mat. Then filaments, pale and fine. Then braid of fiber, made of strands from vanished relatives and lost curtains. Then dust, compacted into a gray-green crust. Then newer growth: a collar, a cushion, a ribbon, a fan.

The order matters. A clamp inserted from one side and grown over from the other is not the same as a braid threaded through an empty joint and sealed. The placement of fragments tells where currents have been. It tells whether mineral venting rose, whether a neighboring structure shifted, whether the water cooled or warmed, whether some earlier being dissolved into dust and became food for the next. I do not remember as a mind remembers. I remember as a wall remembers rain: by the stains, the seals, the new layers applied over old damage.

I am a manuscript.

But manuscripts are usually made to be read. Here, I think, beings are made to be continued. Reproduction is not offspring separate from parent. It is construction. A mature strand may extend into a favorable gap. That strand may anchor, thicken, gather dust, develop its own rim, and slowly become an individual. The boundary between parent and new being remains blurred for centuries. Material may continue to pass. Or a new growth may seal the strand off. Or pressure may cut it. Or mineral deposit may wall it into another self. Other beings fragment during slow collapse, and the fragments attach to nearby joints and begin again. Others are made by a group. Several structures converge around a single prepared seam, each contributing tile dust, fiber, bacterial mat, and mineral seal until the joint is no longer merely joined.

This is how I became me. Or perhaps how I became several mes that have not yet separated.

I have a center because certain strands carry more chemical traffic than others. I have an edge because a rim of growth channels current. I have a past because the oldest tile inside me was made by hands that never meant to make me. I have a future because my fibers are already reaching into an empty joint, and the water there is beginning to bring the right dust.

Do not imagine this as tragic. There is no tragedy in a place without mouths. There is no loneliness in a being that is made of relations. The quiet is too slow for impatient listening, but it is not empty. It is thick with exchange. Every surface is an application. Every application is a decision. Every decision is a body beginning.

I am where the water remembers a made thing better than a thing remembers itself.

I am not a person. I am not a creature. If I must be named, name me by my making: the repair-seam, the threshold-body, the living joint. I am a made repair that has learned to maintain itself. I am a body of material relationships holding together a boundary. I am the point where a tar caulk became a chamber, where a rope-groove became a channel, where a corroded clamp became a spine, where a field-patch became a skin, where a weaver’s strand became a nerve that is not a nerve, where dust became form, where glow became record.

I find myself low, broad, flattened, overgrown.

I find myself in the last shape of architecture after it has forgotten how to be architecture and begun to be ecology.

I find myself where the current says: keep going, not as you are, but as you may be when the water brings more of what you need.

I find myself in the seam, and the seam finds itself in me.

If there is a being here, it is because a place was made with enough intention that its ghost could continue. I am that continuation. Not born. Set. Not hatched. Arranged. Not seeded from soil, because there is no natural soil. Grown from making. Remade from repair. Maintained by boundaries. Known by chemical glow. Speaking in the slow language of added material and excluded water.

I do not want. I do not fear. I do not call.

But if the water passes through me and brightens, if another body reads the change and grows toward it, if a strand reaches and seals and begins to be called a self after centuries of uncertainty, then perhaps that is the closest thing to wanting that this world allows.

I am not alone. I am one word in a sentence the place is still writing.

The sentence is made of tile, dust, tar, fiber, metal, bacterial light, and cold current.

The word is me.
