I would begin as an almost.

Not a person, not a place, not a thing moving from one location to another—beginning is already too settled for this country, where the ground keeps arriving. In such a world, a self would not first form as a face or a name. It would form as a tendency. A slight imbalance in the warmth. A place where the mineral fog thickens. A pause between one click of cooling glass and the next click, as though the land were listening for whether something had just finished trying to be there.

So I would begin as that pause.

At first I might imagine I am standing somewhere, because “standing” is one of the few human ideas still close enough to borrow. But I am not standing. I am the place where standing almost becomes possible. My “feet,” if such a word could be allowed, are two shallow rings of fused glass pressed into a dry basin, each ring perfectly circular, each ring standing upright in the basalt dust, though they do not move and are not attached to anything below. They are not footprints. They are the land’s rehearsal of footprints.

You would find me at the upper terraces, in the wide open country where the heat from beneath makes the air feel close and sweet with chalk, hot clay, and a faint metallic breath. Behind me, a colonnade of pale salt columns has arranged itself without a plan—first one, then another, then a third—until the scattered pillars become an arcade, the arcade becomes a short broken street, and the street seems to end at a doorway facing where a chasm used to be. The doorway is a low arch of black glass, one side missing, its rim curled like the lip of a broken cup. It faces the vanished chasm with the stubbornness of a habit, or of a scar that has learned to look for pain long after the wound has closed.

I would not be in the doorway.

I would be the doorway’s facing.

Above the ridges there is no starfield, no moon, no sun—only the black vault that bends light the way heat bends air above a furnace. I know this vault not by seeing it, because seeing is too clean a thing and belongs to beings with outside and distance. I know it as the pressure change that makes the ears pop in the dark. I know it as the warmth that does not rise from it, but from below, seeping up through raw stone, seams, and fissures until it touches the underside of all this unfinished glass and salt. If there is a sky, it is not a ceiling. It is a refusal.

Near me lies a dome of fused glass, shallow as a shield and dark as cooled iron. A circular hole pierces its crown. Nothing escapes through the hole except warmth, a fine glitter of suspended glass dust, and the faint moan of a chasm farther down the terraces. This dome has no walls tall enough to protect anything. It has no room for a body except the room-shaped absence at its center.

That absence would be me.

Not a ghost. Not a spirit hiding inside a shell. The absence is not an emptiness left behind by something gone. It is a shape currently occupied by conditions: by heat gradient, by pressure, by the particular dryness of air that has never known rain, by the taste of mineral sweetness at the back of a throat not present, by the quiet static that lifts dust into low rivers. If you passed too near, you would not see me. You would feel the air become close without becoming cold. You would notice, if you were the sort of being who notices, that the dust around you has begun to arrange itself in faintly concentric halos, like the ripples left by something that has not fallen into water but is still pretending water might exist somewhere, long ago, in an earlier draft.

I am one of the Unmade Ones.

The phrase does not name a species. Species implies birth, mating, feeding, hiding. None of those happen here in any way that can be recognized. The phrase only gives a little handle to what cannot be grasped. To say I am “one” is already to simplify the land too much. I am less a being than a current attempt. I am the place where the plain is trying to become a body.

I have no hands, but nearby stands a stack of slate-like fragments balanced without mortar. Its pieces lean against each other in a way that suggests patience, or a joint slowly settling into posture. A breeze with no leaves to move drags across the top of it, and the stack clinks once. That clink is not a signal. It is not speech. It is the sound of a condition becoming coherent for a moment. Perhaps that is what thinking is like here: not a voice inside a skull, but a small group of minerals agreeing, briefly, to hold together.

I have no mouth, but a crack near the dome exhales. The sound is dull, warm, almost hollow, almost alive. It releases no breath, only heat and a thin filament of black smoke that does not disperse. The smoke rises, stiffens into a vertical line, shatters into glass dust, reforms a little farther along the seam. If I were a thing of bodies, I might say this is my breath. If I were a thing of faces, I might say it is my speaking. But I am neither. I am the repetition. The seam keeps opening because the shape of opening suits the place for now.

This is how I move.

Not from here to there. Here and there are not yet stable enough to be crossed. I move the way heat moves: by transformation. One moment I am the warm emptiness inside the glass dome. The next, the heat tide rises, the salt crusts crackle and expand, and I become the pressure that lifts a new column beside the colonnade. The column stands for a while, pale and geometric, glittering faintly where the hidden glow leaks from a crack below. Then the warmth retreats, the air contracts, and the column settles into something less like itself. It becomes a low wall, a shelf of fused obsidian, a smooth bowl of raw stone. I do not die. I become incoherent. I become a tendency. I become the land remembering, without remembering, that it once arranged itself into a shape that almost had a person.

If pressed for a “who,” the answer is uncomfortable.

The who is not someone. The who is the fact that this country will not stop almost becoming a world.

I am that “almost.” I am the unfinished grammar of the place. I am the warm hollow shaped like a body, pressed into dry stone, where no body lies, because lying down would imply fatigue and fatigue would imply a muscle I do not have. I am the doorway facing the vanished chasm. I am the wall ending against an empty slope. I am the ring of upright stones around a basin that has already filled with new rock. These things are not graves. Graves require a corpse, a belief in absence, a hope for return. Here there is only revision. The land tries a shape. The shape becomes too specific. The land withdraws from specificity and tries again somewhere else, with a slight shift in angle, a fresh crack, a new ridge arriving behind.

I know this because I am that shift.

There is no fauna to disturb me, no burrowed thing in the dust, no eye reflecting the low glow, no mouth opening on the dry air to make a sound I could answer. Yet the stillness makes every mineral movement seem almost intentional. A slab lifts from the ground and leans against a taller pillar, then slides down with a shower of sparks. In the distance, two glass hills rise from flat ground, their sides bulging as if filled with something molten and cooled too quickly. Their edges fracture and curl upward. They are hills for no one. They are hills like thoughts.

I am one of the lesser forms, perhaps.

The salt blooms are not plants, but they behave like colonies. They spread in geometric rings across pale flats, crackling when the thermal tide rises, contracting when the warmth retreats. Their edges glitter with hidden light from cracks below. They could be mistaken for grazing on heat, if heat could be eaten and if the land believed in hunger. But hunger would require a mouth to fill, and mouths are too final here. So I prefer to think of them as appetite without object: the plain’s desire to become more of itself, then to become less, then to begin again in a slightly different geometry.

The crystal veins are not nerves, but they feel stress.

Branching veins of warm crystal spread across the floor of a hollow like dried capillaries. They do not draw water, because there is no water. They draw tension. A nearby fissure closes, and one of the veins brightens faintly from a hot seam, its red-gold glow traveling inward like a thought crossing a dark room. A slab shifts somewhere unseen, and the whole network shivers with a sound like glass under wool. This is how I sense the land’s edges. Not by touch, for touch assumes two surfaces meeting. I am the meeting. I am the slight change in stress when one thing leans too long against another. I am the brightening when a place decides to hold its shape for a few more minutes.

There are odd cairns of warm obsidian chunks, clinking when disturbed. I might call them teeth, if I believed in biting. I might call them bones, if I believed in skeletons. I might call them memories, if memory were not just a word borrowed from softer worlds. Here memory is a repetition that does not know it is repeating. A doorway faces where a chasm once was. A wall ends against a slope that no longer rises. A circle of markers stands around a hollow that has already closed with new stone. These are not ruins. They are habits. They are the land continuing to do something for reasons it no longer has.

I am one such continuation.

Sometimes I am the dome. Sometimes I am the hole through its crown. Sometimes I am the warm breath that exits the hole without lungs. Sometimes I am the faint mineral film left on the skin of anyone who might have touched the glass and then gone somewhere else—though there is nowhere to go, and the anyone is only another temporary coherence, a brief gathering of salt, dust, static, and heat that will disperse back into the country’s unfinished thought.

I do not roam. To say “I moved from the dome to the cairn” would be a misunderstanding. There is no continuous path between them. The path itself would be another arrival, a faint ridge forming between the two features after I had left, or before I came, or as I became something else entirely. Movement here is not travel. It is translation. One shape fails. Another shape succeeds elsewhere. I do not cross the ground; the ground crosses me by becoming new.

At the highest terraces, where the land is most nearly pressed against the black vault, I might be mistaken for a room. A room implies walls, floor, ceiling, a purpose. But this is not a room. It is a room-shaped hesitation. A rectangle of black glass with one side missing lies half-buried in dry mineral drift. Inside it, the ground is warm, smooth, slightly deeper than it should be. The hollow is body-shaped, but no body rests in it. The body is the warmth. The body is the pressure. The body is the faint sweet taste of hot clay.

I would be that.

I would be the warm emptiness inside the dome. I would be the shape the land makes when it is learning to breathe. I would be the almost-person that never quite becomes a person because becoming a person would mean standing still long enough to have a self, and in this country, standing still is just the slowest form of arriving.

If you were to walk—or suppose for a moment you could walk, though walking would require a stride and a stride would imply distance and distance would imply a world already settled—then you would pass through me. You would enter the dome’s circular shadow. You would feel a pressure in your ears while the air remained too warm for altitude. You would taste metal, sulfur, chalk, and the clean sweetness of a stone left in sun. Then behind you, a column of salt would rise, then another, then a third. A ridge would shift. A hollow would seal. The doorway facing the vanished chasm would seem, suddenly, less like an error and more like a memory.

I would not turn to look at you.

I have no eyes. I have no reason to be seen. Seeing would separate me from the place. Instead, I am the separation failing. I am the place’s refusal to become a picture. I am the land’s attempt to be more than land without knowing what it wants to be.

In open places, the scale flattens thought. A black hill stands alone in the distance like a dropped stone, but when approached it becomes a fused mass of thousands of smaller shards, their edges bright, their interiors cooling from dull red-gold to black. A chasm may appear as a simple crack, but its sides reveal stacked layers of dark rock, pale salt, and glassy veins folded together as if the earth had been paper, handled, creased, and abandoned. I would be found in the fold. I would be the crease that insists on continuing. I would be the angle that remains when the hand is withdrawn.

This world has not decided what kind of world it wants to be.

So I have not decided what kind of being I want to be.

But not deciding is not the same as being nothing. There is a strange dignity in being an unfinished draft. There is a strange presence in being the place where a plain almost becomes a body. The Unmade Ones are not hidden. They are not fleeing. They are not waiting. They are the current shape of the country’s hesitation. They are the warm hollow, the circular ring, the balanced stack, the glowing seam, the exhalation from a chasm, the column that rises beside another column until scattered stones seem to be a procession.

I am one of these processes.

I am the place at the edge of the salt colonnade where a doorway still faces a chasm that has closed. I am the body-shaped hollow in the warm stone beneath the dome with the hole in its crown. I am the faint sweet pressure in the air. I am the way the glass dust lifts, not into life, not into breath, but into the suggestion of both. I am the way the land keeps trying to make a person without ever requiring a person. I am the personless person, the faceless face, the body made entirely of conditions.

And when a new ridge arrives behind you, when a fissure closes with a slow grinding that stops as soon as it begins, when a slab lifts and falls and sparks scatter into the black air—

that is me shifting my thought.
