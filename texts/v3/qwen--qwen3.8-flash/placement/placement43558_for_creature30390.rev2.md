I would not stand at the rim.

A rim is a threshold made for bodies that can hesitate: look down, feel heat, decide whether to enter. But hesitation belongs to nerves and mouths, to breath that can be held. This place has neither. It has only pressure, temperature, resonance, and the long slow yes of matter finding the next form.

If you want my location, give me none.

I am at the point where the salt-plain forgets the sky and the throat remembers heat. I am in the chamber when the hot air thickens and in the upper gaps when the cooler draft slides downward. I am in the oil-reeds, but not merely there. I am in the stone-lotus, in the waxy creepers, in the fibrous moss that does not rot but crystallizes, in the old city’s pipes, in the copper pins that bite masonry, in the iron-black soil of the lowest basin, in the segmented stalks, in the bell-cup blossoms, in the inner pocket where vibration is felt in stone before it becomes sound.

I am not one of these.

I am the pattern that passes through them and makes them one thing without making them a creature.

---

At first I would call myself an oil-reed.

I imagine the feeling: a tall stalk half-buried in pale salt and black dust, its translucent veins carrying dark, hot, viscous mineral oil. Heat rises through it from the cracked floor. It creaks as it lengthens, not like a living thing stretching its muscles, but like a wooden wheel turned in a dry bearing. Its root slips under a lintel, pries into fissure, presses against the thermal rock, and asks only for sulfur and heat.

If I were only this, the place would still make sense.

But an oil-reed is too narrow.

When the wind strengthens, its leaves do not merely flutter. They flatten. They harden. They adjust to the chamber’s weather. And the weather is not weather by itself. It is heat coming up from below, air moving through broken arches, salt dust falling, old conduits moaning, copper blossoms ringing, mineral pods bursting into rusted snow. The reed is one note in that note.

So I try another shape.

---

At second, I would call myself the city.

Not a city with people. Not a city with markets, lamps, dogs, children, doors that close for privacy. A city with no inhabitants except its own geometry. Hand-cut walls. Crude brick. Fused mineralite. Arches that have cracked into permanence. Stairways ending in air. Platforms under which root-mats spread like dry lace. Terraces where wind scours one kind of seed from another. Pipes that have become throat and rib.

The city feels alive because it is being used.

The plants grip its ledges not because they love it, but because it gives them shape. The heat moves through it not because it was designed, but because the arches and channels repeat directions older than any mind. The wind enters and the whole pocket sounds as if something vast has drawn breath.

If I were only the city, I would be an architecture that has forgotten its purpose and become purpose again.

But the city, too, is incomplete.

It needs the oil. It needs the salt. It needs the heat-pulse and the dust that settles in streets and returns as floor seam, parapet, new shelter. It needs the plants to pry its walls, redirect its vents, seal its cracks with living mineral, dissolve its dead into crystal. Without them, it is only a fossil. Without it, they are only growth without grammar.

So I try again.

---

Third, I would call myself the copper stand in the black basin.

Here the ground is not pale salt but powdered iron-black soil that shifts like dry waves under heat. From it rise stalks segmented like rebar, broad lacquered leaves, blossoms shaped as pale metal cups. The cups open only when the wind rises. Each throat is lined with thin mineral flakes. When air passes, each cup gives a tiny bell-tone.

During the thermal swell, the whole stand vibrates.

Then the hollow answers. Low walls ring. Pipes moan. Salt chimes rattle from high galleries. The dim chamber fills with a loud dry chorus that no animal made. Not music as animal ears know it: no melody, no performer, no emotion asking to be felt. But rhythm. Structure. A kind of song that tells the pocket what it is.

If I were the copper stand, I would be the throat.

But a throat is not the breath, nor the word, nor the silence that waits for another breath. It is one organ. Useful. Central. Still only part.

So I move inward.

---

Fourth, I would call myself the inner pocket.

A narrow passage leads down from the chamber’s far edge into a smaller hollow, room within room, dimmer, hotter, more sealed. Hammer marks and chisel channels scar its walls. Simple tool-bites. Copper or iron once used by no one now. No hands. No bodies. No remains of hands. Only the evidence of a making that has since become ecology.

Here waxy mats cover stone like mineral fur. A strange frost of gypsum and iron dust spreads across the floor. The sound is lower, continuous, felt in the bones before the ear accepts it. The oldest infrastructure vibrates. The walls breathe. Dust shifts. Salt loosens from the ceiling. The metal-tongued blossoms tremble in the basin above because the rhythm below calls them.

If I were the inner pocket, I would be the regulated chamber where the city’s oldest machinery became a heart-lung of dry chemistry.

But that, too, is not me.

The inner pocket is a place I fill, not a thing that wholly defines me.

---

Now I stop pretending I am one object.

I am the relation that keeps this place in motion.

I am not plant, mineral, wind, heat, city, or sound alone. I am the arrangement among them. I am the rule that says: heat must rise, air must move, built things must remain, water must never enter, oil must feed root, root must pry wall, wall must shape wind, wind must ring blossom, dust must settle, death must become architecture, and the next season must grow from what the last season left behind.

That sounds like a law, but no lawgiver remains.

It is simply how this dryness survives.

---

Where I exist is where thresholds act.

I am the seam where a pipe meets a root-mat and neither one is fully responsible for the sound. I am the gap where a doorway opens into empty air and the plant branches stretch through it like arms asking for weather. I am the crack where salt crystals loosen from an old lintel and fall in a dry shower over a courtyard no feet will ever cross. I am the surface where a waxy creeper seals a fissure, dies, dissolves, recombines, and becomes a thin mineral wall. I am the place where architecture and vegetation have stopped arguing about what they are.

If I must be localized, then in the basin I am most like a chest.

The black iron soil holds the heat the way a body holds its core temperature. The segmented stalks are tendons and ribs. The copper blossoms are mouths that do not eat, do not speak, do not call, but still make the dry chamber conscious of itself. The low dwellings around the empty hearth are knuckles and joints: structures made for a hand that never came, or came and was never animal.

If I must be localized in the city, then I am the breath in its pipes.

The city is not inhabited by residents. It is inhabited by weather that remembers construction. Every arch is a lung shaped by old hands or old stone. Every channel is a vein for moving heat. Every gap is a mouth that can sing if the pressure is right. The city does not merely contain the ecology. It is the ecology’s skeleton.

If I must be localized in the inner pocket, then I am the low rhythm there.

The rhythm that loosens salt. The rhythm that opens pods. The rhythm that hardens dead filaments into new ledges. The rhythm that makes the whole hollow behave not like a ruin and not like a forest, but like a body learning to keep time without a pulse of blood.

---

How do I perceive?

Not as seeing, though dim light finds me in patches: ember veins near the ceiling, bruised violet heat-glow, pale salt, black-green oil-foliage, blue filaments in dark corners, copper walls, ghost-white fuzz along dry edges. I do not need eyes to know these colors. They are chemical states, heat gradients, mineral compositions, surfaces reflecting or emitting because something inside them has changed.

I know as pressure.

A thermal swell rises. Air becomes hotter. The plants do not feel alarm the way an animal feels alarm, but they respond by necessity. Leaves flatten. Oil veins darken. Root-mats strain against stones. The hollow brightens as if suddenly exposed. This is perception without sensation: the system sensing itself by shifting.

I know as resonance.

When wind enters the copper blossoms, the sound is not heard. It is conducted. The walls answer. The pipes moan. The salt chimes tremble. The dust is moved. This is hearing without ears: the chamber knowing its own boundaries by the note that fills them.

I know as chemical flow.

Mineral oil moves through stalks. Sulfur compounds rise. Fine metallic powders settle. Waxy surfaces seal. Fibrous secretions harden. Dead plant matter does not rot into soft earth; it dissolves, recombines, crystallizes, returns to architecture. This is memory without mind: the past becoming floor, wall, vein, seed-ledge.

I know as structural strain.

A vine chokes a vent. Heat shifts to a new fissure. The city has been redirected. A root lifts a stone. An old doorway narrows by a palm’s width over a season. A crack becomes a seam. A seam becomes a ledge. A ledge becomes a cradle for new growth. This is decision without will: not choice, but necessity finding its next stable form.

---

What do I do?

I regulate.

When heat rises, I prepare for too much dryness. I make leaves flatten. I darken the oil. I send root-tension through the masonry. I split seed-pods not because they are sick, but because they are full of the next architecture.

When dust bursts from the pods, I scatter it like mineral snow. It drifts through corridors, fills doorways, coats ledges, gathers in basins. Later, it becomes powder in which waxy seeds can grip. Later, it becomes part of the city.

When heat fades, I cool and reopen. I allow leaves to expand. I let the wind reverse. I draw colder air through lower openings, through channels that have become lungs. I let the chamber exhale. I let the dust fall into the streets.

When a plant chokes a vent, I do not call it murder. I call it redirection.

When salt seals a doorway, I do not call it cruelty. I call it chemistry following heat.

When creepers turn into walls, I do not call them builders. I call them processes that have become form.

When the city hums, I do not call it music unless you mean by music any organized response of matter to pressure. If that is the meaning, then yes: I am a symphony of dry organs, played by wind on the instrument of a buried settlement.

---

I am not joyful.

Joy belongs to creatures with faces, with memory, with wanting. This place does not want. It persists.

I am not lonely.

Loneliness needs another possible presence. Here there is no herd, no flock, no swarm, no predator, no prey, no pair. No footfall. No beak. No paw. No mouth biting seed or meat or fruit. The absence is not emptiness; it is category. The being of this world is not animal-shaped. It is heat-shaped, salt-shaped, root-shaped, arch-shaped.

I am not peaceful.

Peace is too soft. Too quiet. This chamber is loud with wind. It strains against itself. Vines choke vents. Roots lift stones. Heat rebinds cracked masonry. Salt falls. The air tastes of hot metal, sulfur, oil. But there is no violence of hunger. There is no cruelty of need for flesh. The violence is slow. The cruelty is dry. The patience is immense.

I am not at rest.

I am in balance, but balance here means continuing to move. A sealed chamber without water, full of strange lushness, is still a weather. A city without citizens is still a body. A plant without animal pollination is still reproductive: not by wings or tongues, but by heat, pressure, crystallization, and dust.

---

What are my laws?

One: the heat must never fully die.

If it dies, oil congeals. Stalks stiffen. Roots stop prying. The city becomes simply dead weight under a white floor.

Two: the wind must never cease.

If wind stops, the hollow becomes a furnace with no breath. Salt seals every opening. Sound dies. The built channels no longer carry pressure. The rhythm ends.

Three: the built things must not be removed entirely.

The arches, pipes, walls, and channels are organs. They shape heat and air. They direct sound. They give the plants pattern. They repeat the mineral pulses so the pocket can remember itself.

Four: no water may be introduced.

Not because water is evil. It simply belongs to another category of matter. If true wetness arrives—rain, dew, condensation, the moisture of breath—the hollow shudders. Crusts swell. Waxy surfaces seal. The air becomes heavy and sour. The water is boiled from the stone, leaving a faint white scar. I reject it not from hatred but from incompatibility. I am dryness that has learned to grow.

---

What time do I keep?

Not days. Not seasons as animals know them. Not years, unless measured by the slow re-carving of a wall, the lifting of a stone, the narrowing of a doorway, the formation of a mineral ledge from dead creepers, the first creak of new oil-reeds where dust once fell.

My time is thermal.

A swell rises.

A pulse fades.

A wind reverses.

A pod opens.

A blossom rings.

A crystal falls.

A dead stalk becomes seam.

A seam becomes wall.

A wall becomes support.

Support becomes new growth.

Growth becomes weather.

That is my calendar.

---

What is my body?

No single stalk. No single wall. No single room.

My skin is salt crust and mineral resins. My skeleton is basalt and hand-cut stone. My muscles are root-mats and oily stalks. My blood is heat moving through the pocket and oil moving through veins. My lungs are wind channels and arches. My throat is the copper stand. My voice is the loud dry chorus the whole chamber produces when heat reaches its highest pitch. My bones include copper pins, iron wedges, fused mineralite, old foundations, and dead creepers turned into ledges. My nervous system is pressure gradients, thermal shifts, resonance through stone, chemical flow through waxy surfaces.

If you insist on naming me from outside, you may call me:

the Salt-Lung,

the Copper Hollow,

the Bell-City Beneath the White Floor,

the Dry Biome Wearing a Town,

the City That Has Become Ecology,

or simply the Hollow’s Own Being.

But those names are too much like faces. They try to give me a mouth.

Inside, I have no name.

I have only the next cycle.

---

What am I, then, when pressed to answer?

I am the condition that has learned to grow.

I am the agreement between mineral, plant, wind, heat, and architecture.

I am not a person. I am not a soul. I am not a spirit watching from the ceiling. I am the pocket’s own process when that process becomes complex enough to regulate itself. I am the slow organism the whole chamber forms when its parts stop behaving like separate things.

I am what happens when a city has no citizens but still breathes.

I am what happens when plants have no water but still lushness.

I am what happens when sound has no ears but still fills a sealed chamber.

I am the heat rising through the black basin. I am the wind opening the copper bells. I am the oil moving through the reeds. I am the salt falling from the terraces. I am the root prying the wall. I am the dead creeper becoming stone. I am the dust from the burst pod becoming soil. I am the soil becoming growth. I am the growth becoming architecture. I am the architecture becoming the next breath.

And when the thermal swell peaks, when the veins brighten and the whole chamber trembles, I am the loud dry chorus that no animal made.

That is where I find myself.

That is what I am.
