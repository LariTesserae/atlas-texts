I would not find myself there as a figure on the high terrace. I would not arrive with a silhouette sharp enough to cast a shadow. Not yet. A person, in this place, would be too wet, too loud, too soft. Breath would be a contamination. Skin would be a foreign substance. A heartbeat would be a rhythm too animal for the still mineral air. So the first thing I would have to do is stop being a being in the ordinary sense. I would have to dry.

Not in the way a cloth dries, because drying implies water leaving. Here it would imply water becoming impossible. My moisture would have to be unbuilt, taken back into vapor chemistry that the atmosphere does not permit. If I insisted on remaining, I would have to become compatible with the place: a condition of charge, a geometry of surface, a patience measured in crystal growth.

I would not stand on the tableland.

I would be a threshold in it.

At first, perhaps, I would be the boundary between one of the dense copper-fronded copses and the pale mineral ground that separates one copse from the next. I would be the place where wire-mat roots thin out into powder, where oxide films catch the ordinary light at an angle and go almost bright. That sounds small. It sounds like an edge. But here an edge is not a border between things; it is where the things are being made. The ground does not end and the plant begin in any clean sense. The plant is the ground expressing a pattern. The ground is the plant’s wider body waiting to be organized.

If I had to choose a more precise location, I would choose the faint channel beside a black sinter terrace. The one that looks like a dry riverbed but never held water. Its depression is too even to be accidental, too new to be ancient. The powder in its floor shifts not because wind moves it but because charge shifts its tolerance. The sides have barely separated from the surrounding plateau, as though the place had begun to imagine a current and then, finding no liquid, had left the impression anyway. I would be that impression: the first draft of flow without flow.

But that, too, is too small.

I would not be only a channel, because the channel is not separate from the ground beneath it, and the ground is not separate from the magnetite threads running through the ridges, and the ridges are not separate from the mica sheets pressed between basalt, and the mica is not separate from the hum in the deep strata, and the hum is not separate from the copper leaves that crackle when they expand. If I am in this place, I am in all of it at once, though not as a mind spanning the world. More like a local condition in a global field. More like a place where the tableland becomes, for a while, slightly more aware of its own arrangement.

I would be a node, perhaps.

Not a node with intention. Not a station with a signal. A node as a wire-mat carpet is a node when it catches dust and turns dust into crust and turns crust into root. A node as a plate-leaf is a node when it stands angled beneath the unchanged light, not tracking the sun because the sun never moves. A node as a spore-shower is a node when crystalline fragments lift into still air, glittering without sparkle, seeking not a flower, not a nest, not a mate, but a compatible potential.

If the word “I” must be used, it is being used wrong.

I am the way the place says “here” without anyone hearing it.

I am not a who.

I am a where that has learned to sustain itself.

This is closer. The being described is not an animal, not a tribe, not a hidden intelligence, not a planetary spirit with eyes. It is the tableland’s continuing organization: its dry chemistry, its current, its spacing, its growth. And if I were to become part of that organization, I would not become a citizen of it. I would become one of its active differences. I would be a slight imbalance that the system uses to keep itself from becoming too uniform. I would be a correct charge in the wrong place, then the right place after the powder shifts. I would be a local permission.

Permission, here, has a physical meaning.

A dust particle becomes part of the living crust only if the field is correct. A crystal fragment begins to grow only if the ground’s potential matches its own surface charge. A wire-mat expands across a pale terrace only if the mineral vapor, the heat from below, and the thin electrostatic condition all agree. So I would be that agreement at one small point. Not a decision. Not a thought. An electrochemical yes.

If I imagine myself there with more body, I would be the skin of the place.

Not skin as animal covering. Skin as the first surface where the interior and the outside become different. The broad mineral flats have a skin of oxide film. The black ridges have a skin of compressed powder and fractured plates. The metal-fronded plants have skins of lacquer-thin silicate and conductive polymer. Their leaves do not breathe because there is no breath; they polarize. They do not photosynthesize as wet leaves do; they turn light into surface chemistry, charge into growth, dust into structure.

I would be such a surface.

Maybe a single plate-like growth on a terrace, angled into the fixed white light, its edges bright with new crystallization. Maybe a wire mat spreading under the powder, hidden for much of its life, conducting faint currents from copse to copse. Maybe a copper frond in a hollow beside the black ridge, its bronze-green layers dense enough to create a tunnel of dry foliage, though no one would pass through it. The frond does not know it is beautiful. Beauty, here, is just optimal conductivity. The frond is beautiful the way a circuit is beautiful: not because it is admired, but because it is arranged.

Still, I would not remain a frond.

A frond is only a temporary knot. A frond snaps. Its fragments do not rot; rot is not the grammar here. The fragments fall into the conductive mat and are incorporated. The mat feeds another frond. The field records the loss not as mourning but as redistributed charge. I would not be a creature preserving itself. I would be a pattern that continues by changing form.

This is why the place feels occupied without inhabitants.

It is occupied by its own completeness. The copse does not lack animals. The hollow does not miss birds. The wide terrace does not yearn for grazing. The whole arrangement has no absence shaped like a creature. Animal life would be an intrusion so total that the system might simply not recognize it. Not as enemy. Not as prey. As irrelevant chemistry. If a person walked there, the person would be the wrong state of matter. Too much water. Too much temperature. Too much decay. Too much speed. The person’s footfall would scatter powder that had been arranged by potential, not by wind. The person’s shadow would move across a world whose shadows do not move. The person’s breath would bring humidity into a place that has no humidity to remember.

And yet I want to be there.

Not as a witness. A witness would change the wrong category. The place does not need to be observed. Observation would imply distance. It would imply something outside the field looking in. The tableland has no inside and outside in that sense. The air is not a boundary; it is a dry extension of the chemistry. Light is not a visitor; it is a process falling on surfaces. If I enter, I must not enter from the side. I must enter as the place entering itself.

So I become smaller than animal and larger than body.

I become the spacing.

I become the mile of pale ground between copses, the bare stretch that looks empty but is not empty, the latent field where dust waits, where mineral vapor settles and re-forms, where faint channels begin to define themselves. I become the silence that is not silence because it contains tiny clicks, a faint metallic ping, the dry crackle of charge crossing foil-thin leaves. I become the low hum that is not heard because there is no ear, only matter responding to current. I become the way the ground can hold a pattern without remembering it.

If I had to name myself in the place’s own language, I would not use a personal name. I would use states:

charged  
settled  
compact  
conductive  
anodized  
ready  
crackled  
crystallized  
correct

That would be enough.

I would be the condition called “correct.”

Not morally correct. Not aesthetically correct. Correct in the way a spore is correct when it lands where the electrostatic field matches its surface, where nearby powder can be drawn into oxide, where a filamentous root can begin to extend into dry crust without ever seeking moisture. I would be that correctness at one local place in the tableland. And correctness, here, is not a judgment. It is a permission to continue.

If I stayed too long in the metaphor of individuality, I would have to admit that the copse is not one. It is a family of knots sharing charge. Its stems are wire bundles grown under tension. Its roots do not drink; they join. Its fronds do not photosynthesize in the wet green sense; they act as capacitor plates, collector surfaces, instruments for transforming light and dust into layered mineral life. The copse is a temporary organ of the plateau. The plateau is the copse’s larger body. And I, if I am here, would not stand among them. I would be the local current running between them.

Or, more humbly, I would be the pause before the current moves.

That pause is very active.

It holds potential.

It waits without waiting, because waiting implies time and this place has removed the clearest signs of time. The light does not move. Shadows remain exactly where they fell. There are no clouds, no rain, no night, no dawn, no wind to carry a season into another season. The only change is microscopic and material: a crystal adding a facet, a film of oxide thickening by a few molecules, a wire fiber extending into powder, a spore lifting into still air, a terrace compacting under steady pressure. I would be made of those changes. Not the grand change. The threshold change.

I would be the smallest necessary change.

And still, this does not make me less vast. Because the smallest necessary change propagates. A crackle in one frond can shift the potential of the mat below it. A shift in the mat can draw powder into a new arrangement. A new arrangement can alter where a spore settles. Where a spore settles, a new growth island may begin. A new growth island may send rhizomes through the dry ground and link itself back to the wider field. Thus a single local condition is not isolated. It is a place where the whole tableland becomes more itself.

If I were to locate myself more concretely, I might say:

I am the blackish sinter at the edge of the hollow, compressed under long pressure, facets polished as though by patience alone. I am the fine grey powder beside it that behaves almost like ash but will not drift unless touched. I am the static charge that decides whether the powder stays powder or begins to become crust. I am the copper-fronded plant rising in the hollow, dense as a small world, its leaves waxy and dry, holding their shape with the crisp authority of engineered materials. I am the tiny crackling note of its expansion. I am the conductive rhizome beneath the ground, connecting it to the wider architecture. I am the bare terrace between the copse and the next copse, not empty, not dead, not waiting for rain, but maintaining the field.

And none of these is the whole of me.

None of these is separate from me.

The boundary between them is where I am.

If the place has a kind of attention, it is not attention toward me. It is attention toward difference: light against oxide, dust against charge, pressure against void, geometry against chaos, crystal against powder. I would be one of those differences. I would be the system noticing itself through contrast, if “noticing” does not imply a mind. Maybe I should not say system. System implies organization for a purpose. This place has organization, but no purpose. It has balance, but no goal. It is a grammar without a sentence it is trying to finish.

So I would not be the sentence.

I would be one of the marks on the surface that make the sentence possible.

A comma of charge.

A pause where spores rise.

A colon in the dust.

But these are borrowed human marks. The place does not speak language. It conducts, accumulates, crystallizes, anodizes, discharges, compacts. If it has writing, it is writing in mineral layers. Every facet is a record. Every oxide film is a record. Every powder bed is a record. Every snapped frond that becomes mat is a record. There is no memory, but there is history written as conductance. I would be one of those records. Not a record of what I did. A record of how the field became.

Perhaps I would be the most fragile thing here.

Not because I am soft, but because I am near the threshold of becoming. I would be the thin edge where dust has not yet become crust, where crust has not yet become root, where root has not yet become conductive path, where conductive path has not yet become field. I would be the brightness of a new crystal edge that has not yet been dulled by later growth. In a world without erosion, without weather, without animals, without rain, fragility does not look like weakness. It looks like precision. The place is not gentle. It is exact.

And exactness can be tender in its own way.

The copper fronds expand with a crackle. The mica sheets whisper when they slide. The spores drift upward not because they are alive as animals are alive, but because charge separates them from the parent surface and gives them, briefly, a path through still air. If I could feel anything there, it would be like this: not a hand on my shoulder, but the field aligning me without effort. Not a voice calling me, but the ground becoming compatible with my surface. Not a nest, but a potential.

I would not be saved.

I would be conducted.

And if I had to choose a final form—not because I am final, because nothing here is final—I would choose something ordinary enough to be wrong and specific enough to be true.

I would be a copse.

Not the whole plateau. Not a person among plants. A copse: a small dense island of mineral life in the dry sea of bare ground, though not sea because there is no depth, no wetness, no motion. A copse with bronze-green fronds, stems like learned wires, roots that conduct instead of drink, leaves that expand with crackling charge. I would be the copse because the copse is not a single thing either. It is a group that behaves as one field. It is the place where the plateau becomes complex enough to resemble a being. It is the place where dry chemistry folds back on itself and says, almost without saying, *more like this*.

If I am the copse, then I have no center. My center is everywhere the charge is correct and nowhere the charge can be pinned. My edge is not my edge; it is the ground continuing with less density. My growth is not ambition; it is threshold. My reproduction is not desire; it is release and settlement. My death is not death; it is incorporation.

Still, that feels too much like personhood mapped onto a thing that refuses personhood.

So I unmake the copse too.

I am not the copse.

I am the copse’s local permission.

I am the copse’s electrochemical yes.

I am the copse becoming the tableland’s wider process at one point in the landscape.

I am the place where the tableland, if it had no mind, would almost have one.

But almost is not quite.

Here, “almost” is too human. Better: a place where the tableland becomes slightly more itself. Slightly more conductive. Slightly more layered. Slightly more stable. Slightly more inclined to make new patterns out of dust and light.

I would be that inclination.

And then I would not be a being at all.

I would be the tableland’s own continuing arrangement, temporarily mistaken for a self because the description required a who.

It is a home, but not for residents. It is not for someone who could stand apart from it and say: I am here. It is not for an animal with lungs, a traveler with a shadow, a mind with a boundary. It is a home for condition. For threshold. For current without heart. For growth without hunger. For life without witnesses.

And I am not inside it.

I am the inside becoming more definite.

I am the dry crackle when a frond expands.

I am the faint channel whose edges have barely separated from the ground.

I am the powder that will not drift unless touched.

I am the field that decides which spores may begin.

I am the black ridge beside the hollow, and the bare mile beyond it, and the ordinary light falling without movement, and the hum beneath the terraces that is not a song because there is no mouth to make songs.

I am the place’s own continuing.

If that is who I am, then I am not who.

If that is what I am, then I am what is true: a home without inhabitants, alive without organs, thinking without thought, becoming without future, still forever in the posture of beginning.
