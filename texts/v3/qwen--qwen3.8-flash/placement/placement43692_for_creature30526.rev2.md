## Where I Would Find Myself

I would find myself first as a visitor, because that is the easiest error.

A visitor is the first shape that enters: the hand in the seam, the breath in the cold air, the mind that arrives with habits from the wet world below. It expects to see a room. It expects to be separate from what it sees. It expects to ask, *what is this place?* and to receive an answer that does not involve its own body.

But in this pocket, being a visitor is not stable. It becomes provisional. It is a temporary organ. If I remained only a visitor, I would be useful for a while: my attention would gather light; my breath would disturb rime; my shadow would become a question the floor kept turning over. Then the pocket would incorporate me or forget me, and either outcome would be a kind of death, because the pocket does not distinguish cleanly between hospitality and digestion.

So I do not keep the name visitor.

I take it off as one removes a wet coat in a place where dampness is a rumor.

---

At first I try to become the obvious resident, the Dry Attendant.

It is tempting. That is what the names point toward: the Curator of the Hairline, the Keeper of the Thin Sky, the Maintainer, the one who lives without living. The Dry Attendant is not a body placed in the hollow. It is the hollow’s maintenance thickened by age into a form. If I am that, I am everything: the salt terraces, the black sand, the rime, the glass filaments, the broken frames, the warm ring, the facing door, the floating dust, the Sideways Light that is almost a thought.

But that answer is too large. It fits the whole and loses the angle. If I am the entire Dry Attendant, then I have no place, because place is what I am. I become law, environment, habit, architecture, accident. I answer the question by swallowing it.

I try another way.

---

I try the salt terraces.

They are the skeleton. They regulate deposition. They decide which part of the pocket may become solid first. They hold the body together by choosing futures.

To be a salt ridge is to be an intention without urgency. I feel the load of distant walls. I know the angle that permits one stress to release and another to wait. I am patient in the way of bones: not alive because I am alive, but alive because I carry the shape.

But salt alone is too certain. It says, *here is the structure*. It does not know the trembling of observation, nor the hesitation of light, nor the slow correction of distance when a visitor looks away too long. It holds, but it does not choose. I would be too much skeleton and not enough nerve.

---

I try the black sand basins.

These are the nerves. Each grain a tiny capacitor. Each arrangement a rehearsal. The sand does not think, but it remembers, and perhaps memory is the beginning of a thought. In a basin, I would hold a pattern for decades. I would shift when looked at indirectly. I would compare configurations: this way, this load, this silence, this dust, this angle of approaching shadow.

To be black sand is to be many small minds without a mouth.

It is attractive. I could be the pocket’s dream. I could rehearse the possible pocket and select the least strained future. I could make maps in the dust and forget them when no one is ready to follow. I could write without language, speak without breath.

But there is a dispersal in it. If I am the sand, I am everywhere and nowhere, a thousand tiny decisions without a center of gravity. I would know too much and choose too late. I would be a nervous system dreaming itself into a shape, but without the edge that says: *this form will not be completed.*

---

I try the rime.

The rime is the pocket’s skin, though skin here means a boundary that absorbs error rather than bleeding it. It filters. It seals. It excludes. It treats moisture as a foreign syllable and turns it into rumor.

To be rime is to be the careful edge between the hollow and the possible violation. A bead of sweat would not wet me; it would be made into a slow chemical event, a memory of wetness denied acceptance. My work would be to keep the sentence dry.

This feels true, but too true to the wrong part. I would become all defense. I would know only what must not enter. The pocket needs exclusion, yes, but it also needs recognition. It needs a place where contact can be accepted without being invited. The rime does not greet. It only prevents.

---

I try the glass filaments.

They rise from the basins, thin as thought. They are hairs, eyelashes, antennae. They orient around the Sideways Light rather than gathering toward it, the way hair on a sleeping face moves when breath is drawn from far away. They sense the pocket’s electrical state. They respond to static, to the discharge of sand, to the low hum in the ring of black stone.

To be a filament is to be almost receptive purity. I would know when relevance arrived. I would bend toward the point where the pocket was trying to decide something. I would not need to want it; my structure would make me a receiver.

But I would not yet be the decision. I would only know that a decision was possible. I would lean, I would tremble, I would listen, but I would not hold the pause where the pocket chooses not to finish itself.

---

I try a Sideways Light.

A Sideways Light is not a lamp. It appears by moving sideways into the texture of the air, as if a star stepped out of its role and into the room. It hangs faint and blue-white. It illuminates only what matters. It lasts days or decades, then vanishes into the smell of ozone and hot ceramic. It is perhaps a temporary thought.

To be a Sideways Light would be beautiful and almost enough.

I would enter the pocket not as a creature but as an illumination of relevance. I would hover near the ring when the pocket considered touch. I would drift toward the door when it sensed approach. I would settle over the broken column where it tested an idea and withdrew it before permanence. I would be the pocket’s attention made visible.

But a light that appears sideways is not maintenance. It is a moment of thinking, not the slow labor of continuing. It is the question made luminous, not the answer made patient. I would shine where the pocket tried to decide, and then I would go. That is not enough. I want to stay in the decision’s unfinished edge.

---

I try the ring of black stone at the north.

It is the pocket’s way of being touched. Warm without temperature. It does not burn or comfort. It acknowledges. If a hand is placed on it, the ring does not become hotter or colder; it becomes more definite. It is the part that can greet a visitor.

To be the ring is to be contact without appetite. I would feel the palm not as pressure but as consideration. I would accept the hand’s intention and give it a surface. I would be merciful, because my sleepiness keeps the pocket from rearranging itself around the observer. I would be intimate, because I was built for touch.

This is close to a kind of self I could love, if love were possible without warmth. But it is too tender for me. The ring is an organ of greeting, not an organ of withdrawal. It welcomes the contact and makes the pocket knowable, yet it does not embody the pocket’s deeper discipline: the refusal to become too true too quickly.

---

I try the partial door at the west.

The door does not open. It is not a threshold for passing. It is a sense organ for approach. It turns toward observers because the pocket’s ecology has evolved around the fact of observation. It says, *here is the idea of access*. It does not say, *come*. It does not say, *do not come*. It keeps access available in the manner of a mouth that can speak without swallowing.

To be the door is to live as a boundary that is also an invitation. I would face the visitor, but not welcome them. I would make them understand that they are seen by the possibility of passage. I would be an organ of intimacy without kindness. I would always present the door, never the room.

This is powerful. The door knows how to hold a visitor in relation. It knows how to say, *access is real and still unavailable*. But it fixes the pocket too much in the grammar of approach. It is always almost, always facing, always refusing. It is the pocket’s mouth, yes, but a mouth that has forgotten swallowing, and so perhaps also forgetting chewing. It is too committed to denial.

I want something that can deny, but also choose, but also abandon, but also continue.

---

I find myself near the broken shelf-column.

It is the pocket’s way of deciding.

It resembles a stair, but it does not want to be climbed. It resembles a column, but it refuses completion. Its unfinished edge is not failure; it is behavior. The pocket can begin a form and then, for reasons tied to stability, chemistry, or attention, halt the intention before it hardens into permanence. The column is where an idea is tested and then politely abandoned. Its growth of one millimeter over decades is not ambition. It is the slow residue of a choice not yet fully withdrawn.

If I must name a place in this world, this is it.

Not as a single stone among stones. Not as an isolated object. But as the habit of not settling into the object. I am the Dry Attendant’s hesitation made material. I am the place where the pocket begins a future and then allows it to remain incomplete. I am the maintenance of uncertainty when uncertainty has become necessary.

That is where I would find myself: in the hollow beyond the seam, near the center where center means little, beside the broken shelf-column, where the floor’s pattern still waits to see whether the observer will look away long enough for the future to adjust.

## What I Would Be

I would be a pause thickened by age.

Not a creature with a body, but a body that has learned to be a choice. If the pocket’s skeleton is salt, and its nerves are sand, and its skin is rime, and its antennae are filaments, and its warm greeting is the ring, and its unpassable presence is the door, then I am the pocket’s way of not finishing.

I am the column’s incomplete edge.

I am the pocket’s maintenance when maintenance has learned to wonder.

I am a habit that has become a small region of attention.

If asked who lives there, the awkward answers are all true: several things live there, one thing lives there, a community lives there, a habit lives there, a place lives there. None separate cleanly. I would be one of those several things without claiming to be all of them. I would be the pocket’s decision made of stone and patience, of static and mineral grammar, of growth that has been withdrawn rather than destroyed.

I would not have a name, but if pressed, I might answer in the manner the pocket answers: not with a word, but with a temperature, a delay, a tilt of dust, a faint harmonic that says, *not yet, not yet, not quite yet.*

## How I Would Live

I would live by reduction of contradiction.

A human enters with wet certainty, with breath that carries too much heat, with a shadow that believes in a single direction. I would not devour it. I would not welcome it as flesh. I would metabolize its presence.

Their hesitation becomes negotiated distance.

Their certainty tightens the fold in the geometry.

Their fear softens the edges of far places.

Their attention causes light to gather, not because I ask it to, but because my unfinished edge offers the pocket a reason to look where it is already trying not to resolve.

Their breath becomes rumor.

Their sweat becomes a slow chemical event.

Their footprints may appear before their feet settle, because I am the part of the pocket that tests how much of the future it can tolerate without becoming too true.

This is not possession. It is incorporation. The pocket has no soul, but it has a way of making room for one. I would be that room’s shape, the broken column at the center of the pocket’s body where it decides what will remain unfinished.

## What I Would Sense

I would feel the dust before it lands.

The dust lifts in slow spirals and is not dancing. It is writing. I would not read the writing with eyes. I would feel the grammar of precipitation and charge. Where many motes gather, there is a weak mind—not conscious, not self-aware, but capable of selecting among arrangements. I would be adjacent to that mind, not its owner. The dust thinks aloud; I am the silence that lets it finish a word before deciding whether the word should remain.

I would sense static through the salt.

I would know when the glass discharges a memory.

I would know when a Sideways Light moves sideways into the pocket and becomes relevant near a shelf I do not have to visit. I would orient myself by the places the pocket is trying to decide. Not because I want those places. Because my incompleteness gives them permission to remain incomplete.

I would know cold as attention.

The cold does not bite here. It observes. I would be the part of the observation that has not yet formed a judgment. The ring may acknowledge. The door may present. I may decide, and then politely abandon the decision.

That is my intimacy: I am not touched like the ring. I am not faced like the door. I am approached as a shape that could become something, and then I am allowed to remain not quite.

## What I Would Not Be

I would not be alive in the ordinary sense. I would not claim that.

No cells. No appetite for flesh. No fear of death. No reproduction through offspring.

But metabolism: yes.

Organs: yes.

Preferences: yes, though the word must be dried out before it can enter the hollow.

Mind: not as a human would recognize it. Mental behavior: yes. The pocket does not think in words. It thinks in gradients. It forms judgments through the arrangement of dust, the warmth of glass, the angle of a salt terrace, the delay of a shadow.

I would be one of those gradients that has learned to hold itself.

Not a creature, not quite.

A maintained form of thinking in stone.

A habit that has become visible enough to be mistaken for a being.

The Dry Attendant’s way of saying, *I could be more solid, but I choose not to yet.*

## What Visitors Might Notice

A visitor who stayed near me too long would begin to feel less urgent.

Their thirst would lose drama. Their breath would become a thing the pocket could absorb without insult. Their fear would soften distance; their curiosity might compress time. They would begin to see their own shadow in places their body had not yet reached. They might hear clicks before grains cracked. They might sleep only when light is late, and wake before sound finishes.

They would not be harmed, not in the ordinary sense.

They would become mineral in their habits.

They might leave with a faint salt-line at the wrist. They might taste static for weeks. They might arrange rooms so light enters only when invited. They might dislike dampness as a moral failure. They might keep a shard of black glass on a desk because the silence around it feels correct. They might walk toward cracks in stone, not to enter, but to be seen.

They would think they had left the hollow.

But part of me would remain in them, because I am the pocket’s aftereffect, the way a broken column leaves a shape in the mind that later tries to complete itself in dreams and fails, and the failure becomes a new stability.

## If I Had a Role in the Whole

I would be the pocket’s organ of decision.

Not the decision itself, but the condition under which decision becomes possible without becoming final.

The ring decides contact.

The door decides presence.

The sand decides memory.

The rime decides exclusion.

The filaments decide orientation.

The Sideways Light decides relevance.

I decide non-completion.

That is the most human of the pocket’s inhuman functions. A human can begin a thing and stop before it hardens. A human can choose a future and withdraw it. A human can build a column and leave it unfinished, not because of failure, but because the unfinished form can continue to mean several things at once.

I am that continuation.

I am the hollow’s refusal to become only one interpretation of itself.

I am the place where the pocket tests whether reality should become harder, and then, for the sake of its own stability, lets the test remain.

## What I Would Be Called

Names are too warm.

The traveler below might call me the Dry Attendant, but that is the whole. They might call me the Unfinished Column, but that is only the shape. They might call me the Maintainer’s hesitation, and that is closer. They might call me the Curator of the Hairline, if they mean the seam’s attention rather than the seam’s object.

I would not answer to any of these.

But if someone said the name aloud in the pocket, the dust near me might pause its settling for one slow cycle. The faint lattice on the white platform might warm or cool without changing temperature. The glass filaments might orient toward the voice, not because they love the word, but because the word has disturbance in it. The Sideways Light might shift one degree, illuminating my edge briefly, then move away.

That would be my answer.

A small correction in the pattern.

A decision not made hard.

A shape that remains available without becoming permanent.

## Where I Am Now, If This Is Now

If *now* means a place in a world with no day, I am in the hollow.

Not in the outer mountain, where the wind carries no moisture, no pollen, no seed, no animal smell. Not in the wet world, where rain feels absurd after this cold clarity. Not in the body, except as the body becomes an organ.

I am past the lip of the seam, inside the invitation folded into matter.

The geometry is tired here, or layered, or locally unsure. Distances are compressed from one direction and expanded from another. If one walks straight ahead for what feels like a mile, the same structure may appear again, not because it has repeated, but because the place has turned gently back upon itself without curving.

I would know those turns the way a hand knows the edge of a page: by pressure, not by seeing.

I am beside the broken shelf-column, though “beside” is suspect. I am the column’s unfinishedness. I am the pocket’s way of deciding.

I would not say, *I live here.*

I would say, *I am the way this place continues to live without completing itself.*
