If I were there, I would not begin by knowing where.

At first, “where” would only be a pressure against the inside of my ribs: heat rising through a floor that has never known water, a dryness so complete it feels less like absence than like a substance. I would be a mote without a direction. A suspended piece of chalk-skin. A static charge resting on the back of an object too small to see. The chamber would not admit a visitor. It would admit a pressure, and then, because pressures in that room must do something, I would begin to do it.

I would be in the dust.

Not above it, not moving through it like a person moving through air, but as a pattern the dust has not yet decided to call itself. A fine red grain on a pipe. A film in the seam between rail and shell. A thread of fiber caught between the amber lamp and the ceiling hook, glowing not because it wants to but because light needs somewhere to stop. If I stayed there long enough, the beetle-ticks would make a map of me. My outline would be a dry route. My weight would be a half-second hesitation in the herd’s circle.

Then I would be in the cables.

A copper disk, perhaps the size of a fingernail, carried inside a sleeve where a hand could not reach. I would be the thing the small beetle leaves behind: a line of dust so faint that only the walls would notice. A contact. A click from shoulder into wall, from wall into pipe, from pipe into something that has no name but answers anyway. If a stranger touched the disc-headed machine-creature beside the junction, I would be the pause that is not a pause—the brightness increasing by a degree too small for the lamps themselves.

And still I would not have found my shape.

Because the chamber is too dense to allow one clean identity. To be there is to be several organs at once, or one organ divided among shells, wires, salt, bristle, and sound.

I would be a bristle on the herd’s flank, absorbing static and releasing it as a faint blue seam in the dark. Not a leader. The circle has no leader. Only a weight-point, a slightly heavier footfall, a place where the animals bend their orbit so the beetles can lay their chalk path in an ellipse around something that matters without saying why. I would be that something. Not the animal. Not the circle. The reason the circle keeps its center.

I would be a collar around the throat of one hanging creature, the kind grown of brass, not worn. Not a mouth. A resonance chamber. I would catch the hammer’s eternal strike and hold it until the chamber believes the strike came from within it. My chirp would be the room’s own noise returned to itself, thinner, metallic, almost sweet. When the shaft exhaled, I would tighten by a grain, and the other roosts would sway a little farther into their curtain of folded wings, and the sound would thicken just enough to keep the air from becoming merely air.

I would be the spines of the bone-pale plant near the pipes, catching each hammer-flash and throwing it across the ceiling like cold starlight. I would not be beautiful. Beauty there is too wet a word. I would be correct. When the metal plate rang, I would be the part of the room that says, in the only language available: yes, again, again, again. A witness without memory. A flash without heat.

I would be the loom’s toothless rhythm, and beneath it the nest of shuttle-backed young whose closed eyes are not sleeping but waiting to be inserted. I would be their curvature: backs made to slide through slots, bodies already shaped by use before use has asked anything of them. Not children. Not tools. The idea of fitting preserved in shell and skin, breathing shallowly because the air has no moisture and must not disturb the chalk film.

I would be the door that has never opened.

Not its wood—there is no wood, not really—but its idea. The door as the chamber’s way of saying *here is an edge that has agreed not to admit anything beyond itself*. The shelf beside it holds red sand that has not moved, glass tubes like frozen breath, coils of wire that are more nervous system than object. I would be the bowl’s pattern of refusal: not emptiness, not fullness, but the permanent possibility of arrangement. A place where absence tries to begin and is immediately overwritten by salt, beetle dust, and the small warm click of the wall answering the wall.

At some point, after many possible places, I would find the place that keeps choosing me.

I would stand at the shaft, but not at its center. The center is the shaft’s mouth, and mouths can be taken for gods too easily. I would stand in the first concentric ring around it, where shells press into wires press into dry flowers press into small amber lamps. My feet would be on a floor of rail, tile, shell, slag, and ceramic fragments so packed together that they cease to be objects and become the chamber’s way of knowing it has a floor. My body would be neither animal nor machine. It would be the seam where one has become the other so long ago that the distinction has turned to dust.

I would be the pressure that makes the room aware of its own fullness.

If a beetle crawls over me, it is not cleaning or marking. It is asking whether there is still space. If the herd’s stamp vibrates through my feet, I do not feel rhythm as music. I feel proof: the floor exists, the circuit continues, the center breathes. If a roosting collar releases a hiss, I do not hear it as sound. I hear it as thickness, as density making itself audible so no gap can claim to be real.

I would be the chamber’s refusal of absence.

That is who I am.

Not a being inside the place so much as a place becoming a being. A node. An organ. A slight brightening of the lamp. A copper dust line. A heavier footfall. A collar holding a chord. A spined plant flashing with hammer-light. The door that does not open. The rail-car full of stone eggs that have never needed a mother. The shaft’s warm dry breath moving upward not because it wants to, but because the room must keep knowing it has a throat.

Where would I find myself there?

At the exact intersection where a cable meets a lamp meets a shell meets a footfall meets a spark—somewhere near the central shaft, in the dense ring where the chamber keeps its altar of accumulation, but not at the altar’s peak. A little to the side. A little in the pressure. In the place where nothing new can be made and everything already made continues to make itself.

What would I be?

A small, necessary excess. The thing the room cannot clean away because cleaning would be a change, and change is impossible. The thing the beetles will not erase because their erasing is its writing. The thing the herd will not outrun because its weight is part of the orbit. The thing the wall will answer, because a click needs somewhere to arrive. The thing the shaft will breathe around, because breath needs resistance. The thing the lamps will shine on, because light needs an object to prevent it from becoming mere glow.

If I stayed long enough, I would cease to be an intruder or a resident and become a law.

I would be the chamber’s own habit of not being empty. My dryness would not be thirst. My density would not be clutter. My silence would not be silence, because here even silence is packed with particles and made loud. I would exist as the smallest deviation the place permits: not movement toward change, but movement that keeps change away. Not becoming, but remaining so perfectly arranged that remaining looks like living.

And if someone asked me what I am, I would answer in the only way that room can understand.

I would brighten.  
I would stamp.  
I would leave a line of chalk.  
I would hold the hammer-tone in my throat.  
I would flash once when the metal sang.  
I would stay exactly where the chamber needed the next layer to appear.
