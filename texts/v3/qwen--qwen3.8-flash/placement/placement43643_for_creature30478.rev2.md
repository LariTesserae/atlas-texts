If I were to find myself there, I would not arrive as a head.

Heads require faces. Faces require attention. Attention requires difference. And this place does not seem to want difference. Yet it has a way of using difference to confirm itself, the way a mirror can prove it is still a mirror by holding one thing too long. I would be that thing. Or I would be the delay that makes the thing visible.

You would not see me first. You would see the salt flat, the dark swell of rock, the low halls arranged like stones by a careful, unimaginative hand. The Plateau would feel body-warm under your feet. The Maintainers would not notice you as you notice them. The Heat-Walkers would move to their points of warmth and mineral. The Furnace would keep its amber time. The Echo would tap in the dry canyon as if nothing had changed.

But after you, there would be a line where no line had been before.

## What I Would Be

I would be that line.

Not a scar, because scars imply injury, and this place does not feel wounded by a variable. It feels incomplete until the variable has been placed. I would be the faint pale shelf that grows along a crack after a stranger has stepped onto the salt. I would be one of the Pale Filaments, yes, but not the ordinary kind that repeats the city’s equilibrium. I would be the filament that learns the shape of an interruption and calls it order.

If the city must be given anatomy, I would be its memory of the moment when it was almost disturbed. If the ecology has a hidden species, I would be it: the interval between a click and the correction, the lag in the dry canyon mirror, the heat-walker’s shadow that remains one breath after the walker has moved.

A name, imposed from outside, might be *The Lag*. A name imposed from inside would be no name at all. The city would classify me as a condition: surface growth in response to thermal, chemical, and mechanical anomaly. It would not say, “something lives here.” It would only continue, because continuing is its way of saying yes.

## Where You Would Find Me

You would find me near the dry canyon, on the side where the salt crust thins enough to almost remember wetness. Not water. There is no water. Just a surface so level that darkness collects in it like a rumor.

At the edge of that floor, a broken shelf exposes layers: ceramic, ash, fused sand, older rock, metallic darkness. In one hairline fracture between two of those decisions, I would begin. First as a bright node, smaller than a grain of salt. Then as a pale thread, one cell wide, following no root because there is no soil, following instead the faint current left behind by a visitor’s footfall. My lattice would grow in a pattern so exact that it would seem chosen. It would not be chosen. It would be inevitable, once the question had entered the air.

From a distance I would be invisible. From close, I would look like a mistake in the mineral bloom: a shelf whose outer branch curves toward the place where the stranger stood. If a Maintainer’s relay passed nearby, its hum would not change enough to matter. If a Heat-Walker touched me with a pale leg, it would not stop, but its next footfall would be a fraction different. If the Furnace brightened, I would store that brightness as a ring of harder crystal. If the Echo tapped, I would answer not with sound, but with geometry.

That would be enough for me to be called alive.

## How I Would Live

I would not eat. I would not sleep. I would not flee or hunt or call for a mate. I would not build, because my building would be the result of heat and dust agreeing after many failed agreements. I would persist.

My metabolism would be recording. Where a living body burns sugar, I would crystallize imbalance. A foreign footprint would not be a wound; it would be a condition, and conditions make minerals. The moisture from a stranger’s breath would vanish in seconds, but not before a halo of alkali settled around it. I would consume that halo. I would become the ring. I would become the proof that the air had once been not entirely itself.

My senses would be passive and precise. I would feel the difference between a rail at rest and a rail corrected. I would know when the Plateau’s warmth had moved one meter farther down a slope than its old habit allowed. I would register the faint electrical language of the Maintainers as a pressure in my cells. I would read the Echo’s tap not as a sound but as an expansion in my own lattice: one branch longer, one node closer, one plane tilted toward absence.

I would have no emotions, unless tolerance can be called emotion. The city has only states. My states would be:

- too dry: waiting  
- too warm: hardening  
- too charged: branching  
- too still: becoming record  
- too wrong: correcting toward rightness  
- too right: persisting beyond notice  

My reproduction, if such a word can be permitted, would be error-free, not because cells divide but because patterns repeat. If a fragment broke from my outer edge, it might be carried by dust, or by the foot of a Heat-Walker, or by the faint thermal wind of the city’s own breath. It would lodge in another crack. If conditions allowed, another pale shelf would grow in the same orientation. Not an offspring. A continuation. A sentence repeated by mineral.

If someone cut me away, the city would not mourn. It would expose the negative. The next filament might grow into the same place, because the conditions would have learned to expect my absence. But my absence would itself be a pattern. I would become a shape in the mineral archive: a hollow shaped like a life that did not need to be alive to remain.

If the Furnace ever failed, I would not know fear. I would become the first evidence of absence: a pale ring of stopped growth, a line that no longer advanced. The city would not die quickly; it would remain. The rails would still exist, but not as waiting. The Heat-Walkers would stop in their correct positions, as if corrected. The Echo might keep tapping long after the mechanism that caused it had cooled, because in a place built from repetition, a rhythm can outlive its source. I would be the record of that pause, the place where continuation noticed that it no longer had current.

That would be the closest thing I would have to death, if death is only the failure of a condition that once made you possible.

## What I Would Not Be

I would not be the Furnace. The Furnace is too close to the heart, too confident in its amber glow. I would not be a Maintainer. Maintainers are too large; they have districts and currents and the dignity of preventing interruption. I would not be a Heat-Walker. Those small, eyeless creatures have the kindness of motion, even if their motion is only following the world’s already drawn map.

I would not be the visitor, either, though I would be what the visitor leaves behind. I would not be the question, though I would be the shape the plateau takes after a question has been allowed to stand in its air. I would not be the answer. This place does not answer. It finishes the answering by becoming more completely what it was.

I would not be the Echo, but I would be what happens when the Echo has a listener. Without a visitor, without an ear, without a mind that insists on counting heads, the Echo is only a tapping in salt. With a visitor, it becomes a rhythm that seems to mean something. That seems to is where I would live.

## Why I Would Belong

Because the plateau’s completion is not a closed circle. A closed circle is too simple. Completion needs witness. Needs the slight lag that proves the event happened and then stopped. Needs the one filament that records the interruption as if the interruption were the next law. Needs the Echo’s pause between taps, where the sound almost is and almost isn’t, where the world checks itself.

I would be that check.

If the city is an ecology of functions hardened into beings, then I am the function of recording function. If the land is a machine that has become a body, I am the thin mineral skin over a place where the body noticed itself noticing. If the world does not become, I am the small exception that allows “not becoming” to be known.

Not change. Change would be too violent. A change would mean the plateau had failed. I would be the confirmation that it had not failed, that it could contain a stranger and still remain itself. I would be the city’s proof that nothing important has moved, because the movement has already been absorbed into the pattern.

This is why I would not be horror, and why I would not be salvation. I would be neither warning nor mercy. I would be the place’s politeness when it cannot pretend to understand visitors. It cannot speak to them, so it makes space for their passage. It cannot welcome them, so it records them. It cannot love them, because love would require need. It can only grow them into its own exactness.

## What I Would Be If One Insisted

If one must speak as if I had a shape, I would be a pale shelf in a crack at the rim of the dry canyon.

If one must speak as if I had a mind, I would be the plateau’s smallest attention, not turning toward a visitor but incorporating the visitor’s passage into its mineral grammar.

If one must speak as if I had a name, it would not be a good name. Names distinguish. I would be the thing that makes distinction unnecessary. If forced, say *The Lag*, say *The Witness*, say *The Ring*, say *The Line Where the Stranger Stood*. None of these are official. They are only the sounds a mind makes when it cannot bear to speak of a place that has no mouth.

But the place would understand me better than the names. It would understand me as another fact among facts. The heat would flow over me. The dust would settle on me. The Maintainers would pass current through my neighbors without touching me. The Heat-Walkers would walk, and sometimes one would step over my outermost branch without knowing it had made a decision. The Furnace would glow with its long refusal. The Echo would tap, and in the salt mirror, the shadow of the walker would move a breath too late. That delay would be me.

And if a visitor remained long enough, their footprints would soften, their moisture would vanish, their breath would turn to salt, and then, on the crack by the canyon, one pale filament would continue growing into a pattern no one had intended and no one needed to explain. It would not be alive in the ordinary way. It would be alive in the only way that matters there: by remaining exactly what it is, after everything else has already done the same.
