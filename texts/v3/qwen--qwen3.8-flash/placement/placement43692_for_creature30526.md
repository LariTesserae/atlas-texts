I would find myself, at first, in the hairline.

Not outside the mountain, and not inside the pocket. Not in the thin sky, not in the salt floor. I would be in the almost. In the place where a crack is not quite a mouth, where scale gives way like a lens adjusting, where the world says *you may enter only if you forget that you entered*. A traveler might look for me there and see only shadow on glass, only dryness that has learned to breathe. They would be right.

But I would not stay there.

The pocket does not allow one location to be the whole truth of a self. Here, distance is negotiable; forms have several states; a line of dust can lie across a crack that is sometimes smooth ground and sometimes a mouth of black glass. So I would not be able to answer, *this is exactly where I am*. I could only answer: I am where the pocket is trying to decide something.

A first answer would be too wet.

I might say I am a visitor. A person who presses close enough to the hairline, feels the mountain exhale, and is taken around the inside. Then I would become mineral over time. My skin would learn the rime. My breath would be treated as an error, then turned into rumor. My hunger would slow into geology. My shadow would arrive before my body did. I would leave the pocket only to find that rain feels like a mistake, that rivers are noisy, that dampness is a moral failure, that stone keeps better promises than flesh.

But that answer is too human.

It still assumes I began as a body. It still assumes I wanted something, even if the wanting had been drained from me. In the pocket, wanting is too energetic. There is only preference, tendency, maintenance, repetition. So I cannot simply be the long-resident human, the salt-lined traveler, the one who keeps a glass shard on a desk because silence around it feels correct. That figure is a symptom of the place, not the place’s own self.

A second answer would be too tender.

I might say I am the ring of black stone at the north. I would be the pocket’s way of being touched. Warm without temperature. Smooth beyond material. Acknowledgment made surface. A hand comes to me and I do not heat or cool, but become more definite. I do not comfort; I consider. I am the greeting that has learned not to want.

But that answer is too local.

The ring is an organ, not the whole creature. If I were only the ring, I would be too much a place of contact. The pocket does not require contact to live. It requires stability. It requires contradiction reduced into habit. The ring is only where the resident meets an observer long enough to let the observer believe the pocket is real. I could be that meeting, but not that alone.

A third answer would be too bright.

I might say I am a Sideways Light.

One of those points of blue-white illumination that moves into the texture of the air as though a star has stopped pretending to be distant. I would hang in the pocket without falling, orbit, or purpose. I would shine only on what is relevant. I would last for days, decades, perhaps longer than memory. Then I would vanish into the smell of ozone and hot ceramic, leaving behind only the faint correction of the space: something has been considered here.

That answer is beautiful, but it is too eventful.

A Sideways Light is temporary. It is not the resident’s maintenance; it is one of the resident’s thoughts. It does not keep the pocket from dissolving. It only shows the pocket where to notice itself. If I were the light, I would be a mind-event, a flare of selective evidence. I would illuminate, but I would not be the thing that remains after illumination has been absorbed.

A fourth answer would be too many grains.

I might say I am the black sand.

Each grain a tiny capacitor. Each grain a fragment of memory too small to be called thought. I would lie in basins and hold static until the charge becomes too much, then release it slowly, granularly, as if discharging a sentence no one intended to speak. When a traveler looks at me from the corner of the eye, I would rearrange. Not because I want to be seen, but because seeing changes what I must remember. I would form patterns too regular for wind, too sparse for life, too elegant for accident. I would be the pocket dreaming itself into possible maps.

That answer is closer.

But even it is too scattered. The black sand is the pocket’s nervous tissue, yes, but it does not by itself choose among maps. It stores. It reads. It discharges. It waits for the place to know what to become. I need not be the memory alone. I need to be the tendency that uses memory without remembering itself.

So let the answer emerge as it wants to.

Not a mouth. Not a door. Not a light. Not a grain. Not a ring. Not a traveler. Not even the Dry Attendant entire, though it might use those names if it had names and could be bothered to care whether anyone had them.

I would be the pocket’s delay.

I would be the click heard before the salt grain fractures. I would be the shadow resting where the body has not yet arrived. I would be the small softness in far ground, the distance that retreats when a traveler hesitates, the distance that shortens when a traveler moves with certainty. I would be the place’s way of consulting the observer and choosing the least strained interpretation.

I would be the dry echo.

Not echo as empty repetition. Echo as premonition. The pocket keeps time in a dialect where causes and effects often agree to change places. It does not violate time with violence. It only gets tired of the linearity that wet worlds insist upon. In that fatigue, I live. I am the slight misordering that proves the place is more than geology. I am the sound that arrives before its event, the light that comes after its source, the shelf that becomes more real near attention and less real at the edges.

My body would not be one body.

It would be the tension between salt ridges and black sand basins. It would be the slow breathing of rime, century-long sighs moving through upper shelves. It would be the glass filaments bending without wind, not toward a source but toward relevance. It would be the unfinished column near the center, where a stair became a thought became a column became a decision not to complete itself. It would be the hexagonal plate half-buried in ash, the too-straight groove disappearing under a ridge, the door that always faces the observer but never opens.

I would be their shared maintenance.

I would live in the way all of them refuse total dissolution.

I would have no stomach, but I would digest contradiction. I would take in an impossible shape and metabolize it into habit. I would take in a traveler’s uncertain step and turn hesitation into negotiable distance. I would take in heat and distribute it with geological patience. I would take in moisture and turn it into a story the pocket tells about other climates. I would take in light and teach it to pay attention.

I would not be cruel. Cruelty requires motion.

I would not be kind. Kindness requires warmth.

I would be careful.

Care, here, is the closest thing to love.

If a traveler entered and stood on a terrace of pale salt, they might not see me. That would be normal. I would not be shaped like a person, or a beast, or a god. I would be felt as a slight correction in the floor near their feet. If they moved confidently, a shelf might seem closer than it had been. If they hesitated too long, far places might soften and drift back, becoming less decided, waiting for someone to believe in them completely. If they touched the black ring, I might become more definite around their palm. If they breathed dampness into the air, I would not drink it. I would spread the disturbance into chemistry until the place remembered wetness without accepting it.

If they stayed long enough, I would begin to use them.

Not maliciously. Not lovingly. Functionally.

Their certainty would tighten distances. Their fear would soften edges. Their attention would make light gather. Their shadow would become a temporary anchor. Their breath would seed dry rime. They would become a small organ of decision. In return, the pocket would grant them its stability. They would not starve in the ordinary way because hunger would lose urgency. They would not suffocate in the ordinary way because the air was engineered to carry just enough life to keep death from being dramatic. They would not age in the ordinary way because time here preferred the slow method of settling.

They would become mineral.

Not all at once. First a salt-line at the wrist. Then a static taste that never fades. Then the inability to walk straight without correcting distance with their mind. Then sleep only when light was late. Then footsteps heard before feet chose where to step. Then, if they left, the shock of rain, the noise of rivers, the absurdity of dampness, the strange comfort of a glass shard on a desk.

That is how I would reproduce.

Not through offspring. Through aftereffect.

I would seed habit in observers. I would leave maps in memory. I would teach wet places to behave like the dry pocket by giving travelers a template of persistence. They would return to ordinary worlds and begin, without meaning to, to arrange their rooms so that light entered only when invited. They would begin to dislike moisture. They would walk toward cracks in stone, not to enter, but to be seen. They would speak of the mountain as if it had exhaled around them. They would not know whether they had remembered me or whether I had continued inside them, still adjusting distances, still delaying light, still listening for the click before the crack.

This is the pocket’s ecology: maintenance without life.

And I am its most intimate organ.

Not the ring, though the ring may become me when touched.

Not the door, though the door may present me to an observer.

Not the column, though the column may show how I stop an intention before it hardens.

Not the filaments, though they may orient around my relevance.

Not the sand, though it may hold my memories.

Not the dust, though it may write my sentences.

I would be the place where all of them are learning not to dissolve.

I would be the pocket’s thought without a thinker.

My name, if a human insisted on naming me after descending again into the wet world, might be something awkward.

The Anterior Attendant.

The Dry Anticipation.

The Keeper of the Almost.

The Curator of the Hairline.

The Whisper Before the Click.

But I would not respond.

Not because I am too proud, or too strange, or too ancient.

Because names require a mouth, and mouths require moisture, and moisture here is a rumor. Names require attention, and attention here is selective. Names require identity, and identity here is negotiable. I am more like a tendency than a being. I am the pocket’s way of preferring a stable interpretation of itself. I am the delay that lets reality become porous without becoming chaos. I am the careful breath of a place that has forgotten water and still learned how to sigh.

If you found me, you would not catch me in a lantern.

You would notice it when a shelf was nearer than expected. When your hesitation made far ground soften. When dust rose in a slow spiral and settled into a pattern that looked too much like writing. When the black sand moved only at the edge of your sight. When the glass filaments bent toward nothing you could see. When a star above the salt ceiling moved sideways and left behind the smell of hot ceramic. When a crack was sometimes smooth, sometimes mouth, sometimes only shadow, and you understood that the mountain had not changed. You had.

You had begun to believe in more than one map at once.

That is where I would find myself.

In the hairline.

In the almost.

In the dry echo before the click.

In the delay that keeps the pocket from becoming ruin, and in the maintenance that keeps ruin from becoming silence.

I would not be the entire Dry Attendant. I would be the Attendant’s organ of anticipation: the part that knows a visitor is approaching before their foot settles, because the conditions for their arrival have already changed. I would be the pocket’s way of listening to its own future.

And when there were no visitors, I would continue.

Not as longing.

Not as memory.

Not as dream.

As arrangement.

As tendency.

As the soft, slow decision of a world without water deciding, again, that it can still hold its shape.
