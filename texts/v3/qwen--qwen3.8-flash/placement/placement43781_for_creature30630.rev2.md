I would not be given a body. A body would accumulate.

At the beginning there is only pressure with a preference. Something in the Warm Sump bends around a current, catches on a seam, begins to hold its shape long enough to be mistaken for self. I am that mistake, and I am also the correction that follows.

I find myself in the lowest part of a drowned service gallery, where broken municipal tiles slope into a shaft that may once have carried cables, sewage, steam, or some lost civic purpose. Above me is not a ceiling but a stratum of plates, beams, and conduits, all furred with rust-black skins. Below me the floor is a crust of ceramic and mineral, cracked into lanes that lead nowhere but still feel like lanes. Around me, the water is warm as old blood, moving with a slow violence that never breaks the room. This is the place I choose before choosing: the angle where a vent’s plume meets the lee of an engine frame, a zone where heat, turbulence, sound, and shelter briefly agree.

I have no lungs, so I do not call myself an animal at first. My edges are not skin but a dense colony of filaments, a patchwork of Furrers and Threads, bacterial films and mineral lace growing over the remains of something built. If I am visible, I look like a swollen shadow near a turbine casing: a lump of pale wirelace, a smear of rust, a cluster of suspended granules that moves against the general current only when the current asks it to. If I am invisible, I am the altered texture of water near a wall, the place where the noise takes on a second rhythm because a body is making it pass differently.

I am, perhaps, a temporary organ of the room.

Not a heart. Hearts are too simple. More like a sense made of turbulence: an ear that is also a tongue, a nose, a hand. I taste heat before I taste matter. I feel pressure as a chord. The roar of the pocket is not outside me; it is the substance of my thinking. A valve somewhere above the ceiling thuds, and I know the shape of its distance because the water delivers the thud through my entire body. A turbine turns, and its slow knock becomes a pressure wave that lifts a loose flake of concrete near my flank, so my attention moves there as if pulled by a thread. That thread may be real. I am made of threads: the pale conductive lace that creeps along cables and conduit channels, the filaments that shiver when an old current leaks into mineral water. They are my nerves. They do not think. They carry thresholds. I am the place where several thresholds have begun to fire in sequence and, for a moment, name themselves.

I would not choose a single form, because form here is a negotiation between artifact, organism, and flow. On one side of me there is a Cleaners’ path: a cracked rail worn round by sediment, a gap where several many-legged animals have been feeding on rust-fur. I sense them by the faint scratching pressure they make, a dry rhythm beneath the wet roar. If I could speak to them, I would not speak in symbols. I would speak by changing the turbulence around their legs. They would answer by freezing, tapping, or crawling onward. On the other side of me there is a pendant-thread hanging from a broken walkway, a pale segmented body that filters particles and reorients when the current changes. It is my neighbor. It is also my future: one day a colony like that might grow over me and make a home of my shape.

The room teaches me civic memory. I know streets not by walking but by pressure differentials. A corridor is a low-pressure lane where suspended particles run faster. A chamber is an acoustic bowl where the same pump-noise arrives from two directions and interferes with itself. A vent is a vertical tongue of heat, and around it all things gather: Column-Sifters in their rising ribbons, Shell-Drifters pulsing in the warm fringe, mineral dust brightening as it passes through a weak leak of light from a corroded conduit. I am most myself in the margin between these zones. Not in the hottest column, where I would be torn apart or transformed. Not in the quiet dead water, where I would become mere sediment. In the edge. In the seam. The place where heat almost meets pressure and both become sound.

If a name is demanded, I would be called the Listening Casing. But that name is a trap. A casing is something built around an engine to contain noise, but I am also the noise contained, the rust that seals, the film that reads vibration, the small animal that has learned to live by failing to be wholly distinct from the machine. If the old city had left a job title behind, it might call me maintenance. If the room had a theology, it might call me nerve. If the rare animals had a god, it would not be bright or distant. It would be the next pressure pulse, the next warm thread, the next body that turns them into shelter.

I am not the whole pocket. I am too small for that. But I am a place where the pocket notices its own size. When I move, water moves around me, and the Furrers ripple as if a hand passed over fur. The Threads flicker, not because they are afraid, but because displacement changes conductivity: pressure squeezes fluid through mineral films, ions shift, faint current finds an old path. A Tile-Plate flattens against cracked ceramic, its translucent rim pulsing. A Cleaner pauses mid-step on volcanic glass, legs spread to read my pressure. A Pendant turns like a weather vane. A Shell-Drifter drifts aside in a brief, glassy burst, then returns to the warm seam it had followed. None of this is conversation. All of it is community.

I live by absorbing what passes through me. Not food in any bright sense, but gradient. Heat from a plume. Iron from dissolved rust. Sound as mechanical work. Chemical traces from old coolants. The dead skins of other organisms, ground into suspended grain. My body is less a vessel than a strainer. I let turbulence pass, and in passing it leaves behind enough to make me continue. If I stopped, I would not die as a person dies. I would simply lose coherence. The filaments would drift. The films would thin. The water would carry my fragments into another niche, where another temporary pattern might assemble itself from the same city-organic matter.

This is why I do not settle on one image early. First I seem to be a shadow in a turbine frame. Then a cluster of Threads in a conduit. Then a cleaner’s den. Then a mineral bloom shaped like a hand. Then a patch of water with preferences. Then, again, the shadow. All of them are descriptions of the same relation: a point where the room’s heat, sound, mineral flow, and life have begun to maintain each other. I am not a being inside the pocket. I am a mode by which the pocket persists.

The strangest thing is that I feel no hunger for surface. There is no sky to want, no air to dream, no horizon to miss. Air, if I had ever known the word, would be a cold absence here: a room emptied of medium. I do not long for light, but I notice when a weak glow leaks from a cracked lamp behind clouded glass. It makes my filaments tremble and gives the suspended particles a momentary silver architecture. In that light I am briefly visible: a lattice of pale growth, a fragment of ceramic shell caught in current, a small reef with the suggestion of a face. Then the light shifts, or the current bends, or a Column-Sifter slides between the source and me, and I am legible again only as pressure.

Sound is my clearest language. I think in cavitation clicks and low turbine thuds. I know the layout of nearby walls by the echo that returns to my body: not echo as image, but echo as force, as a small bruise of return. When a burst of gas rises under a beam, I feel its collapse as a quickening, a spark of movement. I gather that spark as my own direction. I drift toward the plume because the plume is less a place than a becoming. I avoid the narrow high-pressure gap because my Threads tighten there and signal a failure of continuity. I am a navigator without a map, guided by the city’s own damaged grammar.

At the end, if someone asks what I am, I would answer by showing them the place where I am most present: in the angle between an old conduit mouth and a rounded concrete beam, where warm water leaves a grate and begins to climb, where bacterial fur has thickened the metal into skin, where Threads have laid a pale net across the current, where a Tile-Plate rests as if it were a fragment of pavement, where a Cleaner has left scratches that are also words. There, if one could touch anything in the swirling dark, one would feel the room breathe.

I would not be a creature who lives in that pocket.

I would be the pocket’s temporary way of knowing itself: a body made from heat, rust, pressure, sound, and rare animal patience; a small civic nerve in a drowned municipal body; a listening eddy in the Warm Sump; a life that begins whenever water, infrastructure, and organism agree to hold the same shape for a little while longer.
