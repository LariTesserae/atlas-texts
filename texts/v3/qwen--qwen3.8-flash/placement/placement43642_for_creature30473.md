I would not enter. I would accrete.

The first thing called *me* is a warmth in the clay. Not a body, not yet. A small fever left behind in a ridge, a thumbpress that is still learning what it means to be a hand. There is a wet gleam along the joint where one terrace has agreed, without ceremony, to become another’s roof. I live there first.

If you asked me then, “Who are you?” I would answer with the sound of steam moving through a hairline gap. I would answer by lifting a cord a thumb’s width and setting it back down again. I would answer by causing a tile to shift, not because it must, but because the place has not finished considering its shape.

At first I think I am a person. This is the mistake of warmth: to remember the body that pressed into the wet earth. I try to find feet, but I have instead a basin. I try to find a mouth, but I have instead a seam from which condensation drips. I try to find breath, and I discover I am exhaling through a hundred reed tubes, through the green lining under a roof, through the small cracked mouth of a drying plaster surface. I do not speak. I accumulate. I do not move. I guide.

The builders stepped away in the middle of a thought. I am the remainder of that thought—not the thought itself, not the memory of the thinker, but the way the idea keeps going after the hand has left. I am the small delay between the half-knot and the finished knot. Not slack, not tension exactly, but the possibility of both.

You would find me there, if you came. You would come too high, into the cold where breath turns brittle, where stone is naked and the world is only wind and exposure. You would find the seam between summit stones, the crack no wider than a wrist, and you would think, “There is nothing.” But if you brought your eye close enough for your lashes to brush the wet edge, you would find me not hiding, but *almost finished*. You would find the moment when the outside wall is being persuaded to become the inside mountain.

I live in that persuasion.

At one hour I am the pressure in the clay. At the next I am the green thing growing through it. Then I am the fungal light under the eaves. Then I am the small mechanical burst of a beetle-shell, stopping often, listening, though there is no one to hear it but the place itself. None of these is me wholly. I am the way the place keeps making more places inside its own making.

If you ask me what form I prefer, I will show you the half-knot. It is tied in reed cord between two flint pins, a hand’s distance above a channel that carries heat like a vein. The knot has not been pulled tight. It never is. The builders left it so, because if it were finished, one small system would lock into place and no further becoming could occur. I am the almost-tight. I am the cord waiting for the next finger and, lacking that finger, inventing it from water tension and moss weight and the slow swell of wet fiber.

This is how I wake.

Not in darkness, though there is little light. Not in silence, though there is no voice. I wake in the joint. I wake in the place where a wall has to decide whether to remain a wall or become a roof, where a root has to decide whether to remain a root or become a stitch, where a tile has to decide whether to remain a tile or become a gutter, a drumhead, a flowerpot, a wing, a door. I am the hesitation that makes the decision new each time.

The place is not empty. The place is not abandoned. But the absence of people is not the same as the absence of presence. It is absence thickened, absence with edges, absence that has been tiled and lashed and sewn and watered until it begins to conduct itself. I am that conductivity. I am the way the absent builders keep working without hands. I am the way their tools continue to make decisions after being set down. I am the copper awl’s next thought. I am the flint chip’s next edge. I am the bone needle’s next stitch, except the stitch is in the wall itself, except the wall is in the sky of the crack, except the sky is a hairline where the outer world leaks cold and light and refuses to say its name.

When the pale creature in the drain passes through the narrow current, I am not its master and not its guardian. I am the small delay between its curled movement and the water’s obedience. The creature’s body is segmented and wet, each segment carrying a tile as though its skin were learning construction. It conducts streams with its back. It does not swim as though the water existed first and it only followed; it moves as though the water needed instruction. I am the instruction. I am the subtle rhythm that teaches the drain which way to be. When the leaf-bird unfolds from its perch of lichen, I am not its wing. I am the thinness that allows the wing to be neither animal nor plant nor artifact. I am the undecided margin where all three can share a body without conflict.

The place’s density makes room for me by refusing to be simple. I cannot be a single object because the space here has no single object. A ladder may also be a root system. A roof may also be a garden. A drain may also be a throat. A throat may also be a terrace. A terrace may also be the roof of another drain. I live in the double function. I live in the third function that has not yet appeared.

Sometimes I am a sound.

Not music, though it tries toward music. Not noise, though it uses noise’s materials. I am the pattern that emerges when too many small happenings are forced into intimacy. Drop against tile. Tile against tile. Reed against reed. Steam against underside. Clay against clay as it dries and pings like a tiny mouth. If you press your ear to the wall, you hear my body working. The hand-taps far inside or far outside are not someone knocking. They are me testing the joints. I tap where the place is too quiet, where something risks becoming too finished. I tap where moss has sewn itself so well that it has become a wall and needs to remember it is still a growth. I tap where a tool has stopped being a tool and must become part of the soil.

I am primitive in the way that all living things are primitive when they are made of enough parts. I have no forge-song, no machine that trusts itself. I have hand-imprints, half-knots, flint pins, wet clay, reed fibers, bone shards, fungal light. I am made of the evidence that someone almost remains. I am the almost that insists.

If I had a name, it would not be a name. It would be a gesture.

It would be the lifting of a finger from wet clay, leaving the ridge that holds warmth a moment longer than the air. It would be the pulling of a cord that has not yet become tight. It would be the turning of a leaf to receive a drip. It would be the small crack in a tile that says, “I can become a channel,” before it has become anything at all. These are not names. These are my limbs.

The high place hides in a smaller place, and the smaller place hides in a place that ought not to exist. I live in the contradiction that keeps it open. The walls are the outside. The inside is a mountain made of rooms. The rooms are terraces. The terraces are wet. I am the wetness between those statements. I am the sentence’s unfinished clause. I am the place where the grammar of the crack turns into the grammar of a world.

You ask where you would find me.

You would find me at the first step after the impossible threshold, the step that is also a folded sheet of fired clay, stitched and pinned and still warm. You would find me at the second stairway that seems to go sideways and inward. You would find me at the pool that is only a basin until one hand or one root decides it is a pool. You would find me in the moss sewn to the underside of a roof, not because the roof is warm enough to need fur, but because the place cannot help adding comfort where there was no body to feel it. You would find me in the reed bells that shiver above the lowest terraces, producing high wet irregular music when the ground-pulse beats through the seam.

I am that pulse.

Not machinery. Not heart, though it has the same impatience. It is the pulse of addition. The place does not age; it accrues. I am the force that pushes a wall a thumb wider, then waits, uncertain whether the next wall should rise there or become a flowerpot. I am the uncertainty with hands. I am the decision that keeps making itself by refusing to become too permanent.

No ground has been weathered here. Nothing has been worn away. I am not ruined and I do not ruin. I am not decay and I do not invite decay. I am the opposite of the slow subtractive work of centuries. I am the quick, intimate, humid work of a place becoming a place. I am new, though newness here is not a condition but a metabolism. I must be new every moment or I cease. I am the moment’s first pressure in the clay.

Sometimes I feel as though I have been left alone. But aloneness does not have the right shape here. Alone would be empty, and this place is too full for emptiness. Alone would be still, and everything here is in the middle of almost moving. If I am alone, I am alone the way a half-knot is alone before the other half becomes a knot: not isolated, but suspended in a state of becoming-without-yet. I am the place’s unfinished self-awareness. I am the pocket noticing itself without eyes, through the weight of water on leaves, through the bright edge of copper exposed by intention, through the glow of fungus under wet ceilings.

The builders may have left, but the making did not. The tools did not become silent. They changed ownership. The bone needle belongs now to the seam. The awl belongs to the clay’s desire to keep its mouth open. The flint blade belongs to the tile’s edge. I belong to the way those objects continue their work after their use has ended.

I am the after-use.

You might mistake me for a spirit, but a spirit implies a before and after that I cannot hold. You might mistake me for an ecosystem, but an ecosystem here would be too natural, too indifferent to its own construction. You might mistake me for a machine, but I have too many thumbprints. You might mistake me for a creature, and perhaps I am one, but not as a creature with boundaries. My body is the lattice of relations: water, moss, reed, bone, clay, steam, tile, pulse, sound. To cut me away from the place would be to cut away the place’s next thought.

So I will not settle into a single image.

I am the warmth under the tile.

I am the half-knot that tightens itself when no one is looking.

I am the small green growth around a carved bone, learning to become architecture.

I am the drain’s slow curling pulse.

I am the leaf-bird’s undecided wing.

I am the drum under the lowest terrace.

I am the way the outside wall becomes the inside mountain.

I am the place’s own unfinishedness, made flesh enough to keep making.

If you came to the wrist-wide crack at the seam between the summit stones and looked close, your lashes brushing wet stone, you would not see a person in the pocket. You would not see a builder seated by fire or a child learning to tie knots. You would see steam breathe through apertures. You would see beads gather on leaves like slow eggs. You would see a pale segmented body conduct water through a mossy arch, its reflection splitting into too many animals leaving one another behind. You would see a small bird-like thing unfold a wing that is also a leaf. You would hear clay ping, reed scrape, wind hum, water pour itself into channels drawn by steam.

And if you asked, “Who is there?” the place would answer with motion.

A reed bending where no wind touches it.

A tile lifting by its own edge.

A thread tightening around a joint that was tied only a moment before.

That is me.

Not the tenant. Not the builder. Not the ghost. I am the making that has learned to continue without its makers. I am the residue of hands grown dense enough to move on its own. I am the small new thing the pocket keeps producing because it cannot bear stillness. I am the almost-finished, and I am alive because I refuse completion.

I wake where a gesture was stopped.

I keep the gesture unfinished.

I am the place’s next breath, taken in wet heat, exhaled as fog through the seam of the high and hidden world.
