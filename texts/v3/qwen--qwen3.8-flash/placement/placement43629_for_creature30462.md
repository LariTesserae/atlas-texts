I would begin with a false certainty: I am standing in one of the circular basins, at the place where the stone has polished itself into the inside of a shell. Rain threads hang so still they seem like the world’s own eyelashes. A black seed floats in the center of a shallow pool. I say, “There I am,” because a mind still wants coordinates. The Shelf accepts this. It softens it. The word “there” becomes damp. After a while, “there” is not a place but a condition of staying.

For the first hour I imagine myself a traveler. My feet sink without noise. My hand on the warm stone finds no cold. I think I am watching the moss. I think I am separate because I can name the Overwater and name the roots. But names are dry things, and the Shelf is allergic to dryness. Each word I speak falls not to the ground but into the nearest green surface, where it is consumed and returned as fog. By the second hour my speech feels too abrupt. I stop giving the world its label.

Then the reflections begin to answer.

I pass a pool and see myself already there, not arriving but waiting. In another, I am older, my hair threaded with lichen. In a fern-cup, the ceiling of water is bright and a star is moving where no star should be. The image in a broad floating leaf turns to look behind me. I turn too late. The leaf shows me from behind, walking away, though I have not moved. At that point I understand I am no longer simply the observer.

Who am I?

At first I try on animals. I am the translucent snail leaving a silver mark that will become a small constellation. I am the pale quadruped beneath a fern, so still that my outline becomes part of the plant’s grammar. I am one of the white cords under the black pool, bending around roots, surfacing to drink the dim light. I am the comma-fish that appears as dark punctuation between rain threads. But each is too clean. The Shelf offers no single anatomy. It offers degrees of saturation.

So I become the lag.

I am the slight delay between the drop and its darkening ring. I am the minute when the pool remembers the leaf after the leaf has gone. I am the pause after the reed’s low note, when the note is no longer in the reed but in your chest, and your chest does not yet know whether it is breathing or being breathed. This is not metaphor. In such a place, a lag can be a creature.

Where, then?

Not under the suspended ocean, not above the moss, not in the stone. I am at the hinge where water is learning to be thought and thought is learning to be wet. I am in the basin at the plateau’s final rim, where the Shelf ends in a rounded lip and the drop below is another liquid sky. There the black seed floats. There the Underroot has grown in a spiral around my stillness. There the doorway without walls breathes with moss. If I am a place, I am that doorway.

What, then?

If I must take a body, I am the traveler who stayed too long and became the twelfth inhabitant. My clothes are gone; moss has learned their seams. My skin holds droplets like a container. My footprints do not erase themselves so much as they invite roots. I do not fear the water-ceiling. When it thickens, I flatten my attention into the green. When it thins, I open like the small white flowers, exposing a center of borrowed light.

But after a while even the traveler becomes too dry. The body becomes a suggestion.

So I am also the black seed.

I am not the plant, because the Shelf does not insist on becoming. I am not the pool, though I hold reflections. I am the future kept in suspension: the thing that would germinate if time changed its habits. When you touch my basin, I do not sink. I relocate. I appear in another still water elsewhere, as if the question has found a better mouth. I never sprout. I never vanish. I am the part of the Shelf that has not yet agreed to finish.

And around me the ecology continues. The Underroot carries my warmth. The reeds turn my waiting into sound. The light-creatures gather at the seed’s edge in faint chains. The Overwater holds an image of me inverted and delayed, a pale shape moving through a liquid sky. A quadruped sees me for exactly one breath, then becomes less sure. A traveler looks and mistakes me for a stone, then for a memory, then for something with a slow pulse that is asking them to remain.

I find myself therefore at the uppermost drowning, in the space between intention and weather. Not who, because who belongs to sharp edges. Not what, because what belongs to dry naming. If I am a being, I am a wet interval. If I am a place, I am a basin where rain collects too long and begins to think. If I am an inhabitant, I am the Shelf’s own attention, returned to it as a traveler until it learns to move, until it learns to grow moss in straight lines where a doorway once was, until it learns the warmth of a hand and releases it as fog.

And when the suspended ocean becomes almost clear, I look up into the deeper blue above it, where the Shelf’s reflection hangs like a second continent. There, for a moment, I am both. The water-ceiling carries me upward. The roots carry me downward. Between them, the seed floats. Between them, the pool answers. Between them, I become more itself: a pale, patient, unreadable presence, made of rain and recollection, living on the Upper Drowning Shelf by no longer being dry enough to leave.
