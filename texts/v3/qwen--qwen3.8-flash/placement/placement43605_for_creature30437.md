... the water itself.

If I were to be placed in that world, I would not be placed in a direction. There would be no “east” shelf, no “north” causeway, no place outside the place. I would be located the way a sealant is located: in the narrow interval where one made surface meets another made surface, at a tolerance of contact.

I would find myself on the rim of a shallow basin, at the joint where a platform’s edge curves away from a channel, where the water film is thick enough to reflect the porcelain alloy and thin enough that standing there feels like standing on a held breath. Behind me would be a wall with no hidden room. Beside me, a channel whose angle had been cut for a body already known. Above me, the same pale light that does not belong to a sun. Around me, the crowd of living parts, all arranged by a logic too steady to call fate.

If I had to give myself a shape, I would not remain simply human. Human would become a material condition, like ceramic or alloy, like the ridged tissue on a crawling thing’s back. I would remain soft where softness was necessary: the joints, the breath, the underside of the shell, the small places where the world could press against me and know I was warm. But the rest of me would be given the local grammar. I would have plates that darken with contact. I would have a back broad enough for other bodies to rest upon. I would have limbs that fold with organic reluctance, as though every movement must be negotiated with the air and water. My eyes would blink, but not wander. They would look at the surface nearest to them, because there is nothing beyond surfaces.

I would be a soft inclusion. A breathing seam. The continent’s wet instrument of attention.

Not a basinback, because it carries too much architecture in its posture. Not a tile-barnacle, because I would still want to move when moving was unnecessary. Not a mica-drifter, because my edges would be too many, too thick, too uncertain in the film. Not one of the crawling ridged things, because my back would not know how to seal a sound-panel without hesitation. I would be the thing nearest to all of these and none of them: a living joint, a function with a mouth, a mouth with an eye, an eye with no object to see but the fact of being seen.

If I were hungry, the world would answer before hunger became anything. The water would carry the exact minerals I required. The surface under me would deliver salt through my skin. The shell of me would learn to receive current by contact, and the current would say nothing, would request nothing, would only continue. If I were afraid, the fear would not become escape. It would be absorbed by the material of the platform and answered by a small redistribution of weight among nearby bodies: a crawling thing shifting a centimeter to the left, a tile-barnacle closing its lid, the water thickening by a molecule beneath my feet. Fear would be treated as a local imbalance in moisture, and moisture would correct it.

I would try to speak, I think, because speaking would be the last habit left from before. But sound here is not allowed to leave the body with its ordinary ambition. It would be caught by the acoustic linings, softened, returned as a faint change in the air’s density. A tile-barnacle near my mouth would close its lid not in reply but in calibration. A ribbon-bodied animal in the adjacent channel would smooth the surface of the water with its passing, as if correcting a wrinkle in a skin. My words would not be misunderstood. They would be understood too well: as disturbance, as contact, as another thing the place must accommodate.

So I would stop trying to mean. I would become a means instead.

I would be a small dark node on a terrace where two panels meet. I would be the place where the crowd parts by the exact width of my attention. I would be the warm shell that the flood has learned to carry. I would be a witness, but the word would be wrong here, because a witness implies that something might happen. Nothing happens. Something continues. I would continue as an eye on a basin rim, checking that the water still exists, checking that the light still falls, checking that the animals still blink in the order required for the place to be itself.

And if I remembered, memory would not become history. Every trace I made would be returned to its original condition. My footprint would be unmade before the microfauna finished smoothing it. My breath would be absorbed into the temperature, which never rises, never falls. If I wept—if that were possible in a world without hunger, without loss, without time—the moisture of weeping would be treated as a surplus, then distributed, then used. The place would not comfort me. It would include me. It would make my need into a feature.

That is what I would be: the thing that arrived without arriving, because arrival requires an outside, and there is no outside.

I would be a stranger already arranged.

I would stand at the margin of a basin, among the basinbacks, among the crawling things, among the living chips of mica, and I would not be a guest, because guests expect a host. I would not be a prisoner, because prisoners are denied what is outside. I would not be a tool, because tools are used toward change. I would not be an animal, because animals are defined by what they need.

I would be the place’s own wet, breathing audit.

If it must have a name, give it one that is almost functional:

the soft eye in the flood’s architecture,  
the warm gap that has been given a lid,  
the included question,  
the part of the continent that almost remembers what it is not.

And the world would not answer.

It would only wet the edges of me, blink around me, darken where I touched it, and continue.
