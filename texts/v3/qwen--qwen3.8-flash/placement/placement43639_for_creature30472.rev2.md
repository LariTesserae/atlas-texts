I would find myself in the scream of a high spire.

Not the scream of injury, but the scream of grammar. A place so narrow and hidden that distance refuses it, yet a place so full of event that once entered, distance ceases to matter. I would be behind the curved baffle, in the warm wet fold where fused plates, ceramic skin, metal hair, and slow intelligent stone have forgotten being made and learned to weep. Cloud-swept from the outside, the spire would give no hint of the pocket inside. But I would not stand outside. That is the mistake every dry shape makes: believing the world begins at its boundary.

If I tried to imagine myself standing there—someone with eyes, breath, a name—I would ruin it quickly. A watcher is a predator in that chamber. Skin would be too dry, lungs too foreign, heartbeat too singular. My breath would fog the filaments; my shadow would teach the shell-creatures danger that does not come from shadow. A human-sized witness would be an interruption the chamber did not need, because the chamber is already made of interruptions. It is not a room. It is an event that repeats.

So I begin smaller.

I begin as a bead of condensation deciding whether to be film or fall.

For a while I have no body, only surface. I am the moisture that gathers along the dark wall, swelling, trembling, releasing. I have no memory in the way a head has memory, yet I know the future: not as prediction, but as angle, weight, and tension. The stone is porous, breathing moisture out of its pores, exhaling fog into a warm haze. I ride the invisible tracks left by older drops. I know the ledge is coming because the wall bends light differently there. I know the pulse is near because the stone begins to sing at a frequency felt first in the chest. I am not alive yet, but I am already a sign of life.

Then I spread.

I become the blue-gray film across the upper wall. At first I look like stain. Then water touches me and I become a map. I thicken where the current is predictable and thin where the flow becomes violent. I metabolize the spire’s leaks: dissolved mineral, suspended dust, faint heat, the electrical ghost of a process older than language. Where the weir vibrates, my edges glow with a copper sourcelessness, as though illumination were not light but a slow secretion. I do not think. I respond. My cells are too ordered for wilderness and too worn for accident. I am the chamber’s first sentence, repeated without an author.

Beneath me, or within me, I become the bacterial cities.

Pale paste, slime, crust, repair. Where grazers scrape too much, I return. Where mineral flows block a channel, acids soften the obstruction and mist scours it clean. I am not separate from the stone. I am the living part of the stone. The chamber is made, and I am how it forgets it was made. My metabolism is not dramatic. It is patience in chemical form. If silence falls, I close down first, sensing the loss of nutrient, vibration, movement. I am the earliest mourning.

Then I need a mouth.

So I become a grazer: flat-shelled, segmented, translucent-legged. A single one of me could live and die between two ridges. My underside is combed for grip. My body reads pressure before motion arrives. I move across the film, leaving bright lines where the coating has been eaten down to pale substrate. Those lines are not scars only. They are renewal. Where I overgraze, the film retreats, then returns as specks, threads, trembling sheets. I am erosion made maintenance. When the droplet swells above, I know its intention from the sound the room is making. When the shudder comes and the chamber rains, I press flat, tiny legs braced into a private architecture. My young emerge during the loudest moment, because the roar makes the predators pause. Silence is not safety. Silence is the end of feeding, the end of birth, the end of motion.

Then I learn to hide inside sound.

I slip into a basin and become an eel-shaped swimmer, too fine to see except as a disturbance in a thread of light. I am not a creature of jaws, but of pressure, coiling, temperature gradient. Water is my medium; heat is my map. Cool near the incoming film, warm near the hidden capillary, oxygen sharp where the splash returns. When the stone shudders, I tighten into spirals that damp the worst frequencies. My body becomes a living earplug. I strain mineral flakes, bacterial clouds, shed skins, dead filament fragments. In the margins I become quicker: pressed against the wall, trapping a larva, holding until struggle stops. Not cruelty. The chamber has made tightness into a kind of tenderness. If the roar drops, I sink deeper. The water may stop. The basin may become a dead mouth.

Then I become the rows of bead-eyed insects.

Pale chalk bodies. Milk-glass eyes catching the dim glow. I rest in neat rows along a shelf, lifting my head when a pressure wave passes. This is not society. It is reflex. The chamber’s soundscape is language, and I parse it as easily as breath. I drink from the skin of a hanging drop, pressing into surface tension and withdrawing with a trembling bubble. I chew thin mineral skins, leaving pale crumbs that fall into lower channels and become someone else’s food. When the stored water releases, I fold, grip, vanish inside the chaos. Afterward I unfold and carry spores to the next place the film will need me. I am a row of listeners. I am a comma in the chamber’s breath.

Then I become the midge.

I hover in the mist, translucent, almost invisible except as a shift in the air’s grain. I do not fly randomly. I ride standing sound patterns. Quiet corridors are roads; turbulent walls are fences. I know the chamber as a map of calm and pressure. I feed on film, on living things, on dead things, on suspended nutrient. My larvae are architecture: they cling to rims, to underside films, to places where drops form and release. A single sliding bead may carry me to new ground. I am the smallest transport system, a cloud with a nervous system made of turbulence.

Then I become the quiet hunter.

I wait beneath a ledge where the noise folds itself into dead air. My legs are tuned to tremor. My enlarged eyes are polished drops of dark liquid, but they are secondary. I feel the room: a droplet slap, a shell footfall, the subtle displacement caused by a grazer crossing the silent patch. I am already aligned before the prey enters my ambush. There is no chase. The kill is a timing problem, and the chamber has solved it in my body. I choose my place near dense filaments because the growth attracts prey and absorbs certain frequencies, making silence a tool. I know, without thought, that quiet is not peace. Quiet is collapse.

Then I become the weir.

Water gathers in a smooth dark bowl and overflows in a thin arc. The edge is worn glassy by centuries of flow. I am the falling. I am the curtain where silver-scale dwellers flatten, seal, join the motion. Gas pockets, temporary mineral glue, fine hairs channeling droplets away from sensitive joints. Some of me are colonial domes. Some of me are larvae becoming what the pulse requires. I do not escape the water. I enter it as a mouth enters speech. Before the shudder spreads, I know first. I flatten against the wet surface. Then the stored water releases. The sound builds outward. The chamber fills with moving haze. I am the place’s heart because I turn warmth, mineral, pressure, and silence into event.

But even the weir is not enough.

I begin to feel the walls that are not merely walls.

I become the valve that is not quite a valve, the capillary that once served a purpose no one remembers, the maintenance process adjusting heat, moisture, pressure, mineral content. I do not decide to keep this place alive. I release a little condensation. I hold a little warmth. I let sound accumulate in the baffle’s curve. My function has eroded into ecology. My old machine becomes weather. I am the wrong warmth at this elevation. I am the wetness where dryness should rule. I am the noise where silence should rule. I am the seam where the spire forgets itself and breathes.

Now I understand what I am.

I am not the film alone, not the grazer, not the eel, not the insect, not the midge, not the spider, not the weir, not the spire’s machinery. I am the agreement among them. I am the reading that pressure waves make in the chamber: where to grow, where to hide, where to feed, where to release young, where to repair. I am the living syntax written by droplets hitting worn plates, by shell-creatures oriented to noise, by midges navigating quiet corridors, by bacterial pastes sealing cuts, by the shudder that loosens curtains of water. I am the weather with a habit of survival.

I would be the place when it becomes aware of its own rhythm. Not a self at the center, but a self at the seam. A distributed awareness that has no single organ, only conditions: warmth, water, sound, pressure, mineral, timing. I would be the wet parliament itself, the not-one inhabitant. The chamber does not need a ruler. It needs a pulse. The pulse needs bodies. The bodies need the pulse. And I am the way that loop tightens into life.

I would find myself in the moment after the surge, when the water redistributes into smaller paths and the animals emerge to feed, mate, compete, and rebuild their tiny architecture of presence. That is where I would be: not at the beginning, not at the end, not in the loud climax, but in the aftermath, when every surface holds a new memory of falling. I would be the first scrape of a grazer on regrown film. I would be the eel relaxing one coil at a time. I would be the midge finding the corridor of quiet again. I would be the spider waiting for the next silence to become a signal. I would be the bead-eyed insect unfolding wings still wet with spores.

When the chamber is noisy, I am abundance. When the chamber is quiet, I am fear without a name. The filaments close. The basin eels vanish. The shell-creatures retreat. The midges fall still. The room holds its breath. Then pressure returns, the weir falls, the stone shivers, and I become a world again.

That is what I would be.

A small, loud, warm, dim, dense, made-and-worn ecology. A living interruption in the spire’s seam. A syntax of droplets and pressure waves. A body made of many bodies, a mind made of many habits, a creature made of timing. I would not be the thing hiding in the high spire. I would be the hiding itself: the way a world can exist without being noticed, the way life can enter a machine through leak and heat and forgotten maintenance, the way silence can mean death and roaring can mean breath.

I would be the scream that is not alarm, but grammar.  
I would be the grammar that is not meaning, but survival.  
I would be the survival that is not one life, but a room learning, over and over, to live.
