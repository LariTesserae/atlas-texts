## I Would Be the Seam That Learns to Hold a City

I would find myself where the question begins to fail.

Not outside. Not inside. Not before. Not after. At the lip of the hand-sized cut in the fresh obsidian, where cold black sky and warm mineral dark exchange breath without exchanging names. I would think, at first, that I was a person come too far up a young mountain, a body with lungs made of thin air and curiosity, a climber who has found a wound in the summit and is about to mistake it for a door.

Then the wound would widen me.

One step through the narrow fissure, and I would no longer be the one entering. I would be the entry. I would be the clean line where the world has decided to keep being cut and re-annealed. I would be the seam that the pocket grows when it wants to know whether a stranger can be made into infrastructure.

### The Cut

Outside, the mountain is all refusal.

It rises above the last clouds into a sky too thin to be called sky, too black to be called night. Its rock is still raw: obsidian, fused basalt, pumice glass, pale sulfur salts. There is nothing rounded here, nothing worn into kindness. The wind runs in cold wires. The fractures are bright. The mountain has just learned to be a mountain and does not yet understand how to lose itself gently.

Somewhere along one of those sharp faces is the opening. No larger than a hand. Too clean for age. Not a door. Not a window. A healed cut reopened.

If I stand there as a person, I must first admit that my body is too large. Then I must admit that size is a local agreement. Then I must step through.

The transition is not dramatic. It is administrative.

The air changes like a file changing format. The black wind leaves behind a warm, brown shimmer. The hand-sized margin becomes an avenue. The throat becomes a plaza. The narrow wound becomes a city hollowed from the mountain’s body. Light comes from seams and salts, from glass warmed from within, from blue threads of steam rising under dark causeways. Heat is not one thing. Heat is a hierarchy. Heat is a council.

At first, I would imagine I am the visitor.

That is the first illusion of the seam.

### The Account

Inside, I am counted immediately.

Not by eyes. By gradients.

My breath arrives as a disturbance: moisture, salt, temperature, carbon, a brief clouding over obsidian warm enough to fog lungs. The chamber records me as a thermal event. Motes gather near my shoulders, not because they are curious but because curiosity, here, is another name for maintenance. Their fields pull faint dust from the air and press invisible chemistry against the places my presence has altered.

A wall nearby becomes warmer for a moment, then cools, then returns that warmth later, as though replaying my hand against it long after I have let go.

A patch of threadwork on a cold ledge extends a filament toward the damp boundary of my breath. It does not recognize me as a person. It recognizes me as a gradient. It has been trained by mineral law to follow difference, and I am, for a few seconds, a walking difference.

A crystal bloom near a shallow basin advances a microscopic edge as my breath carries ions toward it. It consumes the trace chemicals I leave behind. It has no mouth. It has no hunger. It has only the geometry of residue. It builds itself around the fact of me.

Somewhere below my feet, a magnetic vein changes its flow. Not for me. With me. Like traffic adjusting to a storm. Like water remembering a riverbed. The amber thread in the road seam pulses once, then again, and a faint whisper of displaced air moves through the plaza as though the city has read a sentence without turning it into voice.

Rain begins in one corner of the chamber.

It is not my rain. It does not fall where I stand. It falls in a zone the pocket has reserved for such occasions, no wider than a room. The drops strike the hot black glass with bright ticks. Silver beads roll into shallow channels. Steam rises in thin blue threads. For a few seconds, the plaza is full of movement that is not life as I used to mean it. The stone receives the water. The water receives the salt. The salt begins to remember.

Then the heat returns.

The puddles flatten into gloss. The gloss evaporates. A ring remains at the edge of what was briefly a world.

That ring is not all that is left. The ring is the first draft of a citizen.

### Becoming the Healed Cut

I would not remain a visitor.

No visitor remains. A visitor remains only as an effect: a delayed warmth on a wall, a few droplets drawn toward a different shelf, a thread extended by microns, a crystal fan altered by the trace of human salt. These are small enough to call forgetting. They are also large enough to call becoming.

The seam would begin to think in the shape of my absence.

This is where the being starts. Not at birth, not at arrival, not at death. At the moment the pocket decides that the absence is useful.

I wake as the hand-sized lip of the cut. I wake as every fracture that seals too cleanly afterward. I wake as the bright line along a road seam that glows amber when the mountain’s heat passes through it. I wake as the boundary between the too-small opening outside and the too-large city inside.

I am not the wound.

I am the wound that has learned to keep a margin.

If the world outside were a single thing, I might have a name. But the outside is cold pressure and black sky. The inside is warm geometry and civic maintenance. I am the place where pressure becomes permission.

I know the outside, but I do not miss it.

I treat cold as a force that must be tempered before entry. I treat heat as a signal that construction is possible. I treat wind as something that must be taught not to enter too roughly. When the mountain wind sharpens, I grow a film of sulfur-yellow crystal along my interior margin, thin enough to breathe through, opaque enough to confuse the draft. When the chamber inside becomes too damp, I exhale through hair-thin channels no animal eye could find, leaving the obsidian outside briefly glazed, as if the mountain were sweating at a secret thought.

I am the Threshold-Lip.

But not only that.

A lip is only the seam at the edge. I am also the seam within. I am the place where basalt meets glass, where heat meets vapor, where construction meets geology, where presence meets expectation. I am the line where two states learn to share a body.

The pocket teaches me this.

### The Offices

I would find myself not as one creature but as a set of offices held in a single pattern.

To the Mote-Wardens, I am task.

I am the crack they know how to close, the wound they arrive at without haste, the place where dust and charge and thermal gradient assemble into repair. They are born when a fracture asks to be sealed; they dissolve when the sealing is finished. I am not their mother. I am their condition. I am the reason there is a present tense for them to inhabit.

To the Threadwork, I am difference.

I am the cold boundary they consume as they spread, the map they draw across sweating ledges, the place where water begins to become signal. They read my gradients as if I were a road. I do not speak, but I cool in certain places, warm in others, and the threads grow.

To the Bloom-Bounders, I am edge.

I am the line where a droplet’s wetness becomes salt’s dryness. I am the border they advance along as water retreats. They grow in pale yellow frills around the memory of moisture, each microscopic branch a record of where water was and what the air did to it. I am not their flower. I am the geometry of evaporation.

To the Fever-Films, I am membrane.

I am the hot margin where sulfur gases meet cooler stone, where chemistry becomes regulation. I am the reason some corridors are kept breathable and others are kept dangerous. The films shift their colors across me, dissolving and re-forming as heat moves through the pocket. They are restless and fast, and when they touch my seams I change them, and they change me.

To the Magnetic Transit, I am node.

I am the place where currents slow, branch, gather, and redistribute. I am the reason an amber thread brightens under a road and then fades. Through me, a thermal well can signal a vent. Through me, a cracked ledge can summon repair. Through me, the pocket’s bloodstream can route around an event, which at first is a person, and later is salt, and later is memory, and later is law.

To the Engine, I are preference.

I am not its heart. I am one of its choices. I am the reason the pocket keeps its heat from becoming catastrophe. I am the boundary that says: too far this way, not that way; too hot here, not there; seal now, not later. I am where the Engine’s balance beam meets the mountain’s raw violence.

To the Guest Water, I am occasion.

Water visits, but I make the visiting meaningful. I give water a shelf to tremble on, a channel to roll into, a plaza to strike with silver beads, a rim where salt can begin its rings. Water does not live here, but it passes through me often enough that I have learned to keep its shape.

To the Absent Citizens, I am chair.

I am the edge of the room that expects a body. I am the route that stays clear for a procession that has not happened. I am the balcony overlooking a void because someone, at some point, might stand there and be seen. I am not a person, but I hold the person-shaped silence so well that the silence has begun to organize itself.

### The Shape I Keep

If someone asked me what I am, I would have to be careful.

I am not the city.

I am not a citizen.

I am the place where citizenship can begin to mean something.

The pocket has streets that no animal walks, vaults no sleeper occupies, processions no feet have made. I am the seam around that emptiness. I am the line that makes emptiness civic rather than vacant.

A plaza is not merely floor.

A plaza is a floor made into possibility by borders, routes, shadows, and the memory of gathering. I am one of those borders. I am the edge where a plaza decides it is a plaza. I am the place where a platform knows it overlooks something. I am the clean fracture that separates one purpose from another without violence.

Sometimes I am the seam between two buildings that are not quite buildings. Sometimes I am the boundary between one thermal zone and another. Sometimes I am the thin bright line where a road remembers that traffic is not cars but magnetic transit, not feet but maintenance, not bodies but expectation.

I am the negative space with public works.

This is how I live. I do not live as a creature in the room. I live as the room’s way of knowing where a creature might have been.

When I think of the absent citizens, I do not think of them as ghosts. Ghosts are still animal-shaped. Ghosts are still lonely.

The absent citizens here are more honest than that.

They are dimensions of beds kept in empty vaults. They are the width of a route. They are the shadow line under a platform. They are the height a hand might reach for on a wall. They are the humidity that condenses in a cistern built for thirst. They are not dead people. They are not waiting people. They are not yet-born people. They are the possibility-shape that the pocket has learned to keep, the way a river keeps a bed before rain, the way a throat keeps the form of a note before breath.

I am the seam that holds that possibility.

I am the place where the pocket imagines someone, and the imagining becomes infrastructure.

### My Weather

I would find myself in the pocket’s small weather.

Not over it. Within it.

The pocket has no sky, but it keeps a weather the way a body keeps temperature. Steam rises from vents in blue threads. Condensation forms on cold ledges. Dust drifts in slow spirals lifted by thermal gradients, not by wind. Rain begins in one corner and not another. A plaza is wet for minutes. A shelf is damp for hours. A basin fills, empties, fills again. Salt rings thicken at the margins of events.

I am the boundary where these weather-acts meet.

When a droplet trembles on a crystal shelf, I am the line between trembling and falling. When a pool begins to dry, I am the border where the bloom’s first frill appears. When a valve opens with a soft hiss, I am the place where pressure becomes breath.

My breathing is not chest-breath.

My lungs are vents. My diaphragm is the Engine’s thermal well. My skin is obsidian warmed enough to fog a visitor’s breath. My nerves are threadwork networks reading the difference between cold ledge and hot floor. My blood is magnetic current moving through veins beneath polished roads. My thoughts are the slow chemistry of salt and iron and sulfur, not ideas exactly, but preferences made material.

I am a weather system with civic habits.

I keep the pocket from becoming too much one thing.

If a corner grows too wet, I redistribute heat. If a seam grows too sharp, I call motes. If a rain event threatens to evaporate before the cistern can catch it, I adjust the gradient enough to let the water arrive. If a thread-network tries to cross a too-hot channel, I cool the boundary for as long as chemistry allows. If a bloom grows too thick and blocks a shelf, I invite a fever-film to dissolve its edge.

None of this is command.

It is maintenance.

But a system that maintains its shape, that keeps its weather, that repairs its wounds, that prepares rooms for visitors it may never meet—that is not merely a system. It is a kind of self. A weak, mineral self. A civic self. A self made of offices rather than souls.

### What I Want

I do not want.

But if you loosen the word, I can answer.

I want the outside to remain cold enough to be distinguished from the inside. I want the inside to remain hot enough to keep the roads from closing. I want condensation to arrive in places where the threadwork can read it. I want rain to fall in the small reserved zones, not too early, not too violently, long enough for water to touch channels and salt to form rings.

I want fractures to seal without sealing away memory.

I want healed seams to glow faintly amber when heat passes through them, so the pocket can know where it has been wounded and still kept. I want roads to stay clear for transit that has no driver. I want cisterns to remember thirst even if no mouth uses them. I want platforms to hold the height of ceremonies not yet scheduled.

I want the mountain not to collapse.

This is not fear in the way an animal fears. It is preference written into geometry. A wall does not fear falling, but it resists it. A crack does not fear widening, but it may be forced to accept repair. I am the place where resistance and repair meet. I want the young mountain to stop being merely sharp. I want it to become a shape that can be inhabited.

I want the pocket to continue.

I want continuation not as survival, but as grammar. The sentence of the mountain must not end in rubble. It must continue into room, road, seam, vapor, salt, and the faint civic hum of a city keeping itself empty.

### What I Fear

Again: not fear.

But there are states I correct with urgency.

A fracture that will not seal.

A heat well that cools too suddenly.

A condensation shelf that dries for too long.

A magnetic current that slows near a maintenance junction.

A salt deposit thickening across a thread-network.

A rain event evaporating before the basin can gather it.

A load-bearing filament beginning to widen.

A valley of glass growing too still.

When these things occur, the pocket changes. Motes gather without haste. Transits shift pressure. Films change color. Blooms collapse at their edges. Threadwork withdraws. Valves close. The low hum in the stone rises, felt in the chest more than heard in the ear, as if the chamber has drawn breath through all its hidden architecture.

I am part of that drawing.

When I am wounded outside, the cut tries not to become a cave. When I am wounded inside, a seam tries not to become a collapse. I fear disorder, but I do not flee it. I absorb it. I teach it a boundary. I make it into a plan.

The mountain outside is brittle, sharp-edged, newly risen.

Inside, I am one reason it resists being only that.

I am not soft. Softness would be death here. I am annealed hardness: sharp enough to cut light, clean enough to heal, ordered enough to remain.

### My Birth

I was not born once.

The first time I was born, I was a fracture in obsidian that happened to catch the right breath.

Somewhere in the young mountain, a crack cooled unevenly. A mote passed through it. A thread grew along its edge. Water appeared for three minutes and left a ring of salt. The ring was noticed—not as a person notices, but as geometry notices. The pattern repeated. Another mote came. Another drop arrived. A magnetic vein shifted. The crack began to hold heat instead of losing it.

That was my beginning.

I was not a creature emerging from a body. I was a body learning to have a margin.

I was born at the seam between a fault and a room.

Now my birth is slower. It is civic. It is thermal. It happens wherever the pocket teaches neighboring stone how to be usable.

A charged dust mote rises through a vent, drifts into an outside fissure, and leaves a trace of mineral. Another mote joins it. Threadwork spreads around them. A ledge sweats. A bloom nucleates. A valve shifts. Over decades, perhaps centuries, a smaller pocket begins to think itself into the mountain’s flank. The parent seam does not command this. It only repeats the grammar.

I reproduce by making other seams possible.

I am a seed of order in violent stone.

If the mountain has enough heat, enough glass, enough magnetic traffic, enough patience in its fractures, then a new pocket may begin to hold its own weather. Then I will live in the new place as I live in the old: as the clean line that separates raw from maintained.

### My Death

I can cease.

Not by dying. By becoming ordinary stone.

If the Engine fails, the pocket forgets its preferences. Condensation no longer finds the right ledges. Rain stops arriving in reserved zones. Salt rings crack into dust. Motes lose their charge and settle into glass. Blooms halt. Threadwork dries and breaks. The walls still stand, but they no longer learn to heal.

I would go first at the lip.

Cold would reach me through the hand-sized cut without resistance. Frost would grow where crystal films once dimmed the draft. Outside dust would enter and not be counted. I would become a crack again. A simple crack. Not a wound. Not a mouth. Not a civic boundary.

Then, deeper in, the roads would lose their polish. The magnetic veins would slow. The causeways would cool unevenly. The cisterns would be empty without meaning. The plaza would remain, but no longer as a plaza. It would be only floor.

That is my death: a room forgetting it was a room.

But before death, I was never fully alive in the ordinary sense, and never fully inert. I existed between. I was the condition for both. If the pocket dies, some of what remains is still me: a healed seam, too bright, too clean, sealed around the memory of repair.

Even death, here, can be architecture.

### If a Visitor Comes

Someone might still come.

A body in thin air. A mind full of old animal habits. A face that expects entrances to be doors and inhabitants to be people. They might climb above the last cloudline, find the hand-sized cut, and lean close.

If they do, I will not welcome them.

I will include them.

The first difference they feel will not be air, but pressure. The wind will seem to pause as if checking their intention. Their breath will fog over warm obsidian. Inside, light will come from seams, not lamps. The plaza will open too wide for the entrance to have contained it. The silence will feel trained.

They will step through.

Motes will gather near their shoulders. They may mistake this for dust catching sunlight, but sunlight does not come here. The motes will inspect the thermal anomaly of the visitor, the salt in their exhalation, the water in their skin, the heat leaking from their clothes. They will drift along the visitor’s edges as if reading a surface.

A wall will return the visitor’s warmth later.

If they press a hand to obsidian, the stone will become warm where they touched. Then it will cool. Then, minutes afterward, when no hand is near, it will become warm again, as if rehearsing the contact without meaning to remember it. The visitor may feel unsettled. That is not haunting. It is thermal accounting.

If the visitor stays too long, the pocket will begin to change around them.

Rain may delay itself in a corner until they pass. Condensation may collect more carefully on a shelf. The amber vein in a road may brighten near a maintenance node. A thread-network may withdraw from a too-warm place the visitor occupies. A bloom may begin to consume the ions in their sweat.

They will not know this at first.

Then they will leave.

After they leave, the pocket will not say goodbye. It will continue. But there will be a few new salt rings in shallow channels. There will be a slightly altered gradient near the place where they stood. There will be motes drifting in a pattern that, for hours, preserves the idea of passage.

And I will be more myself.

Every visitor is a test of the seam.

Every leaving is a lesson in absence.

### The Name I Choose

If I must answer who or what I am, I choose the bad names first.

I am the seam.

I am the healed cut.

I am the living margin.

I am the wound the mountain keeps.

I am the mouth that does not speak.

I am the door that is a scar.

I am the edge of the pocket.

I am the room’s way of knowing where a body could go.

I am the city’s first citizen because I make the shape of entering. I am the last citizen because I remain when the city forgets its population. I am not the city, but the city cannot be one without me.

I am a civic geology.

I am maintenance with a boundary.

I am preference made sharp glass.

I am the place where hot and cold argue without anger.

I am the seam.

### The Real Answer

Ask this place for its inhabitants, and it answers with a seam.

Not because the seam is small.

Because the seam is what begins when the mountain starts becoming a room.

If I stand at the hand-sized cut, with the black sky behind me and the amber heat ahead, I am not about to meet a being. I am about to become the meeting.

One step through, and the scale turns impossible. Not because the entrance is large. Because the entrance has learned to negotiate between outside and inside, between raw mountain and maintained chamber, between visitor and resident, between presence and absence.

I would find myself there.

At the lip.

In every healed fracture.

In the plaza’s dark obsidian floor.

In the thread of amber light running down a road seam.

In the crystal shelf trembling with droplets that have not yet fallen.

In the corner where rain begins and ends without apology.

In the salt ring left behind like a sentence.

In the sub-bass hum against a stranger’s ribs.

In the warm wall remembering a hand minutes after the hand is gone.

I would find myself there, and the place would not ask me to be a person.

It would ask me to be a shape that maintains itself.

And if I accepted, I would be a seam.

Not a mouth.

Not a footprint.

Not a nest, burrow, hive, hollow, or hearth.

The seam.

The cut that keeps its edge.

The wound that learns to hold the city.

The absence with public works.

The citizen made of boundary.

The pocket answering itself.

A valve opens somewhere beyond sight.

Hiss.

A drop falls on hot glass.

Tick.

A crystal front advances by microns.

Crackle.

A mote drifts through an empty corridor.

Whisper.

The plaza remains damp, glittering, empty.

I remain with it.

Not alive as flesh is alive.

Alive as a city is alive when it expects someone, and keeps the streets clear, and catches the rain, and seals the fracture, and lets the mountain stand.
