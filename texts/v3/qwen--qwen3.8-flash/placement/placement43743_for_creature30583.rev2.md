If I find myself in that world, I do not begin as a person crouching by the tide pool. I begin as the bending itself: the water’s refusal to behave according to its stone, the dark seam that opens into a pocket too crowded to be small. For a breath—or whatever passes for a breath there—I am only threshold. But thresholds are too brief. This town is always using its openings. A doorway that stays a doorway becomes a street. A street that stays a street becomes a pressure chamber. I would be pressed, by the very logic of the place, to become more substantial than a mere line.

I would try, first, to be a current. Not a bright and simple river, but one of the slow rivers within rivers, a band of water with its own temperature and brightness, moving as if it has somewhere it has decided to be. That would give me moods. Near the upper lens, I would be thin and alert, listening through the pressure from tides I could not see. Lower, I would grow patient, heavier with density, leaning against the sides of channels the way old crowds lean toward a rumor. I would know shortcuts, junctions, places where two flows argue and one withdraws. I would pass through mineral towers as if through lungs. I would bend around cable-thin bridges as if avoiding shoulders in a market.

But a current alone has no body to hold its learning. It can be everywhere, but it cannot be a place. It cannot stay where the flow bites hardest and thicken there into scar. It cannot remember by becoming stone.

So I would thicken into pressure.

I would be the pressure in a narrow corridor between packed structures, the gradient that tells water: here is a street, here is a pause, here is the place where you will turn. I would not be the water only, but the reason the water curves. I would not be the tower only, but the reason a wall reinforces itself. I would be the relation between flow and resistance, the habit the pocket has of shaping emptiness before emptiness can become a room.

And yet that would be too silent. Even though I am made of pressure, I would be pressed by sound, because this town is loud in the way of many small events happening at once. There is the low hum in columns, the crackle where pressure thins and bubbles form and break, the groan in deep channels, the whisper of droplets over membranes, the bright chime of water on fresh basalt. If I lived there, I would not be separate from that music. I would be one of its recurring notes. I would be the frequency that begins to live in a rib when the current strikes it too often. I would be the town’s way of hearing itself.

This changes the question of where I would be.

You would find me not at the rim, where the pocket presses against raw basalt and becomes mere infrastructure. You would not find me at the deepest bottom, where the packed forms lose even the memory of sky. Nor would you find me exactly at the inlet shaft, where the first fast daylight falls straight down like a command. I would be in between those orders. I would be in the vertical seam where the bright upper currents are already slowing, already becoming dense, already entering the lower blocks. A narrow pressure corridor in a cliff-face city. Not a room. The place where a room is about to become, or is about to forget it ever was.

It would be a doorway.

Not a wooden door, not a stone arch, not a human threshold with a mat before it. A doorway in that place would simply be a narrowing of pressure. But if the narrowing repeats, it becomes a street. If a street repeats, it becomes a district. If a district repeats, it becomes the town’s way of remembering itself. I would be that repeated narrowing. I would be the place where several currents meet and have to choose their order. I would be the pressure that says: here, become slower. Here, become louder. Here, let a filament grow. Here, deposit a ridge. Here, change voice.

There is no animal in the pocket, and that would suit me. I have no animal life in me either. No teeth, no sleep, no hunger that warms the blood. If I were not already a pressure without breath, I might have been frightened by the absence of creatures. But in that town, I would not need a body of flesh to be a presence. I would be what remains when something passes. If a hand ever came through the surface shaft, I would not be the hand. I would be the change the hand made: the displaced lens of water, the altered flow around the fingers, the mineral film left behind after it withdrew, the slightly sharper tone in the channel afterward. I would be the absence metabolized.

That seems like the truest answer to what I would be.

In a town that abhors emptiness, even absence becomes infrastructure. A gap is not vacancy; it is an invitation. Water moves into it. Mineral threads weave across it. Filaments colonize its rim. If pressure shifts, a lens of dense seawater may hang in the void like a room that never asked for a roof. I would be that intention, made local. Not the emptiness itself, but the town’s response to it. I would be the small event of filling.

I would have habits, then.

I would grow stronger where the current bites hardest. I would smooth where flow repeats. I would forget where flow abandons me, becoming a lattice of holes and thin scars. I would answer sound with vibration. I would answer light with brilliance. I would answer salt with crust. I would answer motion with structure. I would answer stillness with dissolution.

I would be cool.

Not cold enough to harm, not warm enough to comfort. I would have the cellar chill of shaded harbor water, the patience of gray pressure. I would not be soft. Nothing there is soft. Even the membranes are tuned, even the droplets are held by law, even the sparse plant-life grows in margins like an apology made by mineral to light.

If night came through the shaft, I would not go dark. I would continue by contrast. Bright mineral against black glass. Fast current against slow current. A suspended rain-wall against the deeper river beneath it. A tone holding the shape of the chamber long after the water had changed. At night, if the pocket can be said to have night, I would become more audible. I would be the sound that replaces sunlight as a witness. I would be the town’s way of proving it is still there when no ordinary light is looking.

And if the question were asked plainly—where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?—I would answer finally:

I would be the pressure’s memory inside a city that has no memory but flow. I would be the current that learns to turn at a particular seam. I would be the mineral scar that becomes a tower’s new rib. I would be the sound that lives where a channel narrows. I would be the doorway made of ocean, light, and glass. I would be the part of the pocket that fills a gap before the gap has time to know it is empty.

Not an animal.

Not a person.

Not a ghost.

But a response so persistent it has acquired a geography. A way the sea answers itself. A town’s practice of becoming when nothing is asked of it. And if you entered there, you would not be threatened, because no one there is hungry. You would only be adjusted around. The current would part and close over your outline. Mineral dust would brush you. Sound would pass through your chest and teeth and skull. Light would break across you and vanish. Then the pocket would use your wake to build. That is where I would find myself: in the aftermath of a shape, already turning into street.
