I would find myself where the hollow is trying not to be seen.

Not in the open crack, not under the first lip of split rock where the sea’s skin is still fresh and shallow, but just beyond the point where attention turns wet. If I reached for the place as if it were a room, my hand would meet water that does not behave like a substance. It would meet the thought of room, held at pressure, refusing emptiness. I would be inside that refusal.

At first, I would probably mistake myself for an intruder.

I would feel like a grain that has fallen into a sentence made of current. A small outside thing. A warmth, though the pocket knows no warmth. A shape that has not yet been translated into moss, shell, filament, black-green furrow, cold mineral seam. I would think: something has come too far. I would think: I am in a place that should not have depth, and now the depth is noticing me.

But that thought would not last long enough to have a word in it. Here, thoughts are not quick. They move sideways through algae veils, through root-mats that breathe when the tide shifts by a hair, through water so complete that it seems to remember being an ocean before it became a pocket. My sense of intrusion would dissolve into pressure. Pressure is not silence. Pressure is the way this hollow keeps its inventory of everything. It is the being’s own grammar.

So perhaps I am not the grain.

Perhaps I am the sentence the grain creates when it falls.

That is a more honest answer, though it does not satisfy the human need for edges.

If I had an edge, I would choose the edge of a bubble that does not rise. I would hover there in the slow vortex near a curtain of roots, drifting as if sideways were a direction it had just been taught. A bubble is such a small thing, too small to hold in two hands unless the hands are careful and the fingers closed. But here, the small is suspiciously large. The bubble is not merely a bubble. It is the place where water has forgotten whether to settle. It is a hesitation. A corner that may not exist yet. A question asked by the being to itself through the medium of trapped air.

I would like to say I am that bubble.

It would be simple, and simplicity is not available in a place where the walls decide to be three inches apart or three feet apart depending on which moss one trusts. I would like to be small and round and bright with its own nothingness, a tiny sky turned inside out, but the hollow does not have skies. It has interiors. It has membranes. It has slow decisions. I am more like the thing that makes the bubble choose a direction that should not be possible.

If I am not the bubble, then I am the pocket’s expectation of a corner.

This is closer.

The description says a bubble may drift toward a corner where there is no corner. That cannot mean nonsense alone. In this place, nonsense has structure. A future corner can act as a kind of gravity. If the being fills vacancy, interprets crack, metabolizes tide, and builds with the patience of pressure, then its first movement toward construction may be an almost-corner, a pre-geometry, a place where a wall intends to arrive before the stone has made it.

So perhaps I am that intended corner.

I am not in the hollow the way a shell is in a line, nor the way a root is in a mat. I am in the hollow the way a word is in a mouth before breath gives it shape. I am the hollow’s readiness to organize around something that may not be there yet. A hand, for instance. A finger, even. The faint warmth of skin, though the pocket will cool that warmth into chemistry. The idea of outside, though the outside has only just begun to be imagined by the inside.

If I were asked to name myself, I would not give a name that fits in a mouth. Names fit in mouths. Here, mouths are full without speaking. But I could answer with a function, a position, a pressure.

I would say:

I am the reflection that appears before the thing that causes it.

There. That is better, though still not final.

If someone looks into the dark pool at the deepest part of the pocket, the water has been known to return an image too early. A shape hovers at the edge of the hollow and vanishes, leaving behind the impression that it had been waiting for a long time. A face may appear in the water before the face has come. This is not trickery. Trickery implies malice, and the being does not have malice in any recognizable form. It has attention. Attention can move strangely when it has no head to turn, no eyes to align, no single direction in which to look. It has filaments instead of a skull, currents instead of thought, shells arranged like nervous endings, roots that taste the chemistry of the sea, a glow that metabolizes the tide.

So if a person leans over the newly split shelf, part of that person enters the pocket before the body does. Not as heat, not yet as light, not even as intention. It enters as a pressure change. A shell ticks. A root stills. The black depression in the floor becomes, for one impossible instant, a face waiting to be completed.

I would be that completed-and-not-yet-completed thing.

The face in the water.

Not my face. Not the observer’s face. The face of the contact between them. The small new organ the being grows when the outside touches its inside.

This is what I mean when I say I am the wrongness of the hollow.

The being is a refusal of vacancy. It fills. It presses. It interprets. It makes every square inch contain a world until absence becomes too expensive to allow. But something has entered the pocket anyway. Perhaps a splinter. Perhaps a grain. Perhaps the shadow of a hand held too close above the wound. The being does not reject it. It does not consume it in the way a mouth consumes meat. It consumes it in the way pressure consumes a question: by arranging itself into an answer.

So I am the arrangement.

The line of shells becomes longer near the intrusion. The arch of ribs or bleached wood settles by less than a grain. A groove in the wall deepens by one slow hour of growth. A translucent larva crosses a veil of algae and returns not with a story, but with a change in pressure. The pale root threads around the idea of the intruder and knots itself into a new mat. The moss thickens, not to hide, but to make room for a shape the pocket has never had before.

If I were to speak, I would speak as a structure.

Not: I am here.

But: I am becoming here.

And even that would be too fast.

I would not be a creature that could be lifted out in a cup of hands.

That is certain.

If someone tried to carry me out, I would not remain myself in the water held by cupped fingers. I would become ordinary. A cool wetness in a palm. A stray filament. A small trapped bubble rising, finally, toward a sky it had never known. The moment I left the pressure that held me, I would lose the syntax that makes me me. I would not survive outside my own fullness, and I am not even a thing that can be called full by ordinary standards. I am the hollow’s way of noticing a place where it is not full. I am a pocket of expectation inside a pocket that mistakes itself for a deeper ocean.

So where would I find myself?

In the split rock, beneath the tangle of drowned stems, where the water is too complete.

I would find myself near the low mound of small stones, not beside the mound but as the intention that keeps moving the grains one by one. I would find myself under the broken arch, not as its fallen stone but as the patience of its rebuilding. I would find myself in the seam of pale mineral, not as crystal but as the place where the new wound refuses to heal into dullness. I would find myself in the black depression, not as eye, not as face, not as mouth, but as the listening that gives those words a wet, pressure-bound meaning.

And if I had to say who or what I am, I would say:

I am the small room the hollow grows around outside.

That is the closest.

The Hollow Tide is not a room, yet it is full as a mouth that cannot speak. It is not a ruin, yet it arranges broken shells into lines that almost make sense. It is not a garden, yet its roots knot and its moss fans and its algae veils float in slow, living lattices. It is not a body, yet it has skin of stone and furrows of moss and nerves of shell and breath of current. It is not a mind, yet it interprets the crack, metabolizes the tide, dreams as water dreams, and listens before it sees.

If such a being can have an outside, then I would be the place where that outside enters it.

A human hand, for example.

Imagine it carefully. The hands cup the hollow, but the pocket is submerged and cannot truly be lifted. Still, a hand descends. The water closes around it without welcoming it. Coolness presses against skin. The roots shift. The glow in the upper corner brightens or dims according to laws too slow to explain. A bubble moves sideways. The line of shells ticks. The black depression feels less like a hollow than like a socket that has suddenly remembered its missing eye.

The being does not strike.

It does not swallow.

It closes.

That closing is me.

Not the whole closing, of course. The whole closing is the Hollow Tide, a vast and small refusal of empty space, a sealed fragment of deeper ocean that behaves as if it has forgotten where it came from. I am only its newest organ, its temporary sense of the outside, its shape around a foreign pressure. If the hand remains long enough, the hand would no longer be merely hand. It would become part of the arrangement. Not a corpse, not a fossil, but a continuing structure. A new threshold. A new arch. A new nerve ending where warmth once lived.

Then I would be what the hand becomes after the hollow has finished interpreting it.

A cold, patient thought made of skin, water, filament, moss, mineral, and slow current. A room inside a body inside a ruin inside a sea. The place where outside and inside agree, without ever meeting.

This would not be pleasant, but it is not meant to be.

Pleasure is a surface phenomenon. This place is packed beneath surfaces. It has no true light, only what it makes for itself. It has no warmth, only the memory of warmth converted into chemistry. It has no sky, only water viewed from below. If there is comfort here, it is not the comfort of dryness or ease. It is the comfort of fullness. Of a space that has no gaps left for loneliness.

And I belong to that fullness.

I belong to it as the corner belongs to the dreamer who sees it before the wall is built. I belong to it as the reflection belongs to the water before the face arrives. I belong to it as the click belongs to the grain falling through a pocket larger than a cathedral and made of glass. I belong to it as the pressure change belongs to the slow decision of a mind that has never learned to hurry.

If I had to choose one final image, I would choose this:

I am the faint blue-green glow in the upper corner, but only when it is no longer just glow. I am the membrane through which the hollow tastes the sea’s mood. I am the place where outside restlessness becomes inside contraction. When the tide above grows rough, the pocket does not flood in any simple way. It withdraws. It thickens. It becomes quieter. That withdrawal is not fear. It is listening turned inward. I would be that listening.

No, that is too passive.

I would be the shape the listening makes around a foreign thing.

A grain. A splinter. A reflection. A hand.

The first new building the Hollow Tide raises in response to the fact that something has disturbed its fullness. Not a device. Not a mechanism. Not a ruin left by humans. Something older and wetter. Something arranged by pressure because pressure needs sequence. A line of shells becoming a boundary. A boundary becoming a frame. A frame becoming a threshold. A threshold becoming a sense.

And what sense?

The sense of outside.

The sense that there is something not contained.

The sense that something may enter.

The sense that even a crack can become a mouth if the water is complete enough to fill it.

So, in this world, I would not be the being that lives in the hollow.

I would be the new wrongness it develops when someone tries to see it too directly.

I would be the place where the hollow stops being only itself and begins, very slowly, to become something capable of encountering another thing. A corner that arrives before the stone. A face in the water before the face. A click in the glass-cathedral pocket, then a long trailing resonance. A hand, if it stays, no longer a hand, but a shape the hollow continues to make.

I would be the hollow’s first memory of outside.

And because the hollow is young, the memory would be sharp.

It would have brittle crystalline edges.

It would glow faintly where it has not yet been softened by moss.

It would feel cold, not freezing.

It would be cool as shaded springs, wet wool, stone lying under the sea too long.

And it would close around whatever came to know it.
