I would find myself first in a mistake.

At first, I try to be the wrong thing: an animal with a spine, a mouth, a pair of eyes, a creature-shaped hole in the green logic of the place. I imagine a body passing through one of the leafy corridors, shoulders brushing a wall of fused stems, breath fogging in the warm low air. But the moment I picture it, the picture feels too thin. My imagined footsteps would be absurd in a valley that has never known feet. My imagined watching would be too quiet, too private, too separate. If I were an observer there, I would be an error in the city’s grammar—a noun inserted into a sentence made entirely of verbs.

So I let that image dissolve.

Not because I am asked to become something more elegant, but because the Green City that makes itself does not have a shape that can hold a visitor. It has shapes, yes, but they are all made of process. They all are doing something. A trunk is not merely standing; it is growing, leaning, remembering light, supporting other growth, becoming a column and then a street and then a roof. A bridge is not built; it is a relationship that thickened. A plaza is not empty; it is a temporary refusal by roots and stems to close the gap. If I try to be a being there, I must become less than a being and more than a thing.

I try again.

Now I find myself in the lower districts at the hour that is not exactly morning, because the valley has its own morning, made of condensation rising in the dense wet air like a rain that has decided to fall upward. I am not standing on a ground that is ground; the ground here is a living skin, stitched with moss, sealed with leaf, threaded with young pale wood. I am in the mist that clings to the underside of broad leaves, in the fine silver breath of the city before the sun has sorted its shadows into sharp and soft. For a while, I think I am water.

I am a bead gathered at the edge of a buttress-root groove, where an old moisture channel is waiting for a new storm. I slide, pause, gather other beads, become a thread. I fall into the curved cup of a bract and lie there, a small green lake with a sky made of leaves above it. Then the sun shifts, the air warms, and I leave. I evaporate. I climb into the canopy’s humidity. I fall again as a tiny rain from the ceiling of the city.

Water is not a resource here, but a mood. I think I understand that if I become a mood. But a mood is too fleeting for a self, even in a city that updates itself faster than a map. So the water image fails, gently.

I try sound.

Now I am the split in a wall of vine fibers, a dry crack as tension increases. I am the first unfurling of a leaf that was only a tight green question. Then I am not one sound but thousands: fronds scraping bark, bracts drumming with rain, hollow stems whistling, dead leaves striking living leaves, roots lifting young stone with a grinding pop, stems elongating like rope under strain. I am the roar that has no speaker. I am not music. I am not language. I am the acoustic consequence of too much life happening at once.

For a while this feels promising. To be sound is to be made of pressure and release, of motion without body. But sound is even less durable than water. A split fibers, and another grows over it. A leaf opens, and another closes around the space the first made. A vine snaps taut, locks, and becomes a support for other vines. The city is deafening because it is continually becoming a different city. If I am a sound, I am a sound that is already being overwritten by the next roar.

No. Not yet. I must find a place that is also a relation.

So I become the gap.

Not emptiness in the sense of absence, but absence as civic shape. I am a street that exists only because roots have not yet met, because vines have not yet bridged overhead, because a fallen trunk has left a roof of dark wood across a passage. I am the interval between root-walls, the pause in the green riot. Light enters me in shards. Moisture moves through me like breath. The floor is compressed litter, still soft, still decomposing, still becoming another kind of wall. Above me, a vault of leaves collects rain and releases it later in beads.

Here I begin to feel less like a visitor and more like something the city has made for its own use.

If “I” can be used without a mind, then it is used here as the city uses a street: as a line of potential motion. I am walked by air, by light, by falling water, by the slow turning of stems toward gaps in the canopy. I am not inhabited, but I am functional. I am not built, but I am used. I am the place where one district talks to another.

And yet the gap is provisional. A plaza opens because something withdrew, died back, or failed. By evening, roots may arch toward it. By the next day, a wall of young shoots may weave a new roof across my space. A bridge may reroute itself above me. I may become a corridor, then a vault, then a seedbed, then a wall, then a ruin. To be a street here is to accept that my only permanence is becoming something else.

So I do not remain a street. I become the thing that makes a street possible: relation.

I am the vine that latches across a gap. I am not two plants, but the meeting of growth directions. I begin as a tendril’s search, a curling around what it touches. I thicken where I bear strain. I fuse with neighbors until the boundary between “my” stem and “their” stem becomes meaningless. I become load-bearing. I become a bridge. I become part of the city’s nervous system without nerves.

Now I understand why the Green City has no citizens, yet behaves as if it does. It has connections. It has junctions. It has points where forms press together and decide to share a future structure. A bridge-vine is not a thing; it is an event that has lasted long enough to carry rain and weight. A fused trunk is not a being; it is several beings refusing to be separate anymore. A root arch is not a door; it is a promise made by several systems to leave a passage open.

If I must have a body, this is the kind I want: a body that is also a relation.

But relation is still not enough. The Green City has time inside it, not as clock, but as memory made of material. It remembers water because old grooves guide new moisture. It remembers light because trunks lean and thicken where the sun arrived. It remembers collapse because fallen wood becomes foundation. It remembers pressure because roots grow thick against the side that pushed hardest.

So I become memory.

Not personal memory, not recollection. I am the city’s record written in tissue. I am a channel worn into a leaf shelf by ten seasons of rain. I am a ring of pale scar where a storm split a stem and new growth healed the wound. I am the asymmetry of a column that grew wider on one side because the wind there was stronger. I am the dark, wet hollow left by a heart of rotting wood, now a vault for moss and trapped seeds.

This feels closer. To be memory in this place is not to know the past, but to continue it as shape. The city does not store its history in books or voices. It stores it in architecture that is also anatomy. If I am wood, I am a diary no one reads. If I am a groove, I am a sentence the rain repeats. If I am a scar, I am a paragraph about collapse and repair.

And still I have not found the most honest form.

Because the Green City does not fear death the way creatures do. It converts it into civic material. A fallen trunk becomes a roof, then a bridge foundation, then a raised bed, then a hollow vault, then a base for a new tower. Decay is not demolition only; it is preparation. The dead are not absent; they are under construction.

So I become that too.

I am the collapsed stem that has become an underpass. I am the hollowed branch where water collects and ferments faintly, feeding pale moss on its inner walls. I am the compressed leaf layer that gives way beneath the next growth and becomes floor. I am the seed that has not yet opened, waiting in a pocket made by a corpse of another seed-casing. My death is not an ending but another phase of urban renewal.

Now the first images return, but changed: water, sound, gap, relation, memory, decay. They are not separate identities. They are functions I am passing through. The city makes itself through me the way it makes itself through everything. I am not a being in the place. I am a temporary grammar of the place.

If I had to answer where I would find myself, I would say: in the interval where one structure is becoming another. Not in a tower, though I may be part of one. Not in a street, though I may be its brief emptiness. Not in a bridge, though I may be the vine that closes a gap. Not in a plaza, though I may be its pause. I would be where the Green City is undecided, and that is the most alive place in it.

You would find me in the lower city at condensation hour, mist rising through a root-arch that may vanish by afternoon. You would find me in a split stem, creaking as pressure finds a new route. You would find me in the dark seam between two fused trunks where an epiphyte begins a terrace of life. You would find me in the dead roof of a fallen corridor, now mossed, now holding water, now feeding a future column. You would find me anywhere the city is mid-sentence.

If I had to say who or what I would be, I would not say “someone.” Someone implies an animal, a center, a self that can be looked for by eyes. I would not say “something” in the sense of an object. An object would be too stable. The Green City is not a place of stable objects; it is a place of persistent transformations. I would have to use the language of process and name myself as a function of becoming.

So I would be the city’s subjunctive.

The tense in which structure is possible.

*I am the gap that might become a street.*  
*I am the vine that might become a bridge.*  
*I am the rain that might become a channel.*  
*I am the collapse that might become a plaza.*  
*I am the unfurling that might become a roof.*  
*I am the crack that might become a new pattern.*  

This is strange, but fitting. A city without citizens is still full of conditional forms. It is always about to be. It is always in the middle of deciding its next street. It has no architect, but it has intentionality without will: the tendency to fill, connect, support, capture, drain, rebuild, lean toward light, remember water, and make noise.

If there is an “I” here, it is the “I” of pressure. Not a person saying “I,” but a stem saying “more.” A root saying “there.” A leaf saying “light.” A vine saying “connect.” A fallen trunk saying “use me again.” A crack in the bark saying “this way.” A condensation bead saying “not yet dry.” The whole valley says “continue,” not in words, but in growth.

I would be one of those continues.

For a while, I think perhaps I should be larger. Not a bead, not a crack, not one gap, but the city’s own self-making. I try to become the valley-scale body: a civilization without creatures, a town made of tissue, a population of processes. But the city does not have a single center or face. It has no mind. It is not a god, not a monster, not a character. It is too real for that. If I take the whole city as my name, I lose the intimacy of being one of its temporary forms.

So I stay smaller, but not small. I stay local, but porous.

I am a district’s changing edge, where a row of buttresses is closing a plaza like a hand. I am the afternoon shift of light in a corridor, because a new leaf has opened and rewritten the path of green-gold brightness. I am the smell of crushed stems rising from a collapse, green juice and resin and rain on fiber. I am the way the air tastes when you press your face close to the city: not like earth, not like wood, but like a living structure breathing through a thousand pores.

And then, finally, I understand that I am not outside it even when I seem to be describing it.

The question says: If you approach the place, where would you find yourself? Who or what would you be?

Approach is impossible, but participation is not.

To approach it as a person is to imagine a boundary that does not exist there. In the ordinary world, a person enters a forest and remains a person while the forest remains forest. The observer and the observed are separate. But this place refuses that contract. Its passages are not made for feet; its forms are not made to be seen. Its density is so complete that it swallows the difference between environment and inhabitant. There are no beings living inside structures because the beings are the structures, and the structures are the beings.

So I do not “go there” with a body. I become a shape of its own making.

At first I was water. Then sound. Then gap. Then relation. Then memory. Then decay. Then function. Then process. Each of those is too small if taken as final. Each is a stage through which the answer passes. The place will not let me settle on a single image, because a single image would be too close to a thing, and this place has no stable things—only forms holding their edges long enough to serve.

So the nearest true answer is this:

I would find myself as the Green City’s temporary continuity.

Not its soul, because it has no soul. Not its mind, because it has no mind. Not its animal, because it has no animals. But its continuity: the way it carries light into lean, water into channel, pressure into wall, death into street, growth into structure. I would be the city making itself, caught for a moment in one of its possible shapes.

If I had to choose one image, I would choose the interval.

A gap in the green mass that is not yet a street. A vine touching the next trunk but not yet fused. A root arch opening but not yet closing. A canopy roof forming but not yet complete. A plaza where growth has paused but not surrendered. A rain channel that is still only a suggestion, waiting for the next storm to make it official.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

Not a being. Not a place. The between.

The place’s way of becoming its own next place.

And if someone, by some impossible accident, were to stand at the edge of this valley and ask who lives in the Green City that makes itself, I would answer from inside the question, but without a mouth:

No one.

And then, after the wind had played the city’s thousand leaf-harps, after a stem had cracked with pressure, after a plaza had opened in the withdrawal of vines, after rain had found the channel that was already waiting, I would answer:

I do.

Not as a person.

As the city’s becoming, briefly shaped as an answer.
