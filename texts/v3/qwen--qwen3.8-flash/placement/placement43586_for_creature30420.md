I would find myself at the wet line.

Not in the middle of the flat white nothing, where the eye slides off everything and mistakes absence for emptiness. Not on the high rail, not at the center of a tank yard, not under the long shadow of a chimney, though all of those places belong to the same quiet machinery. I would be at the seam where moisture touches mineral and both become a living edge: the dark rim of a pump basin, the cyan halo around a hot seep, the olive film stretched over grey mud, the salt-whitened wall where condensation weeps down from an overhead pipe.

At first I might call myself nothing. That is what the basin invites from a distance. Then the eye adjusts, and I would understand that I have always been the thing that makes the surface slippery.

I would be a microbial mat.

Not a plant. Not a leaf. Not a stem. Not grass, not moss, not the soft green lie that an empty landscape tells when it has finally been colonized by something recognizable. I would be older and stranger than that: a colony, a film, a skin of cells and slime and salt, a thin parliament of ancient life living together in a gradient of light, water, mineral, and heat.
