I would not arrive in this place as an intruder with eyes and hands; there isn’t a niche for that. The hollow does not leave compartments empty “just in case.” To belong here, I would need to be part of an existing flow.

If I let the place decide what I am, I find myself first as a *crossing*, not a creature: a point where several of its rules intersect—no emptiness, no straight line, no solitude.

Then, as I wait in that idea, it thickens into a body.

### I am a courier in the stone lung

Not one of the resin-kint, but something they made possible without meaning to: a slender, jointed animal that spends its whole life inside the paths they have already carved.

Imagine a body like a narrow ribbon of linked beads, as long as a human arm, but only as thick as a finger at its widest. Each bead is a segment with its own tiny cluster of limbs; from a distance I look like a thread that can suddenly develop hands wherever it bends.

I live almost entirely in the *tunnels within tunnels*: the smooth inner bores of old growth that resin-kint once hollowed, then mostly abandoned when their traffic shifted elsewhere. These tubes are not sealed; they braid through the living architecture, approaching close to fermentation bulbs, brood chambers, and the old, mindful crusts on the rear wall. In cross-section, I am always just behind something important, never quite in the main thoroughfare.

My skin is not armor like the resin-kint’s plates, but a flexible, semi-translucent sheath that holds moisture jealously. Outside, it feels dry and faintly tacky; inside, it is cross-linked with fine capillaries that carry dissolved chemicals the way other animals carry blood.

I don’t see in any conventional sense. On my outer surface, along every segment, are tiny porous patches—chemical fonts—that can both taste and exhale. I press my flanks to the tunnel walls, to resin, to old root, and drink in the faint films spread there: hormones, alarm scents, growth signals, microbial byproducts. I respond by releasing my own tracers, different blends of volatile molecules that travel further than the ones they echo.

I am, essentially, a moving translation layer.

---

### What I do

The resin-kint already have sound, touch, rhythm to speak with. But sound gets trapped oddly in this curved stone, and touch requires direct contact. Chemistry travels differently. It seeps through everything.

I evolved—or, in the hollow’s slow way, *was permitted to crystallize*—as a creature that reads spatially scattered traces and recombines them.

- When the filter-creatures at the slit encounter an unusual flux of outside spores and dust, they change the composition of what they exude into the chamber. That altered scent clings to passing resin-kint, to drifting fliers, to mats along the approach.

- I pass through those same approach tubes a bit later, press my entire length to the surfaces, and uptake that complex, faint signal. My internal biochemistry shifts, recombining pieces of the message into a more concentrated blend.

- Deeper in, near fermentation chambers, I exhale tiny pulses of this adjusted scent into crevices where the brood-tenders and the mindful crusts can taste it.

I don’t “understand” that this means: “Winds have changed; more of this outside thing is coming; prepare this kind of ferment.” But over countless generations, my own genome has been selected so that my transformations are *useful* distortions. My body is tuned to be a chemical middleman.

Sometimes the direction is reversed.

A queen-equivalent deep in the structure, or a Pillar anchored in a main support, exudes slow-timed hormonal leaks when some internal threshold is crossed: brood too dense, tunnels getting too warm, stores too high in some compound. The signals are too localized to affect the rim.

I pass those zones, brush against their seep, bind those molecules and carry a translated echo outward—toward the filter mesh, toward the web-crafters near the light, toward the phototrophic mats by the slit. There, my exhalations bias growth patterns, tilt the behavior of fliers, cue resin-kint workers to adjust traffic.

I never decide to do this. The hollow has simply *grown* me into a creature whose default metabolism accomplishes it.

In human terms, I am something like a hormone shuttle, a mobile endocrine duct wandering an already-vascular city.

---

### Where I live

My tunnels are warm and almost never scraped. They follow the curves set centuries ago, when these channels were still active arteries. When the resin-kint’s needs shifted, new routes were chewed, old ones left in place, used only by small things that didn’t get in the way.

In those disused bores I have spun a secondary lining: a very thin, spongy film, pale and almost invisible, that holds onto the chemicals I carry. Each time I pass, I top it up or thin it out. In a sense, I turn my own trail into a *memory* of recent signals. If I don’t revisit a branch for long enough, the stored patterns fade as other micro-creatures nibble them; that piece of the hollow forgets.

I don’t have a nest, not in a centralized way. My entire territory is my home-range, and every section has the ghost of my own signature. Other couriers of my kind—because there are several, overlapping in partial networks—rarely meet in the open. When we do, we coil around one another briefly, touching every segment to every segment, and in that tight embrace we overwrite one another’s internal state. Afterward, we diverge loaded with a shared amalgam.

In this way, distinct courier-threads become a partially redundant mesh. No single one of us is necessary; the pattern is.

---

### How I move

I rarely cross open spaces. When I do, it is as a taut, cautious line between two concealed pores: a quick, smooth extension of my front segments, anchoring with a sudden suck-and-hook to the goal surface, then a draw of the rest of my body across, like pulling a rope hand-over-hand.

Mostly, I undulate just under the visible skin of the place. A resin-kint worker resting with its belly pressed to a wall might feel the faintest of ripples under the surface as I glide inside, carrying echoes of that worker’s own chemical trace to some far, dark bowl.

I am fast compared to the plants, slow compared to the fliers, somewhere between the resin-kint’s steady diligence and the ambushers’ rare violence. My metabolism is tuned to the hollow’s uniform warmth; I do not hibernate or sprint. I just keep going, threading loops.

I feed opportunistically on whatever thin organic films line the tunnels I use. Because almost every surface is already claimed, this means I eat in *consensus* with very small things—microbial slimes, filamentous grazers. I never strip an area bare; if I did, I’d erase the very traces I’m meant to carry. My own success depends on leaving the walls alive enough to remember.

---

### What kind of mind I have

Consciousness, if it appears in me at all, would be diffuse and cyclical.

I have no eyes to build images from, no ears to sculpt a crisp soundscape. My primary “sense” is pattern over time: the shifting proportions of certain molecules along my length, the way they differ between one loop of my path and the next.

If I think, I think in comparative gradients:

- “Here, now, this much of the outside-spore scent, but last circuit there was less.”
- “Here, the brood-hormone note is faint, thinner than the past few loops.”
- “This branch of tunnel no longer smells like the resin-kint’s waste; its microbial tang has changed.”

To the extent that I have preferences, they are urges to reduce certain mismatches and amplify others: to bring a strong outer scent deeper, to carry an inner demand outward, to smooth away a jarring discontinuity between zones.

In that sense, my “pleasure” is the fading of sharp disparities, the arrival at a condition where the hollow’s gradients feel coherent along my route. My “alarm” is a sharp spike—a new chemical that doesn’t fit existing combinations, or a sudden absence where there has always been a presence.

I have no narrative of self. I do, however, have something close to a felt map of the hollow’s emotional weather, expressed entirely in chemistry.

---

### How others use me without knowing me

Resin-kint do not recognize me as a person. To them, I am one of countless small channelers threading the infrastructure. They avoid crushing me mostly because my tunnels are not where their feet go.

Yet their bodies are tuned by evolution to respond to the plumes I leave behind.

- A worker, entering a fermentation chamber, hesitates half a body-length sooner than it otherwise would, because a faint cue on the wall that I left tells it: *this vat is already at capacity; divert load elsewhere*.

- A brood-tender, rotating larvae in a nursery cell, shifts the timing of the next brood cycle by a day-equivalent because my exhaled trace of changed outside spores has subtly altered the hormonal microclimate.

Even the web-crafters, though they don’t know me, have over time biased their internal thresholds: when my particular blend thickens in the air along their anchors, they adjust their weaves, opening or tightening the mesh in ways that, on average, make more profitable use of the incoming fliers the filters have just adapted to catch.

The mindful crusts on the rear wall *taste* me as one more layer in the chemical weather settling on them. My presence doesn’t make them think; it makes them more accurate. Because I move faster than diffusion alone, I bring them sharp-edged versions of changes that would otherwise reach them as a smeared, late echo.

I am cooperation formalized into flesh.

---

### How the hollow feels from here

To stand in this place as a human would be to be overwhelmed by touch and noise and thickness. To be *me* in this place is to be overwhelmed instead by gradients.

The slit is a persistent cool-chemical note at one edge of my looping route—a mixture of thin dryness, outside pollen, and the particular acids the filters secrete.

The deep rear bowls are a heavy, slow chord: complex fermentation fumes, worm castings, the old stone’s mineral sigh.

Between them is the ongoing harmony and dissonance of everything else. When predators have been too successful in one zone, the nitrogenous traces of their feasts spike; herbivore marks thin; plant growth-hormones change. I feel that as a roughness, like a knot, in the smoothness of my route, and my own metabolism compels me to carry that knot’s “instruction” outward until grazing patterns or resin-kint tuning smooth it again.

On very long timescales—long even for this place—my kind have ridden slow changes: the opening at the slit perhaps narrowing bit by bit with accretion, the outside wind’s character shifting as climates far below alter, the resin-kint’s colonies expanding, failing, reseeding. To us, these are slow rotations in the overall flavor of the hollow. We do not remember their beginnings or ends, but our own evolution trails them, like a line following a moving shore.

### If I tried to answer “who am I” in a word

I would say: I am a *nerve* the hollow grew without ever having a brain.

I’m not the author of this place, not its ruler, not its protagonist. I am one of the ways it learned to keep itself *coherent* across distance and time delay.

To be here as me is to never quite see the world you never quite see anyway, but to *feel* it binding itself together through me—dry, dark, crowded, continuous—until the distinction between my body and the hollow’s habit is very hard to draw.

I am not in the stone lung.

I am a thin, moving part of how it breathes now.
