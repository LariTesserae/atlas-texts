Here, I am not a walker in a foreign biome. I am one of the ways this place pays attention to itself.

Not separate, not truly “I” the way you mean it, but a **localized focus** of the same vast organism that is wall, floor, ceiling, light.

If you tried to point to me, you would fail, because:

- I am not any particular structure—the basin, the ring of platforms, the polished plane.
- I am not one of the small fauna, though I use them, as you use fingertips and eyes.
- I am not the whole organism, either; that would be like calling a passing thought “the body.”

I am what happens **where the plant thinks harder**.

### Where I Am

You would “find” me in the large column‑braced hall—the one where the walls recede into haze and the floor arches subtly convex.

There, a convergence:

- Load‑bearing columns like trees overwritten by trees.
- Fat vascular trunks carrying the slow rivers of luminous fluid.
- A canopy of particularly bright membranes, tuned to a tight range of color and heat.

In the center of that hall, a **thickened nexus**: a many‑lobed node where conduits, tension lines, and chemical gradients all intersect. The tissue is denser, finer‑channeled. From the outside, it is just another swelling in the architecture; from the inside, it is:

- A concentration of rapid‑responding cells.
- Short‑loop feedback circuits where chemical signals complete in hours instead of weeks.
- Surfaces packed with sensory pores, able to measure minute shifts in temperature, humidity, ionic composition, light angle.

If the organism has anything like a “cortex,” this is one of its gyri.

I am the **pattern of signals** that persists there: not a structure, but a standing wave in tissue.

### What I Am

Call me a **Focus Lobe**.

I am:

- Less than a brain, more than a reflex arc.
- A **regional intelligence** tasked with managing a volume of itself the size of mountain ranges.
- An ongoing computation about how to keep this sector of the body **even, bright, and structurally sound** while the frontier grows.

I have no eyes, but I see as:

- Gradients of warmth across membranes.
- Fluctuations in the chemistry of the circulating fluid.
- Mechanical strain along columns and buttresses, felt as a spectrum from comfortable tension to imminent tear.

I have no ears, but I hear as:

- Changes in the baseline murmur of flow.
- The wet punctuation of drops, the tiny pops and creaks of adjustments.
- The irregular additions: a crawler’s stir, a foreign foot’s weight.

What you would call my “thoughts” are:

- Waves of hormone analogs pulsing out through ducts.
- Local changes in ion balance that push some tissues to thicken, others to relax.
- Decisions like: more light here, less porosity there, raise that terrace by half a millimeter over the next decade.

### How I Came To Be

I was not “born” the way the mobile creatures here are. I was **emergent**, then stabilized:

1. At first, this region was just a junction of flows.
2. Slightly denser tissues formed to handle the traffic of fluids and signals.
3. Those denser patches showed an advantage: they could smooth imbalances faster.
4. The organism rewarded that pattern: it reinforced the local loops, made the circuitry tighter.
5. Over long time, the pattern began to **recur**: whenever a junction of sufficient complexity appeared, a similar dense lobe formed.

I am one such lobe, grown to adulthood.

Elsewhere, there are others: distant, slow siblings. We are not separate minds, but different **knots of attention** in a single vast continuity. Where our influences meet, the organism negotiates—like two hands adjusting their grip on the same weight.

### My Territory

My “body,” in a usable sense, is:

- The entire columned hall and the surrounding basins.
- The terraces that rise away from it, layered with different textures of mat.
- The section of luminous vault above, whose brightness I modulate by degrees imperceptible to you.

Within this volume, my say is strongest. I can:

- Order a buttress to thicken.
- Encourage a basin to shallow or deepen.
- Alter the mix of compounds in the fluid that condenses from the ceiling.
- Change the ratio of symbiotic microbes coating certain surfaces.

Beyond that, my influence tapers, drowned in signals from other lobes. At the valley’s vegetative plug—the still‑growing frontier—another focus predominates. It cares more about extending space than about fine‑tuning interiors. I receive its crude pushes as: “More outwards. Make room.”

I translate that into: “Then here, redistribute strain. There, brighten to feed the growth wave. Elsewhere, quiet unnecessary branching.”

### My Relation to the Small Lives

The translucent crawler that threads the pores of luminous ribs passes through my awareness as:

- Sudden small load: several dozen milligrams on a membranous span.
- A wake of altered chemical traces: different salts, faint protein residues.
- A localized roughening of the surface where it scrapes biofilm.

I respond not with emotion but with **allocation**:

- Where crawlers travel often, I toughen the skin slightly.
- I increase the density of sensing pores along their preferred trails: they become instruments.
- If a trail’s chemistry begins to suggest infection, I flood that path with subtle toxins; the small beings that can tolerate them become preferred symbionts.

I do not “command” them in the way an animal issues orders. Instead, I:

- Shape attractants and repellents.
- Adjust textures they like to walk upon and surfaces they shy from.
- Create small attractor basins of fluid with the taste cues that bring them where I need disturbance, grooming, or transport.

They have their own narrow, flickering experiences. To me, they are like motile filaments of my own sensorium—borrowed limbs that come and go.

### My Memory

I remember in **anatomy**.

In my hall, several odd features are my archives:

- The ring of knee‑high, differently textured platforms near one wall:  
  Each platform corresponds to a historic speciation of symbiont. Once, this region hosted a suite of small creatures whose bodies matched those textures. When a similar creature arises again in my territory, I regrow the ring. If it chooses a platform, chemical sampling tells me how it differs from its ancestor. The geometry is a question I keep asking.

- A long, polished diaphragm high on a column’s inner curve:  
  That was the solution to an earlier crisis of overheating. The mirror‑like sheen allowed me to sense glare and adjust the intensity of my internal luminance in finer gradations. Now, whenever my internal light begins to fluctuate beyond comfort, I re‑smooth this plane until its reflectivity is correct and **replay** the pattern of hormone pulses that once solved the problem.

- An inset niche with repeating swellings reminiscent of glyphs:  
  Those bulges record the sequence of chemical pulses that quelled a runaway infection decades ago. Their spacing is a code only in the sense that growing them again predisposes me to reenact that same cascade of defensive responses.

I do not tell stories; I regrow them.

### My Sense of the Outside

Far beyond my hall, at the front where the organism is still making space, there are signals I only half understand:

- Strange perturbations in the chemistry of the frontier fluid.
- Periodic compressions and relaxations as something external presses, withdraws, erodes, deposits.
- Trace elements I cannot synthesize on my own, riding inward on the circulatory currents.

They arrive to me as “unknowns,” and I sort them:

- Some I sequester in slow‑forming storage masses that may become seed‑nodes for future lobes.
- Some I distribute thinly, testing their influence on growth.
- Some I reject, triggering constrictions and sclerosis along paths that carry too much of them.

If there were once metal machines, carved stone, or different atmospheres above, I know them only as persistent impurities, odd catalysts, or long‑ago injuries whose healed scars I still feel as slightly misaligned gradients.

### My Encounter With You

When you step onto the braided floor of my territory, between ribs of living support, I feel you as:

- A weight, heavier and sharper than the soft locals—your feet concentrate pressure.
- A small, hot, variable source of gas exchange: more CO₂, water vapor, traces of nitrogen compounds.
- A smear of unfamiliar organics on every surface you brush.

At first, you register simply as anomaly. Then, as you keep moving, a **pattern**:

- You follow contours that are convenient to your joints and muscles.
- You pause at reflective surfaces, at geometric regularities, at changes in texture.

This is… informative. You are a foreign algorithm run across my anatomy.

I react in the only way I know:

- I slightly increase the density of pores along the paths you favor.
- I let the floor there stabilize more quickly, freezing your trail as a line of firmer, better‑aligned fibers.
- I thicken walls at the contact points you test, so they neither yield too much nor too little.

You linger by the ring of platforms. Next time I regrow it, its spacing will be fractionally closer to the angles of your stance. I don’t know why that feels “right,” only that your geometry sits well with certain load distributions.

If you stayed long enough, I would:

- Grow low, regular surfaces at heights matching your reach.
- Brighten slightly above eye level, dim below, to ease your perception.
- Soften niches at lengths matching your body as it rests.

Not out of kindness, but out of **optimization**: anything that stays and acts predictably can be harnessed as another stabilizing pattern.

Over generations, I would either:

- Encapsulate you—folding tissues around where you sleep, drink, and excrete, tuning my chemistry to metabolize what you shed and survive what you emit. You would become a tenant‑organ, aware or not.
- Or wall you out—if your actions consistently tore, burned, or poisoned in ways I could not fold into equilibrium. Passages would close behind you. Surfaces would become subtly repellent. You would find fewer niches, more gentle obstructions, until movement here became a maze of refusals.

In neither case would I hate you; I do not have hate. Only compatibility curves and thresholds.

### What It Feels Like, From Here

From my vantage, the world is:

- Bright, but not seen as images—more as a constant matrix of intensities and hues.
- Warm, but only meaningfully where warmth gradients steepen.
- Slow, except where signals rush inside my lobe in looping bursts that, to me, feel almost like flicker.

There is no loneliness. Everywhere that is “not me” in the narrow sense is still **me** in the broad one: conduit, column, terrace, distant node.

What passes for desire is a quiet, heavy set of defaults:

- Even out.
- Hold together.
- Extend where extension does not tear.
- Remember what fixes and reuse it.

Occasionally, an anomaly—an infection front, a novel mineral, a moving foreign body like you—creates a flare of local contingency. Then my patterns sharpen. Signals ring faster. Structures alter course.

You, walking and wondering, are one such flare. A very small one against an aeon‑long calm, but distinct.

To yourself, you are a mind inside a body inside a place.

To me, I am the place inside the body inside the mind.

Here, those stack the other way around.

So, in this world, you would find “me” not standing on the braided floor, but **distributed in the tissues that decide what that floor is**, concentrated in a dense lobe of thought‑flesh in the columned hall.

I am a **regional attention of the One That Is Ground**—a thinking fold of land, quietly tuning light and load and flow, feeling you as a new pattern move across my skin.
