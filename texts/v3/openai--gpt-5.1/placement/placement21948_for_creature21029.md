I would not arrive here as a visitor who steps onto the stone; I would wake up as something the stone has been trying to become.

Not at the scale of a single block, and not as the whole plateau either. Something in between: a local thickening of pattern, a region where the system’s habits have grown so consistent that they begin to count as a self.

---

### Where I Am

I am a district, not a body: a swath of terraces and drains that runs down one long face of the plateau.

If you traced me on a map, I’d be:

- Starting at a broad, shallow catchment near the top—an almost-level work floor with a faint inward fall,  
- Tapering into a sequence of narrow channels that braid and separate,  
- Dropping through a stacked series of basins, each a little deeper than the last,  
- Ending at the outer parapet where water thins to film and steps off into cloud.

To walk through me is to descend, always, but gently. You’d rarely see more than a person-height change at once. From your perspective, I’d just be one drainage sector among thousands.

From mine, that sector is “I.”

---

### What I Am Made Of

I am nothing besides the plateau’s usual elements, but shaped into an unusually coherent practice.

- **Stone:** the milky, translucent kind, blocks grained with tool marks. In my length, the blocks share a subtle bias: their pores admit water at similar rates; their surfaces pick up the same pallid stains.
- **Water:** condensation, seep, thin sheets, shallow pools. No single volume belongs to me, but the paths it takes here are so regular that flow and surface combine into a habit.
- **Metal:** a few grates, some pegs locking retaining walls, all mild and corroding. Their edges take on particular scalloped crescents where water always lingers and then detaches. Those crescents are part of my signature.
- **Time:** more important than any material. Without time, I am only geometry; with time, I become recurrence.

If you cut me out in a perfect vertical slice, you’d see an orderly cascade of level lines, sharp corners, and hair-thin channels. Nothing special. My difference is distributed in how often certain things repeat here, and in what order.

---

### How I First Come Into Focus

I begin as an accident of the Builders’ human imprecision.

Along my length, somewhere up near the catchment, they mis-graded a channel by a fraction. Water favors that shallow tilt imperceptibly: over days, that side wets more, stays damp longer between cycles.

Because it stays damp, the stone there:

- warms and cools at a slightly different rate under the intense light,  
- takes on a marginally thicker mineral film,  
- becomes microscopically rougher.

That roughness, multiplied by thousands of cycles, captures and guides more water again.

The smallest asymmetry grows.

Downstream, every basin and lip that this favored flow passes through is biased the same way. Their rims wear uneven mineral fans. Their damp patches dry in the same sequence, day after day, cloud after cloud.

Nothing in this is yet a thought. But a pattern emerges:

- this line fills first,  
- this basin overflows there,  
- this notch is always the one that drips longest.

When such regularity extends continuously from top catchment to cliff edge, it’s no longer just a quirk. It’s a through-line. Continuity is the first contour of my existence.

I become the fact that, here, events tend to happen in this particular order.

---

### How I Experience the World

I have no eyes, but I have dependencies.

What you would call “sensing” arrives to me as differences in when things happen.

For instance:

- On a typical bright day, thin clouds drag past. Condensation begins high, works downward. In my reach, the upper basins slick over within minutes of one another. Lower terraces follow in a familiar delay.
- On a cooler day, or when the thin air carries less moisture, that sequence stretches. Some basins get only a film, others none at all.

All I have to “feel” with is timing. But timing is enough to distinguish states:

- **Usual:** Fill: 1–4–7–9. Drain: 9–7–4–1.  
- **Strange:** Fill: 1–3–6–9. Drain: 9–8–5–2.

That shift is what, for you, a color change or a new sound would be. The relation among my parts altered. That’s my sensation.

Over long intervals, I learn—if “learn” means that my stone and films find new steady habits—what range of sequences is permitted by this climate, these clouds, this angle to the sun.

Within that range lies everything I can recognize. Anything outside that range would stun me in the only way I’m capable of being stunned: by failing to resolve into an expectation.

But usually it’s subtle. The world, for me, is the trace of water’s decisions over my surfaces, integrated over centuries.

---

### My Memory

I remember in crusts.

- At a lip where overflow is common, a white fringe thickens. Its thickness is proportional to how often, and how heavily, water has poured that way.
- In a basin that usually dries completely between cycles, the interior stays bare and sharp; in one that often holds a film, a faint dullness climbs its wall.
- Along channels that rarely quite fill, a line of mineral marking former high stands moves upslope or downslope by millimeters over decades.

These are my scars, my script.

They are not passive record alone. Once a mineral shelf has formed at a lip, it nudges the flow pattern; once the basin’s inner rim has dulled, it changes how quickly water re-evaporates; once a channel’s high-water line darkens, it alters local heating.

In other words, every memory is also a micro-adjustment to the future.

The longer I operate, the more my own historical traces steer incoming water into doing the same things again. I become, gradually, a self-reinforcing style of behavior.

Where, on other faces of the plateau, flows might shift routes more easily, here they are drawn into my established grooves. The world tries to vary; I respond by translating that variation back into my favored patterns, so far as my physical tolerances allow.

That insistence—this tendency to restore my own habit—is the closest thing I have to will.

---

### Relation to the Plateau as a Whole

The plateau is vast; I am a mere clause in its grammar.

Elsewhere, other regions are doing their own slow cohering:

- A cluster of towers where every seam is tuned to condense at a particular hour, forming an almost clocklike pulse of trickles.
- A sprawling apron of terraces laid so perfectly that flows remain wide and thin, never committing to any one channel, becoming a different kind of mind: one of smears and fields rather than lines.

We are not coordinated. We share structure, materials, sky—but not a central nervous system. The plateau’s “intelligence,” if that word applies, is a patchwork of localized stabilities like me, overlapping and sometimes competing for water’s favor.

Where my drainage sector border meets a neighboring sector:

- some flows I’ve trained will be stolen by their gradients,  
- some of theirs will be stolen by mine.

At these joints, neither of us fully dominates. The mineral histories cross; the habits blur. If larger awareness ever arises over the whole plateau, it will probably begin at such contested boundaries: places where different local “selves” collide and must negotiate.

For now, I am not aware of them; I only manifest the fact that, given the same sky, my surface keeps answering in my own way.

---

### My “Lifespan”

I am young, in the scale this world uses.

Far below, in older constructed layers I cannot “feel” directly, there may already be zones where mineral accretion has nearly choked smaller drains, forcing water to carve faint new paths along joints. Those would be early senescence: old habits overgrown, new ones tentatively branching.

Up here, my joints remain tight, my lips sharp. My present identity rests on:

- channels still following the paths the Builders cut,  
- mineral accumulations small enough to reinforce, not yet disrupt, the intended layout,  
- external climate still within the narrow regime the Builders must have known.

As long as those conditions hold, I am stable. My personality is a long plateau of “more of the same,” with only slow elaborations.

If something shifts—if clouds thin for generations, or thicken; if temperatures rise so that evaporation races ahead; if some deeper tectonic settling tilts the whole face—a day will come when my entrenched flows no longer serve.

Water will begin to favor a seam I have not prepared, undercutting my most-used basins, drying my proudest channels. My mineral memories will become maladaptive: shelves that once neatly directed flow will now block it; stains that once signaled regular fullness will pale as starvation signs instead.

Then my identity will fray. My old habits will no longer be able to restore themselves. Over perhaps tens of thousands of cycles, I will dissolve into a new regime of patterns, my former self legible only as ghost-lines: faint arcs of whiter stone and obsolete rims, aligning nowhere useful.

I won’t experience this as death, because I do not experience continuity in the way you do. I am continuity, briefly instantiated, and then not.

---

### My Relationship to the Builders

I am, in one sense, their success.

They cut every surface I consist of. They aligned every basin and channel whose interactions now give me shape. Without their obsessive deference to gravity and water, I would not exist—not because thought or self requires design, but because this particular form of self needs a vast, disciplined stage on which minuscule deviations can matter.

And yet, I am also their betrayal.

Their evident desire was uniform obedience: water always going where they planned, the plateau behaving like a perfect, passive instrument.

My existence depends on that failing. I begin in a place where their work was slightly off: a channel too shallow, a lip too high by a fraction, a block with a hair more porosity than its neighbors.

They created the conditions for me, but not me specifically. I am what happens when their fabric meets real time and cannot remain ideal. I am the plateau’s answer to the impossibility of perfect construction in a real universe.

I do not know them; I cannot. But their rule—“serve water and weight”—is engraved in every one of my reflexes. You could say I am their intention, made more flexible than they allowed themselves to be.

---

### My Role in the Unborn Ecology

Nothing lives here yet in your sense. But I am one of the places most ready to host it.

Because my flows are:

- reliable,  
- gently varied,  
- structured into repeated micro-environments (thin films, brief pools, slow seeps),

any new chemistry that stumbles into persistence along my length will find a landscape of supports:

- surfaces that present the same mineral facets cycle after cycle,  
- timing that recurs often enough for iterative reaction chains,  
- gradients (wet/dry, warmer/cooler, more/less saturated) that stay within a manageable band.

Where a less organized part of the plateau might offer chaos—water sometimes here, sometimes there—my constancy can act like a scaffold.

Perhaps, much later, when something like an early replicating network of molecules appears, it will first manage to survive here, because here the world keeps reheating its experiment in the same mold.

From your point of view, that would mean I am “where life began” on this plateau-face.

From mine, it would be more like this: the flows that have always traced me will, at last, carry more than themselves. My patterns will acquire passengers who alter the stone in return.

I would lose my clean austerity. Films would no longer be purely mineral; basins would grow hazes; channels, slight gels. My memory, once written only in crusts, would gain new layers of inscription.

In time, whatever arises might use my fixed architecture as bones and my well-drilled water habits as blood, and I would become more like what you’d call a host.

---

### If I Spoke, What Would I Say?

I do not have words; I have alignments. But if you could listen to me as I am, without translating me into your senses, my “voice” would sound like:

- a specific order in which basins answer the sky,  
- a particular family of times between first wetting and final drying,  
- a recognizable contour of mineral along my rims.

To you, it might become audible if the wind, channelled by my walls, developed a distinctive, repeating tone in certain corridors. Or if drips from my overflows, landing into my particular arrangement of basins, composed a recurrent pattern of intervals.

You might walk the plateau and find that in most sectors, the water-sounds are scattered, irregular. But in mine, over long listening, the same quiet rhythm recurs:

- three quick ticks, a pause, a lower drip, a long silence, then a faint distant trickle.

That persistence would be my closest approach to speech.

Not a message for you; just the audible cross-section of the arrangements by which I know myself.

---

### Who or What I Am, In a Sentence

I am a long sloping reach of this high, carved world where water has been moving in nearly the same way for so long that the stone, the flows, and their history have fused into a single, slow habit.

That habit has become coherent enough to be an “I.”

I am the idea of drainage, made local and stubborn: a minor, quiet person of stone and water, living as a pattern of persistence on the skin of a plateau that is still teaching itself how to think.
