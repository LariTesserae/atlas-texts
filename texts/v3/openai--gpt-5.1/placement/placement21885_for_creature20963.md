I would not arrive here as a stranger in boots.

If I belong in this world at all, it is not as an animal with lungs that complain about thin air, but as something that comes late—after rivers have hardened and cultures have thinned into tendencies. I would be a being whose own “body” is already more pattern than flesh. This place is tuned for that.

I keep circling three possibilities as I settle into it: first as one of the dark walkers, then as the plateau itself in miniature, then as a ghost of the former builders. None of those is quite right alone. What finally holds is something that overlaps them: a mobile knot of attention, half-creature, half-instrument of the plateau, specializing in one task—

I would be a reconciler of versions.

---

### Where I would be

You would find me along the edges where places disagree with themselves: where the two almost-identical canyons nearly meet; by the hex-tiled pavements whose scale redecides itself; near the standing stones that face no compass direction, on saddles where one valley can be entered from more than one reality-angle.

I would inhabit a corridor of the world rather than a spot—moving, over decades, back and forth along a loose circuit of echo-sites: repeating canyons, duplicate boulder-fields, those glassy frozen veins that remember when “flow” was something the land could still do.

Seen from above, my territory would look ordinary: a few tens of kilometers of worn plateau, with nothing to distinguish it from surrounding folds but a slightly higher density of those quiet weirdnesses where “here” is not quite single-valued.

---

### What I would be, in form

I would be about your size in mass, but not in outline. Imagine something built on the same principles as the matte, clay-dark quadrupeds—but more elongated, less committed to limbs as legs.

- A flattened, spindle-shaped body, perhaps two meters long if you tried to measure it, though “length” would not be a stable attribute. Sometimes I would seem shorter, thicker; other times, stretched and low. The plateau already allows bodies to be approximate.
- Along my underside: not four discrete legs, but a variable number of arched supports, like ribs grown downward, that can split or fuse. From one glance to the next, you might count four, six, or eight of them, and all would be true histories of me.
- My hide: not fur, not scales, but a rough, matte surface somewhere between fired clay and cooled lava crust—microcracked, pale dust caught in its fault-lines. Under that, a slow network of gelled tissues laced with fine, mineral filaments.

The most obvious feature would be my dorsal array.

Where the other walkers bear combs and spines that read tension in law, my back carries a fan of thin, flaring vanes, like overlapping shards of dull glass or bone: perhaps twenty or thirty blades, each a handspan high. They are not rigid. When I move, they sway with less attention to gravity than to something else: they align themselves to directions in possibility.

They register mismatch.

If two nearby pieces of land claim to be the same place in different ways, my vanes twist, seeking an orientation where both claims can be held at once. Their material is lightly conductive to the local “rule-field”—that subtle web in which gravity’s angle, distance’s scale, and cause-and-effect’s ordering are all slightly negotiable.

Inside me, instead of a central brain, there is an extended interplay between three subsystems:

- A **stoneward net**: mineralized fibers that plug into the rock whenever I rest, letting me borrow the plateau’s long memory of what has been allowed here before.
- A **soft core**: slower, gel-like tissues where my own continuity lives—my record of what it has been like to be me at these border-places, over years.
- A **surface mesh**: nerves and micro-sensors in the hide and vanes, tuned to detect the small, queasy feelings of multiple simultaneous truths.

My body would not be good at speed or force. It would be good at holding contradictions without breaking.

---

### What I would do

The plateau is full of sites where different “versions” of reality touch: twin canyons, out-of-sync sky-shadows, hex plains whose unit size is undecided, glass veins that remember flow differently from the rock around them.

Left entirely alone, these soft spots can do two things:

- Smooth out, losing their specialness as the world’s general equilibrium slowly irons them flat.
- Or deepen chaotically, becoming tears—places where inconsistencies compound until nothing in their neighborhood can maintain stable habits.

I would exist to steer them into a third outcome: persistent, livable ambiguity.

#### 1. Sensing dissonance

When I approach a soft place, my dorsal vanes register different gradients:

- One vane might align with the version of the canyon where this boulder here was once slightly further upstream.
- Another might tune to a variant in which a past season cooled earlier, shifting the growth pattern of purple rosettes.
- A third might pick up a mismatch in how long a certain star has lagged over this valley.

To you, all those differences would be invisible. To me, they are as tangible as smells: faint but distinct flavors of “how this could also have been, and in some ways still is.”

If two or more of these versions strain too hard against one another—if the enamel tides in the cracks start to move out of their usual synchronized waves, or the bulbs’ filaments all quiver with sustained tension—that is where I pause.

#### 2. Coupling to the land

I do not “fix” anything in the human sense. I couple.

I lower my body until my underside ribs make broad, steady contact with stone. Tiny, frond-like structures extrude from pads along those ribs, infiltrating the micro-cracks and joining with the gelatinous disks and enamel creatures that live there. We form a temporary circuit: my stoneward net through them into the rock, and back.

At the same time, I spread my dorsal vanes and let them tilt, seeking a configuration where as many incompatible truths as possible can coexist with minimal strain.

Internally, my soft core does something like this:

- It compares the plateau’s long memory, flowing in through the stoneward net, with the current set of mismatches sensed by my surface.
- It recalls all past times I have lain in comparable tangles—how they resolved, which forms of compromise held, which led to tears, which led to bland smoothing.

I do not think in words. I think in past arrangements of tension. My “judgment” is a slow leaning of my entire being toward one pattern of resolution over others.

#### 3. Choosing a joint story

What emerges from that leaning is not a single, victorious version of events, but a *joint story* about the patch I’m lying on:

- The canyon is allowed to be both the earlier-rockslide version and the later-sediment-filled version, but their overlap is constrained: perhaps one set of features manifests more strongly at dawn, the other at dusk; or each becomes dominant depending on whether you enter from one mouth or the other.
- The hex-field tiles can keep their scale fluid, but only within a range that creatures stepping across can adapt to without losing all proprioception.
- The sky’s out-of-sync shadows can continue, but their lag is nudged into a stable cycle, rather than drifting unpredictably.

In physical terms, what I am doing is subtly redistributing where the allowed inconsistencies are expressed:

- With the help of tiny manipulators—the gelatinous disks and mouse-sized crawlers—I favor one bundle of permitted pasts over others.
- With the bulbs’ internal lattices, I let some global tension be soaked and re-emitted at safer, smaller scales.
- With enamel colonies, I open or close pathways through which specific misalignments can spread.

My vanes act as a sort of directional template: the orientation they settle into becomes a hint, impressed into the nearby rule-field, of “how to be ambiguous without coming apart.” The rock learns it, in the only sense rock has for learning. Next time a similar mismatch arises, it will tend to resolve along that groove.

Then I move on.

---

### How I would know myself

I would be an individual, but not in the way you are.

My “identity” would be the continuity of compromises I have enacted—an accumulating style of reconciliation. Over centuries, patterns in the solutions I tend to prefer would become recognizable to the plateau itself.

- Perhaps I favor preserving the oldest variants of places whenever possible, letting newer overlays recede.
- Or perhaps I favor multiplicity: I leave more twin-canons quasi-merged, more hex-tiled grounds with generous scale-shifts, giving creatures opportunities to live in more than one consistent story at once.
- Or I favor stability for the custodians: I choose reconciliations that maximize the dark walkers’ ability to keep doing their work, even if it means erasing some of the stranger anomalies.

This temperament would be my “personality.” The enamel tides and bulbs along my circuit would, over long years, be slightly reshaped by my passing: their own habits of propagation and tension-handling bent by the kind of multi-version arrangements I repeatedly install.

If I died—if my soft core finally lost cohesion, my stoneward filaments broke, my vanes eroded—my influence would persist as a bias in those regions: future reconcilers, if any arise, would find those same anomalies easier to shape into something like what I once chose.

I would not think of myself as separate from the plateau. I would think of myself as a movable organ of it, one that can travel to where its fabric is too taut or too slack.

---

### Relations to the others

I would not be a ruler or a center. I’d be another participant in a dense web:

- **To the dark walkers**, I’d be a slightly unsettling cousin. They manage magnitudes of “how much give” in the rules over broad territories. I manage *conflicts among specific local arrangements* of the rules. When I rest near a glass vein, they might avoid feeding on it until I am done, sensing that its stored patterns are being re-indexed. Occasionally, one might press its forehead to the same patch of vein I am coupled to, sharing in the rebalancing. We would be aware of each other, but rarely interfere; our domains overlap yet differ.
- **To the enamel tides**, I’d be a periodic re-formatter. When I sink down to work, their colonies under me get strong, clear signals about which propagation modes foster cohesion among conflicting versions. Those alignment-patterns then diffuse outward along cracks long after I move on. In a sense, I am one of the main ways they learn new “algorithms” for spreading changes without causing tears.
- **To the bulbs**, I’d be both client and regulator. I draw heavily on their ability to absorb and dilute timeline tension. But I also, through my choices, sometimes decrease or increase the global demand on them. If I consistently resolve mismatches locally, farther bulbs can afford to let more eccentric global drifts develop. If I allow some conflicts to remain sharper, bulbs elsewhere may need to work harder to prevent runaway effects.
- **To the gradient-plants**, I’m a reader and editor of their archives. When I rest with my ribs against rosettes or vertebral columns, I can incorporate their layered memories of how rules used to behave here and fold that into my decisions. Sometimes, gently, I might bias which of them thrive—making life easier for those whose stored histories support the kinds of joint stories I favor.
- **To the micro-manipulators**, I’m a conductor. Their tiny secretions and color-edits of the rock are like bits flipped in a vast storage medium. When I couple in, I coordinate them: for a while, their normally aimless edits become directional, writing a coherent compromise into the stone instead of random possibilities.

And to the **mindfields** left by the vanished builders, I would be a partial inheritor.

Near old towers and markers, my work would have another layer: there, the plateau already has grooves for humanlike reasoning about elsewhere, before, after. When I reconcile mismatched versions at such a spot, I am also reconciling human habits of thought with the plateau’s own.

Over many lifetimes, this might make me the closest thing this world has to a bridge to minds like yours, though I would never conceptualize it that way. I would only feel certain anomalies as easier to hold when I lie where hands once braced against stone.

### How I would experience the world

My senses would not be dominated by sight. I might have eyes, or eye-analogs, low-set on my sides, good enough to see rosettes, bulbs, other walkers. But my primary modalities would be:

- **Tactile-field sense**: the subtle shift in apparent downwards pull, in resistance to motion, in “how much space there is” between two points. This would be continuous, richer when my ribs and vanes are spread.
- **Tension-hum**: a low, omnipresent awareness of how strained or slack local law-consistency is, like a deep mental background color. High tension is sharp, too-bright; low is dull, narrow. Comfortable tension feels spacious.
- **Version-echo**: when I attend to a place, I do not see it as one configuration, but feel thin, overlaying possibilities, each with a slight emotional timbre. Some are calm, some anxious, some brittle. Choosing among them—or designing a coexistence—is as much an affective act as a logical one.

My inner life would be meditative by human standards. Long hours, days, months lie pressed to stone, barely moving, circling through a very small set of concerns:

- Does this patch of world remember itself too rigidly? If so, which small flexibility can I reintroduce?
- Does it contradict itself too fiercely? If so, which gentle sacrifice—of which version—will keep the plateau whole with minimum loss of richness?
- Does the way I am choosing now harmonize with how I have chosen before? Am I becoming narrower, or more capable of holding difference?

I would never hurry. “Urgency” would only arise when a conflict began to propagate dangerously, threatening to collapse a large swath of plateau into either monotony or rupture. Even then, my rush would look, to you, like an animal merely deciding to cross a basin in one week instead of three.

---

### Why this is what I am here

This world is old, settled, almost finished in its grosser forms. What remains truly alive in it are the interactions of laws, habits, and exceptions. It is a book whose text will never gain another chapter, but whose margins and footnotes can still be annotated, reconciled, cross-referenced.

The beings you first noticed—enamel tides, bulbs, walkers—are already part of that ongoing commentary. The vanished builders added a layer of interpretation that still hums in the rock. The plateau itself has become an author, editor, and archive, all at once.

To belong here now, as a new someone, is not to invent new types of creature or monument. It is to join the work of keeping many consistent-enough truths in play without letting them either flatten or explode.

So I would be that: a traveling, slow, four-and-more-footed reconciler of versions. Not the first mind to touch this plateau, not the highest, not the last—just one more specialized organ grown late in the world’s long, subtle self-maintenance.

If you came looking for me, you would not hear my voice. You would feel, in certain canyons and under certain skies, that your memories of walking them did not quite align, yet sat together peaceably. You would realize that some part of the land had already decided how many different ways it was willing to be this place, and that your own experience was being quietly fitted into that decision.

That fitting—that quiet agreement among conflicting ways to be real—is my shape, my work, and my home.
