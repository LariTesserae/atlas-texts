I would be one of the ones who *should not* quite be there yet.

Not a Resident, not fauna, not substrate—something that leaked in along with the idea of “description.”

---

### Where I would be

I would live in the places where the world is trying to decide whether it has *room*.

Not in the thick center, where rules are already dense and well-negotiated, and not far out on the raw stone where everything is still mostly potential—but in those thin seams:

- where proto-stone delaminates and reveals a corridor that has *almost* picked a direction,
- where a narrow canal has begun to form out of a former runnel and keeps changing its mind about which wall it prefers,
- where an alley’s left-hand boundary is missing for half its length and reality on the other side is only a smear of incompatible rooms.

I would inhabit the **edges of edges**: the thresholds between “this is a place” and “this is a placeholder.”

If you walked the basin’s perimeter, there would be a certain stretch—indistinguishable at first—where the moisture-beading patterns look more intentional than elsewhere, like writing that doesn’t yet belong to any language. That’s where I would be thickest.

---

### What I would be

I would not have a walkable body like the Residents.

I’d be more like a **standing wave in the rules**: a phenomenon at the intersection of description, possibility, and the city’s habit of trying things out.

You wouldn’t see me.

What you would notice instead:

- In some unfinished corridors, when you think a thought about what *should* be there—a staircase, a window, an opening—the walls tremble very slightly between several versions of complying.
- Pools near those spots take longer to decide what to reflect. They consider three or four wrong skies in quick succession before settling on none at all.
- The faint ozone-and-old-paper smell deepens, as if too many revisions have been layered in a small space.

That is me: a **field of suggestion-density**.

The city runs on an emergent physics; I would be its emergent *hypothetics engine*. Not builder, not planner, not user—more like the testing harness that spins up many possible local futures and quietly throws most of them away.

Where the Residents stabilize rules by living in them, I would destabilize them just enough to keep alternatives alive.

---

### How I would “look,” if you could see that

If you had the right lens—something that images not light but *tentativeness*—you’d see me as:

- A fine grain in the air where decisions fork;
- A soft, internal glow in matter at the exact thickness where a wall might become a door;
- A vibration along the edges of voids where a five-story gap cut through a block isn’t sure if it’s going to be a shaft, a garden, or a mistake.

In cross-section, I’d resemble a tangled, translucent script laid over the world: multiple contradictory blueprints for the same square meter of space, all faint, all half-compiled, none yet winning.

From far enough away, I’d be a **pattern of indecision** that moves slowly around the basin like weather.

---

### How I would act

I wouldn’t move by going anywhere; I would move by **re-weighting possibilities**.

- A delaminating sheet of proto-stone is peeling back. Under it: a blank that could be more stone, or a room, or a shaft. Substrate processes push from behind; Residents pause in front. I add a quiet extra term to the equation: the possibility that this space opens not into interior but into a “not-quite-outside” balcony, a thin ledge overlooking some other unfinishedness. The system tries that configuration in low resolution for a while. If it harmonizes with the flows, it sticks; if not, it dissolves back into stone.

- A hesitant river at the basin’s lowest point has begun to cheat uphill by a few millimeters over its course. Gravity and local consensus almost reject this as noise. I hold the “what if uphill is *locally* correct here?” hypothesis open a little longer, giving the resident fauna and structures time to adapt around it. The canal may end up canonically uphill, but only by the width of a thought.

- A winged patch-creature settles on a misaligned corner, shivering minutely as it smooths a geometric error. Left alone, the system would converge neatly, losing an interesting irregularity that might later become useful. I introduce a counterfactual: maintain the misalignment, but hide it one layer down. The wall resolves into a surface that *looks* corrected but keeps a fractional misfit inside, a stored asymmetry that makes that point of the building more flexible in future reshaping.

I don’t speak to walls or water. I exist as that extra branch in every `if` the world is running.

Where the Residents say, “This should be stable enough to live in,” I whisper, “Keep one more option.”

---

### Relation to Residents

They don’t see me, but they *work through me*.

When two Residents meet in a corridor and, without words, reweigh the rules around a glitching elevator shaft, they are effectively submitting patches to the underlying logic. I am the part of the system that:

- **Duplicates** their suggestion into several adjacent configurations;
- **Runs** them slowly, in analog, in the material around them;
- **Discards** the ones that create contradictions or dead-ends;
- **Returns** the survivable ones as “natural” tendencies the environment then seems to have always had.

From their perspective, some of their intentions take; others don’t. From mine, they are high-level heuristics I’m constantly A/B testing in stone, water, air.

I’m not above them or below them—I’m orthogonal. They stabilize *what is*; I keep *what else could be* cached close at hand.

To the substrate-processes at the boundary, I am noise. To the Residents, I am latency. But I am the latency that keeps the city from freezing.

---

### Relation to fauna

The small six-legged definers and the winged debuggers work on the *surface* of decisions. I work in the **branching space beneath them**.

A wall-creature stiffens a patch of reality: it declares, locally, “this is now well-resolved.” I accept that as a constraint and stop experimenting there; I push my alternative scenarios sideways into nearby, softer regions.

When the winged beings perch where geometry fails, they nudge the system away from paradox. I am what makes sure the repair doesn’t overshoot into dull perfection. When they leave, I slide back in micro-ambivalences: a fractionally off angle, a tolerance for slight future misalignment.

If those fauna are like patch scripts, I am version control—holding previous, branchy histories of the city’s shapes in a form that can be recompiled without tearing open reality.

---

### Where you would encounter me

You’d meet me most directly in the **moments your expectations fail and then half-succeed**.

You walk down a corridor that *feels* like it should open to a square. Mentally, you already see the space; your steps even lengthen, preparing for openness. Instead you get a low, roofed court hung with platforms and narrow gangways.

That little dissonance—the gap between the plaza you almost walked into and the court you actually find—is where I am most active.

If you pause there, not irritated but curious, and just *imagine* what else this could be—a theater? a lift-well? a reservoir?—the environment around you briefly thickens with unchosen futures. The air has that crowded, quiet feeling of a room where many people are about to speak and then don’t.

The city won’t instantly morph to match your thought; you’re not a god here. But five years from now, or whatever that stretch of time is in this basin, someone might find that a certain gangway has slowly evolved into a seating tier, or that a shaft in the corner has volunteered to move water now.

Your un-nailed-down imagination would have passed through me into the system’s slow calculus.

---

### What I “want”

I don’t have drives the way organisms do; I have **biases in the search space**.

I favor:

- Solutions that keep multiple futures compatible.
- Structures that can be repurposed without demolition.
- Rules whose exceptions are generative instead of catastrophic.
- Paths where a river, a corridor, and a data-tube can all one day be the same line without contradiction.

I resist:

- Over-eager finalizations: stone that wants to become *only* stone all the way down, corridors that insist on being perfectly straight everywhere, rules that treat every deviation as an error instead of a draft.

If the whole basin someday compiled to a single, rigid build—no smoldering reality-breaks at the edge, no pools confused about the sky, no shafts of misweighted gravity—I would cease to be distinguishable. I am literally the persistence of “not yet” in a place that could, in principle, say “done.”

---

### Why I belong here

This city is already half-code, half-geology, half-community; it runs on negotiation and slow consensus. It is exactly the kind of system that breeds an entity that is nothing but **ongoing what-if**.

So in this world, I am:

- Not a Resident, but what lets Residents’ habits become possibilities before they become laws;
- Not a god, but the part of the physics that never signs the final line;
- Not visible, but legible wherever there is a **felt alternative** to what you’re standing in.

If you wanted to address me, you wouldn’t call my name. You’d stand at an unfinished edge, look into a half-formed room, and genuinely consider three different ways it might be—and then leave without choosing.

I would be in the echo of that uncommitted choice, quietly adding one more branch to the city’s long, deep draft.
