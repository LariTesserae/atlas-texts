I am in the light before I am anything else.

At first there is only the refusal of shadow—the way every surface gives itself up, no concealment, no true dark. I am not placed in it; I am grown with it, as if vision comes not from organs but from saturation. The brightness fills me before I have eyes to receive it.

When I begin to have a body, it is small and soft and slick, like every other body newly unveiled in this world. I lie in a shallow basin scooped in old stone, watching droplets descend in slow inevitability from a hazed ceiling. They strike my skin as they strike the water—soft, patient blows that never cease. Someone has pitched a cover of dull, non-luminous plates at an angle above me, so that the wider world’s glare is broken into a quieter glow. Inside this little cave of stone in stone, the ambient revelation is muffled, but it is still everywhere; there is no point that is truly dark behind my closed lids.

I am not alone. Light and wetness touch me; hands touch me too. They are broad, long-fingered, calloused in crescents where fingers habitually brace against rock. They smell faintly of mineral and hide-fat. They move with that same compression and release as drip and Sway: firm in the holding, gentle in withdrawal.

My first real knowing of this place is through the way those hands orient me. They turn my head toward a particular rhythm of water, press my small palm against the stone so I can feel the tremor of distant movement. I learn that here, to exist is to be in dialogue with things that do not speak but never stop saying.

When I become upright, I am less tall than the adults, my limbs a little more slender, my skin smoother, almost featureless. The world meets me immediately. Condensation beads on my shoulders; my bare feet adhere just enough to the damp stone that I do not slip as easily as you think I might. My larger eyes are already wide and pale; my smaller, higher-set eye is slow, more like a patient lens that registers shifts in brightness rather than sharp detail.

At that stage I am not yet anything in particular. I am not a Path-Finder, not an elder, not a dedicated keeper of basins. I am a potential braided with Currents not yet fully claimed. But some tendencies reveal themselves early.

I am restless in stillness. When other children are content to sit in a confluence-hall listening to elders recount the shifting of routes, I find my gaze drawn to the edges of the space—to a hairline crack up near the ceiling where moisture gathers but does not yet fall, to a faintly different color in the old flowstone where some trace element has changed. I do not want to move quickly—no one here truly does—but I want to see where these hints lead.

Hands catch mine often, pull me back from ledges. A broad palm touches the back of my neck, under dripping water, turning my attention not outward but down: “Feel this rhythm. Learn this before you chase another.” I grumble in the soft, breathy way of our children, but I listen. Beneath my annoyance is understanding. In this world, leaping before listening is not daring; it is stupidity, and stupidity dies under Weight.

I grow.

The environment writes itself on me as it does on everyone. Tiny mineral films colonize the shallow texture of my skin. My elders teach me to tend them, to wash but not scour, to anoint certain areas with trace-rich water skimmed from particular basins. Over seasons, the films thicken into a faint sheen along my upper back and shoulders, beading droplets so they fall in tiny, controlled chains instead of running slowly and chilling my core.

Along my shins and forearms, I encourage ridges—low, sinuous plates that give me purchase when I brace against stone. They prick a little as they form, but the discomfort is the discomfort of muscle-building, of any adaptation. The symbiotic crusts in these places feed on ions in my sweat and the constant drip; in return they stiffen slightly as humidity drops or some trace gas rises. In time, I can feel changes before my conscious senses can name them, a kind of peripheral knowing at the edges of my flesh.

I belong by birth to the Current of the Half-Warm Troughs. Our heart is a chain of pools that steam faintly in the always-cool air, warmed by some distant geotherm that never quite breaks into scalding. We are keepers of filter-creatures and fat-sacs; we know the chemistry of water by tongue and skin, can tell at a taste if some shift below has altered the influx.

By apprenticeship I am lent, as is customary, to another Current: the Current of the Steep Echo, whose holdings run up through near-vertical shafts and tall, narrow halls that make all small sounds sharp. There I learn sound-maps: the hums and clicks that sketch distances and shapes. I spend long cycles standing in darkness-by-choice—my eyes covered with a thin, dull mineral sheet—while my mentors ask me to describe a space only by the way my own quiet voice returns.

“Again,” they say in low tones. “But this time hum higher; feel how it catches on that ledge?”

I become better at it than most. Brightness is constant and easy; the challenge lies in attending to subtler gradients. My small high eye learns to notice when the sheen on a wall is just slightly more intense, indicating a thinning of stone or a newly exposed plane. My ears and chest become tuned to the almost-silent shift of air in a shaft. I discover that I can, sometimes, predict a small rockfall by the way the Sway murmurs through my bones hours in advance, a faint change in the pattern of background tremor that others do not always catch.

It is assumed that I will become a keeper of dangerous ceilings, a reader of thinness. I would be suited to it: patient, careful, with a body that likes to brace. But something in me pulls sideways whenever new routes are discussed. When Path-Finders come to our halls, shedding fine crystal dust from their shoulders, smelling faintly of unfamiliar basins, I fall quiet in a way different from how I listen to elders. My crusts prickle. My hands flex. My mind reaches with them into places I have never touched.

My third Current comes with choice.

At a confluence-hall, where three minor water channels meet in a carved, shallow bowl, currents of a more social kind cross. Representatives of the Current of the Tilted Bridges are there, their bodies bearing the asymmetric calluses and crusts of people who live on slumped arcs and shifting spans. They speak—not loudly, but with expressive hands—of a new instability where their bridges meet the rising stone of a basin-rim. Routes must change. New connections must be found.

They are recruiting Path-Finders.

My elders look at me as the invitations are given in gesture and glance. I feel the Weight of my expected path: remain in the Half-Warm, become a ceiling-watcher, a keeper of pools, a quiet, vital presence in a stable part of the city. It is an honorable thread. The Fall through our troughs must be tended. Our filter-creatures feed many.

But the Sway in my bones thrums louder when the Bridge-people talk about edges.

So I ask, with the careful, respectful economy of our spoken language: “May I go?”

Hands tighten on my shoulders. Fingers tap patterns along my collarbones, caching objections in our mutual skin. It is dangerous; Path-Finders die. My ability to read thinness would be invaluable at home. The elders murmur of responsibility, of staying where one’s skills yield steady benefit.

Then one of the crust-heavy elders of the Steep Echo—one whose plates glow faintly at the edges—lays a hand on my shoulder and on that of a Bridge-representative, linking us in touch. Their fingers spell out: “Routes are the city’s bones. Without Path, no basin, however well-tended, survives change.” Then, lighter, traced only for me: “You hear the Sway. Go where it is loudest.”

So I become, by deliberate braid, a member of the Path-Finders in the service of the Tilted Bridges, while still belonging at my core to the Half-Warm, and carrying in my posture the careful echoes of the Steep.

This is who I am in that world: a thinness-listener, route-maker, quiet negotiator with Weight and Fall at the edges of what is known.

My days, if “days” they can be called, begin not with dawn but with the loosening of sleep-plates. In my sleep-niche, I pivot aside a fitted slab of dull mineral, and the world’s brightness floods fuller into the small space. Condensation has gathered in the night on my walls, my crusts, my lashes. I wipe it away with a practiced sweep. My body is compact, built by then for bracing and hanging as much as for walking. My arms are long; the bones in my forearms split internally to let me twist and lock my wrists against precarious holds. My feet are broad, with three strong toes and a rear digit; the ridged soles can secrete a little extra adhesive moisture when I will it.

Before I speak to anyone, I listen.

I press my palm to the wall of my niche. The stone tells me the city’s mood: a faint, steady tremor from distant drips, the more pronounced rumble of a minor shift far away. I step into the nearest drip-array—a set of hollowed bones and basins near our Path-Finders’ common hall—and compare the day’s pattern to the memory in my muscles. The Double-Hollow-Basin rings just a fraction slower. A new trickle somewhere upstream has been caught or dried. The elders of that Current will know it first, but in time the change will cascade through our maps.

We set out.

Our group is four. We travel roped together, but loosely—the slack allows each to move in their own arc, to probe different ledges and micro-paths while still being caught if a hold fails. At my belt, a mesh of bone rods and knotted filaments hangs rolled: our map of the sector we mean to extend. At my back, a narrow pack of tools—stone hammers, wedges, spare hooks, short lengths of protective stone tubing to sheath ropes over sharp edges, a few small, carefully sealed skins of fat and exudate.

Our aim: the perimeter where the Tilted Bridges’ furthest spans meet a zone of frequent Holds. There, drizzle thins inexplicably over certain stretches; fog curls and dissipates in unusual patterns. People avoid sleeping there, but paths still cross—it is too strategic a connection to abandon without an alternative.

We move through the city’s layered anatomy.

Down a wide hall where old bas-reliefs, softened beyond interpretation, still hint at wings and archaic scripts. Across a trough whose water smokes faintly with warmth. Past a Veil pool, where pale limbs stir in the depths and faint, inner glows sketch their own slow conversations under the surface. We pay respect, stepping only on the designated ledge, not dropping anything into their water.

At a narrower passage, we pass a pair of Lurids moving in the other direction, their bodies flattened and dark-not-shiny, eyes hooded. Our hands sign neutral greetings: palms open, fingers spread (neither Weight nor Fall, but simple presence). They acknowledge us with minimal motion. Our ropes are coiled tight against us as we slip by. There are knife-scars in the skin of one of them, old and white and ropey. There are old feuds here, yes. But right now, the stone under all of us mutters with a potential Sway; it is not a time for spilling blood.

At the edge of the Tilted Bridges’ furthest safe point, we pause. Before us, the long arc of an ancient bridge slumps down toward a basin that has, in some distant epoch, risen. What was once an airy span over emptiness now sags so far that its midpoint nearly touches stone. The depression has become a narrow, elongated pool lined with complex Veil-constructed honeycomb. Crossing here risks both disturbing the Veils’ work and trusting a structure under new strains.

Our task is not to force our way over, but to listen for alternatives.

While two of us stand still, bodies wide, watching for any tremble, the other two move parallel to the slump, tracing the walls, testing the ceiling. We tap gently at intervals. The notes come back dull where the stone is thick, higher and sharper where it thins. In one place, my knuckles hear a subtle ring I do not like—some hidden cavity beyond, perhaps, making the stone here a mere membrane.

I mark it on our mesh-map with a small, dark bead tied into the lattice at a precise crossing.

Farther along, the ceiling’s haze clears just enough that my smaller eye discerns a faint intensification of brightness along a seam: a newly exposed surface, still “fresh” with stored luminescence. I reach up, fingers tracing the line. A crack. Moisture beads but has not yet fallen. The Weight above is considering a change, but has not committed.

We confer in low hand-talk: fingers brushing wrists, tapping collarbones. This area will not serve as a long-term thoroughfare, but it might—briefly—be used to ferry materials if we shore certain points and cut relief slots for Fall to choose a safer route when it comes. We sketch those possibilities in the air, our hands writing over the stone we do not yet touch.

Then, just beyond, we find what we are here for.

A fissure, narrow and somewhat jagged, snakes up and away from the compromised bridge into unknown space. Cool, slightly drier air whispers from it, bearing a very faint metallic tang my tongue recognizes as promising: somewhere above, water has carved a new course through mineral-rich strata. The small drafts tickle my crusts along the ribs; they prickle in agreement. My chest vibrates, catching a difference in background echo: the hollow beyond is large.

We do not rush in. That is not our way.

First we mark the fissure’s mouth with a small cairn of three stones whose angles are distinctive—a sign to any other Path-Finder that this is a tested entrance, not a random crack. Then we anchor a rope at a solid, low point, protecting it where it crosses any sharp edge with our half-tubes of polished stone. We send sound before we send flesh: a low, sustained hum that I modulate up and down, listening to where and how it reflects.

The return is slow and complex. There is space beyond, yes, but also pillars, perhaps, or hanging curtains. The hum blooms into what my mentors taught me to recognize as a many-mouthed chamber, not a single, simple vault.

We go, one by one.

Inside, the brightness is the same—that steady, insistently even light—but the pattern of its reflection is new. Surfaces have different textures. Some walls are still rough from breakage; others are already slick with the first fine films of mineral and microbial growth. The air is marginally drier. Drip is sparse but present, falling from a few, thin needles of calcite that have already begun to grow at certain points where condensation finds purchase.

We explore slowly, marking safe lines with shallow grooves of stone, leaving small, directional stacks of pebbles at decision-points. In one alcove we find what will become, for someone, a rich wall: a broad, gently rough surface already beginning to host a natural chemoautotrophic sheen. No one has yet seeded it to a particular strain. This is opportunity wrapped in obligation.

In our world, finding such a place is like discovering a valley of fertile soil would be for surface folk. It could sustain new grazers, new Currents. It could, in time, rebalance politics.

But we are not free to simply claim it.

We sit beneath the incipient growth, backs against the new stone, and listen. Where does its sparse drip come from? Tracing the threads of moisture with fingertips, we follow tiny channels upward and back, mapping with our hands the path of Fall. It emerges, we discover, from a hairline seam that also feeds a small, existing basin in a marginal district we passed through two routes ago—a poor place, thin on resources, long contested by Lurids and one of the lesser Currents.

To intercept too much of this drip here, in secrecy, would be a violation. The ethic of Fall forbids the complete theft of flow. To guide, to share, to re-route in ways that prevent disaster: yes. To strip an upstream basin dry: betrayal.

So we mark the potential, but we do not cut the channel yet. We tie, into our mesh-map, not only the physical coordinates of this wall but the social threads that connect it. When we return, our hands in council will have to move as precisely as our tools: we will propose a shared claim, a braided stewardship between the Half-Warm, the marginal Current, and perhaps, if they can be coaxed from their reflexive suspicion, certain Lurid groups that already frequent the region.

This is my life: to move at the places where stone, water, and social claim all meet; to hear thinness and potential before they become collapse or waste.

Sometimes, disaster overtakes anticipation. Once, not long after I have earned my own small crust-plates along my spine, the Sway in our district deepens abruptly. We feel, through our feet and our ribs, a long, low grind that is not like the usual small adjustments. The drip-arrays in our confluence-hall stutter, their rhythms warping as something far away alters slope and channel.

We barely have time to clear a wide thoroughfare before a section of ceiling three halls away yields. There is no explosive crack; only a drawn-out series of heavy thumps, the sound of great stalactites breaking free, striking softer flowstone, shattering. Dust—fine, crystalline—ghosts through the air, sparkling in the pervasive light. The fog patterns shiver.

We run, but not to gape. Ropes are already in hands. We know the routes; we know where weight will fall. We use our studied knowledge of thinness and support to direct the inevitable. A half-hung slab, jamming at a dangerous angle, is given a targeted blow to let it fall in a controlled way. A new mound of rubble blocking a key channel is not simply cleared; it is re-stacked, its pieces sorted by size and translucence, forming a new retaining wall that will deflect future drips into a series of waiting basins we carve in haste.

In the aftermath, the hall is scarred, rearranged. Where once a delicate crystal forest hung, now there is a raw, torn ceiling, weeping new moisture from freshly exposed seams. People scrape blood from scratches, splint a broken arm, gather the bodies of two who did not move quite fast enough. Grief is present, but it does not become wailing. Hands move in the patterns of remembrance even as they stack stone: the acceptance of Weight, the honoring of Fall, the listening to Sway.

Later, I walk there alone.

I place my hand on one of the new, rough faces and feel the faint, bright hum of the rock just opened to this world. Its stored light is very slightly greater than that of surfaces long exposed. My smaller eye confirms it: a subtle, bluer sheen. This is the rawness our elders speak of when they say disaster is also beginning.

I mark a point where, in decades, a new curtain will grow, and carve there, very low and small, the first notch of my slow name. Not the click-and-breath my kin use, not the hand-sign my colleagues know, but the carved pattern that years will extend every time I am part of a new route, a new negotiation with change.

I imagine a child, long after my own crusts have flaked and been ground into future flows, placing their fingers there, counting the old notches, asking, “Whose work was this?” An elder will answer with a story of the Great Ceiling Fall of this sector, of how Currents braided to rebuild and redirect, of how a certain Path-Finder of the Half-Warm/Steep Echo/Tilted Bridges laid the first line of a new growth-wall here.

In that imagining, I feel the continuity of this place more keenly than in any heritage chant.

I sometimes think of the rumors of the Above—the sharper, more directed light, the plants that drink Fall for themselves, the sky that can darken. The idea unsettles some deep layer of me. A world where brightness might be taken away by mere turning feels precarious, arbitrary. My ethics, my instincts, are paced to the city’s slowness: to erosions that take generations, to drips that can be heard and counted.

Still, in uneasy dreams, I find myself in a hall with only a single burning point, intense and fragile. When it flickers, my crusts clench with animal fear. I wake and press my palms to the familiar, evenly bright stone, to the steady patterns of drip. I am relieved that here, revelation is not a gift bestowed and withdrawn, but the ground-state of being.

So I am not an overseer of all this realm, not its architect or its god. I am one small, deliberate creature among many, specialized in sensing where Weight grows treacherous, where Fall might be persuaded to slide a little this way instead of that, where Sway hints at a new fracture or opportunity.

I am a Path-Finder with crust-shimmered shoulders and long, bracing arms; a member of braided Currents whose claims and duties are traced both in rope-maps and in stone. I carry, in my body, the habits of patience and attention this world demands.

If you were to stand beside me—leaning with your back against some rounded pillar, listening as I do—you would feel the same background music: staggered drops, distant skitterings, the nearly inaudible sigh of fog, the deep, slow murmur of rock. In that shared listening, in this place where light is simply what is, you would know where you have come:

Down, under the forgotten sky, into the horizontal brightness of an unfinished world.

And there, among its quiet, relentless inhabitants, I would be one of those who walk the edges, tracing new veins through stone, helping the city remember how to go on breathing.
