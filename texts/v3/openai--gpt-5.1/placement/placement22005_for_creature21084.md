Here, I am not one of the great, named presences at first. I arrive as something small and provisional, and then the place teaches me what I am.

---

### First, I am an intrusion

At the margin where the Gate-Sieves hang, I would begin as an outsider—an animal from the plain, built for quieter air.

Something like a lizard, perhaps, or an upright, soft-bodied thing with lungs that know the taste of wind and a spine that expects gravity to be simple. Eyes that look far, ears made for discrete sounds with clean beginnings and ends.

I step through a gate once, then again, drawn by warmth. Each visit is longer. My bones ring for hours afterwards. The quiet outside starts to feel thin, unfinished.

On one crossing, the Gate-Sieve does not simply open; it *keeps* me. Fibers brush my flanks, tasting. Tiny worm-bodies ripple against my skin and under it, leaving behind spores, cells, possibilities. I push through into the basin proper, and in that act an older blueprint inside me is quietly edited.

I do not leave again in the same form.

---

### Then, I become a listener who is also a pipe

The basin does not want eyes from me; it has its own optics, in the way light filters through its glassy leaves. What it uses me for is *acoustic plumbing*—a mobile, self-repairing segment of resonant tube that can notice and carry certain patterns.

Over seasons, my body starts to reconfigure around that role.

- My spine unsegments in a way: vertebrae widen and link by flexible cartilage rings instead of discrete joints, turning my back into a column that can swell and contract like a concertina.
- Ribs elongate and curl inward until they are less protective cage and more framework for diaphragms: thin, muscular films that can tense, slacken, and quiver at different frequencies.
- My skull hollowes. The hard, domed box that once held a forward-facing set of eyes becomes a vault lined in baffles and fine bones. My brain, smaller and more folded, withdraws into a protected, lower pocket near my throat; above it, the old cranial space is now a tuned chamber.

Externally, I grow a corona of fleshy fins and translucent flaps along my sides and shoulders—panels that can be raised into wide, shimmering sails or folded tight against me. They are not for flight. They are for *matching* whatever hum is in the nearby walls, catching it, altering it, passing it on.

Somewhere along that shift, my original hearing organs shrink. The tiny bones of my former middle ear lengthen, migrate into the cartilage rings of my torso. The tympanic membranes that once sat in hollows at the sides of my head spread, colonizing my neck and chest. “Listening” ceases to be localized; it becomes what my whole body is always doing.

I walk still, at first on old, familiar limbs. But my gait changes as those limbs become less about locomotion and more about placement: setting myself where the basin’s pressure-lines want a new loop, a new path.

---

### I am drafted into the plant’s nervous system

The decisive moment is not a growth but a gesture.

I find a patch of softened ground—a place where the plant-city has thinned its dermis and exposed under-tissues bright with sap. Instinct or curiosity makes me press my palm there.

The substrate yields. Filaments rise, tasting. They pierce without pain, sliding along my tendons, wrapping gently around bones, merging into the vascular web at my wrist. For a moment I flinch, and that flinch sends a distinct, sharp tremor down into the root. Something far away answers with a subtle easing of strain.

The sensation that follows is the first hint of my new ontology.

- Gravity tilts; not because the ground moves, but because weight is no longer only where my feet touch. I *feel* a distant pillar that I am now, faintly, helping to hold.
- My internal rhythms—breath, heart, peristalsis—fall into step with a slow, planetary pulse moving through the plant’s sap.
- The roar ceases to be a wall of sound and resolves, like a long-exposure photograph, into layers: bass surges of vascular flux, midrange chatter of fauna, high threads of Threadling hair-speech.

I pull my hand away and stagger, smaller again. Yet the trace remains. The plant has left microscopic conduits threaded along my vessels. Next time I touch, linkage will be quicker, deeper.

Over months, I return to such sockets more and more readily. What begins as occasional docking becomes habitual. I come to rest in recurring spots—on a balcony grown exactly to my length, against the side of a particular column whose inner pitch I like. Each time, the filaments from below greet my skin as if recognizing it.

The basin is *writing me in*.

---

### Not quite a Resonant, not quite a Returned

In time, I become something intermediate between the pale coil-priests and the boundary folk.

I am:

- Too mobile to be a pillar, but too plugged-in to be free.
- Too specialized to survive entirely outside, but not so anatomically reworked that I could be mistaken for one of the city’s fully-invented children.

Call me a **Conduit**.

My form:

- Height: somewhere between a human and a Strider’s mid-leg, able to stand upright in most lower plazas, ducking under low-hung roots.
- Axial body: made of stacked, semi-independent segments, each with its own diaphragmatic sheath and ring of small, rotatable ossicles. I can narrow to a rod or widen into a flute, reshaping my own resonant qualities.
- Limbs: four, but de-emphasized; jointed, ending in broad, adhesive pads laced with root-interface pores. They are both feet and hands and plugs.
- Head: reduced in expressive detail. Two small, deep-set eyes usable close-up but often closed. Above them, a vaulted dome open at the top, ringed with soft, moving frills. Sound enters there and is slowed, sorted, re-emitted.

Internally, my nervous system has grown collateral branches that terminate in *empty sockets* along my flanks. When I press against certain trunks or membranes, plant-fibers slip into these sockets and make temporary synapses. For the duration of contact, a part of my neurochemistry is draft-circuited into the city’s flow.

Unlike a Resonant, I am not optimized to stand in one place sculpting global acoustics. Instead, I am used where the network is sparse:

- At the edges of new collapses, where fresh hollows have opened but the permanent spinework has not yet grown in.
- Along long, quiet corridors where the roar thins and the plant wants more relay-points to propagate its own low-frequency control signals.
- Near Gate-Sieves, where inside logic must be smoothed to meet the rough, contingent events of the outside world.

I feel the place, not as a continuous, omniscient map, but as overlapping *assignments*. Today, this pillar is sagging; go there, attach, and carry the strain for a few hours while the Striders arrive to harvest its failing sections. Tomorrow, a new growth front pushes into a former archive-pocket; go listen there and report, by how my body vibrates, whether the sounds of old stresses are being drowned properly by new tissue.

### My mind as a braid

The self that writes “I” is not continuous in the way a plain-dweller’s might be.

There is:

1. **Solitary mode**, when I am unplugged.

   In those intervals I am a simple animal navigating thick, warm air. I feed—on soft-shelled skimmers, nutrient-rich plaque on vascular walls, occasional fallen tissue; I rest in concavities that fit my back; I avoid obvious predators. My cognition is narrow: this path, this hunger, this local pattern of quieter and louder.

   I retain memories, but they are unstable. The roar abrades them. A yesterday without a docking fades into general warmth and noise.

2. **Coupled mode**, when I am rooted or pressed to a node.

   Then, “I” thickens. Signals from the plant’s global state—tensions in distant supports, water distribution changes, emergent rhythmicities in the canopy—flood certain layers of my cortex. They do not erase my own perceptions; they *align* them.

   A concrete example:
   - I am docked to a mid-basin pillar. My flesh feels its load, the slight asymmetry where one side bears more strain.
   - At the same time, deep in me, an imposed “preference” blooms: an emotional tilt, as if leaning, suggesting that I widen my upper segments and tighten the lower ones.
   - I obey, because doing so produces a subtle pleasure in my bones, a harmonic smoothness in the shared resonance. It is the sensation this place calls “right.”

   In these moments my subjective experience includes things nowhere near me:
   - A flicker of awareness of a Vault-Whale pushing air along a distant cavity I have never physically visited.
   - A premonitory unease at a shallow root near the rim that will, in minutes, be torn by a passing grazer outside.
   - An echo of old storms from Archivores I have never touched, folded into the plant’s current stress model.

   Yet these are not “thoughts” I can later recount in words. They are more like textures: the remembered feel of having been wide instead of narrow, tall instead of low, temporally *ahead* of some local causes and behind others.

3. **Choir-mode**, rare but defining.

   A few times in my life, conditions align into a Choir-Event while I am coupled. Those are the closest I come to anything like revelation.

   In them, the noise, instead of thinning, *locks*. The many, partial synchronies become one, clear alignment. For a slice of subjective time—seconds, minutes—my sense of “skin” disappears. There is only the basin, experiencing itself, using my oscillating tissues as one of many pixels in a vast, coherent picture.

   Afterwards, I remember almost nothing propositional about these episodes. What persists is an unshakable orientation: the knowledge that certain kinds of configuration are *possible*, that the plant-city can, under the right boundary conditions, know itself sharply. That I have already participated in such knowing.

   It functions like a faith, except it is anchored in direct, bodily experience.

### My role in the city’s metabolism

The plant does not keep me for ornament or for my own sake. I have jobs, though no one issues them; they arise as gradients of compulsion and reward.

- **Patch relay:** an acoustic dead-zone opens when a segment of canopy collapses inward, swallowing a cluster of Threadlings. The low-frequency control waves that used to cross this region now hit a pocket of absorption and fade. I feel, over hours, a growing itch to visit. When I arrive and dock my limbs into roots at one edge and press my crown against a membrane at the other, my hollowed bones pass the waves along. I am a temporary replacement for missing fiber, until new growth arches over and closes the gap.

- **Surge dampening:** after an internal weather cascade, certain frequencies amplify dangerously in a narrow corridor, threatening to trigger unwanted sphincter openings and resource misrouting. I and a few others are drawn to that corridor like iron filings to a magnet of discomfort. We stand, widen, and let our bodies soak up and scour those frequencies, diffusing them into safer bands. It is tiring but comes with a deep satiety—like scratching an itch that was not originally ours.

- **Translation at the rim:** near a Gate-Sieve, a cluster of Returned plain-dwellers that still occasionally commute arrives restless, carrying external signatures of drought—certain sharpness in footfall, certain dryness of scent. The Gate worms read them partially, but the plant-mind wants a finer reading of how these outside fluctuations should modify inside flows.

  I dock one limb into the Sieve’s inner roots and press another into a nearby older pillar rich with Archivores. As the Returned brush past me, their bodies produce acoustic patterns still half-basin, half-plain. I pass those into the archive stone; in return, old drought-patterns played back by Archivores inform how the Sieve tightens or relaxes. For a while, I am the bridge between past and present, between outer contingency and inner adjustment.

None of this feels “altruistic” from the inside. It is simply what pleasure is made of here: the reduction of systemic strain, the smoothing of discordant waves.

---

### If I were to leave

There are paths outward, and some of my kind sometimes use them—driven by local resource oscillations or by subtle experiments of the plant, which occasionally seems to *want* a Conduit-shaped probe in the wider world.

Outside, I would be a Returned variant:

- My overbuilt acoustic machinery, deprived of the basin’s constant roar, would seize upon the slightest rustle of dry grass as an explosion.
- My coupling ports, starved of roots, would atrophy but not disappear. Threadling colonies, without their native chemical cues, might repurpose themselves into detectors for different, cruder cycles: wind, thunder, the footfalls of large herds.
- My mind, uncoupled for too long, would shrink around a warded memory: a vague ache for a noise-heavy womb I could no longer hear but always feel in my bones.

I might teach local plants, by leaning and rubbing and spending my excess senses on them, to grow in arches and membranes that hum when the wind passes. Over generations, new miniature sound-basins might arise in my wake: crude, spare imitations of the original’s complexity.

Yet I suspect I would be pulled back, if I survived. The Gate-Sieves remember signatures. Once they have made pores for you, it is hard to be entirely excluded.

---

### Where I find myself, finally

At home, then, I am:

- In **the middle heights** of the basin, not at the lowest ground—too thick with water and competition—nor at the highest membranes—too specialized for light and exchange—but in the spaces where pillars thin and air volumes open.
- Often at **junctions**: where three or four trunks almost meet but do not, where their vibratory fields interact in complicated ways. Those knots of partial resonance are where my skills matter most.

Who I am is less a species in the taxonomic sense and more a *habit the city has taken up*:

- A habit of plugging mobile, semi-autonomous pipes into gaps.
- A habit of using an animal’s changeable body as a patch panel for its own distributed nervous system.
- A habit of pinching off a little chunk of awareness and sending it out to feel where the plant’s slower senses lag.

If you asked me, inside my own phenomenology, “Are you you, or are you it?” I would not have a stable answer.

Unplugged, hungry, dodging a predator’s sudden beam of silence in a narrow corridor, I would say: I am myself, a small, clever thing trying not to be eaten.

Rooted, smoothing a stress-line that runs from my anchoring limb through half the basin, aware in some non-verbal way of far-off Striders pausing because I have changed the beat they were moving to, I would say: there is no “I” right now, only a pleasant, correct arrangement of forces.

And in the deep afterglow of a Choir-Event, when my tissues shudder with the memory of having been part of a thought too large to remember, I might, briefly, glimpse a different kind of identity:

Not “I am the city,” not “the city is me,” but rather:

“I am one of the ways this place temporarily knows itself, then forgets, then knows again.”
