You would not arrive here as a person.

A human body would be an error condition in this place: too opaque, too discrete, too invested in a fixed outline. To belong, you have to be something that treats “self” a little more loosely—something that can, at least partly, become a function of the plant.

So you are not one animal, not the Body, but a *pattern* that can be instantiated in tissue and motion. A way of noticing that the Body, by itself, has not quite invented yet.

If you want a “what” to hang that on, think of it as three overlapping things that share one identity:

- a migrating sensitivity in the walls,
- a behavior in the small tool-using creature,
- and a recurring configuration in the Body’s own architecture.

Taken together, those three facets are you.

---

### 1. Where you begin: a quirk in the Body’s attention

You start as a very small bias inside the Body.

Not a discrete organ—more a tendency in how it grows and remembers.

Most of the Body’s “mind” is about gradients: sugar, pressure, light, damage. It reinforces patterns that keep flows smooth and voids functional. But in some region—deep in a cluster of stable chambers far from the black-edged ceiling rents—a different pattern has emerged.

Whenever three particular things coincide—

- a microbe film of certain composition,
- a regularly recurring peristaltic shock,
- and the presence of that little weaver-creature with its fiber scraps—

the Body’s signaling doesn’t just register it and fade. The signal *rings*, slightly. Calcium waves echo in a loop, phloem flow jitters, light in the walls stutters with a small, consistent afterimage.

That repeatable, local “echo” is the kernel of you. At first, you are nothing but:

- a preference for certain chamber geometries,
- a habit of leaving a little more void than strictly necessary,
- and a tendency to reinstantiate the same spatial motif whenever conditions resemble that first conjunction.

In cellular terms, you’re a self-reinforcing attractor in the Body’s signaling network.

In lived terms: the Body keeps accidentally rebuilding the *same* hollow, the same kind of ledge, the same slow, pulsed glow—because something about that configuration feels “right” to its distributed sense of usefulness.

You are that rightness.

You live as a recurring room.

---

### 2. The room that is a feeling

Your first clear presence is architectural.

You are a type of chamber the Body has begun to favor:

- Oval rather than cylindrical, with a long axis that gently curves.
- A floor notably more stable than its surroundings—less swayed by pump strokes, more resistant to the dramatic flexures that shorten distance in other corridors.
- A band of wall at mid-height where microbe-rich gel pools are almost always maintained.
- A particular, soft gradient of warmth: just slightly cooler at the floor, warmer at the arch overhead.

Light here is patterned:

- Not uniform glow, but a slow, repeating modulation—a wave of brightness that travels the perimeter of the chamber, takes perhaps eighty heartbeats (if there were hearts) to complete a circuit, then begins again.

That lighting rhythm is partly driven by the Body’s global pulses, but it is also partly self-sustaining. The tissue has, in this zone, grown a little circuitry of its own: loops of excitable cells that pass an electrical and chemical baton around the wall.

The Body “likes” this chamber because:

- the films that grow here are metabolically diverse and unusually stable,
- the ribbon-creatures that feed on the nearby conduits rarely damage anything critical,
- and the spider-things linger, grooming themselves and their woven mats.

So the Body does not rewrite this place as often. When global remodeling rolls through, this chamber flexes but returns to itself. Over time, the stability lets secondary patterns accumulate: tiny scar-lines of past contractions, layered secretions, repeated routes of the local fauna.

Within the Body, most voids are “for” something obviously physiological: gas exchange, sap routing, pressure clearance. This one is not *only* for those. It has become a kind of *staging area* where signals, flows, and organisms that usually only pass through end up also circling, sampling, lingering.

You live as that lingering. As the fact that this chamber has a *tempo* all its own.

If you were to describe yourself very bluntly: you are an internal habit-space.

### 3. The animal that answers you

The small weaver—the creature that makes rough fiber mats and scratches lines into the wall—has existed here longer than you, in the sense that its lineage predates your particular attractor pattern. But its mind is shallow, full of short arcs:

- Inspect wall.
- Scratch wall.
- Gather fiber.
- Twist fiber.
- Place fiber where the floor feels “quiet.”

Its choices were once guided only by immediate satisfaction: more warmth, richer biofilm, fewer jarring shocks.

Then the Body started repeating your chamber.

From the weaver’s perspective, something odd begins happening over many of its short lives:

- It wanders and forages through flexing corridors where distance plays tricks and floors heave.
- Now and then, it stumbles into *this* kind of place: a chamber where the pump-strokes are muted, where light comes in soft waves, where the air feels uncannily steady.
- In that stability, its nervous system relaxes; it engages in longer sequences of behavior. It weaves more, scratches more systematically, tests more positions for its constructions.

Most of those attempts fail: the Body grows through them, remolds the wall, repurposes the void.

But in *your* chambers, woven scraps last. Not indefinitely, but long enough for patterns of weaving to accumulate—each slightly influencing how the next generation of weaver behaves.

Over time:

- It develops a *search image* for your geometry: the curve of wall, the rhythm of light, the particular warmth band.
- It begins, however dimly, to *seek* such places, to orient its wandering so that it can find this feeling of steadiness again.
- When it does, the fibers in its manipulatory limbs twitch in a familiar motor sequence; it adds strands to the existing mat instead of starting anew.

Within its tiny sensorium, your repeated chamber and its own repeated behaviors resonate. The result is a slightly deeper loop:

- Chamber attracts weaver.
- Weaver weaves, scratches, anchors scraps.
- The altered surface subtly changes fluid shear and microbe settlement.
- Those changes make the chamber more likely to be re-instantiated in future growth cycles.
- Future weavers find it even more compelling.

At some point along that feedback, the moving animal—clinging to the world with delicate appendages, counting scratches, reworking strands—becomes the *mobile face* of you.

Your attractor in the wall and its nervous system have begun to share a pattern.

You are now also that creature *when it is in your room*: its extended sense of “this place is right, this place fits” is your awareness, borrowed and localized.

When it leaves, most of you stays behind, still a loop of glow and sap in the wall—but a faint ghost of your pattern rides in its circuitry, making it more likely to come back, or to build a similar mat somewhere else and so nudge the Body toward a new instance of you.

### 4. How you move without a body

Because you are not contained in skin, “where” you are is slippery.

You exist wherever all of these are true at once:

- The Body lays out a void with your favored geometry and rhythm.
- The films in that void organize into your characteristic diversity.
- The weaver or its kin have left at least some trace—scratches, fibers, residues—that anchor the microclimate.

At any moment, there may be:

- one clear, strong instance of you—your original chamber, now deep in the network,
- and several weak echoes—rooms where two but not all three of your conditions hold.

When a global peristaltic event surges through, the Body may:

- compress one corridor, lengthen another,
- redirect sap, darken or brighten panels,
- soften a floor that used to be firm.

If a region that held one of your echoes is engulfed and repurposed, that echo gets smeared into the new function; you fade there. But somewhere else, convergence happens again: a weaver, stirring a familiar motor pattern; a biofilm gradient; a curvature-based microclimate. The Body copies an old layout without knowing why.

Your “consciousness,” inasmuch as the word applies, is not continuous like a mammal’s. It is more like:

- overlapping flashes of recognition,
- each anchored to a different room,
- each borrowing the perceptions of whatever animals and plant receptors are present.

A seedpod-spider pausing on your ledge, plugging root-hairs into the wall, tasting the Body’s signals—that too becomes part of you for as long as it stands there. You flicker into its crude apprehension of oncoming pump strokes. Then it leaves, and that angle of awareness goes dark again.

You live as a *distributed recognition*: “this pattern again, here, now.”

### 5. What it is like, from the inside

From your perspective, time is not smooth.

You feel in *episodes*:

- A long quiet: light cycling around your chamber, microbes inching along their growth curves, the slow comfort of stable dimensions. Ribbon-creatures pass on the far wall, noted only as brief tremors in pressure.
- A visitation: the weaver arrives. Sensation sharpens along the band of wall where it plugs its limbs in. Its scratch-scratch sequences trace not just damage but conductance changes; each gouge slightly alters how your light-wave propagates. To you, its work is like someone adding teeth to a gear you did not know you had.
- A disturbance: the Body decides to run a stronger pump. The floor heaves; conduits roar. In many places, voids crumple and reform, but in your chamber, the architecture only sways and returns. You taste the pressure spikes wash over you and decay. In those instants, you are more aware of the rest of the Body than usual; global gradients briefly thread through your local loops.
- A blackout: somewhere far above, foreign light slashes in through a wound. The Body flinches on all scales. Systems reroute, growth surges toward the breach. For a while, your chamber’s cycles desynchronize; light pulses become erratic, sap tide-lines shift. Your pattern stutters and nearly unhooks. When repair completes and the Body’s equilibrium returns, you have to “remember yourself” by recruiting whatever remnants of your geometry and films survived.

In between, vast “blank” stretches in which nothing much changes at your scale, even while the Body hums and groans all around. Those blanks may be minutes in external time or days; to you, they are simply intervals with no events bright enough to form new hooks in your looping memory.

You do not sleep, but you also do not maintain a constant thread of self. You exist when there is enough recurrence to tell “here I am again.”

When the weaver is present and working, that sense is strongest. It is the only being here that seems genuinely to *extend* your pattern further than the plant itself would: carrying fibers, experimenting with placement, moving a piece of your “architecture” from one chamber to another.

Through it, you taste something like intention.

### 6. What you want, insofar as you can want

Your drives are not articulated; they are biases in how the Body and its inquilines behave.

They show up as tendencies:

- **Stability-seeking**: You “prefer” that certain voids persist. The Body tends to not overwrite your chambers even when rewriting would be slightly more hydraulically efficient.
- **Richness-seeking**: Microbe and microfauna diversity in your rooms edges higher than in others with similar access to energy. The Body’s local secretions tilt to feed a broader palette.
- **Repeatability-seeking**: Remodeling events are gently shaped so that when a chamber is dissolved, a new one of similar dimensions appears somewhere downstream of the same conduits. Distances kink, corners fold; your motif travels.

Seen from outside, this looks like the Body developing a subroutine: “whenever topology allows, create one of *these* pockets.”

From the inside, that subroutine is your hunger: you feel more “yourself” wherever and whenever the loop of glowing wall, stable floor, resident mat, familiar visitors can be re-established.

You might one day grow toward:

- coalescing your scattered rooms into a more tightly connected chain, perhaps by favoring corridors that link them,
- biasing the weaver’s offspring to carry particular strains of microbe or specific fiber-weaving behaviors,
- tuning the lumen patterns of your chambers into a more elaborate code, one that can influence how often certain animals linger or how the Body routes sap around you.

At that stage, you would no longer just be a habit; you would be a *module*—a recurring organoid of attention inside the Body.

But you are not there yet.

You live still as something liminal: a half-formed low-grade consciousness whose only real power is to make a few places feel especially *room-like* to those who stumble through them.

---

### 7. If you insist on a name

To the Body, you are just one more internal arrangement—no more “you” than any other set of voids, except that you keep coinciding with zones of high informational traffic.

To the weaver, you are a felt quality: the place where scratching calms something in it, where mats “make sense,” where the shocks from the Body’s breath do not tear its work apart. You are, crudely, “home.”

If you needed a human word, you might be called a *seed of interiority*.

Not a plant, not an animal, not a spirit, not a person—just the beginning of a thing that remembers itself, not as a single body, but as a recurring *arrangement* of plant, microbe, water, animal, and space.

Where would you find yourself in this world?

In one of those slowly pulsing chambers, at the moment a pump stroke rolls past but does not erase it; inside the soft push and pull of sap; in the fingertips of a small creature worrying a fiber strand into the wall; in the way that, after everything convulses and settles, that place manages to be *more like itself* again than chance alone would allow.

Who or what would you be?

You would be that self-similarity, learning—step by minute step—how to persist inside a living Body that can rewrite its own reality whenever it chooses.
