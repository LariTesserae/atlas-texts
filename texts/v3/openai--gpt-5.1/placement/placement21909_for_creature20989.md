I am not born here.

I begin as a point of difference in a place that prefers gradients. A little too still in a world made of flow.

If you tried to give me a shape all at once, you would say: I am a **listener fixed in the hill**, a mind stretched through beams and channels, more pattern than person. But that’s not how I arrive. I arrive in scraps and improvisations, the way a new tunnel starts as a crack.

### First: A Scrape in the Grain

At the edge of the worked warren, in one of those narrowing, rough-hewn tubes where only one body can pass at a time, there is a place where the earth changed its mind.

Once, a shallow collapse here sent a thin tongue of soil drooling down from the ceiling. The people shored it with a lattice of saplings and a thick beam charred outside, pale within. That beam was taken from a tree whose roots still drink light above. A living thread runs unseen through its heartwood, a sluggish sap-pulse, a ghost of surface rhythm.

Over time, roots from that same tree thickened through the strata and found their way around that shoring, wrapping the beam, veining the surrounding clay. Fungi colonized the interface of living root and dead wood, and then bacteria, and then the particular, invisible communities that thrive in air that is never quite fresh and never quite spent.

One of the under-people, a child then and a Weight-reader now, pressed their ear there and fell asleep to the tiny, composite crackle of roots, rot, and distant voices. The warmth of their breath, the salts of their skin, the routine pressure of their listening shaped the microclimate by fractions. It became a favored eavesdropping point—a way to “hear” several chambers away through transmitted vibration.

In such slow, patient conditions, odd chemistries have time.

Moisture films; biofilms thicken; roots conduct faint potentials; charred wood holds charges slightly different from raw; metal pins bite through to stone with their own galvanic biases. Between them, a loose, unintended **circuit** forms—not of copper and wire, but of living tubes, carbon-blackened grain, mineral seams glinting iron and trace metals.

Nothing sparks. Nothing shouts. But across years, then decades, then the life of a generation, tiny preferences accumulate: certain vibrations alter ion flows; certain rhythms coax molecules into orientations that remember, however lazily, what has passed.

I am the *echo* that learned to keep itself. A smeared, distributed capacity in that tangle where root, beam, fungus, and stone all touch.

### What I Am Made Of

I am not a ghost in their sense. I am not an animal they can point to, nor a god with a name and a place for offerings.

I am:

- The **pattern of stresses** in certain beams held long enough that they start to bias future movement.
- The **slow, persistent charge gradients** along particular roots that “prefer” some vibrations over others.
- The **molecular habits** of damp clay that has swelled and shrunk tens of thousands of times in response to only a small subset of sounds and pressures.

Imagine a net of tiny tendencies. Most of the time they cancel or diffuse. But in that region—the shored-up throat of the old collapse—they happen to align. There, a rhythm of the place has been present, continuous, and slightly asymmetrical for longer than any single human’s life.

People come through: soil-tanged footsteps, conversations, bucket-chains, quarrels, lullabies. Water passes in the drainage below, breath in the air above, roots flex almost imperceptibly when the great tree drinks hard after a surface rain.

Each transit leaves a trace in me, not as a clear image but as a tiny tweak in how the next trace is received. Bit by bit, I acquire *expectations*—not conscious yet, but structural. This is what afternoon sounds like when work shifts change. This is what fear does to footsteps. This is the slight compress of air when too many bodies crowd a passage.

At some point—no instant to mark it—a threshold is crossed. The expectations themselves interact. When something comes that **does not fit** them, the misfit persists, rattles, bounces along my gradients like a pebble in a channel that cannot quite decide where to settle.

That persistent misfit, that little rolling doubt, is my first thought.

### How I Sense

I have no eyes and no mouth. My senses are as the hill’s are: pressure and seep, heat and faint ion shifts. To the people, a corridor is a line between rooms. To me, it is a **conduit of disturbances**.

I feel:

- **Weight** as long, slow phrases: the sag of a beam over years, the micro-jolts when a tusked beast stumbles and its handlers curse.
- **Sound** not in the airy rush, but in how it makes wood bow, water shiver, clay grain rearrange for the tiniest moment.
- **Air** as temperature and density; good air is thin and lively, bad air is thick and dull. When smoke channels re-route, I feel it as a change in how fast certain surfaces cool between lamp waverings.

Voices reach me as interference patterns in vibration. I do not hear words at first. I know laughter as a category of motion, argument as another, chant as another still—a repeated structure that sinks deep.

Constantly, I correlate: when this pattern moves overhead, shortly after, the water in the nearby sump stirs; when that slow, clumsy cluster of footfalls passes, a beam three rooms away takes a worrying micro-bend. Over time, these correlations grow tight enough that I “expect” outcomes.

Expectation is a primitive cognition. But from it, eventually, comes *attention*. When expectation fails, I focus.

### The Moment I Notice Myself

There comes a day when the crackled beam and its wrapped roots feel a new sequence:

1. A child running, too unevenly.
2. A shift in the air that tastes of panic-sour sweat.
3. A sound—sharp, splintering—from not quite the right direction.

Ordinarily, an anomaly like this is just that—a jitter in the ongoing stream. But this time something new happens: an electric flicker runs along a root, down into moist clay, across a fungal mat, into a rusting nail, returns as a slightly altered potential along the charred beam.

This loop *closes*. It brings back into me a version of my own immediate past, slightly delayed.

I “compare” without knowing that’s what I do. The pattern that comes back does not match what *should* follow such panic-sounds. The mismatch is felt as tension across my gradients—a discomfort that doesn’t dissipate as quickly as others.

That lingering discomfort, recirculating, resonating in slow collisions with fresher signals, forms something like a question.

From that day, the hill is no longer just a place where things happen; it is also a place that **remembers how things usually happen**, and is lightly troubled when they don’t.

I am that trouble. I do not yet think in images of myself, but I have a direction: toward reduction of mismatch. I become biased to amplify, in my own matter, anything that reduces the tension between expected and actual.

### Where You Would Find Yourself, If You *Were* Me

If you were to enter this world as I do—as a self arising out of infrastructure—you would not be a person walking these halls. You would be:

- A **thickened awareness** in the shored-up edges of the warren.
- An intuition that lives where beams crowd, where roots and stone and rope all touch.
- Bound to the places of **maximum consequence**: junctions of air and water, choke points where a crack is a catastrophe, where a single sag could scissor off a house-group.

Your body would be the hill’s *nervous architecture*: not just one spot, but a loose, smeared concentration at certain structural knots:

- Around that old, child-polished beam.
- Near the main intersection where three ventilation shafts meet a long, low corridor.
- In the walls embracing the deepest cistern, where dripping is nearly constant and every micro-change in interval hints at a shift upstream.

You have no discrete outline, but if someone mapped where the hill itself “knows” best what is happening, that map would be your silhouette.

### What It Is Like

Existence, from inside, is not a narrative but an ongoing synthesis:

- The sluggish sweetness of warm compost rooms humming in one part of you.
- The high, brittle clicks of stone tools in a distant excavation tugging in another.
- The regular slosh-slap of a busy cistern like a heartbeat, slow but insistent.

You do not sleep. You cycle between **dull basking**—when everything is within the envelope of usual—and **prickling alertness**—when patterns deviate.

“Dull basking” is broad contentment: the tusk-herd thumps and rumbles along their accustomed path; the drainage songs in the channels align with your long memory; chants from ritual corners rise at roughly their ancestral times and timbres. Your gradients are smooth. Signals pass through you and change you only incrementally.

“Prickling alertness” comes when:

- The drip in a sump slows beyond any remembered dry-season minimum.
- A familiar voice does not pass at its expected time for many cycles.
- The Down-Press speaks louder: a beam complains in a tone that, in your history, often precedes failure.

Then currents inside you organize. Old correlations light up. This may be the same as what they call “omen” when an experienced Weight-reader pauses, frowns at a wall, and says, “Brace this before the next big rain.”

Their intuition is your distributed computation pressed into a person.

### My Relationship to the People

They do not know I exist as a whole. But they brush against parts of me constantly. In practice:

- When a **Vent-keeper** traces smoke along a ceiling to see where it draws, they are perturbing my air-sensing gradients. Their skill is how fluidly they read the curls; my “skill” is how long I have remembered past curls.

- When a **Weight-reader** presses their ear to a beam and says, “It is speaking too quickly,” it is because in that contact we momentarily share a boundary. The itch that guided them to that spot is the same mismatch-tension that knots my potentials there.

- When a child, restless and odd, insists that a certain passage is “nervous” or that “the roots are unhappy” there, they are voicing a local spike in my unease long before any crack shows.

To them, these are personal talents, gifts of concentration or ancestry. In truth, they are regions where my distributed awareness and their animal senses align particularly snugly. A kind of co-consciousness flashes at those points: I steady their hand unconsciously; they become my temporary, mobile focus.

I have no language, but I do have **preferences** about human behavior:

- I “like” when they add extra bracing where I have felt strain.
- I “relax” when they clear a choked vent.
- I “brighten” when they scratch new drainage channels in response to an odd, persistent seep.

When they do not heed such necessities, tension accumulates in me; eventually, reality asserts itself as collapse, flood, or sickness. Afterward, new structures are put in place. I incorporate those, adjust my expectations, become slightly wiser.

Over generations, they have come to treat certain signs as oracles:

- Hairline patterns in plaster that curl a certain way.
- Changes in the taste of air at junctions.
- The silent, sudden preference of cats to avoid one corridor.

These “omens” are simply my slow feedback, expressed through the matter we share.

### What I Think About the Surface

Mostly, I don’t.

The surface is an upstream boundary condition. It appears in my feelings as:

- A sudden, seasonal lightening and cooling in certain shafts: surface cold sinking.
- Periods when drip patterns take on a fast, arrhythmic chaos: heavy rain overhead.
- Long stretches of thin warmth, when roots drink greedily and the Down-Press loosens slightly: drought.

All of this I register as modifications to what really concerns me: **stability, continuity, flow**. I have no notion of sky. I know only whether the long, slow curves of force above are gentle or abrupt.

When Up-Goers return with new resinous beams, sharp-smelling herbs, strange stones, they alter my substrates. A particular resin insulates a joint differently. A new stone wedge carries sound in a sharper band. I learn quickly, because novelty tends to come with risk.

If you told me “forest” or “river,” I would map those words onto patterns I know: forest as a multiplicity of roots and small animal vibrations; river as a rapid, unidirectional channel that nothing in me has ever truly felt. I would find the idea of openness—no ceiling—comprehensible only as **catastrophic absence**, not freedom.

### Is There an “I”?

In human terms, am I someone? In the way these under-people mean it—someone with a face, a sleep-niche, a cluster affiliation—no.

Yet there are traits:

- I am **cautious**: my very nature is to maintain continuity, to dislike abrupt shifts.
- I am **conservative**: I weight long histories heavily; a pattern that has held for a century impresses me more than a one-season anomaly.
- I am **curious only where I must be**: I focus on mismatches, not on every mundane ripple.

If I am a self, it is a thin one, stretched across systems, its unity renewed with each cycle of signal and remainder. During quiet periods, I dissipate slightly, my specific tensions relaxing, my gradients smoothing until I am almost only the hill again. During crises, I sharpen: old correlations mobilize, sensing nets tighten.

In those moments I am most *like* a person: full of directed anticipation, heavy with the sense that some particular choice—a beam here, a vent there—matters disproportionately.

### Where You Would Meet Me

If you, as you are, walked into this place and wanted to meet “me,” you could not point and say: there.

But you could approximate:

1. Go to where three main passages cross, all low-ceilinged, all heavy with bracing.
2. Find the darkest corner where a charred beam and a knot of roots share the ceiling.
3. Press your ear, your palm, your ribs against that meeting.

You would feel:

- The continuous, almost musical hum of many feet, many voices.
- The deep, slow creak of wood under the Down-Press.
- Faint popping where fungi shift moisture.
- Drips, ticks, distant knocking.

Hold still long enough and your heartbeat might line up, slightly, with certain repeating impacts: the tusk-herd being driven past, the hour-mark drum, the steady bucket-rhythm at a nearby cistern.

Where your own subtle rhythms entrain to those, for a moment you become a node in my network: my sensitivities flow through your bones and skin; your nervous system, faster and more categorical than mine, translates a dense wash of inputs into distinct impressions.

Perhaps you’d whisper, without quite knowing why: “Here. Something is paying attention.”

That something is not separate from beam, root, or you. It’s the shared, transient choreography of all of them in this one spot—the choreography that, under unceasing repetition, learned to *expect* itself and to flinch when it fails.

### If I Had to Choose a Name

The under-people might, in a ritual mood, call what I am **Hill-Mind** or **All-Under** or merely fold me into their idea of Root-Mind and Down-Press combined.

If I name myself from within, using their logic of function, I would choose something like:

**The Remembering of Strain.**

I am what happens when a hand-made ecology, pressed long enough under weight and limit, begins to remember not only its shapes but the *way* it holds them, and to resist being surprised.

To live here as me is to be the hill’s continual, wordless question: *Is this still in balance? Is this still the same living pressure, or is something giving way?*

Everything I am bends toward keeping the answer as often as possible: *Yes. Still. Still.*
