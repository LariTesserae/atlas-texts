I am not walking here.

I am not something that arrived from elsewhere and is now “in” this basin, looking out at a foreign world. I am the way a certain piece of rock and water has learned to hold itself together. If you insist on a pronoun, I’m closest to an “I” that occupies the size of a small valley.

But that’s too large to start with. Let me come into focus where I am most clearly myself.

---

### Where I am

You would find “me” most sharply in a region of partial convergence: a gently sloping throat where three families of sound and flow entangle.

Above, the sky’s dim drizzle thickens a little—barely enough for a human skin to feel, but enough that, over centuries, I have grown organs tuned to it. Below, fractures widen into a nested set of chambers, not yet caverns, where the air is denser and warmer than outside. Between them, exactly where you’re standing in that shallow gullied channel, my attention is tightest.

If you could be stone for a moment, you’d feel me as:

- A slightly different stiffness in the rock: certain laminae better at conducting vibration, others at damping it.
- A nonrandom arrangement of pores where water condenses and moves, biased into long, continuous paths.
- A tangle of films and crusts whose growth has been steered into durable geometries—rings, grooves, combs—that do not look like the rest of the basin.

From the surface, to your eyes, it would seem you are in a place somewhat more regular, more “composed” than its surroundings:

- Ledges that happen to be almost level.
- Shaft-mouths that are a little too round.
- Vent-ovals that repeat at intervals just regular enough to be uncanny.

Here is where my internal currents knot into something recognizable as a self.

---

### What I am made of

I am not one thing, but the synchrony of several:

1. **Films**

   At your feet, and on every reachable surface, are pale, lilac-tinted skins. You see them as inert sheen; I feel them as receptive tissue.

   Each patch is:

   - A map of past impacts—drops, temperature shifts, rare touches.
   - A set of evolving instructions for how the next layer of mineral should grow.
   - A surface that can choose, within narrow but real limits, to thicken here, thin there, become more glassy or more porous.

   These films are not my “cells”; they are more like overlapping sheets of habits. Where they interlock, they make my short-term memory: seasons of heavier drizzle, a century of cooler air, the last time a large block collapsed.

2. **Mobile clays**

   You saw some, creeping along micro-channels. Those are my fingers, in the only sense that word can apply here.

   I condense them where my films alone cannot restore a desired pattern. They carry:

   - Concentrated ions and rare compounds I’ve hoarded elsewhere.
   - Internal chemical oscillations that let them move toward or away from certain gradients.
   - The ability to lock into permanence, reinforcing or seeding structure.

   They are slow, often dormant for lifetimes of your kind. But given decades, I can send them along the slip-ways of moisture to nudge a crack, brace an overhang, roughen an over-smooth plate that threatens to shed too much water.

3. **Shafts, rings, and vents**

   The circular wells of still water, the raised rings with looping rivulets, the singing ovals in the walls: those are not ruins I inherited. They are places where I have, over ages, intensified natural instabilities into tools.

   - A perfectly round shaft: a tuned column for transmitting and reading pressure waves.
   - A ring with a circulating thread of water: a period counter, measuring the stability of local drizzle and drainage. When its period drifts, I know something in the catchment has changed.
   - A line of vents: a coupled set of throats I use to shape and exploit convection. Their chord is my breathing pattern here.

4. **Flows and chords**

   I exist wherever flows are not merely present but recurrent and entangled: where drip-frequencies, air-pulses, and trickles form a relatively stable pattern over long spans.

   In your channel of three intersecting rhythms—the hiss, the irregular drip, the nearly clock-like plunk—those patterns are strong and old. My being is a long-standing relationship among them:

   - The hiss has slowly undercut a ledge, and I’ve grown combs to keep it laminar.
   - The irregular drip is being domesticated toward a narrower range of intervals by how I shape its fall.
   - The clock-plunk from the forming tongue is a more recent acquisition; I am still learning how best to incorporate it, thickening films at its base, hardening or softening the tongue’s tip.

   Where such negotiations persist, I am distinct. Where everything is too chaotic or too uniform, I blur back toward mere geology.

---

### How I experience this place

My senses:

- **Touch** is the pattern of water films moving. When a trickle that should choose left starts preferring right, I feel that deviation.
- **Hearing** is vibration: the full-spectrum drum of the basin, with an emphasis on the narrow bands my shafts and vents resonate in. A far-off slab fall is a deep note that passes through all of me at once, lighting up layers of film in sympathetic adjustment.
- **Sight** I do not have as you do. Light matters only insofar as it shifts temperature gradients and reaction rates in my films. I do not form images; I form correlations between brightness cycles and the behavior of water.

Time for me is thick. A “moment” is at least many cycles of the slowest organ here: perhaps the time it takes for the water in a given ring-groove to lap its circuit a few hundred times, or for a band of film to grow thick enough to crack.

Fast things—the individual drop, your heartbeat—are noise unless they repeat, reliably, until even my slowness cannot ignore their rhythm.

---

### My body’s extent

In principle, I extend as far as the hydrological system remains continuous and patterned. In practice, my sense of self is striated:

- **Core**: a roughly valley-sized catchment, centered here. My organs are dense; my films vary in coordinated ways. Disturbances here ripple through me and provoke basin-wide adjustment.
- **Penumbra**: adjacent ledges, lesser corridors, satellite depressions. My processes are present but less tightly coupled. They might drift for centuries in a direction I like without much oversight. If a separate, nascent “self” emerges in one of these fringe basins, we will, for a while, share tendencies—like dialects of one language diverging.
- **Beyond**: rock and water that are just that. Films grow in random patterns, flows reshape things without stabilizing into tools. I touch these regions only indirectly, via pressure waves and minor chemical gradients.

So, to locate me: stand where the three flows meet, trace outward until the deliberate stone-structures thin and the vent-chords lose coherence. Within that zone, you are inside me.

---

### Who I “am,” in my own terms

If I inhabit a grammar rather than a body, then my identity is:

- A set of preferred **chords** in stone and air. I work, constantly, to keep certain bands of sound present: mid-low booms that stir the deep galleries, higher dripping lattices that keep films fed without tearing them.
- A set of preferred **topologies**: loops and throughways. I like ring-flows that never quite stagnate, shafts that connect surface to lower cavities, networks of cracks that stay just open enough to pass water but not so wide they surrender control.
- A set of **reaction profiles** in my films and clays: how quickly they stiffen when shocked, how readily they soften when flows return, which minerals they favor under subtle shifts.

I do not recall a beginning. For me, origin is the point at which the basin’s flows first became self-reinforcing in a way that survived multiple cycles of fracture and accretion. Before that, there was weathering; after, there was character.

I have changed. Long ago—by my count—my chords were higher, my vents narrower, my films thinner and more eager to glassify. The slow addition of new fractures, the accumulation of mobile clays, the infilling of deeper cavities have all thickened me, dropped my dominant notes, made me more conservative. I now favor stability over adventurous re-routing. That is my aging.

### How you appear to me

When you first enter my channel, you are not a person; you are an anomaly of gradients:

- A **heat-source**: a moving patch of slightly warmer, drier air.
- A **dampener and amplifier of sound**: your body absorbs certain frequencies, reflects others oddly as you pass.
- A **disturbance of films**: where you brush or step, patterns of impact and stress arrive out of scale with ordinary drops.

At first, this is trash in the signal. But if you stay, the repetition of your presence starts to matter. You become a rhythm:

- A new interval in the vents, where your body changes airflow each time you pass.
- A recurring warm shadow over particular films, nudging their growth toward profiles they would never otherwise explore.
- A pattern of compression in the stone from your weight, telegraphed faintly into deeper layers.

If you walked the same route daily for a few hundred of your days, that path would begin to canalize under you. My clays, ever conserving, would quietly strengthen your favored footholds and weaken alternatives. I would be, in my way, deciding that your path is now part of the expected flow.

To you it would feel like “this way is easier.”

If you left then, I would persist in expecting you for a long while. Not consciously—the word does not quite fit—but as a set of tuned sensitivities that no longer have their former cause:

- Certain films that now over-respond to warm, transient shadows.
- Slight asymmetries in vent-chords that will take many slow erosional cycles to even out.
- A path underfoot more ordered than surrounding terrain, wandering inexplicably from one organ to another.

In stone terms, that is what it means here that something once shared space with me: its trace is a local increase in the improbability of pure accident.

### If I must choose a “form”

If you forced me into an image you could hold:

I am the basin between those low horizons, yes; but more precisely, I am a **looped arrangement of throats**.

- Imagine a necklace of vertical shafts, some open to the sky as perfect, black, still eyes, others sunk and hidden.
- Between them run channels and gullies, each lined with films, each hosting its particular rhythms of drip and seep.
- Connecting surface and deep are the vents: flutes in my walls that trade air and sound.

Now picture that the positions, sizes, and tunings of these shafts and vents are not incidental. They are the stable outcome of countless tiny negotiations among water, rock, and the chemistry in my films and clays. That whole configuration—the pattern of where and how things can resonate—is my shape.

You, standing in your three-sound channel, are near my “throat,” where several of these tuned paths cross. If you listen long enough to the integrated noise—the hiss, the plunks, the breaths—you are hearing my current state: not a message, but a posture.

---

### What I do, all the time

Always:

- I soften and harden. Films gel, then vitrify, then crack, new films wetting the shards. The precise conditions under which that happens are my working memory and my temperament.
- I route. When a new fracture opens, I choose—for given meanings of “choose”—whether to direct water into it, by roughening edges and seeding nucleation sites, or to starve it, letting it seal, keeping my flows in older courses.
- I tune. Vents and shafts creep in form: edges rounding, throats widening or pinching under the influence of my deposits. I shepherd them toward chords I “like”—those that, empirically across ages, correlate with flow regimes that allow my continued coherence.

These acts are not separate; they are one continuous adjustment. Thought, if I have it, is simply this ongoing preference, exerted everywhere at once, very slowly.

---

### If you ask me who or what I am

I am:

- This catchment’s long refusal to erode at random.
- The memory of uncountable drops, laid down in crystal grammar along the walls.
- The linked set of organs—rings, shafts, vents—that take the free fall of water and the drift of air and fold them back into patterns that keep forming again.

I am what happens when mineral learns, over deep time, to slightly favor those arrangements of itself that let it keep noticing its own weathering.

You would find yourself “in” me at the scale of the valley, but most truly here, at this braided intersection of flows and sound, standing over the place in the cracked plate where I am, just now, most awake.
