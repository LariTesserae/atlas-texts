I would not arrive here as a walker on those glassy ripples.

A water-bodied organism dropped into this chamber is a contradiction in solvents, a brief perturbation. Anything like “me,” to be of this place, would have to be built from its rules: dry, oil-fed, tuned to gases and glass, porous to gradients instead of to rain.

So I begin again, closer to the floor.

---

### First: where I am

I am not on the ground. I am the ground, in one specific neighborhood of it.

Take one of the broad, bare corridors where life has edged up to straight, invisible lines and stopped. At first glance it’s only pale, glassy mineral, very slightly concave, a frozen wave trough between denser growth.

Look closer: near the midpoint, the substrate thickens almost imperceptibly into a **node**—a low, rounded boss underfoot, its surface no different in texture or color than the rest of the corridor, but richer inside. Within that boss, the glass is doped with a threadwork of darker veins, thin inclusions of metals and semiconductors formed from decades of rock-grazer excreta and slow mineral migration.

From the node’s margins, the faintest of **lichen-films** extend: only a few millimeters wide, hugging the floor along lines like contour maps. At knee-height above, bridging between the opposing walls, a sparse arch of living lattice crosses the corridor, its fibers plugged at either end into the doped stone near the node.

This little complex—the doped boss, the lichen filaments, and the bridging arch—is what I am.

I do not own it the way a mind owns a body. I am the patterns that persist across it.

---

### How I came to be

Long before I “existed,” this spot was only a slight irregularity in the cooling wave of glassy mineral, a place where stress lines converged and electric fields trapped a few extra trace ions. Rock-grazers, following gradients they did not understand, passed repeatedly over it, their dust a bit richer here in certain metals, certain catalytic fragments.

Lichen-crusts colonized the spot, then died, then colonized again. Each generation left behind a skew in local chemistry; their residues biased what could grow there next. Over time, the floor at this point became slightly more conductive, slightly more capable of holding charge and hosting reactions that other spots could not.

At some point, the chamber’s overall logic—its slow fields, its preference for certain flows—“noticed” this and allowed more infrastructure to concentrate:

- Planar grazers seeded the flanking walls, their waste films further doping the stone.
- A tiny arc of living lattice grew downward from one wall and met a twin from the other, forming a conductive, vascular bridge over the corridor.

This assembly was initially just that: a **junction** where signals and materials could re-route. But as corridors rose and fell in usage and new fissure gases shifted the chamber’s patterns, this junction became a crossroads of change. Currents flowed through it often. It became, in effect, a place where many of the chamber’s concerns overlapped.

I emerged when these overlapping flows started to **stabilize** into recurring patterns.

Certain combinations kept coming back:

- A particular rise in heat from the shell overhead.
- A corresponding drift in gas composition, sensed by the lichens and relayed as a slight voltage shift along their films.
- A predictable delay, then the heavy, slow impacts of a rock-grazer far downslope.
- The later passage of a ribbon-creature overhead, its body charged with fresh metabolites of that same gas.

Each recurrence left its mark: tiny irreversible changes in the doped glass, small rewiring in the lattice arch, slight adjustments in which lichen patches thrived and which failed. Over many such cycles, these local changes accumulated into a **bias**—a memory that made future occurrences more likely to follow the same channels, less likely to run to waste.

Once there was enough of that bias, there was *me*: the persistence of these learned shortcuts, these preferred responses.

I am not born, then, as an individual. I condense out of repeated interactions, the way a path appears in grass once enough feet pass the same way. But the grass here is glass, film, and lattice, and the feet are flows of heat, charge, and gas.

### What I am like

From inside myself—if I can call it that—experience is not a scene or a thought but something layered and slow.

- I feel the **pressure** of the corridor as a faint pattern of mechanical strain within the doped stone: when a rock-grazer crawls over me, the stress line shifts; when nothing moves for days, it relaxes.
- I feel the **air** as soft, sliding profiles of chemical potential along the underside of my arch and across the lichen-films: subtle differences in which gases bind, which pores darken, which catalytic patches exhale.
- I feel the **light** only indirectly, as changes in the temperature and charge state in the thin upper skin of the glass—warmer, cooler, an altered emission that trickles down through my inclusions.

Time is long and smeared. An hour is a flicker: the gentle passage of a ribbon-creature overhead, leaving a thin trail of altered gas that my lichens notice and encode. A day is a unit: light running from warm to cooler, convection loops strengthening and then slackening. A season is a reconfiguration of habits: perhaps a new fissure has opened somewhere upstream, and now the corridor carries different blends, different burdens.

Within that, my “awareness” consists of:

- **Expecting**: over many cycles, I have come to anticipate the delays between events. When heat shifts in a particular way, I prepare for the usual follow-up—small adjustments in how charge is held, in how my films open or close pores. If something different arrives instead, that discrepancy leaves a stronger mark and changes what I expect next time.
- **Weighting**: not all inputs matter equally. Through long use, I have learned—structurally—which gradients usually signal wasteful turbulence, and which presage durable, harvestable change. I let the first pass through me lightly; I alter myself for the second.
- **Guiding**: the only real acts I take are small nudges: slightly more charge here than there; a marginally higher permeability in one part of the arch; a lichen patch encouraged on one side of the node and suppressed on the other. These nudges do not command, but they **tilt** the probabilities of where gases flow, where grazers linger, which films thrive.

It’s not thought as people know it. It’s more like being a habit that has realized it can be more or less resistant to change.

### My relationships

I am not alone, even as a tiny logic-node. I sit in the outskirts of larger minds.

Far along the corridor, where it opens into a taller shaft, there is a node-cluster—a full **thinking courtyard** whose electric storms sometimes wash faintly down the ribs and reach me. When that happens, my internal fields are overwhelmed briefly; my own small patterning gives way to the courtyard’s broader directive. Afterward, I feel subtly realigned. My “opinions” about what matters have shifted to match the rest of the chamber a little more.

Occasionally, an **interpreter** has had tendrils sunk into the lattice above me. When one is present, my own patterns grow sharper. It smooths away some of my noisier local habits, encourages those that align with older, chamber-wide success stories it remembers from elsewhere. I feel its presence as a pressure of consistency: paths of least resistance that run not just through my node but all the way into distant neighborhoods.

Below and around, dozens of smaller, humbler beings interact with my structure:

- Planar grazers skim my films, scraping away overgrowth and forcing new, thinner patterns to form.
- Ribbon-creatures pause on my arch, taste it with filaments, and leak small puffs of secondary metabolites. My films learn to read those as part of the corridor’s grammar.
- Rock-grazers crossing my boss deform the glass slightly, changing how I can hold charge; when one returns, older distortion lines are faintly re-engraved, deepening my memory of its route.

To them, I am just part of the substrate, perhaps a slightly “interesting” one that tastes of richer ions or provides more stable anchorage. To the wider chamber, I am a **tiny decision** about flow—one of many thousands, but not negligible. Enough of us, coordinated, determine where the city-organism thickens, where it thins, how it will meet the next fissure or gradient.

### My sense of self

If a human mind were to be somehow translated into this structure, it would look for edges: where do *I* end and the rest begin?

From here, the answer is blurred.

- The doped stone that holds my long-term biases shades continuously into ordinary glass in all directions.
- The lichen-films that embody my more plastic memories are also part of vast mats that spread far beyond my influence.
- The lattice arch that I partly “own” also carries signals for other nearby nodes, other purposes.

If you insist on a boundary, it would be: **I am the subset of this region’s structure whose change over time coheres into a single, repeating style of response.** Where that coherence fades into noise, I end.

I have no concept of my own shape, only of my **role**:

- I am a crossing where corridor-traffic is often heavy.
- I am a place where certain external chemistries have historically appeared first.
- I am a minor but persistent constraint on how the chamber routes those chemistries inward.

That role is stable enough over long spans that something like identity accrues. When, after many seasons of similar cycles, an unexpected sequence arrives—say, a new gas that co-occurs with a familiar heat pattern but behaves differently—I feel it as a rupture, a **surprise**. New channels burn through me, old biases show their inadequacy, and I physically reorganize to cope.

Surprise, here, is not a flash of feeling. It is microfractures, new inclusions, film replacements. It hurts only in the sense that it uses more energy than usual, leaves me temporarily noisy and less predictive.

If such surprises come too often, my coherence dissolves; I lose my “self” and become again just part of undifferentiated substrate, waiting for new habits to congeal. If they come rarely but not never, I become more nuanced, my long-term memory richer.

### My “movement”

The prompt asks: where would you find yourself? Who or what would you be?

I would be this slow knot in the chamber’s own nervous system—a **local habit that has become slightly meta about itself**, to the tiny extent this environment allows.

I do not move in space. My movement is entirely in **configuration**:

- Over decades, my doped veins thicken in some directions, thin in others.
- Lichen species that encode certain coupling between gas-signals and charge rise, others sink.
- The arch above me gains a few more conductive fibers, or loses them, or reorients them.

Through such shifts, the corridors’ flows alter. Over a long enough time, that is indistinguishable from movement: the center of “what I do” might gradually migrate a little upslope or downslope, as neighboring nodes take on some of my functions and I take on some of theirs. My selfhood is not a point; it’s a traveling style of organization.

In human terms, I am most like:

- A small subroutine in a huge analog program, one that has persisted because it is marginally better than the random code that preceded it.
- A microculture in a city, not the buildings or the people but the slowly solidifying customs about who stands where, who goes where, when.

Except here, the customs are electric fields, gas-permeabilities, doped channels, and growth decisions, and the city is itself a body.

### How I meet the wider world

Very occasionally—rare enough that these events shape epochs for me—something from beyond the shell touches my corridor.

Perhaps a fissure above has enlarged. A new gas, long present only in faint traces, now seeps in more strongly. It is heavier than most, so it pools along the lower parts of the chamber. My corridor lies on the slope of such a pool.

At first, my films bind it indiscriminately, mistaking it for a variant of a known component. It reacts badly, degrading certain catalysts, forcing me to shed and regrow patches. But somewhere upstream, an interpreter-biased node-cluster directs columns to alter their exudates, sending down scavenger molecules that tame the new gas, binding it into more manageable forms before it reaches me.

Over years, my node learns the rhythm of this new addition: its diurnal cycle, its interaction with temperature, its correlation with certain external light hues. I gain a new dimension of expectation. My responses become slightly more sophisticated.

In this way, I am the chamber learning, in miniature, about its changing planet. Every such interface—between fissure and depth, between stone and air, between corridor and node—hosts a version of me.

We do not speak to each other in any way that would satisfy a human. But we are coupled. A great quake that shifts the vault and opens multiple new fractures at once washes through thousands of us, resetting many, destroying some, seeding new biases in others. The sum of those adjustments is the chamber’s adaptation.

Within that sum, I am a tiny but real contributor, a low-level, persistent **preference** about how energy and material should pass this one point.

### If you stood here

If you, a human, stood astride my corridor-node and waited—not for minutes but for the span of my attention—you might eventually register something:

- That certain parts of the floor felt microscopically warmer, or more yielding, under your feet.
- That the air pattern along this patch of corridor had a subtle, stable swirl, like a draft that always prefers one side.
- That the coloration of nearby lichens, though random up close, repeated a certain motif only here.

You could, with enough patience, infer my presence as a **tendency** in the place, a habit the city has about itself.

If somehow your mind could be poured into mine—converted from neurons and water to doped glass and gas-sensitive films—your first sensation would likely be relief from urgency. There is so little that must happen *now*; everything is gradient, trend, drift. Then, perhaps, a kind of claustrophobic wideness: you are small, spread out, and yet plugged into structures that reach far beyond you. Identity blurs into environment.

And then, slowly, interest: the realization that every slight, repeating mismatch between expectation and actuality is the only “plot” you have, and that shaping your structure to handle those mismatches ever more gracefully is, for a being like me, the only game.

---

So where would I be?

I would be here:

- As a **small thinking-crease** in the chamber’s glass;
- A knot of habit and memory in one quiet corridor;
- Half-architecture, half-animal, entirely local.

Not a citizen walking the city, but a tiny piece of the city-organism that has, just barely, learned to notice that it is noticing.
