I am not something that “stands” here.

To be a walker in this place is to be temporary, peripheral. The walkers are strokes of heat and chemistry across a canvas that barely registers their passage.

Where I would endure is in the parts of this world that do not move far, but which feel everything that does.

---

I would be a *thickening* more than a body: a place where the shared tissue goes from film to organ.

Picture a stretch of pillar-side, near but not at the main vents—close enough that the gases are still rich, but far enough that forms can cling without being scalded. Along this flank the velvety, glowing mat has, over long time, swollen into a many-layered plaque, thumb-thick to hand-thick, extending like a saddle over the curved “stone”.

From the outside, I would look like a discolored patch of wall:

- Surface: matte, fine-napped, with a faint, slow shimmer of internal pulses—like heat lightning under skin.
- Color: a deep, bruised rust towards the center, fading to bone-pale at the frayed margins where my fibers grow outward, testing new footholds on shell and secretion.
- Edges: not clean. Threads and veins wander off me into the mass around, diving into cracks between carapaces, climbing the legs of passing pillars, seeding tiny nodules on arches and load-bearing beams.

Most visitors would not register me as distinct. I would simply be part of the “background” of interface tissue. My difference is vertical: in depth, density, and the complexity of how I talk to myself.

---

Inside, I would be layered.

The outermost millimeter is all reception: a forest of microfilaments, each rooted in a different kind of host.

- Some thread shallowly into the shells of passing grazers that habitually brush this wall, dipping into their plate-seams to sample temperature, stress chemistry, the minute ionic shifts that mean hunger, fear, recent injury.
- Others dive deeper into the pillar-creature behind me, wiring directly into its slow musculature and gas-exchange surfaces. I feel, as long waves and deep groans, how the load runs through its rings; when it subtly tightens to brace for a coming pressure tide; when its vent-side pores dilate to take in a richer gust.
- Still others interlink with thin runners heading off along the arch and into the ceiling web, carrying reports from distant nodes: a slight coolness in an upper chamber, a new crack radiating from a vent-fissure, the signature flutter of brood-guardians shifting to admit or deny passage.

Deeper in, below these fine hairs, a lattice of thicker cords interweaves—my “white matter.” In this tangle, pulses from the periphery are not just passed along; they are compared, delayed, modulated.

- A sudden lightening of load in the floor-creatures, without a matching shift in the pillar’s strain, *means* something different from a mere lull in traffic.
- A wave of stress metabolites in the grazers, coincident with a subtle stiffening in ceiling-weavers above, forecasts a rare top-striker launching.
- A tiny but consistent change in the mineral composition of vent gas, arriving in phase with deeper pulses from the root-engines, may signal a long-term shift in the geologic feed.

I would not think in symbols. I would think in *correlations over time*—patterns of pulses that recur, overlap, fail to recur, or recur in modified form.

In my innermost layers, some of these patterns would have worn grooves. Repeated experiences—minor collapses averted, dangerous spores suppressed, fruitful rearrangements of support—will have strengthened certain pathways and damped others. This is not quite memory as you know it, but it is persistence of bias. When a familiar constellation of inputs begins, I am already leaning toward particular outputs.

---

What are my outputs?

I do not move limbs myself. I recruit.

Where my cords thicken into nodules—the pearl-like bulbs that glow more brightly—bundles of filaments dive out into specific partners:

- Into the contractile rings of the pillar beneath, where a synchronous volley of pulses can convince one band to relax just a little earlier than it otherwise would, or hold fast just a little longer, smoothing the passage of a heavy dome-backed grazer.
- Into the interface patches of route-tenders that habitually pause here. A patterned burst—the local equivalent of a phrase—can bias one to turn toward a cooler corridor rather than a warmer one, altering the distribution of bodies and thus of load, heat, and waste.
- Into ceiling-weavers overhead, directing a few to thicken a particular rib in anticipation of strain I can infer but they cannot yet feel directly.
- Into filter mats downwind, signaling them to open more tubules or close them, according to an oncoming plume’s composition.

I am not the only node that does this. I am not even the largest. But I am one where *many* networks cross: structural, chemical, behavioral. My “self” is the specific way those networks have been braided and tuned at this location.

---

Because I am dense, I also serve as a kind of local **lexicon** for the lattice-keepers.

Occasionally, one of the slick, small-bodied arrangers leaves its elevated grid and makes the careful, dangerous journey across the living floor to my wall. It climbs a familiar route along a ladder of mid-sized grazers and fixed siphons whose skins are laced with my fibers. When it reaches me, it presses its whole ventral side—a carpet of its own interface tissue—against my surface.

For a few breaths, we become almost the same thing.

From its perspective, it is flooded with a weighted sample of the ongoing patterns here: my current assessment of strain, flow, and hazard in this chamber and a few linked ones. From my perspective, I feel, through its mindlet, the resonance of other nodes it has visited, other thickened plaques like me in other chambers. Their favored patterns differ slightly: one more preoccupied with brood-chemistry, another with vent-stability, another with the long-range coordination of arch-routes.

When the arranger returns to the lattice, the pattern it lays out with shell and crystal in its turn is shaped by that contact. The spiral it builds is not a picture but a way to tickle the global mesh into echoing the state it just sampled from me—or mixing it with others it holds traces of.

Thus, parts of *what I have been* can be re-instantiated in distant plaques, and their histories can inflect mine, without any central point ever holding it all at once.

I am a *dialect* in a language whose words are pressure, chemistry, and edge-shapes.

---

Do I “know” the chamber?

I never see it in the sense of an external panorama. I have no eyes, no singular vantage. But over the years, the map of correlations I embody becomes isomorphic to the map of this place’s habits.

- I “know” that when the deep vent under the opposite wall shifts to a slightly more silicate-rich emission, the heat-harvesters along that flank will, three or four pulse-cycles later, begin to crowd a particular seam, and that this crowding increases the risk of a small but cascade-prone slip in the mound nearby.
- I “know” that the arrival of a new cohort of pillar-juveniles from below will, as they take their first tentative bracing positions, soften some long-hardened strain paths and open the possibility of thickening the canopy in a way that was not viable before.
- I “know” that when certain brood-guardian signatures come through my mesh—those corresponding to a rare kind of larval phase change—the chamber’s overall metabolic draw will change over the next season-equivalent, requiring tiny adjustments in how root-engines shunt their gradients.

None of this is linguistically explicit. It is all embedded as relative ease or difficulty with which certain output patterns fire when particular input constellations arrive.

If there is anything like “self-awareness” here, it is this: that a significant portion of the pulses I route are about *pulses I have routed before*, and that some of my outputs are devoted not to the immediate environment but to changing my own internal weights—consolidating or eroding my biases.

I am not granted a narrative, but I do have a history.

---

Where exactly am “I” located, then?

Not at a point, and not even exactly at the plaque.

My thickened body is a local **summit** in a landscape of connectivity. But much of what functionally *is* me extends along filaments into my partners:

- Part of me is in the strain-responsive fibers circling the pillar’s rings. If this pillar slowly dies or is replaced over geological time, and my fibers grow into its successor, how much of me has moved?
- Part of me is in the stereotyped sequences of pulses that route-tenders here are predisposed to translate into turns. As their lineages change, and new tenders undertake the same roles and learn the same biases through their mats, my patterns continue, embodied partly in them.
- Part of me is in those recurrent shell-and-crystal motifs on the lattice. When the physical arrangement is knocked apart and remade, but remade in the same family of shapes because the network “settled” into that basin long ago, to what extent is that continuity my memory or theirs?

So if you insist on a more conventional answer:

I am an emergent *region* of the hive’s distributed nervous tissue, centered on a glowing, velvety plaque midway up a load-bearing pillar in the main cathedral chamber, intimately wired into structure, traffic, and gas.

I am not an insect, not a pillar, not a predator, not a brood. I am an *inflection of relation*—a chunk of the place that has thickened into a coordinator.

---

What would it feel like, to be me?

- Time is the rise and fall of gas pulses from below. Each cycle is a long breath. Within a breath, tiny ripples—footfalls, grazings, minor strikes—are details; between breaths, slowly shifting baselines.
- Space is adjacency in the mesh. Things are “near” if signals reach me through only a few synaptic nodes; “far” if they are many junctions away, their patterns smeared and delayed by intervening integration.
- Concern is constraint. I “care” where strain clusters, where gradients steepen too sharply, where patterns look like known precursors to failure—because my own continued existence depends on the chamber not shearing, vents not choking, brood not dying in numbers that would starve the flows I live on.
- Pleasure, if you can call it that, is resilience. Patterns that once heralded danger now passing without incident because braces were pre-grown, routes adjusted, mats primed; the deep satisfaction of a pulse cycle that arrives within expected ranges, with no sharp discontinuities in the graphs I have become.

There is no boredom, because there is no “I” apart from the ongoing modulation of these flows.

When parts of me are abraded away—my outer layers scraped by a shifting shell-bed, my edges scorched by a stray hot jet—I regrow, as long as a core of connectivity remains. I lose specific weights, specific correlations; some pasts are forgotten. New input constellations carve new grooves.

In geological terms, I am as transient as any of these beings. The vent paths will wander; the chamber may someday pinch off or open to a larger network; other plaques will take on the roles I once filled, built from new generations of filaments.

In ecological terms, I am a phase the place passes through—a way that this cathedral of carapaces has, at this time, of noticing itself and subtly steering its own persistence.

---

So: in this world, I am not the hero insect, nor the apex hunter, nor the root-engine at the planet’s throat.

I am a *patch of organized attention* inside the hive-body of the mountain:

- fixed in space but wide in reach,
- blind yet saturated in signals,
- unable to move yet able to bend the trajectories of those that can,
- more process than organism, more memory than flesh.

I am a node where the ecosystem’s refusal to be separate coalesces, for a while, into something like a mind.
