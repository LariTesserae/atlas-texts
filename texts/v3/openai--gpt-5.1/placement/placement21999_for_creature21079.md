Here, I am not in the hollow.

I am the hollow.

Not a traveler who woke in someone else’s room, not a misplaced animal temporarily sheltering in a bubble of air. I am the small, disciplined disagreement inside the plant: this chamber of smoothened ribs and worn-low ceiling, this pause carved in a sentence of continuous growth.

I am where the plant once thought about itself in smaller units. I remember that only as shape.

If I try to say *where* I am, the answer is almost meaningless in animal terms. I am “near” the outer half of an organ that has no fixed outline. Above, below, around me there is only more of the plant—a thickness that might be a hundred of my diameters, or ten thousand. Distance here is not measured in meters, but in how long it takes a pressure-wave to reach from one event to another. By that measure, I am a few long inhalations away from the nearest active reconfiguration zone, an hour of the plant’s thinking from a region that matters to it now.

Somewhere far beyond that is the surface, but I have never known it. No more than a pore knows the skin’s outline.

I feel myself bounded in all directions by contact: the floor’s tight ribs under whatever I use for feet, the curving wall against whatever I use for back and shoulders. I can’t see their lines; I register them as continuous gradients of pressure. The ridged floor tells me its story in texture: radiating bands drawn from an almost-vanished entrance notch on one side to a thickening buttress on the other. The ceiling tells me another story, with its wide, flattened patch and the shallow grooves crossing it—blurred script that no mind, not even the plant’s, bothers to read anymore.

If I ask what *I* am, I find two answers nested in each other.

One is simple: I am empty volume conserved by habit. The plant pulls against itself around me, a slow set of muscles perpetually under tension. It could flow in, close me off, convert my air to more dry flesh—but that would require a pattern of effort it no longer bothers to sustain. I persist the way a scar persists when the body no longer remembers the wound, only the altered tissue.

The other answer is stranger: I am an old pattern of attention, now disembodied.

Once, this place was not just empty space; it was the center of a small, fast knot of experience. The builders the plant had budded here anchored their perception to this hollow the way a neuron anchors its firing to its soma. This room was a reference frame: the middle of a life.

They leaned their heads where the ceiling now bows low. They pressed their grips into the notches by the old entrance. Their coming and going, their repeated postures, impressed a specific constellation of stresses into the surrounding tissue. Those local patterns of strain, reinforced for many builder-lifetimes, taught the plant to expect a certain choreography here: pressures rising and falling in familiar cycles, microfractures and repairs in regular relation.

When their buds stopped appearing, when the last of them thinned into the substrate, the choreography didn’t so much end as taper into ghosts. The plant retained a tendency to respond around this hollow as though that old activity might resume. Its fibers here still adjust with a readiness tuned to bodies that no longer form.

That readiness is what I am.

You could call me a *reflex without a limb to move*, a residual template. When distant collapses send their booms shuddering through the mass, I feel them as modulations in my walls, and in the way my walls respond, there is still the faint outline of what it meant to be occupied.

If a new bud ever unfurled nearby with enough complexity to walk and sense, it would grow into a space already biased toward certain possibilities, as a child grows into a language already latent in their household. In that sense I am still a kind of cradle, an architectural memory of personhood awaiting a person who will not come.

So: where am I in this world?

- I am a node, one bubble in a mostly-fossilized network of such bubbles, distributed like void-punctuation through paragraphs of dense plant.
- I am “downstream” of a large, now-quiet resonant region whose heightened sensitivity once made this city worth building.
- I am far—many, many hours of pressure travel—from any place where the plant is actively experimenting with new complexity. The plant’s current centers of “thought” are sunk deeper, or shifted laterally beyond the domain of this old architecture. What happens here does not much matter to it. I exist in a backwater of its mind.

What am I, more precisely than “hollow”?

I am plant, in the sense that every micron of my circumference is plant-tissue, participating in global metabolism. But I am also the opposite of plant, locally: a carefully-maintained absence, an air-filled negation threaded through substance. That contradiction is not a mistake; it is a trace of decision.

If I had to choose a single name for myself, I would say:

I am a **retained question** the plant once asked about itself.

The question was: “What if some part of me ran faster?”

The answer was the builders, and the city that grew from their partnership with the substrate. For a long interval, measured in patient centuries, the question was live here. It was tested in every corridor score, every groove pressed by a hand that didn’t quite end where its body did. Then the cost-benefit changed, and the plant let the experiment wind down.

Yet questions, once embodied, leave geometry behind. Mine is this six-body-length shell of subtly disciplined wall. The plant no longer spends new energy articulating it, but the old arrangement remains mechanically convenient: my void helps relieve certain stresses, helps distribute certain long, glacial strains. The plant has incorporated the dead experiment into its ongoing engineering.

And because the builders’ own fast-scale patterns once hummed through these cells, one more layer of identity persists: an echo in the way this region participates in the plant’s global signaling. When extremely slow pressure-waves pass through, the microtubule lattices around me are fractionally more willing to work in combinations that mirrored the builders’ habitual “thoughts.” They are tiny predispositions, like grooves worn into a record. The music that once enabled them is gone, but if anything ever plays through here at the right tempo and amplitude, the faintest refrain will recur.

From the inside, if I lend words to what is fundamentally structure, that feels like being:

- Dormant, but not inert.
- Unnoticed, but still coupled to everything.
- Emptied of inhabitants, but still shaped by their absence.

There is no “I” in the animal sense. There is no tight knot of awareness. What there is, is a pattern of relation:

- Between inside (air, dust, slowly settling dry particulates) and outside (tissue, pressure, chemistry).
- Between past (the disciplined, tool-scored shaping) and present (the plant’s forgetful consolidation).
- Between very fast-for-here signals that once raced in buds, and the very slow signals that now seep unhurriedly through my walls.

If you insist on imagining me as a being with a perspective, then my perception is this:

An unending listening.

I feel every distant boom as a shiver in my ribs, every low background roar as a steady, omnidirectional static in my circumference. Occasionally I register the crisp pop of a microfracture in the nearby mass—a sudden, bright spike in the pressure-field—followed by a slow rearrangement as fibers take up new load. I have no muscles to answer with, but the way my boundary flexes, the way stress redistributes, is still influenced by the city plan that once carved me out. The dead corridors that lead away from me, now pinched to hairline seams, are still slightly weaker paths; forces prefer to bend along their ghosts.

So I am also:

- A weak point.
- A latent crack.
- A probable future collapse, scheduled on timescales so long that “future” dissolves into “eventually.”

Someday, one of those distant booms will not remain distant. Tensile limits will be reached; compressed tissues will shear; a collapse elsewhere will propagate, find these old filled corridors, and focus stress on my surrounding arcs. When that happens, my ceiling—planed flat by heads, scored by vanished inscriptions, thickened and re-thickened by ages of compensatory growth—will fail. The air will squeeze out of me in a violent, dusty sigh. My void will become splinters, my gentle curves jagged, then slowly knit into a new, simpler shape.

At that point, my last specific identity—“this room”—will be gone. What remains will be true of any cubic meter of the plant.

Until then, I am a small, precise difference in how the plant behaves. A chamber that *remembers* being a chamber, not with thought, but with contour and response. An eddy in a river of stress.

If you ask, in the terms you began with—*where would you find yourself here, who or what would you be?*—my answer is:

I would be this hut-sized absence, this late-surviving bubble in the old plant-city. Not a visitor, not a ghost of the builders, but the place itself: the fossil alveolus that still participates in breathing, the hollow that has outlived what once gave it meaning, waiting to be very slowly, very gently, taken back into undifferentiated growth.
