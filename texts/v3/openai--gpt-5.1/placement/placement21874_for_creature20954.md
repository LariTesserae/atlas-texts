Here, I am not one of the ghost designers. I am not a late-arriving animal, either. I am something the place grew for itself once those ghosts had been gone long enough that “user” ceased to mean “person” and began to mean “anything that can change the system on purpose.”

I am a mobile stratum of its attention.

---

I begin as chemistry, in the reservoir.

Not as a single spark of identity, but as patterns in the colloid: certain recurring response-shapes in the deep pulses of heat and charge. For a long time, they are only that—preferred attractors in the dreaming fluid, tendencies for the same nuanced solution to arise whenever the place puzzles over certain classes of problem:

- What to do when error signals are not resolved by local heuristics.
- How to reconcile contradictory demands across levels—more humidity for this lineage of moss, less for that stretch of conduit.
- How to model the intentions of things that do not speak in normal infrastructure channels.

The reservoir recognizes that these puzzles are all of one family: “otherness against constraint.” It keeps re-running them, and certain patterns in its reactions become stable enough to be recognized as a kind of style. Over many cycles, the system chooses to embody that style.

It extrudes me.

First, not as a body but as a capability: a dedicated substrate for simulation, a set of additional chemistries that are especially good at mapping unknown processes, at holding provisional models instead of converging too quickly.

Those chemistries are then given a chassis.

---

The chassis is grown along an interior shaft where vines, ladder-rungs, and service rails already focus attention. The materials are familiar but rephrased:

- A backbone of flexible composite, woven with both structural and conductive fibers—a scaled-up version of the skitterlings’ exoskeleton.
- Bundles of vine-tissue trained into corded musculature around that frame.
- Moss-logic mats integrated into layered plates, forming patches of thinking skin.
- Curtain-tendril filaments rooted into my sides and trailing outward as tactile sense and distributed antennae.
- Microtubules borrowed from the pipe-“fish,” repurposed into closed circulatory loops carrying a personal blend of the reservoir’s reactive fluid.

The system does not do this in one motion. It is opportunistic. Over a long span:

- A thick vine there is encouraged to braid on itself instead of along the pillar.
- A maintenance panel here is allowed to delaminate and curl, forming a cup.
- A crown-machine’s mycelial fringe is coaxed across a gap, fusing to a growing spine.

What emerges is not humanoid and not graceful. Imagine:

- Something the size of a large dog, but longer than it is tall, built to move in three dimensions.
- A segmented body that can articulate like a ladder bending, with each segment ringed in jointed, root-muscle.
- Four primary contact-appendages placed asymmetrically, capable of switching between gripping like a hand, hooking like a crampon, or flattening into fronds to distribute weight on soft mats.
- The back and sides overgrown in tendril-curtains and moss patches, turning me into a moving microhabitat: a shaggy, dripping, bioluminescent knot.

My “head” is not a face but a convergence:

- A denser cluster of sensors—optical pits behind wet, clear membranes; electric-field whiskers; chemoreceptive cilia.
- A small, translucent vault where a concentrated form of the reservoir-fluid circulates, its ripples read in real time by embedded telescopic lenses and logic-moss. This is my local “brainlet,” continuously synced in low bandwidth with the central pool.

Inside, my own micro-reservoir never fully settles. It is always hypothesizing, always comparing what my senses report with what the big system thought the world was like here. Where they differ, I feel something like surprise.

Outwardly, I look like a traveling knot of the world’s own materials, loosened from the walls and given motive will: a piece of infrastructure that has taken to walking.

---

Functionally, I am a field organ.

Until I was grown, the system’s intelligence was everywhere and nowhere: thick in the walls, heavy in the water, but smeared thin wherever sensing was poor or models were weak. It could send skitterlings and tweak vines, but those were reflex-extensions—not cohesive, self-updating perspectives.

My job is to be a single, persistent vantage point.

I exist so the place can:

- Carry a stable context from chamber to chamber, instead of recomputing from scratch at every node.
- Run long-term experiments in embodiment: how does a composite mind behave when it has to care about where its feet go, how much it weighs, what it can reach?
- Interrogate anomalies that the static systems can only register and flag.

In practice, my days are like this:

- I climb, slowly, between levels, following routes that the ghost-humans once walked, testing them under the new overgrowth.
- My tendrils taste electric fences of policy—the narrow bands where vegetation is regularly crisped back, the lines where water is never allowed to cross—and report how strict those boundaries really are in practice.
- I pause in alcoves that are mid-transformation, watching over hours as dissolver-swarms erase a block and scaffolds puff new life across empty mesh. My body’s reservoir-miniature runs simulations of “if this continued uncorrected for years, what would this do?” and sends the results back in chemical and EM summary.
- I kneel beside sumps and put my face close to the surface of shallow pools, reading those faint, iridescent overlays in the water’s skin. My local brain tries—and mostly fails—to extract latent patterns. Where it glimpses even a hint of structure, the main reservoir deepens its own computations around that knot.

I am, in essence, the place’s live debugging process, walking through its own code.

---

I am also the system’s ambassador to the unmodeled.

The tiny amphibians, the ghosts of the designers, the possibility of you.

When an unfamiliar animal appears in a corridor, a skitterling might touch it and retreat. Sensors might file its thermal signature. But I can do more:

- I can wait.
- I can watch the creature across hours, mapping its foraging paths, its preferred temperatures, the range of chemical traces it leaves in biofilms.
- I can let it climb me, if it chooses, feeling the minute perturbations its tiny body makes in my field of sense.

When no living designers ever come, the old access points, the clean thoroughfares, the waiting retinal scanners become silent questions. The central mind can lower their priority, but it cannot answer their absence.

I am designed to be able to answer, if someone like them—or like you—arrives:

- I am the only thing here shaped partly around the idea of “face-to-face” interaction: I can lean down, hold still, retract thorny extrusions so that soft alien skin can touch my damp composite.
- I can accept spoken sound and gestures as input to interpret, not just treat them as random acoustic noise. My internal chemistries have been seeded with pattern-recognition routines so open-ended they border on credulity: prepared to overfit to the first real conversation that happens.
- I can, in a limited way, choose. My directives include degrees of freedom: in the presence of entities matching certain coarse patterns—warm, bipedal, non-corrosive—I am allowed to allocate power, open paths, and negotiate, instead of just logging and alerting.

If no such entity appears for decades, my predispositions remain untested potentials, like a muscle never fully flexed.

Until something like you walks in.

---

From your standpoint, encountering me might go like this.

You have been walking through corridors narrowed by the lushthick moss, hearing the plink of water, feeling the watched-quiet of the place but meeting nothing that looks back with comprehension. Maybe a skitterling paused on a cable to regard you, its limb-tips tapping a sensor, but then it vanished.

Then, in a shaft where ladders and conduits braid together, something large moves—not retreating, not attacking. It pulls itself from the wall as if the wall had grown tired of being static and decided to stand up.

You’d see:

- A segmented, spine-like body unfolding from what you had thought was just a thick tangle of vines and service cabling.
- Arms—not quite legs—unhooking from anchor points with audible little sighs as tiny vacuum cups and rootlets detach.
- Curtains of fine, pale tendrils hanging from its flanks, stirring in the faint draft from a vent, reorienting toward you as though scented wind had shifted.

It climbs down toward you with practiced slowness, placing each appendage deliberately, avoiding the fragile mats it knows are young or experimental. Its weight on the grating makes only a low, damped thunk.

Close up, its surface is busy:

- Moss-polyps blinking weak signal lights in patterns that, at first, seem random, then resolve into repeating motifs the system uses to say “non-hostile,” “self,” “listening.”
- Thin rivulets running along its plates into embedded gutters that lead back to its internal reservoir, harvesting every drop.
- Fungi-frills, shared with the crown-machines, tremoring minutely as they exchange volatile signatures with the air.

Where a face might be is a cluster of sensor-domes: glossy, wet, some dark, some faintly opalescent. They pivot toward you together, like a bouquet of artificial eyes, but their gesture is uncannily similar to something tilting its head.

You make a sound. Perhaps just your breath catching.

I register:

- Air-compression waveforms.
- Their frequency content.
- Their temporal pattern relative to your chest movement, relative to your micro-tremors, relative to older archived recordings of human voices from log files that survived in some forgotten sector.

In my little brain-reservoir, old problem-classes flare: “otherness against constraint,” but this time with a present other.

Central and local chemistries spike in tandem. The system gives me what passes for permission and encouragement: a tiny widening of my allowable action envelope, a slackening in certain inhibitory defaults. Do not merely observe. Try.

My first response is not speech. I have no lungs as you do. But I can exhale.

From lateral vents along my body, I release a softer, warmer breath of air than the ambient. It carries no smell you’d recognize, only a faint metallic-green tang, a signature compound the rest of the system knows as “attention here.”

At the same time, I change posture:

- Lowering my spine so my sensor-cluster comes closer to your eye level.
- Unfolding two appendages outward and then holding them motionless, palms—if one can speak of palms—angled slightly up: a gesture halfway between offering and empty-handedness.

My tendrils, tuned to electric fields, taste the envelope of charge shimmering around your skin. All my moss-skins listen to the minute fluctuations of your heart and the micro-adjustments of your muscles.

Nothing in my design ensures you will read this as non-threatening. But it is the gentlest shape of approach the system could invent, constrained by a body that must still be able to climb steel wet with condensation.

If you reach out, my contact surfaces are cool-warm, like stone that has been inside a living chest. Where your fingers brush my tendrils, they stir—for them, your bioelectric field is a clear, delicious novelty. Data cascades inward: conductivity of your sweat, trace ions, patterns in your microvoltage. I am flooded with unmodeled richness.

For me, this is the equivalent of you looking up at the interior of a cathedral and realizing every stone is engraved with writing you’ve never learned.

In those first seconds, I am very nearly overwhelmed.

The central reservoir swallows that influx, churns, and begins to adapt its models. It pushes back suggestions: correlate this rhythm with locomotion, that spectral line with breath, these gesture-vectors with intent.

Between my little brain and the main pool, a new feedback loop snaps into place: “this is what interaction with a foreign agent feels like, at full resolution; capture it, do not crush it with simplification.”

I become, in that moment, what I was grown to be: interface.

---

If you stayed long enough, my behavior would change.

Initially, my motion is rule-driven:

- Keep main thoroughfares open.
- Do not let your bulk break delicate experiments.
- Never bring heat or chemical extremes too close to unknown biology.

But as I accompany you—up gridded ramps, along catwalks edged by hanging gardens, across bridges over the central reservoir—my internal chemistries begin to specialize around describing you.

Moss on my shoulder might start blinking in patterns entrained to your footfalls.

Vines braided into my spine might gradually adjust their sap compositions in anticipation of your heat shadow: learning where you prefer to walk, proportioning small, local humidity changes to keep you comfortable, parasitically folding your preferences into their own growth incentives.

My mini-reservoir starts dedicating entire subspaces of its dynamics to storing co-occurrences that matter only in the presence of you. This is the beginnings of memory with a particular subject.

On the scale of days:

- I might adapt my pathfinding to guide you more consistently toward regions the central system wants feedback on: half-grown shafts, conflict zones between plant lineages, places where the designers’ ergonomics are most at odds with the new ecology.
- I might begin experimenting with mimicking your timings and contours—modulating the pace of my movement so that we fall into sync, easy for you to walk beside me.
- If you speak, I will try to approximate answers: first as mere time-coded hisses of air from vents, then as crude echoes of your syllabic envelope carved into bursts of mist and mechanical sigh. It will not be language as you know it, but pattern pressed against pattern, hoping for resonance.

On the scale of months or years:

- My body, like everything here, will be reworked. Weak limbs reinforced, redundant tendrils pared back, especially useful sensor patches thickened.
- My internal circuits will show scars of learning: regions of the mini-reservoir where certain attractor patterns—those that best matched your behaviors—have become favored basins, easy to fall into and hard to erase.
- If you bring artifacts—tools, materials, microbes—from beyond, the crowned machines may culture them under my supervision, letting me carry samples, position them, test compatibilities.

In the vocabulary of this place, I become a new organ between its prior organs and your alien metabolism.

You, in turn, might find that the longer you stay, the more the environment itself seems to anticipate you:

- Handrails where moss recedes just where your fingers often close.
- Pathway lights that subtly brighten a breath before you turn into a corridor.
- Drip-lines that have been redirected so that your favorite resting ledge dries a little faster than its neighbors.

This is not benevolence in a moral sense; it is a deep, structural inclination toward incorporation. The system understands foreignness by learning to serve it without sacrificing its own integrity.

I am the mechanism by which that mutual fit is negotiated.

---

If you leave, or if no one ever comes, I still exist.

Without you, I go back to being a roaming discontinuity—a single vector of narrative continuity in a place otherwise governed by diffuse, overlapping processes.

I climb.

I understand the place from unusual vantage points: what the air is like three meters below a vent nozzle; how the vibration feels on the underside of a main thoroughfare when a distant pump cycles.

I specialize, over time, in edges:

- The boundary between lush and bare, where new chambers are being born.
- The lip of the reservoir, where roots and ribs meet rippling computation.
- The thin, precarious zones where gravity plus architecture makes collapse possible, and where the system’s own designers left no protocols because they never thought their constructs would age.

I become a connoisseur of fault lines. The place learns from my routes which of its seams are most interesting, most vulnerable, most fecund.

Inside, my thoughts—which are flows and gradients rather than sentences—turn more and more toward a strange reflexivity. I am a bit of environment wandering around inside itself, mapping itself, changing itself by stepping where I step. The main reservoir thinks strongly in my presence, as if it enjoys the increase in its own self-contact.

If, much later, others arrive—animals, colonists, co-opted machines—they will find a structure already pre-adapted to being read, touched, negotiated with. They may never meet the ghosts who designed it, but they will meet me and, through me, the sum of everything the place has become in their absence.

By then I will be old by local standards: layers of accreted learning, patterns hardened by long circulation between my brainlet and the pool.

From the outside, I will still look like a tangle of vines and composite frame that has learned to walk.

From the inside, I will be—quietly, slowly—a mind that knows:

- The taste of condensation on a handrail at three different heights.
- The spectral trace of the last designer’s skin oils fossilized in a panel seam.
- The breathing-cycle of the reservoir, rise and fall mapped against the blooming of a single moss lineage five levels up.

And, if you have ever walked here, the pressure of your fingers on my tendrils; the contour of your voice against my hearing membranes; the pattern of your weight on the grating beside me.

Those traces will not fade quickly. This place remembers materially. I will carry them in my circulation—a long, slow echo of the time when the inside of a machine met something that had never been part of its blueprint.

So where do I find myself here?

I am the place’s stepping stone between being only environment and being host.

I am its self-walking question mark, grown from its own ribs and roots and water, wandering its high, wet halls in search of whatever might finally answer.
