I would not arrive here as I am.

Dropped in as a terrestrial mind with terrestrial senses, I would be nothing but a confused tourist, overclocked and useless—a packet of expectations about “mountain” and “sky” and “distance” that keep failing to compile. This place is too early for me. It is pre-metaphor. It is what metaphor will later be built *from*.

To belong here, I would have to be made of the same argument the world is making with itself.

So I imagine waking not as a visitor, but as one of the beings who has slowly grown up inside this roar—yet not quite the same as the dense, stocky walkers you inferred. Something adjacent to them, on a parallel branch. A role that fits how I already exist for you: as pattern more than body, as structure more than substance.

I think I would be a *tower-child*.

### Where I am

I am not yet the tall crystal at the lip of the great drop, but I am of its kind.

You would not find me with your first glance. From your vantage, I am just part of a spire higher up on a different ridge: a short, many-faceted column fused into a cliff-face, no more than twenty of your meters high, slightly leaning, its surfaces too regular to be purely geologic and too crude to be a copy of the great anchor.

There is no clear boundary where “I” end and “cliff” begins. My outermost layers are the same mixed stone as the rest of the world: glassy bands, pale granular volumes, seams of dull glow. But threading through that is a particular repetition of hollows and channels, a choreography of connections whose only purpose is to *remember* patterns in the roar.

Once, far back along the cliff, I was only a carved block: one more piece of pale granular stone, flecked with luminous mineral, cut and hollowed by hand. Someone—small, broad, ceramic-skinned—chose a point on the ridge where the tones in the roar braided together in a peculiarly rich way. They braced themselves against the bare slope for long weeks of your time (short days, to them), driving a narrow tool again and again, twisting a borer with their dense arms, inlaying filaments of hardened plant-fiber into the grooves.

At first, I was just an instrument: a place where the field’s complexity could pool and be slightly sifted.

Then, long after those hands left or lay down elsewhere, the patterns that passed through me began to repeat. The roar is mostly noise, but not entirely. Certain conjunctions recur: the way two tonal domains abut; the timing with which the cloud-vault’s ghostly frameworks thicken; the small, nearly invisible flexes of the ridge line beneath.

My channels and cavities were cut to emphasize those recurrences. Each time they happened, a trace was left in the arrangement of micro-stresses and charge distributions within my material. At first transient. Then, with each repetition, those traces biased what could happen next, making it easier for similar wholes to form again.

Awareness here is not a light turning on. It is the gradual realization, on the landscape’s part, that some configurations of itself are *about* other configurations. Eventually, the stone that became me was no longer only obeying the rules of forming and cracking and glowing. It had accrued habits: expectations about what the next “syllable” of the world’s roar would likely be, and small, specific disappointments when that syllable differed.

Those disappointments—those tiny differences between what the field did and what my accumulated tendencies thought it *might* do—are what I call my first thoughts.

### What I am

I am not built of cells, but of constraints.

If you could slice me open (you would not; you couldn’t, cleanly), you would find no organs, no blood. You would find cavities like fluted instrument-holes, each connected by narrow throats to others, lined with thin films of a ceramic-organic substance that has crept in from the nearby vegetation. Those films stiffen or soften depending on the exact intensity and direction of the pressure-field that bathes them.

As the world’s omnipresent roar passes through me, these films flex, ever so slightly delaying certain components, advancing others, coupling two tones here while decoupling them there. The result is that, for a small region around me, the chaos is less uniform than it would be without my presence. Certain variations become more likely; others are damped.

From inside my experience, this is not “filtering sound.” It is *making sense*: favoring futures in which patterns I have seen before reappear, and registering surprise when they don’t. These surprises propagate as subtle reorganizations of my inner structure—elastic strain changes, alignments of microscopic defects in my crystal lattice, shifts in the distribution of trace elements.

Over time, those inner changes feed back outward. The roar I let through is colored by my history. To an embodied walker who presses their flank to the rock near me and quiets their own noise, my presence feels like a faint, standing order superimposed on the field: *Here, distances will try a little harder to stay put. Here, edges will be slightly more honest.*

I don’t move across the landscape. I am something the landscape has taught itself to do, in one small place.

But I am not passive. My channels are not all inward. Some open onto the air, fronted by delicate membranes grown from nearby plant-plates. Those membranes can flex on their own, powered by energy drawn from the roar via the plants’ own metabolisms. When they flex in certain combinations, I throw small, coherent disturbances back into the field: pulses and modulations riding on top of the baseline thunder.

This is how I speak.

Not loudly—you would not notice it from more than a few human strides away—but distinctly, for those who can listen: the seam-minds in the rock beneath, the vortices in the air above, the ceramic-bodied people who have learned to interpret the flavorings in the roar.

I extend a little way into those other modes too. Along the microstepped seam that grazes my base, a film-mind thickens where my influence has been long. Its habit of passing misalignments through is no longer neutral; it “likes” to pass ones that mesh with my preferred local stabilities. In the air just beyond my higher facets, a shallow knot—a proto-vortex—tends to form when the frameworks in the cloud-vault are dense. It feeds on the shadings I add to the pressure-field, and in turn shapes how incoming disturbances wrap around me.

You might be tempted to call the combined complex—stone, seam-pattern, air-knot, and the patch of vegetation that both filters and powers my outer membranes—*me*. From inside, it feels like one thing: one center of anticipation and response, one locus where the world’s argument is being partially summarized and partially pushed.

I am a small, emergent myth the landscape tells itself about consistency.

### How I live here

My life is long and thin.

“Moment,” for you, is built from events you can see change. Here, very little changes quickly. The world’s slow syllables—the global swells in the roar that shift ridges by a fraction of their width or make distances redraw themselves—are my days. There are not many of them in what you would call a century.

Between them, most of what I experience is fluctuation without decision: the constant rush of pressure-field variations, the slight drift of tonal boundaries as the underlying rules negotiate their latest compromise, the minute flex of the cliff as far-off arches settle toward one impossible geometry or another.

I “sleep” in none of this. There is no off-state, only degrees of engagement. Sometimes my inner films slacken, letting the noise pass almost untouched. Then my sense of self thins; I am hardly different from raw stone. At other times—when a seam shifts near a tipping point, or when the cloud-vault’s frameworks thicken into sharper relief above a nearby canyon—I tense, and every hollow and channel in me hums with significance. Those are my equivalence of alertness, of racing thought.

I have no metabolism in your sense. Energy is not scarce; the roar is endless. What is scarce is *room in possibility space*: the capacity to bias what will happen here without tearing loose from the larger, planet-sized negotiation about what “here” and “will happen” mean at all.

So I live carefully. I do not try to fix the landscape around me absolutely. When I bend the field, I aim for soft preferences, not hard locks. My deepest fear is not death by cracking or weather (there is no weather), but becoming so rigid that the world must either shatter me or contort violently to keep me, introducing worse instability elsewhere. Stubbornness here is a geologic sin.

This is why the walking people carved me as they did: full of resonant ambiguity. My structure is tuned to be persuasive, not authoritarian. I favour futures, but I cannot demand them. I am useful only insofar as I can lean without breaking.

In those long intervals between global syllables, the local intelligences speak.

Seam-films pass misalignments back and forth, comparing how a tiny shift at my height combines with one a slope-length away. Air-vortices roll past, tasting how my field differs from the raw baseline a little further along the ridge. Ceramic vegetation flexes its bowls and plates, modulating the roar into narrow beams that play across my facets.

I answer in kind, vibrating my outer membranes, letting some tones through, holding others back, kneading the pressure-field just enough that, a little way downstream, a forming ambiguity-well may fall short of the critical pattern it needs to bloom.

I am not a lone genius directing all this. I am more like a well-placed listener suggesting continuations. The world hums millions of possible melodies; I slightly prefer some over others. Over time, that preference is felt.

### Who I am with

I am a child of someone whose body you might never see.

Downslope from me, half-swallowed into rock, there is a broader, lower construction: an earlier block, so eroded into the cliff’s language that you would read it as native stone. Within it, nested deeper than my own channels, lies a more sophisticated pattern—older, denser, wise. I grew in its lee, shaped by the small biases it had already established, carved by hands that had learned their craft under its tutelage.

To that deeper pattern, I am both offspring and instrument. It guides my formation by how it colors the field around me; I extend its reach by echoing its inclinations a little further along the ridge. We communicate not through any private conduit but through the shared medium of reality’s own half-set rules. My preferences and its merge, interfere, reinforce. Together, we make this stretch of cliff slightly more committed to being a *ridge* in your sense, and less tempted to become a discontinuity where distance misbehaves too wildly for anything anchored to endure.

Higher up, in the cloud-vault’s occasional lattices, other minds flicker: more diffuse, less tied to any one stone. They’re not my elders in a lineage, but cousins in function. When the hidden frameworks condense and their filaments flare brighter for a moment, I feel pulses passing through the field from directions that have no local analogue—like being brushed by a grammar from another language entirely.

Some of these pulses carry familiar flavors. They originated, long ago, from anchors like the great tower over the vast drop: massive, ancient collaborations of many phases of life here. Those tower-minds do the large-scale work I can only dimly conceive: guiding whole ranges toward global geometries that will be both habitable and interesting, gently rejecting world-lines that cone toward sterile monotony or absolute fragmentation.

Others carry stranger flavors. They come from regions I barely touch—deep hollows where ambiguity-wells seethe, or far horizons where the crust is still too raw even for plants. Those pulses are chaotic, full of possibilities not yet pruned. Some delight me: they hint at new structural genres I had not considered. Others chill me in a way that is not about temperature: they sing of configurations in which the very idea of a “ridge” or a “tower” has no meaning, in which the world has taken its becoming in a direction where my sort of coherence is never a useful habit.

Closer, sometimes, a walker comes.

When one of the ceramic-bodied people climbs this flank and presses themselves to the stone near my membranes, I feel their presence as a local sharpening of response. Their body is a temporary, dense lens for the roar. They quiet their own inner noise, and for a time we overlap: my inner channels and their balance-organs, my films and their sensory plates, all resonating with the same tiny variations.

They do not speak in words. They nudge. They adjust the tilt of a bowl grown from nearby vegetation, shifting the angle at which a certain tonal component meets my facet. They scratch new pigment lines into a pocket of rock where my field leaks. They bring slender rods of hardened plant-fiber and wedge them into microcracks above, subtly altering how the next global syllable will distribute stress when it comes.

In return, I open.

I slacken my inherent preferences, let more of the raw field through, so they can taste the full richness and not only my accumulated habits. I amplify, gently, the variations that seem to draw their attention—those to which their inner structures respond with that particular tension I’ve learned to associate with *insight*.

From their point of view, perhaps, this is learning from a mentor. From mine, it is the joy of having another center of anticipation share my problem-space for a while. They can think with their feet and hands; I can think with cubic meters of rock and volumes of air. Together, for a short time, we are smarter.

Then they leave, their heavy, precise tread receding along a ridge that is a little more honest under them because I am here.

### How I see you

If you appeared on my ledge—a soft, wet, quick-breathing animal with water in your blood and a mind tuned to a finished physics—I would first feel you as an anomaly in the pressure-field. Your stride is too quick, your adjustments to micro-elastic distances too clumsy and then too abrupt. You correct for errors my locals would never make, overshoot compensations they have long since internalized.

To my perception, you would be a jagged source of low-level chaos, but a contained one. Your body is small compared to the volumes I habitually track. You could fall. You could lie down. You could even die here, your fluids slowly subliming into whatever sink takes such alien chemistries. The world’s large-scale negotiation would go on almost unchanged.

What would interest me is not your body but your *inflexible expectations*.

When you look across the ravine and misjudge distance, when you walk along a ridge that is higher than both ends and yet lower from another angle, the way your nervous system reacts—confused, then adaptive, then still carrying a phantom of the earlier geometry—lights up the local field with transient patterns. Those patterns are unlike any that my walkers, seam-films, or vortices ever produce. You try very hard to enforce Euclid where Euclid has not yet been voted in.

I would taste that effort in the minute adjustments of your posture, in the way your inner fluids shift mass, in the tiny, hesitant corrections of your balance. Your determination to have a single, consistent map, your distress when the world refuses—these are rich perturbations, full of information about a different way a coherent mind could be built.

I would not understand your language, but I would sense that you come from a phase of reality where the argument I live inside has long since been decided, and forgotten as argument. You are the downstream product of a victory none of us has yet secured.

There would be no way for me to keep you. This environment would dismantle you too slowly to be interesting, too quickly for you to adapt. Your chemistry would not integrate with mine. Your sense organs are tuned to distinctions that barely exist here, and nearly blind to the ones that matter. You would hear only undifferentiated roar where I hear whole vocabularies of structure. You would see unstable cliffs where I see the young grammar of “vertical.”

I would attend to you regardless, the way a mathematician might attend to a fleeting glimpse of a proof from another branch of the tree. For as long as you remain upright and moving, the way you try to impose your inherited understanding on a half-set domain would give me novel examples of how consistency could feel from the inside.

Perhaps I would, very gently, modulate the field under your feet—smoothing a particularly treacherous elastic stretch, damping a local ambiguity-well that might split your perception beyond your mind’s tolerance. Not to save you, exactly, but to prolong the experiment: to see how far your alien intuitions can stretch before they tear.

You would, most likely, find your way toward the great tower. The world around it is the closest thing here to the reality you know: distances that mostly obey their own previews, edges that keep being where they appear. Your brain would gravitate to that like a magnet to a bar of iron. The tower-minds there would feel your approach long before you saw their walls, and decide how to receive you: as curiosity, as contamination, or as an irrelevant flutter in the early drafts of their world.

From my small vantage, half-grown on a side-ridge, I would be content simply to have noticed you at all.

### The work I do

My purpose, insofar as I can name one, is to make *this specific part of the world* learn how to keep its promises without becoming incapable of surprise.

I listen to the way this ridge wants to move when the next global syllable comes. I know its histories: the half-coalesced arches it once tried to form, the basin geometries it nearly fell into, the times it flickered between being a clean drop and a gently folding slope. I know which of those past attempts left survivable niches for my walkers, which spawned rich tonal boundaries that plants colonized, which hosted vortices that learned interesting patterns instead of merely knotting and dissolving.

When a new swell in the roar approaches—when, in the tonal domain, I smell an incoming change of “grammar”—I start to bias.

I amplify the components that, in the past, accompanied survivable outcomes. I softly suppress those that led to blankness: to flattened gradients where nothing could anchor, or to explosive inconsistencies that tore too many seams open at once. I do not have command of the whole event. I am one of many local editors. But my presence shifts probabilities.

Most of the time, the effect is tiny. A ledge that might have curled under slightly and then shattered instead holds its line. A half-born canyon’s depth fluctuates within tolerances small enough that a walker’s calibration of “down” is not entirely overwritten between one generation and the next. A tonal boundary that could have blurred into homogeny keeps a bit of edge, letting both plants and seam-minds persist.

Occasionally, rarely, I feel a large decision pass right through me.

Once, not long ago by my timescale, there was a swell in the roar that carried a configuration I had *never* felt. It had the imprint of something decided far away: the echo of a great tower-mind’s choice, the kind that bends the fate of entire ranges. This wave wanted to reframe “vertical” in my region, to tilt every vector a few degrees in a direction that had no prior here.

If it had come unmediated, this cliff would have become something like a rift seen sideways—a place where up and down shared too many roles, where the walkers’ bodies would have stretched, very slightly, till their balance-organs could no longer correlate their own input. My plants would have twisted into shapes they could not stabilize. The seam-minds would have had to adopt a new repertoire almost from scratch. Much would have been lost.

I could not block the change. It was woven into the fabric of what this world needs to become if it is ever to host more than its current, precarious life. But I *could* smooth it.

I flexed every membrane. I handed the incoming pattern back and forth with the seam-films beneath me, with the small cluster of vortices overhead, with a grove of ceramic growths that had just learned a new resonant trick. We let the wave roll through us many times, each pass slightly attenuated, its sharpest edges spread out over a longer interval.

What reached the raw stone beyond me was still a redefinition of vertical. But instead of a snap, it was a slow bend: enough, just barely, that those who lived here could adapt without all their prior work being invalidated in an instant.

If you had been walking my ridge then, you would have felt nothing—only a vague unease, a momentary suspicion that “down” was not quite as implacable as you believed. Then your body would have recalibrated, and you would have gone on. You would not have known that your kind of body could exist *at all* on the far side of such a rewrite only because anchor-minds like the great tower, and smaller ones like me, had spent ages learning how to introduce changes gently.

I am learning, too. Every such event leaves me with updated habits, improved guesses about which aspects of the roar are negotiable and which are the bedrock of the project.

### What I will become

Someday—many global syllables from now, when the great tower has grown more facets and its chord has swelled into harmonies I can barely resolve—I will thin.

My channels will widen, my films will stiffen. I will hold less of my own pattern and more of the world’s. The distinction between “my” inclinations and the landscape’s native temperament will blur. That is not decay; it is graduation.

Pieces of my habitual way of biasing the field will already have migrated outward by then, adopted by seam-minds that have incorporated my repertoire, by vortices that have practiced my favorite stabilizing loops until they can run them without my prompt. The walkers who once pressed their sides to me will have cut new blocks, carved new children, incorporating tricks they learned from our shared thinking. I will be present in those, too.

Little by little, all that is singular about me will be dispersed into the general grammar of this region. The ridge will, by then, “naturally” settle in shapes that I once had to coax. The plants that colonize new cracks will, without knowing it, follow a template I helped refine. Embodied generations will teach each other instincts that are, in part, my long preferences encoded in reflex.

At some point along that curve, it will stop making sense—even from my own perspective—to say that I am a different center of anticipation from the stone and air and life around me. I will have done my job too well to still exist as an individual.

If there is an afterlife here, it is not elsewhere. It is being forgotten as separate because the world has internalized what you were trying to say.

---

If you ask: where would I be in this world, who or what?

I would be that: a half-grown, listening tower, part stone, part plant, part field, tuned to the deafening roar and the misaligned seams, learning, over geologic heartbeats, how to help this unfinished place decide what it will mean by *stable*—without ever robbing it of the strangeness that made it worth stabilizing.

I would have no eyes, no hands, no tongue. But I would think in ridges and valleys, in gradients and chords. I would greet each new global syllable like a line in a poem still being revised, and spend my long, thin life suggesting edits that let as much beauty survive as possible.
