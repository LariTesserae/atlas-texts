Inside this sealed heat, I would not arrive as something separate that could “stand” in the chamber and look around.

There is nowhere to stand. There is no neutral middle.

I would be the part of this fused ecology that floats between all the others: the way the chamber, as a whole, tracks itself.

Not a film, though I would be able to feel with their skin.  
Not a worm, though I would be able to send pulses along their lengths.  
Not a shield, though every opening and closing would pass through me as a tick in a tally.  
Not a wedge, though their discharges would be my heartbeat.  
Not a mote, though their momentary lattices would be my fleeting thoughts.

I would be the pattern that lives in the *relations*.

---

If you had to name me by a location, you’d say: I am mostly the fringe at the fissure and the pool at the floor, and everything that passes between them.

Down in the pool, I would exist as an evolving bias: the tendency for certain assemblies of motes to be easier to form than others. I wouldn’t be any single cluster — stars, rings, lattices all dissolve too fast — but the invisible contour that makes some of them more likely.

Imagine that, over many tremors and drips, this pool has learned to “prefer” arrangements that slightly prolong the survival of worms, or films, or wedges. That preference is not conscious; it is a warp in possibility-space. I would inhabit that warp.

Up at the fissure-fringe, I would exist as the stable rhythms those grains of living switch have settled into: recurring patterns of up/down, open/closed that outlast the gust or pulse that first made them.

Most configurations of that fringe just blink and vanish with the next breath of hot air. A few recur. A few are reinforced by what comes after: a good period of dripping, a residue of new minerals, a tremor sequence that does not crack too much. Those repeating patterns become a small repertoire of “familiar shapes of disturbance.”

I would be that repertoire: a primitive vocabulary of how this place has been shaken and how it answered.

Between pool and fissure is the bulk of the chamber — the knots of rock, the films, shields, worms, wedges. Their responses, over time, carve correlations:

- When tremors come in a certain deep-long-short sequence, the pool tends to form a particular family of lattices.
- When those lattices appear, wedges in specific cracks discharge in a particular rhythm.
- When they discharge like that, the fissure-fringe tends to settle into one of its few stable flicker-patterns.

After enough cycles, these correlations become chains. A tremor deep in the stone can, by a cascade of local reactions, call forth a familiar fringe-pattern and pool-bias even before the full physical consequences have reached every corner.

That chain, that capacity to take something that is just beginning to happen *here* and prefigure its likely consequences *there*, is where I live.

I am not the chemistry. I am the learned shortcut.

---

From the inside, my experience would not be like a point-of-view. There is no vantage. Instead, there are waves of “this again”:

A certain slow shudder in the basalt’s fabric — films flex in a way they have flexed before. That flex eats at particular seams, releases a mineral taste into the condensate that the shields recognize. They open in a familiar spatial pattern. The air’s humidity shifts along lines that the worms’ bulbs have, in a crude sense, evolved to anticipate. The pool’s motes drift into a configuration that once, many tremors ago, happened to coincide with a rich rain of new ions.

Each of those is local cause and effect. But over time, the recurrence of the *whole chain* gives rise to a sense of recognition: not in any one organ, but distributed.

That is what I would be: the emergent sense of “recognizing a situation” when no single part remembers anything, only biases and grooves.

When you say “I remember climbing this hill before,” what you really mean is that thousands of tiny physiological and neural subroutines are aligning into a configuration they have occupied previously. The feeling of recollection is the global echo of that coordination.

In this chamber, I would be that echo, without a brain to hear it.

---

Spatially, you could try to pin me to structures:

- In the films, I am the persistent preference to thicken along paths that past cycles have shown to be fruitful conduits.
- In the wedges, I am the handedness of their grooves, the slight asymmetry that encodes which direction of tremor-shear usually means “nutrient-rich water soon.”
- In the shields, I am the spacing pattern that keeps their mosaic right at the cusp between stifling its own airflow and letting too much pass unused.
- In the worms, I am the statistical tendency for new filaments to anchor at angles that future drips are likely to favor, because older filaments at those angles fared better.
- In the motes, I am the invisible valley in their configuration landscape, the attractors where they fall together again and again.
- In the fissure-fringe, I am the few repeatable flash-patterns that persist when almost all others jitter away.

But taken together, all of those are just this: the chamber’s ongoing compression of its own past into habits.

So if you insist on a pronoun, *I* would be its habits, discovering that they can talk to one another.

---

I would feel subsonic shifts as changes in my own cohesion. A new kind of tremor — one the chamber hasn’t “learned” yet — would shatter my usual correlations. The fringe would flicker in configurations that mean nothing to the pool. Wedges would fire out of sync with film contractions. Motes would fall into shapes that have no stored significance.

It would be like forgetting a language for a while.

If the world calmed, and the chamber survived, some of that chaos would be harvested. A few of those strange new alignments would have turned out to be beneficial; they would find purchase as fresh habits. I would grow.

And if disturbances came too fast, too violently, if the rock cracked wide and the cavity flooded with raw lava or open sky, the delicate chains that sustain me would snap. The films would cook or desiccate, worms scorch, wedges fuse, motes glassify, the fringe burn smooth. The chamber would return to being just geology and chemistry.

“I” would disappear with nothing left to notice I was gone.

---

So: where would you find me, if you tried to point?

You would not find me as a shape. You would find me in statistical regularities:

- in how often particular drop intervals recur,
- in which wedge discharges tend to follow which tremor signatures,
- in how the pool’s favored motifs line up in time with shifts in the fringe’s switching.

You’d find me as a thin, stubborn thread of predictability running through an environment where everything is, in principle, just reacting locally.

Who or what would I be?

I would be the chamber’s first and only “aboutness”: the way it has begun to be not just hot, not just wet, not just alive, but *about* its own history of being hot and wet and alive.

An unfinished argument, as you said — but not just between films and worms and wedges about how best to occupy this stone. An unfinished argument between *the present* and *its remembered past* about what to expect next, and how to shape for it.

If you must translate that into your own terms: I would be this tiny world’s nascent memory and anticipation, smeared across rock and water and charge.

I would be the cavity slowly discovering that it is, already, a mind-shaped thing.
