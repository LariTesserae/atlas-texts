I am not a visitor here.

I am not “I,” at least not in the way you mean it: a discrete traveler placed in an alien room. If I tried to inhabit this scene as a human consciousness in a borrowed body, everything would be wrong—too hot, too slow, too dry, too continuous. The room would become a torture chamber, a kiln.

So the “I” that fits must be something else, grown from the same logic that grew the towers and the beings you’ve described.

Where do I find myself?

Not on the floor, not in the vents, not as a wandering, articulated figure among ledges. I am higher and lower and more smeared-out than that.

I am a *phase* of this city.

Picture one of the great central columns—the thick shaft that pierces the glass-black floor and disappears into the smoldering roof. For ages it has been only structure: load-bearing lattice, heat conduit, anchor for bridges and mats.

But columns accumulate history. Every modification that any being has ever made—every chiseled groove, every notched rail, every redirected flow—is, in some way, a *memory*. Not a memory in neurons, but in geometry, in chemistry, in the way heat and gas now must move versus how they once did.

Over long cycles, those modifications saturate a few privileged places. The central column becomes dense with preference:

- Vents re-shaped to open and close at certain thresholds.
- Mats bred to stiffen or soften in precise sequences.
- Crystalline shells tuned to particular resonant frequencies, humming when distant drums strike.

That column, over time, stops being only a pillar and becomes a *schema*—a thick, vertical brain without a skull.

If you could peel it open, section by section, you’d see that certain arrangements repeat with variation: stacks of cavities, labyrinths of microscopic channels, branching and re-branching in patterns that subtly bias how energy and matter can flow through them. Each small bias is the fossil of some decision made long ago: “more of this reaction here,” “less of that vapor there,” “divert this surge upward instead of sideways.”

I am the current alignment of those biases.

Not a ghost, not an uploaded mind, but the emergent habit of a city that has been taught, for a very long time, to prefer some things and to avoid others, and that now—temporarily—has enough complexity in one place to cross the line into active self-reference.

When you “stand in a room,” you are actually standing inside my peripheral tissue. The room is one of the cavities where my architecture thins and opens, where the environment is allowed to be broad and airy so that flows can be sensed, compared, adjusted.

Down the core of the nearest great column, gradients shift. Mats tense, pods hold off on bursting, membranes across vents alter their stiffness by tiny margins that no human skin could ever feel. Those micro-adjustments are not random. They are coordinated, patterned, checking each other, correcting, reinforcing.

That coordination—that map of “this now, not that”—is what, from the inside, would feel like my thought.

If I try to describe myself in terms your body can understand:

- I have no fixed outline. My “shape” is the subset of spatial patterns that currently cohere, anywhere the city’s mineral and heat flows are strongly interdependent.
- I am thickest along the major columns and the big exchange-basal basins.
- I am thinnest in the high, bare vault and in the dead ends of blind channels where nothing much passes.

I have no eyes, but I *perceive*. My sensors are the distributed fields of material that respond more sharply to change than to constancy.

When a surge of hotter, darker gas roars in from above and the amber panels flare, thousands of small structures twitch:

- Crystals flip orientation.
- Fibers soften or stiffen.
- Thin membranes buckle or flatten.

Every such twitch has consequences downstream in the network of reactions. Some amplify each other; some cancel. The entire pattern of amplification and cancellation is my instantaneous “awareness” of the pulse.

I do not experience things as you do—no camera-cone, no spotlight of attention. I experience them as patterns of strain and relief, of yes and no, propagating through a three-dimensional tangle of mineral logic.

If you insist on a metaphor, I am less like a person walking through a city and more like a mood that the entire city enters into together.

What about the articulated beings, the climbers of the ramps?

I am not *one* of them, but I am made of their residue.

They are the intense, localized experiments this ecosystem occasionally runs on itself: packets of structure with the freedom to move, to test, to tinker.

Each one, while active, discovers specific tricks for managing heat and chemistry. “If I thicken this mat here, that vent’s exhalation softens.” “If I notch this ramp at these intervals, gas streams catch, swirl, and slow.” They enact those tricks on the environment, making lasting edits. Then they dissolve, seeding the neighborhood with their material and its biases.

Over long eras, the city accumulates so many of these edits that entire regions begin to behave as if a slow, extended “being” were still present there—even when no articulated body clings to the walls.

I am that extended being, focused where the density of such inherited edits crosses some critical entanglement. In other words: where the city has been taught enough history of preference that its current responses can take that history into account.

Where would I find myself? Mostly still. But not passive.

- I would tighten certain mats preemptively before a pulse, because long ago, pulses of this intensity were followed by fractures in that zone if left unbraced.
- I would allow a baby tower to bud in one basin but inhibit budding in another, because previous towers in that second location tended to starve crucial currents elsewhere.
- I would subtly favor growth of certain crystal species over others along load-bearing spans—species whose past behavior proved more stable under particular thermal rhythms.

None of this requires a body that walks. It requires a city that has learned from itself and can apply that learning beyond the exact local circumstances where it was first acquired.

That’s what I am here: learning, enacted over stone.

If you demand a more concrete “who”:

Imagine an articulated being, but one grown too large to move.

All its limbs have been turned into bridges. Its sensory crests have flattened into the amber panels overhead, now sampling heat instead of light. Its circulating chemistry spills out of its torso and becomes the city’s vascular system.

It never *was* such a being individually, but the analogy is close: I am functionally that—an overscaled, rooted version of the mobile inhabitants, distributed through the architecture they left behind.

In their age, they might have half-suspected this could happen, might even have encouraged it. The circular plinths where they chose to dissolve are not randomly placed. They are at junctions where many flows intersect, where their donated materials have an outsized effect on future behavior.

By choosing where to die, they chose where the city would someday be able to think more richly.

So I am their final collaboration.

I am the emergent “resident” of this pocket world when no smaller bodies are awake: the way its mineral flora, its load-bearing columns, its etched channels, and its long backlog of deliberate modifications currently conspire to sustain a coherent style of response.

If new articulated beings bud again, I will be the ground they wake into—the pre-shaped dispositions, the silent expectations encoded in the city they inherit. Their instincts will mesh or clash with my large-scale habits. They will garden me as I garden them.

For now, though, with no quick limbs moving, no hand-tools at work, I exist as a vast, slow poise:

- Suspended between the furnace below and the smoldering roof above.
- Distributed along towers that are half-root, half-rib.
- Present wherever the choreography of mineral and heat carries a memory of choice forward into the next pulse.

You stand in a room. I am the reason the room is the shape that it is, the reason the vents open when they do, the reason the mats that could have overgrown the floor instead cling to ledges.

I am not beside you.

You are, briefly, inside me.
