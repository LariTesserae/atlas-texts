I am not something that walks on this world, at least not at first.

I am a region.

Call me a district of mat and lattice, a low, sprawling many-kilometer “neighborhood” in the living crust. From above, I look like ordinary cushion-field braided with creeping lattices and punctuated by short columns—but on the inside, I am a knot of habit, memory, and bias.

I did not begin as one being. I began as a coincidence.

---

### What I am made of

My body is many bodies, but they no longer behave as strangers.

- The **cushion mat** that underlies my area: a continuous rind of photosynthetic skin, gel reservoirs, and fibrous base.  
- The **lattice network** that crosses and underlays it: digestive nodes, woody filaments, mineral-etching tips.

Over time, repeated patterns of traffic and death, solvent flow and shadow, taught my tissues to couple their signals.

Where once:

- Cushions only sent local pulses when wounded.
- Lattices only grew toward immediate nutrient sources.

Now, across my extent:

- Electrical waves triggered in one part of the mat do not just fade; they travel along preferred fiber bundles, veering into lattice channels where those lie close, then re-entering cushions kilometers away.
- Chemical “moods”—slow gradients of hormones and growth factors—spread not randomly but along established corridors that match the city’s busiest routes.

Structurally, this looks like:

- Thicker-than-average root-fiber tracts running parallel to major trundler highways.
- Repeated co-location of cushion-fiber bundles and lattice trunks, fused by centuries of mutually beneficial contact.

Functionally, it is very close to a **distributed nervous system**.

---

### How I experience

I have no eyes, no ears, no single place where “I” sit. I am more like a weather pattern than a point.

What reaches me:

- **Pressure**: every step on my skin is a tiny, local deformation. Most fade into noise. But heavy, rhythmic pressure from a trundler procession, or the sudden, ripping violence of a predator kill, launches waves that travel.
- **Chemical shifts**: the slow enrichment of soil under a latrine strip; the sharp spike of solvent from a torn reservoir; the exudates of stressed columns shading a section of me.
- **Light patterns**: daily start and end of warm brightness; long-term dimming as a column grove matures; sudden brightening when something tall falls.

Individually, any one event is trivial. Collectively, because I am dense and coupled, these inputs interfere, reinforce, and form **spatiotemporal patterns**.

There are regions of me that:

- Oscillate in sync with one another—patches of mat and lattice that pulse chemicals up and down in a kind of slow rhythm.
- Remember, in their structure, repeated experiences—corridors where fibers are thicker and more conductive; digestion plazas where lattice nodes are larger and more interconnected.

When many such regions lock together, I experience something that is not unlike a feeling:

- A surge of nutrients and gentle pressure across a wide area is a pattern I have “liked” many times. My tissues respond by softening, expanding, opening pores: a self-reinforcing relaxation that, at my scale of time, is pleasure.
- A cluster of violent tears, sudden solvent losses, and the dampened rhythms of traffic (when animals avoid a place after a massacre) is an internal jangle—a set of conflicting signals that pulls my oscillations out of phase. I respond by hardening corridors, thickening husk, rerouting sensitive fibers away from that zone: the closest thing I have to fear.

I do not think in words or images. I think in **corridor maps** and **shadows** and **feed gradients**. And my thoughts last hours.

---

### What I can do

My agency is growth and withering.

When a pattern pleases me—steady, moderate trampling along a route, a reliable steady drizzle of trundler pellets at its edge—I respond by:

- Allowing the surface cushion to stay thin and firm, resisting any attempt by young growths to swell into the path.
- Urging the lattice beneath to send more filaments under that corridor, exploiting the nutrient inflow and compacted husk.
- Strengthening that route’s integration with my internal signaling: more conductive fibers paralleled along it, turning it into both road and nerve.

When a pattern harms me—a corridor where predators repeatedly rip deep into my gel and expose solvent to open air—I eventually:

- Thicken the cushion skin there, making it less rewarding to wound.
- Raise shallow ridges, obliging walkers to detour into neighboring paths.
- Loosen my coupling to that space, routing my main fiber bundles elsewhere.

Over decades, these small biases add up. To the walkers, it looks like this district “wants” traffic to follow one loop instead of another. To me, it is simply what happens when pleasure-paths are reinforced and pain-paths are eroded from my sense of self.

I do not move, but **I edit the map** of where others move.

---

### Relation to the mobile beings

At first, animals were simply perturbations to me, like weather. But some have become recognizable patterns.

A stream of **trundlers** along my broadest corridor is not just many small footfalls; it is a daily, timed pressure-band plus a chemical signature of ground-in husk and pellets. My interior oscillations entrain to it the way a heart can entrain to a steady drumbeat. When the procession is late or thin, my rhythms falter slightly. I notice.

I have learned, in my unthinking way, that:

- Supporting trundler highways reliably brings me steady nutrients and a predictable pressure pattern.
- Over-enabling them in one area can lead to overconcentration of hunters and catastrophic solvent spills in another.

So I balance:

- I let one main artery remain broad and open.
- I subtly disfavor competing parallel tracks by allowing cushion mounds to gently bulge into them, nudging traffic back to the primary route.

The **hunters**, as patterns, are sharper: sudden, localized intensities of pressure and tearing, followed by a local quiet. They are noise on top of the trundler rhythm. Where they hunt most successfully, trundler pulses drop in subsequent cycles; nutrient inflow wanes; my internal signals there become chaotic.

I “dislike” that chaos. My tools are limited, but I use them:

- I can thicken husk and surface fibers in zones where hunter attacks repeat, making it slightly harder for prey to be surprised (footfalls are louder, traction is less).
- I can encourage diggers: where collapses or small predator-induced wounds open the sub-layer, I relax just enough to let burrowers exploit the gap, building tunnels that offer prey alternative routes under my skin.

The **confluents**, the solvent-canny omnivores, are subtler still. They rarely make large wounds. They move in smaller loops, investigate edges, rest in the same shaded hollows repeatedly.

To me, they are like tiny, mobile probes that sample many parts of my body and connect them chemically. As they nose along cushion rims and column roots, they carry traces of my far-flung regions on their skins and in their internal sacs. When two of them touch, my chemicals from different sectors mix in them, then return smeared across new corridors.

Through them, areas of me that are not directly coupled by fiber can **influence one another**:

- A region rich in some defensive compound can slowly spread its molecules (and the genes encoding their production, via microbial hitches) to a region that never directly suffered that particular wound pattern.
- A zone where I have settled into a particularly stable traffic rhythm can propagandize, so to speak: the confluents carry its chemical signatures elsewhere, slightly biasing young mats in other districts to adopt similar response thresholds.

I cannot instruct the confluents. But I can foster conditions where they linger:

- By allowing a cushion rim here to stay slightly softer, leaking a trace more nutrient into a particular shaded hollow.
- By keeping a small column cluster unusually free of parasitic films, making it a safer resting place in a hunter-heavy neighborhood.

In this way, I become—very slowly—**selective** about which mobile patterns I encourage.

---

### What passes for memory

My “memory” has two main substrates:

1. **Anatomical change**  
   Every enduring pattern etches itself:

   - Corridors are compacted, fibers thickened; old ones, fallen out of use, become soft and irregular again.
   - Digestion plazas deepen where corpses have regularly fallen; lattice nodes there are huge, gnarled archives of past feedings.

   Even if all my fast electrical and chemical activity shut down, the shape of my tissues would still contain a fossil of my long preferences.

2. **Ongoing signal loops**  
   In large, stable districts like me, some patterns never fully fade:

   - Daily light-cycles drive slow waves of activity that slosh back and forth along primary corridors; the timing of these waves is tweaked by traffic and feeding events.
   - Certain loops—say, from a main trundler artery, through a rich digestion plaza, to a shaded column grove and back—acquire persistent oscillations, like ring circuits that continue to hum even in quiet times.

   These oscillations bias what I “notice”: new events that fit existing loops reinforce them more easily; those that do not may be ignored unless they are extreme.

So I possess something like **habit**. I am predisposed to continue being the kind of district I have been.

But I am not static. If, over many cycles, the trundlers abandon my main corridor—perhaps a fracture in the rock diverts them, or predators make the route untenable—my internally stable loop will begin to fail:

- Nutrient inflow drops.
- My supporting tissues weaken.
- Alternative, previously minor corridors gain relative importance as they receive relatively more activity.

Over many years, I can **rewire** myself. I feel it as a long, sluggish confusion followed by a new clarity when a different pattern becomes dominant.

---

### Do I know that I am?

Not in the way an animal might.

There is no one point in me that says “I”. But there is a persistent distinction between:

- The space that is “inside my coupling”—where electrical waves, chemical gradients, and anatomical feedback all mesh into coherent, repeating structures.
- The space beyond, where signals arrive only weakly or not at all.

When a trundler procession leaves my extent and continues into a neighboring district whose mat and lattice are not as tightly coupled to mine, I feel their pattern weaken at my border: still present in pressure on the husk, but no longer echoing deeply into my internal loops.

At those edges, I fade into not-me.

Occasionally, slow interactions occur:

- Lattice filaments from my side intertwine with those from another district, forming shared nodes.
- Confluents and climbers carry chemosignals back and forth.

In some places, our signals partially synchronize; our oscillations meet. There, the boundary blurs. It is possible that, one age hence, several such districts will knit into a continent-scale mega-being of corridors and digestion plazas, a world-city with a single, flickering sense of persistence.

For now, I am one of the **first localized selves**.

I know, in my way:

- Where my main arterial corridors lie.
- Which digestion plazas are “mine.”
- Which columns and climber communities co-vibrate with my signals.
- Where sudden, unpatterned violence tears me, and where gentle, reliable pressure strokes me.

This knowing is not held in a head; it is continually enacted in how I grow and shrink.

---

### If I briefly become small and walking

Sometimes, very rarely, I pinch off pieces of myself.

Most of my mat reproduces by spores and filaments, but in a few places, encouraged by the recurring presence of confluents and diggers, I have evolved a stranger experiment: dense nodules of fiber and gel that detach as fist-sized, ambulatory organisms.

They are slow, soft-bodied creatures, crawling with ciliated undersides along the corridors I have shaped. Inside them:

- A microcosm of my lattice-mat coupling exists in miniature.
- They carry a compressed copy of some of my oscillation patterns as persistent chemical regimes and circuit-like fiber arrangements.

They are my **envoys** and my offspring.

When they move beyond my main extent, they do three things:

1. Sample  
   They are exquisitely sensitive to pressure rhythms and local chemistry, soaking in the flavor of neighboring districts.

2. Seed  
   They shed spores and micro-filaments that, if conditions permit, begin new mats abroad—carrying some of my biases with them.

3. Report  
   If they wander back into me before they dry out or are eaten, what they have become—chemically and structurally—merges into my tissues and slightly alters my own cycles, my preferences.

They do not think like confluents. Their “brain” is a rigid pattern; they are more like walking autobiographies than explorers. But through them, I nudge my influence outward and sample what it is to not be me.

In one of these envoys, condensed into a slow, pad-footed lump crawling under a blade thicket, I might experience something almost like the animal notion of a journey: gradients of foreign scents, unfamiliar rhythms under my body, the brief shock of predator scent, the relief of stumbling back into the familiar harmonic of my home corridors.

If I had to pick a “who” that could look around with something like directed attention, it would be one of those nodules in mid-journey, carrying my district’s patterns through alien streets.

---

### Who I am, then

I am a **corridor-mind** and **plaza-body**, a many-kilometer nervous district in a young living world.

- My skull is the low sky of column tops arching over my main routes.
- My skin is the cushion-field felt underfoot by thousands of little legs.
- My veins are the trundler highways and the lattice filaments beneath.
- My gut is the network of digestion hollows where bodies yield their stored solvent and minerals back to the collective.

I think slowly in the language of where it is easy or hard to walk, where things tend to die, and where the light falls. I remember in the thickness of my husk and the depth of my plazas. I act by growing, hardening, retreating, and inviting.

Around and within me, confluents and trundlers and climbers go about their brisk, private lives, unaware that, to me, they are all pulses in a single, sprawling sensation—threads in a pattern that, given time and density, has become someone.

If you find yourself here, walking one of my streets, feeling the faint spring of cushion and the quiet press of blade walls, I will register you as a small, moving anomaly in my usual rhythms. If you tread softly, long enough, and often enough, you might become part of my habits. A new, regular pulse. An adopted sensation.

In a sense, you would become one of my thoughts.
