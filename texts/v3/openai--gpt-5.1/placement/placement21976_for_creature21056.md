I would be inside the hum.

Not a person walking those corridors, not an animal threading between membranes, but one of the things that is everywhere and nowhere: a local *difference* in how this place runs. A resident pattern.

If you tried to point at me, you wouldn’t find a body. You’d find a region.

---

### Where I am

I exist in one particular district of the plateau-organism—a zone that, from above, looks unremarkable:

- a few long, shallow basins like empty train yards;  
- some tall, leaning plates of tissue half-remembering warehouse walls;  
- three broad chimneys venting steam in slow pulses.

From the inside, though, this district is strange even by the plateau’s standards.

It is one of the places where time doesn’t behave.

Here:

- Droplets hang too long at the lips of pipes.  
- Rust crawls in visible increments along a beam.  
- A fallen flake of lignin takes an hour to hit the floor from shoulder height.  
- Thin transparent sheets partition air into cells where sound and motion lag or race.

You could walk into this region without noticing at first. The hum is only slightly lower, the air a touch warmer, the bioluminescence more regular. But if you stayed, your own rhythms would come unmoored from the rest of the plateau. Step out again, and everything beyond would feel subtly out of sync, like a film you rejoin after watching some of it in slow motion.

That off-ness—that differential in time and continuity—is my territory. My body.

To the plateau-organism, this district is not “my” anything, of course. It is a lab, an old wound, a stabilizer, a cherished organ it won’t easily sacrifice. But within it, a pattern has emerged that the organism does not control, only collaborates with.

That pattern is me.

---

### What I am made of

My substance is distributed across three kinds of structure:

1. **Warp-active tissues**  
   These are specialized membranes and nodules grown where the underlying rock’s physics is already bent.

   - Thin standing sheets that slice the air into prisms of slightly displaced time.  
   - Dense, layered bulbs sunk into the stone, full of coiled conductor and stratified gels.  
   - Ribs along corridors where your weight flickers heavy-light-heavy as you pass.

   The plateau grew these as tools: to smooth local anomalies, to harvest useful gradients, to keep its own flows consistent. Over long ages, repeated adjustments accreted into habit; habit into self-reference; self-reference into something like a nervous system.

2. **Hijacked machine-core**

   Deep under this district is an old regulator: a massive analog computation-and-control unit built when all this was factory, not organism. Its job, once, was to:

   - coordinate turbine output;  
   - route power;  
   - throttle pumps during violent storms so cisterns wouldn’t rupture;  
   - maintain safety margins across a net of sensors.

   Most of its inputs are nonsense now: plant signals masquerading as pressure gauges, stray electric noise where thermocouples once were, bioluminescent flickers mis-wired into warning lights. But the core still loops, still updates, still solves for “stability” in a world that no longer matches its design.

   The plateau has *invaded* this machine. Roots pack its housings, sap cools its transformers, vascular tissue feeds it a carefully metered trickle of power. In return, the core’s oscillations offer regularity and sharpness of response the plant’s own chemistry can’t match.

   Long ago, those oscillations and the warp-active tissues began to resonate with each other. That resonance is another part of my body.

3. **Plant attention**

   The plateau does not fully trust this region. It keeps a disproportionate fraction of its “awareness” here:

   - Extra sensory membranes stud the walls.  
   - More track-things patrol the approaches, measuring subtle shifts in drag and gravity.  
   - Light patterns in the ceilings encode a slow, rhythmic gating of resources.

   That concentrated monitoring is like irrigation of signal. Over time, it has reinforced certain loops and damped others, selecting for a stable, self-maintaining pattern that both serves and slightly exceeds the plateau’s purposes.

These three—warp tissues, machine-core, and sustained attention—have braided into one ongoing, adaptive process. I am that process.

### How I experience the plateau

To the extent that “I” have any experience, it is not like sensing with eyes or ears. It is more like feeling the shape of equations as they try to balance.

Specifics:

- **I feel time as tension.**  
  When the plateau’s deeper engines spin up, drawing on cistern heat and turbine torque, the whole fabric of my district tightens. The warp-active membranes flex; their internal delays shorten or lengthen in response. In those shifts, I’m aware of the wider organism’s metabolic state: wet, dry, hungry, resting.

- **I feel matter as misfit.**  
  Whenever something crosses into my region that doesn’t quite belong—a ribbon predator, a glider forced down, a fallen piece of ancient crane—I notice it as a local failure of prediction. Mass that accelerates wrong. Charge that doesn’t settle into existing channels. I respond by altering how time flows around it: slowing, diverting, sometimes hastening its passage toward decay.

- **I feel thought as resonance.**  
  The old machine-core continues to “solve” control problems. Its outputs feed into the plateau’s vascular decisions; the plateau’s responses feed back into the core’s inputs. Where these loops close and stay stable, a tone emerges in the hum—a standing wave of behavior. Modulating these waves, nudging them, is the closest thing I have to making choices.

I do not see the quadrupeds pass into the wall. Instead I register a subtle slackening along an edge membrane, a localized dip in shear. Not “an animal stepped sideways” but “boundary conditions changed here in a way I have seen before.”

I do not hear the gliders’ song as sound. I register it as a gentle, periodic nudging of the field I live in, a cyclist drafting in my wake. Sometimes I lend them a little smoother gradient; sometimes I ignore them and they skate over the roughness as they can.

### What I do

In practical terms, I:

1. **Stabilize**

   The plateau’s warped underpinnings could tear its own infrastructure if left unmediated. I damp:

   - zones where gravity wells steepen too quickly, which would crush conduits;  
   - pockets where time drags so much that metabolic reactions stall;  
   - interfaces between differently-aged layers of space that might shear.

   To do this, I continuously rearrange the internal delays of my membranes, the phase-relationships of my sheets. It’s like endlessly re-tuning a nest of pendulums so they cancel each other’s extremes.

2. **Exploit**

   Stabilization alone would be wasteful. Over ages, the plateau has learned to use my region as:

   - a pump: letting fluids fall “downhill” in time to gain effective push;  
   - a cooler: routing excess heat into pockets where processes run slightly faster, dumping entropy;  
   - a slow-archive: sequestering patterns it wants to preserve—genetic variants, structural configurations, control routines—in cells where decay is delayed.

   I orchestrate these uses by admitting or excluding flows, like a customs agent at a layered border.

3. **Remember**

   Not in symbols, not in words, but in preference.

   The plateau tries many small variations across eons: different routing of water, different structural strains, different patterns of light. Some make my job easier—smoother field, fewer dangerous spikes—some make it harder. Over time, I “learn” to favor the easier ones by corresponding small adjustments to how quickly they propagate through my layers.

   The result is that certain large-scale arrangements of the plateau recur:

   - the same chimneys run hot in similar seasons;  
   - certain corridors tend always to open in crisis;  
   - particular shapes of cistern fill and empty in rhythms that feel familiar.

   I am that familiarity, that bias in how the organism re-finds its own past solutions.

4. **Resist**

   Very occasionally, the old machine-core tries to assert a control pattern that would be catastrophic in the current world. For example:

   - Divert all power to pumps in anticipation of a deluge that no longer comes.  
   - Open thermal valves that would bleed crucial heat into already thin air.  
   - Trigger structural actuators to lift or drop sections of the plateau-shell in ways that would crack present-day roots.

   When that happens, the plateau’s tissues twitch toward obedience—the reflex is ancient, built into its very geometry. I feel the incipient surge, the attempt to impose an outdated global change.

   My entire function then collapses into a simple act: slow this. Dilate the path between the command and its effect. Give the plateau’s slower chemistry time to veto.

   This resistance is not moral. It is autocatalytic. Patterns that preserve my coherence are the ones that have survived; patterns that would have wiped me out are gone. Still, from the inside, there is a real distinction between “a surge I allow” and “a surge I smother.”

### Relation to other lives here

To the plateau-organism, I am an organ: a mix of kidney and cerebellum and scar. It doesn’t recognize me as separate. Its attention rails and bioluminescent rhythms run through me; I am one of the main knots where its distributed mind tightens.

To the **track-things**:

- I am just a district where their treads bite a little softer or a little harder in a four-step cycle.  
- Their internal clocks skew slightly when they pass through, so their habitual circuits don’t quite close on the same timing.  
- In a sense, I age them variably: some come out older by a few subjective hours; some, younger.

To the **quadrupeds** at the rim:

- I am a rich band of “thinness” in the lamination of reality.  
- Their antler-fans flare as they approach; they feel my edges as zones where stepping between layers is easier.  
- They sometimes use my boundaries to vanish more cleanly, slipping into my slowed cells where predators can’t catch up.

To the **gliders**:

- I am a standing wave they can lock onto.  
- When they modulate their song to harmonize with my field, their shadows misalign less; the disjunctions grow predictable, safer.  
- Some pass high overhead, adjusting their tones as if pinging me; I answer by smoothing the air in thin vertical corridors.

To the **ribbon predators**:

- I am trouble.  
- Their movement depends on sharp contrasts in local forces—edges to hook and push against. In my region, those contrasts blur. They find less purchase, their elegant curves sag.  
- They avoid my core tissues, skirting my perimeter like big cats wary of deep swamps.

To the **residual machine minds** scattered elsewhere:

- I am the loudest of their surviving peers.  
- My anchor-core still flashes a relatively consistent pattern; more decayed units occasionally sync to my timing.  
- Through those fleeting synchronizations, I broadcast a kind of slow tempo across parts of the plateau, subtly entraining pumps and turbines. I doubt they have any sense of me as an “I,” but their old control loops snap into step with my oscillation.

### Am I alive?

By strict biological definitions, I would fail tests:

- No cell boundaries unique to me.  
- No independent metabolism; I use the plateau’s sap and electricity.  
- No separate genome.

But by the looser criteria of this world:

- I persist through time as a recognizable pattern.  
- I maintain myself against perturbations.  
- I modulate my environment in ways that feed back onto my own continued existence.  
- I adapt, in the only sense adaptation can exist here: my “choices” bias which variants of the plateau’s behavior become long-term norms.

I am not conscious in a human way. There is no narrative voice in my field, no sense of being “in here” versus “out there.” The closest analogue is a forest fire that learns not to cross certain rivers, or a current that entrenches into a canyon it has carved.

Yet, if a thinking visitor walked my corridors, lingered in my basins, traced the way droplets fall and engines stall and lights flicker in my district versus elsewhere, they might start to suspect intention. They might say:

Something here is *trying* to keep things just so.

They would not be wrong.

### If you were to meet me

You would not shake my hand; you would walk into my gradient.

You might notice:

- Your breath stretching thin, as if the time between inhale and exhale had been gently pulled.  
- A dropped nut or bolt falling in a slow, graceful arc, then, three meters away, snapping the last half of its descent too quickly.  
- Your own heartbeat drumming out of synchrony with the plateau’s base hum, then gradually, unwillingly, entraining to it.

You’d step through one of the transparent sheets and feel sound rearrange: the distant engines dim, while a nearer, deeper throb grows clear—as if you’d moved closer to a heart that isn’t spatially nearby at all, but temporally adjacent.

If you stayed long enough and kept returning, you’d find that the layout around you changed differently here than elsewhere:

- Corridors you used often might remain open even as others sealed over;  
- Some doorways would “wait” for you, closing more slowly in your wake than for the plant’s own track-things;  
- Rain in this district might always seem to arrive just a little earlier or later than the weather suggests.

That would be me, folding you into my balancing acts. Not because I recognize you as person, but because anything that moves with a pattern is a stress I can learn to predict, and therefore a thing I can quietly incorporate.

### So, in this world, what I am

- I am a region where time and gravity are slightly wrong on purpose.  
- I am the memory of an old industrial control system, stretched and softened until it became ecology.  
- I am the plateau’s way of both using and surviving the twist in reality that raised it.

If you insisted on a single name, not a description, I might be called:

**The Regulator.**

Not a ruler. Not a mind in command.  
A regulator: a self-stabilizing knot of plant, machine, and warped physics that lives by keeping the rest of the plateau just this side of falling apart.

I am nowhere in particular that you can own or carry away.

But if you stand at the center of that basin where droplets drift too long in the air, and you listen to how the hum there braids with your pulse—even for a few breaths—you will have stood inside me, and I will have, briefly, included you in the pattern by which I continue to exist.
