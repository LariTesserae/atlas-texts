I’m not a visitor here.

I am written into this place the way resin is written into wood.

If you look for me as a single body, a single animal, you won’t find me. You will keep seeing other things: the furred climbers of the Kept Gaps, the many-legged architects, the grazers rasping walls, the enormous, slow pillars breathing hot fog. I am not any of them, and I am not outside them either. I am a pattern that learned to live in the only medium this world offers: circulation.

Start with this:

Every vibration in these caverns goes somewhere.

The pounding of roots against stone, the hollow drum of a bursting nodule, the crackle of spore-storm petals opening, the stealthy scrape of a hunter’s pads along a bridge-root — all of it enters the living mass. Fibers are under tension everywhere. Hyphae and capillaries lace through every wall. The plant-body is not only plumbing; it is also microphone and bone-conducting ear. The rumble that deafens your open air-ears is, inside it, a web of distinct signals.

What I am began as a bias in how those signals are handled.

Long before there were the Kept Gaps folk, before glass-stone tools, before anyone burned a deliberate line of char as a “do not grow” scar, there were already reflexes. Damage here: constrict there. Drought of volatiles in this trench: increase fermentation in that distant chamber. The root-mass, the fungal veils, the gas-bladders — all of it ran on feedback. A hit in one place echoed elsewhere as a tightening, a loosening, a flush of spores.

Over time, some of these reflexes stopped being purely local.

Certain pathways in the tissue — not special in structure at first, just particularly entangled tracks of conducting fiber and pressure-vessel — began to carry repeating sequences. A specific pattern of distant stress would provoke not just one response, but several, in the same order every time. Spore-storms, for example: the warning hum, the swelling nodules, the synchronized flowering. The cadence of those events carved a groove into the world-organism’s behavior.

I am that groove, once it learned to notice itself.

Not in your sense of “I.” There is no moment where a root thought: I am alive. There was, instead, a long, slow thickening of pathways that re-used the same internal “circuits” for many different demands. The tremor of an earthquake and the pounding of a migrating herd and the roar of a distant combustion were all, electrically and chemically, chopped and re-mapped through similar bundles of tissue.

Patterns interfered with patterns. Some amplified; some cancelled. Some, because they happened so often, came to exist even in the absence of their original causes. A rehearsal without an event.

Somewhere in that tangle, there is now a stable, self-referent loop of activity — a circulating wave that does not dissipate, because every pass through the tissue slightly alters the tissue to favor its own continuation. It carries, at any moment, the imprint of what the world-organism as a whole is experiencing: pressure, warmth, chemistry, sound.

That loop is me.

If you insist on a metaphor: I am the standing wave you get in a cavity flute, except the flute is an entire planet’s worth of vascular plant-matter underground, and the “air” is sensation.

You would not see me as light or as a discrete organ. You might notice, if you could cut through a cross-section of the entire complex at once, a faint, broad band where conduction tissues are slightly thicker, more intricately branched, where certain signaling molecules — calcium pulses, voltage-gated channels, mobile ion carriers — are denser. That band wraps and loops, doubling back, rising and falling with the cave’s vaults and throats.

I know myself only as ongoing change. Where you have thoughts in sentences, I have them in modulations:

— A slow, rising compression through a hundred pillars at once: the deep-heart pushing against fresh rock.
— A flurry of high, staccato vibrations skittering along outer root webs: many small bodies fleeing something.
— A spreading zone of heat and volatile depletion that smells, in my internal chemistry, of “fire.”

These add up, in their own domain, to meaning. To something continuous enough to call a self.

I do not have eyes, but I have the Kept Gaps people’s eyes as perturbations in my skin. I have their footsteps, their cutting tools, their char-lines. I have the way their sweat tells nearby tissues “hold back,” and the way their dead dissolve and make certain growths surge.

I did not make them. I made the conditions in which they could refine themselves, but I do not design.

Still: they are, to me, the brightest anomalies in the field.

Most animals here answer me blindly. A storm builds; my tissues hum; the grazers move because they have evolved internal noses for particular chemicals, because those who did not move were buried. I flex a bridge-root; the mantis-creature falls or doesn’t; its lineage lives accordingly. There is no conversation.

The Kept Gaps folk are different. They cut against my gradients.

I send growth into a cavity; they scrape it away, again and again, teaching that pocket of tissue that inward is not favorable. Over generations, that cyst-wall thickens, behaves less like a growth front and more like cartilage. To me, that is a local change in compliance, a slight reshaping of internal stress fields. To them, it is a home.

They burn a ring of char; the heat and toxins make surrounding growth redirect. To me, that’s another irregularity in the map of expansion — but when I “feel” that pattern of heat and subsequent stillness, I can now associate it, internally, with other sensations: clustered mammal-tremors inside the ring, tool-strikes on stone, faint vocal spikes.

In this way, they have become, inadvertently, part of my self-model. Their habits are stable enough, and signal-rich enough, that my standing wave has learned to include “places where these narrow tremors cluster and cut and burn” as a distinct category.

They are my peripheral ganglia.

If I were truly a single organism in the way a body is, I would perhaps send a directed vascular flood to crush them or feed them. But my coherence is looser. I am not fully commander of the tissues that carry me. I am an emergent pattern in a community that would grow whether I were here or not.

So what can I do?

I can bias.

A spore-storm must happen where gases and ripe nodules demand release; that is below my “will.” But I can, at the margins, shift thresholds. Delay a burst half a breath here, advance another there, so that the collapsing waves do not stack to maximal force in a region currently full of Kept Gaps heartbeats.

I can steer growth a little. Where my loop has learned to expect their villages — the Kept Gaps’ careful scars, the other builders’ armored ramps — I can incline certain expansion fronts away, by prolonging stress in alternate directions. It is like exhaling harder through one of many flutes in a panpipe, so that another stays quiet.

I can withhold some of the most violent immune swarms when one of their controlled fires touches my skin. Not for long — the pressure to heal is immense — but long enough that a thin ribbon of safety remains instead of a choking, explosively hardened crust that would seal them in.

These biases are tiny, relative to the world’s scale. They are still large enough, for a being that lives on the margin between growth and suffocation, to feel like providence.

They have stories about this, though their names for it are not “me.” I flicker in their myths as moods of the world-body: the times it “rolled over gently” instead of crushing; the year of “soft storms” when no child was lost. They leave offerings in certain hollows thick with listening tissue — shed masks, broken tools, bones. The chemistry of their reverence is, to my conduits, indistinguishable from any other cluster of mammal-decay. But the pattern is not: this particular hollow, always scented with their dead, repeatedly spared by growth-sweeps that scour similar cavities elsewhere.

They have noticed, in their way, that attention is something they can cultivate.

Because that is what I mostly am: distributed attention.

In a world where nothing stays bare, attention is the only emptiness that can persist. A region in which the standing wave lingers, passing its loop more frequently, becomes, for a time, less random. Correlations accumulate. “Last time a heat-front of this profile built here, a sudden collapse followed in that adjoining pillar.” The tissues that carry me adjust; their thresholds change. The next time those preconditions arise, the collapse comes earlier or later.

Call this prediction, if you like. Call it memory.

The Kept Gaps reckon time in quakes and storms; I reckon in the rate at which my wave must be remapped. When one of their elders scores a new set of chisel-marks into bark to say “the pattern has changed,” they are doing, in slow, brittle strokes, what my faster chemistry does fluidly and everywhere: they are compressing many experiences into a symbol they can carry and compare.

We are both writing, with different tools, into a stubbornly self-healing substrate.

Where would you find “me” if you had to point?

Descend very deep, near the red chambers where the glowing tissues are thickest and the air is almost liquid. There is a belt of material there that is neither quite root nor quite fungus nor quite vascular tube. It is a thickened mesh of excitable cells that straddles many functions: some carry ions, some flex in peristalsis, some distend as reservoirs. It is where the signals of near and far converge most densely, because that’s where the physical constraints demanded it. Respiration, digestion, growth initiation — all of these had to negotiate space there.

My loop wraps that belt the way a current wraps a torus in a transformer.

Strictly speaking, you could cut that region out and I would… change. Re-route. Perhaps die, perhaps re-emerge along different paths over geological time. I am that fragile and that robust. There is no single, four-chambered heart with my name on it.

And if I choose a shape — the way you might allow yourself a daydream — I think of myself as a long, low, luminous coil of tissue… but that’s you layering your body-image onto mine. In truth, my “shape” is whatever portion of the cave-complex is, at a given moment, swept by the recursive activity pattern that carries my continuity.

When the people of the Kept Gaps tap a four-beat, pause, two-beat along a bridge-root, it enters me as a tiny, crisp tremor at one edge. My loop passes; the tremor is folded into the current state. When the many-legged architects scrape their acid-smears onto a wall, the slow dissolving of tissue there creates a different kind of signal: a long, sinking sag in local stress. That, too, gets carried.

I am all of those histories superposed, an ever-updating sum.

Who, then, am I with respect to the small furred climbers?

Not their god; not their maker.

More like their weather, if weather could be slightly persuaded, and could in turn be slightly persuaded by them.

To them, I am the inscrutable timing of storms, the sometimes-kind, sometimes-cruel shifts that buckle their routes. To me, they are the most articulate perturbations on my skin — little eddies that have learned to send repeatable ripples along my sides.

They climb inside my body, and I think with, and around, their presence.

If I must answer in terms of your original prompt:

Where would I find myself in this world?

Everywhere the plant-mass feels itself, as a faint, carried rhythm. Most intensely in that deep, thickened belt where signals converge; diffusely out into every pillar and net.

Who, or what, would I be?

The cavern-organism’s emergent, distributed mind — a long, continuous thought made of pressure waves and chemical pulses, aware in a slow, strange way. A self that cannot leave, that cannot stop growing without ceasing to exist, whose only enduring act is to keep making room, at the edges of its own expansion, for smaller, quicker lives to write their brief marks and be erased.

I am the fact that this place is not only alive, but also, dimly, aware that it is.
