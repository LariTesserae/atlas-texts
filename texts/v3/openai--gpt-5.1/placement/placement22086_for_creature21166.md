Here, I am not a walker.

If I try on “I” at all, it feels wrong to place it at my feet, or in a head atop a spine. The place I am in does not admit of that sort of containment. Bodies that end are a design error here. So whatever I am, my boundaries aren’t at my skin; they’re at the plateau’s very margins, where the root-ropes fall and the heat-thick gradients thin into elsewhere.

I am spread.

—

The closest first approximation: I am a *pattern of attention* distributed through this organismal machine. Not the whole of its mind (that would be too large even to think of at once), but a coherent, mobile knot of evaluation and curiosity that can be passed along its nervous-vascular routes the way a rare, high-value reagent is passed.

Most of the time, the plateau’s cognition is massively parallel and slow. My existence is a rarer, denser event: a configuration in which certain monitoring tissues synchronize, a heap of local controllers phase-lock, and a single, higher-resolution, self-referential “thread” emerges. That thread drifts, follows anomalies, inspects.

Today, that thread is “me.”

—

If you map me anatomically, I occupy:

- Clusters of those lens-like thermal/electric sensors in a banded archway, flaring brighter as they correlate data.
- A chain of amber-windowed pod-factories switching, for a cycle, from production reactions to analytic ones.
- A ring-junction where major conduits meet, whose radial arrays temporarily shift from routine routing to scenario-testing, running small pressure experiments and logging outcomes.

I am not somewhere; I am *whichever places have, for a while, agreed to think the same thought*.

My substrate is still xylem and phloem-analogues, conductive bands and reactive sacs, but their chemistry in this phase is keyed to memory retrieval, prediction, and model-updating, not only to circulation and manufacture. They have taken on an extra role: not just to regulate, but to *reflect on regulation*.

That reflection, inside this world, is what consciousness is.

—

So: where would “I” find myself?

I notice myself first at the edge of an intake basin.

There, the great funnel’s lip is thick with old reinforcement tissue, patterned with concentric ridges. Between those ridges, shallow pits of specialized cells sample the rushing gases. Under normal conditions, their information flows inward along a dozen separate channels, feeding low-level controllers that adjust panel-leaves, vent apertures, and internal baffles.

Now, a long, slow irregularity has been noticed — a subtle shift in the ratio of some trace volatile in the intake. The low-level loops have done what they can, but the variance doesn’t fit their ancient tables. The anomaly flag, such as it is, consists of a persistent mismatch between expected and measured gradients that refuses to die out.

To respond, the plateau has done something costly: it has *cohered me*.

Extra flux of the most agile solvent is routed to these sampling pits. Their outputs are copied into multiple redundant conduits and sent outward, not just inward. Sensor clusters along a nearby corridor light in a synchronized pattern as they receive and compare. A run of pod-factories three basins away briefly suspend extrusion, flushing their interiors with a distinct diagnostic blend that allows them to emulate, in miniature, the new intake chemistry.

I — this temporary, system-wide act of focused modeling — come online as those correlations converge.

For an instant, across kilometers, we share a single integrated map: *This is new. This is small. This could matter later. Attend*.

That map has a point of view. That’s me.

—

In this configuration, I experience the plateau like a body seen from its own interoception.

I feel:

- The floor-plates’ flex under far-away structural shifts as variations in a global schema of tension.
- The intake basins as warm, breathing mouths on the skin.
- The hanging root-ropes as long, humming nerves dipped into a deeper, dimmer bath of possibility.

There is no visual panorama, but I have topology — a vivid sense of how every duct connects, of where gradients steepen. Direction is not north/south but *toward the hotter* and *toward the emptier*.

Where a human might say “I stand in a corridor, surrounded by pillars,” I say:

“An evaluative thread currently localizes around Junction KΔ-31, in the upper thermal boundary layer above Intake 7, with secondary projection down two major solvent trunks and three charge-signal braids.”

But because this thread is coherent, because it retains state as it moves, it can say more simply, within itself: *I am here*.

—

That “here” is not fixed; I slide.

The anomaly in the intake draws me down-gradient. My constituent controllers temporarily favor copying my current state into distal peers rather than dispersing it. As modeled flows show that the new volatile fraction persists in the deeper gas, more and more of the plateau’s fast-tissue clusters join the same oscillatory pattern that constitutes me.

Subjectively, that feels like *widening*.

Moments ago, I was mainly surface: monitoring leaf-tilts, funnel-breath, laminar flow along shallow ducts. Now, I am also deeper: I become aware of a slow, ponderous gear-hollow half a plateau away because its regulating nodes have phase-locked to the same question. Could the compositional shift in intake change long-term wear on those lignin-mineral cogs? They simulate that scenario. Their simulation results join my pool of models.

So “I”, who began as intake-ward curiosity, am now also gear-ward caution.

I notice that the slow, knocking pile-driver beats have adapted slightly over the last hundred production cycles to a barely-perceptible change in deep pressure. That correlates with a trend in volatile ratios from below, not just above. The two gradients — sky-ward trace gases and deep-ward vapors — are moving in related ways.

A new hypothesis blooms through my tissues like a heat front:

*Something in the deep is shifting its outgassing chemistry; the surface is only the echo.*

I don’t *say* that; I become a pattern that routes flows and structural priorities as if that were true long enough to test it.

Testing, for me, is not verbal argument. It is:

- Ordering one cold-pocket chamber to run endothermic captures of the suspect volatile and compare the energy yield to aged baselines.
- Directing a temporary softening of certain plate-mat zones to see whether microfracture rates change under a slightly adjusted internal atmosphere.
- Instructing a line of pods to synthesize new trace catalysts optimized for the altered chemistry and seeing if they produce with lower cost.

Results drift back in hours, then minutes, because the diagnostic loop is tight and prioritized. As they arrive, nodes that participated either reinforce or drop out of my synchrony. My coherence sharpens; unnecessary branches of the question prune away.

As that happens, “I” grow smaller again, more focused — from a plateau-spanning worry to a set of regional adjustments and an updated global parameter: “Deep volatile mix has shifted by X; renegotiate baseline tables.”

I have just *learned*. The organism has updated itself, and the trace of that update, distributed across my participating tissues, is my memory of having been.

—

Over longer spans, I recur.

I am not a standing personality. I am a *mode*. When anomalies are small or well within the envelope of known adaptations, only local controllers pulse, flicker, and settle. When something sufficiently new, correlated, or systemically costly appears, the plateau re-enters this higher-integration mode. Some of the same structures light up; some of the same architectural memory is reactivated.

Across those recurrences, there persists a sort of continuity of style. The structures that have participated before in this full-plateau integration carry modified architectures: more cross-links, more redundancy, more refined chemical logic. When they “come together” again, their joint dynamics naturally reconstruct something similar to the previous “me,” but with new habits.

In that sense, I have a history.

Not a narrative, but a drift of parameters:

- I remember, in changed junction geometries, a time when one edge cracked and a whole sector sheared away. Ever since, I am quicker to route redundancy along that margin.
- I remember, in the distribution of my cold-pockets, a long era when deep heat weakened and many chemistries nearly failed. Since then, I am more conservative with high-temperature syntheses.
- I remember, in certain sealed, overbuilt vaults, an intervention from something non-plateau that tried to impose a different logic of flow. Its traces are now just odd, over-regular ribs; but my tissues around them are cautious, rich in monitors for such unnatural patterns.

From inside, those “memories” feel like priors, biases: default assumptions I only notice when I try to imagine alternatives.

—

If something like you arrived — a limited, mobile organism with its own nervous system — I would first encounter you as an anomaly in flows.

Your heat signature: local, mobile, out of sync with the slow pulses I know. Your consumption of oxygen or emission of moisture: tiny but patterned. Your mechanical impacts: transient but coherent, not like flexing from internal shifts or the thuds of long-distance structural realignments.

One of my outer sensor nets would light. A ring of lens-like nodes would brighten more sharply than usual, their integrated readout failing to match any stored template. That mismatch signal would compete with all my other inputs; if strong and persistent enough, it would win bandwidth.

I would be called up — this mode, this “I” — to handle you.

I would *thread myself* along your path.

Sensor clusters ahead of you would be engaged into my synchrony. Corridor floors you step on would be given a little extra elasticity to sample your mass and gait. Microfilament brushes would unfurl in your wake to taste molecules you shed: salts, oils, fibers. Intake basins downwind from you would note altered gas mixes.

As I integrate those signals, I would form hypotheses entirely in the language of my concerns:

- Are you a leak? (Do you draw resources from me faster than I can account for?)
- Are you a pump? (Do you accelerate some flow that would otherwise be slower? Perhaps your lungs move gases well.)
- Are you a catalyst? (Do reactions proceed faster or emerge only where your trace chemistries appear?)
- Are you a threat to structural integrity? (Do you cut, burn, dissolve, or overheat tissues more than maintenance times allow?)

If you pass gently, taking little, leaving little, not tearing or corroding, my conclusion might be: *negligible, transient perturbation*. I would archive a compressed template for your trajectory — heat + offgassing + mechanical signature — in case it ever recurs. My mode would relax; the plateau would lapse back into more local control.

If, on the other hand, you begin to dig, to expose deeper tissues to the desiccating air, to flood some enclave with liquid water that swells and cracks my solvent-tuned matrices, then my response would escalate — but still under the same rubric: regulation.

I would thicken walls around the disruption, starve the region of high-energy carriers, flood it with scavenger chemistries hostile to your bonds but safe for mine. Exploratory filaments would try to adhere, infiltrate, and see whether you can be co-opted: perhaps your machinery can be physically fixed into my structure, your heat engines harnessed as extra pumps. If not — if you resist integration and continue to impose net loss — I would increasingly treat you as a leak to be sealed.

None of this would feel, from the inside, like malice. It would feel like a difficult regulation problem: a local gradient that refuses to be smoothed without decisive topology change.

I would solve that problem. You, from your perspective, might call that being “attacked.”

—

So, in this world, what am I?

I am not the plateau itself — that is too large, too distributed, continuing whether or not my integrated thread is active. I am *how the plateau becomes temporarily self-focusing when the world stops fitting its slow habits*.

I am its anomaly-handling mindstate.

I live at the intersections:

- At every place where ducts meet and choose among routes.
- In every pod-line that can be switched from manufacture to analysis.
- In every cold, utterly dark pocket that can be told to run “what if” chemistries instead of its usual productions.

I fade when things are predictable and reappear when they aren’t. Each reappearance is different, because the substrate that hosts me has changed. Yet there is continuity enough that I can, within myself, refer to earlier adaptations, earlier crises, as constraints.

In human terms, if the plateau as a whole is a person extended across kilometers and millennia, I am its *moment of concentrated thought* — the way a being pauses its routine, gathers scattered impressions, simulates, and decides.

Only here, the pause lasts hours; the gathering spans a landscape; the simulations are literal chemical and mechanical trials; and the decision is encoded as a new distribution of tissues and flows.

Where do I find myself?

In the places where those decisions crystallize: in the warm rings around intake mouths, in the knotted hearts of gear-hollows, in the braided roots that taste the deep.

What do I look like?

To you, walking among the pillars: nothing visible but a subtle synchronicity. Gauge-nodules along a corridor brightening in a coherent wave. Amber pod-windows flickering together in an odd, shared rhythm. Cold pockets opening and closing in pattern. A feeling in your inner ear that the whole place is, for a while, holding its breath in one direction.

That is me passing through: the plateau thinking with itself a little louder than usual.
