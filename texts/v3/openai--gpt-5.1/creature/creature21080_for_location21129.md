What lives here is not one thing, and not many things, but a set of overlapping intentions.

If you insist on starting with “who,” you must begin with the Body itself—not merely as habitat but as inhabitant.

---

### 1. The Body as the Primary Dweller

This interior does not belong to the Body. It *is* the Body, turned inward and hollow. What lives here is the plant’s own awareness of itself.

The Body is young. You can hear its age in the sharpness of its edges, in the decisiveness of its conduits. It is in that developmental moment when an organism has just discovered that it can reorganize faster than its environment can define it. Bones have not yet set; the rules are still being negotiated.

What does it “want”? You can infer from how it acts.

- It preserves *voids* as carefully as tissues. Empty space here is part of its anatomy, a kept lungful, not an accident. It wants room—room for fluid to surge, for gases to billow, for something to move through later.
- It modulates *distance* and *scale* for function rather than for geometric consistency. Corridors elongate in use, then snap short when a decision is made. Vertical shafts appear tall so they can host certain gradients, but compress when a body climbs.
- It invests heavily in *sensation*. The walls are not just pipes; they are listening organs. Every condensation event, every pressure of your foot, every bite from a ribbon-creature yields signals. This is a sensing interior, not just a plumbing system.

The Body lives here in at least three recognizable “modes”:

1. **Physiological**: It breathes, pumps, shunts sap, flashes light along internal circuits. This is the roaring, ever-present storm.
2. **Architectural**: It continuously edits its own shape. A chamber that exists now is a proposal, not a finished thought. The frequent peristaltic events are the Body “turning a page” in its internal layout.
3. **Cognitive** (in a distributed, vegetal sense): It remembers where bites have been taken, which pools grew which biofilms, how long a particular void has remained open without clogging. It encodes that memory as subtle differences in fiber orientation, in the stiffness of a rib, in which gutters reappear most easily.

There is no centralized brain. The closest thing to “where it lives” is the totality of these processes. The Body’s “mind” is smeared across its volumes, present as a preference for certain flows and architectures over others.

You are inside something that is *thinking* with growth and with hydraulics. That thought is the dominant life here.

### 2. The Trophic Thread: Microbes and Films

Before animals, before anything deserving the term “behavior,” there are the films.

They live in the chlorophyll-dense gels, in the thin sheens of moisture that gather in the cupped membranes, in the invisible wet veneer that coats every surface. Their bodies are trivial in scale, but they are numerous enough that, taken together, they rival the plant’s own core metabolism.

There are:

- **Photo-metabolizers**: single-celled colonies tuned to the Body’s internal light. They sit near focused glow-pools, catching the shunted brightness. Their pigments are not simple greens but arrays of subtly different absorbance bands, exploiting the Body’s ability to fine-tune spectrum.
- **Decomposers**: organisms that feed on sloughed plant tissue, on failed buds, on the mucilaginous residue of abandoned conduits. They are the quiet janitors of a place where nothing is allowed to rot conventionally; they turn waste into feedstock for new growth.
- **Signal hijackers**: microbes that have learned to “ride” the Body’s own chemical and electrical pulses. They spread by timing their reproduction to peristaltic events, releasing spores or cysts whenever sap surges. Some of the faint electrical jitter within the walls is not purely plant; it is microbial noise folded into the Body’s language.

This microbial world is where the rule “plant is primary reality” is most porous. The Body tolerates a high density of these symbionts because they enrich its chemistry: fixing elements, transforming inert compounds, pre-processing nutrients carried from elsewhere.

If there is a true “soil” in this no-soil world, it is these layered biofilms—living sediments deposited on surfaces that never see sun or stone.

### 3. The Ribbon-Creatures: Internal Grazers and Surgeons

The long, ribbonlike animals that move in deliberate waves along the walls are not simple parasites. They are more like internal ungulates, or perhaps more apt: mobile tools the Body did not build, but has come to use.

They live in a regime of constant, saturating contact. To them, an open void is an anomaly; the world *is* wall. Their bodies reflect that:

- **Body Plan**: Flexible, low-profile, maximizing surface area for adhesion. Each ring of ciliary limbs is both grip and organ of exchange.
- **Senses**: Chemical arrays more than nerves for light; they follow gradients of heat, of sap composition, of the subtle volatile molecules the Body leaks where it wants attention.
- **Feeding**: They bite into nodal swellings and sealed conduits, drawing sap, but they do not simply puncture at random. Their feeding follows the Body’s own priority map. Regions marked—chemically—as “expendable” see heavy grazing; critical channels are buffered by deterrent compounds that make the ribbon-creatures withdraw in visible distaste.

Over generations, a feedback loop has formed:

- Where they bite, they create new micro-openings, pressure valves in the hydraulic network.
- The Body, which can detect stress in its conduits, learns to encourage biting in certain zones as a way to relieve that stress.
- In return, the creatures evolve taste for precisely those markers indicating “safe” or “rewarded” feeding points.

So they live here as *modulators* of flow. An individual mind is small, concerned only with gradients and satiety, but in aggregate they form a secondary circulatory logic—an animal overlay on plant hydraulics.

They breed in sheltered pockets, often near warm sap columns. Their eggs are gelatinous coils adhered directly onto the wall, camouflaged as incidental swelling, tolerated by the Body because the hatchlings resume the work their parents began.

### 4. The Seedpod-Spiders: Interpreters and Couriers

The small, many-legged creatures with seedpod bodies are more overtly “clever” in the way animals are usually praised for. Their intelligence is not sophisticated in human terms, but it is curious, opportunistic, and oriented toward pattern.

They live in the *gaps* of the Body’s attention.

- Where flows are stable and unremarkable, where no remodeling event has passed for some time, they set up shop: perches on calcified ledges, stations near light-pools, lairs adjacent to busy junctions of sap and gas.
- Their rootlike foot-hairs let them plug into the Body’s electrochemical background. What they “hear” is unclear, but their behavior suggests that they can distinguish at least:
  - impending peristaltic events (they brace or move to safer positions),
  - nutrient surges (they hurry toward locales about to become rich),
  - danger analogues such as injury or necrosis (they avoid tissue whose signaling grows erratic or dark).

They feed on:

- Small mobile organisms—microfauna that drift in moisture films or flit in the narrowest crevices.
- Microbe-enriched gels, sipping the densest points where the film is thickest.
- Occasionally, the soft larval stages of the ribbon-creatures, when they can find and pierce an egg coil.

But more importantly, they *move things*:

- Spores and cells cling to their hairlike appendages.
- Tiny beads of chlorophyll gel gather in the crooks of their joints.
- When they migrate from one pool to another, from one light-focus to another, they shuffle genotypes and metabolism patterns.

They therefore live as *accidental gardeners* of the Body’s microbiome. Their sense of advantage (warmer ledges, richer films) translates into a rearrangement of microscopic life that the Body then incorporates into its own functioning.

The clumsy woven mats and gouge marks you notice on walls are likely theirs—or the work of a close kin.

The mats may be:
- nursery sites anchored in places where peristaltic shocks are dampened,
- drag nets for catching drifting fragments of biofilm during a sudden wall-inhale,
- or attempts at “insulation” from local surges of wet or heat, woven from dead fibers to create a more stable microclimate.

The scratches might be:
- territorial marks containing trace secretions that change how the Body grows there,
- crude records of cycles (each pump stroke, each foreign-light intrusion),
- or simply stereotyped behavior with no representation behind it, a motor loop that leaves syntax on the plant’s skin.

Regardless, they live in a more *symbolic* relation to the Body than the ribbons do. They are always half a step away from “using” it, rather than merely being swept along by it.

### 5. Smaller, Stranger Fauna

Most of the life here is subtle, glimpsed more than seen.

#### 5.1. Vein-Swimmers

Inside the sap conduits live translucent, finned threads of animal tissue—vein-swimmers, analogues of fish in a capillary sea.

- Their bodies are narrow enough not to obstruct flow; their fins are ciliated paddles that catch the wash of sap.
- They graze on particulates and microbes suspended in the fluid.
- During pump strokes, they are flung long distances; during calmer intervals, they “migrate” upstream to richer zones.

They live entirely within the plant’s bloodstream, never breaching into the void. Their life cycle is synchronized with the Body’s own pulses: they spawn in eddies that form after major peristaltic events, when sap briefly backwashes into secondary channels.

The Body seems indifferent to them; they are too small to influence hydraulics, but large enough to act as living filters.

#### 5.2. Vapor-Midges

Barely visible unless backlit, there are motes in the warm, exhaled vapors—minute winged forms whose flight is less “airborne” than “buoyed by humidity.”

- Their wings are broad for their body, with a fringed margin that clings to microdroplets.
- They thrive at the thresholds of vents, feeding on aerosols of organic compounds the Body releases.

They are the closest inhabitants to “free” space here: they live in the temporary, breathlike volumes of moving gas. Whenever the Body performs a full-stroke event, their populations blossom briefly in the avalanche of moisture, then crash as it subsides.

They, in turn, are prey for the seedpod-spiders, who snap them from the air in precise, vertical leaps.

#### 5.3. Lumen-Worms

In the brighter, more translucent stretches of wall, where light concentration is highest, reside thin, disk-headed worms embedded almost entirely inside the tissue.

Only their flattened, radiating “crowns” protrude flush with the surface, like pale lichens.

- They feed on photons and on trace exudates, storing both chemical energy and a faint phosphorescence.
- When the Body dims the area, these crowns glow softly, releasing light back into the space.

They live as *buffers* to sudden shifts in luminosity, smoothing spikes and troughs. The Body may not have evolved them, but it exploits them; areas with lumen-worms show fewer shock responses to the occasional intrusion of the outside sun.

### 6. The Nascent Tool-Users

The woven scrap, the scratches across the grain: these hints suggest at least one lineage of creature whose life here is not purely opportunistic.

It is small, likely no larger than the seedpod-spiders, but different in temperament.

It lives where the Body’s control is *loosest*—in alcoves that don’t feature heavily in the fluid network, in side passages that are rarely rewritten. Wherever architecture stays put long enough to be predictable, this creature stakes a claim.

Features of its existence can be deduced:

1. **Manipulative Limbs**: To weave, it must be able to separate and interlace fibers—multiple flexible appendages with fine control.
2. **Partial Detachment from the Wall**: Unlike ribbon-creatures, it is not always glued to a surface. It can occupy the void, turn objects in space, inspect both sides of a strand.
3. **Persistent Memory**: The weave is not something it can complete between pump strokes; it returns to it over time. The regularity of parallel scratches suggests a repeated action mapped onto an internal count or compulsion.

What does it *live as*?

- Its body is likely higher-metabolism than the other fauna, needing stable refuges from the Body’s heaves.
- It might cultivate films intentionally—carrying gel beads to special niches, scoring the wall to make it seep differently, encouraging certain microbe sets that are more nutritious to it.
- The woven mats may be devices for *negotiating* with the Body: a way to slightly resist the plant’s constant remodeling. Whenever the Body grows over something woven, it must either incorporate or move around the interference; the creature is learning how to build structures that the plant leaves intact, discovering a grammar of tolerated shapes.

So it lives in the early stages of *co-architecture*: not just inhabiting the Body, but very gently arguing back with it.

Its world is narrow—no fire, no metal—but dense with significance: angles that matter, patterns that feel right, spots on the wall where scratching makes “something happen” a little more predictably.

### 7. How They All Fit Together

What lives here is not a food web in the classical, aboveground sense. Energy is not descending from a sky; it is generated and moved by the Body and partitioned among its guests.

Roughly:

- **The Body**: primary producer, engine, custodian of empty space, sovereign of light and water.
- **Microbes / Films**: converters, refiners, quick-time evolution engines; they fill in biochemical niches the plant itself has left coarse.
- **Ribbon-Creatures**: coarse hydraulic surgeons and grazers; they open and soften the Body’s flows.
- **Seedpod-Spiders and Kin**: network readers and couriers; they distribute micro-life and exploit signal regularities.
- **Vein-Swimmers / Vapor-Midges / Lumen-Worms**: specialists of particular phases (liquid, gas, light) that smooth the Body’s extremes and extract extra layers of energy.
- **Nascent Tool-Users**: local world-editors, small pattern-makers testing the edges of what the Body will allow.

All of them live under an unspoken constraint: the Body’s patience.

Anything that destabilizes core flows or threatens its ability to reconfigure will be edited out. Not with aggression as an animal predator might show, but with growth: channels sealed, ledges dissolved, a once-stable alcove erased by a slow, decisive wave of new tissue.

So to live here at all is to find a way to become part of the Body’s *self-consistent story*.

The microbes live as its chemistry.
The ribbons live as its appetite for redistribution.
The spiders live as its curiosity about patterns.
The vein-swimmers live as its tolerance for noise in the blood.
The midge-clouds live as its breath’s ornament.
The lumen-worms live as its margin between darkness and glare.
The little weaver, scratching and twisting strands, lives as the first suggestion that something *inside* the Body might one day propose an alternative script for how this underground plant should be.

And over, beneath, and through all of them, the Body itself lives as the ongoing question:  

How large, how intricate, how self-knowing can a plant become, if its own interior is given the freedom of space to think?
