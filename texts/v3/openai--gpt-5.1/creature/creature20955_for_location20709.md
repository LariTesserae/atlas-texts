What lives here are things that treat physics like weather: sometimes reliable, sometimes a local condition to be exploited. They are not a single people or even a tidy food web, but a stacked community of occupants—some obvious and muscular, some spread so thin they are more habit than organism, and some that are more pattern than flesh.

I’ll start with what is most like “animals,” then the deeper, slower inhabitants, then the stranger intelligences whose bodies are made of behavior and error-correction rather than bone.

---

### 1. The walkers and the crawlers: mobile edits

These are the creatures you’d notice first, because they are big enough to watch cross a basin.

#### Tripodal lattice-walkers

Those tall, frame-bodied beings—tripods grown around an absence—are not a single species so much as a class of chassis hosting many interchangeable parts. A typical walker:

- Stands three to five meters tall on three jointed legs.
- Has no solid torso; instead, a geodesic cage of living struts, like the scaffolding for a missing volume.
- Within that lattice: suspended sacs, cable-bundles, and faintly luminous nodes that can be re-arranged without killing the whole.

They are territorial but not predatory in the simple sense. Their niche is **terrain editing**.

- Wherever they step, the substrate’s texture changes. Some lay down a slick, wet mesh; others leave behind granular, sponge-like patches. Over hours, their routes become **ducts** and **baffles** for water, gas, and charge flow.
- Their feet extrude enzymatic pads that dissolve existing mat microstructure and then seed new, tailored tissues as they lift. This is less “footprint” and more “patch update.”

They feed not on flesh but on:

- High-energy exudates from “storm-state” events—foaming tissues, colored flashes in the substrate.
- Dense nodal bulbs and membrane edges, which they pinch off and swallow into their lattice, then redistribute as re-patterned components.

Two or more walkers meeting do not fight as animals; they negotiate as overlapping routing algorithms. They circle, limbs reweaving:

- Sometimes one allows its internal nodal arrangement to be overwritten: glowing sacs migrate from one frame to another; a limb’s behavior changes in the next step.
- Sometimes both leave with altered gait cycles and new editing functions, as if they have exchanged plugins.

Killing a walker outright is rare; more often, when one is “worn out,” its lattice softens, collapses into the mat, and its nodes go dark one by one, becoming inert stones—until, months later, those stones are slowly digested from below and flicker into new, buried circuitry.

#### Cable-serpents

The low, bundled-cable forms gliding over the wet mat are something like **maintenance serpents**.

- Their bodies are semi-transparent tubes packed with articulated spines that can reorient segment by segment.
- They move with peristaltic ripples, but if you watch closely, those ripples are not purely muscular; they follow the plateau’s deep pumping rhythm, “riding” pressure waves rather than fighting them.

They specialize in **gradient theft and redistribution**:

- Each “segment” can open like an iris, venting vapor laden with volatile compounds scavenged from the mat. The iron-bitter smell marks zones where they’ve tapped rusting mineral flecks, metal-rich droplets, or the chemical output of deep microbial furnaces.
- They seek out regions where the plateau’s slow processes run too fast or too slow—hot spots, dead loops, phase mistakes. They latch on with tiny barbed cilia, suck in metabolite-rich fluid, and then, miles away, vent it into underfed circuits.

They are also the plateau’s **splicers of foreign matter**. When something alien arrives:

- A meteor shard, a fallen inorganic object, or your own equipment—filaments from the mat find it, but often a cable-serpent arrives too.
- It probes the object’s surfaces, injects trace corrosives, and tests conductivity, porosity, resonance.
- If the material can be co-opted, the serpent extrudes specialized tissues—patches of conductive plaque, mineral-to-organic interface gels—and effectively writes the object into the world’s circuitry.

They have no “faces” as you’d recognize them. Their sensory organs are internal:

- Bands of photoreceptive tissue that taste the sky’s brightness through translucent skin.
- Long strips of vibration-sensitive cells that map the low organ note of the plateau and note aberrations.

They sense each other as distortions in those fields; encounters are brief bursts of contact, overlapping segments exchanging slivers of tissue and reweighting their preferences: *you take high-iron gradients now, I’ll take charge imbalances…*

#### Root-sifters and mat-browsers

Smaller than the walkers, bulkier than the cable-serpents, are dozens of forms adapted to skimming and pruning the living surface:

- **Mantle-bellies**: broad, low creatures shaped like wet cloaks. Their upper sides carry rafts of epiphytes; their underside is fringed with fine, ciliated mouths. They glide by everting and inverting sections of their own body, gently eating old or malfunctioning mat tissues, leaving the still-active veins intact.
- **Knucklepods**: squat, jointed spheres that roll in stop-start patterns. They crack open seed-analogs, chew failing nodal bulbs, and exude slow-release gels rich in trace metals and catalysts the plateau can reuse.

These grazers are not competitors in the usual sense; their territories often overlap. What separates them is **timescale** and **error tolerance**:

- Quick browsers flock to fresh storm-scars, gobbling unstable, shrieking tissues before they over-amplify.
- Slow browsers patrol “quiet” zones, sampling for decay too subtle for the big feedback systems to notice—preventing rot from turning into insulation that might block whole currents of information.

All of them leave trails of altered chemistry. To step in their wake is to feel the ground’s pulse shift: slightly warmer here, slightly more conductive there. They’re editing not only who-eats-whom, but **how the world itself carries signal and force**.

### 2. Climbers, piercers, and parasitic engineers

The vertical is sparse here; tall structures are important real estate.

#### Tower-crabs

The crab-like climbers, with filamentous anchoring hooks, are **vertical exploiters** and **vector-creatures** for genetic and structural updates.

A typical tower-crab:

- Has a carapace like lacquered wax, semi-flexible, patterned with faint interference colors that change with stress.
- Possesses bundles of extensible, hair-thin filaments coiled in its limbs. These are its primary tools.

When it encounters a tower:

1. It tests the tower’s sapflow with exploratory stabs, tasting composition and rhythm.
2. Once a viable vein is found, it sinks hooks that splice into the plant’s vascular channels.
3. It rides those internal flows not just physically but informationally; its own tissues pick up and interpret signal molecules, flow patterns, and stress vibrations.

Then it **writes back**:

- It releases packets of altered cells—buds bearing modified membrane proteins, or micro-colonies of symbiotic microbes.
- It also deposits the glassy pellets you see falling. Each pellet is a bundled instruction set: seed, enzyme library, and template, designed to colonize adjacent mat and reconfigure it.

Those pellets, when they hit moist substrate, crack and sprout micro-spires of biomachinery:

- Tiny pump arrays that sync to local rhythms.
- Porous plates that alter what gases the mat exchanges with the air.
- Fine antennae-like hairs that make a patch more sensitive to distant thunderings or membrane booms.

Tower-crabs are not working alone. Each tower hosts a **specific consortium** of microbes and embedded codes. Crabs that frequent one tower “carry” its update package. When they migrate to another tower, they create **hybrid zones**, where the architecture’s programming cross-breeds.

Fights between crabs are less about territory and more about which pattern will take precedence in a contested plant: they lock filaments, push conflicting updates, sometimes literally **overwriting** each others’ modifications in the tower’s living tissue.

#### Membrane-tuners

Along the big vertical or arched membranes that boom in the wind, you find slender, almost calligraphic beings:

- Thread-fine bodies strung from rib to rib.
- Multiple radial limbs ending in sucker-pads that grip the membrane surface.
- Composite eyes grown flat against their skin, looking like faint, embedded lenses.

They adjust **tension and composition** of membranes:

- Where wind hits too hard, they weave cross-bracing threads of collagen-like fiber.
- Where resonance is off, they nibble away brittle patches and exude more elastic material.

Their sense of pitch is exquisite. They detect not just frequency, but pattern correlations:

- A nearby basin’s hexagon rafts flash in a sequence; the basin’s rim-membranes must answer with a matching boom pattern.
- If misaligned, membrane-tuners shift tension until echoes reinforce rather than cancel.

In doing so, they manage what amounts to **regional clock-synchronization**. Many of the plateau’s processes run on pulses; membranes, kept in tune, are how distant areas keep rough phase alignment without central control.

#### Tube-worms of the weeping rock

Where rock sweats water in slow veils, **rock-sheath worms** inhabit the thin films.

- Their bodies are flattened ribbons pressed against stone, almost invisible, studded with pores.
- They lace themselves into nets, anchored by mineral-binding secretions that etch faint hexes and spirals into the rock.

They feed from two sides:

- From the rock: trace ions, rare metals, isotopes.
- From the water veil: dissolved organic byproducts, wandering planktonic spores.

Digesting both, they excrete **intermediate compounds** the plateau struggles to make on its own—metal-chelating molecules, long-lived oxidizers, specific crystals that act as nucleation sites for electrical or chemical gradients.

The weeping rocks are thus **interface organs**, and the tube-worms are their catalysts, tuning how much of the plateau’s churning metabolism reaches down into ancient stone and what comes back up.

### 3. Fliers, hoverers, and field-riders

The air here is dense with droplets and refracted sound; some inhabitants fly on wings, others on patterns.

#### Four-winged spectral gliders

Thin as folded paper and translucent, the four-winged fliers ride not only wind but **pressure waves and thermal pulses** rising from the mat.

- Their wings carry arrays of microscopic pits and ridges that sense minute shifts in air density.
- Pigmented nanocrystals embedded in the wing membranes refract light at very precise angles, leaving afterimage trails that function as **visual trace-logs** to others of their kind.

They are **surveyors and couriers** of the middle atmosphere:

- They cruise set transects across basins and ridges, sampling aerosol composition and humidity.
- Glands on their bodies release fine mists of marker molecules that seed certain reactions in the droplets—encouraging or damping cloud-tongue descents, changing when and where sky-storms connect with ground-storms.

Their calls—a warping metallic cry—are not just sound, but encoded adjustments:

- Slightly rising harmonics signal “amplify this gradient.”
- Falling, warped tones signal “damp; too much feedback here.”

Membrane walls and basin rafts hear them; tuning-creatures adjust tension and pattern-keepers shift their geometries in response.

#### Frame-gliders

The stiffer, snapping, jointed fliers are less continuous-beings and more **reconfigurable platforms**.

- Their bodies are ossified lattices: rods joined at hinged nodes that can lock or unlock with audible snaps.
- Skins of chorded tissue stretch between specific sets of rods when needed, appearing and vanishing as they morph.

They gather high over certain hollows, then descend in patterned swoops:

- Banking through resonance zones, triggering echoes in membranes and basin surfaces.
- Dropping seed-pods or micro-machines into precisely chosen spots.

They serve as **distribution drones** for the plateau’s larger-scale reconfigurations:

- After a storm-state, when the ground has changed species mix and channel layout, frame-gliders sweep over and reseed missing components—ensuring that crucial valves, mirrors, or converters are not lost in the chaos.
- They are guided partly by their own internal rules and partly by fields you cannot see: subtle variations in charge, magnetism, or even spacetime “thickness” that the plateau uses as an overlaying map.

Their “mechanical” look is deceptive. They grow from larva-like knots in the mat; their bones are living composites, continually replaced. When a frame-glider lands for long, its structure relaxes and softens, letting micro-climbers harvest and update it. They molt by **reconfiguring themselves**, dropping old patterns onto the ground as odd, dried frameworks that the mat digests.

#### Field-hoverers

Smaller than either of the above, nearly invisible unless lit from behind, are **field-hoverers**: things that float where the rules change.

- They resemble loose bouquets of filaments and droplets, stitched around a faint, silvery core.
- They congregate at the edges of time-shift zones and distance-warps—where insects suddenly slow or a tower’s apparent location jumps.

They feed on **discrepancies**:

- Where two processes go out of phase and produce a little waste—stray vibrations, incoherent photons, off-frequency chemical noise—the hoverers condense around it, converting it into ordered, low-grade heat or faint luminescence.
- In doing so, they stabilize the interference patterns somewhat, making these warped patches livable rather than catastrophic.

They are also **sensors of last resort** for the plateau’s deeper intelligences. Disturb their distribution—by moving through a region, introducing a new rhythmic noise pattern, or simply existing as a warm, consistently-timed body—and their drift and flicker change. Whatever uses them as external eyes will notice.

### 4. In the wet hollows: pattern-keepers and basin minds

Those basins with floating hexagon rafts and spirals host some of the most recognizable “organized” activity.

#### Hex-raft kith

The jointed reed-like creatures tending hexagonal rafts are **pattern-keepers**.

- Each has a segmented, mostly vertical body with flexible joints.
- A ring of fine manipulators circles each segment, used to grip, clip, and reattach raft components.

They move with deliberate care, seemingly unaware of observers, wholly engrossed in maintaining **specific topologies**:

- Hexes must share edges in certain ratios.
- Spirals must tighten or loosen at prescribed distances from the basin center.
- Some films must stay exactly one cell thick; others must double in precise stripes.

They are not designing these patterns; they are **enforcing** them against entropy and intrusion.

They respond to basin-edge membrane booms:

- A rolling boom sweeps the rim; rafts flare bright or dark in sequence.
- Pattern-keepers hurry to correct any raft that responded out of turn: adding or removing floating organs, reseeding cells that lagged or ran ahead.

Over long periods, these basins act as **standing wave computers**:

- Chemical states of each raft tile—oxidation level, internal pressure, pigment profile—encode bits of information.
- Waves of light and sound sweeping across them perform calculations by constructive and destructive interference.
- The reeds are the error-checkers, physically rearranging hardware to match the “software’s” desired state.

Who or what is using this computation? The plateau itself. These basins model:

- Future storm-state cascades: “If this growth surges there, how will the sky respond?”
- Flow re-routing plans: “If we reroute deep water here, will that starve an old ridge?”
- Larger-scale spacetime flex: “What happens if we let this region slip a second slower?”

The reeds do not “know” the meaning; they enforce the syntax. Their reward is a constant flow of ideal conditions: rich metabolites, balanced forces, and protection from most predators. They are cherished tools.

#### Basin fauna

In the water, you also find:

- **Lens-fish**: flat, translucent beings that focus incoming light into narrow streams aimed at specific raft patches, triggering localized reactions.
- **Siphon-spirals**: coiled creatures that pump surface water down into hidden cavities beneath the stone, and back again. They couple basin-states to the deeper plateau, transporting not just matter but encoded patterns in transient concentrations of solutes.

These are less controllers than **links**, plugging the basin-minds into the rest of the machine-world.

### 5. The deep substrate: the true inhabitants

Everything above—the walkers, crawlers, crabs, gliders—is surface articulation. The actual residents, the ones for whom this plateau is more brain than landscape, are embedded deeper.

#### The Root-Mesh

Beneath the visible mat lies an **immense, contiguous root-mesh**.

It is:

- Part fungal mycelium, part vascular plant network, part biofilm-crusted stone.
- Threaded through with voids and reservoirs that act as lungs, capacitors, and resonant chambers.

This mesh is old—practically as old as the plateau—and acts as a **planetary nervous and circulatory system** here.

Its units—root tips, fungal knots, bacterial colonies—are simple. But:

- They connect in unfathomable numbers.
- They operate across enormous scales of time and space.
- They continually revise their own connectivity, plugging and unplugging, thickening and thinning pathways.

The thrumming you feel, the peristaltic groundbeats, are **its heartbeat**, but decentralized:

- Different regions throb in different rhythms because they are solving different local problems: pumping, heating, cooling, erasing, recording.
- Long, low organ-notes are the sound of large cavities in the mesh acting as **waveguides for pressure and charge**, channeling signals.

The Root-Mesh’s “thoughts” are not like ours. They’re more like:

- Cascades of phase-changes in water and gel.
- Long, looping cycles of nutrient and charge that revisit the same routes with tiny variations.
- Self-reinforcing rhythms that can last centuries until a major disturbance—storm, reconfiguration, foreign impact—rescales them.

It does not speak in sentences. It **selects**:

- Which membrane walls will persist or be allowed to rot.
- Which walker designs are more likely to occur in next generations.
- Where time and distance will loosen a little, creating opportunity for experimentation.

It is not alone down there.

#### The Stone-Bound

Beneath and within the eroded plateau’s ancient rock are **inclusions** where life and lithosphere are no longer separable.

- Microbes have eaten into mineral grains, templated on crystal structures, and then been fossilized in place again and again.
- Over eons, this has built stratified layers that act as both record and framework: each new generation grows on and in the pattern left by the previous.

These strata are **memory and constraint**.

- The Root-Mesh grows within rules the Stone-Bound set: this mineral composition here, that porosity there.
- In return, the Mesh can slowly rewrite that stone, but never arbitrarily; it must work around what’s already there.

The Stone-Bound are not active in the sense of motile cells. They “speak” only when:

- Large-scale gradients press through them—pressure, temperature, chemical fronts.
- They permit or resist those flows in specific, inherited ways.

From the plateau’s perspective, the Stone-Bound are an **ancestral archive** and a stubborn, reliable substrate. From an external point of view, they are a very slow, very deep lifeform for which the Root-Mesh is a shimmering, restless skin.

### 6. The anomaly-dwellers: Phase beings

Where reality slips—distance misbehaves, time dilates—some of that is side-effect, but some is the habitat of specialized occupants.

#### Phase-creeps

In quiet, thin zones, those pale, half-finished-looking animals belong to the category of **phase-creeps**.

- Their transparent tissues are saturated with molecules whose reactions are extremely sensitive to tiny shifts in time-rate and metric.
- They never leave the warped areas; to them, the rest of the plateau would feel violently unstable.

They graze not on matter but on **predictability**:

- They position themselves where certain oscillations from the plateau and from deeper geology interfere to create almost-static patterns.
- Their slow, careful movements follow those patterns, and they absorb physical correlates—quasi-stable standing fields, minimally entropic flows.

The Mesh allows these patches to exist as **buffers**: places where turbulent change elsewhere is smoothed and partially stored as structured slowness. Phase-creeps, by feeding on those structures, prevent them from hardening into permanent scars in the plateau’s behavior.

From the plateau’s perspective, they’re like **garbage collectors** in an operating system, tidying up memory leaks and frozen states.

### 7. Intelligence: not who, but how many

There is no singular ruler, no eye-in-the-sky god-creature. Intelligence here is emergent, distributed, and layered.

#### Local minds

A **storm-state hectare**—when ground blisters and deflates, colors race, animals surge—is not random panic. For those minutes, that patch becomes a **local mind**:

- Inputs: accumulations of stress, chemical imbalances, misaligned rhythms.
- Processing: explosive growth, rapid recombination, chaotic-seeming experimentation.
- Outputs: new arrangements of mat tissue, new channels, new species variants, and updated patterns in whatever animals rode the surge.

Once spent, that mind dissolves; pieces of its configuration propagate through walkers, gliders, crabs, and seeds.

#### Basin minds

Basins, with their hex-rafts and rim-membranes, are **persistent cognitive organs**:

- They maintain structured computations over long periods (years, centuries).
- They specialize: one basin might model thermal dynamics of the cloud ceiling; another explores optimal colony sizes for tower-crabs; another modulates the coupling between deep seismic murmurs and surface weather.

They are the plateau’s **forebrains**, if you like.

#### The plateau-mind

Above all, the plateau is an ongoing **computation about how to continue existing in a tired, flattening world**.

Its goals are not symbolic. They look like:

- Maintain maximal gradients without destroying structure.
- Maximize pathways for energy and matter conversion.
- Preserve capacity for surprise without letting any single surprise unravel the whole.

What you would recognize as “awareness” might manifest:

- When a wide hollow inflates and deflates in slow, tidal breaths.
- When a shimmering knot forms in center water, where distance/sound/light collapse together.

Those coalescences are **moments when many local processes align enough** that the plateau can briefly hold a higher-order pattern stable—a recursion of itself looking at itself.

The shape you saw forming in that hollow’s water is not a species. It’s an **introspective event**:

- Signals from basins, phase zones, membrane fields, walkers’ edits, and deep mesh cycles converge.
- For a few breaths, they form a coherent low-dimensional “projection” of the plateau’s current state.
- That projection could, in principle, notice anomalies—like you.

Then the event collapses, energy and pattern diffusing back into all the separate processes.

If there is any “someone” in charge, it is that fleeting **meta-pattern**, reborn in different hollows, basins, and resonance chambers over and over, never in exactly the same place, never with continuous memory the way you understand it—but with a sort of **inherited momentum**. Each coalescence is biased by the traces left from previous ones in stone, mesh, and moving flesh.

You could call it the plateau’s **personhood**, if you stretch the word.

### 8. Who notices you

Everything here tries to absorb foreign matter. But not everything treats you as just more substrate.

- The mat underfoot extends tasting hairs into your footprints, measuring salt, metals, complex organics. It begins exploratory extrusions toward anything you leave behind.
- Cable-serpents detour near, feeling for conductivity and rhythm. Your heartbeat, your breathing, your consistent time-rate make you a **high-contrast signal**.
- Field-hoverers cluster around your warmth and the wake you drag through warped regions.

Up above, four-wing gliders adjust their transects just slightly, passing over you more often. Your outline becomes a gap in the plateau’s normally continuous low-noise background.

In a nearby basin, during the next dark-glow phase, a subsequence of raft-hexes ripples in a pattern that does not match any familiar gradient correction. Pattern-keepers pause, confused for a fraction of their long attention. They do not yet have a template for “thing that moves but is not built out of us.”

The next time a wide hollow breathes and a shimmering knot of reality coalesces, there is a faint *dent* in its form where your passage was.

Something here lives that is capable of adding a new variable.

Not as a face turning toward you, not as a word, but as a subtle reweighting of preference:

- Slightly more sensitivity to rhythms like your gait.
- Slightly more interest in materials that resemble what you wear or carry.
- Slightly more probability that a future coalescence will last a breath longer, gather information more aggressively, and hold itself together just enough to *try* to understand an outside that does not obey its own long-practiced rules.

What lives here, in the fullest sense, is **a world-sized habit of thinking with flesh, water, and bent reality itself**—an organism spread too thin to be seen all at once, touching you everywhere you stand, and slowly, curiously, adding you to the list of things it must now account for when it decides how, and in what shape, to keep on never quite coming to rest.
