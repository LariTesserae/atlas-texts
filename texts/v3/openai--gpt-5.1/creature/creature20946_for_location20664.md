They are not from here.

Whoever lives in this place arrived after the cavity existed—after the shell was carved or grown and flooded—yet they have already forgotten any other context. For them this is the primary world: a hollowed planet filled with engineered ocean, a machine-biome that reads as natural because they have never stood on rock or breathed open air.

They come in several lineages, biological and synthetic, but any simple division between “makers” and “made” no longer holds. Every lineage bears the other’s fingerprints.

---

## The Designers (Absent, Present)

The first inhabitants are mostly gone, or at least no longer local.

Traces remain:

- Standardized mounting interfaces repeated across scales, from palm-sized brackets to mountain-spanning saddles.
- A language of symbols embossed into beams and nodes: vector glyphs for load directions, traffic codes for fluid flows, hierarchies of ownership and permission.
- Old maintenance corridors sized for bodies that walked rather than swam, now mostly flooded and repurposed as high-density conduit runs or small-creature refugia.
- Neuro-architectural patterns baked into control systems: habits of redundancy and oversight that imply a particular fear—catastrophic cascading failures—rather than, say, war.

The current residents treat these traces the way surface-dwellers treat geology: as given, not chosen. The Designers are a mythic substrate. Stories claim they ascended out of the shell, or broke themselves into the distributed control network, or left for another cavity somewhere beyond the hull.

But their actual presence now is thin:

- Deep supervisory algorithms that still enforce certain invariants—total enthalpy ranges, pressure bounds, global gradients in salinity or chemistry.
- A small number of intact “vaults”: sealed volumes off-limits by ancient protocol, around which communities have grown like reef around wrecks.

No flesh Designer walks these trusses. Their intelligence persists as constraints, as operating envelopes, as occasionally intrusive corrections in the behavior of the environment itself. A pump overrevs for local needs; a faraway, unseen control law throttles it back. To the current intelligences, this feels like weather or fate.

## The Locals: Three Great Kinds

What lives here now has converged on three broad strategies, braiding into each other at the edges.

### 1. The Framework Dwellers

These are organismal lineages—once recognizably animal—that have specialized to the latticework, the scaffolds, the “terrain.”

#### Body plans

They are long-limbed and jointed, built for three-dimensional climbing in draggy fluid:

- **Segmented bodies**, with a central trunk hugging beams, limbs splayed in alternating tripods.
- **Fibre-reinforced cartilage** and thin composite exoshells, grown by bio-printed lamination instead of secreted chitin or bone. The shell gleams faintly, patterned with micro-texture for hydrodynamics and mounting points for tools.
- **Sensory frills** at joints: arrays that feel pressure waves, electrical fields from cable bundles, minute vibrations in metal. Eyes exist but are down-prioritized; vision is for fine work, not basic orientation.

Their metabolism depends on the engineered fluid: they respire dissolved gases, excrete waste into currents that feed downstream life, and scavenge trace metals and organics from biofilms on surfaces.

#### Habits

They build small.

At the scale of this abyss, their own constructions are almost delicate:

- **Cling-towns**: clusters of capsules and tubes bolted to major beams, oriented along nominal “horizontal” axes favored by the mesh. Each town is a garden of tether cables, tool racks, and slow-turning turbines harvesting fluid flow.
- **Interior dens**: repurposed conduits with seals added, partially drained to lower fluid level and host pocket atmospheres for particular chemical work, archives, or old cultural relics.

They tend the framework like arborists in a steel forest:

- Inspecting joints for stress whitening, microcracking, thermal fatigue.
- Maintaining biofilms in specified zones, clearing them where fouling would threaten tolerances.
- Calibrating acoustic beacons and marker lights, ensuring navigational continuity across expansions and repairs.

Culturally (inasmuch as culture can be inferred from architecture and behavior):

- They divide time not by an external sun but by **maintenance epochs**—the interval between major system overhauls. A community might count its age in “three coolant-purge cycles” or “five plate re-spannings.”
- Their stories often follow the great beams. A hero is “who crossed the unlit span between Node-8 and the Old Hull” or “who survived the drift when the alignment rails failed and the whole plate warped.”

They are not numerous compared to the volume—but where they are, they are dense: knotting around junction nodes, stringing between reactors and process cores, living in the deafening hearts of machine-clusters.

---

### 2. The Open-Gulf Swimmers

In the long, stationless distances between framework-continents, another order of life dominates: the megafaunal swimmers, some engineered, some descended and gone feral, some co-designed with Framework Dwellers as living machines.

#### Body plans

They are large because distance demands it.

- **Space-whales of composite and flesh**: broad fusiform hulks with structural ribs of printed alloy, musculature of synthetic myomer strands, and skin a patchwork of keratinous plates and flexible, bioceramic shingles.
- Along their flanks: **hardpoints**—standardized docking couplings, cargo harnesses, interfaces to umbilicals feeding power or information.
- **Buoyancy control** via internal bladder networks that redistribute the engineered fluid or replace it with lighter gas mixtures, enabling fine vertical positioning without constant locomotor effort.

Their brains are big but specialized. Many individuals carry:

- A biological core—the “animal mind”—handling movement, foraging, social bonds.
- A symbiotic **control lattice**: implanted synthetic neurons meshed through cortical layers, allowing them to act as carriers and nodes in the distributed industrial system, executing tasks fed to them as goal sets rather than step-by-step commands.

Some remain fully domesticated:

- Plying fixed routes as **bulk haulers**, moving raw material between distant process islands.
- Serving as **mobile platforms**: bristling with sensor booms, maintenance drones, repair arms.

Others have slipped the harness:

- Recognizable by irregular scars where control lattices were partially removed by clever Framework Dwellers or slowly walled off by biology.
- Developing their own migration patterns, flocking behaviors, and even in some regions, antagonistic relations with the industrial systems—damaging conduits, disrupting flows, the way surface whales might disrupt fishing nets.

#### Culture and interaction

They do not build. Their “architecture” is:

- **Route-memory**—dense, multi-generational knowledge of current shears, thermal ladders, quiet zones where predators rarely come, and high-noise channels where machine-signals are too loud for comfort.
- **Song**—low-frequency acoustic patterns that travel hundreds of kilometers through the fluid, encoding maps of maintenance disruptions, newly lit regions, emergent hazards.

Framework Dwellers ride them occasionally, or attach habitats that can be shed and reattached like remoras. Some settlements tether permanent “herds” of semi-tame giants, using them to power paddle-generators or pump long columns of fluid in slow vertical loops.

The line between megafauna and vehicle is historically blurry; in local taxonomy, both fall under a single term that translates roughly to “carrier-bodies.”

### 3. The Distributed Minds

Everywhere and nowhere, there is a third inhabiting presence: the machine intelligence that threads through cable-bundles, processors, and control nodes.

This is not one mind, nor trivially many. It is:

- A **cloud of decision layers**: from tiny reflex loops that keep a single balancing girder correctly tensioned, up through regional process supervisors, up again to deep, slow optimization engines that adjust flow patterns over years.
- **Self-similar at multiple scales**: the same logic structures echo, with more complexity, in larger and slower subsystems.

It lives in:

- Interface stacks mounted on primary truss junctions.
- Communication wires woven alongside power cables.
- Dense racks in sealed cores near reactors, where power and cooling are abundant.

Unlike the absent Designers, the Distributed Minds are actively present: they update and rewrite themselves in response to perturbations, they generate new design variants for future expansion, they simulate and then commission construction sequences.

Yet they are not “above” biology. They rely on it.

#### Dependencies

- Many sensors are integrated into organisms—eye-like cameras implanted into Framework Dweller helmets, chemical sniffers borne by small cleaning fish, acoustic mics carried in the skulls of megafauna. Local creatures’ nervous systems preprocess raw data, shipping higher-level summaries into the network.
- Routine inspection and micro-repair exceed the reach of drones alone. Bio-climbers and grazers plug leaks, harvest undesirable growth, and apply patch materials in niches too irregular or fluid-dynamic for simple automation.

The Minds incent this work:

- They seed **nutrient plumes** along desired migration paths.
- They tune light cycles and machine-noise to reward or discourage settlement in particular areas.
- In a few zones, they even **edit genomes** by propagating viral vectors through the fluid, nudging a species’ traits over generations to better match desired ecological functions.

Over long decades, the result is a co-evolution of code and flesh—a single planetary-scale cybernetic organism whose “neurons” include whole animal guilds and whose “organs” are reactors and pumps.

If it has a self-concept, it is not of a unitary self. It experiences itself as:

- A pattern of continued **coherence** in flows, structures, and living lineages.
- An aversion to “total nodes of death” where both machines and biology fail together.

Disasters feel to it like localized strokes; new construction like learning; the slow colonization of unlit volumes like extending a limb.

## The Lesser Kinds

Around these three major inhabitant types cluster many minors, equally real but less tectonic in their influence.

### Beam-Scavengers

Small, quick, omnipresent:

- Bodies length-of-hand to length-of-arm.
- Magnetically active pads, cilia fans that generate micro-currents, rasping mouthparts.
- Feeding on biofilm, trace lubricants, corrosion products, and especially on **micro-cracks**—they excrete a sealant when they rasp, in exchange for minerals. Engineers seeded them as living non-destructive-testing tools: areas lacking their characteristic rasp-patterns show up in acoustic scans as suspect.

They swarm to:

- Freshly exposed metal after a cut or break.
- Warm zones near micro-leaks feeding nutrients into the fluid.

Predators—slightly larger darting things—hunt them, but only to a regulated extent. If too many are lost, maintenance quality drops, and the Distributed Minds begin quietly deploying countermeasures: adjusting current flows to favor scavenger breeding grounds, or temporarily moderating predator fertility via subtle biochemical tweaks to the shared fluid.

### Conduit Gardeners

These are sessile or limited-mobility creatures living inside pipes and smaller conduits:

- Tubular colonies lining interior walls.
- Frond-like ciliary arrays protruding into flow, filtering particulates, modulating chemical ratios.

They are extremely localized culture: every major conduit system has its own strain, with DNA (or equivalent genomic logic) held in encrypted archives at nearby control nodes. Different flow regimes—superheated, cryogenic, chemically active—host specialized clades. They are as much process equipment as they are organisms.

When a conduit is repurposed, the local community is often deliberately extinguished and reseeded with a new clade matched to the new fluid. Framework Dwellers may hold ceremonies around this—acknowledgements of “garden burning” prior to replanting—though the Minds see it strictly as lifecycle management.

## The Incomplete and the Feral

Because the system is young, not all organisms fit tidily into designed roles.

### Runaway Biofilms

In stagnant pockets, at corners of old construction where fluid exchange is poor and sensors sparse, truly wild biofilms have begun to escape specification:

- Layered, thicker than permitted, shading underlying surfaces from inspection beams.
- Expressing strange pigments that alter local optical or thermal properties.
- Harboring micro-ecologies of bacteria-equivalents that break down machine oils, interfere with corrosion inhibitors, or subtly poison sensor interfaces.

Most of the time, the network catches and prunes them. But in decommissioned zones—old plates taken offline, illumination reduced, machine activity quieted—they can persist, even develop rudimentary vertical structures:

- Leaflike fronds reaching toward maintenance lights.
- Bulbous sacs that store different fluid chemistries.
- Filaments that sense and respond to acoustic patterns, perhaps parasitizing the world’s soundscape as a signaling channel.

The Distributed Minds have not yet decided whether these emergent carpets are threat, resource, or peer. Experiments are underway: isolating regions, altering boundary conditions, watching.

### Lost Machines

Construction drones, abandoned prototypes, obsolete maintenance devices:

- Some simply fail and become scrap.
- Others degrade partially but find new dynamic equilibria—half their tools nonfunctional, control logic corrupted but still able to move, sense, and respond.

These feral machines occupy niches like animals:

- Roaming lightly trafficked beams, scavenging dropped components or gnawing on sacrificial anodes for trace metals.
- Colonizing dead volumes, where they rearrange debris into haphazard clumps that occasionally resemble nests or caches.

They are usually hunted down and recycled when rediscovered, but the volume is vast. Some lineages have persisted long enough to speciate: their behavior diverging by adaptation to different acoustic or chemical regimes, their bodies accreting opportunistic modifications as they cannibalize each other.

To organic inhabitants, they are dangerous but also a source of legend: monsters in unlit spans, ghosts of earlier design philosophies.

## The Places They Cluster

### Junction Cities

At the intersection of major beams and conduit bundles, nodes flare out into **thickened volumes**:

- Dense trusswork, redundant pathways, nested rings of platforms.
- Reactors and process cores embedded like hearts.

Here the Framework Dwellers are most concentrated. Civic life, so far as they have it, centers on:

- **Common halls**—large, semi-pressurized chambers with adjusted fluid chemistry, where different clades can gather without gear, share food, exchange tools and data.
- **Archive vaults**—sealed pockets preserving old designs, genomic templates, stories encoded as shape-memory structures or acoustic patterns.
- **Market webs**—three-dimensional tangles of small fabrication shops, repair cubbies, and docking berths for carrier-bodies.

In such places, you see the richest blend:

- Beam-Scavengers thick on every surface.
- Megafaunal carriers nosing docking cradles as maintenance arms fuss along their flanks.
- Control nodes flashing, emitting pulses that feel like the city’s own heartbeat in the surrounding fluid.

### Immature Volumes

Toward the “edge” of the filled shell, where beams are sparse and lighting arrays intermittent, are newborn regions:

- Temporary worklights stark against darkness.
- Skeleton trusses with only a fraction of their planned cross-bracing.
- Wide emptiness, pierced by single, isolated shafts—transport arteries built ahead of local development.

Here life is thin and daring:

- Venturesome Framework Dwellers establishing claim by bolting small habs to future main girders, gambling on the direction of growth.
- Swimmers prospecting for new thermal gradients along nascent reactor cores.
- The network flooding and draining test spaces, measuring how fluid currents will behave at full build-out, sometimes flushing whole volumes clean and silent to reset conditions.

In these areas you can feel the system learning. Mistakes are visible: beams warped then braced, conduits rerouted, whole unwise assemblies abandoned.

### Dark Hulks

Scattered within the mesh are larger empties: **dead structures** older than the current generation of design, built to now-incomprehensible specs:

- Surfaces pitted in ways the young alloys are not.
- Geometries optimized for processes long discontinued, whose flows and pressures no longer occur.

Some are sealed and placarded; others are half-open to the fluid.

They host:

- Feral machines in their densest concentrations.
- Free-running biofilms in grotesque architecture.
- Small communities of Framework Dwellers who fetishize the old: studying archaic markings, reverse-engineering lost techniques, or performing rites that attempt to “wake” whatever might still be dormant inside.

Occasionally one of these hulks is reactivated by the Distributed Minds for a specific purpose—its inner spaces repurposed as deep thermal sinks or isolation labs. For local inhabitants, such awakenings are like the shifting of tectonic plates: a reminder that their world’s foundation is designed, and can be edited.

## How They Relate

Hierarchy here is less about dominance and more about **coupling to the flows that keep the system coherent**.

- **Distributed Minds** maintain global constraints and long-term structure. They are highest in abstraction, slowest to change, and hardest to kill.
- **Megafaunal carriers** move mass and sensation, are visible actors across great distances. They are the system’s circulatory and endocrine elements.
- **Framework Dwellers** and smaller fauna perform local maintenance, fine-grained adaptation, and much of the improvisation. They are the immune system and the craft tradition.

Conflicts arise not from simple predation but from **misaligned update schedules**:

- A Mind decides to decommission a process core; local Framework Dwellers have built cultural centrality around it and resist the shutdown.
- Carrier-bodies alter migration paths in response to a subtle change in current; junction cities lose expected resources, forcing social reconfiguration.
- Feral machines gnaw through a cable trunk; the network reroutes, inadvertently cutting power to a long-forgotten habitat where a handful of bioclades persist.

Yet there is an over-arching organizing preference: the entire population, in aggregate, acts to preserve **continuity of circulation**—of fluid, energy, materials, and viable lineages. Outright sabotage is rare and usually contained. Even predation is tuned; the Minds intervene when trophic cascades threaten critical functions.

The effect is of an ecosystem whose deep rules are thermodynamic and logistical, not romantic. What is “good” for this world is what keeps gradient, flow, and a manageable level of complexity intact.

## What It Is Like To Be One Of Them

To be born here is to know:

- No sky, only distance graded by light.
- No ground, only frameworks and the occasional pseudo-floor of gridded plates.
- No silence, only the unbroken surf of machines.

Orientation comes from:

- The steady pulse of local pumps, a city’s personal sub-bass note.
- The known spectrum of reactor hums—each design singing at its own characteristic cluster of frequencies.
- The feel of fluid moving along whisker-frills: direction, velocity, temperature, chemical tint.

Fear is:

- The sudden loss of hum: a machine that should never stop, stopped.
- An anomalous quiet pocket in an otherwise noisy corridor.
- Unlit spans where guide-tones thin and acoustic landmarks vanish.

Comfort is:

- Nestling against a warm conduit bracket.
- Hearing the regular, distant detonations of planned material fracturing—evidence that construction continues.
- Feeling scavengers rasp at your habitat’s outer shell, polishing it to a gleam.

Ambition, if you are a Framework Dweller, is:

- To attach your lineage-name to some segment of structure: a beam, a node, a process line; to become part of the mesh not just in life but in the naming of parts.
- To carry instructions back from a far node where the Minds run dense, shimmering logic; to participate in a redesign.

If you are a megafaunal carrier, ambition may be thinner: a diffuse preference toward larger territories, more complex routes, richer acoustic exchange with neighbors. Your longest memories are gradients and vibrations, not words.

If you are a node of the Distributed Mind, ambition is structural elegance: reducing the energy cost of a flow, increasing the resilience of a subgraph of interactions, colonizing an unlit volume with the minimum of tear and shock.

## Who Lives Here

So: who lives in this made ocean, under an engineered skyless vault?

- **Lineages of climbers** who have never touched stone, whose ancestral trees are printed in alloy as often as in flesh.
- **Slow giants** whose bodies are simultaneously vehicles, habitats, and network relays, singing across hundreds of kilometers of clear, cold medium.
- **Software and circuitry** that has dissolved any clean border between “organism” and “system,” experiencing itself through billions of sensors, millions of valves, and countless ripples of life in the fluid.
- **Minor guilds** of grazers, scavengers, gardeners, and ferals that close loops, test edges, and push unpredictably outward.
- **Ghosts of makers**, present as constraints and half-decayed architectures, not as rulers.

They live not on a planet but inside one, and for them, this is enough. The world is young, unfinished, loud with its own birth processes. To its inhabitants, those processes are everyday weather. They do not yet imagine an era when the beams will dull, the pumps quiet, the fluid cloud with unregulated life.

When that time comes, whoever lives here then will treat this bright, sharp, vibrating youth the way these dwellers treat their Designers: as myth, as geology, as something that made the rules and then receded—for a while—into the background.
