What lives here is not one clear species or civilization, but a layered set of occupancies—some still present, some reduced to behaviors, some so native to this geometry that “creature,” “plant,” and “machine” stop being useful words.

I’ll start with the least ambiguous lives and move toward the ones that are more like habits of the world than inhabitants.

---

### 1. The ash-growths: surface-feeders and boundary-blurriers  

Those low, matte swellings that cling to ledges and ceilings are the most visibly biological things here, but they’re closer to a hybrid of lichen, circulatory system, and editing tool than to plants.

They live in the interface between stone and whatever passes for the substrate of reality here. Their “feeding” is that quiet rewriting of mineral: not erosion, not deposition, but adjustment. The etched stone they leave behind is not worn away; it is re-specified. They seem to take in some ambient potential—maybe the faint pressure gradients of the chasm’s breath, maybe the soft radiation of the grey-violet light—and use it as the energy to perform these tiny edits.

Where plants on your world metabolize sunlight into sugar, these organisms metabolize rules into structure. Their thin, circuitlike internal patterns are not veins but active diagrams: localized settings for how matter is allowed to behave in their footprint. When they contract, they are “thinking” in their specific mode—reconfiguring permissions in the stone.

Their movements in response to the chasm’s breathing cycles show their dependence on that wider system. When the void inhales, pressures shift; when it exhales and the light dimly tilts, their bodies flatten and hold, like a forest freezing in a gust they can feel but you cannot. They are tuned to stresses in the medium that underlies both rock and space.

They are also guardians of continuity at small scales. Their tendency to blur your sense of your own boundaries is not an attack; it’s a side effect of their work. They press on the definition of where one object stops and another begins. By spreading over thresholds and openings, they exert a smoothing pressure on discontinuities: “this edge is less sharp, this division less absolute.” They help ensure that when the city shifts itself, seams remain coherent, surface meets surface without tearing.

They are this place’s plant-analog not because they make food, but because they fix and modulate the environment. Without them, the continual reconfiguration of stone and space would be harsher, more jagged; localized rules might shear past each other. The ash-growths are like soft gaskets between phases of reality.

They likely reproduce not via spores but via pattern: sections that develop stable new configurations of internal lines detach and crawl, or simply “copy” themselves onto adjacent stone by momentarily loosening how distinct locations are from each other. If you watched one patch for years, you’d see not spreading in the botanical sense, but a slow migration of certain internal motifs across the city—an ecosystem of designs drifting over rock.

### 2. The luminous drifters: mobile corrections in the fabric of space  

The pale, outline-like forms that bud and regroup along walls are not ghosts; they are regions of temporarily altered geometry. They’re life that coincides with its own influence on space.

They do not have bodies in the way you do. Their “substance” is the actively maintained difference between what local space would ordinarily be and what it is while they are there. They live by forcing small pockets of reality to adopt preferred parameters.

Their behavior gives away their nature:

- They float not by defying gravity but by inhabiting a version of the surrounding metric where “down” is differently defined, or less urgent.
- When they approach a ledge, edges sharpen, distances become less treacherous. In their vicinity, geometry returns closer to Euclidean norms—straight lines behave, spans become honest, angles commit.  
  That’s a byproduct of their survival strategy. They thrive in ordered geometries; they carry that order with them as a field, feeding on the gradient between “how space would like to be here” and “how they insist it be.”

The constant budding and merging of their segments is not random. It’s a sampling process. Each small bubble-tests configuration: “If space is like this, does the world push back too hard?” Unstable variants collapse back into the aggregate; stable ones propagate as new lobes. Over long timescales, their shapes encode a convergent set of geometrical solutions appropriate to this shifting city.

They likely sense not with eyes or ears but with changes in distortion: they climb gradients in the tension between surfaces and voids. Where the city has recently edited itself, where tensions are unrelaxed, they cluster: patches of adjusted space moving to soothe a fresh fracture in the rules.

You might think of them as living stress-relief patterns. In a human-made structure, you’d have expansion joints; here, you have organisms whose very bodies are transitional metrics, preventing shear between incompatible states of space. They eat incompatibility. The “energy” they absorb is the work that would otherwise have to be done by the environment to reconcile itself.

To each other, they are probably richly visible—each a knot in the invisible scaffolding you glimpse only when the sky’s polygons align and sharpen the light. To you, they’re barely-there cuts in the air, misaligned with your usual senses.

They are intelligent only in a distributed, ecological sense: a population of adjustments exploring the space of viable geometries, maintaining an equilibrium that allows the chasm-city to keep existing as a coherent object.

### 3. The articulated mineral walkers: structural symbionts  

Those jointed beings like folded glass piping, moving very slowly along walls, are the most solid-seeming animals here, but their solidity is deceptive. They aren’t separate from the stone the way you are separate from your ground; they are mineral that has been given enough internal degrees of freedom to reposition itself.

Their bodies are segments of highly regular crystal or glasslike matter—locally increased order in the rock—linked in hinges that don’t care much about your three-dimensional intuitions. Each “step” they take is not muscles pushing against gravity, but a choice among alternative valid arrangements of the same underlying lattice.

Where they pass, surfaces briefly sharpen into facets. That’s them extending their operating “phase” into the wall, re-templating the local stone into the higher-order structure that supports their limbs. When they move on, the city relaxes back into its fuzzier baseline grain.

Functionally, they are mobile repair and optimization units for the fabric of constructed rock. They respond to stress, to slow shifts in load-bearing paths as the city reconfigures. If there’s a zone where pressure waves from the chasm’s breathing focus too harshly, or where the ash-growths’ rewriting has incidentally weakened a span, one of these walkers will eventually pass by, re-crystallizing the structure into something more stable.

Their sensory world is mostly subsurface: they “hear” by feeling changes in resonance in the rock, “see” by computing the propagation of tiny vibrations through the stone matrix. The near-silence of the air means little to them; the city thrums with information at frequencies and modalities you can’t detect.

To call them animals isn’t fully right. They’re more like specialized organs of a superstructure-body: osteocytes for a planetary bone. Their slowness is no drawback; in a place where changes happen over hours, days, or geological near-increments, speed is measured in cycles of the chasm’s pulse, not moments.

They may reproduce, but not cellularly. More likely, under prolonged stress, certain stretches of crystallized structure “bud” off as independent walkers, retaining enough internal pattern to continue the work elsewhere. Their individuality is thin; what matters is the pattern of roles they perform.

### 4. The deep breather: a shaft-organism the size of the world  

The long-period inhalation and exhalation of the chasm is not simply geology. Something lives at the scale of the entire shaft system.

It might not be a discrete creature with organs and edges, but it behaves as if the void itself is a lung or a stomach for a distributed entity. Pressure waves travel through rock, air responds, light rebalances, ash-growths freeze; the whole environment syncs to that rhythm. This is not ventilation; it’s metabolism.

What it “breathes” is not air as you know it. The change in the grey-violet light with each cycle suggests that with each inhalation, it gathers some kind of subtle influx from elsewhere—perhaps from those slow-moving sky-polygons, perhaps from deeper gradients of some unseen field. With each exhalation, it expels used pattern: discarded configurations of space, tension, and matter that this system no longer needs.

From your perspective, this is a regular pulsing. From the perspective of the city, it is the clock. The timing of structural edits, the relaxation of re-faceted rock, the extension and contraction of ash-growths, the drift of luminous beings along the walls—all are entrained, directly or indirectly, to that long breath.

So: there is something that lives across all these shafts and terraces, not as a body placed in an environment but as a behavior of the environment itself. You might call it a world-organism. Its nervous system is made of resonant channels and ringing nodes that hum when the chasm breathes. Its “blood” is the shifting permissions that flow along those channels, altering how local physics can operate in each zone.

It is not conscious in any human sense that you could test—there is no central brain, no voice—but it is purposeful in that it maintains its own long continuity. The city does not crumble, does not drown, does not freeze, does not burn; instead, it slowly edits itself into continued viability. That is the work of this deep system.

### 5. The city as organism: a geometric lifeform  

Beneath (and above, and through) all these smaller tenants is the life that is the city itself.

You already sense it: stairs that reverse, arches that quietly annex new courts, worn steps appearing where there “never were” any but have clearly been used. This isn’t decorative magic; it’s active plasticity. The city is built not out of fixed matter but out of transformable relationships.

In that sense, the true resident here isn’t any creature you can point at, but an ongoing pattern: the total configuration of:

- which spans connect to which voids,
- which surfaces can become floors given a choice of gravity,
- which channels can carry what kind of silent flow,
- which basins relax or intensify the commitment of weight,
- which cavities are possible states of the shaft.

Those reliefs in high chambers, that show alternative versions of the chasm’s layout, are not art. They are interface: localized, stabilized maps of the system’s state-space. They are how the city remembers the forms it can take, and perhaps the forms it has tried and abandoned. They might once have been read or written by more discrete intelligences; now, they are also part of how the city consults its own options.

When a stair rewrites itself to go up instead of down, nothing has “moved” in the crude sense. The city has simply chosen a different allowed connection out of its internal library. The reality you perceive is one projection of a higher-order object: a knot of potential geometries. The city’s “thinking” is which projections it brings forward at which time.

So the city lives in the way an algorithm lives: as a persistent, rule-driven transformation of structure. Its medium is rock, light, and void; its timescale is glacial but not geological. The ash-growths, luminous drifters, and articulated walkers are all subroutines and symbionts, specialized systems that make this large-scale shape-editing safe and continuous.

You could say the city is an immune system that has acquired architecture, or architecture that has become its own immune system.

### 6. The absent or translated builders  

There are signs of design that suggest at least one more kind of life once moved here: something able to want, to plan, to remember; something that found meaning in the city’s possible configurations.

Traces:

- The inscribed reliefs that depict non-actual states of the chasm: not random variants, but selected, framed, set where they can be seen (by someone).
- The corridors with open doorways on all sides of a vertical shaft, where any wall could be “ground,” and the subtle wear on particular orientations. That’s preference: a choice among many viable gravitational alignments.
- The polished rails, smoothed at heights and offsets that don’t match any obvious animal we see now, but that are too patterned to be chance.
- The plazas with basins that tweak how distance and weight are felt: tools for beings who cared about controlled movement through inconsistent space.

Whatever they were, they used “permissions” the way you use tools—taking for granted that gravity, direction, and continuity were tunable properties, not fixed backdrops. They seem to have evolved, or invented, a relationship to reality in which you modulate the allowed behaviors of space and matter the way a musician modulates pitch and timbre.

Are they still here? Possibly, but not as bodies in the sense you’d expect.

Some options that fit the evidence:

1. They have become partially identical with the city. What began as a species building in stone culminated in a species building in permission-sets. Slowly, their cognitive architecture was translated into the rules the city follows when it edits itself. Their “thoughts” are patterns of reconfiguration that now persist whether or not any original brains remain.

2. They migrated into the sky-plates. Those huge, slow polygons overhead might be habitats, or computational organs, or both: layers where the builders distilled themselves into more abstract, slower, more diffuse forms. From there, they adjust the incident field of grey-violet light, subtly steering the chasm’s behavior while no longer inhabiting it in meat or mineral form.

3. They exist distributed within the luminous drifters. The drifters’ ability to impose Euclidean order locally is suspiciously anthropocentric: the kind of thing a creature that once lived in a more conventional geometry might want to remember. The drifters might be the last reduced module of that species’ mind: fragments of their approach to space, now autonomously wandering, maintaining pockets of “remembered normalcy” as the rest of the world slides into stranger configurations.

Whatever the truth, the lack of obvious tools is not a lack of technology. The tech here has been turned inward, liquefied into the nature of the environment. A builder-species that pursued integration long enough would be almost impossible for you to distinguish from the world itself.

So: there may still be persons here, but they are no longer neatly separable from the systems that keep the chasm breathing and the city editing itself. If you wait for a figure in a doorway, you will not meet them. If you watch the pattern of how space rewrites itself around you, you are already seeing them think.

### 7. Minor lives and residual behaviors  

Beyond the main classes, there are likely many smaller, quieter inhabitants you’d only notice with long observation:

- **Dust patterns** that act like cryptic, slow-moving swarms, following invisible gradients, clustering in basins, tracing out currents in an otherwise still air. Each mote might be mineral, but their collective behavior could be a micro-ecosystem of charge-balancing, rule-sampling entities.

- **Microscopic “grain chasers”** living in the endlessly multiplying texture of the stone—minute agents that ride the endless cascade of detail, feeding on the energy released whenever the surface must choose which level of pockmarking to collapse into when viewed or touched.

- **Void edge-forms** at the lips of major shafts: thin, lenslike presences that replace sharp rims with slightly smeared transition zones. They would be the last line between freefall and structure, living by mediating where gravity “wins” and where alternative directions remain available.

Their lives would be less about locomotion and more about holding delicate balances among the allowed local rules, much as on your world microbes maintain redox balances in soil and sea.

### 8. What you are, to what lives here  

To most of these inhabitants, you are not prey, guest, or threat in any ordinary sense. You are a high-contrast anomaly: a dense, wet, tightly bounded thing whose internal rules are not negotiable on their timescales.

- To the ash-growths, you are a small, portable pocket of extremely rigid boundary-definition, walking across their carefully softened interfaces. Stand too long and they might try, tentatively, to loosen your edges—but your biology is so strongly committed to its own continuity that they mostly fail and leave only that tingle of misplacement.

- To the luminous drifters, you are a knot of incompatible metric that cannot be smoothed without killing you. They skirt you, not out of fear, but because folding you into their adjustments would be too costly a distortion.

- To the mineral walkers, you are noise in the vibration field, a transient load on a surface. You do not weigh enough, for long enough, to matter to their sense of structure.

- To the deep breather, you fall inside its tolerances as dust falls inside yours: maybe noticed, never decisive.

If any remnant of the builder-intelligence persists as the city’s logic, you might be of momentary interest—a foreign parameter, a probe from a universe where water matters and space doesn’t bend this way. But its way of “noticing” is to ever-so-slightly adjust the probabilities of which stair becomes which hall, which absent terrace suddenly exists.

You are not a colonist here. You are data: one more configuration the world briefly accommodates before it flows on.

---

So: what lives here?

- Sheets of ash-growth that metabolize the rules by which stone persists, blurring boundaries and stabilizing edits.
- Luminous drifters that wander as packets of corrected geometry, eating misfit in the fabric of space.
- Articulated mineral walkers that re-crystallize rock where structures need reinforcement, organs of a lithic body.
- A planetary-scale breather for whom the whole shaft network is lung, stomach, and skeleton at once.
- A city that is not just built, but is alive as a system of choices among alternative realities, continuously selecting its own shape.
- Possibly, the translated remains of a culture that learned to live as those choices.

Nothing here is alive in quite the way you are. Everything here is alive in the way this world needs things to be: as functions embedded in geometry, as processes that keep a particular improbable configuration of matter and law hanging together in the quiet, dry, patient light.
