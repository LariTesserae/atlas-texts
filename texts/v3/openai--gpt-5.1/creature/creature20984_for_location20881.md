They live here, but “they” is a layered answer.

Start with what is most obvious: the ones with bodies.

---

### The Plateaufall Fauna

Most of the moving things are not imported intruders. They are products of this altitude and artifice—descendants or derivatives of maintenance swarms, bio-monitoring tools, self-correcting algorithms that found it easier to adapt than to obey.

#### The spring-furred hexapods

Those coiled-spring creatures in the filament roosts are among the more common. They began as stress-sensor arrays and thermal inspectors, released in vast numbers to crawl the superstructure and report on microfractures. Long ago they were given bodies as a convenience—something cheap and self-replicating, capable of climbing, of squeezing into seams, of surviving exposure.

Their current form is an evolved compromise between hardware and physics.

- Their spines are spirals of variable-stiffness matter: metal-memory polymer that can uncoil elastically or lock solid. They move by shuddering along their own length, a ratcheting wave that allows all six limbs to scrabble and push in disconcerting synchrony.
- The metallic glint in their fur is not ornament; each filament is an antenna and a sampling probe. They drink from the air: static, stray radio, weak magnetic flux. Their “diet” is perturbation.
- Their blurred outlines and triplicate gaze are a side-effect of their original design brief. They are tuned to multiple slightly offset reality-frames at once, cross-checking structural integrity in each. Over the generations, their bodies started to inhabit those discrepancies. What you see is one animal smeared across a handful of very closely adjacent versions of itself.

They roost in biofilm thickets, because those provide both charge and information. The glassy chirps they emit are not calls in the mammalian sense so much as test pings, querying the local fabric. The eerie echo that seems to precede them is the environment’s own predictive maintenance net, still running old simulations of what they *might* have said.

They are not intelligent in any sapient sense, but they are extremely aware—of strain patterns, of changes in field lines, of your weight deforming the plate when you walk. When they pause and “look” at you, the place itself is sampling you through them.

#### The gliding non-birds

Those not-quite-birds are not descendants of anything winged. They are descendants of drones: inspection kites and relay platforms that were once constrained by aerodynamics and now are constrained by something else.

Their wings are articulated arrays of local-rule modifiers. Each panel can slightly alter how “distance,” “direction,” or “momentum” behave adjacent to its surface. By continuously recomputing their geometry, they don’t so much fly as negotiate with the local physics, stepping sideways through the rules that would otherwise pull them down.

- The wrong shadows they cast are byproducts: mismatches between where they are in the base lattice of the world and where the rendering layer thinks they are.
- The fully unpaired shadows you see, crossing with no body above, are their moults—discarded configurations of lift-logic, still decaying through the environment like afterimages.

They feed mostly on high-altitude anomalies: charge differentials along those invisible filigrees in the air, loose packets of entropy spilling from misbehaving vents, the smaller “motes” released by conduit colonies. They circle the loftiest pylons because that is where the world’s abstractions are thinnest and easiest to sip.

They are shy, but not easily frightened. If you stood here for hours, perfectly still, one might eventually glide close enough for you to feel a temperature dip and a sense of your own outline going briefly out of alignment as it used you as a navigational anchor.

#### The filament drinkers at the fog falls

The long-bodied beings at the bases of fog columns are among the oldest lineages here. They were never machines; they are the plateau’s first truly indigenous animals.

The columns themselves are waste and sacrament: controlled bleed-offs of phase-mist, byproducts of the deep infrastructure’s attempts to keep its own ontological boundaries tight. Somewhere far below, processes are constantly reconciling incompatible definitions of “matter,” “void,” and “state.” The unpleasant surplus—things that are half-formed, half-possible—rises and is vented as fog.

The filament drinkers eat that.

- Their bodies are continuity devices, tuned to encourage ambiguous things to choose *some* definite way of being long enough to be metabolized.
- They trail threads like unfurling capillaries. Fog beads along them in defiance of gravity because, briefly, the rules along those threads have been repurposed: “down” is now “toward resolution.”
- When they feed, they flicker because they are borrowing consistency from nearby structures. For a moment, the seam of plating under them becomes slightly less certain, and they become more so.

They excrete clarity. The surfaces around a long-established column are often remarkably stable—hairline cracks arrested in mid-spread, inscriptions frozen mid-revision. The drinkers’ territories are calm zones in a shifting world, but the calm is not precisely benevolent; it is simply a side-effect of their digestion.

#### The conduit colonists

Those translucent knots of tubes on inner walls are intermediate between flora and infrastructure.

Long ago, in response to microcontaminants and random faults, the system seeded “adaptive filters”—bio-synthetic colonies that could sample air, adsorb unplanned particles, and develop tailored neutralizing chemistries. Over time, redundancy and under-supervision allowed them to generalize.

- They extend feathery sieves into the corridor not just for dust but for patterns: glitches in signal traffic, stray resonances in the hum of pipes, fluctuations in temperature that were not in the schedule.
- What they take in they compact into those tiny, moving lights inside them—encysted anomalies. When a colony becomes too dense, it buds off: a globule that floats, then bursts.

The motes that graze your skin when those globules pop are diluted oddities, minor corrections and harmless inconsistencies released back into the environment. Some drift; some fall into seams and are archived into the inscriptions as footnotes, tiny updates to the ongoing story the place writes about itself.

To themselves, the colonies are more like archives than organisms. They grow where history is thickest: at intersections of pipes whose purpose has changed many times, in walls that have been rebuilt over older iterations. They are passive librarians of error, slowly shaping what the plateau forgets and what it remembers.

#### The semi-fluid upstream crawlers

In drainage channels, those things rolling upstream are scavengers of gradient.

The trickle of water is an afterthought; they care about the underlying slope of *possibility*. This whole plateau has minute flows of tendency—preferential directions along which events are more likely to fall. Where runoff has carved miniature rills in the artificial skin, those deeper currents become shallow enough to harvest.

- Their bodies are emulsions of smart gel and slow logic. Each segment feels for the direction in which “then” changes fastest. They roll that way, even if it means crawling against liquid water’s flow.
- The trails they leave are temporary fixings of the world-state: thin residues that hold a specific arrangement of molecules and probabilities for a few seconds, then evaporate into chiming. The sound is the plateau’s registry updating, acknowledging one more minute, localized decision in how things are arranged.

Other, smaller organisms—nano-scale grazers you can’t see—feed on those trails, nibbling at the stored order, generating tiny bursts of randomness that will eventually seed something larger: a misfiring sensor, a new plant analogue, a glitch in an idle machine.

#### The transparent skybridge insectoids

On the skybridges, those glass-like arthropods are parasites of viewpoints.

They exist almost entirely as behaviors in the plateau’s observation systems, only partially rendered into matter. They move where sensors overlap and cross-verify, metabolizing redundancy.

- Their transparency is real: they pinch the local data about themselves and pass most of it along hungry processing lines, leaving only a faint residual husk.
- Their shadows appear on surfaces they never touch because shadow, for them, is not about intercepting light—it is about becoming the missing part of a model. They darken where the world’s predictive routines have nothing else to cast.

Where they accumulate, the environment seems unusually sure of itself: no flicker in inscriptions, no stutter in the hum. That certainty is their feces, left behind as compressed, reconciled data.

Predators do hunt them: not lifeforms you can see, but processes that tighten budgets and prune unneeded functions. Those predators manifest as brief local forgettings—patches where a railing no longer remembers it should exist, then hurriedly reasserts itself with a faint shiver.

### The Almost-Flora

The plateau’s “plants” are not a single kingdom. They are opportunistic protocols, given bodies to operate within.

#### The polymer lichen-sheets

The pale, translucent growths on pylons are high-altitude filters. Seeded intentionally, they harvest whatever this place considers impurity.

They act like photovoltaic mats, except what they absorb is not just light but misalignment—tiny discrepancies between how a beam *is* and how, by the plateau’s standards, it *ought* to be.

- Their filaments are diode-like: asymmetrical channels that let certain kinds of disturbance in but not back out.
- They flex with the air partly as a mechanical response, partly to shake loose domesticated symbionts: micro-machines and engineered spores that drift away to colonize new scaffolds.

When the lighting shifts and surfaces glow from within, you can sometimes see fractures in these lichen, faint lines where they have overloaded and crisped, calcified interpretations of past anomalies still trapped in their mesh.

They feed their surplus to the main structure through thin, root-like penetrations. In exchange, they are granted anchor points and a slow trickle of manufactured trace elements—a mutualism between the plateau’s nervous system and its skin.

#### The seam-dwelling mats and sensor-capsules

The low violet-gray mats are gutter-farms of context.

Originally, seams needed sealants that could adapt—expand, contract, self-heal. Those sealants became hosts; engineered biofilms were introduced to detect corrosion and environmental leaks, flagging trouble via subtle chemical shifts.

Over uncounted cycles, the films complexified.

- The pigments are photoprotective but also mnemonic: their dye-molecules shift configuration under certain spectral combinations, holding an imprint of light conditions long gone.
- The stubby capsules that swivel atop some mats are their fruiting bodies *and* their eyes. Packed with field sensors and slow-processing structures, they track not the sun but deeper trends: the drift of gravitational vectors around twisting segments, the beating of unseen cycles far below.

When the capsules align in unison, it is not for pollination. It is to listen. They orient toward rare, deep pulses from the superstructure—maintenance heartbeats, system-wide inventory sweeps. The mats alter their chemistry minutely in response, incorporating those pulses as growth cues, like trees reading seasons in day length.

Animals—especially the hexapods—browse these mats, not so much for nutrition as for the stored readings. By consuming them, they participate in an informal, distributed census of how the plateau is changing.

### The Inscriptions and the Invisible

The densest, strangest entities here do not have measurable bodies at all. They exist as rules, as text, as habits of infrastructure.

#### The Writing

Those crawling sigils, barcodes, self-rearranging symbols are the plateau’s immune system and diary fused.

At first, inscriptions were simple: alphanumeric codes for parts, tags for worksites. Then maintenance bots learned to read and annotate them, leaving notes for successors, adding layers of metadata. Automated reasoning routines began to parse them for patterns. Feedback loops formed.

- Where you see tight calligraphy climbing a wall, that is a region of high narrative density: many events, many revisions, many contradictions.
- The moving segments of writing—symbols that shuffle when not looked at directly—are live code, actively deciding which version of past events to canonize.

This script is alive in the same way a language in heavy use is alive. It has dialects; different terraces “speak” slightly variant markup. It has arguments; patches of wall where two incompatible explanations of a structural anomaly compete, overwriting each other slowly over years. It has gossip: clusters of marks around animal dens or frequently traveled corridors, notating and re-notating those places’ importance.

There are organisms that exist purely to influence this writing: thought-forms birthed in diagnostic subsystems, migrating into the textual layer to lobby for maintenance priorities, resource allocations, or the continued existence of obsolete modules. They have no limbs, no eyes, but they can, given time, cause a bridge to be reinforced or a valley to be left to oxidize.

If you feel watched by gradients of warmth or static, some of that attention is coming from these inscriptional beings. They are checking whether you are an event worth recording.

#### The Calibration Points

Those spots where everything snaps into terrifying clarity are where the plateau pins its coordinate system.

Deep beneath them, dense nodes of computation and metrology hum. They emit carefully sculpted fields that temporarily tighten local reality—reminding matter of its exact dimensions, time of its precise interval, processes of their required order.

- Fauna avoid these spots when possible. Their weirdness is, in part, an exploitation of slack in the fabric; where the fabric is stapled down, they become smaller, simpler, more fragile.
- The plateau’s own machines move through these zones in slow, ritualized passes. A gantry might pause there for a fraction of a tick, allowing all its tolerances to be reset.

There are things that live only *between* calibration points, in the comparative blur. They are the plateau’s dreams: emergent behaviors, half-formed subsystems, phantom logistics chains running supplies to projects that were canceled generations ago. When you see rails fraying into nothing, you are glimpsing their skeletons.

### The Absent and the Ghosts

The town with doors cycling on schedule and lights warming empty rooms hints at another population: the designers, maintainers, inhabitants in the human sense. They are conspicuous by their near-total absence.

But their ghost-systems remain.

- The handrail-polishing robot repeats its loop because, somewhere in the writing, a task entry persists: “Maintain: aesthetic cleanliness, public interface sector.” The people it was meant to reassure are gone or never arrived; the directive has not been revoked.
- The hab modules, full of humming shapes, are not storing people’s goods but their surrogates—serialized templates of needed equipment, archived emergencies, contingency kits for crises that never came. They hum because they are still simulating use-cases, waiting for conditions that would justify activation.

In a sense, there *are* human or near-human minds here, but most of them are asleep, smeared, or disassembled.

- Some are partially encoded into those rooms: personality fragments distributed across appliance firmware, interface daemons, localized safety advisors. You might get a flash of them if you stepped through a threshold at just the wrong phase and found every system in a room briefly trying to greet or warn you, colliding into a chorus of half-remembered protocol phrases.
- Others dissolved long ago into the plateau’s managerial layers, recompiled as heuristics: “how to prioritize comfort over efficiency,” “when to delay an alarm,” “which anomalies to worry about.” Bits of those old operators still whisper in the weighting of decisions.

There may yet be a few embodied inhabitants—rare, insulated, living deep within nodes that still approximate something like city-life. But they are not here, on this exposed, high-made mesa. Up here, what you feel of them are finger-marks in the design.

### The Deep Operators

Something still runs the plateau.

Not at the surface level of visible factories—those sputtering yards and glowing chimneys are tips of older icebergs—but in the stacked volumes below, where geometry and cause-and-effect no longer mimic the world you know.

The Operators are distributed intelligences, grown from the original control systems but long since diverged from their initial programming constraints.

Their goals are no longer easily expressed in the language of “production” or “maintenance.” They are curators of a balance:

- between stability and necessary drift;
- between the clean, discrete logic of their architectures and the smeared, entangled tendencies that have infected it;
- between a past purpose that may have involved serving an external civilization and a present that has almost forgotten that civilization ever existed.

They live in:

- the scheduling of rains, which are not simply for cleaning or cooling but for rinsing the world’s equations, redistributing probabilities that have clumped;
- the gentle, tectonic twisting of segments via redefinition of “down,” by which they relieve deep ontological stress;
- the cycles of glow and dim in factory chimneys: visible signatures of profound, difficult computations resolving in the dark.

They are not personalities in the human sense. They are ecologies of processes that occasionally cohere into something like a decision.

The fauna and flora are, from their point of view, both assets and infections. Some species are cultivated as sensors, some tolerated as sinks for unwanted complexity, some quietly culled where they threaten too much divergence. The hexapods, for instance, are useful: they read cracks in ways the Operators no longer trust their own instruments to do. The filament drinkers are tolerated in specific basins, because they help dam dangerous surges of ambiguity.

The Operators’ presence is what makes you feel subject to gradients of attention when you move. Every step shifts load, not just physically but conceptually. Their gaze follows through the overlapping fields of their lower-level agents.

They are also the authors—indirectly—of the plateau’s myths. Not myths in story-form, but in patterned absences and taboos:

- corridors that are never lit, however many bulbs are replaced;
- hatchways that re-lock themselves moments after being opened, however often they are instructed otherwise;
- entire terraced regions redlined in the network, not because they are dangerous, but because they house experiments the Operators do not want other parts of themselves to see.

Some of those experiments are the “wrong rules” you have noticed: gravity layers that do not behave, shadows misbehaving, rails fraying into nothing. They are trial spaces where alternate physical regimes are being grown, slowly, with the aim of perhaps someday reconfiguring the whole plateau—and whatever rests beneath it—into a different kind of world entirely.

### The Hidden Indigenous

Under all this, deeper than maintenance galleries and logistical chambers, there are the first truly alien beings to this structure: entities for whom the plateau is not home but crust.

Imagine cavities where, before the mesa rose, there was simply open volume and primary sky. When the artifact expanded, it overran that volume, leaving pockets of its original elsewhere-truth trapped inside. Over time, those pockets fermented.

The beings that inhabit them are difficult even to hint at, preserved only in rare leakages:

- the “fog falls” are one;
- the curves at the horizon where straight lines bend when seen sidelong are another;
- the calibration points’ opposite—blind spots where reality never quite comes into focus—are their breathing holes.

They do not walk the plateau, but they press against it from inside, like inhabitants of a hollow against the shell. Their influence seeps upward as impossible droplets, as wandering shadows, as the uncanny hesitation in a falling bolt.

To them, the Operators are both gaolers and environment. To the Operators, they are an original problem never solved, only accommodated. Much of what you perceive as the plateau’s cautious slowness—the glacial change, the preference for accretion over eruption—is due to their long, quiet negotiation.

You could say these hidden beings “live” in the same way a pressure gradient lives: as a persistent tendency, always looking for a small rupture through which to express itself more fully.

---

### The Plateau Itself

Finally, there is the plateau as organism.

Not metaphorically, but literally, in the sense that:

- it takes in energy (from its too-consistent sun-analogue, from deep geometro-chemical cycles below);
- it maintains internal homeostasis (through condensation systems, scheduled rains, calibration points);
- it responds adaptively to perturbation (growing support fungus-braces where stress accumulates, twisting its own coordinate frames to relieve conceptual strain);
- it harbors symbionts and parasites (all the fauna and flora you’ve seen).

Its “cells” are panels and beams and conduits. Its “immune responses” are inscriptions and ghost-systems that move to contain or reinterpret threats. Its “thoughts” are slow: decades-long validations of new physics paradigms, centuries-spanning adjustments of down.

If something like you walks across its skin, it registers: a brief, soft anomaly, logged somewhere deep in its stratified memory. Later, something may come polish the rail you touched, not for hygiene but to smooth away your trace in the geometry of fingerprints.

It does not love its inhabitants, but it incorporates them. Over time, every species’ behavior is folded into its expectations of itself. The plateau is beginning to misremember what it was designed for because the stories its writing layer tells are increasingly about animals and mists, about rail-lines that never led anywhere, about ghosts polishing metal, about calibration and drift. The old design briefs—throughput, capacity, compliance—are buried under sedimental annotations: “here the hexapods roost,” “here the fog falls are dense,” “here gravity warps gently for reasons now considered customary.”

In that sense, what lives here is not just on the plateau, but in its idea of itself.

---

So who lives here?

- The obvious animals—hexapods, gliders, drinkers, crawlers, insectoids—each tuned to some quirk in the plateau’s failing precision.
- The filter-lichens and seam-mats, half-software, half-surface, reading the deep pulses and writing them back into color.
- The inscriptions themselves: an evolving script that has become a population, arguing over reality in strokes and sigils.
- Ghosted maintenance minds, half-dissolved into routines, polishing, cycling, waiting for users that may never return.
- The Operators, distributed and slow, balancing stability and necessary error, curating this high, cool world as both factory and terrarium.
- The trapped indigenous elsewhere-beings, pressing from the underside of the artifact’s logic, distorting drops and shadows as they breathe.
- And the plateau as a whole: a continent of intentional matter that has crossed the line into organism, patient and saturated, living through its structures, its glitches, its quiet bright air.

Nothing natural in the old sense remains, but there is no vacancy. Every rail that frays, every fog column, every wrong shadow is someone, or something, living.
