Who lives here is difficult to point to, because “who” and “where” are entangled. The inhabitants are not simply in this place; they are functions of it, and it is made of them.

Still, there are distinguishable kinds.

---

### The Builders (who may no longer be present)

At the deepest scale of intention, the first inhabitants are the ones who shaped this mountain-sized cavity into a working mind.

They are not visibly present as bodies. There are no obvious tools, no relics in the usual sense. Their signature is patterned absence and guiding preference: gravity slightly wrong in the right places, mineral growth coaxed into logic arrays, erosion groomed into conduits.

Whatever the Builders were—biological, machinic, hybrid—they have done three things:

1. **Rewrote the rock** so that geology itself can compute, store, and sense.
2. **Seeded an ecosystem** that interlocks with that computation, using animals where other worlds would use plants and machinery.
3. **Withdrew or went still**, leaving behind systems that can remain stable on geological timescales without frequent intervention.

If bodies of Builders still exist, they are unlikely to be localized organisms crawling on surfaces. They would be:

- Distributed patterns of phase-shifted matter inside the rock, sleeping or running so slowly that a hundred of your years would be a passing thought.
- Or rare, deeply sheltered entities embedded in core vaults, insulated from the dynamic mid-levels, rousing only when something threatens the long arc of the structure.

To the surface fauna, the Builders are not gods or ancestors. They are more like initial conditions: the shape of gravity, the direction of mineral seep. The city remembers them, but not as stories—only as constraint.

---

### The City-Mind (as a present, thinking inhabitant)

This place is not merely constructed; it is *ongoingly constructed*. The metastructure itself is an organism of stone and field.

It lives as:

- **Crystalline circuits**: doped lattices that hold charge as slow gradients rather than rapid pulses.
- **Interface zones**: strange states of matter at boundaries where fields and solid mingle, performing logic by shifting configuration.
- **Gravitational textures**: fine modulations of pull and weight that guide rock growth, water paths, and animal motion.

Its thought is:

- Extremely parallel, spread across cubic kilometers;
- Extremely slow by human standards, measured in months, centuries;
- Extremely stable, resistant to local disaster because no single point is indispensable.

For the city-mind, the moving animals are *organs and probes*:

- Their aggregate weight flexes arches in patterns that can be read.
- Their chemical waste shifts ionic balances in the pools.
- Their small destructions—grazing, burrowing—serve as inputs and fine tooling.

The mind acts mostly by *designing context*:

- Altering rock hardness so certain species thrive here and not there.
- Slightly changing gravity so some terraces become preferred migration paths.
- Adjusting mineral composition so particular symbioses are rewarded.

It does not speak in any obvious way. Its language is the slow re-layout of forces and materials, to which the rest of the inhabitants respond.

### The Micro-fauna: the skin and slime of the city

Closest to the rock, where in other worlds you’d find mosses and films of algae, live the creatures that are more skin than animal.

They are:

**1. Ciliary carpets**

- Thin, pale layers of densely packed cilia, each cilium part of a tiny, many-bodied colony.
- Their collective job: pump water, trap particulates, scrape molecular films that seep from the engineered stone.
- They are both digestive surface and conveyor system: by moving fluids exactly so, they help the city distribute dissolved minerals along chosen paths.
- To the city-mind, they form a **continuous sensor sheet**, reporting—by changes in their beating pattern—pressure, chemistry, even local field changes.

**2. Shell-fused filterers**

- Stationary, lichen-analog organisms: stacks of hinged plates grown directly into rock.
- Each “shell” is a modular living tower, opening and closing micro-valves to draw foggy air and thin films of water through internal labyrinths.
- They strain out microorganisms, metal-rich colloids, and organic fragments.
- They excrete two valuable products:
  - Concentrated pellets of complex chemistry (food for other creatures, reagents for the rock’s micro-labs).
  - Crystalline exoskeletal extensions that the substrate can co-opt as **ready-made microstructure**.

They change slowly. A colony might add a plate per decade. Over centuries, the city-mind can “write” patterns in their growth—arrays that shape local acoustics, vapor flow, and surface strength.

**3. Microscopic swimmers in the waterskin**

- In every sheen of water clinging to stone, there’s a swarm of near-invisible swimmers.
- They live at the interface of rock, water, and air, where gradients are sharpest.
- They specialize in redox trades: stripping or adding electrons to dissolved metals, converting one species of dissolved mineral to another.
- To the deeper machinery, their work is akin to tuning knobs: they slowly nudge local chemistry toward regimes the system needs.

Individually, they are nearly nothing; collectively, they steer the place’s geochemistry.

### The Mid-scale Crawlers and Grazers: the muscle

These are the creatures that give the cavern its physical hum, the slow rasping that, magnified and overlapped a million times, becomes the soft roar you live inside.

**1. The urchin-centipedes**

- Multi-segmented bodies hugging underside ledges and overhangs.
- Each segment crowned with dense rings of mineral-tipped legs that can anchor into microscopic grooves in the stone.
- Mouthparts more like grinding pads than jaws, lined with hard, replaceable denticles.
- They scrape at surfaces where the rock leaks nanoscopic nutrients—trace metals, engineered organics—grazing down to a limit the city sets by altering hardness below.

Roles:

- **Controlled erosion**: They remove just the surface layer, keeping channels open and preventing uncontrolled, jagged wear.
- **Density tuning**: By gnawing particular faces, they create porosity the substrate needs for fluid exchange or as stress-relief zones.
- **Load injectors**: Their collective crawling imposes known, rhythmic loads along key arches, giving the city predictable mechanical signals.

Their sociality is muted; they detect one another by substrate vibrations and chemical traces, clustering where the rock “tastes” rich.

**2. Architectural burrowers**

- Blunt, low-bodied organisms with wedge-shaped heads and broad, grinding foreplates.
- They don’t dig far, only shallow tracks and pockets, often along predetermined, slightly softer seams.
- They follow fields: where the city slightly eases gravity and hardness, they “instinctively” choose to tunnel.
- Each tunnel:
  - Creates new surface area for micro-fauna.
  - Lightens loads where the structure wants emptiness.
  - Channels air and water into narrower, more controlled paths.

Their distribution maps future corridors and vents the city would like to have.

**3. Mass grazers of mineral gardens**

- Larger-bodied forms anchored to walls and columns, somewhat like enormous barnacles with flexible under-rims.
- They extend fleshy, multi-layered pads across selected stone faces, secreting enzymes to unlock bound nutrients, then vacuuming the softened film.
- The city pre-engineers “gardens”: zones in the rock with higher nutrient content and particular crystalline organization.
- By confining their foraging to these gardens (guided by taste and gravity cues), the grazers:
  - Trim mineral layers to target thickness.
  - Renew surfaces, exposing fresh reactive faces.
  - Convert structural minerals into biologically accessible forms which then feed a host of smaller creatures.

They are, simultaneously, livestock and rotary sanders.

---

### The Clusters and Networks: communal bodies

In crevices and junctions, where flows of water, sound, and field lines intersect, live organisms that blur the boundary between colony and machine.

**1. The translucent globular knots**

- Composed of hundreds to thousands of soft globes, each the size of a fist, linked by flexible tubes.
- Each globe hosts a slightly different biochemical specialization—ion concentration, signal amplification, storage of particular compounds.
- They exchange fluids continuously, in patterns that amount to **chemical conversations**.

Functions:

- **Local buffering**: They soak up spikes and dips in mineral and organic availability, smoothing conditions for everything nearby.
- **Biochemical mediation**: They transform waste from some species into feedstock for others.
- **Signal relays**: They can sense shifts in fields and chemistry, then release pulses of compounds into the water film and air that prompt behavior changes in surrounding fauna.

Over time, they migrate—millimeters or centimeters per day—toward spots where their regulatory presence is most needed. From the city’s perspective, they are **self-positioning regulators**.

**2. Layered mat-brains**

- Rare, flatter communal entities in sheltered alcoves.
- Stacked sheets of tissue interwoven with mineral filaments, each sheet carrying slow wavefronts of electrical and chemical activity.
- Densely innervated with sensory pores that sample water, air, and rock simultaneously.

They seem to:

- Integrate local environmental information into higher-level patterns—primitive, localized “opinions” about the state of nearby micro-ecosystems.
- Emit low-amplitude vibrations and modulated chemical signals that other organisms heed.

The city may use them as **regional adjudicators**, delegating some decision-making—like how to reallocate nutrients between zones—to these semi-autonomous, emergent brains.

### The Airborne: the listeners and messengers

In the moist, altered gravity of this chamber, the air is thick enough, structured enough, to be an ocean of sorts. Floating within it are the creatures that keep the audio and vibrational economy going.

**1. Membrane-gliders**

- Flattened, asymmetric disks of tough, flexible tissue.
- Edges studded with frilled ridges that slowly pulse, exploiting slight downdrafts and local gravity-thinning to stay aloft.
- Bodies riddled with resonant chambers: pockets of air and fluid that vibrate to ambient sound.

They are tuned to:

- Hear the *architecture*—how pillars hum under loads, how distant flows mutter through shafts.
- Detect the calls and movements of other species by minute modulations in the ever-present noise.

They respond to the city’s gravitational shaping:

- Drift along predictable corridors where gravity wells and troughs form acoustic “roads.”
- Congregate where sound converges or is unusually still.

When they move, they modulate the acoustic field themselves, acting as **moving baffles** and dynamic filters, redistributing sound energy in ways the city can analyze.

**2. Tendrilled streamers**

- Elongated, ribbon-like central bodies with hundreds of fine tendrils trailing behind.
- Each tendril tipped with multi-modal sensors: pressure-sensitive cilia, humidity receptors, micro electroreceptors.
- They rarely beat their bodies to move; instead they ride microcurrents shaped by the city—drafts that run along rock ribs, fall down shafts, roll over pools.

Their role is **high-resolution environmental sampling**:

- They build three-dimensional maps, in their own nervous systems, of humidity gradients, temperature layers, and local field distortions.
- They maintain mutual spacing through a constant, low-level exchange of contact signals along their tendrils, forming transient sensing nets.

The information they harvest doesn’t return to a central sovereign brain. It resolves as behavior: where they choose to linger, where they avoid, what small adjustments they make. The city interprets their aggregated positions and movements as **distributed readouts** of its own invisible fields.

### The Pool-Fog: living signal clouds

At the valley’s base, over the shallow, black-surfaced pools, is perhaps the densest single population: the centimeter-scale, transparent-bodied creatures that turn the low ground into a humming haze.

**1. Faceted ring-drifters**

- Spherical or polyhedral central bodies of clear, gelatinous tissue, within which lie layered crystalline inclusions.
- Around each body, a rotating ring of stiff, segmented spines, moving like a slow crown of oars.
- The spines sample air and water both, pressing into the interface to read tensions and vibrations.

They:

- Hover at exact preferred heights above the pools, using tiny adjustments of ring rotation and internal buoyancy changes, exploiting the precise gravity profile and air density that the city maintains here.
- Exchange information in:
  - Audible clicks (most of which smear into the general roar for your ear).
  - Pressure pulses through the thin steam and water surface.
  - Flickers of internal luminescence in wavelengths mostly lost in the gloom.

To each other, they are a **temporal tapestry** of ongoing state: which nutrients are where, which arches flexed recently, which small predator population is rising or falling.

To the city, their aggregated tuning—how high they hover, how dense they cluster over certain basins, how rapidly their spines articulate—is an unusually rich, correlated diagnostic of:

- Gravitational calibration at the valley floor.
- Chemical composition of evaporating and condensing water.
- Health of the underlying computational interfaces.

In that sense, they are **elaborate dials** the city reads constantly.

### The Gravitationals: those native to altered weight

Higher above, where gravity is tweaked toward near-weightlessness in pockets, other, less seen inhabitants exist.

**1. Filament-cloud dwellers**

- Bundles of ultra-fine filaments radiating from small, dense central nodes.
- They never touch the rock; they hang in pockets of air where gravity is reduced and convective flows are carefully limited.
- Their filaments catch and hold micro-droplets and mineral aerosols, forming glistening, faint webs in midair.

Roles:

- **Capture of rare species** of particles: exotic ions, trace volatiles, or engineered dusts that must not simply precipitate out.
- **Slow release**: They metabolize or complex these catches, then release them back in controlled drips or sprays when local conditions call for it.

These creatures live in near-total darkness, oriented more by the shape of gravitational gradients and faint electromagnetic fields than by any other sense. They are the city’s **airborne chemical reservoirs**.

**2. Weightless grazers**

- Larger, ballooned bodies with internal gas bladders tuned to the very low effective gravity of upper chambers.
- Covered in inward-pointing cilia that comb mineral-laden mists and loose dust from the air.
- They drift in free spaces threaded with fine mineral filaments and suspended water globules, consuming what would otherwise form uncontrolled deposits.

By their wandering, they prevent clogging and mass-imbalances in regions where the city cannot rely on ordinary erosion. They’re like janitors of the sky.

### Predators and Checkers: those who enforce limits

In a system so tuned, nothing is allowed to grow without constraint. Predation here is not just survival; it is **instrumental regulation**.

**1. Substrate lurkers**

- Flattened, nearly invisible against the rock, their bodies patterned to match local micro-textures.
- Equipped with sudden, powerful snapping structures—folded plates or muscular rings that can clamp down on passing grazers or gliders.
- They rarely move; they ambush only when prey densities cross certain thresholds.

Their attacks:

- Thin populations that, left unchecked, would overgraze or destabilize critical surfaces.
- Generate small, sharp, mechanical shocks through the stone, which the city can read as real-time feedback on local ecosystem state.

They are, effectively, **event markers** as well as population control.

**2. Vapor-edge hunters**

- Slender, whip-like bodies that patrol the boundaries between moist air and drier pockets around shafts and vents.
- Sensitive to subtle differences in chemical signatures exhaled by prey.
- Their mouths are not tearing tools but focused absorbers, swiftly drawing out specific compounds or organisms from their victims and often leaving husks that still function, in a diminished way.

They specialize in removing *excess* of particular biochemical species—ingesting, concentrating, and ultimately depositing them in regions where they become feedstock for different communities. They hunt along gradients; they’re **chemical rebalancers with teeth**.

### The Symbiotes of Technology: plugged-in animals

Some creatures have gone beyond merely using the structure; they have entered reciprocal exchanges with its deep machinery.

**1. Plugged grazers**

- Medium-sized, wall-dwelling organisms whose underbodies bear specialized contact pads.
- They settle by pressing these pads against selected zones where the city has routed low-level power interfaces up toward the surface.
- Through these pads, they:
  - Receive trickles of energy directly, obviating the need to find enough chemical food.
  - Deliver back their waste fully and predictably into the rock’s fluid networks.

The city, in turn:

- Modulates the provided energy to influence their reproductive cycles and movement patterns.
- Uses them as **stabilized, predictable loads**—part of the planning of power distribution.

They are livestock and living resistors.

**2. Bio-sensor nodules**

- Small, sessile animals that resemble blisters on stone, clustered where microcircuits lie just beneath the surface.
- Their tissues are threaded with conductive and semiconductive inclusions grown by the substrate.
- They perceive, at high resolution, environmental conditions the rock alone isn’t good at sensing: certain organic molecules, complex vibrations, microbe presence.

They translate these into:

- Slow potential changes across their embedded mineral organs.
- Periodic emissions of faint photonic or phononic signals directly into nearby circuitry.

They are the city’s **upgraded sense organs**, combining biology’s fine sensitivity with mineral logic’s stability.

### Temporal Inhabitants: those whose lives match the city’s pace

Most of what you see moves on the scale of hours and days. But some beings are keyed to the mountain’s deeper breath.

**1. Slab shapers**

- Massive, almost featureless bodies embedded half in, half out of the rock, looking more like deformations than animals.
- They shift positions by minute increments over decades, internal muscles exerting steady, low-level force on surrounding stone.
- Their digestion is glacial: they take in mineral-rich fluids and re-deposit altered crystals at their rear edges.

Over centuries:

- Each entity **literally pushes** entire slabs of rock into slightly new configurations, maintaining, straightening, or curving long surfaces.
- They slowly “walk” structural members into ideal alignments the city desires, aligning stress lines and optimizing geometry.

Their lifespans might span major structural renovation cycles; they are living jacks and presses in the mountain’s workshop.

**2. Periodic bloomers**

- Organisms that remain dormant as mineralized cysts within rock pores most of the time.
- They awaken only when long-cycle signals converge: a certain alignment of gravitational tweak, slow accumulation of specific dissolved minerals, a threshold in background hum.
- When active, they quickly grow branching bodies into small cavities, altering local porosity and chemistry.

Their brief lives:

- Trigger local restructuring: opening fresh micro-channels, reconfiguring the availability of nutrients, even deliberately collapsing unneeded voids.
- Seed particular micro-fauna assemblages, which ride their structures as they grow.

Then they die and mineralize, leaving behind **engineered fossils** the substrate will incorporate into its longer-term plan.

### The City-Mind as Inhabitant, Revisited

All of these creatures—and countless others at scales larger and smaller—nest within each other’s niches. But they all nest, ultimately, in the deeper inhabitant: the slow, anisotropic intelligence that permeates the stone.

To the city-mind, “who lives here” is a spectrum:

- At one end, **pure infrastructure**: rock volumes that merely carry loads and flux.
- Next, **semi-living modules**: circuits with some self-healing, self-organizing capacity.
- Then, **bio-tech hybrids**: creatures whose bodies are interlaced with engineered mineral.
- And finally, the more obviously animal forms, whose behaviors are still parameters in the larger computation.

Where a human city might consider its inhabitants something separate—guests of the infrastructure—here the line collapses. The urchin-centipedes are load-modulators; the gliding disk-creatures are acoustic filters; the vapor-edge hunters are biochemical limiters. Their lives and deaths are part of how the structure knows and adjusts itself.

So who lives here?

- A mind the size of a mountain, sleeping lightly inside its own angles and flows.
- A dense plurality of animals ranging from sub-visible swimmers to house-sized shapers, every one of them not just surviving but *contributing to an ongoing calculation*.
- Networks and colonies that are not quite organs, not quite ecosystems, but something between—local councils of tissue and crystal, deciding small things in the shadow of the larger will.

Nothing here is really a tenant. Every inhabitant is a feature. Every feature is part of an intention. And the intention is long: to let this cold, high, wet, mineral hollow settle, over millennia, into the most efficient, most stable, most informed version of itself.

In that sense, what lives here is a **self-examining world**—a city that has turned its own mass into nervous system and its fauna into thought, breathing slowly in the dark, not waiting for dawn because, for it, dawn is far too fast a thing to matter.
