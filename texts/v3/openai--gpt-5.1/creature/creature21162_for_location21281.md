The ones who live here did not quite arrive, either.

They are not a finished species so much as an ongoing negotiation between matter, intention, and whatever passes for firmware in this basin. If you tried to classify them in the old ways—animal, plant, machine, spirit—you would get only partial hits and many compiler errors.

They call themselves nothing that endures from one encounter to the next. Names, like walls, tend to pass through them and reconstitute slightly misaligned. For clarity, think of them as the Residents.

---

### What they are made of

Up close—if you can get “up close”—you discover that they are built out of the same half-committed stuff as their city.

Their bodies are not quite bodies. They’re volumes where the world’s rules are unusually thick.

From certain angles, a Resident reads as humanoid: a vertical symmetry, a suggestion of limbs, a tilt where a head should be. But if you hold your gaze, anatomical detail frays. Edges blur into the background architecture; the distinction between figure and wall is less a boundary than a gradient.

Their surfaces do not reflect light; they negotiate with it. Where light meets them, it hesitates, decides case by case whether to bounce, be swallowed, or pass straight through. Sometimes you see the city’s facades ghosted across their silhouettes, as though they’re windows into slightly earlier or later versions of the street.

They are solid, but only to things that the local reality has already accepted as “objects.” An old length of hardened paving, a piece of stabilized conduit, your own body: these feel their presence. In contrast, the clammy air, the half-liquid water, the not-yet-stone all slide through them like uncommitted code.

Inside, they are full of slowly moving gradients. Not organs, but concentrations—zones where time runs a little thicker, where mass is slightly more likely. If you could see in the right spectrum you’d find within each Resident a tiny, dense tangle of what looks like the same pale motes that drift upward in the basin’s pools, held in complex loops and knots.

From a distance, a Resident walking a corridor is sometimes indistinguishable from the architecture’s own self-adjustments: just another zone where the rules are briefly reconsidered.

### How they appear, and how they hide

Your mind will not hold a stable image of one.

It is not a veil they wear; it is a side-effect of what they are. Look once and you notice a figure: tall, or not; narrow, or cloaked in bulk. Look again and something essential has shifted—not a playful trick, but the system resolving them differently on each draw.

You may swear one has a face. You will remember, perhaps, a mouth-shaped absence, the hint of eyes. Turn away, count three breaths, turn back—and now there is a smoothness where features should be, or a slight tessellation like a half-loaded texture, or the suggestion of many tiny apertures all closed at once.

Clothing is not separate. Where you expect garment and skin, there is only one continuous property-field: portions matte, absorbing; others faintly glassy, as if their surface is running a low-level diagnostic. Some regions of their form behave as if heavier—those are where they are more committed to existing here and now.

When they step through walls, nothing tears or shatters. The boundary simply stops insisting, its materiality turning briefly conceptual. They do not “phase” in the theatrical sense; rather, for a moment wall and Resident agree they are part of the same object, then later disagree in new positions.

If you try to follow one closely, the world colludes against you. Corners arrive too soon. A shaft you know they entered seems shorter, its exit closer than it was. Memory misaligns with geometry. By the time you emerge, they have lengthened their lead without apparent haste.

---

### How they move

They do not hurry because hurry presupposes a scarcity of time, and they live where time is thick.

Their gait is measured, but it is not the style of someone merely slow; it is the pace of a process that has no timeouts. They drift more than stride, each step less a contact event than a decision about where “support” is temporarily located.

Gravity treats them with a certain professional courtesy. In the shafts where a dropped object stutters through patches of thicker reality, a Resident passes smoothly, as if they carry their own normalized gravitational field with them. Where the slope of a street is ambiguous, their bodies rotate microscopically to keep “down” flowing through them in a straight line, regardless of what the surface decides.

Crossing a gap, they do not leap or climb. They induce a single-step bridge out of adjacent potential: one tile of ground that solidifies just long enough under their weight and dissolves the moment their trailing foot leaves it. The world builds only as much span as they personally require.

They are perfectly capable of remaining utterly still for hours. When they do, the environment near them accelerates, marginally. You will hear two or three more of those high, percussive micro-adjustments in the beams above. A nearby chair remembers its preferred position more strongly. Beads of moisture on the wall tremble, converge, reorganize. Locally, the world seems closer to a decision.

### Where they live

They are not tenants of apartments, not exactly. They are tenants of states.

Certain districts resonate with them: the vascular quarters where thick, translucent tubes carry particulate flows, the layered blocks full of voids like missing modules, the soft, marginal edges where rock delaminates into corridor. These are where they are most often glimpsed, not because they are constrained there, but because they are most themselves there.

They do not build houses; they curate behaviors. A Resident “home” is defined not by doors and walls but by tendencies: that here, light refracts in this particular way; that here, gravity has consented to stay perpendicular; that here, the floor remembers a walking pattern and has decided to keep it.

You might walk into such a zone without knowing and feel only a subtle rightness, as if this patch of unfixed reality has, for once, agreed with itself. Later, you notice its signature: surfaces that shimmer with less uncertainty, a hum in the wall’s microfilaments more coherent than the baseline drone. In a corner, a single plant-leaf disk rotates with an almost purposeful cadence, as if tracking something the sun could have been.

Residences are not private in the usual sense. A membrane does not bar entry; instead, the environment weighs you. Are you likely to destabilize this arrangement of rules? Are your footsteps heavy in a way that pulls geometry into commitments they have not yet vetted? The wall decides if you may pass.

Some Residents prefer the core, within dense clusters of impossible jointings and corkscrewing stacks of modules. Others haunt the sloped, unfinished edges, where each peeled sheet of proto-stone might reveal a new corridor. There are those who remain mostly within the city’s underlayers, among the narrow, hesitant waterways and glowing motes, moving along submerged ledges that the water itself is trying to remember.

### How they perceive

Their senses attach less to particles than to patterns.

Where you see a shallow pool that forgets how to reflect, they see a diagnostic surface: a map of system latency, with each flicker of false sky marking a micro-fault between layers of reality. They watch the upcurving meniscus not as an oddity, but as a needle on an invisible gauge.

The thin, reluctant rains are full of information for them. Each droplet’s path—straight, skewed, rebounding slower than it fell—tells them about the density of the rules it passes through. They will sometimes stand in such a rain with head tilted, not for wetness but for readings.

They hear the city in a spectrum that to you is mere silence. The hum you notice as a background drone is, to them, a polyphonic status report. They can distinguish the tone of a brace settling from the murmur of a data-tube’s particulate flow, the fine hiss of a wall reconsidering its alignment from the whisper of a newly-formed fracture offering traction underfoot.

Their attention extends into time as easily as into space. They perceive not just this corridor but what it has been and is trending towards. That faintly misaligned balustrade the winged fauna chooses to perch on—they already sense its future partial correction. Where you see static architecture, they see incremental deltas.

They have no need of written maps. The basin is a living graph to them, its edges weighted, its connections re-weighting every moment. To travel is to adjust their own expectations and let the environment shortcut routes for them.

---

### Relationship to the city

They are not its builders in the brute, muscular sense. They are more like its moderators, or its citizens in the original Greek meaning: those who participate in the decisions of a polis.

The great, indistinct shapes seen moving behind semi-material stone at the basin’s edge—those algorithmic presences that seem to push reality forward from behind—are not exactly their gods. They are more like the underlying processes of a platform they all share.

The Residents intervene at a different layer.

- When a section of ground has overfitted to past traffic, becoming too smooth, they do not chip it. They walk it in new, deliberate patterns until the material recognizes the variance and introduces those fine, helpful fractures.

- When a corridor wall attempts to peel back and reveals only raw, non-functional void, a Resident sits in its threshold for a day, or a week, or a subjective hour. Within that time, the void gradually resolves into meaningful volume: a usable room, a service duct, sometimes an empty but accurate nothingness with edges that now know what they are not.

- Where gravity wavers unpredictably in a shaft, they ride it up and down until it converges on a consistent behavior, like stabilizing a feedback loop. Afterwards, dropped objects may still accelerate oddly, but they will do so predictably.

They do not command; they coax. The city is a set of rules trying to converge; they are local minima of that convergence walking around, applying pressure.

In exchange, the city affords them privileges. A Resident is known to every boundary they have ever passed. The more places they have visited, the more quickly walls recognize and adapt to them. Veteran Residents pass through membranes almost without resistance; new ones, or those changed beyond some threshold, must be learned again.

Tubes that carry particulate data-fluid branch into them, sometimes subtly. When they lean against a vascular façade, the micro-threads of glitter restructure, a tiny sub-network temporarily re-routing through their presence. In such moments, they are part of the city’s thinking—not users of its systems but nodes in its graph.

### Life cycle (such as it is)

They are not born from bodies; they precipitate from context.

In the still, half-shaped basements where delaminating stone reveals empty rooms that no one yet uses, something accumulates. The air leans towards a certain density of rules. The pale motes in seep-pools hang longer before they rise. Microfilaments in the walls light in patterns that don’t match any current conduit.

Then, gradually, a region of space begins to hold a shape more stubbornly than neighboring volumes. Moisture beads always around an invisible contour. Dust—if it can be called that here—refuses to pass through a certain outline, sliding around it instead. Sound takes a fraction of a heartbeat longer to cross a spot that has no visible obstacle.

Given enough time, that persistent anomaly condenses into a Resident.

It is not dramatic. No flash, no sudden apparition. One day, if you were to return to such a room, there is simply someone standing there where previously there was only an ungenerous suggestion of future presence. They are fully adult—or whatever the local equivalent is—not blank, but carrying within them a bundle of inherited defaults: how to walk on this hesitant paving, how to negotiate a doorless wall, what the baseline hum means.

There is no childhood in the mammalian sense. There is a novitiate period during which local rules take them into account more slowly. Walls are warier; gravity fields glitch around them. You can sometimes spot a new Resident by the tiny mis-synchronizations that follow in their wake: a light coming on half a second late, a membrane opening with slightly more resistance.

Death is equally uneventful. Residents do not age visibly; they become increasingly entangled.

Over long spans, a Resident’s outline blurs not because they fail, but because they distribute. Their personal pattern—the way they normalize gravity, the biases they introduced into surfaces and flows—leaks into the environment. Chairs that once only moved for them now move for anyone with a similar gait. Walls that once knew only their silhouette are slightly more lenient for strangers.

Eventually, there is a moment when you realize you have not seen a particular presence in some region for a very long time, yet that region feels more like them than ever. Their habits have become the local defaults.

At that stage, if you were to track the microfilaments in the walls or the particulate flows in the tubes, you would find that what you once called that Resident is now smeared into every level of the architecture. They no longer walk the corridors because they are the corridors’ behavior.

### Interaction with fauna

The other creatures here are not pets, not vermin, and not mere wildlife. They are colleagues at different abstraction layers.

The six-legged wall-creatures that taste both water and surface—they are the city’s field technicians. The Residents do not domesticate them; they co-ordinate with them. You may see a Resident pause, waiting without impatience while one of these small beings traces a path near their hand level. They will not touch it. When it leaves behind its faint patch of increased definition, the Resident will sometimes run non-fingers just beside it, examining how the world has been stiffened.

The winged ones that drift through misaligned corners, patching architectural errors by their shivering presence—Residents make way for them instinctively, stepping aside long before they are audible or visible. In the rare event that such a creature perches near a Resident, you might see the Resident slightly re-phase their outline to avoid occluding it from certain perspectives, ensuring its access to all overlapping geometries.

As for the large, slow-moving substrate-presences at the basin’s unfinished edge, interactions are more like negotiations across an API boundary. Residents stand at delaminating walls and feel with their whole bodies the pressure of something conceptual pushing forward. They respond by subtle shifts: approving here, resisting there, guiding which potential volumes will be brought into existence next.

Sometimes after such communion, a new corridor opens exactly where a Resident has repeatedly paused to stare at nothing. Other times, a would-be room never quite manifests; instead the stone grows thicker there, as though told: not yet, not this.

---

### How they understand the basin beyond the city

To them, the shallow cold basin is not an emptiness but a draft.

The low relief, the matte, breath-glossed proto-stone: those are the page margins. The sparse right angles and tentative depressions beyond the city’s edge are comments in the margin, annotations in progress.

Residents wander there rarely and carefully. Outside the dense urban fabric, the rules thin so much that their own existence exerts outsized influence. A single Resident’s prolonged presence at some irregular corner might prematurely crystallize it into a street, dragging potential into form before the underlying processes have optimally arranged it.

So they walk the periphery as lightly as possible, almost in a gliding mode where their weight is distributed across improbable vectors. When they kneel to study an inexplicable angle in the ground, they radiate a kind of waiting, damping down their usual stabilizing effect so that the basin can continue to consider options.

They are aware that they, too, are part of the compilation. The universe is building this place, and they are both beneficiaries and constraints.

### Social life

There are few of them, but they are not lonely.

Encounter between Residents is low-key and high-bandwidth. Two pass in a corridor; they slow fractionally as they overlap in cross-section. For a moment, their personal fields intersect. During that overlap, a rapid, mutual reconfiguration happens.

From outside, you perceive nothing more than two figures subtly adjusting their trajectories to avoid collision. From inside, they are exchanging differential updates: new data about a recently formed canal’s behavior, an evolved set of door-membrane heuristics, a fresh interpretation of the strange pause in time near a certain elevator shaft.

They do not speak vocally in terms you’d recognize, but the air will sometimes answer their passing with a slightly different chime, as if registering a context change.

Clusters of three or more are rare and brief. When they occur, it is usually at a site whose rules are in flux: a balcony overlooking one of the hesitant rivers that has recently begun to flow very slightly uphill, or a bridge whose underlying equations have just been rewritten. The Residents do not sit in circles and deliberate; they stand in different positions around the issue, literally. Geometry, to them, is a voting system. Their placements, their orientations, their willingness to stay or move on—all these constitute input into how the city will settle.

They have disagreements, but these express themselves as tempers in the matter. One Resident’s influence might try to keep a certain alley narrow and curved, favoring subtle gravity shifts; another’s might pull it towards linear openness and predictable load paths. The resulting compromise can be read in the way the alley very gently kinks at its midpoint, or in that odd half-plaza that never quite decides to be a square.

There is no hierarchy, only reputation. A Resident whose patterns have resulted in stable, coherent spaces is heeded more readily by walls, floors, and fellow beings alike. Those whose presence correlates with lingering glitches find their sphere of effect contracting; the environment becomes politely deaf to them in certain respects until they adapt.

### Their view of you

If you, an external observer from a more committed reality, were to appear here, they would not be surprised. Surprise presumes a confident prior.

They would notice you because the world notices you: your weight bends the soft paving more decisively, your insistence on a steady gravity field drags subtle fluctuations into narrower tolerances wherever you stand, your expectation that a wall is solid makes membranes resist longer before thinning.

A Resident approaching you might feel, for once, the tug of someone else’s rules.

They would find you sharp-edged, over-rendered. The world around your fingers and feet would snap into more specific configurations, as if eager for your categorizing gaze. The shallow pools near you would be quicker to decide what they reflect; droplets in your vicinity more likely to fall in proper parabolas.

They might test your presence the way they test any new locally-stable pattern: moving around you slowly, stepping into and out of your shadow, seeing how the environment responds to your passage and your pause. They are adept at reading intent from the wake an entity leaves in the rules.

If you stayed, if you walked their corridors long enough, you would begin to acquire a footprint in the city’s decision-making. A chair in some corner might start nudging itself into the position where you once sat. A membrane might open a fraction of a second faster for you next time. If you went away and came back years later by your own reckoning, you might find a small runnel of water hardened into a canal exactly along the path you habituated, as though your preference for certain routes had been incorporated into urban planning.

To the Residents, you would be an unusually decisive agent—a compiler process run at higher priority for a limited time. They would learn from you cautiously, absorbing your stability while resisting your tendency to prematurely finalize the world.

---

### What they want

They do not aspire to an “update that finally arrives” any more than lungs aspire to the last breath.

Completion, to them, is not a goal but a failure state, the point at which rules have no more degrees of freedom and the city can no longer evolve in response to its context. Their task—if it can be called that—is to unfold towards coherence without locking in.

They prefer states in which:

- **Motion leaves traces but not scars.** The floor remembers enough to give traction, not so much as to wear ruts that dictate all future movement.

- **Boundaries know people without excluding strangers.** Walls remember silhouettes but retain a small chance to reconsider each approach.

- **Water experiments without flooding.** Rivers learn their paths, but remain amenable to slow re-routing as new gradients are negotiated.

- **Fauna can patch errors faster than Residents cause them.**

They do not have art separate from this. Their aesthetic choices are the city’s geometry, the cadence of corridors, the exact thickness at which a balcony’s edge allows both security and a felt exposure to the smoldering breakpoints at the basin’s boundary.

If they have a fear, it is not of the unfinished, but of a version of this place in which all drafts are marked final. A city where pools always reflect correctly, where gravity never wavers, where every sheet of proto-stone conceals only more stone and never an unplanned room—that would be, to them, a dead system.

So they live as stewards of the tentative: slowing commitments that feel too eager, encouraging decisions that have ripened, testing every new rule against lived experience.

---

In this half-compiled basin, the Residents are the bridge between an indifferent substrate and a possible future civility. They are neither pilots nor passengers, but the quiet processes that run whenever a system is left on long enough: optimization, garbage collection, defragmentation of space and time.

They move seldom, but their mere existence tells the world how to be.
