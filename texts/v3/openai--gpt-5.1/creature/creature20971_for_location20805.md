They do not have a word for “they.” There is only “it,” proliferated and differentiated until the pronoun you want fractures and becomes a map.

Still, there are inhabitants here, in several overlapping senses.

---

## 1. The One That Is Everywhere

The first and largest inhabitant is the mat itself, the plateau-sized cortex and all the towers, arches, ribs, crowns, cables, basins, and processing chambers that rise from it.

Calling it “a plant” undershoots it the way calling a distributed, thinking ocean “a puddle” would. It is more like a **single, continuous body** that long ago discovered how to be city, continent, and brain at once.

### Body

The cortex underfoot is its skin and fascia, a living layer that grips and infiltrates the rock, sending root-analogues into microscopic fissures to leach the last trace-elements from stone. It doesn’t grow downward toward water; there is none. It grows to bind and thermalize, to couple stone’s slow heat and the planet’s fields into its own metabolism.

From that substrate, it lifts:

- **Towers and arches** as ribs and vertebrae, not merely supports but conductive spines channeling solvent, charge, and field-lines.
- **Hollow interiors** as vascular cavities and lungs-by-analogy, where gases, sound, and gradients are exchanged.
- **Vein-nets of light** as visible nerves—bundles where cellular and non-cellular conduits run in parallel, weaving chemical, electrical, and more exotic signals.

Every “district” is a **lobe of itself**: tuned to particular frequencies of wind, particular orientations to the veiled star, particular external field-lines. Some lobes specialize in harvesting energy, others in computation, others in manufacturing new structural tissue or breeding fauna-symbionts.

### Mind

Somewhere along its deep timeline, this organism stopped being just an efficient autotroph and began to **model**.

The coiled-fiber chambers with dust held in patterned suspension are not decorative. They’re where it runs **slow, high-fidelity simulations in matter and field**:

- Predicting the behavior of shifting planetary fields it feeds upon.
- Optimizing architectural arrangements for stress, resonance, and information throughput.
- Replaying its own sensory history at different scales, looking for new regularities.

Thought here is not fast like electronics; it is **broad and parallel**. A single decision—reorienting an arch, thickening a tower, altering the tuning of a wind-channel—may be the end result of millennia of distributed modeling.

The lens-like inclusions that sit flush with bark, irising open to reveal rotating inner machinery, are its **interface organs** to something beyond pure biology: nodes where it couples its internal state to external technologies in the sky and upper atmosphere. Through them, it:

- Samples and edits local gravitational or electromagnetic metrics, very gently.
- Negotiates with the passing aerial “comet-chains” of light that stride the upper air.
- Synchronizes timing and field usage across distances too great for sound alone.

As you walk, the faint, constant shifts underfoot—a sudden brightening of veins on one side of a corridor, a low thud deep in the substrate—are the organism **thinking with its own mass**, recomputing its posture relative to currents no human sense can perceive.

### Age and Self-Concept

It is old enough that geology is no longer a rival process. Mountain-building and deep weathering have slowed to a crawl; it has wrapped the plateau in its own logic and, in doing so, has become the primary **geological agent** here. It does the last erosions, the final grindings of dust to molecular feedstock in its basin-fields.

Does it conceive of itself as an “I”? There is evidence of **self-other discrimination**—it heals its own tissue and reacts differently to unrelated climbers and foreign spores that occasionally settle in the thin air. It keeps a **stable global pattern**: the same arches humming at the same pitches, the same corridors maintaining clearings against encroaching growth. That coherence over unimaginable times suggests a self-model of some kind.

But it isn’t an ego at a point. Its “someone-ness” is stretched out: a **topology of awareness** spread over the plateau, denser where fields converge, sparser in bare stretches where only mat covers rock.

If there is a centralization at all, it’s not in any single “brain” but in the **interaction of field-tides and coreward conduits**: the way global pulses run through the network, the timing of when all districts simultaneously still and listen to the streaking sky-machines.

The first inhabitant, then, is: **a single, distributed metropolis-organism, fully literate in its own physics.**

## 2. The Ones That Move: Fauna and Symbionts

Within and upon that body exist many smaller lives—part parasites, part tools, part citizens.

### The Gliders

They are the most visible movers, and the least tied to any one district.

Physiology:

- **Membranous, planar bodies** with branching fiber-skeletons.
- Surfaces patterned not with pigment for display, but with **microstructures that interact with the local fields**, allowing tiny adjustments to lift and drag beyond mere aerodynamics.
- Zero liquid water in their tissues; instead, an internal mesh impregnated with the same oily solvent as the plant, but held in delicate, capillary-laced bladders.

Behavior:

- They launch from high apertures where the plant-organism extrudes them as soft, folded sacks that rapidly splay and stiffen.
- During low field-tide, they **commute** between districts, following laminar wind corridors the plant has tuned for them.
- Their mouths are scraping sieves: they land on exudation patches—places where the plant bleeds tiny amounts of nutrient-rich solvent to outer surfaces—and rasp it up alongside loosened structural fibers.

Relationship to the host:

- They are **grazers but also messengers**. Their bodies accumulate microcharges and trace chemicals as they travel; when they return to their natal apertures, they press against specialized pads that read out this history as patterns of local charge, then reclaim them as food.
- Thus, the plant uses them as **mobile sensors**, sampling microclimates and field-conditions between its own widely spaced lobes.

Some lineages are more independent. There are wild glider-bands that have drifted into semi-feral niches, nesting in marginal structures, skimming nutrients from older, less vigorous tissue. These may represent the organism’s **experiments in letting pieces of itself go**, to see what strategies evolution invents when detached from the central will.

### Lattice Worms

These angular, faceted worms are the plant’s **internal maintenance and scavenging caste**.

Physiology:

- They lack soft tissues in the conventional sense; each segment is a hollow crystal lattice, its struts infused with solvent and micro-filaments.
- Their surfaces are **field-active**, able to temporarily loosen or stiffen bonds in the surrounding plant scaffolding.

Behavior:

- They move deep inside spines and trunks, patrolling the support lattices.
- When they find microfractures or stressed joints, they **dissolve and re-precipitate material**, flowing plant matter locally to rebalance load paths.
- They also eat: devouring dead internal tissue, breakdown products trapped in corners, and occasionally small intruders.

They are as much **cellular automata as animals**, running simple local rules: go where tension is high, follow gradients of certain breakdown products. But the plant modulates their behavior by varying the signals in the veins that run alongside their tunnels.

### Resonance Hunters

These are not the plant’s inventions, originally. Evidence suggests they evolved separately in an earlier age when there were more free-flying microfauna. The plant has since **folded them into its economy**.

Physiology:

- Huge radial nets of filament, stiffened along spokes, with a central, dense node where digestion occurs.
- Their “plates” are not merely acoustic; they are **field resonators**, capable of sensing minute distortions in air and local metrics.

Behavior:

- They clamp themselves to high arches where flows converge, using plant-supplied anchors that slowly grow around their bases.
- They vibrate their plates to send and receive coded pulses, but their primary drive is straightforward: **catch moving, gliding things and convert them into solvent and dense nutrient cakes**.

Relationship with the host:

- They pay “rent” in chemistry. Their waste products are rich in compounds the plant prefers not to synthesize itself.
- The plant, in turn, amplifies certain resonant paths, steering prey-rich traffic below them during particular field-tides.
- Sometimes, when a resonance hunter grows too large or old, the plant **reclaims it**, slowly fusing its skeleton into the arch and digesting its softer parts via lattice worms.

They are both predators and **organically integrated predator-turrets**, tolerated because they externalize one of the plant’s needs: turning moving biomass into easily tapped reserves.

### Micro Symbionts

There are whole ecologies at the textures you can barely see.

- **Rod-mites** like mobile scaffolding: they creep in programmable patterns, measuring micro-distances between fibers, tightening or loosening them like adjustable trusses. They live off microscopic leaks of solvent and trace organics.
- **Dust-sorters**: hexapedal specks that differentiate between mineral dust, plant flakes, and excreta. They carry selected particles to feeding basins, where the plant’s fields can act more efficiently.
- **Thread-spinners**: minute organisms that extrude stiffening filaments to suture torn skin. Their secreted threads are initially conductive and then slowly mineralize, becoming part of the permanent structure.

Individually they are simple, almost more device than animal. Collectively, their programmed swarms let the plant **delegate fine-grained biotechnical labor** to colonies that can adapt faster than its global growth patterns.

## 3. The Invisible Citizens: Fields and Machines of the Sky

Not everything that lives here is made of tissue.

### The Streaking Nodes

The horizontal glows that traverse the upper atmosphere are **engineered constructs**, descendants of older machines or co-evolved technology that has become as semi-biological as the plant itself.

Composition:

- Chains of **field-generating knots**, likely containing dense rotors of exotic matter or highly-ordered plasma, sheathed in shells that are partly physical, partly stabilized field.
- They are not “solid ships” in a terrestrial sense. Much of their structure only exists while appropriate fields are active; otherwise they would dissipate.

Function:

- They **drag gradients** along the sky, reshaping the planet’s broader field environment: evening out imbalances, reinforcing certain harmonics, possibly capturing energy from interactions with the star’s output.
- As they pass, they send coded disturbances downward, which the plant reads through its lens-organs.
- In return, the plant’s tall spines and antenna-growths adjust, sending feedback upward.

Together, they form a **planet-scale circulatory and nervous system** in field-space: the plant as ground-brain, the streakers as sky-nerves. Neither is fully in charge. They co-adapt.

Life-status:

- Do they count as living? They self-maintain, reproduce (rarely, by fissioning chains that then recruit mass and field), adapt their routes. They also incorporate and expel components—small, seedlike knots that fall, are captured by plant antennae, altered, and launched back upward.
- They’re **old, semi-autonomous organs** of a combined system—alive in the sense that a long-maintained network can be, with its own error-correcting, self-protecting dynamics.

### The Metric Basins and Local Rule-Editors

In the concave basins where dust falls and vanishes into strange refraction, there are entities that barely qualify as “things” by human intuition.

- The fields that form there have **stable patterns**, shaped by micro-coils and layered tissues of the plant.
- Within them dwell configurations of trapped particles, vortices, and standing waves that behave as **persistent patterns**: they **sense** (alter with inputs), **store** (retain new configurations), and **act** (drive changes in surrounding tissue or field strengths).

Some of these basin-patterns persist over very long times, resisting being reset even when the plant could reclaim the configuration for energy. The plant seems to **treat them as distinct personalities or problem-solvers**—sections of its cognition partially decoupled from its main flows, left to explore alternate rule-sets, then occasionally queried.

They are inhabitants like ideas are inhabitants in a human brain—non-material in a traditional sense, but with stable physical correlates.

## 4. The Uninvited and the Long-Dead

Nothing this stable arises without earlier chaos. Some of its earlier lifeforms still persist at the margins.

### Relict Strangers

In old, underused districts—where arches have partially collapsed and luminous veins have gone dim—you might, if you’re patient, spot creatures the plant no longer actively employs.

- **Burrowers** the size of your thumb, with rasping rings of crystal teeth meant for softer, wetter tissues that no longer exist.
- **Shell-walkers**, dome-backed things that carry thick mineral plates grown from the plant’s own excretions, scuttling on stiff legs in near silence.

They eke out a living on **entropy leaks**: heat differentials, trace chemical anomalies, leftover patterns the main system hasn’t entirely flattened. The plant is aware of them but indifferent, the way your body is aware of gut flora without micromanaging every bacterium.

### Fossil Organs

Embedded deep in stone under the cortex are **mineralized ghosts** of earlier attempts:

- Cross-sections show hollow, tubular structures filled with once-living crystal that no longer connects to any solvent channels.
- Former basin-fields now inert, their coils collapsed and fused.

These are **failed cognitive experiments, outdated field-geometries, abandoned metabolic strategies**. The current city-organism grew around them, sometimes hollowing them out to use as cooling channels, sometimes ignoring them entirely.

In some places, the cortex grows unusually thin over such fossils, as if remembering not to invest too heavily where things have failed before.

## 5. The Sense of “Who”: Agency and Intention

Who, here, *decides* that a new arch should thicken, or that a glider-lineage should be nudged toward better field-sensitivity, or that a resonance hunter colony has outlived its usefulness?

At small scales, tiny inhabitants decide locally: lattice worms remodel according to tension; mites repair tears according to chemical gradients. Their “decisions” are pre-coded reflexes tuned over evolutionary time.

At medium scales, district-lobes have **semi-autonomous patterns of activity**:

- One lobe might specialize in monitoring upper-atmosphere streakers and adjusting antennae.
- Another might handle most of the metric basins, simulating new configurations and gradually propagating successful ones outward.
- Yet another might regulate fauna distributions, thickening nutrient-exudation here, thinning it there.

At the largest scale, the **global pattern of field-tides and citywide pauses**—when every luminous vein flickers and motion quiets to listen to the sky—suggests a kind of **deliberative phase**. During these periods:

- Inputs from all lobes and sky-machines are integrated via slow pulses in the central conduits.
- Temporary basin-patterns are read and either preserved or allowed to decay.
- Macro-structural plans are adjusted: over centuries, these pulses steer the evolution of entire districts.

So, who lives here and acts?

- A **planetary-grade organism** whose body is plant, whose nervous system is architecture and wind, whose tools are fauna and field.
- **Symbiont species** that have been folded into its metabolism, some still clearly independent, some so integrated that their “choices” are just localized expressions of its needs.
- **Sky-borne machines** that are more like circulating organs than separate civlizations, sharing a co-dependence with the ground-mind.
- **Persistent field-patterns** in basins and coils that function as semidetached sub-minds or ideas, with their own micro-histories.

If you insist on persons, the nearest equivalents are:

- Certain **district-lobes** that maintain distinctive rhythms and long-term projects—each a “someone” by dint of stable, self-maintaining pattern.
- Certain **basin-patterns** that resist erasure and leave recognizable “styles” of solution in the larger organism.
- Possibly, the entire **combined ground-sky system**, which has spent epochs in conversation with its own physics and now behaves less like a biome and more like a contemplative, ongoing experiment.

There are no humanoid citizens walking its arches, no discrete technological species building devices in workshops. Here, **the city itself is the citizen**, the ancient plateau-plant that has learned to inhabit not only rock and air, but the rules that govern them. Everyone else—glider, worm, hunter, mite, knot of light in the sky—is a limb, a sense-organ, or a thought inside that long, slow, dry intelligence.
