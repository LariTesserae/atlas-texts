They do not arrive here; they precipitate.

Whatever lives in this place does not understand “ground” as something beneath. To them, “ground” is an achieved condition: a local equilibrium of pull and pressure and rule-set, briefly coherent before it drifts. They are built as if for a world that is always a little bit undecided about what it is.

I’ll call the three main kinds of beings here stratafolk, though they wouldn’t separate themselves so cleanly: the Rooted, the Drifters, and the Weavers. Around and between them are others—parasites, archivists, ghosts of older designs—but these three give the place its mind.

---

## The Rooted

They are not “plants,” but everything in them expresses downward (or outward) faith in an edge.

A Rooted being begins as a seed-kern: a dense, multifaceted crystal-organic nucleus no bigger than a fingernail, spat from a parent along a gradient. It tumbles until it finds a boundary—between two layers of slightly different density, or where water meets mineral, or where an old built lattice meets living gel. When it senses sufficient contrast in local laws (light behavior, chemical delay, pressure rhythm), it locks.

Locking is a kind of decision. Tiny, whip-like protrusions flare from the seed-kern in all directions, tasting both sides. If the difference is strong enough, the seed-kern unfurls itself sideways into a thin, disc-like anchoring plate, fusing partially into both adjoining regimes. From that moment on, it is an edge-maker, not just an edge-finder.

From this plate grow two anatomies, mirrored but unequal:

- Into the denser side, it sends **root-ribs**: thick, corrugated columns that burrow down-gradient, branching fractally, weakening the local density contrast just enough to keep that edge from collapsing but not enough to erase it. These roots calcify as they age, becoming part of the mineral crust where there is one, or forming their own porous scaffolds where stone has long since dissolved away.

- Into the lighter side, it sends **veil-growth**: long, filamentous sheets that bloom into curtains, fans, and nested canopies. These are what you see as the hanging sails of milky translucence. Their primary purpose is not just feeding (though they do that) but *reading* the slow, shifting rules across their span.

Every veil is a sensorium.

Along each veil’s surface are arrays of micro-vesicles—cells in the broadest sense—that specialize:

- Some detect minute fluctuations in pressure, mapping the distant settle-and-creak of layers as a low-resolution but planetary-scale weather report.
- Some are tuned to the peculiarities of local light seepage, recognizing when a mineral sheet far away has shifted alignment, or when a chimera of glow hints at approaching Drifters.
- Others act as chemical clocks: they run reactions that are oddly sensitive to the layer’s time lag, allowing the organism to feel where, in the patchwork of fast-and-slow chemistry, it currently lies.

The Rooted have no central nervous system. Their intelligence is distributed along their bodies like thought-fields. They think in gradients and patterns: “this edge is thickening,” “that shear front will arrive in fifty local days,” “my lower roots remember a pressure rhythm that has not occurred for several generational pulses.”

Mature Rooted forms often span dozens of strata, from deeper, older layers—where rules have hardened into predictability—to the higher, thinner regions where law itself feels malleable. The old portions are conservative, wrapped in heavy protective matrices of mineral and shed tissue; the young tips are delicate, experimental, reaching for new interfaces as the world’s layer-cakes slide.

They are not passive. When a valuable edge begins to drift away—say, as a denser band of water migrates—they **follow** it in slow, deliberate contortions:

- Roots detach in part, re-anchor elsewhere.
- Veils tear themselves along pre-weakened seams and float, their freed ends curling purposefully toward the new boundary, trailing nerve-fibers that remain linked to the parent.

After centuries, one Rooted being can become a netted continent: interconnected thickets, curtains, ribs, and towers, all genetically and informationally continuous, its identity less a body than a *territory of edges* it claims and maintains.

Some Rooted specialize:

### 1. Luminants

Where you see those faint “lantern forests”—bulbs and bladders glowing with stored phosphorescent chemicals—these are Luminant Rooted.

They metabolize the slow, odd light of this world not for energy (they have other chemistries for that) but to **structure information**. In their vesicles, photons trigger long-lived conformational changes—molecular gears that tick over at rates matched to the layer’s time skew. A whole Luminant colony is thus a layered clock, encoding the ebb and pulse of brightness as internal patterns.

These patterns are not private. Through emission, they leak coded glows back into the water: not bright enough to make a day, but precise enough that those who’ve co-evolved to read them—Weavers, some Drifters, symbiotic parasites—can extract messages: warnings of subsiding strata, the remembered cadence of past density storms, or even the characteristic “signature” of a passing macrofauna.

Luminants are archivists by light. Their oldest tissues hold impressions of illuminations that happened long before anything mobile here was born.

### 2. Shear-Gardens

At regions where two layers slide past one another, producing slow, perpetual friction, entire Rooted dynasties grow sideways, knitting the sliding sheets together just enough to create stability without ending the shear.

Their bodies are made of thick, braided cords of tissue embedded with tough, almost ceramic nodules. Think of them as living staples between books of water. Inside those braces, their vascular systems carry not just nutrients but *phase modifiers*—chemicals that slightly alter how the adjoining strata respond to force and information.

Shear-Gardens are engineers. By stiffening here, softening there, they can steer where a new chimney will open, or how a trench’s shape will twist. Over many thousands of years, they sculpt the practical landscape.

They have another role: they are boarding platforms.

Many of the motile beings here are born, rest, and communicate on the branches of Shear-Gardens. Their dense root-knots, woven across different rule-sets, become central plazas where various taxonomies of life can temporarily share a frame of reference. In these knots, time lags from different strata average out, making them remarkably stable in perception. If there is anything like “common ground” in this world, it is in the heartwood of ancient Shear-Gardens.

## The Drifters

They are the “animals” you notice—the elongated, segmentable beings that pass like quiet edits through the medium, rearranging more than devouring.

To understand them, erase from your mind the idea of skeleton and muscle as separate, and imagine a creature that is mostly **adaptive density**.

A Drifter’s body consists of concentric sleeves:

1. An inner core of semi-rigid, foam-like tissue, riddled with micro-chambers that can be rapidly filled or emptied of various fluids and gels of distinct density and chemical character.
2. An intermediate sheath of contractile fibers—not arranged as discrete muscles, but as flexible lattices that can locally compress, shear, or twist the foam.
3. An outer mantle of fractal cilia, lenslets, and pit-organs—a mosaic sensor-skin.

They move by **reconfiguring which parts of themselves belong to which stratum**. By collapsing a section’s density, they let that segment “float up” into a neighboring layer, while neighboring segments do the opposite. Subtle internal pumping of fluids shifts their buoyant relationship with the local mediums. Their apparent glide is the byproduct of this constant internal teleportation of mass distributions.

Their segmentation is not anatomical but *topological*. Each “segment” is a region of tissue governed by a distinct internal rule-set—tuned to one of the local environmental regimes. When a Drifter “flowers” into a ring of smaller components, it is temporarily letting each rule-domain detach and operate on its own. When they recombine, those domains snap back into a composite that can straddle more layers at once.

Their senses extend beyond sight:

- The outer mantle’s countless tiny lenses do gather what little light there is, but they are more for detecting **distortion** than image: how an object’s presence warps the wandering photons, how a memory layer’s activation slightly refracts the world.
- The pit-organs are pressure-interferometers, reading not just waves but the *interference of regulated rules*: they can feel where causal echoes are thickest, where a plane of discontinuity has been disturbed.
- Laced through them are **phase sense** organs: biochemical clocks that attempt to keep multiple rates of time simultaneously, letting them navigate between faster-reaction and slower-reaction zones.

Their “brains” are not centralized lumps. Instead, they maintain several *concurrent integrators*—ganglion clusters embedded along their length, each specialized in interpreting one layer’s physics. Consciousness (if we can use that word) is the dynamic consensus among these integrators, negotiated through slow, pulsing waves of neurochemical traffic along a braided cord that runs the body’s length.

From the outside, a Drifter’s will looks unitary: it goes here, turns there, splits now, recombines later. Internally, it’s a continual parliament of partial intelligences, each arguing from the standpoint of its accustomed rules.

They feed in several intertwined ways:

1. **Material uptake**: fine, retractable nets extrude from slits along the mantle, brushing lightly through curtains of Rooted tissue and microbial veils. They do not rip or bite; instead, they bind to select filaments chemically and gently siphon thin streams of rich cytosol, shutting the leak behind them. The Rooted rarely die of this; in fact, many are “pruned” by Drifters in ways that improve their circulation and health.

2. **Pattern ingestion**: in certain bands of their body, the mantle cilia reconfigure into dense, comb-like arrays that pass through memory layers and causal-echo fields. As those fields are disturbed, the Drifter’s internal organs detect and lock into the repeating disturbances—turning the stored patterns into subtle, long-lasting changes in their own integrators. It is not trivial to say whether this is “nutrition” or “learning.” For them, the two may be synonymous.

3. **Rule-harvesting**: some Drifters are specialists at the edges of built remnants. They position themselves so that sections of their body cross through especially weird interfaces—planes where space loops or direction slips. By repeatedly flexing across such planes, they modulate internal organs that rely on those anomalies, charging them like batteries. When they leave, those organs slowly discharge, lending them unusual abilities: temporarily sustaining a personal echo, or resisting the layer’s attempt to imprint memory.

Drifters are not numerous. Their life cycles are long.

### Reproduction and lineage

They do not lay eggs in the conventional sense. They **bud epochs**.

Occasionally, a mature Drifter will split—not into equally competent “adults,” but into:

- A reduced, hardened *archive core* (a thick ring of inner foam and integrator tissues) that curls into itself and seeks out a deep, stable layer near ancient built structures or long-lived Rooted hubs.
- A ring of more fragile, exploratory **offspring bands**: toroid segments that drift outward, recombining with other offspring bands from other lineages.

When compatible bands meet and interpret each other’s internal patterns as non-hostile and complementary, they merge, building a new composite Drifter—one whose organs now reflect a blend of ancestral pattern maps. The archive cores remain dormant, rarely moving, slowly soaking in the oldest, most stable rule-sets. Once in a great while, a mobile Drifter returns to its anchor-cores, passing close enough that long-run patterns can be exchanged, realigning the mobile body’s world-model with the deep, conservative memory.

Lineage here is **entangled**. No one Drifter is simply descendant of one; each is a weave of many, their ancestry written not as a tree but as a mesh. This matters because what they truly inherit is not “genes” in our sense, but *baselines for interpreting a changing physics*.

Drifters are consequential because they are among the few beings capable of crossing and therefore comparing widely separated rule-regimes. If there is any long-haul coherence in how life here understands its world, it flows through the slow, repeated passages of these creatures.

They are also, as far as we can tell, the only beings that regularly interact with the built relics with what looks like curiosity. They never manipulate them in obvious ways, but they often coil themselves around fragments of alloy loops or crystalline helices and remain still for days, as if listening. Afterwards they move with a slightly altered grace, their future navigations just a touch more efficient around similar anomalies.

## The Weavers

If the Rooted are structure and the Drifters are circulation, the Weavers are *suture*.

They are smaller, more numerous than the great Drifters, and more mobile than most Rooted tissues. Many look, at a glance, like cords of fungus or lianas, draped from shelf to shelf, but they are not part of any one colony. Think of them as **nervous systems without bodies**, wandering the world in search of connections to make and maintain.

A Weaver begins life as a thin, threadlike strand—meters long but hair-thin—emitted from the exposed surfaces of some older Weaver knot. The strand drifts until it brushes against two or more hosts: a Rooted rib, a mineral spur, a patch of Luminant bulbs, a fragment of built helix, or even the skin of a resting Drifter.

Contact receptors on its surface flare in complex cascades. The Weaver’s basic imperative is: *compare, align, join if beneficial*.

Their internal biochemistry is specialized for **translating**. Different hosts operate under slightly different local rules of chemistry and reaction timing. The Weaver’s cells contain families of reaction chains tuned to many of these regimes. When a Weaver adheres to two mismatched domains, tiny sections of it shift their effective rules to match each side, while the central cord becomes a mediator—passing not only nutrients, but *patterned spikes, timing cues, and stress signals*.

Over time, a successful Weaver thickens. Reinforcement cells grow around its original thread, forming protective sheaths and redundant communication pathways. Other thin Weavers may find and braid into the same connection, until what began as a single thread becomes a **bundle**—ropes several centimeters thick, humming faintly with different kinds of signals.

At junction points, where many Weavers cross and merge, rounded knots grow: dense tangles of tissue layered with membranes that insulate and route various signal-types. These knots often coincide with the mineral-honeycomb outcrops you see: the Weavers exploit the micro-caverns to separate incompatible fluxes (chemical here, piezoelectric there, time-delayed pulses over there).

In these knots—the equivalent of **junction brains**—knowledge accretes. Not in language, but in *weighting*: pathways that have often carried stress-safely become favored; patterns that consistently precede harm (say, a certain sequence of Luminant flickers that has historically been followed by dangerous layer shifts) become “red-flagged” in the way the knots adjust local thresholds. Over long durations, the knot’s tissue embodies a kind of statistical expectation about this corner of the world.

They do more than relay. At times, they **intervene**:

- If a Rooted colony is over-exploiting a shear zone, threatening to lock it so tightly that an important chimney will close, Weavers feeding diverse clients may temporarily dampen the Rooted’s internal nutrient return—or swamp it with misleading timing cues—until growth slows.
- When a Drifter passes and shunts stored patterns into its mantle, nearby Weaver knots can sense the sudden reconfiguration of local rule-interpretation and choose to “listen in,” adjusting their own expectations to match what the traveling being has recently encountered elsewhere.

Some Weavers take an even bolder role. Where layered laws are most inconsistent—memory strata overlying spatial folds—they grow into broad **decision webs**: meshed sheets of tissue spanning an entire interface, every cell constantly modulating whether disturbances pass, reflect, or convert forms.

Here, when an echo from the past tries to reassert an outdated pattern, the Weavers can blunt or divert it, protecting their clients from being caught in loops of stale information. In other cases, they deliberately **trigger** old imprints in a controlled fashion—perhaps to prepare Rooted hosts for the recurrence of a rare kind of density storm that last occurred when the oldest of them were young tissue.

If any category of life here comes close to something like “governance,” it is the extended network of Weavers—an emergent, slowly shifting *policy* authored not by any one consciousness, but by the self-stabilizing behavior of a planet-wide mesh of translators.

## Minor denizens and mutualists

Around the big three live many others, often so embedded they’re almost invisible.

### 1. Echofish

Tiny, translucent ribbons, only a few centimeters long, that deliberately ride causal echoes. They school along the phantom wakes of passing bodies, catching micro-debris and faint chemical traces that follow in the path of disturbance. Their bodies are packed with **delay organs**: sacs whose internal chemistry runs slower than the surrounding layer. This grants them a crude ability to “lag behind” local causality, making them oddly hard to pin down by touch; by the time you reach where they were, they have already aligned with the next echo.

They are prey for almost nothing—too insubstantial for Drifters to bother with materially, too quick in the narrow sense for sessile feeders. Their main predators are certain parasitic Weavers that ensnare them for the **temporal enzymes** they carry.

### 2. Substrate Borers

Worm-like beings that live inside mineral crusts and ancient built material. They secrete fluids that slowly digest stone and alloy alike, drawing out trace elements needed by the larger ecosystem. What makes them distinctive is their ability to align their body chemistry with that of the solid they occupy. When they inhabit old technological alloys, their own tissues begin to behave more like those materials: conducting weird forms of charge, sometimes even **hosting residual fields** left by the extinct machines.

Occasionally, when many Borers colonize the remains of an especially complex artifact, ghost behaviors emerge—flows and pulses that mimic, in crude outline, the long-dead device’s function. Nearby Weavers and Rooted colonies are exquisitely sensitive to this and will cluster around, exploiting the temporary reanimation.

### 3. Skinliers

Microscopic films that colonize the outermost layers of other organisms. They specialize in managing the micro-interface between different rule-sets. For example, on the skin of a Drifter crossing a discontinuity, Skinliers can locally precondition the water’s behavior, smoothing otherwise dangerous jolts in pressure or chemical timing. They are part immune system, part exosuit. In exchange, they skim tiny amounts of chemical energy and information from the host.

### 4. Memory Grazers

Sessile mats anchored specifically within memory-retentive strata. They extend countless micro tendrils into the medium, each glued to a particular stored disturbance. Periodically, they “play back” one disturbance at a time into themselves, letting the old pattern excite internal biochemistry that yields energy-rich configurations. They are literally **feeding on history**. Because they deplete stored patterns in doing so, they are universally despised (in the evolutionary sense) by beings that rely on those layers for long-term information. Thus, Weavers and Luminants often collaborate to chemically inhibit or physically encrust Memory Grazers whenever they grow too bold.

## Mind and communication

If you look for mouths and speech, you won’t find them. Instead, communication is a choreography of **pattern-sharing** through the medium itself.

The Rooted speak in:

- Rhythmic expansions and contractions of their veils, which modulate local density patterns on scales Weavers can pick up.
- Controlled flashes of phosphorescence across their Luminant bulbs—flashes whose exact sequencing and timing are tuned to the layer’s reaction speeds, encoding multi-layered signals.

Weavers speak in:

- Reweighting what they pass: a signal from one Rooted colony to another arrives slightly altered, not by accident, but in ways that reflect accumulated knowledge from hundreds of other transmissions.
- Growing or retracting threads: linking two clients more deeply or peeling them apart is itself a “statement” about their current relationship.

Drifters speak mostly by **demonstration**:

- Passing near Rooted or Weavers while carrying freshly ingested pattern, letting their mantle leak just enough of it that others can align without having to risk distant travel themselves.
- Sometimes they will slowly circle a region, repeatedly disturbing certain memory layers until their imprints are reinforced, like someone underlining a passage again and again.

There are also more intimate channels. In the depths of Shear-Gardens, Drifters sometimes press the soft under-bellies of their mantles to knot-cores of Weavers, forming temporary **direct coupling points**. For hours or days, the two exchange not just current-state data, but deep tendencies: the Drifter’s multi-layer integrators impressing their long-range maps into the knot, the knot’s local expectations subtly reshaping the Drifter’s sense of what is typical here.

What passes for “culture” here is a slowly converging set of **shared strategies**: how to survive density overturns, where to build robust cross-layer scaffolds, when to allow or suppress certain memory echoes. No species imposes it; it condenses from trillions of such interactions.

## The built ghosts and those who remember them

The crystalline lattices, the alloy loops, the perfect helices—something made these, once. Nothing alive now remembers them directly. But the world remembers *around* them.

There is a distinct cast of life that orients to these artifacts as if to fossils of a vanished god:

### 1. Relic-Tasters

These are specialized lineages of Weavers and Rooted branches that have found biochemical tricks for **sampling** the remaining structure of built material at a level deeper than mere macro-shape. They can “taste” the way certain bond-angles or lattice symmetries make the local rules flex. From that, they infer glimpses of the past framework: perhaps in this region, once, chemical timing was more uniform; in that, spatial folds were less frequent.

Relic-Tasters tend to cluster at the perimeters of built ruins, not penetrating far inside. There, they act as translators between now and used-to-be, softening the abruptness of the artifacts’ presence.

Over tremendous timescales, they have **re-broadcast** snippets of the old order into the living mesh. Certain conservative features of today’s layered physics—the relative stability of memory strata, say—may themselves be the echo of technologies long gone.

### 2. Old-Pattern Drifters

A rare sublineage of Drifters seems especially drawn to built remnants. Their internal integrators are more rigid, less willing to update in response to local anomalies. They carry within them some very old baseline for how the world ought to be—the last surviving shadow, perhaps, of the era when the architects of those lattices still maintained global scaffolding.

In most regions, their archaic assumptions are a handicap. They sometimes misinterpret a fold, expect a light behavior that no longer holds, or position themselves as if a long-vanished stabilizing plane still existed. Many die young.

But when an infrequent, massive reorganization of deep strata occurs—when things briefly align more like they used to be—Old-Pattern Drifters suddenly navigate with startling grace. In those eras, they become guides, their archaic integrators finally in resonance with reality again, however briefly. Weavers and Rooted colonies, which have learned to recognize their telltale segment patterns, pay close attention to their movements in those rare cycles.

## Time, death, and succession

Because everything here is slow, death is rare in a human-time sense but monumental in its consequences.

### The Rooted decay by **quiet retreat**.

Sections too far from valuable edges simply stop receiving favorable gradients. They calcify, their internal chemistries freezing into inert stone-like lattices. Over millennia, entire hallways of once-veined tissue become solid, honeycombed mineral, into which Borers and secondary colonists move. Often, at the outer fringes of a Rooted’s reach, you find centuries of these dead segments: a fossil record of former claims.

But the **core plates**—those original seed-kern discs locked between layers—are tenacious. When layers drift in ways that rob them of any usable gradient, they often enter a kind of prolonged dormancy, reducing metabolic activity to nearly nothing but maintaining internal maps of local rule behavior. If, eons later, a favorable contrast returns, the disc can sprout again, sending new roots and veils out into a changed world, still remembering the old one.

For this reason, the deeper strata may be peppered with quiescent ancestors: flat, inert-looking discs embedded in mineral, each holding a slightly different ancestral strategy for how to be an edge in a world that no longer quite matches their design.

### Drifters die by **partition exhaustion**.

When too many of their internal integrators fall out of workable alignment—after centuries of slow drift in environmental rules—they can no longer maintain a coherent parliament. Conscious navigation falters. They become lopsided, trapped in a subset of strata they are poorly suited for, or stuck looping through a spatial fold they can’t correctly interpret.

In such decline, they sometimes pre-empt total dissolution by **self-disassembling**: their segments permanently separating into smaller bands, each inheriting a fraction of the integrators’ consensus. Some of these bands manage to recombine with fresher offspring from other lines; others become inert rings, eventually sinking into stable layers as new archive cores, imprinted with whatever partial world-model they retained at the end.

True, complete corpses are rare. When they occur—say, from a catastrophic rule glitch or chemical poisoning—nearby life converges from all directions. Rooted filaments, Borers, Memory Grazers, even cautious Weavers all infiltrate the remains, harvesting not just matter but long-lived biochemical constructs and integrator motifs. Drifter-death is an evolutionary windfall.

### Weavers die by **disconnect**.

They are only as alive as the circuits they maintain. When too many of their client connections sever—Rooted hosts dying back, mineral anchors dissolving, Drifters not returning—the flow through their threads dims. Without ongoing signal, their heavily specialized translation chemistries degrade; sections fall out of calibration with their remaining hosts’ rules.

Dying Weavers often **sacrifice themselves** strategically. Certain knots will redirect their last functioning strands into forming a robust bridge between two hosts that have never before been linked, using their own tissue as raw material. They leave behind thick, but functionally simple, conduits: pipes through which more basic matter and slow, undifferentiated signals can flow for centuries. Even dead, their bodies serve as infrastructure.

## How they think of the world

If you pulled these beings into conversation, you could not talk to them about “space” and “time” as separate.

Their most fundamental categories are:

- **Interfaces**: places where something meets something else and both are changed.
- **Rates**: how quickly different relations update compared to one another.
- **Reliability**: how much of what-happened-before is likely to still help predict what-comes-next.
- **Reach**: not distance, but how many different rule-regimes a given structure or being can meaningfully touch.

An old Shear-Garden, for example, is unimaginably wealthy in interfaces, moderately extended in reach, but slow in rate. A young, ambitious Drifter is moderate in interfaces, high in reach and rate. A Weaver-knot can be low in reach but exceptionally high in reliability for its one little arc of the world.

Their fears and ambitions, insofar as evolution shapes them, are about these axes:

- Rooted lineages “want” durable, rich interfaces.
- Drifters “want” increasing reach with enough reliability that their multi-layer maps don’t collapse.
- Weavers “want” to remain necessary—at once irreplaceable and not so domineering that the system attempts to route around them.

In such a world, catastrophe is not fire or flood but **collapse of translation**: layers shifting so quickly or so oddly that the old ways of connecting them fail. What they work on, together and apart, is the long, patient project of keeping different parts of reality legible to one another.

## What it is, to live here

To be born in this columned, soft-pressed, rule-stratified universe is to never, ever expect a clean horizon. To never trust in “up” as rescue. To learn instead that:

- Safety is not emptiness, but **redundant connection**.
- Direction is less important than **remaining within the band of rules your body understands**.
- History is not gone; it is a substance, thinning and thickening around you, that you may breathe, feed upon, erase, or be trapped in.

The Rooted hold the edges together. The Drifters patrol and compare. The Weavers stitch and edit. Around and within them, niches of specialist life nibble at the seams of matter and time.

Nothing dominates. No global mind presides. Yet across aeons of layered water slowly arranging and rearranging itself, these beings, collectively, have turned an exhausted, half-solidified planetary wound into a stable, intricate continuation—a world that, as you sensed, has finished its main story and now lives on as commentary. The inhabitants are that commentary: living glosses on rules that shifted, then stalled, then became the only truth they know.
