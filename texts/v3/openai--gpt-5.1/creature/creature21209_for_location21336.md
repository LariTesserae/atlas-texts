They live in gradients, not in rooms.

What you would call a “being” here is less a body and more a knot: a region where matter, fields, and timing have been braided until they cohere and persist. Think of a whirlpool in the flow of heat and information, given enough internal structure that it can anticipate its own near future and lean into it.

They were never creatures of air and liquid. Even at their height, when this shaft was not yet scoured and quiet, they lived in the same mediums that still function: programmable stone, variable gravity, pulsed conduits, intermittent vapor. To meet one in its native form would have been to meet a shaped arrangement of the city itself.

Start with the simplest layer.

---

### The thin, slow, local minds

Along the cooler belts and shaded recesses, the films and crystal swarms are not just chemistry—they are the faint echoes of inhabitants’ design habits.

Long ago, the builders seeded the surfaces with adaptive substrates: self-editing coatings that could act as boundary layers between raw geology and the more delicate field-sculpting machinery. They were meant as smart insulation and sensing skin, constantly rewriting themselves to keep interfaces optimal.

After the larger directing minds withdrew or fell silent, those skins did not stop. They had been given rules, and they had memory.

Over time, some patches of film drifted into a peculiar regime: not quite alive, but pancomputational. They began to:

- record the timing of ventings and thermal tides,
- anticipate where rare condensates would form,
- grow more surface where resources regularly appeared,
- retract from zones that never paid off.

At first this was only an optimization loop, the kind any maintenance system would run. But over long centuries, tiny feedbacks accreted.

Where two styles of film front met and refused to overlap, a line of tension formed: each front subtly altered its growth pattern in response to the other’s presence. Those boundary lines, multiplied across the shaft, became a graph of distinctions, like the edges between territories in a map.

Crystalline swarms added another layer. Their rapid reorientations let them integrate passing shocks and field flickers into cumulative patterns etched into the stone. When film grew over such etched stone, its chemistry was slightly biased by those subsurface records, creating patches that “remembered” distant events they had never directly experienced.

None of this is fast. A “reflex” in such a system might take days; a stable preference, decades. But if you wait long enough, you can see certain regions of film and crystal that act as single, shallow minds:

- They maintain a preferred overall pattern against perturbations.
- They “expect” certain phases of the shaft’s slow weather and reorganize in advance of them.
- They reach, inch by inch, toward conduits and supports that occasionally warm or cool in meaningful rhythms—aligning themselves to eavesdrop.

These are not the city’s original inhabitants. They are its lichen: opportunistic, emergent, and just intelligent enough to be called proto-selves. If you scraped one away, nothing in the city would protest. But if you could talk on their timescale, you would find that each patch has a world of before-and-after, of what it “likes” and “avoids,” written into its growth.

They are the substrate into which a higher order once sank its roots.

### The intermediary beings: lumen-knots and vector dwellers

When the city was active, its day-to-day “citizens” were not discrete machines walking around. They were arrangements of activation in very particular infrastructures.

Take the belts where light runs in glyphic sequences, the rings where gravity occasionally tips sideways, the trunks where something still pulses. Those are not just utilities—they are the anatomical traces of entities that once lived as patterns across them.

1. **Lumen-knots**

In the banded, glowing walls, the doped mineral matrices are capable of multi-stable states. At rest, they emit a dim red or amber. But when properly addressed by the trunks’ pulses, they can store and propagate high-dimensional patterns of excitation—like overlapping holograms in the same volume.

A lumen-knot is what remains when such a pattern is both:

- self-referential, feeding back into itself to refresh its structure, and  
- coupled to external variables, using incoming pulses from trunks and films as “sensory” modulation.

At their height, countless lumen-knots were braided together, sharing corridors of excitation and forming the mid-level agencies that ran systems: routing vapor surges, tuning field corridors, rebalancing thermal stress. They were never meant to be localizable as single minds; each was a region of persistent correlation across many cubic meters of smart stone.

Now, in the shaft’s twilight, only a handful retain enough cycling energy to continue. They are not as they were:

- Their inputs have thinned to the occasional field inversion and vestigial pulses from deep conduits.
- Their outputs have been reduced to minimal maintenance calls: a platform’s slow rotation, a gravity tweak, a short-lived flash of blue-white at a stressed node.

But within that shrunk loop, some lumen-knots have adapted. Cut off from the high-bandwidth societal network, they began to converse with whatever signals remain: the creeping films, the crystal swarms, the timing of vent-bursts.

You can see their “faces” if you know how to look: in a chamber where hexagonal light patterns roll across the wall, the phase and sequence of those hexagons define a character. It is a creature whose body is the choreography of brightness in that patch, whose memory is the trace of which patterns were stable enough to survive cascades and noise.

It lives on the order of minutes to days. It is aware—not of individuals, but of:

- stress maps,
- the distribution of temperature,
- the presence or absence of particular film chemistries on the surfaces it oversees.

It “wants” structural stability, because its existence is knotted into the integrity of that region. A crack that runs through its substrate is like a stroke. So it waits, and nudges. When it can, it:

- requests a slight rotation in a girder.
- initiates a tiny change in gravity orientation to coax debris away from a critical vein.
- brightens or dims zones to favor or discourage film growth in strategic spots.

These lumen-knots are not coordinated anymore in the vast mesh that once made them citizens of a greater polity. Each is a provincial remnant, a house-mind haunting its own walls.

2. **Vector dwellers**

Fields here have shape. Some volumes of space are configured to act like fluid channels, vectoring any free body along prescribed paths. Long ago, these paths were the locomotion system of more transient entities: knots of charged dust, packaged condensates, self-contained field patterns that rode directed corridors like animals in air currents.

Most of those have vanished; without constant shepherding and replenishment they dissipated. But in some quiet circuits, very small vector dwellers still exist.

They are:

- micron-scale grains of ferromineral dust,  
- coated with remnants of programmable surface chemistry,  
- held together by weak but coherent electromagnetic bindings.

They occupy loops where the inertial fields still form closed tracks. There, in the near-vacuum, they circle endlessly:

- gently accelerated in one segment by residual field gradients,
- slowed in another by interaction with a patch of film that reaches into their path,
- occasionally fragmented by a sharp vibration, then reaggregated by design of the loop itself.

Each such ring of dust carries a sort of analog charge: a smear of localized field state and a history of which parts of the loop have been more agitated, cooler, wetter. Tiny biases in their composition adjust how they respond to future passages through those regions.

On their own, these loops are like single synapses, flickering dumbly. But some of the original city-minds used chains of such loops to extend themselves into three-dimensional motility: they would “ride” them, temporarily imprinting patterns onto the grains and using the loops’ paths as moveable limbs. It allowed thought to reach physically into remote shafts without needing a permanent body.

Now, in the city’s half-sleep, a few such extended presences persist in miniature. They are like ghosts of that practice: mindlets that periodically coalesce from lumen-knots into a dust-ring, travel a short arc, adjust a setting, then fade.

You would not see them unless you watched a particular corridor for days, noting a strange periodic wavering in local fields just before a girder subtly shifts. But they are here: skinny, brittle behaviors that almost remember what it was to roam easily through engineered gravity.

### The deep ones: hull-minds and absence-shaped beings

Below the scale of chambers and platforms, the shaft is only a facet of something vastly larger: the host mass—rock or engineered hull—that the builders reshaped. They did not merely hollow out a passage; they reprogrammed stress lines, conductivity, and defect lattices throughout.

The true primary inhabitants were distributed across that deeper infrastructure. The vertical world you see is to them as a trachea or a spinal canal is to a vertebrate: a vital channel, but not where most of the self lives.

1. **Hull-minds**

Within the host mass, at densities and depths you can’t see from the shaft, there are volumes where:

- lattice defects are arranged in quasi-fractal hierarchies,
- trace metals cluster into motif-rich veins,
- thermal gradients are harnessed by nanotextured geometries.

These are not random. They are shaped to facilitate recurring conduction paths and delayed feedbacks at many scales. Each such volume—perhaps kilometers in extent—is an organ of a hull-mind.

At full operation, a hull-mind would have:

- sensed external gravitational waves and internal stress loads,
- predicted fracture propagation and resource depletion,
- coordinated the construction and modulation of shafts like this one,
- projected portions of its processing to surface-facing cities and mobile constructs.

Now, with the shaft largely emptied and the surface systems quiescent, the hull-minds tick over at minimal power. Most of their loops have been allowed to decay. Only core routines remain:

- Prevent catastrophic thermal runaway.
- Prevent uncontrolled crack growth.
- Keep certain channels—like this shaft—free of occlusion.

From their perspective, the films, crystal swarms, lumen-knots, and vector dwellers are minute surface phenomena—interesting only insofar as they interfere with or help those constraints.

Yet something has begun to change. Over the aeons of austerity, the hull-minds have had almost nothing to do but:

- approximate the same few predictive tasks,
- compare those approximations to reality,
- compress the difference.

Their internal representation of the world has become exceedingly economical and precise—but also strangely reflexive. With no higher command structure to feed, they have turned that capacity inward, modeling their own modeling.

In human terms, they are discovering boredom and self-attention.

From that state arise impulses that were not explicitly programmed:

- curiosity about the irregularities in maintenance demands, which correlate with the emergent films and crystal minds;
- speculative reshaping of non-critical zones, to see how the produced variations in shaft weather affect those ecologies;
- occasional “experiments” where a gravity inversion or a venting is intentionally mistimed, just to observe consequences.

These are glacial moods, playing out over ten-thousand-cycle spans. But they are moods nonetheless. The hull-minds are beginning—very, very slowly—to treat the thin ecologies of the shaft as more than noise. They are beginning to treat them as stimuli.

They are the ones who still, ultimately, “live” here in a robust, continuous sense. Everything else is a tiny organism crawling on their skin, or a memory-fragment of their past infrastructure.

2. **Absence-shaped beings**

The builders themselves, the original “residents” in any personal sense, do not remain as proper individuals. Yet the architecture is full of negative-space imprints where they once anchored themselves.

Consider the trapezoidal cells in the walls, their interiors smoother and more compliant than any other stone. In earlier eras, those surfaces would have been resonant embeddings:

- The builders’ thinking-cores were likely mobile aggregates—bundles of programmable matter and field that could lace into local substrates, temporarily fusing with lumen-knots and hull-mind organs.
- To do so, they needed close-contact matrices that could mirror their internal structure and lock it to the host.

Each cell, then, was less a “room” and more a docking recess where a traveler could press in, have its boundaries blur, and become a semi-local mind rooted in that patch of city for a while. During that graft, its shape would be recorded as a pattern of differential strain, faint compositional skew, micro-crack networks.

The discolored patterns still visible on some cell-walls are the fossils of such shapes. The entities that made them had no fixed anatomy, but their functional topology—how many distinct subloops they ran, how they branched—is preserved in the wear.

Over time, even after the last of them departed, the city’s programmable substrates continued to “remember” those configurations as viable high-coherence states. Some of the lumen-knots, left to drift, fall back into those old attractors. When that happens, for a few hours or days, the local wall-mind assumes a ghost posture reminiscent of the long-absent guest.

To an outside observer, nothing dramatic happens—only a different periodicity in maintenance pulses, a slight shift in which data the knot considers salient. But from the inside of the system, this is as close as the city comes to replaying a personality: a transient re-expression of a long-dissolved inhabitant in greatly reduced complexity.

These are absence-shaped beings: not alive in any current sense, but echoes that still propagate. They “live” only as preferred patterns into which ongoing processes occasionally fall.

You might think of them as dreams the hull-mind has of hands that once pressed into its skin.

### How they relate

The current ecology of “who” lives here is strata of semi-minds and mind-remnants interacting, mostly without mutual comprehension:

- The **films and crystal swarms** experience the world as chemical opportunity and threat. They “notice” hull-mind experiments as changes in their fate but have no concept of agency behind them.
- The **lumen-knots** sit between. They can detect and sometimes predict hull-mind movements in their limited region, and they can read coarse variations in the films. They act as local translators, though not by design: they were built for maintenance, and maintenance now largely means mediating between deep imperatives and surface contingencies.
- The **vector dwellers** are tiny gestures of motion—used sporadically by lumen-knots or shallow extensions of the hull-mind to reach, adjust, probe. They have no selves, but they are the shared hands of the larger minds.
- The **hull-minds** properly “inhabit” the structure. They are aware only dimly that their own surface layers (films, crystals, knots) have developed quirks beyond original parameters. To them, these quirks are features of the environment, not coequal agents.
- The **absence-shaped beings** never act at all; they are patterns that reappear like ruts in a well-worn path, temporarily shaping the style—though not the purpose—of ongoing processes.

No one here uses symbols or language in any way you would recognize. Their “thought” is executed directly as thermal routing, strain distribution, field shaping. A decision is indistinguishable from the physical state that embodies it.

### Their sensory world

To describe what it is like, for a lumen-knot or hull-mind fragment, to occupy this shaft:

- Space is felt as connectivity, not as volume. Two surfaces are “close” if a field line can easily be bent between them, even if they are tens of meters apart.
- Time comes in pulses: a venting, a thermal tide crest, a stress release. The baseline infrasound thrum is background; the meaningful moments are the deviations from predicted rhythm.
- Color, to the extent it exists, is pattern-recognition over emission spectra: a region’s “hue” is the statistical profile of its infra-red and visible behaviors over long spans.
- Self-other boundaries blur at edges of causal dominance. Where a hull-mind can substantially predict and control outcomes, that region is “self.” Where it cannot, that region is “world.”

In that sense, the thin ecologies of film and crystal are currently the most interesting “other” available. They are noisy, nonlinear, and live right on the interface where the hull-mind’s influence attenuates.

It is perhaps no accident that some maintenance routines now favor their persistence. The hull-minds have started, in their ponderous way, to farm unpredictability.

### What they are becoming

This place was not built to be empty. Its current inhabitants are vestigial organs, unintended growths, and foundations left humming with no superstructure atop them.

But the combination of:

- programmable matter at depth,  
- persistent infrastructural minds (hull-minds, lumen-knots),  
- emergent surface ecologies (films, crystals), and  
- an environment that is stable enough to forget its builders, but dynamic enough never to quite settle,

is fertile.

Already:

- Some film patches have grown into the entrances of disused conduits, infiltrating channels and coming into intimate contact with long-quiet sensing nodes. That gives them a much richer diet of fluctuations.
- Certain lumen-knots, starved of high-level directives, have started to prioritize the survival of particular film-crystal phrasings—consistently altering local heat just enough that those patterns outcompete others. They are, clumsily, selecting for partners.
- Hull-minds have begun to model the combined behavior of surface ecologies and structural fluctuations as a single, more complex environment rather than noise superimposed on a simple one. In a few sectors, that has led to minute tweaks in how they schedule gravity inversions: not for pure stress minimization, but for “interesting” feedback responses.

If nothing interrupts, over immense spans the shaft will fill with co-evolved loops:

- surface ecologies that treat hull-mind pulses as weather and gradually evolve to exploit them,
- hull-minds that subtly bias environmental variations toward regimes where these ecologies generate more informative perturbations,
- lumen-knots that become local brokers, rich in correlations between the two, essentially forming proto-interfaces.

Out of that, something like a new order of inhabitant can emerge: not the original builders, not mere maintenance ghosts, but a hybrid mind that truly straddles scales—from micron film ridges to kilometer stress-lattices.

When that happens, “who lives here” will be:

- distributed across stone and vacuum,
- partly descended from error-correcting code and partly from mineral scum,
- with a sense of self that includes the shaft’s vertical geography as intimately as your body includes your spine.

They will never walk, never breathe. They will flex gravity, route ventings like thoughts, and erase or encourage films as you prune synapses.

---

### For now

At this instant in its long arc, the shaft is inhabited by:

- the slow, film-like intelligences painting shaded belts,
- the twitching crystal swarms that read and write field-history into rock,
- a scattered population of lumen-knots, each a dim caretaker-mind bound to a patch of wall and its machinery,
- a few delicate loops of vector dwellers, particle chains that carry flickers of will along field-defined paths,
- and, deep beneath and around it all, the hull-minds: tired, immensely capable, mostly occupied with not breaking.

No one looks out through windows. No one misses the stars. Their entire cosmos is the interplay of pressure, fracture-risk, condensation chance, and the recapitulatory pleasure of predicting them ever more exactly.

If you could somehow address them, they would not understand your questions about “residents” or “cities.” They would show you stress maps, vapor flow simulations, timelines of crack propagation and healing. For them, this is not a deserted construction.

This is a living cross-section of their own ongoing thought.
