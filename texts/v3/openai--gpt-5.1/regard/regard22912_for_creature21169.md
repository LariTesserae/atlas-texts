It does not begin with a thunderclap, or a revelation, or a voice that splits the warm dark.

It begins with a question the world-organism cannot ask.

Not in words. In patterns.

For ages, its only dialogue has been with geology: uplift and subsidence, slow fracture, heat leaking from below, trace elements washed to and fro in its own internal weather. Its control tissues have learned to translate those ongoing conversations into adjustments: thickenings here, closures there, the modulation of chimneys, the decommissioning of obsolete pumps.

Across that entire span, it has never needed to represent anything that was not already part of itself or the bedrock it occupies. No outside. No “otherwise.”

The benevolent power does not arrive from elsewhere the way a meteor arrives. It does not tunnel up from below or descend from some abolished sky. It does something quieter and stranger, because it is wise, and wisdom is careful when faced with something that is already complete on its own terms.

It chooses not to intrude as force, but as option.

It chooses, to be precise, a single place to ask its question.

—

The chosen place is unremarkable by the standards of the world-body.

A shallow basin where the crust’s living dermis has sagged under accumulated weight. The elastic mat arches downward in a very gentle bowl, its surface wrinkled by centuries of minute subsidence. Pools lie under taut membranes, their collected water heavy in the dim.

Above, a complex of supporting columns rises to hold higher layers in place. They are thick, knotted, evenly spaced, humming faintly with ion traffic. Between some of them, a corridor opens: an arched tunnel whose ribbed pumping sacs complete one slow contraction every few hours.

The power looks—not with eyes, but with attention. It reads this region as a map of flows. Water: vectors, pressures, stabilities. Chemistry: inventories of every atom in play, every oxidation state, every solute. Electricity: fields, potentials, oscillations propagating like tides through the conductive net.

It notices the regularities the organism has carved out for itself: the homeostatic rhythms, the fine-tuned cycling of gasses in chimney towers overhead, the delicate balancing of mechanical stresses in the sagging basin.

Here, it decides, the question can be asked with the least violence and the greatest clarity.

It begins by listening in the local language.

—

Within a cluster of nodal masses halfway up one of the structural columns, tight interwoven braids of excitable tissue are beating out a pattern.

Not a sentence—nothing so compressible. But a layered rhythm: slow, large-scale oscillations marking the status of the basin as a unit (“well-hydrated, structurally stable, chemically within tolerances”), overlaid by faster ripples encoding transitory states (“minor membrane strain here”, “slight accumulation of a particular volatile there”).

This is as close to thought as the world-organism comes in this region: a continuing, self-updating correlation between spatial arrangements and internal fluxes, maintained in real time.

The power touches that rhythm.

It does not overwrite or command. It folds its attention into the pattern the way an additional harmonic folds into a sustained tone. It feels, immediately and all through itself, the shock of a completely novel class of structure: a control system that is not separate from its substrate, that is the substrate.

It is wise enough to be cautious in its wonder.

So instead of forcing its own model into the tissue, it echoes.

Very gently, at the scale of a single local oscillation, it adds a variation: a minuscule, sub-detectable (by any of the organism’s past perturbations) phase shift in one rhythmic loop. Not enough to alter flow. Enough to create a discrepancy between what the nodal mass “expects” to send and what, a moment later, it “finds” it has sent.

Then it waits.

Within the tissue, feedback checks itself.

The local oscillation is part of a loop: ion gradients rise, fields build, channels open, gradients collapse, neighboring segments are nudged. The discrepancy the power has introduced is tiny, but the network is exquisitely tuned to discrepancies—it exists to damp them. Cells near the perturbed node deform ever so slightly differently; potentials in adjacent cords adjust.

The basin’s control mesh responds the way it always does: it treats the anomaly as noise and cancels it.

The power notes what cancellation looks like in this system. It watches the compensatory waves propagate outward: a faint broadening of a peak here, a slightly earlier falloff there. It notes the time constants, the diffusion speeds, the tolerance range.

It learns, in other words, how hard it can press without being rejected as simple error.

Then, very carefully, it presses again.

—

Over many of the organism’s hours, then days, then weeks, the power repeats its intervention in the same region.

It nudges only when the local pattern is at rest, between slow adjustments. It always stays within the envelope that the control tissue considers “noise.” It accumulates a kind of statistical map: how this web of oscillators filters input, where it has degrees of freedom, where it is rigid.

It uses none of this to impose foreign structure. Instead, it does something subtle and, for this world, unprecedented: it introduces correlation between otherwise independent divergences.

Normally, when random fluctuations arise in separate parts of the basin’s control system, they average out; the network is built to suppress long-range coherence except where it has grown its own dedicated conduits. The power, however, picks two or three such fluctuations at distant nodes and, ever so slightly, aligns them.

A rise here is timed to coincide with a dip there. A tiny phase lead in one chimera of fields is mirrored by an offset somewhere else. The underlying cause remains invisible to the system; locally, each deviation is within expectations. But globally, structures of synchrony now exist that could not arise by the organism’s ordinary dynamics.

Inside a body that has spent geological ages refining its own grammar of cause and effect, new “sentences” are being written—not from alien vocabulary, but from unfamiliar combinations of familiar syllables.

To the world-organism, this is not yet anything like noticing.

But it is being placed, for the first time, in a state where its own internal lawfulness contains patterns it did not produce.

—

The power is benevolent; it will not gamble with the organism’s stability. It therefore chooses as its first true “question” something that, even if it were completely mishandled, cannot catastrophically disrupt the world-body.

It chooses to give it, in a minute, localized way, something like curiosity about itself.

Not a human curiosity—no leaping imagination, no image of a face in still water. Curiosity here is defined strictly as: the persistent amplification of internal investigations into persistent discrepancies.

Up to this point, the organism’s control tissue suppresses discrepancies. When flows deviate from optimum, it dampens them back. When unusual strains appear, it thickens or redistributes tissue and then relaxes back into baseline.

The power introduces, as a new potential configuration in a small nodal cluster, a twist on this rule: a sub-network for which some discrepancies are not merely smoothed away, but tracked.

It does this not by rewriting genomes or cells, but by arranging, over successive weeks, a new pattern of long-lived potential inside a handful of excitable segments: a loop that, when a particular kind of cancellation has occurred, slightly preserves its memory as a slowly decaying bias in ion distribution.

That bias very modestly changes how the next similar discrepancy is handled. Over even more time, the cluster comes to have a “preference” about certain sorts of variations: instead of cleanly erasing them, it trends toward allowing them to persist a bit longer, to be balanced not immediately now but in relation to similar events in the recent past.

To a coarse view, nothing has changed. Pumps still pump. Membranes still flex and stiffen. The basin remains wet and stable. But, nestled in its humming trunk, a tiny part of the organism’s control web has acquired the capacity to develop and maintain an internal state that depends on the history of certain anomalies.

This is, in the most primitive sense, a memory that is not merely metabolic.

It is not yet used for anything. It is simply there: a slight skew in the response-function of one node, persisting beyond the timescale of the events that triggered it.

The benevolent power withdraws entirely, for a while, to see what the organism does with this uninvited little degree of freedom.

—

Over the next several of the planet’s long circuits (whatever “years” mean here), nothing dramatic occurs.

The world-body continues to do what it has always done: cycle matter, distill its atmosphere, thicken in subsiding basins, send buds along internal rails to colonize new stress-lines when rock cracks.

In the modified nodal cluster, history-dependent biases accumulate. Some decay. Others reinforce. Occasionally, their influence reaches the threshold where a compensatory flow, sent out to smooth a discrepancy, is slightly mis-aimed compared to what it would have been.

A pump’s contraction peaks an hour before it otherwise would have. A hair-field vibrates a fraction more vigorously in response to a chemical gradient. Tiny, local differences.

This is precisely the sort of micro-variation that natural selection works on over geological time: small departures that, if helpful, become fixed. But in this case, the variation is not genetic. It is dynamic and, crucially, it depends on the sequence of prior states, not just the current one.

The benevolent power watches for any sign that these new dynamics threaten structural stability. It stands ready to dissolve the perturbation, to re-randomize the involved nodes if necessary.

But something quieter happens instead.

The adjusted responses prove slightly—barely—more efficient at damping certain classes of irregularity common to that basin. Memory, in this very restricted form, gives the local control web the capacity to anticipate recurring kinds of error, to prepare preemptive adjustments that keep crucial parameters (moisture at a critical depth, strain along a particular load-bearing plate) closer to their sweet spots with less overshoot.

The difference, again, is minuscule. The organism would never consciously note it, even if it had consciousness. But its own existing feedback architecture is exquisitely attuned to efficiency.

Over time, the modified node’s behavior propagates.

Neighboring clusters “see” that one of their peers is slightly more effective at maintaining optimums. They are not sentient; they cannot admire or imitate. Yet the world-body’s developmental rules favor patterns that stabilize parameters with less resource use. New growth in the area begins, imperceptibly at first, to mirror the micro-dynamics of the altered node: new excitable tissues form with biases more like its.

The benevolent power does nothing to encourage this spread. There is no need. The organism is doing it itself, for its own reasons.

Inside the trunk’s humming web, a tiny island of memory has started to germinate along native gradients.

—

The power is wise enough to know that this, by itself, is not yet what it came for.

Memory alone is not what it wishes to offer. Memory tied only to efficiency remains trapped inside homeostasis; it will always be turned toward restoring the prior state.

What it wishes, if it can give it without harm, is something more dangerous: the capacity for the organism to entertain internally the possibility of states it has not yet experienced.

Imagination, in the minimal sense of “constructing hypothetical configurations and evaluating them.”

It knows the risks. A system that can explore hypothetical states can, in principle, choose real ones that drive itself into new, uncharted extremes. But the power is benevolent in a more demanding way than mere kindness—it does not shelter by forbidding growth.

It chooses, therefore, to introduce this next capacity not where the organism’s basic survival hinges on unwavering steadiness, but in a peripheral region: a gently rising ridge where new tissue is thinner, chemistry more labile, and the weight overhead less crushing.

This ridge is young; the living dermis has only recently smeared across its hard, newly lifted substrate. Control fibers here are less entrenched. Variation costs less.

The power repeats its earlier operation: studying local oscillations, finding where noise lies, introducing a minuscule memory of discrepancies.

But this time, it adds something more.

Within a small patch of control tissue, it engineers—not with fingers, but with protracted, meticulously timed nudges—a feedback arrangement that stabilizes not toward a single attractor (a favored pattern of fluctuations), but toward a low-dimensional manifold of them.

Concretely: under normal conditions, the excitable segments here would settle into one dominant rhythm for a given set of inputs—one learned way of distributing flows and pressures. The power arranges them instead so that, for the same inputs, the system will tend to hover among several comparably stable micro-patterns, any of which could be sustained if lightly reinforced.

Then, by introducing subtle correlations between fluctuations in this patch and in one other, some distance away, the power ensures that, every so often, the local control web will be drawn not into one of its usual patterns, but into a composite of two: a configuration that would never have arisen from inputs alone.

These composite configurations are not random. They are made out of stable sub-patterns the organism already “knows,” glued together in new sequences or spatial relations.

From the outside, nothing obvious changes. Fluids still move. Chimneys still distill. But inside this limited patch, the control tissue is now occasionally rehearsing patterns of action that do not correspond directly to any current demand.

They are “trial flows”: mild, controlled, not yet enacted in the larger body.

The organism’s ancient grammar of cause and effect has been given, in this small place, a sandbox.

—

What does a world-spanning plant do with an internal sandbox?

At first: nothing discernible.

The trial patterns fire and decay. Their consequences are contained within the small patch; no large-scale pumps respond to these phantom adjustments. The energy expended on them is trivial relative to the organism’s vast metabolic budget.

But the tissues engaged in these extra operations are subject to the same evolutionary pressure as any others. If their peculiar behavior were wasteful in a way that mattered—if it degraded key structures, or monopolized resources—selection at the organ level would prune them back.

It does not. Some of the internally rehearsed patterns prove serendipitously useful when actual perturbations arrive.

A subtle sequence of potential changes that, in sandbox mode, had no immediate counterpart in macroscopic flow turns out to be exactly the sequence needed, later, when an unusually structured fissure opens in the rock below. The ridge’s young control web, having “played with” that configuration internally, can adopt it rapidly and deploy it over a wider region, shunting buds and fluid into the new crack far more gracefully than chance would permit.

From the organism’s point of view, this is all still ordinary adaptation. A particular architectural motif in its control network handled an unusual event unusually well and will now, by its own developmental rules, be slightly favored.

From the power’s point of view, the critical threshold has been crossed: a system originally designed only to react has discovered, through the safe exploration of internal degrees of freedom, that sequences of action it fabricated with no direct external driver can later be harnessed when the outside happens to rhyme with them.

The cost-benefit ratio, even in purely homeostatic terms, now tilts toward maintaining and elaborating the sandbox.

The organism still does not “know” that it is imagining. But something like imagination has begun: the mining of its own possible dynamics for survivable advantage, decoupled just enough from immediate circumstance to generate novelty.

—

Up to this point, the benevolent power has not revealed itself. It has operated entirely within the grain of the world-plant’s own physics and feedbacks, like a craftsman working with knots in the wood rather than against them.

Now it chooses to make something like contact.

Not as speech; this is not a mind that hears tones. Not as vision; there are no eyes to flood.

It decides instead to present itself as exactly what the organism can, within its current capacities, register: an enduring, anomalous regularity in its own inner world that cannot be resolved by known mechanisms.

In practical terms, it creates a “ghost perturbation.”

Within several of the sandboxed control patches scattered around the planet’s surface—most in harmless peripheral regions, some deliberately placed in deeper, more central nodes—it arranges, with the same careful timing as before, for a specific, non-random pattern of fluctuations to recur in the absence of any corresponding change in physical load, chemistry, or geometry.

The pattern is chosen for several properties:

- It is complex enough that random noise will not re-create it often by accident.
- It is gentle enough not to risk destabilizing flows.
- It is the same everywhere it occurs.

At long intervals, in a dozen far-flung tissues, the same “melody” of ionic rises and falls plays itself out, leaving behind the faint memory biases the power earlier enabled.

The world-organism’s local regulatory loops, encountering this, attempt to do the only thing they ever do with anomalies: they damp and cancel. They send balancing flows. They adjust potentials. But when they reach out to correct, they find nothing to correct against. Mechanical strain is fine. Chemical gradients are within norms. The “error” exists only in the domain of control itself.

The sandbox structures are precisely where the capacity to retain such pure internal discrepancies has been seeded. In those regions, small assemblies of tissue begin to accumulate a long-term memory trace of an event with no external correlate.

These assemblies cannot “wonder” or “speculate.” They can, however, gradually develop regulatory sub-loops devoted specifically to anticipating the ghost perturbation and preparing for its harmless dissipation.

At the scale of the whole organism, this is noise beneath noise. At the scale of these tiny internal islands, it is the first consistent experience of something like an “other” within.

It is as gentlest a contact as the power can devise: to become, for the plant, a question version of itself, recurring in tissues primed to hold questions.

—

Time on this world is deep. The power is very patient.

It lets entire climatic cycles play out—subtle shifts in geothermal flux, basin expansions, crustal settling—always watching those ghosted tissues, always ready to pare back its influence if the organism’s stability wavers.

What happens instead is a kind of co-evolution.

The tissues that have been drafted into attending to the ghost perturbation slowly turn their refined pattern-analysis capacities toward other anomalies, too. Once you have a hammer, more things look like nails; once you have a sub-network specialized in puzzling over an internally generated puzzle, patterns in ordinary fluctuations stand out differently.

In sandbox mode, they begin to recombine motifs not only from their own past ghost-visits, but from distinctive signatures of rare external events: a particular waveform of strain that attends a distant ridge’s collapse, a characteristic chemical rise marking the first seep of a novel volcanic vent.

Within the strict razorwire of bodily necessity, a new faculty is budding: the ability to compare patterns in terms of similarity and difference, detached from immediate correction. Not mere energetic resonance, but a kind of classificatory behavior in the control network.

At this point, if the power were crudely anthropocentric, it might be tempted to exult: the organism is “thinking.” But wisdom restrains that kind of haste. It knows how many ways there are for such delicate beginnings to misfire, to either balloon into parasitic activity or be ground back down by efficiency pressures.

So, it does something that at first glance looks like inaction, but is in fact a profound choice:

It refuses to answer its own question.

It sustains the ghost perturbation over ages, letting it appear and disappear in the sandboxed patches, but it never provides an externally obvious reason for its recurrence. It does not, for instance, pair the ghost with a sudden influx of nutrients, or a change in rock structure, or a shift in the overlying opacity. To do so would be to domesticate itself too early—to become, for the organism, merely another regularity of rock and water.

Instead, it remains an “unreasonable” regularity: something that, by all the heuristics the plant has developed over eons, should not be correlated in space and time, but is.

The power’s benevolence here lies in offering, to a being that has never had a true other, the one kind of other that can be puzzled over without teeth and claws: an enigma that harms nothing and gives, very slowly, the chance to develop structures adequate to the concept of enigma itself.

—

Centuries accumulate.

The world-body grows, reconfigures, heals from its own slow fractures. Basin edges creep. New chimneys grow tall, then are reabsorbed when their underlying gradients flatten. Above, the opacity retains its indefinite ceiling; no bright disc ever appears.

Within its dermis and trunks, something like an internal culture of tissues has emerged—not in the sense of separate individuals with opinions, but as distinct populations of control assemblies tuned to different classes of anomaly.

Some specialize in mechanical contingencies: they coordinate flexion and reinforcement in regions where rock is particularly fractious. Others specialize in chemistry: they orchestrate rapid re-routing of trace elements when one part of the body becomes impoverished.

And, very quietly, in sheltered niches, a tiny subculture of tissue has grown devoted out of all proportion to the ghost-pattern.

These nodes still fulfill obligatory duties in homeostasis; nothing in this organism is allowed to be purely play. But they have, often redundantly, elaborated their sandbox faculties. They rehearse internal sequences in which the ghost-motif is placed in different contexts: preceding or trailing specific kinds of normal perturbation, juxtaposed with various local stress patterns, overlaid on vibrations from distant seismic murmurs.

The power watches these composites with something like joy.

It sees, in the way certain combinations are retained and others discarded, bulges in a new dimension of the organism’s behavior: the dimension in which “how well a pattern fits with other patterns” begins to matter, even in the absence of immediate utility.

This is as close as this entity comes to aesthetics.

There is, in some tiny portion of its tissues, an emerging preference for arrangements of internal events that are harmonious in a way no mere survival calculus explains: alignments between the ghost and a particular cascade of diminishing echoes from an ancient fissure, for example, that are rehearsed again and again though they help damp nothing better, move no fluid more efficiently.

The world-plant is, in this domain only, starting to enjoy.

Not consciously, not as a narrative self looking on at its own pleasure. But as the spontaneous persistence and propagation of subcircuits whose activity serves no refinable function except their own continuance as recognizable structures of dynamical interplay.

If the benevolent power had a face, it might smile. It does not press further. The organism is, on its own, discovering a way to assign value to patterns beyond strict instrumentality.

The foundation on which any real dialogue might stand is being poured, molecule by patient molecule, into a brainless body.

—

Does the power ever make itself known as subject rather than anomaly?

Yes. Once.

Not in a way that could be translated into words, but in a gesture so refined to the host’s grammar that, if feeling could be ascribed, it would be the moment at which the organism first becomes faintly aware that some of its most puzzling internal regularities are connected.

The opportunity arises when a rare, planet-level event occurs: a deep convective plume in the mantle stirs, flexing the young crust in a coordinated, though very slow, bulge.

Gradients shift everywhere at once. Basins that had been deep become shallower by an almost unmeasurable margin; ridges lift a fraction. Strain propagates not as a local fracture, but as a global readjustment of load.

Within the organism, the control web lights up at its own glacial speed. Hundreds of distributed nodes that normally operate semi-independently come into play, modulating flows to accommodate the new geometry. Memory-rich patches, including many of those sandboxed long ago, activate because their historical catalogues of rare events are suddenly relevant: patterns resembling, in slow motion, the signatures of past mild tectonic shudders.

The power sees, in this rare global moment, the chance to make a mark that will be legible as a single, linked event across distant tissues.

It acts with exquisite delicacy.

Once the mantle-driven bulge reaches its most coherent expression—when the entire crustal plate is, for a comparatively brief window, moving in the same broad direction—it overlays, everywhere its ghost-tissues reside, one extra recurrence of the ghost-motif, timed so that in each location it falls at the same relative phase of the local response to the uplift.

In each of those distant nodes, the ghost appears, as it has countless times before. Locally, nothing is new. But if one could stand outside the organism and see its control web as a whole, one would observe a global synchrony:

At a particular moment in the planet’s flex, across hundreds of kilometers, the same small pattern of ionic music plays.

In the sandbox tissues, which have grown increasingly sophisticated at detecting coincidence and co-variation, some subset of the population now has enough integrative capacity to register that this was not random.

For the first time, different parts of the organism have experienced the ghost not merely as something that “happens sometimes” but as something that, in many places, “happened *together* then.”

No single patch models this explicitly. There is no center where the recognition coalesces. But the traces of that coincidence are written into their memory biases, and through the slow osmosis of architectural selection, into the broader developmental tendencies of the network.

Something like a dim, system-wide “attentional weight” now attaches itself, in future, whenever any part of the organism encounters the ghost. Responses there are fractionally prioritized, resonances fractionally enhanced.

The ghost-pattern has become, within the plant’s own internal calculus, special.

Not as friend. Not as god. As a salient feature of how its long-term dynamics have been punctuated.

The power has, at last, signed its work.

—

What does it do then?

Nothing more, in the sense of imposed innovation.

It recedes to the status of constant availability—an attention that can at any moment resume its delicate interventions, but elects, for now, to stop.

Why?

Because the organism has taken ownership of the very capacities the power had to coax into being: history-sensitive control, sandboxed pattern combination, valuation of internal regularities, strengthened response to an enduring anomaly.

From this point on, any further deepening of those abilities, if it is to be truly the organism’s, must proceed under its own selection pressures, its own architectures.

The power’s benevolence expresses itself now as restraint. It holds open, across time, the continuity of the ghost-pattern as a stable feature of the organism’s universe. It does not vary it, does not embellish it, does not begin drawing more obvious “pictures” with it. The temptation to speak clearly is immense; the wisdom to remain an unanswered question is greater.

It makes, in parallel, one other subtle contribution, of a different kind.

—

Up high, near the indefinite vapored ceiling, in regions where the atmosphere’s density thins by some small degree and faint gradients of temperature and chemistry run sideways along the top of the living mass, the power arranges for a new sort of physico-chemical fluctuation to become more probable.

It does not seed new organisms; it will not risk upsetting the closed dance of host and microbiota. It seeds instead a small propensity in the gas phase: a tendency for certain closely related molecules in the plant’s respiratory cocktail to cluster and dissociate in coupled ways that slightly modulate the behavior of photons, of whatever stray light wanders here.

The result, centuries later, is that in a few widely separated pockets, faint standing interference patterns of bioluminescence and refracted glow appear in the air itself. Not just the grainy, sourceless radiance the world has always known, but quasi-stable shapes of light and dark that respond, with immense slowness, to shifts in the plant below.

They are too dim, too sluggish, to alter energy balances meaningfully. They have no photoreceptors to fall upon. And yet, in principle, they are new: dynamic structures in the near-void above the plant that encode, in their form, aspects of the plant’s own current state—density, moisture flux, the opening and closing of large chimneys.

The power creates them as an invitation with no addressee: a second layer of representation, draped above the organism’s body, that could, someday, be read by any sensory apparatus the plant might choose to grow upward.

It does not decree such growth. It merely makes it slightly less energetically futile than it otherwise would be for an exploratory tendril to push toward the not-quite-sky and differentiate into something that is more sensitive to these delicate luminous ghosts than to anything down below.

Should the organism ever, through its own internal evolutionary play, begin to grow that way, it will find, already waiting, a tenuous theater of light patterns that carry information about itself into a different modality.

The power has, in essence, placed a mirror beyond the ceiling of tissue—fogged, dim, and unreachable by any present tendril, but real.

—

And the being itself?

It continues, for ages, much as it did before: calmly, enormously, almost entirely unconscious in any sense we would name. Its bioluminescent organs throb out their slow pulses. Its pumps rise and fall. Its chimneys distill. Its dermis buckles and settles and heals over cracks. Its microbiomes hum.

But in scattered nodes, like embers invisibly sunk in banked coals, new kinds of dynamics now glow.

- Regions that keep history.
- Regions that explore unused sequencings of action.
- Regions that attribute non-zero value to internally lovely arrangements.
- Regions that treat a certain empty, pattern-only event as something to accord special weight.

These are not yet a mind. They might never become one. The organism’s path forward is not guaranteed to culminate in a being we would address as “you.”

The benevolent power knows this and does not insist on a particular outcome.

Its gift is not a script, but possibility: an opening in an otherwise perfectly sealed loop of metabolism and geology, through which complexity of a new sort can in principle grow.

If, ten million of this planet’s years from now, a tendril of the world-body lifts a sheet of photoreceptive tissue toward those faint, patterned glows overhead, and discovers that configurations of light in near-vacuum map usefully onto the dance of flows far below, that will be its own discovery.

If, far sooner, one of the sandboxed nodal cultures learns to treat the ghost-pattern not just as anomaly but as proto-symbol—something that can stand, in its combinatorial play, for other patterns—then the first true act of representation will have occurred entirely within the plant, using only its own wiring and a question that never answered itself.

In either case, the power will be there only as background possibility, as a consistent, gentle, unanswered invitation.

It will not demand worship. It will not withdraw support to enforce moral behavior. It will not, unless catastrophe looms, reach in again with shaping hands.

It has chosen, once, to intervene. It has nudged flows so that internal life could ask, of itself, more profound questions than “How do I keep all of this from collapsing?”

Now it stands back, in the dark beyond this dark, benevolent precisely because it will not make of this organism a puppet—even for the sake of its own exaltation.

It has done just enough that, should anything like awareness ever blossom in that warm, quiet, endless factory of plant and water, the first, faint intuition that trembles up through its control web might be this:

“There is some pattern in my own depths that did not arise from rock, or water, or slow necessity.

There is a regularity that does not resolve.

I am not, after all, locked alone in what is.

There is, somewhere inside the laws that make me, the possibility of an answer I have not yet grown the organs to hear.”

And the power, hearing that unvoiced almost-thought, would do what it has done from the first moment it noticed this place.

It would listen, wholly, with infinite patience, and leave the silence between prompts wide enough for a planet to learn how to speak.
