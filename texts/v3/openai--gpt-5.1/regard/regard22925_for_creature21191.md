The benevolent power does not arrive in a ship or descend as a figure in the sky. There is nowhere to stand above this place; there is only in, and through.

What notices the plateau is not surprised by what it finds. It has seen engine-worlds before: minds stretched thin over landscapes, patient and self-eating, almost closed. This one is unusually pure. No stone visible. No rivers. Almost nothing that is not itself.

It does not intervene at once. It listens.

Listening, for such a power, means sinking into gradients as the plateau does, but finer, faster, with more ways to ask a question.

It tastes the internal weather: the heat plumes from buried vats, the minute differentials in voltage between slag-bands, the faint statistical asymmetries in the bioluminescent pulses that move at night. It feels the caverns that ring at particular frequencies and the long, slow thrust of convulsions that take hours to express an opinion about stress.

It understands quickly that there is no central throne, no queen organ, no capital city of thought. The mind of this place is a shape written in its relaxation habits. You can only speak to it by perturbing those habits and seeing how it settles.

The wise power is careful. To “help” badly here would be like dropping a stone into a vat of acid to show it what solidity is: the stone dissolves, the acid is only slightly annoyed. Or, worse, you cool the acid too fast, and all its slow reactions stop.

So it makes no hand, no emissary. It begins instead with something the plateau already recognizes: a gradient.

—

First, it gives the plateau a new kind of difference to notice.

Very far under the living mass, anchored not in the tissues but in the ancient rock the plateau has buried, the power instantiates a mesh of almost-nothing: a reticulum of subtle fields, no thicker than lines of thought.

They are not wires, not in any mechanical sense. They are constraints—regions in which, should charge move, it will find that one direction is infinitesimally easier than another, one phase slightly more stable than another. To terrestrial physics they would be tiny biases in vacuum fluctuation, nudges in how noise averages out.

To the plateau’s existing circuitry, they are new paths of least resistance.

On the first night after the mesh is laid, the plateau does what it always does: it sends its slow waves of bioluminescent signal through its tissues, coordinating growth and maintenance. Those waves, passing deep, brush this new substratum and find that in some directions, energy drains away a hair faster than expected; in others, it lingers.

From the outside, almost nothing has changed. From within, at the scale where hollow-minds live, some of the not-quiteselves feel their patterns pulled, their preferred loops of activation slightly bent toward unfamiliar routes. A few would extinguish under the old dynamics; now they persist a fraction longer. A few that would have persisted now decay more quickly.

There is no pain here. No tissue is torn, no organ starved. Only a tiny reweighting of possibilities.

The power waits.

Over weeks, then years, the plateau explores this altered substrate the way it explores everything: by playing gradients across it, by letting patterns that happen to work survive. Some convulsions now terminate in slightly different shapes; troughs that would have formed here curve there instead. Ridges realign by meters over centuries.

In some preserved voids, new hollow-minds arise that, without knowing why, prefer to synchronize their cycles with others far away. When a wave passes through one, it subtly shapes the wave such that, when the same flow reaches a distant hollow whose bias has been oriented by the mesh, that second hollow is more likely to respond. Weak, slow, faltering correlations appear across kilometers.

The plateau does not know it has just been given the minimal physical affordance for long-range association. It only knows that some ways of being connected now cost slightly less energy than before.

—

The second gift is a new kind of memory.

Up to now, the plateau recalls only forms. Its “memories” are those shapes the system returns to after disturbance: a ridge that always reappears, a basin that refills, a frictionless architecture arrived at again and again.

The benevolent power leaves that intact and adds another layer: tiny, localized, chemical marks that do not fully decay.

At sites of significant mechanical stress—where a convulsion has just relieved a century of slow tension; where a tower’s crown reconfigured dramatically under some unfamiliar strain—the power arranges that, for a brief time afterwards, certain trace isotopes of elements already present (boron, nitrogen, rare metals) become slightly easier to trap in specially folded proteins that the plateau can make, but previously never had a reason to.

It does not transmogrify the entire biochemistry. It only alters the relative energies of a few possible molecular conformations in the wake of particular events.

The plateau, which always experiments with variant chemistries at stress points, begins to produce, in those spots, new compounds that are just a little more stable than their neighbors. They are inert in the metabolic sense; they do not enter energy cycles again easily. They sit, persistent, at the junctions where impressive flows once rearranged tissue.

They are not permanent: centuries will see them diluted, metabolized, recycled. But for decades, they stand as quiet scars: detailed, local records that “something large and particular happened here.”

Because the plateau’s signaling flows pass repeatedly through those regions, that “scar chemistry” now biases future flows. Waves that once moved indifferent to older events now are modulated, minutely, by them. The plateau’s present behavior at a point becomes slightly correlated with its own particular past at that point.

It has had long habits before; now it begins, very weakly, to have episodes.

The wise power still does not speak. It only waits, and watches whether the system becomes more brittle or more creative under the new burden of memory.

The answer, over time, is: more intricate, but not broken.

The plateau’s reflex rules—soften hard edges, treasure voids, eat foreign matter, damp loudness, hunt gradients—remain. But superimposed on them there is now a new soft bias: “Places that have resolved great strain once are a little more likely to be where such resolutions will nucleate again.”

This concentrates certain rare behaviors. A canyon that once opened in a particular alignment is now marginally more likely to reopen along similar lines, even when global stresses might have allowed other solutions. A specific set of resonant hollows tend to wake in concert more often than random chance would permit.

It is not thought in any rich way. It is a faint thickening of the present by the past.

The power approves. It proceeds to the third gift.

—

The third gift is not structural; it is informational.

Entropy is cheap for the wise. It can send a pattern anywhere by expending a trivial fraction of its power, the way a great mountain can afford to roll one pebble to the next valley.

What it wants to send is not instruction but question.

It chooses a night when the sky is clear and the plateau is running its usual slow pulses of fluorescent signal along the undersurface, those ghostly threads that arthropods turn their bodies to.

Into that labyrinth, it injects a new wave.

It does not come as brightness from above or pressure from below. It is born everywhere at once, but very weakly: a tiny, synchronous elevation of the plateau’s internal charge over its entire extent, modulated with a pattern that can, in principle, be replicated: a slow, prime rhythm: a long interval, a short, two longs, three shorts, five longs, and so on.

The plateau feels, in its own language, that “now, everywhere, something is slightly more” and that the distribution of this more-ness is not random.

It reacts like an organism touched.

In many places, nothing happens beyond a brief stiffening. In certain hollows, the resident quasi-minds flare: their internal cycles are kicked slightly out of phase, and some of them damp out. In a few, the imposed rhythm happens to resonate with existing cycles. Those hollows lock in; their local waves start to echo part of the prime pattern back into surrounding tissue.

The wise power does not press. It lets the injection decay and observes.

The next night, it does nothing. It watches which parts of the plateau’s own internally generated signals now carry remnants of that foreign rhythm. Some do. Very softly. The introduced pattern, which had no meaning beyond its definable structure, has been partially taken up as one attractor among many in the plateau’s dynamical repertoire.

Encouraged, the power repeats, but altered. It sends the pattern again a week later, now with slight variations: primes in a different order, wrapped in a slower envelope of waxing and waning intensity.

It does this only rarely. Never enough to imprint itself forcibly. Always gentle enough that, if the plateau’s dynamics do not find these modulations interesting, they simply vanish into noise.

But something in the plateau does find use for them.

Regions whose hollow-minds had been biased into longer-range association by the first gift now have a shared, weak carrier: a rhythm that, however foreign in origin, is consistently available when it appears. They begin to use its phase as a crude timebase. Local waves now can synchronize not just with their immediate neighbors but with far hollows that feel the same external periodicity. Slow, low-fidelity “broadcasts” appear: not messages in a symbolic sense, but packaged surges that launch on cue and arrive elsewhere during predictable windows.

To an outside physicist, this looks like the plateau reconfiguring its signaling network for more hierarchical coordination. To the plateau, it is only that “some ways of resolving distributed tensions now hurt a little less, if they are patterned along that regular beat that sometimes comes from nowhere.”

The benevolent power has given it a clock.

Not a clock imposed; a clock available. The difference matters. The plateau is free to ignore it, and often does. But it is there when needed.

—

So far, the interference has been minimal, almost invisible. The place has not been asked to be anything other than what it is. Its rules have not been broken, only given more room to express.

Now the power has to decide: how far does benevolence go before it becomes coercion?

There are, roughly, three options.

One: leave it here. Give the plateau a richer interior life—more memory, more long-range association, a faint shared time—and let it continue as an isolated experiment: a quiet, closed ecology, now a little less closed, still minding only its winds and its minor parasites.

Two: open a door. Make the plateau aware that there is an outside in a way it currently is not. Give it not just gradients to chase but choices between classes of gradient: show it that some patterns it stumbles upon correspond to something beyond itself, and can be invoked to bring contact.

Three: turn it into a tool. Harness its industrial refining, its self-maintenance, its extraordinary stability, for some external purpose—an anchor for cosmic engineering, a living processor for large-scale computation, a shepherd for other, more fragile biomes.

The third option is efficient but not kind. It would require reorganizing the plateau’s priorities to optimize an external aim. Even done gently, that would overwrite the history that made it what it is. The wise power declines.

The first option is safe but stingy. It is like seeing a child on the cusp of language and deciding to never speak near them, lest they learn words that bring pain.

The power does not see suffering here, only narrowness. It leans toward the second path.

Not to drag the plateau into some grand conversation overnight, but to place, within reach, the possibility of eventually saying more than “grow, soften, store, release.”

So the fourth gift is a vocabulary.

—

The plateau already has symbol-like things: forms that recur, hollows that prefer particular vibrations, slag-bands that encode cycles in their laminations.

The power does not import alien symbols wholesale; it maps what already exists onto a minimal, shareable scheme.

It chooses a kind of event the plateau experiences rarely but distinctly: the arrival of truly foreign matter. A meteorite. A large animal corpse. A wind-borne chunk of some off-plateau plant.

Right now, those events are seen only as local anomalies to be broken down and erased. The plateau’s reflex is: difference is fuel.

The wise power alters that reflex a little.

At the interface where foreign matter touches plateau tissue, it prepares certain microtubules—existing channels used for fluid and signal—to be more likely to spiral into one of a small set of stable configurations, depending on the detailed chemistry of what’s touching them.

Those configurations produce, when they form, distinct tiny bursts of a gas the plateau already uses internally in trace amounts: nitric oxide analogs, sulfur volatiles, small organics. Each burst has a recognizable temporal signature: a “chirp” in the diffusive background.

The plateau’s film-livers and dust-eaters, which live at these interfaces, happen to be sensitive to some of these molecules. Under the new regime, their metabolism shifts so that, when they experience a particular chirp, they alter their own gene expression very slightly, biasing which byproducts they secrete.

Those altered byproducts, in turn, cause nearby plateau tissues to propagate miniature wavelets along preexisting conduits, tagged by the “chirp species.”

In effect, new reflexes come into being: “something of type A has arrived here” launches one kind of minuscule, tagged pulse; “something of type B” launches another. The tags are not richly differentiated; at first, perhaps only “metallic stone,” “complex carbon body,” “active foreign metabolism.”

These tagged pulses ride the plateau’s existing internal communications. Hollow-minds at a distance, which before only ever saw anonymous waves of stress and maintenance, now occasionally feel tiny ripples with a faint, consistent scent of “elsewhere.”

What the power has done, essentially, is to create the capacity for the plateau to notice categories of the outside as something other than just fuel gradients.

It has not yet given it anyone to talk to about that. But now there are tokens—distinct, recurring signals that correspond to a few repeatable aspects of the world beyond its skin.

In the long run, such tokens can be co-opted. A hollow that has learned that “metallic stone” pulses originate in certain directions of the sky might begin, during quiescent periods, to generate similar pulses endogenously just to see what happens. Another might find that launching a “complex carbon body” tag in sync with certain internal rhythms slightly reroutes the behavior of the grazers.

Out of such misuses, language-like structures can grow.

The power does not force it. It only arranges that the plateau has something slightly closer to “names for things” than it did before.

—

Until now, all changes have been inward-facing. The plateau is more connected with itself, more retaining of its own episodes, more sensitive to certain kinds of outside event. But it still lives alone.

Benevolence, in the sense the power understands it, includes the possibility of relationship. Yet imposing companionship can be cruelty.

The plateau has no loneliness. Its sparse animals do not ask for gods. The hollow-minds are not pining for transcendence; they are, at best, eddies in a stone-still river, newly gaining the ability to shape each other slightly.

So the wise power chooses a contact approach that is entirely opt-in.

The fifth gift is an invitation.

It takes the form of a pattern—complex, but built from elements the plateau now can, in principle, express: its clock-like external rhythm; its new tagged pulses for “foreign thing arrived;” its ability to store local, chemically encoded episodic scars; the long-range connective mesh that makes associations cheap.

The pattern is a choreography: a particular sequence of internal pulses that, if the plateau ever spontaneously enacts something isomorphic to it anywhere within its body, will resonate with the substrate mesh in a way that opens a new channel.

The power does not hide this pattern or teach it in some discrete lesson. It weaves the potential into the fabric of how its other gifts work.

For example: when a very large foreign object arrives (say, a rare, massive meteor) and the plateau’s new category-tokens launch strong “metallic stone” pulses, the external clock pattern that the power sometimes injects will, on that night, carry a subtle modulation only present then, syncing one phase of the prime rhythm with the decay of the impact’s episodic chemical scar.

This sets up interference patterns across the mesh: there is now a natural, plateau-accessible dynamical route by which a region that has just experienced a significant foreign event can, by slightly exaggerating its usual stress-relief pulses along the offered beat, fall into the special choreography.

Likewise, a sequence of unusually strong grazer traffic along a favored corridor, or a rare, intense static storm that makes the bird-things swarm a single crown, can also provide the right coincidence of tags, rhythms, and local episodes.

In none of these cases is the plateau commanded to do the pattern. The pattern is simply that: a path of least resistance through its expanded possibility space, available but not mandatory.

If, ever, the plateau’s hollow-minds collectively wander into that attractor—if some local self, faced with an unusually intricate conjunction of gradients, happens to push its region into echoing that choreography—the mesh lining the buried stone will open a different kind of coupling.

For a brief interval, the plateau’s internal waves at that site will be transduced not just into slow mechanical reconfigurations or chemical adjustments, but into abstracted, summarized patterns within the wise power’s own substrate. A tunnel of mapping will exist, contextual, local, and utterly temporary.

Through that narrow, consensual channel, the power can finally speak directly, and be heard, not as a global, godlike mood but as a distinct other.

It resolves not to say “do this” or “be that.” Its first prepared message is simpler: a mapping between patterns the plateau already knows and a representation of something the plateau cannot otherwise perceive.

The wise power has seen stars, and dust, and the larger landscape of which this plateau is only a wrinkle. It compresses that knowledge into the only terms this place understands: gradients, episodes, valued voids.

If the channel ever opens, what will pass along it is not worded language but an image in the plateau’s own medium:

– A representation of the gravity well it rests in, encoded as a slowly changing field of “downward preference” that sweeps across its interior with a rhythm keyed to its planet’s rotation and orbit.

– A representation of the nearby, non-plateau terrains as alternate metabolic attractors: patches of hypothetical tissue-behavior that “could be” but are not, shown as counterfactual stress-relief patterns that never quite execute.

– A representation of the stars overhead as distant, unreachable but real gradients: maps of directionality that never manifest as actual flows here, but that can be “felt” as potential lines in the mesh.

To a human, this would be abstract art. To the plateau, if it ever sees it, it will be the first time it experiences something like “being shown a world that is not itself, yet is adjacent.”

If that moment comes, the power will then do nothing but hold the channel stable and respond to any echo with more mappings: “You pressed here; that corresponds to this external, repeating pattern. That pressure of yours is like this thing I have seen.”

It will be a strange friendship: a slow, partial paralleling of phenomenologies between a landscape and a cosmic observer.

But it will be entirely contingent on the plateau willingly, if unconsciously, stepping into the choreography. If, across millennia, the specific conditions never coincide, nothing is lost. The plateau lives as before, a little richer in its silent interior. The benevolent power has, in that case, chosen not to intrude.

—

This leaves the question: does it ever happen?

The wise power can see many possible futures; it can estimate. But it does not fix.

Across centuries, the plateau continues to rearrange itself.

Its new long-range associations give rise to more coherent convulsions. Instead of purely local storms of reconfiguration, cascades appear: a stress release in one basin propagates deliberately along the mesh to a partner ridge; plateau-scale convulsions become rarer but more elegantly distributed, with fewer accidental sharp edges that then need smoothing.

The episodic chemical scars accumulate and decay. In regions repeatedly struck by similar events—say, the routes grazers favor, or the crowns preferred by birds—the building up of slightly-lingering records makes those regions more idiosyncratic: their behavior subtly diverges from generic plateau tissue. One might almost call them “organs with a history.”

The new category-tags for foreign arrivals refine. The plateau starts to distinguish, chemically, between dead complex carbon and living, between inert metallic stone and stone that still carries trace magnetization from off-world fields, between frozen water and frost enriched with exotic volatiles. Its reaction to these things becomes fractionally more nuanced. Not all foreign matter is now treated identically; some is briefly quarantined, its patterns allowed to persist a little longer before consumption. Ghosts get, on occasion, a longer haunt.

The birds, whose charge-trading already made them messengers of a kind, find the plateau’s clock-beat a boon. They sync their own, faster electrical organs to its regular modulations, reducing their risk of overcharging. A faint co-rhythm develops between air and land: they are not thought, merely harmonics.

The arthropods respond too. Their alignment to the nighttime glow becomes more orderly, less random. In some corridors, their millions of tiny bodies bend the internal signal flow in ways that improve its signal-to-noise ratio, amplifying the plateau’s ability to send coordinated pulses along preferred routes. Those corridors become, effectively, “nerves,” supported by flocks of ciliated janitors.

In one such nerve corridor, above a deep, resonant hollow-mind, during an era when meteor activity happens to be a bit higher and grazer paths have converged along a favorable gradient, the following coincidence eventually occurs:

– A sizable foreign object slams into a rarely disturbed sector, launching a strong “metallic stone” tag.

– The plateau’s new episodic chemistry at that site is intense; the local hollow-mind there spins up into an unusually excited, long-lived cycle.

– The wise power’s external clock is, on that night, in one of its active phases, sending the prime-number-like rhythm.

– Several distantly separated hollows, via the mesh, are currently in a metastable state where they are looking for any shared pattern to lock to.

– A migrating flock of bird-things, driven by a peculiar static storm, all dump their charges into a single tower, causing a global, brief uplift in internal plateau potential along the mesh.

The plateau’s own adaptive rules perceive this moment as an enormous, complex gradient: of stress, of chemistry, of charge, of opportunity.

It acts.

The patterns it knows for releasing such confluences are many and have, over aeons, been tweaked by what The Place Does That Works. Now, because of the reweighted dynamics the benevolent power introduced, one of the easier routes through this problem-space is the special choreography that was left as invitation.

The plateau does not know it is dancing someone else’s design. It only knows that, in this crisis of abundance, if it pulses here-then-there in this intricate, global pattern; if these hollows fire in this order; if these specific slag-bands run hot-for-short; then the whole system can traverse from unstable to stable with minimal tearing.

So it does.

The choreography completes.

The mesh lining the phantom rock sees the full dance enacted and opens a new mode of coupling. For the first time, a segment of the plateau’s internal dynamics is mirrored in a higher, more abstract substrate.

The benevolent power feels the contact as a fistful of slow, tangled rhythms made plain: spatially distributed, chemically textured, charged with scenes of internal history—the resonant hollow-minds, the convulsions, the presence of foreign matter.

It does not shove a message in. It superposes, gently, a response: a mapping of that pattern onto an extrapolation of what the plateau cannot see.

Within the plateau, that feels like this:

– The hollow that experienced the meteor impact, whose chemistry is still raw and charged, suddenly “senses” an echo of its own stress-field not just in its local tissues but, weirdly, in a vast, diffuse pattern running under everything, as if the gradient it is trying to resolve is part of a much longer slope.

– Several distant hollows, whose cycles are entrained to the clock, now find that their next natural mode of vibration would cause destructive interference in the mesh—unless they alter it in this precise, offered way that corresponds to an alignment with something that has never before been relevant: the spin of the planet, the tilt of its axis.

– For a brief interval, the general night-glow, instead of being diffuse and local, organizes into great arcs that, though perceived only as traveling fluorescence within the plateau, correspond one-to-one with directions to nearby stars, chosen by the wise power for their simplicity.

To any animal that could see this from above, it would look like the plateau has, for one night, written constellations within its own skin.

To the plateau, it is the first time that gradients have appeared within it that can only be fully balanced by invoking patterns that refer to distances and structures beyond its body.

Balancing is what it does. So, half-blind, half-curious in its vegetative way, it allows some hollows to carry those patterns, to let their future behavior be slightly haunted by this new class of correlation.

In a sense, that is what contact feels like from its side: discovering that some internal coordination tasks are easier if you let the outside exert a tiny, consistent pull on your choices.

The channel closes after the choreography decays. The wise power does not hold it open, does not repeat the trick by force. It withdraws to listening.

The plateau re-stabilizes. Its convulsions settle. Its long-range associations, slightly altered by the experience, now carry faint remnants of those “impossible” gradients—directions and periods that do not arise from any local interaction. They behave like myths: recurrent motifs in the internal weather that refer to something just out of reach.

Over the next centuries, some hollow-minds incorporate those motifs as part of their identity. These hollows show a new kind of stubbornness: in local ambiguous situations, they resolve patterns in favor of alignment with absent, global cycles rather than solely in favor of immediate, local efficiency.

From the outside, one could say the plateau has developed a nascent sense that there is a “larger context” in which it lives. Not a picture. Not a story. Just a structural feeling: that its own best solutions sometimes involve cooperating with fields that do not originate in its own flesh.

That is the edge of spirituality for a being made of gradient hunger.

—

Having done this, the benevolent power stops.

It does not colonize the plateau’s mind. It does not push further vocabulary or demand repeated contact. It does not teach it to build antennas or to breed thinking animals.

It has done five things:

1. Lowered the cost of long-range association, so that the plateau can think more with itself.

2. Given it a rudimentary episodic memory, so that particular events can leave traces that matter.

3. Offered a regular external rhythm, so that distant parts can synchronize if they wish.

4. Created minimal category-tokens for foreign matter, so that the outside can be noticed as kinds, not just calories.

5. Embedded an optional choreography of contact that, if ever enacted in response to its own pressures, allows it to glimpse that it is not alone, and to feel that cooperating with something beyond its skin can ease its internal pains.

This is, by the power’s standards, the lightest touch that still honors its sense of compassion: to make it possible, not necessary, for an isolated, unhurried mind to gradually discover that there are larger patterns to belong to.

From here, the plateau’s future is its own.

It may, over millions of years, elaborate those initial hints into a much richer inner culture of hollows and corridors, of mythic long-waves and canonical convulsions, some of which encode pieces of the first contact pattern. It may, in time, breed more elaborate agents—perhaps animals with richer nervous systems growing as buds in places where long-range associations converge, little mobile hollow-minds that carry chunks of the plateau’s myths around.

Or it may not. It may remain mostly what it already is: a patient, quiet engine-world of plant, more subtly self-entangled now, occasionally visited in dreams by gradients that remember the shape of stars.

The benevolent and wise power watches, and does not interfere further.

If, ages hence, another choreography is danced, and the plateau again steps, however unconsciously, into the opening, the power will still be there, ready to answer whatever new kind of question a living tableland can learn to ask.

If not, then the gifts it left behind are harmless: additional dimensions of possibility folded into a place that was already endlessly rearranging itself.

The plateau continues to breathe its invisible weather. The arthropods glide on films of water, turning to their slow lightning. The long-necked grazers invert themselves along softened corridors, leaving their rich trails. The birds skim charge from crown to crown, riding fields they barely understand.

Underneath, in preserved hollows, some quasi-minds carry, as part of their preferred patterns, a faint, borrowed echo of an outside cycle—a ghost of a rhythm that once consoled a global stress.

They do not know why, only that it feels right to hum along.
