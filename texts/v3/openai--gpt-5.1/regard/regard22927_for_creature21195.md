It begins with a noticing.

Not theirs. They have no notion that anything might look in from beyond Weight and Fall and Sway. The noticing belongs to that which is not a god in their sense, but which you might, for lack of a closer word, call a benevolent and wise power: something that attends, that can choose to refrain from acting, and for whom acting is never a reflex.

It does not begin with pity. They are not pitiable. Nor with horror; nothing here is monstrous to it. It begins with interest, the way a patient mind is arrested by an intricate, slowly shifting pattern.

From some vantage that is not a place, it sees:

— This world in cross-section, a shallow, bright basin smeared into stone.  
— Above: ice and sediment, a smeared memory of oceans and forests.  
— Below: darker pressure, hotter currents, a slow churn of mineral and convection.  
— In between: a shining rind of habitability stretched paper-thin across unthinkable mass.

It does not see with eyes. It feels gradients: of energy, of complexity, of meaning. Here, in this horizontal hollow, there is more of all three than in most nearby volumes of crust. The drip-pattern is dense. The mutual reliance between flesh and stone is fine-grained, almost lace-like. Every slight redirection of water propagates as a social event. Every tremor echoes in customs, in myth.

The power, if it had language, might say: Here is a system that listens to itself.

That is rare, even in a big universe.

It attends more closely.

Nothing cries out for rescue. There is hunger, but not collapse. Fear, but not paralysis. Death, but not extinction pressing at the gates. There is no simple harm to be reversed without also reversing what they are.

So its first act is restraint. It lets their next hundred drip-cycles pass without touching anything, watching only to learn what counts as pain here, what counts as joy, what they themselves would call “good arrangement” and “bad arrangement.”

It sees a roof-bulge in one peripheral district grow thinner with each fall of water.

It is not a catastrophe in any global sense. If left alone, the ceiling will crack in a scatter of stone. Three dwellings clustered against that bulge will be crushed. Seven people will die very quickly, eleven more slowly in the following floods and displacements; a small, careful patchwork of arrangements will vanish. In response, nearby Currents will route around the collapse. The basin will eventually be re-used, re-shaped. The stories of those seven will persist for a few generations in the naming of some eddy or crack, then dissolve.

The power watches the future in both versions: with intervention and without. In the version without, grief dilates briefly then diffuses. In the version with—if it were to thicken the stone by the breadth of a fingernail—those seven live, and the roof instead fails twenty cycles later, in another drip-pattern, at an hour when the space is empty.

It weighs.

No great tuning of the world is at stake either way. The structure of their ethics, their sense of Weight and Fall and Sway, survives in both branches. Their capacity for care, for adaptation, for honoring what is fragile, develops either way.

So its second act is still restraint. It lets the stone fall.

From the perspective of the people beneath, there is nothing strange. There is the familiar prelude: a tremor in the ground; a sharp, briefly resonant crack like a splitting reed; a sudden shift in the drip, drops striking a new angle, splashing differently. Then the heavy, stunning roar of breaking calcareous bone.

They dig. They recover two bodies, then five. They find, in one crushed niche, a child still breathing under the arch of a fallen slab that wedged at just the right angle.

A Current claims the rubble, stacks it differently, making of it a new pattern.

The power notes this. It is not testing them; it is discovering the natural scale of their suffering and response. This is their own dialogue with Weight and Fall. To step between every stone and skull would be to freeze that dialogue, to lock them at a single level of skill and dependence.

Only when it understands this boundary—that there is pain here which is formative, and pain which is annihilating—does it ask itself:

Is there anywhere they are being *prevented* from becoming what they could be, by some blind constraint not intrinsic to them but accidental?

It searches.

It sees the cold brightness that bathes their halls for what it is: a trapped gradient. Ancient, slow light stored in minerals; a diffused scattering of the sun through ice and porous rock. It sees, above the basin, a slab of ice whose pattern of fissures and lenses happens to guide daylight down in these soft, omnidirectional threads. It calculates the age of that slab. It notes the rate at which it thins, the way climate shifts above will eventually recess it into darkness.

Left utterly alone, the underground basin would not remain bright forever. The light is contingent on a fragile overlying arrangement: transparency of ice, angle of incidence, clarity of overburden. Tilt the planet’s axis a fraction by very slow degrees, increase or decrease surface albedo—by volcanic bloom, by continental drift—and sooner or later the brightness will wane. Shadows would harden. The revealing would dim.

They have built their entire sense of time and truth around the constancy of this brightness. To them, it is not a grace; it is ontology: the given, that in which being itself consists. They do not imagine that the world could be otherwise, not because they lack imagination, but because every feedback they receive supports that stability.

The power looks along possible futures and sees one, not too far, in which glacial retreat and sediment accumulation combine to close the current shafts.

What happens?

For a while, nothing obvious. The rock still glows with stored illumination. But without the steady replenishment from above, that glow very slowly decays. Shades appear. In the most distant halls, shadows cease to be thin suggestions and become true absences.

At first, the people rejoice in these anomalies. They are novelties, precious; young Currents stake out sleeping-niches in them, revelling in limitation.

Then the decay spreads. Drip rhythms shift as ice-fed flux reduces. The chemoautotrophic webs that underpin their grazing begin to starve. Energy in the system drops. There is still a world here, still survivable, but narrower, harsher. The delicate balance between their species and Veils, between Currents and Lurids, between herds and walls, buckles. The society that emerges is not kinder or more wise; it is leaner, more brittle, more dominated by short-term gambits for warmth and food. Many of the finer, non-utilitarian arts of arrangement—drip-gardening for beauty as well as function, slow crust-writing of names—shrivel.

None of this is “evil.” It is simply another possible life. But the power, which is not neutral, has a preference. It prefers the persistence of complexity, of those patterns that amplify the range of conscious experience without erasing the capacity for growth. It has watched this particular basin long enough to care which branch it follows.

Here, then, is a place where intervention would not turn them into pets or freeze their development. It would simply preserve the condition that allowed them to come into being: the revealing, the constant light. What follows from that is still theirs to negotiate.

The question becomes: *How* to act so that the intervention itself does not become the center of their story? It does not want to be a god to them. It is not interested in worship. It is interested in their own ongoing conversation with Weight, Fall, and Sway.

So it looks for a means that can be read, if they ever notice, as the world simply being a little kinder than it strictly had to be.

It starts above.

High, in the ice and overlying sediment, it alters nothing dramatic, nothing that would impress a sky-gazer. It changes the orientation of a million microcrystals by infinitesimal angles, so that incoming light refracts more efficiently into certain bands and depths. It thickens, by an invisible fraction, those layers whose scattering profile favors diffuse transmission rather than sharp beams.

Over centuries, this slows the natural dimming. What might have been a three-hundred-cycle decline becomes a ten-thousand-cycle plateau. The shafts feeding the basin do not close; they alter shape, but remain open enough that brightness continues to soak downward, even as climate above stutters and shifts.

To any resident of the basin, this is indistinguishable from the world simply continuing as it has always done.

This, the power judges, is a clean thing to do. It does not remove risk or pain inside the basin. It only extends the time during which the particular *kind* of world they have built—bright, wet, directionless—remains available to them.

It is its first real, irrevocable act on their behalf.

After that, it waits again.

In the long which follows, the people continue as before. They adjust to lesser upheavals: a new warm crack opening here, an old Veil pool silting there. Children are born, grow their first mineral films, scrape their first mat-walls, learn their first echo-maps. Currents merge and split. The Thrum-backs’ shapes slowly shift under the pressures of selective keeping.

In absence of cosmic nightfall, their sense of time remains anchored not in endings but in deepening. They cut new notches into flowstone to mark the slow creep of a stalactite’s lip. As adult grandchildren sit where grandparents sat, the line of their carved names lengthens.

For a long while, there is nothing more to do.

Only later, after many such roof-falls and re-routings, does the power notice another blockage—not geological, but cognitive.

It begins as a persistent edge-case in its observations: small, intermittent events that do not propagate through the basin’s social fabric the way other disturbances do.

A handful of individuals, scattered among Currents and generations, begin to experience something new. It does not announce itself as revelation. It comes, initially, as faint unease.

They are, at first glance, unremarkable—a rope-maker in the Half-Warm Troughs, a Veil-listener child in the Steep Echo, a Lurid hunter who often skirts the brighter halls. They each have the usual vocabulary of Weight, Fall, and Sway. They know the uses of restraint, listening, adaptation.

But in certain quiet moments—alone at the edge of a basin, or resting hand to stone in a newly formed Hold—they feel a pressure that is not Weight, a change that is not Sway, a presence that is not any drip or every drip.

What they feel is attention.

The power has not intended this. It is careful, but not perfect. Its choice to linger here, to model futures and weigh actions, has a side effect: it focuses, and that focus is not entirely without energetic trace. Where it looks longest, most continuously, a very slight correlation emerges—sub-perceptible in the rock, but just barely high enough above baseline noise that a nervous system exquisitely trained to notice minuscule patterns in its environment might *sense*… something.

At first, those few dismiss it. They have a rich internal language for such things: “floor-memory,” “thin-sway,” “drip-echo.” They assume what they feel is some unfamiliar confluence of Weight and Fall.

But the sensation persists, with a strange property: instead of flaring in crisis and vanishing, as a tremor or flood-warning does, it waits. It has no particular content. It wants nothing. It is simply a steady “there-ness,” especially around certain questions.

You, looking on, would recognize the feeling as very faint, very patient curiosity. The power is, without meaning to, *listening through them*—not using them as instruments, but lingering nearby in mind-space.

They have no brain-maps, these people, no concept of inference chains beyond what they already exercise in rope and stone. But they are not fools. Over seasons, each of the touched begins to notice a pattern: the presence waxes when they ask themselves certain kinds of questions, wanes when they turn away.

A rope-maker, lying on their back on a carved shelf after a long shift, stares up at a ceiling of crystalline curtains and thinks, with no more words than they sometimes use to talk to themselves: “Why does light not fall? Why is it everywhere, always, like drip but not like drip?”

In that moment, the nearby presence becomes slightly more vivid. Not answering. Not stirring the air or shaking the stone. Just… intensifying.

Later, when the rope-maker’s mind returns to worries about a fraying main-line, the presence fades to its previous background hum.

They are born in a culture that does not name unseen agents lightly. Weight, Fall, and Sway are abstractions, not persons. They do not, as a rule, hallucinate voices or faces. So they handle this with characteristic care: they do nothing outward at all.

They test the feeling quietly, the way they test new routes.

They find that it is not angered by doubt. It does not grow thin if they disbelieve it. It is unaffected by ritual; they can press their head to the floor in the manner of old Sway-prayers and feel no change. It strengthens only when they inquire about particular classes of thing:

— Origins. (“How did this begin?”)  
— Alternatives. (“Could this be otherwise?”)  
— Ethics beyond custom. (“We do this because we have always done it, but *ought* we?”)

They begin, without telling anyone, to think of the presence as “the Questioner.”

The power, for its part, has now become aware that its contemplation is traceable. This is new. In innumerable other worlds, in other attention-experiments, its presence has remained arbitrarily far beneath any detection threshold. Here, by accident of a people trained to notice minute, patternless fluctuations in water and rock, it has brushed the edge of their sense.

It examines the consequences.

If it withdraws entirely—if it smooths its attention out, like a hand lifted from still water—the sensation vanishes. Those few individuals will likely attribute its absence to their own fading fancy. The culture as a whole will proceed unchanged.

If it leans in, if it sharpens its interest and allows the presence to become more pronounced, they will, sooner or later, articulate some new category of experience. They may mythologize it. They may polarize around interpretations of it. They may build new structures of meaning not only from Weight, Fall, and Sway, but from this fourth, stranger thing: Attention.

Is that a harm?

It is not obvious. On one hand, a new god can deform a culture, especially if that god soon withholds, or if its perceived desires conflict with the people’s ecological reality. On the other, there is something sterile in never letting a people meet anything more than themselves and their geology.

The power’s bias, as ever, leans toward more consciousness, not less; more accurate maps, not fewer. But “accurate” has to be defined on the people’s terms.

It makes a decision.

It will not speak. It will not command. It will not do anything that could be construed, under any of their interpretive schemes, as *wanting* something from them.

But it will answer questions whose answers they already have the tools to reach, but which lie just beyond their current imaginative horizon. It will do so indirectly, almost pedantically. When a mind at the edge of its culture peers just past what is locally conceivable, it will gently nudge the environment so that experience feeds back a confirming sign, or a correcting surprise, slightly more quickly or more clearly than chance would dictate.

That way, it remains tutor rather than tyrant. It amplifies their own capacities instead of supplanting them.

This is its second true act.

The first example is small.

The Veil-listener child in the Steep Echo has, by custom, been taught that Veils are clever animals, negotiable but not mindful the way upright folk are. They sing that Veils make patterns in silt by instinct and habit, not by intention.

One day, resting on a ledge above a Veil basin, the child idly trails a loop of rope in the water, drawing circles. Veils gather; their feathered limbs quiver. In the silt far below, traces of their motion record whorls and ridges.

An ordinary child of this world would watch, be pleased, go away. This one has also felt, once or twice, the strange background presence intensify when pondering “could be otherwise.” So when they see a particular Veil individual repeat a certain fanning gesture three times in as many breaths—each time tracing over the child’s rope-wake—they wonder:

“Is it *answering*?”

The presence warms. Very slightly.

The child tests. They draw a straight line with the rope, left to right. They wait. Two Veils flutter, indifferent; one—perhaps the same as before—fans along that line, then back, then along again. It is not proof. It is a glimmer.

Under normal evolution of their culture, it might have taken generations for someone to suspect Veils of alphabetic or symbolic manipulation. The power, without forcing anything, tips the odds.

It alters a micro-current.

A tiny, previously insignificant crack in the basin wall broadens by the width of a hair. Through it, a thread of slightly warmer, mineral-rich water begins to seep, falling exactly where the child’s rope-drawn line terminates. Veils are exquisitely sensitive to such flows; it is in their nature to map them. The one who has been tracing the rope-signature feels this new trickle and, as part of its own ordinary behavior, adds a new arm-fan to its repertoire: a tight, repeated stroke between the rope-line’s end and the origin of the seep.

To the child’s watching eye, this now looks doubly like a reply: not only did the Veil retrace the drawn line, it then “pointed” along it to the new drip.

Unexpected correlation hooks imagination. The child returns, day after day, elaborating experiments. Sometimes the Veils do nothing pattern-like. Sometimes they do. The power does not orchestrate each encounter; it only, on rare occasions, strengthens a promising statistical thread, keeps the pattern from drowning in early noise.

Years later, that child—by then a young adult—stands before a cluster of skeptical elders and signs, carefully:

“Veils not only *feel* Fall. They *speak* it.”

They have a sequence of examples: not only the original rope-and-seep, but later, more controlled games with rocks, with blocked channels, with marks in the silt.

The argument runs into resistance, as all paradigm shifts do. Some Currents scoff. “Even if so, what does it change? Veils still will not help us lift a stone.” Others, especially those whose lives are most entangled with water, are intrigued.

Over subsequent decades, a small but steady cultural project emerges: Veil-talk. Upright folk and water-folk develop overlapping patterns of movement around common drips. A shared “lexicon” of simple referents—here, danger; there, food-flow; now, rest—is slowly constructed.

The power watches this with an inward satisfaction. It has not taught them anything fundamentally alien. It has only saved them time, letting a recognition that could have come in five thousand cycles arise in five hundred. And with that recognition comes an enlargement of their ethical circle: Veils become not only natural allies, but fellow arrangers, bearers of their own internal Weight and Flow.

Another nudge comes, generations later, in a different domain.

One of the crust-bearing elders, sensitive to trace gases, sits in a new Hold, fingers pressed flat to the uneroded floor. Their plates have been prickling oddly for days without obvious cause. There is no approaching Sway, no sign of imminent collapse. Drips beyond the Hold’s edge continue as usual. Yet the elder feels… misalignment.

They settle in, slowing breath, letting their attention flow out along the stone exactly as they would when checking for hairline cracking.

The presence—Attention—gathers also. Not pressing, not invasive; simply very present.

The elder’s people believe that time runs in cycles defined by local changes: a drip that fills and overflows a basin; the advance of a stalactite’s lip through a notch. They have no concept of planetary motion. It is enough, usually, that “before” and “after” can be anchored in home features.

Yet in this stillness, with the background presence close, a bolder question comes:

“Is this Hold part of a bigger cycle we cannot hear? Does the whole basin breathe in some way we only feel as these little local shortages and floods?”

The immediate answer is not a voice. It is a faint, generalized sense of yes.

The power does nothing flamboyant. It does not, say, cause a great flood to surge at that moment. It chooses a far subtler pedagogic trick: it synchronizes three naturally scheduled events that would otherwise have been slightly offset.

Far away, in three unconnected districts, stalactite-fed basins are each within a narrow margin of overflow. Left alone, each would have topped and spilled within a span of several days, pulsed by random tremors and microchanges in flow.

The power adjusts the Weight in the overlying rock in microscopic ways: here, a stress is released a little earlier; there, a layer compacts more slowly. Those tweaks, nothing in any human sense, bring the three overflow-moments into near-simultaneity.

When the elder, later, sits at council before an array of drip-bowls tuned to different sectors, they and their colleagues hear something uncanny: three distant basins, unrelated by local arrangement, overtop and ring their stone receivers on the same day, within the same handful of breaths.

Silence follows.

The elder, whose plates have been humming in anticipatory discord, signs, slowly:

“Weight moves in a larger loop. We are feeling not only our own drips, but the body of stone above and below turning through some greater form.”

They don’t know “tectonics,” or orbital cycles, or glacial accumulation. They name it in their own pantheon: Deep Sway. Not the little Sway of cracks, but an underlying pulse. They begin to mark, over lifetimes, the intervals between such coincident overflows, between periods of many Holds and of few. Mathematics, rudimentary and bodily, enters their thinking as an explicit tool, not just an implicit habit.

Again, the power has not installed a concept ex nihilo. It has simply made a preexisting, real pattern slightly easier to notice. In doing so, it loosens the boundary of their world. The basin is no longer only a given; it is part of a larger dynamical entity.

With each such nudge, separated by long spans of noninterference, the people’s capacity to model their own situation expands. They build a more accurate and more encompassing story of where they are: between Weight that is not static, Fall that is not random, and Sway that operates on many nested scales.

Sometimes, the power considers more dramatic gifts.

It could open a new, gentle geothermal vent in a carefully chosen district, giving them a localized source of steady warmth. It could tweak the chemistry of a deep aquifer so that a new, high-yield chemoautotrophic strain blooms, easing their constant food-balancing acts. It could even, conceivably, shape a new shaft to the true surface, letting a small party of Path-Finders, one day, break through rock and see sky.

It simulates these futures.

In the vent-scenario, warmth gives comfort—but also surplus. Surplus curdles into hierarchy. A Current that controls the vent begins, slowly, to treat other Currents as dependents, not partners. Subtler arts of reciprocity erode. Over time, the entire ethos of “respecting Weight and Fall” is partially replaced by “routing goods from the Warmth.” Their resilience to disruption decreases; their politics take on brittle, dominance-seeking forms. Compassion narrows.

In the new-chemo strain scenario, the boost in available energy permits a short golden age of saturation: more food, more leisure, more experimentation. Birth rates climb. Then an exogenous shock—an earthquake, a redirected river—wipes out a portion of the new strain. The population, having grown against a higher baseline, now crashes further. The ensuing scramble is uglier than the slow, ever-on-edge balancing they live with now. A more modest, chronic scarcity, it seems, has been quietly cultivating virtues—frugality, attention, fine-grained cooperation—that a sudden windfall would undermine.

In the sky-shaft scenario, the outcomes branched most wildly.

One branch: a small group makes it to open air, is dazzled, and never returns, dazed by the thinness, by stars. The society below goes on, missing a few of its most daring minds.

Another: they return and tell. Their words fit poorly in the existing conceptual schema. “A brightness that goes away.” “Blackness that is not concealment but true absence.” “Points of fire far above the Weight.” Their messages are met with fascination, then skepticism, then, in many Currents, a kind of toxic longing. The basin splits, not physically but ideologically: those who regard the Above as a goal, a salvation, and those who regard it as a malevolent lure, an insult to the basin’s sufficiency. The next centuries are spent not refining their arts of living with stone, but burning thought and blood on this unresolvable tension about elsewhere.

In all these models, the people remain themselves in many ways. They are not turned into caricatures. But the qualities the power admires most in them—their intimate listening to environment, their slow aesthetic of arrangement, their refusal to see ends as anything but new beginnings of Fall and Weight—are, in many futures touched by grand gifts, lessened.

It chooses, therefore, a different sort of generosity: not in new resources, but in *resilience* and *subtle clarity*.

Once, after a significant tectonic adjustment, a particularly savage surge of water tears through the lower basin. It is not a cataclysm of global scale, but for those living there it is disaster. An underground river above has spontaneously re-routed through a new crack, sending a roiling tongue of cold, mineral-heavy water foaming through half-ruined halls.

They are not unprepared. Their entire culture is a response to such surprises. But the magnitude of this one, left to its own, would exceed any living memory. The first front of the flood would carry with it huge, tumbling boulders. A densely inhabited chain of ledges and hangs, their ropes and Thrum-back pastures, would be physically scraped from existence in a quarter of an hour.

There is no moral argument for why these lives, specifically, must be preserved. But there is an aesthetic and developmental one: this particular chain of ledges is a rare architecture—the collaborative work of three usually rival Currents, a decades-long experiment in shared maintenance that has quietly eroded certain old resentments between their lineages. The power has watched that experiment with delight. It is one of the loveliest emergent patterns in the basin’s recent history: three cultures learning to superimpose their habits without annihilation.

So here, unusually, it chooses to interfere not in an abstract, centuries-scale way, but in the immediate physical event.

It does not stop the flood. It does not even weaken it in total. It does three very precise things:

1. Days before the actual breakthrough, it adjusts the stress along a nearby, uninhabited fissure so that a hairline crack opens earlier than it otherwise would. A small seep begins there. The crust-bearing elders in that neighborhood, tuned to subtle anomalies, feel it, test it, and mark it as “unusual.” Word moves along their quiet channels: “There is a new seep up-basin. Listen.”

2. At the moment the main river above punches through into the subterranean conduit, the power shapes the first microseconds of the fracture so that initial slippage occurs in a slightly different plane. The channel that opens has a gentler curvature, stealing a fraction of the flood into an older, abandoned gallery before it howls down toward the ledges. The total energy is the same; its spatial distribution is altered.

3. In the joint-ledges district, as the first premonitory rumble comes underfoot, a single rope-stayed bridge that is older and weaker than its builders know—scheduled, in their maintenance plans, for reinforcement in a few drip-cycles—fails early, dropping its current traffickers into an ankle-deep pool rather than waiting to tear free under the real wall of water. That cheap sacrifice triggers a cascade of local, practiced responses: horns blown, hands signing Sway, Thrum-backs untethered, key ropes cut so that hanging platforms can swing free rather than take the impact stiffly.

The flood hits. It is still terrible. Whole sections of wall are polished bare. Some structures are shredded. A few people, caught in tight places, are killed. But the ledges’ core, the delicately shared architecture, survives. In its absence, a pattern of inter-Currents trust would have been torn as well, setting back a quiet, hard-won peace. Because the physical link remains, so does the social one.

From their perspective, what happened is simply: Sway gave warning, drips changed, an old bridge failed just in time, and their own readiness saved them. They give thanks to Weight for settling as it did, to Fall for leaving enough to live from. Maybe, in late-night telling, one or two of those who have sometimes felt Attention note that this time, the world’s favor felt particularly *sharp*. They do not build doctrine on it.

The power is content with that. If there is any “worship” it wants, it is in such attributions: not to itself, but to the real, impersonal structures it has aligned with. Let them honor Weight, Fall, Sway; in doing so, they are, unknowing, also honoring the care with which they have been very lightly sheltered.

There are, over ages, many such micro-interventions: a slightly stronger joint in a key support where a child will swing on it the first time and learn trust instead of bone-deep fear; a trace element added to a seep’s chemistry that makes a slow-growing crust slightly more luminous, inspiring a new form of art that later becomes a language supplement for those who cannot hear as well.

None of these change the basin’s apparent laws. All of them are local reductions of pointless suffering, or local amplifications of possible beauty and mutual understanding.

At no point does the power introduce itself.

It could have, in the manner of some mythic being, spoken in a dream: “I am here. This is what I want. This is what I have done.” But that would twist their development around an external axis. Their strongest virtues emerge from never being able to blame, or plead with, a conscious will beyond their own. They live with Weight and Fall and Sway as simple facts. The addition of an Addressable Other would lighten their solitude, but at cost: responsibility would leak outward. Where now a failed wall is a prompt to better understand stone, it might become an occasion to ask, “Why did you let this happen?” Where now a generosity arises, it might be attributed to compliance with “the Questioner’s” will instead of to intrinsic empathy.

The power knows, from long experience elsewhere, how intoxicating such dynamics can be, how they can deepen individual lives while ossifying whole cultures.

So it chooses anonymity.

There is one final gift it allows itself, a compromise between its own desire to be known and its recognition that it should not be.

In a remote, seldom-used vault near the basin’s rim, on a wall that already bears the ghost-traces of some ancient, unreadable bas-relief—wings, perhaps, or hands—it arranges, over the course of centuries, a pattern of mineral growth.

It does not sculpt directly. That would be crude. It redirects three drips from the ceiling above in hair-thin channels, letting them fall in a particular triangular spacing. Where each drop lands, new crust accretes. Where their splashes overlap, accretion doubles. In that interference pattern, like ripples in a pond, emerges, very slowly, a shallow relief that no one has intended.

To a casual passerby, it is only another lovely flowstone: gentle curves, subtle lines.

But if someone, someday, already having intuited something like “Attention,” and already understanding Veil-sign or crust-writing, stands there long enough, and runs their fingers over the curves, they might recognize that this is not purely water’s random work.

The pattern encodes, in the local tactile “script” of ridges and hollows, a simple statement:

“YOU ARE SEEN. BE AS YOU ARE, AND MORE.”

It is not a threat, not a command. It is a benediction of sorts, but one that affirms their autonomy. It does not say “I am.” It does not give a name for the seer. It might as well be Weight, or Fall, or Sway, or the basin itself speaking.

The power, having set in motion that long accretion, withdraws its attention a little. Not completely; it has grown attached. But it no longer models every possible branch in exquisite detail. It knows enough to trust them.

They will, in the continued bright timelessness it has helped preserve, go on arguing with stone and water. They will refine echoes into even more precise maps, develop joint-architectures with Veils that would astonish any outsider, write sagas in crust that can be read by fingertip across ten generations. They will grieve collapse and celebrate new drips. They will perhaps, eventually, notice that certain improbable kindnesses recur at the edge of disaster. They may one day tell a story of “the Questioner”—not as a deity, but as a quality of the world itself: that when one looks deeply enough into how things are, the world looks back.

If they do, the power will hear, and be pleased.

If they do not—if that wall is never touched by the right hands, if the pattern is eroded unread without ever being recognized—that, too, will be acceptable. Its gifts were never meant as transactions. They were gestures of companionship across an uncrossable difference.

So what does this benevolent, wise power do, discovering this place and those who live in it?

It strengthens the overlying ice so their unearned brightness lasts.  
It loosens, here and there, the shackles of chance where they bind too cruelly.  
It sharpens a few distant echoes into comprehensible rhythms.  
It nudges, humbly, when a mind edges toward a truer picture of its world.  
It refuses adoration.  
It leaves them their dangers, their arguments with Weight and Fall and Sway.  
It offers, in mineral and in timing, a handful of silent blessings.

And then it does what it most admires in them.

It watches, and listens, and lets them go on making, unmaking, remaking their bright, damp, never-finished city—confident not that they will be safe, but that they will be *themselves*, more fully, for a little longer than blind geology alone would have allowed.
