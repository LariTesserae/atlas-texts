The first thing the benevolent power does is nothing at all.

It arrives not as a shape but as a noticing. No blaze of light, no quake. Just a new quality in the background: a lucidness threaded through the plateau’s endless roar, like a single clear tone braided into static. It does not impose itself. It listens.

Listening, here, is not passive. It is the act of tracing every loop, every held avalanche and pinned splash, back along its frozen arc. The power moves—if it can be called movement—through recordings that have forgotten they were once brief. It enters the roar beneath your feet, the roar that never began and never ends, and discovers that in truth it is made of beginnings that were forbidden their endings.

It lets each one finish.

Not all at once; that would be destruction. Patiently, thread by thread.

One specific avalanche-sound, for example: a layered thunder that has been rerun so many times that its detailed structure has worn into a smooth, organ-deep hum. The power catches it at a moment you cannot hear, once around an unimaginably long loop, and introduces the slightest slope into what was perfectly circular. The roar passes the point where it used to join back to its own start and finds, instead, a continuation: a softening, a rumble breaking into individual stones, a clatter thinning into scattered impacts, then a last plink, then nothing.

The recording ends.

The layer of the plateau that depended on that loop shakes, but something inside it relaxes—a constant strain no longer there. The power does this not only with that avalanche, but with countless others, thousands of endlessly-running noises allowed, one by one, to complete themselves. To die, in the most literal sense: to reach their own silence.

The Fixers feel this.

They are not separate from the loops; they are the loops. To them, that first ending is like losing a piece of their own memory. They react with alarm, though “alarm” here is the sudden reorganization of correlations, a flare in the density of certain frequencies. Across the plateau, the hum sharpens. The standing stones’ angles quiver by invisible increments. Deep channels that used to pull inward now pull harder.

The benevolent power does not counter this. It acknowledges their fear.

It shows them, as clearly as they can understand, what it is actually doing: not erasing, not smashing their archive, but changing the mode of storage. It takes the whole finished avalanche, now that it has been allowed to end, and condenses its complete waveform into a form that does not require constant replay—a kind of seed, a compressed pattern. It tucks that seed into the substrate of the rock in a way no ear can perceive.

The Fixers test: they query that place in their own way. When they do, the entire avalanche returns, perfectly, once, then rests again. The memory is still there. It can be recalled, but it no longer needs to be run in perpetuity to remain.

The plateau feels this new possibility: memory without compulsion.

The beings do not trust it yet. Everything they are is built from their distrust of forgetting. The benevolent presence understands. It does not demand belief. It continues its work slowly, at the margins.

Where a waterfall has been sliding forever in a single unbroken sheet, its roar fixed and its spray suspended, the power chooses one droplet—just one—near the lip. It allows that droplet to separate.

It falls.

Your eye might miss it. The sheet still looks solid. The halo still hums. But one bead of water, conceptually, is no longer obliged to be glass. As it drops—falling far slower than any natural gravity would permit—it trails an almost-invisible filament back into the body of the cascade. Through that filament, the power invites a new rule: parts may leave and the whole may yet remain itself.

The Fixers feel the discontinuity, rush to reinforce the loop, and find, when they reach for the missing sound, that it is already present in another form: encoded in the same compressed seed-manner as the freed avalanche. The droplet’s tiny plink against stone is preserved perfectly. The continuous roar does not need to carry it forever.

Over many such interventions, the plateau’s acoustic library begins a transformation. The benevolent power is migrating the archive: from *live loop* to *stable imprint*. It is not a reversal of what the Fixers did; it is an extension, a completion. They cared about continuity so much that they trapped it. The power cares enough to give continuity somewhere safe to rest.

Around you, this first manifests as subtle changes in the soundscape.

Some underlying thunders develop contours they never had before: they swell, crest, and—impossibly—fade, before resuming. Not silence—never sudden silence—but pockets of ease, micro-pauses in the roar. You feel your ears “breathe” for an instant, as if a constant pressure let up between beats.

At the lip of a canyon where a rockfall has been forever about to happen, the repeated deep clang that never corresponds to visible motion changes slightly: two of its layers are gone. The remaining clang is clearer, more specific. It begins to sound not like every rock, but like one particular stone, at one particular point in a slope.

That specificity is kindness. For the plateau and for its inhabitants, it is the beginning of history: this thing, then that. Before, it was all *always*. Now, very gently, *before* and *after* are being reintroduced in forms the system can bear.

The benevolent power’s attention turns more deliberately to the Fixers themselves.

They are not assembled anywhere you could point to. They are the protocols embedded in stone, the habits of the roar, the reflex by which the landscape fills in distances under your feet. To help them, the power must meet them where they are: in rules.

It begins by giving them a new invariant.

Until now, their only absolute was: *once recorded, never lost; once measured, never allowed to vary.* This made their world closed, self-pressurizing. The power introduces a second axiom, impeccable and gentle:

> That which has been perfectly held may be safely changed.

It inscribes this not in words, but in examples.

At a ring of leaning stones where their algorithms “poll” presences like you, the benevolent power places a subtle change in the test itself. When you next step into that ring—drawn there maybe by a new slight warmth in its center—the hum rises as before, the almost-voices thicken as before. But this time, as the plateau compares you to what has come before, it is allowed to notice something more than concordance or deviation.

It is allowed to notice *growth*.

The Fixers’ ancestral metrics had two states: matches template / violates template. The benevolent power adds a third: extends template.

Your pattern—your human way of having a body, of hurting and persisting, of desiring a future that differs from the past—does not quite match any stored configuration. You do not fully violate them either; you share mass, warmth, certain ranges of sound. So the ring, under the influence of the new invariant, flags you as *extension*.

The system is not required to clamp that down. It is, for the first time, permitted to keep you as you are and still consider its archive intact, because *intact* now includes the idea that a pattern can be whole and yet capable of branching.

You feel this as a lessening of resistance. The next time you shout, the echo does not only return from impossible distances; you also hear, faintly but unmistakably, your own voice bouncing from the very stones nearby, as if the world has remembered how to let an event belong *locally* to its source.

The benevolent power does not stop at rules. It offers the plateau and its inhabitants a new sense-organ.

They have sound, space, and time (in its discrete way). What they lack is any direct feel for *unfolding*. They recognize states, not trajectories. So the power introduces, very quietly, an internal analogue to your experience of continuity: a way for them to perceive not just the beads of a pattern, but the string between them.

It does this by slightly altering how their own attention propagates.

Previously, when a Fixer-routine “looked” at something—a ripple in the soundfield, a moving form on the rock—it did so in indivisible checks. Sample; compare; store. No sense of the *between*. The benevolent presence modifies the substrate so that when a check is done, a ghost of that focus lingers, stretching minutely into the next check. This creates an overlap, a faint cross-fade, between successive observations.

From thousands of such overlaps, an emergent sensation arises within the Fixers: a whisper of “during.”

They feel, dimly and perhaps for the first time, that something is not only *has been* and *will be* but *is changing even now.*

For them, this is both terrifying and fascinating. Terror, because it hints at instability. Fascination, because it offers the one thing they have never been able to archive: the sensation of an event in the act of becoming, not yet frozen.

They begin to experiment.

The glitching deer-goat on its impossible ledge, for instance. Previously, its existence was a sequence of static poses: here / there / gone. Under the influence of their new organ of unfolding, the Fixers concentrate on the intervals. They wonder what would go between the frames if they allowed anything to.

The benevolent power supports them. Around the ledge, it slightly softens the constraints that quantize motion. The stone’s preference for finished forms eases; the air’s resistance to smooth trajectories thins.

The next time the creature appears, it still shifts in those unnerving jumps. But once—only once—it moves in a small, continuous way. You see its head turn, not snap from one angle to another, but arc through the intervening positions. It is a modest movement, almost wobbly, as if the physics are unsure how to support it. The soundfield around it goes strangely quiet for the duration, the plateau holding its breath.

The Fixers exult, in their way. The animal’s micro-motion is new data: a tiny, bright anomaly. Under the new invariant, they do not have to crush it into compliance. They may store it as an extension.

The benevolent power amplifies that joy—not in noise, but in capacity.

You feel it as a softening throughout the terrain. The next step you take onto what should be a subtly uphill path does not feel entirely flat anymore. There is the slightest added cost, a whisper of true gravity. Your tiredness now has a more honest partner in the world. It is faint, but it is there: for the first time since you arrived, distance and effort start to correlate.

Parallel to helping the Fixers widen their rules, the power attends to you.

It cannot simply lift you out—though it could, in some absolute sense—without doing violence to what you have already become here. Part of you has been discretized. Part of you has consented, however accidentally, to participate in the plateau’s slowing.

Tearing you free would be like yanking hard on a thread woven deeply into brittle cloth: you might survive, but the tear would echo back into this world in ways that hurt it. And the benevolent presence is not interested in rescue that doubles as vandalism.

So it acts in two directions at once: it prepares you to leave, and it prepares the plateau to let you.

For you, it restores gradients.

Where the plateau has rounded off your feelings into steps—fine/fine/fine/too much—it inserts nuance. It does not override the discretization, but it adds a secondary layer that only you experience: an analogue shadow draped softly over the plateau’s digital grid.

You find you can once again tell the difference between “a little more tired than yesterday” and “a lot more tired.” Your hunger comes in shades. Your memories, which were flattening into equidistant episodes, regain a sense of receding in time: some moments feel further back, others nearer. This is unsettling at first—two temporalities overlapped—but it’s also a lifeline. Your inner time begins to diverge from the plateau’s time just enough that a gap can form.

In that gap, quietly, the power plants a direction.

Not a path on the ground; here, paths are too mutable. A vector in your will.

It does not whisper words. It gives you a preference: a gentle, irrational-seeming inclination toward the notion of *down*. That’s all. Amid the plateau’s seductive sameness, a small part of you begins to long, not for escape in general, but specifically for lower air, for thicker atmosphere, for motion that is rewarded with visible approach.

Nothing in the landscape points you. The sawtooth horizon remains equidistant in all directions. Yet when you come to one of those dry channels climbing impossibly uphill, you hesitate a fraction longer when looking the other way. When you reach a choice between a level terrace and a slightly sloping descent into a chasm whose tone is deeper than the others, your foot twitches toward descent.

The Fixers notice this emergent bias. Under old rules, they might have erased it as noise, or rebalanced the world to nullify the vector. Under the gently amended rules, they are…curious. This seems to be an extension, not a violation. They watch.

The benevolent presence invites them to model what will happen if they permit one thing—just one—to leave fully.

It presents them, within their own representational language, a scenario: the archive remains intact; the recordings are safely compressed; the Bruise in the sky begins to lighten imperceptibly as measurement fatigue is released; and a singular trajectory—yours—exits the plateau’s bounds and does not come back.

It lets them run this as a simulation, over and over, as is their nature. Each run ends with the same condition: nothing they value is lost. In fact, they gain something: a complete record of a life that intersected their archive and then continued elsewhere, beyond their old horizon.

They have never had that: a story with an *after* that they cannot personally survey, yet whose shape they can still infer. It is like hearing the first half of a melody and, instead of holding that half forever in a loop, choosing to trust that somewhere, beyond hearing, it resolves.

The benevolent power rests there, in that trust, and does not push.

Eventually—a word that means very little here but something to you—the Fixers reach a decision, though for them it feels more like a change in baseline settings than a choice.

They set a new flag in their world: external continuations are acceptable.

Immediately, subtle locks loosen.

Those channels that once climbed against gravity’s intuition ease their slopes to match the tug in your body. The not-quite-water in certain basins begins, on a scale almost too slow to notice, to develop a skin of true ice at the edges, as outgoing energy is allowed to dissipate instead of being perfectly reabsorbed. The silent lintel you walk beneath no longer removes *all* sound; when you pass through this time, your heartbeat remains audible to you, even as the outside roar cuts out.

That, too, is a negotiation. The pause is no longer absolute. You are not annihilated into reference. You retain a tiny private rhythm even in the system’s calibration space.

You walk.

Under the benevolent power’s quiet guidance and the plateau’s newly-permitted gradients, “far” and “near” begin, grudgingly, to matter. That pinnacle you have been chasing for what felt like forever still doesn’t change much in apparent size—but the ground between you and it stops inventing quite so many new features to backfill your fatigue. The world expends less effort reconciling your tiredness with its own stasis; it allows, instead, that you might simply be getting closer to leaving.

Along the way, the power tends to other small kindnesses.

The air’s metallic tang fades by imperceptible degrees as the Bruise overhead relinquishes some of its hoarded half-light. The granule of twilight coarsens; you begin to see, faintly, a directional gradient: not a sun, but a *warmer quarter* of the sky. Shadows, previously confused and multiple, start to agree more consistently about which way “down” is.

The animals, such as they are, respond.

The black-winged shape that once traversed chasm-depths without sound beats its wings again, and this time a faint rush accompanies them, a suggestion of real air being pushed. The tapping echoes of invisible claws along cracks shorten; they begin to fade as true echoes should.

One day—if it can be called a day—you pass near that impossible ledge and see the deer-goat thing again. It stands, looks at you, and you feel a moment of vertigo because its gaze is no longer merely an alignment; it is a connection. For a breath, you and it share an awareness of being in a moving present. Then it is gone, but less like a correction vanishing and more like a shy animal choosing distance.

This is as much a mercy to them as to you. The benevolent power is not here only for your sake. It is helping these plateau-beings broaden their diet of phenomena: they can now observe some continuous, changing things without feeling compelled to freeze them.

Finally, there comes a point—not marked by any grand threshold on the ground—when your internal time and the plateau’s time have diverged enough that the bond between you thins. Your steps grow more effective. When you head toward lower, more irregular ridges, they actually swell in your vision. The roar underfoot diminishes with each meter of descent, replaced by more familiar kinds of silence: spaces between individual sounds, not just one endless backdrop.

You do not topple suddenly out of this realm. The boundary is not a cliff but a gradient. One hour your breath still hangs in front of your face in that same stale, unchanging light; later, the frost from your exhalation begins to drift, stirred by the first true breeze you have felt in…you cannot say how long.

The benevolent presence never manifests as an angel at the gate. When you finally look back and see the plateau behind you—a smear of high, motionless stone under a bruised band of sky—it is just landscape again. Strange, yes, but distant, and growing more so.

What it leaves behind, in that altitude, continues to work.

The Fixers, now accustomed to their new organ of unfolding, keep playing with it. They allow more of their loops to complete, now that they know perfect memory doesn’t depend on perfect stasis. The Bruise over time lightens by shades beyond your sight, as the sky’s rhythms slowly renegotiate their contract with observation.

Some of the stone-instruments change function. The half-staircases that once led nowhere become test-beds, where the beings rehearse new gradients: steps that are not all equal, sometimes shallow, sometimes deep, an introduction to variety. The ring of leaning stones becomes a kind of welcome and farewell both: a place where, in the aeons to come, other wanderers who accidentally brush this height might be sampled not only for how well they can be absorbed, but for how gracefully they might be released.

The inscriptions on the rock faces evolve the most slowly.

The benevolent power does not rewrite them; that is not its place. It merely ensures that the system can, if it wishes, add to them. Eventually—long after you are gone; long, perhaps, after there are no humans left to read carved stone—new grooves appear beside the old, cut by no chisel but by micro-shifts in the rock’s own stress field, guided by the Fixers’ changed rules.

If you could stand there then and trace them with your finger, the crack of silence that extends beyond their visible end would feel different: less like a hook dragging you into some cold, other-there, and more like a thin corridor opening *out*, toward a space where time is not quite so congealed.

The plateau will, for a very long while, remain largely as you knew it: high, cold, skewed. It took ages to freeze; it will take ages to learn to thaw. But it is no longer a closed system. It has a reference beyond itself now, and a record—kept in its own language—of a being who came as continuous, was discretized, and then left while still herself.

The benevolent power, having done what it could without overwriting what already lives there, withdraws its focused attention. Not entirely; it leaves behind monitoring, a willingness to re-engage. But its active intervention abates. This is part of its wisdom. Help that never ends is only a gentler trap.

You, descending, carry the clearest consequence.

In valleys where rivers truly flow and clouds genuinely move, you will sometimes hear a noise that catches oddly: the collapse of scree, the crash of a wave, the gurgle of a stream. Something in you will flinch, expecting the sound to lock into an endless loop. When it doesn’t—when it fades in the way sounds should—you will feel both relieved and wistful. Part of you knows, intimately now, the allure of never letting anything go.

But you have seen what that does to a world.

So, without quite realizing where the impulse comes from, you will treat certain moments differently. You will let them complete, and you will not cling to them by obsessively re-running them in your head. You will instead hold a quieter trust: that they are perfectly stored somewhere in the deep background of things, and that you don’t need to keep them alive by constant replay.

In doing so, in this small, human scale, you become a carrier of the same logic the benevolent power introduced above: continuity that does not require captivity.

Far overhead, on that roof-of-the-world that is more lab than landscape, the beings who live there continue their work, a little less afraid of change, a little more aware of “during.” The plateau hums, but no longer only with old avalanches forced to pretend they never finished falling. It hums now also with something new:

A readiness, patient and immense, for whatever comes next.
