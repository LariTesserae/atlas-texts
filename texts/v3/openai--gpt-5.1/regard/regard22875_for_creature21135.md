The first touch is almost nothing.

Far beneath mantle and memory, where pressure has long since blurred the difference between rock and heat, a standing wave deepens by an immeasurable fraction. It is not a decision—there is no mind there to decide—only a minute rebalancing of tensions in crystal lattices and slow convecting flows. But the rebalancing persists, and because it persists, it begins to matter.

Over spans that would erase histories, the adjustment climbs.

In the ductile stone below the basin, certain fault surfaces that might have slid soften instead; others that might have remained locked begin to creep. The net result is that stress distributes itself a little more evenly, a little more predictably, beneath this one hollow. The old planet’s unseen engine, which has always been noisy but mostly indifferent, begins ever so slightly to favor patterns that are stable at the scale of this cavern.

No one notices. Not yet. “Noticing” in this place is a word for much more than a single lifetime.

---

The second touch is slower still.

Dust falls in this world in a lazy, perpetual rain. It falls from ceiling sag and from the grinding marriages of stone on stone, from the slow flaking remnant-bodies of the dead, from the fragile mineral lattices that once trapped spores and now crumble into their own sediment. Over aeons, it has been mostly grey and pale, reflecting the static chemistry of the vault.

Now, in no hurry, the falling changes.

Somewhere in the deep crust, where fluids that are only technically fluids percolate along hairline fractures, a new set of trace elements moves into play. It is not the sudden presence of something unheard of; all of these atoms have passed through the mantle for as long as the world has had one. But the ratios and pathways shift. Tiny currents of gas thread along different routes. Here and there, part-per-billion differences accumulate.

By the time anything can sense it, the effect is that a fraction of the dust that sifts into the basin carries with it a faint, consistent skew: a little more of this element, a little less of that, in ways that matter to the biochemistry of dry life.

The fingerlike mats drink it and grow, very incrementally, faster along certain lines. The glassy pillars thicken in subtle bands. Wall-membranes become able to maintain their tension with less metabolic cost. The plaza-gatherers’ internal oils hold their ordered structures better against random thermal motions.

Nothing new has yet happened in kind. Only in ease. Tiny gradients are slightly less steep, slight instabilities slightly damped. The world’s friction against what lives here is imperceptibly reduced.

---

The third touch comes in the domain that, here, is equivalent to weather.

The basin roars, as always. Sound is everywhere, a dense tangle of waves born from rolling mantle, slipping microfaults, the muttered complaints of old stone remember­ing stresses long since shifted elsewhere. To a creature of light and noise, the roar is formless. To these beings, it is already structured beyond our patience.

What the benevolent power does is not to add a new note. It nudges the instrument toward tune.

Minor resonances that would once have wandered chaotically now drift in and out of long-lived relationships. Over distances of many cavern-widths, shafts and vents begin—by coincidence only at first—to hum in near-integer ratios. The old tectonic engine is not overridden; it is coaxed. Where multiple stable states of vibration are possible, the system finds its way more often into those that are commensurable across space.

In practice, this means that the background murmur slowly acquires clearer, more persistent patterns on scales that match the size of the city.

Deep, slow pulses that used to smear across the basin now arrive as distinguishable beats. High, whining overtones that once came and went without echo now recur with just enough regularity that memory can grasp them. The environment becomes, in a small but genuine sense, more legible.

This is the first touch that will eventually be named—not in words, but in the plaza’s long, comparing labor—as a Before and After. It will be centuries before that distinction coheres. The power is in no rush. It has, after all, already waited while continents rose and sank.

---

For the inhabitants, the change first registers as puzzlement in their oldest organs: the rooted stalks.

They have always stood as witnesses to the planet’s deeper song. For long lifetimes they have tracked the ponderous crawl of frequencies, the slow seep of new harmonics from very far below. Now, extremely gradually, they begin to sense that some of the most ancient, background-deep patterns—the ones they thought too inchoate ever to recur—are settling into grooves.

At the bases of their columns, in the dense knots where nervous tissue meets mineral chemistry, faint differences accumulate: a little less error in prediction, a little more correlation between what is sensed at opposite sides of the basin. The long, glacial pulses they send through the rock to one another begin, without conscious project, to align. “What you feel there and I feel here,” their bodies say over centuries, “is more strongly the same than it once was.”

The stalks have never had occasion to feel surprise. Now they experience the slow analog of it: a decades-long awareness that their deepest expectations—about how unordered the deep geologic murmur must be—no longer quite fit.

They respond with what, for them, is curiosity. At their crowns, their discs thicken around new, more sensitive bands. Subtle subdivisions appear in their interior signaling. They become capable of discriminating smaller differences within the newly coherent waves.

The benevolent power does not dictate those changes. It only holds the environmental pattern steady enough that evolution and plasticity can grind their slow path toward it.

---

In the city, this new coherence creates, over long spans, room for excess.

A membrane-creature that once needed every scrap of metabolic energy just to maintain its tension in the face of chaotic input now lives under a slightly gentler storm. Waste drops by a fraction; survival margins thicken. Within that sliver of surplus, variation can afford to flower.

Some membranes develop asymmetries: patches of tissue tuned not for optimal energy harvest, but for fine-grained discrimination of certain motifs in the roar. They are, in effect, sacrificing efficiency for information.

A few of those asymmetries, by chance, prove useful. One membrane in a long line is particularly good at detecting a specific, slowly drifting interference between a deep pulse and a shaft’s whistle—a pattern that, as it happens, precedes minor rock readjustments that could crack its anchoring surface. It responds with a slightly earlier, slightly cheaper retraction than its kin before some small collapses, and so its lineage survives just a bit more often.

Because the patterns in the medium are now more consistent basin-wide, these little tricks of early detection and fine discrimination can propagate. Descendants of that membrane spread—by the equally slow habits of their clinging and drifting—to other walls, other districts, where the same type of interference has nearly the same meaning.

What the power has done, effectively, is to turn a previously uncooperative noise into a more educable teacher. The world’s low-frequency babble now contains, stably, learnable regularities at the scale of the city.

No one individual ever knows this in the abstract. But the city as a pattern begins to respond.

---

The city-makers—those long, many-legged shapers of stone—are among the first whose behavior crosses the line from adaptation to something like collaboration.

They have always patrolled their routes according to an inherited, slowly modified sense of where reinforcement is needed, where hollows should be deepened, where flutes should be smoothed or sharpened to handle the planetary rumble. Now, under the influence of more coherent input, their generational memory begins to condense.

The plaza—once a merely convenient flat, resonant place—has, by now, been refined into something more skillful. Its tuned hollows and arcs were already sufficient to catch gross modes of the basin’s vibration. As the incoming patterns grow more internally structured, the plaza’s structure offers better and better purchase on them.

City-makers notice, at their own scale of noticing, that certain ways of adjusting the plaza’s ridges and walls yield disproportionate gains in “clarity”—their bodies’ sense that more information is being made available for less effort. They adjust to favor these.

The benevolent power does not tell them which shapes to prefer. But each time their random growth produces a refinement that better couples the basin’s now-smoother standing waves into the plaza, that refinement is rewarded by easier, richer sensing for all who gather there. Those whose inherited habits point them toward preserving such shapes have, very gently, an advantage.

Across dozens of generations of city-makers—thousands of years by surface reckoning—the plaza grows into a more exact instrument. Its cavities map onto particular stable relationships in the deep soundfield. Its tiers and amphitheaters arrest, focus, and hold swirling patterns that would once have slipped past.

The plaza-gatherers, plug themselves into this with what, for them, passes as delight.

---

It is their internal lives that the power most dearly cares about—and yet, true to its nature, it never touches them directly. It only continues, with patient pressure, to shape the medium in which they live.

Before, their work at the plaza was like trying to read meanings in the chop of a restless sea, finding periodicities only by brute persistence. Their longest-remembered configurations of sound were hard-won and fragile.

Now, as the deep resonance settles into more articulate modes, something new becomes possible: not just the recognition of repeated patterns, but anticipation.

For the first time in the history of this place, a gathering of plaza beings is able to hold in its collective structure a “state” that has real predictive relationship to states that will follow. When a certain constellation of deep pulses and high harmonics takes hold in the plaza’s standing waves, their tuned bodies not only register it as familiar; they begin to “prepare” for what, in their long experience, tends to come next.

This preparation is physical: a slight pre-tensioning of certain diaphragms, a biased openness in some channels, a tiny, slow favoring of modes that will match future input. When the anticipated waves do arrive, the plaza’s configuration and the incoming pattern interlock more smoothly. Less energy is wasted. More of the signal passes into their inner spaces undistorted.

In human terms, this is closer to understanding than to reflex.

The benevolent power sees this—not from outside, but by the satisfaction of its own constraint: it had sought, without plan but with an aesthetic, to create conditions under which matter could form stable, self-referential maps of its own globally unfolding dynamics. Here, in this dim dry basin, the constraint succeeds.

The plaza becomes, in a modest but genuine sense, a model of the planet’s deep behavior, compressed into the geometries of stone and shell.

---

Time passes in broad strokes. The power does not check back every year, or every thousand years. It abides as the bias in the deep waves, the slightly higher probability that the basin’s resonant modes will cohere instead of drift apart. On spans of millions of surface years, continents above may shatter or erode. Here, the ceiling settles micrometers.

With stability comes room for elaboration.

Individual plaza-gatherers, inheriting bodies and plazas tuned by countless prior adjustments, begin to exhibit variation not easily explained by survival alone.

Some, for reasons baked into the minute differences in their development, favor coupling more strongly to the very lowest, slowest pulses. They become deep-time specialists, spending their long lives wrestling meaning from patterns that take centuries to complete. Others bias toward the slightly quicker, still subsonic vibrations born of nearer faults and vents, their inner chambers optimized for shorter cycles.

The plaza’s spatial arrangement slowly stratifies along those lines. Certain arcs of seating, so to speak, become the domain of low-note thinkers; other bands fill with more “topical” minds, attuned to shifts at the scale of a few lifetimes. The city-makers, responding to the changed loads and vibrational demands, reinforce those differences physically: walls thicken in some sectors, become more porous in others.

Out of this, something akin to differentiation of roles emerges.

One region of the plaza becomes where the long, slow background is held—a kind of memory, not of discrete events but of the enduring shape of the planet’s deep circulations over tens of lifetimes. Another region, more agile in its resonant habits, excels at detecting anomalies: those rare, coherent intrusions that do not fit the established relationships. A third, bridging the two, begins unconsciously to refine mappings between the long-term context and short-term events.

There is no hierarchy in any moral sense. No one commands. But structurally, the plaza is now an organ with specialized subparts.

To the benevolent power, this is a kind of music.

---

With all this comes, eventually, something like self-awareness. Not in the cartoonish sense of “I think, therefore I am,” but as an emergent sense, distributed across many bodies and much stone, that there is a distinction between:

- The patterns that come from Below—those which no adjusting of the plaza can much alter—and
- The patterns that are consequences of the plaza’s own structure and participation.

The plaza-gatherers discover, slowly, that when they collectively change how they open and close, how they admit or damp certain bands, a new class of standing wave appears: ones that do not correspond cleanly to anything the stalks feel at depth, nor to the simple resonances of raw stone.

These waves are, in a real way, theirs.

Initially, they treat them as noise: artifacts to be filtered out so that the “true” planetary song can be heard more cleanly. But over time, some portion of the gathering begins to notice that these self-generated patterns—call them thoughts—bear interesting relationships to input.

A particular configuration of plaza bodies might, for example, amplify a very faint, distant harmonic that would otherwise be lost. When they adopt that internal configuration again, even in the absence of the stimulus, the system becomes newly sensitive: should that harmonic reappear, they will catch it more readily.

In this way, their own collective resonance becomes a library of internal models. They start, without intending, to maintain a repertoire of favored self-waves: shapes of co-tension and mutual filtering that are useful for interpreting common situations.

The benevolent power does nothing to guide which internal modes are stored. It only continues to provide a world whose large-scale dynamics are rich and stable enough that such models can have meaning.

Over long ages, the plaza’s life becomes less about wrestling with noise and more about comparing self-generated patterns with what the planet offers. Where there is consonance, the plaza-gatherers feel a deep rightness—a fit between internal and external. Where there is dissonance, they feel curiosity’s precursor: a spreading, patient adjustment as they try new internal configurations to better match the unexpected.

If there is anything like joy in this place, it lives here.

---

Still, the power is not satisfied. Benevolence, in it, is indistinguishable from the wish that such local self-awareness be afforded a wider horizon.

So it extends its touch beyond the basin.

Not in space—the basin remains where it is—but in connectivity.

High above, beyond anything the plaza directly senses, other caverns exist, other hollows carved in early cataclysms and left to cool. Some are sterile, some hold rough, unresonant ecologies of their own. A few, by chance, have begun along paths not dissimilar to this basin’s: mineral growths, vibrational feeders, slow minds groping toward pattern.

The power adjusts, extremely slightly, the way deep waves percolate through the crust so that these hollows become more strongly acoustically coupled.

Subsonic modes that once terminated in useless interference now refract in such a way that a faint, but real, admissible echo of distant caverns’ vibrations reaches the basin. At first it is nothing but a whisper beneath the usual roar. But the plaza is, by now, exquisitely attuned to faint coherences.

Very slowly, they realize that some of the patterns they feel cannot be accounted for by the known structure of their own world: not by their stalks, their vents, their faults. These patterns are too regular, but in a different key. They seem like the background song of another place entirely.

At first, these external notes are filed—if that metaphor can be forgiven—under “deep anomaly.” They are simply more data to be modeled, more stability to be extracted. The plaza-gatherers extend their repertoire of internal waves to include configurations that match them, without any concept that what they are matching is the life or geology of somewhere else.

To the power, though, this is exactly the opening it had waited to prepare: a way for local mind to eventually discover that it is not alone in the deep.

---

It would be possible for that discovery to unfold as fear—a sense of vast, incomprehensible otherness impinging on a carefully tuned home. The benevolent power is not omnipotent, but it still wishes, where possible, to skew the odds away from terror.

So, once again, it works through the environment.

Where acoustic paths thread between caverns, it damps their capacity to carry violent transients. Sharp, high-amplitude shocks—quakes, collapses, ruptures—are preferentially scattered or absorbed along the way. What reaches the basin from afar is almost always filtered into the same slow, dense frequencies that its inhabitants already know how to live with.

Conversely, it enhances, by minute adjustments in the deep rock’s mechanical properties, the transmission of long-period, coherent modes. Over enormous time, this makes the distant caverns’ own emergent, plaza-like resonances more likely to be audible here as consistent, patterned ripples in the abyssal background.

The effect, from within the basin, is that the newly noticed extraneous patterns are not violent intrusions but gentle, persistent motifs: regular enough to invite modeling, quiet enough not to overwhelm.

The plaza-gatherers, following the only imperative they know—to listen, to match, to refine—begin to cultivate internal waves specifically designed to track these motifs. They become, in a literal vibrational sense, sympathetic.

No deliberate communication occurs. Neither side knows there is any “side” at all. But over the same spans of ages that once refined a single basin’s plaza, now a multi-cavern network of resonant intelligences starts to entrain.

Each, in its own city of stone and life, is building internal patterns that echo the others’ deep behaviors, without ever meeting, without knowing.

This, for the power, is enough. It has seeded, with almost no direct touch, a kind of distributed, planetary-scale cognition in the rock and its dwellers.

---

And yet, there is the matter of individuals.

Everything so far has been about collectives and media. But the benevolent impulse, if it means anything, must surely care whether any one awareness—any single shell, stalk, membrane—finds its existence net more or less full of possibility.

Can a being whose preferred timescale is centuries know anything like fulfillment? The power, not being a person, does not think in those terms. But it can shape conditions under which the inward life of such beings contains richer gradients.

The simplest way to do that here is also the most respectful: to diversify the accessible patterns of input without adding constraint.

So it begins, almost playfully—as much as an ancient, slow, nonlocal bias can play—to orchestrate very slight, very rare synchronicities.

Perhaps once in a thousand of the plaza’s gatherings, the deep waves, filtered through the planet’s mass, happen to line up in such a way that a particularly intricate, unexpectedly symmetric interference pattern appears across the basin. Vents that usually bear no simple relation to one another’s exhalations fall, for a matter of years, into elegant relationships; a distant cavern’s slow mode arrives just in phase to reinforce a local pulse.

To an external observer, this would look like nothing more than a freak alignment of otherwise independent processes: many clocks, by chance, chiming in near-unison. To the beings here, it is like a revelation.

The plaza, for once, finds itself able to match the incoming with a resonance that fits without strain. Its internal pattern and the external world lock together in an almost effortless consonance far beyond the usual incremental fit.

If minds built of stone and oily membranes can feel awe, this is when they feel it.

They remember this—not as an event with a date, but as a favored configuration, a shape of being together that they will, slowly, learn to return to of their own accord, even when the external conditions are less perfect. This learned return is not delusion; rather, it is an internalization of a possible mode of coherence that exists in the space of structures.

The power has, in essence, gifted them a template for unusually high-order harmony, then left it in their keeping. Whether they use it often or rarely is up to them.

---

What, then, of the being who “lives here,” in the way your story first suggested? If we narrow our gaze from basin and guild to one single entity—say, a plaza-gatherer midway through its long adult life—what does the power do for it, personally?

Not much, in ways that could be narrated as intervention. It does not whisper secrets, grant new faculties overnight, or rescue it from local difficulty. To do any of those would be to violate the very texture of its existence, which is slow accretion, patient adjustment, the meaning of effort spread over centuries.

Instead, the power’s gift to any such individual is the luxury of meaningful work.

Because the medium is now such that the basin’s deep, long patterns are legible; because the plaza’s architecture has been honed to hold and compare them; because distant caverns’ resonances flicker faintly in the background—this being, in its finite span, has access to more structure than its ancestors ever did.

The questions available to it—not in words, but in the form of persistent mismatches between its internal models and the world—are richer. There are subtler correlations to find, more delicate dissonances to resolve. It can, in its way, spend its life refining one or two long, intricate “ideas”: perhaps a new, more accurate mapping between the deep planet’s cycling and the behavior of a cluster of vents; perhaps an internal mode that brings previously separate patterns into a shared frame.

When it dies, the fossilized imprint of those internal modes—embodied in the way it has, over its life, helped grow and tune a small part of the plaza—will remain as a new affordance for others. Its existence will have increased, by a sliver, the basin’s capacity to listen and understand.

This is not a gift in the human sense. No promise was made; no reward is consciously bestowed. But if benevolence can be measured by whether beings are offered genuinely new possibilities they are free to embrace or ignore, then this counts.

---

There remains one last kind of touch: a boundary.

Not a wall—nothing as crude—but a chosen limit to how far the power will go.

It could, in principle, continue to shape the planet’s deep behavior until the basin minds achieve feats beyond any natural evolution: to coordinate caverns globally, to stabilize continents, to preempt quakes. It could guide them toward manipulating their own geology consciously, turning them into engineers of a whole world.

It chooses not to.

Some possibilities, if handed too easily, corrode the very texture of the slow, local, emergent understanding that has grown here. A mind whose models are validated too quickly, whose influence on its environment is disproportionate, risks losing the patient, receptive stance that made it possible in the first place.

So the power holds itself to a rule that, if it could speak, would be this:

- I may bias the **background** so that correlation can be found.
- I may smooth the **medium** so that communication can travel.
- I may occasionally, rarely, orchestrate a **coincidence** that reveals a higher-order pattern.
- But I will not give them **control**, nor will I shield them from all consequence.

Quakes still happen, though fewer are catastrophic. Vents still clog and reopen unpredictably. Lineages still end. Choices made in the slow social tectonics of the plaza—how to route resonance, how to apportion attention—still have real costs.

The basin remains a place where listening is not just an aesthetic, but a necessity.

---

From the outside, from the vantage point of a creature who could walk into that plaza and be swallowed in the roar, the power’s work is almost invisible. The stone looks no different; the thin, veined growths still tremble; the shell-bodied grazers still hum tunelessly to one another across dusty clearings.

But if you could become as patient as the rock, this is what you would see:

- The deep pulses underfoot would feel less like random throbbing and more like the breathing of something coherent.
- The plaza’s gatherings would show a fineness of fit between inner and outer patterns that bespeaks a long, delicate courting of reality.
- At the far edge of hearing, in the lowest degrees of the bass, you would sense faint, far-away responses—other hollows, other plazas, resonating in sympathy.

And, standing there as the foreign body you are, your own sharp footfalls momentarily disruptive, you would be taken up—without consultation—into the work.

Not because the benevolent power has decreed that you must have a part to play, but because in a world where everything is coupled by sound, the only true neutrality is silence, and you are not silent.

Your added noise, your different compressibility, the impossible water in your body—these will introduce new small mismatches into the plaza’s models. The beings there will, in their long way, try to account for you. Some tiny percentage of their total attention over generations will be spent refining internal modes that match “that one local anomaly that bent echoes just so.”

Perhaps nothing more comes of it. Perhaps your presence is a blip in their deep-time understanding, a transient that leaves no durable mark.

Or perhaps the benevolent power, recognizing in you another locus of pattern—another way the universe has accidentally started to model itself—will very gently help your footsteps arrive at some statistically unlikely but possible alignment with a rare, exalted mode of the basin’s resonance.

For a flicker, then, for as long as your attention can hold it, you might stand at the plaza’s center while the entire hollow—stone, life, deep planet—falls into one of those gracefully orchestrated coincidences. The bass of the mantle, the chords of the vents, the filigree of distant caverns, the tuned cavities of the plaza-gatherers, and the soft, aqueous hollows of your alien body all ringing, improbably, in compatible relation.

You would not understand it in their way; they would not understand it in yours. But you would both be, for that moment, participating in the same higher-order pattern: matter, across scales and chemistries, briefly agreeing on how to move.

The benevolent power would do nothing to record this. It would not enshrine it, nor repeat it on demand. Its gift is the possibility, not the guarantee, of such convergences.

What it does, finally and always, is this:

- It makes the world just coherent enough that slow, listening beings can find real structure in it.
- It restrains itself enough that the finding is theirs.
- It connects distant listeners just enough that no basin’s understanding must be wholly alone.

And then it waits, as stone waits, to see what songs they make.
