The wise power arrives the way this place understands arrival: as a change in gradients, not as a thunderclap.

Not a god in the sense of a person with hands, but a presence that can notice everything at once and choose, very precisely, where and how to lean.

It studies the basin for what, to it, is a long pause and to the basin is no time at all. It learns the rhythms that are almost not rhythms: the constant exhalation of the underplates, the unhurried circuits of the heavies, the slow hormonal speech of the trees, the soft, shared gossip of fungal mats. It listens to the percher’s stray, puzzled attentions. It tastes the air and all the lives suspended in it.

It discovers, very quickly, that this place is not broken.

There is no famine here, no drought, no predator swollen beyond all others. No cycle is on the verge of tearing itself apart. Everything is already woven into a steady, self-reinforcing hum.

So the first decision the wise power makes is what *not* to do.

It does not cool the air, because that would change the chemistry of every breath, every enzyme, every delicate balance of decay and growth.

It does not thin the haze, because that would sharpen light into beams and shadows, birthing edges in a world that has never had to learn them.

It does not carve channels in the stone sheet beneath, does not give the basin rivers or an outlet to some imagined elsewhere. This place has never believed in elsewhere; forcing such a belief would be violence.

It does not choose a single species and raise it up over the rest. The percher does not receive fire; the braider is not gifted a sense of self. No hero story is imposed on a landscape that has never lacked for its own, impersonal completeness.

The wise power’s benevolence, confronted with a system that is stable and deeply itself, turns to a narrower, gentler question:

Is there any suffering here that *this* world cannot already mend?

It looks closer.

It sees that death is not agony but loosening. It sees that hunger is never spiking terror, only a soft, constant drift toward and away from richer patches. It sees that the only pains that exist are local, brief, and almost always dissolve into someone else’s nourishment within heartbeats—or whatever serves as heartbeats here.

So even here, there is nothing to *fix*.

But there is something else, small and quiet and easy to miss, that draws its attention.

It is in the ones that watch: the percher folk.

In them there is a flicker that does not have a niche yet. A restless noticing, a sense—not of danger or shortage—but of *difference* between one moment and the next. They are not tormented by it. They are simply… unused to it, the way a new organ is unused at first. They watch patterns with no clear instinct for what watching is for.

The wise power sees this and understands: here is the one place where something might be offered not as rescue, not as correction, but as *possibility*.

It also understands the danger. A gift too sharp could cut the whole fabric that keeps this place in its deep equilibrium. A push too hard toward abstraction, and soon there are tools, clearings, fire, stored surpluses, anxieties about tomorrow—imported weather into a weatherless world.

That is not wanted. Not by the basin; not, in truth, by the power itself.

So it chooses a different kind of intervention: one that does not introduce scarcity or fear, that does not elevate any creature above the others, that does not ask this place to believe in elsewhere.

It chooses to give the percher folk a kind of *extra warmth* in the part of them that already glows.

Not language. Not plans. Just one new capacity:

The ability to feel that something is *beautiful*.

Not useful. Not threatening. Not edible or inedible. Simply… compelling in a way that quiets the restless attention instead of stirring it.

It works like this:

In a handful of percher embryos—scattered widely, in different family clusters across the basin—the wise power makes a very small adjustment. Inside the thickets of their nerve cells, where patterns of sight and sound and chemical smell converge, it tweaks the way similarity and difference are weighed.

It does not add new senses. It only changes the *weighting* of existing ones.

Where before a percher might notice: “This fungal veil is here today and gone tomorrow,” it will now also feel, faintly: “The way the light lies on this veil—this is pleasing.”

Where before it might scan a branch junction for safe footing and stored food, now a new, low thrum will arise when it looks at the same junction from many different angles: an emergent comfort, as if the configuration of curves and moist gleams is, for some reason, exactly right.

And crucially: this thrum has no demand attached. It does not say build more of this, hoard this, protect this at all costs. It only says: *stay, just a little longer*.

The wise power then does one more thing—a kind of negative act, a deliberate omission.

It does *not* give these perchers any new motor habit to go with the feeling. They do not suddenly start arranging leaves or weaving vines into patterns. They have no impulse yet to change the world in order to get more beauty.

They only, sometimes, pause.

---

These pauses ripple, lightly.

A percher that pauses on a particular vine-knot because the way the mist beads on it delights something it cannot name will stay a few heartbeats longer. Its weight and warmth and slight movements will encourage those vines to thicken there. Its excretions will fall in a tighter circle. The fungal spores caught in its damp fur will slough more densely onto that knot.

Over years, in a few places, such knots become slightly more elaborate than their neighbors: thicker, smoother, more layered with epiphytes. They are not art. They are not altars. They are simply the places where many tiny, unhurried decisions to “stay a little longer” have stacked.

The braiders, sensitive to tension and thickness, will notice those knots as convenient anchor points. They will route more of their living cords through them, further reinforcing the structure. Small gliders will land there more often because the crossing-lines make for good launching pads. Mists will catch, drip, and feed special growths.

To any eye not looking for it, these small thickenings are indistinguishable from the rest of the complexity. To the wise power, they are the faintest beginning of a new kind of pattern: structures selected not only by what feeds, shelters, or stabilizes, but also by what *invites lingering*.

It is not culture yet. It is not even preference in the rich sense. It is a tilt.

The percher’s experience, in the meantime, is modestly altered.

Most of life still feels like gradients: thicker, thinner, louder, softer. But occasionally, when one of the gifted ones catches a certain crossing of vines, a particular fungal sheen, a way that mist and filtered light and insect motion combine, there is a small inward easing. A sense that *this* arrangement is somehow more complete than its neighbors.

Nothing they do next is very different: they eat, groom, move on. The feeling leaves no plan in its wake. But it accumulates as a faint, private joy, an undercurrent of satisfaction that has no direct survival value and yet does no harm.

It is, in the most literal sense, gratuitous.

The wise power is content with that.

If, over ages measured in the slow inhalations of the underplates, this slight bias grows—if the percher folk’s descendants someday begin to nudge a leaf here, favor a certain curve there, maybe even to prefer the company of those who linger at the same pleasing configurations—that will be their doing, not the power’s.

If, conversely, the basin’s immense equilibrium simply absorbs this new capacity, dissolving it into the general hum so that beauty here never leads to making, only to quiet seeing—that is also acceptable.

The power has given a gift that does not demand an answer.

---

It considers other alterations, and one by one, discards them.

Should it gift the trees with sharper memory, so their chemical calculations might become narratives of old storms and old clearings? No: sharper memory without disruption would only turn into thicker habit. And if it *did* disrupt—if trees began to compete for specific remembered advantages—that would tilt this entire world toward contest.

Should it give the heavies some new way of knowing one another, so that their endless pressing and re-pressing of the land might become dance rather than blind drift? No: they are already exactly the scale and softness this basin needs. To make them more deliberate would be to make them instruments of change in a place that has no use for change of that kind.

Should it thin the microbial haze in a narrow band near the surface so that some small region knows clearer air, sharper shadows, stars? No: that would create an edge. All edges here become corridors, and corridors, over time, become roads—vectors along which elsewhere tries to creep in.

It leaves all those threads untouched.

Instead, it makes only one more adjustment, and this one is even smaller.

It slightly increases the *resilience* of the underplates’ shared borders.

At present, those territories of sameness blend and trade parcels calmly. Once in a very long while, a foreign microbe or a stray mineral surge from beneath could lead to one plate outcompeting another too thoroughly, thinning diversity in some region. This is not catastrophe, but it’s a trend that, extrapolated over unthinkable spans, could make the basin more monotonous than it is.

The power adds a tiny buffer against that: a bias, deep in the underplates’ biochemistry, in favor of *mutual tolerance when conditions are good*.

When nutrients are abundant—and they almost always are here—the signal that flows through underplate tissues now leans fractionally toward “share” rather than “overwrite.” When things tighten (as they occasionally do in very small pockets), they will still compete. But the frequency of those competitions will be held down just a little.

It’s a safeguard against a kind of slow, zoologists’ tragedy-of-the-commons that might otherwise unfold across eons, unnoticed: the system collapsing into fewer, more dominating underplate lineages.

With that, the wise power has done as much as it can without ceasing to be benevolent.

---

It does not speak to anyone. There are no visions, no visitations, no chosen ones.

What it offers is threefold and almost invisible:

- A new, harmless pleasure in the eyes and nervous systems of a scattered few watchers: the capacity to feel that some arrangements of the world are beautiful.
- The time and space for that pleasure to matter or not matter as it will, without hunger or fear forcing it into tools.
- A minuscule, subterranean reinforcement of the basin’s already-strong tendency toward stable diversity.

Then it withdraws its attention in the way you stop staring at your own hand: not by destroying anything, but by no longer holding every detail in focus.

The basin goes on humming.

In a fork of a particular tree, a young percher—one of the altered ones—settles onto a braid of vines. Mists drift below; insect-clouds tremble through shafts of amber-grey. The fungal shelves below her perch have, by pure happenstance and countless prior grazings and regrowths, taken on a spiral pattern. The way the dim light catches their damp edges makes them look almost like a slow whirlpool turned inside out.

For the first time in this world, a living thing experiences, without any added hunger or fear: *this is good to look at*.

She does nothing about it.

She only stays there a little longer than she otherwise would have, limbs loosely wrapped, attention resting without restlessness.

Below, the underplates breathe. A heavy shifts its bulk and presses the soft earth into a new, nearly imperceptible shape. Mists condense and fall, re-evaporate, rise again.

Equilibrium, slightly richer by one unneeded sweetness, continues.
