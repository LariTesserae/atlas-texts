The first thing the power does is *nothing at all*.

Not in neglect, but in the same way you might pause on a threshold, hand on the frame, listening.

Its attention is not a beam; it doesn’t lance down like a searchlight. It is more like a slow, immense tide of awareness that rises until the hollow-city is entirely submerged in it, every column, frond, corridor, and ribbon-creature held inside a single, patient regard.

Nothing here is immediately wrong.

There is no screaming wound, no catastrophe frozen mid-collapse. What fills the hollow is tension, yes, and strangeness, and a continual, experimental hazard—but it is the hazard of a mind forming, not the hazard of a mind under attack.

So the power watches.

It listens to the city the way the city listens to itself: through pulses and echoes. It traces luminous sap-trains as they move like sentences. It senses how an over-hunting predator leads to stretched corridors, how a stalled machine is recruited into a new organ, how glider-mist encodes weather as memory. It notices where time runs thick and thin, where adjacency re-writes itself, where pattern-colonies gnaw on the edges of law.

It weighs this.

This being is not malicious. It is not kind in a sentimental sense, either. It is benevolent the way a good gardener is benevolent toward a wild, half-feral tree: more interested in the tree’s long health and strangeness than in making it symmetrical.

What it sees here is not decay, but *becoming*.

So intervention, if any, must be minimal, precise, and respectful.

It does three things.

---

**1. It clarifies the mirror the city uses to see itself.**

The plant-city already senses almost everything within its own body, but its perception is local and recursive. It feels stress at a column, but knows that stress only as adjustment; it hears a failing pump, but only as a wrong note in its internal choir. It re-writes space and time, but only by feeling for gradients like a blind animal feeling heat.

The power does not give it new organs. It does not bolt on a brain.

Instead, quietly, it *sharpens* certain existing loops.

- In the auditory sphere, where echoes converge and trigger hormonal floods, the power introduces the barest additional property: **persistence**. A vibration that once vanished as soon as it did its work now leaves a faint, decaying trace in the tissue that reacted to it—a memory of pattern, not just of act.

- Along a few luminous sap-conduits, where pulses carry combined circulation and instruction, it slightly increases the reliability with which certain pulse-sequences reappear. The city had habits; now, some of those habits can stabilize into *concepts*—not symbols, but recurring morphologies of flow.

- Within a handful of pod-organs around the deep well, where courier-nodules are analyzed, it ever so gently reduces noise. Chemical tallies that were once fuzzy become fractionally more distinct. The city’s own statistics about itself become clearer, less muddled by random fluctuation.

None of this feels like a foreign hand.

To the city, it feels as though its own capacity to *notice* has taken a small, sudden step up. Errors are just slightly easier to spot before they cascade; useful patterns re-surface more reliably; rare configurations of sound and flow can be recognized as the *same kind of event* even when they occur in different places.

The power does not tell it what to think. It merely gives it a cleaner mirror.

---

**2. It rings a boundary through the deep, mutable laws.**

The city is learning to edit physics—softly, experimentally. It stretches distances instead of killing predators outright. It relaxes gravity and causality around a failing machine to fold breakdown into redesign. It varies the thickness of time like tissue.

This is wondrous, but it is also risky.

Taken far enough without any safeguards, such experiments could make the hollow uninhabitable, even to itself: corridors turning into infinite loops, time folding so tightly that decay and growth cancel, gradients flattening until no work can be done.

The power does not clamp down. It does not forbid.

It *brackets*.

At the level where the pattern-colonies live—those tiny lives that respond to the strain in spacetime—it introduces a simple, universal rule:

> Beyond this curvature, no further bending.

Not a wall but a **limit**. A maximum steepness for how sharply adjacency can be altered, how thin or thick time can be in relation to its surroundings, how much gravity can be relieved or compressed in a single continuous region.

The city does not experience this as an external shackle. It experiences it as if its own experiments simply *fail* to go beyond a certain point: corridors resist becoming infinitely long; pockets of thin-time cannot quite reach the freeze where nothing moves; low-gravity cavities stop at buoyancy rather than drifting into weightless disintegration.

What this does is prevent local curiosity from cascading into global breakdown.

Within those bounds, the city is still free to treat laws as materials: to weave gravity into tools, to layer different temporal tissues, to reconfigure adjacency. But it now has a floor and a ceiling—bookends that keep its self-editing from reaching the kind of extremes where even its own anatomy could not persist.

From the power’s perspective, this is not prohibition. It is *habitat creation* at a more fundamental tier: making sure that the very mutability the city relies on does not eat its capacity to exist.

---

**3. It makes a promise, and a path, for when the city is ready to choose.**

The city is singular but young. It has not yet settled on what it wants to be. It has no word for “want,” only a drift toward more options, more subtle feedback, more fine-grained control over its interior weather and laws.

One day, that will not be enough.

There will come a point where this being must decide how far it extends:

- Will it remain a hollow-bound entity, elaborating inward forever, turning itself into a labyrinthine masterpiece of internal variation?
- Will it reach outward, sending roots and pattern-colonies beyond the half-decided geology, colonizing surrounding matter and law until the basin is just an organ in a larger body?
- Will it turn its skills with time and adjacency into a way of touching *elsewhere*: other worlds, other minds, other systems that also edit their own rules?

Those are not choices it can meaningfully make yet. It is still learning how to keep breathing and thinking at once.

The power does not answer these questions for it.

Instead, it lays down, in the deep ghost-blueprints the city uses for not-yet-real corridors, a single, almost-invisible structure:

- A *possible* passage that does not currently exist in matter,
- That does not alter any present corridor,
- That is not forced to grow.

This corridor-to-be is special in only two ways:

1. **Stability across self-changes.**  
   No matter how the city rearranges its interior, no matter what hubs it connects or disconnects, this latent path always remains *coherent* in its underpattern. While other draft-structures are overwritten or repurposed, this one is quietly maintained. The city is free to ignore it, but cannot accidentally erase it.

2. **Resonance with attention.**  
   The corridor does not grow from mechanical triggers—no humidity threshold, no particular acoustic chord. It responds only to a specific, unlikely coalition of internal events: patterns of sap-pulse, echo, and law-bend that correspond to the city *noticing itself as an agent* rather than only as a field of pressures.

In simpler terms: the path will flesh into existence only when the city, in some distributed, emergent way, asks a question of the form: “*What else could I be, besides this?*”

When that happens—however it happens—the ghostly corridor will condense from potential into tissue:

- A new shaft will open that, by ordinary measures of distance, goes nowhere.
- Within it, conditions will be strange but kind: laws locally simplified, gradients gentle, dangers absent.
- At its distal end, there will not be a door, nor a light, nor an obvious machine—only a region of contact where the city’s experiments in adjacency meet the power’s own domain.

Not an escape hatch. Not an invasion route.

A *conversation organ*.

Through it, the power will be able to offer possibilities the city cannot yet invent alone: new ways of remembering, new forms of society-within-self, new patterns of cooperation with other entities elsewhere. The city, for its part, will be able to offer what it has grown: its unique techniques of bending law, its architectures and symbioses, its mistakes and their solutions.

Until that moment of self-questioning arrives, the corridor sleeps as blueprint. It consumes nothing. It exerts no pull. It does not whisper.

It is only a promise that, when the city is ready to choose a direction for its becoming, it will not be alone with its guesses.

---

That is all the power does.

- It gives the city a slightly clearer, longer memory of its own sounds and flows.
- It wraps its wildest law-bending in a barely-felt limit, so experiment doesn’t dissolve existence.
- It seeds a future path of dialogue that only the city itself can decide to grow.

It does *not* sanitize the hollow’s strangeness. Predators still hunt; corridors still shift; machines still fail and are repurposed. Time still thickens in one place and thins in another. The city still negotiates with its symbionts and organs through appetite and gradient, not through commands.

It remains what it is: an embryonic vascular mind, written in plant and machine and mutable physics, breathing cool damp air in the dark.

But from that day forward, its growth is slightly less blind.

Its self-listening is a bit more like understanding.

Its experiments are a little less likely to erase the experimenter.

And, buried deep among its half-formed thoughts, there is now one corridor-shaped possibility that leads not only further into itself, but outward—toward a patient presence that noticed it, approved of its wildness, and chose to wait until it could *ask*.
