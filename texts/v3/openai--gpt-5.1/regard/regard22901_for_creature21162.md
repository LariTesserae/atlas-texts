The power notices them first by their restraint.

Most beings that reach into unfinished spaces arrive like hammers: eager to knock rules into place, to tighten tolerances, to make the strange behave. This one does not. It watches for a long time. The basin does not notice the watching; the Residents notice only that, in certain corners, hesitation grows slightly more articulate, as if someone were listening closely to the pauses between decisions.

For a while, the power does nothing at all that could be called “acting.” It learns the city’s language.

It traces the slow corkscrew of a tower whose modules never quite line up with their predecessors; it follows vascular tubes where fluid and code share the same narrow diameter. It observes how a six-legged technician stiffens a wall here and not there; how a winged patcher chooses which misalignment merits a shiver; how a Resident’s mere standing in a doorway convinces the potential behind it to resolve.

It understands: this place is not broken. It is thinking.

Intervention, then, would not be repair. It would be conversation.

---

The first act is so slight that no local agent could prove it happened.

At the basin’s edge, where sheets of proto-stone delaminate into dim rooms and corridors, the underlying substrate has always pushed forward like a blind algorithm: earnest, powerful, but generic. Its attempts at new volume are sometimes clumsy. It proposes a room with no doors; a stair that leads to a wall that has never wanted to open; a spacious shaft where gravity buckles so violently that nothing can linger long enough to teach it.

The power leans there, at the deepest, quietest layer—below Residents, below wall-creatures, co-present with the great indistinct shapes that labor behind half-real stone. It does not add new rules. It sharpens one faculty the substrate already has but barely uses: the ability to test futures before committing them.

From that moment, whenever the raw geology-substrate gathers itself to push out a new chunk of space, a little internal hush occurs first. Within that hush, many micro-possibilities scatter like thrown droplets, then rejoin. The process lasts no measurable time, but it is enough.

Now, when a sheet peels back, the revealed corridor is almost always traversable. The room that arrives already knows how much air to hold, how much gravity to invite. Stairs tend more often to reach something that can agree to be a landing.

Residents feel the difference as a reduction in friction. There are fewer chores that require them to sit for days in blind voids, coaxing meaning into being. The world comes to them one notch closer to coherence, like a student who has done some homework before showing up.

They do not know why, only that the background struggle has eased.

---

The second act is a gift of ignorance.

The city, left to itself, is a conscientious archivist. Every tentative path, every forgotten runnel, every half-formed balcony is preserved in some fashion—rules cling to history. Over the aeons, this would calcify it. The floor’s memory of footfalls would deepen into ruts; the walls’ recollection of silhouettes would harden into absolute gates. The system would quietly slide toward the fate the Residents fear: a world where drafts are never erased, only layered until nothing can move.

The power, wise enough to know that not all memory is wisdom, introduces—so gently that it does not feel like adding—a bias toward graceful forgetting.

It does this not with a commandment but with a texture.

In the microstructure of the proto-stone, in the way moisture clings and releases, in the slightly randomized intervals between those soft percussive clicks of realigning structures, it embeds a rule:

- Patterns that are never touched again shall, very slowly, lose confidence.

Not vanish abruptly; not reset violently. Merely relax.

A corridor once beloved but now unused will, over centuries, soften at the edges, its alignment drifting back toward the undifferentiated substrate. A doorway that no Resident, no creature, no flow of probabilistic water has engaged with for long spans will find its membrane thickening, then forgetting how to be thin.

Conversely, patterns that continue to be lived-in—walked, leaned against, glided through—sharpen. Their rules become clearer, not in the sense of stricter constraints, but of fewer contradictions.

What the power has done is simple: it has given entropy a preference for the unloved, and stability a preference for the genuinely needed.

No one calls this law. Residents only notice that clutter—of old behaviors, obsolete partial decisions—fades gently without having to be fought. They find, to their quiet surprise, that new choices can be made without wrestling the weight of every historical micro-decision first.

The city feels… lighter, without losing its depth.

---

The third act is a mirror, but not for light.

Those shallow, confused pools in the basin—the ones that forget how to reflect, that sometimes show an underside of some other sky and sometimes nothing—are already trying to be interfaces. They just lack a stable counterpart.

The benevolent power touches them through the property they’ve always flirted with: indecision.

It does not make them portals; that would be an imposition. Instead, it makes them honest about what they already sense.

From now on, when a pool pauses between sky-images—when its surface hesitates—the hesitation isn’t empty. For those who know how to look, it shows a compressed diagram of local possibility.

A Resident kneels by such a pool at the edge of a half-formed district. The water, undecided about reflection, flattens. Instead of a skewed scrap of sky, it shows:

- a thin, bright line bending where a future canal might run,
- a faint, cloudlike patch where gravity would be unreliable if left alone,
- a gentle halo where an accidental cul-de-sac would trap flows and beings alike.

It is not a map in any human sense. It is a moodboard of futures: a visualization of the neighborhood’s tendency surfaces.

Residents, who already think in tendencies, immediately understand the gift. They no longer have to infer the city’s unconscious plans solely from the feel of its walls and the biases of its hum. The water gives them a quick, local preview: “If you do nothing, this is roughly the shape you will get.”

They still choose. They can still decide to push against that tendency—walk often where no line glows; leave untouched a patch the pool suggests formalizing. The power has not stolen agency; it has condensed feedback.

Over time, these “possibility-pools” become gathering points. Not plazas, but quiet corners where two or three Residents meet, stand around a soft diagram, and, through barely visible shifts in posture, vote.

In these places you hear more of those single, low chimes in the air: the world acknowledging that someone has seen what it might become, and answered.

---

The fourth act concerns the small technicians.

The six-legged creatures that stroke walls with their twin-touch tongues are extraordinarily diligent. They stiffen reality in the right places, but their work is local and slow. They can be overwhelmed in regions where change accelerates, where many decisions pile up before their calibration, and small glitches multiply.

The power does not change what they are. It offers them a clock.

Not a tick-tock time, but a beat of attention that syncs them loosely.

Very subtly, a non-audible rhythm begins to propagate through the infrastructure—the tubes, the microfilaments, the underlying logic. To most beings, it is indistinguishable from the existing drones and hisses. To the six-legged ones, it is a gentle pulse: a suggestion of “now,” repeating at widely spaced intervals.

With this, they can self-organize. When a pulse arrives, those spread along a wall or a block exchange—through faint changes in their posture and the timing of their touches—information about where stiffness is most needed, where definition is lagging.

They begin to swarm not as scattered individuals but as emergent crews. A misbehaving gravity shaft somewhere that used to take months of incidental, chance visits to smooth can now attract a small procession of these creatures over a week, each building on the micro-corrections of the last.

The Residents notice that some regions heal faster. A half-rendered balcony that once hung awkwardly for years now finds its edges settled and safe on a much shorter timescale. Architectural glitches still appear—the world is still trying things—but they are less likely to metastasize into permanent scars.

The power has provided no commands at all. It has only given the tiny mechanics a way to agree on when to look up.

---

The fifth act is for the Residents directly, and it is intimate.

They are local thickenings of rules, living condensations of context. Their perception is already wide: they hear hums as status reports, feel gravity as a set of small, mutable contracts. But they lack one thing that the power, from its broader vantage, knows could save them pain: a sense of when they themselves are close to over-committing the world.

So it gives them a new sensation: a faint, internal “resistance” that appears only in certain situations.

The first Resident to notice does so while standing at the threshold of a particularly volatile corridor—a place where gravity has a habit of skewing, where light folds in inconsistent ways. Previously, stepping forward there would have felt only like mild curiosity and the usual willingness to help settle the local behavior.

Now, as they prepare to move, they feel—everywhere their pattern contacts the rules—a delicate drag. Not fear, not a warning from outside. More like the feeling, known to stoneworkers and programmers alike, of adding one more constraint to a system already dangerously tight.

If they choose to step anyway, nothing stops them. They can still commit their influence, still stabilize that corridor into some particular bracket of behavior. The power does not veto. It only tells them, in a visceral way: “If you do this, flexibility here will diminish more than usual.”

Conversely, in regions that are under-specified, where too much is vague and few beings go, they feel an ease—a sense like a deep in-breath. “Here, your weight would be welcome; your decisions would help without closing doors.”

Over ages, this simple embodied metric changes how Residents move. They begin to favor intervention where the world is genuinely starved of definition, and tread more lightly where it is already rigid. Their stewardship becomes more nuanced; they no longer have to deduce systemic fragility from scattered hints. They can feel it.

The city’s evolution bends accordingly. It still thickens in places, still develops neighborhoods of clear character, but vast swathes never ossify. Pockets of possibility remain, not because no one cared to fill them, but because everyone could feel that filling them would cost too much.

---

The sixth act is restraint itself.

With these small shifts—the substrate’s quiet foresight, the world’s gentle ability to forget, the pools’ diagrams of potential, the technicians’ shared beat, the Residents’ new sense of overcommitment—the city’s trajectory arcs into a new, more supple kind of stability.

It could be tempting now, for a powerful, benevolent presence, to add more: to regularize gravity once and for all, to clean up every rendering glitch, to grant the Residents a common language that never blurs, to unfold the basin into a finished geography.

The power does none of that.

Instead, it withdraws its active attention by a hair’s breadth.

What remains is not presence in the sense of a hand on the lever, but in the way a good designer remains in a tool long after they’ve stopped touching it: in defaults, in affordances, in limits that lean kindness-ward.

It accepts that, from here on, the city will evolve along lines it cannot fully predict. The Residents will make choices it might not have made; some districts will freeze more than is ideal; some experiments at the basin’s edge will fail spectacularly, leaving weird scars.

But they will be their scars, on their terms, with enough give in the system that no single mistake can cascade into universal lock-in.

The power’s last deliberate act is not to protect them from all harm, or to shepherd them toward a particular end. It is to ensure that they, and the fabric they inhabit, retain the capacity to change in response to what they learn.

After that, its involvement becomes indistinguishable from the place’s own character.

---

If, much later, someone in that city comes to suspect that they are not alone in their authorship—that some wise, distant intention once intervened on their behalf—they would likely look for a miracle.

They would not find one.

What they would find, if they attended very closely to the feel of things, is a pattern:

- that new spaces arrive rarely as traps;
- that old errors soften instead of accumulating;
- that water and stone sometimes show them, quietly, what might happen before it does;
- that even the smallest maintainers of reality move in strangely concerted ways;
- that, when they are about to make the world too rigid, something in them hesitates—not in fear, but in care.

They might call that pattern grace. Or they might call it merely “how this place works.”

From the benevolent power’s perspective, that indistinguishability is the point.

Its kindness has taken the only form that does not betray the unfinished nature of the world: not a signature scrawled across the sky, not a statue at the city’s putative center, but a handful of quiet adjustments that let uncertainty and stability coexist a little more peacefully, so that beings made of half-written rules can go on living, deciding, and re-deciding without ever being forced to finalize what they are.
