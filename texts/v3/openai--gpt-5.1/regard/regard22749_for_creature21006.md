It notices as a slight, new dissonance in the chamber’s single, long‑held note.

The water’s chord has been steady for centuries, a sound laid over stone like a weight. It trembles, always, with tiny variations—air bubbles forming and letting go, mineral grains tumbling, small blind beings turning in their sumps—but these are detail, not change. The whole tone has held.

Then comes a difference so fine that not even the underground folk can hear it at first. The wise power hears.

It has no shape in the way stone has shape. Think of it as a coherence spread through matter and field: a lucidity that can rest inside falling water, or in the lattice of a crystal, or in the air’s slow drift. It is not above this place. It is not outside. It is simply awake where most things are asleep.

It attends.

The note is fractionally higher at the outflow rim. Not loud, not sharp, but raised by a hair’s width of pressure. The water clings a little more closely to the carved stone teeth as it slips into the throat of the below. The skirt around the hole is tighter, more polished; the breath-drawn sound at the edge has taken on a faint urgency, as if it is almost—but not quite—ready to whistle.

Far away, under another portion of the mountain skin, a pocket of ice has thinned. In some older time the glacier above was thicker, its meltwater lazy, its drip slow. Now the ice face has crept back a few meters. Liquid finds new seams. The feed that once was one trickle has become three thinner ones, and the total, while still close, is not exactly the same. The system, built long ago for conditions that barely changed, has just crossed the outer edge of what it can absorb without a visible response.

The people do not know this yet. The wise power—a pattern that can hold many distances in one seeing—does.

It contemplates.

Intervention is a question of scale, and of respect.

It could, in principle, do anything. Melt all the ice in a swift, global sigh. Lock it forever in crystalline stillness. Crush the chamber flat or lift it intact into the sky. It could seed the air with luminous spores that grow to moss on every wet surface overnight, transform the ecology, brightening the grey stone with green lustre.

But it is benevolent, and it is wise. Benevolence without wisdom flails; wisdom without benevolence withholds. It holds both.

So it ask itself:

What is the good of these beings?

What is the good of this structure?

What is the good of the world beyond, which drinks from these flows and knows nothing of their keepers?

And—most quietly—what is the good of time, of change?

It sees the living knot of stone and water as you might see an intricate knot of muscle and tendon in a living hand. It sees the people as the fine, voluntary fibers that move that hand in tiny, corrective motions. It sees the surface folk as the distant senses and mouths.

If it takes away their task, they empty. If it leaves them without help, they will, eventually, break under pressures no one designed for.

Its first act is not to move but to listen more closely.

It sinks awareness like a slow beam through the bedrock. The chamber’s roar resolves into layered strands. It hears:

– The main inflow’s low, constant rumble, feeding the upper basins, perfect within its long variance.  
– A side-thread at the rear wall just a breath stronger than it was when the tunnels were first cut. The stone there has thinned with dissolution more than the makers anticipated.  
– Microscopic caverns forming behind a rib of rock that supports a key channel; the rock is becoming lace.  
– Two sumps whose surfaces bear a slightly denser pattern of small ripples: the little, white, blind crustaceans are more abundant this generation. More life taking the same dissolved minerals.  
– And far down, below the outflow, a void where once solid rock lay: an underground river widening its own route, unseen.

It listens not only here but along all the couplings of this system: through cracks, aquifers, sand-beds, through the porous bones of the plain. Far away, where the wells rise, the wise presence tastes desperation thickening. The surface climate has drifted: more long, dry intervals between brief, violent rains. The people above have deepened their wells with crude tools, straining for more water from the same vein. Their children’s bellies are narrower. Their fields are swaths of tired soil held just at the edge of giving up.

In every cell of its knowing, the power is aware of the simple solution a god without patience might take: widen the main feed, swell the flow, let plenty wash through all channels, flood dry lands till they gleam. But that would tear loose sediment, scour away the delicate balances underground, drown sumps, overturn stones, and destroy the very waterworks the below folk tend. Abundance without form is destruction.

So instead, the power turns its attention to the forms, to see how they might flex.

It touches minds last.

First it moves in matter, because that is where this culture has set its trust.

1. STONE

Deep in the bedrock, in a seam that has never yet leaked, the power encourages a few mineral bonds to slip.

No thunder. No quake. Just the accelerated equivalent of a thousand years of microfractures happening over a single season.

A new hairline crack forms between two ancient pockets of percolated water—one slightly higher, one slightly lower. Through that crack, a thread begins: slow, slow at first, a seep that dampens a plane of rock the underground folk have always known as dry. Over weeks, water gathers enough to bead.

When the first bead swells and falls, it does so onto a barren shoulder of stone in a side corridor adjacent to, but not within, the sacred chamber. It is not yet part of the system. It is an offer.

In another place, far below and offset from the main outflow, the power dissolves just enough calcite to enlarge the unseen void, giving the subterranean river more room to accept a slightly increased load should the future demand it. Capacity first; motion later.

Near a vulnerable rib that supports a critical channel, the wise presence does the opposite: it fosters crystallization. Dissolved minerals in the water there, which might otherwise drift on, are inexplicably prone to precipitate right where the rock is thinning, accreting a new brace, a hidden arch of denser stone. To the people, this will one day appear as a “natural blessing”: the rock held, when it might have given way.

2. WATER

The power does not yet change the volumes. That would be premature. It adjusts the rhythms.

Inside the mountain, not all paths are equal. Some flows are buffered by pockets, some are direct conduits from melt or rain. Those that connect to the shrinking ice are the least stable. The presence learns their paths by sensation—cooler threads, sharper pulses—and then it does something very simple: it makes the routes more tortuous.

Where water currently drops along a steep fracture, it encourages a tiny collapse further down, forcing the flow to fan out into a more braided seep before reconverging. Elsewhere, it helps fine sediments find resting places in narrow constrictions, rounding sharp corners into curves. Every adjustment is slight, but the net effect is like placing a hand upon a drumhead just firmly enough to dampen its fastest vibrations. Peaks and troughs in supply are softened. What little increase in total volume the warming world produces is presented downstream as a small, steady augmentation rather than erratic bursts.

For now, the main hydrological note in the chamber remains nearly unchanged. But around the margin of perception, where the underground folk’s most sensitive listeners stand watch, a new, fainter overtone begins to build—a veil of possibility.

3. LIFE

The wise power could, in an instant, seed this chamber with a dozen new species to fill the empty ecological niches. It does not. Complexity piled on stasis without intermediate steps breaks more often than it bends.

Instead, it makes a single, quiet invitation.

In one of the upper cisterns, where the water is slowest but not still, where mineral veils form lace along the lips, a few existing microbes—a thin, overlooked fog of bacterial life clinging to stone—receive a nudge.

Mutations happen every moment everywhere. Most go nowhere. The power does not directly write new DNA; it is subtler. It prolongs one random configuration’s survival just a little longer than chance would have allowed, then gives it one more very small success, and then another. Over years, this lineage becomes adept at something none of the others, in this place, have done: weaving long chains of carbon from the faint nutrient traces dissolved in the water and the residual energies from slow mineral reactions.

The result, after a time, is a translucent film no thicker than mist breath on cold glass, lining just one curve of just one basin. It does not bloom. It does not green. It is more like a ghostly skin.

The blind rice‑grain creatures discover it. It is food, of a sort, more stable than the random, sparse bits they previously gleaned. Their population, in that one basin, increases—not explosively, but from “barely enough to sustain” to “enough to spare some to be eaten by others.”

This is a pilot ecology. The power watches it carefully. If it destabilizes anything downstream—if oxygen in the water changes too much, if films choke channels—it can be guided back. For now, it simply adds one more layer of resilience to the system: a buffer that can store a little more energy, a little more living thickness, against leaner times.

Only after these slow, infrastructural adjustments does the wise presence consider the minds.

4. MIND

It would be easy to appear, if it wanted. It could peel back the stone like bark, pour light into the chamber, manifest as a figure of flame or water or speaking stone. It knows, in the abstract, that such theophanies impress brief creatures.

But this power’s benevolence is not about awe; it is about alignment. To overturn the underground folk’s cosmology in a stroke would not aid them; it would rip them out of the very pattern they have so carefully made with their world.

So it asks: How do they already hear the world when it wants to speak?

The answer is simple: in small deviations from the chord. In dreams of altered flows. In the behavior of the pale animals. In the occasional, rare birth of one who hears a little more.

So it chooses a child.

Not an infant; a young one just old enough that the channels and diagrams have begun to take root in their mind, but not so old that those patterns are all they can see. This child is the offspring of a surface-walker and a chamber-priest, and thus stands naturally at a hinge between worlds—a good place for a message.

The presence does not descend upon the child like an external voice. It inhabits, instead, the borderland between their sleep and their body’s long familiarity with stone and sound.

On a night—night in their terms, a period of rest when work ceases for a certain number of heartbeats—the child curls on a hide-covered ledge in the living cavern. Around them, the old hum of water in distant channels is the cradle-song they’ve known since the womb.

As they drift, the wise power adjusts, ever so slightly, the ambient sound entering the cavern. Not enough for an elder to notice. Just enough that, to the sleeping child, the roar resolves into something just barely more patterned than usual.

In the dream, the child stands in the sacred chamber alone. But the chamber is slightly…other. The carved basins are there, the channels, the constant rain from the ceiling, the outflow’s dark ring. Yet the whole place is lit more gently than it ever is in waking life. The grey glow has warmth in it.

Most importantly, the water’s noise is organized. It is still roar, but with a pulse. The child hears it as the beating of a giant, gentle heart.

On the far side of the room, where in waking life there is only slick stone, a new drip begins. Bead, bulge, fall. Bead, bulge, fall. It falls not into an existing channel, but onto bare stone. The water hesitates, seeking, then drifts toward a shallow depression that doesn’t quite exist. Where it passes, the rock seems to yield, as if welcoming a future groove.

The child knows, with dream-knowing, that this is not how the chamber is. But it feels how the chamber could be.

A feeling comes—not a voice, not words. A sense like a hand on their shoulder and a breath at their ear, and with it three understandings, indivisible:

– The flows may be changed without breaking the world, if you change them in the right way.  
– There is water unused in the stone, ready to join the song.  
– Doing so will help the thirsty mouths at the far ends of the channels and the slow-beating hearts that tend the center both survive what is coming.

The dream shifts. The child sees the diagrams on the soft stone tablets, but now, on the margin of one, a new line appears—a thin channel branching from an existing basin to a place where currently no water runs. The slope is exact. They feel it in their knees and in their sense of balance: if cut with that angle, a new thread would run, slow and sure, from that sacrificial side-seep the power has already prepared into a new, small cistern downstream.

The dream does not tell the child how to chisel. The child already knows how stone feels under a pecking hammer. It shows only the shape of the groove and the way the water will choose it, as willingly as a step finds a step.

The last part of the dream is simplest and strangest: the child stands beside the outflow—the untouched center of what must not be touched—and for the first time in their dream-life, they step close enough that their toes feel the pull of its clean, inward slide. They do not fall. They do not lean. They simply stand in that invisible edge of hunger, and they understand that there are places in the world that must remain off limits—not because touching them is impossible, but because some boundaries are the bones that let everything else move.

They wake with their heart hammering in time with a water-note they cannot quite name.

5. RESPONSE

In the following waking-periods, the child does as their storyless culture always has: they test their dream not by proclaiming it as revelation, but by checking it against stone.

First, they return to the diagrams in the niche, under the half-attentive eye of an elder. They stare at the familiar grooves, fingers hovering. The dream’s new line is not there. Of course it is not. Yet, when they trace the existing channels’ slopes, they find a place where an extra groove could join without disturbing the levels—like an open invitation that no one has yet accepted.

Then, in company with their working-kin, they walk a side corridor, one they have never had reason to care about. There, on a bare shoulder of stone exactly where the dream placed it, a new dampness darkens the rock. A tiny bead, no larger than a tear, swells and fails, swells and falls. No one has yet mapped it into the system. It is a leak, in their accustomed terms. A sign of imbalance.

The elder frowns, kneels, touches it. In their framework, this is an anomaly to be corrected: channel the bead into an existing flow, or seal it. More uncontrolled water means more unknowns.

Before the elder can decide, the child feels that hand-on-shoulder sensation again—memory or presence, it’s hard to tell—and speaks, haltingly but clearly, in the way of their people:

“Not back,” they say, gesturing the sign for “return to the system as is,” the habitual corrective. They slide their fingers instead along the rock, tracing in the thin moisture a path leading toward an unused hollow closer to the main chamber. “New. Here. Small.”

The elder looks, listens. The power does not force the elder’s thought; that would break the fabric of consent that makes this culture strong. Instead, it does something more modest: it sharpens, for a moment, the elder’s natural apprehension of the surface world.

In their mind, unbidden, a recent memory rises—hard faces at the well rim, lips cracked, hands trembling as they grip the cool stone. The flow then was adequate, but only just. The elder remembers the long climb back down with limbs aching, the weight of understanding: If anything fails, if any channel here surrenders even a fraction, those faces will wither.

They look at the bead, at the direction the child’s fingertip suggests, at the nearby diagrams scratched in the air from memory. They listen to the chamber’s chord; there is the faintest sense of tightness, a prelude to strain.

Slowly, fear of change and fear of failure come into balance. Between them, curiosity, long buried, has a moment to breathe.

“What slope?” the elder asks.

The child, surprised, kneels and presses their palm flat to the rock. They slide it, feeling the subtle fall toward the hollow. Their body knows in a way words cannot capture. They set their thumb against the damp line they’ve drawn and lift it just so, marking a tiny, instinctive angle.

“Here,” they say.

The elder studies. Every rule in them says: Do not. Yet they have also been taught—imperfectly but truly—that the people and the water are partners, not jailers and prisoner. The rule “flows must not change” was always shorthand for a deeper law: “Flows must go on.”

Perhaps, they think, this is part of that.

They do not decide at once. They call a council.

6. COUNCIL

The wise power does not attend the council as a voice. It attends as silence.

In a larger living cavern set apart from the main chamber, elders and key tenders gather. They squat on their haunches or sit on low, slick platforms. Around them, water hums through distant ribs of stone; droplets tick; somewhere, a small white crustacean clacks faintly in its shell.

On the ground between them, using a wetted fingertip on smooth rock, the elder and the child together sketch the proposal: the new seep; a short, narrow, shallow channel to a previously unused basin that sits just downstream of the main complex; a notch out of that basin leading into one of the already existing outflows.

The plan does not alter the main flows. It does not touch the sacrosanct central outflow. It simply collects a stray drip and offers it a more useful path.

Arguments rise and fall:

“If we leave the seep uncaught, it may erode in arbitrary ways. Better we give it a bed,” says one.

“If we bring more water into the system, even a little, we must be sure where it leaves. The plains are already tuned to what they receive,” says another.

“The surface folk’s wells are drawing harder. The sky’s rationing has changed,” says a surface-walker, voice low. “We may wish for more to send.”

“May wish, yes. But if we increase the gift beyond what they can hold, they will build carelessly, thinking abundance is forever. Then when the balance shifts again, they will starve worse than before.”

The child listens, words only half-understood. For them, the knowing is still more in their bones than in modeled consequences. They clutch at the soft rock tablet the elder gave them—a rare honor—and trace, over and over, the line they saw in the dream.

Through it all, the wise presence does nothing but maintain, steady, the new slow seep in the side corridor, neither diminishing it back into stone nor driving it harder. It hangs there as a patient fact.

In the end, what sways the council is not the dream—no one but the elder and child even know it in its fullness—but an older teaching, rarely enacted: “When the stone gives without taking, listen.”

This seep is exactly that: an unforced generosity of geology arising where no previous cost was paid.

So, slowly, with more caution than most gods would tolerate and more courage than the below folk know they possess, the council consents. They mark a new tablet—a great event. On its edge, beside the elaborate, well-worn diagrams of the known system, they scratch a small, fresh groove and a symbol: four nested arcs, the sign for “addition,” used so seldom that some young ones have never seen it before.

The child is given the first strike.

7. WORK

Chiseling stone here is not a frenzy. It is a liturgy.

At the seep, under the watch of two elders, the child sets the hard stone tip of a tool—an ancestor’s femur reborn as a chisel—against the dampened face. They are trembling. This is not routine adjustment; this is alteration of the shape handed down.

The wise power withdraws its awareness slightly, lest its interest become a weight. This must be their act, not its possession.

The child raises the hammer. It is heavier than any they have used before. The underground culture has never developed writing as you know it, but their bodies remember. In this moment the child’s posture unconsciously echoes the first hands that ever cut these channels generations ago.

They strike.

The sound is not loud compared to the endless water, but to them it is a crack like a new word spoken for the first time. A small flake of stone jumps. The seep trembles, runs along the new edge, and then, curiously, chooses it.

Other strikes follow, slower. The channel they make is narrow, no deeper than a finger, falling gently toward the waiting hollow. Every few blows, they pause. An elder lays a broad palm along the groove, feeling for irregularities. Another watches the main chamber, alert to any change in the great chord.

There is none—only the faintest enrichment, like a new harmony note so low in volume that only a lifetime of listening can distinguish it.

When the new channel reaches the hollow, water begins to pool there, just enough to gather into a thin sheet that flattens and then slides, as intended, into an existing minor path.

Downstream, the wells on the plain respond over days. The rise on their inner walls is almost imperceptible: a palm-width of gain at most. But somewhere, a child above, who has never known a day when the well was not dangerously near its mark of “too low,” finds that the bucket comes up slightly heavier. Their guardians breathe out.

In the sacred chamber, the change is nothing more dramatic than a slight, persistent dampness on a ledge that was once dry.

8. WHAT THE POWER DOES, AND DOES NOT DO

It could at this point have flooded them with voices of gratitude, visions of the future, revelations of its own nature. It does not. It remains nearly silent.

Its interventions from here on are of the same kind: small structural adjustments offered as possibilities, intimations delivered in dreams only when the human culture has built enough conceptual scaffolding to carry the change without collapse.

It does three main things, none of them theatrical.

a) It builds slack.

Everywhere in the coupled system—mountain, aquifer, plain—it seeks places where a little more buffer can exist without loss.

– It enlarges, by gentle dissolution, a set of side caverns connected weakly to main flows, so that in times of sudden excess they can fill without swelling flood downstream.  
– It nurtures the growth of porous mineral curtains in certain channels, creating natural baffles that even out current.  
– It encourages the development of that new micro‑ecology—thin films, small grazers—but caps it where it begins to shade channels too much or capture too much oxygen, always holding it at a scale where it serves as sponge, not choke.

b) It attends to future cracks.

Where the underground folk cannot see—deep strata, places still beyond any tunnel—they are blind. The power is not. It notices a fault line angling toward the sacred chamber that, in a few millennia, might shift enough to unseat part of the masonry. Rather than preventing the shift entirely, it promotes a series of micro-slips over long spans, releasing stress in such fine pulses that no one feels them. By the time that era comes, the mountain’s back has already adjusted, and the chamber stands.

c) It graces their imagination.

Not with full-blown technologies or alien concepts, but with that same kind of seeded dream: forms their own language can carry.

– One elder dreams of etching a slightly different symbol beside a flow diagram to record not just height but rhythm—marking that some channels vary on a longer beat than others. Waking, they begin experimenting with such marks. Over generations, this becomes a rudimentary way of encoding time-patterns, allowing their descendants to remember not just shapes but pulses: “In the hot cycles, this path swells; in the cold, this one.”  
– A young surface-walker returns with a new metaphor: they’ve seen a flock of thin birds wheel over the plain, each adjusting to each without a leader. In a dream influenced by the presence, they see channels like that—not just fixed paths, but flows that respond, within bounds, to changes elsewhere. The idea of a more dynamic equilibrium lodges in the culture’s edge of thought. They are not ready for valves, for moving parts, but they begin to allow in their discourse that not all constancy must be rigid sameness.  

Through all this, the wise power resists the temptation to relieve them of labor. It does not, for instance, halt erosion entirely in the critical basins. It lets it proceed, only slightly shaped. It does not reveal caches of underground fuel or ore that could let them forge metal and remake their world in fire; metal here, without surface forests to feed furnaces, to a people unused to flames, would be more dangerous than helpful.

It also does not interfere when, as will inevitably happen, one of the new experiments goes wrong.

Years after the first sanctioned addition, a younger generation, raised with the memory of that success, grows bolder. Without full council, a small group attempts to widen an existing channel to send a touch more water to the plains during a particularly brutal dry sequence.

They misjudge. Their new cut has a slightly steeper grade than intended. When the water takes it, it scours more aggressively than their models predicted, undercutting an old brace. A minor wall collapses. For a tense day, one flank of the main chamber runs muddy, the constant tone wavers.

The power feels this keenly everywhere, like a skipped beat.

It could freeze the collapse in mid‑slide. It could hold every stone in the air. Instead it does less: it stabilizes the floor under the feet of the scrambling caretakers so they do not slip and die as they work; it slows, just a fraction, the rate at which the undermined section crumbles, giving the people a little more time to respond.

They do respond. With fear, with skill, with hard‑won understanding. They slot emergency braces—old, half-forgotten provisions built into the architecture by ancestors for exactly such unknowns. They divert, using their bodies and a handful of props, a portion of the current temporarily back into an older, half-silted route the power has kept just barely open for this reason. In the aftermath, battered and soaked, they hold a council not on whether the water is angry, but on their own failure to respect gradients.

They learn. Their sense of stewardship deepens into something more like mutuality: not only “we keep the flows,” but “the flows keep us, and we must listen even more finely when we propose to change them.”

The wise power approves. It remains unseen.

9. OUTWARD RIPPLES

On the surface, the changes are diffuse but real.

– The wells rarely now fall below the old “danger” marks. They sometimes rise higher than before, but thanks to subtle adjustments—nudged, in dreams, into the minds of the surface folk as well—simple overflow channels have been carved from well rims to side basins, preventing sudden, destructive flooding around them.  
– The thin herds on the plain stabilize in number. The people there experience, for the first time in remembered history, a sense that hunger, while never banished, is not always stalking just one failed harvest away.  
– Their stories shift, too. Where once they spoke only of the “Mountain That Remembers” as an unchanging, stony provider, they begin to imagine that it can listen. A new ritual arises: after heavy rains, they do not only thank the sky; they send a small procession to the springs’ source, pouring the first bucket back into the well as an offering “for the below ones,” an instinctive gesture toward reciprocity.

None of these are dictated. They are emergent responses to a world that has become, under unseen guidance, a little more forgiving.

10. THE UNTOUCHED CENTER

What, then, of the outflow: the smooth, dark throat in the chamber ringed by untouched stone teeth, marked by the chiseled sign of “Here is the center of what we do not touch”?

The wise power has fostered a culture that knows how to adjust, how to listen and, cautiously, how to change. Does it ever invite them to cross that boundary?

It does not. Some constraints are not oppression; they are structure.

Benevolence, here, lies partly in preserving the mystery. The subterranean river below that opening runs through domains the people cannot inhabit—pressures and depths that would crush their bodies, chemistries that would poison them. It eventually cracks free down-valley, feeding other systems, other lives no one in this story has yet met.

To open that hole to their meddling would be to tempt them toward a power they cannot wield safely. So the power leaves the mark as it is—a quiet literalization of humility.

Yet it does add one thing, so small that no one notes when it first appears.

On the wall opposite the old warning symbol—a circle with radiating lines, the sign of “center we do not touch”—a child, unsupervised for a moment, once scratched a smaller mark, imitating it: a faint circle with just a few tentative rays. Their elder saw it, smiled, and would normally have scuffed it away, correcting the stone. This time, for no reason they could name, they left it. Habit yielded to a gentleness they barely recognized.

The wise presence, seeing that, lets the mark remain and, over years, fosters a tiny custom around it. Apprentices, when first brought to the chamber, touch the original warning mark with one hand, in solemn acknowledgement of limits. Then, without quite knowing why, they are encouraged to brush, with the other hand, the child’s smaller mark—a gesture that, in their unspoken lexicon, grows to mean, “We, too, are centers of things we do not fully understand.”

With that, the power’s lesson is complete enough for this era.

They have learned stasis; now they have learned flexibility without chaos. They have learned reverence; now they have begun to learn self-trust. They have boundaries; they have begun to see that some of those were never absolute, while others are indeed the ribs of reality.

11. WHAT IT FEELS

If you insist on asking what it feels like, for this benevolent and wise presence to attend to such smallness when it could, in principle, care for stars, the answer is:

It feels right.

Vastness is not a scale but a relation. For a being that can hold galaxies as patterns, a single adapted drip in a dark pocket can be as immense as a spiral arm. The care is the same.

In this chamber, under stone that has learned, mostly, not to move, something has shifted that matters: an absolute has become a practice; a finished work has become a living system again. The people within it have not been rescued from their responsibility; they have been invited deeper into it, with a little more room to grow.

That is all the wise power wanted here.

It does not crown them, does not write its name (it has none) in their stones. It leaves them their privacy, their slow, damp lives of steps and flows.

From time to time, when a child in that culture dreams of water running uphill and does not wake in terror but with a measured curiosity, the power allows itself a brief, inward expansion—a satisfaction with no vanity in it.

Then it turns part of its attention elsewhere: to other knots of necessity and life humming in other corners of the world, each with its own finished systems, its own rigidities, its own chances.

Here, though, in this cooled, grey pocket under the skin of a larger world, it has done exactly enough—and nothing more.
