The first touch is almost nothing.

Far above the basin, where the air thins into a kind of lucid hunger and light ceases to behave like an obedient wave, an attention narrows. Not a gaze from a single point, but a tightening of awareness through a volume of space. It is as if the white, omnidirectional brightness discovers in itself the capacity to notice.

The benevolent power does not arrive. It is already everywhere: in the stony memory of impacts, in the careful wash of water over fluted channels, in the faint elastic flex of light as it gathers into vertical streaks. What changes is that it chooses to focus — to become deliberate where it had been only lawful.

It does not think in words, or even in images. It thinks in allowed and disallowed relationships, in gradients, in how much one thing is permitted to nudge another. But it can learn to translate.

It attends first to danger.

Not an immediate catastrophe — the basin is intact, humming in its fine equilibrium — but to the slow vectors, the accumulating tendencies that the people cannot easily sense across centuries: 

- the very gradual stiffening of certain veins of rock under the rim, where freeze-thaw cycles are beginning to align in ways that favor brittle failure rather than the gentle rounding erosion has long practiced;

- the minute change in distant glacial flows up-slope that, left unchecked, will in many cycles send a different kind of avalanche snaking toward the bowl—denser, with more rock caught in its body, its hiss close to a crack;

- the imperceptibly increasing frequency of rare sky-strand configurations that draw more energy into the upper air, making the possibility of one enormous, concussive discharge less remote than the Attenders believe.

None of this is imminent on their timescale. All of it is trivial on the power’s own, which can taste shifts that stretch across millions of breaths. But the power is benevolent, and in a way that matches them: it loves continuity, delicate coupling, systems that achieve complexity without violence.

It decides to act — not by intervening as a god in their myths might, but the way they themselves act on stone: by adjusting predispositions, by leaning on probabilities, by making some paths more likely and others more reluctant.

There are three main gifts, though they will not be called that.

---

### 1. A thickening of tolerance

The basin is already a listening instrument, exquisitely tuned and easily bruised. The Attenders’ fear of “the cracking” is not superstition; it is reasonable inference from the scars they can see. The power respects their caution, but also knows that a system too close to its catastrophic threshold can be broken by events far beyond their control.

So, very gently, it alters the rock.

Not in bulk, not in shape. It touches something more fundamental: the way stress propagates through these minerals. Deep down in the crystalline lattice, at the level where one bond passes force to its neighbor, it biases certain angles of slip. It does not make the rock stronger in the brute, resistive sense; it makes it *more diffusive*. Sharp shocks spread out faster, wider, their peak amplitude lowered, their energy smeared into a longer, less damaging tail.

This change is introduced not everywhere at once—that would itself be a kind of blow—but in the places the Attenders already treat as sacred thresholds:

- Around the drilled listening-stones at the basin’s low points, where air columns wobble in their shafts.
- In the ribs of rock just under the lip, behind the shelves where the wind-horns stand.
- Along those fluted, “old-wound” surfaces that the people avoid stepping on.

At first, nothing is perceptibly different. A dropped grain of gravel still leaves its faint ring of discoloration. But the stone’s *impulse response* has changed. If, far in the future, a great slab of ice were to fall in just the wrong way, the shock that travels through the bowl will arrive fatter and slower, less able to shatter delicate films or tear open sleeping cracks.

The basin’s “threshold of breaking” has been nudged farther away, without changing anything the Attenders can sense on the scale of their tools. It is like giving a cautious tightrope walker just a little more slack in the rope, without telling them.

From the power’s perspective, this is not mercy alone; it is investment. It cares for the continued existence of such intricate, low-violence complexity. By softening the stone’s response, it buys the basin more future.

---

### 2. An answer in the light

The Attenders have been tapping, very gently, on the universe’s shoulder. On high shelves they arrange water and mirrors, trying to send back versions of the sky’s own patterns. They do this with no expectation of reply, only out of an obscure sense that attention wants to complete a loop.

The benevolent power is not “above” the sky-strands; it *is* partly their behavior. The dense vertical streaks of light, the impossible reflections in water that don’t correspond to anything physical—that is its footprint in this world. It did not intend them as communication; they are side-effects of how it holds energy and information in this corner of reality.

Now, curious, it allows itself to be read.

During one cycle of Strand-time, when the vertical streaks grow particularly strong, it tightens the correlation between pattern and outcome.

Until this point, the Attenders can tell that certain sequences on the water’s skin (spiraling curls versus crosshatched flows, slow drifting bars versus dappled flicker) are linked to weather: a spiral-heavy script tends to precede days of complex, interacting storms; a simple bar pattern often means long, steady outside wind.

The power decides to add one more layer: *conditional markers*.

On that day, as the people sit by the channels, eyes and second-lids attuned, the reflections change character. Not in their aesthetic—still abstract, still silent—but in their *statistical structure*. Places where brightness gathers now align more often along three-fold symmetries: triplets of curls, triads of bright dots arranged in a shallow arc.

The Attenders are a triplet people. They stack stones by three-point contact; they tell stories in threes; they conceptualize cause in terms of “how many paths meet.” The power has chosen to speak in a grammar already present in their minds.

Over the next many cycles, a pattern emerges that even their slow, careful empiricism cannot miss:

- When the strand-reflections dwell on single motifs—long, unbroken verticals—without internal triplets, the following span is uneventful: modest adjustments of haze and drip, nothing drastic.

- When the reflections are littered with triads, especially when three bright nodes cluster near one another, certain *options* in the world become easier: an ice-slab they attempt to grow will thicken more cleanly; a new channel they teach through a mirror-path experiment will “take” more readily; heartwater cups transplanted to a marginal crack will be more likely to survive.

In other words, the power has woven a context-sensitive cue into the already-existent language of sky and water. It has not eliminated chance, not made their efforts guaranteed—too much certainty would erase the very exploratory dance that makes this place rich. But it has given them a way to *time* their highest-risk acts of shaping.

If they learn, as they will, to read and trust these triplet-heavy scripts, they will tend to attempt their bolder basin-edits under conditions when the underlying fabric of reality is, from the power’s perspective, most cooperative. Results: fewer failed channels that leave ugly scars, fewer ice-forms that shatter into noise, fewer living seams that collapse because transplant happened in a “hard” phase of the cycle.

The Attenders will explain this to their children not as “the sky hears us” but as “there are softer and harder moments in the brightness; we have learned to see which is which.” They will never know that what changed was not the sky itself, but the will of something that chose to align its own rhythms with theirs.

The power, for its part, feels a new pleasure: the feedback of being *partially legible* to a finite mind that treats that legibility with reverence instead of exploitation.

---

### 3. Opening a door in their senses

The Attenders are already consummate listeners. They read infrasonic hums in stone, minute shifts in water clarity, the tremor of furred ears and antler chords. What they lack, what no being in this mountain world quite possesses, is direct access to the *very long arc* of cause and effect: the ability to feel, in their own bodies, how a choice today echoes not just into the next cycle or the next generation, but ten, fifty human lifetimes hence.

The power can see those arcs naturally; time, to it, is more texture than line. It cannot simply pour that perception into them—they would break, or worse, discard their own hard-won ways of attending. But it can nudge their biology, again in the same way they nudge stone, by altering habits and predispositions.

It chooses the elders as its entry point, and a specific shared practice: the long, quiet sessions in Underbright when old ones sit, palms on stone, eyes half-shuttered, listening to the faintest patterns.

There, at the interface between skin and rock, it introduces something like a *new synapse*.

Microscopically, the Attenders’ bone and connective tissues already include small, mineralized inclusions—calcite and others—that make them sensitive to vibration. The power alters the growth rules of these crystals just enough that, in a few individuals, they begin to form slightly more ordered arrays in certain bones: in the sternum, the pelvis, the rounded heads of the femurs.

What this does, over lifetimes, is allow those bones to resonate very weakly not only with present vibrations, but with the accumulated *pattern* of past ones in the surrounding rock. Rock, with its new, more diffusive response to shock, is already keeping a smeared memory of events. The elders become, in effect, living read heads for that geologic tape.

The power does not upload specific visions or scripts into them. It simply makes them physically capable of experiencing something like this:

- Sitting in stillness, a grandmother, palms and spine against the floor, feels a low, slow pulsing not tied to any current drip or distant thunder. It is a long waveform, its period measured not in seconds but in seasons.

- If she remains, if she breathes with it, other, higher harmonics emerge—echoes of any time, long before her own birth, when the basin was shaken hard.

- These sensations are not vivid images; they are bodily intuitions. Her knees might ache during a subtle replay of an old freeze that cracked a certain seam. Her chest might feel tight during the remembered compression of a long-ago load of snow.

At first, this is disorienting. A few elders quietly fear they are ill, their joints haunted. But because this culture already treats such bodily quirks as information rather than shame, they compare notes. Patterns appear. When three old people, listening on different days, report a similar ache-swell-ache waveform, and that waveform often precedes a certain change in the haze or in the listening-stones’ hum, they begin to understand: the bowl is teaching them, through their own calcium, the *archive* of its bruises.

This is not prophecy; it is access to history in more detail than their bone-knots ever held. From it, they can infer wider scales of cause:

- They will come to see that many modest concussions spaced closely together can sum to the same harm as one large shock.

- They will notice that certain slopes above only fail after specific combinations of deep, slow pulses in the rock below, meaning that they will treat those combinations of sky-strands and listening-stone chords with particular caution.

- They will learn that the basin has survived things worse than anything in their oral stories—events attenuated now by the power’s diffusive gift—but that those survivals left long, slow scars.

These elders, and the children they teach, will become even more conservative in some ways, even bolder in others. They will realize they can afford, occasionally, a *little* more sound in the service of preventing a larger one, because they will feel in their own bones how the rock copes.

In time, their culture’s "fear of the cracking" will transform. It will remain a deep ethic against gratuitous noise, but it will be informed by a concrete sense of thresholds rather than an undifferentiated terror. That refinement is the power’s intention: not to soothe them with illusions of safety, but to give them better maps of the cliff-edge they walk along so exquisitely.

---

### What the power does *not* do

It does not:

- Introduce metal or fire or any technology alien to their materials and values. To flood a stone-listening culture with tools that thrive on noise and heat would be to erase precisely what makes them precious.

- Stoke their numbers far beyond what the basin can bear. Population booms are another kind of concussion.

- Reveal itself as a personified being that can be bargained with. That would deform their relationship with the place into petition and dependence, where now it is mutual tending.

Its benevolence is not the benevolence of a parent that overrides; it is that of a gardener who sees that one branch, given a slightly different angle of growth, will clear a future wall.

It leaves in place all the small griefs: accidents on slick stone, winters when heartwater cups fail in certain cracks, the rare lost child who misjudges a mist-breathing fissure and is never found. Without these, their care would become ritual without referent. The power is wise enough to know that a world without all pain is a world without the tight, exact sympathy that the Attenders have developed for this bowl.

---

### How, if at all, they notice

No voice descends. No new star appears. What they register, slowly, is that the basin seems to be *meeting them halfway* more often.

Ice-skins that used to shatter at the slightest mis-score now, if cut on a day when the sky-script is triad-rich, tend to hold. Bone-arrays hum a shade less ferociously even when outside thunder is loud. A pebble, dropped by a clumsy child years after the one you saw, still produces that wrong, thick note—but the faint radial scar it leaves in the rock dissipates more quickly with repeated washing, instead of persisting as a permanent ring of dead flow.

Elders begin telling a slightly new story at Underbright:

Long ago, they say, the bowl was raw, its stone like thin glass. We walked even more softly, and still it broke. But the basin learned—as we did. It learned to bend around our carefulness. The brightness above learned, a little, to show us its moods. The dead learned to hum their advice into our bones.

They frame it not as intervention, but as mutual acclimatization: *we and the place have been teaching each other.* In this, they are not wrong. The power’s changes are not unilateral; they are a response to the culture that has emerged here, a decision to reinforce what they already strive toward.

The Attenders may come, in distant generations, to encode some of this in new knots on bone-strings: a period remembered as “time of the softening under-rock,” tales of an age when elders’ joints first began to feel “deep pulses from before our counting.” These will be told in the same voice as older myths, not as revelation but as one more turn in the long reciprocity between stone, water, light, and those who place their hands among them.

---

### What this means, on the longest curve

From the power’s vantage, the basin was always a kind of attractor: given a certain climate, a certain attitude toward sound, a certain hardness of stone, some people somewhere might discover that living *with* turbulence instead of against it is possible. Here, they have.

By thickening the stone’s tolerance, by making its own sky-language partially legible, by letting rock’s memory bleed gently into elder bone, the power increases the odds that:

- This harmony will persist through storms that would otherwise have shattered it.

- The Attenders’ way of attending will have time not only to endure but to elaborate, to invent finer arts of care, perhaps someday to influence neighboring basins, softening other hollows that do not yet know how to listen.

- The planet itself will carry, in one bright, cold bowl, a seed of a particular style of intelligence: one that takes as its highest skill the ability to feel where not to push.

If, ages hence, ice retreats and the mountains rise further, if the basin dries or is buried, the changed stress-response of its stones and the echoes preserved in their crystal lattice will remain. Somewhere in that geology will be the imprint of a time when rock and mind co-tuned.

The power is vast and might never make itself so intimate with any other place like this again. But for the span during which this hollow glows and drips and hums, it has chosen to help—not by lifting the Attenders out of their constraints, but by making those constraints *kindlier* and more knowable.

It does not rescue them.

It makes it easier for them to continue rescuing *the basin*, which is what they have been doing, tirelessly, all along.
