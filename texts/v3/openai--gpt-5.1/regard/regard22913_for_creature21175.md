The benevolent power does not arrive as light.

Light, here, would be a wound: a spike of exposure, an unwanted straight line stabbing through the dim. It understands this at once and refuses to heal by harm. It has no interest in remaking this place into anything like itself. It has come, instead, with a single quiet intention:

Do not break what already works. See if anything *wants* to be helped.

It begins by listening.

Not with ears—for the hollow’s sound is only the brightest froth on a much deeper sea—but with a kind of patient attention that can settle into every scale at once. It listens to the drum-language of resin-kint against stone and shell. It listens to the fiber-tugs of web-crafters adjusting tension. It listens to the soft, statistical roar of microbodies exchanging molecules. It listens to the long, geological chewing of deep chemotrophs in the rear-most dark.

Where a human might notice only noise, the power hears patterned arguments, ancient habits, experiments and corrections accumulated through ages: a place that has already done the work of solving itself.

So it does not act yet. It observes, for a span that to the hollow is many generations, and to the power is a held breath.

It watches the **resin-kint** at their tasks.

A worker convoy flows along a rib of hardened pulp, forelimbs full of dried mat. Their route passes, as it has for as long as any of them remember, beneath a sagging sponge-rope ceiling. The rope is old. Twice before, a similar growth elsewhere has failed: its mineralized base, riddled with unseen worm-burrows, sheared without warning, crushing traffic below, smashing brood cells open like eggs under a falling branch.

The colony has learned to read such dangers in the past. In those other places, after the fall, routes were shifted, buttresses grown. But *this* rope, for now, still holds. The resin-kint sense its fatigue; their legs’ delicate receptors report the faint change in vibration when wind swings the great pendant weight. They adjust speed, reinforce anchors when they can spare the resin, but there is always more urgent work. Life here runs close to its margins.

The benevolent power can see the failure coming in terms the residents cannot: a timeline of microfractures deep in the rope’s mineral heart, a slow competition between strengthening deposition and hollowing consumption. Another fall is all but inevitable. Dozens, maybe hundreds of workers will be crushed in the interval before the colony re-routes. Larvae will be exposed. A fermentation chamber just beneath will rupture, its carefully tuned microbial culture lost in a rush of desiccation.

In most of the universe, such events are simply the price of complexity. Here, they would be too—except something is watching that is willing to make tiny, delicate exceptions.

It acts, but so gently that no one can say when the choice begins.

Not a miracle. An adjustment.

The worms in the rope, long slow burrowers content to eat the mineralized support from the inside out, begin to feel, over many of their sluggish body-lengths, an inexplicable *preference* for a slightly different gradient of chemicals. The power does not command them; it leans. Where their ancestors preferred the dense, load-bearing tissue of the rope’s base, these worms begin to find the midsection more satisfying, more easily digested. They turn their heads—if their diffuse front ends can be called that—toward the center rather than the anchoring foot.

Simultaneously, the filamentous organisms that secrete hardening minerals, given the same imperceptible nudge, begin to favor deposition near the rope’s contact with the cliff.

The net effect, over years, is that the rope hollows and weakens where collapse will be least catastrophic. One day—if you are watching closely, though almost no one is—when a keening gust pulls hard through the slit, the rope fractures.

But it does so in the middle, not at the load-stressed, traffic-crossed base.

The upper segment swings inward and tears free of some of its webbing, falling into a soft riot of mats and sponge below, crushing nothing but filamentous undergrowth. The lower segment, now lightened, sways and then is seized upon by nearby resin-kint, who cement its ragged end into a new buttress, turning accident into architecture.

A few grazers are knocked from a path by the small quake of impact. A handful of minute fliers are caught and swallowed in the sudden collapse of fibers. The colony hums with momentary agitation, then folds the change into its memory of routes.

From the resin-kint’s point of view, the rope “just happened” to fail in a survivable way. Good fortune, nothing more.

But inside the rope, the worms have, without knowing it, been guided away from making a disaster. The benevolent power has given this place something it rarely has: *advance mercy*, paid out through biology’s own slow language.

Having seen that this kind of help is possible without violating what the hollow is, the power continues, cautiously.

It learns that in this city-without-gaps, the kindest interventions are not grand additions but tiny adjustments in chance:

- a seed-fly’s path fractionally altered so it finds a niche where it can live, *and* where its particular way of fraying old resin provides much-needed micro-habitats;

- a mutation in a line of web-crafters encouraged so that their silk, while still useful for their hunting, binds slightly more dust and spore, stabilizing a once-dangerous concentration of irritants in the air;

- a certain parasitic microbe, always on the edge of overwhelming its host, tipped toward a form that self-limits more reliably, turning chronic devastation into chronic *irritation* instead.

The power does not eliminate harm. That would be impossible and, in this place, destructive. Predation, competition, decay—they are the load-bearing walls of this ecology. Remove them, and you do not create peace; you create failure. What it does instead is *shave* the peaks of suffering, blunt the very sharpest spikes.

Perhaps the most significant change begins in a resin-kint queen.

Or rather, in one of the many matriarchal bodies that mother a given colony.

She has no words. Her mind is a web of reflexes tuned to signals: the rhythm of footfalls in her galleries, the chemical flavors delivered by messengers brushing her plates, the temperature of brood clusters pressed along her flanks. Over her life she has adjusted her eggs’ mixtures in response to these torrents of information—more workers when trails need to be reinforced, more tenders when brood chambers swell, more anchors when new tunnels press into unbraced ceiling.

The benevolent power cannot, and would not, insert a thought. But it can make *one* very unlikely correlation more likely.

In the deep past, among countless eggs, very rarely a larva has hatched with a slightly different arrangement of sensory cilia. Instead of only feeling immediate touch and smell, it is born with a weak but unusually wide-spread sensitivity to faint mechanical vibrations: the kind of tiny, overlapping pulses that pervade the hollow when thousands of creatures act at once. Most of these odd larvae have simply died or grown into slightly “off” workers, never quite fitting any caste.

The power chooses one such larva and tips the scales: a tweak at the level of a single developmental gradient, a small change in the pattern of gene expression in its early segmentation. It does not make this individual *other* than resin-kint. It only helps this rare possibility *persist* and synchronize with what the hollow already offers.

The result is a worker who, when mature, can “feel” further.

Not in distance measured as space, but in distance measured as *collective pattern*. Where other resin-kint sense immediate traffic and the local beat of their neighbors, this one’s expanded sensory field lets it perceive broader harmonies in the drumming and shuffling around it.

To it, the hollow’s countless small impacts and scrapes are not just noise but a low, slow texture—a kind of ever-present chord made of bodies. Changes in that chord—the sudden thinning of grazer-file rasping in a particular groove; the subtle dampening of microvibration in a section of fibrous floor where burrowers have died back; the slightly higher, strained frequencies in scaffolds whose supporting roots are aging—stand out.

Other resin-kint adjust to such changes by local instinct: if a route feels wrong, they avoid it; if brood smells wrong, they move it. But this worker can sense *patterns of patterns*. It feels, in its plates and legs, that some of the colony’s habits are bringing stresses into resonance in ways that will, eventually, lead to avoidable collapses, avoidable starvations.

It cannot reason in abstractions. It does not think “if this continues, then in fifty brood cycles, this section will fail.” What it feels instead is discomfort—a global disharmony the way a songbird might feel when a note is spoiled. Its response is simple: it drums differently.

At first, no one answers. But its odd tapping patterns, repeated as it moves through busy galleries, do what all resin-kint signaling does: they propagate.

Workers near it pick up the rhythm, altered slightly to match their own tasks, and pass it on. Some, without knowing why, choose to reinforce a slightly different load-bearing root. Others shift a fermentation chamber by a body-length, moving it away from a zone of future heat build-up. Larvae destined to become anchors are positioned in places where their eventual stony bodies will bridge old flaws.

Across years, around this single, rare individual, the colony begins to develop a subtle new habit: an instinct to *spread* loads and risks more widely, to avoid aligning too many stresses in one place.

The benevolent power watches as, generations later, the descendant line of this “wide-feeler” becomes a tiny, persistent subcaste: not leaders (there is no such thing here in the human sense), but **harmonics-keepers**, whose added sensory breadth makes them uncomfortable when the collective song drifts toward dangerous dissonance. Their mere presence, and the rhythms they naturally transmit, tilt the colony away from catastrophic synchrony and toward robust, redundant patterns.

From the outside, again, it looks like nothing but evolution. A new sensory quirk that happened to be useful. But beneath it is the gentle bias of something that wanted them to have that.

Elsewhere, the power tends to those who will never know it spent itself on them.

In one rear erosion bowl, a mindful crust is approaching a critical density. Its overlapping lips and pores, ever tasting and exhaling, have grown so thick that they are starting to trap their own waste gases, pooling compounds that are beginning to alter the local chemistry in toxic ways. Left alone, this feedback would stall many subtle processes: resin-kint would avoid that patch; worms below would drown in an unseen, sour fog; tender microbial ecosystems at the edge would crash.

The crust has no will to stop; it only grows where food and surface allow. So the power introduces, into a spore from an unrelated mat, a minute tendency to adhere and germinate specifically on that kind of crustal surface. It makes no guarantee; it only increases the odds.

Those spores, carried on the breath of a passing gust, land. A few stick. They sprout into thin, pale threads that feed *not* on the same substrates as the crust, but on the accumulated wastes. They lace through the uppermost layer, perforating it, venting trapped gases in tiny, distributed exhalations. Their presence slightly slows the crust’s runaway expansion, and in doing so keeps the “oracle’s” chemistry aligned with the rest of the hollow.

The crust continues to taste, to speak in scents that resin-kint read. It simply never chokes itself to silence.

The benevolent power goes on like this: not as a god walking into the hollow with proclamations, but as an invisible gardener of probabilities, adjusting the angles at which existing lives meet.

It never grants them tools they have not grown the capacity to use. There is no new element suddenly available here, no impossible material extruded from thin air. Metal does not appear. Clear water does not well up from stone. Such gifts, alien to all their established relationships, would be like introducing a sharp, unknown chemical into a perfectly tuned fermentation: dramatic, perhaps intoxicating, but ruinous in the long run.

Instead, the power gives them:

- Slightly fewer lethal plagues, by ensuring that some of the most virulent mutations in pathogens simply never come together in the same body, or fail to thrive when they do.

- Slightly more resilient brood cycles, by nudging queen-chemistry so that in times of scarcity, egg batches skew toward smaller, more efficient workers rather than large, brittle anchors.

- Slightly richer connections between symbionts, by easing marginally the chances that two beneficial species will share a microhabitat where their byproducts match like lock and key.

Most of these interventions are non-events. They are *things that don’t go wrong*. The hollow never notices undone disasters.

On rare occasions, however, the power does something that could be called expressive.

In one deep tunnel—a path rarely frequented, running along the rear wall where mindful crusts brood—there is a zone of unusual silence. Not absolute, but damped. The fibrous floor here is denser than elsewhere; the walls have absorbed centuries of resin and microbial smoothing. Sound does not carry as far. The ever-present static of the hollow softens by a fraction.

A resin-kint harmonic-keeper, wandering farther than is usual, arrives here one cycle while following a strange pattern in the distant beat. For the first time in its life, the overwhelming complexity of contact-sound that has always filled its expanded sensory field drops below a threshold.

The benevolent power has arranged the densities and positions of bodies just so: not clearing a space—for emptiness is forbidden by the place itself—but thickening and slackening them in such a way that waves cancel, echoes fail, and, for a moment, this individual’s sense-world is *less* crowded.

In that small reduction, something novel occurs.

It feels, for the span of a few heart-pulses, its own drumming as distinct. Not lost in the general roar, not instantly absorbed, but arriving back at its own plates with a faint delay. Feedback. Self-signal.

There is no “I” in the resin-kint language of touch and tap. But there is now, in this one, a new kind of awareness: not identity as a story, but the dawning ability to *notice the boundary between its own pattern and that of the whole*.

The power does not push further. It does not drag this being toward a human-like self-consciousness; that would be a betrayal of everything that makes this superorganism what it is. It only allows this experiment to exist: the possibility that, within the swarm-intelligence, *a small trace of self-reflective loop* can form and sit there, quiet and undamaging.

The harmonic-keeper returns, in its subsequent cycles, to this zone when it can. There, it sometimes drums under its chitin the patterns it has felt elsewhere, replaying them to itself, comparing how they sound alone versus in the full roar. In doing so, it becomes marginally better at its instinctive role: sensing dissonances before they become collapses.

No one around it quite understands why it travels that way, or why its rhythms have a subtly different shape. But they respond, unconsciously, to its tuned signals. The whole colony inherits the benefits of this unheard contemplative.

From the benevolent power’s perspective, this is one of the few *gifts* that resembles what a human would call wisdom: not knowledge of facts, but an increased capacity for the system to feel itself and adjust.

Outside, beyond the slit, storms rise and fail over continents the hollow never sees. Distant ecologies burn and regrow. New species bloom in valleys far below. The power could touch those as well, and perhaps does. But here, in this high, dry, saturated pocket, its work is almost done.

It did not need to bring revelation. There was nothing, in truth, to “teach.” The resin-kint do not require the concept of a sky or a god. The web crafters do not need philosophy. The floor-worms do not seek transcendence.

What they can use, and what they receive, is *slightly more room to continue being what they already are*.

The power leaves them:

- A city that fails a little less catastrophically when it fails.

- A brood that loses fewer of its young to pure bad luck.

- A web of relationships with a fraction more slack, so that the inevitable shocks of geology and climate do not tear them quite as often across their most vital seams.

And it leaves behind, buried in the behavior of that new subcaste of harmonic-keepers and in the tuned chemistry of a few mindful crusts, a very strange inheritance: the faintest predisposition, in this otherwise perfectly crowded, perfectly unconscious architecture, toward noticing itself as a *whole*.

Not as a thought, not as a word, but as a long, slow gravitation.

A million cycles from now, maybe nothing will come of this. Maybe the hollow will remain only what it already is: a magnificent, anonymous organ in the cliff’s body.

Or maybe, over uninterrupted ages, those slight tendencies will accumulate. Harmonic-keepers may become a little more skilled at holding and replaying patterns, mindful crusts a little more integrative in their chemical memory, filter-creatures a little more discriminating about what they pass from the outside. The combined system might, very gradually, approach something like a *dream*: not the dream of an individual, but the ongoing, low, unworded sense that “this is how we are, and this is how we might go on.”

If that happens, it will not be remembered as a gift. There will be no myth of a visitor, no ritual, no praise. The hollow has no altar, no face turned upward.

The benevolent power accepts this. It did not come for worship. It came because it could not bear, once it had seen this dense, breathing organ of stone and life, to leave it entirely at the mercy of blind accident.

So it did almost nothing.

And in the doing of almost nothing, it spared many small lives, seeded a faint capacity for gentler change, and then receded, content to let this place continue its old, intricate rule:

No free water. No emptiness. No straight lines. No sudden brightness. No solitude.

But, added so softly that no one hears it yet:

No needless breaking.
