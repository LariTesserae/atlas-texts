Preparing to visit this place means preparing to be the wrong kind of thing in the wrong kind of world—and surviving that mismatch without being erased, unstitched, or quietly “averaged out” by the environment.

Think of it as visiting a living, low-energy, geometry-based organism using your own fragile, water-and-oxygen biology. You are not its enemy. You are just…non-native. Your task is to:  

1. Remain physically safe in a space whose geometry does not respect your habits.  
2. Keep your mind from slipping into the same blurred, self-editing state that defines the city.  
3. Avoid destabilizing the local rules that are keeping you allowed here.

I’ll break it down like an actual expedition plan.

---

## 1. Baseline Reality Check: What Kind of World Is This?

You’re entering:

- A **deep chasm-city**, vertical more than horizontal.  
- **Almost no water**—not just dry, but a world where “wet” is not a permitted state.  
- **Thin, metallic air**, quiet to the point where sound feels unwelcome.  
- **Geometry that’s dynamic**: distances are inconsistent but repeatably so; directions of gravity may be locally negotiable.  
- **Matter that resists scrutiny**—surfaces blur when you look too closely, and the city edits itself in small, retroactive ways.

Nothing here suggests it was made for a creature like you. That is your guiding assumption.

So preparation divides into:

- Physiological survival (air, temperature, mechanical support).
- Cognitive stability (staying sane and oriented).
- Interaction protocols (what to touch, what not to touch, when to leave).
- Instrumentation (what you can trust when your senses cannot).

## 2. Physiology and Environment: Staying Alive

### 2.1 Atmosphere and Breathing

The air here is:

- Thin—high-altitude equivalent or worse.  
- Metallic and dry, no humidity.  
- Possibly non-toxic but **energetically wrong** for your lungs.

**Assume: you cannot rely on the local atmosphere to sustain you safely.**

Bring:

- **Full closed life-support**:  
  - Rebreather or compact life-support backpack with at least 48–72 hours of oxygen/air mix.  
  - CO₂ scrubbers with wide safety margins; the calm quiet will make you underestimate time and exertion.
- **Positive-pressure helmet** with:
  - Clear visor that minimizes glare distortion (the local lighting is sourceless and strange).  
  - Internal heads-up display (HUD) that can provide your own notion of “up,” distance, and local time.

Keep your helmet sealed the whole time. Even if the air is technically breathable, breathing it directly means inviting that “boundary-weakening” feel into the inside of your body. You do not want the place learning the shape of your lungs.

### 2.2 Temperature, Moisture, and Skin

The environment is:

- Cool but not freezing.  
- Utterly desiccating—no humidity at all.  
- Filled with subtle, rule-level pressures (the ash-growths, the “cool” that weakens boundaries).

Wear:

- **Layered, flexible environment suit**, not bulky combat armor:
  - Outer layer: dust-shedding, non-reflective (you don’t want to be the only shiny thing in a world that avoids shine).  
  - Inner layer: moisture-retentive, keeps your sweat from rapidly evaporating and freezing your skin from the inside.
- **Full coverage**: no exposed skin. Those ash-like growths don’t sting, but they *disarticulate* you subtly. Do not give them bare contact.
- **Gloves with textured grip** for brittle, porous stone and for surfaces whose friction may change when the city “breathes.”

Bring:

- **Hydration system inside the suit** (bladder or micro-reservoir, with spare water). Your body is anomalously wet here; protect that.

### 2.3 Locomotion and Gravity

Main hazards:

- Sheer drops everywhere; “horizons that fall.”  
- Gravity works but not always in a consistent direction.  
- Distances can’t be trusted by the eye.  
- Surfaces may edit themselves, or reassign what counts as “down.”

Equip yourself with:

- **Climbing-rated harness** integrated into the suit:
  - Multiple anchor points, rated well beyond your body weight.  
  - Slow, auto-locking descent devices and mechanical ascenders.
- **Metastable tether system**:
  - Monofil or braided tether spools you can fire or place into rock.
  - Anchors that use physical grip, not exotic fields. Do not rely on magnetics; this rock doesn’t feel like it cares about metal.
- **Footwear with adaptive grip**:
  - Soft, high-friction soles that can handle crumbly, porous stone.  
  - Edged side for precise placement on narrow ledges.

Movement rules:

- **Three points of contact** at all times when near drops. No “casual” walking near edges.  
- **Clip in whenever possible**—if there’s a rail or structure that can hold an anchor, use it.  
- Treat any “short bridge” or “close ledge” as a lie until verified by instrument (more on that below).

## 3. Instrumentation: Replacing Your Untrustworthy Senses

Your sensory apparatus assumes Euclidean space and stable surfaces. This city does not.

### 3.1 Mapping and Orientation

Do not rely on:

- Your eyes to judge distance.  
- Your vestibular sense to know “up.”  
- Your memory to know if something “was always there”—because here, sometimes it wasn’t.

Bring:

- **Inertial guidance unit** (IMU) / dead-reckoning system:
  - Tracks your own motion in 3D regardless of visual illusion.  
  - Gives consistent coordinates in your *own* frame, so at least you know where you’ve been, even if the city edits around you.
- **Laser rangefinders** and **time-of-flight sensors**:
  - To measure actual distances to key surfaces, independent of visual mis-scaling.  
  - Use them more than your eyes when deciding whether to cross a gap.
- **Simple topological mapping tool**:
  - Not “maps” (they will become wrong as the city edits itself) but a running graph of which nodes (places) are connected to which via traversable paths.  
  - Update continuously and log timestamps; if a route rewrites, you want to know when and where.

On your HUD:

- **Artificial horizon** locked to your starting “down.”  
- **Breadcrumb trail** marking your path in 3D.  
- Marker for the entry point—treat that as sacred.

### 3.2 Reality-Check Tools

Because surfaces blur under scrutiny and distances misbehave, bring small, dumb, stubborn tools:

- **Physical measuring tape**:
  - It will show you when your sense of scale is off, and it doesn’t negotiate with geometry, it just stretches.  
  - Use it occasionally to recalibrate your expectations.
- **Laser pointer / collimated beam**:
  - To see how beams bend or terminate in foggy air or in those condensed strata, revealing invisible planes.
- **Plumb line or small weighted tether**:
  - To see what direction local gravity *actually* chooses at a given spot; compare it to your internal sense and your IMU’s reading.

## 4. Cognitive Safety: Keeping Your Mind Intact

This environment doesn’t attack you psychologically with horror. It does something subtler: it invites you to **forget your reference frame** and accept its fluid rules.

### 4.1 Mental Hazards

- Surfaces resist close examination; focus slides off.  
- Hard to hold in mind how distances actually behaved.  
- The city rewrites itself retroactively—stairs that “have always gone up,” courtyards “that were always there.”  
- The ash-growths and some structures induce a weakening of bodily boundaries; your sense of where your limbs are can smear.

Risk: you become **acclimated** to this style of reality and start doubting your own continuity instead of noticing the world’s shifts.

### 4.2 Protective Practices

Before arrival:

- Set **rigid personal rules** and log them:
  - “My entry orientation is: chasm below, sky above.”  
  - “My starting point is position A; if my map disagrees, trust the recorded coordinates, not my recall.”
- Practice **brief, scheduled self-checks**:
  - Every 10–15 minutes by timer:  
    - State your name, origin world, mission, and time since entry into a recorder.  
    - Confirm landmarks against your topo-log.  
  - Hearing yourself over time forms a separate memory line from the city’s quiet edits.

On site:

- Use an **external timepiece** with strong, regular cues:
  - Audible beep or haptic pulse every X minutes. The city is slow, and time will stretch; maintain a regular tempo.
- Avoid prolonged fixation on:
  - The threshold stones that resist scrutiny.  
  - The ash-growth patterns, especially where circuits or “veins” glimmer.  
  - The drifting luminous life-forms’ rearrangements; they will seduce your sense of shape and normality.

If you notice:

- Difficulty remembering the exact path you just walked—even with your HUD showing it.  
- A sense that your fingers or limbs are not sharply located in space.  
- A pleasant acceptance that “of course this stair always went this way” when your log shows otherwise.

Then:

- Stop moving.  
- Grip a known, anchored object (your own tether, a marked post).  
- Run a full self-check verbally and compare to your last few log entries.  
- If discrepancies increase over two cycles, **begin a structured retreat** toward your entry point.

## 5. Interaction with Life and “Infrastructure”

### 5.1 The Ash-Growths

These are the world’s “plants” or low-level processors:

- Color of old ash, bruised tones.  
- Matte, sheet-like, with occasional inner circuitry-pattern glimmer.  
- Feed by etching stone, smoothing or rewriting mineral under them.  
- Sometimes hang in vertical tongues, curtains over openings.  
- Close proximity makes your skin feel less bounded; linger, and you lose precise proprioception.

Protocols:

- Do not touch them, even with gloves, unless absolutely required.  
- Do not step on them; treat them like active circuitry rather than moss.  
- Do not cut or damage them—this is both ethical and practical; you don’t know what feedback they trigger in the city.

If you must pass close:

- Move steadily, no lingering.  
- Keep at least one physical reference (hand on untouched rock, tether) to ground your sense of body location.  
- If your fingers/body start to feel unfixed, retreat the way you came, slowly.

### 5.2 Drifting Luminous Life-Forms

They:

- Drift in vertical planes, like cuts in the air.  
- Divide and reunite, forming suggestive but never stable shapes.  
- Locally sharpen geometry; distances around them behave more “normal.”

They don’t show overt interest in you. But:

- Do not block or intersect their path if you can help it.  
- Do not shine bright lights directly onto them; you don’t know if light is a control signal.  
- Observe from a distance; they are, in a sense, moving patches of “reality correction.” Being caught between overlapping such fields might tear at your own stable body plan.

### 5.3 Articulated Mineral Creatures

These are the jointed, glass-pipe-like beings:

- Move very slowly along walls, by self-reconfiguring rather than gripping.  
- Where they pass, stone briefly clarifies into crisp facets, then relaxes.

Protocols:

- Give them wide berth. They’re unlikely to chase you; speed is on your side.  
- Do not cross directly over their path if they are on a surface you plan to use. That surface may change its mechanical character as they pass—imagine stepping on a floor just as it transitions from grainy stone to crystal facets and back.
- If one comes near:
  - Pause movement.  
  - Let it pass and wait a few minutes longer for the local stone to “relax” before trusting it with your weight.

## 6. The City as Active Geometry

The city itself is the dominant “being” here. It:

- Has no seams or joints—structures precipitated, not built.  
- Updates itself quietly: stairs changing direction, new courtyards that are “old,” altered chasm configurations.  
- Uses **“dynamic infrastructure”**—reality as piping and wiring.

### 6.1 Reading the City’s Mood

The main large-scale sign is the **void’s breathing**:

- Long, slow oscillations in pressure through the rock.  
- Deep inhalation: sensed as subsurface pull.  
- Exhalation: air moves subtly; light dims; ash-growths flatten.

Interpretation for a human traveler:

- During **exhale**:
  - The system is actively adjusting. The ash-growths freeze: they are listening or syncing.  
  - Avoid crossing delicate structures (bridges, arches) at that time.  
  - Avoid standing in the circular basins or between monoliths—they’re likely to be “live.”
- During **inhalation** or neutral phases:
  - The environment is relatively stable; plan major moves then.

Watch the **sky’s polygonal plates**:

- Slow rearrangement is normal.  
- Rare overlaps that sharpen shadows indicate:
  - A temporary increase in the city’s resolvability: seams, joints, and suspended motes become visible.  
  - Use these moments to:
    - Check for hidden joints in structures you rely on.  
    - Spot suspended “scaffold” points in the air you might otherwise walk through.

Do not linger too long in those moments staring; they are also when the city is most “data-rich” and potentially most able to notice you.

### 6.2 Functional Sites: Channels, Nodes, Basins

- **Channels in stone**:
  - Carry no visible fluid but hum faintly when the chasm breathes.  
  - Treat as conduits—do not block or sit in them.  
  - Avoid placing heavy gear directly on intersections.

- **Ringing nodes / bell-stones**:
  - Rock that “rings” quietly when tapped.  
  - Do not intentionally resonate them at rhythmic intervals; you might be sending a signal in a system you don’t understand.  
  - One test tap on arrival to note their frequency is acceptable for study; repeat only if you absolutely must.

- **Circular basins with standing stones**:
  - Dust spirals in them toward the center, forming precise coils.  
  - Stepping inside alters your sense of weight; distances may dilate or contract.  
  - Treat as **geometry engines** or “edit stations.”
  
Use them very cautiously, if at all:

- Only step in if you have robust tethering and a clear, logged plan.  
- Expect a single step to land you somewhere a different number of strides away than expected.  
- Never enter when the chasm is exhaling or when light has just dramatically rebalanced—those may be editing “windows.”

## 7. Movement Strategy: How to Actually Traverse the City

### 7.1 General Principles

- Travel **slowly and methodically**. This is not a place for speed; haste will outpace your instruments and reality checks.
- Maintain **continuous logging**:
  - Position (IMU), visual capture, and audio notes.
- Move in **short legs**:
  - Go from clearly identifiable node A to node B, verify your path, then proceed.  
  - Avoid long, blind traverses where subtle shifts could accumulate unnoticed.

### 7.2 Dealing with Inconsistent Distances

When you encounter:

- A **bridge that seems longer** than the time it takes to cross:  
  - Mark both ends with physical tags (paint, beacons, or scrap tokens).  
  - Measure actual step count and IMU distance; log the discrepancy.
- A **ledge path that takes longer** than its visible arc:  
  - Likewise, mark points and log.  
  - Recognize that “going around” may be slower than “going across” in more than a navigational sense—space itself is being folded.

Choose paths not by apparent safety but by **predictability over repeated trials**:

- If a bridge yields consistent metrics on three traverses in a row, trust it more than a ledge that never matches its own numbers.

### 7.3 Vertical Navigation

Because the city is wrapped around a chasm:

- Treat **downward exploration** as high-risk:
  - You are entering regions where the breathing void exerts more influence.  
  - The most extreme geometric edits likely occur in the depths.
- Start by exploring:
  - The uppermost terraces.  
  - Stable-looking vertical spans where structures are dense and ash-growth is minimal.
- Set a **hard depth limit** relative to your entry elevation:
  - For your first visit, do not exceed this.  
  - Remember: climbing back in thin air takes more time and energy than descending.

Use **semi-permanent anchors**:

- Leave small, non-intrusive hardware at key waypoints (bolt equivalents or wedged devices in natural cracks).  
- Mark them with numeric or symbolic codes that have meaning only to you.

## 8. Ethics and Impact

You are entering something that is **ongoing**, not ruined. It is not “abandoned” in any useful sense.

Behave under three principles:

1. **Non-interference with processes you do not understand**.  
2. **Non-contamination by your wet, noisy biology**.  
3. **Reversibility of your presence** as much as possible.

Concretely:

- No graffiti, no carving symbols, no leaving trash.  
- Minimize biological shedding:
  - Suit integrity to prevent skin flakes, hairs, microbes from entering the open environment.  
  - If your tech allows it, run mild internal sterilization cycles on suit exhaust.
- Avoid removing samples unless:
  - They are small, and  
  - You can do so without interrupting obvious function (do not scrape ash-growth, do not chip “bell-stones,” do not alter nodes or basins).

## 9. When to Abort and How to Leave

You should treat retreat as success in this kind of expedition.

Immediate withdrawal triggers:

- Sudden, significant change in:
  - The direction of apparent “down” without corresponding shift in your IMU.  
  - The character of light (beyond normal plate movements)—for instance, if the whole chasm’s tone “jumps” as if a filter changed.  
- More than one structure around you quietly “rewrites” in a short timeframe:
  - e.g., two or more stairs/arches that you know were different in your log are now “always like this.”  
  - This suggests a broader phase shift, during which being a non-conforming object might be dangerous.
- Increasing difficulty voicing your self-checks:
  - If simple facts feel irrelevant or hard to say, that’s likely the city’s informational texture seeping into your cognition.

Retreat protocol:

1. Stop all forward exploration.  
2. Pull up your breadcrumb trail and identify last confirmed-stable node (waypoint with matching multiple logs).  
3. Move back toward it using:
   - Tethers.  
   - Pre-placed anchors.  
   - Minimal interaction with new or changed geometry.
4. Once on the uppermost, most “normal-feeling” terraces:
   - Wait through at least one full breathe-cycle of the chasm while stationary, observing.  
   - If conditions remain stable, complete your exit.

On exit:

- Do not look back for “one last image.”  
- Once you cross whatever threshold you used to enter (portal, rift, transit point), treat the world as sealed for the remainder of that journey.  
- Check your logs outside, in a safe reality, not on the lip of the chasm.
