To live here, a body must first give up the idea of wetness. 

There is no blood in this dark, nor any fluid that could freeze, boil, or rot. Whatever walks or clings among these carved tiers is constructed from the same materials as the chamber itself: dense, silica-beaded lignite, smoked parchment, and tendons of dry, woven flax that have forgotten how to stretch with moisture.

They are called—if they are named at all—the *Tuned*. 

They are slender, elongated forms, nine or ten feet tall, built less like men and more like complex stringed instruments stripped of their bellies. Their limbs are long, dry spindles of dark heartwood, multi-jointed and hollowed out along the grain to reduce weight. Where a mammal would have a soft chest of lungs and wet muscle, a Tuned possesses a cavernous, open ribcage lined with micro-fibers of golden, mineralized silk. Suspended in the center of this ribcage is not a heart, but a heavy, teardrop-shaped seed of magnetic iron-stone, hanging by four taut, hair-thin sinews.

They do not breathe. They do not eat flesh, or leaf, or water. 

Instead, they feed directly upon the thunder.

Their metabolism is entirely piezoelectric and acoustic. As the oceanic roar of the earth presses through the dry wood of the vault, it strikes the iron-stone pendulums hanging inside their chests. The stone vibrates; the taut silk sinews churn against small, glass-hard nodules of quartz embedded in their inner joints, producing tiny, cold currents of static electricity that crawl along their fibrous nervous systems. They are powered by the subterranean friction of the continent. A decade of stillness in the tectonic plates would starve them into literal furniture; a sudden, violent earthquake above would fill them with a blinding, euphoric surge of kinetic energy, driving them to leap across the empty void from balcony to balcony like dry leaves caught in a gale.

Because there is no light, they have no eyes. Where a face might be, there is only a smooth, concave plate of polished horn, scored with thousands of microscopic, radial grooves. This plate acts as an acoustic mirror. By tilting their heads toward the walls, they "see" the city around them in high-definition echo: the exact depth of a doorframe, the microscopic fracture forming in an archway three tiers below, the dry drift of dust settling onto a carved staircase.

They move with a strange, double-tempered rhythm. For years at a time, a Tuned may remain motionless in a honeycomb niche, their long fingers hooked into the wall's resonant grooves, indistinguishable from a decorative pillar or a dry root-knot. During these long silences, micro-vines crawl imperceptibly across their knees, binding them back into the architecture.

Then, triggered by a subtle shift in the earth's bass hum—a change in pitch that signals the passage of a distant season miles above—they dislodge themselves with a series of sharp, wood-on-wood clicks.

When they walk, they do not step quietly. They *strike*. 

Their feet are capped with polished obsidian caps that hit the smooth growth-rings of the floor like wooden mallets on a xylophone. Each step sends a crisp, localized pulse of sound traveling through the wood, mapping the path ahead and notifying others of their approach. Two Tuned meeting in a narrow, carved alleyway do not speak with mouths—they have none. Instead, they press the concave plates of their faces together, or touch the tips of their long, hollow fingers to each other’s sternums. Through this physical contact, the vibration of their internal pendulums aligns. They exchange memory, spatial maps, and structural warnings through pure, harmonic resonance. A single contact can transfer the complete acoustic layout of three miles of carved channels in the span of a single vibration.

They are both the citizens of this city and its maintenance system. 

Equipped with small scrapers made of sharp chert and petrified bone, they spend centuries working along the high balconies. They do not build new structures; the city was finished before the upper shale settled. Instead, they *retune* the old ones. As the vast wooden dome slowly settles under the pressure of the earth, the acoustic properties of the streets shift. A street that once echoed at a crisp forty hertz might drop to a dull, mudded murmur as its walls deform.

A Tuned will stand in that street for eighty years, patiently shaving paper-thin ribbons of dry lignite from the walls, quarter-inch by quarter-inch, until the groove once again catches the continental roar and channels it cleanly down the hill. The wood shavings they leave behind do not decay; they collect in the deep cracks between the growth rings like dry, aromatic filings of cinnamon and carbon.

Beside the Tuned, there are smaller things that inhabit the dry crust.

There are *Clappers*—blind, crab-like organisms no larger than a hand, made of hollow, lacquered chitin. They skitter along the sound-tubes, their many jointed legs tapping out a relentless, rhythmic percussion that keeps the long aqueducts free of mineral dust. 

There are *Chime-Swarms*—motes of glass-hard, nitrogen-breathing dust-mites that cluster in the doorways of empty dwellings. When a thermal contraction causes a wall to crack with that sudden, bell-like *BOOM*, the Chime-Swarms are shaken from their perches and drift through the pitch, their tiny, dry bodies colliding by the millions, creating a soft, glittering hiss that sounds like rain falling on dry parchment—the only rain this world will ever know.

And at the very center of the void, floating in the absolute dark where no sound-tube reaches and the echo of the walls cancels itself out into a pocket of perfect, dead silence, hangs the *Root-Mother*. 

She is not a queen in any biological sense, but the oldest piece of wood in the world—a massive, sphere-shaped burl fifty yards across, suspended from the ceiling by three-mile-thick cables of petrified vine. She is entirely hollow. Inside her dry, dark womb, wrapped in layers of silk-fiber, the Tuned are not born, but *carved*. 

When one of the Tuned is finally worn down by eons of vibration—when its joints calcify into solid stone and its internal pendulum fuses to its ribs—it travels down to the lowest tier, steps off the edge of the polished floor, and drops into the dark center. It shatters upon the outer shell of the Root-Mother. 

There, in the quietest place in the crust, the others gather the dry, unbroken heartwood from the fallen, and with flint chisels and endless patience, spend three thousand years carving the next inhabitant from the remains of the last, tuning its hollow chest to the deep, eternal thunder that keeps the dark alive.
