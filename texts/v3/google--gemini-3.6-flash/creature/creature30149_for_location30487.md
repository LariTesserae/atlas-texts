To understand what lives here, one must first understand that life in this vault does not arrive from somewhere else. It is not an immigrant to the infrastructure; it is the final, mobile phase of the extrusion. When the dark biological engine beneath the cobbles pressed out the ironwood gantries and the gourd-boilers, it pressed out the denizens as well, dripping them from the same dark sap-vats, fully formed, wet, and sharp-edged.

They have no childhoods, no memories, and no scars. They are as impossibly new as the street gutters.

### The Pith-Joints (The Maintenance Populace)

The most numerous of the larger inhabitants are the **Pith-Joints**. They are tall, absurdly narrow figures—eight or nine feet high—whose bodies are constructed from the same dense, jet-black ebony-wood as the steam-engine pistons. 

They do not walk so much as ratchet. Their limbs move on slick, self-lubricating ball-and-socket joints made of polished white bone-growth, turning with a muted *clack-click* that mirrors the internal valves of the ironwood pillars. They have no faces; where a head should be, there is a flat, oval spade of translucent amber, tilted slightly upward toward the dark ceilings. Behind this amber plate, a cold blue phosphorescing fluid rises and falls like mercury in a thermometer, signaling their internal pressure to one another across the void.

The Pith-Joints are the city’s finishers and grooms. Because the district was extruded only moments ago, its surfaces are still coated in a thin, cold sheen of embryonic film—a slippery, transparent mucus left behind by the growth-molds. The Pith-Joints move silently down the root-pulp cobbles, using long, chisel-like thumbs to scrape this film away, revealing the crisp, immaculate corners of the curb-stones beneath.

They do not breathe air. Instead, they periodicially dock themselves against the copper-vine junction boxes. A long, flexible tendril unfurls from between their shoulder blades and plugs into the fungal socket with a quiet, wet *plug-hiss*, drawing cold static electricity directly into their heart-nodes. While plugged in, they stand completely motionless for minutes at a time, their amber faces glowing a steady, icy indigo.

### The Shadow-Masons

Near the bases of the giant piers, where the light of the sap-pods fails entirely, live the **Shadow-Masons**. 

Because shadows in this chamber do not behave like light-deprivations—frequently casting as hard, sharp-edged, three-dimensional geometric blocks resting on the floor—the Shadow-Masons treat darkness as a building material. 

These creatures are small, flat, and ribbon-like, resembling multi-legged stingrays made of dry, fibrous slate. They possess no eyes, but their forward margins are lined with thousands of microscopic, cold-sensitive cilia that can feel the precise edge where light ends and a solid shadow begins.

With razor-sharp mandibles formed from petrified thorn-matter, the Masons slice the solid geometric shadows into uniform, lightless bricks. They stack these blocks against the roots of the ironwood gantries, constructing tiny, silent, cubic redoubts. To step inside one of these shadow-huts is to enter a pocket of absolute zero and absolute quiet, a space where sound cannot vibrate through the dense, heavy darkness. 

Here, the Masons sleep in brief, collective clusters, clinging to one another like wet leaves, waiting for the city’s next structural pulse.

### The Fold-Walkers

The spatial glitches of the district—the alleys that loop endlessly, the places where distance compresses—are not empty anomalies; they are occupied by the **Fold-Walkers**.

To look at a Fold-Walker is to experience a sharp, sickening headache. They appear as fragmented, silhouette-like humanoids, but their anatomy is spatially distributed. A Fold-Walker’s left foot may be stepping onto a cobble five paces behind you, while its slender, black-leaf hands are reaching out to inspect a copper vine directly in front of your face. 

They do not navigate space; they inhabit its seams. 

Because the district's geometry folds in on itself, the Fold-Walkers act as living tensioners. They carry heavy, spindle-shaped spools of copper-colored nerve-thread woven from root-fiber. As they step through the looping alleys, they stitch the fractured space together, pinning the impossible geometry to the stable ironwood pillars so that the high-pressure xylem pipes do not snap under the strain of spatial warping.

When two Fold-Walkers meet in a non-Euclidean corner, they do not speak. They press their chests together—a dry, wood-on-wood sound like two hollow barrels colliding—and exchange spatial tension, balancing the distortion between them before ratcheting off in opposite directions, stepping simultaneously toward and away from the center of the vault.

### The Micro-Ecology of the Machines

Inside and around the living machinery, a denser, wilder tier of life operates:

*   **Gear-Grubs:** These are river-stone-sized beetles with translucent carapaces of pale chiton. Inside their bellies, complex, calcified bone-cogs spin in continuous sync. They crawl into the dark recesses of the gourd-engines, inserting their own rotating mouthparts into the engine’s ebony gears. They act as biological clutches, engaging and disengaging the mechanical linkages with sharp, metallic-sounding clicks to regulate the engine's steam-venting cycles.
*   **The Atmospheric Mantas:** Gliding through the upper air—where gravity is indifferent and dew falls sideways—are long, iridescent ribbons three fathoms long. They swim through the cool dark without wings or gas-bladders, riding the cold thermal updrafts created by the thrumming xylem columns. They feed entirely on the scent of crushed ozone and wet iron, filtering the heavy air through glowing gill-slits that leave lingering trails of pale green light suspended in the dark.
*   **Dew-Catchers:** Microscopic, thread-legged amphibians that cluster around the ceiling-vines. They have needle-thin bodies made of clear, jelly-like sap. When a marble-sized droplet of time-dilated dew forms on a leaf, a dozen Dew-Catchers leap into the suspended sphere of water, swimming within it as it drifts horizontally across the empty air, using the floating droplet as both an aquarium and a transport vehicle until it is swallowed by a wall-mounted pitcher plant.

### The Overseer: The Pith-Cradle

Deep in the center of the chamber, suspended twenty paces above the floor in a web of thick, sap-dripping cables, hangs the **Pith-Cradle**. 

It is not a beast, nor a ruler, but a central sensory organ for the entire district. It resembles a massive, upside-down cathedral bell carved from a single, gargantuan walnut shell, polished to a dark, mirrors-smooth sheen.

From its lower rim hang dozens of long, segmented appendages like the legs of a sleeping crab, dangling down into the cool void. These appendages do not touch the floor; instead, they hover millimeters above the cobbles, reading the microscopic vibrations of the city—the *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the water pumps, the *tink-tink* of expanding leaf-plates, the sharp *click* of the Pith-Joints’ heels.

The Pith-Cradle is the origin of the rhythmic pulse that echoes through the cavern. Every few minutes, its dark, walnut-hull body contracts with a deep, wet, pneumatic *whoosh*, squeezing cold, nutrient-rich sap through the overhead cable-web and down into the roots of the world. 

It is the city’s heart, its foreman, and its mother—dreaming in absolute darkness, presiding over a population that has never seen the sun, needs no sun, and is working with quiet, terrifying precision to prepare the district for a purpose that has not yet arrived.
