To survive here, an organism must solve a single, brutal problem: the world has no grip. 

There is no soil to root in, no soft silt to burrow through, and no rough stone for a claw to catch. Every surface is an uninterrupted stretch of cold, vitrified ivory, ground to a mirror-slick polish by the endless transit of heavy brine. To sit still is to be swept away; to move without understanding the water’s shape is to be smashed against a ceramic terrace miles downstream.

The life that occupies this drowned plateau is built entirely of hydrodynamics, suction, and cold-stiffened muscle.

***

### The Anchored: Wedge-Shells and Valve-Plugs

In the hollowed drainage ports, structural conduit mouths, and ancient cable-chases that pit the pale composite floor, the primary anchor-feeders have taken root. 

They are the **Wedge-Shells**—creatures shaped like smooth, teardrop-shaped river stones of dense, calcified enamel. They possess no eyes, no fins, and no soft external limbs. Their undersides are fitted with a broad, muscular ring lined with thousands of microscopic, mucus-secreting papillae. When a Wedge-Shell finds an eroded architectural socket, it backs into the cavity, expands its lower mass to lock itself flush against the vitrified walls, and fires a quick-curing, icy secretion that seals the seam. 

Once secured, the creature is effectively part of the building. The violent, high-velocity currents pass over its hydrodynamic shell without finding a single millimetre of drag. From the narrow tip of its carapace, facing downstream, it unfurls its feeding array: long, iridescent banners of fine, chitinous cilia that trailing out into the shear-flow like living lace. These filaments do not fight the current; they vibrate within it, hummed into a fine blur by the speed of the water, straining out the microscopic flakes of cold organic marine-snow swept down from the upper ocean.

Beside them, occupying the larger circular vents, are the **Valve-Plugs**. These are thick, rubbery organisms shaped like heavy pistons. They do not merely attach to the architecture—they inhabit its internal plumbing. By expanding and contracting their dense, muscular bodies inside the drowned city’s pipe-networks, they control the passage of water through individual conduits, creating micro-pockets of low pressure behind them where their fragile, translucent young can float safely until their shells harden.

***

### The Wall-Skimmers: Velvet Sheets

Where the open plazas sweep across miles of featureless, pale-grey ground, nothing can stand. But things can cling.

Moving across the ivory slides are the **Velvet Sheets**—flat, broad detritivores no thicker than a blade of leather, measuring several feet across. Their upper surfaces are dark, oil-slick black, coated in a lubricating mucus that forces the rushing water to slide over them with zero friction. Their undersides are a continuous, undulating mat of microscopic vacuum-chambers.

The Sheets crawl against the direction of the roaring currents, moving with agonizing slowness across the scoured ceramic. They survive by feeding on the invisible, microscopic lipid films and bacterial smears that freeze onto the pale composite during split-second lulls in the fluid shear. To a predator, a Velvet Sheet is almost impossible to harvest; it has no lip to peel back, no tail to grab, and holds to the bone-smooth floor with a hydraulic bond so intense that to tear it free requires tearing the creature’s tissue apart.

***

### The Slipstream Riders: Glass-Ribbons

In the mid-waters, twenty feet above the terrace floors where the currents shear into turbulent boundary layers, float the **Glass-Ribbons**.

They are long, schooling creatures—eight to twelve feet in length—yet they weigh almost nothing. Their bodies are completely transparent, constructed from a cold, dense gel wrapped around a flexible, needle-thin spine of pliable cartilage. They have no red blood; their fluids are clear, icy, and rich in copper-based compounds that absorb oxygen directly through their slick skin.

The Ribbons are entirely blind. In place of eyes, their flanks are mapped with a continuous, hyper-sensitive array of electro-receptive pits and micro-vibrissae. They do not see the drowned architecture; they *feel* its acoustic and pressure imprint. They know where an ancient pillar stands because the water pressure spikes three yards before impact; they know a massive staircase is dipping beneath them because they feel the fluid beneath their bellies drop into a spinning micro-vortex.

They move in undulating, synchronized ribbons miles long, weaving through the hollow archways and around the thinned sea-stacks like glittering silk scarves lost in a gale. They do not swim against the violent brine-jets; they ride the shear-lines. They throw themselves into the heart of a down-welling, let it shoot them across a submerged plaza at terrifying speed, and then bank sharply into the calm wake-shadow behind a colossal pillar to rest, their bodies vibrating like struck tuning forks in the low, muffled thrum of the deep.

***

### The Apex: Keel-Hounds

Where there are Ribbons, there are the things that eat them.

Cruising the slate-blue twilight of the upper causeways are the **Keel-Hounds**. They are the apex predators of the drowned plateau, built like hydrodynamic torpedoes carved from dense, slate-grey rubber. Their bodies are completely smooth—no scales, no external ears, and no soft fins that could be ripped away by the fluid shear force. Instead, their bodies are defined by sharp, cartilaginous keels running along their spines and bellies, cutting the high-velocity water like blades.

The Keel-Hounds do not chase prey through open water; that would waste vital energy in a world where the water itself is a wall. Instead, they use the architecture as an ambush weapon. 

A Keel-Hound will wedge its muscular bulk into the tight, stagnant pocket behind a rounded column or deep inside an eroded stair-well, remaining perfectly motionlessly while a jet-stream of icy water roars just inches above its snout. Its eyes—large, flat yellow lenses set completely flush into its skull—watch the twilight above for the faint, shimmering glint of a Glass-Ribbon school being tossed about by a violent eddy.

When a school stumbles into the wake, the Hound strikes. It doesn't swim out; it vents a high-pressure jet of water from internal chambers along its gill-slits, blasting itself forward like a hydraulic piston. Its jaws do not snap shut—they slide open on double-hinged bone tracks, sucking in water, fish, and debris all at once, before the creature banks hard, catches the outer edge of a passing current, and lets the water’s momentum fling it smoothly back into the shelter of the next pillar.

***

### The Sound-Makers: Archway Leviathans

Deepest of all, resting in the vast sky-wells where the ancient city plunged into the unseen abyss below, live the largest inhabitants of this sterile world: the **Archway Leviathans**.

They are not active swimmers. They are massive, barrel-shaped organisms—fifty or sixty feet long—with thick, calcified hides that resemble the very vitrified ceramic they rest upon. Eons ago, their ancestors settled into the great circular transit-arches that bridge the gaps between the city’s high terraces. Over generations, their hides grew into the worn, bone-like composite, grafting the living creature directly into the drowned frame of the world.

The Leviathans are living siphon-pipes. The massive, down-welling currents that crash off the upper plazas are funneled directly through their gaping, ringed mouths and forced out through series of internal, ribbed chambers along their flanks. 

They do not need to hunt. The sheer volume of water forced through their bodies every second brings them all the organic detritus and small ribbon-fish they require to sustain their slow, cold metabolisms. But as the millions of tons of icy water tear through their internal chambers, it vibrates massive, leathery diaphragms deep within their chests.

They are the source of the world’s voice. They take the kinetic rage of the plunging ocean and convert it into the deep, rhythmic, subsonic thrum that hums through the bone-pale floors. It is a song of pure hydrodynamics—a deep, resonant organ-note that echoes for miles through the drowned galleries, letting every blind Ribbon, every hunting Hound, and every anchored Shell know the exact speed, depth, and temperature of the water rushing around them.
