To understand what lives here, you must abandon the idea of a body as a container with an inside and an outside. In this place, where the stone is soft-melted and the air is as hot as a lung, life does not pack itself into hides, shells, or membranes. It does not carry its own water; it *is* the water, modified, thickened, and given a slow, stubborn intent.

What lives in the sunken city is a single, vast, distributed hydrology—an ecosystem of living fluids, mineral-eating gels, and atmospheric tides that treat the monumental architecture as both skeleton and shell.

***

### The Film
At the lowest level, covering the broad slabs and the submerged bases of the colonnades, is the **Pore-Skin**. 

It is not merely a biofilm; it is a unified nervous tissue that spans miles. To the touch, it feels like cold fat, though it is as warm as the stone beneath it. It occupies the micro-porosities of the poured rock, extending billions of microscopic, dark-blue fibrils into the stone’s interior to feed on the trace metals and old sea-salts baked into the structure. 

The Skin does not see or hear, but it registers mass and displacement. When a heavy drop of condensation falls from a lintel thirty feet above, the Skin around the impact site ripples with a tiny, sulfurous spark—a momentary cold-blue nerve-discharge that travels six inches across the plaza before dying out. When steam surges from a floor vent, the Skin in that sector darkens, expanding its rubbery surface to drink the heat, swelling until the stone looks as though it is bruised. 

It is the Skin that remembers the shape of the city. If a section of stone sloughs off or erodes, the Skin fills the crack, secreting a dense, silicate-rich gel that hardens over centuries into the same dark, smooth stone as the rest of the plaza. It is maintaining the ruin, keeping the edges soft, keeping the channels open.

***

### The Upward-Tides (*The Veins*)
The water moving *upward* along the fluted obelisks and monoliths is not an optical illusion, nor is it broken gravity. These are **The Seeps**—viscous, colloidal organisms that have no fixed shape, ranging in volume from a spilled goblet to a thousand gallons of living brine.

A Seep moves by capillary hunger. It is denser than the surrounding rainwater, impregnated with complex lipids and heavy organic salts that give it a high surface tension. It clings to the stone like clear, hot mercury, inching upward against gravity by pulling its leading edge into the microscopic grooves of the fluting, then drawing the rest of its body up after itself in a smooth, heavy pulse.

As a Seep climbs, it grazes. It strips the Pore-Skin of its accumulated mineral waste, filtering out sulfur and copper and leaving behind a strip of pristine, black, wet rock that is instantly recolonized by the film behind it. 

When two Seeps meet on the face of an obelisk, they do not fight; they slide into one another with a low, wet *thrum*, exchanging heat and genetic information through chemical waves that crest across their combined surfaces like iridescent oil slick. At the summit of the tower, fully gorged on minerals and atmospheric heat, the merged Seep spreads thin across the crown, evaporates into the black, low-hanging sky, and leaves its heavy salts behind to drip down the inside of the hollow stone like tears.

***

### The Drifters (*The Lenses*)
The spheres of suspended water holding the bruised-purple refractions are the city’s only airborne inhabitants. They are called **Lenses**, though there is no one here to name them.

A Lens is born when a hot steam vent erupts beneath a shallow, mineral-rich pool. A bubble of volatile organic gas—secreted by the Pore-Skin—is coated in a double-layered membrane of water and lipid-rich oil. Instead of bursting, it detaches from the floor and rises on the thermal plumes, held in shape by extreme surface tension and the dense humidity of the air, which prevents evaporation.

They drift through the vast colonnades like blind, bloated eyes, moving at the speed of a slow breath. They are indifferent to the stone, but they are intensely sensitive to heat differentials. If they float near a cooling seam in the rock, they contract; if they drift into a plume of boiling mist, they swell.

The purple light inside them is an internal chemical process—a slow, non-thermal phosphorescence caused by anaerobic microbes that spend their entire life cycles trapped within the sphere’s liquid walls. The microbes feed on the gas inside the bubble; when the gas is exhausted, the membrane's surface tension snaps. The Lens collapses in mid-air, falling as a single, fever-warm splash onto whatever lies beneath, seeding the stone with a new concentration of nutrients.

***

### The Curtains of the Domes
Deep inside the hollow, fluted domes—where the heat is most suffocating and the dark is absolute—hang the largest organisms in the valley: **The Great Draperies**.

They are vast, translucent sheets of living jelly, some fifty feet wide and thin as parchment, anchored to the apex of the ceilings by thick, fibrous tendrils that have rooted directly into the stone. They hang down into the dark like the bells of inverted jellyfish, swayed only by the rising columns of steam from the deep floor vents.

The Draperies are the lungs and heat-engines of the city. They absorb the boiling mist that collects under the domes, filtering out airborne sulfur and atmospheric nitrogen. As they fill with water, they stretch, growing heavy and dark, swinging silently in the gloom. When they reach capacity, they contract with a massive, soundless hydraulic spasm, wringing themselves out. 

This is the source of the heavy, perpetual rain that falls inside the monuments—a rhythmic, warm deluge that washes down the internal staircases and feeds the shallow lakes on the plazas below. 

***

### The Quiet Intelligence
There is no consciousness here that a human mind would recognize as an individual. There are no decisions made, no hunting, no flight, no fear. 

Yet, the city is not dumb. It possesses a slow, thermal intelligence that operates on the timescale of centuries. The network—Skin, Seeps, Lenses, and Draperies—functions as a single, closed thermodynamic loop. 

If a section of the city becomes too cool, the Pore-Skin in that area shrinks, sealing the stone's hairline vents and forcing the hot, underground steam to divert toward other, warmer plazas. If a monolith begins to lean too far under the weight of wet millennia, the Seeps alter their migration routes, congregating on the opposite side to deposit thick layers of mineralized silica that reinforce the base.

They are not keeping the city alive. They *are* the city, adapted so perfectly to its dark, boiling, ironless tomb that the distinction between the poured stone and the things creeping over it has long since dissolved. They sit together in the pitch-black warmth, dripping, swelling, and breathing steam, perfectly content in the silence of a world that needs neither light nor legs.
