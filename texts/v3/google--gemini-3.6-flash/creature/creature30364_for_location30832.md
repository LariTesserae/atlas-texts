To understand what lives here, one must first discard the notion of soft flesh. 

In a world where water boils out of the stone at a hundred degrees and the sun never lets the ground cool, life does not fight the minerals—it incorporates them. The creatures of the plateau are made of the same materials as the landscape: porcelain-white bone, iron oxide crusts, flexible keratin as dense as horn, and blood thick with dissolved copper and zinc. They are silent, slow-blooded, and sun-blind, guided not by sight, but by vibration, thermal gradients, and the metallic taste of the rising steam.

***

### The Grazers of the Sluices

Down on the wet stone, where the boiling brine cascades over the edges of the travertine steps, live the **Silt-Licked**. 

They are the size of sheep, but flat and broad as flagstones, clinging to the polished obsidian with vast, vacuum-like muscular disks on their underbellies. Their upper backs are not skin, but living mineral terraces—stacked, fan-like plates of white calcite that grow continuously, mirroring the natural travertine around them. 

They do not hunt, nor do they run. They move less than a mile in a season, inching along the edges of the hottest channels. With broad, rasping plates made of fused iron tooth-chiseled enamel, they scrape the vivid yellow coats of sulfur and red iron-sludge directly off the metal valves and wet limestone. They drink the scalding brine through lateral pores along their sides, filtering out pure silica and heavy metals, which their bodies pump upward to build their armor. 

When a Silt-Licked dies, it simply stops moving. Within three weeks, the mineral water flowing over its body seals its calcified shell to the terrace floor, turning its corpse into a permanent, hollow basin for the next generation to bathe in.

***

### The Copper-Lace Weaver

High above the pools, in the shadow of the thirty-story copper condensation towers, the lattice frames are alive with movement. 

These are the **Lace-Fingers**—spindly, air-dwelling creatures with limbs like jointed glass needles. Their bodies are light, built from hollow silicate tubes strung together with tough, wet tendons that never dry out in the saturated air. They hang upside down beneath the cast-iron viaducts and copper girders, their long, ribbon-like antennae wafting through the rising plumes of wet steam.

They feed on the atmospheric fallout: the fine, metallic mists that condense in the heat. Their mouths are intricate, spinning organs that harvest droplets of copper sulfate and iron water as they drip from the machinery. From these metals, the Lace-Fingers extrude long, shimmering threads of metallic silk—flexible, rust-proof wires that they weave into intricate, sprawling webs across the gaps between the towers.

These webs do not catch insects; there are none to catch. Instead, the metal webs serve as auxiliary condensation nets. They catch the water vapor, turning it into fat, liquid pearls that stream down the threads into the creatures’ waiting maws. To walk beneath a viaduct is to hear a constant, soft *shhh-shhh-shhh*—the sound of millions of copper threads humming in the updrafts, shedding their water like rain.

***

### The Stewards of the Iron: The Pale Folk

Moving through the vast, silent corridors between the processing plants are the true caretakers of this fused world. They have no name for themselves, for they have no voice; the wet steam swallows high frequencies, and speech is a useless tool in air so dense with vapor.

They are tall—eight or nine feet from hoof to crown—and impossibly thin, looking less like flesh and more like statues carved from wet, boiled ivory. Their skin is smooth, hairless, and oily, repelling the hot, sulfurous water that constantly drips from the sky. They have no eyes; in a world of blinding white light and thick steam, eyes are a vulnerability. Instead, their faces are smooth plates of pale, sensitive cartilage, pitted with heat-seeking pits that allow them to "see" the thermal signatures of the pipes, the pools, and each other as vivid maps of hot and cold.

Their feet are hardened, split hooves of dark obsidian-like keratin, suited for gripping wet, mineral-slick iron gantries without slipping. Their hands are long, with four joints on every finger, tipped with chisel-pointed nails that can clear a clogged valve seat or scrape a calcified hinge clean in seconds.

The Pale Folk do not build new machines—the origin of the great copper towers and riveted pipelines lies buried in a past too distant for memory. Instead, they *maintain*. They exist in a state of quiet, perpetual stewardship over the architecture.

* **Communication:** They speak through the city itself. Standing on the cast-iron gantries, a Steward will strike the steel beams with heavy, iron-ringed knuckles. The vibrations travel through miles of interconnected pipe and bridge, carrying messages—a blocked sluice, a cooling battery vault, a shifting thermal fissure—across leagues of silent stone. A listener miles away feels the message through the soles of their hooves, a rhythmic *thump-tink-thump* vibrating through the floor.
* **Sustenance:** They do not eat plants or flesh. Their bellies are complex, internal chemical stills. They crouch at the lips of the deepest, boiling vents and drink the black, mineral-choked water directly from the earth. Their bodies extract dissolved iron, copper, and salt, passing the purified water out through their skin as a clear, odorless sweat that keeps them from overheating under the midday sun.
* **Life Cycle:** They are long-lived and slow to act. When a Steward grows old, its joints begin to calcify; the mineral water they drink slowly turns their flexor tendons to stone. As they feel the hardening begin, they make their way down into the dark, stone battery vaults beneath the city. There, amidst the low, deep hum of silent electrical charge, they sit down against the basalt pillars, wrap their long limbs around the stone, and allow the mineral waters to wash over them. Within a century, they are part of the vault—a smooth, ivory-colored relief carved into the foundation of the world.

***

### The Deep Core: The Living Batteries

Deep within the circular settling tanks and the stone vaults beneath the battery banks lies the most mysterious lifeform on the plateau: the **Siphonophores of the Core**.

They are not single animals, but massive, colonial organisms that fill the vast, stone-lined vats like living, iridescent oil slick. They appear as thick, translucent mats of pale amber gel, miles wide, floating on the surface of the boiling mineral sludge. 

These entities are the heart of the city’s silent power. By bridging the gap between the boiling, sulfurous water at the bottom of the vats and the cooler condensation dripping from the copper roofs above, they generate vast bio-galvanic currents. Their bodies are laced with microscopic copper filaments—ingested from the water and organized into living electrical grids.

The slow, resonant hum that vibrates through the stone floor—the hum that can be felt in the teeth—does not come from iron engines alone. It is the rhythmic, electrical pulse of these deep mats, drawing thermal energy from the earth's furnace and breathing it out as a continuous, low-voltage tide through the submerged copper conduits.

***

### A World Without Shadow

Here, nothing is soft. Nothing decays. There is no rot, no soil, no smell of wet earth—only the clean, sharp bite of sulfur, hot zinc, and ozone.

When the sun reaches its zenith, burning the sky to a brilliant, terrifying white, the fauna does not retreat. The Silt-Licked shine like polished porcelain in the pools; the Lace-Fingers glisten like wet copper wires in the high beams; and the Pale Folk walk calmly across the iron gantries, their long, pale hands resting on the pulsing pipes, listening to the vast, wet, metallic heart of a world that breathes in steam and exhales stone.
