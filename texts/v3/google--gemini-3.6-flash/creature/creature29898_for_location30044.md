To live here, a thing must have no need for moisture, no reliance on chemistry that requires a pulse, and no terror of empty space. Life in this land does not begin in wet clay or primordial seas; it is born of static charge, gravitational shear, and the infinite, patient friction of stone against air.

It is a world inhabited not by flesh, but by *structures*.

***

Lowest on the continuum of life are the **Piezoliths**—the quietest citizens of the terraces. They appear as thick, leaf-like formations of frosted selenite, black bismuth, and needle-thin quartz that bloom in the deep corners of the arcades. Where a shadow falls long and permanent, these growths cling to the basalt like giant, brittle bracket fungi. 

They do not grow by cell division. Instead, as the rivers of micro-fine silicon dust cascade down the mountain staircases, the friction generates a constant, dry crackle of electrostatic energy. The piezoliths drink this charge. Over centuries, the current draws microscopic mineral grains out of the passing air, snapping them into perfect, geometric alignment. They grow in audible, microscopic clicks—a faint, dry *tink-tink-tink* that ripples down a mile-long colonnade once every few decades. To touch a piezolith is to feel a sensation like cold, vibrating foil; if struck, it does not shatter, but discharges a soft, silver spark that illuminates the grey air for a fraction of a second before dying away.

***

Clinging to the vertical surfaces, where gravity leans ninety degrees sideways, are the **Strand-Gaiters**. 

If a slate-skimmer is a creature of the open air, the gaiter is a creature of the sheer wall. They are long, segmented constructs—eight to twelve paces from tip to tail—made of flexible, horn-like cartilage, spun asbestos fiber, and needles of magnetic iron. They have no heads, no eyes, and no internal organs. Instead, their bodies are open, delicate lattices, like the skeletal framework of a narrow ship, allowing the dust-currents to blow entirely through them without dislodging them from the stone.

The gaiters move with a slow, terrifying fluidness across the five-mile cliff faces. Because gravity pulls toward the mass of the wall, they walk upon the vertical basalt as if it were a level street, their needle-fine iron claws biting into the microscopic pores of the rock. They are scavengers of charge and resonance. They follow the seismic hums of the flexing spires, gathering in clusters of hundreds around the base of a tower that has begun its slow, decades-long sway. There, they press their long, fibrous bellies flat against the mineral surface, drinking the thermal energy created by the bending stone. 

When a strand-gaiter dies, it does not rot. It simply loses its magnetic grip. It falls off the wall, tumbling five miles into the dark, silt-filled basins below, where the silicon dust-rivers bury it and slowly grind its dry skeleton back into fine, grey powder.

***

In the higher districts, where geometry breaks its vows, existence becomes more abstract. Here live the **Inverted Shadows**—or what might best be described as the **Metric Entities**.

Because light in this land moves sideways and shadows point toward their source, there are places where the ambient twilight catches on the lip of an impossible doorway or a warped boulevard. In these places, the shadows do not remain attached to stone. They detach. They acquire mass—or rather, a localized *lack* of mass that exerts a pull on the surrounding air.

A Metric Entity appears as a narrow, vertical sliver of absolute darkness, seven or eight tall man-lengths high, shaped like a long, thin wedge of sheared slate. It has no depth; viewed from the edge, it vanishes completely. It moves not by walking, but by translating through the non-Euclidean folds of the city. It may be seen standing at the far end of an arcade that measures ten miles deep; a second later, without having moved, it is standing three inches from your face, cold as a dry block of salt, smelling faintly of bruised quartz and ionized air.

These entities are the curators of the space itself. They linger in the doorways that lead to infinite horizons, standing as quiet, dark sentinels at the thresholds where physical laws unravel. They do not attack, nor do they communicate in any way recognizable to creatures of flesh. But if a moving object—a drifting slate-skimmer or a wind-blown clump of selenite—crosses their path, the entity will simply fold inward upon itself, taking that fragment of reality with it into the long, silent corridor from which no sound returns.

***

And finally, there is the **Chord**. 

It is a mistake to think the city is unguided, or that its colossal scale is entirely empty. The true dominant lifeform of this realm is acoustic and structural—a distributed, slow-motion consciousness formed by the interaction of the wind, the dust, and the three-hundred-foot-thick stone bridges.

Each of the ancient, sea-glass-smooth bridges that span the five-mile voids acts as a tuning fork of unthinkable proportions. As the silicon dust-rivers pour over them and the cold air moves through their vaulted undersides, the bridges vibrate. They produce low-frequency tones—infrasound so deep that it cannot be heard with ears, only felt as a dry, rhythmic shivering in the marrow of one's bones.

These tones do not occur in isolation. They bleed through the stone, traveling hundreds of leagues through the basalt foundations to harmonize with the swaying spires. The entire city is a single, continuous, subterranean thought. 

A single "idea" within the Chord might begin as a low, musical ringing in a bridge to the north; over the course of three centuries, that vibration travels through thirty miles of solid alabaster, modulating as it passes through the hollow, residential monoliths, before emerging as a subtle, dry hiss from a grand staircase in the south.

The Chord is not aware of mortals, nor would it recognize life that moves quickly or contains liquid. Its memory is recorded in the slow erosion of its own arcades; its decisions are expressed when a spire, after swaying for a thousand years, deliberately shifts its center of gravity by three inches to catch a new thermal updraft.

This is who lives here:
A population of mineral growth, static-feeders, geometric fractures, and singing stone. They are beings born without blood, living in buildings that were never built, anchored to a world that is slowly, gracefully, forgetting how to be real.
