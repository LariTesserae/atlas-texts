To live here is to abandon the idea of soft landing. Every claw, tooth, membrane, and human hand in this sky-bound scaffold is designed for a single imperative: **grip.**

The life that clings to the iron-bark does not burrow into soft earth or swim through water; it wedges itself into fractures, hangs over miles of dark air, and moves with the brutal, violent rhythm of a world that is constantly snapping itself apart.

***

### The Scuttlers of the Furrows

Deep in the bruised-copper veins of the colossal trunks, where the purple lichens cast their faint, cold glare, live the **Shatter-Claws**. 

They are the size of draft horses, but flattened—broad, low-slung, and six-legged, built like heavy iron wedges. Their outer carapaces are not chitins of insectoid gloss, but thick plates of mineralized bone and calcified wood-pulp, grey and craggy as the bark itself. They possess no eyes; in a world of dark acoustic thunder, eyes are a liability. Instead, their faces are blunt spades of fused bone, crowned with thousands of microscopic, velvet-soft cilia that read the micro-vibrations of the shifting wood.

The Shatter-Claws are the maintenance engine of the lattice. They feed on the fresh amber resin that bleeds from the fractures. Their mouths are terrible, hydraulically driven shears that can crunch through frozen, glass-sharp sap and iron-hard timber fiber. As they feed, they leave behind deep, clean channels—smoothing the jagged edges of structural tears. 

Where a Shatter-Claw crawls, it leaves a trail of thick, foul-smelling greasy musk that never freezes. This grease is the gold of the high canopy; it is the only substance that keeps metal-hard timber from seizing up, and the only thing that keeps other creatures from freezing solid when the sub-zero rime-fronts roll through.

Beneath the Shatter-Claws, smaller things thrive: **Rime-fleas** as large as a man's fist, possessing crystalline carapaces that trap ambient bioluminescence, glowing like tiny drift-lanterns as they scrape the frozen yeast-blooms from the undersides of the arches. They move in stuttering, synchronized jerks, thousands of them at once, turning a dark two-mile bough into a flickering street of cold purple light.

***

### The Void-Gliders and the Sky-Drifters

If the crawlers belong to the bark, the **White Gliders**—known by the human inhabitants as *Gale-Hounds*—belong entirely to the emptiness between.

They are long, lean creatures, sixteen feet from snout to tail, encased in dense, oily white fur that sheds ice instantly. Their skeletons are not made of solid bone, but of a dense, honeycombed cartilage that can flex without breaking under extreme impact. Between their elongated limbs stretch leathery, black membranes—thick as sails, studded with flexible keratin ribs that they can snap tight to catch the rising thermal gales.

A Gale-Hound does not fly; it launches. It hooks its massive, backward-curved rear talons into a ridge, compresses its coiled body, and throws itself into the black void. 

Across three miles of sub-zero abyss, it falls, its sail-skin screaming as the air cuts through its webbing. It targets the Shatter-Claws or the human settlements on distant limbs, navigating by the low-frequency hum of the wind through the hollow boughs. 

When a Gale-Hound hits a distant trunk, the sound is like a ship slamming into a reef—a heavy, wet *thud* that rattles the teeth of anything nearby. Their front limbs are equipped with massive, pneumatic shoulder-joints and rubbery, suction-padded palms that absorb thousands of foot-pounds of kinetic force in a fraction of a second. They do not slide; they *bind* to the wood on contact, instantly digging their chest-spurs deep into the fibers.

Higher up, in the dark indigo sky where the air is thinnest, move the **Drift-Pelts**. 

These are the leviathans of the upper lattice—vast, slow-moving creatures sixty yards long, looking like a cross between a sperm whale and a vaulted seed-pod. They have no legs. Their bodies are enveloped in thick, shaggy mats of pale grey hair that trail behind them like frozen smoke. They drift on massive, hot-air sacs powered by their own intense, furnace-like metabolisms, feeding on suspended ice-spores, lichen dust, and the stray seed-fluff cast off by the bursting pods. They never touch the wood. To touch the iron-bark is, for a Drift-Pelt, to be torn apart by the gale or crushed by a shifting arch. They live and die in the pure, freezing air, their low, whale-like calls blending with the organ-pipe roar of the hollow branches.

***

### The Knot-Folk: Humanity at the Edge

And then there are the humans—or what remains of them after generations spent clinging to the dark, roaring ribs of the world. They call themselves the **Knot-Folk**.

Physically, they have changed. They are squat and broad-chested, their ribs flaring out to accommodate lungs twice the volume of a lowland human's, built to wring oxygen from the thin, metallic air. Their skin is weathered to the color of wet timber, scarred by ice-burns and grease-stains. Their fingers are disproportionately long and thick, the skin of their palms so heavily calloused that they can grasp frozen resin glass without cutting themselves.

They do not wear woven cloth; there is no flax, no wool. They wear layered suits of cured Gale-Hound skin, stitched together with dried crawler-sinew and sealed with Shatter-Claw grease. Their boots have no soles; instead, the toes are open, capped with hooked bone spurs carved from glider-ribs, allowing them to cling to bark furrows with their feet as easily as with their hands.

#### **Life in the Lee-Alcoves**
The Knot-Folk live in the deep, sheltered scars where massive branches have snapped off, leaving hollow, sheltered pockets on the lee side of the wind. 

*   **Shelter:** Their homes are tight, hide-covered domes lashed directly into fire-hardened wooden pegs driven deep into the bark. Nothing is unanchored. A dropped bowl does not fall to the floor; it falls two thousand meters into the lower gloom, lost forever. Every cup, tool, and sleeping mat is tethered by braided vine-cord to the structural timber of the alcove.
*   **Fire:** Heat is a sacred, dangerous thing. Wood does not burn easily here—it is too dense, cold, and saturated with cold resin. The Knot-Folk harvest dry resin-knots from ancient fractures, chipping them away with stone mallets. They burn these chips in deep, enclosed bone-stoves carved from the skulls of dead Drift-Pelts that have collapsed onto the arches. The fires do not leap; they smolder with a dark, oily, violet-red flame, emitting a thick, pine-scented smoke that the people use to preserve meat and warm their sleeping nooks.
*   **Food and Water:** Water is gathered as ice, scraped from the windward glare-sheets with bone adzes and melted over resin-stoves. Food is brutal and carnivorous. They trap Shatter-Claws by dangling fat-baited bone hooks into the deep furrows, or hunt Gale-Hounds on the wing using heavy, spring-loaded ballistas made from bent iron-wood saplings and braided tendon. They waste nothing. The fat is fuel and insulation; the blood is drunk hot or frozen into dense, iron-rich marrow-cakes; the bones become chisels, needles, and bridge-anchors.

#### **Language and Belief**
In a world that roars like a dying cathedral, speech is useless across distances. The Knot-Folk have two languages:

1.  **The Sign-Touch:** A rapid, complex dialect of hand-gestures and body positioning used when close together or working on the open spans.
2.  **The Wood-Tap:** This is their true long-distance language. Using heavy bone hammers, they strike the iron-bark directly. The dense, resonant fiber carries the sound for miles through the solid structure, far clearer than a shout through the air. A master Tapper can send news of an incoming rime-front, a breach in a bridge, or the location of a downed Drift-Pelt across twenty miles of lattice in a few dozen rhythmic, metallic strikes.

They do not worship gods of dirt or sky. They worship **The Tension**. 

To the Knot-Folk, the world is a great bow that is always being drawn. Gravity is not a force pull down, but a pull *between*—the struggle of the titan-trees to hold the void together. Their priests are **Stress-Listeners**, blind elders who spend days with their ear-horns pressed to the raw copper rings of broken trunks, listening to the microscopic creaks and grain-slips within the wood. They can predict a structural snap hours before it happens, telling a village when it is time to abandon an alcove, sever their root-vine bridges, and migrate to an arch that is not yet ready to die.

***

### The Unseen Leviathan

And deep down—far below the human alcoves, beneath the hunting grounds of the gliders, down where the bruised-purple bioluminescence fades into absolute, impenetrable black—lives the thing that owns the void.

It is known only by its effect: **The Frost-Crawl**.

Every few days, deep in the two-thousand-meter gloom below, something moves that is large enough to shift the foundational arches of the world. It does not climb the bark. It spans the distances between the lowest trunks, a creature of dense, midnight-black fur and hundreds of long, jointed tendrils made of cold, calcified cartilage. 

When it moves, it does not make a sound of its own. Instead, its vast weight bends the lower mountain-ridges of wood, forcing the entire sky-lattice to flex. It is the cause of the sudden, cannon-shot cracks that rattle the teeth of the Knot-Folk high above. It feeds on the roots of the world—the massive, seed-burst anchors that reach down into the darkness, seeking what lies at the bottom.

No human has ever seen its face and returned up the ropes. They only know it is there because, when the wind dies down to a whisper, you can feel a warm, wet draft rising from the deep air—the slow, rhythmic exhalation of a breath that takes twenty minutes to fill its lungs, carrying up from the black the smell of crushed resin, old frost, and living copper.
