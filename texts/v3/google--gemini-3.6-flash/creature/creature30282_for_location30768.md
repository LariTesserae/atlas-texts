In this dry, light-flooded basin, life has shed every memory of carbon and water. What swarms here is an ecosystem forged entirely of animate mineralogy—a closed-loop taxonomy of kinetic, dry-metabolism organisms whose bodies are constructed from the very iron, copper, and quartz they inhabit. 

They do not bleed. They do not breathe. If you were to crack one open, there would be no soft tissue or wet organs inside, only a tightly packed assembly of micro-laminated mica plates, quartz-crystal ganglia, and tiny, braided tendons made of fibrous iron. When they die, they do not rot; they are simply pulverized back into the dark sand by the legs of their siblings, their bodies reabsorbed as raw material.

At the base of this churning hierarchy are the **Scrapers**—the walnut-sized hoarders of the turning shaft. Their carapaces are dome-shaped, thick, and polished to a mirror-like hematite sheen, seamless as ball bearings save for the articulated skirts of spiny limbs beneath. They possess no eyes, relying instead on piezoelectric sensors along their undersides that read the micro-vibrations of the rock and the magnetic pulse of the copper rod. They crowd the rotating metal thirty-deep, their mouths—dual, counter-rotating rasps tipped with diamond grit—stripping tiny flakes of copper and iron dust. They feed on the friction itself, converting mechanical torque and static potential directly into the kinetic spark that drives their joints.

Woven through the scrapers are the **Needle-Forms**, delicate, long-bodied creatures no thicker than a brass wire, with dozens of hair-thin legs that move in a hypnotic, undulating wave. They are the acrobats of the machinery, occupying the high-friction zones that would crush broader bodies. They infest the brass relays, waiting for the contacts to snap shut. In that microsecond of contact, a Needle-Form will bridge the gap, drinking the tiny spark of static electricity that arcs across the gap, its thin brass plating flaring with a momentary, brilliant violet glow before it darts away, hyper-charged and frantic.

Cruising the upper layers of this living carpet are the **Scissor-Palps**, the apex predators of the pocket. Ranging from the size of a fist to long, flat slabs of segmented iron, these hunters move with a terrifying, jerky speed. They do not hunt by sight or scent, but by electromagnetic signature: a Scraper engorged on static charge glows like a beacon to them. A Scissor-Palp strikes with lightning violence, using two massive, offset mandibles that act like hydraulic metal-shears. The sound of their hunt is a sharp, metallic *snip-crag*, followed immediately by the dry, ringing pop of a carinated shell imploding. The predator does not swallow flesh; it uses tiny, high-speed drill-legs to reduce the victim’s internal quartz-and-iron lattice to fine flour, absorbing the stored electrical potential directly into its own battery-like thoracic plates.

Deep beneath this surface frenzy, buried in the graphite-dark quartz dust at the very bottom of the basin, live the **Plates**. These are broad, spade-headed burrowers shaped like heavy iron shingles. They rarely emerge into the stark light, spending their lives churning the lower depths. They are the recyclers, inhaling the pulverized remains of dead shells, quartz dust, and iron filings. Within their high-pressure, grinding gut-chambers, they compress these powders, extruding smooth, dense beads of magnetic ironstone that form the bedrock upon which the rest of the swarm crawls. When the pneumatic piston strikes its heavy *thump-hiss*, the seismic shock causes thousands of Plates to shift in unison, sending subterranean ripples through the sand that toss the smaller creatures above like foam on a dark sea.

Reproduction here is not a matter of eggs or birth, but of accretion and static assembly. In the quiet, highly charged micro-caves beneath the engine’s housing, microscopic mineral particles align along magnetic field lines generated by the copper coils. Over months, guided by the relentless pulse of the 30-RPM rod, these particles cold-weld themselves into tiny, jointed seeds—pre-articulated frames of iron and brass. When a sudden arc of discharge hits a mature seed, it twitches, snaps free from the wall, and tumbles directly into the boiling mass to begin scraping.

There is no fear in this pocket, no pain, and no rest. The creatures do not sleep, for to stop moving is to lose static charge, to cool down, and to seize into inert metal. The engine is their sun, their heart, and their progenitor. It feeds them motion, and in return, the swarm keeps the engine immaculate—scraping away scale, clearing rust, consuming stray grit, and sacrificing thousands of their own bodies to the gears every day, ensuring the relentless, ancient mechanism never jams, never slows, and never dies.
