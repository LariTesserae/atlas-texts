In the Low Basin of Vael-Trellis, life does not cling to the margins of the machine; it is the machine’s nervous system, its maintenance scab, its churning digestive tract. Nothing here is soft in the terrestrial sense. The fauna is forged from the same periodic table as the floor beneath it—rich in iron, silica, and dense structural polymers, powered not by the gentle combustion of sugars, but by thermal gradients, piezo-electric friction, and the relentless chemical soup of the basin’s runoff.

### The Floor-Rattlers and Scrapers
On the vast, sweeping terraces, the noise of movement is buried inside the acoustic roar of the water, but up close, it is a localized, mechanical frenzy. 

These are the **Solder-Shells**, the broad, flat-bodied grazers that blanketing the smart-stone in their millions. Ranging from the size of a hand-blade to the width of a shield, they resemble trilobites cast in dark bronze and dull pewter. They have no eyes—photons are useless in the perpetual, churning mist—but their carapaces are laced with crystalline sensory ridges that register the microscopic vibration of every falling drop and every shifting archway.

Their undersides are a terrifying marvel of adaptation: a ring of hydraulic muscular pads that create vacuum-seals against the slick alloy, interspaced with magnetic cilia that lock them to the metal floor even when a wall-sized surge of warm, pressurized fluid rolls over them. They move in stuttering, ratcheting surges. Their mouthparts are rotary rasps tipped with lab-grown diamond grit, constantly grinding away at the thick, black mats of iron-oxidizing fungi. 

As they scrape, they emit a high-frequency acoustic chatter—a screeching, metallic choir that rises and falls in wave patterns across the basin. They aren't singing; they are clearing their palps of iron filings and venting excess steam through tiny thoracic valves. To stand among them is to stand in a hail of micro-sparks and wet grit.

### The Conduit Parasites
Clinging to the undersides of the high-velocity pressure pipes and suspended habitat spheres are the **Valve-Drillers**—the pale, crustacean-like creatures, long as horses, that look as though they were sculpted from wet lard and milky quartz. 

Their bodies are soft compared to the grazers, protected only by a thick, translucent rind of self-sealing lipids that keeps the scalding, ozone-laced condensate from burning their flesh. They are blind, sluggish, and intensely specialized. Their front limbs are not claws, but hollow, needle-like spears made of bio-grown tungsten. 

They locate microscopic stress fractures in the city’s plumbing by listening to the high-frequency whistle of escaping steam. When a leak is found, a Valve-Driller drives its spear into the composite, tapping directly into the pressurized artery. They do not drain the pipe dry—that would invite destruction from the architecture itself—but feed on the rich stream of dissolved heavy metals and thermal energy, acting as living pressure-relief valves. When a pipe surges dangerously, a cluster of a hundred Valve-Drillers will bore in simultaneously, venting plumes of golden mist into the air and preventing structural rupture. Their bellies glow with a faint, bioluminescent amber as the superheated fluid sloshes through their transparent digestive tracts.

### The Thermal Cloud-Swarm
Above the terraced floors, where the air is a turbulent, sun-baked column of rising steam, the sky belongs to the **Mica-Flocks**. 

These are not birds, but elongated, segmented flyers built like armored ribbons. Their wings are thin, rigid plates of biogenic mica, hinged on complex, triple-jointed shoulder girdles made of flexible carbon-composite. They do not flap their wings so much as vibrate them at resonance frequencies, creating a sound like a thousand iron shingles sliding down a slate roof.

The Mica-Flocks never land on the ground; the grazers would scrape their delicate wing-joints to powder. Instead, they live entirely on the wing, soaring up the thermal chimneys rising from the city’s heat exchangers and diving down through the golden mist columns. They feed on the wing, opening wide, scoop-like maws lined with electrostatic filaments to filter suspended fungal spores, liquid oil droplets, and the tiny, floating larvae of the Valve-Drillers out of the air.

When they mate, they entwine in mid-air, falling thousands of feet through the mist in brilliant, flashing spirals of mirrored mica before pulling up bare meters above the thunderous drainage gutters, dropping their heavy, iron-hulled eggs directly into the raging current.

### The Deep Resonators
And then, moving slowly through the architectural churn—so vast and deliberate that a casual observer might mistake them for shifting buttresses—are the true lords of the basin: the **Strut-Mappers**.

They are limbless, mountain-gray behemoths, thirty meters long, shaped like flattened obelisks wrapped in thick, oily blubber and articulated plates of heavy composite armor. They possess no eyes, no wings, and no mouths in the traditional sense. They move along the major conduits using broad, crawling treads made of densely packed, self-repairing muscle-fibers that can secrete an adhesive resin strong enough to anchor them against the force of a falling river.

The Strut-Mappers are the sapient, or semi-sapient, conductors of this kinetic ecology. They do not eat the fungus or tap the pipes; they tap the *physics*. 

Along their flanks are deep, cavernous resonance chambers. By drawing in pressurized water and venting it through internal acoustic chambers, a Strut-Mapper can generate profound, subsonic frequencies—notes so deep they vibrate the teeth out of a man's jaw from a mile away. 

They use these sounds to speak directly to the smart-stone.

When a Strut-Mapper rests its massive, armored brow against a shifting archway and unleashes its subsonic hum, the nano-matrix inside the architectural alloy responds. The stone softens; the flow of the cold tar accelerates; a conduit re-aligns its curve to better handle a coming flood. The Strut-Mappers are the gardeners of a city that grows itself, guiding its morphing geometry with song, ensuring that the water keeps flowing, the heat keeps venting, and the vast, deafening machine of Vael-Trellis never starves, never cools, and never falls silent.
