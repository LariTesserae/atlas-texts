The life on this shelf does not belong to the slow geology of soil and seed. It is a biological spray, thrown off by the collision of hyper-dense technology and virgin stone. Everything living here is crisp-edged, high-pressure, and translucent, built to exist in a world where water is a ceiling, stone is a mirror, and the air smells forever of wet wool and heated copper.

***

### The Glass-Skimmers
The frantic, clicking hexapods that swarm the obsidian slabs are less like insects and more like living crystal syringes. Their bodies are fused silicates—hollow, six-sided carapaces of pale greenish glass, through which a lavender fluid can be seen pulsing under immense internal pressure. They possess no eyes; instead, their needle-thin legs are tipped with delicate, oil-coated sensory hairs that read the vibrations of the smooth stone and the exact tension of the water sheet.

They move in sharp, angular zigzags, never crossing the water’s surface but standing atop it. The surface tension beneath their feet dimples into tiny, lens-like shadows that slide across the black rock below. 

Their entire existence is tethered to the violet-glowing circuits bleeding from the dark rods. They gather around these thermal fractures in dense, clicking rings. Lowering hollow, needle-like mouthparts through the water sheet, they pierce the thin film of ionized mist clinging to the hot stone, drinking the charged mineral salts directly. 

Because their bodies are brittle and pressurized, they do not age into decay. When a skimmer’s internal fluid loses its electrical charge, the tension holding its glass carapace together collapses. The shell shatters with a quiet, musical *ping*, and within seconds, the caustic, warm sheet-water dissolves the shards back into clear, liquid silica, leaving not a trace of the body behind.

### The Drift-Discs
Above the skimmers, occupying the stagnant, lavender-tinged humidity, are the saucer-shaped floaters. They are soft, milky, and entirely boneless—living bladders the thickness of heavy canvas, shaped like broad, inverted bowls. 

Their upper mantles are filled with superheated gas—a mixture of steam and methane harvested directly from the hissed emissions of the rock-pores. A single, dark, plum-colored mass sits at the center of their translucent bodies: a primitive stomach and heart surrounded by a web of silver nerve-fibers. 

The floaters move with slow, rhythmic contractions—*whuff, whuff, whuff*—forcing warm air through a ring of muscular muscular vents around their rim. They have no eyes or ears, but they are exquisitely sensitive to static fields. As they drift through the thick sky, they trail long, sticky strings of clear, bio-electric mucus behind them. 

These strings sweep the air for the midges—tiny, short-lived sparks of bioluminescent tissue that burst spontaneously from the warm pools like tiny bubbles of light. When a disc passes over a pool, its trailing threads catch dozens of midges at a time; the static charge running down the thread instantly liquefies the prey, drawing the nutrient-rich sludge up into the central plum-stomach.

Their only hazard is the spinning torus. The spinning ring’s gravitational shear produces sudden, localized sparks of static discharge. When a floater drifts too close, the air snaps, blue light arcs through its translucent mantle, and the heat causes the gas inside to expand violently. The floater twitches in a spastic, erratic arc, tumbling through the mist until its internal temperature stabilizes and it can resume its blind, rhythmic *whuffing*.

### The Thrum-Stalks
Glued directly to the dark, seamless housing of the spinning torus are the shelf’s only plant-analogs. They have no roots, for there is no soil to penetrate, and no leaves, for there is no sunlight to catch. Instead, they are pale, rubbery tubes—like translucent white fingers—attached to the vibrating metal by broad, suction-like discs.

These stalks live on motion itself. They use the constant, tooth-vibrating hum of the spinning torus to drive their internal hydraulics. As the torus spins, the kinetic energy pulses through the stalk’s body, forcing mineral-laden fluid up and down its length. 

At the tip of each stalk is a feather-like crown of stiff, glass-bristle cilia. These crowns fan back and forth in the thick air, catching the airborne quartz dust thrown off by the steam jets. The stalks do not photosynthesize; they chew stone dust using kinetic vibration, slowly growing longer and thicker as they encrust the warm, alien engine in a pale, trembling beard of living fiber.

### The Flat-Lies (The Undermelt)
Invisible at a distance, living entirely beneath the four-millimeter film of running water, are the flat-lies. They are organisms with no height at all—sheets of living, rubbery membrane no thicker than a film of oil, clinging to the vitrified tar-rock like wet cellophane.

The flat-lies are the clean-up mechanism of the terrace. They are perfectly clear, visible only as subtle, shimmering ripples on the black stone surface. They lie stationary across the fractures, anchored against the slow, continuous glide of the water sheet. 

When a glass-skimmer shatters, or when a floater loses gas and alights upon the wet rock, the flat-lies slide over the remains. They secrete a concentrated, caustic enzyme that breaks down organic tissue and silica instantly, reducing it to liquid nutrients. They absorb what they need through their broad, flat bellies and pass the rest into the water sheet to be washed over the precipice. 

They are the reason the terrace remains immaculate. Nothing accumulates on the black glass; nothing stains the sharp, mathematical angles.

### The Rim-Climbers
Occasionally—perhaps once every few hours—a sound disrupts the rhythmic *click-clack* and *hiss* of the shelf. A thick, wet tearing sound arises from the outer edge, where the water sheets off into the lower grey soup.

Something comes up from below. 

It has no permanent name, for it belongs to the sea of clouds beneath the shelf, but here it is a rare visitor. It is a large, low-slung creature shaped like a wet leather shield, three meters wide, with dozens of short, jointed limbs terminating in vacuum-pads. It pulls itself up over the razor-sharp outer rim, completely unbothered by the vitrified edge that would cut softer meat to ribbons.

It does not hunt the skimmers, nor does it disturb the floaters. It creeps slowly across the smooth slabs, dragging its heavy, dark mass against the current of the water sheet, heading straight for the cluster of glowing violet rods. 

Once there, it flattens itself directly over the glowing circuits, draping its cold, leathery skin across the hot thermal bleed. It lies still for long stretches, shuddering violently as it drinks the raw electrical and thermal output of the tech-nodes. Its dark hide turns a mottled, bruised violet as it charges. 

When it is full, it crawls back to the edge, slides its heavy body over the smooth rim, and drops back down into the dim, grey lower atmosphere, leaving behind only a temporary gap in the water sheet—a dry footprint on the un-eroded stone that disappears in seconds as the water heals over itself again.
