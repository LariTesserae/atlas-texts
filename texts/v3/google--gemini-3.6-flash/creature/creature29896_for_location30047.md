Here, life has abandoned all pretense of liquid. There is no lymph, no sap, no blood; to bring a wet cell into this vault would be to destroy it, to watch it instantly hiss, shrivel, and flash-bake into a smudge of carbon on the hot quartz floor.

The inhabitants of the vault are built entirely from the elements that survive the dry heat: nickel, iron oxide, raw zinc, calcified silicates, and dry structural chitin so dense it rings like bell metal when struck.

To look closely at the walls is to realize that what appears to be bare basalt is actually covered in a living, shimmering pavement. 

### The Carapace-Plates

The dominant species—if "species" is the right word for organisms so thoroughly integrated with the machinery—are the **Lichen-Tiles**, or *Anode-Carapaces*. 

They are flat, hexagonal plates the size of an outstretched palm, overlapping like the scales of a pangolin or the shingles on a slate roof. Their uppersides are tarnished nickel, dimpled with micro-pits that capture the floating iron dust; their undersides are a dense, fibrous web of iron-sulfide thread that has grown directly into the atomic structure of the cast iron and basalt. 

They do not possess eyes, lungs, or digestive tracts. Instead, their bellies are lined with microscopic, abrasive rasps made of synthetic corundum. These rasps ride directly against the churning flywheels and sliding pistons. As the heavy machinery operates, these creatures do not resist the movement; they feed on the friction. They consume the heat and the microscopic shavings of iron oxide thrown off by the metal-on-metal wear. 

Their respiration is an exchange of dry nitrogen and micro-particulate slate. The "breathing" of a million tiles creates the fine dust that floats in the warm air currents. When an Anode-Carapace reaches the end of its functional lifespan—when its nickel plate wears down to paper-thin brittleness—it does not rot. It simply shatters under the continuous vibration of its neighbors. Its debris falls between the gears, where it acts as a dry, graphite-like lubricant, while a younger clone, already budding beneath the parent's shell, hooks itself into the newly cleared patch of metal.

They are the engine’s skin, its oil, and its wear-plates, fused into a self-repairing living armor.

### The Piezoelectric Needle-Thickets

Clinging to the seams of the warm brass conduits are the **Zinc-Spun Sedge**. 

These are not soft plants, but delicate, crystalline formations that grow like blown glass or mineral frost. Their stems are hollow tubes of flexible silica, thin as human hair, ending in sharp, multi-faceted needles of pale amber zinc. 

They do not require sunlight. Instead, they are *piezo-trophic*—they feed directly on the mechanical violence of the vault. Every time a trunk-sized piston strikes the basalt floor with its heavy *thump-click*, a shockwave passes through the rock and into the copper pipes. The Quartz-Sedge bends under this wave, generating minute pulses of static electricity within its crystalline stems. 

This static charge creates a faint amber phosphorescence at the tip of each needle. The light is not a byproduct; it is a waste energy, bled off into the bone-dry air so the plant does not shatter from its own internal voltage. 

In the hollow interior of their silica stems, these plants pull metallic vapor out of the nitrogen leaks, slowly thickening their crystalline stalks year after year. If you were to brush a hand across a patch of Zinc-Sedge, you would not feel soft leaves; you would feel a freezing, rigid brush of glass needles that would slice through skin without trembling, singing with a high, ringing tone like crystal wineglasses struck in unison.

### The Conduit-Slugs

Deep within the copper pipes themselves, where pressurized dry nitrogen flows at hundreds of pounds per square inch, live the only creatures in the vault that possess any true mobility: the **Vane-Worms**.

Made of segmented rings of polished brass-colored chitin, these creatures are long, ribbon-like, and blind. They live entirely *inside* the closed-loop gas lines, suspended in the dark, rushing nitrogen stream like ribbon-fish in an invisible current. 

Their bodies are lined with flexible, paddle-like fins made of dry, woven keratin. By angling these fins against the nitrogen flow, they sail through the interior of the pipework, clearing blockages, polishing the inner surfaces of the brass valves with their abrasive hides, and maintaining the airtight seals with a dry, amber-colored wax they secrete from their joints. 

They never enter the open vault. To them, the vast room outside the pipes is an uninhabitable vacuum of unpressurized space. They exist only in the high-pressure circulatory system of the room, traveling endlessly through the brass veins, driven by the same pumps they work to maintain.

### The Acoustic Consciousness

Because nothing moves across the floor, and nothing flies through the slate-dusted air, the vault can initially seem devoid of agency. But the life here is not individual—it is environmental.

The deafening, metallic stridulation—the continuous rasping roar of millions of nickel carapaces vibrating in harmony with the *thump-click* of the pistons—is not random noise. It is an acoustic computation network. 

If a gear deep within the stone begins to wear unevenly, its resonance changes by a fraction of a hertz. Within seconds, the Anode-Carapaces attached to that specific casing adjust their vibration frequency to damp the abnormal oscillation. If a brass pipe develops a microscopic stress fracture, the Zinc-Sedge growing along its seam reacts to the change in static charge, altering its growth pattern to span the crack with dense, glass-fiber scaffolding.

The vault does not contain an ecosystem so much as it *is* an organism—a single, sprawling, biometallic clockwork hive that thinks in resonance, eats friction, excretes dry dust, and breathes dry nitrogen. 

It has forgotten whatever surface world created it, if a surface world ever existed at all. It requires no intervention, no rain, and no sun. It sits thirty meters deep in the dark granite crust, perfectly self-contained, glowing with the dull amber light of its own internal voltage, endlessly grinding its own bones to keep its heart beating.
