They are known only by the quiet hum they leave in the air—a subtle, ionic vibration that precedes them like the smell of dry thunder. 

To look upon the inhabitants of this subterranean expanse is to see the logical conclusion of a world without water: creatures constructed not of soft, fluid-filled sacs, but of cured horn-wood, sheet-brass, polished quartz, and tightly wound copper filament. They are tall and long-limbed, their proportions echoing the narrow, geometric grandeur of the iron-wood trunks. They walk upright, though their silhouettes are sharp and multi-faceted, free from the rounded contours of creatures shaped by gravity and liquid pressure.

Their outer skin is a dense, seamlessly fused lattice of compressed cedar-shive and paraffin-impregnated chitin, buffed to a smooth, matte sheen that range in color from pale amber to dark, unpolished iron. Where a human’s skin would fold or wrinkle, their joints reveal gleaming ball-and-socket mechanisms of cold-worked bronze, bathed in perpetual thin films of high-viscosity synthetic lipid. They move without the jerky motion of simple clockwork, but with a fluid, heavy grace—silent, deliberate, and entirely devoid of heat.

They do not bleed. If a shell-plate is cracked or a limb severed by a falling slate-blade, the wound exposes an interior of tightly packed, grain-aligned timber threads soaked in dry volatile ether. Instead of liquid, a pale, fragrant mist of volatilized terpene puffs briefly into the crisp air, instantly evaporating and leaving behind a crust of crystalline resin that seals the break in seconds.

### **Physiology and Metabolism**

Because no liquid flows within them, their "blood" is a closed, pressurized circulation of light, aromatic ethers and liquid-paraffin compounds driven by a small, magnetic pulse-pump located high in the chest cavity. This pump does not beat with the moist thump of a heart, but spins with a continuous, low-pitched whine, driving the dry lubricants through micro-tubular channels carved directly into their wooden skeletons.

They do not breathe in the biological sense; there are no lungs within their rib-cages of riveted sheet-iron. Instead, their chest cavities are lined with banks of fine, silver-plated copper mesh. As they move through the ventilation currents, air passes through perforated copper grilles on their flanks, stripping static electricity from the atmosphere and charging internal galvanic cells. They "eat" by pressing their palms—which are tipped with fine, brush-like arrays of bronze needles—against the glowing copper conduits or directly into the bark of the living iron-woods, harvesting the static potential that pulses through the forest skeleton.

For raw material, they consume thin, dry wafers of compressed lipid-powder and resin-granules, which are broken down in an internal thermal chamber to replenish their outer wax coatings and maintain the flexibility of their silicated joints.

### **Sensory World and Communication**

Their heads are angular blocks of carved iron-wood, dominated by a horizontal band of multi-faceted quartz prisms in place of eyes. These prisms do not perceive color as wet-eyed surface dwellers do; they see the world as a pristine, high-contrast map of thermal gradients, static potential, and structural tension. To them, a high-tension copper cable glows like a searing white thread through a landscape of cool, pale-grey timber, while the living iron-wood trunks pulse with intricate, lattice-like networks of golden energy.

Sound, in this acoustic-sponge world, is a secondary sense. They are largely mute—they possess no vocal cords, no lungs to push air through a throat. Instead, they communicate through two distinct methods:

1. **Optical Dialect:** By modulating the current through small, internal magnesium filaments, they project rapid, flickering pulses of white and amber light through the quartz plates on their brows. Across the broad, sunlit gulfs between factory tiers, two figures can exchange complex technical data or social intent in a fraction of a second, the light bouncing off mica fronds like silent lightning.
2. **Substrate Resonance:** For close or private communication, they press a heel or a copper-shod fingertip against the nearest piece of horn-wood or iron railing. By vibrating an internal piezoelectric crystal, they send micro-tremors through the continuous structural skeleton of the city. A thinker sitting in a quiet, polished-wood plaza three miles away can "hear" the message through the seat of their frame as a distinct, rhythmic hum in their spine.

### **Life, Origin, and Purpose**

These dwellers are neither born from eggs nor forged in assembly lines; they are *grafted*. 

When a new inhabitant is required—whether to replace one whose frame has finally crystallized into brittle inertia after centuries, or to expand the steward-workforce—the elders locate a young, square-angled shoot of iron-wood growing from a ceiling buttress. Over decades, they train its growth, shaping its core timber with brass clamps and silver wire into the rough blueprint of a spine and limbs. 

Once the wood has reached the density of horn, it is harvested with diamond-toothed saws. Master craftsmen—their fingers tipped with diamond rasps and fine oil-brushes—carve the timber, fit the bronze ball-joints, lace the copper mesh through the grain, and seal the entire frame in a bath of warm, dry ether and melted resin. Finally, a dormant galvanic core, charged by the primary magnesium arcs of the cavern ceiling, is slotted into the chest. The wood absorbs the charge; the internal pump spins up; the quartz eyes ignite with cool, steady light.

The inhabitant wakes up knowing the geometry of the world. There are no children here, no frail old age—only a gradual, beautiful calcification. Over centuries, as an individual's wooden bones absorb more quartz dust and mineral lipids, they grow heavier, stiffer, and more luminous, until at last they choose a place along a major structural support, root their limbs to the iron scaffolding with fresh resin, and become a permanent, living part of the architecture.

### **Social Order and Daily Rhythm**

Their society is one of profound, effortless harmony—not because they are brainwashed or mindless, but because their needs and the environment are perfectly mirrored. There is no concept of private property, for there is nothing to decay, rot, or spoil. A tool forged from cold bronze five hundred years ago is as sharp and bright today as the hour it left the anvil.

They live in small, loose guilds defined by the systems they tend:

* **The Wire-Keepers:** Slender, spider-limbed figures who spend their lives suspended on safety cables thousands of feet above the cavern floor, replacing burnt magnesium filaments, polishing the copper arc-conduits, and ensuring the static potential flows smoothly from ceiling to floor.
* **The Bark-Engineers:** Heavier, broad-shouldered stewards armed with heavy, gear-driven chisels. They guide the growth of the vast iron-wood forest, carving catwalks and factory platforms directly into the trunks *as the trees grow*, ensuring the industry and the ecosystem remain a single, unbroken organism.
* **The Lipid-Refinement Guild:** Stationed in the silent, perforated-metal refineries that sit in the fern-glades, they manage the distillation of volatile dry-oils from crushed resin-bark, filling the fuel-lines that lubricate the entire regional grid.

In the quiet hours—though "hours" have little meaning where the bright, daylight-intensity arcs never dim—the dwellers gather in the wide, empty horn-wood plazas. They do not sit to rest tired muscles, for their muscles do not tire; they stand or lean in groups of three or four, their quartz eyes flashing in rapid, silent conversation. 

They watch the soft, ash-like gliders drop silently through the white light from the ceiling to the fern-tops. They feel the distant, rhythmic thud of the two-mile-away compressor humming through the floorboards—a deep, reassuring pulse that assures them the air is dry, the light is bright, the stone is sharp, and the great machine of their world is running as it always has, and as it always will.
