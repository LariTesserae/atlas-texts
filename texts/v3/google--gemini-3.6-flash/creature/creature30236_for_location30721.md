They are known to no taxonomy, for no eye from the ground has ever seen them, but within the geometry of the knuckle, they are as precise and necessary as the bolts that pin the girders. 

To look closely at the mass is to realize that they do not merely inhabit the gallery; they are its living cladding, a second skin of glass and copper grown directly over the raw toolmarks of the iron.

Up close, their glass-hard exoskeletons are not smooth, but fluted like the barrels of high-pressure hydraulic cylinders. The amber light of the wire-caged bulb does not reflect off them so much as it enters their carapaces, bending through the thick, translucent chitin to illuminate the internal mechanics of their bodies: slender, silvery filaments that flex like braided cable, and dark, gel-like organs suspended in clear, non-freeze hemolymph that circulates without a heart, driven purely by the violent, forty-cycle tremor of the iron deck beneath them.

They have no eyes. Where a head should be, there is only a blunt, copper-rimmed cowl housing a cluster of sensitive, hair-like acoustic pits. These pits are tuned precisely to the high shriek of the pin-hole relief valves. When the valves whistle, the cilia vibrate in sympathy, directing the creature's blind, incessant movements toward the points of highest mechanical stress.

Their feeding is a physical communion with the machine. The mouthparts are triple-hinged, tipped with self-sharpening plates of a dark, dense alloy that matches the hardness of the cold-rolled steel. They do not consume metal to destroy it; they lick the microscopic bloom of zinc that oxidizes on the galvanized bolt-heads, and they lap the cold, sulfurous grease that seeps from the stuffing boxes. What they ingest is never digested in any organic sense. Instead, their internal tracts filter the heavy hydrocarbons and exude them back through microscopic pores along their flanks as a clear, frictionless fluid—an icy, synthetic bile that keeps the raw, un-filed flash-lines of the sand-moulds from binding against one another as the girders flex. They are the pump’s self-replenishing oiling system, locked in an endless, zero-waste loop.

Among the squirming thousands, subtle variations in form reveal a strict, structural hierarchy:

Deep in the seams where the three massive girders lock together—where the pressure is so great that a drop of water would be crushed into a momentary burst of heat—live the *seam-wedges*. These are smaller, no larger than a child’s palm, with carapaces flattened into thin, leaf-like blades. They force themselves sideways into the microscopic gaps between the un-ground iron flanges. Driven in by the frantic thrashing of their hindmost legs, their bodies act as living shims. Their glass carapaces deform slightly under the multi-ton load, taking up the slack in the raw construction, preventing the structural knuckle from shearing its rivets under the thump-hiss of the thirty-foot piston. They are compressed so tightly that their hemolymph turns cloudy and dense, yet they do not crush; they simply transmit the strain from girder to girder.

Clinging to the high, arched undersides of the bronze hydraulic pipes are the *stridulators*. These are the forearm-length specimens, their bodies longer and more segmented, like cold-forged lathe screws. Their copper sucker-pads do not merely hold them fast against the wet, vibration-slick metal; the pads act as acoustic transducers. By pressing their bellies flat against the bronze conduits, they drink in the destructive, resonant frequencies generated by the fluid rushing within. They transform this mechanical energy into their shrill, incessant scraping—drawing the heavy, serrated edges of their hind legs across the milled edges of the structural flanges. 

This collective stridulation is not communication. It is energy dissipation. Millions of glass legs rasping against raw iron convert the massive, structural vibration of the pump into sound and heat, bleeding off the kinetic violence that would otherwise shatter the brittle, cold-rolled steel of the gallery. The deafening chatter is the sound of the machine’s own destruction being continuously blunted and thrown away into the fog.

At the absolute apex of the recess, tucked into the dark pocket behind the glass bulkhead lamp where the grease paste is thickest and the air is coldest, sits the anchor of the mass.

It is a single, immobile specimen, four times the size of the others, known only by its absolute permanence. Its carapace is no longer clear, but has aged—if it can be called aging—into a dense, milky porcelain, yellowed by decades of soot from the amber bulb. Its legs have long since lost their jointed mobility; they have grown around the threads of a central two-inch tie-rod, calcifying into the metal itself until chitin and cold steel are indistinguishable. 

It does not scrape. It does not lap at the oil. It sits embedded in the structural knuckle like a cast-iron boss, its translucent abdomen pulsing in absolute, phase-locked synchronization with the thirty-foot steam piston behind the wall. *Thump-hiss. Pulse. Thump-hiss. Pulse.* It is the master tuning fork of the gallery. Through its fused legs, the rhythm of the atmospheric pump passes into the squirming thousands below it, dictating the speed of their jaws, the frequency of their legs, the circulation of their icy blood.

None of them have ever dropped from the metal. If one were pried loose with a cold chisel, it would reveal no soft underbelly, no delicate entrails—only a precise, vacuum-sealed recess fitted perfectly to the contours of the rivet head it had been covering. But they are never pried loose. The grip of their copper pads is absolute, held by the wet suction of the freezing condensate and the molecular attraction of clean metal against clean metal.

They do not dream, for they have no nervous system capable of sleep. They do not rest, for the machine never stops. In the thick, amber-lit fog, six thousand feet above the world, they squirm, scrape, and pulse—a living, cold-blooded mechanism built to ensure that the un-smoothed iron remains forever un-smoothed, the sharp edges remain forever sharp, and the vast, cloud-swallowed pump continues its blind, eternal breathing.
