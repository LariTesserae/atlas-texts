The air at this altitude does not cool; it thickens, sweltering and heavy with moisture, clinging to the skin like a hot, wet silk.

Here, miles above any recognizable sea level, a vast urban geometry is carved directly into the heart of an impossible mountain wall. Sheer basalt cliffs drop thousands of feet into a bottomless white abyss, yet the heat remains stifling, rising from the stone itself. Spanning this vertical world is a metropolis of ancient, tech-less density: grand arcades, winding step-streets, terraced plazas, and endless tiered colonnades, all seamless, as if grown or smoothed out of the living rock by centuries of continuous, gentle wear. There are no gears, no wires, no metals, nor any mark of tools. The architecture is purely stone and bio-calcification, dense as an old reef, stacked house upon house, boulevard above boulevard, rising into the misty sky until the details blur into the upper atmosphere.

Water saturates every surface. It does not rain in sudden squalls; instead, the sky is a bright, clear vault of normal daylight that somehow bleeds a continuous, fine condensation into the warm air. Every wall weeps. Thick, crystalline sheets of warm water slide perpetually down the carved stone facades, coating the stairways in an inch-deep, smooth-flowing current that never slips or hastens. 

The water defies simple gravity, bound by the spatial distortions that hold this place together. Vast aqueducts, broad enough to carry wide rivers, bridge the voids between mountain spurs. In places, these aqueducts curve upward in gentle, impossibly smooth arcs, carrying cascades of thermal water uphill to feed high-altitude fountains before looping seamlessly back down into deep, carved basins. Water falls from high balconies in curtain-like sheets, plunging hundreds of feet, only to bend gracefully mid-air, sweeping horizontally across open public squares to water the lush hanging terraces before vanishing into hidden sluices that never overflow.

The vegetation is rampant, dense, and unnaturally deep green. Broad-leafed ferns the size of sails burst from the masonry joints. Heavy, swollen vines with thick, waxy leaves rope the pillars together, binding tier to tier in a living web. Fleshy mosses, drenched in warm moisture, coat the carved balustrades like velvet, glowing with rich emerald pigment under the steady, golden daylight. Yet despite this overwhelming jungle-like abundance, there is not a single animal presence. No insects skim the pools of water. No birds roost in the vast, shadowed stone vaulted ceilings. No fish dart through the clear, uphill-flowing canals. The lushness is utterly solitary, a kingdom of pure flora, stone, and fluid.

The sound is immense. It is a deafening, acoustic violence that fills every inch of the air, so loud it becomes a tactile vibration in the ribs and jaw. It is the unceasing, thunderous roar of millions of gallons of water falling, rushing, and looping through millions of stone chambers simultaneously, combined with a deep, subterranean resonance—a low hum generated by the thermal wind trapped in thousands of tall, carved stone wind-pipes that riddle the cliffside architecture. Because the cityscape acts as an enormous, passive acoustic instrument, the roar does not echo or fade; it is an omnipresent, solid wall of sound, so loud that thought itself feels heavy and slow beneath its weight.

Nothing changes here. The dynamic of the world is locked in a absolute, eternal equilibrium. The water never runs dry, nor does it rise to flood the lower terraces. The leaves of the massive plants are perpetually heavy with dew, never growing larger, never yellowing, never shedding. The heat is constant, held at a permanent, sweltering peak that never cools into night or warms into noon. The light remains fixed at a bright, rich afternoon glare, casting long, sharp shadows through the carved archways that never shift an inch across the wet flagstones.

Erosion reached its final, settled balance eons ago. The steps are rounded, the relief carvings smoothed into soft, tactile forms that feel warm to the touch, but the stone wears no further. The water glides over the surfaces without carrying away a single grain of sand. 

Standing on one of the outer promenades—looking out over a valley-sized gulf where stone bridge-complexes suspend themselves across impossible gaps—the landscape feels at once infinite and tightly closed. Dense architectural layers descend into the warm fog below and climb into the humid haze above, completely silent of life, drowned in light, wrapped in emerald green, and screaming with the majestic, unyielding thunder of perpetual water.
