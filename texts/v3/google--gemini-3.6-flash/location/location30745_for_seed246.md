High in a mountain notch where the sky hangs pale and unclouded, the wind does not blow quietly; it strikes the rock like a hammer on an anvil. 

The notch is small, no larger than a mountain hamlet, bound tightly between two enormous, rounded shoulders of grey granite. These peaks are impossibly old—smoothed down over eons by vanished glaciers and relentless winds until no sharp edges remain. The rock feels soft underfoot, rubbed down to a polished, bone-like finish, carved with deep, curving grooves that look less like fractures and more like the worn paths of ancient, liquid flow.

Built directly into the floor and back wall of this high basin is a terraced settlement, though no tool ever touched it. It is an urbanity born of biology and water: thousands of years ago, vast colonies of air-breathing, shell-secreting lime-polyps lived in the basin’s ancient melt-pools, depositing dense, layered comb-structures that petrified as the mountains rose. What remains is a perfectly organized, multi-story ghost town of stone. There are narrow, stepped avenues, curved plazas, and stacked, cell-like chambers with arching doorways and high, rounded windows. The architecture is dense, layered, and unmistakably town-like, yet its surfaces bear the smooth, organic flowing lines of sea-coral or petrified wood.

The air is bitter, biting cold—a stark, razor-sharp frost that makes every breath plume into thick white vapor. Yet the high-altitude sun is bright and unyielding, blazing down through clear air and striking the stone with blinding clarity. Where sunlight touches dark basalt inclusions in the wall, thin sheets of frost snap and liquefy, sending fast, shimmering ribbons of meltwater racing through the carved stone gutters of the empty streets. 

It is loud here. Tremendously loud.

The hollowed-out chambers, windows, and perforated stone spires act as a gargantuan acoustic engine. As the freezing mountain gales roar into the basin, the ancient, empty city sings. The air vibrates with a booming, low-frequency hum that rattles the teeth in your jaw, layered beneath higher, screaming whistles that pitch up and down as clouds scud across the sun. The sound echoes off the worn granite walls, amplifying until the very stone underfoot seems to hum like a driven tuning fork.

Despite the frigid cold, life clings aggressively to the moist pockets created by this acoustic churn. Condensation coats every interior shadow. Inside the roofless stone rooms, protected from the direct gale, grows a lush, emerald underworld. Deep cushions of wet, velvet-like moss carpet the floors of the limestone cells. Frost-hardy ferns with silver-rimed fronds spill out of carved windows like tapestries, dripping cold, crystal-clear droplets into small, stone-bound basins. Liverworts in shades of violent purple and chartreuse coat the street-curbs, misted continuously by the spray of tiny, wind-shattered waterfalls tumbling down from the high terraces.

The city is mostly hollow space. Huge empty avenues lead past vacant, gaping archways where nothing lives but the moss and the roaring wind. 

Occasionally, the dynamic balance shifts. A dense wall of mountain fog will surge through the notch, plunging the sunlight into a dim, ghostly blue, instantly freezing the water channels into long needles of clear ice that shatter under the pressure of their own growth with sharp, metallic crackling sounds. Ten minutes later, the wind tears the mist away, the sun bursts back through, and the melt begins again in a flurry of splashing droplets and steaming rock.

Fauna is exceedingly rare, but it belongs here entirely. High on a third-tier terrace, a solitary creature appears from an arched chamber. It is heavy-bodied and compact, covered in dense, oily, iron-grey wool that barely moves in the gale. It has six short, broad-padded limbs that grip the polished stone with rubbery friction. Unbothered by the deafening acoustic roar, it moves deliberately down a carved stone staircase, its flat snout dipping into a moss-lined gutter to drink the ice-cold runoff. It grazes briefly on a patch of purple liverwort, its dark eyes blinking against the intense solar glare, before retreating back into the wind-shielded, moss-lined warm-pockets of the lower city, leaving the sunlit stone avenues empty once more.
