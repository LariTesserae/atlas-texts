Below the surface of the dry outer crust lies a vast, sun-bright hollow—not a cave in the traditional sense, but an cavernous underworld basin whose sky is a continuous, translucent vault of amber-colored pith. 

The heat here is immense and unmoving, held at a humid, baking equilibrium. The light does not come from a sun or a flame; it radiates directly through the fibrous ceiling and from the thick, glowing sap-channels that run like white-hot cables through the floor. It is a harsh, shadowless glare that saturates every square foot of space in shades of scalded yellow, pale lime, and wet ivory.

There is almost no stone here. The geology of this chamber is entirely embryonic and biological. The ground beneath is a soft, compressed substrate of unborn plant tissue—a dense, pale cellulose bedrock that feels underfoot like damp, unbaked bread or dense green wood. It has no grain yet, no annual rings, no signs of decay or weathering. It is raw mass, coming into being without the intervention of wind, frost, or running water. Because there is no erosion, there are no sharp crags or worn riverbeds; instead, the terrain rises and falls in smooth, swelling lobes, vast subterranean dunes of solid living starch, miles long, that curve softly into one another.

The scale of the hollow is regional—a sunken valley floor extending far beyond the eye’s reach beneath the low, luminescent arch of the canopy. Yet despite its open expanse, the density of material is stifling. Millions of vertical stems, each as thick as a temple column and packed so closely their bark touches, form endless translucent colonnades. Above, their crowns merge into a solid, unbroken ceiling of compressed leaf-flesh. Below, the floor is crowded with millions of tight, translucent nodules, giant unblown buds, and pale, fleshy roots that intertwine until they form a second, elevated ground level. Every available inch of volume is occupied by plant matter in a state of perpetually arrested growth.

It is damp, but not wet. There are no rivers or lakes, only pockets of trapped, oily moisture. Warm condensation beads continuously on the vast, smooth faces of the vertical stalks, gathering into heavy, marble-sized drops that hang motionless for hours before slipping down the smooth bark. Where two giant roots meet, small depressions hold shallow pools of lukewarm, fragrant water, so clear and still that the glaring ceiling is reflected in them without a single ripple.

Scattered throughout this dense, living architecture are the faint, fossilized traces of human or pre-human presence. These are not stone ruins or metal structures, but crude, hollowed-out nodes of hardened, petrified resin—tiny, single-room enclosures grown directly into the sides of the massive stalks. Some resemble beehive huts; others are simple dry shelves carved into the pith, their edges now rounded over and partially swallowed by the slow, relentless expansion of the plant wall. Inside one such alcove, a low bench of calcified root remains perfectly intact, covered in a thin, dry film of crystallized sugar. There are no tools here, no rusty blades, no fragments of glass or woven cloth; whoever inhabited these small, scattered cells worked entirely within the organic logic of the vault, using the living plant as both quarry and timber.

The landscape is almost entirely quiet. The atmosphere is too heavy, too thick with heat and moisture, to carry sound far. The only noises are rare and isolated: the distant, heavy *clop* of a condensation drop falling fifty feet into a sap-pool, or the muffled, bass resonance of liquid shifting under immense pressure deep within a subterranean taproot. 

Life among the fauna is exceedingly rare. Days pass without anything moving that is not a drop of water. Occasionally, on the side of a glowing sap-conduit, a broad, disc-shaped creature—its carapace as clear as amber and entirely hairless—will shift its position by an inch, sucking lukewarm moisture from the fiber before falling motionless again for weeks.

Nothing changes here. The light never fades to night; the heat never drops; the humidity never rises to a storm. The vault exists in a state of frozen equilibrium, an immense, hot, bright nursery where the world is constantly on the verge of forming, but remains forever locked in its first, motionless, unblemished moment.
