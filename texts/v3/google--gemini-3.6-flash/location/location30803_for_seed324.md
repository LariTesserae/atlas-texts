It is a pocket of cold steel high above any floor, set deep into the upper ribs of an immense vertical works.

The space is small—scarcely wide enough for three men to stand abreast without touching shoulders, though there are no men here, nor have any left a trace. It is an elevated niche, a recessed drainage bay set eight hundred meters up the flank of a seamless, riveted tower that vanishes both upward into dense grey cloud-cap and downward into the wet gloom below.

Nothing here was grown or weathered into place. The deck beneath is a single sheet of heavy-gauge, diamond-tread alloy, its corners crisp and unrounded. The welds along the seams are bright and precise, showing the neat, stacked-bead pattern of recent machine-torch work, uncorroded and unsoftened by time. The walls are smooth, cold plates of dark-passivated iron, joined at sharp ninety-degree angles that create a tight, three-sided box open on its fourth side to the empty, cloud-stuffed air.

Despite its youth and its hardness, the niche is utterly drenched.

High altitude pulls mist directly through the open face, where it collides with the freezing metallic bulkheads and instantly collapses into water. It does not merely drip; it sheets. Streams of cold, clear condensate run down the vertical plates in parallel ribbons, collecting in precision-milled gutters that crisscross the floor. Somewhere above, a heat-exchanger or relief manifold vents at regular intervals, sending a thick, pulsating veil of tepid water cascading across the open mouth of the bay like a liquid curtain. The air smells of wet zinc, cold machine oil, and ozone.

The sound is monumental. It fills the small space until it ceases to feel like sound and becomes a physical weight pressed against the chest and ears. A deep, sub-bass shudder vibrates through the tread-plate every few seconds—the stroke of a massive hydraulic RAM buried somewhere deep behind the iron walls. Overlaid on this thrum is the continuous, tearing roar of falling water, amplified and bounced between the hard, flat metal surfaces until the space rings like the inside of a struck bell. Steam vents hiss with a shrill, deafening scream that rises and falls with the cycling of hidden pumps. You could shout at the top of your lungs two inches from another person’s ear and convey nothing but the vibration of your own throat.

Light is scarce and flat. There is no sun, only the dim, sour-milk glow of the high cloud-layer bleeding into the open side of the alcove, and a single, low-wattage indicator lamp set into a recessed conduit box near the deck. Its lens is heavy glass, cast in a dull amber that casts long, trembling shadows through the streaming water. The slick metal surfaces catch this faint light in sharp, thin highlights that shift with the pulse of the runoff.

Density is low; the space is mostly empty volume. A pair of heavy, iron-flanged pipes rise vertically through the floor, pass through two heavy rubber gaskets, and disappear into the ceiling plate. A solitary brass wheel-valve, its rim still bearing the sharp, cross-hatched knurling of the lathe, sits mounted on the back wall. Otherwise, the niche is bare—a precise, functional void designed solely to collect and route moisture away from a critical seam in the superstructure.

Life has found almost no purchase here. On the warm underside of the lower pipe—where a faint residual heat from the interior machinery counters the mountain chill—a thin, translucent film of dark green algae has taken hold. It is no thicker than a coat of paint, a glistening slick that feeds on the mineral-laden condensate and the amber lamp-glow. It does not branch or flower; it simply exists as a damp stain on the machined iron, devoid of insects, worms, or airborne spores.

The room operates on a rapid, unyielding dynamic. Every forty seconds, the pressure within the wall shifts: the hiss of the steam vent cuts out abruptly, the cascade across the entrance thins to a heavy spray, and the diamond-tread deck drains completely through its slotted floor-grates in a matter of heartbeats. Then, with a loud, metallic *clack* that vibrates through the bones, a valve re-seats within the bulkheads, the roaring curtain of water crashes down anew, and the niche fills once more with cloud-mist, thunder, and cold, relentless spray.
