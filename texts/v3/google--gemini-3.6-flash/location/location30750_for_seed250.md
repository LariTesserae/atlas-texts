The water is not an ocean sitting upon a crust, but an atmospheric mantle—a cold, hyper-dense fluid layer miles deep, suspended at extreme altitude around the jagged spine of a young world. 

To be here is to be entirely submerged, yet bathed in a brilliant, penetrating light. The liquid is so pure, so devoid of silt or organic drift, that illumination from the distant sun pierces down through three leagues of fluid without dimming to twilight. It is a world of pale turquoise, sapphire, and blinding, silvery caustics that dance across empty space.

Beneath this vast body of cold water lies raw, embryonic geology. The seabed is not a place of soft sediment or smooth continental shelves; sand has not yet had time to form. Instead, the sea floor is a high-altitude plateau of freshly extruded basalt and obsidian, thrown up by violent, deep-seated tectonic pumps. The mountain ranges are sharp as broken flint, their needles and ridgelines rising like rows of black glass spears into the clear liquid. There are no rounded edges, no soft slopes—every rock face is crisp, crystalline, and brand-new, fractured into pristine geometric facets that haven't felt the dulling rub of time.

Between these monumental obsidian ridges lies staggering emptiness. You can float upon a high peak and look out across fifty leagues of illuminated, crystal-clear fluid before the cyan haze finally swallows the view. There are no fish. There are no floating polyps, no swimming jellies, no drifting plankton to cloud the medium. The water is utterly sterile. The only sign of vegetation is sparse, clinging to the thermal lips of deep volcanic fissures: rare, hair-like filaments of inorganic silica-algae that glow with a faint, cold phosphorescence and shiver violently in the currents.

The motion here is relentless and thunderous. Though there are no structures, no cities, and no machinery to be seen, the physics of this place are clearly bound to an advanced, invisible engineering. Unseen gravimetric fields—perhaps generated deep within the planet’s heavy-metal mantle—manipulate the fluid on a cosmic scale. 

The water does not merely ebb and flow; it shears into impossible, mile-high vertical rivers within itself. Great laminar curtains of cold liquid race past stagnant pockets of warm brine at hundreds of knots, forming frictionless boundaries that act like invisible glass walls. In the mid-waters, massive planetary-scale tornados of water twist upwards toward the sky, carving hollow tubes of near-vacuum through the sea before collapsing back into roaring hydraulic shockwaves.

The sound is deafening and continuous. Because the medium is dense and cold, acoustic energy travels for thousands of miles without fading. The environment echoes with a massive, sub-bass roar: the deep groan of newborn basalt cracking open under the weight of the sea, punctuated by the sharp, metallic *crack* of immense cavitation bubbles imploding in the shear zones. It sounds like a thousand jet engines throttled wide open inside a cathedral of glass, a chaotic symphony of liquid violence striking unyielding rock.

Up near the surface of this high-altitude sea, where the fluid meets the rare upper atmosphere, the water breaks into standing waves five miles high—curtains of water that rise, freeze into hyper-pressurized liquid arches under localized force fields, and then shatter into torrential cataracts that fall back down for twenty minutes at a time. 

Everything is in a state of rapid, violent genesis. The land is still being born from the mantle; the water is being sculpted by field geometries that warp its density and surface tension in real time. It is a vast, loud, sunlit wilderness of cold glass and roaring liquid—an empty, high-tech engine of a world, running hyperactive and sterile under a clear sky.
