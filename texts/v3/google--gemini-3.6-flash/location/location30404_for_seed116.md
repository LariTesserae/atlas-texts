The water here is warm—bath-warm, skin-warm—and it fills everything. 

It is not an ocean, though it has the weight and volume of deep water. It is a pocket of liquid suspended thousands of feet above any unseen earth, trapped inside a vertical brass-and-iron cistern no larger than a small chapel. This is a single, forgotten chamber built into the outer flank of a sky-piercing industrial spire. Outside its curved glass ports, the air drops away into dizzying, cloud-streaked abyss; inside, the chamber is entirely submerged, drowning in a continuous, heavy current that cycles endlessly through its ancient works.

Nothing here was born of soil or rock. Every square inch of the enclosure is built. Wall plates of heavy riveted iron, thick copper conduit bundles, and giant cast-bronze filter grates form the entire geography. Yet there is no sharpness left to the construction. Millennia of warm, mineral-thick water rushing through this narrow space have worn the metal down to a primordial smoothness. The heads of massive bolts have been scrubbed into soft, metallic domes, like river pebbles made of steel. The edges of submerged staircases are rounded off, their risers softened until they resemble stone shelves carved by a mountain stream.

Despite the total lack of natural earth, life has taken a ferocious, lush hold. From every recessed seam and corroded pipe-joint, thick curtains of emerald silk-weed stream out into the flow. They are not ocean kelp, but something adapted entirely to warm, electrified water: long, feather-soft tendrils that glow faintly green where daylight penetrates the upper glass dome, anchoring themselves to iron rust-blooms. Dense mats of dark moss coat the inactive machinery, forming soft cushions over silent gear-teeth and brass pressure meters whose glass faces were swept away centuries ago.

The space is quiet only in its layout—there are few objects here, leaving generous spans of open water between the far wall and the central turbine assembly. But acoustically, it is deafening. 

Sound travels through the dense, warm fluid as a physical pressure against the chest. From somewhere below the grate floor comes the slow, thumping heartbeat of a titanic piston—*th-thump, th-thump*—sending rhythmic shockwaves through the water that vibrate the silk-weed in unison. High-pressure jets burst periodically from rusted bypass valves, roaring like underwater firehoses and stirring up clouds of golden, oxidized flakes. Electrical hums bleed into the water from copper-sheathed coils mounted along the walls, a steady, low-register drone that teeth can feel before ears can hear it.

Light enters normally, descending in thick, golden shafts from a reinforced skylight far above the chamber’s ceiling. It cuts through the clear, amber-tinted water, illuminating a thriving, strange micro-fauna that has claimed this high-altitude engine nook. 

Small, translucent organisms—resembling a cross between a mantis shrimp and a mechanical clockwork spring—skitter across the smoothed brass surfaces, using tiny claw-feet to graze on grease-loving bacteria. Schools of slender, iron-scaled fish, no longer than a finger, dart through the open spaces between pipes. Their scales are dull brass and silver, picking up the sunlight as they turn in unison to hunt the microscopic larvae drifting out of the filter grates. Near the top of the chamber, where a trapped pocket of pressurized air forms a shimmering silver ceiling, larger air-breathing amphibious creatures cling to the iron ceiling plates, their long, pale tails trailing down into the water like translucent ribbons.

Everything here is in motion, bound to a strict industrial ecology. Water enters through a set of roaring wall-intakes, gets churned through the slow-turning blades of a central bronze wheel, feeds the lush, swaying fields of iron-weed, and cascades down through the floor grates into the dark shafts below. It is a world without a shore, without a bottom, and without a sky, operating inside a hollowed-out piece of heavy machinery that has forgotten its original purpose, settling instead into a permanent, warm, wet, and thrumming paradise.
