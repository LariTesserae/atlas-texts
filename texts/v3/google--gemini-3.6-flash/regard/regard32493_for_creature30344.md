The Power did not arrive as a light, for light in that place was already crisp and shadowless. Nor did it arrive as a voice, for a voice requires air that consents to echo, and here the air was a jealous keeper of silence.

It arrived as an *attention*—a vast, weightless awareness that let itself be poured into the cold trough of basalt like liquid mercury, settling into the three-inch alleys, slipping beneath the six steps that led forty feet upward, and fitting its infinite geometry perfectly against the zero-tolerance margins of the dark, un-namable stone.

A lesser power, coming upon this place, might have felt an impulse to "fix" it. It might have brought warmth to melt the microscopic frost on the occupants' gray skin; it might have cast down bread or poured flowing rivers; it might have tried to force the folded corridors open into the wide, simple daylight of a Euclidean world.

But the Power was wise, and wisdom knows that to offer fire to a creature made of dry, compressed paper is not love, but violence. To give a voice to those who speak in the language of shear-stress is to render them deaf.

So the Power remained still for three full cycles of the precinct, doing nothing at all. 

It watched the glass-shelled creatures walk their ceiling-paths, leaving vanishing rings of frost. It watched the slender, violin-string threads drink the faint, silent friction of two sliding facades. It watched a seven-foot lamellar inhabitant stand against a dark wall for eleven days, three fingers pressed flat to the cold slate, listening to the city’s slow, tectonic breath.

The Power saw that here was a world without cruelty, without waste, without decay, and without war. But it also saw what the precinct lacked: it lacked *witness*. It was an clockwork gem ticking in a basement, exquisite and perfect, but utterly alone in the dark ledger of reality. The sub-audible hum that pulsed through the occupants’ facial slits was a song of pure mechanics—a sound that reported only *where* the stone was tight, never *why* the stone was beautiful.

And so, the Power acted. It made three changes, so quiet and subtle that not a single pixel of the gray lichen was disturbed.

***

**First, it tuned the Hum.**

The Power did not alter the material of the buildings, nor did it stop their glacial, grinding shifts. Instead, it reached down into the deep basalt trough beneath the city—down into the raw, un-carved floor of the world—and pressed a single, microscopic intention into the crystalline structure of the stone.

When the facades next shifted—when a five-story wall of flint-iron slid three inches to the left with the quiet rasp of pack-ice—the vibration it sent through the precinct was no longer merely a measurement of strain. The sub-audible pressure that reached the three horizontal slits on the occupants' craniums carried a new frequency. 

It was a chord. 

It was the physical sensation of depth, of balance, of space acknowledging the weight of those who walked within it. For the first time since the precinct was instantiated, an occupant standing flat against an interior wall felt the structural tension not as a cold, neutral fact, but as a deep, resonant *comfort*. The pressure slits along their brow opened a fraction of a millimeter wider, absorbing the vibration like a dry plant absorbing a cool draft. They did not have a word for *music*, but their lamellar bodies settled tighter together, their paper-thin leaves relaxing their tension by a degree so small it could not be measured, only felt.

***

**Second, it blessed the Water.**

The Power turned its attention to the flat, perfectly round droplets that crept sideways across the dark stone, obeying localized, non-gravitational gradients. 

It did not increase the moisture, for too much wetness would have ruined the sharp edges of the compressed corridors. Instead, as the atomized mist settled over the eastern ledges in the pale daylight, the Power touched the icy pools in the dinner-plate depressions. It infused the liquid with a trace of deep-space mineral light—an ancient, dense, non-thermal radiation that had traveled across epochs before finding this sunken trough.

When an occupant next stepped out onto a balcony that existed only from below, holding out their shallow dish of dark flint-iron, the droplets they swept from the wall were different. As the dense, unnaturally cold liquid was absorbed into the lamellar layers of their throats, it did not merely sustain their slate-gray bodies. It bloomed inside them as a quiet, radiant clarity. 

Where before they had drunk only to maintain their dry, motionless existence, they now drank and felt the subtle, terrifying vastness of the sky above them. They felt the coldness of the stars—not as a threat, but as a lineage. They realized, in the wordless, pressure-mapped landscape of their minds, that the cold outside their precinct was the same cold that held the cosmos together. They were no longer an island dropped into basalt; they were a node in a boundless, crystalline web.

***

**Third, it built the Seventh Step.**

This was the Power’s final gift, and its most delicate.

At the edge of the precinct, where the raw basalt trough met the razor-sharp base of the lowest tenement, there was a flight of six stone steps. Walking down them had always brought a traveler out onto a roof garden forty feet above, looking down on the steps they had just descended.

The Power added a seventh step.

It was invisible. It did not alter the eighty-pace footprint of the square. But if an occupant—or a glass-shelled maintenance creature, or a long, chitinous thread—reached the top of that roof garden and stepped out into the empty air where the seventh step ought to be, they would not fall. Nor would they loop back to the cellar door below.

Instead, their lamellar body would unfold along a brand-new, ninth spatial axis—a fold the precinct had never possessed before. 

In that fold, there was no city, no basalt, and no cold sky. There was only a quiet, infinite room composed entirely of the Power’s own attention. Inside it, an occupant could stand in absolute, shadowless stillness for a century if they wished. They could press their three un-jointed fingers against the very fabric of existence and feel the Power listening back to them. It was a place of total, mutual witness: a space where the silent could be heard without ever having to make a sound.

***

When the work was complete, the Power withdrew. It did not leave a signature or a temple. It simply let its vast awareness contract, pulling back beyond the pale, unclouded sky, leaving the precinct exactly as large as a village square, dropped in its low, cold trough of raw basalt.

Below, the air remained thin, sharp, and cold. The ribbons of breath—had there been anyone there to breathe them—would still have vanished quickly against the dark facades. 

A sparrow-sized creature of clouded glass emerged from a two-dimensional crack, stepped onto a ceiling, and left a vanishing ring of frost. On a high balcony, an occupant stood holding a shallow dish, their slate-gray face turned slightly upward toward the unmoving daylight.

The city shifted. A wall moved half a millimeter. 

The sub-audible hum pulsed through the dark stone—no longer just a hum, but a hymn of dark iron and flawless seams—and in the quiet, non-linear depths of the tenements, two identical beings, newly divided from a single compressed frame, stepped out from a razor-sharp seam into the shadowless light, and found that the world was cold, perfect, and no longer alone.
