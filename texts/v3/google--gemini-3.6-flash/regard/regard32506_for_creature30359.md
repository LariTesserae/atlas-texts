The Power came not as a thunderclap, nor as a shaft of blinding light, for it was wise enough to know that a thunderclap would shatter the delicate resin-domes on the nineteenth tier, and a sudden glare would blind thousands of amber eyes adapted to the soft twilight of the bluff.

It came as a stillness—a quiet so profound that for a brief, single beat of a glider’s wing, even the warm convection drafts suspended their drift.

The Power was vast, ancient, and fluid, born of places where oceans roared and stars wept ice. Yet as its awareness bent toward the butter-colored sandstone bluff, it did not bring the moisture of its origins. It paused at the lip of the alcove, hovering in the blinding, sun-scorched air of the open canyon, and *looked*.

It saw a marvel. 

It saw a people who had taken the harshest negation—the total absence of water—and turned it into a dense, humming tapestry of life. It saw the four-handed builders slathering plant-glue; it felt the rhythmic, musical *tap-tap-tap* of bone mallets vibrating through the rock; it smelled the hot, rich perfume of cinnamon, resin, and tallow. It recognized that here, in a space no wider than a wagon, was a masterpiece of fierce, uncompromising existence.

A lesser power might have pitied them. A foolish power might have commanded a cool, sweet spring to burst forth from the rear wall of the cave, thinking itself a savior—and in doing so, would have dissolved their homes into mud, drowned their granaries, rotted their lichen, and poisoned their hot, salt-dense blood.

This Power was not foolish. It understood the *Law of the Cold Spark*. It understood that to give this world water would be an act of genocide, and to give it fire would be an apocalypse.

So the Power did what only the truly wise and benevolent can do: it honored the geometry of what was already there, and set about securing its eternity.

***

First, the Power extended a thought toward the sky above the bluff. 

It did not alter the climate of the world, but it wove an invisible, permanent aerodynamic curve into the stone brow above the overhang. From that moment on, no matter how the sky raged, no matter if a freak, once-in-a-millennium deluge swept across the arid high plateau, the wind would catch the rain and hurl it fifty yards clear of the cliff face. Not a single stray drop of sky-solvent would ever curl into the shade. The alcove was sealed in an eternal, dry envelope.

Second, the Power turned its attention to the sandstone itself.

The rock was ancient, soft, and worn to the consistency of poured wax. In three thousand years—a mere breath to the Power—a sheer plane deep within the cliff would give way under the stress of thermal expansion, dropping the entire shelf into the bottomless void below. 

The Power reached into the primordial grains of quartz. It did not turn the stone to granite or diamond, for if it did, the Pith-bound’s flint chisels and river-pebble scrapers would break, and their rock-talk would be silenced by the change in density. Instead, it welded the microscopic silica bonds with a silent, everlasting grace. The sandstone remained soft to the touch, warm, and butter-colored, yielding easily to tiny tools, but its structural core was made immortal. It would never shear. It would never slide. The shelf would hold its burden until the sun grew cold.

Third, the Power looked at the life within.

It saw the elderly extractors pressing the swollen, silver discs of the succulents for their pale lipid-milk. It saw the young scouts leaping alongside the thyme-furred gliders. It saw that the margin between abundance and starvation in the alcove was thin as a moth's wing, balanced entirely on the health of the resin-thorns and the hanging beard-lichen.

The Power did not drop food from the sky. To do so would break the sacred rhythm of their labor, which was the very soul of their identity. Instead, it whispered to the roots deep within the stone. It coaxed the resin-thorns to push their roots a fraction deeper, into mineral veins rich in iron and fragrant terpenes, ensuring their blooms would forever smell of hot spice and their sap would never run dry. It touched the microscopic mineral dust drifting up from the plains, enriching it so that every frond of golden lichen grew thick, supple, and laden with high-energy spores.

Finally, the Power looked upon the Pith-bound themselves.

It observed a mother resting in a fifteen-minute trance, her long, double-jointed arms wrapped around two small, velvety young, their amber eyes clicking shut behind snake-skin membranes, their hot breath smelling of dried amber.

The Power felt a deep, solemn affection for these small, furious sparks of life. It did not speak to them in words, nor did it reveal a face in the dust. But before it withdrew, it leaned its vast consciousness against the exterior face of the sandstone bluff and *struck a note*.

It was not a sound that human ears could hear, nor was it a tremor that would knock a mud-brick from a terrace. It was a subtle, fundamental frequency—a low, sweet, resonant hum embedded directly into the bones of the cliff.

Instantly, down in the lowest districts, a master builder paused. She held her bone rod against the floor. She felt the rock-talk. 

The stone was singing. 

It wasn't a warning, nor a command. It was simply the clearer, richer transmission of their own voices. The rock had become a perfect acoustic instrument. A tap on the lowest tier now traveled to the twentieth with the crystalline clarity of a struck chime. The builders’ coordination became effortless; their messages of love, work, and architecture rippled through the density of the city like music through a harp.

The Pith-bound did not know a god had visited them. They had no word for such a thing. They simply thought the rock had settled into a particularly fine mood that afternoon. 

High in the lichen fronds, the gliders clicked their patagia and leaped. In the resin-brick alleys, the walnut-scarabs dragged their stone slabs up the smooth ramps. On the gut-bridges, the elders sat in the warm, reflected amber light, rubbing fragrant tallow onto their palms, listening to the ceaseless, deafening, glorious roar of their crowded home.

Out in the canyon, the Power turned and faded into the vast, bright sky, leaving the tiny fortress of dust and wax untouched, uncorrupted, and perfectly, endlessly alive.
