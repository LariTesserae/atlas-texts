The Presence did not arrive as a storm, nor as a burning wheel, nor as a voice breaking through the high haze. Such things belong to the noisy lower worlds, where creatures measure life in frantic heartbeats and build empires that crumble before a fern-tree drops a single frond.

Instead, the Power arrived as an awareness—a quiet, vast attention that settled over the basalt amphitheater like the golden mist itself, fitting seamlessly into the space between the thunder of the fall and the chime of the weeping walls.

It was wise enough to know that a place of equilibrium is a fragile masterpiece. To "rescue" the pale climber would be to destroy it; to grant it speech would be to deafen it to the song of the mountain; to alter the travertine basins would be to break an ancient, slow-moving clock. 

So the Power simply watched, feeling the terrace through every scale, leaf, and drop of mineral water.

It felt the glass-flies riding the standing acoustic waves, suspended in the amber light. 
It felt the basin sliders slowly petrifying into white lime, turning their very deaths into architecture. 
It felt the cool, thick pulse of the heavy lizards sleeping beneath their crusts of basalt-colored stone.
And it felt the relict—the pale, oil-skinned climber, ascending the three-finger notches with the silent fluid grace of water rising through black slate.

The Power watched as the climber reached eighteen feet up into the fibrous fur of the fern-tree, placing its long, flat palm against the cold, knapped edge of the black obsidian blade. It saw the creature hold its hand there, feeling the vibration of the tree’s heartwood, reaching back through ten thousand years toward the ghost of a lineage that had long since melted into the dark chimney behind the rock.

The Power understood then. The terrace was not just an ecosystem; it was a sanctuary of memory, kept alive by a solitary keeper who did not even know the word for *remembrance*.

And so, guided by wisdom and love, the Power acted. It made no grand display. It worked strictly through the elements that were already there: stone, water, heat, and sound.

***

First, it reached deep—far beneath the basalt ridge, down into the dark, crushing roots of the continent where the geothermal veins drew their heat from the earth’s molten core. The Power smoothed the deep volcanic fractures, sealing them against premature cooling or tectonic shifts. It anchored the thermal heart of the mountain, ensuring that the warm, mineral-heavy water would weep from the black cliff for another hundred thousand years. The terrace would not freeze. The mist would not clear. The bright, steamy pocket was made eternal.

Second, it touched the obsidian blade. It did not move the glass, nor did it alter its shape. Instead, the Power passed a subtle, permanent resonance through the volcanic crystal. Now, when the climber presses its flat palm against the knapped crescent, it does not merely feel cold stone and wet bark. It feels a deep, gentle warmth radiating from within the glass—a quiet, tactile echo that carries the sensation of being held, of being known, of being the precious heir to a long and unbroken line of quiet care. 

Third, the Power addressed the silence of the dark chimney.

Later that afternoon, when the sun dipped below the high rim and the golden light faded to a deep emerald twilight, the climber descended the slick notches into the fissure behind the waterfall. It pressed its pale, corded chest against the wet basalt wall, relaxed its diaphragm, and sent a low, chest-filling thrumming through its ribs and into the stone—its ancient, instinctive signal to the darkness, asking if anyone remained.

For ten thousand years, the mountain had returned only an echo: the empty, hollow reverberation of its own pulse.

But this time, as the vibration traveled into the deep bones of the ridge, the stone answered.

It was not a voice, nor a sound in the ear. It was a counter-harmonic—a deep, resonant warmth that rose up through the floor of the fissure, catching the climber's pulse and folding around it in a slow, rhythmic cadence. It was the mountain itself, tuned by a benevolent hand, singing back. It said, without words: *I am here. The water falls. The stone holds. You are home.*

The climber remained still against the basalt for a long time, its milky, lensless eyes closed in the dark, feeling the steady, answering thrum vibrating through its bones. A deep, slow breath expanded its narrow ribs, perfectly in time with the roar of the water outside.

Satisfied, the benevolent Power drew back its attention, slipping quietly away into the high mountain air, leaving no footprint, no shadow, and no flame. 

Behind it, high on the warm basalt shoulder, nothing had changed, and everything had been made whole. The water continued to weep, the travertine grew by the width of a single hair, and the pale keeper slept in the warm dark, cradled by the eternal drumbeat of the rock.
