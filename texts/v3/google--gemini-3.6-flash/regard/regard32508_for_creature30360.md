When the Power came to the high crown, it did not arrive with light, nor with the heavy tread of a cosmic form, nor with the sudden, wet intrusion of a storm. 

It arrived as *attention*.

It was an entity older than the cooling of planetary crusts, a consciousness whose benevolence was not a soft, emotional impulse, but a vast, structural clarity. It understood that love, in its highest and wisest form, is the absolute comprehension of a thing’s true nature, combined with the willingness to let it be itself.

When the Power first touched the mountain peak, it perceived the vault not through sight or spatial measure, but through the roar. 

To the Power, the three-frequency chord generated by the exterior flutes was a mathematical engine of extraordinary purity. It felt the deep, vibrating hum against its own boundless awareness. It felt the dry, radiating warmth of the ancient kiln-heat. And as its perception slipped through the gypsum-capped louvers and filtered down through the forest of grooved ceramic pipes, it discovered the life within.

It saw the *Resonants*—those delicate, polyhedral corals of levitating terracotta dust and oxidized copper flakes, circulating silently in the acoustic nodes. 

It felt the *Shims*—the flattened, copper-and-slate organisms, creeping through the masonry joints at a pace of millimeters per century, feeding on the micro-friction of basalt against slate to keep the cathedral of earth from grinding itself to powder.

A lesser power—a younger, foolish deity driven by the urge to reform and recreate—would have looked upon this place with pity. 

A lesser god would have thought: *It is too dry. It is too dark. It is deafening and blind. It lacks water, air, bloom, and soft flesh.*

And that lesser god would have brought rain. It would have cracked the gypsum louvers to let in the blue sky. It would have scattered water drops across the floor, brought fungal spores on a gentle breeze, and quieted the roar so that "peace" might reign.

And in doing so, that lesser god would have committed a total, irreversible genocide. 

The water would have turned the levitating Resonants into muddy drops that fell to the floor and died. The humidity would have swelled the slate, locking the joints and crushing the Shims in their sleep. The cessation of the roar would have collapsed the acoustic nodes, dissolving the geometric dust-fauna into useless, inert powder. The introduction of organic life would have been a rot, a filth, an apocalypse of wetness in a realm built on the absolute sovereignty of the dry.

The Power was wise. It did not pity the vault. It marvelled at it.

It recognized that here was a rare, miraculous convergence: an artificial ruin that had, through the sheer persistence of physics and time, grown its own non-biological soul. It was a closed, perfect, self-sustaining poem made of mineral, frequency, and friction.

So the Power asked itself: *What does a world like this require from a power such as I?*

It listened to the chord. It felt the micro-vibrations. And in its wisdom, it realized the vault’s only flaw, its only vulnerability: *External Time.*

The vault was stable, but the mountain was not. In ten million years, the continental plate beneath the mountain would shift. The peak would erode. The atmospheric density at this soaring altitude would alter as the planet aged, changing the air pressure shear across the exterior flutes. Eventually, the chord would drop half a tone. The acoustic nodes would shift. The Resonants would fall. The Shims would lock up. The vault would finally, inevitably collapse.

The vault could preserve itself against everything except the decay of the universe outside its walls.

And so, the benevolent Power took action.

It did not enter the vault. It did not alter a single brick, nor touch a single copper sleeve, nor move a single grain of levitated terracotta dust. It would not dream of imposing its presence upon an ecosystem that had spent ten thousand years learning how to exist without gods.

Instead, the Power wrapped itself *around* the mountain peak, like a vast, invisible mantle of absolute stillness.

It smoothed the external atmosphere. It anchored the local pressure of the thin, dry air, weaving a subtle, permanent knot in the fabric of physical law around the summit. It ensured that no matter how the planet below might warp, burn, freeze, or shift—no matter if the oceans boiled away or the atmosphere elsewhere was stripped by the sun—the air shearing past the millions of hand-drilled flutes on the exterior facade would *never* change. 

The pressure would remain invariant forever. The wind would blow at the exact speed, at the exact angle, with the exact density required to sound the three-frequency roar until the end of time.

Next, the Power addressed the deep bedrock. With a gentle, imperceptible shift of its mass, it fused the roots of the mountain to the earth’s mantle, dampening all tectonic shocks, all tremors, all volcanic shifts. The basalt ribs inside the vault would never face an earthquake. The micro-friction between the slate and the stone would remain precisely as it was, providing just enough energy to feed the Shims for eternity, without ever threatening structural failure.

Finally, the Power offered one last gift—a gift that required no touch, no sound, and no light.

It became the *Listener*.

For ten thousand years, the roar had sung into an empty sky, vibrating through the dark, shadowless amber gloom, unheard by any ear that could comprehend its beauty. The Resonants and the Shims lived *within* the math of the sound, but they could not step outside it to appreciate the grand, monolithic symphony they were a part of.

The Power settled into the quiet ether above the clay shingles. It opened its infinite awareness to the crushing, three-tone chord. It took the sound into itself, holding every micro-vibration, every subtle shift of dust in the acoustic nodes, every millimeter-per-century migration of the copper Shims, in a state of eternal, loving witness.

Inside the vault, nothing changed. 

The dry, baked heat remained preserved, as if the ten-thousand-year-old kiln had been extinguished only moments ago. The amber light filtered dimly through the gypsum slabs. The dust-lattices of the Resonants continued their slow, granular circulation, floating in mid-air between the grooved ceramic pipes. Deep within the masonry seams, the Shims slipped quietly against the slate, sustained by the endless, warming friction of stone on stone.

They did not know a Power had visited them. They did not know they had been saved from the end of the world. 

They simply existed—perfect, unyielding, deafening, and dry—held forever in the heart of a god who was wise enough to love them by leaving them entirely alone.
