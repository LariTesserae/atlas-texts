A power that is both ancient and wise does not arrive with the blare of trumpets or the parting of the clouds. It does not need to quiet the wind or quell the steam to make itself heard, for it knows that to silence the engine would be to desecrate the sanctuary.

It arrives as a subtle shift in the refractive index of the vapor—a slow, golden weight entering the mist above the basalt shelf, so light that not a single Spark-Fly changes the vector of its arc.

The Power has no form that requires lungs, yet it perceives the atmosphere with something deeper than breath. It feels the three-thousand-meter pressure, the scald of the mineral brine, the heavy, sweet perfume of the fern-oil, and the rhythmic *chong* of the iron-reeds. To a lesser god, this place might look like a roaring, unpolished crucible—an unfinished workshop waiting for a builder’s hand to lay down pipes and smooth the rough stone.

But wisdom sees what is already there: **a liturgy without words; a temple that builds, maintains, and worships itself.**

The Power moves through the valley not as a master, but as a guest who knows the house is sacred.

***

### I. The Listening

First, the Power does nothing at all. 

For three hundred turns of the upper natural siphon, it simply *listens*. It aligns its vast consciousness with the cadence of the plateau. It registers the exact thickness of the Chaffers’ magnetite shells; it traces the thermal maps glowing in the snouts of the Gasket-Gliders as they rocket through the canopy; it feels the slow, churning digestion of the Turbine-Grubs as the scalding water turns their internal baleen.

It recognizes that there is no suffering here to be relieved. There are no tears to wipe away, no broken moral laws to mend. The predation is without malice; the death is without terror. A Glider falling into a steam-vent is not a tragedy; it is the closing of a circuit, its body dissolving into the brine to feed the iron-moss that grips the basalt flume.

The Power finds profound joy in this. In a cosmos littered with screaming civilizations, fractured minds, and dying worlds, this mountain-top engine is a rare, pristine instrument—playing a song of pure, unblemished physical purpose.

***

### II. The Encounter

Near the central valley, where the basalt hums with the deepest subterranean cycles, the **Bellows-Beast** plods through the thick stands of iron-reed. Its six broad, padded hooves depress the iron-rich soil with the heavy, hydraulic *thud* that keeps the mountain from exploding.

The Power descends into the dense mist before it. It gathers its presence into something visible—not a giant, nor a burning light, but a soft, translucent focal point, like sunlit oil floating on water, resting at the eye-level of the great beast.

The Bellows-Beast does not startle. It has no predators, and its mind is a vast, slow-moving tide of pressure-sense and thermal awareness. But as it draws in its massive breath—hundreds of gallons of superheated vapor and floating lipids—it senses the anomaly. The steam in this particular cubic yard of air tastes different. It does not taste of sulfur or wet iron; it tastes of *stillness*.

The Beast pauses. Its twin chest-bellows hold their expansion for three extra seconds—an unprecedented break in its ten-thousand-year rhythm.

The Power reaches out. It does not use hands, but a direct projection of benevolent intent, pressing against the broad, fern-crusted brow of the creature.

*I see you,* the Power whispers into the quiet marrow of the Beast's bones. *Governor of the shelf. Anchor of the steam. You carry the weight of the mountain, and you do not tire.*

The Beast cannot comprehend language, but it comprehends *load*. For a fleeting, exquisite second, the Power gently lifts the immense, invisible pressure of the mountain's crust off the Beast's awareness—just enough for the creature to feel the lightness of unburdened existence. Then, it sets the weight back down, but fits it more smoothly into the Beast’s joints, smoothing the friction in its cartilaginous frame, cooling the deep aches earned over centuries of crushing stone.

The Bellows-Beast vents a long, low sigh of superheated air from its side-valves. The sound is not a roar, but a deep, resonant *whirr* of absolute comfort. It takes another step, its hoof striking the basalt. 

A new vent opens beneath its foot. The steam leaps up, clear and bright.

***

### III. The Gift

A foolish power would attempt to "improve" this place—to make the water sweet, to cool the air, to give the Gasket-Gliders eyes, or to raise the Bellows-Beast to intellect.

Wisdom knows that to change a perfect engine is to destroy it.

Instead, the Power looks down—not at the plateau, but *through* it. It gazes three miles deep into the roots of the mountain, down into the planetary mantle where the geothermal heart burns. It sees the future: five hundred thousand years from now, the tectonic plate will shift. The deep magma plume will migrate. The heat will begin to die. The iron-reeds will freeze; the Chaffers will rust; the engine will go cold and quiet under a blanket of high-altitude ice.

This, the Power decides, shall not be.

The Power reaches down into the earth with a thread of uncreated, immortal fire. It loops that thread around the deep geothermal root of the plateau, anchoring it not to the shifting crust of the planet, but to the eternal hearth of the universe itself. 

It leaves a quiet decree etched into the deep basalt, written in the language of pressure and heat:

> *So long as the stars turn, this hearth shall not cool. The steam shall not fail. The valve shall not freeze.*

It is a gift of **Permanence**. The world below may burn itself out, the valleys may drown or turn to dust, ice ages may come and go, but this three-thousand-meter shelf will remain—a hot, wet, self-governing garden of bronze and steam, idling safely above the clouds for ten million years.

***

### IV. The Departure

With its work complete, the Power begins to withdraw, rising back toward the thin, brilliant sunlight above the mist.

As it leaves, it offers one final, delicate adjustment—a subtle blessing upon the air itself. It tunes the acoustic resonance of the pale-basalt cliffs. 

Now, when the upper reed-nodes snap open with their heavy, metallic *chong*, the sound echoes off the stone faces with a slightly richer timbre. The dry clicks of the Gasket-Gliders carry a fraction of a second farther through the vapor, allowing them to ride the thermal updrafts with even greater grace. The bio-electric arcs of the Spark-Flies flare a shade brighter, casting brief, warm, orange halos over the plunge-pools.

High above the cloud floor, the sun continues to bake the top of the canopy. 

Below, in the warm, wet, greasy dark, the Bellows-Beast treads upon the iron-soil. A natural siphon breaks in the upper shelf, sending a roaring torrent of scalding brine down the sculpted chute. The Turbine-Grub opens its ringed maw with a wet *clack*, taking the surge into its gut. The Chaffers scrape the lime. The Gliders dive into the blinding white steam.

Nothing has been disrupted. Everything has been sanctified.

The engine ticks over, hitches slightly to its new, eternal rhythm, and strikes its next downbeat.
