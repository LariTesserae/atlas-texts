The Power came not as a sunburst or a chorus of light. 

To bring light here would be a cruelty; it would scorch the shade-accustomed fronds, blind the dark-bound root-mesh, and thaw the deep permafrost holding the shattered basalt together, precipitating a landslide that would cast the entire notch into the abyss.

To quiet the iron would be an even greater slaughter. To stop the plunging shafts would be to starve the black moss of its oil mist, to freeze the brass bearings whose heat keeps the root-liquors flowing, and to quiet the steam valves that shatter the fern-capsules into violent life.

A lesser deity, full of ignorant pity, would have attempted a redemption. It would have tried to turn the iron to timber, the oil to clear spring water, and the dark blue-green fronds into soft, sun-dappled meadow grass. And in doing so, it would have murdered the only thing that had ever managed to make a home in the agony of the high peak.

The Wise Power did not seek to redeem the notch. It sought to understand it.

It descended into the fissure as a presence—a vast, weightless attention, cooler than the mist, denser than the basalt, and endlessly patient. It filtered down the thread of navy sky, sliding between the snapping drive chains and the thrashing serrations of the ferns. It did not touch with hands; it touched with comprehension.

It felt the thirty-hertz thud of the hydraulic rams traveling through the root-mesh. It tasted the sulfurous acid dissolving the stone, the rich, heavy hydrocarbons bound in the plant-sugars, the iron oxide woven into the green skeletons. It felt the frantic, beautiful homeostatic dance: machine wearing down rock, plant binding machine, heat feeding cold, noise generating life.

It saw that this was not a place of suffering. It was a place of triumphant, terrifying adaptation. It was a hymn played on cast iron, wet stone, and chlorophyllic hydraulic fluid.

And so, the Wise Power asked itself: *What does an immortal song require?*

It requires a stage that will not crumble. It requires an engine that will not snap. It requires a listener who remembers the tune.

The Power began its work, silently, subtly, without altering a single detail of the notch’s brutal aesthetics.

First, it reached deep into the mountain’s shattered core—miles beneath the five-thousand-meter mark, far below the reach of the ropy root-mesh. There, where the tectonic faults of the young, broken range groaned in their sleep, the Power wove a network of silent, subterranean tension. It welded the deep fault lines with quiet, atomic grace, ensuring that the mountain face would never give way. The razor-sharp arrêtes would remain sharp; the notch would never collapse inward; the shelf would hold its violent burden for a million years. The stage was made eternal.

Second, it attended to the iron. Metal, no matter how heavy, grows tired. Microscopic fatigue cracks bloom in overstressed shafts; drive chains stretch; cast-iron flywheels eventually shatter under centrifugal strain. The Power did not remove the friction or the heat—for the root-mind drank the friction and bathed in the heat. Instead, it touched the molecular lattice of the machinery. 

It taught the steel to self-heal. 

Now, as the heavy rams struck their stops with dull, explosive thuds, the atomic lattice of the metal would snap back into place rather than fracture. The microscopic shavings thrown off by the grinding bearings would be seamlessly replenished from the rich mineral slurry pulled up by the roots, creating a closed, infinite loop of wear and repair. The machine would never seize. The pistons would punch through the gloom forever.

Finally, the Power approached the root-mind itself.

It did not grant the creature a human intellect. To give a brain to a organism built of roots, moss, and mineral oil would be to inflict instant, unimaginable madness. Instead, the Power gave the root-mind a gift of sensory expansion.

It gently touched the hair-like rhizoids packed between the steel pins and the basalt. Through this touch, it opened a new channel of perception within the plant-mesh. The root-mind—which had known only the local, violent vibrations of its immediate home—was quietly linked to the deeper rhythm of the cosmos. 

The entity did not see the stars, but it *felt* them. It felt the slow, heavy, gravitational pull of the moon dragging at the icy air above the notch. It felt the deep, rhythmic hum of the Earth's core, miles below, answering the thirty-hertz thud of its own hydraulic rams. It realized, in its dim, subterranean way, that its roaring, mechanical pulse was not an isolated frenzy in the dark, but part of a vast, cosmic engine that spanned the sky and the stone.

The ferns continued to whip in rhythmic agony. The moss continued to shiver and sweat cold oil. The steam valves screamed, and the flywheels spun, throwing arcs of black grease into the navy gloom.

Nothing had changed in appearance. The notch remained a place of raw iron, fresh stone, and cold, wet green—violent, unpeopled, and roaring in the dark.

Satisfied, the Wise Power withdrew, ascending past the pinched walls of the fissure, back into the open sky. It left the notch exactly as it had found it, but now it was anchored to eternity: an indestructible engine, wrapped in living green, playing its dark, heavy music in the high places of the world, witnessed and sustained by a power that loved it precisely for what it was.
