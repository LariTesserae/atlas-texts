The Power arrived not with light—for light would have blinded the white fish in the sump—nor with thunder, which would have disrupted the three-part cadence of the iron. 

It arrived as awareness. It descended through the saturated mud of the lower bottoms, passing through thirty feet of ancient, waterlogged clay, until it touched the cold outer face of the northern concrete wall.

A foolish power, seeing this place, would have torn open the concrete ceiling. It would have cast down sunlight, swept away the black grease, dispersed the hot steam with a clean mountain breeze, and carried the stooped man up into a meadow of clover. And in doing so, it would have committed a cruelty. The white fish would have boiled in the light; the deaf rats would have died of terror in the wide, empty air; the tender’s eyes would have shredded in the dry sun, his lungs collapsed without the heavy, wet blanket of vaporized oil he had breathed for forty years.

The Power was wise. It understood that holiness is not a matter of white marble and high arches; holiness is any place where a life and its realm have achieved a absolute, unconflicted balance.

It looked through the concrete, through the thick, grey mist, and saw the man. It watched him step between the whipping belts of vulcanized rubber without looking. It watched him skin a fat frog with a thumb-nail tear, drop the skin to the midges, and chew with a quiet, rhythm-matched jaw. It saw that he was not miserable. He was complete. He was a gear that had found its housing.

Yet, benevolence cannot look upon a home without offering a gift. It does not alter; it sanctifies. It does not rescue; it sustains.

So the wise Power did three things.

First, it touched the metal. 

It reached into the heavy iron casings of the green centrifugal pumps, into the bronze bushings and the main drive cylinders where the friction of endless motion constantly threatened to heat the metal to failure. It did not stop the wear, for to stop the wear would be to change the machine’s sound, and the tender lived by that sound. Instead, it smoothed the microscopic grain of the iron. It turned the abrasive drag into a perfect, frictionless glide. The brass grease-lines would never clog; the vulcanized rubber belts would never fray or snap; the four discharge pipes would flow with their heavy, black water until the city above crumbled into dust. The machine was granted its immortality, ensuring that the man’s world would never break beneath him.

Second, it touched the living.

It breathed upon the blind, candle-white fish, making their cold flesh resilient, their small hearts untiring. It touched the dark-shelled snails, ensuring that the black-green algae on the concrete would always bloom just fast enough to feed them, never choking the intake grates, never starving the herd. It touched the deaf rats, taking away the pain of their worn joints and old injuries, leaving them to walk the hot return conduits in perpetual, wet-furred ease.

Third, and finally, it touched the man.

It did not change his skin, which remained soft, pink, and smudged with graphite. It did not open his ears to the deafening roar of the pit. Instead, as he lay down in his triangular gap behind the western pump—laying his heap of oiled hemp rope and burlap sacks—the Power reached through the concrete wall against which he pressed his forehead.

The man shifted in his sleep, his bare skin resting against the cold, raw grain of the pine-board impression. 

Usually, he felt only the dead, heavy resistance of the wet earth outside, pushing silently against the hot iron heart of the node. But tonight, as his forehead touched the stone, the Power let a feeling pass through the wall.

It was not a thought, nor a vision. It was a profound, bone-deep resonance. It was the feeling of the earth acknowledging him—a vast, patient presence holding him from behind, while the pounding machine held him from the front. It brought him a sleep so deep, so entirely free of fear or cold, that his ragged breathing synced perfectly with the *thwack-thwack-thwack* of the drive belts. 

The Power did not stay. It had no need to be worshipped, nor did it require a shrine in the mud. 

It withdrew back up through the saturated clay, leaving the yellow-grey twilight untouched, the steam rising unbroken from the sump, and the small, heavy machine-pocket locked in its endless, sweltering, and now perfectly guarded present.
