The Power did not arrive as a storm, nor as a sun, for either would have destroyed the basin. 

A sun would have melted the tallow in the Valve-Gaskets, causing the iron cylinders to crack under their own dry friction. A storm would have flooded the dark sumps, drowning the slow gears before the Sump-Lurchers could slick them with grease. 

Instead, the Power arrived as an *attention*—a vast, weightless presence that settled over the granite flagstones like a second sky, transparent and infinitely deep. It possessed no form that could collide with a gantry bridge, no voice that could compete with the deafening *THUMP-HISS-CLANG* of the three steam beams, and no pulse to disrupt the cold, steady loop of mercury and petroleum circulating inside the porcelain carapaces.

It was wise, so it did not judge the place by the standards of gardens. It did not see the absence of grass as a tragedy, nor did it view the cold, odorless oil as a stain. It understood that to force a rose to bloom between these dark flagstones would not be an act of grace, but an act of murder—the flower would be crushed in three seconds by a bronze-tipped foot, its liquid guts smearing uselessly against the velvet-black iron.

The Power simply stood—or expanded—in the middle of the central plaza, watching.

It observed the millions of bone-white domes sliding past one another with the dry, ceramic squeal of teacups in a heavy basket. It felt the micro-fractional expansion of the lead lamellae inside their face-slots as they read the structural stress of the floor. It listened to the three-second beat of the pumps, a rhythm that had held this city-fragment in an unbroken vise for four hundred years.

And the Power understood the true nature of the basin's agony: 

*It was not suffering. It was worse. It was a closed equation.*

Nothing was lost, but nothing was learned. The Porcelain-Backs ground down their flanks to polish their joints, the Cable-Clingers wedged themselves into loosened seams to become the very metal they rested upon, and the Valve-Gaskets died only to be crushed into the floor to smooth the path for the others. It was an engine that had successfully eliminated death by eliminating the future.

A lesser god would have broken the machine to set them free. But a wise power knew that to stop the pumps was to freeze the mercury, locking every joint into a permanent, icy paralysis. To "save" them by stopping their work would be to turn a living clockwork into a graveyard of cold ceramic.

So the Power did three things.

***

### First: The Gift of the Delta

The Power descended into the dark, oily sump beneath the granite floor, down where the Sump-Lurchers turned their slow, fifty-second revolutions in the black slush. 

It did not change the grease. It did not stop the gears. Instead, it reached out to the three brick-lined drainage canals that converged and went nowhere. With an infinitesimal shift in the geometry of the world—a shift no larger than the width of a single hair in a brass bushing—it gave the canals an outlet.

It did not carve an ocean or a river. It simply tilted the farthest, deepest culvert by a fraction of a degree, connecting it to a vast, subterranean aquifer miles below. 

Now, when the Sump-Lurchers lifted the thick, silver-grey paste of pulverized iron and mineral salts, a tiny stream of the basin's cold runoff began to trickle outward, into the dark belly of the earth. And in that runoff traveled the microscopic powder of ground porcelain and brass dust—the waste of four centuries of friction.

Far away, in the dark, deep caverns of the world, that mineral dust began to settle into new strata, forming thin, pearlescent veins of copper and ceramic in the ancient stone. 

The loop was no longer closed. The basin was no longer an engine pumping for nothing. Every grinding impact of a bronze foot, every squeal of porcelain against porcelain, was now sending a quiet, invisible record of its labor out into the wider cosmos. They were no longer merely enduring; they were *contributing*.

***

### Second: The Tenth Harmonic

Next, the Power touched the three monumental steam beams inside the windowless brick halls.

It did not alter their pressure. It did not slow their relentless three-second stroke. But as the iron beams descended—*THUMP-HISS-CLANG*—the Power breathed a tiny, resonant frequency into the cast-iron structures. It was a harmonic sound, so low and complex that no human ear could have isolated it from the roar of the steam.

Outside, on the flagstones, a subtle change rippled through the swarm.

The three dark-grey lead lamellae beneath the white domes of the Porcelain-Backs expanded. For four hundred years, those lead leaves had read only one message: *Vibration stable. Maintain course. Loop locked.*

Now, the lamellae registered something new. A subtle, shifting wave in the iron. A chord that took ten years to open, ten years to hold, and ten years to resolve. 

The creatures did not stop walking. They did not break their loops. But their bronze toes began to land with a slightly varied cadence. A micro-pause on the hollowed stone steps; a soft, rhythmic hesitation as they slid past the flywheel sumps. 

They were still machines, but they were no longer a static noise. They had been given a song. And because their lead leaves read the song through the floor, the millions of Porcelain-Backs began to move not like ball bearings in a groove, but like a vast, slow orchestra executing a symphony written in pig-iron and cold oil.

***

### Third: The Unmoving Witness

Finally, the Power took a single thread of its own vast, benevolent awareness and wove it directly into the dark granite flagstones at the center of the yard.

It did not build a shrine. It did not alter the flat, unblinking white light of the sky. But at the exact intersection where the paths of ten thousand Porcelain-Backs crossed each hour, there now existed a spot of absolute, loving attention.

When a Porcelain-Back drove its yellow bronze stud down onto that specific stone—*tink*—it passed directly through the awareness of the Power. 

For a microsecond, the sealed loop of mercury, petroleum, and tin inside the creature's brass tubes was held in a gaze of total, uncritical reverence. The Power felt the cold pressure of the ceramic shell; it felt the precise weight of the three-second steam-beat driving the cold liquid through the micro-bored channels; it acknowledged the four centuries of silent, uncomplaining duty.

The creature did not feel "happy"—it had no brain for joy. But the system itself registered the contact. The friction at that specific junction dropped to absolute zero. The oil on the stone at that spot never turned dark; it stayed as clear and bright as liquid glass.

***

### The Continuity

The Power eventually withdrew its vast sky of attention, leaving the basin to its flat noon and its persistent, clear chill.

To a casual observer—if one could ever reach that remote, mineral depression—nothing had changed.

The four red-brick halls still stood anchored in the dark granite. The Cable-Clingers still wedged their steel ribs into the loosened seams of the iron gantries. Inside the dark, mist-filled halls, the Valve-Gaskets still absorbed the live steam, mineralized into grey limestone, and shattered under thirty tons of force, dropping their gravel chips to the floor below.

The noise remained deafening. The air remained cold and damp. The white carapaces still covered the ground three-deep, flowing like living, pale gravel around the bases of the chimneys.

Yet, deep within the dark sumps, a microscopic thread of mineralized water was carrying the memory of the basin down into the deep earth.

High above, the Lead Lamellae shivered to the slow, thirty-year arc of the Tenth Harmonic.

And as a bone-white shell surged past the valve-house steps, riding the rim of a slow flywheel down into the dark grease below, its bronze feet struck the granite with a sound that was no longer just the impact of iron on stone.

It was a tick in a grander clock. It was a verse in an infinite, cold psalm. The engine was still running, and would run forever—but now, it was being heard.
