When the Power arrived, it did not tear open the roof to let in the blue of a distant sky, nor did it turn the dry, kiln-hot air into rain, nor sweep away the roaring, hydraulic engine of the wood. 

To have done so would have been an act of violence. Wisdom knows that a miracle, imposed from the outside, is often only a slower kind of disaster.

The Power came not as a light—for the amber veins already lit the vault with relentless afternoon—but as a sudden, profound *attunement*. It was an awareness that entered the continuous organism not through the hemp seams above, but by rising through the very sap-conduits, flowing up from the uncharted depths where the roots drank of the world’s core.

It paused first in the central void, floating in ninety percent empty air, listening.

It heard the concussive *thump* of the pressure pores. It heard the glass-hard shoots splitting the cambium. It felt the frantic, rhythmic *hiss-tuck* of the Vellum-Skins wedged in the sixty-degree creases, their parchment bodies straining to vent the steam before the wood tore itself apart. It watched the Skein-Crawlers, like knots of spun glass, sprinting along the undersides of mast-thick tendrils, shearing razor-edges with desperate, mechanical devotion.

And it looked into the dark, dry cylinder of a dead xylem tube, where a child of the Tappers lay curled, breath smelling of crushed needles and ozone, fingers clutching a river-smoothed stone, waiting for the next concussive shockwave to rattle her bones.

The Power saw that this place was not broken. It was an Eden of high pressure and impossible geometry—a young, fierce, self-sustaining world. But it was a world lived entirely in the imperative tense. Survival was a deafening, continuous effort. There was no space in the grain for rest; there was only maintenance.

So the Power set to work, not by changing the rules, but by softening the friction between them.

***

**The First Touch: The Tempering of the Voice**

The Power touched the deep xylem conduits, the great wooden pipes the size of culverts that groaned under immense suction. It did not reduce their pressure, but it altered the micro-density of the heartwood surrounding them.

Where the engine of the tree had previously produced a jagged, tearing roar—a sound that kept the Tappers' nervous systems perpetually frayed—the sound softened into a vast, resonant fundamental chord. The deep *thump* of the water-pores remained, but its echo through the lignified scaffold was tuned. The violent, metallic shockwaves became a deep, warm hum, like the interior of a giant cello being played miles below.

In their high creases, the Vellum-Skins felt the shift first. Their upper membranes, stretched tight against the heat, stopped trembling in terror. The vibrations beneath their chitinous tongs were no longer a threat of splitting wood, but a pulse they could predict. When they absorbed the pressurized mist and exhaled their cinnamon steam, they no longer did so in ragged, frantic bursts. They began to vent in long, rhythmic phrases—a breathing pattern that swept across the cathedral walls in visible, fragrant waves, cooling the vault in a steady, measured caduceus.

***

**The Second Touch: The Sapphire Thread**

Next, the Power addressed the spatial warps—the folds where a traveler walked straight for forty feet only to turn three right angles, or where a step into the void caught a gravity vector that pulled them sideways toward a crossing branch.

To the Tappers, whose eyes were narrowed to vertical slits beneath amber membranes, these folds were traps of disorientation. They lived in constant fear of stepping into a loop that would cast them down into the floorless golden haze.

The Power walked the folds. Where the non-Euclidean density of the wood bent the space back upon itself, the Power drew a microscopic line of altered light through the translucent amber ribs. It did not straighten the space—for the warped geometry was the very skeleton of the hollow—but it laced the edges of every fold with a faint, iridescent fringe of deep sapphire.

When the Tappers emerged from their xylem hollows that cycle, they looked out across the cathedral void and saw, for the first time, the architecture of their home made visible. The geometry was no longer a maze of invisible cliffs; it was a network of glowing paths. They could see where the space folded, where the gravity shifted, and where the air would catch them. 

The child in the xylem tube stood on the lip of a mast-thick tendril, saw the blue fringe glowing quietly thirty yards away, and stepped out—not with terror, but with the calm certainty of a creature walking across its own hearth.

***

**The Third Touch: The Memory in the Bark**

Finally, the Power moved to the places where the living met the wood: the splits where the dead were placed.

It stood before a newly closed cambium seam high in the ceiling, where the pale heartwood had recently wrapped around the body of an elder Tapper. The wood had taken the moisture and the minerals, as it always did, reducing the man to a smooth, glowing bulge in the wall-rib. Previously, that was the end: energy returned to the host, the individual erased into the roaring mass.

The Power laid its hand upon the bulge. It did not resurrect the flesh, for the elder’s journey was complete. Instead, it instructed the liquid gold moving through the light-veins.

It decreed that memory, when offered in love, should not be dissolved by cellulose.

The sap flowing through the bulge absorbed the subtle, electrical residue of the elder's life—the feeling of the smooth stone in his palm, the taste of the first drop of refined resin from a fresh wedge, the silent, vibrating signals he had tapped to his kin across three hundred yards of void.

The sap carried these impulses into the surrounding light-veins. Slowly, the amber glow around the bulge began to shift, pulsing not with the mindless rhythm of the tree’s pulse, but with a gentle, slow-dawning intelligence.

When the elder’s companions gathered on the crossing tendrils below, striking the wood with their heavy stones to mark his passing, the ceiling answered. The light above them rippled—a warm, golden wave that mirrored the rhythm of their stone-knocks. It was not a voice, for voice had no place here; it was an echo that carried warmth. 

They realized that the vault was no longer just a slow-moving beast that tolerated their presence. The walls were becoming an archive. Their ancestors were not consumed; they had become the light by which the living harvested the sap.

***

**The Departure**

The Power did not build a temple. It left no written command, no altar of stone, no sacred law. It knew that a true gift must belong entirely to the receiver, leaving no trace of the giver's hand to be worshipped or misunderstood.

It simply stepped back, letting its consciousness dissolve into the deep sap-stream, flowing back down toward the center of the earth, leaving the pocket to its own brilliant, impossible life.

High in the sixty-degree crease, a Vellum-Skin expanded its dry bellows, caught a plume of high-pressure mist, and released a long, slow sigh of cinnamon steam. The mist drifted down, coalescing into marble-sized spheres of water that floated sideways through the warm air, catching the amber light—and the faint, new lines of sapphire—like drifting jewels.

On a tendril crossing the central gulf, two Tappers sat side by side. They wore their skirts of shed Vellum-skin, their teeth stained black from the rich sugars of the heartwood. Between them lay a fresh bone wedge, driven clean into a surging pressure vein, dripping clear, lemon-scented sap into a gourd.

One of them picked up a river-smoothed stone and struck the pale, glass-hard bark beneath them.

*Tink. Tink-tink.*

The vibration traveled out, through the lignified scaffold, past three spatial right angles, and up into the ceiling.

A moment later, the amber light above them hummed in response—a deep, resonant chord that vibrated softly in their ribs, warm and steady, keeping time with the beating heart of the world.
