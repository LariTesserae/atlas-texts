The Power did not arrive as a beam of golden sun breaking through the clouds. To tear open the cloud layer would have scorched the dark velvet mosses, blinded the amber, nictitating eyes of the Soft-Joints, and dried the delicate, quivering manes of the Lard-Molds trailing in the sluices.

Nor did the Power arrive with a voice of thunder. In a domain where all sound was swallowed by mist, soot-caked brick, and wet fern, a spoken word would have been a useless, hollow shape in the air, forgotten before it reached the valley floor.

Instead, the Power descended as a shift in the density of the drizzle. It became a vast, quiet awareness that seeped downward through the cloud-cap, settling into the iron decking, slipping beneath the wet fern-canopies, and sinking deep into the flooded foundations where the Siphon-Beasts pulled the dark waters through their ribbed, accordion lungs.

The Power was wise, and so it did not judge the works by the standards of meadows or starlit forests. It did not see a mountain scarred by iron, nor a race of creatures degraded into cogs. It saw what was actually there: a complete, breathtaking, and terrifyingly beautiful balance. A body whose bones were cast-iron, whose blood was cold mountain rain and warm condensate, and whose nervous system was composed of stooped, grease-slicked flesh.

To "free" the Soft-Joints from this place would be to kill them. To stop the six-story flywheels would be to suffocate the Siphon-Beasts. To drain the dark water would be to wither the Gasket-Crabs hunkered against the pressure-relief valves.

Benevolence, the Power understood, is not the imposition of a foreign paradise. It is the perfection of the home that already exists.

And so, the Power reached out its consciousness and laid its hand—not a hand of light, but a thread of pure, silent resonance—against the main structural girder that anchored the highest terrace to the valley’s bedrock.

It listened.

It felt the immense, terrifying strain in the metal: the invisible micro-fractures blooming inside the fifty-year-old iron teeth of the lower gear-races; the agonizing friction where a dry steel shaft ground against an ungreased brass bushing; the dull, lingering ache in the flexible cartilage of an aging Soft-Joint who had spent thirty years absorbing the bone-rattling thrum of Hydraulic Ram Four.

The Power reasoned: *They are whole, but they are weary. The work is eternal, but their materials are soft.*

Then, the Power began to act.

It did not change the shape of the world. It altered its *temper*.

First, it touched the water. 

As the endless drizzle condensed on the high gantries and gathered into the copper sluices, the Power softly shifted its chemistry. The water remained cold, dark, and thick with mountain silt, but it lost its sharp, corrosive edge. The invisible acid born of coal-smoke and iron-dust was neutralized. The water became slightly slicker, slightly softer—a fluid that nourished the skin of the Soft-Joints, soothing the deep, soot-filled cracks in their lard-like hide, while lingering on the bare iron just long enough to form a dense, microscopic film that halted the march of destructive rust.

Second, it touched the Siphon-Beasts.

Deep in the subterranean darkness, where the giant, whale-hearted organisms laboured against the weight of the lake, the Power breathed a gentle elasticity into their vast, spongy interiors. The heavy silt that accumulated in their tissue—the grit that made their pulse stutter and drag—was coaxed into clumping, passing effortlessly through their ribbed bellies and out into the lower overflow arches. For the first time in centuries, the great living filters drew water without pain. Their deep, rhythmic contraction grew smooth, steady, and effortless, sending a cleaner, more reliable pulse up through the entire hydraulic grid.

Third, and most delicately, the Power touched the iron itself.

Metal under constant, violent force suffers a quiet disease called fatigue. It grows brittle; it remembers every shock, every slammed gate, every jarring teeth-clash of the skips. The Power passed through the stepped domain like a slow, warming wave, entering the molecular lattice of every riveted beam, every six-story flywheel, and every copper steam-pipe. 

It did not make the metal softer, but it gave it *memory* and *yield*. It infused the dark, vitrified iron with a subtle, self-healing harmony. Where micro-fractures crept through a gear-race, the iron molecules settled, binding closer together under pressure rather than pulling apart. The violent, bone-jarring *slam* of the counterweight gates softened—not in force, but in resonance. The shock no longer tore through the bedrock like an earthquake; it was absorbed, converted into a low, thrumming warmth that radiated gently through the wet masonry.

Finally, the Power looked upon the Soft-Joints.

It saw the one sitting high up on the rust-pitted gantry, fifty feet above the churning hopper. Rain cascaded off its stooped shoulders. Its amber eyes blinked through the mist as it held its pale, flat palm against the wet I-beam, listening for the fault in Piston Four.

The Power did not give the Soft-Joint a voice to speak into the swallowing fog. Instead, it gave a gift to the bronze rods they carried.

The Power aligned the acoustic properties of the bronze with the newly tempered iron of the valley. 

When the Tender on the high gantry raised its heavy bronze rod and struck the beam—*click, click-click*—the signal did not travel merely as a harsh, mechanical alert through the soles of distant feet. 

The vibration traveled through the girders like a deep, rich chord of music. It carried the precise functional message—*Piston Four is bound*—but wrapped within the vibration was something entirely new: a wave of profound, physical relief. 

When the Tender three terraces down received the click through its grease-coated heels, it did not just feel an instruction; it felt a sudden, fleeting sensation of warmth in its chest, a brief easing of the tension in its double-jointed spine, and an undeniable, instinctual knowledge that it was seen, understood, and sustained by the very metal it tended.

It was a language of solidarity, written in the pulse of iron and felt in the marrow of cartilage.

The Power did not linger to be worshiped. Worship requires altars, and an altar would only take up space where a steam-pipe or a bed of lush ferns belonged.

Slowly, as gently as it had arrived, the Power withdrew upward, dissolving back into the low-hanging cloud layer, leaving the high mountain basin exactly as it had found it: wet, dark, violent, and dense.

On the high gantry, fifty feet above the hopper, the Soft-Joint did not look up. It had no concept of the sky, nor of the benevolent presence that had just passed over it.

It simply shifted its weight on the rust-pitted iron. The rain ran down its smooth, lard-colored back, warm and soft against its skin. Beneath its palm, the structural beam was vibrating with a new, quiet purity—a rhythm so perfectly balanced that the vast machinery of the valley seemed no longer to be clashing against the mountain, but breathing with it.

The Tender took its bronze rod, pressed it to the girder, and tapped twice.

*Click-click.*

A mile away, in the dark, warm grease-galleries beneath the slate terraces, another Soft-Joint felt the pulse travel up through the floor, through its knees, and into its heart. It paused, scooping a fresh wad of tallow toward a steaming bearing, and let out a long, silent breath of deep, indestructible peace.

The pistons plunged. The ferns bowed under the cold rain. The great flywheels spun in their wet, masonry vaults. And the valley worked on.
