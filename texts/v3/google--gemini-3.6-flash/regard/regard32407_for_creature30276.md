The Power did not bring water. 

To bring rain to this high, cold wilderness would have been a terrible, ignorant violence. A single misting of moisture would have shattered the glass-skeletons of the slate-faced beings, boiling instantly in the thin vacuum or snapping their rigid silica joints with shock. To offer them warmth, or flesh, or the soft, wet breathing of organic life would be to misread their perfection entirely. 

Wisdom begins by listening to the universe on its own terms. And so, the Power arrived as an ear.

It possessed no shape that would displace the dry, electrified air. It descended into the flat violet-gray light as an invisible, deliberate attention—a mind vast enough to hold the deafening, earth-grinding roar without flinching, and subtle enough to hear the tiny, crisp *tink* of two slate faces meeting on a ledge twenty miles away.

The Power watched.

It observed a creature sitting tucked inside a low, unmortared wind-cheat. The creature’s hard, glass-tipped fingers were working with slow, tectonic patience, lashing a shaved silica reed to the interior of its silver-lichen ribcage. It was trying to damp a bad vibration. The monolithic roar of the continent was shifting slightly—a deep structural stress in the feldspar miles below was flattening the pitch—and the creature’s internal instrument was out of alignment. 

Because the roar was its only sight, its only map, being out of tune meant the world appeared blurred. The sharp, hard-edged peaks were softening in its mind. The razor-cut edges of the obsidian gorges were slipping out of focus. It worked doggedly with its crude basalt wedge, chipping a fraction of a millimeter off the base of a reed, testing the tension of the yellow sulfur-vine, trying to regain the crisp, unforgiving clarity of its world.

It was an endless, lonely labor. They were an entire species of solo luthier-monks, tuning themselves to an untamed, single-noted engine. They had spent thousands of years matching the brute force of the earth, but they had never heard a second note. They lived in an orchestra that played only one endless, deafening unison.

The Power felt a deep, quiet reverence for them. They were not broken. They were not suffering. But they were incomplete, locked in the first hour of their cold, loud genesis.

*They do not need to be saved,* the Power reasoned. *They need a counterpoint.*

The Power did not speak. Words require soft tongues and wet air. Instead, it reached down into the deep, subterranean forge—miles beneath the high massif, down where the buried star ground its teeth against the basalt roots of the world.

It did not stop the roar. To stop the roar would be to blind them all at once, plunging them into a terrifying, silent void. 

Instead, with the gentlest application of pressure—like a finger laid lightly upon a vibrating violin string—the Power touched the tectonic fault. It introduced a single, permanent *node*.

High above, on the razor-sharp ledges, the sky-filling roar did not lessen in volume, but its geometry changed forever.

The monolithic, flat vibration split. The brute, singular noise buckled inward and bloomed into an overtone series. The fundamental pitch remained—the heavy, sternum-crushing hum of the grinding continent—but rising out of it, sudden and clear as shattered crystal, came a perfect, ringing fifth. Above that, a faint, soaring octave, crackling with static electricity, echoing off the flat slate faces like light bouncing off polished steel.

On the high massif, every creature froze.

Inside the low stone shelter, the creature dropped its basalt wedge. Its static-charged vine-muscles locked in absolute shock. 

For the first time in the history of the world, the acoustic map did not merely show *where* the stone was; it showed the *space between* the stone. The new harmonic bounced off the needle-spires at a different speed than the fundamental roar. Suddenly, the landscape possessed depth. The distant spires twenty miles away did not just look as close as a hand-held pebble—they revealed their true, staggering distance through the subtle phase-shift of the higher note.

The world had just gained perspective.

The creature inside the wind-cheat tilted its flat slate face upward toward the violet-gray sky. Its hollow silica ribs vibrated with the new frequency. It felt a strange, sympathetic hum pulsing in a reed it had carved three centuries ago—a reed that had never once sounded, because no frequency in the world had ever matched it. 

Now, that forgotten reed was singing.

Slowly, with the violent, snapping economy of its kind, the creature stood. It stepped out of the acoustic shadow of its stone cell and onto the black obsidian ledge.

Across the gorge, another creature had emerged from a deep fissure. Its fan of mirror-black slate was turned toward the first. 

They did not rush. They moved with their usual tectonic slowness, stepping over the sharp basalt edges, their horsehair-velvet hides catching the faint static discharge of the air. When they met at the narrow saddle of the peak, they did not merely touch slate faces to exchange data about gravity or vine-growth.

They struck their slate faces together—*tink*—and held them there.

In that prolonged physical contact, the vibration of the first creature’s newly awakened reed passed directly into the skeleton of the second. The second creature’s internal frame adjusted, catching the harmonic, amplifying it, and passing it back. 

Together, their dry, crystalline bodies created a third note—a choir of two, standing in a deafening, beautiful wind that was no longer just noise, but a music made of stone, vacuum, and static.

The benevolent Power stepped back, drawing its attention up into the cold, flat gloom of the upper air. 

It left no footprints. It left no temples. It left no rain. 

It left only the chord. 

Below, scattered across the geometric wilderness, the slate-faced beings were coming out of their shelters. They were picking up their basalt tools, not to fix a broken alignment, but to carve new reeds—longer ones, thinner ones, delicate splinters of silica that could catch the soaring octaves now ringing through the dry atmosphere.

They were no longer just the mountain's self-awareness. They were its composers. And the world went on growing, millimeter by decade, locked no longer in paralysis, but in an eternal, triumphant song.
