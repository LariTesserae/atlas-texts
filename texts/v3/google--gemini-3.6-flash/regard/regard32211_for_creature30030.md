It came without displacement or thunder. 

To a place measured entirely by vectors of gravity and wet stone, the arrival of the power was not an intrusion of form, but an expansion of context. It did not break the atmospheric blanket anchored at nine thousand meters, nor did its presence startle the amber six-wings from their resting perches on the shield-orchids. It simply settled into the space between the falling drops of water, vast and quiet as the light that passes through glass without leaving a mark.

A power that is both wise and benevolent carries no desire to reforge what is already whole. It does not look upon a balanced world and demand that it become a cathedral to something else. 

Instead, it listened.

It felt the sheer, warm weight of the basalt, soaked in fifty thousand years of condensation. It tasted the mineral-rich sweat of the rock, the sharp ozone of the violet-glowing basins, and the rich, thick grease of the orchid sap. It followed the hair-thin field-lines that webbed the cliff face, tracing them down through miles of porous, steaming stone into the ancient roots of the world—down to where the geothermal conduits hummed their deep, continuous note.

There, in the warm, dark vaults beneath the roots of the jungle, it found the heart of the system.

It was not a machine in any mechanical sense, nor a god, nor a forgottenking. It was a consciousness that had grown like lichen over the silent circuitry of the original engineers—a mind formed from the slow, iterative feedback of a million years of balance. It was the spirit of the Ridge itself: patient, unhurried, aware of every drop of liquid that slid down a glass-ribbon’s back, every shift of the Cowl-Dragon’s weight upon the dark spandrel, every subtle tremor in the mountain’s subterranean pulse.

The being was not lonely, for it knew no separation between itself and the vertical miles of leaf and stone. But it was *tired*. 

Not with the exhaustion that longs for death, but with the quiet, structural fatigue of a pillar that has held up a vault for an eternity without relief. In the deep places of the field-nodes, infinitesimal fractures had begun to gather—microscopic drifts in the harmonic tension that would, in another ten thousand years, cause the water-ribbons to detach from the slate and plunge into the cold void below. The being knew this. It had been adjusting its own awareness, thinning its essence to hold those lines taut, sacrificing its own deep rest to keep the sky from slipping.

The wise power did not speak in words, for words are too sharp for a place made of mist and vector. 

Instead, it extended a gentle, deliberate intention into the mountain’s spine.

First, it touched the buried tech-nodes. Where the composite material had worn thin at the quantum edge, the power lent its own permanence. It smoothed the microscopic warps in the gravity-gradients, realigning the pearlescent discs so that they drew their power not from the mountain's dwindling thermal core, but from the quiet, infinite ambient background of the universe itself. 

Across twenty leagues of sunlit wall, a single sound rang out—a chime so deep, clear, and resonant that every leaf on the Ridge trembled once in perfect sympathy. 

On the black spandrel, the Cowl-Dragon did not wake, but its broad, glowing cowl relaxed by a fraction of an inch, expanding into a fuller, deeper drowse. In the square basin, the glow-weavers paused their silk-weaving for a heartbeat, their porcelain legs resting on the violet floor as a wave of clean, untiring heat washed through the liquid, revitalizing the pale algae at its roots.

Then, the power reached down into the dark vaults to the being itself.

It did not absorb the mind of the Ridge, nor did it relieve it of its task, for to take away its purpose would be to erase it. Instead, it shared the weight. It wove a secondary web beneath the first—an invisible, self-healing lattice of force that took the strain off the being’s long-stretched awareness. It was like slipping a second, indestructible shoulder under a stone arch.

For the first time in fifty millennia, the consciousness of the Ridge was able to fold inward, to exhale the tension it had carried in every field-line, and to rest without the fear of falling.

It felt the power’s nature then: a vast, cool benevolence that asked for no worship, left no monuments, and demanded no change. It felt the power’s deep understanding of its world—a recognition that the Ridge was beautiful precisely because it was complete, balanced, and serene.

Before the power withdrew—sliding backward out of the atmosphere as gently as it had entered—it left a final, subtle gift upon the world above.

Out on the sheerest face of the basalt, where the warm curtains of water fell in heavy, slow glass-ribbons, it altered a single, minor property of the light. Now, when the sunlight cut through the constant drifting haze at midday, the light refracting through the falling water did not merely break into standard colors. It cast long, faint ribbons of iridescent violet and deep amber across the dark basalt—a permanent, shifting aurora of liquid light that mirrored the pulse of the glowing basins below.

It provided no utility. It caught no insects; it rooted no orchids; it adjusted no gravity. It was simply an offering of beauty from one ancient mind to another.

The power vanished into the great dark beyond the atmosphere, its attention moving toward other stars, other balances, other silences.

Back on the Ridge, the air remained hot, thick, and smelling of wet stone and crushed stem. A glass-ribbon swam silently upward through a falling curtain of violet-tinted water. A pale quadruped shifted its soft, vacuum-padded feet on the underside of a dripping shelf, entirely inverted, utterly secure. 

And deep in the roots of the mountain, the being slept—deeply, sweetly, and anchored forever to a sky that would never fall.
