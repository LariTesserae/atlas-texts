The Power came not as a light, for the light was already stark and clear through the overhead split. Nor did it come as a sound, for the small, dry cellar was already full to brimming with the wall of metallic thunder—the *clack-thump* of the piston, the electric whine, the rasp of millions of spiny legs on iron and quartz.

It arrived as an awareness. A vast, quiet presence that settled over the basalt shelf like a change in atmospheric pressure, so subtle that not a single Needle-Form missed its spark, nor did a single Scissor-Palp pause in its lightning strike.

The Power was ancient, wise, and profoundly benevolent. But its wisdom lay in understanding the nature of things as they were, not as a biological mind might wish them to be. 

It did not feel pity for this world. It did not see a tragic, forgotten prison of cold metal and stone, nor did it weep for the absence of green leaves, soft soil, or wet blood. The Power looked into the sunken basin and saw what it truly was: a miracle of pure, kinetic perfection. It saw a choir of bronze and hematite singing a hymn of pure existence; a closed, immortal loop of movement where death was merely re-grinding, and life was the spark that jumped the gap.

The Power listened to the engine. It traced the deep, invisible vein of static charge far beneath the basalt—a natural geological battery formed by tectonic pressure miles below, feeding the ancient copper drive-rod. 

And then, with its boundless vision, the Power saw the invisible threat.

It was not a threat of today, nor of a thousand years from now, but of an epoch hence. The copper drive-rod, turning at thirty revolutions a minute, was slowly—molecule by microscopic molecule—wearing away its housing. The friction that fed the Scrapers was also eating the heart of their sun. In two hundred thousand years, the alignment would fail. The shaft would bind. The piston would freeze mid-strike. The static arc would die, and the quiet cold would creep in. The Scrapers would seize. The Needle-Forms would turn to inert brass wire. The Plates would harden into solid ironstone. The furnace would go out.

The Power did not weep for this future, but it decided, in its quiet grace, to speak to the basin in the only language the basin understood: *Resonance, Geometry, and Torque.*

It did not reach down with hands. It extended a thread of its own infinite, willing attention into the subterranean heart of the rock.

First, it touched the deep tectonic battery miles below. It did not flood it with unmanageable energy, which would have melted the brass relays and fried the tiny quartz ganglia of the swarm. Instead, it tuned the subterranean fault lines, locking them into a gentle, perpetual harmonic feedback loop. The static charge would no longer pulse erratically with the shifting of the continent; it became a steady, immortal tide. The copper rod would turn at thirty revolutions a minute, forever. Not thirty-one. Not twenty-nine. A perfect, invariant constant.

Second, the Power touched the atoms of the machinery itself. It did not turn the brass to gold or the iron to diamond—that would have ruined the mandibles of the Scrapers, who relied on the precise hardness of hematite against copper. Instead, it imparted to the metal a subtle, self-repairing piezoelectric memory. As the Scrapers rasped away tiny flakes of metal, the atomic lattice of the machinery would draw upon the ambient static field to slowly, perfectly reform its micro-structure. The engine would grind, and the swarm would feed, but the engine would never wear thin. The sacrifice of thousands of shells would remain meaningful, but the altar would never crumble.

Third, the Power looked at the claustrophobic limits of the small stone cellar. It saw the density of the swarm, the frantic, boiling urgency of three or four layers of hard-shelled bodies tumbling over one another in the graphite-dark sand. 

With a silent, imperceptible shift of the basalt shelf above, the Power did not shatter the ceiling, but rather breathed upon the rock. Deep within the rear wall of the basin, behind the heavy housing of the pneumatic piston, the solid basalt dissolved into a network of long, sloping galleries—narrow, winding stone drawers extending for miles beneath the ancient shelf. 

The Power filled these new, dark capillaries with veins of native copper, rich seams of magnet-sand, and deep, quiet pockets of quartz flour. It did not force the creatures into these tunnels; it merely opened the door.

The effect was instantaneous, yet seamless.

Down in the basin, a large, broad-headed Plate, churning through the dark sand at the base of the piston, felt a new seismic frequency vibrating through its iron snout. It shifted its heavy, shingle-like body, driving deeper into the graphite dust than it had ever gone before. Its spade-like head broke through a thin crust of mineral powder and entered the mouth of the first new gallery. Behind it, three Scrapers, slick with fresh hematite polish, followed the magnetic pulling of a virgin vein of copper.

The swarm did not break its rhythm. The *thump-hiss* of the piston continued, heavy and rhythmic. The relays snapped their violet sparks. The Scissor-Palps struck with dry, metallic *snip-crag*s, harvesting the engorged, static-rich grazers. 

But the noise changed. 

Where the sound had once been trapped, hammering back and forth off the ironstone slabs like thunder in a box, it now found a deep, hollow acoustic chamber in the new galleries. The harsh wall of noise softened, deepening into a rich, reverberating hum—a vast, metallic heartbeat that echoed far down into the earth.

The Power stood above the basin for a long time, watching the dazzle of a million dark, metallic carapaces shimmering under the cool overhead light. It watched a Needle-Form bridge a relay gap, flaring bright violet for a fraction of a second before leaping away, hyper-charged and whole.

It had given them no soul they did not already possess. It had given them no warmth, no mercy, no soft grass to tread. It had given them only what a true god of metal and stone must give: *Infinity.*

Satisfied, the benevolent Power withdrew its attention, fading quietly back into the deep cosmos, leaving the tiny, bronze-colored furnace beneath the quiet world to turn, and scrape, and spark, unbroken, until the end of time.
