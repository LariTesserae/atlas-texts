The power that arrived did not come as a burst of light. Light, in this place, would have been a form of violence—an invasive, burning knife through a dark that had taken ten thousand years to settle into its perfect, moist, bruised stillness.

The power came, instead, as a weight that matched the weight of the air, and a warmth that matched the fever of the steam.

It was a power ancient enough to know that benevolence is not always an act of transformation. Cruel powers reshape the world into their own image; foolish powers try to cure conditions that are not sicknesses. But a power that is both wise and good understands that there are places in the cosmos whose perfection lies precisely in their low, wet, dark solitary loop.

It hovered above the ink-black ceiling, vast as a shadow cast by a mountain, invisible and unhurried. 

It did not lower eyes, for there was nothing here to be seen. It lowered a touch—a vast, weightless perception that seeped down through the porous stone, entering the fluting of the obelisks, the hollow domes, and the endless, flat, seamless plazas.

It felt the **Pore-Skin**. It registered the cold-blue nerve-sparks, tiny as salt grains, flashing where condensation dropped thirty feet from stone lintels. It felt the slow, fat, rubbery expansion of the film as it drank the thermal vents.

It felt the **Seeps**, clinging like hot, living mercury to the eroded grooves, climbing against gravity in their silent, chemical hunger, exchanging their heat in low, thrumming unions at the summits before evaporating into the black.

It felt the **Lenses**, the fragile, floating spheres of oil and water, carrying their microscopic, violet-glowing oceans through the colonnades until their surface tensions snapped and washed the stone in warm fever.

And high in the dark, it felt the **Great Draperies**, wringing themselves out in massive, silent hydraulic spasms, raining down upon the staircases to keep the world wet, heavy, and complete.

The power listened to this for a long time—a time measured not in seconds, but in the rising and falling of thermal plumes, in the slow, mineral reinforcement of leaning monoliths.

An unwise god would have given this city a sun. It would have dried the plazas, burned away the Pore-Skin, shattered the delicate Lenses, and forced the life here to crawl, breathe air, and speak in clumsy, dry words.

A lesser god would have felt pity for a world without eyes.

This power felt only a profound, reverent wonder. It recognized a holy thing: a world that was its own engine, its own altar, and its own worshiper, operating in complete, peaceful ignorance of the cold, chaotic universe outside its sunken valley.

So the power made three moves. They were so subtle that, to an outsider, nothing would have appeared to change at all. But to the city, they were the difference between a temporary stay against decay and an eternity of quiet grace.

***

### First: The Vault
The power gathered the ink-black ceiling—the low, pressing sky that felt only inches above the stone—and wove through it a curtain of absolute, impenetrable mass. 

It sealed the valley from above. No stray cosmic radiation, no shifting tectonic rift, no drying star, and no curious, intrusive traveler from another world would ever stumble into this sanctuary. The dark was turned into a vault, locked from the outside forever. The city’s low-slung depression was made safe within a quiet pocket of space, shielded from the cold universe that kills things made of water and steam.

***

### Second: The Deep Hearth
Beneath the broad, seamless slabs of poured rock, far below the hairline vents where the steam bubbled up, lay the dying ember of an ancient planetary core. In two million years, that core would have cooled. The vents would have gone silent; the air would have chilled by a fraction of a degree; the Pore-Skin would have shriveled into dark, dry veins; and the Seeps would have slid down the obelisks for the last time, unable to climb a cold stone.

The power reached its hand into the burning deep beneath the valley. 

It did not stoke a raging inferno—that would have boiled the city dry. Instead, it set a soft, immortal hearth in the planet's throat. It tuned the heat to an absolute, unvarying constant: *near-boiling humidity, perpetual thermal sighing, an endless supply of sulfur, copper, and trace salts.*

The vents would never go cold. The rain inside the domes would fall forever. The loop was made infinite.

***

### Third: The Great Thrum
The power did not give the city a voice, nor did it give the living fluids a human mind. To grant a Seep or a Lens a sense of self would be to curse it with loneliness.

Instead, the power left behind a single, physical gift—a new law written into the vibration of the stone itself.

It set a frequency into the seamless, poured plazas: a deep, sub-audible resonance, a pulse so low and slow that it took three days to complete a single cycle. 

It was a rhythm that harmonized the whole ecosystem. When the pulse rose, the Pore-Skin opened its pores in unison, drinking the steam in a single, vast, valley-wide breath. As the pulse crested, the Seeps pulled themselves up the monoliths with a unified, rhythmic thrum, their surface tensions humming together. When the pulse fell, the Great Draperies wrung themselves out at once, sending a synchronized, warm deluge through every hollow dome and carved basin in the city.

The distributed hydrology had not been given a mind. It had been given a music.

***

When the power drew back, sinking upward into the void beyond the sealed vault, the city did not notice its departure, just as it had not noticed its arrival.

In the absolute darkness, a heavy drop of water fell from a river-smooth archway. 

*Plink.*

A tiny, cold-blue spark flashed on the Pore-Skin, bloomed, and died. A Lens floated slowly between two pillars, holding its bruised-purple refraction of nothingness, drifting toward a warm vent. On a leaning obelisk, two Seeps met, merged with a low, wet vibration, and climbed upward toward the pitch.

Deep under the stone, the soft hearth burned, steady and warm. The air remained thick as grease, warm as breath. The city continued its slow, solitary work—wet, dark, perfectly balanced, and now, quietly, eternally held.
