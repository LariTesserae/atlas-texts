A foolish power would have brought the sun.

It would have parted the ceiling of ice and rock, flooded the drowned trough with warmth, slowed the heavy currents, and filtered the churning silt until the water ran clear and pale. And in doing so, it would have committed an absolute genocide.

The tendon-cords would have rotted without the shearing current to flex their cartilage sheaths. The chemo-piezotrophic fungi would have starved without the piezo-electric sparks of clashing quartz. The blind wedge-skimmers, built to endure the skittering of gravel across slate-hard backs, would have found no mud to swallow, no infrasonic shadows to navigate. The vast Silt-Plows would have suffocated in silent, thin water, deprived of the rich mineral slurry that sustained their massive, hydrostatic bodies.

A wise power understands that love does not mean converting the abyss into a garden. It means recognizing that the abyss is already a garden, carved in the only language the cold stone knows.

So the benevolent power came down not as light, nor as heat, but as a *listening*.

***

It descended through the ice, through the compressed strata of ancient rock, entering the basin without displacing a single grain of quartz dust. It took no physical form that would disrupt the boundary layers of the flow. Instead, it became a presence as dense as the water itself—a conscious weight, attuned entirely to the frequencies of the trough.

First, it listened to the roar.

To an unseeing god, the basin was not dark; it was a symphony of tremendous, low-frequency music. The grinding of the multi-ton boulders across the basalt hummocks produced deep, rhythmic chords that echoed through the bedrock. The taut tendon-cords, vibrating in the hydraulic rams of the current, sang a resonant, endless bass. The micro-fracturing of the quartz—the tiny discharges of violet light—struck against the darkness like silent, electric percussion.

The Power realized that the creatures here were not miserable. They were exquisite. They were the ultimate expression of endurance, forged by eons of pressure into forms of flawless, severe perfection.

And so, the Power decided what it would do. It would not change their world. It would *tune* it.

***

First, it addressed the great Silt-Plows.

As the vast, scaleless leviathans pushed through the wet slurry, navigating entirely by the echo of rolling stone, the Power gently touched the molecular structure of the basalt bedrock throughout the valley. It did not melt the stone; it merely aligned the micro-crystals within the basalt, giving the entire trough a subtly enhanced acoustic resonance.

Suddenly, the valley spoke to the Silt-Plows with breathtaking clarity. 

The low-frequency keels along their flanks caught echoes of incredible precision. They could "see" the exact geometry of a mud slide three miles ahead; they could hear the subtle density shift of a fresh bed of organic detritus settling beneath a hummock. The violent, chaotic noise of the basin smoothed into a rich, navigable map. The Leviathans did not know why the dark had suddenly grown so articulate, only that their long, slow journeys through the slurry were now filled with a deep, comforting certainty.

Next, the Power leaned down to the Lee-Side Fungi and the Tendon-Cords.

Where the quartz cobbles rolled and clashed, generating the faint, bruised-violet flashes of triboluminescence, the Power adjusted the friction of the strike just a fraction. It made the spark last a millisecond longer; it made the mineral discharge a fraction richer in chemical energy. 

The dark fungal stains on the lee-side of the rocks expanded, thick and greasy with new life. The black, whip-like cords grew denser, their overlapping scales of quartz grit locking together with perfect, flexible strength. When the heavy mud-rivers tore past them, the cords whipped and vibrated, zippering their sheath-guts with vigorous, healthy rhythm, singing their low bass notes into the floor of the world.

***

Finally, the Power moved to the quietest place in the entire drowned world: the deep basalt joints where the ancient human tools lay wedged.

It drifted into the narrow crevice where the five-thousand-year-old basalt hand-axe remained jammed tight, holding back the weight of the tearing current. It looked upon the tiny, glass-like hydroid—that impossibly delicate, unarmored creature living in the pocket of still water, surviving entirely because a human hand had once wedged a stone into a crack.

The Power felt a profound, aching tenderness for both the living thread and the ghost of the hand that made its home possible.

It knew that in time—perhaps a thousand years, perhaps ten thousand—the endless friction of the water would eventually wear down the basalt joint. The stone chopper would lose its grip, the current would invade the crevice, and the fragile hydroid line would be instantly swept away, erased in a single heartbeat of grit and force.

The Power refused to let that memory fail.

It did not build a wall. It did not alter the hydroid. Instead, it reached out to the ancient, flaked hand-axe and the calcified vegetable fiber bound to the sinker-stones.

With a thought that carried the weight of the mountain above, the Power fused the ancient human artifacts directly into the bedrock. It petrifying the rotted fibers into indestructible black agate, turning the hand-axe into an immovable, perpetual keystone of the basalt joint. 

The tools would never slip. The crevice would never collapse. 

The current would continue to roar three inches away, scouring the valley with tons of grinding quartz, keeping the great Silt-Plows fed and the tendon-cords humming. But here, in the acoustic shadow of an ancient human’s forgotten effort, the quiet water would remain still forever. 

The glass hydroid extended its thin, translucent tentacles into the dark, completely unaware that the wall behind it had been made eternal.

***

When its work was done, the benevolent power did not leave a mark, a shrine, or a light. 

It simply withdrew, rising back through the cold rock, through the ice sky, leaving the basin exactly as it had found it: cold, dark, heavy, and violent.

Yet deep below, in the bruised-violet dusk, the valley sang a little sweeter. The Leviathans glided through the slurry with perfect grace; the quartz cobbles sparked like stars in the mud; and in the dark behind a stone chipped by a long-dead hunter, the softest thing in the world continued to live, safe in the shadow of a deliberate act.
