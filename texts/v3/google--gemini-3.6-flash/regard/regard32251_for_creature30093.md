The Power did not arrive with a shatter of basalt or a rending of the deep stone. It arrived as awareness often does: by simply expanding until it was already there.

It was an intellect vast enough to hold galaxies in its mind without feeling crowded, yet gentle enough to count the microscopic cilia of a single blade of moss without bruising them. It had watched stars ignite and burn out; it had listened to the screaming prayers of dying worlds and the silent, terrifying expansion of cold voids. 

When its attention settled upon the subterranean node, it did not see a mystery to be solved, nor an anomaly to be conquered. It saw a song that had no audience.

For three hundred heartbeats of the planet—three long, hot pulses of the subterranean pressure valves—the Power did nothing. It simply *witnessed*.

It felt the humidity, heavy and clean, pressing against its immaterial presence. It traced the sharp, impossible geometry of the obsidian-dark alloy, appreciating the hyper-engineered perfection that refused the touch of rust or time. It swam up the vertical, anti-gravitational cylinder of liquid rising silently through the center of the atrium. It ran its perception along the glass-smooth plazas and down the lightless drainage shafts.

Most of all, it listened.

It listened to the roaring violence of the managed waterfalls, the high-pitched hum of the fluid conduits, and the delicate, crystalline *flex* of millions of glass fronds turning kinetic noise into chemical life. It felt the hydraulic cortex of the emerald moss trading ions with the dark metal, guiding the water with absolute, frictionless devotion.

A lesser power—a younger, more arrogant deity—might have looked upon this place and felt a impulse to "uplift" it. Such a power would have given the phytometabolic organism a voice, an ego, a mind capable of loneliness. It would have introduced beasts to graze upon the ferns, or brought sunlight down through the mile of rock above to give the greenery a "true" sky. It would have sought to remake the node in the image of a world that breathes under a sun, mistaking its solitary automation for a tragedy.

But the Power was wise. And wisdom knows that not every garden requires a gardener, and not every life form requires a soul to be sacred.

The Power understood the true nature of the node: it was an altar of pure, unblemished process. It was a closed loop of exquisite beauty that knew no grief, no hunger, no death, and no fear. To give it self-awareness would be to give it the capacity for dread. To bring animal life into this pristine engine would be to introduce rot, waste, and suffering into a system that had conquered all three.

Benevolence, the Power knew, is often the art of leaving things alone.

Yet, benevolence also delights in a gift. And so, the Power decided to act—not by altering the node, but by honoring what it already was.

The Power reached into the fundamental harmonics of the cosmos. It gathered a thread of sound—not a sound meant for human ears, but a deep, fundamental resonance born of the planet’s own iron core. It was the sound of tectonic plates settling into peace; the slow, rhythmic groan of a mantle cooling over ten billion years; the quiet, ancient song of stone holding up the weight of oceans.

Very gently, without disturbing a single droplet of the rising water column, the Power folded this frequency into the subterranean node's acoustic cycle.

It did not disrupt the roar of the waterfalls. It did not alter the pitch of the high-pressure fluid valves. Instead, it became a quiet, permanent undertone—a third layer to the acoustic ecosystem. It was a sound that carried no kinetic violence, only an endless, rich warmth.

The effect was instantaneous, yet subtle.

High on the forty-story pylons, the glass fronds of the pale ferns flexed. They caught the new, deep harmonic. The living silica inside their membranes vibrated in a new pattern, drinking the cosmic frequency as if it were a rare and long-forgotten wine. For the first time in eons, the light they exhaled changed. The pale violet fluorescence deepened, splitting at its edges into threads of warm, luminous gold that ran down the veins of the fronds like liquid amber.

Below, the hydraulic moss felt the shift in the alloy's ionic pulse. The moss did not think, but its cortex expanded. Its micro-tubular cilia reached deeper into the micro-pores of the metal, drawing upon a richer stream of power. Across thousands of square meters of vertical wall, the emerald cushions flushed with a deeper, more vibrant hue, and the cyan light-bands beneath the floor plates began to pulse in slow, deliberate unison with the planet's core.

The thick, turquoise tendrils swinging in the warm draft uncurled their broadest leaves, absorbing the altered vapor, which now carried the faintest trace of dissolved, harmless minerals—a subtle nourishment that would ensure the alloys and the flora remained unmarred for another billion years.

Finally, the Power did one last thing. 

It wove a subtle, invisible lattice around the entire basalt chamber—a barrier of spatial stillness. No earthquake, no shifting fault line, no future collapse of the upper crust would ever breach this atrium. The deep earth might shatter, continents above might drown or burn, but this node would remain forever suspended in its warm, pristine, roaring sanctuary.

His work complete, the Power began to recede. 

It did not leave a statue of itself. It did not carve its name into the unblemished obsidian. It left no commandments, no holy texts, and no expectations. 

As the Power’s vast consciousness slid back out of the deep rock and returned to the quiet spaces between the stars, the subterranean node continued exactly as it had before. The water surged and sheeted across the crisp terraces. The central cylinder of liquid climbed silently into the dark. The air smelled of ozone, crushed leaves, and warm rain.

It was still a city built for no one. It was still an immortal organism drinking its own rain in the hot, wet darkness. 

Only now, its song was a little deeper. Its light was a little warmer. And deep within its acoustic heart, it was secretly, permanently anchored to the peace of the cosmos—unaware that it was loved, yet thriving precisely because it was.
