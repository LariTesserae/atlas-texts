The Power arrived not as a light, for light was already absolute in the hard cyan sky, nor as a voice, for a voice would have been crushed to dust against the thousand-boiler roar of the vents. 

It arrived as a *listening*.

It settled into the high air around the iron pavilion, vast and unhurried, spanning the empty leagues above the fog like an invisible lens. A foolish god might have looked upon the cage and seen a prison—a forgotten piece of mechanical cruelty left behind by some extinct, lungless empire, trapping primitive flesh within steam and rust. An arrogant god might have reached down to tear open the lattice, to sweep away the roaring iron, to "save" the rubber-skinned dwellers and force them to look upon the green earth three thousand feet below, or the star-hung cosmos above.

But this Power was wise, and wisdom knows that a world is not defined by its boundaries, but by the completeness of the peace found within them.

The Power watched the forty-foot flywheel complete its three-hour turn. It traced the path of a brass-feathered swift as it dropped through the floor-mesh into the blinding blue, caught the thermal updraft of the pavilion’s sweat, and returned with an armored beetle in its beak. It felt the tiny, rhythmic *ping-ping* of a turquoise lizard striking its chin against a wet copper conduit, claiming a sunlit patch of moss.

And finally, it attuned its consciousness to the iron itself.

It felt the world as the truss-dwellers felt it: a vast, warm, reverberating harp of cast metal and bronze. It felt the low, bone-rattling hum of the central vents—not as noise, but as the continuous, comforting pulse of a mother’s heart felt from inside the womb. It saw four dwellers gathered behind the central gear-housing, their rubbery limbs intertwined, their foreheads pressed flush against the vibrating brass, lulled into deep, dreamless rest by the perpetual shudder of their universe.

They were not suffering. They were not diminished. They were a perfect stanza in a song that had forgotten its author, but had not forgotten how to sing.

The Power felt a profound, quiet tenderness for them—for their patience, their exquisite double-jointed caution, their absolute trust in the metal that held them above the endless white void.

*What does one give to a world that is already full?* the Power reasoned. *One gives it tomorrow.*

The Power moved. It did not alter a single leaf of the oar-ferns. It did not disturb a single plate-beetle scraping rust in the dark gear-wells. Its actions were executed in the quiet physics of the deep background, beneath the notice of flesh and eye.

First, it descended beneath the fog line, down into the cold, screaming winds where the invisible anchorages vanished into the blue. Far below, deep within the structural pylons where the heat was born, the Power found the source: a ancient, self-sustaining thermal core, slowly growing uneven in its venting. Left alone, in another four thousand years, a secondary valve would have seized from mineral crusting. The heat would have spiked; the pipes would have burst; the greenhouse would have frozen in the high-altitude air, turning the moss to black glass and the truss-dwellers to ice.

With a thought as light as a spore falling on wet copper, the Power cleansed the deep valves. It re-seated the ancient bronze plungers, smoothed the thermal channels, and balanced the deep combustion. The heat would now bleed evenly, warm and greasy and eternal, for a million years more.

Next, it examined the structural integrity of the cage. It reached into the molecular heart of the plum-colored rust—that stable oxide skin protecting the girders. The Power infused the skin with a subtle, atomic resilience. The metal would never fatigue. The heavy, three-hour strokes of the hydraulic rams would never shear their mounting pins. The floor-grates would never yield beneath the deliberate, threading steps of the dwellers. The cage was made immune to the slow wear of time, rendered as permanent as the sky it inhabited.

Then, the Power returned to the upper lattice, where the light cut in golden, geometric shafts through the mist.

It looked upon the dwellers sleeping behind the gear-case. It saw their pale, Amber eyes shut beneath thick membranes, their broad nostrils breathing the thick, iron-and-sulfur air. 

The Power wished to leave a gift—not a lesson, not a command, but a grace. Something that belonged entirely to them, spoken in the only language they knew.

The Power leaned close to the pavilion. It did not use hands; it used intention. It pressed its presence against the main structural arch, right beside the slow-moving flywheel, and tapped the iron.

It was not a blow. It was a resonance.

The Power introduced a new harmonic into the perpetual roar of the pavilion. It was an infinitely subtle sub-frequency, a deep, rhythmic note woven seamlessly into the rumble of the boilers and the groaning sigh of the displacement fluid. It did not make the world quieter, nor did it make it louder. 

It made the iron feel *loved*.

In the warm hollow behind the bronze gear-case, an old truss-dweller stirred in its sleep. 

It did not wake. It merely shifted, its calloused, rubbery forehead resting a little firmer against the vibrating metal. Through the bone of its skull, through the cartilaginous pad, flowed the hum of the pavilion.

For the first time in the history of its species, the vibration did not just carry the news of water, or moss, or the movement of a companion. 

It carried the feeling of absolute safety. It felt like the warmth of the steam pipe when the high wind blows outside. It felt like the grip of four digits locking securely around a three-inch girder. It felt like the certainty that the floor would never drop, that the heat would never die, and that the iron underfoot was holding them up with a vast, gentle, and deliberate care.

The old dweller gave a long, silent exhale of warm mist from its nostrils. It curled tighter against its kin, its rubbery spine relaxing, its sleep deepening into a peace so profound it touched the edges of eternity.

High above, the Power stepped back into the silent, hard-cyan sky. 

It did not linger to be worshipped. It did not leave a mark. It left the small, iron jungle floating in its roaring upper air—infinite, patient, perfectly loud, and now, entirely immortal.
