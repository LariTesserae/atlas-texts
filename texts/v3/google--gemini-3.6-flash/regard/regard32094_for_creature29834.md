The Power did not arrive with the blare of trumpets or the rending of the clay plain above. It came down the easternmost flue at zenith, sliding down the solid pillar of white-gold light like oil moving through warm glass.

It had no form that a human eye could parse without weeping, but here, in the blood-warm liquid, it accommodated itself to the geometry of the space. It became a subtle shift in the water’s refractive index—a density that made the caustic ribbons on the floor burn a fraction brighter, a warmth that sat just a half-degree above the geothermal flux.

It was wise, and so its first act was silence. It did not speak into the bather’s mind. To speak to someone who had built a forty-foot roaring chamber of stone and water precisely to drown out the noise of words would have been a cruelty. 

Instead, the Power listened.

It felt the bone-vibration of the two plunging waterfalls. It read the history in the sharp, un-eroded mortar lines: the aching lower backs of the masons, the smell of slaked lime cooking in the desert heat, the deliberate calculations of the architect who had aligned the four flues with the solstices. It saw the jewel beetles pinned against the heavy bronze grate, their iridescent armors shining like tiny emeralds in the dark drain.

And it saw the bather.

The bather was anchored to the second step, their fingers wedged into the crisp, sharp joint where two red tiles met. Their hair floated outward in black, silk-thin plumes, mimicking the nascent hair-algae. Their lungs were empty, resting at the absolute bottom of a exhaled breath, their eyes wide open in the stinging, clear current, staring up at the silver mirror of the trapped air.

The Power understood. Above, on the baked clay plain, lay the dry world: the demands of lineage, the heat of judgment, the friction of speech, the dust of dying seasons. Down here was the womb of the world—raw, loud, unyielding, and clean. The bather had not built a palace; they had built an engine for un-making the self.

A lesser power would have filled the basin with miraculous fish, turned the water into wine, or carved glowing runes into the virgin plaster. A lesser power would have tried to *heal* the bather of their solitude.

The Wise Power knew that solitude was not a disease to be cured, but a temple to be consecrated.

So, the Power acted, working entirely within the materials the humans had provided: fired earth, lime, bronze, water, and sun.

***

First, it touched the **Mortar and the Clay**.
The structure was young—seasons old—and destined, by the cruel physics of water and stone, to eventually decay. Hydraulic plaster softens over centuries; lime leaches; bronze corrodes into green crust; the sharp right-angles cut by human chisels round off under the constant assault of sweeping currents.

The Power breathed a quiet, permanent intention into the chemical bonds of the chamber. It did not make the stone magic; it simply made it *faithful*. The slaked lime was granted the endurance of deep-trench basalt. The sharp red edges of the terracotta tiles would remain unnervingly crisp for ten thousand years. The mortar lines would never pit or soften. The human geometry—that desperate, beautiful attempt by a fragile creature to impose straight lines upon the chaotic earth—was locked in time, a permanent monument to the mason’s plumb-line.

***

Second, it touched the **Acoustic Storm**.
The roaring of the waterfalls was immense, a deafening thunder that rattled human bone. Prolonged exposure to such volume would eventually deaden the bather’s ears, stealing the very hearing they used to drown their thoughts.

The Power adjusted the acoustic physics of the vault. It did not lower the volume—for the bather needed the storm—but it harmonized the reverberations. It filtered out the damaging, tearing frequencies of the impact and turned the underwater hum into a pure, resonant carrier wave. The bone-rattling vibration became a deep, subterranean chord—a physical massage that untangled the tight knots in the bather’s nervous system, allowing them to remain submerged without damage to their inner ear. The noise remained a wall, but it became a wall made of velvet rather than iron.

***

Third, it touched the **Micro-Life**.
The Power looked at the microscopic pioneers—the spores, the diatoms, the tiny tufts of green hair-algae striving along the mortar. It gave them a quiet instruction. 

They were forbidden from clouding the water. The fluid must remain forever as clear as mountain air, blood-warm and immaculate. But along the lime seams—the grid lines mapped by the masons—the algae and cyanobacteria were invited to bloom with absolute mathematical symmetry. Over the coming years, the algae would not spread messily across the tiles; it would grow only along the mortar, filling the clean joints with a brilliant, emerald-green velvet. The room would become a living mosaic, an emerald grid framing the fiery orange terracotta, bathed in golden light.

As for the drowned jewel beetles on the bronze grate? The Power granted them a small grace. They would not rot into ugly sludge. The sweeping current and the mineral-rich water would slowly calcify them, turning their delicate carapaces into permanent, gleaming lapis and emerald studs, embedded against the dark hammered bronze like a constellation of fallen stars.

***

Finally, the Power touched the **Bather**.

It did not alter their fate. It did not make them immortal, nor did it grant them the gills of a fish. To do so would ruin the ritual; the danger and the effort of holding one's breath were essential to the peace the bather sought.

Instead, as the bather sat wedged against the second tier, eyes open to the shimmering silver sky of trapped air, the Power allowed a single, microscopic grace to slip into their blood.

It was a blessing of *Depth*. 

Under the influence of the tuned roar and the consecrated water, the bather’s heart rate slowed to a calm, ancient rhythm—the heart-beat of the earth itself. The panic reflex of the lungs receded, dissolving into the warm fluid. A single breath stretched. Two minutes became four; four became six. Not through magic, but through the total, unreserved relaxation of the flesh. 

The bather’s mind, battered by the dry world above, finally quieted. The internal monologue—the constant, frantic churning of human thought—stalled, washed away by the roaring hum of the intake pipes. For ten glorious, weightless minutes, the bather ceased to be a person with a name, a past, or a sorrow. They became simply a dark shape suspended in golden liquid, a piece of living geometry resting on red clay, illuminated by four shafts of eternal sunlight.

***

When the sun moved past zenith and the four light-pillars began to lean, sliding silently up the western faces of the massive square pillars, the Power drew itself back up the eastern flue.

It left no sign of its visit. There were no burning bushes, no divine inscriptions, no lingering scents of incense. There was only the cistern: raw, red, loud, and brilliant.

Below, the bather released their grip on the bronze grate and rose effortlessly through the golden water, breaking the surface into the cool, silvery air pocket with a sharp, joyous gasp of life. They climbed the broad, sharp steps, their skin glistening red-gold in the light flues, completely unaware that the space they loved had just been made imperishable, or that the universe had looked down through thirty feet of clay and agreed: 

*This is good. Stay as long as you need.*
