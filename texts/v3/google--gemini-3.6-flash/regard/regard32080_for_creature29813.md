The Power did not arrive with light. To bring light into such a place would be a violence—a blinding, burning intrusion that would scorch the pale fronds and shatter a stillness millions of seconds in the making.

Instead, the Power arrived as a presence: a vast, patient attention that folded itself into the chamber, fitting into the spaces between the heavy air molecules, settling over the wet basalt and the flaking green paint like a second layer of atmosphere.

It was a power that had watched galaxies spin themselves into dust and had heard the first cries of a billion nascent civilizations. It understood hunger, ambition, sorrow, and the terrible, restless grief of creatures who look at the stars and realize how far away they are. 

Here, there was none of that. 

The Power listened. It heard the dull *thunk* of a single droplet striking rusted iron. It felt the slow, rhythmic pulse of the pale hide resting against the copper manifold. It traced the microscopic root-cilia drinking the thin film of oil and sulfur. It registered the total, absolute absence of terror.

A lesser god—a young power, full of zeal and the clumsy desire to improve—might have sought to "save" this place. It might have split the rock ceiling to let in the sun, turned the mineral puddles into sweet river water, or touched the tallow-skinned creature to gift it with eyes, speech, and a soul that could ache.

But the Power was wise, and wisdom knows the profound cruelty of unnecessary light. To give this creature eyes would be to sentence it to a lifetime of staring into pitch darkness; to give it a mind would be to invent loneliness where only peace had ever grown.

The Power looked into the future of the chamber. 

It saw the cold world above. It saw the century where the copper pipes deep in the rock would finally freeze, where the steam vent would clear its throat one last time and go silent, where the iron housings would lose their warmth, and the dark would turn from heavy and lush to dry and dead. It saw the creature’s slow, uncomplaining stillness as its exotic metabolism slowed, cooled, and hardened into a pale, forgotten crust against the basalt.

The creature would not suffer in that death—it had no concept of tragedy—but its silence would end. The small, perfect song of its existence would simply stop.

The Power decided that this song should not end.

It did not change the creature. It did not touch the indigo leaves. It did not clean the rust or wipe away the smell of machine oil. 

Instead, the Power reached down—far down, beneath the twenty feet of surface earth, past the fresh granite fractures, deep into the ancient, silent roots of the tectonic plate. 

With a movement lighter than the fall of a leaf, it nudged a single, deep-earth thermal vein. It did not create a volcano or a rupture; it simply shifted a tiny capillary of liquid heat in the deep stone, aligning it forever with the basalt floor of the chapel-pit. 

Then, it touched the forgotten iron conduit. 

The Power wove a quiet, eternal physics into the metal and the rock. The steam vent would never run dry. The deep earth would pump its quiet, sulfur-rich warmth up through the cast-iron housings forever. The pressure would build, and every twenty minutes—without fail, through the birth and death of surface oceans, through the collapse of cities and the freezing of stars—the vent would release its soft, pneumatic *hiss*.

Next, the Power sealed the roof. 

It smoothed the stone far above, filling the microscopic fissures with solid, impenetrable density. No drill from the surface would ever catch on this pocket of rock. No surveyor’s sonar would ever map its cavity. The world above might be stripped to ash or covered in ice; it would never break through to touch this dark.

Finally, before it withdrew, the Power left a gift. Not a thought, nor a vision, but a heat.

It pressed a faint, eternal warmth into the black, glossy facets of the fresh granite—a temperature just half a degree warmer than the surrounding stone, pulsing in a slow, invisible wave that matched the twenty-minute breathing of the vent. 

Deep behind the largest compressor housing, the tallow-colored mass shifted. 

Its sheet-like margin flowed, dripping with condensation, extending a soft fold of pale, violet-bruised skin toward the newly warmed basalt wall. Its heat-sensitive pit-pores drank the new gradient. Across its back, the faint glowing veins of plant-spores brightened just a fraction, pulsing in the dark.

It felt no gratitude, for it knew no gods. It felt no wonder, for it knew no surprise. 

It simply felt that the stone was good, the iron was warm, and the wet dark was vast enough to last forever.

Satisfied, the Power pulled its vast attention back into the deep cosmos, leaving the chamber exactly as it had been found: untouched, uncorrupted, and perfectly, beautifully obscure.
