It did not descend as a body, nor as a light that would dim the piercing mountain sun. It arrived as a sudden, vast *attentiveness*—a shift in the quality of the void above the mountain’s crown. 

A lesser power, driven by a shallow or sentimental pity, might have looked upon this stone throat and seen a place of torment. It might have stayed the violently churning water, softened the razor-edges of the black obsidian, or warmed the freezing abyss into a quiet, sunlit meadow-pool. 

But the Power that paused above the fissure was wise, and its benevolence was not the soft mercy that smothers, but the profound reverence that understands.

It felt the deafening roar of the hydraulic tempest—not as chaos, but as a single, complex, blindingly pure note. It felt the violent thud of the pale ribbon-creatures bouncing off the quartz spires, and in the dense, rubbery resilience of their flesh, it recognized not suffering, but an absolute, ecstatic perfection. To give these beings a quiet pond would be to drown them in stasis; to smooth the rock would be to strip them of the edges they needed to shed their old skins. Their joy was speed. Their peace was pressure. Their liturgy was the furious, sun-drenched collision of muscle and volcanic glass.

The Power listened deeper. 

It felt the cold, mineral pulse of the deep vents. It traced the light channeling down through the golden, needle-thin glass-weeds, turning photons into sugar within the dark bulbs. It felt the tiny, fluorite-shelled barnacles on the human rope, drinking the terrifying vibration of the water and converting bone-rattling thunder into a soft, bioluminescent ember.

And then, the Power turned its attention to the human mark.

It saw the crude copper stakes driven into the newborn basalt. It saw the fire-hardened oak, scarred and stripped, and the braided animal sinew humming under a tension that would snap a iron cable. It saw the basalt sphere—the smooth, rounded intruder in a world of knives—swinging in its wide, underwater arc.

The Power understood the human story written in that wood and sinew. Someone had climbed this newborn spine of rock. Someone had bled on these quartz blades, breathing air thin as ice, driven by curiosity, or hunger, or a sacred need to measure the wild heartbeat of the earth. They had left a piece of their world suspended in the blue vortex, and in doing so, had unwittingly given the pale ribbons a altar—a carousel to ride, a stone to hold against the violent drift.

The Power realized that this place was whole, save for two small, quiet fragilities.

First: the mountain was young, its bones still settling. A single deep fault-line was creeping through the basalt at the base of the chimney. Within three winters, a seismic shift would collapse the throat, sealing the thermal vents, shattering the glass-weeds, and suffocating the sky-bound aquifer in darkness.

Second: the human sinew, though taut as iron, was decaying. In a few months, the violent current would fray the last braid. The basalt sphere would break free, plunge into the black abyssal vent, and shatter the organ-pipe sponges below. The bridge between the world of hands and the world of jets would be broken forever.

The Power did not alter the water. It did not quiet the storm. It did not speak. 

Instead, it acted with the subtlety of a light ray passing through ice.

Deep within the mountain’s roots, where the organ-pipe sponges clung to the subterranean furnaces, the Power touched the stress-fractures in the basalt. It did not weld them with gold or magic; it simply aligned the crystalline lattice of the rock, settling the mountain's bones into an ancient, unyielding geometry. The fault line dissolved. The throat of the fissure was given ten thousand years of absolute stability. The vents would pulse, the pressure would build, the sponges would seal and burst, but the rock would never fall.

Then, the Power moved its awareness up to the human rigging.

It brushed the fire-hardened oak wedges and the braided sinew cord. It did not make them immortal, for that would make them alien to the earth. Instead, it whispered a mineral memory into the organic threads. The sinew absorbed the iron and silica dissolved in the roaring water, slowly petrifying over the course of decades—not into brittle stone, but into a tough, flexible, jade-like fiber that would never rot, never snap, and never lose its resonant hum. 

The human mark was sanctified. The pendulum would swing for as long as the mountain held the sky.

Finally, the Power offered a gift to the pale ribbon-creatures—a gift so small that only a god or a wild creature would recognize it.

It adjusted the piezoelectric frequency of the tiny fluorite barnacles clinging to the sinew rope. Now, whenever the warm, mineral pulse surged from the depths and set the basalt pendulum swinging, the barnacles did not merely glow with light; they emitted a soft, harmonic warmth—a localized, gentle thermal pulse that washed over the smooth basalt sphere.

Below, in the dark vents, a six-foot ribbon-creature caught the acoustic ring of the swinging stone. It shot upward through twenty yards of violent, turquoise void, jet-siphons blasting, cutting through the white-water spirals like a silver blade. 

It reached the peak of the chamber, where the piercing mountain sunlight sliced through the clear cold. It looped its dense, pale body around the sweeping basalt sphere, riding the human pendulum through its blinding arc. 

As it pressed its muscular flank against the smooth stone, it felt the new warmth of the barnacles—a sudden, deep, comforting hum that vibrated through its solid cartilaginous core, answering the wild pressure of the water with a moment of pure, radiant grace.

The creature let out a jet of water, spun off the pendulum into a shower of sun-lit caustics, and plunged back down into the roaring blackness of the bottom vents, exhilarated and alive.

The Power watched the pale ribbon fall. It heard the metallic song of the quartz, the heavy boom of the hydraulic ram, the steady, rhythmic *swish-hum* of the petrified pendulum, and the silent, blinding joy of the glass-weeds drinking the sun.

Satisfied that the song was safe, the Power withdrew into the high, quiet air, leaving the mountain to its beautiful, roaring solitude.
