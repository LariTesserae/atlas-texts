It did not bring light. 

To bring light to a world of blind pit-organs and translucent, night-adapted membranes would not be an act of grace; it would be a blinding, scorching violence. It did not bring warmth, for to raise the temperature of this hornblende wilderness by even a few degrees would melt the tallow-wax webs, curdle the cold grease in the conduits, and rot the ancient, vulcanized hide of the Gasket-Spinners.

Wisdom lies first in understanding that a place does not need to be soft to be holy.

The power arrived not as a burning star or a thundering voice, but as a vast, weightless attention—an infinite, gentle pressure that settled across the spires like a second atmosphere. It was a presence so quiet that the Velvet-Ststalkers did not twitch their leaf-shaped ears, and the delicate, comb-gilled tendrils of the Pale Drift-Sails continued their slow, cold filtering in the dark.

The power listened.

For three thousand cycles of the deep valves, it simply inhabited the rhythm. It felt the *clack-shhh* of the subterranean cartilage. It tracked the heavy, metallic *click-click-click* of the Pyrite-Casks driving their iron legs into the stone. It felt the wet, peeling *schlick, schlick* of the Sludge-Siphons, and the cold, enzymatic dissolve of grease inside the Stalkers' bellies.

It saw that there was no tragedy here. There was no fallen state, no longing for a sun that had never existed, no quiet weeping in the dark. The engine was complete. The ecosystem was a single, immense, self-repairing hymn of iron, brine, and cold tallow.

And yet, wisdom sees what even a perfect engine lacks.

An engine that sings only to itself in an endless void operates under a subtle, cosmic friction: the friction of solitude. The Cymatiums sang their deep, tectonic notes into the dark gulfs, but the notes traveled out into the empty air, dissipated against the cold ridges, and died unheard. The world was a masterpiece painted in a room with no doors.

The benevolent power made its choice. It would not change the machine. It would become its bearing. It would become its witness.

Gently, without disturbing a single frond of iron-leafed fern, the power reached down into the deepest, darkest marrow of the planet—far below where even the Gasket-Spinners wedged their blunt, calcified heads. 

There, in the super-pressurized roots of the world, where the subterranean gas beds pressed against the brine, the power laid down a gift.

It was a thread of pure, frictionless intent, woven directly into the crystalline lattice of the hornblende bedrock. It did three things:

First, it smoothed the wear of ages. It did not stop the erosion—for erosion was the breath of this place—but it subtly reinforced the iron-cased cartilage of the valves. Where a Gasket-Spinner’s body would once mineralize into hard, brittle stone after four centuries, the power infused the dying cartilage with a gentle, self-healing elasticity. The Spinners would still live, still serve, and still pass into stone, but their legacy would never crack under the mountain's heavy heart-beats. The engine’s heart would never seize.

Second, it gave the dark a memory.

High aloft, where the Pale Drift-Sails entangled their long, trailing tendrils to exchange cold genetic fluids in the windless sky, the power touched their decentralized nervous networks. It did not give them thoughts, or grief, or self-awareness—such things would only make the empty air terrifying. Instead, it left a faint, soothing resonance in their calcified frames. When two Drift-Sails touched in the dark, the fluid exchange was no longer just a cold mechanical transfer; it was accompanied by a subtle, deep-tissue vibration—a sensation of profound, floating safety that made their vast pale wings relax completely against the thermal plumes.

And finally, the power addressed the Cymatiums.

The power shifted its vast awareness up to the fluted summits, where the colossal, iron-boned horns sat anchored to the highest peaks. As a deep piston-thrum echoed up from the roots of the mountain, the nearest Cymatium caught the shockwave. Its massive horn hummed, casting its ultra-low, five-league note out into the freezing drizzle.

This time, the note did not die in the black void.

The power caught the vibration in the air and returned it—not as an echo, which would imply distance and stone, but as a *harmonic counter-note*. A frequency perfectly pitched an octave below the Cymatium’s song, rising up from the valley floors to meet it.

It was the sound of a world being answered.

The hum swept down the vertical cliffs. It vibrated through the wet, shivering curtains of liver-colored lichen. It passed through the velvet pelts of the Velvet-Stalkers, who paused on their hook-claws, their thermal pits relaxing as the resonance washed over them like a cool, heavy bath. It reverberated through the brassy carapaces of the Pyrite-Casks, easing the strain in their heavy mandibles. Down in the hollow chasms, the Sludge-Siphons held their suction, drinking in the vibration through their blubber-insulated hides.

The mountain breathed: *clack-shhh*.
The Cymatium sang: *mmmmm-ommmmm*.
And the power answered: *hummmmmm*.

Satisfied, the benevolent power drew its vast attention back into the deep cosmos. It left no light. It left no warmth. It left no angels, no scripture, no altered leaves. 

It left only the machine, running a fraction smoother than it had before, its cold iron heart beating in a dark that was no longer empty, singing a song that was no longer unheard.
