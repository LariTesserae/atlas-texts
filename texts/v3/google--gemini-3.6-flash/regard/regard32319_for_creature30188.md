The Power came not as a storm, nor as a blinding flash that would startle the motionless fronds or crack the silica glaze. It arrived as a subtle shift in the weight of the air, a presence as soft and heavy as the golden light that rested on the wet basalt.

It was an entity that had watched stars ignite and cool into slag, that had listened to the songs of dying leviathans in dark oceans and the birth-cries of worlds. Yet, as its gaze settled upon the eighty paces of warm, dripping stone, it paused. It stayed its hand. For wisdom lies in knowing the precise measure of a thing, and benevolence lies in honoring its nature.

A lesser power might have looked at the terrace and seen poverty. It might have brought birds to fill the silence with song, or insects to multiply in the wet silt. It might have smoothed the creature’s slate skin into something fair, or given it speech to lament its solitude. 

A proud power might have carved a grand hall into the rear cliff, crowning the wet stone with marble pillars and burning braziers.

But the Power was wise. It spent the equivalent of three human lifespans simply *listening*.

It felt the slow, heavy thrum of the geothermal seam deep within the mountain’s roots. It tracked the three-season journey of a glass-smooth seed-pod as it rolled down the water channels. It felt the creature’s heart—a thick, muscular pump beating with the patient cadence of a subterranean tide—and it felt the cool, dense weight of the single egg resting in the creature’s chest, waiting for an epoch that had not yet arrived.

The Power realized that this place was already complete. It was a masterpiece of static perfection, a clock whose gears turned once every hundred years. To accelerate it would be to ruin it. To add to it would be to crowd it.

And so, the Power decided what it would do.

*It would build a wall of time, and anchor the heart of the stone.*

First, the Power reached down—not with hands, but with an intention that sank through eighty feet of basalt, down through the fractured granite of the mountain shoulder, deep into the dark mantle where the earth’s primal fires burned. There, it forged a permanent knot in the geometry of the rock. It ensured that the warm spring would never dry, that the silica seam would never choke, and that no shift of tectonic plates would ever collapse the arched recess behind the velvet fronds. The pocket of heat was granted an immunity to the decay of the mountain itself. The earth around it might crumble over ten million years, but this terrace would remain, a floating island of warm water and wet fern suspended in the sky.

Next, the Power addressed the lip of the amphitheater, where the warm vapor met the freezing, thin-air winds of the peak. With a breath, the Power wove a subtle curvature into the local air. It was not a glowing shield or a visible barrier; it was a gentle deflection of probability and cold. The mountain gales, carrying frost and gravel, would now swirl smoothly *around* the terrace, leaving the air inside as still and undisturbed as a greenhouse glass. The eyes of passing eagles—and, in centuries to come, the eyes of men with glass lenses and iron climbing-spikes—would slide off the niche, seeing only a shadow on a barren cliff face, a place where nothing of interest could possibly dwell.

Finally, the Power approached the tender.

It did not manifest in form. It dissolved a sliver of its own vast, gentle consciousness into the weeping water high on the wall. The water carried the grace down the swooping, petrified-mud channels, over the amber mats of gel, and into the middle soaking bowl where the slate-skinned creature lay submerged to its chin.

The creature did not startle. Its wide, pale, lidless eyes registered only a slight change in the water’s refractive index, a golden shimmering that merged with the afternoon sun. 

The Power’s gift entered the creature through its skin, mixing with the silica micro-crystals in its epidermis and settling into its slow blood. It was not the gift of thought, for thought brings anxiety. It was not the gift of memory, for memory brings grief. 

It was the gift of *assurance*. 

For the first time in its ancient, solitary existence, the creature felt a deep, resonant warmth answer the pulse in its chest. It understood, in the wordless way a stone understands gravity, that its long tending was seen. It knew that when its joints finally calcified and its slow breathing ceased, the water in the bowl would remain warm. The lime would settle gently over its remains. The mineralized shell of its egg would dissolve in peace, and the child that emerged would find the obsidian blades waiting on the stone shelf, the jade succulents weeping their bitter sap, and the warm steam rising into the bright, quiet sky.

The creature’s chest expanded. It took one of its two hourly breaths, drawing the mineral vapor deep into its lungs. 

A single, large bubble rose from its lips, broke the surface of the glassy pool, and sent a smooth, perfect ring of ripples expanding outward until it tapped against the notched V-shaped spillway.

*Clink.*

A drop fell from the cliff above.

*Clink.*

The Power withdrew, stepping back into the vastness between the stars, leaving the terrace exactly as it had found it—wet, warm, utterly silent, and now, entirely eternal.
