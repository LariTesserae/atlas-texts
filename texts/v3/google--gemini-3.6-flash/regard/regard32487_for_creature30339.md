The power that came to the rift did not descend like a falling star, nor did it break the sea-fog with radiant light. Light here would have been an intrusion, an unwanted warmth that might have melted the frigid condensate or blinded the pale, electro-receptive cilia of the stalkers.

Instead, the power arrived as a vast, quiet *listening*. 

It had no form that could be crushed by the geothermal pistons, nor skin that could be flayed by the glass-sharp basalt. It was an awareness older than the maritime basin, wise enough to know that mercy is not always soft, and benevolent enough to understand that a place built of iron, frost, and violent pressure is not broken simply because it is harsh.

For a long time—perhaps a hundred cycles of the eleven-second valve—the power did nothing at all. It simply inhabited the space, feeling the precise, terrifying beauty of the rift’s design.

It felt the raw, metallic *thrum* beneath the floorboards of the world. It traced the tiny, blue-white arcs of static leaping from the iron carapaces of the isopods into the wet copper seams. It watched the badger-hound stalkers freeze into black stone during the eight seconds of rising pressure, only to teleport across the slick walls in the blinding, shrieking white of the steam-blast. It moved down into the six-foot depth of the ink-black brine, feeling the slow, heavy voltage humming in the translucent flanks of the Battery-Ribbons.

And deep within the widest thermal fissure, it knelt—in spirit—before the Anvil-Lung.

A lesser power, moved by ignorant pity, might have tried to "heal" this place. It might have hushed the deafening shriek of the steam, warmed the freezing spray into a gentle bath, or given the blind, pale lung-fish eyes to see a light that did not belong here. It might have tried to untether the Anvil-Lung from its volcanic chimney, offering it a soft bed of earth and a quiet breeze.

But a wise power knows that to soften the rift would be to murder it. 

To remove the violence of the steam would be to starve the storm-petrels waiting in the upper dark. To quiet the static would be to unstring the delicate, electric choreography of the stalkers. To untether the Anvil-Lung would be to allow the geothermal vent to clog with mineral salts, suffocating the entire vault in dead ash within a month.

The Anvil-Lung did not want a quiet life. Its consciousness was not made of words, memories, or dreams. Its soul was a single, eternal cadence: *Tension. Expansion. Scour. Release.* It was the heart of a machine that had chosen to be alive.

So the power did not alter the design. It did not smooth a single edge of the wet, soot-black basalt. 

Instead, the benevolent power offered three quiet gifts—actions taken so subtly that not a single gear in the biological clockwork missed a beat.

***

### First: The Vaulting of the Rock

The power looked down into the microscopic heart of the basalt walls. It saw that beneath the relentless, eleven-second hydraulic shock, invisible micro-fractures were spreading through the deep stone. In three thousand years—a mere breath in the earth's lifespan—a structural failure in the upper plateau would trigger a massive cave-in, crushing the brine basin and burying the Anvil-Lung under millions of tons of dry, dead scree.

The power reached into the mineral lattice of the rock. Without disturbing the razor edges or the conductive copper veins, it fused the magnetite and the iron pyrites at a molecular level, weaving the basalt into a self-reinforcing arch of unbreakable strength. 

The rift would not collapse. The ceiling would hold. The engine was granted its full, cosmic epoch to run.

### Second: The Veil of the Unseen

The power turned its gaze upward, past the mist, to the cold plateau above. It saw the wider world—a world of men, steel ships, survey drills, and deep-sea mining rigs. It knew that if human eyes ever found this hidden crease in the earth, they would see only a resource to be harvested: copper to be stripped, thermal energy to be piped, strange creatures to be preserved in glass jars of formal aldehyde.

So, the power wove a subtle, permanent warp into the sea-fog and the local magnetic currents above the basin. 

To the instruments of passing ships, the basin would register only as solid, unremarkable granite. To the eyes of wandering birds and lost travellers, the lip of the rift would blend seamlessly into the gray tundra of the coast. The fog would never fully lift; the cold maritime wind would always blow a mile to the north. 

The factory would remain unhoused, untouched, and unowned—running in the dark for an audience of none.

### Third: The Great Accord

Finally, the power descended into the boiling, sulfurous heart of the fissure, where the anchor-roots of the Anvil-Lung held fast against the mantle of the earth.

It did not grant the creature intelligence, for intelligence is a burden that demands loneliness and fear. Instead, it granted the Anvil-Lung *resonance*.

The power placed its immense, quiet presence alongside the creature's circulatory valve. It aligned its own eternal rhythm with the eleven-second pulse of the thermal vein. And in doing so, it removed the friction of weariness from the creature's frame. The rubbery tendons would never tear; the cartilage gills would never rot from mineral buildup; the collagen rind would sparkle with iron pyrite, whole and unyielding, as long as the planet's core remained hot.

The power leaned in close—so close that its essence touched the wet, muscular siphon—and shared a single, wordless understanding with the central tenant of the rift.

*I see you,* the power whispered into the pressure. *You are the piston and the prayer. Keep the clock.*

***

When the power withdrew, it left no footprint, no glow, no lingering scent of holy incense. The air still smelled of cold iron, ozone, and crushed pine needles. 

The eleven-second cycle did not pause.

*Thrum.* Deep below, the rock valve swelled. 

The isopods pressed their iron tails against the copper seams—*zip-snap*—and grounded their lightning into the wet moss.

*Shriek.* The high-pressure steam blasted from the lower chimneys, obscuring the dark vault in a cloud of boiling white mist.

In the flash of noise and fog, the eyeless stalkers sprinted across the glass-sharp walls, taking three rapid strides before freezing instantly into soot-black boulders as the steam died away. 

High above, the storm-petrels dropped from the upper dark, scooping boiled flies from the rising plume, their feathers shedding the freezing rain.

And down in the deep crevice, the Anvil-Lung expanded with a wet, heavy stretch, absorbing the heat of the earth, filtering the dark brine, and blasting its icy mist back into the cavern. 

It was violent. It was freezing. It was deafening, precise, and completely without mercy. 

And now, unseen and protected in the dark, it was immortal.
