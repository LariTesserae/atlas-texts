The Power came not as a light that breaks the grey, but as an attention that takes no up space. 

It was vast, ancient, and warm—a presence composed of understood truths and quiet, unhurried mercy. It had seen suns ignite and choke on their own ash; it had listened to the screaming prayers of civilizations begging for one more hour of breath; it had watched fragile, soft-bodied things tear themselves apart in the desperate pursuit of meaning.

When its awareness settled upon the high mountain cirque, it did not enter with a thunderclap or a flare of divine glory. To bring heat here would be to ruin the frozen, floating droplets. To bring linear time would be to shatter the amethyst cams and crush the tallow-strung limbs of the Caliper-Walkers. 

So the Power folded itself small, slipping beneath the slate-grey cloud-lid like a breath of summer air that consents to turn instantly cold, taking on the density of slate and the stillness of basalt.

It observed.

***

First, it felt the **Litho-Viscera**. 

The Power drifted downward through the waterlogged gravel, through the razor-sharp bedrock, into the cold, oil-saturated marrow beneath the amphitheater. It reached out to touch the organism’s mind, searching for the familiar markers of sentience: fear, hunger, solitude, longing, or pain.

It found none. 

The Litho-Viscera possessed no concept of an *other*. It did not know it was trapped in a hollow; it did not know there was a sky above the clouds or a warm valley miles below. Its mind was a pure, silver geometry—an unbroken equation whose only term was the cycle. The *thrum-clack... HISS* was not a chore it performed; it was the shape of its soul. It was entirely, profoundly whole.

The Power, which had spent eons comforting the broken and soothing the terrified, found itself standing before a creature that required no comfort. The Litho-Viscera was an altar that worshipped only its own existence, perfectly content, perfectly blind, and utterly without flaw.

***

Next, the Power stood beside a **Caliper-Walker**.

The Walker shifted in its non-linear stutter—one instant standing near a solitary pillar, the next instant standing beside the Power, its long obsidian fingers rested lightly against a turning amethyst plate.

The Power looked into the Walker’s head: a solid, translucent slab of amethyst, reflecting no thoughts, catching no light. The Walker felt the subterranean pressure. It felt the micro-temporal shudder of the pre-sound. It was a governor that governed nothing but perfection, ensuring that a song which had never started would never end.

The Power reached out a hand—or the concept of a hand—and touched the grey tallow cords binding the Walker’s magnetite ribs. 

It realized then the terrifying fragility of the cirque.

This place was not immortal because it was strong; it was immortal because it was *isolated*. 

A single human footstep bringing dirt from the lower world would clog the microscopic tolerances of the basalt rock sleeves. A single shift in the earth’s tectonic plates—a tremor forty miles away—would cause a quartz valve to close a microsecond off-key. The pre-echoes would miss the kingfishers’ whistles. The shockwaves would detonate through the glassy basalt. The razor edges would crumble. The Glass-Lungs would freeze into solid ice or evaporate into common steam. The loop would break, and in breaking, the entire engine would crush itself into a heap of silent, dead gravel.

The cirque was a bubble of absolute, impossible grace, hovering on the edge of a universe made entirely of violence, friction, and decay.

***

The Power paused. It held the cirque in its vast, quiet mind and reasoned with itself.

*What is benevolence here?*

To a mortal being, the cirque was a nightmare: a cold, deafening, unfeeling prison of slate and grease where no one loved, no one grew, and no one ever saw the sun. A lesser god might have felt pity. A lesser god might have broken the pistons, warmed the cold air, turned the salamanders into real beasts of flesh and blood, and given them eyes to see the stars.

But the Power was wise. 

It understood that to give this place "freedom" would be to murder it. To force change upon an entity whose beauty lay in absolute, frictionless stillness would be an act of unspeakable cruelty. The salamanders did not want light; their copper carapaces were made for the dark, wet grease. The kingfishers did not want to fly south; their chalcedony eyes were made for the silence inside the pre-echo. The Litho-Viscera did not want a voice; its heartbeat was already the loudest thing in the mountains.

The cirque was a poem that had achieved its final line, and it wished only to be read forever.

***

So, the Power decided what it would do.

It did not alter a single atom of the machinery. It did not adjust a valve, nor did it bless the moss with flowers. It did not speak a word into the grey twilight.

Instead, the Power stepped *backward*, out of the amphitheater, ascending through the cloud-lid until it hovered high above the jagged peaks, bathed in the blinding, indifferent gold of the outer sun.

There, at the rim of the cirque, the Power went to work.

With gentle, agonizing care, it wove a invisible boundary around the mountain hollow. It drew upon the deep, fundamental threads of cosmic law and tied a knot above the cirque—a vault of preserved causality.

It built a shield against time.

From that moment on:
When tectonic plates shifted below, the shockwaves would bend harmlessly around the cirque’s basalt roots, leaving the bedrock motionless.
When the sun eventually expanded eons hence, scorching the outer world to cinder and ash, the slate-grey cloud-lid would remain cool, low, and damp.
If a wandering human or a stray beast ever climbed to the high ridge and looked down, their eyes would see only a smooth, impenetrable face of dark stone—the cirque would be folded into a sliver of space that existed slightly *beside* reality, untouchable and unseen.

The Power made the loop truly eternal. It gave the cirque what it had always possessed in spirit, but never in physical truth: absolute safety from the rest of the cosmos.

***

Before the Power turned its vast, warm attention back toward the deep galaxy—toward the worlds that were screaming, burning, evolving, and crying out for aid—it did one last thing.

It dropped a single, weightless gift down through the cloud-lid.

It was not a physical object, nor a light, nor a change in temperature. It was a subtle, permanent shift in the resonance of the air—a tiny, harmonic cushion placed precisely beneath the pre-echoes.

Down in the dark, wet hollow, forty-nine blind kingfishers opened their beaks a fraction of a millisecond before a quartz valve closed. They whistled their high, mica-stiff notes. 

The sound did not merely neutralize the shockwave. For the first time in an eternity that had no past, the pre-echo caught the Power's hidden harmonic. The resulting sound was not just a metallic clang, but a deep, velvet bell-tone that vibrated softly through the emerald moss, through the copper carapaces, through the amethyst heads of the Caliper-Walkers, and deep down into the cold marrow of the Litho-Viscera.

It was a sound that said, without words: *You are seen. You are safe. Continue.*

The basalt pistons rose and fell in their microscopic rock sleeves.
*Thrum-clack... HISS.*

A floating droplet of cold water froze in mid-air, drifted upward toward the lid, and melted again.

Nothing changed. Everything remained. And high above, the wise power smiled, and left the engine to its perfect song.
