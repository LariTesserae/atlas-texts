It arrived not as a light from the sky, nor as a voice in the wind, for in a place where light bends into ribbons and the air is a shattering wall of acoustic thunder, such manifestations would be lost like droplets in a surging river.

The Power came instead as a *stillness of attention*. 

It was vast—older than cambium, older than soil, a consciousness that had watched suns ignite and freeze in the deep dark. Yet it possessed the gentle precision of an artisan who touches a delicate thread without snapping it. 

When it first descended into the low, sun-warmed depression, its perception filtered the catastrophic noise. Where a lesser being would have felt only the agony of torn eardrums and shattered bone, the Power heard a liturgy.

It heard the Valve-Borers adjusting their abdominal valves, tuning the organ-pipe trunks to a precise sub-bass chord that kept the air in the plaza circulating. It heard the wet, rhythmic *crack* of the seed-pods, a vital clock beating out the centuries. It heard the hydraulic roar of the sap—millions of gallons surging under high pressure—and recognized it as the pulse of an ancient, solitary heart that had spent ten thousand years learning how to build its own sky.

And it saw the dwellers.

The Power watched a tall, five-jointed entity step off the lip of an amber terrace, flip upside down, and walk along the underside of a suspended vine with impossible grace. It watched the entity stop, press its flat, wood-like sternum against the wet bark, and strike its inner calcified rib. 

*Thump-thump-click.*

Across the plaza, three hundred yards away, another dweller paused mid-stride on a vertical cliff face, its amber head-dome tilted, receiving the message through the microscopic cilia on its feet. 

The Power observed the entire cycle: the soft, newborn galls dissolving in the acid pools; the tender infants hauled out and pressed instantly into the chorus; the ancient elders quietly slowing down, letting the bark wrap around their knees, willingly offering their own bones to become the next staircase, the next flying buttress, the next organ pipe.

It was a closed system. Perfect. Fierce. Terrifyingly beautiful. It asked for nothing. It knew no gods, no stars, no sky beyond the green canopy it had woven for itself.

A foolish power would have tried to "fix" them. It would have quieted the deafening roar to give them peace. It would have straightened the bent light so they could see the stars. It would have offered them speech, or iron, or a life after death that did not end in becoming a wooden step.

But wisdom knows that to cure a creature of its environment is to erase the creature entirely. To quiet the thunder would be to deafen them; to straighten the light would be to blind their amber domes; to save them from becoming architecture would be to rob their lives of their grandest, most selfless resolution.

The Power asked itself: *What does a master garden lack?*

It looked deeper. It slipped its perception down beneath the sloughing bark, past the swarming red-shelled scavengers, deep down through the wet loam, into the dark, petrified roots that anchored the entire basin.

There, it found the vulnerability.

The monumental plant was ancient, but it was bound to the earth. In three thousand years, a shift in the tectonic plates below would drain the water table. In seven thousand years, a blight from the distant wild forests would drift on the wind, threatening the primary vascular conduits. In ten thousand years, the sun above would shift its angle, and the basin would grow cold.

The city was a masterpiece, but it was fragile in the scale of deep time. It was a single organism holding up a world, and one day, its strength would fail.

The Power made its choice. It would not rule them. It would not teach them. It would not even make its presence consciously known to them as a deity. 

It would become their **Root-Anchor**.

Without causing a single tremor, the Power expanded its essence into the deep stone beneath the basin. It wrapped itself around the primary taproots, ten fathoms beneath the churning mud. 

It did three things:

First, it wove an invisible, biological grace into the plant’s cellular walls. From that moment on, no parasite could rot the cambium unless the dwellers willed it; no blight could curdle the sap; the water table beneath the root-bowls was bound to an eternal, subterranean spring that would never run dry. The Power granted the plant *endurance*.

Second, it reached into the strange, warped physics of the plaza—the pockets of distorted gravity and bent sunlight—and stabilized them. It did not untangle them; it simply locked them into place, turning what was a chaotic byproduct of hyper-growth into a permanent, protected sanctuary. The basin became a pocket of quiet spatial eternity, shielded from cosmic impacts, extreme weather, and the slow, cold death of the surrounding world.

Third—and this was its true gift to the dwellers themselves—it participated in the conversation.

The Power had no chest, no ironwood ribs, no bark. But it possessed the fundamental resonance of the universe. 

Deep beneath the basin, against the thickest, oldest taproot, the Power delivered a single, sustained vibration. It was not a sound in the air; it was an acoustic wave that traveled up through the vascular pipes, into the organ-trunk tenements, through the sky-bridges, and out into the flat leaf-terraces.

It was a frequency so low, so deep, that it sat comfortably *beneath* the sub-bass thrum of the sap-conduits and the roaring beetles. It did not drown out their noise—it gave their noise a floor. A baseline.

Up in the warm daylight of the plaza, every citizen froze at once.

On the high sky-bridges, three dwellers dangling upside down paused. On the vertical bark cliffs, six-legged tortoises stopped their slow ascent. Down in the condensation pools, an elder holding a newborn pith-child held the infant still against the wood.

Through the soles of their feet and the broad, flat planes of their sternums, they felt the new note.

It did not carry words. It carried a structural truth. 

To the dwellers, the vibration translated into a simple, undeniable physical reality: *The foundation has deepened. The ground will hold. Strike your ribs as hard as you wish; the wood will never split.*

A young dweller on a central terrace was the first to move. It stepped forward, pressed its chest against the main pillar of a tenement trunk, and struck its calcified rib with all its might.

*THUMP.*

The pulse traveled instantly through the sap-soaked wood. But this time, reinforced by the Power’s subterranean chord, the signal traveled twice as far, ringing clear and bright across the full span of the cathedral-void, echoing into roots that had been silent for a thousand years.

A dozen other dwellers pressed their chests to the bark and answered. *Thump-thump-thump.* The sky-bridges shuddered with the sudden, joyous exchange of messages. The Valve-Borers flexed their abdomens, sending fresh blasts of pressurized sap through the organ pipes, harmony building upon harmony until the very air of the plaza shimmered with the geometric bands of fractured light.

Far below, wrapped around the ancient roots, the Power listened.

It did not demand worship. It did not require their love. It simply remained there, a quiet, benevolent weight in the dark, holding the floor of the world steady so that the silent citizens of the roaring wood could go on building their sky, birthing their children from exploding fruit, and singing their bone-deep song until the end of time.
