The Power came not from above—for height was a vanity this world had buried a billion years ago—but from *around*. 

It was an attention vast enough to wrap a galaxy in a soft fold of thought, yet gentle enough to settle into the two-inch depth of a warm ironstone pool without spilling a single drop over the rim. 

It had watched a trillion worlds. It knew the loud, frantic geometries of young planets: the jagged teeth of young ranges carving up the clouds, the desperate, vertical panic of trees fighting for light, the tragic, brief species that built towers out of mud and marble, always reaching up, always falling down, always screaming against gravity until their suns went dark.

When the Power turned its gaze upon this ancient, bronze-lit shield, it did not see a desert or a wasteland. It saw a place that had *graduated*. 

This world had survived its mountains. It had endured its volcanoes, shed its oceans, folded its continents into a single, polished slab, and laid down to rest. It had solved the problem of posture. There was no hubris left here, no ambition, no edge to crumble, no height from which anything could fall. It was a world entirely at peace with its own center of mass.

A lesser power—one young, eager, and foolish with its own strength—might have looked at this low expanse and felt a impulse to "restore" it. It might have reached down with a fiery finger to lift up great ribs of granite, to carve fresh, jagged canyons, to seed the air with tall, leafy forests and grant the slate-sliders wings so they might conquer the sky. 

To do so would have been an atrocity. It would have been forcing an old, wise monk back into the foolish armor of a adolescent knight. 

The Power was far too wise for such violence. It understood that benevolence does not always mean creation; sometimes, benevolence is the grace of absolute appreciation.

So, the Power did the only holy thing there was to do in a world without hills: 

It flattened itself.

It shed its altitude. It let go of its vast, celestial stature and poured its consciousness out across the regional shield, spreading thin and wide, until its awareness was no thicker than the film of warm water sliding over the dark red ironstone. It laid its cheek directly against the basement of the world.

It felt the heat radiating up through the rock—the deep, quiet heartbeat of a planet’s core, ancient and steady. 

It tasted the wet iron, the bitter tang of warm algae, and the faint, turpentine sweetness of the wire-stemmed shrubs. 

It listened.

For a thousand years—a mere breath in the life of the shield—the Power simply laid there, taking in the roaring, ringing chorus. It learned the distinct, rattling click of every glass-winged dragonfly that defended its patch of golden silt. It tracked the slow, invisible creep of the black lichen across an eighth of an inch of granite. It felt the rhythmic, thrumming shockwaves of millions of dark-green frogs as their mustard-yellow throats ballooned and struck the bedrock, sending waves of pure sound through the water sheets.

It found the oldest shield-turtle in the widest, deepest basin, buried to its stone-flat back in red mud. The Power sat beside it in the tea-colored dark, waiting forty-eight minutes in absolute, blissful patience until the turtle thrust its tiny, tube-like nostrils through the surface to take a single, silent breath of the dense, humid air. 

The Power matched its own breath to the turtle’s. It found the rhythm divine.

When the thousand years had passed, the Power decided what it would leave behind. It would not leave a spire. It would not leave a book, or a altar, or a new law. 

Instead, it gave the world a Gift of Resonance.

Very gently, without causing a single ripple on the surface of the widest pool, the Power pressed a blessing into the molecular matrix of the basement rock. 

It tuned the ironstone.

From that afternoon onward, the stone held the heat just a little longer into the humid nights. The rain, when it fell in lukewarm, sweeping sheets every few afternoons, fell with a softness that smoothed the rock without wearing it away. And deep within the granite—far below the fissures where the dragonfly grubs fed on micro-worms—the Power placed an eternal, silent murmur: a profound, vibrating reassurance that echoed back up to the surface.

A creature living on the shield would never "think" of this murmur, for they had no need for thoughts. But the flat-worms undulating through the algae felt it in their thin, gelatinous skins as a deep, unbroken safety. The reed-legged waders felt it through the soles of their feet as they guarded their granite-speckled eggs on the dry crests, knowing without knowing that no storm would ever overturn them. The slate-sliders felt it against their warm, soft bellies as they sunned themselves in the bronze glare—a feeling that the stone beneath them was permanent, loving, and held fast by a great, invisible hand.

Satisfied, the Power slowly withdrew its vast attention, peeling itself back into the quiet void between the stars. 

It left the world exactly as it had found it: wide, warm, flat, and entirely self-contained. 

Down on the iron pavement, the sun began to dip, turning the vast pane of water into a single, polished mirror of liquid bronze. A slate-slider glided two inches through the shallows, leaving a wake that vanished almost instantly. A dark-green frog inflated its mustard throat and struck the wet stone with a sound like a small, hollow bell.

Miles away, another frog felt the strike against its belly, answered it, and the chorus rose again—loud, endless, and perfectly content at the bottom of the sky.
