The Power did not descend in fire, for fire in this place would be a blasphemy, an apocalypse of dry cellulose and roaring wind. Nor did it come with rain, for a deluge would flood the hollow flues, drown the amber-blooded borers in their galleries, and turn the ancient, polished timber into a slick, rotted marsh, destroying the hard-won elegance of those who walked upon it.

A lesser power, looking upon the five-thousand-meter shelf of lignified tissue, might have pitied it. A lesser power might have thought: *Here is a world made of a corpse. I shall bring soil. I shall bring soft grass and iron and deep, muddy rivers.*

But wisdom is the art of recognizing perfection where it has already paid its price.

The Power was an entity of deep resonance—a consciousness whose thoughts moved not at the speed of light, but at the speed of sap rising through ancient xylem, at the frequency of dry wood contracting under a midnight frost. It listened first. For three hundred grain-shifts, it did not move. It simply felt the sub-audible vibration of the continent humming against its feet; it read the sight-burn glyphs etched on the ironwood chimneys; it felt the dry, rhythmically pulsed tapping of a father’s fingers against his daughter’s forearm, telling her in the shadow of a Choked Flue: *Hold fast. The thermal comes.*

The Power saw that these people were not broken by their world. They were its sharpest, purest expression. To soften them would be to erase them.

So the Power did three things—quietly, beneath the floor of the world, where the petrified vascular bundles sleep in the dark.

***

### I. The Tuning of the Deep Conduits

Far below the sun-scoured surface, five kilometers into the compressed heart of the dead continent, lay the vast, dry tubular networks of the primeval trees—conduits as wide as cathedrals, choked with dry peat and petrified resin.

The Power reached down with a touch lighter than a glider’s wing-strut. It did not fill these conduits with water from outside. Instead, it awoke the latent memory of the wood itself.

Through a process of microscopic, molecular friction, the Power induced a perpetual, low-energy thermal gradient within the deepest strata. It caused the cold, dense air trapped in the miles-deep peat to cycle upward toward the bases of the Choked Flues, while drawing the baking heat of the noon plateau downward.

The result was not a sudden flood, but an eternal, flawless breathe.

Inside the dark, interior wells of the spires, where the resin galls hung fifty feet above the floor, the cold sweat of the wood became steady and pure. The bitter tannins—which had for generations poisoned two out of every five wells with harsh acidity—were naturally filtered as the moisture rose through the newly cleared, porous cork walls. The water gathered in the bowls clean, sweet, and cold, dropping with the rhythmic regularity of a slow, clear heart: *drip... drip... drip.*

The pitch-spinners, feeling the refined dampness, drew back their golden nets slightly, taking only what they needed from the gliders, leaving the central pools open, unclouded, and forever full.

***

### II. The Chord of the Bronze Shells

The second act was an adjustment of sound.

The roar of the drum-beetles was a terrifying thing to endure—a mechanical, bone-shaking clatter that deafened the Grain-Walkers and limited their world to the touch of fingers and the burn of resin-lenses. The beetles did not strike their bellies out of malice, but out of a desperate need to map the shifting density of the floor and find one another across the vast, empty timber-plateaus.

The Power did not silence them. To silence the stridulators would be to render the gliders blind and leave the plow-palps without their acoustic guideposts.

Instead, the Power gently reshaped the inner fluting of the Choked Flues. With invisible, sweeping passes, it smoothed the internal micro-ridges of the hollow ironwood trunks, turning every vertical chimney into a perfectly calibrated acoustic resonator.

When the millions of bronze beetles struck their carapaces against the bark, the harsh, deafening rattle was caught by the flues, folded in upon itself, and converted into a deep, oceanic harmonic. 

It was still loud—it still thrummed through the soles of the Grain-Walkers' calloused feet—but it was no longer a roar. It became a rich, complex, low-frequency chord, a great hymn of bronze and ironwood that echoed across the five-thousand-meter plateau. It no longer shattered speech; it carried it. A high-pitched human whistle could now ride the harmonic crest of the flues for ten miles, carrying messages across the open grain as clearly as a bird’s call across a still lake.

The beetles, feeling their signals amplified and returned with absolute clarity, reduced the violence of their drumming. Their bronze shells gleamed in the high sun, humming in collective, peaceful cadence with the floor beneath them.

***

### III. The Eyes of the Ancestors

The Grain-Walkers had always taken their dead to the high windward fissures of the spires. There, bound in vine-gut and anointed with dammar resin, the dead mummified into dark, hard, translucent statues—sentinels standing forever in the flutes of the ironwood, watching the slow expansion of the world.

The Power looked upon these dead, preserved for centuries in the dry gale, and found in them a profound dignity. They were not forgotten; they were the landmarks of the living.

The Power touched the amber-beaded, resin-sealed eyes of the mummies.

It did not bring them back to life—for their life had been lived fully, hard and light and complete. Instead, it set within their hardened, optic resin a subtle, permanent refraction.

When the blinding noon sun reached its peak—the moment when the continent trembled and the three-mile-long plates of dry bark snapped under thermal stress—the sunlight passing through the eyes of the high-hung ancestors was focused into fine, glowing beams of deep gold.

These golden shafts of light projected downward onto the dark, polished grain of the plateau floor.

Where the floor was whole and safe to cross, the beams laid down a steady, glowing line. Where a hidden stress-fracture was preparing to snap—where a timber-plate was crowned and ready to burst with catastrophic force—the ancestral light split, scattering into a sharp, flickering warning of crossed lines.

The Grain-Walkers, running across the sun-bleached cork on their horn-soled feet, no longer had to guess where the floor would give way. They looked ahead and saw the path laid out before them by the golden gaze of their grandfathers and grandmothers, shining down from the towering flues.

***

### The Unbroken World

When the work was done, the Power did not leave a monument. It built no temples of stone; it left no written testament in a language the wind would only strip away.

At midday, the heat rose off the timber-plain in shimmering waves of liquid glass. Far out on the dark late-wood ridges, a herd of plow-palps grazed in a long, steady line, their keratin chins making their rhythmic *skritch-skritch-skritch* against the lichen. Above them, ribbon-tailed gliders caught the thermal updrafts, their translucent wings glowing red against the pale wood-dust sky, their tail-ribbons tracing sharp arcs in the hot air.

In the split of a cathedral-sized flue, a Grain-Walker family sat within their bark-shingle pod.

The father leaned over the edge, dropping a vessel made of stitched glider-skin into the interior well. When he drew it up, the water was cold, clear, and so pure it smelled of sweet cedar without a trace of bitter tannin. He handed it to his child.

Below them, the floor gave its ancient midday roar—a tremendous *crack-snap* as a three-mile plate of bark shifted by half an inch. But the family did not startle. They had seen the golden grid cast by the ancestors’ eyes an hour before, and their shelter hung safely in the neutral zone of the fissure.

The father reached out and tapped his fingers against his daughter’s arm—a swift, rhythmic sequence of light, dry strikes against her scorched-walnut skin:

*The well is sweet.*
*The floor is set.*
*The wind is singing.*

Through the soles of their feet, through the bark shingles of their home, through the very marrow of their thin, strong bones, they felt the deep, resonant chord of the world—a million bronze beetles drumming against a continent of ironwood, perfectly tuned, endlessly vast, and watched over by a presence that demanded nothing of them except that they continue to endure, light and hard, upon the endless grain.
