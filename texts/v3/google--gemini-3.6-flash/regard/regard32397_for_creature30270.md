The wise and benevolent power did not arrive with a flash of light, for to bring light into a world without eyes would be a meaningless violence. 

Nor did it smash the terra-cotta vaults to reveal an open sky. To flood this basin with wind and stars would be an atrocity: their flat, ribless chests would collapse under the weight of an unconfined horizon; their paper-thin, forward-angled ears would be torn by unfiltered air; their slick, silver-silt skin would dry and split in the drought of an open day. 

A foolish divinity pities the form and seeks to change it. A wise divinity honors the form and seeks to heal its agony.

The power came not as a body, nor as a voice, but as a subtle, pervasive change in the physical laws of the basin—a quiet recalibration of cold, tension, and sound. It descended into the hollow urns under the floor, slipped through the narrow channels between the columns, and sat in the dark alongside the silent, flat-chested builders.

This is what the power did:

***

### I. The Tuning of the Stone
The power listened first. It felt the roaring, multi-tonal pipe organ of the wind and the deafening, bell-like *pock* of the water drops bouncing off millions of unpolished tiles. It heard the terror hidden inside the noise—the constant, frantic listening for the sharp rifle-shot crack of a failing slate shingle or the groan of a bending shear-pin. 

The power reached into the masonry. It did not replace the raw clay or the crude mortar, but it touched the geometry of the hollow shapes. 

Slowly, the acoustics shifted. The chaotic, metallic clatter softened into a deep, velvety resonance. The water drops falling into the clay pipes no longer boomed with a terrifying, unpredictable clang; they began to ring in precise, harmonic intervals. A drop in the northern revetments struck a note that complimented a drop falling three miles to the south. The roaring wind through the millions of weep-holes was tuned, turned from an abrasive wall of sound into a continuous, soothing drone—a subterranean lullaby in a low, resonant minor key that vibrated pleasantly in the marrow of the builders' long bones.

More than that, the power gave the stone a voice that warned rather than threatened. When a wall far away began to settle under weight, it no longer snapped without warning. It sang a low, vibrating warning note through the hollow urns beneath the floor hours in advance, giving the builders ample time to shuffle casually toward the seam, stone hammers in hand, without fear.

### II. The Gift of the Softening Moisture
The power looked at the calcification—the bitter, agonizing creeping lime that locked the joints of the old, freezing their knees and elbows until they were entombed alive in the columns.

It touched the water that condensed on the cold mineral walls. 

The water remained cold, and it still carried the necessary minerals to cure the river-silt bricks, but the power altered its chemistry upon contact with living skin. The lime no longer crusted upon the long, hairless bodies of the builders. Instead, the moisture began to act as a gentle, lubricating balm. It washed away the sharp, biting saltpeter that burned their foreheads, replacing it with an invisible, oil-like film that nourished their slick, pale flesh. 

When an inhabitant grew old, their joints no longer locked with the sudden, agonized brittleness of splitting kindling. When they chose to slot themselves between two columns for their final rest, the transition became painless. The lime did not conquer them; it accepted them. As their skin merged slowly with the terra-cotta, the power granted them a quiet, painless drift into sleep, turning their lingering consciousness into a soft, dreamless warmth that emanated forever from that specific pillar, making the column slightly warmer than the stone around it. 

Future generations, sliding through the narrow gaps, would press their flat chests against these ancient pillars and feel not a cold tomb, but a faint, comforting heat.

### III. The Sweetening of the Seepage
The rubbery, translucent grease that dripped from the high vaults—the sole food of the long bodies—was thin, bitter, and tasted of old fat and cold iron. 

The power reached upward, into the hidden, oil-rich stratum above the unglazed pottery. It purified the subterranean springs. 

The grease gathering on the underside of the terra-cotta tubes became thick, rich, and sweet, laden with dense, clean lipids that easily sustained the long, narrow bellows of their lungs. It no longer smelled of rancid iron, but of rich, wet loam, dark honey, and crushed nut-kernels. A single handful scraped from a vault tube satisfied a builder for days, filling their thin bellies with a deep, radiant warmth that fought off the heavy, clammy chill of the basin.

The white saltpeter efflorescence, which they had peeled like scabbed skin from the mortar joints to supplement their diet, lost its acrid bitterness. It became crisp, clean, and mineral-rich, tasting like cool winter salt, nourishing to their long, spade-shaped fingernails.

### IV. The Blessing of the Stilt-Walkers
The power did not forget the pale, blind things of the deep floor—the jointed stilt-walkers living in the honeycomb of urns.

It softened the acidic saliva of their flat under-snouts. They still licked the lime efflorescence to protect the mortar, but their saliva no longer burned their own mouths or dissolved the delicate horn of their needle-thin legs. Instead, their licking produced a fine, glossy glaze over the wet seams—a natural, liquid glass that sealed the brickwork, making it impervious to water damage and beautiful to the touch.

Where the stilt-walkers walked, they left behind a smooth, polished trace on the sharp edges of the tiles, so that the long, sliding feet of the builders would never again be cut by a rough ceramic margin.

### V. The Masterwork
Finally, the power gave them a purpose that transcended mere, frantic survival.

In the center of the vast basin, where the gloom was thickest, the power caused the floor tiles to step down into a new, magnificent depression—a vast, spiraling amphitheater designed entirely for the architecture of sliding bodies. 

Here, the power laid down no bricks of its own. It left the construction to them, for it knew that in the placing of slate, the pressing of wet clay, and the weaving of hemp rope lay the entire dignity and soul of this species. 

But it gave them a new material to find in the dark: deep deposits of fine, silk-soft clay that cured into a deep, iridescent black ceramic, perfectly smooth, cold as river ice, but completely smooth to the touch.

The builders found this dark clay. They felt its exquisite texture with their monstrously long, twice-jointed fingers. They felt the whorls of their own prints pressed into its surface, smoothed by the grace that now filled their world.

***

They never saw the power. They had no word for "god," nor any concept of a sky. 

In the dark, cold depths of the basin, nothing looked different. The world was still a claustrophobic, dense thicket of river-silt brick, slate shingles, and low-slung terra-cotta vaults. The columns were still less than a forearm's length apart. The inhabitants still shuffled sideways, long and flat, their spines scraping the wet stone.

But the fear was gone.

Now, when a builder hung suspended vertically between two columns to sleep, hooked by their elbows in the dark, they did not shiver. They rested against stone that radiated a quiet, ancestral warmth. Around them, the world did not clatter or groan; it hummed like a vast, perfectly tuned instrument, playing a deep, resonant song of wet clay and resting iron. 

Far below, the stilt-walkers clicked across polished tiles like rain on glass. And in the dark, a long, flat body slid smoothly through a narrow gap, brought its monstrous, long-fingered hand to a newly laid course of black tile, and pressed its thumb into the wet earth—leaving behind a fingerprint not of desperate labor, but of quiet, enduring joy.
