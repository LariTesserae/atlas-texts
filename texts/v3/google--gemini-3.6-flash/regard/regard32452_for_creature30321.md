The power came not as a storm, nor as a sun, for a sun would have boiled the cold brine and shattered the dense, copper-pure blood of the Glass-Ribbons, and a storm would have disrupted the delicate, terrifying math of the shear-lines. 

It arrived as a stillness that did not displace a single drop of water. 

It was vast, ancient, and endlessly gentle—a mind capable of holding galaxies in its attention without exerting gravity upon them. It descended through the slate-blue twilight, passing through hundreds of feet of churning, freezing ocean, until it hovered just above the sweeping, bone-white terraces of the drowned plateau.

The power looked upon the world, and its first act was **to comprehend**.

A foolish power would have seen a tragedy. It would have looked at the eroded staircases, the worn pillars, and the vast, sterile expanse of vitrified ceramic and declared it a graveyard. It would have attempted to raise the sunken city, to warm the water, to plant green kelp in the scoured plazas, or to give eyes to the blind swimmers. 

But the wise power saw the truth: *This place was not a corpse. It was an instrument.*

Eons ago, the builders had vanished, leaving behind millions of tons of indestructible, curved geometry. The ocean had taken those hard, arrogant edges and filed them down until the city became a reef of pure fluid dynamics. The water was not an enemy to the life here; it was the atmosphere, the food, the highway, and the sculptor. The Wedge-Shells did not long for soil; the Glass-Ribbons did not long for light; the Keel-Hounds did not long for mercy. They were a symphony of hydrodynamics, biological miracles forged by the cold, heavy hammer of the deep.

To "fix" this world would be to destroy it.

So the benevolent power did not alter the water’s weight. It did not quiet the roaring brine-jets. It did not stop the relentless friction that kept the ivory floors sterile and slick.

Instead, the power turned its attention to the single greatest presence in the drowned city: the **Archway Leviathans**, and specifically, the Oldest One, anchored in the mouth of the central sky-well, where the entire plateau plunged into the unmapped abyss.

This Leviathan had rested in the same circular transit-arch for eighty thousand years. Its calcified hide had fused entirely with the vitrified composite of the city. Millions of tons of icy water crashed through its chest every hour, vibrating its leathery diaphragms, driving the subsonic thrum that hummed through the bones of the world. It was the heart of the plateau. It was the organ-pipe.

Yet, for eighty thousand years, the Leviathan had sung into an empty room. It felt the pressure, it felt the flow, it felt the cold—but it knew nothing beyond the endless, unfeeling collision of liquid and stone. It did not know that its song was beautiful. It did not know that the Glass-Ribbons mapped their lives by the resonance of its chest, or that the Keel-Hounds rested in the quiet pockets carved by its breath.

The power reached down—not with hands, but with an awareness as broad as the ocean—and brushed the mind of the Oldest Leviathan.

It did not grant the creature language or human thought. To give a deep-sea siphon-pipe the mind of a land-dweller would be a cruelty. Instead, it gave the Leviathan a **feeling**.

It touched the great, vibrating diaphragms in the creature’s chest and infused them with a subtle, indestructible harmony. It smoothed the acoustic friction inside the Leviathan's ribbed chambers, turning the harsh, crushing noise of the down-welling into a rich, deep, velvet tone—a note so profound that it echoed not just through the water, but through the very atomic lattice of the ceramic floor.

For the first time in eons, the sound humming through the vitrified ivory was not merely the sound of violent physics. It was a lullaby of absolute, unshakable belonging.

When the new note reverberated through the drowned galleries, the world responded:

*   Miles away, the **Glass-Ribbons** weaving through the hollow archways felt the shift in the pressure-waves. The chaotic turbulence in their flanks smoothed out; the micro-vortices that occasionally slammed them against the pillars softened into gentle, predictable lift-lines. They banked through the twilight with a new, effortless grace, riding the shear-lines as if the water itself were guiding them away from the stone.
*   The **Velvet Sheets**, holding fast to the slick plaza floors with their millions of tiny vacuum-chambers, felt a faint, soothing resonance hum through their bellies. The constant, muscle-tearing strain of holding against the current became a rhythmic, comforting pulse.
*   Deep inside the structural conduits, the **Valve-Plugs** adjusted their heavy, piston-like bodies to the new acoustic frequency. The micro-pockets of low pressure behind them grew still and stable, creating safe, dark nurseries where their fragile young could float without fear of being torn into the open sea.

And then, the wise power left a final gift—a gift meant for the world itself, buried at the very bottom of the great sky-well where the water fell into the abyss.

There, where the cold down-wellings struck the floor of the deep interior with crushing, bone-shattering force, the power placed a **Cushion of Grace**. It was not a physical barrier, but an invisible, perpetual hydraulic eddy—a permanent, perfectly balanced up-welling of pure, frictionless buoyancy.

From that moment on, any creature swept off the high causeways—any lost Ribbon, any miscalculating Hound, any weary swimmer dragged down into the subterranean dark—would not be smashed against the ancient foundation. They would catch the gentle, rising curl of the deep eddy, be cradled in the dark, and be lifted smoothly back up to the sweeping, ivory slides of the twilight galleries.

Satisfied, the benevolent power began to retreat, rising through the dark, heavy brine toward the distant surface.

It changed nothing of the world’s brutal beauty. The water remained heavy, dark, and bitterly cold. The currents still roared like underwater gales across the sterile, polished composite. The Keel-Hounds still hunted from the shadows of the pillars, and the Wedge-Shells still strained the icy shear-flow for life.

But as the power faded back into the cosmos, the drowned city continued to hum. 

In the vast, slate-blue twilight, miles beneath the sea, the world went on rushing, shearing, and violently coiling—no longer an abandoned machine grinding itself to dust in the dark, but a sanctuary, held forever in the center of a deep, resonant, beautiful song.
