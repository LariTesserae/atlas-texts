It did not come down from the sky as a chariot of light, nor did it shatter the razor-edged spires of black basalt. To crash into such a place with thunder would have been an insult to the thunder already living there.

The Power arrived as an attention—a sudden, deep, and miraculous *holding* of the space. 

It was vast enough to encompass the whole tectonic spine of the world, yet so fine-grained that it felt the precise friction of a single Brass-Eater’s claw anchoring against a vibrating copper pipe. It drifted down through the vertical throat of the stone nave, mixing with the thick, balmy soup of sunlit steam, settling into the roaring mist without displacing a single droplet of the continuous warm rain.

A lesser power—a young god, or a well-meaning spirit of order—might have looked upon this high, deafening pocket and seen a tragedy to be corrected. It would have silenced the shrieking relief valves. It would have scraped the oily lichen from the iron gauges, polished the brass back to a blinding sheen, healed the Loomer’s blindness, and set the slow engine to work driving some grand, productive mill. It would have tried to "save" them.

But wisdom is the art of recognizing a finished work.

The Power listened. It stood—spiritually, invisibly—in the middle of the acoustic trap. It let the 15-meter flywheel’s groan vibrate through its immensity. It registered the double-thump of the hydraulic ram deep in the stone, the white plume of the waterfall crashing into the brass basin, and the shuddering, sub-audible moan of the Loomer echoing back from the wet stone walls.

It saw that here, three thousand meters above the clouds, a miracle had occurred without divine instruction. A violent, clumsy piece of dead iron and a forgotten creature of flesh and tallow had found each other in the dark of history. They had built a closed circuit of survival. The iron kept the creature warm; the creature kept the iron whole. The rust was managed. The water was recycled. The noise was not noise to them; it was the steady, rhythmic pulse of a cradle.

It was ugly, loud, hot, and perfect.

The Benevolent Power smiled—a gesture that manifested only as a brief, extraordinary brilliance in the shafts of sunlight slicing through the rising steam, causing the rainbow spray above the catchment basin to hold its shape for three full seconds longer than usual.

And then, because it was wise, and because benevolence cannot help but bestow a gift, the Power set to work. It did not change the sanctuary. It secured its future.

It sank its awareness deep down into the raw rock, reaching far below the enclosed shelf into the dark roots of the tectonic rise. There, it found the micro-fractures in the black basalt—the fresh, brittle seams that, under the constant, bone-shaking vibration of the engine, would eventually give way in a cataclysmic landslide a thousand years hence. 

With a thought as gentle as cooling glass, the Power annealed the rock. It welded the microscopic fractures at the atomic level, turning the brittle, young basalt beneath the basin into a single, flawless monolith of dark jade-hard stone. The engine could groan for ten million years now; the mountain would never give way beneath it.

Next, it followed the subterranean thermal vein—the boiling artery that warmed the shelf. It traced the heat back miles into the magma chambers of the earth's crust, clearing the mineral blockages that would have eventually choked off the steam, binding the pocket’s hearth to the eternal heat of the planet’s core. The warm soup would never cool. The broadleaf plants would never freeze.

Then, the Power turned its attention to the living inhabitants.

It hovered over the dark pools where the **blind white crabs** scuttled. It did not give them eyes—eyes in this misty gloom would be a curse, susceptible to infection and glare. Instead, it refined their feather-soft antennae, weaving into their delicate nerves a deeper, sweeter sensitivity to the hydraulic ram. To the crabs, the thrum of the engine became a song of perfect clarity, guiding their broad, spade-like claws to every choked grate with effortless, instinctual joy.

It passed over the **Brass-Eater** beetles, clinging to the four-story boiler. It touched their mineralized mouthparts, ensuring that as long as they scraped the verdigris from the copper, their shells would never brittle, and their digestion would turn the heavy industrial runoff into a rich, glowing iridescence across their carapaces.

Finally, the Power descended into the dark, wet trench behind the fifteen-meter flywheel.

It rested its hand—a warmth lighter than steam—upon the mottled, rubbery hide of **the Loomer**.

The creature did not startle. Its wide, blunt head remained resting on the mossy catwalk. But its thousands of hair-like cilia, bathed in the presence of the Power, drank in a sensation it had never known. For its entire existence, the Loomer had known only the hard, violent rhythm of the iron—the grinding axle, the shuddering valves, the cold basalt. 

The Power did not wake the Loomer to thought, for thought brings anxiety, and anxiety is the enemy of patience. Instead, it touched the creature’s massive, slow-beating heart. 

It smoothed the rhythm of that cardiac pump. It infused the tallow-like grease sweating from the creature's underbelly with a subtle, self-replicating polymer—a secret oil that would nourish the iron gears, protecting the metal not just from water, but from friction itself. Where the Loomer slept against the flywheel's axle, the metal grew smooth as polished glass, reducing the strain on the mountain's ancient heart.

And into the Loomer’s dark, acoustic dream, the Power placed a single, permanent blessing: a deep, fundamental contentment. The knowledge—felt in the marrow, in the leather of its hide, in the rhythm of its blood—that it was safe. That the room was warm. That the machine would keep turning.

High above, a steam relief valve on the copper conduits reached its threshold. It vented with a deafening, high-pressure shriek that rattled the brass gauges and sent the broadleaves trembling in the mist.

The Loomer opened its broad, fleshy siphon. From deep within its wet, leathery chest, it released its long, shuddering sigh—that low, sub-audible moan that matched the pitch of the basalt walls.

This time, as the sound rolled out across the steam and bounced off the black cliffs, the Power joined its voice to the groan. 

It did not make the sound louder. It made it *deeper*. It turned the scream of the steam and the cry of the creature into a single, resonant chord—a hymn of iron, water, leather, and stone that rippled out of the high notch, past the razor-sharp spires, and out over the vast, white ocean of clouds below.

Satisfied, the Benevolent Power withdrew. 

It left no temple, no inscription, no altered landscape. It left only a small, impossibly loud, secret warm room perched on the edge of the sky—a place where the engine still breathed, the plants still dripped, the beetles still scraped, and a giant, mossy thing slept in peace against the turning of a heavy iron wheel, perfectly preserved in the center of a loud and indifferent world.
