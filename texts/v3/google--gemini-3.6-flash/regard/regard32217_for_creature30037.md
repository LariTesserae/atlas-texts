It came not as a storm, nor as a fiery wheel descending from the upper atmospheric haze, but as a shadow without a sun—a vast, silent cooling that settled over the high terraces like a breath held in reverence.

The Power was ancient, born of worlds where flesh was soft and trees bled amber, but its wisdom was total. It did not possess the clumsy, impatient benevolence of lesser gods who see a desert and demand a forest, or who encounter a silence and fill it with noise. It looked upon the vast, red-grey continent of cast lime—this vessel fresh from the cosmic kiln—and it understood.

It saw that this place was not broken. It was not waiting for soil. It was not grieving for rivers or forests it had never known. It was simply *young*, operating on a scale of time where a thousand years was merely a brief evening spent cooling on the bench.

The Power observed the *strata-strips* deep within the razor-thin expansion joints, holding their breath of mineralized brine until the midday heat pinched the continental plates together. 

It observed the *sinter-sheets*, brittle as unglazed porcelain by day, softening into rubbery leather under the pre-dawn fog to etch the world’s very first trails of wear across the pristine floor.

It observed the *vent-fans*, their translucent kelp-like sails singing a low, dry harmonic in the twenty-degree thermal winds, catching the microscopic dust of the world to feed their calcified roots.

And it observed the *pavement-marrows*, six inches thick and twenty feet long, endlessly eating their own tails and casting themselves forward in the dark concavities, turning the hard, factory-fresh striations of the earth into something worn, polished, and lived-in.

The Power realized that to drop a single handful of rich, dark earth onto these plates would be an act of unspeakable violence. Soil would choke the narrow expansion joints, suffocating the strata-strips in their sleep. A sudden, lush canopy of clouds would block the harsh solar radiation that powered the thermal engines of the marrow-slabs. A torrential river would fracture the six-inch terrace lips that gave the sinter-sheets their shade.

The life here did not need to be saved from the stone. The life *was* the stone, learning how to feel.

So the Power did what only the truly wise and benevolent can do: **it became the world’s guardian of time.**

***

First, it descended beneath the continental casting. 

Plunging miles below the dense, hydraulic cement floor, the Power settled into the raw, dark mantle beneath the plates. There, it spread itself thin, becoming a vast, resilient cushion—a thermal and kinetic damper. It absorbed the deep, violent tectonic tremors that threatened to snap the continental plates along un-jointed lines. It ensured that when the earth expanded under the midday sun, the plates would slide along their pre-cast seams with fluid, perfect precision, never buckling, never shattering the sharp, clean lips of the terraces.

Second, it reached up into the sky. 

It did not alter the pale, hot sun, for the life below required that hard, unforgiving glare. Instead, the Power woven an invisible, high-altitude lattice—a subtle atmospheric lens. This lens filtered out cosmic radiation and solar flares that might boil the deep moisture out of the atmosphere too quickly. It stabilized the weather cycle of the floor itself, guaranteeing that every night, without fail, the pressure in the broad concavities would shift at precisely the right hour, drawing down the thick, cool dew that the sinter-sheets needed to rehydrate.

Third, and most gently, the Power gave the world a voice to match its own.

The Power listened to the world's music: the sharp clicks, the dull metallic *tongs*, the deep, resonant groans of expanding masonry, and the dry, whistling sigh of the strata-strips venting steam. 

The Power did not speak over this music. Instead, it joined it. 

Deep within the stone, the Power began to vibrate at a frequency so low it could not be heard, only felt through the soles of an imagined foot. It provided a fundamental bass note to the continent’s expansion—a smooth, lubricating resonance that made the heavy plates move with less friction. Where the *tongs* of the terracotta had once been harsh and sudden, they became deep, resonant chimes, ringing across fifty miles of terrace like cathedral bells forged from baked clay and iron.

***

Now, the world continues as it was, but it is watched.

High above, when the localized cumulus clouds form over the steaming gutters, they trail shadows across the red floor that are slightly cooler, slightly more comforting than they used to be. 

Down in the six-inch shadows, a sinter-sheet slides across the damp striations of the floor. It does not know that a god is watching it. It knows only that the stone beneath its belly is slightly easier to etch today, that the lime is slightly richer, and that the morning fog arrives with absolute, loving punctuality.

In the dark seams, a twenty-foot strata-strip feels the vast terracotta plate press down upon its cartilage body. The pressure is immense, but it is clean; it does not crush. The strip vents its rhythmic jet of steam, a tiny, whistling sigh of contentment that rises into the hot air.

And out in the wide concavities, a pavement-marrow exhales its ring of warm, humid fog into the freezing night. It casts its lime slurry forward, inching along a path that will take a thousand years to complete. 

It moves in safety. Above it, the stars do not fall. Beneath it, the floor does not break. Surrounding it, the immense, quiet world continues its long, slow work of cooling—and beside it, invisible, vast, and infinitely patient, the Power sits on the edge of the terrace, listening to the stone learn how to breathe.
