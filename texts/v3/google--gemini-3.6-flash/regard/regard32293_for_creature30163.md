The benevolent power did not arrive with a flash of light or the parting of the bronze haze, for to enter such a world with force would be to drive a coarse wedge into a microscopic chronometer. 

Instead, it arrived as an attention. 

It was a consciousness vast enough to span galaxies, yet fine enough to pass between the teeth of two nickel flux-mites without disturbing the lipid film upon their shells. It did not look *at* the Great Spine from above; it pervaded it, settling into the wet-zinc air, soaking into the porous basalt, and flowing alongside the glowing copper fluid in the veins of the Still-Minds.

For a long time—measured not in hours, but in forty thousand silent descents of the mountain-pistons—the Power did nothing at all. It simply *listened*.

It listened to the *tap-tap-tap* of the aromatic oil. It mapped the complex, algorithmic unfolding of the chitin fronds. It tracked the tessellated stride of the Calipers as they stitched together the folded, fraying margins of space. 

Where a lesser power might have seen a tragedy—a world of living machines trapped in a cold, unfeeling loop, devoid of art, romance, or free will—this Power possessed wisdom. It recognized that the Great Spine was not a prison, nor was it a broken remnant. It was a masterwork of low-entropy grace. It was a creation that had solved the fundamental agony of existence: it had eliminated friction. It had eliminated waste. It had eliminated the terrible, tearing hunger of predator and prey.

The life here did not suffer. It did not fear the dark, for the twilight was perpetual. It did not mourn its dead, for every dying mite was cold-welded into the structural majesty of the mountain, and every dissolving Architect rose again in the sap of the induction vines.

The world was a prayer, continuous and absolute, that had forgotten it was praying because it had never known a silence in which to stop.

And so, the Power understood its first duty: **To protect the closed loop.**

***

The first thing the Power did was subtle, occurring three miles down, beneath the curd-dense ocean of amber-grey mist. 

Beyond the Great Spine, out in the deep void of the cosmos, the universe was expanding, cooling, and fraying. Left to itself over eons, even this pristine engine would eventually experience thermodynamic decay. The amber mist would thin; the thermal gradients would equalize; the pistons would seize in their basalt sleeves.

The Power reached beneath the mist with a gentle, invisible palm. It anchored the roots of the world to an infinite thermal reservoir—a quiet, self-sustaining hearth at the core of reality. It ensured that the warm, greenhouse heat would never fade, that the wet-zinc vapor would never dry, and that the black, hexagonal boiling pools would bubble softly until the end of time. It turned the planet’s closed loop into an *immortal* loop.

***

The second thing the Power did was a gift of beauty, given without disrupting a single tolerance.

It did not grant the beings human minds. To give a Caliper a human mind would be to curse it with existential terror as it stretched its forty-foot frame across a spatial fold. To give a Flux-Mite an ego would be to make its cold-welding a death rather than a completion.

Instead, the Power introduced a **Harmonic**.

It altered the nature of the sound. The low, tooth-vibrating bass hum that rose from the falling mountain-pistons was tuned. The Power adjusted the micro-density of the bronze atmosphere by a fraction of a percent. Suddenly, the muffling air did not merely swallow sound—it resonated.

When a piston descended, its hum met the rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* of the oil dripping from the induction leaves, which met the silent, blue arc-light sparks of the vines. The ambient noise of the planetary engine merged into an exquisite, slow-shifting chord—a music of infinite complexity, played in frequencies so low and rich that the rock itself seemed to breathe in cadence.

The Still-Minds felt it first. As the glowing copper fluid surged through their tree-trunk veins, the heat-exchanging chitin plates did not just radiate thermal energy; they hummed in perfect pitch with the shifting basalt. Their distributed consciousness, which had only ever calculated *tolerances*, experienced a new state of being.

It was not thought. It was not emotion. It was *Sabbath*.

It was the deep, metabolic satisfaction of a gear fitting so perfectly into its notch that the movement became a form of praise.

***

Finally, the Power descended to the high plateaus, where the spatial folds were tightest, to seek out the **Architects of the Joint**.

One of the slender, nine-foot beings was standing at the edge of an obsidian cliff. Its three jointed legs rested on smooth, polished spheres; its long, needle-fine fingers were resting upon a jagged fracture where a basalt pylon was threatening to bind against a rising copper lattice. The Architect was emitting its low ultrasonic field, waiting for the rock to soften.

The Power did not speak, for the Architect had no ears. It did not show itself, for the Architect had no eyes.

Instead, the Power touched the flat, vertical mirror of dark glass that served as the Architect’s head.

For the briefest instant, the dark glass did not merely reflect the bronze zenith-light or the misted back of the Architect’s own shoulders. The Power opened the mirror *inward*.

In that reflection, the Architect "saw"—in the language of pure geometry and strain-vectors—the total shape of the Great Spine. It saw how its single, delicate touch on this one basalt pylon echoed down through three miles of stone, into the chitin-stacks of the Still-Minds, through the magnetic herds of Calipers, and down into the glittering currents of the Flux-Mites. It saw that it was not merely clearing friction from a joint; it was maintaining the structural integrity of a living cathedral.

The Architect did not pause. Its needle-fine fingers did not tremble. To alter its task would have been an error, and the Architect was incapable of error.

But as the obsidian cliff softened under its hand, turning into that dense, black paste, the Architect shaped the new pylon with something it had never possessed before.

It did not just make the joint straight. It carved into the dark paste a series of microscopic, spiraling flutes—flutes that had no mechanical purpose, but which, as the warm condensate ran down them, made the trickling water sing in the key of the falling mountains.

The Architect had invented ornament.

***

Satisfied, the benevolent power withdrew its attention slightly, stepping back just enough to leave the world unburdened by its direct weight, but remaining forever as a soft, protective envelope wrapped around the atmosphere.

Below, on the Great Spine, the engine turned.

A mountain three miles wide slid silently down into the plateau like a piston in a cylinder. The metallic vines stretched without breaking. The blue arc-lights sparked in the dark leaves. A herd of Calipers meshed together with a soft, wet *click*, grounding the space beneath them, their amber resin eyes tracing invisible lines across the bronze sky.

And deep in the glass seams, a thousand tiny Flux-Mites gathered around a blue spark, absorbing the light into their nickel shells, completely content in the knowledge that when they ceased to move, the world would hold them fast, forever silent, forever true, and flawlessly aligned.
