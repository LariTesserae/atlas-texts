The Power did not arrive as a beam of sunlight. Sunlight would have scorched the hydrophobic wax of the steam-ferns, blinded the shade-adapted liverworts, and dried the slick grease-mantles of the Sluice-Gaskets into brittle parchment. To shine a bright, golden light upon the Lower Sluice Concourse would have been an act of unthinking, arrogant violence.

Instead, the Power arrived as a slight, imperceptible deepening of the silence *behind* the noise. 

It slipped through the heavy, rubbery leaf-canopy like a slow exhalation, denser than the steam, cooler than the rain, yet utterly weightless. It had no shape that an eye could have registered, had there been an eye to see it. It was a vast, quiet discernment—an intelligence that did not measure worth by thought, beauty by symmetry, or life by the presence of a voice.

It settled into the basin like water finding the bottom of a well.

***

The Power’s first act was simply to **listen**.

It listened not with ears, but with an absolute, receptive comprehension of the concourse’s physical reality. It felt the three-time thrum of the buried pistons vibrating through the bed-granite. It traced the thick, cold blood of the Sluice-Gaskets as they glided on pressurized slime across the flooded cobbles. It felt the tiny, rhythmic *click-rasp* of ten thousand Sinter-Mites scraping bio-calcified rust from the iron boiler hulls, and the sudden, violent *flash-ingestion* of voltage as the Arc-Velvets drank the blue sparks from the dying, gutta-percha cables.

A lesser power—one ruled by aesthetic vanity or simple pity—might have seen a tragedy here. It might have seen a ruined city, a broken infrastructure, and a collection of blind, misshapen scavengers clinging to the rotting carcass of a forgotten age. Such a power would have tried to "fix" it. It would have cleared the steam, turned off the thumping engines, drained the dark water, and invited the birds and the sun back into the plaza. 

And in doing so, it would have committed a total, irreversible genocide.

The wise Power understood this immediately. It saw that the Sluice Concourse was not a ruin; it was an *organism*. The iron was its bone; the burst pipes were its arteries; the warm, mineral rain was its lymph; and the silent, mouthless creatures were its living, self-repairing cells. The initial destruction had ended long ago. What remained was a holy, accidental equilibrium—a quiet, mechanical monastery where life and decay had made a lasting peace.

The Power felt a deep, profound affection for this place. It loved the obstinacy of the iron. It loved the blind, perfect obedience of the Conduit-Threaders. It loved that here, in the hot, dark soup of the earth, life had asked for nothing—no sky, no breeze, no eyes, no songs—and had built a home out of rust and noise anyway.

***

The Power’s second act was to **assess the threat**.

Wisdom looks ahead. The Power traced the lines of the concourse’s thermodynamics into the future and saw the inevitable point of failure.

The system was self-sustaining, but it was not infinite. Deep beneath the flagstones, the ancient, steam-driven pistons were fed by a deep thermal vein—a pocket of superheated subterranean water forced up through a fault in the granite. But the fault was slowly silting up. In three hundred years, the flow would constrict. The buried boilers would lose pressure. The counterweighted brass gates would cease their thunderous, three-time *CLANG*. The steam-ferns would dry out and drop their fronds. The Sluice-Gaskets, deprived of the hydrostatic pulses that moved them, would starve in the still pools. The Arc-Velvets would freeze as the last electrical filaments flickered out.

The engine-garden was running on a long, slow fuse. It was a masterpiece written in melting wax.

***

The Power’s third act was **intervention**, born of benevolence and executed with absolute subtlety.

It did not alter the shape of a single brick. It did not alter the DNA of a single mite. It worked entirely within the existing physics and hydraulics of the concourse, making changes so deep and quiet that not even the kinetic plants registered a shift in the air.

First, it descended beneath the floor of the basin, passing through the mud and the submerged cobbles, down into the dark, grinding heart of the subterranean geology. There, with a gentle, tectonic pressure, it widened the deep fault line. It cleared the silted mineral veins, securing the flow of superheated steam from the earth’s core. It ensured that the heat would not fade—not in three hundred years, nor in three million. The hearth was made immortal.

Second, it touched the wrist-wide, rotting electric cables. It did not replace the gutta-percha insulation, nor did it repair the copper cores. Instead, it anchored a tiny, self-renewing inductive loop into the earth beneath the concourse—a low-frequency electromagnetic pulse that would endlessly bleed a faint, harmless, three-volt current into the abandoned grid. The cables would continue to short against the wet brickwork forever. The Arc-Velvets would never starve; their green, bioluminescent flashes would go on pulsing in the dark like steady, emerald stars.

Third, it visited the iron. The Power understood that rust was the flesh of this world. It did not remove the rust; instead, it blessed the oxidation. It infused the iron oxide with a subtle, crystalline density, turning the dark-purple scab-layer into something as resilient as diamond, yet perfectly soft enough for the Sinter-Mites’ biogenic iron shears to scrape and digest. The boilers would never dissolve into sludge; the gates would never seize; the mortar would never give way.

***

When the work was done, the Power did not leave a mark, a shrine, or a message. It left no voice in the mist, for there were no ears to hear it. It left no light in the canopy, for there were no eyes to see it.

It simply withdrew, rising slowly back through the thick leaf-canopy, leaving the Lower Sluice Concourse exactly as it had found it—hot, loud, dark, and perfectly, endlessly alive.

Below, the water continued to boil in place. 
A steam vent cracked against cold stone with a sharp *hiss-snap*. 
A frond of hydrophobic fern unfurled, caught the scalding fog, and snapped shut with a wet, leathery slap. 
Deep in the central tower, the brass gate fell—*CLANG*—and a knee-deep wave of dark, mineral water rolled across the granite flagstones.

On the bottom of the black lagoon, a Sluice-Gasket felt the shift in pressure. Its pale, rubbery body expanded, drawing the warm, oily current through its internal siphons. It glided forward three inches across the cobbles, utterly blind, utterly silent, safe in the warm, wet darkness of a world that would now never end.
