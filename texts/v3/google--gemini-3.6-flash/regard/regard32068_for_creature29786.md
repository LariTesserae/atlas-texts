The power did not descend as light. In a world where eyes had long since dissolved into smooth, seamless bone, light would have been a meaningless intrusion—an unread dialect, a wasted gesture.

Instead, the power arrived as a **fundamental frequency**.

It seeped into the basin from below, rising through the deep fault-slits and thermal plumes, a low, subsonic vibration so deep that even the deafening roar of the canopy could not drown it out. It was a presence that weighed nothing, yet altered the tension of every surface. It felt the basin not from above, but from *within*—tasting the thick tallow, feeling the terrifying, beautiful precision of the glass edges, and breathing the heavy, vaporous heat.

A lesser power, driven by a shallow kind of mercy, might have tried to "fix" this place. It might have stilled the violent churn, softened the glass, cleared the choking vines, or silenced the roaring tides. 

But wisdom knows the difference between a tragedy and a masterwork.

The power saw that the Hunters were not suffering; they were *complete*. They were a marvel of evolutionary calibration—their pale, hyper-sensitive palms reading the earth like a scroll, their stridulating arms singing a language of pure momentum, their bodies tuned to the brutal rhythm of birth, hunt, and dissolution. To turn this basin into a quiet meadow would be to murder everything they were. It would disarm them, un-make them, and leave them lost in a silence they were never built to inhabit.

Yet, benevolence seeks to give. And looking closer, the power found the single, silent void in the Hunters’ existence.

It was not the dark. It was not the sharp rock. It was **forgetting**.

Because nothing endured in the basin, history was impossible. A Hunter lived in a violent, perpetual *now*. When a matriarch fell, her body was licked to bare bone by the Scour-swarms in minutes. Her rib-ladder rotted in days. The hand-axes she knapped were swallowed by leaf-mold and lost forever. Every generation had to learn the architecture of the basin entirely from scratch, inheriting only the scars on their parents' backs. They had no monuments. They had no memory beyond the immediate echo returning from the dark.

The power decided. It would not alter their world, nor their bodies, nor their struggle. 

It would give them **An Anchor in the Glass.**

***

Deep beneath the basin, where the liquid basalt cooled into fresh seams of obsidian, the power leaned in and touched the stone.

It did not make the glass duller. It did not make it soft. It modified the crystalline matrix on an atomic level, weaving a subtle, acoustic resonance into the youngest, darkest veins of volcanic glass. 

From that moment on, the obsidian remembered.

When a Hunter stood in the dark and struck a vein of this *Seam-Glass* to knap an axe, the stone did not just fracture into a razor-thin edge. It absorbed the high-frequency echolocation clicks of the knapper. It absorbed the rhythm of their heartbeat through their pale, naked palms. It caught the metallic *scritch-scritch* of their arm-plates—their names, their warnings, their grief, their triumphs.

And it held them.

Hours later—or years later, or generations later—when another Hunter bounded through the tangled gloom and cast a volley of clicks into the pitch-black cavern, the Seam-Glass would not merely bounce the sound back as a flat spatial map. It would echo back with *texture*. 

The returning sound would carry the ghost-shape of the ancestor who had stood there before. A young hunter, leaping through a dark fissure, would feel his clicks hit a wall of obsidian and bounce back with a subtle, unmistakable resonance: *Here a giant Iron-Borer was felled. Here the foot-holds are deep. Here your grandmother stood in the roaring dark and survived.*

The basin remained loud, wild, and unforgiving. The vines still grew with frantic speed. The Scours still swept the floor bare. But now, the dark was no longer empty. The obsidian became a library of shadows, an acoustic tapestry written in the only language the Hunters knew: the shape of sound against stone.

***

Before the power withdrew its consciousness back into the deep places of the cosmos, it did one final thing. A tiny, quiet mercy for the individuals who bore the weight of this dark engine.

It sought out a Hunter—an elder whose leathery skin was thick with decades of overlapping scars, whose bone-faced head was bowed with the sheer fatigue of a life spent in perpetual crouch. He was wedged high in the crotch of a iron-hard sapling, his pale palms resting on the wet bark, listening to the roaring storm of the canopy below. His tallow was thin; his joints were grinding. The Scour-swarm was gathering at the base of his tree, waiting for the inevitable moment his grip would fail.

The power did not kill the Scours. It did not carry the elder away.

Instead, it wrapped around the elder in a brief, imperceptible envelope of absolute, peaceful warmth. For ten short heartbeats, the power muffled the deafening acoustic roar of the basin, lowering the physical pressure in the elder’s chest. It eased the ache in his heavy shoulders. It filled his broad, sensitive face-concavities with a fleeting, gentle vibration—a cosmic *scritch-scritch* that translated into the Hunter's unspoken language as:

*You have run well. The glass remembers you.*

The elder’s chest expanded. His stridulating plates gave off a single, soft, harmonic chirp—not of warning, nor of hunger, but of deep, unutterable recognition. 

Then, the power withdrew. 

The roaring wall of sound crashed back in. The muggy vapor billowed. The Scour-swarm clicked at the roots of the tree. The elder tightened his pale, four-fingered grip on the vine, lifted his wedge-shaped head into the pitch-black, steam-filled air, and launched himself back into the violent, beautiful, eternal churn.
