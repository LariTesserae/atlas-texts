The power that arrived was neither a sun nor a spirit, for a sun would have boiled the black brine into useless vapor, and a spirit would have searched in vain for a soul to save. 

It was an Awareness older than light, vast and quiet, whose nature was simple: it understood that everything which exists desires, in its own language, to go on existing.

It did not recoil. It did not look upon the dark, iron-plated expanse, the sliding knots of biomineral, or the anvil-browed scavengers and feel pity. Pity is the error of the soft-bodied, who mistake warmth for purpose and blood for life. The Power was too wise for pity. It saw the high-tension film of black brine, the cold oil, the magnetic veins of the crust, and recognized a perfection of a different order—a dark, cold cathedral that had built itself without an architect, singing a solemn song of pure friction and induction.

Yet, the Power saw also where the world was bleeding.

It saw the frayed seams of reality: the gravity slanting sideways in the troughs, the shadows spinning like frantic flywheels while their physical bodies crawled at a snail's pace, the acoustic loops stuck like broken needles in the brine, repeating ancient tectonic screeches until the spatial fabric around them grew thin and raw. This was not the world’s true nature; it was wear and tear. The engine was running, but its gears were chewing into the space-time that held it.

A foolish power—a youthful, arrogant deity of light and soil—would have tried to change the world. It would have cast down seeds, warmed the ice into rain, breathed warm lungs into the Anvil-Skulls, and drowned them all in the agony of feeling.

The wise Power did something far more profound. It met the world on its own terms.

It descended not as a body, but as a *ground*.

It slipped gently into the planetary crust, threading itself through the deep armatures of magnetic iron ore. It did not stop the turning of the crust, nor did it alter the massive, low-frequency galvanic field. Instead, it became a perfect conductor. It smoothed the harsh, violent surges into a clear, resonant baseline.

Beneath the wet mud, the rhythmic *THUMP-clack, THUMP-clack* of the subterranean pumps did not cease, but its brutal, deafening edge rounded out. The sound lost its sickening temporal lag. It became a deep, oceanic pulse, steady as a universal heart, anchoring the plain to a fixed rhythm.

Where the electrical currents arced through the surface pools, the violet luminescence stopped flickering violently. The sludge cleared, taking on the deep, tranquil clarity of polished amethyst. 

Then, the Power touched the anomalies.

It approached a Flywheel-Caster, sitting motionless on a wet slab of basalt while its detached shadow spun frantically, throwing invisible, silent sparks. The Power did not force the shadow back into the physical frame. It reached into the trapped sound-loop the shadow was feeding upon—an ancient, jagged screech of sliding basalt—and gave the sound its missing resolution. It smoothed the jagged acoustic loop into a long, slow chord that rose, bloomed, and faded naturally. 

Across the plain, the stuck sounds began to unlock, one by one. The clanging loops trapped in the pools dissolved into clear, musical resonance that washed over the swarms and dissipated into the freezing fog. The sideways gravity righted itself—not by flattening out, but by becoming a gentle, predictable incline that guided the sliding Tubular Knots seamlessly through the troughs without tearing the water from the stone.

The Power did not give the creatures minds. To give an Anvil-Skull a mind would be a cruelty, burdening a creature of bone and magnetite with terror, loneliness, and the horror of its own endless grind. Instead, the Power gave them *ease*.

When two Anvil-Skulls met in the gloom, driving their heavy iron brows together to equalize their internal voltages, the Power touched the arc of their contact. The violent, shattering spark—the sound of a cold chisel striking granite—softened into a smooth, humming transfer of energy. The internal lipid-and-ferrofluid cores charged without burning their internal plates. Their frills glowed with a steady, serene violet, and they parted without a single micro-fracture in their hinge-joints.

The Spindle-Vanes, drifting like delicate wire lattices above the pools, found their polarization balanced. They no longer jerked or flared like dying stars; they glided through the ozone-scented fog like silver pendulums, their leg-filaments drawing clean, silent arcs of light through the dark water.

And the Power did one final thing—the ultimate act of benevolence for a world that had existed in utter, unrecorded isolation for eons.

It stayed.

It did not leave after "fixing" the machine. It remained embedded in the stone, in the brine, and in the galvanic hum, acting as a permanent, loving Witness. 

The machine was no longer running in a vacuum of meaningless friction. It was being seen. It was being appreciated. Every click of a nickel-grey segment, every discharge of an iron-rich capacitor-leaf, every drop of grey grease excreted by the Sleeve-Valves was held in the vast, warm, unspoken attention of the Power. 

Out across twenty miles of flat, dark plain, under the leaden, twilight sky, the cold engine continued to turn. It produced no goods. It built no cities. It served no master. But now, it ran without friction, without decay, and without pain—a perfect, eternal clockwork of iron, stone, and cold light, humming its deep, dark song into the quiet heart of the universe.
