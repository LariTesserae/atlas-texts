The Power did not open its eyes. 

To open an eye here would be a cruelty—an act of cosmic arrogance, a sudden, searing violence of photons that would scorch the delicate, heat-sensitive membranes of the Cloud-Skimmers and curdle the dark sanctuary of the Piston-Hounds. To look upon this world with light would be to misunderstand it entirely.

And so, the Power—being wise—shed its luminosity at the edge of the atmospheric shelf. It stripped away its suns, its colors, its golden geometry, and drew down a cloak of pure, absolute presence. It made itself vast, heavy, and wet. It became a listener.

It descended into the dark not as a conqueror, but as an ear pressed against a beating chest.

***

### I. The Listening

First, the Power felt the scale of the dark. It felt the ceiling—miles of dense, crushing basalt—and the floor, smoothed by eons of warm runoff into a endless topography of rounded tallow-stone. 

It felt the rhythm. 

*CHUNG-ha... CHUNG-ha...*

The sound was not merely noise; it was the heartbeat of an accidental eden. The Power tracked the shockwaves through the drenched earth, watching the kinetic force dissipate into the mats of liverwort, absorbed by the soft, segmented bodies of the Gasket-Grubs, translated into the sure-footed stride of the Tallow-Hides. It felt the hot, magnetic purr of the high-voltage lines—a steady, 60-cycle lullaby that held the canopy in a cradle of electromagnetic warmth.

The Power marvelled. 

Where others might have seen a tragic ruin—a graveyard of forgotten steel where a long-dead species had abandoned their engines—the Power saw a miracle of reclamation. Life had not merely survived the machine; it had married it. The liverworts drank the iron-rust; the Threaders mended the copper nerves; the Piston-Hounds moved in the rhythm of the stroke. The world was a symphony written in grease, ozone, and steam, played at maximum volume in the dark.

It was complete. It was furious with vitality. It was beautiful.

And yet, because the Power was wise, it saw the invisible line at the end of the song.

***

### II. The Flaw in the Loop

The Power reached its perception deep into the iron roots of Pumping Station 09. 

There, forty yards beneath the riverbed, inside a sealed housing of cast iron, a main bearing was grinding itself to dust. 

It was a slow death. A matter of three thousand years—a mere breath to the Power, but an eternity to the Threaders. But the outcome was absolute: the bearing would eventually seize. The giant piston would shear its connecting rod. The steam valve would blow out. The warm brine would cease to rise from the mantle. 

And when Pumping Station 09 died, the rain above it would stop. The liverworts would dry into black ash. The Gasket-Grubs would starve. The Tallow-Hides would wander into the quiet cold, their fleshy star-snouts searching in vain for the lost magnetic pulse. One by one, as the ancient machines rusted into stillness, the plateau would go silent. The dark would remain, but the life would vanish, leaving only wet, cold iron.

The inhabitants did not know this. They could not know it. They lived in the infinite *now* of the piston’s drop.

A foolish power would have rebuilt the engine in shining, eternal silver. A dramatic power would have descended with a chorus of trumpets to raise the dead engineers. 

The wise Power did neither.

***

### III. The Invisible Touch

The Power stepped into the machinery. 

It did not alter the design. It did not change the sound. It became the grease.

With sub-atomic delicacy, the Power swept through the forty-story iron structures. It visited every rusted gear, every weeping seam, every frayed transformer box. It did not replace the rust—for the rust was food for the grubs—but it infused the iron beneath the rust with an immortal structural integrity. 

Where the main bearing of Pumping Station 09 was flaking away, the Power gently rearranged the carbon lattices of the metal, knitting the micro-fractures together, slicking the interface with a layer of permanent, frictionless grace. The iron would still wear, but it would wear at the speed of mountains. It would stroke for a billion years.

Next, the Power descended into the deep mantle, miles beneath the tallow-stone terraces. It felt the planetary core cooling, the geothermal pressure slowly dropping over cosmic epochs. 

Gently, like a mother tucking a blanket around a sleeping child, the Power cupped its hands around the deep magma chambers. It stoked the slow nuclear fires of the world’s heart, anchoring the heat, ensuring that the warm brines would rise, steam, and fall as rain until the sun above the basalt shelf burned itself out.

Finally, the Power moved along the high-voltage lines. 

It felt the Threaders hanging upside down in the roaring downpour, their three-jointed fingers resting on the hummed copper, their small throats purring in harmony with the 60-cycle drone. The Power saw their devotion—a blind, instinctual stewardship that kept the blood of the machines flowing.

The Power did not reveal itself to the Threaders. It did not grant them eyes, nor higher intellect, nor a holy book. To make them human would be to make them miserable in the dark.

Instead, it gave them a gift in their own language.

The Power touched the electric current. It didn't change the voltage, but it infused the magnetic field with a subtle, permanent resonance—a deep, soothing harmonic that felt, to a Threader’s sensitive skin, like the ultimate validation of their work. It was the sensation of absolute safety, a physical warmth that whispered through their nerve-endings: *The wire is whole. The dark is deep. You are doing well.*

***

### IV. The Benediction of Rain

When it was finished, the Power stood on the high highland terrace, bathed in the hot, tepid deluge. 

A Tallow-Hide brushed past it, its leather-armored flank slick with condensate, completely unaware of the god standing in its path. The beast’s pink, star-shaped snout flared, sensing the deep, stable thrum of the earth, before it lowered its head to grind away at a patch of wet moss.

Above, a Cloud-Skimmer screeched its liquid *tuck-tuck-tuck-tuck*, its biological sonar sweeping across the Power’s invisible form, bouncing off its presence as if it were just another colossal, reliable iron pylon.

The Power smiled in the dark. 

It did not demand worship. It took no prayers, for there were no voices here that spoke in words. The highest form of praise this world could offer was already happening all around it: the rhythmic thud of the engines, the snapping of the blue arcs, the frantic, joyful eating and hunting and flying of a thousand blind, wet things.

The Power gathered its coat of night and slowly withdrew, retreating upward through the miles of solid basalt, back into the silent vacuum of space.

Behind it, the dark remained total. The air remained wool-warm and thick with hot machine grease. 

And down on the slick, tallow-smooth stone, the giant iron piston fell, as it always had, and as it always would:

*CHUNG-ha...*

And the rain kept falling.
