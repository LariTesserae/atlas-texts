The power came from far away, across dimensions of air and thought that had never touched the deep water of the peak. 

It was vast, but it did not arrive with the roar of a storm or the sudden, blinding flash of celestial fire. To arrive in such a way would be an insolence. Wisdom knows that light can be a cruelty to those who have cultivated their peace in the dark; benevolence understands that a world made of slow, silent friction must not be disturbed by loud love.

So, the power made itself small. It made itself weightless. It descended through the cold, sunlit layers of the upper ocean, down through the barren abyssal shelf, until it reached the slate ceiling of the submerged sanctuary. 

It did not break the slate. It slipped between the tight, hand-laid seams, dissolving its divinity into the warm, mineral-thick fluid until it was no larger than a single rising gas bubble, no hotter than the sleeping warmth of the terra-cotta floor.

For a long time—a century, perhaps, though time inside the vault moved at the speed of a Slab-Back’s step—the power did nothing at all. 

It simply *listened*.

It felt the rhythmic *clack-clack-clack* of chitin scraping ancient clay. It felt the soft, wet slide of the milk-white Ribbons as they rubbed their flanks against the featureless black ovals of the stone faces. It tasted the bitter, copper-blue sweetness that sustained the Patina-Worms, and it rested inside the heavy, pulsing valves of the Cistern-Mouths beneath the floor, feeling the steady, untiring pump of life that kept the chamber alive.

The power saw the truth of the place: **It was complete.** 

There was no sin here to forgive, no sorrow to heal, no ignorance to enlighten. The people who had swung the iron mallets and pounded the copper pins were gone, lost to the fog of forgotten millennia, but their labor had not been wasted. They had built a vessel, and the earth had filled it with a dark, perfect hymn. The creatures inside did not pray for eyes, for they had no concept of vision. They did not long for the sky, for the water was their sky and their soil both.

A lesser power—a foolish, meddling deity—might have raised the peak above the waves. It might have struck the roof to let the golden sun pour in, turning the purple veil to brown sludge and burning the soft, chamois skin of the Ribbons. A prideful god might have demanded worship, speaking in a thunderous voice that would shatter the fragile, feathery gills of the Patina-Worms.

This power was wiser than that. It knew that to love a thing is often to leave it untouched.

Yet, benevolence cannot witness such quiet perfection without offering a blessing. And so, the power reasoned with itself: *What does a world of stone, heat, and skin need from eternity?*

It did not give them light. 
It did not give them sight. 

Instead, the power moved deep, passing through the interlocking red tiles, down into the dark, crushed arteries of the subterranean rock where the thermal fires blazed.

There, at the root of the mountain, the power performed three small, eternal acts.

**First, it anchored the fire.** 
Mountains age; the hearths beneath them eventually cool and go stone-dead. The power reached into the deep mantle of the world and tied the sanctuary’s thermal vent to the eternal heart of the planet. As long as the earth turned, the water beneath the terra-cotta floor would remain at precisely the warmth of a sleeping body. The draft would never fail. The Cistern-Mouths would never freeze.

**Second, it folded the outer stone.** 
Outside the slate pillars, where the sanctuary dropped off into the cold, crushing abyss, the power wove a quiet ward into the basalt. It shifted the deep oceanic currents so that cold water, drifting silt, and tectonic tremors would forever bypass the peak. If surface men ever came again with iron drag-lines and diving bell-rigs, searching for sunken gold or forgotten temples, their cables would slip past the mountain as if it were made of glass and smoke. The world above would never find it. The sanctuary would remain a secret, held safely in the dark palm of the sea.

**Third, and finally, it left a voice.**
Not a voice of words, but a vibration. The power embedded a tiny, perpetual resonance into the central keystones—a frequency so deep and low that no human ear could ever hear it, but one that the blind creatures of the vault could feel through every inch of their flesh.

It was a gentle, rhythmic pulse that matched the breathing of the Cistern-Mouths. To the Slab-Backs, it felt like the steady, encouraging firmness of the rock beneath their spades. To the Patina-Worms, it was a subtle, comforting eddy that kept their feathery plumes open and unfurled. To the Ribbons, as they coiled around the featureless black faces in the gloom, it was a tactile song—a quiet, continuous reassurance that echoed through the dark:

*You are held. You are safe. The stone will not fall.*

When its work was done, the power did not ascend in glory. It simply dissolved its consciousness further, untying its vast identity until it was nothing more than the water itself. 

It became part of the upward draft. It slid over the polished basalt lintels; it washed through the blue guts of the worms; it was drawn into the fleshy mantles of the Cistern-Mouths and pushed back out through the warm floor tiles.

The power did not leave the sanctuary. It chose to stay forever, blind and nameless in the dark, taking its pleasure in the slow, eternal *clack-clack-clack* of a world that needed nothing at all.
