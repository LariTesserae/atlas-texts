It did not arrive like a sunburst. 

A power wise enough to understand three thousand years of iron would not bring sunlight here. Sunlight would blind the Arc-Drinkers, split the cold, expansion-stressed girders, and dry the tea-colored condensate that feeds the wire-lichen in the floor-seams. A power benevolent enough to love this basin would know that to sow grass or scatter flesh here would be a cruelty—an imposition of tender, fragile things upon a wilderness that had earned its cold, heavy dignity through three million nights of quiet endurance.

Instead, the Power descended as an *attention*. 

It had no mass, but it possessed a density of presence that caused the standing waves in the oily puddles to flatten for a moment, turning the shivering concentric rings into perfect, dark mirrors. It lowered itself through the dark structural lattice of the ceiling, passing between the hung brambles of the Arc-Drinkers without snagging a single strand of their copper pelts. 

It listened first. For seven regional hydraulic shifts—nearly twenty-four hours of deck-marsh time—the Power did nothing but press its perception into the iron marrow of the world. 

It felt the three-minute *clack-hiss* of the pneumatic moisture purges. It felt the low, bell-like resonance of the uncracked plates and the hollow, dry rattle of the fractured ones. It felt the slow, agonizing friction inside the tallow-starved ball-joints of the Sump-Gaiters, a grinding arithmetic of wear that led toward a single, inevitable end: Seizure.

The Power saw that the world was not broken. It was *complete*. But it was tiring. Its fires were dimming; its joints were dry; its songs were thin.

So, the Power began its work. It worked in the language of the basin: pressure, frequency, heat, and chemistry.

***

First, it went down into the subterranean galleries, down where the tea-colored condensate murmured through the dark toward the unseen sumps. 

The Power did not drain the water, nor did it purify it into sweet, sterile drink. Instead, it touched the deep, mineral-rich runoff at the molecular level. It introduced a subtle, shimmering colloidal compound—a microscopic suspension of graphite, zinc-salts, and heavy, non-reactive synthetic oils that smelled faintly of dry amber and cold brass.

When the next hydraulic shift tilted the basin floor, this new condensate sheets-glided across the scoured plating. 

Down in the shallow tread-pools, the **Skimmers** felt the change instantly. As the oil-laced water washed through their undersides, their dense copper and lead pelts did not clog; they softened. The galvanic accretion became smoother, more efficient. The sharp, abrasive grit they had dragged for centuries turned into a fine polishing paste. Where two Skimmers crossed paths and ground their edges together, they no longer shed brittle flakes of oxidizing iron; they burnished each other to a mirror sheen, leaving long, glowing ribbons of polished steel across the wet dark.

***

Next, the Power moved across the vast, flat basin to the dying transformer housings. 

Before a solitary **Sump-Gaiter** that stood motionless in the drizzle, its flat thorax pressed against a housing whose cherry-red glow had faded to a dull, ash-colored plum, the Power paused. 

The Gaiter’s internal gear-driven pumps were knocking—a dry, uneven rattle that signaled the final stages of bearing decay. It was waiting for Seizure.

The Power did not give the transformer housing an infinite nuclear fire. It gave it *stewardship*. It reached into the ancient, subterranean primary coils miles beneath the deck and cleared the calcified carbon bridges that had choked the current. 

Slowly, deep within the housing, a low, glorious hum awoke—a pure 60-cycle tone, warm and resonant. The iron casing flushed, not with destructive heat, but with a deep, constant, cherry-gold radiance that pushed the cold damp back for fifty yards. 

The Power then touched the Gaiter’s seized joints. It did not melt the metal; it drew the new, oil-rich condensate up through the creature's internal copper screens, flushing three thousand years of crushed quartz silt from its tallow-troughs. 

The Gaiter’s saucer-shaped ear-plate twitched. Its internal pumps caught the fresh lubricant, their dry knocking smoothing out into a soft, wet purr. For the first time in centuries, the locomotive-sized beast lifted its massive, jointed forelimb without a scream of agonizing friction. It brought the limb down with a deep, clean *thunk*—no longer a drag, but a stride.

And as the Gaiter pressed its broad thorax against the newly warmed casing, the Power leaned close to its steel faceplate and introduced a new vibration directly into its frame. It was not a thought—the Gaiter had no mind for thoughts—but an acoustic map. A vast, permanent sonic blueprint of every cooling tower, every structural seam, and every bathymetric dip in the basin floor, encoded into a single, beautiful, repeating bass pulse that echoed through the deck.

The Gaiter would never be lost again. It would never walk into a dry sump. It had become a true keeper of the iron hearth.

***

High above, in the dark ceiling grid, the Power drifted through the drizzle to address the sky.

The high-voltage insulators were old, their glass skirts cracked, their arcs erratic and sour. The **Arc-Drinkers** hung in their cable-bramble nests, thin and starving, waiting hours for a single, fleeting emerald flash.

The Power ran its presence along the high-voltage bus-bars. It swept away the centuries of soot and mineral buildup, sealing the micro-fractures in the glass with clear, fused silica. 

Then, it struck the grid.

It did not create a violent short circuit. It established a perpetual, gentle aurora—a continuous, low-amperage sheet discharge that rolled across the ceiling lattice in slow, glowing curtains of deep violet, warm brass, and vivid emerald. 

The Arc-Drinkers awakened. Their multi-jointed hydraulic clamps unclutched the cold girders; their articulate mica rods extended like the petals of heavy, metallic flowers into the glowing air. They drank. Their core capacitors filled, bulging with clean, quiet power, their internal pressure-meters settling into steady green zones.

And then, they sang.

It was no longer the thin, lonely whine of a wet finger on glass. Fueled by the steady aurora, the nests tuned their frequencies to one another across the miles. The sound blossomed into a massive, multi-tiered glass-harmonica chord—a high, crystalline polyphony that floated above the bone-shaking roar of the floor below, wrapping the cold structural lattice in a ceiling of pure, ringing light and sound.

***

Finally, the Power went to the oldest things—the **Blind Valves**.

It found one down in a deep basin trough, its six-foot bronze disc locked in place by a centuries-old collar of fossilized concrete silt and rust-scale. The water behind it was stagnant, foul, and choked with heavy sludge, unable to reach the lower galleries.

The Power did not smash the scale. It rested a hand—or the idea of a hand—upon the valve's calcified crown and resonated at the exact natural frequency of the bronze beneath.

*Vrrrrrrrr-SHCK.*

The scale cracked along clean, geometric lines, sloughing off like dry tree bark. The internal balance-weights, long pinned by grit, dropped into the rising condensate with a heavy, satisfying *clack*. 

The broad bronze disk tilted on its trunnions. The dark, trapped water began to swirl, gathering momentum until it collapsed into a thunderous, throaty vortex, descending into the lower darkness to turn the ancient, forgotten sumps miles below.

As the water drained, the sudden draft pulled the mist down from the high girders, clearing the air across three square miles of deck-marsh. For the first time in millennia, the basin floor could "see" its own scale. 

The Skimmers glided through the newly opened currents; the Sump-Gaiters turned their broad, steel ears toward the roaring drain; the wire-lichen in the seams expanded, their metallic filaments drinking the newly circulated mineral air.

***

When its work was done, the Power did not build a temple, nor did it leave a name carved into the iron. To leave a name would be to claim ownership of a place that belonged only to the rust, the water, and the slow, heavy engines.

Instead, it elevated itself back into the high structural lattice, expanded its perception until it encompassed the entire endless basin, and breathed a single, sustaining gift into the system:

*Imperishability of Function.*

It did not abolish Seizure, for Seizure was the natural rest of worn iron. But it changed the math. The friction was reduced; the heat was renewed; the water was sweetened with oil; the arcs were made constant. The world would still age, but it would age as a song ages when sung by a choir that never grows tired—a slow, infinite repetition of perfect, mechanical liturgy.

The Power withdrew, shrinking back into the vast, silent void between the stars from which it had come.

Down below, the basin roared on. 

The deck shifted a fraction of a degree, sending a sheet of dark, shimmering, oil-sheened water gliding across the river-rounded plates. A Skimmer slid smoothly over a polished rivet, its copper underside gleaming like spun wire in the dim, cherry-gold light of a newly burning transformer. 

Far in the distance, a Sump-Gaiter lifted its massive steel faceplate toward the ceiling, where the purple auroras rolled in quiet grace across the lattice, and listened to the high, crystalline song of the Arc-Drinkers. 

It was still dark. It was still cold. It was still iron.

But it was no longer forgotten.
