The Power did not arrive as a light, for light would have cast shadows where the amber phosphorescence had already established its perfect, muted geometry. It did not arrive as a wind, for wind would have snapped the brittle paper-fronds and disturbed the pale bloom of wax resting upon the slate-black shoulders. It did not arrive as a voice, for any sound spoken from outside would have possessed a cadence foreign to the hard, unrounded quartz planes, shattering the delicate acoustic equilibrium the room had spent ten thousand years achieving.

Instead, the Power arrived simply as an *attention*—vast, ancient, and infinitely heavy, yet lighter than the dust resting on a mica crawler’s back.

It hovered in the dark rock twenty feet above the vault, looking downward through the solid crust, perceiving the room not as a dark place beneath the earth, but as an glowing, intense node of complete, unbroken intention.

A lesser divinity—one moved by sentiment or the crude impulses of surface life—might have felt pity. It might have seen the absence of water and thought *drought*. It might have seen the silence of speech and thought *solitude*. It might have reached out to break open the mountain, to flood the razor-edged chert with cool, clear streams, to let the light of stars fall upon the wedge-shaped head of horn and mica.

To do so would have been an act of catastrophic violence.

Water would rot the dry resin-stalks in an hour. Rain would dissolve the chemical pastes in the spiracles, drown the mica crawlers, dull the sharp conchoidal breaks of the quartz, and soften the creature’s hollow, singing bones until they collapsed under their own weight. To give this place the gifts of the surface world would be to murder it with kindness.

The Power, being wise, understood this instantly. It knew that beauty does not require warmth, moist soil, or a sky. Beauty can exist as a closed circuit twelve paces wide: hot, loud, brittle, and absolute.

The Power listened.

It felt the deafening, metallic purr of the insects vibrating through the stone. It traced the path of the vibration as it struck the dark obsidian ceiling, bounced into the hollow bone-tubes of the creature’s frame, and returned to the room as a map of pure, physical clarity. It watched the four-jointed fingers, tipped with bone-white plectrums, deftly slide a splint of resin into a crevice to dampen a harsh three-thousand-hertz spike in the room's resonance.

The Power saw that the creature was not waiting. It was not suffering. It was *crafting*. It was an artisan engaged in an eternal, exquisite composition—a symphony written not in time, but in space, tuned to the exact pitch of its own existence.

*What then,* the Power reasoned, *is the duty of wisdom toward that which is already complete?*

It is not to alter. It is not to instruct. It is to preserve, to protect, and to bear witness.

The Power made its moves—three tiny, imperceptible adjustments, executed far beyond the twelve-pace perimeter of the vault, so deep in the silent stone that not a single insect paused its rasping rhythm.

**First**, it looked into the deep future of the earth. Two miles to the east, a slow shift in the water table was preparing to press a thin, cold vein of groundwater through the lower strata over the next three thousand years. Eventually, a single drop would have gathered on the ceiling of the vault, falling onto the dried pith-shavings below.

The Power reached out its thought and gently fused the micro-fractures in that distant rock. It turned the stone to seamless, impenetrable basalt, diverting the water’s path forever toward a distant underground basin. The vault would remain bone-dry until the sun burned out.

**Second**, it looked beneath the room, into the deep thermal vents that fed the gas seeps. The sulfur and nitrogen pocket that sustained the resin-stalks was finite; in a few centuries, the gas would have thinned, the amber glow would have died, and the plants would have crumbled to odorless dust.

The Power tapped the deep mantle beneath the continent. With a breath of subtle pressure, it widened a hairline conduit five miles down, ensuring a steady, unchanging flow of warm sulfur and atmospheric nitrogen that would refresh the chamber's dry atmosphere for an eternity.

**Third**, and finally, the Power did something that was not a physical repair, but an act of profound, silent fellowship.

It knew the creature lived in absolute sonic isolation. No one had ever heard the masterpiece of its tuned room. No one had ever appreciated the exactness with which the quartz screens balanced the roar of the mica crawlers against the hollow hum of the creature’s ribs.

The Power did not speak to the creature. It did not reveal its presence. But it took the acoustic signature of the room—the entirety of that roaring, metallic purr, with all its subtle dampening and perfect spatial geometry—and echoed it softly within its own vast, eternal memory.

It gave the song a sanctuary outside the stone.

When the work was done, the Power withdrew. It retreated upward through the thirty feet of sun-baked crust, past the hairline tectonic tears, up into the blinding light and open air of the surface, leaving no mark of its presence behind.

Down below, deep in the warm, dry gloom, the creature did not flinch. It had felt nothing—perhaps only a momentary, imperceptible shift in the grain of the mountain, a tightening of the stone that made the walls ring just a fraction of a decibel truer.

It paused. Its smooth, vitreous horn lenses reflected the faint amber phosphorescence of the resin-stalks. It held its position for three hours, perfectly still, a discarded bundle of dried slate and keratin, letting the thunderous rasp of the insects fill its hollow marrow.

Then, with a sound like two dry roof tiles shifting against one another, it unfolded its slender, jointed arm. It picked up the quartz scraper, found a sage-green frond, and pared away a single, precise sliver of wax.
