The Power did not come as a sun. 

To bring a sun to the horn of slate would be a slaughter. A warm light would scorch the milky, sensitive cilia crowning their eye-stalks; a warm breeze would thin the thick, glycol-rich oil of their hemolymph until their heavy, single-beat hearts burst in their chests; a drying wind would kill the sulfur-lichen and strip the mist-sweat from the basalt, leaving the colony to turn to brittle, iron-flecked husk.

Wisdom knows that pity is often just the vandalism of the comfortable. Benevolence does not rewrite a species’ soul to match a stranger's vision of grace.

So the Power came without light, without fire, and without heat. It arrived as a vast, patient weight—a presence colder than the slate, yet deep as the roots of the mantle. It descended through the black cloud-sea, silent and unhurried, settling over the overhanging brow of stone like a second atmosphere.

It listened first. 

For three hundred years of the kettles' time, the Power simply stood upon the threshold of the shelf, absorbing the rhythm of the niche. It felt the heavy, collective thrum of ten thousand cold hearts beating out of phase. It tracked the slow, agonizing vortex of chitinous bodies grinding against chitinous bodies—the endless, selfless rotation where the freezing outer rim crawled inward toward the two-degree sanctuary of the core, and the warm core yielded to the edge. It heard the Masons: *tink, tink, tink*, three weeks to carve a single crescent groove for a fist-sized amber sphere.

The Power saw that here was life pared down to its most absolute, heroic equation: *Hold the stone. Drink the mist. Keep the song.*

There was no cruelty in this world, because there was no excess to breed cruelty. There was no despair, because there was no memory of a sun. There was only the immense, solemn dignity of endurance.

And so, the Power decided what it would do. 

It would not change them. It would not raise the temperature by a fraction of a degree. It would not smooth the razor-edges of the unweathered granite. 

It would *tune the mountain*.

First, the Power extended its mind deep into the newborn horn of slate, down through the fractured, iron-veined basalt, down six miles to the tectonic fault where the earth was still groaning and thrusting upward. The rock here was young, wild, and prone to violent, internal shearing. A single catastrophic fracture could, in a second, send the entire chapel-like shelf sliding into the black clouds below, obliterating ten thousand years of patient stonemasonry.

Gently, with the slow pressure of a mountain sleeping upon a mountain, the Power annealed the deep fissures. It welded the base of the horn to the spine of the continent. It did not stop the mountain from growing—for the kettles belonged to a high place—but it ensured that the shelf would never shear. The granite would hold. The cradle was made safe.

Second, the Power touched the water.

The droplets that fell from the high ceiling-spikes were heavy, random, and cold. The Power did not alter their temperature, but it adjusted the subterranean seeps three hundred feet above. It guided the water through a dense layer of iron-salts and trace minerals before it reached the ceiling spikes. 

Now, when a droplet detached and fell into the dark—*tink*—it carried a tiny, vital gift of mineral nutrition for the sulfur-lichen creeping in the crevices. And the sound changed. The *tink* became pure, carrying a crystalline, resonant pitch that bounced off the slate with mathematical perfection.

To a human, it was just the sound of water on rock. But to the kettles, whose blind world was built entirely of acoustic shockwaves and stone-vibration, the sound was a revelation. 

The new resonance of the dripping water acted as a natural sonar beacon. The violent echolocation of their mandibles—*krk-krk-krk*—no longer died in flat dampness; it sang back to them through the basalt in crisp, perfect detail. A Mason kettle, raising its heavy tail-knuckle to strike its chitin chisel, could now "see" the micro-fractures inside the rock with twice the clarity. They could carve their egg-crescents deeper, faster, with half the wasted effort.

Third, the Power reached out to the egg-capsules.

It did not crack the amber seals. It did not hasten the slow, multi-year drift of the pale embryos floating in their cold oil. Instead, as it brushed its presence over the smooth, hard spheres cemented to the rock, it left a tiny, permanent harmonic within the stone beneath them. 

The unborn kettles, suspended in darkness, felt the continuous, rhythmic vibration of their parents' stone-cutting coming through the basalt. But now, woven into that vibration, was a subtle, comforting frequency—a quiet, low note that hummed through the mountain itself. It was the acoustic signature of stability. It told the embryos, in the only language their nerves would ever understand: *The edge is firm. The cold is holding. The mass is here.*

Finally, the Power withdrew its weight.

It did not leave a shrine. It left no golden light, no fragrant flowers, no voice speaking from the clouds. To leave a mark would be to demand worship, and the kettles had no room in their compressed, mineral minds for gods. They needed all their focus for the stone.

As the Power drifted away, rising above the dark brow of the mountain into the thin, starlit silence of the upper atmosphere, it looked back one last time.

The shelf hung in the bruised violet dusk, silent to the soft, warm world below, but deafeningly alive in its own dark realm. 

Below the overhanging stone, the mass squirmed in its slow, eternal thermal vortex. Slick black carapaces scraped like iron files. High on the needles, a blind Mason clamped a bound shard of chitin in its pincers, felt the pure, singing resonance of a falling water droplet bounce through the basalt, raised its tail-knuckle, and struck.

*Tink.*

A tiny crescent of slate flaked away. The egg held fast against the abyss. The mountain answered the blow with a low, solid ring that promised another ten thousand years.

And far above them, unseen and unneeded, the Power smiled—satisfied that in all the cold, sharp, terrifying places of the universe, the dark was being carved with precision, and the dark was holding its own.
