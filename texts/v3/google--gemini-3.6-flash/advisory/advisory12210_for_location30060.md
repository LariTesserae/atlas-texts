Visiting this chamber is not like exploring a cave; it is like stepping inside a live, sterile organ that is actively manufacturing its own reality. You are entering an environment of high-speed genesis, razor-sharp geology, and non-Euclidean biology. 

Because you are an ordinary human made of soft tissue, slow-moving liquid, and organic complexity, this environment presents several immediate physical and existential hazards. 

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Physical Access: The Squeeze
The entrance is a crack "no wider than an elbow" three yards beneath a dry salt-plateau. 

*   **Body Profile:** You must be physically capable of a tight, flat crawling compression. If you are broad-shouldered or carry excess bulk, you will get stuck. Practice suffocating crawls in claustrophobic conditions before attempting entry.
*   **Gear Transport:** You cannot wear a backpack, a belt, or even thick jacket pockets through the fissure. All gear must be packed into a long, narrow, slick-shelled dry bag tied to a line around your ankle and dragged behind you as you crawl.
*   **Outer Wear for the Crawl:** Wear a cheap, sacrificial outer suit (like a heavy canvas or smooth nylon mechanic’s jumpsuit) for the squeeze through the salt crust. Once inside the chamber, you will peel this off—it will be covered in salt dust, which you *do not* want to introduce into the pristine room.

---

### 2. Clothing: Cut Resistance and Thermal Layers
Inside, the temperature drops to a flat, wet 45°F (7°C), illuminated by a blinding, cold glare. The stone is un-weathered marl and gypsum, meeting at angles sharp enough to cut leather.

*   **Cut Protection (Vital):** Do not wear standard outdoor gear made of thin ripstop nylon; the rock edges will slice it open like a razor. You need a **full-body base or mid-layer made of Level 5 Kevlar or Dyneema cut-resistant weave** (the kind used by glass-handlers or abattoir workers). Cover your knees, elbows, and forearms with reinforced tactical plating. 
*   **Footwear:** Wear hard-soled, narrow-profile boots with steel or composite shanks and cut-proof leather/synthetic uppers. The soles must be rigid. *Crucial warning:* **Do not stand still.** The floor is actively growing via biological grease-to-stone conversion. If you stand in one place for twenty minutes, the rock may begin to coat and absorb your boot soles, just as it swallowed the stool leg.
*   **Thermal Protection:** Beneath your cut-resistant layer, wear a moisture-wicking synthetic thermal base layer. You will be sitting at 45°F surrounded by suspended micro-dew; if your skin gets wet, hypothermia will set in quietly.

---

### 3. Vision: Polarized and Tinted Protection
The light dropping through the selenite crystal lens is "sharp, high-key, and surgical," bouncing off thousands of microscopic water beads and pale, fresh gypsum facets.

*   **Glacier Glasses:** Bring dark, side-shielded glacier glasses or polarized tactical goggles (Category 3 or 4 tint). Without them, the glare off the virgin crystal facets will cause rapid snow-blindness and severe migraines. 
*   **Eye Seals:** Your eye protection must seal snugly around your face to prevent floating dew drops or microscopic, flying air-fry from drifting into your eyes.

---

### 4. Respiratory & Biological Shielding
The air tastes of cold salt and iron. Tiny fish swim through the atmosphere, grubs dissolve into grease, and stiff, spider-silk fungal threads stretch across the rock.

*   **Respirator:** Wear a tight-fitting N95 or half-mask respirator with particulate filters. You do not want to inhale the floating dew (which may contain airborne micro-organisms), nor do you want to breathe in airborne fungal spores or the clear grease of dissolving grubs.
*   **Hydration & Food:** Do not eat or drink anything inside the chamber. Do not lick your lips. The ambient moisture is participating in hyper-rapid life cycles. Bring your own water in a sealed bladder with a covered bite-valve.

---

### 5. Mindset and Behavioral Protocols (The Rules of the Room)

To survive a space where logic is "unspooled," you must strictly adhere to the room's physical laws:

*   **Beware Your Shadow:** The grubs in this room "mate with their own shadows" to reproduce, and light duplicates creatures. It is currently unknown how human biology or human shadows interact with the selenite glare. **Try not to linger directly under the beam of light cast by the crystal lens.** Keep your shadow short, flat, and away from the walls.
*   **Do Not Touch the Wooden Stool:** The stool is carved from an alien, tarry wood and is being digested by the growing floor. It is an artifact of a previous traveler or inhabitant. Do not sit on it, do not touch it, and do not attempt to pry it free.
*   **Do Not Touch the Notches:** The eight notches carved into the wall were cut recently with a copper tool. They are likely a tally of time, visits, or environmental shifts. Leave them untouched.
*   **Move in Straight Lines:** The local fauna (the translucent planarians) only turn at 90-degree angles. While you cannot physically move like them, try to keep your movements predictable, slow, and linear. Rapid, fluid, organic movements might disturb the delicate balance of the air-swimming fry or trigger defensive reactions in organisms you cannot see.
*   **Watch the Dew:** Remember that air currents move horizontally here. If you feel a sudden draft on your cheek, step back—a cluster of drifting dew or a school of air-fry is about to snap against the wall.

---

### 6. The Ultimate Hazard: Time and Exit
This room is **building itself from the inside out.** The fauna live, die, turn into grease, and harden into fresh layers of stone in a matter of minutes.

*   **The Exit is Shrinking:** Because the room is continually adding stone to its own floor and walls, **the elbow-wide entrance crack is actively closing.** 
*   **Set a Hard Timer:** Do not stay longer than **45 minutes**. Bring a loud, mechanical kitchen timer (digital screens may act strangely under the selenite glare). When the timer ticks down, crawl out immediately. If you stay for several hours, you may find the crack has narrowed by half an inch, sealing you inside the egg forever.
