To visit this world is to enter a frozen, sun-blinded acoustic furnace. It is a realm stripped of time, night, and softness, operating on a harsh, static balance that will systematically break down an unprepared human body within forty-eight hours. 

You are not preparing for weather—the weather here never changes. You are preparing for **sensory siege, altitude sickness, mineral poisoning, and acoustic trauma.**

Here is how an ordinary human multiverse traveler must prepare to survive the Pass of the Unmoving Sun.

---

### 1. Acoustic Defense & Sound Decoupling
The sound in this realm is not mere noise; it is a physical force. It attacks you from two directions: a sub-bass vibration surging through the granite beneath your feet, and a piercing, high-frequency metallic scream filling the air. 

*   **Dual-Layer Ear Protection:** Standard foam earplugs will fail within minutes. You need custom-molded, high-attenuation silicone plugs (NRR 33+) inserted deep into the canal, overlaid with industrial-grade, low-profile electronic noise-canceling earmuffs. The electronics should be tuned to phase-out high-pitched biological stridulation (the slate-beetles).
*   **Vibration-Damping Footwear:** Because the sub-bass hum travels through the marrow of your legs, walking barefoot or in hard-soled boots will cause deep joint pain, nausea, and intense fatigue. Wear thick-soled mountaineering boots lined with **viscoelastic polymer insoles** (Sorbothane or equivalent gel-foams) designed to absorb low-frequency mechanical energy.
*   **Acoustic Etiquette (Survival Rule #1):** **Do not pitch a tall tent.** Erecting a pole tent, a flag, or any rigid structure higher than your knee will catch the high-altitude thermal streams and create localized acoustic resonance. You will effectively construct a giant whistle that will vibrate your shelter to pieces and deafen you. Sleep flat. Use bivouac sacks pegged close to the stone, just as the locals do with their grazer hides.

---

### 2. Ocular and Skin Protection (The Shadowless Noon)
The sun here does not warm; it burns. In thin air, under a deep violet sky, ultraviolet radiation is brutal, and the polished granite acts as a giant, pale mirror.

*   **Category 4 Glacier Glasses:** You need total-coverage mountain goggles with dark, polarized, Category 4 lenses (transmitting less than 5% of visible light) with leather side-shields to prevent ambient glare from entering the sides. Without these, you will suffer photo-keratinitis (snow blindness) within an hour. Bring a spare pair; losing your eyewear here is a death sentence.
*   **Total Physical Sunblock:** Chemical sunscreens will degrade quickly under this direct glare. Use thick, zinc-oxide or titanium-dioxide pastes that leave a visible white barrier on every millimeter of exposed skin. 
*   **Lips and Nasal Salve:** The air tastes of iron and grease, drying out mucous membranes instantly. Apply a heavy lanolin- or petrolatum-based ointment inside your nostrils and on your lips to prevent deep, bleeding fissures.
*   **Sweat Management:** The cold air freezes sweat into a sharp, agonizing salt-crust. Wear a snug, moisture-wicking merino-wool or synthetic balaclava under your headgear to pull moisture away from the back of the neck before it can freeze against your skin.

---

### 3. Respiration & Thermal Layering
Though the light looks like a desert at noon, the air is locked in a dry, bitter freeze. Combined with high altitude, hypoxia is a constant threat.

*   **Supplemental Oxygen:** Unless you have weeks to acclimatize, pack a lightweight pulse-dose oxygen system or chemical O2 canisters. The thin air will make every step across the shale feel like dragging lead weights.
*   **Low-Profile Layering:** Heavy, puffy down jackets are dangerous if they catch the wind or high-frequency vibrations. Opt for high-density, low-loft windproof shells (Gore-Tex Infinite or heavy soft-shell fabrics) over tight thermal wool layers. 
*   **Grease-Coating the Outerwear:** Take a cue from the locals. A thin layer of grease or synthetic wax applied to the knees, elbows, and seams of your outer gear will stop the ambient rock-dampness from soaking into your clothes when you crouch in the granite flutes.

---

### 4. Hydration and Nutrition
There is no wood, no fuel, and the thin air makes lighting an open fire nearly impossible. The water looks pure, but it is a trap for the digestive system.

*   **Water Treatment (Heavy Minerals):** The condensation pooling in the rock basins is crystal clear, but it leaves chalky rims for a reason—it is heavily loaded with dissolved carbonates, iron, and silica. Drinking it straight will cause severe stomach cramps and kidney distress. 
    *   Bring a **portable Reverse-Osmosis (RO) hand pump** or a specialized heavy-metal/scale-filtering micro-purifier. 
    *   Carry **electrolyte tablets** to balance the stripped, hyper-filtered water.
*   **Flameless Heating:** Traditional camping stoves will struggle in this oxygen-poor, windless freeze. Pack **flameless chemical ration heaters** (MRE heaters) that activate with a splash of water to warm your food. 
*   **High-Fat, High-Salt Rations:** Your body will burn massive amounts of calories simply staying warm and filtering out the background stress of the sound. Bring dried meats, pemmican, hard fats, and high-density nut butters. (If offered sun-cured meat by the locals, eat it—it is dark, tough, and tastes heavily of salt, but it is sterile and packed with dense energy).

---

### 5. Psychological Survival & Chrono-Management
The complete absence of shadows, night, or change will quickly break your perception of time. The mind panics when the sun refuses to move.

*   **Total-Blackout Sleep System:** To sleep, you must construct a darkness that the world denies you. Pack a contoured, zero-light sleep mask. When you lie down in your low-profile bivy sack, put the mask on, keep your earplugs in, and use a wrist-based chronometer with a vibrating alarm to force yourself into a strict 8-hour sleep cycle.
*   **Circadian Supplements:** Melatonin (3–5mg) is mandatory to force your brain into sleep mode under the oppressive glare of permanent noon.
*   **Mental Anchor:** The sensory isolation caused by wearing heavy ear protection and dark goggles while standing in a roaring, frozen landscape can induce severe dissociation. Carry a tactile "grounding object"—a smooth stone from home, a heavy coin, or a textured grip—to hold when the sensory deprivation/overload balance tilts.

---

### 6. Interaction with the Local Environment & Inhabitants
The locals are survivors of a world stripped to its bones. They are quiet, efficient, and bound to the rules of the stone.

*   **Approach Pattern:** Do not march straight across the open shale amphitheatres; move along the natural granite flutes. Keep your body profile low. 
*   **Barter Items:** The locals have obsidian, jasper, bone, and hides, but they lack **metals, salt, and dense fats**. A small bar of pure sea salt, a titanium needle, or a tube of heavy pure lanolin will buy you safe passage, water rights to a basin, or a space under a granite shelter.
*   **Respect the Lichen:** Do not unnecessarily trample the black lichen in the shale fields. It is the sole food source for the long-necked grazers that keep the human population alive.
