### FIELD PREPARATION GUIDE
**Target Destination:** The Sunken Steam-Shed / The Root-Engine Basin  
**Classification:** Hybrid Industrial-Botanical Micro-Climate (Acreage: ~0.5 acres)  
**Threat Level:** Low-Biological / Moderate-Mechanical / High-Structural  

---

### Understanding the Destination

This location is deceptive. It is small, contained, and structurally self-sustaining. There are no megafauna, no hostile locals, and no active weapon systems. However, the basin is a **closed-system hydraulic vise**. 

What you are entering is a slow-motion explosion. Plants growing with thousands of pounds of internal sap-pressure have spent decades bending four-inch steel plates, wrapping high-tension lianas around heavy flywheels, and sealing themselves into pressurized brass fittings. The environment is warm, perpetually dripping, intensely loud, and saturated with organic and industrial compounds.

You are not visiting a ruin; you are visiting an engine where the pistons are made of wood and the pipes are made of copper and root-matter.

---

### 1. Apparel & Thermal Management

The basin operates like a sealed tropical conservatory, but the rain falling from the iron trusses overhead is cold, mineral-heavy condensation. If you wear traditional heavy waterproofs, you will bake from the inside out; if you wear standard trekking clothes, you will be soaked to the bone in five minutes and bruised by sharp iron within twenty.

*   **Base Layer:** **Synthetic or merino-blend tropical weight baselayer.** It must be fast-wicking and anti-microbial. The air smells of "bruised starch and mint," indicating high aerosolized organic compounds that will cling to fabric and rot cotton quickly.
*   **Outer Shell:** **Lightweight, abrasion-resistant tropical rain gear with generous venting (pit zips).** Avoid heavy GORE-TEX, as high humidity stops breathability. A tough, wax-treated canvas jacket with reinforced forearm patches is ideal—it will resist the razor-sharp torn steel of the ruptured boiler while shedding the heavy, perpetual overhead drips.
*   **Headwear:** A **wide-brimmed, hard-shell safety helmet with an integrated gutter or brim.** The rain overhead is not coming from the sky; it is condensing on rust-encrusted iron beams. A single falling piece of saturated moss, or a rusted rivet dislodged by a shifting vine, falling from 30 feet up could fracture a skull. The helmet keeps wet moss and iron flakes off your neck.
*   **Gloves:** **Kevlar-lined, nitrile-coated work gloves.** You will be grabbing wet, moss-covered surfaces. Beneath that moss are raw, torn steel edges, jagged copper verdigris, and sharp, rubbery plant spines. Do not touch *anything* with bare hands.

---

### 2. Footwear & Ground Navigation

The floor is your single greatest hazard. You are walking on a **five-to-six-foot-deep suspended mattress** of wet peat, liverwort, and fibrous root-cables. Beneath this mattress lie rusted, flaking iron floor-plates suspended over underground water conduits.

*   **Boots:** **High-cut, waterproof leather or rubber-composite boots with a steel or Kevlar shank and puncture-resistant soles.** Standard hiking boots will soften and fail in the tannin-rich, acid-amber water. The steel shank is vital to prevent rusted iron spikes or torn floor-plate shards from piercing the bottom of your foot when the peat compresses.
*   **Gaiters:** **Knee-high rubberized gaiters.** Seal them tightly to your boots. The peat is "soaked through like a sponge." Every step will sink several inches, squeezing cold, tannin-heavy liquid up your legs.
*   **Movement Tool:** **A heavy, solid-wood or fiberglass wading staff (do not use collapsible trekking poles).** You need a rigid probe to test the ground before every step. If your staff punches through the peat mattress and clangs against hollow, echoing iron, *do not step there*. The iron floor-plates beneath are rusted into soft orange scales and may collapse under a human’s concentrated weight, dropping you into a subterranean root-lined chimney of rushing water.

---

### 3. Respiratory & Sensory Mitigation

The atmosphere is a stew of ambient industrial chemistry and aggressive plant respiration.

*   **Respirator:** Bring a **half-mask respirator equipped with combination Organic Vapor/P100 particulate filters.** 
    *   *Why:* The description notes the smell of **transformer fluid**. Older industrial transformer fluids frequently contain PCBs (polychlorinated biphenyls) or mineral oils. In a warm, enclosed, highly humid basin, these chemicals off-gas continuously. Breached copper bus-bars and wet iron also create an aerosolized metallic dust. You can remove the mask for short periods to take in the scent of mint and starch, but prolonged exposure without a filter risks heavy metal toxicity or PCB inhalation.
*   **Hearing Protection:** **Filtered acoustic earplugs (e.g., musician’s plugs or industrial pass-through plugs).** 
    *   The ambient noise is a constant, loud, low-frequency thrum: water roaring underground, pressure valves *clack-hissing*, and iron beams groaning. 
    *   Crucially, the 8-foot cast-iron pulley turns every few hours with a foghorn-like screech that echoes off the brick walls. In an enclosed brick basin, a sudden metal-on-bronze screech can reach dangerous decibel levels and cause disorientation. Filtered plugs will dampen the harsh, damaging spikes while allowing you to hear the subtle *creak* of shifting wood that warns of structural movement.

### 4. Essential Equipment & Gear

*   **Illumination:** Even though light falls from the open roof, the canopy of reed-ferns and climbing figs is dense, and the real mechanisms are buried beneath the floor. A **waterproof, high-lumen headlamp (IP68 rated)** is essential for looking into split boiler seams or beneath root-mats.
*   **Eye Protection:** **Sealed safety goggles with an anti-fog coating.** The perpetual heavy rain overhead carries iron rust particles, moss spores, and tannin-rich water. A single drip of sap-heavy, acidic water in the eye could cause severe chemical irritation.
*   **Chemical/Sap Neutralizer:** Carry a small squeeze bottle of **diluted isopropyl alcohol and clean water**, along with sterile wipes. The giant succulent breaking the boiler and the climbing figs wrapping the conduits are operating under enormous hydraulic pressure. If you accidentally nick a leaf or a pressurized root-junction, sap may spray out under tension. Botanical sap capable of surviving on mineral iron often contains caustic alkaloids.
*   **Structural Wedges / Pry Bar:** A lightweight **titanium pry bar or heavy wooden shims.** If a boot or strap gets caught in a shifting mechanical pinch-point (such as a root pulling tight against an iron wheel), you will need a way to create clearance quickly.

---

### 5. Rules of Engagement & Field Protocol

1.  **Treat All Vegetation as Taut Cables:** The lianas and reed-ferns are tied directly into the physical structure of the building. A vine wrapped around a pulley is under hundreds of pounds of mechanical tension. **Never cut a vine or liana.** Severing a stem under tension could cause it to whip back with lethal force, or cause a multi-ton iron flywheel to suddenly drop or spin.
2.  **Avoid the Pressure Valves:** The wet *clack-hiss* sound indicates active, spring-loaded mechanical valves triggered by ambient sap and steam pressure. Stay at least five feet clear of any exposed brass pipe-fittings, especially those where roots have "screwed themselves into the threading." These seals are watertight because they are under extreme pressure; disturbing them could trigger a jet of scalding steam or high-pressure sap.
3.  **Do Not Climb the Boiler:** The 16-foot steel boiler is being torn apart from the inside by the giant agave. The 4-inch-thick steel plates are bent like orange peel, held *only* by the friction of the fleshy leaves. The structural integrity is a delicate, static equilibrium. Adding the weight of a human could cause a steel plate to sheer off or snap outward.
4.  **Mind the Amber Pools:** The water pooling in the iron troughs is clear but amber-tinted. The floating carpets of thread-like roots look solid enough to stand on ("can support the weight of a stone"), but **they will not support a human.** Step only where your wading staff confirms a solid foundation of iron or deep, dense root-matting.
5.  **Watch the Clock:** Movement in this basin is tied to the sun. As heat builds in the afternoon, the plants expand, driving the hydraulic circuits and turning the wheels. As night falls and air cools, the system contracts. The environment physically shifts twice a day. Plan to conduct your observations during the middle of the day when thermal expansion is at its peak and structural movement is at its most predictable.

### Mental Preparation

Visiting this location feels like standing inside the chest cavity of a sleeping, mechanical leviathan. It is not peaceful in the way a forest is peaceful; it is relentless. The forces at play move at the speed of plant growth, but they possess the power of heavy industry. 

Respect the balance. Keep your weight distributed, keep your ears protected from the grinding iron, keep your eyes on the dripping ceiling, and remember: **you are walking across the top of a living machine.**
