To step into the Lower Sump of Fracture-Nine is to step inside the throat of a silent, overheating engine. You are not visiting a city in any traditional sense; you are entering an automated, industrial balance held together by iron, pressure, and raw earth. 

An ordinary human body is ill-equipped for this place. The environment will try to cook you, cut you, dehydrate you, and confuse your sight. 

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Clothing & Body Armor: Cut-Resistance and Thermal Shielding
The two primary physical dangers of the Sump are the **unforgiving heat** and the **knife-sharp basalt walls**.

*   **The Inner Layer (Moisture Management):** You will sweat instantly and continuously. Wear a non-synthetic, high-grade wool or advanced technical moisture-wicking base layer. Avoid cheap synthetics that will melt if exposed to a sudden blast of steam. 
*   **The Outer Layer (Cut & Burn Protection):** The rock faces are freshly fractured basalt—they will slice through denim, canvas, and skin like broken glass. Wear heavy-duty, flame-resistant work trousers and a jacket made of aramid (Kevlar) or heavy, oil-tanned leather. Your elbows, knees, and shoulders must be reinforced; you will frequently be squeezing through narrow gantries where you’ll brush against either scalding iron or razor-edged stone.
*   **Footwear (Grip and Heat Isolation):** The gantries are sweat-slicked, rusty, and dripping with condensate. The floor plates under your feet may be hot enough to soften standard shoe soles. You need high-top, steel-toe work boots with **oil- and water-resistant Vibram soles** and a heavy shank. The tread must be aggressive to catch on wet iron grates.
*   **Gloves:** Do not touch anything with bare skin. Iron pipes may be boiling hot, metal edges are sharp, and grease is everywhere. Bring multiple pairs of **heat-resistant, cut-level A5+ leather work gloves**.

---

### 2. Respiratory & Ocular Care: The Air of the Sump
The air smells of tallow, iron, and heavy steam. It is stagnant, dense, and hot.

*   **Respirator:** While the air is breathable, long-term exposure to vaporized tallow, iron dust, and sulfur compounds from the lichen-covered rock will cause lipoid pneumonia or heavy metal toxicity. Carry a half-mask respirator equipped with **P100 / Organic Vapor filters**.
*   **Eye Protection:** The dull amber carbon-filament lighting means your eyes will dilate to catch whatever light exists, making you vulnerable. Sudden bursts of localized steam from pressure-relief valves can cause thermal burns to your corneas. Wear **vented, anti-fog safety goggles** with a slight amber or clear tint.
*   **Skin Salves:** The ambient heat is dry, but steam bursts create localized humidity. Your skin will chafe quickly. Coat thighs, armpits, and feet in anti-chafing ointment before dressing.

---

### 3. Hydration & Nutrition: The Death of Fluids
You will lose fluid at an alarming rate simply standing still. 

*   **DO NOT DRINK THE WATER IN THE SUMP.** The condensation may look clear as it drips down the rock, but it is laced with dissolved minerals, rust, tallow residue, and sulfuric compounds. It drains into **lead-lined troughs**. Ingesting it will cause severe systemic poisoning.
*   **Carry All Your Water:** Pack a minimum of **4 to 6 liters of water per day**, stored in an insulated hydration bladder so the ambient heat does not warm your supply to unpalatable temperatures.
*   **Electrolyte Replacement:** Plain water is not enough. You will sweat out salts rapidly, leading to heat cramps and disorientation. Every drop of water you consume should be mixed with high-sodium, high-potassium electrolyte powder.
*   **High-Calorie, Non-Melting Food:** You need dense, salty calories that will not spoil or melt in a furnace-like environment. Hard cured meats, salted nuts, hard cheeses, and high-protein biscuits are ideal. Avoid chocolate, soft bars, or anything requiring cooking.

---

### 4. Navigation & Rigging Gear: Surviving the Web
There are no streets. You will be climbing vertical webs of riveted iron, narrow walkways, and corrugated zinc roofs suspended over dark, narrow drops.

*   **Personal Fall Arrest System:** A fall here means tumbling through levels of sharp ironwork and dropping into the dark abyss of the lower basalt trench. Wear a low-profile climbing/industrial harness with a **double-legged lanyard featuring large steel scaffold hooks**. As you move along the gantries, always stay clipped to a pipe or structural beam.
*   **Hands-Free Lighting:** The carbon bulbs are few and far between, casting long, deceptive shadows that hide missing floor plates or rusted-out steps. 
    *   Bring a high-CRI (Color Rendering Index) headlamp with a warm warm-white beam (around 3000K–4000K). Cold blue/white light will scatter uselessly in the heat-shimmer and mist.
    *   Keep a high-lumen tactical flashlight on your belt for piercing deep shadows below catwalks.
*   **Measurement & Pathfinding:** Because the trench is only 200 yards long but five levels deep, it is easy to get vertically disoriented. Bring a thermal-imaging monocular—it will help you distinguish between cold basalt walls, safe warm gantries, and scalding, dangerous steam lines.

---

### 5. Interaction & Environmental Rules
The Lower Sump is sterile of animal life, but it is an "automated organism."

*   **Respect the Machinery:** The Sump operates on physical feedback loops. Water buckets tip automatically; pressure valves vent without warning; relay boxes snap shut with heavy mechanical force. Treat every pipe, lever, and cable as if it is about to move, release steam, or carry high electric voltage.
*   **Leave the Flora Alone:** The pale, thread-like liverwort and rubbery sulfur-yellow lichen feed on mineral-rich sweat and volcanic runoff. They may harbor caustic chemical compounds. Do not brush against them, harvest them, or disturb them.
*   **Watch the Steam:** Every few minutes, upper-gantry relief valves sigh and vent vapor. If you hear a high-pitched *click* or a breathy hiss above you, step immediately beneath a corrugated zinc shelter or a solid iron plate. A direct hit from a steam relief cloud will give you second-degree burns through light clothes.

---

### 6. Psychological Preparation: The Sound and Silence
The atmosphere of Fracture-Nine can be strangely unnerving to the human psyche.

*   **The Absence of Life:** The complete lack of flies, rats, birds, or human voices triggers a primitive dread. You will feel as though you have walked into a tomb that hasn't realized it is dead yet.
*   **The Acoustic Rhythm:** The slow, rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of deep rock pumps can induce a mild hypnagogic state or nausea over prolonged exposure. The sound travels through the metal rather than the air.
*   **Tactile Anchors:** Bring earplugs designed for industrial work to dull the low-frequency thrumming if you need to rest. Carry something familiar—a small, cool object from home—to ground your senses when the claustrophobia and oppressive heat shimmer start to distort your perception of space.

Approach the Lower Sump of Fracture-Nine not as a tourist, but as a technician entering a giant, unmonitored boiler room built inside a cliff edge. Respect the heat, trust only your own gear, keep your lanyard clipped, and watch where you step.
