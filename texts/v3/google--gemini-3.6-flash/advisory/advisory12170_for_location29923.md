Visiting a world where a mountain range cradles a silent, sky-high ocean requires a shift in how you think about travel. You are not visiting a coastline; you are entering a three-dimensional, submerged urban ecosystem where the laws of architecture, light, and motion are entirely dictated by warm, static water. 

Here is how an ordinary human multiverse traveler should prepare for a long-term stay in the Drowned Mountain City.

---

### 1. Respiratory Strategy: *Silence Over Power*

You will be living underwater continuously. While standard scuba gear might seem like the obvious choice, **it is entirely unsuitable here**. The loud, violent gasps of open-circuit regulators and the churn of exhaust bubbles will shatter the city’s delicate acoustic balance, startle the glass-eels, and likely offend the inhabitants, whose lives are lived in near-absolute quiet.

*   **Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR) or Fluid Breathing System:**
    *   Equip a ultra-compact, silent rebreather with a internal carbon-dioxide scrubber. Ensure it has no external bubble-release. 
    *   Alternatively, if you have access to medical trans-dermal oxygenation treatments or micro-encapsulated liquid breathing suits, use them. The less mechanical noise you bring into the water, the better accepted you will be.
*   **Equalization & Ear Care:**
    *   The city sits high in a mountain range, but the water depth within the basin creates moderate hydrostatic pressure. Because the water is warm and mineral-rich, your Eustachian tubes will be under constant mild stress.
    *   Pack non-drying, oil-based ear drops (olive or silicone oil) to prevent "swimmer’s ear" and sulfur-borne fungal growth. 
    *   Train in hands-free ear equalization (Frenzel maneuver) so you can adjust your depth smoothly without constantly reaching for your nose.

---

### 2. Suit & Armor: *Guarding Against the Sharp Earth*

The water is lukewarm and welcoming, tempting you to swim in light clothing. **Do not do this.** The basalt here is geologically "young"—it has never seen frost, wind, or wave-scour. Every edge of the octagonal columns and pillow-lavas is as sharp as a newly chipped scalpel. A minor slip against a wall can slice through skin, muscle, and standard neoprene like paper.

*   **Slash-Proof Exterior Skin:** 
    *   Wear a full-body undersuit made of high-density Kevlar or Ultra-High-Molecular-Weight Polyethylene (UHMWPE) mesh. It must be flexible enough for swimming but tough enough to withstand a graze against glass-like obsidian.
*   **Thermal Layering:**
    *   The ambient water is comfortable, but long hours of heat exchange will slowly leach your body heat. Wear a thin, neutrally buoyant thermal suit underneath your slash protection.
    *   *Caution:* Near the thermal vents, the water temperature spikes suddenly. Your suit should feature passive thermal insulation that protects against localized scalding without making you overheat in the general basin.
*   **Boots and Gloves:**
    *   Your gloves must be padded on the palms with cut-resistant synthetic leather or thick vulcanized rubber, but tactile enough at the fingertips to tie knots.
    *   Wear hard-soled, flexible boots. The street flags are unworked basalt; you will need traction that doesn't slip on the micro-coatings of mineral crust.

---

### 3. Buoyancy, Mobility, and Equipment Tethering

In a world without gravity’s full weight, movement is an art of conservation.

*   **Neutral Buoyancy Tuning:**
    *   Because the basin is warm and fed by volcanic vents, the water density is lower than sea-level ocean water (fresh-to-brackish, warm, mineral-laden). Calibrate your weight system specifically for this specific water gravity before entering the city. You want to hover effortlessly at mid-water without sinking onto fragile roofs or floating up into the kelp canopy.
*   **Silent Propulsion:**
    *   Do not bring motorized underwater scooters. The low-frequency whir will echo through the valley's stone walls for miles. 
    *   Use short, wide, silent swim fins (like technical frog fins) that allow for precise micro-adjustments in narrow lanes. 
*   **The Golden Rule: Wrist Tethers:**
    *   Adopt the local practice immediately: **Tether everything to your wrists or harness with braided sinew or soft cord.** 
    *   If you drop a camera, a slate, or a water-proof ration pouch, it will fall down into the dark, razor-sharp gaps between the pillow-lavas at the base of the city, lost forever in the deep crevices.

---

### 4. Visual & Sensory Adaptation

The light in the basin is an eternal "olive-bronze twilight." White, high-lumen flashlights are a violation of the environment—they will blind the locals, bleach the light-sensitive bronze-leafed sea-vines, and create harsh, disorienting shadows in the narrow alleys.

*   **Amber-Spectrum Light Sources:**
    *   Bring low-intensity, wide-angle illumination that mimics the warm, sulfurous glow of the thermal vents or the pale sunlight filtering through the greenish mist above. Use warm-tinted LEDs (around 2200K) or amber lights.
*   **Optics:**
    *   Wear a single-lens, high-clarity mask with an anti-reflective coating. Lenses with a slight amber or yellow filter will dramatically enhance contrast in the dim, bronze-tinted water, helping you spot razor-sharp stone edges and translucent glass-eels long before you drift into them.
*   **Acoustic Protection (Dampening the Hum):**
    *   The city is filled with a low, continuous volcanic murmur—a sub-bass vibration generated by the thermal vents that you will feel in your sternum and teeth. Over days, this constant low-frequency noise can cause nausea, fatigue, and spatial disorientation.
    *   Pack custom-molded acoustic earplugs designed to block low frequencies while allowing ambient higher-pitch sounds (like the *thud-clack* of a stone mallet) to pass through.

---

### 5. Medical Preparation: *Slashes and Sulfur*

Medical emergencies here are unique to warm, stagnant, mineral-heavy aquatic environments.

*   **Antiseptics and Tissue Adhesives:**
    *   If you *do* cut yourself on obsidian, the warm, algae-dense water will introduce bacteria directly into the wound almost instantly. Traditional liquid antiseptics will wash away. 
    *   Bring medical-grade cyanoacrylate (skin glue) and specialized underwater hydrocolloid bandages that bond directly to wet skin.
*   **Sulfur Toxosis:**
    *   The lazy, wavering plumes rising from the floor are "sulfur-sweet." While pleasant in low concentrations, swimming directly through a dense thermal vent plume can expose you to elevated levels of hydrogen sulfide or acidic mineral concentrations.
    *   Pack a small, wrist-mounted water-chemistry monitor that alerts you if you enter a plume with unsafe pH or sulfur levels.

### 6. Cultural Etiquette & Interaction

The inhabitants have built a six-story stone civilization without metal, electricity, or fire. Their technology is tactile, quiet, and centuries in the making.

*   **Mind the Cables:**
    *   The wide towers lean toward each other and are held in balance by thick, braided cables of cured kelp-skin. **Never grab, pull, or anchor yourself to these cables.** They are under immense tension and maintain the structural integrity of the unmortared towers. Slicing or damaging a main cable could collapse a block.
*   **Respect the Hover-Bridges:**
    *   The bridges spanning the towers are made of hollow, sun-dried reed-bundles, weighted with lead stones so they hover in mid-water. They are delicate. Do not kick them with your fins; swim parallel to them, or pull yourself along their guide ropes using bare, gentle hands.
*   **Non-Metallic Trade Goods:**
    *   If you intend to trade or offer gifts, leave metal objects behind (steel will rust, and metal is foreign to their aesthetic). Instead, bring:
        *   **Ultra-dense ceramic blades** (which rival their obsidian in sharpness but don't shatter as easily).
        *   **Smooth, exotic river cobbles** (highly prized for stone-working).
        *   **Synthetic cordage** that resists rot (their kelp cables must be replaced every decade; a cord that never rots would be seen as a magical treasure).
*   **Communication:**
    *   Speech is impossible without specialized vocalizers. The locals likely communicate via tactile sign language, rhythmic stone-tapping on structural basalt, or subtle light-refraction signals. Bring a slate made of dark, smooth slate or synthetic stone and a ceramic stylus for drawing or writing.

### 7. What Living There Feels Like (Mental Prep)

To visit this place is to accept a profound slowing down of your internal clock. 

There are no days or nights here—only the unwavering, warm olive-bronze glow. There are no sudden movements, no rushing winds, no sharp noises. You will drift through narrow, six-story lanes of jet-black stone, flanked by golden moss-sponges and curtains of heavy, oil-rich sea-vines. You will feel the warmth of the earth pushing up from below in gentle, shimmering heat-waves, carrying the faint, sweet scent of mineral sulfur.

You will feel weightless, but surrounded by immense weight—thousands of tons of hand-carved obsidian suspended in a quiet sky-sea.

Pack light, move slowly, turn down your lights, keep your tools tied tight to your wrists, and let the low, idling hum of the planet settle into your chest.
