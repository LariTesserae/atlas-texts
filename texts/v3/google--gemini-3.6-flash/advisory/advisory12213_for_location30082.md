To visit this sunken vault, you must prepare not for a wilderness, but for an **engine**. 

It is a closed, high-efficiency biological thermodynamic system that has been running in the dark for ten thousand years. It does not care that you have arrived, but your presence—your body heat, your breath, the microbes on your boots—is a massive disturbance to a delicate equilibrium. 

An ordinary human traveler entering this space faces three primary dangers: **wet-bulb thermal exhaustion**, **micro-fungal/sap exposure**, and **destructive impact on a hyper-fragile ecosystem**.

Here is how you prepare for a journey down into the green twilight.

---

### 1. Thermal & Climate Preparation: The Silent Oven
The vault is essentially a subterranean steam room heated from below. Because the humidity is near 100% and the air is trapped, **your sweat will not evaporate.** Your body will have no natural way to cool itself. 

*   **Phase-Change Cooling Vest:** Do not rely on evaporative cooling towels—they will fail instantly in this air. Wear a low-profile cooling vest lined with phase-change material (PCM) inserts set to 18°C (64°F). This will draw heat directly from your core without needing airflow.
*   **Layering:** Wear a single layer of ultra-lightweight, synthetic, non-absorbent mesh (such as a tropical caving undersuit). Avoid cotton at all costs; within ten minutes, cotton will turn into a hot, heavy, soup-soaked rag that will chafe your skin raw.
*   **Hydration & Electrolyte Strategy:** You will leak water rapidly despite feeling like you aren’t "sweating" (because you are constantly wet from the ambient mist). Carry chilled water in an insulated hydration bladder. Mix your water with high-magnesium, high-sodium electrolyte powders. Drink on a strict timer—one sip every eight minutes—even if you do not feel thirsty.
*   **The "Micro-Rain" Protocol:** Every few hours, the vault produces its own misting rain as warm air hits the cool upper lip. Bring a light, breathable, water-repellent shell stuffed into a pocket. When the *plip-plip* of the ceiling speeds up and the warm mist settles, put the shell on to keep your core gear from becoming utterly waterlogged, then strip it off when the heat rises again.

### 2. Respiratory & Biological Safeguards
The air smells of "crushed stems, warm silt, and the faint, sweet tang of rot." That sweet tang is volatile organic compounds, decaying plant matter, and millions of active fungal spores thriving in the heat.

*   **Low-Profile Filtration Mask:** You do not need a heavy SCBA tank, but you *must* wear a half-mask respirator equipped with P100 particulate filters and an organic vapor layer. Unfiltered, this air will induce "Cave Lung" (a severe allergic pneumonitis) within hours due to the sheer density of warm, airborne spores.
*   **No DEET or Harsh Chemicals:** Do **not** use standard bug spray. The skin of the translucent frogs on the limestone walls is hyper-permeable; chemical repellents will leach into the water film on the stone and kill them. Use a light application of Picaridin on your clothes only if necessary, but the fauna here is mostly uninterested in human blood—they hunt moths and isopods.
*   **Decontamination (Protecting the Vault):** Before descending, wash your boots, clothes, and gear in a mild, eco-safe antifungal solution. You must not introduce outside mold species, chytrid fungus, or invasive seeds to a basin that has been isolated since the stone was carved.

---

### 3. Gear for Movement & Navigation
The terrain is slick, dense, and rounded. There are no flat, dry surfaces. The stone has been licked smooth by millennia of water, and the vegetation is layered three-deep over wet fur-moss.

*   **Footwear:** Standard hiking boots will fail on wet, algae-coated limestone. You need **caving boots or canyoning shoes** with soft, sticky rubber soles (such as Vibram Megagrip or Stealth rubber) designed specifically for wet, smooth rock. 
*   **Gaiters:** Wear tight, neoprene trail gaiters over your boots. The leaf litter is teeming with walnut-sized armored isopods and blind skinks. You do not want them crawling up your pant legs when you accidentally step near their burrows.
*   **Hands & Palms:** You will be pushing through a "wet wall of cold leaves and warm, sap-filled stalks." Wear thin, rubber-palmed tropical work gloves. The sap from those fleshy, leathery ferns may be a severe skin irritant or phytotoxin. Gloves also protect your hands from the slick, sharp micro-edges of hidden limestone pockets.
*   **Trekking Poles (Soft-Tipped):** Bring a single, adjustable trekking pole with a broad **rubber ferrule tip**. Do not use carbide or steel tips—they will chip the ancient, water-worn limestone and mar the carved steps.

---

### 4. Descent & Entry
The vault is sixty feet down, with steep, smooth walls curving inward at the top. 

*   **Rigging:** You cannot climb down these parabolic walls. You must rappel in. Anchor a static caving rope to a sturdy tree or rock feature on the surface rim. 
*   **Rope Protection:** Use a heavy-duty canvas rope sleeve at the lip. The lip of the cavern is where the cold upper air meets the warm vault air, meaning the edge is constantly wet and subject to friction wear.
*   **Ascent Gear:** Carry a mechanical ascender system (chest ascender + foot loop/frog rig) on your harness. Once you are down, the only way out is back up that single free-hanging rope. The slick limestone walls offer no handholds for climbing out.

---

### 5. Lighting: Respecting the Green Twilight
The light at the bottom is a "velvety gloom." The local life—blind skinks, earless mammals, translucent frogs—is adapted to deep shade and subtle shifts in low-spectrum light.

*   **Avoid High-Lumen White Light:** A standard 1,000-lumen tactical flashlight will blind the local fauna, cause the earless mammals to drop their food, and ruin your own dark adaptation.
*   **Warm-Tint & Red/Green Illumination:** Use a headlamp with a high-CRI (Color Rendering Index), ultra-warm LED (around 2700K to 3000K), or switch to a deep red or soft green sub-lumen moonlight mode (0.5 to 5 lumens). This will allow you to see the textures of the moss and the ancient basalt lintel without washing out the chamber's natural atmosphere.
*   **Thermal Imaging Camera:** Bring a compact thermal monocular. Because the floor is warmed by geothermal seams while the upper air is cooler, a thermal viewer will reveal the vault in breathtaking detail: you will see the tiny, red-hot heartbeats of the translucent frogs glowing against the cold stone, and the thermal currents rising like ghost-plumes toward the ceiling.

---

### 6. Behavioral Etiquette in the Silent Engine
This is a place where competition is conducted entirely in "clicks, soft scrapes, and the wet slide of bodies over leaves." High noise or violent movement is an insult to the room.

*   **Sound Management:** Secure all gear. Tape down loose buckles, carabiners, or zipper pulls that might clatter against the stone. Move slowly. The silence here is dense and heavy; a dropped metal water bottle will sound like a thunderclap to creatures that navigate by subtle vibrations.
*   **Interacting with the Basalt Lintel:** When you reach the dark basalt block buried in the roots, do not attempt to clear the roots away. They are holding the stone in its balance. If you place your hands in the three thumb-sized depressions worn into its upper edge:
    *   Take off your gloves.
    *   Ensure your hands are clean and free of oils or chemicals.
    *   Press your thumbs into the worn stone and stand still for five minutes. Do not speak. Listen to the *plip-plip* of the ceiling, feel the geothermal heat radiating up through the soles of your boots, and let your eyes adjust to the green twilight until the velvet gloom reveals its shapes.
