To visit this shelf-valley, an ordinary human traveler must prepare for a rare and brutal combination of environmental extremes: **blinding, shadowless radiation; sub-freezing, high-humidity cold; and a hyper-dense, constantly shifting vertical urban landscape.** 

You are stepping into an ecology of wet slate, tallow, ice, and absolute silence. If you enter unprepared, you will be struck blind by noon, hypothermic by afternoon, and crushed or lost in a dismantling alleyway by nightfall.

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Vision Protection: Stopping the Hard White
The light in this basin is not merely bright; it is a weapon. Trapped between cloud-silt and ice, reflected off millions of wet, black slate surfaces, the glare comes from every angle—including straight up from your feet. Standard sunglasses will fail you because light will leak in around the frames, burning your retinas.

*   **Ocular Gear:** You need **Category 4 glacier goggles** with dark leather or silicone side-shields, polarized to cut wet-rock reflection. Better yet, carry a pair of traditional Inuit-style bone or wooden slit-goggles as a backup. The narrow horizontal slits restrict light to a fraction of a percent while retaining depth perception.
*   **Facial Prep:** Apply a heavy layer of dark, non-reflective zinc or lampblack paint to your nose, cheekbones, and around your eyes. Without it, the light bouncing off the wet slate will burn the underside of your eyelids and nostrils within hours.

---

### 2. Attire: Cold, Wet, and Narrow
Most cold-weather gear is built for *dry* snow. Here, you will be hit by steaming mineral spray, ice-cold runoff, and saturated mud, all while ambient temperatures freeze your breath into crystals. Furthermore, the alleyways are so narrow you must turn sideways to pass. Bulky puffer jackets will snag on bone ladders, tear against raw slate, and soak up grease.

*   **Outer Shell:** A tough, streamlined, 100% waterproof hard-shell (or oilskin/tallow-treated leather if you wish to blend in). Avoid exposed straps, loops, or bulky side-pockets. Your profile must be as smooth as a river stone.
*   **Insulation:** **Synthetic fleece or boiled wool only.** Do *not* wear down insulation; the ambient moisture and constant spray will wet-wet down into a useless, freezing sludge.
*   **Gloves (Critical):** You will be constantly touching freezing water, greasy bone, and rough basalt. Bring **heavy neoprene or rubberized cold-water diving gloves** lined with thin wool. Standard leather gloves will saturate, freeze solid, and strip the skin from your palms.
*   **Footwear:** Mountaineering boots with hard plastic soles will slip endlessly on wet liverwort and slate, creating loud, disruptive clattering. Instead, wear **canyoneering or wet-suit boots with soft, sticky-rubber soles** (like stealth rubber), paired with thick wool socks and waterproof vapor-barrier liners. You need flex, tactile feedback for slick ladders, and quiet footing.

---

### 3. Biological & Physiological Tuning
*   **Altitude & Air:** The air is thin, cold, and heavy with moisture. Acclimatize at high altitudes before descending here, or carry a prescription altitude aid (like acetazolamide). Your lungs will burn from the frost-crystals; wear a breathable wool neck gaiter or damp mask over your nose and mouth to humidify and warm the air before it hits your chest.
*   **Caloric Intake:** Your body will burn vast amounts of energy staying warm in damp, sub-freezing air. Pack high-density, shelf-stable fats (pemmican, butter, lard, hard cheeses). The locals live on rendered tallow, dried fish, and larvae. If offered food, accept it—your body will crave the pure fat.
*   **Skin Barrier:** The combination of ice-spray, mineral-heavy water, and high-altitude UV will shred bare human skin. Coat exposed facial skin in a thick barrier of unperfumed lanolin or pure tallow. Do not use water-based moisturizers; the water content in them will freeze on your face.

---

### 4. Navigational Logistics in a Moving City
This town is a living coral made of dry-stacked stone, constantly being built and torn down beneath your feet. 

*   **The "No Anchors" Rule:** **Never leave your gear down.** If you set a pack on a stone ledge and walk away for twenty minutes, you may return to find the ledge has been dismantled, the stone passed up a terrace, and your pack buried beneath a newly diverted water-flume. Everything you own must be attached to your body in a single, tight, waterproof haul-bag.
*   **Ladder Safety:** The ladders are lashed thighbones and notched birch, slick with animal grease and ice. Keep three points of contact at all times. Do not grip the rungs with bare hands.
*   **Surge Awareness:** Listen for the wet *gargle* of the rock seams above. When a subterranean pulse releases warm, steaming mineral water, watch the locals. If they scramble, **scramble with them immediately.** Do not stand in the channels; a sudden warm pulse is quickly followed by freezing runoff that will glaze the walkway into an ice-slide.

---

### 5. Social Etiquette: The Protocol of Silence
The town operates in near-total silence. Loud voices do not carry well in thin, wet air, but more importantly, noise disrupts the delicate, ant-like coordination required to manage the water flows.

*   **Vocal Restraint:** Do not shout. Speak in low, glottal whispers if you must speak at all. Use hand signals. 
*   **Yielding the Way:** Water-diverters carrying slate baffles, mud spades, or heavy stone slabs *always* have the right of way. In the narrow chimneys between houses, press your back flat against the slate wall, turn sideways, and tuck your chin to let workers pass.
*   **Fauna Interaction:** The six-legged iron-hide hounds and featherless tallow-birds are working animals and heat-sources, not wild threats or pets. The hounds sleep three-deep in the lower cells to provide ambient heat. If you crawl into a cell for warmth, lie still, do not make sudden movements, and allow the animals to pack against you. Their breath smells of sweet earth; their greasy hides will keep you alive through the night.

### Summary Checklist Before Crossing the Threshold
1.  **Eyes covered?** (Glacier glasses or slit-goggles on, face painted black).
2.  **Profile tight?** (No dangling straps, slimpack zipped, shell sealed against wet spray).
3.  **Hands protected?** (Neoprene/wool gloves on; hands ready for cold, slick stone).
4.  **Footing silent?** (Soft, sticky rubber ready for grease, liverwort, and wet slate).
5.  **Mindset set?** (Observe the water, move softly, trust the flow of the city, and keep moving).
