### Field Preparation Manual: Destination "The Obsidian Engine Shelf"
**Classification:** Extreme Thermal / Zero-Moisture / Non-Biological Biosphere  
**Risk Level:** Critical (Lethal without active environmental control)

---

### The Reality of the Arrival

When you step through the transit point onto this shelf, your senses will immediately register a paradox: you are standing under an open sky, yet you are inside a kiln. 

There is no air movement. The silence is heavy, broken only by a sub-audible 50-Hz vibration that you will feel in your teeth and pelvic bones before you hear it. The rock beneath you is not merely hot; it is radiating energy at temperatures approaching $300^\circ\text{C}$ to $325^\circ\text{C}$ ($570^\circ\text{F} - 620^\circ\text{F}$). The air is entirely devoid of humidity. If exposed, your tears, sweat, and the moisture in your lungs would flash-evaporate, causing catastrophic internal thermal burns in seconds.

Furthermore, the ground is a literal minefield of surgical blades. Because this basalt and obsidian have never been touched by rain, wind, or dust, every edge is micro-fractured glass. A single stumble in soft-soled shoes will slice through fabric, leather, and flesh down to the bone.

This is not an ecosystem that wants to kill you; it simply operates on an industrial and mineral chemistry that takes no account of wet, soft biology. To visit safely and appreciate its quiet, incredible beauty, you must prepare like an astronaut entering an active forge.

### 1. Environmental Protection (The Suit)

Standard outdoor gear, hiking boots, or basic hazmat suits will fail instantly. You are undertaking an Extra-Vehicular Activity (EVA) under severe conductive and radiant heat conditions.

*   **Active Cooling Garment (ACG):** Beneath your outer shell, wear a closed-loop liquid cooling undergarment. This pumps chilled fluid across your skin to draw away body heat. Without this, your suit will become an oven within two minutes.
*   **Radiant & Conductive Outer Shell:** Your suit’s exterior must be a multi-layered composite:
    *   *Outer layer:* Aluminized or gold-reflective para-aramid (Kevlar/Nomex blend) to bounce the harsh, unscattered light falling through the sky-vent.
    *   *Mid-layer:* Aerogel thermal insulation blankets to stop the radiant heat baking out of the black obsidian.
    *   *Inner layer:* Puncture-proof chainmail or ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) weave to protect against the razor-sharp glass terrain and mineral plant structures.
*   **Footwear:** Standard rubber or Vibram soles will melt and weld to the rock instantly. You need **titanium- or ceramic-shod boots** with integrated heat-break layers between the sole and the footbed. The tread should be studded with micro-gasket ridges to grip mirror-smooth obsidian without skittering.
*   **Atmosphere & Hydration:** **Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) with rebreather.** Do not rely on ambient air filters—there is no oxygen-producing photosynthesis here, and the atmosphere may be rich in ozone, hot bitumen vapors, and sulfur gases. Your water supply must be integrated into an internal hydration port within your helmet. The water must be actively chilled; otherwise, ambient thermal transfer will turn your drinking supply to boiling water within an hour.

### 2. Sensory Adaptation & Navigation Tools

The physics of this pocket-space are slightly warped by magnetic and gravitational anomalies created by the subterranean mineral plates. Relying on your raw eyes and spatial intuition will cause disorientation.

*   **Visor & Optical Filtering:** The light coming through the vertical vent is harsh, direct, and unscattered by dust or vapor. It hits mirror-black obsidian, creating brilliant, blinding glares adjacent to pitch-black, hard-edged shadows. You need an **auto-dimming, polarized gold-coated visor** with high dynamic range (HDR) contrast filtering. 
*   **LiDAR Rangefinder / Spatial Overlay:** Because perspective buckles across short distances—making a succulent 30 feet away appear directly in front of your face—**do not trust your depth perception**. Wear a Heads-Up Display (HUD) fed by real-time LiDAR or ultrasonic distance sensors. It will overlay an accurate 3D spatial grid over your vision, preventing you from reaching out to touch a plant that is actually ten yards away, or walking off a ledge you thought was distant.
*   **Inertial Navigation System (INS):** Traditional magnetic compasses are useless here. The shadows point toward the mountain’s internal magnetic field (North-Northeast), and local electrical arcs will cause needle-drift. Use a gyro-stabilized INS or satellite-equivalent beacon placed at your entry portal.
*   **Active Noise-Canceling Audio Integration:** The constant 50-Hz hum from the earth engine can induce severe nausea, disorientation, and anxiety over long periods. Your helmet audio should feature inverse-phase active noise cancellation tuned to damp sub-bass vibration, allowing you to hear the subtle, beautiful "wine-glass" chimes of the quartz leaves without being worn down by the subterranean resonance.

### 3. Electrical & Kinetic Hazards

You are walking across a living, self-assembling circuit board. 

*   **Grounding and Isolation:** The copper-moss and cable-like roots carry active electrical currents derived from thermal differentials. Low-voltage static arcs ($100\text{V} - 500\text{V}$) leap between fronds. Ensure your suit is coated in a non-conductive dielectric spray and carries a trailing grounding wire tuned to bleed off static safely.
*   **Piston Terrain Awareness:** The basalt pillars in the open zones rise suddenly and silently on hydraulic mineral expansion. **Never step on a round or socketed basalt block.** Look for the brassy pyrite seams that line active sockets. Map your path exclusively across contiguous, cracked obsidian plates. 
*   **Resin Exposure:** If you break a plant stem, it will bleed a molten liquid polymer/bitumen mixture that seals instantly. This sap is extremely hot and adhesive. Carry a chemical solvent (such as hot-mineral-oil release agent) in a spray bottle. If resin splashes on your suit joints, it will harden into a rigid shell, locking your knees or elbows until dissolved.

### 4. Psychological Preparation & Mindset

Visiting this chamber requires a mental shift. 

*   **The Absence of Decay:** Every environment humans inhabit is defined by weathering, dust, softness, and decay. Here, everything is *virgin*. The stone is fresh, the edges are brand-new, and nothing rots. It can feel uncanny—as though you have stepped inside a newly manufactured machine that has not yet been turned on, or one that has been running perfectly for ten million years without a human operator.
*   **Patience:** Nothing moves quickly here except the occasional arc or rising pillar. The plants grow by vapor deposition over centuries. Match your pace to the mountain. Move deliberately, plant your feet intentionally, and refrain from sudden movements.

### What It Will Feel Like

If you prepare correctly, stepping into this chamber is one of the most sublime experiences the multiverse has to offer. 

You will feel completely weightless in your isolation, suspended high above the clouds in a room lit like a gallery stage by a single vertical beam of white light. Beneath your suit’s quiet hum, you will hear the sharp, crystalline *clink* of flexible quartz leaves swaying as the thermal stress shifts. You will watch copper moss glint with tiny, violet static arcs, and smell the crisp, clean tang of ozone cut through the bone-dry air. 

You are a guest in a cathedral of pure geometry—a place where the Earth builds itself without needing a single drop of water, or a single blade of green grass, to be alive.
