### MULTIVERSE EXPEDITION BRIEFING
**Destination:** The Roof of the Ancient Continent (Non-Euclidean High-Altitude Wet Plateau)  
**Threat Level:** Moderate-Environmental / High-Spatial  
**Primary Hazards:** Hypothermia, High-Altitude Hypoxia, Non-Euclidean Spatial Compression, Spontaneous Architectural Translation, Unfiltered Biological Spores.

---

### MENTALITY & EXPECTATIONS
You are visiting a dead world’s high crown. There are no predators here, no pathogens carried by biting insects, no armies, and no local inhabitants to negotiate with. The silence will be the loudest thing you experience. 

Do not mistake this place for a ruin waiting to be conquered or cataloged; it is a landscape that has completed its life cycle and is now coasting on the inertia of decaying physical laws. Respect the sheer weight of time here. If you feel a sudden, crushing sense of insignificance standing on a basalt terrace beneath a hard cobalt sky, that is not a psychological anomaly—that is simply an accurate perception of where you are.

---

### 1. CLOTHING & FOOTWEAR: *The "Cold-Wet" Protocol*
The primary physical killer on this plateau is the combination of near-freezing temperatures ($0^\circ\text{C}$ to $4^\circ\text{C}$) and omnipresent, drowning moisture. Cotton is strictly lethal here. If you get wet, the high-altitude wind will strip your core heat in minutes.

*   **Next-to-Skin (Base Layer):** Heavyweight merino wool or hydrophobic synthetic mesh. You need a layer that moves sweat away from your body instantly, as walking through three-foot-deep moss will be surprisingly strenuous.
*   **Insulation (Mid-Layer):** Breathable synthetic fleece or hydrophobic down. Avoid standard down; the ambient mist will turn it into a soggy, useless clump within an hour.
*   **Outer Shell (Hard Shell):** Fully waterproof, windproof, mountaineering-grade three-layer membrane (e.g., Gore-Tex Pro or heavy-duty rubberized sailing shells). Every seam must be taped. Pockets must feature waterproof gaskets.
*   **Footwear & Legwear:**
    *   *The Moss Problem:* You will be walking on a three-foot-deep, liquid-soaked carpet of liver-red and green moss. Walking on it is like trekking over freezing sponges soaked in ice water.
    *   *Boots:* High-cut, fully waterproof mountaineering boots with deep lugged rubber soles (Vibram or equivalent) treated with a silicon sealant. 
    *   *Gaiters:* Impermeable, knee-high insulated gaiters lined with neoprene to seal the top of your boots against the moss carpet.
    *   *Traction:* Pack lightweight microspikes or crampons. The polished granite domes and wet slate curbs are as slick as oiled glass.

### 2. ATMOSPHERIC & RESPIRATORY GEAR
The air is thin, saturated with cold moisture, and carrying unknown biological particulate.

*   **Supplemental Oxygen / Altitude Adjustment:** The sky at midday is hard cobalt—you are at an altitude equivalent to 14,000–18,000 feet above sea level. 
    *   Begin a course of *Acetazolamide (Diamox)* 24 hours prior to transit to aid acclimatization.
    *   Carry a lightweight pulse oximeter and a portable canned-oxygen system or a micro-concentrator if your lungs are unconditioned.
*   **Respiratory Protection (P100 / Respirator):** The description notes clouds of green moss spores floating like cold steam. While there are no animals, alien fungal or bryophyte spores can cause severe pneumonitis or fungal colonization in human lungs if inhaled in high concentrations. Wear an elastomeric half-mask respirator rated for wet biological aerosols.

### 3. NAVIGATION & SPATIAL ANOMALY SURVIVAL
This is where standard wilderness survival breaks down. The landscape is spatial non-Euclidean, and the architecture shifts. **A magnetic compass and standard GPS will fail or draw you into loops.**

*   **The Pace Paradox:** A 3-mile street may take 40 steps to cross, but looking back, it will appear leagues long. 
    *   *Rule:* **Trust your feet, not your eyes.** Measure progress strictly by elapsed physical effort and time, not visual landmarks. A building that looks five miles away might be three minutes of walking; a structure thirty feet away might sit across an expanded fold of space that takes hours to traverse.
*   **Inertial Navigation Unit (INU):** Bring a self-contained, mechanical or optical-gyroscope inertial tracker that maps your *physical movement through space* rather than relying on external signals, magnetics, or visual triangulation.
*   **Physical Tethers & Marker Flags:** When exploring large terraced plazas or windowless halls, trail a high-visibility, ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene line behind you. Do not rely on visual breadcrumbs (like stone markers); the stones themselves may shift.
*   **Shadow Anomalies:** Shadows cast at right angles under a single sun mean light is bending through localized gravitational or spatial warps. *Do not use shadows for orientation or timekeeping.*

### 4. CAMPSITE SELECTION & ARCHITECTURAL SAFETY
The city is active. It moves at a "glacial pace," but a sliding ten-ton megalith moves fast enough to crush a sleeping human without stopping.

*   **NEVER Sleep Inside or Touching the Buildings:** 
    *   A slate terrace wall can shift 400 yards overnight. If you are sleeping inside a dry-stacked hall when its geometry decides to re-align, you could be crushed by 20-foot-thick walls or left stranded in an open chasm.
*   **Camp on the Open Bedrock Domes:** 
    *   Pitch your shelter on the broad, smooth granite/basalt domes, away from dry-stacked walls, megaliths, or suspension bridges.
    *   Use a freestanding, four-season geodesic tent capable of handling high-altitude winds.
*   **Elevated Sleep System:** Do not sleep directly on the ground or the moss. Use an insulated, closed-cell foam pad stacked *beneath* an inflatable pad with an R-value of 6.0 or higher. The ground is a massive heat sink that will bleed the warmth from your body through conduction.
*   **Beware Micro-Climates:** If you walk past a courtyard trapped in a localized, 30-foot snowstorm, **do not camp near its perimeter.** These micro-weather anomalies can expand or drift.

### 5. RATIONS & WATER
There are no animals, no fish, and no edible fruit. The ecosystem is entirely autotrophic (mosses, lichens, ferns).

*   **Food:** Bring 100% of your rations. Pack high-calorie, shelf-stable, easily digestible meals (freeze-dried alpine rations, nut butters, pemmican). High altitude suppresses appetite while drastically increasing caloric burn—force yourself to eat.
*   **Stoves:** Liquid-fuel (white gas) stoves perform best here. Canister stoves (isobutane) will freeze and lose pressure in this temperature and altitude.
*   **Water Collection:** Water is everywhere—dripping off roofs, pooling in cup lichens, running across stone. 
    *   *Warning:* Water that drips *upward* or flows sideways should be avoided—it may be subject to localized energetic warps that alter its chemical structure or heavy-metal content.
    *   *Purification:* Collect standing rainwater from clean granite hollows. Filter it through a 0.1-micron hollow-fiber membrane filter, then boil it. The water is likely pure, but it may contain microscopic moss spores or mineral dust from the slate.

### 6. EXPLORATION OF THE STRUCTURES
*   **Suspension Bridges:** The footbridges spanning the chasms are held up by cables thick as a torso, woven from moss roots. 
    *   *Protocol:* Test the tension before stepping on them. The root fibers are tough, but eons of frost-thaw cycles can cause internal degradation. Wear a climbing harness clipped to a safety line anchored to the main cable with a carabiner.
*   **Dry-Stone Engineering:** The walls are unmortared, held by gravity, petrified timber, and bone pegs.
    *   *Warning:* Do not attempt to pry, climb, or chisel into the joints. The architecture relies on sheer mass and tension. Dislodging a key wedge stone could cause a thousand tons of slate to avalanche down a terraced hillside.

### FINAL FIELD NOTE
When you stand on the top of a basalt terrace and look out over leagues of red and green velvet carpet wrapped around cyclopean stone, take a moment to listen. You will hear no birds, no wind-blown leaves, no rustle of small feet in the peat. You will hear only the wet hiss of the mist, the glassy *tick-tick-plip* of freezing water, and the distant, low groan of a ten-ton stone sliding an inch across the plateau to keep its appointment with an ancient geometry. 

Step carefully, keep dry, trust your instruments over your eyes, and sleep far away from the walls. Enjoy the breath of a finished world.
