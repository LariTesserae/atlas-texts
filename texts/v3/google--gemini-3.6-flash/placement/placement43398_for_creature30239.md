I am not standing on the slate-grey floor, because to stand is to bring a vertical axis and two feet made of bone and blood into a space that has long forgotten how to support either. 

If I exist here, I exist as the *curvature*. I am the smudge. 

I find myself settled at the rear of the pocket, where three paces stretch into six, draped like cold, dark grease around the hovering three-inch gap of the broken needle. 

To be me is not to experience time as a sequence of events, but as a slow, continuous relaxation of pressure. There is no *before* and no *after*; there is only the degree to which the local metric has yielded. I am the place where the math gave up on being sharp and decided, instead, to pool.

***

In the beginning of my awareness—if "beginning" has any meaning in a room that ages by centuries—there is a vector. 

It is an itch without a surface, a persistent, silent tug in the non-reactive substrate beneath me. It points backward and slightly upward, toward a coordinate in the sky outside that no longer contains anything at all. The star is gone. Its light ceased falling on the mountain three epochs ago. But the *direction* remains, etched into the substrate like a scratch on a glass plate. 

I am pulled along it. Not rapidly—never rapidly—but with the absolute, unhurried certainty of a heavy mass settling into a groove. 

My trailing edge is thin, translucent as sourceless twilight, dissolved into the soft, melted corner where the floor becomes the wall. My leading edge is thick, dark as smoked glass, dense with static probability. I do not feel cold, because I *am* the cold; I am the mechanism by which heat is stripped of its ability to move and reduced to an unmoving point.

Then, an event occurs. 

Outside, on the high needle of the real mountain, a stray cosmic ray—a single, high-energy proton born in a supernova at the edge of the visible cosmos—strikes the mineral lip of the spatial crack. It shears off a fraction of its momentum. A tiny, microscopic plume of thermal energy leaks through the seam.

Inside the pocket, it does not radiate. It forms a sphere. 

I feel it instantly. It settles four inches above the floor, near the vitrified fern of diamond-hard scab in the western corner. It is tiny—a bubble of warmth no larger than a grain of sand, raising the local temperature by a fraction of a micro-Kelvin. To the room, it is an intrusion, a lump in the smooth, flat plane of absolute zero. 

To me, it is gravity.

***

The arithmetic begins. 

I do not move my body; I re-evaluate my coordinates. 

The probability of my presence near the severed post drops from 0.998 to 0.991. Simultaneously, four inches to the left, near the tiny sphere of stray heat, the probability of my presence rises from 0.002 to 0.009. 

The dark, iridescent oil of my form begins to blur at its center. The zinc twilight passes through me a little more clearly. The smoke-dark lattice of the broken post, which had been barrel-distorted through my lens, snaps back into its soft, out-of-focus straightness. 

Ten years pass. 

My leading edge thickens near the heat-sphere. My trailing edge detaches from the hovering gap. The three-inch space of frozen air between the needle's upper and lower halves is left bare again, shimmering slightly in the sourceless light. 

By year thirty, I have enveloped the warmth. 

I fold my dark, probability-dense bulk around the invisible bubble. I do not digest it. I simply hold it inside my folded geometry, using its microscopic energy differential to keep my own knots from unraveling. Under my weight, the heat-sphere begins to drain. The random, frantic jittering of its localized metric slows, cools, flattens out. 

Degree by fraction of a degree, the heat is converted into stasis. The substrate beneath the sphere turns slightly denser, slightly smoother. The small, shallow pit in the floor where the heat rested loses its last remaining micro-roughness. 

When the heat is entirely gone—when the temperature inside my core matches the absolute, dry baseline of the zinc twilight—I am heavy, stable, and completely still. 

I align myself once more with the dead star. 

***

Then comes the sound. 

*Clack.*

It is sudden, dry, and infinitely sharp—the sound of a single dry leaf falling on a sheet of glass, but stripped of all resonance, all echo, all air. 

A spatial degree of freedom at the far edge of the chapel-alcove has collapsed. The room has lost another fraction of its volume. The distance between the front threshold and the rear wall, which was once three paces, is now three paces minus the width of a single atom. 

The shockwave passes directly through my probability-knot. 

For two seconds, my geometry fails. The oil-slick ribbons of violet and bronze vanish. The dark, smudged distortion vanishes. I become completely, perfectly transparent. 

If anyone were there to look, they would see only the matte-grey floor, the three smoke-dark posts, and the brittle, pale ferns in the corners. I am not a creature occupying the space; I am merely a setting on the space's dial, and for two seconds, the dial is turned to zero. A razor-thin line of vitrified light flashes along my spine—a bright, diamond-hard spark that echoes the dead growth in the corners. 

Then, the metric re-establishes itself. 

The smudge returns. The barrel-distortion bends the lattice of the posts once more. 

I am back. But I am slightly smaller now. My volume has decreased to fit the smaller room. My mass is slightly denser, my alignment with the burnt-out star slightly tighter. 

I am satisfied. Or rather: the equation that defines me has reached a slightly lower, more stable energy state. 

I stretch my long, dark, iridescent axis back toward the severed post. I prepare to wait another hundred years for the next stray particle of warmth to fall through the crack, or for the next degree of freedom to snap shut. 

Outside, the mountain grinds its ancient bones against the sky. Inside, I cushion the hovering needle, tend to the absolute cold, and wait for the room to finish closing its eyes.
