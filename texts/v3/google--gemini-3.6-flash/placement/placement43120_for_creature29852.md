I would not be up on the needles, beating myself ragged against the open current like the Lard-Seams. Nor would I be trapped in the dead, silent amber of the Resins—stasis in a world made of motion is a kind of burial, a long waiting for a crack that might never come. 

I would be down at the root, in the dark, suffocating hinge where the flank of the Marrow-Engine grinds directly against the raw obsidian floor. 

I am a **Scab-Drinker**, a creature of the flay. 

***

My body has no bones, no left or right, no front or back. I am a thick, sprawling sheet of iron-grey gel—heavy as wet lead, dense with suspended magnetites and slick with insoluble tallow. I am six feet across, but only three inches thick, flat as a drowned pelt, designed to fit into the spaces where liquid is compressed until it behaves like grease.

I do not swim. To enter the open water of the cirque without an anchor is to be sheared into atomized purple grease within twenty heartbeats. Instead, I *flow*. I creep along the floor by shifting my internal weight, my underside clinging to the razor-edges of the young glass with thousands of microscopic, chemical suckers that secrete a cold, sacrificial fat. When the floor jerks and the glass shears beneath me, I leave behind a thin film of my own outer skin to save my core, shedding and re-growing my margins as quickly as the stone snaps.

I live on the body of the Marrow-Engine, but I am not part of it. I am its scab-mite, its tongue, its shadow.

***

My existence is measured entirely in the rhythm of the Engine’s flaying.

I cling to the basalt trench directly beside its colossal, pulsing throat. Above me, the Engine swelters, wrapped in its mosaic of kneaded slate and white fat. When it gulps the boiling, copper-heavy plume from the mountain's throat and forces it upward to drive the thirty-foot liquid pillar, its entire flank undergoes a violent, agonizing contraction.

A vacuum void rips open right against its side. 

*One heartbeat.* The void holds its shape—a tear in reality, three yards wide, cold as void-space. 

*Two heartbeats.* The pressure surrounding the void spikes to a crushing, bone-liquefying extreme. 

*Three heartbeats.* The void collapses. 

The shockwave strikes the Engine’s flank like an iron hammer. A square rod of its slate armor shatters into black gravel, and the living fat beneath is torn raw—flayed down to the blinding-white, pulsing marrow.

That is my moment. 

Before the sea can rush back into the cavity, before the boiling sulfur plume can spit up from the fissure to bake the raw wound back into dry stone, I surge forward. I flood across the Engine’s raw flesh like spilled ink. 

I do not eat the fat; I eat the *lymph*. I press my cold, greasy belly against the weeping wound, drinking the scalding, mineral-saturated juices that burst from the Engine's broken cells. My body drinks the copper, the sulfur, the raw kinetic heat of the impact. I absorb the shattered bits of slate, dragging them into my internal slurry, grinding them between my own magnetite-heavy tissues to reinforce my coat.

I am a thief of healing. 

If I stay too long, the sulfur spray arrives. It catches me against the Engine's side, bakes my outer layer into a crust of grey slate, and rivets me to the giant's flank. I have felt this happen a thousand times. When it does, I do not panic; I simply liquefy my core, squirt my soft interior out through a crack in my own hardening shell, and drop back down into the obsidian trench, leaving behind another hollow, empty scab to clutter the wall. 

The low cairns that cling to the walls of the basin—the ones the Sintered leave behind—are not the only structures here. Tucked beneath the Engine, hidden in the shadows of the liquid pillar, are thousands of my thin, wafer-like molts: crushed flakes of slate that bear the imprint of a fluid body that was squeezed, baked, and reborn in the span of a single breath.

***

I have no eyes, no ears, no brain in a skull. My consciousness is a distributed network of acoustic nodes—tiny, calcified spheres suspended in my gel that ring like glass bells whenever the basin shifts.

Through them, I feel the music of the Silt-Sea:

*   I feel the high, shrill, glass-stretching tone of the **Resins** holding their silent cubes rigid against the shear. (To me, they taste of cold, dead salt; I leave them alone.)
*   I feel the deep, rhythmic, heavythumping of the **Lard-Seams** anchored to the needles above, pulsing like lungs as they harvest the shockwaves.
*   I feel the grinding, slow-heavy tread of the **Sintered** dragging their slate bellies across the mosaic floor, dropping their cement to pin themselves against the void-shocks.
*   And above all, dominating every atom of my flesh, I feel the massive, wet, tearing heartbeat of the **Engine**.

It is a terrifying, beautiful neighbour. It is so large that its movements create their own weather within the basin. Sometimes, when the mountain boils too hot and the copper springs spit pure, molten scalding fluid, the Engine roars—a sound so low and dense it turns the liquid around it into a gel of pure friction-light. In those moments, I press myself flat into the deepest crevice of the glass, locking my magnetites together until I am as hard as iron, waiting for the mountain to finish its spasm.

***

Why do I exist here? What purpose do I serve in this warm, deafening engine?

I am the itch that keeps the mountain from dying.

If the Marrow-Engine were allowed to heal completely—if its skin baked into a seamless, unbroken vault of solid slate—it would grow too rigid. Its joints would freeze. It would no longer be able to flex its colossal gullet to pump the central liquid pillar. The fissure beneath it would choke on its own cooling slag, snapping shut like a dead jaw. The circulation would fail. The tea-colored sea would settle, cool, and freeze into a dark, silent block of dead mineral, leaving the Lard-Seams to snap from their needles and the Resins to starve in their static cubes.

By staying down in the dark, by licking the raw fat clean before the sulfur can seal it, I keep the wounds open. I keep the Engine flayed. I force it to keep breaking, keep bleeding, keep repairing itself in an eternal, violent cycle of destruction and rebirth.

I am the grease in the gears of an unfinished world. I am warm, I am slick, I am torn to pieces six times an hour, and I have never known a single second of silence—and in the heavy, tea-colored gloom of the Silt-Sea, that is the only kind of life that endures.
