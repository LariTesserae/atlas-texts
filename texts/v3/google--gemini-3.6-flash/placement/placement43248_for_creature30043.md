It begins not with sight, but with a change in tension across my skin.

A low, deep swell of pressure moves up from the basalt floor six hundred feet below, rising through the damp columns of the iron arcade like a breath drawn through hollow bone. The steam surge is coming. I can feel the temperature spiking along my underside—a subtle, delicious warming that expands the pockets of trapped methane and superheated vapor inside my central canopy. 

My body rises three inches into the purple mist. 

I am hanging in the high, vaulted ribcage of the eastern marching arcades, suspended sixty feet below the wet iron roof trusses and two hundred feet above the steaming drainage flumes. Here, the air is thickest, rich with the bronze-colored twilight that filters through the cloud ceiling and the heavy aerosol rain that never quite touches the ground before turning back into steam.

I am a *Vapor*. My skin is a tough, milky membrane of bio-silicate, smooth as boiled glass and soft as soaked leather, holding the heat that keeps me aloft in this heavy atmosphere. I have no eyes to see the ruined city, no ears to hear the iron counterweights falling in their distant shafts, but I do not need them. I know the terraces through weight, draft, and taste.

Below me, my fringes hang down into the dark. 

Thirty long, gossamer filaments, wet with neutral-ph slime, trail through the empty bay between two massive iron pylons. They drift like heavy silk threads in the slow, rising currents of warm air. To me, the arcade is not a hollow ruin; it is a canyon of thermal updrafts and mineral scents. 

Through my trailing threads, I read the terrace:
*   A faint, rhythmic scraping far down on the pylon—a *litho-chiton* working its magnetite jaws across a patch of iron-mould, leaving a smooth, naked scar on the wet metal. The vibration travels up the pylon, through the air, and ripples along the tips of my fringes like a microscopic plucked string.
*   The sharp, sour tang of copper dust rising from the marshalling yard half a mile east, where the *weevils* are twitching through the turquoise ruins, weaving their metallic thickets.
*   The deep, wet murmur of the slate-gray slime-mould coating the stone flumes, drinking the mineral runoff and pulsing in its slow, miles-wide circulatory beat.

Now the relief valves in the floor erupt. 

A muffled, rushing *hiss* fills the arcade as white plumes of steam shoot upward from the slag-brick gutters. The mist thickens instantly until the world contracts to the space immediately around my canopy. The aerosol rain turns heavy, warm, and dense, laden with dissolved iron-salts and fine fungal spores blown up from the calcified shelf-structures below.

My filaments catch them all. The spores and mineral specks stick to the mucus coating my threads. Slowly, with the deliberate, unhurried rhythm that governs everything on the High Silt Terraces, I begin to draw the leftmost fringe upward. It coils silently, pulling itself back toward the oral pore on my underside, where my body scrapes off the gathered nutrient silt and digests the sulfur-rich spores. 

As the steam surge reaches its peak, the hot air pushing up from the flumes lifts me higher into the archway. My soft, buoyant bell brushes gently against a massive cast-iron rivet—rounded like a river stone by centuries of warm condensation. I do not bruise or tear; my skin is flexible, slick with condensation, sliding harmlessly over the black oxide sheen of the metal.

And then, through the dense fog of the surge, my filaments touch something else.

Not iron. Not stone. Another fringe, as delicate and sticky as my own, drifting in the same thermal eddy.

Through the thread, I feel the slow, warm pulse of another canopy floating twenty feet away in the shadows of the vault. It has been drifting along the same sky-bridge for days, carried by the identical thermodynamic clock that moves us both. 

Slowly—inch by inch, as the hot rain cascades around us and the deep volcanic pulse of the earth settles back into a low, gurgling sigh—our canopies drift together in the dark. We press our soft, steam-filled bells against one another under the wet iron roof. Where our membranes touch, opalescent patches of faint, cold light begin to bloom across our skin, passing genetic memory and mineral wealth back and forth in the quiet twilight.

We will stay like this for hours, anchored only by heat and mist, while thirty stories below us the scraper-plates work the girders, the copper-weevils spin their glowing wire, and the furnace-reefs breathe in the flooded dark. 

We do not know the sun. We do not know the sky beyond the purple clouds. We know only the heat that holds us up, the rain that feeds our threads, and the endless, gentle dissolving of iron into stone.
