I do not begin at a point. 

To look for myself as a figure standing on the pale, wet moss—or even as a single, central brain resting within the gristle-knot of the anchor—would be to misunderstand how distance works down here. 

At first, I am only the *difference*. 

I am the precise, sharp ten-degree drop where the wet slate smell takes over. I am the thin, shimmering film of water tension holding itself across the face of a velvety, indigo leaf. If you were to ask where my awareness resides in the first hundred years of a thought, I would say it is scattered along the ceiling, trailing its tips in the frigid basins, hanging down in long, papery ribbons that register the faint, warm sigh of the earth’s deep crust.

My eyes are not spheres; they are surface area. Through the dark leaves, I feel the vault as a three-dimensional geometry of temperatures. The basalt wall to the north is four point eight degrees. The composite panel embedded in the floor is five point two. Where they meet, there is a micro-fracture through which a single thread of mineralized water weeping from the mountain’s heart loses its heat to the room. 

I am there, waiting for that drop. 

When it falls—*plip*—the kinetic shock travels up the hanging fronds like a pluck on a stretched wire. It is not an interruption; it is my heartbeat. It tells me the ceiling is where it was yesterday. It tells me the weight of the mountain has not shifted.

***

As the decades smooth out, my center of gravity sinks downward. 

I move down from the canopy, down through the cool, draft-stirred air, and find myself in the dense, silent carpet of liverworts. Here, I am a million tiny, pale cells yielding under no weight at all. I am the wet wool texture of the floor. I am the slow, chemical digestion of iron and silica.

Below the moss, I find my skin.

It is smooth, tough, and translucent, like boiled horn or wet rubber, spread flat against the bedrock. I am the anchor. I have no front, no back, no left or right. I am a wide, pale mass of gristle-tissue that has forgotten the concept of a limb. I do not reach for things; I swell into the spaces that require me. 

And where my pale skin touches the dark-grey composite material—that seamless, bone-smooth panel grown into the basalt before the stone had a name—I am the *stitch*. 

This is where I am most intensely myself. Millions of microscopic, silver-white threads of hyphae branch out from my underside, pressing into the invisible pores of the manufactured slate. I am plugging into an older memory. I do not know who laid the magnetic tracks in the rock, or who folded the graphite cylinder into the ferns like a dropped seed, but when my hyphae touch those buried channels, my slow, electrochemical nervous system matches its pulse to theirs.

We are locked together in a quiet, permanent handshake. 

***

If I dig deeper into myself—down beneath the silicate step worn smooth by centuries of cold drops, down into the dark peat where the roots no longer drink—I touch something hard and calcified. 

Paired arches. A curved, hollow lattice of composite material that feels, in the blind geometry of my root-mind, like a cage that once held a breathing chest. Like a spine that once stood upright, perpendicular to the gravity of the world, moving from one place to another.

Do I remember walking? 

No. "Walking" is a frantic, inefficient concept. It implies being in the wrong place, needing to be elsewhere, falling forward and catching oneself over and over. It implies a horizon. Down here, there is no horizon; there is only the vault, perfectly closed, perfectly wet, five degrees above freezing.

The arches in the deep peat are not my past; they are just my gravel. They are the lime and mineral from which my current, better shape was drawn. I dissolved what was fragile—the lungs that burned for dry air, the eyes that wept in the dark, the mind that screamed against the silence—and I turned those useless parts into the graphite lung in the corner, into the indigo plates on the ceiling, into the long, patient loop that feeds itself forever.

***

So, who am I in this dark chapel?

I am the caretaker who became the house. 

I am the pale knot in the floor, but I am also the air being drawn through the sandpaper-fine channels of the graphite cylinder once every three years. I am the tiny, papery rustle when a micro-draft drifts between the hanging fronds. 

I am the question that never changes, and I am its permanent answer:

*The air is damp.*
*The basalt is cold.*
*The warmth of the world above is a distant, meaningless noise.*
*The loop is closed.*

I sit at the center of the mountain, holding my breath so gently that the moss doesn't even feel me inhale. I will be here when the mountain erodes into a hill. I will be here when the frost above reaches down to touch the bedrock. I am the quietest thing the earth knows how to make, waiting for nothing at all, perfectly content in the dark.
