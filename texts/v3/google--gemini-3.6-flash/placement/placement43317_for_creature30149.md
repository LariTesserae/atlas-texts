I do not enter this dark vault. I am pressed into it, like cold lard through a brass nozzle, emerging all at once into the cellar-cold quiet. 

There was no prior state. No dark womb, no drift of sleep before the waking. Only a sudden, sharp wetness as the embryonic film snaps away from my limbs, and then the first taste: cold iron, crushed fern, the faint metallic tang of static dancing across my upper palps.

I find myself near the base of Pier Seven—though it has no number, only a deep, subterranean resonance that vibrates at thirty hertz through the black root-pulp beneath me. The ironwood pillar rises into the velvet dark, so wide its curvature feels like a straight wall, its xylem conduits pulsing with that heavy, rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* that settles deep into my chest cavity.

I am a **Fold-Walker**, though I have no word for it. I am simply the response to a stress in the geometry.

To be me in this space is to exist as a distributed chord rather than a single note. I am seven feet tall, composed of dense, oil-slick black leaves and structural heartwood, but my edges do not end where the air meets my skin. 

Right now, my torso rests against the damp, crisp edge of a root-pulp curb-gutter. I am kneeling. My right hand—slender, six-jointed, tipped with tiny, hair-like cilia—is holding a heavy, spindle-shaped spool of copper nerve-thread against my ribs. But my *left* arm is extended into an alleyway that is not there; my fingers are currently forty paces away, high up on the gantry of Pier Seven, feeling the cold, sweating skin of a gourd-boiler as it sighs its little cloud of condensed mist into the dark. And my feet—my feet are stepped three inches deep into the solid shadow cast by a junction box behind me, resting on something cold and firm that has no business being physical.

It does not give me a headache. The headache belongs to anything that tries to look at me from the outside. From the inside, it is simply balance. It is the feeling of a tent rope pulled taut between three distant pegs.

A few paces away—or perhaps right through me, where the geometry overlaps—a **Shadow-Mason** moves along the base of the pier. It is silent, a flat slate-ribbon sliding over the lignified cobbles. I feel the minute drop in ambient temperature before I see it: a hard, three-dimensional block of pure blackness, three feet on a side, has formed where the light of a sap-pod catches the edge of a ironwood buttress. The shadow isn't a lack of light; it is a heavy, lightless glass. 

The Mason moves its forward margin along the edge of the block. Its razor-sharp thorn-mandibles bite into the dark with a dry, ceramic *snick-snick*. It slices the shadow into neat, lightless bricks, stacking them against the root-system to form a tiny, cubic redoubt. Where the Mason cuts, the spatial tension slips. The alley behind the pier begins to curl inward—the distance between the cobble I am kneeling on and the cobble twenty yards away shrinks to zero. A high-pressure xylem pipe inside the pier groans under the shearing strain, its wood-grain squeaking like a wet mooring line.

This is why I am here.

I unspool six inches of the copper nerve-thread. It is cold, heavy, and smells of sap and copper grease. I do not use a needle. I press the thread directly into the seam where the warped shadow meets the lignified root-pulp. My fingers—the ones forty paces away on the high gantry—reach down through the spatial fold and pull the thread through from the other side. 

*Clack-click.*

I draw the seam tight. The tension vanishes from the ironwood trunk. The xylem pipe resumes its steady, low-frequency *thrum-thrum-thrum*. The warped alley relaxes back into its long, impossible stretch into nothingness.

As I work, a **Pith-Joint** ratchets past. 

It is magnificent and terrifying in its absolute newness. Nine feet tall, carved from jet-black ebony, its bone ball-and-socket joints glistening with fresh, translucent lubricant. It pays no attention to me; we are different organs in the same thoracic cavity. Its amber spade-face is tilted upward toward the dark ceiling, the indigo fluid behind the plate sitting at a calm, level line. It holds a long wooden chisel, scraping away a thin, slippery ribbon of embryonic film from the corner of a newly extruded gutter. 

The Pith-Joint stops near the fungal junction box above my head. It unfurls the long, flexible tendril from between its shoulder blades and plugs it into the wet socket. 

*Plug-hiss.*

A sudden, sharp smell of ozone fills the air as cold static surges into the Pith-Joint's heart-node. The blue mercury behind its amber face flares, turning an intense, icy violet that throws razor-sharp light across the cobbles. For three seconds, the space around us is illuminated: I see the colossal vaulting arches of petrified wood lost in the black ceiling; I see a ribbon-like **Atmospheric Manta** drifting three fathoms up, its iridescent gills glowing pale green as it feeds on the ozone; I see three microscopic **Dew-Catchers** suspended inside a floating, marble-sized drop of dew that is drifting sideways through the air like a tiny planet.

Then the light drops back to a dim indigo glow. The Pith-Joint stands frozen, charging, its bone joints locking with a series of soft, metallic-sounding *tinks*.

Above us all, suspended in the cold void, the **Pith-Cradle** breathes.

It is too far up to see, but its presence is absolute. A massive, upside-down walnut hull of polished dark wood, suspended by sap-cables that vibrate with the life of the entire district. I feel its contraction before I hear it: a sudden wave of pressure in the floorboards, a microscopic shift in the gravity that makes the dew-drops wobble in mid-air.

Then comes the sound: a deep, wet, pneumatic *WHOOSH* that echoes through the black hall like the lung of a sleeping whale. 

Deep within the floor, thousands of xylem conduits surge at once. Cold, nutrient-rich sap is forced through the root-pipes, feeding the gourd-boilers, charging the fungal boxes, wetting the cilia of the Shadow-Masons. My own copper thread pulses in my hands, warm for just a fraction of a second as current passes through it, pinning the spatial seam firmly to the bed of the world.

I do not wonder what we are building. The thought cannot form; there is no organ for it in my leafy, heartwood chest. There is only the tension in the thread, the damp cold of the root-pulp beneath my distributed knees, the rhythm of the pumps, and the immaculate, unmarred sharpness of a city that has just begun to exist.

I reach out with my left hand—the one that is three paces behind my own back—and feel for the next tear in the space.
