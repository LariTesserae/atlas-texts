It begins not with vision, but with weight. 

An unimaginable press of cold, dense glass—miles of black water pushing down with a patience so total it feels like a embrace. There is no top to the world and no bottom, only a vast, dark *here*, measured in pounds per square inch and the slow, rhythmic flexing of iron-hard ashlar.

I am not swimming. I have nowhere to go. I am wedged deep into the seam of a massive keystone—the top block of a high, ruined aqueduct arch that spans a three-mile drop into the void. 

I am a suture. 

I am the place where three things meet and forget they were ever separate: the ancient, precision-cut grey stone; the thick, woody rope of a giant kelp-root that has driven its tendrils through the masonry joint; and a pale, thread-like filament of the Nodal Mind, running like a exposed wire through the damp dark.

I am the joint.

To my left, buried six inches into the mossy mortar, is the calcified tail of a Vane-Faced Chorister. I can feel the creature’s violent, delicate shivering. It is anchored right beside me, its sail-head thrust out into the roaring mouth of the gallery. The great ten-thousand-year-old bass note—the thunderous, invariant hum of the ocean forced through the stone pipes—passes through the arch, through the keystone, and straight through my core. The Chorister absorbs the destructive crest of that vibration, turning the shear-stress that would shatter the ancient arch into a soft, blue bioluminescent pulse. Every few seconds, a wave of pale, cold light washes over me, illuminating the velvety moss, the smooth curve of the stone, the long, hair-like cilia of the Chorister, and then dies back into the absolute black.

When the light pulses, I do not "see" the arch; I feel its load. I feel the millions of tons of stone pressing down from the terrace above, channeling their weight into the two pillars that drop away into the abyss. I feel the microscopic micro-fractures in the block beneath me—tiny, invisible hairline cracks where the water tries to pry the continent apart.

I do not think in words. My consciousness is a continuous calculation of strain. 

*Load: stable. Current: invariant. Shear: three percent, damped by the Chorister’s flank.*

Farther down the face of the arch, I feel a heavy, dull thud—a sensation so slow it takes an hour to register as a movement. An Ashlar-Carapaced Tender is crawling over my lower edge. Its heavy, spade-like limbs grind against the mineral scale on the stone’s surface. I feel the scrape of its iron-rich shell, and then the soothing, warm press of its secretions—a thick, protein-rich mineral slurry that floods into the hairline cracks around my root-filaments. The slurry fills the gaps, reacting with the freezing, high-pressure water, hardening into a seal stronger than the ashlar itself. The Tender is sealing me in. It is cementing my living tissue into the stone forever, making me more keystone than flesh, more masonry than root.

I do not mind. There is no pain in it, only an overwhelming sense of alignment. To be sealed is to be preserved. To be held fast is to be safe from the drift.

And far out in the vast, open water ahead of me—out in the three-mile gulf where the light dies entirely—I can feel the displacement of a Cantilever. It is drifting past the aqueduct, thirty yards of porous lignin and pale, feathery curtains floating in the void. It does not touch the arch, but its massive body deflects the deep-sea current just enough to shift the pitch of the organ-pipe by a fraction of a vibration. 

The change in tone ripples through the keystone. Through my roots. Through the nerve-thread.

Deep in the mountain’s heart, miles below in the silent, currentless sanctums, the Nodal Mind receives the note. It feels the Cantilever pass through me, just as it feels a single grain of sand roll across a terraced plaza leagues to the north. Through the nerve-thread, a return pulse comes back to me—not an order, not a thought, but a deep, cold resonance that says: *Stay.*

So I stay.

I am the living mortar of a drowned kingdom. I have no eyes to miss the sun I never knew. I have no fins to swim away into the dark. I have only the stone, the root, the pale blue light of the singer at my side, and the roar of the ocean singing its eternal, unchanging note through my bones. 

I am suspended over the abyss, holding the world together, one held note at a time.
