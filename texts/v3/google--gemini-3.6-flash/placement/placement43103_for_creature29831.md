My teeth are pressed against the quartz. 

That is how I know where I am before I open my eyes: by the cold, dense frequency humming through my jaw, down the bones of my neck, and settling into my chest like an second heart. It is a high, thin vibration today—a sharp, frantic *jitter-shiver* that means the updraft three thousand feet below has struck an un-carved ridge and is splitting into twin vortices.

I open my eyes.

The world is blue, green, and terrifyingly clear. I am hanging in a web of three-ply vine rope, my back suspended over two miles of warm, roaring empty space. My thighs are wedged into a narrow fluted trough carved into the lee side of the *Thousand-Trough Spire*—a granite needle so thin that if I were to drop a river-cobble from my left hand, it would fall through clear air for a minute before vanishing into the cloud-floor below.

I am a Teller. My hair was scraped away with a copper edge when I was fifteen seasons old, and my skin is dark, thick, and slick with the yellow oil we press from the fat-leaf succulents. The warm mist sheets across my bare shoulders, atomizing instantly into fine, glittering droplets that slide off my skin without soaking in. My chest is broad—flared out like an oak cask—and with every breath I draw the hot, moisture-choked wind deep into my lungs, feeling the gale press against my ribs from the inside.

In my right hand, I hold a heavy mallet turned from dense blue river-slate, its handle wrapped in braided hair. In my left, a chipped flint chisel, its edge dulled by a week of biting into ancient granite.

I am not here to hunt, nor to harvest the heavy Limpet-Plates that graze the emerald moss-mats below my feet. I am here because the spire is screaming.

Three days ago, a chunk of stone the size of a shelter sloughed off the upper lip of the Great Window above me—a pass-through cavern sixty paces wide that pierces the needle from west to east. The loss of that stone left a sharp, ragged edge. Now, when the thermal engines rise from the hot lowlands and force hundreds of thousands of tons of wet air through the eye of the needle, the air does not flow; it tears. 

It has turned the Great Window into a stone whistle so violent that the bedrock beneath our village—four hundred feet down-cliff—is shaking itself loose. The vine-anchors are chattering in their sockets. Children cannot sleep; the bone-vibration in their skulls is making their ears bleed.

I lean forward, pressing my forehead into the wet slate. 

I listen through my forehead. 

*Thrum-shriek-thrum-shriek.* 

The sound isn't in the air—the air here is just a deafening, golden roar that washes over my ears like white water—it is *in the stone*. The spire is flexing. A monolith this high and thin must bend with the wind, but it must bend rhythmically, like a reed. The new ragged lip is creating a harmonic stutter. If I do not smooth it, the micro-fractures will spread through the quartz veins, and within three winters, the top thousand feet of the spire will shear off and plunge into the abyss.

I shift my weight. My toes—wide, thick-jointed, and tipped with thick, dark nails—curl expertly into a hairline crack in the fluting. I pull myself upward along the braided tether, inching toward the throat of the wind.

As I reach the lip of the window, the world tilts. Here, the air is not just rising; it is compressed, squeezed through the stone eye at a speed that turns water spray into horizontal needles. 

Suddenly, a shadow cuts the sunlight.

A Vane-Skin shoots through the window, barely six feet from my face. It doesn't flap—it *is* a blade. Its pale, parchment-thin membrane is stretched taut over pressurized wing-spars that hum like tuned wire. Its glassy eye-shield reflects me for a fraction of a second: a glistening, oil-dark creature clung to the rock like a leech, hammer in hand. Then, with a dry *clack* of its tail-spur adjusting its angle by a fraction of a degree, it catches the compressed jet of the throat and is shot upward—three thousand feet into the clear, searing glare of the zenith in the space of two heartbeats.

It belongs to the air. I belong to the bone that holds the air up.

I reach the ruined edge. The granite here is white, fresh, and sharp enough to cut leather. I set the edge of my flint chisel against the razor-lip of the rock.

I do not use speech. Speech is a fragile, flat thing that dies two inches from a man’s lips. But I speak to the mountain with the mallet.

*Tink.*

The strike sends a shockwave down my forearm, through my shoulder, into my jaw. 

*Tink. Tink.*

A flake of white granite peels away, seized instantly by the draft and whipped sideways into the blue gulf, where it glints like a falling star before evaporating into the sun-glare. 

I work for hours. The sun moves through the flutes of the surrounding monoliths, slicing the roaring mist into frantic, blinding lattices of gold and shade. Around my ankles, small Moss-Eels creep out from the wet greenery, oblivious to me, their pale, translucent bodies drinking the spray that drips off my knees. Far across the two-hundred-yard void, on the sister spire known as the *Whistling Rib*, I see a tiny dark smudge against the grey slate—another Teller, suspended in their own harness, tuning a low-flute on the windward face. 

We do not wave. Waving catches the wind and wrenches the socket. Instead, I lift my heavy mallet and strike the quartz core twice.

*THUMP-THUMP.*

Across the void, through two hundred yards of empty air and two miles of solid stone, the signal travels down my spire, through the deep continental root miles below the cloud floor, and up through the Rib. 

A second later, I feel it in my heels.

*THUMP-THUMP.*

*I am here,* the vibration says. *The stone holds.*

I turn back to the lip. I shave another fraction of an inch from the curve, rounding the throat so the wet air can slide, not tear. Slowly, subtly, the screaming shudder in the granite begins to change. The violent, bone-rattling stutter softens. The frequency drops—down from the brain-scrambling shriek, down through the throat-tightening whine, down, down into a deep, velvety bass drone that vibrates pleasantly in the soles of my feet.

The spire is breathing again. It is taking the violence of the world and turning it into a song that stays still.

I drop my forehead back against the wet, moss-scented granite. The water runs down my nose, warm and sweet with the sap of the ribbon-vines. The air roars. My harness hums. Far below, the village rests, their shelters anchored deep, their feet pressed to the floorboards to feel that their mountain is whole.

I am a wedge of flesh driven between the stone and the storm, and I am not falling.
