I would not be on the high gantries, nor down in the dark gaps where the basalt curtains meet the plain. 

You would find me at the **Third Tier Sluice-Junction**, directly beneath the shadow of the Great Flywheel—where the wide granite aqueduct narrows into a series of stepped, iron-lined channels, and the air is so thick with warm, aerosolized mineral broth that the white-gold light turns the color of curdled cream.

I would be a **Crust-Tender**—a Thrum whose life is measured by the creeping thickness of teal silica along the spillways.

***

### I. The Sole and the Stone

Before I am a mind, I am a weight. 

My feet—eighteen inches across, flat and thick as slabs of salt-pork—are pressed hard against a band of dark, glass-smooth magnetite. The iron rail beneath me is warm, heating the fibrous fat of my soles until my tallow-coated skin aches with a deep, comforting glow. 

Through the magnetite, the world speaks in its true voice:
*   *Thrum-thrum-thrum:* The high, prickling vibration of the brass Whistle-Pipes three hundred yards to the east, choked with spider-silk and singing at thirty-two cycles.
*   *Hrrnnngh-pah:* The distant, twenty-minute heave of the Great Walking Beam as it reaches the peak of its stroke, hesitates in a cloud of boiling spray, and drops back into the earth.

I do not hear this. The amber cartilage plates where my ears should be are cool and silent, sealed against the acoustic fluid of the atmosphere. But my ribs—the massive, barrel-vaulted cage of my chest—act as a sounding board. Every time the Flywheel’s central axle turns its half-mile circumference, my sternum hums like a plucked copper wire.

I am crouching. My center of gravity is barely three feet off the wet brick. In my thick, four-fingered hand, I hold a heavy, broad-bladed chisel fashioned from the discarded back-shell of a Great Brine-Skimmer. The blade is tipped with iridescent teal silica, harder than cold iron, mineralized by a century of immersion in the runoff.

***

### II. The Work of the Stroke

The silica builds constantly. It creeps up the sides of the granite channels in scalloped, turquoise ridges, like frost on a winter pane, threatening to choke the smooth flow of warm, copper-laden water toward the lower tenement walls where the Siphon-Slugs hang. If the channels choke, the water backs up into the dry axle-pits of the Flywheel. If the pits fill, the grease turns to sludge. If the grease turns to sludge, the twenty-minute pulse of the Basin stutters.

And so, I scrape.

I do not work quickly. Speed is a flaw of the air-creatures who live below the basalt curtains—beings whose lives are brief and twitchy as the pale swifts darting through the stack-steam. I align my scraper with a scalloped ridge of teal crust. I wait.

I wait six minutes for the Flywheel’s third spoke to pass overhead, casting a momentary, pale-grey shadow through the mist. 

When the seismic shudder of the Walking Beam’s descent ripples through the magnetite floor, I use the earth’s own kinetic push to drive my weight forward. *Srrrch.* A curling ribbon of orange-and-teal crust peels away from the granite, dropping into the warm, rushing channel with a soft splash I feel as a tiny prickle in my shins.

A small Brine-Skimmer, no larger than my palm, slides out from beneath the scraped ledge. Its turquoise lamellae glisten in the cream-colored light. It does not fear my broad, tallow-slick hands; I am simply a large, warm stone that moves on a different schedule than the others. I gently nudge it back into the main pool with the flat of my scraper.

***

### III. Contact

A shadow cuts through the misty glow—not the sharp shadow of a machine, but a low, wide silhouette moving across the plaza with the slow, swaying gait of our kind.

It is **Kael-Varn**—or rather, the individual whose footprint carries the high-frequency, three-tap heel-resonance of the Lower Boiler Tier.

They do not call out. They step onto the same magnetite rail I occupy. The vibration of their arrival rises through my left heel: two heavy, rolling thuds followed by a lingering, rhythmic drag of the instep. *The aqueduct at the Fourth Terrace is weeping clean water. The Siphon-Slugs are fat.*

I stand up slowly, my low, thick spine straightening with a series of small, wet clicks. My amber eye-membranes sweep horizontally across my irises, clearing away the fine, warm dew that has settled on my vision.

Kael-Varn approaches until we are inches apart. We do not look into each other’s eyes—eyes are for watching the oil-sheen on the pools and the rise of the thermal drafts. Instead, we extend our forearms.

We press the smooth, hairless hide of our inner arms together. The contact is firm, tallow sliding against tallow.

Immediately, the micro-tremors in Kael-Varn’s shoulder muscles transfer into mine. I feel the deep, internal humming of their heart-vault, beating at nine sluggish strokes a minute, perfectly synchronized with the cooling cycle of the drum-houses. Simultaneously, the vascular network beneath my chest and arms flushes with hot, oxygenated blood. 

Across my forearms, my skin blooms into shifting patches of dark chestnut brown and bright mustard yellow. Kael-Varn’s hide responds, flushing in pale, marbled swirls.

*Through the skin-flush and the muscle-thrum, we converse:*
*   **My skin (Mustard yellow along the radial vein):** *The silica at Sluice Three is hard this cycle. Heavy copper content. It takes two Beam-Strokes to clear a yard.*
*   **Kael-Varn’s muscle-pulse (A low, rhythmic flutter against my wrist):** *Leave the lower margin. The Whistle-Spiders have anchored three silk funnels in the drain-mouth. They are collecting spore-cake. Do not disturb the anchor-threads.*
*   **My skin (Chestnut darkening to iron-red):** *Acknowledged. I will scrape only the upper lip.*

We hold the contact for a full minute, letting our body heat mingle until our tallow coatings fuse slightly, then we pull apart with a soft, suctioning sound.

***

### IV. What I Am

In this world of endless, thunderous mist and wet, rusted iron, I am not a person in search of a destination. I have no desire to see what lies beyond the sheer basalt curtains of Ostra, nor do I wonder who built the chimneys whose crowns are worn smooth by three thousand years of steam.

I am the living margin where the iron meets the rock. 

When my work at Sluice Three is done, I will walk thirty paces to the hollow brick wall of Drum-House Twelve. I will climb into my spider-silk hammock, suspended three inches above a warm exhaust duct. I will press my broad, flat feet against the exposed cast-iron structural tie that runs through the heart of the masonry.

As I drift into sleep, my amber eye-membranes closed against the white-gold steam, the city-engine will continue its ancient, self-sustaining song. The Walking Beam will rise; the Flywheel will turn; the warm brine will weep down the granite canyons.

And through the soles of my feet, even in my dreams, I will feel the heart of the world beat once every twenty minutes, keeping me whole, keeping me deaf, keeping me anchored in the permanent, beautiful warm noise of the High Basin.
