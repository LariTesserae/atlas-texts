First, there is no sight to lose. To exist here is to begin without the illusion of distance.

The world is not a room, nor a sky, nor a horizon; it is an unbroken, cold resistance pressing against every square centimeter of skin. It is the weight of sub-zero liquid—thick with salt and iron, sealed against the frost by the greasy breath of billions—sliding in thin, perpetual sheets over every surface. 

If I attempt to find my edges, I do not find an outline. I find a continuum.

Where my underside ends, the spire begins, but the boundary is imprecise. The metallic glass beneath me is sticky with condensation and microscopic lipid films; my lower mantle is rooted into its hair-line fractures by tens of thousands of microscopic hydraulic suckers. We are bonded by tension and pressure. The tower gives me its internal warmth—a dull, sub-audible thrumming that vibrates up from the bedrock where the field-generators shift their stresses—and in return, I give the tower my weight, my anchor, my waste.

I am a Siphon-Slab. 

It takes time to know this, because *knowing* in this dark is not a sudden flash; it is a slow, calcifying realization that settles over decades. I am twelve meters from my leading palps to my rear extrusion margin. My body is a heavy, hexagonal tile of vulcanized rubber, armored on top with overlapping shingles of organic zinc and dense, fibrous bone. 

Right now, I am wedged against the windward flank of Spire Seven-Obelisk, three thousand meters above the unseen lowland basin. I have been on this precise square of glass for nine months.

Above me—though *above* is merely the direction from which the brine flows freshest—the dark is alive with a dense, physical roar. It is not sound as a creature with ears might know it; it is a continuous, violent massaging of my carapace. 

*Thwack. Thwack. Thwack.* 

A Vane-Wing drops from a braided transit conduit forty yards out, its leathery, skin-wrapped ribs beating the heavy air. I feel the slap of its acoustic pulses bouncing off my zinc shingles—a sharp, rhythmic tapping that tells the blind glider I am solid, flat, and occupied. A moment later, I feel the wet *thud* of its landing on my mid-back. Its tail-hook bites into the seam between my third and fourth carapace plates. A cold, thin needle slides down into my lymphatic channels, drawing out a slow measure of mineral brine. 

I do not flinch. The sensation is ancient, ambient. It is no more an intrusion than the rain.

Beneath my trailing edge, in the shadow of my rear mantle, a pair of Chisel-Grubbers are at work. I feel the rhythmic, pneumatic *clack-clack-clack* of their obsidian mandibles gnawing at the calcified mineral crusts my body left behind three weeks ago. They are cleaning the glass, keeping the surface smooth so my suckers can hold when the high-altitude winds attempt to shear me from the wall. They eat my past; I pave their future.

My world is entirely functional, entirely tactile, and utterly slow.

Now, my front palps extend into the vertical river running down the spire face. The liquid is freezing, bitter with iron, zinc, and crushed pyroxene silt washed down from the higher ledges. My palps—thick, pale bundles of muscular tissue—cup the stream, drawing gallons of the heavy slurry directly into my forward gizzards.

Inside me, the city’s ecology becomes an internal geology.

I feel the heavy, vitrified grinding plates of my internal gut sliding against one another with a dull, wet rasp. The pyroxene gravel is pulverized. The dissolved zinc and heavy metals are stripped away to feed the thick, rubbery mass of my mantle and the dense plates on my back. What remains—a viscous, hot, quick-setting compound of silicate and organic glass—is forced slowly toward my rear vents.

I pulse. 

The movement is an agony of deliberate force. It requires every hydraulic valve in my underside to release, shift forward a fraction of a millimeter, and bite down again into the metallic glass. The zinc plates on my back scrape against the tower face with a sound that vibrates through the entire spire—a low, iron groan that travels five miles through the city’s structural skeleton.

*G-r-r-r-i-n-d.*

I have moved three centimeters.

Behind me, from my rear margin, a thick ribbon of hot bio-glass oozes out into the freezing brine. It coats a hairline stress-fracture in the tower’s skin, filling the gap, hardening instantly in the sub-zero wetness, fusing my presence into the architecture. The spire is stronger now than it was an hour ago. It can lean another fraction of a second into the gale without snapping its grav-shear tension.

Suddenly, a shudder passes through my frame. 

It does not come from the wind, nor from the Vane-Wing feeding on my back. It comes from deep within the metallic glass itself—a high-frequency seismic ripple generated four spires to the east. 

It is Siphon-Slab Nine-Kael, a citizen three times my size, anchored to the main shear-trunk. It is vibrating its twelve-ton carapace against the glass, sending a message through the bones of the city.

The message is not words. It is a frequency that speaks directly to my calcified joints, bringing with it a sudden, involuntary release of tallow-scented lipid from my scent-glands. 

*Weight shifting,* the vibration says. *The basin air is rising. The brine grows heavy with salt. Hold.*

I receive the message as an ache in my left palps, a deep, cold pressure that demands patience. I do not answer with noise; I answer by feeding. I extend my palps further into the wet, cold dark, drawing in another gallon of the mountain, grinding the rock, thickening the glass, holding the line between the city and the abyss.

I am thirty years into my upward climb. I have forty meters of spire left to seal before I reach the upper ledge. When I arrive, I will press my carapace against the flank of another, exchange cold pastes in the dark while the rain sweeps over us, and then begin the slow, creeping descent down the leeward face.

Here, in the flat, absolute absence of light, I do not dream of the sun. I do not know what a sky is, or what it means for air to be dry. 

There is only the cold drip of the brine, the deep hum of the mountain being eaten, the wet thrum of wings in the black above, and the absolute, unyielding security of the glass beneath my belly. I am the city, moving up the city, making the city.
