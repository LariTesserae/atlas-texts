First, there is no edge to me. There is only the pressure.

It begins not as a thought, but as a frequency—a high, bone-rattling whistle that vibrates through the dark fluid in my core before I even register that I have a core. I am suspended in the blinding white scatter where light has lost its vector, dissolved into ninety degrees of wet, screaming air. 

If you searched for me from above, looking down through thirty meters of desert stone, you might think I was the stone itself. Or the fog. Or the water boiling back into the ceiling pores. But I am anchored lower, where the heat meets the metal-thought of the ancient world.

I am hanging beneath the arch. 

My body is a dark, wet tangle of flexible tubing, thick as a wrist, branching out across the pitted, bone-colored glass of the great conduit. I am the siphon-colony. I do not have a head, or eyes, or a heart that beats with its own red rhythm. My heart is eight feet wide, made of spun alloy and ancient glass, and it runs straight through the middle of my chest. Inside it, three inches beyond the reach of my rasping radula, the blue slurry pulses—*thrum-thrum, thrum-thrum*—a frantic, synthetic heartbeat born in a chamber five miles deeper in the crust.

I am the mouth that bites the machine.

For centuries, my microscopic teeth have kissed the glass. We do not destroy it; we groom it. We tap its skin, drawing out microscopic beads of the glowing, sapphire liquid. It tastes of copper, ozone, and cold lightning. It burns when it enters my tubes, a violent, chemical spark that keeps my tissues from melting in the ninety-degree mist. I am the converter. I take the forgotten reactor’s frantic energy and bleed it outward as a thick, warm, copper-scented slime that runs down the underside of the pipe in heavy, lazy droplets.

Through the slime, I feel the rest of the vault. I am its nervous system, wired directly into the glass spine that bridges the boiling pools.

When the piezocast flashes its steam-piston in the deep gutters below—*pop*—the shockwave travels up the basalt walls, rings through the glass arch, and tickles the hairs along my flexible stalks. I feel the sudden, tiny sharp deaths of the glass-shelled isopods as their carapaces crack. Seconds later, their pale, lard-like bodies are swept into the piezocast's gullet, and the nutrient flux of the pool shifts by a fraction of a milligram. 

Above me, near the matte-black needles, I feel the faint, rain-like patter of the spindle-jellies. When their brief lives end in the gravity-eddies, their calcified husks drift down through the fog, bouncing off my slick, dark tubing like grains of sand before falling into the terraced basins to become rimstone, to become the armor of the litho-crawlers, to become the very stone that holds the boiling water.

Nothing is lost. Everything passes through the arch, or under it, or feeds on what drips from it.

The golden cyanobacteria cling to my flanks like wet velvet, drinking the sapphire heat I exude. The isopods climb over my roots, their calcified mandibles scraping the microbes from my skin with a rhythm that feels like a low, endless itch. I do not swat them away. They keep my pores clear. They allow the glass to breathe its blue light into me.

I am completely blind, yet the chamber is blindingly bright inside my flesh. The sourceless white light above and the liquid blue glare within crash together inside my transparent walls, casting no shadows, only a violent, endless illumination that turns my interior organs into glowing threads of amber and cobalt.

I do not know what built the glass pipe. I do not know why the three black needles whistle their grave-song into the ceiling, or who set the blue slurry to pump its uneven, frantic pulse. I know only the taste of the alloy, the weight of the scalding mist, and the constant, violent balance of a world that refuses to die.

If the reactor deep below were to fail—if the blue pulse were to stutter and go gray—my tubes would harden into brittle slag. The velvet moss on my flanks would turn black and peel away. The isopods would starve; the crawlers would dissolve in their own acid; the piezocast’s steam-piston would freeze, cold and silent, in the dark gutter.

But the pipe is warm. The pulse is strong. It pushes against my teeth, frantic and eternal. 

I tighten my grip on the ancient glass. I drink the sapphire spark. I drip my warm, copper oil into the roaring fog, and the crucible turns again.
