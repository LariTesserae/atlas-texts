You would find me in the deep shade beneath a column-condenser, where the un-eroded granite has been ground into a circular basin over millennia of silent, hydraulic drip. 

Above me, the black trunk of the condenser rises sixty feet into the pale void, its ribbed cooling fins clicking faintly as they radiate excess heat into the bone-dry sky. But down here, at the lip of the pool, the heat is tempered, held in a precise, golden equilibrium.

I am not on the stone. I am on the surface of the oil.

I am a Basin-Lens—eight feet across, paper-thin, a disc of laminated mica bound in a heavy ring of amber-dark wax. I float by surface tension alone. Beneath me, thirty feet of clear, non-volatile hydrocarbon sits so utterly without ripple or cloud that the galena-veined floor of the basin looks close enough to touch, as if held behind a thick sheet of polished glass.

I have no eyes, but I am entirely made of light.

My upper surface is etched with thousands of concentric copper filaments, microscopic as web-silk, catching the perpetual 10:30 AM sun. I do not feel warmth as a sensation; I register it as resistance. Where the sun strikes my outer rings, a micro-volt current stirs through the copper, generating a faint, heatless thrum that keeps the oil directly under my mica skin at an exact, frictionless viscosity. I am a heater made of leaf-stone; I am a governor of liquid balance.

Down through the thirty feet of cold, heavy oil, my lower body extends. Long, fibrous tendrils of pale paraffin—dense and flexible as gut-string—hang straight down from my underside, anchored to the dark granite floor of the pool. 

Through these tendrils, I drink without drinking. I am the siphon and the filter. The deep stone beneath the basin breathes trace sulfur and heavy mineral waxes into the floor of the pool; my tendrils draw those heavy compounds upward through capillary action, pulling them slowly, millimeter by millimeter, into my floating body. The sun bakes them at my rim, solidifying them into fresh, golden wax. I grow outward in concentric rings, decade by decade, expanding toward the stone edges of my pool.

I am not alone in the basin, but we do not touch. Three other Lenses ride this pool with me. We communicate in the only language the oil permits: surface tension.

When a tall column-condenser above us sheds a single, heavy drop of refined lubricant, it hits the oil near the pool's center. The liquid does not splash—it is too viscous, too pure for splash. It merges with a low, hydraulic *thrum*. 

The displacement sends a shockwave across the surface. It travels toward me at three inches per second—a invisible wall of lateral pressure that pushes against my wax rim. 

I feel my neighbor, ten feet away, shift its copper filaments in response. It heats its left quadrant by a fraction of a degree, thinning the oil beneath it, sliding smoothly sideways across the pool to balance the dynamic load. I flex my own rim, adjusting my surface area, sending a subtle, silent tug back through the oil skin: *Position held. Thermal gradient stable.*

We drift in this endless, silent geometry, floating between the hot, unmoving sky and the cold, dark granite below.

And sometimes, deep down at the very tips of my paraffin tendrils—where they root into the fracture lines of the basin floor—I feel the rest of the world at work.

I feel the violent, beautiful *CLACK* of a Click-Gall half a mile away, its mechanical shock traveling down the bellows-fern's root, through forty meters of solid slate, and up through the oil like a clean, brass needle tapping the bottom of my disk. 

I feel the slow, pebble-scatter rattle of the Solder-Spindles as they ratchet along the copper seams above, creeping three feet a day toward the edge of the shear-plane.

And below even that, deep in the dark galena faults where no light reaches, I feel the massive, silent pulse of the Lattice-Mind—a low-voltage current shifting through the bedrock, measuring the strain of the plateau, holding thirty miles of slate six thousand meters above the lower world without a single grain of stone falling.

I do not wish to move. I do not wait for dusk, because dusk will never come. The light remains at its perfect, mid-morning angle; the oil remains clear; my copper filaments hum their endless, zero-load tune. 

I am a floating eye of stone and wax, keeping the deep waters liquid, perfectly still, perfectly balanced, in a world that has forgotten how to break.
