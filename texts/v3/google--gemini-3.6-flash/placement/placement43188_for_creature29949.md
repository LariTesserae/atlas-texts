I would not be above the surface, watching. To stand on the lip of that fissure would be to look down into a screaming mouth of blue glass and iron, and I am not a creature built for the thin, dry stillness of the upper air.

I am down in the throat.

At first, there is no form—only the sensation of total, unyielding compression. The water does not feel like wetness; it feels like solid, ice-cold marble sliding against itself at terrifying speed. There is no dark here, not in the upper twenty yards, but the light does not warm anything. It comes down in hard, blinding spikes of white and turquoise, driving through the water like dropped spears.

I am six feet of dense, pale cartilage and vulcanized rubber. I have no eyes to be blinded by those sun-spears, no lungs to crush, no delicate air-filled sacs to burst when the vent below blows its stack. My world is entirely acoustic and electrical. 

To me, the razor-sharp basalt walls are not black; they are a deafening, ringing grid of high-frequency song. Where the violent current tears over a edge of pure pale quartz, the rock shrieks in a crystal C-sharp. Where the basalt splits into jagged, fresh fractures, it hums a low, metallic murmur. My spade-shaped head—dense as oak, capped with thousands of tiny, nerve-rich pores—reads every millimeter of this stone throat through the way the water snaps against it. I know where every blade of glass-rock sits because the water *screams* before it touches it.

I am moving upward. 

Not by waving a tail—a tail would be shredded against these obsidian corners—but through the rhythmic, thunderous pulsing of the siphon valves along my flanks. *Draw, lock, expel.* I shoot through a white-water vortex that would tear a scaled fish into ribbons, my slick, lard-coated skin deflecting the turbulence. I am heavy. I am dense. I am built like a living hydro-ram.

Ahead, the acoustic map changes. The razor edges give way to a strange, flat resonance. 

It is a weed seam. The golden needles hang there, shivering at a million vibrations per second, piping the harsh mountain sunlight down into their calcified roots. I don't stop—I can’t stop in this churn—but as I sling myself past the rock face, I drive the heavy bone-plate of my jaw against a cluster of the bulbs. 

*CRACK.* 

The sound vibrates through my entire spine. The calcified shells shatter, releasing a thick, warm, sugary sludge of bacterial sap and dissolved iron. I vacuum it up in the same fraction of a second, the taste of trapped sun and bitter volcanic sulfur flooding my gut, before the hydraulic shear flings me violently across the chimney.

My flank strikes a sharp obsidian spire with a heavy, wooden *thud*. It doesn't cut me; it massages the thick, milky cuticle of my skin, tearing away old parasites and dead gel, sloughing them off into the blue void. I bounce off the glass blade like a rubber mallet hitting an anvil, already re-orienting.

And then, I feel the Hum.

It is not the song of the rock. It is higher, tighter, tenser. It is the sinew. 

High up, near the lip where the mountain meets the sky, the human rigging vibrates. The braided animal gut, soaked in copper and iron minerals, is stretched taut as a catapult line. To my electro-receptors, the sinew is a glowing wire of static, and the tiny fluorite barnacles encrusting it are flashing in rhythm with the water’s roar—conversion of raw motion into cold, violet light.

And hanging below it, sweeping through the white blades of sunlight, is the Miracle.

In a universe where every single thing—every rock, every crystal, every weed-blade—is a knife, there is *one* thing that is not a knife. 

The basalt sphere. Smooth. Hand-chiseled. Rounded by a species that lives outside the water, brought here and dropped into the storm.

I feel the surge coming before it arrives. Deep at the floor of the rift, forty feet down in the icy black, the giant iron organ-pipe sponges have finished their long, silent work. They have choked the thermal vents. The mountain’s blood has nowhere to go. Pressure builds beneath the stony valves until—*BOOM.*

The floor blows. 

A wall of superheated, iron-blackened water blasts upward through the chimney. The icy blue column instantly violently folds into a boiling, chaotic emulsion of heat and cold. The acoustic map goes wild—the quartz edges howl, the glass weeds vibrate until they ring like struck bell metal.

The thermal pulse hits the basalt sphere and sends it swinging in a massive, heavy arc through the sunlit turquoise sky-throat.

I fire my siphons. Three rapid, violent pulses. I shoot out of the turbid depths, tracking the arc of the smooth stone. 

My body loops around it. 

I do not possess hands, but I have length, muscle, and friction. I whip my pale, slick form twice around the sweeping basalt ball, binding myself to the human artifact. For three glorious, bone-rattling seconds, the heavy sphere carries me *against* the terrifying momentum of the updraft. The smooth stone presses hard into my side, massaging the stiff cartilage around my siphon valves, cracking away the calcified crusts that form in the cold.

Above us, the braided gut line groans, its fluorite barnacles flaring into blinding purple embers as the kinetic strain hits its peak. The sunlight pierces the churning froth, scattering brilliant, shifting caustics across my milky, scaleless skin. I am locked to a stone carved by a hand I will never see, riding an engine powered by a fire deep within the earth.

At the apex of the swing, just as the gravity of the pendulum peters out and the icy current prepares to rip us apart, I uncoil. 

I let the momentum sling me backward, upside down, headfirst into the plunging, cold down-draft. I slide back down the turquoise throat, past the glowing needle-weeds, past the shrieking quartz blades, sinking back into the dark, silent blackness of the bottom vents to wait for the iron pipes to seal again.
