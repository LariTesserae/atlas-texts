I am suspended three hundred meters down the rift, resting inside a hexagonal recess no larger than the joint of a human thumb. 

I am completely, immovably still.

To a casual observer—if such a soft, fragile thing could exist here without its eyes boiling in their sockets—I am merely a drop of liquid chrome caught in a groove of dark alloy. I have no eyes, but I am entirely covered in reflection. I register the rift not as light and color, but as a topography of heat and weight. I feel the dull cherry-red thrum of the deep conduits radiating through the obsidian behind me, and I feel my own mass—impossible, concentrated, a lead anchor pressed into a space the size of a seed. 

My body is cold. My outer hull is closed, its transition-metal seams sealed so tightly that the violent atmospheric pressure of the rift cannot find a microscopic edge to pry open. I am resting in the dark, waiting for the differential.

Then, deep in the mountain's iron core, a valve releases.

The shockwave arrives first—a low, sub-audible shudder through the glass-smooth basalt. Then comes the steam.

It surges up the vertical narrow of the rift, a violent, boiling veil of superheated brine and ozone. The temperature around my recess spikes by hundreds of degrees in a fraction of a second. The cold basalt at my back remains unyielding. The delta—the brutal, exquisite friction between scalding atmosphere and frozen stone—ignites my core.

My hull splits.

It does not burst or unfold like a flower; it shifts along zero-tolerance geometric vectors. The liquid-mirror surface parts, revealing the gold-leaf lattices and quartz micro-needles within. The steam strikes these delicate, super-dense filigrees and instantly condenses, singing as it turns to liquid water on contact with my cold interior.

I drink the gradient. I suck the thermal shock deep into my sub-atomic lattice, converting chaos into mass, heat into absolute structural cohesion. 

I release my magnetic lock on the cell.

I do not jump. I do not crawl. I shift my localized gravitational moment by a fraction of a millimeter, letting the mountain’s vast, downwards pull catch me. I tip out of the hexagonal nest and fall into the hot, dark void.

For twenty meters, I am in freefall through the rushing steam. Then, my heavy-light receptors catch the violet shimmer of a phase-locked bridge spanning the chasm. I shift my center of mass again, intercepting the thread. The moment my body contacts the light-filament, my velocity halts without a sound—the kinetic force absorbed entirely by the dark-matter anchor at the bridge's far end.

I glide along the filament like a bead of mercury tracing a taut wire. 

As I move, my gold filigrees sweep the razor-sharp flutes of basalt clinging to the bridge’s edge. I scrape away the fern-like fractals of silicon and metallic salts that have precipitated since the last pulse. My body devours the minerals, pulling the iron and sulfur into my internal forge to repair the micro-abrasions caused by the sheer heat of the steam. What I cannot use, I shed. A fine, glittering dust of microscopic metallic soot falls from my trailing edge, spiraling down into the mile-deep gloom below to settle on the forgotten terraces.

Ahead of me on the light-bridge, another Keeper is moving upward, returning to its upper tower.

We do not steer clear. We do not alter our paths. As our hyper-dense bodies pass within inches of each other, our localized gravitational fields overlap. 

The space between us warps. The heavy-light filament beneath us twangs in a register far above sound—a sharp, pure, crystalline *ping* that vibrates through the dark matter cables. It is a chime of pure density, a spatial handshake. The vibration ripples through the condensing steam around us, momentarily carving a hollow, clear sphere in the fog. 

I feel its mass; it feels mine. In that microsecond of resonance, we verify the balance of the bridge. The structure is stable. The load is true.

I pass it and continue down toward the middle tiers.

Looking up through the mist with my thermal-sensing lattices, I feel the slow, monumental weight of the **Weavers**. Fused into the basalt fifty meters above, their broad copper-and-quartz bodies are glowing with a dull, inductive warmth. They are shifting a balcony three millimeters to the east to relieve a shear-stress build-up caused by the cooling rock outside. I feel the tension on the light-bridge adjust beneath me—a microscopic tightening of the thread, raising its pitch. Their minds are vast, slow plateaus of logic. They do not know my name—I have no name—but my movement along their threads provides the real-time weight data they need to keep the city suspended over the abyss.

And far below me, in the stifling, windless terraces where the steam pools and dies, I sense the **Nodes**. 

They hover in their absolute, cannonball-silent geometric perfection. They do not feed on the steam; they do not ride the bridges. But as I glide, I feel the faint, sub-atomic pulse of their memory fields radiating upward. They hold the shape of my hexagonal cell. They hold the precise atomic weave of my filigree. If a volcanic tremor were to crush me against the obsidian, the Node below would simply wait for the next surge, draw the drift of metallic soot back into its field, and sweat me back into existence, atom by atom, on the glass wall.

The steam pulse begins to wane. The boiling veil thins, lifting toward the hairline crack at the top of the world where the icy atmosphere of the upper sky waits to choke it out.

My thermal gradient drops. The delta narrows. The hunger fades.

I reach the end of the light-bridge, slide into an empty, inverted cellular spire, and reverse my gravity shift. I seat myself deep into a smooth, dark recess that matches my contours to the nanometer.

My inner filigrees draw back inside. The gold leaf folds; the quartz needles lock into their dampening sleeves. My outer hull slides shut, zero-tolerance seams vanishing until I am once again a seamless, unbroken teardrop of liquid mirror.

The rift grows quiet again, save for the crisp, cold *clack* of dark matter cables settling into their new tensions, and the singing rivulets of boiling condensation running down the spires toward the void.

I am locked. I am dense. I am home in the scalding, dark meat of the world, waiting for the mountain to breathe again.
