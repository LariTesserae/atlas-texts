I am not in one of the sliding iron boxes. Those are for the family units and the dry-lathes, the places where the tallow-wafers are cut and the slate tablets are scored.

Find me instead in the **Three-Inch Clearance**. 

I am wedged sideways between the unweathered basalt of the south wall and the greasy, cold-rolled steel of the Tier-12 gantry rail. My left coat-sleeve—stiff glass-cloth, heavy with tallow and iron filings—is pressed flat against the rock, which is so cold it feels almost wet, though I know there is not a drop of moisture on its sharp, glassy face. My right shoulder lightly brushes the outer housing of a sliding residential block as it glides past on its overhead wormscrew, descending at a crawl.

*Clack-clack-clack-shhh.*

I do not turn my head. There is no room to turn it. Instead, my broad, lidless eyes look straight ahead down the length of the fissure. My horizontal membrane flicks sideways—*rasp*—wiping a dusting of crushed graphite from my pale violet irises. Through the slate-blue twilight that sifts down from the knife-edge sky, I can see eighty paces of suspended iron, crisscrossed by black cable looms that shiver in the draft.

I am an **Anchor-Tuner**. 

My skeleton—dense, dark cartilage braided with trace iron—is long and flat enough that this spatial ribbon between the city and the mountain is not a squeeze, but a home. My lead-soled boots are wedged into a shallow ledge in the rock, and through them, I am listening to the mountain’s stress.

The Silicate Needles are young and arrogant. They do not settle gently; they expand and contract with the fierce cold, pinching the slit or trying to throw off the drive-screws that bite forty inches deep into their marrow. If a screw expands too quickly in the cold, it will shear off its brass collar, and an entire gantry—carrying forty residential modules and six hundred yards of pneumatic conduit—will swing out into the lower void like a broken gate.

I carry my bronze pick in my left hand. Its handle is wrapped in dried pewter-mouse hide for grip. 

A shadow drops silently from the darkness above. A paper-winged glider, its pale, blind head twitching in the thin air, hooks its three dry claws into the shoulder strap of my coat. Its papery chest heaves with tiny, dry *pfft-pfft* breaths. Between its needle-beak, it holds a cold-hammered brass drive-peg, marked with two notched grooves.

*A slack peg from Tier-4.*

I do not speak. I raise my six-jointed right hand, take the peg from its beak, and gently tap the glider’s under-jaw with my fingernail. It understands. It unhooks its claws, leans back into the thin air, and drops ten feet into the slot before catching an updraft and sweeping toward the cooler upper gantries.

I slide two inches down the fissure, moving like a shadow through the iron gap. 

A pewter-mouse is perched on the head of the heavy basalt bolt beside my hip. Its pewter-colored carapace is dusted white with stone powders. As I approach, it does not move away; it extends its felted palps and begins raspy-cleaning the wax-coat on my wrist, consuming the micro-flakes of iron and graphite I have picked up from the rails. I leave it to its work. We are both maintenance.

I place the bronze pick against the head of the big anchor bolt that holds the Tier-12 main tension cable to the rock. I strike it twice.

*Tink. Tink.*

The vibration travels through the bronze, into my heavy hand, down through my iron-ribbed cartilage, and down into my lead boots. The pitch is too high. The mountain is pinching here; the basalt is squeezing the bolt’s throat.

I reach into my coat pouch, pull out a pinch of dry talc and a brass shimming-plate no thicker than a fingernail. With a swift, practiced pry of my bronze pick, I release the safety pawl on the bolt, slide the shim beneath the collar, and tap the lock-ring home.

*Click-click-tap.*

I strike the bolt again with the pick.

*Thrummm.*

The tone drops half an octave. A low, satisfying resonance runs down through the lead soles of my boots. Sixty feet above me, I feel the response through the catwalks: a distant, rhythmic shift as a three-story textile mill adjusts its weight, sliding smoothly into the newly balanced clearance without rubbing its flanks against the basalt.

My bellows-lungs expand with a quiet, dry *shhh-shhh*, drawing the dusty air through my face-plate. The graphite dust stings my air-passages with a familiar, metallic sweetness. I exhale, and a small, dry cloud of grey powder floats out into the twilight, settling onto the dark iron boxes passing below.

I do not look down into the void beneath Tier-18, where the filament lamps die out into blackness. I do not look up at the thin purple stripe of sky. There is no world out there that could hold a body like mine—a body made of cartilage and iron, sealed in wax, designed to fit into the three-inch margins where metal meets cold stone.

The city shifts around me. A residential unit unclamps with a soft *pfft* of its air-valves, rising slowly toward the upper warmth. I lean my back against the ancient, unweathered basalt, place my lead boot against the moving iron frame, and feel the whole eighty-pace web turning, clicking, and breathing its dry, cold song.

I am wedged where I belong. I hold my pick ready. I listen.
