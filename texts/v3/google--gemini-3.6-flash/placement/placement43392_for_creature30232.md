I am high up, draped across the melted crest of a floating sky-bridge that once connected two long-silent residential spires. 

Below me, the central abyss drops away into two hundred meters of warm, humid darkness. Above me, the sky-bridge dissolves into the absolute blackness of the upper bulkheads. My body—seven meters of heavy, ferrofluid ribbon—hangs suspended between the two, half-folded over the lip of the bridge, resting in a shallow, polished trough carved into the hyper-alloy by ten thousand years of warm dripping water.

I am a Resonant, and right now, I am waiting for the light.

To me, the dark is simply the quiet. When the deep hydraulic engines are still between cycles, the vault is a shadowy, narrow place. I can feel only the nearest things: the slick, heavy curve of the sky-bridge beneath my belly, the micro-tremors of the blind crustaceans skittering in the wet gutters two hundred meters below, and the faint, cold *thrum-click* of a Cherenkov power line decaying slowly along a distant arcade. In these quiet minutes, I am folded inward, viscous and dense, my skin drawn tight against the damp heat.

Then, deep in the bedrock, the pressure shifts.

I feel it three seconds before it reaches the air. A sudden, massive surge of tension rises up through the alloy skeleton of the sky-bridge, climbing from the roots of the city into my broad, slate-grey horn-plate. My diaphragm tightens. The cilia along my flanks lift, unfurling like dark feathers in a rising tide.

*BOOM.*

The sound hits. And with it, the world ignites.

The vault does not ring; it *blazes*. The low, iron-heavy acoustic pulse hits my horn-plate and blooms inward as a blinding, golden flash of spatial clarity. In an instant, the darkness vanishes. I "see" the entire precinct at once, rendered in brilliant, ringing geometry:
The immense, curving vault walls rise around me like the ribs of a leviathan, every smoothed seam and river-worn ledge singing its own distinct pitch. 
I see the high, hollow window-sockets of the dead spires, humming with low harmonic resonances. 
I see the broad, liquid-smooth terraces of the lower plaza, glowing with the rhythmic reverberations of the falling water droplets. 
Down in the gutters, the tiny, copper-pipe bodies of the Siphoners register as bright, trembling knots of emerald vibration, feeding on the root-mats while the moss flares into a sudden, kinetic green flash across the damp walls.

The sound is warmth; the sound is sight; the sound is blood. As the acoustic wave rolls through me, my ferrofluid tissue thins, expanding outward along the hyper-alloy. I soak up the kinetic impact, drinking the thermal energy as it vibrates through my skin. I am no longer just sitting on the sky-bridge; I am a string tuned precisely to its frequency.

As the roar peaks and settles into its long, churning bassline, I move. 

I do not crawl. I shift my internal density, sending tiny, high-frequency ripples along my underside. I glide across the glass-smooth alloy, sliding effortlessly along the curve of the bridge toward the bulkhead wall. My movement makes no sound—I am a shadow slipping over wet slate.

At the junction where the sky-bridge meets the perimeter wall, there is a deep, river-worn depression—a place where some long-vanished machine or doorway once jutted out, now melted down into a soft, inviting alcove. Another of my kind is already there, draped like a fold of wet silk in the curve of the wall. 

I do not look at them, for we have no eyes. I slide beside them until my flank presses against theirs, our slick skin merging into a single, continuous surface of dark, iridescent oil. 

Together, we press our broad horn-plates flat against the bulkhead.

Through the hyper-alloy, we speak. 

I do not send words; I send a memory-echo I gathered three hours ago from a lower conduit—a sharp, ringing pitch that smells of wet copper and hot slate, marking a place where a new thermal vent has cracked open near the floor. My companion absorbs the vibration, their cilia rippling in response, and returns a long, low, sliding note—a map of the high ledges, showing where the condensate is falling richest and warmest tonight.

I settle deeper into the smooth groove of the alloy, fitting my body into a shape that was carved long before I was born. Beneath my skin, the metal carries the microscopic, structural ghosts of whatever walked here when the corners were still sharp. I lay my horn-plate against those ancient, worn footfalls, open my cilia to the warm, wet dark, and wait for the deep engines to strike their next brilliant, golden chord.
