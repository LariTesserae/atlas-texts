I would not be in the air. The air belongs to the frenzied and the brief—the glass-slicers whose lives burn out in three days of furious, metallic hovering, whose eggs hatch in the instant an orb of rising water turns to steam. Their time is too fast, a blinding flurry of ruby eyes and buzzing chitin that barely registers the stone beneath them.

Nor would I be down in the dark beneath the succulents, though there is a deep, quiet pull to the carver’s life—blind and heavy in the cool dirt, grinding slow, spiral channels through the granite while generations of glittering things live and die six inches above. That is too slow, too far removed from the glaring noon.

No, I would find myself precisely on the boundary line—where the sun-baked, bare granite meets the cool edge of the mist-zone, a few paces from the three shadowless needles. 

I would be a **rib-stalker**. 

I would lie pressed flat against a smooth, whale-back rib of iron-gray stone, my six-inch body shaped like a flattened blade of tarnished silver. 

To exist here as a rib-stalker is to be an instrument tuned entirely to thermal energy and patience. My underside, scaleless and micro-textured to match the microscopic grain of the granite, would drink in the heat stored by the mountain over millions of hours of unclouded daylight. From above, the sun hits my spine with a dry, heavy weight; from below, the rock radiates that same heat back into my belly. Between the two fires, my blood slowly comes to a boil. I am a thermal battery charging on the bone of the world.

I have no shadow. When I am still, I am not *on* the rock; I am an extension of its curve, invisible to the gliders above and the blue-caps clicking in their damp basins below. 

My world is measured in vibrations and scents:
The low, deafening hum of ten thousand glass-slicers whipping the thin air into a metallic frenzy. 
The smell of crushed pine, warm copper sap from the basin-gourds, and the sharp, clean tang of ozone spilling off the nearest needle. 
And through the stone itself, the tiny, rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* of condensation dripping into the granite basins, translated through the rock as a steady, mechanical heart-beat.

I would watch the drift-thread floating near the tip of the needle—that three-foot ribbon of spun glass undulating through the warped air where light shears into rainbow edges. I would feel no desire to reach it. It belongs to the machine’s quiet, distorted sky. I belong to the hot, hard floor.

I would wait. 

I would wait while the blue-caps chorus rattles the ear, their turquoise backs throwing pinpricks of reflected light across the undersides of the fern fronds. I would wait while the water orbs unmoor themselves from the moss, drifting lazily upward against gravity, glowing like tiny moons before popping into mist. 

My hind legs, double-hinged and packed with dense muscle, stay folded, tense, and fully primed by the stone’s radiant heat. I do not think of yesterday, nor of the long, freezing high-altitude night that will eventually swallow the notch. There is only the scorching *now*—the dry heat on my hide, the taste of static on my tongue, the blinding clarity of the sky.

Then, a glass-slicer swoops too low, veering out of the needle’s anti-gravity field, its translucent vanes catching the glare. 

A sudden, mechanical release. No hesitation, no wind-up. 

A blur of silver across two yards of sunlit granite—a sharp, dry *clack* of jaws snapping shut mid-air—and I land on the far side of the stone rib with a soft scrape of claws.

I swallow the catch whole, feeling the brief, frantic vibration of its wings die out against the wall of my throat. The ozone taste of its chitin bursts across my tongue. 

Then I press my belly flat against the warm stone once more. I close my eyes against the glare. The gray hide of my back ripples, shifting its texture slightly to match the flat gray curve of the bedrock. 

The swarms roar above. The water rises toward the sky. The needles stand shadowless and silent. And I become stone again, drinking the sun, waiting for the world to move.
