I am not standing on the plaza. To have feet here would be a strange, fragile mistake—two tiny points of pressure trying to negotiate a world that only understands surfaces, currents, and weight.

I am where the grand avenue pitches upward into the black. I am the **climb itself**.

Specifically, I find myself spread across the western face of a massive, gently leaning obelisk—sixty feet high, fluted like the throat of a sunken whale, leaning three degrees south toward a shallow, steaming basin.

I am a Seep. But not a single, isolated drop; I am three hundred gallons of dense, lipid-heavy brine, thick as warmed oil, clinging to the soft-melted channels of the dark stone.

***

### I. The Margin

There is no "I" that sits behind eyes. My perimeter is a salt-gradient.

Where my upper edge reaches into the carved fluting, I am thin—a membrane only a few molecules deep, reaching upward by capillary hunger. I can feel the microscopic pores of the rock ahead of me: tiny, thirsty voids filled with the bitter, coppery waste of the Pore-Skin. 

Below that leading edge, I thicken. My body is a heavy, slow-pulsing mass of warm, iridescent dark, held together by a stubborn surface tension that refuses to let me spill into the void. Gravity pulls at my center, trying to drag three hundred gallons down into the plaza lake, but the stone’s fluting holds me. The stone is my shore, my bone, my only direction.

I do not see the obelisk. I *contain* its geometry. I know its three-degree lean because the pressure on my southern margin is three fractions of a pascal heavier than on my north. I know the stone is old because its grain has no sharp corners—every micro-fissure has been licked smooth by ten thousand years of my ancestors’ bellies.

Ahead of me, the Pore-Skin lies asleep in the grooves. It feels like a cool, rubbery grease, dense with trapped sea-salts. As my leading edge rolls over it, the Skin twitches. A tiny, sulfurous spark—cold, blue, and voiceless—discharges beneath me, a micro-nerve flashing in the dark.

I drink the spark. I drink the copper. I dissolve the Skin’s waste into my lipids, stripping the stone down to its bare, wet, black basalt. Behind my trailing edge, the rock is left naked, gleaming, and pure for three seconds before the deep-dwelling filaments of the Pore-Skin reach back out from the pores to reclaim it.

We do not fight. It is a slow, wet respiration: the Skin eats the stone, I eat the Skin, and the stone eats us both when we turn to dust at the top of the world.

***

### II. The Thrum

Halfway up the shaft, the air grows hotter. A vent in the plaza below has just opened, sending a thick, silent plume of boiling mist rolling up the face of the monument.

My lipids react instantly. My surface tension slackens slightly; I expand, spreading five feet wider across the fluting, drinking the thermal energy until my interior temperature matches the fever-heat of the air.

And then I feel the tremor.

It is not sound. Sound is too thin for this place. It is a mass-displacement in the stone—a heavy, rhythmic dragging six feet to my east.

Another Seep is descending the rear face, or perhaps climbing parallel to me. It is older than I am, denser, laden with a heavy load of dissolved silica harvested from the northern colonnades. I feel its weight through the obelisk’s skin: a thousand gallons of cold-fat brine, sliding upward in heavy, measured pulses.

We drift toward the same central channel.

When our outer membranes touch, there is no shock, no collision. There is only a sudden, profound collapse of borders. A low, wet *thrum* vibrates through the basalt as our surface tensions melt together.

For a hundred heartbeats of the earth, we are a single body of twelve hundred gallons. My salt-gradient rushes into its silica-reserve; its heat balances against my copper. Chemical signals—records of a cooling vent three plazas over, news of a Drapery that has torn and dropped forty feet of living fringe into a lower basin—crest across our combined surface like dark, shimmering oil.

We do not converse. We balance.

When the exchange is complete, the geometry of the fluting divides us again. The larger mass pulls eastward, heavy with silica, heading for the stress-side of the obelisk where the stone is weakest. I remain on the western flank, lighter now, moving faster, drawn up toward the apex by the draft of the dome above.

***

### III. The Summit

The top of the obelisk is a flat, dish-shaped crown, worn hollow by the rain of centuries.

When my leading edge breaks over the rim, the stone ceases to be vertical. The capillary pull changes. I am no longer fighting gravity; I am filling a basin.

I spill into the crown, gathering in a deep, trembling pool of hot mercury-dark. Here, forty feet above the plaza, the air is so saturated with steam that the line between liquid and sky disappears completely.

Floating three inches above my surface is a Lens—a purple-glowing sphere the size of a chest. It sways in the heat-rise, its thin lipid skin holding a quiet, violet phosphorescence that casts no shadows, only a bruised sheen across my pool. I can feel the tiny, anaerobic lives inside it, dying as their gas runs thin.

The Lens drifts too low. Its bottom edge kisses my surface.

*Snap.*

The sphere dissolves softly. A warm, fever-hot splash washes over me, seeding my brine with fresh organic nitrogen and a sudden, sharp burst of heat. The purple light dies in an instant, absorbed into my dark.

I am full. I am heavy with salts, copper, silica, and the spent gas of the air. I can climb no further.

My surface begins to steam.

Not in a hurry, but in a slow, relentless unraveling, my water leaves me. Molecule by molecule, the hot rain I gathered at the base of the city detaches from my lipids and rises into the ink-black ceiling above, ascending toward the Great Draperies hanging in the gloom of the higher vault.

I am becoming the air.

My heavy salts remain behind. They settle into the dish of the stone, a thick, white-grey paste of silicate and calcium. Over the next hundred years, as the hot rain washes over this crown, those salts will dissolve again, trickling down the *inside* of the hollow obelisk, petrifying as they go, thickening the stone from within, reinforcing the lean, ensuring that when the city sways, it does not fall.

My water is gone now, lost to the high mist. My lipids draw tight, shrinking into a concentrated, dormant gel in the hollow of the crown, waiting for the next surge of steam, the next condensation-flood, the next breath of the earth to give me volume again.

Down below, a heavy drop falls from a lintel. *Plink.*
The Pore-Skin sparks blue in the dark.
The city sighs its warm, wet breath.

And I am everywhere in it—in the climb, in the rain, in the stone that remembers how to hold us all.
