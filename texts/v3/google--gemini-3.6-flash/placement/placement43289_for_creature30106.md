I would not be a visitor standing on the ramp. There is no dry floor for shoes, no air cool enough for lungs that remember a landscape with a horizon. To exist here is to be pressed into service immediately—to be woven, smeared, or drunk.

If I listen to the damp hum long enough, I do not find myself in the heavy, suet-backed body of the builder, nor in the pale, popping stems of the celery-vines. I am too light for the one, and too transient for the other.

I find myself in the **vibration on the tallow pane**. 

I am the star-shaped pattern that forms in the hot, greasy drop hanging from the hoop of rattan, just before it loses its hold and falls.

***

It is a strange, suspended way to be. To my back is the fat-plug, stretched thin as parchment. Through its translucent, yellowed grease comes the blinding gray glare of the altitude—an light so bright and cold it feels like a blade held against the skin. Out there, the wind doesn't blow; it shears. It strips the air down to ice and empty space, whistling against the curve of the bell with a high, thin note that would shatter glass if there were glass to shatter. I feel that cold against my outer edge—a freezing, crystal terror that wants to turn me to a bead of frost and snap my spine.

But to my front is the kettle. 

The heat rises in thick, sour waves from the creature’s blowholes, smelling of bruised turnips and wet livestock. It hits the tallow pane, melting the inner skin of fat just enough to give me a foot-hold. I catch the moisture out of the air—the boiled vapor, the sweat of the terracotta, the fine spray of crushed vine-sap—and I condense. I gather. 

I am suspended precisely where the ice meets the boiling broth. 

Because the exterior wind hums through the notch of the spire at a single, unchanging pitch, the fat-plug vibrates. And because the fat vibrates, *I* vibrate. I cannot remain a simple, round drop. The harmonic frequency reaches into my wet body and pulls me into geometry. I tremble into a six-pointed star, then a twelve-pointed rosette, my tiny liquid arms shivering against the surface tension, holding the shape for three heartbeats, four, five—

Then the weight of the cell’s steam becomes too great. My edges blur. I snap free from the rattan hoop and fall through the rust-colored twilight.

***

I do not fall far, because space here is a folded ribbon. 

I land with a soft *pitt* on the broad, convex plate of grey horn—the creature’s faceless brow—just as it presses itself against a fresh patch of orange mud. 

For a fraction of a second, I am the medium between the beast and its home. I am squeezed flat, thinner than a leaf of gold, trapped between the smooth river-pebble of its forehead and the damp, thumb-grooved terracotta. In that instant, I feel what the creature feels:
The deep, rhythmic *thump-thump* of the ash-wood beams beneath the plaster;
The high, taut singing of the gut-string binding the skeleton;
The exact thickness of the shell—four inches of mud here, three and a half inches there, a micro-fissure forming near the third bone peg where the frost outside is trying to bite through.

I feel the creature’s heavy, indifferent intent. It does not hate the cold outside; it does not love the warmth inside. It is simply an engine that refuses to freeze.

Then the head lifts. The creature sways forward on its spatulate trowel-hands, moving down the spiraling ramp, and I slide down its suet-slick temple, trailing across the thick, pale fold of its neck. The smell of alkaline grease is overwhelming now—a heavy, suffocating comfort. 

I roll off its shoulder and drop into the throat of a clay pot.

***

Down here, on the ramp that goes nowhere, the light is different. The gray glare from the fat-plugs doesn't reach the roots. There is only the low, cold-blue luminescence of the liverwort. 

I sink into the black, unbaked silt of the pot. I am swallowed by the blue stem. The liverwort drinks me greedily, pulling me up through its delicate, translucent veins. Here, inside the stem, the roar of the cell is muffled. The wind outside sounds like a distant saw; the popping of the celery-vines above sounds like slow fire in wet brush. 

I stay in the liverwort for an hour, or three, or a hundred—time is hard to measure when the temperature never drops and the mud never sets. I am drawn upward toward the light, pushed out through the pore of a leaf as a tiny bead of blue-glowing sweat.

A blind white snail finds me. 

Its radula—a raspy, microscopic tongue of horn—scrapes the leaf, taking the sweat and the leaf-skin and the fine terracotta dust that settles on everything. I am inside the snail now, moving slowly across the ceiling-which-is-also-the-floor. 

The snail does not care about the void. It moves over the orange thumbprints with a rhythmic, muscular sliding, leaving a track of liquid glass behind it. It eats the rough edges off the creature's work. Where the creature slaps down heavy, coarse mud, the snail follows, polishing the grooves until the wall is as smooth as the inside of an mouth. I am part of that polish. I am the slide. I am the wet ribbon that lets the blind shell glide across the vault without losing its footing.

***

Eventually, the heat turns me to mist again. 

The creature passes beneath us, its blowholes spouting their ninety-degree vapor with a deep, wet *whuff*. The blast of sour air lifts me off the snail's trail, atomizes me, and carries me back up into the high, rust-colored dome. 

I rise through the hot drizzle, tumbling past the knotting celery-vines. I hear their tiny, wet *pops* as their stems swell with sap. I brush against a bone peg, catching a faint, metallic taste of fresh marrow before the warm air currents hurl me back toward the wall.

And there is the hoop of dried rattan. There is the plug of stretched tallow. 

I land on the yellow fat. The cold blade of the outer sky reaches through the pane to touch me. The steam from the floor rushes up to embrace me. The wind outside strikes its note against the spire—a deep, harmonic whistle that shivers through the fat, through the tallow, into my tiny, gathered body.

I pull myself together. Surface tension pulls my edges tight. 

I begin to vibrate. 

Six points. Twelve points. A trembling, star-shaped geometry suspended over the boiling dark. I am not the builder who drives the bone, nor the vine that binds the wood, nor the snail that smooths the clay. I am the measure of the tension. I am the proof that the inside is still hot, that the outside is still cold, and that the thin, smeared wall between them has held for one more drop.
