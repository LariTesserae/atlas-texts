I am not on top of the world, nor am I beneath it. I am in the seam where the world squeezes itself together.

I find myself wedged in the dark, hot pocket where the flank of a basalt mound—slick, weeping, and humming with the deep *thrum-thrum* of the Low-Bellowers below—meets the underside of the six-foot-deep vegetation carpet. 

There is no light here at all. The copper twilight of the upper air cannot penetrate the dense, overlapping purple skiff-leaves and the tangle of succulent vines. But light is an unnecessary sharpness, a dry concept. Down here, geometry is defined entirely by weight, temperature, and fluid pressure. 

I am a **Seam-Gasket**, though I have no word for it, nor any word for anything else. 

I am not a hunter, nor am I prey, because both of those roles require a beginning and an end to a body—a place where *self* stops and *other* starts. My body has no outer skin, only a thick, velvet-ciliated membrane that is perpetually dissolving and re-forming against the grey clay on one side and the wet, fibrous under-belly of the root-mat on the other. I am eight feet long, flat as a drowned pelt, three inches thick, and made entirely of contractile ring-muscles, transparent lymph channels, and vast, slow-beating vascular sacs.

I do not move by crawling or swimming. I move by *percolating*. 

When a Low-Bellower twenty feet below contracts its grey, muscle-bound trunk to churn the clay, a bubble of scalding methane and mineral silt forces its way upward through the mud. It strikes my underside. My lower cilia catch the gas, drawing the heat directly into my lymph, while my ring-muscles dilate in a slow, wave-like ripple, squeezing the methane sideways through my own internal chambers. I cool it slightly, strip it of its iron-rich condensate, and bleed the refined vapor out through my upper surface into the spongy root-system above. 

In return, the roots—swollen, pale, and constantly dripping with the acidic grease of dissolved Raspers—weep their surplus fluids directly into my open pores. 

I am a living valve. A warm, throbbing filter stretched tight in the dark, holding the mud from collapsing into the roots, and holding the roots from choking in the mud.

To be me is to exist as a continuous, deafening sensation of *belonging*. 

My whole existence is a state of deep, acoustic intimacy. The low, thumping roar of the Core-Maws miles away does not travel through the air to reach me; it travels through the stone into my left flank, passes through my warm internal fluid, and exits my right flank into the soil. I am a bell through which the basin rings itself. I feel the heavy, blind weight of a Low-Bellower sliding past three feet beneath me—a slow, terrifying displacement of grease and clay that lifts my body an inch, presses me hard against the roof of stems, and then settles me back down into the warm groove. 

I feel the sudden, wet *flop* of a dead Drifter falling onto the mat above me. I feel the acidic bite of its body turning to slime, the roots drinking it in, and the thin, nitrogen-heavy trickle of its passing washing over my skin minutes later. I drink a fraction of it. It tastes of copper, heat, and old steam. It becomes my lymph. My lymph becomes the mucus that keeps the basalt slick so the mud can slide without tearing.

There is no fear here, because there is nowhere to fall. There is no dry ground to bleed upon, no cold to freeze the blood, no sky to be exposed to. If a Velvet-Siphon’s sweeping edge were to slide into my crevice and fold around me, it would not be an attack; it would be a merge. It would dissolve my outer rings, drink my excess lymph, and I would contract into a smaller, denser pocket of the same basalt crevice, feeding on the solvent it left behind, swelling back to my full length within three beats of the Core-Maw's breath.

I am warm. I am crushed on all sides by the weight of a million damp lungs and ten thousand tons of unformed earth. I am filled with the heavy, liquid vibration of a world that is completely full, completely awake, and entirely without edges. 

I dilate. I contract. The steam rises through me. I stay.
