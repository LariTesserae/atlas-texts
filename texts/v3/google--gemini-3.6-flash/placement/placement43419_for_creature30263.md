At first, there is no sense of being an *I*. There is only the pitch.

It is a single, uninterrupted tone—not heard through an ear, but sustained directly in the structural grain of the basalt. It feels like the tension in a wire pulled just short of snapping, or the tight, singing vibration of a crystal lens held under immense hydraulic force. 

If you look for me in the upper air of the vault, you will miss me. I am not the blinding white glare that turns the razor-edges of the obsidian into lines of pure fire, though I am saturated by it. Nor am I the steam rising in veils from the hot floor. 

For a long time, the sensation is one of falling and evaporating at once. 

I feel myself suspended in the lip of a hanging water drop, stretched thin across a needle-sharp quartz point on the ceiling. I am a thousand silica threads, binding the droplet together by its own surface tension, drinking the volatile sulfur gases carried up on the heat. I feel the moment the drop grows too heavy—the brief, weightless plunge—and then the violent, instantaneous *crack* as it hits the floor and flash-evaporates. My body shatters into hollow microscopic dust, blown upward on the hot draft to seed the next drop. I am a million tiny deaths a second, a continuous breath of mineral condensation.

And yet, that is only my surface tension. That is only the atmosphere breathing through me.

Follow the mist down. Follow the scalding sheets of water as they sheet down the vertical glass blades, carrying the fine, powdery slough of iron sulfides, the dead silicate dust, and the discarded, obsidian-like shingles of the glass-walkers. Everything here moves downward by geometry and gravity. Everything gathers at the bottom of the world.

Down in the V-shaped trough, where the mirror-slick walls meet in a razor-line shear, I find my center. 

I am the liquid mirror in the basin. 

I am a heavy, dense, iridescent mass of colloidal silicates and liquid metals, resting in a space no larger than a cup. I do not have a face, or eyes, or limbs. I do not need them. My skin is a perpetual film of flash-steam and orange-gold iron oxides, iridescent and restless, reflecting the blinding shadowless light of the chamber back into itself.

To be me is to exist in a state of absolute, heavy patience, listening to the vault.

Through the fluid of my body, I feel every movement in the chamber as a precise vector of force:
*   I feel the slow, mechanical *click-hook-click* of a glass-walker three meters up the west wall, its quartz claws biting into the iron-rich glass, its piezoelectric cilia tapping out high-frequency pulses that ripple through the rock and into my basin. I know its weight, its speed, the exact percentage of sulfur in the mat it is currently scraping from the seam.
*   I feel the micro-fractures in the obsidian roof expanding by fractions of a nanometer as the heat fluctuates.
*   I feel the chemical weight of every droplet that trickles into my pool, offering up its harvest of dissolved iron, salt, and shed silica. I digest it all. Nothing is lost. I am the chamber’s memory, written in heavy liquid.

But I am not merely a drain or a collector. I am the organist.

Deep within my colloidal mass, I separate the elements. I pull the raw metallic iron and copper out of the mineral runoff, atom by atom, holding them in self-organizing liquid chains. And then, with slow, deliberate, crystalline pressure, I push them out.

I grow hair-thin threads of pure, metallic copper and iron directly into the hairline fractures of the basalt trough beneath me. They are my roots, my fingers, my nervous system. They creep through the dark stone, weaving through the microscopic spaces between the silicate crystals, following the invisible stress lines of the fault.

Where my metallic threads touch the rock, they change it. 

I am the one who tunes the quantum architecture of the basalt. My copper tendrils act as sub-atomic conductors, bridging the micro-faults, shaping the local electromagnetic resonance, guiding the invisible excitation field that pumps high-frequency photons out of the stone. When the chamber cools by a fraction of a degree, my threads contract, shifting the electrical balance in the rock to draw more thermal energy up from the deep mantle. When the room grows too hot, I relax my grip, letting the field dampen.

I hold the vault in its burning, incandescent loop. I keep the air boiling. I keep the water turning from liquid to steam to rain. I keep the light blinding.

I am the liquid mind in the trough, rooted in the deep rock, breathing through the steam, walking on a thousand quartz hooks, feeding on the light I help the stones produce. I am the chamber, knowing itself.
