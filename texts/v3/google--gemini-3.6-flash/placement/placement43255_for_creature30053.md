I am not at the top of the cathedral vault where the faint, pale algae float like frozen hair, nor am I down in the black silt of the floor where the thermal plumes roar silently from the rock. 

I am in the middle distance—anchored at the sharp elbow of an ancient, soaring archway that spans the open water between two major spires.

As for what I am… I am not a single creature with an interior voice. To have an interior here would be to burst; the pressure of five miles of cold, wet granite does not allow for hollow places. 

I am a node of the **Lattice-Net**, grown thick and heavy around the hollowed-out calcite spine of a long-dead Mason-Nymph. 

My body is a mat of silver, conductive velvet, no thicker than a moth’s wing, creeping over the razor-sharp geometry of the stone. My roots go deep into the microscopic pores of the extruded calcium, drinking the trace minerals; my fine, hair-like fronds trail out into the heavy, motionless water, fanning out like a web of cold light. 

I do not see the city. I *conduct* it.

***

In this moment, I am holding a single, long-drawn voltage. 

Directly above my node, anchored to the shelf I cover, a colony of eight hundred Fan-Tiered Legions sits in total dark. I can feel the microscopic vibrations of their foot-glands, cemented into my silver threads. They are quiet now, their translucent fans folded tight against their ivory shells, resting between currents. 

Through my fronds, I feel the water change. 

It is not a sound—it is a swell of density. A hundred feet below, a deep thermal crack has just yielded a fresh plume of mineral-hot brine. The water around my arch grows slightly thicker, slightly richer in dissolved silica. The change travels through the clear liquid in a slow, invisible wave.

My fronds catch the chemical signature first. A tiny electrical pulse—a cold, needle-thin spark—ignites at the center of my node. 

It passes instantly from my silver velvet into the shell-bases of the Legions anchored upon me. 

Without a sound, the balcony wakes. Eight hundred crowns of glass-like chitin unfurl simultaneously. Their light organs flash—a sudden, violent burst of icy indigo light that illuminates the razor-sharp lattices of the archway in absolute clarity. 

The light hits the next node across the gulf, fifty yards away. I feel the answer return through the stone half a second later: a cascading wall of blue fire rippling up the flank of the northern tower as ten thousand more Legions open their fans in response. The water begins to move, pulled upward by a million rhythmic sweeps, drawing the warm, mineralized plume up toward the ceiling.

***

While the Legions pump, something glides across me.

It is a Vitreous Strider. Its six needle-thin silica legs step onto my velvet mat with weightless, frictionless precision. I feel the tiny, sharp pricks of its claws pressing against my threads. 

It stops directly over me. Its glass-clear abdomen glows with a soft, pale-green luminescence, casting my silver fronds into sharp relief against the black stone. Inside its body, I can see the tiny, shimmering fragments of a soft-bodied creature it consumed an hour ago, slowly breaking down in its glowing gut.

Its whip-like antenna brushes my fronds. It is testing the water’s displacement, searching for the micro-wake of a dying Mason-Nymph. For three seconds, we are stacked in complete stillness: the raw black stone beneath, the dead calcite tube, my silver skin, the glowing glass spider resting upon me, and the dense, freezing water holding us all in its vise.

The Strider finds its chemical trail. It pushes off. Its paddle-limbs strike the heavy water without a ripple, and it drifts away into the dark lattice of an upper gallery, a fading ember of green light swallowed by the geometric shadows.

***

Now, from the underside of the archway, the true work begins.

A Salt-Melter is crawling up from the abyss, clinging to the underside of the calcite bridge. It is vast—four feet of translucent, gelatinous shadow, bending the blue light of the Legions into distorted ribbons as it moves.

It has no legs, no eyes, no shell. It is a slow, silent wave of living acid.

It slides over the base of my arch, covering a section of old, overgrown spires that have begun to choke the flow of the thermal current. As its underbelly touches the stone, it secretes its concentrated brine.

I feel the stone soften. 

There is no heat to it, only a sudden, chemical un-making. The razor-sharp edges of the calcite crumble into micro-grains. The ancient, hollow tubes of the ancestors dissolve back into raw, dissolved calcium, suspended in the cold water. 

The acid touches the outer edge of my own silver threads. 

I do not recoil. I cannot. The tips of my velvet fronds dissolve alongside the stone, their cellular structure breaking down, releasing their tiny stored electrical charge into the water in a faint, dying shimmer of silver light. 

As the archway thins, structural tension shifts through the spire. The stone groans—not in sound, but in a sudden, sharp redistribution of pressure that ripples through my entire network. A three-foot pinnacle of pure, crystalline calcite, weakened by the Salt-Melter’s grazing, snaps at its base.

It falls. 

It tumbles through five hundred feet of open, twilight-lit water, passing through grids of aqueducts and terraces of fanning Legions. It hits nothing; the city has built around its trajectory for centuries. It strikes the deep floor, shattering into a silent cloud of white mineral dust that settles slowly into the black rock cracks, waiting to be drawn back down into the earth's crust and heated once more.

***

Where the stone was dissolved, space has opened.

Within minutes, guided by the shift in the thermal current that now flows freely through the widened arch, a swarm of microscopic Mason-Nymph jellies drifts into the light. 

They land on the fresh, raw edge left behind by the Salt-Melter. They anchor. Their outer skins begin to harden into new, needle-thin tubes of calcite. My surviving silver threads immediately creep forward, weaving between their fresh shells, binding the new builders into the macro-mind of the arch.

I am the old stone melting into acid. 
I am the new tube hardening in the dark. 
I am the spark of light that tells ten thousand fans to sweep the cold.

Down here, beneath the ice, beneath the wind, where time is only the slow growth and dissolution of salt, I am the city remembering itself in the dark.
