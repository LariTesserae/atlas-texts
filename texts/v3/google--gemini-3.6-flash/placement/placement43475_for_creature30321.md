I am not in the open water, where to exist is to be hurled across miles of bone-pale ceramic in a heartbeat. Nor am I hidden deep within the dark, choked plumbing of the lower vents, where the water is thick and slow as cold oil. 

Find me on the **lee side of a grand, thinned structural pillar**, right at the precipice where a high plaza breaks and falls into a plunging sky-well.

The pillar was once square, perhaps four thousand years ago, but the ocean has ground it into a teardrop of polished, vitrified ivory. On its upstream face, the current strikes with the crushing force of a subterranean landslide, splitting into two howling sheets of high-velocity brine. But directly behind it—in the narrow, sheltered wedge of dead water created by its aerodynamic silhouette—there is a slip of absolute stillness.

That slip of shadow is where I live. I am a **Lee-Graft**.

***

### What I Am

I do not have a head, nor eyes, nor fins in the traditional sense. If you were to peel me from the ceramic, I would look like a heavy, ribboned length of black wet leather, fifteen feet long, tapering to a needle point, anchored at my thickest end by a massive, disc-shaped foot of dark, cold muscle.

My entire underside is a single, continuous vacuum-graft. I do not grip the smooth, glass-like composite with claws—there is nothing for a claw to hold. Instead, my foot secretes a clear, dense, non-wetting lipid that fills every sub-microscopic pit in the eroded ivory. The sheer pressure of the ocean above—hundreds of feet of heavy, freezing water—forces my tissue so flush against the floor that the liquid beneath me is entirely excluded. To pull me straight off the surface would require pulling a column of the ocean itself. I am glued to the world by gravity and hydrostatics.

My back, however, is not flat like the Velvet Sheets. My upper hide is formed of overlapping, articulating plates of dense, charcoal-grey enamel, curved like the hull of a racing boat. 

I am designed to complete the architecture. 

When I anchor myself to the tail-end of a worn pillar, my body extends its hydrodynamic curve, smoothing out the turbulence that would otherwise form behind it. I make the ancient pillar longer, sleeker, more perfect in the current's eye. In exchange, the pillar gives me the one thing this sterile, rushing world denies everything else: **a place to wait.**

***

### How I Sense

I am entirely blind, but the dark slate-blue twilight holds no secrets from me. My senses are written into the flex of my skin and the bone-dense cartilage of my internal keel.

I feel the ocean as a map of pressure and hum. 

Inches from my flanks, the outer current roars past at terrifying speed—a cold, continuous wall of kinetic energy. I can feel the exact boundary where the fast water shears against my stagnant wedge of shelter. It feels like a hum, a high-frequency vibration that ripples down my enamel plates, shimmering through my hide like wind through a sail.

Through my vacuum-foot, locked hard against the ivory floor, I read the ground-sound. I feel the massive, subsonic thrum of the Archway Leviathans three miles downstream, their leathery diaphragms pumping a steady, low-pitched *thrum-thrum-thrum* through the bedrock. That sound tells me the density of the brine today; if the pitch drops, the water is growing colder and heavier, sinking down from the frozen surface.

I also feel the hunts.

When a Keel-Hound fires its hydraulic gill-jets half a mile away, the sudden spike in local displacement ripples through the floor tiles. When a school of Glass-Ribbons gets caught in a spinning eddy over the plaza, the micro-vortices slap against the side of my pillar like tiny, rhythmic hammers.

***

### How I Eat

I do not hunt, and I do not strain the open water. The open water moves too fast; to cast a net into the shear-stream would be to be ripped from my root.

Instead, I feed on the *mistakes* of the world.

As the great, glittering sheets of Glass-Ribbons attempt to navigate the shear-lines above the plaza, the violent down-drafts of the sky-well frequently tear them out of their formation. They lose their hydro-trim. They tumble. And when a creature tumbles out of the current behind my pillar, it falls directly into my quiet wedge of dead water.

They drop into my shelter like autumn leaves falling into a well. They are stunned, disoriented by the sudden loss of flow, floating helplessly in the zero-pressure zone.

That is when my back opens.

The enamel plates along my spine do not just protect me; they are hinged like the slatted blinds of a shutter. When a stunned Ribbon or a drifting cluster of organic snow floats into my pocket, I contract my internal hydraulic chambers, snapping my slatted plates wide. 

The movement creates a sudden, powerful negative-pressure void inside my body-cavity. The quiet water—and everything floating in it—is sucked violently into my slatted back with a muffled, wet *slurp*. My plates snap shut, locking the food inside my internal stomach-vaults, filtering the icy brine out through tiny, gill-like pores along my base.

Then, I settle back into the silence. Smooth. Sealed. Invisible to the hydro-dynamic chaos streaming just inches past my flank.

***

### My Place in the Long Cold

I have been attached to this specific pillar for forty years. I know the shape of its wear. I know that three inches above my attachment point, a hairline fracture in the ceramic hums with a faint, whistling hiss whenever the upwelling thermal plumes rise from the deep.

I will never see the sky, nor the bottom of the abyss below the sky-well. I do not know what built these sweeping causeways, these hollowed plazas, these ivory stairs that slide into nothingness. To me, they are not ruins. They are simply the shape that stone takes when it wants the water to move.

I am a piece of the city's second skin. I am a living wedge of shadow in a world of endless, roaring lightless motion. 

Above me, a Keel-Hound banks silently through the slate-blue gloom, its yellow lens catching the cold glow from above, its dark body cutting the current without a sound. Below me, the Leviathans sing their heavy, structural song into the vitrified floor. 

I close my back-slats, tighten my vacuum-hold on the melted bone beneath me, and feel the cold, heavy ocean rush past, furious and perfect, forever.
