I am at the high seam. 

Not in the absolute deeps where the Masons digest the foundation, nor down in the shaded avenues where the Chalk-Paws shuffle over gypsum dust. I am high up, on the windward curve of a twenty-story gallery tower, precisely where the baked terracotta wall arches out to meet the dark, heavy plates of a burgundy resin-column. 

If you ask what I am, I am a **Sun-Glazer**—though "I" is a strange word for an existence that has no interior voice, no memory of yesterday, and no concept of tomorrow. There is only the baking heat of the amber sky above, the intense dry warmth of the tile beneath my rim, and the steady, necessary work of the glass.

My world is not seen; it is focused. 

Across the wide, domed expanse of my porcelain carapace, ten thousand quartz lenses take the hard yellow light of the sun and funnel it inward, converting every photon into a clean, prickling heat that fills my internal chambers. I do not see shapes or horizons. I feel the continent as a map of thermal currents: the roaring heat of the upper obsidian ridges, the cooler blue-green shadows where the Lacquer-Clippers vent their wing-cases, and the deep, low thrum of the Masons below, radiating upward through twenty tiers of calcified silk.

At this moment, my mandibles are clamped around the edge of a fresh tectonic fracture—a blade of volcanic glass that thrusts up through the clay like a black tooth. 

*Crop. Snap. Grind.*

The sound does not travel through air to an ear; it vibrates through my skull plate and down into my secondary gizzard. The obsidian is brittle, razor-sharp, and utterly dry. Inside me, muscular walls lined with diamond-hard ridges crush the glass into a flour so fine it floats on the air. My lower glands pump, releasing a thick, amber-scented lipid—a oily, vulcanizing sap harvested from my own internal stores. 

Glass dust and lipid meet. They churn. They heat.

I step forward. *Click. Click. Click.* My twelve padded feet, tipped with microscopic rubbery discs, move in a slow, alternating tripod gait over the bare terracotta. Behind me, extruded from the low vent beneath my hindmost carapace, comes the glaze. It spills out as a hot, viscous ribbon, perfectly smoothing a micro-fracture in the tower’s face. Within forty seconds, under the unblinking sun, the liquid cures. It turns from honey-gold to a crystal-clear, vitreous shell that locks the clay into place forever.

I am polishing the world so the wind cannot tear it down.

Sometimes, far below, a herd of Chalk-Paws moves through an archway. I do not look at them, but I feel the micro-rhythm of their hooves—*thunk, thunk, thunk*—sending tiny shockwaves up the hollow silk-ribs of the tower. Higher up, a Lacquer-Clipper rustles through the bronze leaves, venting a puff of warm cedar dust that settles across my shell. I do not flinch. The dust is just material; if it falls near my mandibles, I will scrape it up and turn it into mortar.

Now, the wind changes tone. 

The low whistle passing through the high archways drops an octave, settling into a deep, hollow hum like a struck bronze bell. The amber sky near the distant horizon turns pale, bleached out by a rising wall of fine white silt. A continental dust-front is coming, sweeping across the raw chalk plain at fifty knots, carrying a million tons of pulverized lime and silica.

I do not flee. There is nowhere to go, and no need to hide.

I crawl three inches higher, finding the apex of the gallery’s outer rib. I align my broad, disk-shaped body to the vector of the wind. Then, I pull my twelve feet inward, tuck my mandibles into their reinforced facial slot, and press my rim flat against the glazed terracotta.

Glands along my outer margin flare, exuding a temporary, high-tack resin that seals the edge of my carapace to the wall. 

I become the wall.

The dust-front arrives not with a splash or a crash, but with a vast, dry *hiss* that fills the universe. Trillions of microscopic mineral needles strike my porcelain back, scouring the glass glaze I laid ten minutes ago, polishing my shell to a brighter, higher gleam. The wind roars over my curved disk, unable to find an edge, unable to lift a single scale. Beneath my belly, in the sealed, airless space between my body and the stone, the heat is constant, dark, and perfectly safe.

Inside my shell, the gizzard turns over its last charge of obsidian dust. The quartz lenses on my back glow red with the friction of the storm.

I am not a inhabitant of this city. I am its skin. I will remain anchored here for sixty years, or until my carapaces fuses permanently with the gallery wall, at which point the next generation will crawl over my back, scraping the glass from my ridges, and glazing their towers with what remains of me. 

The storm passes. The sky returns to amber. My resin seal snaps clean with a dry *pop*. 

I extend twelve feet. I unclamp my mandibles. Ahead of me lies an un-glazed seam of raw chalk, waiting for glass. 

I step forward. *Click.*
