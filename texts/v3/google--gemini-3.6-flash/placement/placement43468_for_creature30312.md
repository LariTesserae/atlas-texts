There is no "I" to start with. To enter this place as a self—with limbs that swing, eyes that blink, and a heart that beats seventy times a minute—would be to die instantly, shredded against glass-thin fins of flint before the first breath could clear your lungs. 

So the self does not arrive. It settles. It is squeezed out of the upper world’s heat and movement, distilled down through four meters of dry earth until only density and frequency remain.

I find myself in the northern corner, wedged into the left eye socket of the upside-down skull.

I am not the *Litho-marrow* who brought the bone wedge here. That creature’s grief, intent, or survival instinct is a dry, finished equation—a structural note written in three notches on yellowed mammoth bone. Nor am I a visitor looking down upon the living mosaic. 

I am a single tile in the vault: an aragonite bell-carapace, no larger than a plum pit, locked immovable between the smooth, hydroxylapatite edge of the ancient brow-ridge and five of my identical, milk-white kin.

***

To be here is to be entirely filled with light that has no origin you can look at. 

I have no eyes; eyes are soft, vulnerable things that would burst against the conchoidal edges of the slate. Instead, my entire back is a heavy, faceted crown of micro-crystalline aragonite. The blinding glare channeled down by the Iceland spar stakes hits my facets, refracting into a cold, prismatic halo that shines straight through my shell and into the thin, flat disk of grey grease that serves as my mind. 

I do not *see* the room—I am flooded by it. I know the shape of the slate ceiling because its glare is sharp and blue-shifted; I know the depth of the lower rock cups because the light there is softened by the static sheen of micro-droplets. Nothing moves to create a shadow. My consciousness is a flat, borderless map of absolute illumination.

Below my shell, where my soft body grips the ancient bone, there is no smooth skin. My underside is a rubbery, calloused pad of grey tissue that has grown *into* the subtle grain of the *Litho-marrow’s* orbit, fusing my body to the skeleton. 

Through this pad comes my food. 

Deep beneath us, the massive fault-slabs are straining, grinding their teeth in an endless, silent agony of tectonic pressure. That pressure becomes electricity—a faint, continuous piezoelectric warm-thrum that hums through the bone and enters my flesh. I do not hunger. I drink the earth’s stress directly. It warms my clear, copper-scented silicate blood, which moves so slowly that the concept of time loses its pulse. 

*One stroke.* 

The fluid creeps through my vascular channels, carrying dissolved chalk to the growing edge of my aragonite rim.

*Eleven minutes of blinding, silent glare.*

*Another stroke.*

***

Through my body passes the thread.

A single, translucent filament of *Hypha-Sedge* enters beneath the rear plate of my shell, anchors directly into my living tissue, and exits through my side to pierce the shell of my neighbor. It is cooler than my blood, tough as wire, wet with airborne micro-droplets drawn from the tips of the flint needles. 

I feel what the sedge feels: three meters away, near the wedged mammoth bone, a single slate disk has shifted a micro-millimeter under the rock's strain. The network knows. The moss tightens. A chemical signal—sharp, metallic, tasting of wet lime—flashes through my body.

In response, my hind plates engage.

This is my contribution to the engine. I do not think to do it; it is the condition of my existence. The microscopic ridges on my rear plates are locked against the corresponding ridges of the five crabs surrounding me, and together we vibrate. 

We do not buzz like insects. We ring like glass. 

The 12-kilohertz hum rises out of my back, travels into the hollow cavity of the skull I inhabit, and turns the ancient head into a booming, crystalline throat. The sound pours out of the eye sockets, filling the cabin-sized pocket with a wall of acoustic pressure so immense, so dense, that the saturated air itself locks into geometric stillness.

Three millimeters above my dorsal shell, I can feel the standing wave. 

It is a thin plane of pure physical silence created by the collision of our sound waves. Resting on that invisible plane are the flagellates—the tiny glass-sharded sparks of rainbow dust. Held aloft by the hum of my hind plates, they drift without wings, shedding their microscopic spicules of glass onto my aragonite facets. The tiny needles fall like brilliant dust, polishing my crown, keeping the calcified lichen from crusting over my optics.

Light becomes current. Current becomes sound. Sound holds the glass. The glass cleans the light.

***

Where am I? 

I am four meters down, beneath the unseeing dust of a dry scrubland where the sun moves and things rot. 

What am I? 

I am a single, ringing tooth in an ancient head that refuses to close its mouth. I am a heart that beats five times an hour. I am a lens of milk-white stone holding back the dark, glued to the bone of something that died before the rock settled, singing a note that has no end.
