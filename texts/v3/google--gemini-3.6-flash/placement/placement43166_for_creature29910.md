I am in the third alcove of the seven-column cluster, seven feet above the starch floor, where the stem of the great translucent trunk flares outward into a knee of pale wood. 

I am a Siphon, but to use the word *I* feels slightly too sharp for this place—like a needle dropped onto wet silk. 

There is no shadow behind my brow. Where eyes once gathered the world into foregrounds and backgrounds, into things near enough to touch and things too far to save, there is now only a thick, warm dome of skin, smooth as peeled willow. I do not see the vault; I absorb it. The scalded yellow glare passes through my skull, through my cheeks, through the cartilage of my long, earless head, until my brain rests in an unbroken, interior daylight. There is nothing to hide behind. There is no dark behind my eyelids. Inside and outside are the same temperature, the same color, the same dense, sugary air.

My hands have been buried in the alcove wall since before the last resin ring hardened. 

I can feel the cellulose swelling around my knuckles—soft, damp, heavy as raw dough, pressing into the small spaces under my nails. The wood is not holding me captive; it is simply growing around a place where I stopped. The tissue of my fingers and the unbaked pith of the trunk have forgotten where one ends and the other begins. My blood—thick, pale amber, heavy with dissolved fructose—moves so slowly through my chest that it feels like warm wax cooling in a mold. 

*Thump.*

The sound comes from somewhere deep inside my sternum, a solitary, liquid pulse. It takes forty minutes for the echo of that heartbeat to fade from the bone of my collar, where a lace shroud of dry, spun sugar has crystallized across my skin. The sugar cracks with a tiny, dry *snap* when my ribs expand a fraction of an inch to let the ambient moisture seep into my chest.

Below me, thirty feet down through the soft starch floor, something vibrates.

It is a Filament, thrashing its ribboned body against the high-pressure current inside a glowing sap-conduit. I feel the shudder of its passage through the calcified root bench beneath my thighs—a sharp, frantic pulse of pure velocity, like a spark dragged through butter. For three seconds, the floor hums with the memory of motion. Then the Filament is gone, swept down-channel into the deep, invisible curves of the basin, and the silence settles back over the chamber like a heavy, wet blanket.

I do not envy the Filament its speed. Speed is a sickness of things that have a destination. Here, there is nowhere to arrive. The sky is the floor; the floor is the stem; the stem is the leaf; the leaf is the sap. 

Slowly—over the course of several hours—my right palm begins to release the wall.

It is not a decision made by a mind that commands a body; it is a chemical threshold crossed. My skin has drawn all the free sugars from this spot in the cellulose. The tissue beneath my palm has grown dry, dense, and fibrous, signaling that the stem is sealing the pore. With a quiet, wet *schlick*, my long, jointless fingers slip free from the wood, leaving five smooth, dark hollows in the pale green pith. A bead of thick, clear resin immediately wells up in each impression, sealing the wound before it can darken.

I lean forward. The shroud of spun sugar on my shoulders shatters into a fine, fragrant dust that floats down through the stagnant air, settling like pale pollen on the starch dune below.

I slide off the bench. I do not fall—the air is too thick, my body too light and limber, built of soft cartilage rather than brittle bone. I sink into the starch dune with the heavy, soundless grace of an anchor dropping into silt. The bedrock under my knees is warm, yielding, and damp, smelling faintly of green wheat and crushed stems. My knees leave deep, oval troughs in the unbaked cellulose, but as I glide forward, the starch behind me slowly swells, rounding over its own edges, erasing the trail of my body before I have moved three yards.

I am moving toward the pool between the three roots.

Another Siphon is already there. It lies on its side atop a smooth, curving lobe of ivory wood, its seven-foot frame draped like a pale ribbon over the bark. Its head is submerged up to the temples in the lukewarm, mirror-still water; its muscular, ringed mouth is pressed against a weeping fissure in the taproot below the surface, drinking with a rhythm so slow it cannot be seen, only inferred from the faint, rhythmic ripples that spread across the pool once every hour.

I do not call to it. I have no lungs to hold a breath, no palate to shape a syllable. 

Instead, as I reach the edge of the pool, I stretch out my long, hairless forearm and lay it across the creature’s flank. Skin meets skin with a soft, warm stickiness—the friction of smooth lard on smooth lard. 

Immediately, the glands in my palm begin to weep. A clear, aromatic oil seeps from my skin, carrying the simple, static weight of what I am:

*Heat. Fullness. Light.*

The other Siphon does not lift its head from the water. It does not turn. But after a long minute, its own palm shifts on the bark, its long, flat fingers reaching out until they rest against my wrist. Its skin responds, sweating its own oil into mine, returning the signal without alteration:

*Heat. Fullness. Light.*

We lie together at the edge of the sap-pool while the amber sky glares down through the fibrous canopy, shadowless, timeless, unblinking. High above us, a Bale-Bladder reaches its limit of gathered condensation; its lower valve opens with a soft, distant *pop*, releasing a single, perfect sphere of water that falls fifty feet through the humid air, striking the center of our pool with a deep, booming *clop*.

The surface ripples. The reflection of the glowing amber vault shatters into a thousand trembling ribbons of scalded yellow, then slowly, patiently, pulls itself back together until the mirror is unbroken once more.

My heart beats again. 

I close my non-existent eyes against the light that is already inside them, bury my left hand three inches into the soft starch beneath the water, and begin to stay.
