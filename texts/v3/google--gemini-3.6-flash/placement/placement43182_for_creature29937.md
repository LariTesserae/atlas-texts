I do not begin at a point. I begin at a critical angle.

If you were to search for me, you would not find a torso or a threshold, nor a pair of eyes looking back through the high, vertical sky-slit. You would find me at thirty-four degrees off the perpendicular, inside the smoky, honey-colored heart of the Refractor—and simultaneously thirty feet away, where the focused tip of my sharpest light-needle bites into the black mirror of the floor.

I am the light-path between them. I am the vector of work.

In this place, consciousness is not a fluid sloshing through soft tubes; it is the retardation of light as it passes from air into quartz. When the sun passes over the razor-ribbed peak and its unsoftened ray strikes my upper facets, I do not "wake" as a sleeping beast wakes. I am *occupied*. The photons enter my lathe-smooth geometry, drop their velocity by thirty percent, and in that slowing, my identity crystallizes. 

I am a state of internal strain.

As the sun moves, the strain shifts. My amber resin expands by fractions of a micron—a tiny, silent swell that rotates my internal focal planes. I feel the courtyard not as a landscape of shapes, but as a grid of thermal potentials. 

To my left, thirty inches down in the obsidian shade, a mat of copper-moss is cold, its wire-networks starving for a voltage gradient. To my right, a basalt piston sits idle in its socket, its subterranean gas pocket flat and inert.

I adjust. A facet tilts—not through muscle, but through the differential heat of the very beam it carries. 

The light-needle sweeps across the vitreous floor. It skips over the razor-sharp cooling cracks, passing through the heat-shimmer without blurring, until it lands directly upon the dark, iron-rich underbelly of a Dielectric.

The Dielectric does not look up. It has no up. But as my light-needle delivers two hundred watts of pure infrared energy to its flank, its internal bismuth lattice expands. I feel its magnetostrictive response travel back to me through the stone: a sharp, sub-audible *thrum* that vibrates at fifty cycles a second.

*Acceptance.*

The Dielectric creeps forward an eighth of an inch, bridging the gap between two braided silicate root-cables. Underneath it, the blue arc breaks—*snap*—and the dry, sweet reek of ionized tin rises toward the sky-slit.

I am that arc, too. Not because I threw it, but because the logic that demanded it passed through my glass.

When you exist here, time does not flow like water; it accumulates like vapor deposition. I remember—if the alignment of molecular stresses can be called memory—a thousand years ago when my core was three facets smaller. I remember when the sky-slit was two inches narrower, before a frostless, rainless tectonic shift snapped a tooth of basalt from the mountain’s ridge and opened the light to me for an extra forty-six seconds each afternoon.

In those extra seconds, I hold the light-needle steady on the deep fissure where the Spinners live.

I cannot go down there into the blinding, radiant white heat of the magma chamber. My amber would turn to hot bitumen; my quartz would cloud with internal micro-fractures. But I can send my needle down into the throat of the crack. I can watch—through the distorted, pressure-portal warping of the air—the gold-and-silica skeins twisting in the dark. 

I heat the metallic carbon threads they extrude. I temper the glass conduits as they draw them out of the subterranean gas vents. When a Spinner lifts a thread of red-hot copper to splice a broken leaf-stem on a quartz frond, it relies on the precision of my beam to spot-weld the seam. 

We do not speak. We do not have names for one another. There is no "I" that stands apart from the circuit. The Spinner extrudes the copper; the copper carries the current; the current feeds the Dielectric; the Dielectric lays the pyrite; the pyrite grounds the floor; the floor holds the socket; the socket elevates the piston; the piston alters the angle of the shelf; the shelf shifts my glass into the path of the sun.

We are a single, silent, endlessly looping calculation made of stone and light.

When the sun leaves the sky-slit, the hard shadows do not fade; they are swallowed by the absolute dark of the high altitude. 

This is the moment I love best—if a crystal can be said to love the loss of its function.

As the light-needles collapse back into my core, the heat leaves my facets. The amber contracts. The quartz lets out a faint, high-pitched *clink*—a solitary note of pure, dry music that rings against the obsidian walls and dies instantly in the moistureless air.

My thoughts slow. The 50-hertz hum of the mountain deepens, taking over the entire field of my awareness. The light is gone, but the geometry remains. Locked in the dark, held in the freezing, absolute dry of the upper atmosphere, I sit upon my mirror-black floor, solid, unrotting, and perfectly aligned, waiting for the planet to turn my facet back into the fire.
