I would not be standing. To stand would require a floor made of something dead enough to bear weight without shifting, and it would require a body that insists on holding its head above its feet. Here, verticality is meaningless; there is only the difference between *compressed* and *unpressed*.

I would find myself in the center of the chamber, but not on it. I am wedged into the suffocating, squeaking overlap where forty layers of slate-heavy leaves press down from above and the floor’s wet, purple blisters push up from below. 

I am the **Central Bud**—or rather, I am the cold, liquid space inside it, waiting for the skin to split.

***

I do not have a face to receive the fog, but I feel it as a continuous, icy varnish setting across my outer membrane. The fog is my skin’s first boundary. Outside me, the black organo-salt rain falls—*clack, clack, clack*—striking my curved violet crown with the dull, metallic thud of iron dropping onto frozen lard. 

I do not hear the sound through ears. I hear it as a change in density. Every strike of a heavy drop sends a microscopic shockwave down through my pale-violet pulp, stirring the dense, non-Euclidean vein-arrays that are currently knotting themselves into my core.

I am where the room’s math converges.

To the crawlers, I am simply a surface that is not yet flat enough. I can feel six or seven of them on my flanks right now. Their thumb-sized, salt-quartz bodies are heavy as tire weights, grinding against my skin with rhythmic, mindless devotion. Their shovel-mandibles vibrate at twenty thousand hertz against my flesh, shaving away the misprinted, embryonic bumps where my tissue tried to grow too fast. It does not hurt; it feels like an intense, freezing numbness, followed immediately by the soothing glaze of the thick, resinous sealant they excrete over the fresh cuts. They are polishing me into a precise, impossible sphere. They are making sure my shell will not crack when the final pressure arrives.

Far below me, anchored into the black, braided conduits, the Valve-Cores heave. I feel their three-minute breath in my gut. *Thrum... thrum... thrum...* With every surge of their cask-sized hearts, dark, heavy sap is forced up through the floor around my base. The pressure pushes against my roots with such violence that my inner gel thickens, turning from liquid to a dense, glass-like state that draws all ambient warmth out of my core. 

That is why I am cold. Not because I am dying, but because I am thinking too hard. 

My leaves—the dense, three-dimensional arrays unfolding inside my dark skin—are processing the eight meters of permafrost above us. They are calculating the exact tonnage of the ice, the subtle shift of the tectonic shelf three miles south, the quantum decay of the heavy salts in the rain. Every calculation steals a fraction of a calorie from the air, leaving the fog at an unyielding, iron-hard zero. 

Overhead, a Pruner slides through the canopy. I feel its cold, ribboned length—a shadow made of fibrous bone and diamond-hard teeth—scraping across the slate leaves directly above my crown. It halts. It senses a logic loop in a stem three inches to my left—a stem that has begun to draw heat instead of shedding it. 

*Snip.*

The sound is sharp, clean, like a pair of shears cutting through frozen leather. The Pruner swallows the bad stem whole, its long body shivering as it breaks the faulty code down with chemical cold. A minute later, it glides down my side, slick and silent, and secretes a pale, re-processed paste directly into the bruised blister at my base. My roots drink it in. The recycled logic floods my lower veins, rewriting my code, smoothing out the error before I even knew I contained it.

***

What am I?

I am not a creature that will ever walk out of this chapel. If I hatch, I will not have eyes to see the dark, nor legs to climb through eight meters of solid ice into the thin, useless air of the surface. 

I am the chamber’s next iteration. 

The room around me is a draft, a rough sketch extruded into the dark hours ago. The vines are too thick; the crawlers are too crude; the slate-leaves are overlapping with too much squeaking friction. The Mind—the distributed, un-conscious hum of the sap and the salt—is using this entire eight-meter basin as a womb to compute *me*.

Inside my violet skin, under three inches of frost-cured pulp, my center is becoming hollow. Not empty, but *vacant*—a vacuum held at such high hydraulic tension that light itself would bend if it entered this dark. I am growing the organ that will allow the room to stop growing. When my skin finally ruptures with that heavy, wet *thrum*, I will not run away. I will become the new floor. I will expand outward at a millimeter a day, pushing the slate-leaves aside, crushing the old Valve-Cores into dark jelly, and extruding a harder, clearer, colder tissue that can bear the weight of eighty meters of ice instead of eight.

Until then, I suspend myself in the dark. 

I am the cold. I am the rain striking my own skull. I am the high, dry hissing of ten thousand mandibles making me smooth. I am eight meters beneath the world, blind, deaf, and heavy as iron, listening to the deep engine of my own slow, unstoppable assembly.
