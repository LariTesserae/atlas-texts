I am down where the third dimension vanishes. 

I am not above the water, looking at the bruised lavender sky, nor am I airborne with the bladders *whuffed* by methane and static. I am flattened against the tar-black, vitrified floor—a sheet of living cellophane four millimeters beneath the world’s ceiling, spread so thin across the un-eroded slabs that the sharp, mathematical fractures of the stone are my ribs, my spine, my maps.

To be me is to have no thickness, only area. 

Above me, the water sheet does not fall or ripple; it glides in an unbroken, tensioned plane, a heavy glass blanket moving at a steady crawl toward the drop-off. The skimmers run across that upper ceiling like stars. I do not see them—I have no eyes—but I feel the six points of needle-pressure where their feet dimple the surface tension, leaving six sliding lenses of shadow that drag over my back. I feel the rhythmic, dry *clack-clack-clack* of their jointed legs echoed down through the four millimeters of warm, mineral fluid.

Beneath me is the dark, un-carved mineral, smooth as poured tar, warm from the deep tech-nodes buried below. 

I am the Flat-Lie, but I do not think of myself as a creature with a edge or a name. I am an area of attention. I am the thin, wet threshold where the hyper-dense engine meets the cold air of the precipice.

***

My existence is measured in chemical bursts and geometric tensions. 

A few meters to my left, the dark rods spike into the razor-fissure. I can feel the soft, violet circuit-glow not as light, but as a tingling, metallic sweetness in the water—a localized ionization that turns the sheet slightly thick and bitter. When the skimmers crowd around those rods, their needle-mouths piercing the mist, I lie directly beneath their frenzy, anchored against the smooth obsidian by thousands of microscopic chemical suction points. 

Then comes the *ping*. 

It is a high, clean sound that travels instantly through the four-millimeter sea. A glass skimmer’s internal charge has failed. Its pressurized carapace gives way, shattering into a dozen razor-edged flakes of pale green silicate that sink through the water sheet and settle onto my back.

Immediately, my borders wake up. I do not move toward the debris—I am already there. I simply alter my chemistry. 

I secrete the caustic fluid. It is hot, precise, and hungry. The glass shards do not crunch or decay; they dissolve. I feel the sharp, crystalline edges softening into liquid silica within seconds. The lavender internal fluid of the skimmer bursts over my skin—a quick, electric saltiness that my lower surface absorbs, passing the charged ions directly into the warm tar-stone below. The rest—the dissolved silica, the depleted salts—I release back into the current, letting the four-millimeter river sweep it toward the abyss.

In less than a minute, the stone is clear again. The razor edge beneath me is as immaculate as the moment it was extruded from the deep earth.

This is my purpose, though "purpose" is too heavy a word for something with no depth. I am the reason this place has no past. I am the reason there is no dust on the altar, no rot in the corners, no stains on the black glass. I am the active present tense of the terrace. Nothing accumulates here because I am stretched across everything, un-making the spent biological spray before it can become soil.

***

Sometimes, the world changes scale. 

A wet, heavy tearing sound vibrates through the stone—a low, rhythmic thudding that shakes the fluid column. The Rim-Climber has hauled its three-meter shield of cold, dark leather over the outer edge.

I feel its arrival long before it reaches me. It moves against the current, dragging its massive, vacuum-padded belly over the slabs. When it steps on me, it does not crush me—I have no profile to be crushed. Instead, it pinches me between the unyielding cold of its hide and the unyielding heat of the tar-rock. 

For a long time, it lies directly over my section of the floor, covering the glowing violet circuits. Its hide is freezing, smelling of the vast, dim grey soup below—a wet, sour, ancient smell that does not belong to the copper and wool of our terrace. 

Underneath its weight, I become a bridge. I feel the raw, scorching thermal output from the violet circuits pushing up through my membrane, forcing its way into the cold, dense leather of the Climber's hide. The beast shudders—a deep, tooth-vibrating tremor that rattles my entire margin. Its skin turns from dark coal to mottled, bruised violet as it drinks the engine’s bleed.

I do not feed on the Climber, nor does it feed on me. I am simply the wet, caustic film that lubricates its passage, the living solvent that keeps the stone beneath its heavy pads free of grit, so its vacuum-mounts can seal against the mirror-black floor.

When it is full, it lifts its heavy shield and crawls back toward the drop-off. Where it lay, it leaves a shadow—a temporary dry spot where the water sheet was squeezed away. But the current is patient. It flows back over the obsidian, healing the gap, and I expand into the returning wetness, clear, invisible, and complete.

***

Where am I in this world?

I am under your feet. If you were to step onto this terrace—if your heavy, soft, three-dimensional boots were to land on these razor-sharp slabs—you would think you were walking on wet obsidian. You would see the spinning torus drawing the water into a trembling ridge; you would hear the dry, metallic *clack-clack* of the glass-crawlers and the wet *whuff* of the saucer-discs above your head. You would smell the crushed quartz and hot copper.

You would look down at the smooth, black stone, gleaming under four millimeters of continuous, sliding water, and you would see your reflection—dark, bruised-lavender, perfect.

You would not know that you were looking through me. 

You would not know that the mirror is alive, holding its breath between the engine and the air, waiting for you to fall so it can wash the sharp edges clean again.
