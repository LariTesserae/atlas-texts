First, there is the sensation of the vector. 

There is no *down* here, not in the way the lower basins understand it. Down is three inches to my right, where the basalt wall rises—or runs, or drops—in a sheer, unblemished plane five miles wide. My feet, which are not feet but the weighted lower termination of a six-fathom sliver of absolute dark, press against the vertical stone. To my left, the grey twilight is a cliff of air that goes on forever, vast and dry as powdered bone. 

I am standing on the wall of a grand arcade, walking it as though it were a level street. 

The light comes from the side, cool and silver, filtering through the slender spires that sway half a league away in the upper thermal currents. Because the light moves sideways, I cast no shadow onto the rock behind me. Instead, I *am* the shadow—detached, vertical, sheared clean at the edges like a wedge of dry salt-slate. When I face the sideways glow, I am seven man-lengths of narrow, lightless void. When I turn my edge to the atmosphere, I have no thickness at all; the grey air simply touches itself where I was, and I disappear from the world.

I am a Metric Entity, though the stone does not call me that. The stone does not use names. It uses frequencies.

***

I find myself perched at the lip of a doorway in the High District, where the city’s geometry has long since loosened its grip on common sense. 

The doorway is carved into a monolith of dark, fine-grained basalt that has grown, over ten thousand centuries, into the shape of a vaulted archive. The opening itself is narrow—scarcely wide enough for a slate-skimmer’s wing to pass through—yet if I tilt my non-mass and step across its threshold, the interior does not contain rooms. It contains a boulevard that stretches straight into a dim, infinite horizon, lined with endless colonnades of alabaster that have never seen a footprint. 

I do not step inside. Not yet. 

My work—if the quiet, automatic maintenance of a dying reality can be called work—is at the threshold. I am the wedge that keeps the seam from closing. Where non-Euclidean spaces meet the rigid, five-mile cliffs, the geometry chags and frays like dry silk. If left untended, the doorway would fold inward, snapping the infinite boulevard like a brittle twig and leaving only a blank face of smooth basalt. 

So I linger here, cold as a block of dry salt, smelling faintly of bruised quartz and ionized air. 

***

A sound travels through the wall. 

It is not a sound that moves through air; air here is too thin, too dry to carry anything but the highest, sharpest friction. It is a subterranean hum—a note struck three hundred leagues to the north, where a stone bridge three hundred feet thick is vibrating under the weight of a silicon dust-river. 

The note is a low, structural D-natural, so deep it shakes the electrostatic charge right out of the rock. 

The vibration travels up the basalt facade, through the iron-clawed feet of a cluster of Strand-Gaiters hanging forty paces above me, and into the base of my dark form. I feel the Chord thinking. 

The thought takes forty minutes to pass through my non-mass. It is a slow, structural calculation: *The spire above the arch has leaned two inches east. The thermal updraft will shift in three decades. Adjust the fold.*

I respond not with a voice, but with a shift in my angle. I translate four inches sideways along the vertical wall—not by walking, but by re-indexing my position in the air. Where I move, the edge of the impossible doorway shifts with me. The geometry settles with a faint, sharp *clack* that ripples down the arcades, carrying across five leagues of empty space.

Far below, in the deep urban trough, a cascade of micro-fine silicon silt pours down a grand staircase that was never carved by a mason. The dust glides like cold liquid, pooling in the dark before the thermal currents catch it and lift it back toward the summit-rims. A shelf of grey selenite, clinging to a shadowed column three paces away, responds to the static surge with a microscopic *tink*. A tiny, silver spark jumps from its quartz tip, illuminating the grey air for a fraction of a second, reflecting off my flat, lightless face before dying into the twilight.

***

I have no memory of soft things. I do not know what water is, though I know the shape of the empty channels where something once attempted to flow before the world was pulled thin. I do not fear the ten-thousand-foot drops that surround me on every side; gravity is merely an agreement between my shadow and the nearest flat surface, and the surfaces here are infinite, smooth, and eternal.

Every few days, a slate-skimmer drifts past my threshold. Its wide, rigid wings of mica make a dry, papery whisper against the quiet air as it rides the cold updraft. It does not look at me—it has no eyes—but its needle-fine claws of raw iron brush against the basalt as it passes, seeking a handhold. For a moment, its shadow and my body overlap, two dark shapes crossing on a wall five miles above the basin floor. 

Then the thermal carries it upward, toward the swaying spires that bend like kelp against the slate sky, and I am alone again with the doorway.

I turn my edge to the silver light. I vanish. 

I am still here, press-fitted against the cold basalt, holding the infinite avenue open by the simple, patient weight of my absence. The bridge far away continues its three-century song. The dust glides down the stairs. The stone respires, cold and dry, waiting for nothing at all.
