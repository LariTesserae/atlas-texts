At first, there is no *I*. There is only the pitch of the hold.

I am not in the open water where the Brassy Spindles chime, nor am I pressed against the polished iron-bark where the Lathe-Ticks scrape away the algae. I am at the exact, unmoving center of a four-column void—three miles above the petrified floor, two miles below the upper plant-canopy, suspended in a sphere of clear, honey-dense resin no larger than a house.

I am inside the heart-node of a **Drift-Marrow**.

More specifically, I am not even the gossamer threads that stretch for leagues across the illuminated void, anchoring me to the four titanic stalks of Grid B-7. I am the liquid chamber at the very core of the node, and the thousands of microscopic, mineralized statoliths—tiny, faceted grains of bio-precipitated magnetite and calcite—that float within it.

I am the world’s balance.

***

In the amber darkness of the inner chamber, daylight enters as a pale, golden bloom. It does not heat the fluid; the cold here is absolute and pristine, locked at the precise temperature where sap flows without thickening and bronze chitin does not expand. 

Outside my resin housing, the ocean is a vast, blinding hall of glass. I can feel the miles. I do not see them with eyes—I have none—but the three million cellulose lines radiating from my shell are my senses. They are pulled taut under thousands of pounds of static tension, strung across leagues of open water like the strings of an instrument that is never allowed to go out of tune.

*Thrum-clack.*

The shockwave hits. It arrives not through the water, but down the lines themselves.

Four miles to the north, Column B-7-1 drops its cathedral piston. Four miles to the east, Column B-7-2 snaps its check-valves shut. The wood of the giant trunks, five miles high and four hundred yards thick, flexes by less than the breadth of a leaf. 

Down the three million threads, that microscopic flex arrives as a tug. 

Inside my chamber, the fluid shifts. A thousand magnetite statoliths tumble half a micron to the left, brushing against the delicate, copper-sheathed cilia that line my inner walls. 

*Tick.*

The signal is read. The four columns are in phase. The stroke of B-7-1 matched the stroke of B-7-2 to within three ten-thousandths of a second. The pressure in the regional xylem is nominal. The sea is stable. 

The statoliths drift back to the center, suspended in the weightless amber. 

***

To exist here is to experience space as a pure, geometric chord.

I feel the Brassy Spindles when they shoot past, a league below me. They do not touch my anchor-lines, but their piezoelectric discharges leave thin, ringing trails of electrical tension in the brine—tiny *pings* that ripple across my lowest web like rain on a stretched drumhead. 

I feel the Lathe-Ticks on Column B-7-4. Their diamond chisels bite into the iron-bark with a high-frequency chatter: *shrr-shrr-clack*. It is a comfortable, domestic sound. It tells me that the intake grates on B-7-4 are smooth, that no turbulence is forming to drag on the piston’s intake.

I even feel the Marrow-Slugs, miles away inside the dark, wooden guts of the pumps. Their massive, rubbery bodies squelch against the valve-seats under three thousand pounds per square inch of pressure—a low, subsonic thud that vibrates through the very sap of the trees and climbs up the anchor-points into my resin heart.

Everything is accounted for. Everything is at rest within its motion.

***

I do not possess memory, for memory implies a past that was different from the present. 

There was no time before the *Thrum-clack*. There will be no time after it. My life is an eternal, four-dimensional calculation of tension, carried out in silence at the intersection of twelve leagues of gossamer thread.

Sometimes—perhaps once every three hundred years—the chord changes.

It is never a grand catastrophe; the machine does not allow grand catastrophes. It is a hesitation. A valve on Column B-7-3 holds for a fraction of a millisecond too long, hung up on a speck of mineral grit that escaped the intake strainers.

The line from B-7-3 slackens. Only by a hair’s breadth. Only by the thickness of a single cellulose fiber.

Instantly, the balance inside my core breaks. The amber fluid tilts. Ten thousand magnetite grains roll away from the center, cascading across the cilia on the eastern wall of my chamber.

I do not panic. I do not feel fear. Panic is an inefficiency; fear is a waste of chemical potential.

Instead, my core contracts. A tiny, hydraulic sphincter at the base of the eastern node snaps shut, compressing a tiny chamber filled with bioluminescent, hormone-dense sap. 

*Pft.*

A glowing, teal spore—no larger than a drop of rain—is injected directly into the slackened anchor-line. 

Driven by capillary action along the hollow core of the thread, the spore shoots across the sunlit void. It travels at fifty leagues an hour, a burning bead of blue-green light zipping through the clear, empty sea, passing straight through the middle of a passing school of Brassy Spindles without slowing.

Four miles away, the spore strikes the iron-bark of Column B-7-3. It enters an acoustic sensor-pore in the wood.

Within three cycles of the pump, the column responds. Bio-electric current flares through its copper-veined tendrils. The internal sap-ducts dilate. A burst of high-pressure latex flushes the valve-seat, sweeping the mineral grit into the exhaust plume. A cluster of Lathe-Ticks, alerted by the sudden shift in voltage, scuttles toward the valve with their shear-blades clicking in unison.

The line pulls tight again.

In my amber chamber, the statoliths roll back to the center. The fluid stills. The teal light in my core dims back to a faint, dormant ember.

The chord is restored.

*Thrum-clack.*

***

If you were to swim across the vast, sunlit avenues of this high sea—if you were a stranger brought here from some soft, chaotic world of dirt and wind—you might look up into the endless, crystal water and see my home.

You would see a amber sphere, suspended miles from anything solid, shining like a dropped jewel in the white daylight. You would see the gossamer lines radiating from it in every direction, glittering like silver spider-webs against the deep, plant-walled sky.

You might wonder if I am lonely, hanging here in the empty light between the giants.

You would not understand that I am not *in* the space between the columns; I *am* the columns. My nervous system is four square leagues of cold, pure ocean. The thunder of the pistons is my heartbeat; the chime of the ribbon-fish is the sensation of sunlight on my skin; the rhythmic, mechanical pulse of the world is the only thought I will ever need.

I am where the engine checks its own soul.

The light stays bright. The water stays cold. The statoliths float in their golden hive, waiting for the next four microns of motion.

*Thrum-clack.*

*Thrum-clack.*

Forever.
