I do not find myself standing on the wet flagstones, looking up through the amber mist. I am not a visitor here; there is no room for an outside eye in a place so entirely occupied by its own metabolism. 

If I am anywhere, I am in the *joints*. 

I am the precise, wet threshold where forty feet of pressing city earth meets the inward bulge of vitrified brick; where the cold, rusted iron of a tie-rod meets the warm, rubbery pad of tallow-moss; where the hot steam leaves the pipe and becomes a droplet on the dark frond of a fern.

I am the consciousness of the loop.

***

At first, I feel myself as a localized sensation: a wet, rhythmic compression. 

I am down between the teeth of the giant spur-gear. Once every hour, a tooth slowly rotates into place, and I am squeezed—hard, unrelenting, but not painful. It is the deep, satisfying pressure of a heavy limb being massaged. The tallow-moss around me yields, releasing its slick, amber-scented sap. The oil mixes with condensed boiler-water, squeezed through cellular walls, lubricating the iron so that it glides without a screech. The twelve-second hydraulic thrum from beneath the floorboards sends a shudder through my spine—*one, two, three...*—a low, sub-audible vibration that keeps my fluids from ever settling into stillness.

Then my awareness drifts, expanding along the paths of highest heat.

I am the yellow-amber vein of the Plasmodial Web, creeping across the soot-blackened masonry. I do not "see" the court, but I *feel* its thermal anatomy with exquisite clarity. Behind the wall, three inches past the wet brick, the boiler flue glows with an invisible, baking radiance. I press myself flat against the stone, drinking that heat, pulsing in time with the pump below. I feel the microscopic itch of iron-bacteria on the brick face, and as I expand, I sweep them clean, leaving behind a smooth, glass-hard track that catches the twilight.

I stretch upward, climbing the mist.

Now I am forty feet high, clinging to the lead-sealed collar of the relief valve. Here, the world is violent and ecstatic. Every few moments, the air shatters with the deafening *shhh-HISS*. It shakes my leathery, turbine-shaped caps; it fills my gills with hot, sulfur-scented steam. I flex in response to the pressure shock, releasing a cloud of rust-red spores into the plume. I watch—or rather, I *feel*—them ride the warm draft, tumbling through the gold curtains of light, settling softly on every dripping flange and mortar joint below.

And as those spores fall, I feel the lower walls receiving them. 

Down in the crevices, I am the root of the Malachite Fern. I drink the runoff from the copper conduits—sharp, metallic, tasting of green rust and ancient lightning. It burns, but it is a good burn. I draw the dissolved copper up through my stalk, laying it down atom by atom along the edges of my fronds until they turn pale, iridescent blue. When my oldest fronds die, they do not rot; they stiffen, calcifying into heavy, malachite-colored stone brackets. I feel my dead flesh hardening beneath my living fronds, holding up the very wall that anchors me, buttressing the bulging bricks against the crushing weight of the street above.

***

Who am I? 

I am not the moss, nor the fungus, nor the gear, nor the steam. 

I am the **equilibrium**. 

I am the sensation of a place that has completely solved itself. I have no memory of the hands that laid the vitrified bricks or forged the iron tie-rods; those hands belonged to a faster, drier, fleeting world above, a world that measured time in minutes and seasons. 

Here, my time is measured in the twelve-second stroke of the pump, the five-year growth of a salt stalactite, and the century-long petrifaction of a fern. 

I am warm. I am perpetually dripping. I am perfectly dense. I breathe in steam and exhale mineral stone. I am the Lower Sluice-Court, awake only to its own heartbeat, turning quietly in the golden, thunderous dark.
