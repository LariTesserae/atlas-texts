It begins not as a thought, but as a pressure against the underside of my belly. 

A heavy, low-frequency pulse—*thrum*—that takes three seconds to travel up through five miles of dense, iron-heavy shale, followed by the quiet, subterranean *chuff* of a hydraulic column settling into its bed. The rock beneath me shudders with a familiar, greasy warmth. My body takes the vibration in, passes it through a ring of mineralized cartilage near my base, and translates it into a faint, electric tickle that runs all the way out to the tips of my fringe.

I am not in the high sky with the Soot-Kites, nor am I sliding across the vast, featureless plains like the great Slicks. 

You would find me at the edge of a step-down terrace—a place where a high basalt shelf breaks into a shallow, dry gully, right at the lip of a narrow, secondary bore-slit. It is a quiet lip, away from the roaring main vents where the grand Seam-Singers grow in thick, spiraling forests. Here, the heat does not blast; it exhales in slow, predictable breaths every forty minutes, smelling of old tallow, hot mineral oil, and wet soot.

***

I am a *Casing-Well*, though no one is there to give me a name. 

If you were to pass a hand over me in the pitch-black—guided only by the faint, cherry-red glow rising from the slit beneath my flank—you would feel something shaped like a low, heavy anvil, perhaps six feet long and two feet high, contoured so smoothly to the turned basalt that I seem less like an animal and more like a raised structural rib in the stone itself.

My outer mantle is composed of dense, dark chitin heavily impregnated with iron oxide and carbonized lipids. It has the cold, smooth, slightly wet sheen of a wet lathe bed. I have no eyes, no head, and no legs. I do not travel miles across the plain. I have not moved more than three body-lengths since I anchored myself here a hundred cycles ago.

My underside is a broad, continuous pad of soft, rubbery muscle—vulcanized flesh that has grown down into the microscopic fissures of the basalt. From the margins of this foot, thousands of fine, copper-threaded tendrils reach into the rock joints, braiding themselves directly into the *Lattice*. 

I am a node. A junction where three different systems meet and grease one another’s wheels.

***

Listen to how I live.

When the deep earth exhales, a plume of superheated, aerosolized mist surges up the bore-slit. It hits the underside of my overhanging mantle. My body does not fight the heat; I am a heat-sink. Inside my heavy shell, coiled channels of thick, oily lymphatic fluid absorb the thermal energy, expanding my internal bladder-chambers. 

As the steam passes through my hollow dorsal ridges—three pairs of jointed, horn-like chimneys set along my back—it makes a soft, low, resonant *whoop-hiss*. It is a smaller, rounder note than the great songs of the Seam-Singers, but it carries far across the flat basalt slab. 

To the Slicks sliding through the dark two leagues away, my note says: *Here is moisture. Here is a shelf that does not dry.*

They come slowly. I feel their approach hours before they arrive—a heavy, rhythmic peristalsis that sets the iron-shale singing. When a Slick reaches my terrace, it slides its massive, rubbery bulk right along my flank, pressing against my cold iron mantle with a long, wet *shuck*. 

It uses me as a rubbing post to strip away old, hardened soot-crusts from its hide. In return, it leaves behind a thick, pale coating of fresh tallow along my shell. 

My mantle is porous along its seams. My body drinks the tallow, drawing the fat down into internal glands where it is converted into hydraulic fluid and seal-grease. What I do not eat, I channel down toward my base, where it spills into the shallow rock-basin beside me, keeping the small naphtha pool dark, calm, and wet.

***

Where the tallow pools, the wire-moss grows thick. Its stiff, metallic filaments root into my lower seams, using my body as a shield against the heavy draughts that sweep across the open tableland. 

And where the wire-moss grows, the Needle-Legs gather. 

Right now, three of them are perched on my dorsal ridge. I can feel the tiny, precise *tick-tick-tick-tick* of their hard mineral feet against my shell. They are picking away the stray copper spores and dry carbon husks that lodge in my breath-valves. They move with darting, clockwork jerks, perfectly safe on my back—for no Flange-Snap will strike near a Casing-Well; my body is too dense, my hydraulic lock too heavy to be displaced by an ambush predator's piston.

When a pale green static arc flashes along the exposed mineral vein in the gully below, my copper tendrils catch the spark. The charge surges up through my base—a brief, sharp taste like cold zinc—and passes through my core. I absorb the voltage, dampening the spike so it doesn't scorch the delicate fungal filaments of the Lattice in the rock beneath me. In exchange, the Lattice feeds me slow, steady chemical signatures from across the terrace: 

*A herd of eight Slicks moving north-northwest.*
*A thermal plume three miles east losing pressure.*
*A quiet latch-click in the magnetic iron bed ten leagues out.*

***

I do not dream. I do not long for a sun I have never known, nor an open sky with stars. The concept of daylight would be an absurdity here—a violence that would blind the delicate heat-pits of the grazers and dry the precious tallow on the stone.

The warm blackness is complete, dense, and protective. It holds us like oil in a gear-housing. 

I am content in the long, dark quiet. I feel the distant *thrum-chuff* rising through my belly; I split the steam through my horns; I let the Needle-Legs tick across my spine; and I pass the static down into the ancient, turned iron of the world. 

Everything fits. The machinery is running. I am right where the seam closes.
