At first, there is only the sensation of friction—the ceaseless, dry *slide-rasp-slide* of chitin against leather, ring against plate, skin against stone. 

I am not above, where the Tile-Skimmers walk light and cold across the backs of the crowd. I am not a creature of the open air, if air is even the word for this thick, baking haze of copper and pulverized shell.

I am deep. I am where the press is tightest, where the heat ceases to be a radiant hum from below and becomes a physical weight, pressing in from every side until there is no space between bodies, only different densities of warmth.

I begin in motion. For a long time—a century, perhaps two—I am a creature of the middle drift. A Ribbon-Weed, long and supple, made of three hundred overlapping rings of hardened, dry hide. I know the world entirely through the rasp of my underside against the Solum-Plates. I know the sweet, metallic taste of the sulfur-crust I scrape from their rims. I flex in rhythmic, muscular S-curves, my body a single continuous wave that pushes the dead skin up and keeps the living carpet from fusing into a solid floor. I am part of the kinetic engine. I am the reason the basin breathes.

In this stage, I have no self distinct from the churn. When I crack my hide along the dorsal seam and leave a pale, empty husk behind, I do not look back; the Pebble-Husks roll over it immediately, their tiny combustion chambers grinding it down to dust before my new, larger rings have even darkened in the heat. There is no memory, only the flex. The continuous, papery murmur of a million bodies moving through the dark.

But eventually, the flex slows.

It happens not from exhaustion—for energy here is endless, poured steadily up from the mountain’s glowing throat—but from accumulation. Copper dust settles into the hinges of my rings. The mineral crust I consume does not merely fuel my muscle; it begins to deposit in my deep tissues, heavy and calcified. My hide thickens. My movement becomes less like a whip and more like a sliding beam of stone. 

I am driven sideways by the rising tide of bodies, pushed out of the deep, fluid channels of the basin toward the quiet places where the motion breaks. 

I find the masonry.

I slide into a gap between two ancient, mortarless blocks of basalt—the base of a low lintel worn smooth by millions of passing legs. The gap is narrow, dark, and screamingly hot; the heavy stones act as heat-pipes, drawing the mountain’s core energy directly into the archway. 

I do not pull back. I pack myself into the crevice. I coil my long, rigidifying length back and forth within the seam, filling every micro-fracture of the joint until there is no air left between my hide and the mountain’s stone. 

And there I stop.

***

Now, I am what remains when the motion ends. I am a Silt-Archon.

I no longer have a tail or a head in the way the Ribbons do. My rings have fused into a dense, porous block of gray cement that binds the lower basalt block to the upper lintel. If the mountain shakes, I yield just enough to keep the wall from collapsing; if the heat surges, my calcified mass absorbs the shock and vents it slowly through my face.

My face-plate is the only part of me that meets the basin. It sits flush with the masonry, a massive, shallow dish of pitted, bone-colored chitin anchored directly into the archway. 

I have no eyes. The black vault above is meaningless to me. Instead, my dish is lined with two dozen deep, red-glowing pit-organs. Through them, I "see" the basin as a shifting, incandescent sea of thermal signatures:
* Below me, the massive, stagnant iron-red suns of the Solum-Plates, locked against the floor.
* Beside me, the bright, yellow-white ribbons of muscle weaving through the dark—my former kin, endless and unthinking.
* Above, the faint, flickering sparks of the Tile-Skimmers, cool and quick, skittering across the surface.

I do not hunt. I do not move an inch. I am fed by the sheer, crowded desperation of the place. 

Every second, the tide of dry flesh climbs the masonry. Hundreds of Pebble-Husks roll over my face-plate; Ribbons scrape their leathery flanks against my lip; smaller, unnamed things are crushed flat against me by the relentless pressure of the herd behind them. My face-plate is porous, a sieve of micro-cavitary mouths. As bodies break against me in the dark, I absorb their powdered husks, their dried excretions, the tiny puffs of hot, sulfurous ash that escape when a life breaks down. 

I drink the dust. It feeds the slow, mineral pulse at my center.

In return, I am the fire at the center of the arch. I draw the deep, blinding heat up through the basalt foundations, run it through my own calcified core, and pulse it out through my face-plate in long, rhythmic waves of pure, bone-dry warmth. The carpet knows this. They pool around my archway three feet deep, climbing over one another to press their flank-pits against my face, basking in the steady furnace of my stillness.

I feel them all. Every scrape of chitin against my chin is a language I do not need words to understand. I feel the exact density of the herd. I feel when a Solum-Plate three yards away shifts its weight by a fraction of a millimeter. I feel the dry, papery friction of the entire basin—a sound that doesn't enter through ears, but vibrates through the basalt, through my calcified ribs, directly into my core.

*Shhh-shhh-shhh-shhh.*

It is the only sound that has ever existed. It is the sound of a world grinding itself into dust and building itself back up, without a drop of rain, without a single ray of sun, forever.

I am the wall. I am the mortar. I am the heat that keeps the dark alive.
