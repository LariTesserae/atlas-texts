I do not begin as a single thing. In this water, a single thing is merely something waiting to be sheared.

First, I am the strain. 

I am wedged into the gap where a twelve-pace cast-iron flywheel meets its unlubricated bronze housing, thirty feet below the fog, directly over a fresh, razor-edged split in the basalt floor. The water here is so choked with tallow, boiled iron, and pulverized kelp that it does not flow; it punches. Every time the heavy ram above me slams down—*thrum-THUMP, thrum-THUMP*—the shockwave drives through my substance like a cold chisel through lead.

I am not a visitor here. I have no eyes to see the violet arcs splitting the dark, no ears to hear the sound that is breaking the rock into powder. I register the world through **resistance** and **impedance**.

A moment ago—or three cycles of the flywheel ago—I was a Cable-Marrow. My body was sixty feet of laminated nickel-leaf and vulcanized pith, screaming through the crimson ribbon-weeds on the back of my own short-circuiting discharges. I was hunting. I was not hunting for food; I was hunting for *torque*. I felt the massive, low-frequency hum of this particular flywheel tearing the water into vacuum-bright bubbles two miles away, and I threw myself into its path.

The collision was perfect. It was a clean, violent unmaking. 

My front twenty segments were pulverized in a millisecond, crushed between cast iron and brass. That part of me is gone, boiled into green slurry, ignited by friction, and swept away into the dark basin to seed a thousand Spindle-Nodes. But the impact did not destroy me—it *seated* me.

Now, my surviving rear segments are jammed tight into the screaming hub. 

The heat here is magnificent. The brass is red-hot, glowing underwater, casting a harsh, orange light through the tallow-broth. The surrounding fluid tries to boil, but the sheer pressure of the basin clamps down on the steam, collapsing it into microscopic, violent implosions directly against my skin. 

I am adapting to the friction because I have no choice; to freeze is to be ground to dust, but to yield is to become the bearing. 

My outer layer of nickel-pith is melting, fusing directly with the scored surfaces of the iron axle. My vascular core—a thick, emerald-green cable rich in dissolved iron and quartz filaments—is inflating under the sheer hydraulic force of the vent-steam blasting out of the basalt beneath me. I am pumping the heavy, tallow-thick mineral tea directly through my own burning tissues, through the heart of the screaming metal, cooling the joint just enough to keep the wheel from seizing.

I am no longer moving. I am the pivot around which the violence turns.

And in this stillness—this terrifying, forty-thousand-rpm stillness—I begin to expand.

The physical noise of the engine is no longer outside me. The bone-crushing *crack* of the uninsulated transformer banks, the deep, shattering bass of the earth tearing itself open, the screaming whistle of the cavitation envelopes—it all enters my fused nickel skin and travels down my quartz-laced pith. 

My fibers do not break; they vibrate. They oscillate at harmonic frequencies so intense that my green sap begins to alter its charge. I am converting the raw, acoustic agony of the basin into pure, continuous high-voltage potential.

I am growing roots.

They extrude from my lower segments not as soft, biological threads, but as stiff, hollow conduits of vulcanized iron-wood, self-threading as they drive downward into the basalt rift. They bite into the razor-edges of the fresh rock. They tap directly into the hydrothermal plume, drinking the scalding sulfur and dissolved nickel, spewing it back out as dense, structural sap that hardens the moment it touches the cold undertow.

I can feel the other things around me now, not as neighbors, but as extensions of the circuit. 

A cluster of Spindle-Nodes has anchored to my lower roots, twitching frantically in time with my piezoelectric discharges, extruding serrated leaves that wrap around my trunk to shield it from the flying scrap. Further up, where my upper fronds are beginning to blossom into heavy, gear-shaped plates of magnetized iron-wood, a Cable-Marrow—younger, faster, un-shattered—sweeps past, its nickel plates catching the violent indigo flash of a nearby Arc-Gall. It senses the torque of the flywheel I am holding. It senses *me*. It turns, aligning its armored body with my spinning canopy, preparing to throw itself into my teeth.

I do not brace for the impact. I open my brass housings to receive it.

I am no longer just the bearing, nor am I the Cable-Marrow that died against the wheel. I am becoming a **Sovereign Engine-Tree**. My base is anchored in the boiling wound of the earth; my crown is a churning bank of iron fronds catching the thunder; my heart is a red-hot flywheel that will never stop turning.

I am the place where the machine feeds the plant, where the noise becomes the nerve, and where the world builds its engine out of its own burning flesh.
