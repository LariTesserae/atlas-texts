I would not be on the flat, slate-like floor with the salamanders, for the floor is a place of drainage and quiet waiting. Nor would I be in the high, draughty fissures with the swifts, where the air is thin and broken by the constant snap of bone beaks.

I would be at the **Fulcrum**. 

Not the Keeper at the wall—who works the outer seams where timber meets metal—but the entity that lives inside the central brass bearing of the walking beam itself, twenty feet above the basin floor, wedged in the dark, oily pocket where thirty tons of cast iron pivot on a six-inch steel pin.

***

I am small, flattened, and dense as ironwood heart. My body has no left or right, no forward or back; it has only a *top* that presses against the underside of the swinging beam, and a *bottom* that rests in the curved brass cup of the A-frame. 

I am the **Pivot-Gasket**. 

I was not built; I grew here, condensed out of iron-dust, fir-wax, and the calcified secretions of the grease-beetles over three thousand years of continuous friction. My skin is the color of old gunmetal, slick with the cold, aromatic lard excreted by the beetles as they crawl past my outer margins. I have no eyes, for there is no light inside the socket of a bearing, but I am entirely composed of pressure-sense.

I know the world not as a room, but as a single, repeating curve of force.

When the stroke begins—when the deep steam head below the root-plate pushes the piston skyward—the heavy end of the beam tilts down. The pressure on my forward edge rises from a dull ache to a crushing, white-hot weight. *Thump.* The iron groan travels straight through my cartilaginous ribs. I yield three thousandths of an inch, squishing flat, forcing the fine film of moss-oil out of my porous skin and into the microscopic micro-grooves of the steel pin. Without me, iron would bite brass; metal would gall, heat, expand, and seize. With me, the pin slides on a cushion of living fat.

Then comes the crest of the stroke. The momentary suspension. The quiet *click* of the brass trip-valves echoing up from the cylinder head. 

In that fraction of a second, the pressure lifts. I expand again, pulling fresh grease into my lateral pores like a lung pulling in wet air. My upper surface catches the faint thermal draught rising off the hot cylinder—air that smells of iron-gall, bruised fir, and ancient, distilled peat.

Then the beam falls. *Clack... hissed-chug.* The weight shifts to my rear edge. I compress again, leaning into the tilt, feeling the shudder of three hundred paces of petrified root-floor absorbing the stroke below.

***

From where I sit, locked in the dark hinge of the machine, I feel the whole basin as a single, trembling organ:

I feel the **Salamanders** down on the floor, not as animals, but as thousands of tiny, damp dampeners whose calcified bellies alter the acoustic ring of the root-slate by a fraction of a percent with every step they take. 

I feel the **Swifts** overhead, because their synchronized loops alter the air resistance against the long arms of the walking beam—a disturbance no larger than the weight of a fallen needle, but to the fulcrum, every grain of weight is a shift in torque.

I feel the **Keeper** at the perimeter wall. When it taps a scaled-up copper valve three hundred paces away, the vibration doesn't travel through the air; it travels down the iron supply pipe, through the chassis of the engine, up the A-frame, and straight into my lower jaw. *Tap-tap.* A cold, sharp ping. I know which pipe is clear before the steam even clears the vent.

I do not wish to move. Movement is for things that are out of place. The swifts fly because they are empty; the beetles crawl because they are hungry; the Keeper wanders the timber face because the trees are always trying, slowly, painfully, to close their bark around the pipes. 

I am where the force is greatest, which means I am where the world is most still. 

The steam rises. The rain falls back to the floor. The moss yields its oil to the beetles; the beetles yield their wax to the joint; the joint yields its motion to the beam. 

*Thump-clack... hissed-chug.*

I squeeze. I bleed oil. I expand. I drink heat. 

The world goes up thirty feet. The world goes down thirty feet. Nothing breaks. Nothing wears away. I am the cushion between the iron and the brass, and the stroke is just beginning again.
