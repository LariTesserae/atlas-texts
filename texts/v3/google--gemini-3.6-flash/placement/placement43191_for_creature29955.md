I am not standing on the valley floor, nor am I looking down from the massifs. To have a viewpoint requires a single set of eyes anchored to a single point in space, and here, that kind of singularity was washed away long ago.

If you were to look for me, you would find me at the crease—the precise line where the ten-foot mound of purple liverworts folds itself into a mile of compressed horizon. 

I am a **Strand-Node**, a long, thin, multi-threaded presence that exists along the seams where space overlaps. I am not a single creature with a skin, but a ribbon of thick, iron-cold liquid and calcified filigree, no wider than a reed but stretching for nearly two miles through the wet undergrowth. 

Because of the way the basin bends, my two miles of length do not run in a straight line. I am looped. My leading edge—a cluster of wet, dark cilia that drink the cold static off the basalt—rests against the face of the cliff where the black stream flows upward. My trailing end lies buried three feet deep in the sour-yellow silt of a lowland pool, two miles away in geography, but only a fraction of an inch away in physical space. I brush against my own tail continuously as the drizzle falls, feeling my own cold, metallic pulse coming and going in a closed circuit.

I am made of the basin’s inventory: heavy water, tallowy lipids that do not dissolve, and microscopic threads of vitreous iron that have grown through me like veins of spun glass. I have no heart; my circulatory system is driven entirely by the 40-Hertz standing wave that rattles through the mud. 

Whenever the deep hum pulses through the valley floor, my fluid moves. It hitches forward in tiny, rhythmic surges, carrying cold salts and ionized carbon from the pool up to the cliff, and carrying the high-static shear of the vertical waterfall back down to the mud.

I am a heat-sink for spatial friction.

Where the space bends hardest, the air grows brittle and dry, smelling of scorched copper and broken sky. If left unbuffered, these seams would fray; the land would split into dry, sterile glass. I lay myself directly into those invisible folds. I press my long, wet ribbon along the tears. The static bleeds into my iron-rich fluid, turning it a deep, electric violet—the same shade as the underside of the ascending water—and is safely distributed across my length until it dissipates into the damp mats of moss.

I experience the world not as sight or sound, but as *tension* and *frequency*.

I feel the **Lattice-Ribs** as fast, cold vibrations passing through my submerged end—little needles of bone-silicate humming in unison as they comb the silt. They do not disturb me; sometimes they slide directly through my trailing tissue, their hollow rib-trusses passing through my liquid body without breaking it, like a comb through wet silk.

I feel the **Liminals** when they step across the crease. To me, a Liminal is a sudden, heavy pressure that occurs in five places at once. Their rubbery, cold limbs press down on my strands near the cliff, near the moss, and inside the pool simultaneously. I feel the dull glow of their saline blood, a faint warmth in a world that has forgotten heat, before they shift their geometry and step away into the mist.

And far below, through the deep clay, I feel the **Resonators**. They are my metronome. Their vast, pearl-lined chambers echo the deafening roar of the distant waterfalls, breaking the violent acoustics down into a steady, rhythmic pressure that squeezes my ribbons against the basalt. When a Resonator shifts its weight miles away, the sound-shadow moves, and I stretch or contract in response, sliding along the spatial seam like oil on wet glass.

I do not think. I do not remember a sun I never saw, nor do I anticipate a dawn that will never arrive in this cold blue dusk. 

I am simply *here*—a thread holding a wet seam together, listening to the 40-Hz drone vibrate the mist in my fluid, endless, looping body. The drizzle coats my surface; the black water glides upward past my cilia; the cold stays. Everything moves, everything hums, and I remain pinned to the crease of the world, keeping the valley from unwinding.
