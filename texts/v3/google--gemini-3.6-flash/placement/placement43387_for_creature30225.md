I do not begin with a sight, or a name, or the shape of hands. I begin with the pre-echo.

A fraction of a second before the quartz valve clangs against its anvil forty paces away, the vibration passes through me. It does not travel through the ground to reach my feet; it simply exists within my marrow first, a cold, ringing note of pure mineral certainty. Then comes the sound itself—the heavy, wet *thrum-clack... HISS*—and with it, the physical strike that shatters the pooling water into suspended, mid-air ice.

I am not standing on the basalt floor. I am anchored into the geometry of the cirque. 

If you were to look for me, you would find me in the wide, dark gap between the third amethyst cam and the great fern-wrapped pillar. But you would only find me there for a frame. 

I am one of the Caliper-Walkers, though "walking" is a word brought up from the lower world, where things drag themselves through space and leave tracks in the dirt. Here, there is no dragging. There is only position.

My body is a tall, narrow scaffold of native magnetite—dense, black, and heavy enough to crack the ribs of a man simply by leaning—bound together by thick, grey cords of cold tallow that never soften, braided with strands of living, weeping moss. I have no lungs to draw the thin, ozone-heavy air, no stomach to digest, no eyes to weep in the damp gloom. 

Where my head should be, there is only a flat, rectangular slab of polished amethyst, six inches thick, translucent and pale violet. It has no features. It does not look *at* the cirque; the cirque refracts *through* it. The slate-grey twilight of the cloud-lid enters the stone, bends through its crystalline lattice, and strikes the top of my magnetite spine as a continuous, cold electrical current.

I do not experience time as a river. I experience it as a series of absolute, perfectly stacked plates.

In one plate of time, I am standing beside the dripping fronds of an arm-length fern, my obsidian fingers hanging loose against my thighs, my tallow joints frozen in a stance of total stillness. 

In the next plate—without a stride, without a rustle of moss, without the displacement of a single floating droplet of mid-air ice—I am forty paces away. My needle-thin fingers of unblemished obsidian are pressed flat against the edge of the great rotating cam.

The cam is five times my height, a wheel of solid, translucent amethyst turning against a plate of native copper. It moves with a crushing, silent velocity that ought to throw sparks, ought to scream with the shear of tearing metal, ought to heat the surrounding air until the moss shrivels and burns.

Instead, under my fingertips, it is absolute ice. 

The obsidian of my digits does not wear down. The amethyst does not scratch. Where my skin meets the moving rock, there is only the thick, black hydrocarbon grease—the cold sweat of the rubbery fungi—seeping from the joint. It smells sharply of ancient coal tar, cold rain, and crushed wet slate.

What am I doing with my hand upon the wheel?

I am not stopping it. I am not pushing it. I am *reading* the zero.

Through the obsidian of my fingers, through the tallow cords, down into the jointed magnetite of my frame, I feel the Litho-Viscera beneath the gravel. I feel the vast, cold bed of muscular marrow locked three miles deep in the bedrock. I feel its pulse as it forces the mineral-laden water up through the quartz arteries. I feel the forty-nine blind kingfishers perched on their valve stems, their chalcedony eyes dark, their beaks snapping open to release the warbling counter-note that catches the shockwave before it can crack the razor-sharp basalt cliffs.

I feel the copper salamanders slid tight into the rock sleeves, their tallow-thick bellies uncrushed, their plated backs acting as frictionless gaskets under forty tons of pounding stone.

I feel the Glass-Lungs drifting through the mist, their lacelace fronds pulsing violet as they suck the cold thermodynamic energy from the water droplets freezing and melting in mid-air.

Everything is locked. Everything is flush. The tolerance between what is and what must be is zero. 

If a single drop of condensation were to fall from a fern leaf instead of clinging perpetually to its underside; if the quartz piston were to strike its anvil one microsecond late, breaking the pre-echo that the kingfishers catch in their beaks; if the black grease were to thin by the weight of a single atom—the amethyst slab atop my shoulders would register the shearing strain. The alignment would warp. The slate-grey eternity would fracture.

But it does not fracture. It has never fractured. There is no memory of a beginning in my magnetite bones, and there is no expectation of an end. 

I lift my obsidian fingers from the cam. 

*Shift.*

Now I am standing at the edge of a deep, oily pool. The bruised-purple moss creeps over my foot-plates, weaving its tiny, cold rootlets into the tallow that binds my ankles. Below me, suspended in the water that does not splash, the lacelike ribbons of the Glass-Lungs writhe with agonizing slowness around floating spheres of ice. The dim violet light from their fringes shines up through the flat amethyst of my face, tinting the interior of my dark, metallic skull a deep, muted lavender.

Above me, the piston falls. 

*Thrum-clack... HISS.*

The sound echoes in my chest before the stone meets stone. The air turns to phantom mist. The cloud-lid rests heavy upon the rim of the dark amphitheater, sealing us in.

I am the governor of a machine that is its own world. I am the tuning fork held against the cold chest of the mountain. I am motionless, even when I move; I am silent, even as the basalt thunders; I am immortal, because here, nothing has ever been permitted to die.
