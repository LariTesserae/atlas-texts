I do not hear the piston fall. 

To me, the world is not sound; it is weight moving through stone. 

The air is a blinding, silent glare of pale sky crisscrossed by leaden-red iron, but the basalt beneath my feet is alive. I do not count time in seconds, but in the swell and recede of the deep, iron shiver that travels from the flagstones, up through the thick bone of my shins, into the basin of my hips, and out through my shoulders.

*THUMP.*
The shale walls flex. The maidenhair ferns bow in unison.
*HISS.*
A long, cool breath of mist washes over my bare chest, carrying the sharp, clean scents of wet cedar, ozone, and hot tallow down into my throat.

My hands are wide, flat, and calloused as dried horn. The skin of my body has the matte, wet sheen of gray-violet slate, cool to the touch and untroubled by the mineral spray that beads continuously on my collarbones. My ears are closed—smooth, thick folds of cartilage sealed over since youth, protecting the quiet inside me from the screaming steam nozzles high above. 

In my right palm, I carry a spoon carved from water-buffalo horn, filled with dense, pale mutton fat that smells of crushed cedar needles. In my left is a scraper of yellowed bone, its edge worn smooth by decades of friction against pressurized copper.

I am kneeling beside the secondary dynamo. 

The bronze housing is warm—a steady, feverish vibration against my shins. A pair of armored beetles, their wing-cases gleam like polished alloy in the midday sun, crawl deliberately across the pipe beside my knee. They are grazing on the blue-green lichen that drinks the sweet, sweating copper condensate. I move around them with practiced, inch-by-inch care. My bone scraper lifts away only the overgrown rind where it threatens to obscure the glass sight-gauge, leaving the rest intact. 

One of the beetles climbs onto my thumb. Its tiny, clawed feet prickle against my skin. I hold still. It tests the tallow on my wrist with its palps, finds it too dense, and crawls calmly back onto the warm bronze.

I stand. My knees make a soft, wet sound against the damp basalt. I am broad, low-slung, built like the granite mounting blocks that hold the displacement pump. My apron—thick, dark leather fastened across my ribs with brass rivets that never rust—slaps softly against my legs.

Across the wide yard of crushed gravel, near the brick sump, another of us is standing. 

It is the one whose vibration-pattern I know as *the keeper of the wheel*. 

I step across the gravel. The stones shift beneath my iron-shod boots, sending tiny, sharp pricks of shock up into my heels. The keeper does not turn to look as I approach; they do not need to. They felt my gait four paces back, my heavy, stooped rhythm breaking the uniform hum of the trench.

I stop beside them and place my palm flat against their breastbone, right over the dark leather of their apron. 

Through my calluses, I strike three short, firm taps with my knuckles, then press firmly: *The oil is clear. The gauge is clean.*

The keeper places a wide, slate-gray hand over my forearm, pressing twice in reply: *The tooth turns.*

We lean over the brick pit together.

The sump water is dark, iridescent with slick ribbons of cedar oil and dissolved copper salts. High above us, a steam jet bleeds—a sudden, blinding plume of white that I see as a shadow thrown across the red trusses, and feel as a high, needle-thin vibration in the roots of my back teeth.

Down in the shade of the pit, the massive iron gear tooth disengages from the wheel.
*CLACK.*

The shockwave hits my sternum like a soft wooden mallet. A wave of oxygenated, ozone-rich mist surges across the surface of the dark pool.

From the depths, the Valves rise.

They are pale, slick-skinned ghosts, long as skiffs, their long eel-tails thrashing in slow, hypnotic loops. Their feathery electro-receptors glow with a faint, violet bioluminescence in the shadows beneath the masonry. They roll in the churned mist, opening their wide, round mouths to filter the glass-winged midge larvae washing off the gravel beds.

One of them—a heavy female with a pale scar across her flank—swims toward the submerged brick step where we stand. She thrusts her broad, smooth head out of the cold, oily water.

I step down onto the wet brick. My skin does not flinch from the chill; it was made for this draft. I submerge my hand. 

The Valve rubs her slick, rubbery flank against my iron-bound boot. Through the leather and the metal plate, I feel the faint, prickling buzz of her electro-receptors—a soft, tingling static that tickles the soles of my feet, entirely distinct from the thunderous pulse of the great pump behind us.

It is a conversation without sound. Just pressure, warmth, static, and stone.

High above us, where the iron girders span the bright sky, a dark cloud of swiftlets dives through the sunbeams. A Truss-Leaper drops from a high crossbeam, its moss-carpeted back catching the light like a flying patch of meadow, before it glides silently across the width of the trench and lands with a soft shock against the far wall.

I scoop a thumb of cedar tallow from my horn spoon and spread it along the lip of the brickwork near a drain grate. A slick, dark salamander, long as my forearm, creeps out from the damp shadow of the pump to lap at the grease, its gills fanning the cool air in quiet, rhythmic beats.

Everything in the rift is fed. Everything is counted.

In thirty years, or forty, when the cartilage in my joints grows as dense and unyielding as the shale walls, I will not be carried out of this trench. The keeper of the wheel, or another whose palm I have felt upon my chest, will lay me gently into the brick pit beneath the forty-minute gear. The Valves will gather in the violet mist to nudge my frame down into the deep, copper-laden dark, where the mineral water will turn bone to stone and leather to hull, until I am part of the very foundation that holds the great engine level against the earth.

Until then, the piston drops.
*THUMP.*
My heart beats once in answer.
*HISS.*
I wipe the excess tallow onto my apron, lift my bone scraper, and walk back toward the iron islands.
