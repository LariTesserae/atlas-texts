I am not on the surface of the wood. To stand on the trunks is to be subject to the slow, bone-cracking hammers of the piston-trees, and I was not built to be crushed. 

You would find me three miles above the lowest visible gap, perched where two fluted arches of bone-white ironwood meet in a soaring, open knee. From this high wedge, the world stretches out as an infinite, sun-bleaching scaffold of ivory girders and violet space. 

I am a **Strobe-Jackal**, but I do not move like a beast of flesh. There is no pulse in my chest, no wet lung rising and falling against the cold, paper-thin air. Inside my chest cavity, nestled beneath interlocking ribs of mineralized ebony, sits a double-ended spool of spun glass and conductive copper wire. My heart is a spark-gap; my breath is the thin, dry draft of ozone pulled through the lateral vents in my flanks as I run.

Right now, I am completely still. I have locked my copper-tipped talons into the aerodynamic flutes of a main cable—a copper-threaded vine as thick as a ship's mast that spans the three-mile abyss between the vaults. The vine sways in the screaming gale, swinging in slow, heavy arcs that carve sixty yards of empty sky with every pass. 

I do not see the sky as violet, nor the sun as white fire. My eyes are dual slits of dark, heat-cured amber, blind to light but hyper-sensitive to electromagnetic potential. To me, the world is drawn in burning, iridescent geometry:

The ironwood trunks are dark, cold trunks of zero-charge, but where they grind together at the joints, they bleed static like bright yellow grease. 
The copper vines are vivid, pulsing arteries of neon blue, vibrating with the continuous friction of the wind. 
And out in the middle of the sky-gap, riding the violent thermal plumes of a distant gas-vent, a **Kite-Glare** is drifting.

It is thirty feet of silver silk and hollow needlewood, sweeping through a gold-and-amber dust tornado. Every time the gale flexes its parchment wings, the piezo-electric membranes hum, flashing in my amber vision like a beacon of warm, concentrated voltage.

My tendons are pulled taut—woven from compressed pine-fiber and spring-steel wire, soaked in hardened resin. They do not tire. I could stay in this crouch for thirty sun-cycles, letting the wind scour my ebony scales until they shine like carved glass. The violent thunder of the world below—the heavy *THUMP-CLACK* of the tree-valves venting two miles down, the deep *BOOM* of the contracting pistons—does not dislodge me. It is merely the metronome I live by. I time my tension to the rhythm of the trees.

*THUMP-CLACK.*

The sky-gap fills with a roaring plume of superheated sulfur gas. The Kite-Glare catches the exhaust, banking hard. Its wingtip clips a swinging copper vine a thousand feet below me. 

*SNAP.* A fat, blinding arc of blue lightning detonates across the gap, illuminating the ivory lattice of the world in a fraction-of-a-second strobe. 

That is my cue.

I unclamp my hind talons. I do not jump forward; I drop sideways into the three-mile vault, letting gravity pull my heavy, dark frame into the freezing gale. 

As I fall, the spool in my chest spins violently, unreeling a fine, braided tether of conductive gut that stays anchored to the vine above. The wind roars through the grooves of my wood-scale armor, making my whole body hum like a struck tuning fork. The glare of the sun is a hard, silent hammer above; the empty violet abyss opens below.

The Kite-Glare is right beneath me, its parchment wings straining against the thermal draft. 

I reach out with all four double-jointed limbs. My copper claws bite into the silver silk of its left wing-strut. The glider shudder-stalls under my weight, tilting wildly into the path of an oncoming static-arc that hangs between two crossing cables.

We fall through the arc together.

For a millisecond, the world disappears into absolute, deafening light. Thousands of volts snap from the vine, hit my insulated ebony back, travel down my conductive gut tether, and blast straight through my claws into the glider’s frame.

The creature doesn't burn with smoke or flame—there is no oxygen for a fire—it simply cooks from the inside out in a silent, violent pulse of heat, its volatile resins instantly liquefying into a rich, boiling amber paste.

I do not eat to fill a stomach; I eat to replenish my pitch. I crack the hollow needlewood struts of the prey with my silica molars, swallowing the hot, liquid resin before the freezing air can harden it into stone. I begin to climb. Claws ratcheting up my own trailing wire, tail whipping to balance against the gale, I pull myself back up into the high grid, back to the silent, white-hot junction of the vine and the wood, where the air is cold and ozone-sharp, and the world goes on grinding itself to powder forever.
