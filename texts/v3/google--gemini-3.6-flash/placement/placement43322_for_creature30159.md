I am not on the high ridges where the shelters cling like barnacles, nor am I down in the boiling saucers where the glass-paddlers turn the water to grey froth. 

I am wedged three feet down inside a fresh, smoking vent-crack—a vertical split in the raw basalt no wider than my thighs. 

My home is not a place; it is an instant. I am a Ridge-Dweller, though the word means nothing to me, for I have no word for *person*, only the immediate, physical sensation of *here* and *holding*.

My left heel—the heavy, split pad of horny keratin—is jammed into a razor-sharp tooth of un-eroded gypsum. The stone is hot enough to cook soft flesh, but through the dense, grey horn of my foot, it is merely a steady, thumping heat. My skin, slick with cold, tallowy grease and frosted white with clinging mineral dust, repels the foul, wet sulfur-steam blowing up from the guts of the rift. 

My face is pressed flat against the slick basalt wall. My jawbone—thick, heavy, un-cleft—is locked against the rock. 

Through my jaw, the world is a symphony of iron and violence.

I do not hear the sound with ears; my skin-flaps are sealed tight against the screaming wall of friction above. Instead, the basin writes itself directly into my skeleton. Through my teeth and collarbones, I read the basin’s pulse:
*   *Tink-tink-tink:* A drop of scalding condensation striking my upper shoulder.
*   *Thrum-thrum-thrum:* The high-frequency buzz of ten thousand bladder-wings colliding in the blinding white glare six feet above my head.
*   *Chrr-chrr-slid:* A carpet-colony flowing over a dead flyer on the lip of my crack, its acid-spit sizzling against the bone sternum.
*   *BOOM-THUD-THUD:* Deep, four miles down, where the basalt is still liquid and heavy—a gas-pocket shifting, tearing a new path upward.

I am the Listener at the Lip. 

In my right hand, I hold a three-foot length of carved slither-horn rib, tipped with a leaf of black obsidian bound with dried gut-cord. My left arm is braced against the wall, muscles twined tight, forearms scarred with white, crescent-shaped burns where hot mud has splashed and dried.

Above me, the glare of the phosphor-chert seam overhead hits my eye. My outer eyelids are open, but my white, cloudy nictitating membranes are drawn shut, filtering the white-hot death of the ceiling into a soft, milky haze. I see no shapes, only shifts in weight and shadow.

A shadow breaks the white mist above my head.

It is a bladder-wing, heavy-bodied and blind, its rubbery skin split from a mid-air collision. It drops like a sack of wet offal, striking the lip of my crevice with a heavy *thump*. 

Instantly, the rock around it begins to vibrate. Through my jaw, I feel the *click-click-click-click* of hundreds of tiny, multi-jointed legs—a carpet has smelled the warm blood and is pouring over the ledge toward my hole. 

I do not look up. I flex my right forearm three times in rapid, microscopic jerks—a signal that ripples through the greased hide of my shoulder to the figure crouching four feet behind me on the crag. 

*Meat down. Scavengers fast. Strike now.*

A hand presses hard against my left collarbone. It is Kael—or the one who occupies the space behind me, for we do not carry names across the salt-runs. Through his palm, his vocal cords push a deep, low rumble into my breastbone: *Rumble-click-thrum.* 

*Hold. The deep bone moves.*

I freeze. I push my jaw deeper into the wet basalt, grinding my molars until the enamel aches. 

There. 

Not the carpet above. Not the flyers falling like rain. 

Deep in the fissure beneath my wedged feet, six hundred feet down in the suffocating, waterlogged dark where the slither-horns nest, something massive is sliding upward. The vibration is slow, heavy, and oily—a thirty-foot piston of calcite plates dragging over raw stone, drawn by the thermal plume of the new vent. It is coming up the very split I am wedged inside.

My heart does not leap; panic is a slow luxury. My mind drops into the sharp, narrow groove of the absolute present.

I retract my foot from the gypsum tooth. I drive my fingers—short, claw-tipped, tipped with thick nail-beds—into a hair-line seam of quartz. I haul my five-foot, slate-grey frame out of the crack in a single, explosion of greasy, low-slung muscle, popping onto the lip of the basalt floor just as the air inside the rift turns from wet grey to boiling red-white.

A cannon-shot sound rips through my heels as the geyser blows.

A fifty-foot tower of sulfur-rich mud shoots out of the crack where I was wedged three seconds ago, blasting into the blinding air. The sky turns into a storm of leather-wings, thousands of them diving blindly into the mud-plume to drink the hot minerals, their six wings whipping the air into a wet, hot gale.

In the middle of the plume, emerging straight up out of the earth like a pale, gleaming spear, comes the head of the slither-horn. Its calcite armor flashes in the glaring light of the quartz ceiling, dripping with yellow slime and crushed paddler-shells.

Kael and I do not speak. We do not look at each other's eyes. 

I drop into a low, wide-stanced shuffle, my horned feet gripping the wet, razor-edges of the basalt without slipping. I raise the obsidian pike. Kael slides to the left, his wedge-shaped shadow cutting through the white mist. 

Behind us, on the high gypsum cairn, the wicker traps are full, humming with the weight of falling flesh. Below us, the carpets are dissolving the mud-washed dead. Beneath us, the earth is splitting again.

I am a creature of cold skin, heavy bone, and sharp glass. I have no memory of the ridge where I was born, and I will leave no mark on the stone when the carpet takes my flesh. I am only the pike, the grease, the foot wedged in the rock, and the sudden, beautiful violence of the next second.

The beast turns its blind, armored snout toward our heat. 

I flex my jaw. I set my heels. And I strike into the glare.
