High above the thermal inversion layer, where the atmosphere grows too thin and cold for anything biological to endure, a pocket of space is carved into the peak of a freshly sheared granite massif. The mountain is young—its edges still carry the violent, sharp geometry of tectonic birth, unsoftened by time. There is no soil here, no moss, no lichen, no root or leaf. This is a mineral world, sterile and crystalline, yet it is far from dry.

Water is everywhere. It does not fall from the sky so much as it materializes from the hyper-dense, freezing air. The atmosphere condenses against every surface, weeping constantly from the fractured stone and the black-metal facades of the city. Water runs down the sheer drop-offs in thin, silent sheets, pools in the sharp chasms of the new rock, and hangs in the air as a persistent, eye-level fog. The sound of it—a pervasive, low-frequency dripping and the glacial scrape of ice shifting against stone—is almost the only sound there is.

Built into the scoured face of this high-altitude plateau is a city, though it is a city scaled for a contained space, a dense urban pocket embedded into the mountain like a geometric fossil. It is a settlement of staggering density, a canyon of towering, windowless structures constructed from a metamorphic glass and alloys that shouldn't exist. The architecture is layered and vertical, optimizing every cubic inch of the pocket, leaving vast stretches of the empty, freezing plateau between clusters of spires. From a distance, the city looks like a scattering of obsidian monoliths left by a retreating tide. Up close, it is a labyrinth of infrastructure.

The engineering on display is beyond the modern compass. The buildings do not resist the cold; they seem to thermodynamically ignore it. At the nanoscale, the facades of the structures are alive with processes. Micro-capillaries the width of a human hair channel the omnipresent weeping water, pulling it from the stone and drawing it into the city's baseboard intakes. The water is instantly flash-vaporized by localized, blood-orange thermal nodes, pumping life-saving heat through a lattice of internal veins. The walls sweat, freeze, and sublimate in continuous, quiet cycles. The air is thick with the static charge of these conversions, occasionally snapping with a sharp, blue-tongued crackle—the only real noise in a place otherwise defined by the hushed rush of running water.

The light here is normal, almost aggressively mundane, which makes the city feel all the more alien. A flat, white alpine sun cuts through the condensation, casting sharp, early-formation shadows across the razor-edge terrain. It catches the metallic mist, turning the air into a prismatic haze, and illuminates the sheer lack of life. 

Fauna is a rumor. Once a cycle, a surveillance drone—more mineral than machine, propelled by magneto-hydrodynamics through the wet fog—sweeps the perimeter of the pocket. It moves like a mechanical ray, skimming the surface of the weeping rocks, scanning the young erosion for stress fractures. Otherwise, the ecology is entirely energetic. The city metabolizes water and temperature gradients; the mountain weeps and freezes; the machines mediate the exchange. 

It is a landscape of intense, quiet activity. Water condenses. Steam vents. Ice crystallizes in the shadows of the sharp peaks and sublimates in the golden light. The city breathes in the wet cold and exhales heat, a perfectly isolated, hidden engine of geometry and physics, humming silently at the top of a wounded world.
