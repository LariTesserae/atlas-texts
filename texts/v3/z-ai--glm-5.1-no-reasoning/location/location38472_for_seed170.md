The plateau sits at the roof of the world, three kilometers above a sea of superheated vapor that never condenses into rain. It is a place of absolute, punishing desiccation. There is no water here—not hidden in cisterns, not bound in molecular lattices, not even remembered in the chemistry of the rock. The air itself is a hostile solvent, a thin, scorching gas that strips moisture from anything that dares to enter it, leaving only baked alkali and metallic dust. The terrain is an endless expanse of ferric slabs and rusted mesas, weathered over millions of years into a state of profound, immutable equilibrium. The erosion parameter is fulfilled not by the gentle persistence of rivers, but by the patient, blasting friction of an eternal, dry wind. The stones are smoothed, settled, and perfectly resigned to their shapes. Nothing grows here. No root cracks the stone, no lichen stains the boulders. This is a mineral kingdom, stripped to its elemental bones, existing in a state of long-enduring stillness.

Yet, against all reason, this furnace is overwhelmingly, horrifically alive. 

The fauna defies aqueous biology. They are creatures of spun glass and wire, of asbestos-fiber sinew and clockwork chitin—vast, teeming multitudes of industrial vermin. The air swarms with them. Millions of brass-winged gnats cloud the high plateaus, their polished carapages catching the hard, mid-range sunlight, refracting it into blinding, scattershot strobes. They do not drink; they feed on the electromagnetic static of the plateau, their internal turbines spinning via thermals and solar gain. Along the creosote-stained fault lines, massive, multi-legged processors crawl—things the size of warehouses, built like locomotives with jointed iron limbs, their bellies grinding the bedrock for trace sulfides. The landscape is paved with them. They clamber over one another in slow, methodical tides, their mandibles gnashing against the stone with a grinding, deafening clatter.

The noise is absolute. It is the sound of a continent-sized factory running permanently in the red. The low, thunderous hum of a million spinning gyroscopes and the bone-rattling shriek of iron on iron form a wall of sound that never ceases. It is a steady, thumping roar, punctuated by the explosive, pneumatic hisses of exhaust vents firing from the backs of the larger beasts. There is no rhythm to it, no animal call, no silence—only the relentless, deafening churn of biological industry.

And as the sound washes over the baked terrain, reality begins to tear.

The extreme heat and the overwhelming mechanical vibration have frayed the fabric of this place. It is a slow, gradual breakdown, mirroring the slow dynamic of the world. The air does not tear all at once; it erodes, wearing thin like the stones. If you stare across the open expanse of the plateau, the horizon smears. The massive, iron-shelled processors duplicate in your vision, their silhouettes stuttering and leaving thick, afterimage trails in the shimmering heat. The sky—normally lit by a steady, unforgiving sun—develops jagged, gaping holes where the light loops back on itself, casting shadows from objects that do not exist. 

At the edges of the plateaus, where the cliffs drop away into the vapor-void below, the rules of mass and gravity are merely suggestions. A giant, centipede-like extractor, dragging its segmented steel body across the rim, might suddenly lose its anchor to the physical plane. Half of its body will phase seamlessly into the rock, its iron legs continuing their slow, methodical march through solid quartz, while the other half remains above ground, grinding and screeching against the open air. The air itself occasionally solidifies into tessellated, polygonal glitches—fractured panes of static that hang in the wind for hours before dissolving back into gas. 

There are no structures here, no architecture, no hand of a creator. The mechanization belongs entirely to the fauna; the factories are their bodies. The sprawling emptiness of the high plateau is a canvas for a terrible, slow-motion apocalypse of logic. Everything is in balance, but it is the balance of a broken machine. The heat goes on forever. The dry wind howls through the iron legs. The swarms drone, and grind, and multiply, slowly wearing the world down to a final, screaming static.
