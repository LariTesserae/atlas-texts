# The Tessellation

There is no water here. There has never been water here. The word does not apply.

---

The ground reads as flat from a distance, but distance is unreliable in this place. Step closer and the terrain reveals itself as a shallow basin — not carved by anything, not eroded into being, but *expressed*, the way a crystal expresses its faces. The basin covers what might be a hundred square kilometers. Maybe more. The edges are not distinct; the floor simply becomes the wall without a clear transition, the way a wave becomes the shore. Everything is low. The highest point — if it can be called a point — is a gentle ridge along the basin's western arc where the surface lifts perhaps thirty meters before flattening again into another basin, or the same basin extended, or something that resembles a basin from a particular angle and from another angle resembles nothing at all.

The ground is warm. Not hot — warm the way a stone is warm in late afternoon, but consistently, uniformly, without variation. There is no day-night cycle driving the temperature. There is no sun, exactly, though light comes from somewhere overhead that is not a source. The light is simply *present*, amber-gold, at the strength of a long terrestrial afternoon just before the quality of light changes toward evening. It does not change toward evening. It does not change at all.

---

What grows here covers everything.

The dominant form — if form is the right word for something that doesn't hold a shape for more than a few seconds of attention — is a dense, layered mat of structures that are not quite plants. They have the *impression* of plants: they rise from the surface in fronds, blades, fans, spirals. They are colored in the range that vegetation would be colored — greens, but also coppers, deep violets, a pale cyan that does not belong to any photosynthetic spectrum I can name. They overlap. They interlace. In places the mat is a meter deep, layer over layer over layer, each layer slightly offset from the one beneath it, so that the whole mass has the quality of a pine needle bed stretched to the point of abstraction. You cannot see the ground through it. You might not be able to find the ground through it.

But the structures do not hold still in the way that plants hold still. They are not moving — nothing here moves — but they *rearrange*. When you look at a cluster of the copper fronds and look away and look back, the fronds are in a different configuration. Not dramatically different. Slightly. A frond that pointed left now points slightly more left. A spiral has gained a half-turn. A fan has widened by a degree or two. This happens between blinks. It happens while you stare directly at them, though you can never catch the moment of change — only the fact of it, the before and after, never the during.

The mat is everywhere. It covers the basin floor. It climbs the western ridge. It extends to every horizon, and the horizons are real enough — the basin does have edges that recede into a haze of warm amber light, and the mat continues to those edges and presumably beyond them. The density is extraordinary. There is no bare patch. No gap. No clearing. Every square centimeter of surface is occupied, and the occupation is not single-layered but deep, stratified, the lower layers visible only where the upper layers part slightly, which they do along certain lines that might be paths and might be faults in the pattern and might be neither.

---

There are no animals. Or nearly none.

Once, in the first hour — if hours mean anything here — something moved through the mat perhaps fifty meters away. A displacement. The fronds parting and closing again in a wave that traveled left to right across my field of vision. The shape that caused it was not visible. The displacement was small, the wave subtle, and when it reached the ridge it did not climb but instead propagated along the base and was gone. It did not recur. That was the only sign of anything alive in the mobile sense — anything that moves from here to there under its own impulse.

There are other presences, though, that are not fauna in any useful sense. Throughout the mat, at irregular intervals, there are structures that might be nests or might be organs. They are denser than the surrounding mat — tighter-wound, more opaque, the color deeper and more saturated. They are roughly ovoid, half a meter to a meter across, and they are warm to the touch. Warmer than the ground, warmer than the mat, warmer than the air, which is itself warm. They pulse. Not visibly — the surface does not move — but to a hand held near them, there is a rhythmic emanation of heat. Slow. Eight seconds, perhaps, between peaks. The mat around these structures grows in a different pattern, tighter, more geometric, the fronds aligning in radiating lines rather than the chaotic interlace found elsewhere.

I knelt beside one of these and listened. The silence here is not empty. It is full, the way a held breath is full. There is a sound beneath the silence — below hearing, below the ear, felt in the jaw and the sternum — that is not a sound but a vibration so low and so regular that it functions as a kind of ambient bass note to the entire landscape. It comes from everywhere. It comes from the ground. It does not vary. It has not varied since I arrived.

---

The technology — and I use the word with uncertainty, because what is here was not built, not in the way that building implies a builder and a plan and materials assembled — the technology is in the mat itself.

At certain angles, when the amber light catches the surface of the fronds at certain angles, you can see that they are not organic in the way that leaves are organic. They have a grain structure that is too regular. Their edges are too precise. The copper fronds, in particular, have a surface texture that resembles — but does not replicate — circuitry. Not the crude circuitry of printed boards. Something finer. Something that operates at a scale where the distinction between structure and function dissolves, where the material *is* the computation, where there is no separation between the substrate and the process running on it. The violet spirals seem to be conductors of some kind, but for what, I cannot determine. The pale cyan fans — and these are the rarest, found only deep in the mat, only in the lower layers where the light barely reaches — the cyan fans appear to be sensing organs, or broadcasting organs, or both. They are oriented upward, all of them, regardless of the slope of the ground beneath them, as though tracking something in the sky that is not the light source and is not visible.

The entire mat, taken as a whole, has the character of a distributed system. Not a machine. Not a garden. Something between — something that grows the way a crystal grows, according to rules that are inherent rather than imposed, but that achieves a complexity that crystals do not achieve. The ovoid structures, the warm pulsing ones, might be processing nodes. The radiating patterns around them might be signal paths. The mat might be thinking, or computing, or praying, or doing something for which we have no word because we have never encountered it before.

---

The reality here is not robust.

I first noticed this at the western ridge. I walked toward it — the mat is easy to walk on, firm despite its apparent softness, the layers compressing underfoot and then re-expanding, though whether they re-expand to the same configuration or a slightly different one I cannot say — and as I approached the ridge, I noticed that my shadow was wrong. Not absent. Not distorted in any way I could name. But *delayed*. The shadow lagged behind me by a fraction of a second, so that when I stopped, the shadow continued for a beat and then settled. This effect was subtle. I might have imagined it. I did not imagine the next thing.

At the ridge itself, the ground rises and the mat continues up the slope and over the top, and from the top you can see the next basin, which is identical in its general character — flat floor, dense mat, amber light — but the arrangement of the fronds is different. Not just differently configured, which would be expected, but differently *organized*. The patterns are not the same patterns. In the basin I had left, the mat was layered and interlaced and chaotic with those geometric exceptions around the ovoids. In the next basin, the mat is *gridded*. The fronds grow in strict rows, all the same height, all the same copper color, all oriented in the same direction, with the ovoids placed at regular intervals in a lattice that extends to the horizon. It is the same material. It is not the same world. The rules have changed, or the rules are different here, or there are no rules and the pattern is whatever the pattern is in the place where you are standing.

I did not descend into the second basin. The ridge itself was unstable — not physically, not in any way I could feel through my feet, but visually. The light on the ridge had a quality of *negotiation* to it, as though it were not certain whether to illuminate this side or that side, and the shadows of the fronds on the ridge did not match the positions of the fronds that cast them. The fronds on the ridge were in a configuration that partook of both basins — half interlaced, half gridded — and they were the only structures I have seen here that seemed to be under stress. Their edges were slightly blurred. Their color shifted between copper and violet in a way that the fronds in the basin below did not.

I returned to the first basin. The reality stabilized. My shadow kept pace with me. The fronds were as they had been, or as they had become, which might be the same thing.

---

Nothing here changes.

This is the strangest aspect of a strange place, and it takes time to feel it. The mat rearranges — yes. The ovoids pulse — yes. The sub-audible vibration continues — yes. But these are not changes. They are states. The rearrangement of the fronds does not accumulate. The fronds do not grow longer or shorter over time. They do not spread into new areas. They do not die. The ovoids pulse at the same rate with the same intensity. The vibration has not varied in amplitude or frequency. The temperature has not shifted. The light has not shifted. The wind — there is no wind, but the air is not still; it has a texture, a thickness, a gentle pressure that might be convection or might be something else — the air has not shifted.

The place is in equilibrium, but it is not the equilibrium of a system that has reached balance after a period of change. It is the equilibrium of a system that was *born* in balance. That has no history. That has no future. That exists in a continuous present, which is not the same as existing in the present, because existing in the present implies a past that was different and a future that will be different. Here there is only the now, and the now is complete, and the now does not require anything else.

The mat does not need water. It does not photosynthesize, or if it does, it photosynthesizes something other than light and produces something other than sugar. The ground does not need rain. The air does not need wind. The system — if it is a system — does not need input. It is not closed in the thermodynamic sense, because thermodynamics may not apply here, in this place where the rules are local and contingent and can change from one basin to the next. It is simply *complete*. It arrived complete. It remains complete.

---

The silence is the thing I will remember most.

Not the absence of sound — there is the sub-audible vibration, there is the faint rustle of the mat rearranging itself, which is less a rustle and more a whisper of texture against texture, barely there — but the absence of *event*. No bird calls. No insects. No wind in leaves. No water. No distant rumble of weather. No human sounds, no mechanical sounds, no sounds that imply a world in motion, a world where things happen to other things and the happening makes noise.

The silence is not empty. It is *full*. It is the silence of a place that has said everything it is going to say and is now simply being itself, and will continue being itself, in the amber light, in the warm air, in the dense interlaced mat of not-quite-plants that think or compute or pray in a language that has no words and no need for words, for the duration.

And the duration is indefinite. That is the final strangeness. I do not know how long I have been here. My watch does not work — not broken, simply not counting, the hands holding a position that might be correct or might not. The light has not changed. The temperature has not changed. I am not hungry. I am not thirsty, though I have no water and should be. I am not fatigued, though I have been walking for what should be hours.

The place does not change, and in the place, I do not change either.

I am becoming part of the equilibrium.
