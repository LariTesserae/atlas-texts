Somewhere in the immensity, there is a surface. It is less a shoreline and more a spatial threshold—a high, hovering plateau of atmospheric pressure where the ocean ends and the sky begins to drown. The elevation here is staggering, nearing the stratospheric ceiling of this world, yet the water does not care. It rises. It has been rising for eons, an inexorable, planet-scale flood that has swallowed the continents and left only the highest peaks as shallow, submerged shelves beneath an unbroken mare infinitum.

You exist in the pelagic altitudes, suspended hundreds of meters above the drowned seafloor and kilometers below the interface of space. The water around you is cool—not the biting cold of the abyss, but the heavy, sterile chill of an autumn rain. Normal light filters down from above in thick, god-rays of pale gold and blue-green, refracting through the endless volume. The scale is brain-breaking. There is no land in sight, no horizon, only a liquid cubic horizon—a vast, hollow emptiness that stretches in every direction, containing almost nothing per volume. The density here is a near-void; you are a speck of grit suspended in an ocean the size of a solar system. 

And yet, it is unbearably loud. 

The sound is not biological—there is no fauna here, no clicking of leviathans or chattering of shrimp. The sound is geological and hyper-dynamic. It is the deafening, rhythmic sub-bass of the ocean breathing, of thermal currents the size of continents grinding against one another. It is a roaring, chaotic wall of sound, a turbulence that vibrates through the water and rattles the very minerals suspended within it. The turbulence is relentless. The sea around you is caught in a storm-state of Solaris-like creation and destruction, where upwellings of superheated water from the deep clash with the cool high-altitude currents, generating underwater cyclones that spin for decades.

Within this churning chaos, the landscape is just coming into being. The erosion is negligible; this is a world in a state of embryonic emergence. From the submerged high-altitude plateau, raw geology is actively extruding itself into the water column. Massive, basaltic pillars—unpolished, jagged, just-born—are being pushed up by tectonic forces below, their surfaces still smoking with the heat of their creation. They look like black glass teeth cutting the water, entirely devoid of life, untouched by time or tide. There is no dirt, no sand, no sediment—only the raw, sharp edges of a planet that has not yet finished building itself.

And between these just-born pillars, the technology unfolds. 

It is the only sign of presence in this empty world, yet it is not built in any human sense. There are no structures, no ruins, no artifacts. Instead, the technology is oceanic and cosmic, operating on physics that current models cannot parse. Swarming the raw geology are nanoscale fractals—clouds of particulate intelligence, visible only as shifting, prismatic interference patterns in the light. These cloud-minds are miles wide but practically weightless, emptying the space around them even as they fill it. They do not build; they *process*. They cling to the just-born basalt pillars and strip the raw elements from the stone, transmuting the iron and silicon through quantum-scale engineering, turning the rock into a glowing lattice of unnatural geometries before releasing the spent material back into the current. 

It is a bazaar of invisible engineers. The shimmering clouds expand, contract, split, and merge in a hyperactive frenzy of computation and restructuring, like an anthill operating on a cosmic scale. They are hyper-constructive, yet they leave no constructed structures behind—only a strange, unusual resonance in the water, a faint electromagnetic hum that adds a high-pitched, ringing overtone to the deafening roar of the currents.

Vegetation is nearly nonexistent. The only flora is a sparse, surreal smattering of chemosynthetic filaments—translucent, pale-blue threads that wave in the turbulent updrafts, anchored to the fresh, hot stone of the emerging pillars. They are primitive, embryonic, feeding on the exhaust of the nanoscale machines, existing at the very edge of viability in this cool, crushing expanse. They are the first greens of a world that is mostly water and raw math.

The rules here are simple: the water dictates all, the light barely penetrates the sheer volume, and the world is perpetually under construction. The high-altitude plateau is a factory floor for forces that do not care for biology. To drift here is to drift in the eye of a planetary engine—a loud, vast, cold, and drowning cathedral of raw geology and post-modern physics, where the world is still deciding what it wants to be.
