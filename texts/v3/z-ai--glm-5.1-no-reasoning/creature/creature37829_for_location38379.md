Something lives here.

Not in the way that things live in forests or fields, with the theater of metabolism—eating, fleeing, mating, dying. Those dramas require gradients, require hunger, require a future that is different from the present. The Terrarium has exhausted its futures. What remains is a mode of existence so reduced, so stripped of incident, that it barely qualifies as life by any standard that involves *change*. And yet it persists, and persists thoroughly, and occupies every surface, and is the reason this place does not feel merely dead.

It is difficult to name because it is not a singular organism. It is an assemblage. A co-opetition so old it has collapsed into a single breathing thing. The mosses and the ferns and the vines do not compete here; they have not competed for what may be centuries. Competition implies that one might win and the other lose, that the balance could shift. The balance will not shift. What lives here has resolved every tension between its constituents into a permanent armature. The ferns hold their fronds at the angle that catches the maximum drip from that specific pipe overhead. The mosses grow to the exact thickness that insulates the steel beneath them from the air, slowing the oxidation to the precise rate that produces the particulate slurry that washes down to the grating, which is the medium in which the vine's root tips find purchase.
