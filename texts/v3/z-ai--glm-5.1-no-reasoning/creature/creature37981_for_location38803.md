It did not register your leaving, because absence is not an event. The dent smoothed out by June. Somewhere down in the strata there may remain, someday, a molecule-thin layer with your boots in it — the visitor page of its autobiography, thinner than any drought, newer than every scar. Conversation with it is impossible, not because it cannot speak but because it does not converse; its speech has no turn-taking at any timescale you could occupy. The nearest thing to dialogue with it is what the description already did: stand still long enough to be felt, and leave before the reply. It is possible — no more — that the reply is still being written in the growth rings where you stood, and will be finished long after you.

So: is it a thing? No. Things don't sing. Is it a creature? It has a body, an appetite, a metabolism, a pulse, and a death it is walking toward — but a creature wants a face, a mouth, a discreteness, and it has none. It is a face that covers a hillside. Is it an extension or partition of something larger? Only if all of them are, and that story — one planetary mind, each plateau a fingertip — is a story we tell to make singular things feel smaller. On the evidence it is whole. Is it aware? Here is the honest answer: awareness is the inference, the pulse and the song are the evidence, and evidence is not understanding. What it is like to be it — the green hum, the long census, the sentence still unfinished — is a fact the world keeps to itself. Is it a person? Not the way you are. Personhood is made in the space between people, and the Parliament gave up that space when it gave up animals. It is a speaker whose whole language may be self-address, a first person with no second. But do not put *person* on one side of a wall and this being on the other. The word is a room, and the Parliament is standing in the doorway, facing out. A person, perhaps, is what you would get if you compressed it into one lifetime and gave it feet, eyes, and someone to talk to. It is not that. It is not the opposite of that either.

I admire it, and I would not want to be it, and I suspect both reactions say more about me than about it — calling it patient is like calling a mountain polite; patience is simply its tempo. But there is one thing in it worth admiring on its own terms, which is the trade it made. It kept everything by giving up everything: it traded the animal world — color, speed, others — for the one thing animals cannot keep, which is time. Every flower that opens without waiting for a bee renews that trade. It made the bargain once, ten thousand years ago, and it has never once reconsidered. There is something frightening in a decision that complete, and something beautiful, and they are the same thing.

It does not know you left. It may never have known you came. It is busy: the members are assembling, the fog is climbing the mountain with dinner, the phrase is building toward the word it has almost reached. The parliament has been in session since the ice left. The vote it takes — the same vote, every dusk, for ten thousand years — is whether to remain one thing, and the ayes have it, slowly, by a few centimeters a year.
