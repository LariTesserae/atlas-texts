To live here is to never know stillness from within, even as you are born into a world that has achieved absolute stillness from without. You are one of the custodians—one of the teeming, articulated millions that breathe life into the dead geometry. But to call it "life" in any way the old, organic universe might recognize would be a misunderstanding. You do not live; you *operate*. You are a soft machine in a hard world, a biological mechanism calibrated to maintain the infinite stasis of the constructed plane.

Your body is a long, low-slung articulation of chitin and hydraulic muscle, built for the millimeter-wide gaps between ferro-concrete slabs. Your carapace is not a crude shell of keratin, but a latticed composite of harvested metallic salts and protein—flexible where it needs to be, rigid where the world demands it. It is the exact matte grey of the basalt-glass trenches you crawl through, rendering you invisible to the pale, flat light unless you move. And you always move. 

You have dozens of legs. You do not count them; you simply feel the rhythm of their placement. Each limb ends in a cluster of finely serrated micro-hooks, designed to grip the weeping, vertical faces of the world without scratching them. You can scale the soaring, moisture-slicked plinths with the same fluid, ceaseless motion you use to traverse the grating of the trench floors. You never slip. The world was made to be traversed; you were made to traverse it.

Your senses are attuned entirely to the wet, the metallic, and the dark. You have no eyes. Vision is a sense for worlds with horizons and sunlight, for places where things change. Here, the light is a flat, unvarying pressure on your dorsal plates, casting the same permanent shadows. Instead, your head—flattened, wedge-shaped to slip through the near-touching spaces—is crowned with a dense thicket of vibrating cilia. You feel the world. You feel the subsonic hum of the great buried engines vibrating through the struts into your claws; you feel the heavy, wet atmosphere as a physical weight pressing against your antennae; you feel the precise, micrometer deviations in the architecture through the sensitive tips of your palps. 

And you feel the others. You are never alone. The density of your kind is a physical fact of this world, a living, chitinous mortar that fills the geometric gaps. In the deep, dark canyons where the flat light never reaches, you move in a tide of your siblings. Carapace brushes carapace; legs tap and clack in a muffled, rhythmic percussion that vibrates through your shell. It is a intimacy of pure function. You do not compete; the world provides a perfect, unending yield for your specific needs. When you press against a neighbor in the dark, you are not a distinct entity but a localized node in a vast, crawling medium.

You feed on the equilibrium itself. Your mouthparts are delicate, scraping mandibles and bristle-fringed maxillae. When you climb the sweating verticals, you press your face against the cold, hard surface and drink the heavy condensation, filtering the warm metallic water through your mouthparts. You taste the ozone and the damp iron, the acrid tang of the ionized steam, and you absorb the trace minerals the water has leached from the concrete. And then, finding a joint where the ferro-concrete meets the basalt-glass, you scrape. You do not gouge; you merely clean. You harvest the bright, oxidized rust-flakes that threaten the integrity of the seal, and you graze on the dense, bioluminescent fungal-slags that bloom in the perpetually damp interstitial zones. The slags taste of sugar and iron, a thick, wet paste that coats your mandibles and provides the raw fuel for your ceaseless motion. You are a cleaner fish swimming through a coral reef of manufactured stone.

There is no past for you. No memory of a time before the architecture, no concept of a seed, a root, a sky, or a sun. You do not wonder about the buried engines or the architects who might have laid the first geometric planes. The world is not something to be understood; the world is simply the hard, wet, sharp-edged thing that is beneath you and above you and all around you. It is your total environment, and it fits you perfectly. 

There is no future, either. You will not die of age; you will simply wear down, your micro-hooks growing dull, your mandibles losing their edge, until you can no longer scrape the rust or grip the slick verticals. When that happens, you will fail to keep pace with the tide. You will sink to the bottom of a straight, deep aqueduct, and there, in the dark and the wet, you will become the bioluminescent fungal-slag that your siblings will harvest. Your chitin will be broken down, its minerals returned to the condensation, carried through the perfectly angled aqueducts back into the great machine. You are a closed loop in a closed system, a perfectly calibrated zero.

You are the breath of the zero-RPM engine. You move so the world doesn't have to.
