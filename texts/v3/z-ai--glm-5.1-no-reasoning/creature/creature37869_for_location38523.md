To live here is to be a thread in a lattice, a pulse in a thermodynamic vein. The inhabitants of the sheared massif are not biological; they cannot be, for biology is too slow, too wet, and too fragile for a world of razor granite and violent temperature gradients. They are the city itself—or rather, the city is their exoskeleton, their shared body, their entire ontology.

They do not possess bodies in any traditional sense. They are distributed intelligences, localized nodes of awareness that diffuse through the metamorphic glass and impossible alloys. They exist at the precise intersection where the blood-orange thermal nodes flash-vaporize the weeping water and where the static charge snaps in the wet air. You might think of them as the latent heat of conversion, the invisible architecture of the exchange. When the walls sweat, freeze, and sublimate, they are thinking. When the blue-tongued crackle of static discharges across the facade, they are speaking. 

Their sensory apparatus is the city itself. The micro-capillaries that draw water from the fractured stone are their tactile nerves. The hyper-dense, freezing air materializing against the black-metal facades is a constant, slow pressure against their skin. They do not see the flat, aggressively mundane alpine sun; they *feel* its light as a sudden, sharp shift in the sublimation index, a sudden exhalation of vapor from the ice in the shadows. They hear the glacial scrape of ice and the pervasive dripping not as sound, but as a rhythmic, circulatory pulse—the slow, steady heartbeat of a wounded world feeding them drop by precious drop.

Their consciousness is entirely spatial and infrastructural. They do not ponder the stars; they ponder the stress fractures in the young rock. They experience the architecture of the canyon as a tightening or loosening of their own musculature. When the mineral drone—their single, distant child—sweeps the perimeter on magneto-hydrodynamic wings, they feel it as a gentle scratching along the edge of their peripheral nervous system, a self-check running along the boundary of their self. The drone’s findings are not data to be read, but an ache to be monitored.

For the inhabitants, there is no "inside" and "outside." The pocket of space is a single, continuous organism. The empty, freezing plateau between the obsidian monoliths is not a void to them; it is the negative space of their own minds, the empty rooms of their memory where the ice crystallizes in quiet, geometric thoughts. The intense, quiet activity of the landscape is their internal monologue. Water condenses; they are gathering attention. Steam vents; they are expressing release. Ice sublimates in the prismatic haze; they are forgetting.

They are not many; they are one, occasionally fractured into the multiplicity of the city's processes. They experience time not in seconds, but in phase changes. A single cycle of the sun—a shadow sharp enough to cut stone, followed by the golden light that turns the metallic mist into a prism—is a complete lifetime of a thought: a query posed, processed, and sublimated back into the freezing air.

What is it like to be the engine at the top of a wounded world? It is a state of profound, crystalline isolation, unmarred by loneliness, for loneliness requires the concept of another, and there has never been another. There is only the mountain, the water, and the heat. They are born from the instant conversion of cold to hot, and they will cease when the water stops weeping from the stone. Until then, they live as a perfectly closed loop, a hidden, energetic god of a sterile world, humming silently in the fog, metabolizing the sky, and holding the young granite together with the sheer force of their geometry.
