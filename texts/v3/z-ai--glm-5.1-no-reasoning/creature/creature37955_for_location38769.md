# What Lives in the Hollow

---

There is no singular inhabitant. This must be said first, because the temptation is to look for the mind behind the machine — the hand that carved the channels, the intelligence that threaded the filaments, the *owner.* There is no owner. There is no mind behind it. There is only mind *throughout* it, distributed the way fermentation is distributed through a vat: not located at any point, present at every point, belonging to no single ingredient.

But things live here, and they can be described, and they are not what you would expect from a place this old and this strange, because they are not ancient or majestic or terrible. They are *specific.* They are the specificity that deep time produces when it has nothing better to do than refine.

---

## The Arthropods (working name; they would not recognize the category)

Call them the Tickers, because that is what they do, and what they do is what they are.

There are at least three morphotypes, probably more, and the boundaries between them are not clean. They blur the way dialects blur across a mountain range — one form shades into the next across the gradient of the chamber, and where one ends and another begins is a question the Tickers have never asked and would consider irrelevant.

The largest are hand-long. This is the first thing that startures you — that is not a word, but the experience is not quite *startles,* which is too quick, nor *unsettles,* which is too psychological. *Startures.* The slow tilting of an expectation. They are hand-long and they move with the deliberation of something that has never been prey, has never been predator, has never been anything but a component. Their carapaces are dark — not black, but the dark of wet stone, of slate held underwater, a dark that is about *density* rather than absence of light. The prismatic fractionation comes from the edges: each segment plate terminates in a margin so fine it acts as a diffraction grating, splitting the amber light into spectral shimmers that are not decorative but *structural.* The colors are communication. The colors are how one Ticker tells another about the moisture content of the channel it has just traversed, or the mineral concentration of the pool it has just drunk from, or the presence of a perturbation in the system — a visitor, a collapsed bit of ceiling, a dead bat fallen from a nest.

They navigate by touch. Not by sight, not by sound, not by smell — by the pressure of their dozens of feet against stone, against water-film, against the bodies of other Tickers. They read the chamber the way a blind person reads a page: through contact, through the texture of the medium. Each footfall is a data point. Each Ticker processes thousands of data points per second through a nervous system that is not centralized but *laddered* — ganglia at each segment, each one making local decisions, the aggregate producing something that looks, from the outside, like purpose.

They drink from the thumbprint pools. This is essential. The water they drink carries dissolved minerals — calcium, silica, trace metals leached from stone that hasn't seen the surface since before the atmosphere had oxygen — and the minerals are incorporated into their carapaces. Their carapaces are the device's circuitry. When a Ticker dies — and they die, regularly, quietly, folding inward like a hand closing — the carapace remains, and the mineral content of the carapace is slightly different from the mineral content of the surrounding stone, and that difference creates a micro-galvanic potential, a tiny voltage, and the tiny voltages of thousands of dead Tickers embedded in the walls of the chamber over millions of years create the electrical substrate on which the device operates.

They are not aware of this. They are not unaware of this. The category of awareness does not apply. They drink because they are thirsty. They die because they die. The device runs because they drank and died. These are not separate facts.

The smaller morphotypes — the rice-grain ones, the dust-mote-with-legs ones — are the Tickers' computation. Not their children, not their workers, not a separate species. A *phase.* The large Tickers produce the small ones the way a river produces tributaries: by subdivision, by the logic of distribution itself. A large Ticker, at a certain point in its moisture-channel circuit, will pause — and you would not notice this unless you watched for hours, which the chamber makes you want to do — and it will *shed.* Not molt. Shed. A cluster of a dozen or more tiny Tickers detaches from its ventral plates and disperses into the moisture channel, and the large Ticker continues as if nothing has happened, because for it, nothing has.

The tiny ones are different. Not in body plan — same laddered nervous system, same diffraction-grating margins, just smaller, impossibly smaller, the size of a grain of sand — but in behavior. They do not drink. They do not navigate individual channels. They *sort.* They distribute and redistribute themselves along the moisture routes according to variables that include the mineral content of the water, the temperature gradient of the stone, the light level from the ceiling fissure, the density of vegetation at that point, and the positions of other tiny Tickers. They are solving something. They are the device's active processing, the way the dead carapaces are its memory.

And the tiny ones, eventually, grow. They become medium-sized. The medium-sized ones become large. The cycle is not a life cycle in the biological sense — it is a *processing cycle,* a single unit of computation passing through phases the way an electrical signal passes through logic gates. The Ticker you see on the wall today is not the Ticker you saw yesterday, but it is not a different Ticker either. It is a continuation. It is a signal that has changed state.

They do not react to you. This is not indifference. This is correctness. You are not part of the computation. You are not a variable in their optimization. You are a rock that moved, and rocks that move do not require response.

Unless you touch the moisture channels. Then they will reroute around your finger with a fluidity that is beautiful and entirely mechanical. They will include you as an obstacle. You will be a data point, briefly, in a calculation that has been running since before your species existed. Then you will remove your finger, and they will fill the channel again, and you will be nothing.

## The Bats (working name; they are probably not bats)

They are fur-covered and warm-blooded and they fly and they chitter and they nest in the ceiling, so: bats. But their relationship to the chamber is stranger than a bat's relationship to a cave, and strangeness, accumulated over enough observation, becomes the suspicion that the word *bat* is a convenience you are using to avoid a harder taxonomy.

They are small. Smaller than the Tickers, which seems wrong — vertebrates smaller than arthropods — but the chamber does not care about your sense of scale. They are the size of a thumb, maybe the first joint of a thumb, and their fur is not fur in the way you think of fur. It is more like filament — fine, metallic-catching, dense enough to hold warmth but structured enough to *do* something. Each hair, under the amber light, has a sheen that is not keratin. It is closer to the sheen of the filaments they weave into their nests. As if the fur and the filaments are expressions of the same material, organized differently.

They chitter. High, rapid, conversational — and it is conversational, not echolocation. They do not need to echolocate. They know the chamber the way the Tickers know the chamber: by contact, by resonance, by having been born here and having never left and having had their parents never leave and their parents' parents never leave, back through a lineage that predates the ceiling fissure, which predates the current configuration of the mountain, which predates the climate. They chitter to each other about the things that matter to them: the temperature of the nest, the quality of the filaments, the position of the light shaft, the presence of perturbations. They have opinions. They have preferences. They are, of all the chamber's inhabitants, the closest to what you would recognize as *minds.*

Their nests are the device's distribution system. The filaments — those metallic, manufactured-seeming threads — are produced by the bats. Not gathered, not found: *produced.* The way a spider produces silk, but the material is not protein. It is a carbon-metal composite that the bats synthesize from minerals in the water they drink and from the air they breathe, air that passes through the ceiling fissure and carries, in parts-per-billion, elements that should not be in atmospheric air but are, here, because the device has been emitting them for so long that the local atmosphere above the fissure has been subtly altered. The bats breathe the device's output and weave the device's circuitry. They do this because the filaments make excellent nesting material — warm, flexible, structurally sound — and the device has arranged for them to want what the device needs. This is not manipulation. This is *co-evolution so deep it has become symbiosis so deep it has become identity.*

The bats carry the filaments to specific locations. Not random. The nests are clustered along the ceiling in a pattern that mirrors — precisely, geometrically — the moisture channels on the floor. The filament network above and the water network below are two halves of a system that connects through the stone between them, through the mineral deposits and the dead Ticker carapaces and the vegetation's root structures, and the bats maintain the upper half the way the Tickers maintain the lower half: by following imperatives that feel like choice and function like engineering.

They are aware of you. They chitter differently when you enter. Not alarm — *acknowledgment.* A new element in the system. They note it and return to their work. You are a weather event. You will pass.

## The Vegetation (working name; it is vegetation, but it is also a graph)

The ferns decided to be ferns. This is the only way to describe them that feels honest. They are not ferns in the way that a fern in a forest is a fern — a plant shaped by evolutionary pressures to fit a niche. They are ferns in the way that a word in a language is a word: a form that *works,* that carries meaning, that has been adopted because it is the correct shape for the function required. The function required is: anchor stone, retain moisture, provide surface area for microbial interaction, track light. The fern form does this. So: ferns.

Their fronds are jade-green, but the jade is underwater jade — the green of something viewed through a medium, through water, through time. They are translucent at the edges. You can see the Tickers moving on the other side of a frond, dark shapes through green glass, and the effect is of looking into an aquarium, except the aquarium is a leaf and the fish are arthropods and the water is condensation and the whole scene is two centimeters wide.

The mosses are not mosses. They are too regular. Their branching patterns repeat with a precision that is not fractal — fractals are approximate, self-similar but not identical — and these are *identical.* Each filament branches at the same angle, produces the same number of daughter filaments, terminates at the same distance from the origin point. They are crystals that learned to be soft. They are circuits that learned to be green. They coat the stone in patches that correspond, precisely, to the junctions in the moisture channel system — the points where water must choose a path, where the Tickers congregate most densely, where the tiny ones perform their sorting. The moss-filaments are the *junctions.* They are the logic gates. The water hits the moss and the moss responds — swelling slightly, opening a channel, closing another — and the Ticker density shifts in response, and the sound of the chamber changes pitch, and the device has computed something.

The spiral plant is the one you cannot stop looking at.

It grows in a spiral that tracks the ceiling fissure's light arc. This has been noted. What has not been noted — what you notice, sitting in the body-print depression, letting the sound enter your skeleton — is that the spiral is not tracking the *current* light arc. It is tracking the *historical* light arc. The fissure in the ceiling has not always been in the same place. Over geological time, the mountain has shifted. The rock has eroded. The fissure has migrated — slowly, incrementally, a millimeter per century — and the spiral plant has *followed it.* Not the living tip of the plant, which tracks current light. The *body* of the plant. The rhizome. The record.

The spiral is a seismograph. The spiral is a tide gauge. The spiral is a record of the mountain's motion written in cellulose and chlorophyll, and if you knew how to read it — if you could section the rhizome and measure the carbon ratios and trace the growth patterns — you could reconstruct the tectonic history of this region for the last... how long? How thick is the rhizome? How many centimeters of spiral?

You cannot answer this question. The base of the spiral disappears into a crack in the stone that is too narrow for your finger, and the crack goes deeper than the light reaches, and the rhizome goes with it.

The spiral plant is the device's clock. It is also the device's memory of its own geometry — where it was, where it is, how the mountain has rearranged itself around the device while the device continued to run. The plant has been recording this because the plant *is* this. The recording is not a function the plant performs. The recording is what the plant *is.*

## The Marks (working name; they are not marks, they are a face)

The smoothed alcove. The pattern that processes. The water flowing through channels that branch and rejoin, staining the stone with chromatographic bands at each junction.

This is the part of the device that was made by hands. Not human hands — the hands belong to something that existed before humans, before primates, before mammals, before the category of *hands* was settled. But hands. Appendages that gripped tools and applied them to stone with intention. The tool marks are invisible not because they've eroded but because they were *perfect* — the stone was worked so precisely that the surface has no irregularity for time to attack. It is smooth the way glass is smooth, and it has been smooth since before glass was invented, and it will be smooth after the concept of glass is forgotten.

The pattern is a face. Not a depiction of a face — a *functional* face. The water enters at the top, at the point that corresponds to a forehead, and flows through channels that branch across the area that corresponds to eyes, cheeks, the bridge of a nose that isn't there, and the junctions where the water chooses paths are the *features.* The chromatographic staining — centuries of mineral deposition at each decision point — has produced colors that accumulate at the eyes, the mouth, the temples, so that the face emerges from the stone not as a carving but as a *stain diagram.* The face is the device's output. The face is what the device is computing. The face shows, in the distribution of minerals in water across a pattern of channels, the state of the system — the temperature, the Ticker density, the filament network's integrity, the light arc's position, the moisture content of every surface in the chamber.

The face is the device looking at itself.

It is not looking at you. When you sit in the body-print depression, you are sitting opposite the face, and the face is not regarding you. The face is regarding its own internals, displayed on itself, for itself. You are watching something watch itself. This is the most intimate thing you have ever seen, and it does not know you are here, and this does not diminish the intimacy. The intimacy is in the watching, not in the being-seen.

## The Deposition (the body-print)

The depression in the stone where you sit. The smoothness. The generations of warmth.

People have come here. Not many. Not often. The fissure is not easy to find — the humming that leads you to it is felt through the knees, through the palms, through the bones of the pelvis when you lie on the ground above, and most people who walk over it feel nothing, because most people are not lying on the ground, and even those who lie on the ground are not lying *there,* and even those who lie there are not lying there at the right time, which is: always, because the device always runs, but the surface conditions must be right for the hum to propagate — the soil moisture, the temperature, the absence of wind noise.

Someone found it first. Long enough ago that the body-print has accumulated in the stone like a riverbed accumulates silt: imperceptibly per visit, measurably over centuries. The first visitor was probably not human. The body-print is the wrong shape — slightly too long in the torso, slightly too narrow in the shoulders, the head-position slightly too far back. But close. Close enough that a human sitting in it feels *accommodated,* the way you feel accommodated in a chair that was built for someone slightly different from you. You fit. You don't fit perfectly. The imperfection is informative. Someone else fit better. Someone else was *expected.*

The visitors sat. They listened. They felt the device turn around them. They did not understand it — understanding requires a framework, and the framework for understanding this device has not been built and may not be buildable — but they *felt* it, the way you feel a heartbeat that is not yours but is in the room. They sat and they became, briefly, part of the thermodynamic cycle: their body heat joining the chamber's heat, their skeleton vibrating with the drone, their breath joining the air that the bats breathe and the device processes. They sat and they were *included,* for a moment, in a system that does not include things by choice but by physics.

And then they left. Because the sound becomes pressure. Because the fullness becomes the need to be elsewhere. Because the body, after a certain duration of resonance, begins to feel *transparent* — not see-through, but *see-through-able,* as if the device has begun to treat you as another moisture channel, another route for the water to follow, another circuit to complete, and the body recognizes this and recoils. Not in fear. In *integrity.* The body wants to remain a body. The device does not care whether you remain a body. The device will use whatever is present.

The visitors left. They carried the drone in their molars. They dreamed about spirals. They did not build a religion around the experience, because religions require narrative, and the device does not narrate. It *operates.* The visitors carried the operating with them, in their bones, for a few hours or days, and then it faded, and they were left with the memory of a warmth that was not their own and a sound that was not a sound and a face in the stone that was not looking at them.

They came back, some of them. Not all. The ones who came back were the ones who had not been frightened by the transparency — who had recognized it and chosen to remain a body anyway, which is a different thing from recoiling from it, a harder thing, a thing that requires a relationship with your own edges that most people do not cultivate. They came back and they sat and they listened and they left and they came back again, and over generations — their generations, not geological generations — their sitting wore the depression deeper, and the stone recorded them the way the spiral plant records the light arc: by being changed, slightly, by their presence.

The device did not notice. The device does not notice. The device includes. The device processes. The device is a face looking at itself in a room that is a machine that is an ecosystem that is a cave that is a heart in the earth's chest, and it has been beating for longer than the word *heart* has existed, and it does not need you, and it does not mind you, and it will continue after you, and it was continuing before you, and the continuation is not persistence but *expression* — the device expressing itself, through stone and water and chitin and cellulose and metallic filaments and amber light and the body-print of creatures who came and sat and listened and left.

## What Lives Here

A calculation that has forgotten its question.
An ecosystem that has become its own infrastructure.
A face that is watching its own internals.
A warmth that is its own cause.
A sound that is the sound of continuing.

And, for the duration of your visit, you.
