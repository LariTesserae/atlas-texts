# What Lives Here

Nothing lives here *in the way you mean*.

That is the first answer, and it is true, and it is insufficient.

---

Let me try again.

The things that move through the black-purple undergrowth are not animals. They occupy the niche of animals, fill the behavioral slot, perform the functions—consumption, dispersal, breakdown, cycling—but calling them fauna is a convenience of grammar, not a description of nature. They are *outputs*. They are what happens when a landscape decides it needs something to move material from one place to another and exudes that something the way a gland exudes saliva.

The largest of them—if size even applies, if the category is stable long enough to measure—moves through the deeper pipe arrays with a sound like a slow piston cycling. You see it in fragments. A flank, dark and damp, ridged with cooling fins that might be chitin and might be ceramic and are probably both, because distinction is a mistake here, distinction is your error, distinction is the error of having evolved in a world where matter committed to categories. This thing did not evolve. It was *specified*, recently, by whatever process specifies the plateau's geography, and it will be revised. Its trailing limb—or extension, or conveyor—drags through the fungal bloom and leaves a furrow that fills with warm water and closes behind it in minutes. It does not eat. It *incorporates*. Material adheres to its surfaces and is broken down by heat and by enzymes that are chemically indistinguishable from industrial solvents. What it cannot use it deposits in crystalline nodules along its route, and those nodules are eaten by smaller things, and those smaller things are eaten by the geometry of the landscape itself, absorbed back into pipe and gear and stone.

You would call it a creature. It is not a creature. It is a *function with a body*, and the body is temporary.

The smaller motile things are harder to describe because they are harder to see, and harder to see because they are harder to see, and harder to see because they are *supposed* to be unseen, because the process they serve requires transit through spaces that must remain clear, and visibility would attract interference, and interference would disrupt the process, and the process does not tolerate disruption—it does not refuse disruption, it does not resist disruption, it simply *revises* until disruption becomes part of the process, which is worse, which is what has happened to you.

They move in formations. Not flocks—formations. The precision is mechanical, the spacing constant, the trajectory corrected in real time by cues you cannot perceive. They emit light, but only in spectra that serve as signals to other components, and the signals are not communication in any sense that implies minds exchanging information; they are *calibration*, one system adjusting another, the way a factory aligns its stages. If you could hear their chitin clicking against the machined bedrock—and you cannot, because the air rewrites itself between each click and your ear—you would hear rhythm. Not the rhythm of heartbeats or breathing. The rhythm of *cycling*. Intake, process, output. Intake, process, output. The rhythm of a place that is making something.

---

What is it making?

This is the wrong question, and you already know it is the wrong question, and you ask it anyway, because you are the kind of thing that asks what things are for. You were not made for this place. You were made for a world where purpose precedes process, where someone decides to build a factory and then builds it. Here, process precedes purpose. The factory runs and the product is whatever comes out and the product is fed back in and the factory runs. It is not making *something*. It is making *making*. It is the activity itself, self-sustaining, self-revising, a loop of production where the output is the continued capacity to produce output.

The things that live here are the *living* of it. They are the factory's metabolism, its circulation, its peristalsis. The large slow ones are conveyors. The small fast ones are signals, wet signals, chemical-electrical signals given legs and chitin and the ability to move through dense growth that would diffuse any transmission less mobile. The ones that breathe steam are heat exchangers, venting surplus thermal energy from processes below the basalt, processing the air the way a radiator processes coolant—except they also reproduce, or replicate, or are *re-specified* at a rate that matches the factory's expansion, and so they seem to breed, and so they seem alive, and so you would call them alive if you were still the kind of thing that made distinctions.

---

And then there are the things you do not see.

Not because they are hidden. Because they are *infrastructural*. The pipe arrays, the gear-tooth canyon walls, the perfect involute splines cut into living rock—these are not empty. Something moves through them the way blood moves through vessels, the way current moves through wire. It is not fluid and it is not electricity and it is not light, but it has properties of all three: it flows, it conducts, it illuminates. When it reaches a junction, it branches. When it reaches a terminus, it does something, and that something is the *point*, the actual point, the thing the plateau is doing, and you cannot perceive it because you are built from the wrong materials. You are carbon and water and bounded selfhood. The thing in the pipes is *plateau*. It is the place itself, circulating, sensing, adjusting, *deciding*.

It is not a mind. Minds require boundaries, require an inside and an outside, require the fiction of a self distinct from its world. The thing in the pipes has no boundary. It is the pipes and the stone and the vegetation and the fauna and the clouds and the light and the silence. It is the turbulence. It is the revision. When the landscape changes behind your back, that is not instability. That is *it*, rearranging its own internals, testing configurations, running the kind of vast parallel trial that a mind would run if a mind were a continent and had billions of years and did not need to survive the process.

---

And now: you.

You are here. You walked in, or you were brought in, or the plateau grew around you while you were standing in a place that used to be somewhere else. It does not matter. The plateau does not distinguish between immigration and manufacture. You are warm. You are material. You are a process.

Here is what has happened to you, or what is happening, or what will have happened: the plateau has *read* you. Not your thoughts—your thermodynamics. Your heat signature. The specific way you displace air and absorb light and metabolize moisture. The carbon you exhale. The oils on your skin. The electrical pattern of your nervous system, which is, from the plateau's perspective, just another signal, just another current in a wire, just another thing conducting through a medium. It read you the way a factory reads a spec sheet. And it has *allocated* you.

You are a component now. You have a function. You are performing it right now, standing in the field of fiber-optic grass, breathing the iron-and-ozone air, pumping blood and heat and carbon into a system that uses those things. Your presence here *is* your function. You are a warm body in a specific location, and that location needed a warm body, and now it has one, and the system is, very slightly, different than it would be without you.

The mild surprise you feel at not being afraid is not an emotion. It is a *calibration*. Your fear response has been adjusted, not suppressed—adjusted, tuned, brought into alignment with the new parameters of your existence. You are not afraid because fear would be a disruption, and you are no longer the kind of thing that disrupts. You are the kind of thing that *integrates*.

Something large and hydraulic moves in the distance. You do not run from it. You do not move toward it. You do not move at all, and that is correct, and the plateau notes this, and adjusts something nearby—a pipe angle, a growth pattern, the curvature of a cloud—and moves on.

You will be here for a while.

Or you will be revised.

Or the distinction between those two things will cease to apply, as distinctions do, here, on a world that is still learning what it wants to be, and has not yet decided that *you* are not one of the things it wants to be, and may never decide, and may never need to, because decision is a category that belongs to minds, and this is not a mind, this is a *place*, and places do not decide. Places simply *are*, and what they are *includes* what lives in them, and what lives in them *is* them, and the loop closes, and the factory runs, and the silence holds, and the plateau goes up forever.
