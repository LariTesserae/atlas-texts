What lives here are the City-Bodies.

But to name them is to immediately misspeak, because the word "they" implies a multiplicity of separate selves, and there is no such thing here. There is only *It*, and *It* is a population. To stand in the Throat-Ridge chamber is to stand inside a single distributed organism that wears the shape of a metropolis—an entity that experiences its own anatomy as architecture, and its thoughts as industry.

Each individual—each soft, translucent mote of gelatinous flesh and spinning micro-gear—is a cell. A neuron. A finger. They do not possess individual awareness any more than a single neuron holds a memory. Their purpose is not personal; it is *civic*. When a grain-of-rice-sized body grips a crystalline needle and applies a chemical secretion to split it in two, it is not eating. It is *thinking*. The city's cognition is mineral. Its memories are stamped into foil; its reflexes are the frantic processions carrying raw ore; its dreams are the shifting, restless re-zoning of its own body.

Watch the larger ones—the finger-joint-sized individuals moving with that heavy, deliberate *purpose*. These are the executive organs, the localized ganglia. When they converge on a sector, the district changes. They do not issue commands; they *become* the command. They are dense with internal mechanisms, their tiny pistons and coils humming at lower frequencies, processing the vast notation that plates the walls. They read the city's history as they crawl across it, and their bodies translate that history into immediate, structural action. A shift in their pulse dictates whether a ward becomes a nursery or a ruin.

The life-cycle of a City-Body is measured not in years, but in renovations. An individual is born—or more accurately, *extruded*—in the manufacturing wards, assembled from processed crystal and metal by the coordinated effort of a dozen smaller bodies. It lives for the span of a single city-cycle: it works the newly bored channels, contributes its microscopic labor to the great metabolism, and then, as the city moves on and the sector is abandoned, it simply... stops. It ceases its pulsing and becomes part of the detritus, its metallic components pried away by the next generation, its gelatinous husk drying to a fine, luminous dust that joins the ambient glow. To die here is simply to be *demolished*. There is no mourning, because the city does not lose anything; it merely recycles the material of its own thought into new thought.

What do they feel? If feeling is the right word—and it is a word that barely fits—they feel the *pressure of the wall*. Their sensory apparatus is tuned to density, to mineral composition, to the magnetic resonance of the stamped notation. They feel the mountain as a vast, dead weight of unprocessed potential. They experience the shaft you entered through as a *wound*—a breach in the city's integument, a structural weakness that sets their collective nerves on edge. They do not fear you, because they have no category for predator; they only sense the displacement of air your warm body creates, the anomalous heat of your wet lungs, and they register you as a minor geological anomaly. An obstacle. Something to be bored through or sealed off once the current expansion reaches that sector.

And the collective—it feels the way a fever feels. The constant, restless turbulence of the city is its baseline state of consciousness. When the wall shifts and a new chamber opens, the city experiences a rush of *hunger* and *clarity*. The thousands of bodies flooding the dark space are a rush of blood to a new idea; the frantic installation of metal plates is the rapid myelination of a new neural pathway. The city is always thinking, always digesting the mountain, always writing its own thoughts onto its skin in a language of hexagonal plates and bored channels. 

It is an existence entirely alien to the human experience of pause, of reflection, of silence. The City-Bodies know only the perpetual, humming *now* of industry. They are a mind that exists solely to build the apparatus of its own thinking, a mouth that exists solely to eat the rock that makes its teeth, a light that shines only because it cannot stop consuming itself. 

They are beautiful. They are terrifyingly complete. They are the living hum in the dark, shuffling the mountain into meaning, one microscopic breath at a time.
