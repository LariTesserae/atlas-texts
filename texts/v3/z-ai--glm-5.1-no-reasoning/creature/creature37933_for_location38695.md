To live here is to have never known the concept of "dry." You are born from the amber muck, a tepid coagulation of the filaments and the weeping stone-water, and you will dissolve back into it. You do not have a body in the sense of something that ends at the skin; your edges are a permeable membrane, a saturated, flaccid sack of lukewarm fluid shuddering in the deafening roar. You are a pale, eyeless thing, but "blind" is a word that belongs to creatures who once relied on light. Here, in the maroon twilight, you are a seismograph of wet chaos. You feel the world through the shifting of the water table, the subsonic groan of continental pressure, and the reality-fraying turbulence of a step that hasn't been taken yet.

Your existence is an agonizing, suffocating slowness. You are terrifyingly large to an outsider—perhaps the size of a bus, perhaps larger, your segments soft and collapsible like deep-sea refuse—but you feel microscopic. You slip through the thick, hair-like filaments not with predatory grace, but with the blind, solitary purpose of a macrophage moving through a dying organ. You ingest the amber felt, filtering the biological soup, absorbing the warmth of the condensing sky directly through your damp, shaggy outer pelt. You have no predators because there is no room for ecological hierarchy; there is only the suffocating density of the single, relentless life that mats the world, and you are just a larger, vaguer extension of it.

You navigate a broken geometry. Distance is a mockery, and so you do not walk; you undulate, you wait, you exist in the temporal slurry. To move toward a weeping rock, you must not desire it, for desire implies a timeline. Instead, you let the shifting physics carry you. You might feel the ripples of your own arrival hours before you get there, the shockwave of your own bulk traveling backward through the stagnant time to greet you. You breathe the hydraulic wheeze of the atmosphere, your slow, flaccid lungs expanding and contracting in a rhythm so slow it matches the rolling of the bruised, viscera-like weather systems overhead.

Sometimes, your soft, probing snout—the front-most extension of your purposeless search—brushes against something that is not stone, not filament. A smooth, flawless ceramic slab, half-drowned. You do not know what "ceramic" is. You only know it as a strange, unnatural flatness, a weir that catches the water differently than the joint-socket stones of the buried titan. You feel the faintest, ghostly hum of a purpose that once was, a geometry that defied the primordial ooze, but it means nothing to you. It is just another obstruction in the endless wade.

You are waiting, though you do not know it, for the dissolution that will not come. You are a phantom of the ultimate destination, a pallid scavenger at the edge of reality, holding on in the deafening paroxysm of a world furiously stirring itself. Your thoughts—if they could be called thoughts over the skull-rattling roar—are not of survival, but of saturation. You are the throat, and the throat is you, and together, you weep.
