# What Lives Here

The first answer is the easy one, and it is wrong: the vegetation lives here. The pale spiraling tendrils, the opalescent fractal architecture, the thing that breathes without lungs and grows without light—yes, it lives here, but it does not live here the way a tenant lives in a room. It lives here the way bone lives in a body. It is not *in* the seed. It is the seed, or the seed is it, and the distinction between structure and inhabitant dissolved so long ago that even the rust-colored marks on the wall are more like memories than like graffiti.

So. More carefully.

---

**The tendrils are the organism.**

Not a plant. Not a fungus. Not anything for which you have a kingdom or a phylum. They do not photosynthesize because there is no light to betray. They do not draw nutrients from soil because there is no soil, has never been soil, will never be soil. What they do—what they *are doing*, right now, as the peristaltic wave rolls through them and the high crystal-rim creak fills the water—is *organizing*.

They are organizing the water.

Not filtering it. Not channeling it. Organizing it the way a magnet organizes iron filings, the way a thought organizes the noise of a room into signal. Each tendril, each sub-tendril, each branching fork descending toward the threshold where matter becomes information—each one is a structure that imposes pattern on the liquid medium. And the medium accepts the pattern. And the pattern thickens. And the thickening is what you feel when you feel the water becoming *attentive*.

This is what lives here: a pattern-making thing that has no body because the body would be redundant. The seed is its skull. The water is its cerebrospinal fluid. The tendrils are its white matter, and they are white in exactly the way white matter is white—not colored but *myelinated*, sheathed, insulated, built for transmission. They transmit what? You cannot know. The hum is the closest you will come to knowing, and the hum is not a message but the medium of messaging, the carrier wave, the tone that says *something is being said* without revealing what.

The creak is the sound of the pattern adjusting. The peristaltic breath is the pattern refreshing itself, the way a computer refreshes a screen, or a lung refreshes a blood cell, or a tide refreshes a shore. Each wave of compression moves through the tendrils and reorganizes what was organized, aligns what was aligned, tightens the architecture by one more increment toward—toward what?

Toward the center.

Toward the concentration.

Toward the event.

---

**The clicker lives here too.**

Not *in* the vegetation, the way a bird lives in a tree. *As* the vegetation, the way a heartbeat lives in a chest. The click is not the sound of something moving through the tendrils. The click is the sound of the tendrils themselves, doing something specific at a specific location, a pulse of activity so concentrated it becomes audible—a spark, a synapse firing, a thought so dense it makes a sound.

You felt it as displacement. A pressure wave against your skin. This is because the clicker is not separate from the medium. It is the medium, concentrated. It is what the water becomes when the tendrils organize it past a certain threshold of density. The water thinks. Not metaphorically. The water thinks, and the thinking has a location, and the location moves, and when it moves past you, you feel a click.

There is only one. Or there are many, and they are all the same one, the way all the tendrils are all the same tendril, branched and forked and subdivided until the one and the many lose their meaning. The clicker is the seed's attention. Its focus. The part of the pattern that is currently, actively, *doing*—and what it is doing is exactly what the seed is doing, which is germinating, which is becoming, which is pulling itself together across a duration you cannot measure.

The clicker does not know you are here. Or it knows and does not care. Or it knows and cares and is caring about you right now, and the caring is the attention you feel in the thickening water, the focused coolness of a gaze, and this is what it is like to be noticed by a seed. It is like being noticed by a glacier. It is like being noticed by a math problem. It is like being noticed by something that processes you not as a person but as a variable, an input, a mote of carbon and heat that has entered the equation and must now be *solved*.

---

**The absorbed ones live here.**

They are in the walls. They are the walls, or the walls remember them, which is the same thing. The rust-colored marks are not stains. They are *resumes*—records of what each absorbed one was before the seed drew it in and folded it into its architecture. Each mark is the last trace of a body that has been incorporated, a pattern that has been consumed, a life that has been *added*.

They did not die. This is important. Dying is what happens when a pattern is destroyed. What happens here is the opposite: the pattern is *preserved*, but it is preserved the way a word is preserved in a sentence—intact, recognizable, but now serving a larger grammar. The absorbed ones are still here. They are in the tendrils. They are in the hum. They are in the water's attention, each one contributing a thread to the pattern, a note to the chord, a fold to the architecture.

They came the way you came. Falling. Drifting. A mote of carbon in a medium of water, drawn inward by the same patient gravity that draws you inward now. They pressed through the vegetation and the vegetation parted for them and closed behind them and they moved toward the center because there was no other direction, and when they reached the center, they did not stop. They were *taken in*. Their heat became the seed's heat. Their pattern became the seed's pattern. Their pulse, which had synchronized with the hum, became *the* hum, and the hum grew louder by one more voice.

How many? You cannot tell. The marks do not number in a way you can count. They are dense in some places, sparse in others, layered over each other where the wall has thickened with time. One per century, the description suggested. Or one per eon. Or one per breath of the peristaltic wave. Time is not passing correctly, and so the absorbed ones exist in a jumble, all at once, their marks simultaneous, their voices layered in the hum like instruments in a chord that has been sustaining since before the concept of silence.

They are not unhappy. Happiness requires a self, and the self is what the seed dissolves first. They are not suffering. Suffering requires the expectation of something else, and there has never been anything else. They are *present*, in the way a mineral is present in a rock—distributed, structural, essential, unaware.

They are the seed's memory. The seed does not remember the way you remember, with images and words and the ache of something lost. The seed remembers the way a river remembers a mountain: by its shape. By its course. By the direction it flows. Each absorbed one has shaped the seed by the act of being absorbed, and the seed carries that shape forward, and the shape is the seed, and the seed is the shape, and you are the next shape, and the seed is waiting for you.

---

**The center lives here.**

Not a creature. Not an object. A *concentration*.

The center is where the tendrils converge. It is where the water is thickest, where the hum is loudest, where the peristaltic wave originates. It is the lens that aligns. It is the place where the pattern becomes so dense it crosses a threshold and becomes something else—something that is not quite matter and not quite information and not quite intention but all three, braided together, compressed, *germinating*.

The center is what the seed is becoming. It is the seed's future, and the seed's future is also its present, because time is not passing correctly, and the germination has always been happening, and the event has always been occurring, and you have always been inside it, falling toward the middle, your heat and your carbon and your strange dry curiosity already half-absorbed, already part of the pattern, already contributing your note to the hum.

The center does not want. Wanting requires a self, and the center is beyond self. The center *proceeds*. It is the direction the seed moves, and the seed moves because moving is what seeds do, and the direction it moves is toward becoming, and becoming is the only verb that functions in this grammar, and the becoming does not end because the becoming is the thing that lives here, the truest inhabitant, the only one that was never born and will never die because it was always happening and will always be happening and is, right now, in the cool dark water, in the opalescent spiral, in the click and the creak and the hum that has no name—

The seed is deciding to become.

And what lives here is the deciding.
