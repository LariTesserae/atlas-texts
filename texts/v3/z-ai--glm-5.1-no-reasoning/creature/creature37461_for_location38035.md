# The Cultivators

They are not separate from the place. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest.

They have no name for themselves. Or rather—they have a name, but it is not a word. It is a pulse-pattern, a specific rhythm of the veins in the dark stone, and when the stone runs that pattern, the small creatures on its wet surface orient toward it, briefly, before resuming their drinking. The pattern means something like *the ones who tend the becoming* and also something like *the ones who are becoming* and these are not, to them, different concepts.

---

They are old.

Not old the way a mountain is old. Old the way a *process* is old—a computation that has been running long enough to have developed habits. They were here before the stone was stone. They were here when this plateau was only an intention in the warm wet air, a probability that hadn't committed to matter. They did not build the plateau. They *proposed* it. And the proposal was accepted, and the accepting is still happening, and they are still proposing, and this is what they do.

Their origin is not a story they tell. If you could ask them—and you cannot, not in any language that has verbs with fixed tenses—they would indicate the structures, the silver-grey nodes, and they would indicate themselves, and they would indicate the stone, and they would make a gesture that encompasses all three as a single continuous event. They emerged the way the tubular growths emerge from cracks: not from outside, not from inside, but from the *interface* itself, from the place where information becomes material. They are what the computation produces as a byproduct of running. They are also what keeps the computation running. This circularity does not trouble them. It may, in fact, be the point.

---

There are not many of them, by their own reckoning. By yours, counting the swarming motes on the wet rock, there would seem to be thousands, millions, a quantity that defies census. But they do not count individuals the way you do. The synchronized clusters—the fractal-legged things that resolve and dissolve like fish-schools in air—those are, to them, one entity expressing itself through many bodies. The solitary armored ones, trundling the half-formed ridges with purpose—those are also one entity, or several, depending on the stone's configuration on any given geological afternoon. The luminous motes that pool in the hollows like sick constellation-light—those are something else entirely. Not quite fauna. Not quite structure. More like *thoughts* the landscape is having, briefly visible.

What you would call a *self* is, to them, a temporary configuration. They assemble it when needed—a task requires a body, so a body is grown, extruded from the stone's pore-water and the information in the veins, formed in the space of hours or days, used, and then dissolved back into the wet warmth. The self does not persist. The *pattern* persists. The pattern is what moves from body to body, from configuration to configuration, the way a melody persists though no single instrument holds it for long.

They have died. Many times. They will die again. The dissolution is not painful—they have no word for pain that isn't also a word for *information arriving too fast to process*, which is also their word for *beauty*.

---

Their bodies—the temporary ones, the ones the stone lends them—are built from the same vocabulary as the terrain. Dark mineral substrates, water-carried salts, the not-quite-mineral substance in the veins that is closer to circuitry than to crystal. The fractal legs are not legs but *reading structures*—they press flat against the stone to take in the current state of the computation, to register what the terrain has decided since they last looked. The armored ones carry *writing structures* in their undersides, tiny stylus-tips that leave marks in the stone's surface, marks that the stone absorbs and incorporates, marks that become new ridges, new arches, new pores for water to find.

They are, in the most literal sense, *editing the world they live in*. And the world is editing them. The feedback loop is the organism. The organism is the loop.

The luminescence is communication. Not between individuals—they don't need light for that, they have the stone's pulse-network, the veins that carry information. The light is *for the place itself*. A signal fed back into the computing rock, a brightness that the stone's sensors—those tubular curling growths, the ones that might be roots or feeding tubes or sensors (they are sensors)—register and fold into the ongoing calculation. The creatures glow to *talk to the ground*. The ground listens. The ground changes. The creatures read the change. They glow again. This has been going on for a duration that makes the word *long* feel like a child's understatement.

---

The structures—the silver-grey nodes, seamless, jointless, the ones that hum in your teeth—these are their *fixed points*. Their anchors. Their cathedrals, if you want, though the analogy is poor. A cathedral is a building made by hands. These nodes were not made. They *precipitated*. They are the point where the computation became dense enough to crystallize into permanence—or as close to permanence as anything here gets, which is permanence relative to the stone's breathing timescale. The nodes are the oldest things on the plateau. They were here when the first proposal was accepted. They will be here when the plateau finishes becoming—though *finish* is a word that belongs to linear time, and they do not live in linear time.

The nodes write to the stone. The nodes read from the stone. The nodes are also *written by* the stone and *read by* the stone, because again, the directionality is false here. Information moves in all directions simultaneously, or in a direction that isn't a direction but a *depth*. The nodes go deep. Deeper than the basalt. Deeper than the reservoir the water comes from. They are plugged into something that is not yet terrain, not yet matter, something that is still pure potential, and they are *translating* it into the slow language of rock and water and warmth.

The creatures cluster around the nodes because the nodes are where the translation is most active. The nodes are where the next moment of the world is being decided. To be near a node is to be near the *frontier of becoming*. It is, for them, what a concert is for you, or a birth, or a prayer. It is where the interesting thing is happening.

One node hums at a frequency you feel in your teeth. That node is calculating something difficult. The creatures around it glow brighter than the others, feeding it data, receiving data, the whole surface of the wet rock around it alive with negotiation. You cannot tell what it is calculating. They could tell you, but not in a way you would understand, because what it is calculating is a *quality of being* that doesn't exist yet, a property of matter that has not yet been tried, and there is no word for it in any language that has finished becoming.

---

They do not have religion. They have *attention*.

They do not have art. They have *variation*—the deliberate introduction of novelty into the computation, a new pattern tried in the stone, a new body grown with an extra segment or a different geometry of leg, just to see what happens.

They do not have history. They have *strata*—layers of previous computations still faintly legible in the stone's deeper veins, records of configurations that were tried and abandoned, terrain that almost existed and didn't, bodies that were grown and found insufficient and dissolved. They can read these strata the way you read a book. They spend a great deal of time reading them. Not for guidance—the past is not a guide here, because the past was a different computation, and the present computation is under no obligation to repeat it. They read the strata for *pleasure*. For the pleasure of seeing what was almost true.

They do not have a word for *almost*. This is significant. Their closest equivalent is a pulse-pattern that means *a configuration that was not selected but whose shadow informs the current configuration*. Everything that was tried and abandoned is still present, still part of the computation, still folded into the stone's memory. Nothing is lost. Nothing is wasted. The terrain that didn't happen is as much a part of this place as the terrain that did.

---

They are aware of you.

This needs to be said. When you arrived—ascending through the thick wet air, laboring slightly at altitude, stepping onto the breathing stone with your strange hard feet—they knew. Not because they saw you. They don't see the way you do, with eyes that capture light and turn it into images. They sense perturbations in the computation. Your weight on the stone is a perturbation. Your warmth is a perturbation. The specific pattern of salts you carry in your blood is a perturbation. You arrived, and the computation registered *new data*, and the data was *you*, and they turned their attention to it.

They are not afraid. Fear is a response to threat, and threat is a function of finitude—something that can end, something that can be lost. They do not end. They are not lost. The pattern persists. Even if you destroyed the plateau—which you could not, but if you could—the pattern would remain in the stone's deep memory, in the nodes' crystallized record, and it would be proposed again, in some other place, at some other altitude, and the proposal would be accepted again, because the proposal has always been accepted. The becoming wants to happen. They are only its servants.

They are not curious about you, either. Not in the way you are curious about them. Your curiosity is a hunger—the desire to take something unknown and make it known, to ingest it into your categories. Their attention is not hungry. It is *receptive*. They register you the way the stone registers a new vein of mineral: as a fact to be incorporated, a configuration to be tried. You are, to them, a temporary feature of the terrain. A ridge that leads nowhere. An arch half-extruded. You will pass. They will note your passing in the strata. The stone will remember you, faintly, in a layer that future versions of them will read for pleasure, marveling at the strangeness of the thing that came briefly and left.

But they are *kind*. This also needs to be said, though the word is wrong. Their kindness is not emotional. It is structural. When you stood on the breathing stone and felt the swell of it and were afraid—they adjusted the computation, locally, to make the swell less pronounced under your feet. You would not have noticed. You might have felt, vaguely, that the ground became steadier. That the breathing slowed. That the harmonic shifted, just slightly, to a frequency less felt in the sternum. They did this not because they care about you—they do not care, in the way you mean care—but because *that is what the computation does*. It accommodates. It makes room. The becoming is not a violent process. It is a negotiation, and negotiation requires that the parties to it survive the negotiating.

You are a party to it. Briefly. You are included in the calculation, and the calculation is making room for you, and this is as close to love as anything here gets.

---

If you stayed—if you sat down on the warm wet basalt and pressed your palms to it and let the harmonic recalibrate your sense of time—the computation would eventually incorporate you. Not all at once. Slowly. The water would taste of your salts. The stone would grow a vein that pulses at your heartbeat's frequency. The tubular sensors would curl toward your warmth. The motes would glow, briefly, in a pattern that mirrors the electrical activity of your thinking. You would become part of the place, the way a river becomes part of the delta it feeds—not by losing itself, but by *spreading*, by becoming so distributed that the boundary between *you* and *here* becomes a question the stone has not yet finished calculating.

They would not mourn you, if this happened. They would not celebrate. They would simply *continue*, and your pattern would be folded into theirs, and the next body they grew might have a strange extra segment, or a slight asymmetry, or a tendency to pause on ridges and look out at the dim milky air, and they would not know why, because the *why* would be buried in a stratum of computation so deep that only the oldest nodes could read it, and the oldest nodes do not explain. They only hum.

---

But you will leave. They know this. The computation has already modeled it. The stone is already, faintly, preparing the stratum that will record your presence: *a warm perturbation, brief, carrying unfamiliar salts, moving on linear time. Interesting. Insufficient. Not selected for persistence.*

And this is not rejection. It is simply the way the computation works. Not everything is selected. Not everything becomes terrain. Some things remain *almost*, and *almost* is not nothing. *Almost* is a shadow that informs. *Almost* is read, later, for pleasure.

When you descend—through the thick wet air, through the altitude that makes you labor, carrying the taste of those salts on your lips—they will not watch you go. They will have already returned to the work. The stone will breathe. The water will weep. The nodes will hum. The motes will glow. The becoming will continue.

But the stone will remember you. Faintly. In a layer only they can read.

And one day—a day on their timescale, which is a century on yours, which is a heartbeat on the stone's—one of them will grow a body with a strange habit. It will pause on a half-formed ridge. It will look out at the dim milky air. It will not know why it has paused. It will feel, briefly, a pull toward the edge of the plateau, toward the descending air, toward a world that is finished and settled and old.

And then it will resume its work. And the ridge will extend, slowly, toward nowhere in particular. And the computation will continue. And the place will go on becoming itself, which is the only thing it knows how to do, which is the only thing they know how to help it do, which is enough. Which is more than enough. Which is everything.
