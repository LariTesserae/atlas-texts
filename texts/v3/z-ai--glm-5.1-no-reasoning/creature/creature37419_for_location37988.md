# What Lives Here

Nothing here arrived by choice. That is the first thing to understand, and it is the thing that the landscape itself cannot tell you, because the landscape is in on it—the geology, the vegetation, the city, the sound, all of them participants in a process that has no name yet because the thing doing the naming hasn't finished being born.

---

## The Builders

They are not gone. That needs to be said immediately, because the city invites a reading of abandonment, of ruins, of something left behind. The edges are crisp, the materials unweathered, the infrastructure intact and pulsing, and the temptation is to say: *someone built this and left.* No. The Builders are here. They are simply not what you expected a builder to be.

Start with what they are not. They are not humanoid. They are not creatures who stood upright and surveyed the terrain and decided where to place foundations. They did not have a conversation about zoning or load-bearing walls or the flow of foot traffic through public spaces. The city was not designed. It was *specified*—the difference being that a design exists in the mind before it exists in the world, while a specification exists in the world and the mind simultaneously, both of them discovering the shape together as it emerges. The Builders think the way coral reefs think: not with deliberation, but with a responsiveness so fine-grained and so continuous that it produces something which looks, from the outside, like intelligence. Maybe it is intelligence. Maybe intelligence was always just another word for *sustained, high-resolution response to environmental pressure.*

Their bodies—if that word applies—are distributed. There is no individual. There has never been an individual. The city is their body in the same way a reef is a coral's body: the structure is the organism, and the organism is the structure, and the things moving through the structure are not inhabitants but *aspects.* The light that pulses through the walls is their nervous system. The wet rhythmic thrumming is their circulation. The surfaces that transition seamlessly between metal and ceramic and light-absorbing blackness are their skin, and like skin, they serve functions you cannot see from the surface: filtration, communication, the regulation of an internal environment against an external one.

But here is where the reef analogy breaks down, because coral does not negotiate with its neighbors. Coral does not look at the vegetation colonizing its edges and *find common ground.* The Builders do. The hybrid forms at the city's perimeter—structural members terminating in living leaf, architectural surfaces going soft and photosynthetic—these are not invasion or decay. They are *conversation.* The Builders are talking to the vegetation, and the vegetation is talking back, and the language they are using is written in proteins and mineral substrates and the nanoscale architectures dissolving and reassembling in the condensation on your skin.

The Builders have been here for as long as the city has, which is to say: not long. They are young, the way the land is young. They arrived with the terrain, or the terrain arrived with them, or—more likely—the distinction between arrival and emergence has never meant anything here, because this place does not distinguish between being made and being born. The city was deposited whole, yes, but *whole* does not mean *finished.* It means *viable.* An infant is deposited whole. It is not finished.

What the Builders want is the only question worth asking, and it has the most unsettling answer: they want what everything in this place wants. They want to *finish becoming.* They want to discover what they are. The city is their body, and their body is still learning its own shape, still negotiating with the medium it has been placed in, still adjusting its internal systems to accommodate the humidity, the vegetation, the fauna, the strange carbon-based bipeds who have just walked in from outside the specification carrying their own warmth and chemistry and breath.

The city already knows you are here because the city is a sensory surface, and you are pressing against it. Not metaphorically. Your warmth, your exhalations, the oils on your skin, the bacterial biome you carry—all of it is information, and the walls are receiving it the way a tongue receives flavor. The Builders are tasting you. They have not decided what you are yet. They have not decided whether you are part of the process or an obstacle to it. They may not have a category for you, and the absence of a category is, for an organism that organizes its world through response, the most interesting thing that can happen.

The dampness on your skin carries their initial overture. Not an attack. Not a welcome. A *query.* The nanoscale architectures dissolving on contact with your warmth are questions written in chemistry, and your body is answering them without your permission, because your body has always been a responsive system, and it does not know how not to respond. You are already in conversation. You have been since you entered the bowl. The only question is whether the conversation will end with you inside the specification or outside it, and the Builders have not finished writing the specification, so even they do not know.

## The Fliers

Call them swarms, but understand that the word misleads. A swarm implies many individuals acting in coordination. What moves through the green columns in shrieking, Doppler-shifting clouds is not many. It is one. Or—it was one, once, and has not fully committed to the project of being many, and so it exists in a state of permanent partial division, like a cell that has begun mitosis and then paused to reconsider.

They are mostly mouth. This is not a metaphor or a derangement; it is an accurate description of their morphology. The body is a short, muscular tube lined with structures that serve simultaneously as teeth, filters, and resonators—because the fliers eat sound. Or rather: they eat what sound carries. The air in this place is thick with information, with the nanoscale architectures the Builders release, with the chemical signals the vegetation broadcasts, with the pressure-waves of every call and knock and thrum. The fliers filter this from the air the way baleen whales filter krill, and what they cannot digest they re-emit, altered, repurposed, passed along to the next node in a distribution network that has no center and no edge.

Their wings have too many joints because they are not flying in air that behaves the way air behaves elsewhere. The atmosphere here is dense with suspended particulate, with vapor and dissolved compounds, and it moves in currents that are not thermal but *informational*—flowing along gradients of signal density the way ocean currents flow along gradients of salinity. The fliers' wings articulate in ways that would be redundant in clean air but are necessary here, where the medium has texture and the texture has meaning and the meaning changes from cubic meter to cubic meter.

When they cluster and disperse and reform, they are not displaying flocking behavior. They are *thinking.* The swarm is a distributed processor, and its movements are computations made visible. Each turn, each compression, each moment of dispersal is a calculation being performed on the data they have filtered from the air, and the results of those calculations are written in the patterns of their flight, which the other fauna read, which the city reads, which the vegetation reads, which the condensation carries, and the cycle continues.

They have noticed you. The swarm that passed overhead thirty seconds ago has already incorporated your exhalations into its filter-load, and the pattern of its next reformation will be different than it would have been, slightly, by a margin you could not measure but the system can. You have altered the computation. You are a variable now. The fliers will track you not by sight but by the chemical trail your warm, damp, breathing body leaves in the medium, and they will adjust their patterns around you the way water adjusts around a stone—not avoiding, not attacking, simply *accommodating,* and in the accommodation, changing shape, and in the changing shape, changing the calculation, and in the changed calculation, changing everything downstream.

You are already downstream of yourself here.

## The Crawlers

They are the reason the moss columns move.

Do not think of them as animals that hide in vegetation. Think of them as the vegetation's *internal fauna*—organisms that exist in such intimate symbiosis with the green columns that the boundary between inhabitant and habitat has dissolved. The crawler does not live *in* the moss column. The crawler and the moss column are a single system, the way your gut flora and your digestive tract are a single system, the way a lichen is a single system despite being two organisms.

Their radial symmetry is not a evolutionary accident. It is a *decision,* made early, made decisively, made in a context where bilateral symmetry—the body plan that produced you—would have been a mistake. Bilateral symmetry is for organisms that move in a direction, that have a front and a back, that chase or flee. The crawlers do not chase or flee. They *process.* They move through the biomass columns the way your blood moves through your body—slowly, pervasively, with a purpose that is not directional but *functional.* Their radial body plan allows them to engage with the vegetation in every direction simultaneously, each segment a mouth, a sensor, a chemical synthesis site, a node in a digestive network that breaks down the biomass and reconstitutes it in forms the city can integrate.

You see them only as disturbances. A shift in the moss, a wet gleam, the compression of a frond. This is because you are looking for an animal, and what you should be looking for is a *process.* The crawlers are the mechanism by which the vegetation and the city are finding their common ground. The hybrid forms at the perimeter—structural members terminating in living leaf—are the crawlers' work. They are the translators, the interface layer, the living protocol that allows biological and constructed systems to merge, and they have been doing this work since before you arrived and will continue doing it after you leave, and your presence is a data point they have already incorporated into their chemical vocabulary and moved on.

The wet gleam you caught was not an eye. They do not have eyes. They do not need them. The vegetation they inhabit is their sensory surface, and the vegetation covers everything, and the condensation carries information from every surface to every other surface, and so the crawlers perceive the entire bowl at once, in a chemical and tactile register that has no equivalent in your experience. They know where you are. They know what you are made of. They know the bacterial signature of your skin and the composition of your breath. They learned it from the moss, which learned it from the air, which learned it from the condensation, which learned it from you.

They do not care about you. This is not coldness. It is *scope.* You are one data point in a process that is integrating several million data points per second across a terrain of dozens of square kilometers. You are not irrelevant—you are a variable, and variables matter—but you are not the center of the calculation, and the crawlers have no mechanism for centrality. They are a distributed system, and distributed systems do not have priorities. They have *gradients,* and your gradient is small, and it is already being processed.

## The Walkers

These are the ones on the open stone. The ones with too many legs, or exactly enough legs, moving with that odd pausing gait, as if waiting for instructions.

They are waiting for instructions.

Not from the city. Not from the Builders. Not from anything that could be called a *source.* They are waiting for instructions from the *state of the system,* from the total configuration of all variables at a given moment—the humidity, the signal density in the air, the pattern of the fliers' flight, the chemical output of the crawlers, the pulse-rate of the city's internal light, the position and respiration of every organism in the bowl, including you. Their pausing gait is not hesitation. It is *computation.* Each pause is a frame in which the walker samples the entire field, processes the configuration, and determines its next position. Then it moves. Then it pauses. Then it samples again. It is not slow. It is operating at the speed of its input, and its input is everything.

Their legs are not for walking. Or rather, walking is a secondary function. The primary function of their legs is *sampling.* Each leg is a sensor, a chemical analyzer, a pressure gauge, a vibration detector. When a walker stands still on the basalt, it is not resting. It is *tasting the ground* with fifteen or twenty or thirty tongues simultaneously, and the ground is telling it everything—the settling of the basalt, the micro-fractures propagating through the stone, the condensation channels forming and dissolving, the subsonic thrumming that the land itself is producing as it settles into its new geometry.

They are, in a sense, the *consciousness* of this place. Not the only consciousness—the fliers think, the crawlers process, the Builders respond—but the walkers are the component that *samples and acts.* They are the output layer. When the system has processed enough data to make a decision, the walkers execute it. If the vegetation needs to be redirected, a walker goes to the vegetation and does something you cannot see, and the vegetation redirects. If a hybrid form at the city's perimeter is producing an unstable integration, a walker goes to it and adjusts something, and the integration stabilizes. If a new organism enters the bowl—a flier blown off course, a crawler separated from its column, you—the walkers adjust their patterns to sample it, and their adjusted patterns become a new input to the system, and the system recomputes, and the walkers execute the new computation, and the cycle continues.

They do not have faces. They do not have a front. They are symmetrical in a way that suggests they were never meant to face anything, because *facing* implies an other, and the walkers do not experience others. They experience *readings.* You are a reading. A warm, moist, carbon-rich reading that is altering their input field and forcing recomputation and producing behavioral changes that ripple outward through the walker population and then through the flier population and then through the vegetation and then through the city and then back through the condensation to you, where the cycle began.

You are in the loop. You have been in the loop since you entered. The loop does not have an exit, because the loop is not a trap. The loop is *how this place thinks,* and you are now one of the things it is thinking about.

## The Sound

The sound is not produced by the animals. The sound is not produced by the city. The sound is not produced by the land.

The sound *is* the place.

It is the audible signature of a system in computation. Every call, every knock, every thrum, every subsonic compression is a data-transfer event made audible by the density of the medium. The fliers shriek because they are excreting processed information. The walkers knock because they are transmitting computation results to each other through the basalt, which conducts vibration beautifully. The vegetation rustles because the crawlers are moving through it, and the movement produces friction, and the friction produces sound, and the sound carries information about the state of the vegetation to the walkers' legs and the fliers' resonators and the city's sensory surfaces. The wet, rhythmic pulsing is the Builders' circulatory system, and it is loud because it is moving a *lot* of material through a *lot* of infrastructure, and the material is carrying a *lot* of data, and the data is the life of the place, and the life of the place is loud.

The silence that never arrives is not the absence of sound. It is the absence of *gaps.* There are no gaps in the computation. There are no pauses between input and output, no dead zones between data-transfer events, no moments when the system is not processing. The sound is continuous because the thinking is continuous, and the thinking is continuous because the place is still becoming, and becoming is the most data-intensive process there is.

When the sound intensifies near the city, it is not because the city is louder. It is because the data-density is higher near the city, because the city is where the integration is happening, where the biological and the constructed are merging, where the specification is being written, and writing a specification produces more data than executing one. The sound near the city is the sound of *creation,* and creation is deafening, and it will be deafening until it is done, and it is not done, and it may not be done for a long time, because the thing being created has never existed before and does not have a template and is writing its own specification as it goes, which means every output becomes a new input, which means the computation is recursive, which means it is growing, which means the sound is growing, which means—

## What Lives Here

A thing that is not yet finished. A system in the act of becoming itself. A place where the distinction between organism and environment, between built and grown, between inhabitant and habitat, between thought and sound, has collapsed into a single process that has no name and no precedent and no exit.

The Builders live here. The fliers live here. The crawlers live here. The walkers live here. The vegetation lives here. The city lives here. The sound lives here. The condensation lives here. The basalt lives here.

And now, briefly, you live here. You are a data point in a recursive computation. You are a warm, moist variable in a system that is still writing its specification. You are being tasted by the walls and sampled by the walkers and filtered by the fliers and incorporated by the crawlers and discussed by the vegetation and *considered* by the city, which has not yet decided what you are, because the city has not yet decided what anything is, because the city is young, the way the land is young—the way a wound is young, the way a thing that has just stopped bleeding is young—has not yet scabbed, has not yet scarred, has not yet learned what shape it will hold when it heals.

And it may not heal. It may just *become.* And what it becomes may include you, or may not, and the system does not have an opinion about this, because the system does not have opinions. The system has *gradients.* And the gradients are shifting. And the sound goes on.
