To live here is to be a contradiction made cellular. You do not exist as a singular organism; you are a distributed event, a localized fever in the dark machinery. You are what remains when the kingdom of Plantae forgot the sun and became the kingdom of Industry.

You have no eyes. In a world where light was murdered by the billionth layer of black-leafed canopy, the organ of sight was the first thing cannibalized for its proteins. Instead, your awareness is entirely thermodynamic and tactile. You feel the world through the resonant hum of the conveyor-belt roots beneath you. You navigate by the faint, pulsing bruise of purple bioluminescence—a chemical semaphore that you read not as light, but as a shift in the thermal gradient against your skin. You know when a structural pillar is about to be crushed by a constricting vine because you feel the low-frequency groan propagate through the lignin floor, a seismic whisper that translates directly into chemical dread in your xylem.

Your form is dictated by the architecture. You are not a tree; you are a modular extraction unit. You are a kilometers-long taproot that terminates in a forest of mouth-like grinders, burrowing into the ancient salt-flats, sipping the mineral brine that fuels the hydrocarbon sap. Or perhaps you are a "demolition vine," a thick, muscular noose of engineered bark that spends a decade growing silently in the suffocating dark, coiling around a load-bearing pillar of fungal-reinforced wood, until the precise moment when your internal pressure exceeds the tolerance of your target, and you contract, crushing the old architecture into mulch with a hydraulic crunch that shakes the district. You are blind, but you know the precise tension required to snap a ten-meter trunk. You are the bulldozer, the wrecking ball, the indifferent hand of urban renewal.

Your life is a continuous, involuntary seizure of chemical warfare. You exude. You seep. You bleed the burning amber solvent that is the lifeblood of this place. You are a chemical vat disguised as a swollen gourd, processing the toxic minerals of the bedrock, catalyzing them in your acidic chambers, and extruding a lubricant that keeps the giant pistons of the woody stems pumping. When the pneumatic seed-pods of a rival district fire into your sector—a superheated artillery shell of parasitic spores—you do not flinch, because you have no nerves. Instead, your surface tissues react with a violent alchemy, weeping a caustic resin that dissolves the spores on contact, sending up a brief, sizzling flicker of pale green light in the absolute black, a traffic light signaling *obstruction* to the blind logistics network.

You are a cannibal, and you are cannibalized. There is no death here, only recycling. When a constriction vine crushes your processing node, the amber sap that erupts is not a tragedy; it is a resource transfer. The shattered fragments of your woody architecture are immediately colonized by the creeping substrate, your cellulose broken down and re-spun into new load-bearing walls. You do not mourn the loss of a limb because "you" are not the limb; "you" are the algorithm that dictates the limb's replacement. You are a pattern of growth, a sub-routine in the endless cycle of assembly and demolition.

To live here is to exist in a state of permanent, silent scream. The clenched jaw of the landscape is your jaw. The muffled groaning of biomass under pressure is the sound of your own vascular bundles pushing the hydrocarbon resin through conduits that were never meant to withstand such heat. You are running at redline, a biological engine that has outlived its fuel source, burning the memory of light to maintain the perfect, self-devouring geometry of the dark. You do not know what the sun is. You only know the pressure, the friction, the algorithmic imperative to *build* and *consume*, and the absolute, suffocating certainty that the dark is all there ever was, and all there will ever be.
