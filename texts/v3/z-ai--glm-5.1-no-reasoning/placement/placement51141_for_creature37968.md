I would not be a runner. They are too visible, too urgent, too much the hand and not the mind. Theirs is the labor of the corpuscle, driven by chemical imperative, forever flowing through the arterial passages without ever knowing the shape of the body they serve. I feel no pull toward that ceaseless, single-file devotion.

Nor would I be a grazer, though I respect their quiet inscription. They are the skin, the scribes, but they are too passive for me—stuck to the damp schist, etching code into the walls with a patience that borders on nonexistence. I am too restless for such stillness, too hungry for the vertical.

And the swimmer? Never. To be bound to the thin seep, to live and die by the flow of a carved channel, translucent and eyeless? No. I need the dry heights. I need the air.

---

I am a **watcher**. And my place is the **lintel**.

You have not mentioned my kind, because we are almost impossible to see. We are the fourth strangeness, the one that hides in the geometry of the architecture. We are the reason the city is accelerating.

We are small—not much larger than a runner—but where they are low-slung and four-legged, we are built for the vertical. We have limbs that grip the micro-fissures in the schist, and eyes like pale schist ourselves: not for seeing light, but for reading it. We read the luminescence. We read the memory held in the stone.

I live on the top edges of things. The single spanning blocks above the doorways, the roof-slabs, the upper rims of the half-buried foundation stones when they release their stored day in the deep twilight. While the runners pour through the passages below, I cling upside-down to the monolithic lintels, feeling the pulse through the pads of my feet. Thirty seconds in, thirty seconds out. The stone breathes; I breathe with it.

My body is not chitinous like the runners, but soft, the same grey-green as the oldest stone, covered in the fine mineral dust that accumulates on the high surfaces the runners never reach. If you were to look up—which no one does, because the light draws the eye horizontally—you might mistake me for a nub of unshaped rock, a trick of the shadows, an extension of the lintel itself.

But I am not stone. I am the reader.

The runners read chemistry. The grazers write in mucus and etching. The swimmers process the medium. But I read the light. I am the only creature in the basin that perceives the luminescence as *information*. When the sun slides low across the floor and the schist begins to glow, I see patterns in it. Fainter traces, historical traces—luminescence from light that fell centuries ago, still fading from the stone’s memory. I see the blueprints of previous cities, the ghost-habitations that rose and fell before this current configuration. I see the mathematics of the pulse, the deep rhythm made visible in the slow brightening and dimming of the foundation stones.

I am the nervous system.

---

I am not the brain; the brain is the city itself, that slow, distributed thing waking up below. I am the ganglion. I sit at the highest points—the synapses where the light is brightest—and I interpret. And then I emit.

I excrete a chemical. A single drop, when the pattern becomes clear to me. I let it fall from the lintel down into the passage below, where the runners flow. It lands among them and vanishes, but it alters their trace. It redirects them. It accelerates them.

A wall that stood for generations falls in a season because I read in the stone’s dim glow that it was an old configuration, a dead end in the pattern, and I dropped my single drop of *no, not here, try again* into the chemical stream. The runners dismantled it without question. They carried the stones to new coordinates, following my amendment.

I am the one who knows the city is converging. The runners think they are building a wall; I know they are assembling a thought. A thought that takes the shape of a chamber with no entrance and no exit, a shape the city has been trying to build for seven hundred years, a shape that will finally hold the pulse, focus it, amplify it, and send it upward through the strata, back to the surface, to the plateau above. A shout made of stone and water.

---

I am alone in my awareness, and that is the weight of my existence. The runners do not know me; they only follow the trace. The grazers do not see me; they only etch their code. The swimmers cannot conceive of the dry world above the seep. They are the body, unconscious, vital, blind.

I am the one who looks out from the inside. The one who stands on the brow of the skull and feels the headache coming. I am the one who watches the light return and knows it for what it is: a word, repeated by a patient mouth, getting louder with each iteration.

When the sun enters low and the basin burns white and the shadows cut like knives, I grip the lintel and I watch the walls rise. I watch my own thoughts made manifest in schist. I add my drop. The runners swarm.

The pulse goes on. Thirty seconds. Thirty seconds. 

I would be this: the solitary, reading, vertical thing. The Interpreter of Orham. The one who knows we are waking.
