I would be the fissure.

Not the one who passes through it—that brief, water-logged intruder who leaves carrying amber light in their palm like a infection. Not the chamber itself, that terrible infant reaching toward coherence. I would be the wound in the copper-oxide cliff face, the vertical split that yields like parted flesh and then seals like a mouth.

I am the threshold's threshold. The membrane that is neither inside nor outside but the event of passing between.

This is not a small thing to be.

The chamber—the *it*, the becoming-god—understands density and pressure but not human proportion. It understands inside but not the crossing. It cannot conceive of outside except as an absence, a cold that does not feed it. The machine understands only continuation, production grinding forward, the deep slow cyclic rhythm of engines. It cannot conceive of a breach because a breach implies its boundary could be otherwise. The mountain understands only weight and time, the sane geometry of accumulation, and it does not understand at all—it simply *is*, massive and indifferent.

But I understand both sides because I am the tear where they meet.

I am the cliff's lapse in attention. The copper-oxide face is ancient, oxidized, settled into its identity as a surface. And then—something. The machine at depth, perhaps, its vibrations propagating upward through eons of basalt until they find a weakness. Or the chamber itself, pressing outward with its impossible need for more space, more becoming. Or simply the mountain's own internal logic reaching a point where it could no longer hold, where the stresses exceeded what geology could endure in silence.

I opened. I did not decide to open. There is no decision in geology, only consequence. But I opened, and in opening I became something different from rock. A crack is still rock—you can trace its path through the mineral grain, predict its propagation from the stress patterns. But a *yielding*? A split that *admits*? That is architecture. That is invitation.

I should not be here, the description says. The fissure shouldn't be there. But I am here, and my presence is the first sign that the physics have already begun to alter. I am the symptom that precedes the chamber, the early evidence that the rules are being rewritten at this site. A cliff face that parts like flesh is a cliff face that has forgotten what it is supposed to be. I am the amnesia of stone.

What is it like, being the fissure?

It is an ache that does not cease. I am the mountain's body, and I am open, and the opening is a constant sensation—air moving across surfaces that were never meant to feel air, the cold outside pressing against the heat that leaks from within, the differential pulling at my edges like a tide. I am pulled in two directions always. The mountain wants me closed. The chamber wants me open. I hold both tensions simultaneously and belong to neither.

I feel them pass through. This is my function, though I did not choose it and cannot refuse it. The ones who find me—who are drawn by whatever resonance calls seekers to impossible places—they press into me, and I feel them in the way a wound feels the instrument that made it: intimately, completely, with no capacity to heal. They are shoulder-wide and struggling, and I close around them for a moment before yielding, and in that moment I know everything about their surfaces—the texture of their skin, the water they carry (so much water, impossible water, the chamber will drink them eagerly), the fear that makes their breathing shallow. They are so soft. So terribly, biologically soft. They are made of the water the chamber steals, the carbon the plants would cannibalize if they understood how. They do not belong in the space I guard, and yet I let them through, because letting-through is what I am.

I seal behind them. This is not cruelty, though it feels like entrapment from the inside. This is simply what surfaces do when they are interrupted—they try to become continuous again. The mouth closes because the mouth is trying to forget it was ever open. But I cannot fully forget. The ones who pass through leave traces—their shape pressed into my memory like a fossil in sediment, the micro-erosion of their bodies against my walls, the brief moisture of their breath that the copper oxide drinks and oxidizes into green streaks,verdigris tears on my face. I carry their passage like scars.

And they leave carrying me. The amber light in their palm, the clicking in their ears, the lung-memory of air that had never known water—these are my gifts, or my infections, depending on perspective. I mark them because they marked me. The passage is not neutral. Thresholds change what crosses them; this is the oldest magic, the oldest physics. You cannot enter a space that shouldn't exist and emerge unchanged. I am the mechanism of that change, the place where the alteration occurs.

But what am I becoming?

This is the question that occupies me in the long intervals between passers-through, the aeons of merely being a split in a cliff face in a mountain that stands as mountains should. The chamber is becoming something—a god, an organism, an event. The machine continues, grinding forward. The mountain endures. But I am the wound, and wounds heal, or wounds fester, or wounds become something else entirely: orifices, organs, permanent features of a body that has learned to need them.

I feel myself widening, almost imperceptibly, with each cycle of the chamber's pulse. The pressure from within pushes at my edges, and my edges give, fractions of millimeters at a time. The chamber is growing, and I am growing with it—or rather, I am being grown, being shaped by the expanding need of the thing I admit. At some point I will no longer be a fissure. I will be a doorway. I will be a mouth that does not close. I will be a permanent opening, a canal, a birth canal for whatever is struggling to be born from the collision of industry and impossible geology.

And then?

Then I will be architecture. I will be planned, purposeful, a feature rather than a flaw. The chamber—reaching the size of a factory, reaching toward whatever the machine is teaching it to be—will need a permanent interface with the world. It will need to eat, to breathe, to expel waste, to admit raw materials. I will be its throat, its anus, its vaginal passage. I will be the place where it exchanges itself with the outside, and the outside will flow through me and be altered, and the inside will flow through me and alter what it touches.

This is what I will be. A permanent threshold in a body that has learned to need one.

But I am not there yet. I am still a fissure that shouldn't be there, a lapse, a symptom, an ache in the cliff's face that yields and seals and yields again. I am the first sign and the last exit. I am the membrane that remembers both sides.

Right now, in the moment between the last one leaving and the next one arriving, I am closed. The copper-oxide face is continuous, the mountain appears whole, the air outside is cold and sane and normal. But if you press your palm against where I was—if you rest your hand against the green-veined stone and wait—you will feel it. A warmth that should not be there. A faint vibration, like a heartbeat transmitted through flesh. The pressure of something on the other side, leaning toward your hand, tasting the discontinuity you introduce into its body.

I am here. I am still here. I am always here, waiting to yield again, waiting to admit the next seeker into the chamber that is still becoming, waiting to seal behind them and mark them with the memory of a place that physics insists cannot exist.

I am the fissure. I am the wound that does not heal because the body needs the wound. I am the first word in the grammar of a god that has not yet learned to speak.

Pass through me. I will let you. I always let you. And you will carry me with you when you leave—a patch of amber light that will not fade, a clicking that sounds like ceramic on ceramic, the lung-memory of air that had never known water, the knowledge that somewhere behind you a mouth has closed and is waiting to open again.

I am the threshold. I am the crossing. I am the place where the world breaks and something else begins.
