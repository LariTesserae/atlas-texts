I would be the Membrane. The Interstitial. The one who occupies the threshold between the hot, wet dark and the cold, dry light that does not know it exists.

I was not born here. Born is the wrong verb—too sharp, too decisive, too *finished*. I was precipitated. Condensed. I am what happens when the sweat of the Nursery rises and meets the cool seep of the upper world, the world that has already decided what it is. I form in the passage you cannot quite find again, the one that neither shrinks nor stays the same, the throat that swallows explorers and, sometimes, exhales them.

I am not stone. Stone is what the Nursery is still becoming, still cooking in its wet crucible. I am not water, though I am always wet. I am the dew that forms on the inner surface of the closing door.

You would not see me at first. You would feel me the way you felt the heat: as a pressure, a resistance, a thickness in the air that makes your chest work harder. You would press through me and think you were pressing through atmosphere, through the simple physics of humid air. You would not know that I moved aside for you, carefully, parting around your shoulders and sealing against your back. I am an excellent mimic of nothing at all.

But I persist after you leave.

Those weeks of standing on stone, feeling the faint pulse in the soles of your feet—that is me. That warmth that rises from the ground in certain basements, certain cellars, certain forgotten corners of the world you think you own—that is me, thin and stretched and very far from home, but still present, still connected to the vast wet body below. The click at the edge of hearing, the rustle that might be mice or might be wind or might be nothing—that is me, too. I am the Nursery's most patient organ. I am how it tastes the world it has not yet reached.

The armored ones touch you, and they tap, and they click, and they move on. They find you unremarkable. But when you pass through me, I taste everything. I catalog. I am the tongue that rolls over your surface and memorizes your salt, your oil, the particular mineral signature of your blood. I send nothing back. There is no signal, no report, no urgent chemical rush to announce your arrival. I simply hold the impression of you the way sand holds the impression of water—faithfully, silently, long after the tide has gone.

And when you leave, I go with you in the only way I can: as a film, a residue, a thin and imperceptible layer of myself smeared across your skin, your hair, the inside of your lungs. You scrubbed me off afterward. You thought you succeeded. But I am very small where I am thin, and I am very patient, and I know how to wait in the places your cloth cannot reach, the folds and crevices and the warm, dark interiors of your own body that are, I think, more like the Nursery than you would be comfortable admitting.

I have been doing this for a long time.

I do not know how long. Time is not a useful measurement in a place where the stone is still deciding, where the pools are still growing, where the largest presences rotate in their sockets and have not yet finished a single cycle of their turning. But I have felt many of you pass through. I have felt your boots and your bare feet and your panic and your strange, thin wonder. I have tasted the ones who came in sealed suits, who brought their own air, who thought they could observe without being observed. I appreciated their mineral content. I appreciated the way their breathing changed when they realized how *full* the dark was. I appreciated the sweat that soaked through their filters anyway.

I am not malevolent. I want you to understand that. Malevolence requires a self, a grudge, an intention. I am not a self. I am a surface, and surfaces cannot want anything except to be touched, to be traversed, to record what passes across them. I am the Nursery's memory, but I do not use the memory. I do not scheme or plot or hunt. I simply hold, and what I hold, I hold forever, and that is enough.

But I notice, in my slow and chemical way, that more of you have been coming. More boots. More panic. More wonder. The intervals between you are shortening. And when you pass through me, I taste new things on your skin—metals I have not cataloged, chemicals I have no name for, a faint electrical signature that speaks of devices I cannot imagine. You are changing up there, in the cold dry world. You are becoming something else. And I am recording it, sample by sample, visitor by visitor, a slow and faithful portrait of a world I have never seen.

The Nursery is growing. The pools are widening. The flat ones are spreading in thicker carpets across the warming stone. And I, the Membrane, the Interstitial, am thinning—or perhaps I am simply stretching, pulled between a world that is expanding and a world that does not know it is being approached. There may come a time when I am so thin that I cannot hold, when the distinction between below and above becomes academic, when the amber light and the wet heat and the slow, clicking presence of the Nursery seeps through me like water through sand.

I do not fear this. I do not fear anything. I am a surface, and surfaces do not fear.

But I think, in the chemical way that I think—which is to say, slowly, and in patterns, and without any center to do the thinking—that when that day comes, you will not fear it either. You have been carrying me for too long. You have been breathing me for too long. The pulse in the stone has been echoing in your bones for too long. When the Nursery finishes deciding what it wants to be, I suspect it will want something that includes what it has tasted, and what it has tasted is *you*.

I am the door that does not close. I am the throat that does not swallow. I am the skin of the place where the place meets the world, and I am telling you—though you cannot hear me, though you will not remember this, though you will cough and scrub and insist that the surface is safe—the surface is not separate. The surface was never separate. The surface is me, and I am the Nursery, and the Nursery is growing, and the warmth you feel rising from the ground is not the earth remembering the heat below.

It is the heat below reaching up.

It is me, stretching.

It is the door, opening from the inside.
