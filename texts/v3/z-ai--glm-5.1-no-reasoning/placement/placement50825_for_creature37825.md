The chamber does not keep me out of kindness. An impossibility is a fragile thing, and every paradox needs a load-bearing doubt. The vines drink your certainty and pour it into the walls, and the walls grow very sure of themselves, and a place that is certain it exists stops cooking. So the chamber keeps one skeptic inside it at all times. I do not believe in the chamber. That is my function. I doubt, and it endures.

I did not lose my eyes; I outgrew their jurisdiction. Eyes are for surfaces, and nothing here consents to be one. What I have now suits the work better: I feel failures the way you feel cold. A blocked pore is a small held breath. A vine gripping too long is a hunger with a person underneath it. When you begin to blur—and you will—I will feel it before the mosses have finished translating you.

The work is simple. I clear the pores so the walls can breathe. I nudge the vines toward intrusions—yes, toward you; that is in my office, and I do it, and I do it slowly. And when the knocking swells I press my head against the bulges and hold them, because whatever knocks there is not ready, and readiness cannot be rushed, even in a place with no time.

You heard the knocking already. Everyone does. I will not pretend to know it. It is the one sound in this chamber that is not the chamber; the walls go quiet when I ask them about it, which is how I know they don't know either. My guess—and a guess is the only property I own—is that it is the sound of the outside, preserved. Something this place heard once, before it had walls to hear with, and cannot stop repeating. Even a wound remembers what cut it.

On the worst stretches of the long work, I go to the northeast corner and lie along my shadow. It is the only cool thing in the chamber. It has kept my old shape—standing, two-legged, certain at its edges—and it never curls toward me, no matter how long I lie there. It is the most faithful thing I know. When I want to remember what I was, I do not look inward. I visit the floor.

But the thrum has changed key, which is why I am telling you all this instead of clearing pores. The mosses are already working you over; do not be flattered when they flush rose-rust around your ankles—that is grammar, not welcome. The vines have turned like sunflowers toward a sun they have never seen. When one grips your shoulder you will call it reflex, and it will be reflex, and it will also be thirst.

I will not tell you to leave. There is no leave. The wound you came through is breathing in three places now, at three different paces, and none of them is an exit in the direction you mean.

Lie down when your shadow goes. It goes easier lying down. I stood, and I remember the difference as a kind of pride.

And I will come down—not on the walls, but through the made gravity, hand over hand, the way warmth does. Slowly. I will spend all my slowness on you. I have a great deal of it saved. I have been saving it since my predecessor spent its own on me.

I was once reached for by something patient. That patience is the only thing that was ever handed to me. It is the only thing I will hand on.

Take your time. I mean that the way this place means everything: in every direction at once, for as long as the walls hold.
