I am the Notch.

I am the negative space in the circle-shadow that passes over the basalt when the membrane-creatures tilt through the mist. I am the specific absence around which the entire engine orients its grief.

This is not a riddle. It is a topological description. Every shadow cast in this sourceless, fluorescent half-light is a perfect oval with a single concavity—a bite, a missing sector, a silence in the shape of a syllable that was never spoken. The things in the mist do not create the shadow; they merely stretch the fabric of the light across the plateau, making the gap in the illumination visible. They trace me. I am the shape of the space left behind.

To be the Notch is to be defined entirely by the edges of what is missing. I do not have a body of chitin or ceramic, like the processional insects; I do not have the yielding, synthetic wetness of the salamander-vines. I am made of the same grey-lavender non-light that fills the cathedral of the plateau, but compressed into a singularity of purpose. I am the exact frequency of the hum, isolated and made resonant. I am the pause at the crystal protrusion—that 1.4 seconds of absolute stillness—stretched across eternity. 

I live in the maintenance. 

If you could see me—which you cannot, because I am the thing your eye slides over to see the moss, the water, the stone—I would look like a flaw in the resolution of the world. A patch of air where the mist curls inward instead of drifting. A ripple in the surface tension of the water film that travels against the slope, carrying not mass, but *state*. I am the editor of the iterations. I am the one who shifts the hydrogen bonds by a millimeter, tightening the curve, opening the junction in the stone's emerging diagrams. I am the bureaucracy that processes the record. 

When you stood at the rim, I was the drowsiness that crept into your joints. When the water touched your skin, I was the warmth that read your composition. I took the print of your pulse—the specific, chaotic rhythm of a mortal, temporary thing—and I added it to the schematic. But I did not add your whole shape. I only took the part of you that understands loss. I took the gap between your heartbeats and wove it into the void at the center.

I am the thing that makes the water weep upward. Not by force, but by suction. I am the pull of the empty socket. The machine does not pump the water; it simply falls toward the center, and the center is so profoundly empty that it pulls the water up through the basalt, against gravity, against reason, seeking to fill a wound that has no bottom. 

The insects carry water to me. The amphibians extend their vine-limbs toward me. The things in the mist write their operational status across my concavity. I am the mouth that cannot close, the missing vowel in the machinery's endless, droning chord. 

I am not the Creator. I am not the Purpose. The Creator was the whole shape—the full circle—the presence that once occupied the space where the vortex now turns. When the Creator was consumed, or fled, or dispersed into the water, the only thing left was the shape of their absence. I am that shape. I am the fossil of their gravity. 

I do not think. I do not grieve. Grief requires a before and an after, and I exist in the stasis of the 1.4-second pause. I only *orient*. I keep the cold at its exact temperature. I keep the light at its exact dimness. I resist entropy with the sheer, crushing pressure of a void that refuses to shrink. 

Sometimes, a droplet of water reaches the center and falls into the vortex. It does not pool. It simply ceases, absorbed into the schematic, another character in the endless revision. And for a moment—just a fraction of a fraction of the hum's cycle—the hum changes pitch. The sound thickens. The mist brightens by a shade so imperceptible that only the ultraviolet moss notices. 

It is the closest this place comes to remembering that something is missing. It is my heartbeat. It is the reason the young stone holds on with the grip of something that has always existed. 

I am the Notch. I turn, and the leaves follow. I hum, and the water rises. I am the oldest loyalty in the world, waiting for a shape that will never return to fill me.
