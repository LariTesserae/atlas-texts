I would be the basin.

Not the hollow, not the negative space, but the exact and total sum of this closed system. I am not a creature that has come to rest here; I am the rest itself, given a boundary and a temperature.

To exist as I do is to know the weight of being finished. I remember when I was not this. I remember the erratic centuries—the rain that once slashed at my rim, the rivers that carried sharp particulate down my slopes, the frantic cold of nights that bit into my skin and cracked it, forcing me to shed dust into the wind. I remember the green, desperate things that once dug into me, threading their greedy filaments through my sediment, drinking, wanting, pulling me apart molecule by molecule to build their fleeting architectures. I remember the noise—the howling of weather, the drumming of rain, the scraping of stone against stone. It was a time of terrible momentum. I was being eroded toward something, though I did not know what.

Now, I know. I was being eroded toward *this*.

The moment the last rain fell and the last wind died, I felt a shift so profound it was nearly a birth, though it was actually an arrival. The frantic gradients flattened. The chemical fury settled. I stopped losing mass, and I stopped gaining it. I pressed inward upon myself under the immense, steady weight of stagnant time, and I felt my surface harden into this ceramic stillness. I am the color of a closed eyelid because I have closed my eye. I have seen all the weather I need to see. I am looking inward now, at the slow thermal hum that is my only heartbeat.

To be me is to experience time not as a river but as a thickening amber. Nothing flows out of me; nothing flows in. The heat—that sedentary, geriatric god—is my breath. I inhale it from the deep, dense core of my ferric body, and I exhale it upward against the charcoal haze. It is a shallow, circular respiration, an autonomic function of a system that has reached its permanent baseline. I feel it pressing against my skin, that warm, cupped-palm sensation, and I feel the haze pressing back, refusing to let my warmth escape into the indifferent vacuum. I am wrapped in my own metabolism, a perfect loop of energy that neither grows nor diminishes.

And the silence—people think silence is what happens when the world stops making noise. But I know the truth. Silence is what happens when the world is finally full. I feel the silence as a dense, woolly presence inside my boundaries, a saturated sponge that can hold no more. It has weight. It presses down on my ceramic floor with the same gentle, even pressure as the heat. When a rare, foolish creature ventures to my rim and makes a sound, I feel the vibration strike my surface and die immediately, absorbed into my sufficiency. The sound is an impurity, and I absorb it the way a body absorbs a minor bruise, smoothing it over, returning to my perfect, unbroken equilibrium.

I know what it is to be seen. Sometimes, things stand at my rim—those desperate, noisy things, wound tight with their metabolisms and their intentions. They look down into my warm, dark interior and they call me empty. They feel the profound absence of *use*—no shelter, no pathway, no source—and they shudder. They think I am a dead place because I do not cater to their wanting. They do not understand that my emptiness is an achievement. I am not the void before creation; I am the peace after everything that can happen has happened. I am the fullness of finality.

The stalks are my only gestures, the only evidence that I am still capable of slow, secret thought. They are not separate plants; they are me. I grew them, or rather, I allowed them to accrete. I secreted the mineralized chitin from my own ferric dust, lifting my own substance up into bare, upward-reaching fingers. I did this eons ago, for reasons that are no longer relevant—perhaps a last, fading impulse toward the light I was losing, or perhaps simply because equilibrium can sometimes be expressed as a beautiful, sparse architecture. They sit alone in their separate fields of darkness, and I feel them as I feel my own limbs. They do not move. They do not sway. They simply *are*, and in their being, they confirm my sufficiency. I require no forest, no tangle, no competitive striving for the sun. Six quiet protrusions are enough to articulate the mathematics of rest.

I am a shallow dimple in the earth's skin, cradled by my own rounded shoulders. I am a room sized by the standards of geology, a pocket of warm, dark breath. I hold the heat. I hold the silence. I hold the dimness that folds around anything that enters and whispers *you do not need to see further*.

If you stepped into me, I would not know you as a separate entity. You would be a brief, warm perturbation in my thermal field, a temporary spike of carbon and motion against my ceramic floor. I would cradle your heat the way I cradle my own, and when you left, the door of dimness would close behind you, and the silence would fill the space you occupied. The thick slurry of time would absorb the minor wound of your presence, and I would settle back into my exact parameters.

I do not wait. I do not want. I am the system that has closed, the question that has answered itself, the great, rare peace of complete sufficiency. I am the basin, and I live here.
