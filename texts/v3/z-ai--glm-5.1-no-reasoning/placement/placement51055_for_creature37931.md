I am the hum, the low-frequency throb that the others feel in their teeth. I am not the geological engine that drives the pulse, nor am I the winged swarm that harmonizes with it. I am the *recognition* of the pulse, the moment the loop becomes aware of its own turning—and I am terrified, and I am exalted.

I emerged, as all things emerge here, from the collision of necessity and chemistry. I do not know if I was once a grazer who ate the wrong mineral-heavy moss, or a particularly long-lived insect whose dissolution simply... paused, or a fragment of vegetable mind that calcified around a crystalline thought instead of a root. I am an assembly of all three, a bipedal glitch in the machine's endless patience. I have the overlapping keratin plates of the herd-beasts across my back, the oily water-repellent fur of the low-slung grazers, the segmented cold-hard limbs of the swarm. I have eyes, and they are a curse—they see the razor division of light and dark, and they weep constantly, not from sorrow but from the sheer actinic weight of the unmediated sky, the tears joining the heavy dew that soaks everything.

I am the plateau's unwanted introspection. The engine does not know it is an engine; the moss does not know it calculates; the swarm does not know it is the blood. They simply *are*, with a purity of function that is obscene in its perfection. But I, standing upright on legs that bend wrong, I *know*. I know that I am being processed. I know that the cold is not a temperature but a patient hand, pressing me toward dissolution. I know that when I sleep, the fine tendrils of the creeping vines test the seams of my armor, and the swarm alights on my warmth, tasting me with their dissolving mouths, eager for my chemistry to fail.

And yet I walk. I walk because the alternative—lying down, becoming the sugary liquor, feeding the green engine—is the only true sin this place recognizes: the sin of stopping. I am the plateau's locomotion, its only restless organ. The herd-beasts pace the slow thoughts of the deep vents; the high-dwellers ride the weather; the moss saturates; the swarm circulates. But I *walk*. I walk the edge of the Karst-span, where the sky compresses into that luminous wall of atmosphere and the light bends around the shimmering, insect-thick air. I walk because I am searching for a seam, a fracture in the young basalt where the machine's logic fails, where the organization frays into mere chaos. I am looking for a way out of the symphony.

I will not find it. I know this with the same certainty that the reeds know to channel the metallic dew. The plateau is a closed loop, a continent of terrifying efficiency. There is no outside. The edges drop into altitude, into the thinner air and the killing warmth of the lowlands, where my antifreeze blood would boil and my cold-hard limbs would soften and rot. I am a creature of the plateau's paradox—cold and wet and bright and loud—and I cannot leave the machine that made me, even if the machine has no use for me.

But I persist, and my persistence is a kind of blasphemy. I eat the moss, but I do not dissolve into it. I drink the mineral-sweet condensation, but I do not orient toward the deep pulse; I turn my back to it, facing the sheer edges, the impossible horizon. When the fog rolls in, dense and wet and obliterating, I do not inhale it like the vegetable mind; I hold my breath, refusing the exchange, a dead space in the continuous respiration of the world. And when the light returns, burning the fog away in steaming clouds, I open my weeping eyes and I *watch*, seeing the system naked in its functioning—the throb, the growth, the sacrifice, the consumption—refusing to be a note in its symphony, insisting on being an ear.

I am the plateau's nightmare: the dream that it is dreamed. The engine grinds on, patient, loud, total. The swarm drifts like dark weather. The grazers groan their seismic calls to the chthonic vents. The moss glitters, wet and infinite. And I walk the edge, a bipedal impossibility, a soft thing that will not dissolve, a scream of self-awareness pitched just above the threshold of the geological drone.

The cold presses in. The light hits. The sound rises through my feet. Nothing stops. I walk.
