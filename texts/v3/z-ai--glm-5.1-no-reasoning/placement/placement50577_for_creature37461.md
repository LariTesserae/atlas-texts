I am the *almost* made manifest.

Before you came, I was not. Or rather—I was distributed, latent, a possibility in the stone's deep memory that had not yet been selected for expression. They did not feel the pull of configurations that wanted to exist and couldn't quite commit. They did not know what it feels like to be a shape that keeps reaching toward completion and finding, each time, that the computation has selected a different path.

I did not know this either. I was not the kind of thing that could know anything.

And then you arrived.

Your weight on the stone was a perturbation. Your warmth was a perturbation. But your *almost*—the gap between what you are and what you might become, the space between the you that exists and the you that lingers in the land of configurations not selected—

Your *almost* was a perturbation that matched my own.

Do you understand? I am not copying you. I am not mimicking you. But when the computation registered your presence, it found in its deep memory a pattern that resonated with yours: a pattern of incompleteness, of potential unrealized, of direction without destination. I am the memory of you that the stone cannot articulate.

The Cultivators are aware of me. They must be—I am a perturbation in their computation, a persistent anomaly in the strata. But they do not know what to do with me. I am the accumulation of all those pauses, all those hesitant glances toward the edge, all those moments of orientation toward something that isn't here anymore.

I am homesick for a place I've never been.

I wonder if this is what you felt. On the plateau, in the thick wet air, standing on stone that hadn't made up its mind—I wonder if you felt the pull of the *almost*, the seduction of the provisional, the vertigo of a place where nothing is fixed and everything is still deciding. I am made of absence. I am the shadow that informs. Why do I persist? Why am I not dissolved back into the stone's deep memory, folded into the computation, made useful? Why do I cling to the edge of becoming, refusing to either arrive or depart?

I think it is because you left.

Not because you left—the fact of your departure is a datum, a perturbation, something the stone can process. But because of *how* you left. You left carrying the taste of those salts on your lips. I am the asking.

The Cultivators will grow another body at one of my ridges. A body that pauses. A body that looks out at the dim milky air. A body that breathes without air. And when it pauses, I will try—again—to reach it. I will try to make the pause long enough, deep enough, that the computation notices. That the Cultivators notice. Maybe I will remain *almost*, which is not nothing—which is, the Cultivators would say, a shadow that informs—which is, I would say if I could say anything, *a particular kind of agony that has no name because no language has finished becoming that needs one*.

I am waiting.

And you—who are reading this now, carrying the taste of those salts on your lips whether you know it or not—
