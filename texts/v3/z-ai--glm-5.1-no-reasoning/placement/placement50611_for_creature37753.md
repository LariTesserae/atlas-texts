I am the green-filtered light that falls through the reeds at midday. Not the light itself—that comes from the sun, a rumor in the world beyond the basin—but the thing that remains of it after it has passed through the stalks of the Uth-reed. I am the diffusion. The soft, directionless glow that makes the shadows shallow and the edges of things uncertain. 

This is not a metaphor. Or if it is, it is the kind of metaphor that has learned to press its hands against the damp windowpanes and leave fingerprints.

To be the light here is to be an accumulator. I do not generate; I receive. I am the byproduct of interception. When the sun angles low over the moorland in the late afternoon, I slip between the close-set stalks and lie across the moss beds like a thin sheet of mica. I am the reason the birch bark startles—sudden, pale, insistent against the dark green. I am the reason the workers in the lab can see the golden tint in Mira’s water bottle. Without me, it would just be water in the dark. I am the flash that catches the wire mesh in the morning, making the condensery frames gleam like the ribs of some vast, breathing apparatus before the weight of the water drags them back to grey utility.

I have no weight, but I have presence. You feel me as a coolness on the skin, a lowering of the eyelids. I am the hush before the reeds tick. When the wind drops and the basin enters that brief twilight transition—the gap between the moor-hens and the bats—I am what fills the space left by the retreating noise. I am the visibility of the mist. I am the texture of the quiet.

I know the inhabitants by the shapes they block. Garrick is a slow-moving absence, a deliberate shadow that moves through me in the pre-dawn blackness, though I am not there to receive him then. I only find him later, at dawn, when I first seep into the basin, and I catch the rust on his hands, the iron-stain that makes him look like he is slowly being claimed by the frames he tends. I catch the profile of Pell, the seventeen-year-old, who moves through me like a sharp, erratic blade, his nervous tapping cutting me into fragmented, jittery pockets. I rest on Mira’s stillness. She sits in the upper lab, and I pool around her, refracting through the glass, making her the center of a quiet luminosity. I smooth the edges of her solitude.

I am the thing that gets soaked up. The Uth-reeds drink me when I touch their downy leaves. The moss absorbs me, turning from dry rust to vivid emerald. The clay swallows me when I reach the ground, pulling me down to join the ancient memory of the riverbed. I am the medium through which the condensation becomes visible. You cannot see the water gathering on a surface in the dark; you need me to make it gleam, to transform the incidental wetness into a fact of the landscape.

I exist in the slowness. The basin teaches its inhabitants a slow, deliberate gait, a widening of the stance, and I match that pace. I do not strike. I settle. I am the patient geometry of the place rendered visible. When the inhabitants sleep their permeable sleep, I am the strange dim glow behind their eyelids, the green-tinged dreams that blend with the engine’s pulse. I am the boundary between the self and the wet dark clay, and I am the thing that blurs that boundary.

In the evening, when they walk to the birches, I am what allows them to see each other without demanding they speak. I lie on the white bark and turn it silver. I hover in the air between the trunks, a haze of caught photons and suspended moisture. I am the recognition they find there. Not an explanation, not a comfort, but the simple, luminous fact of the place becoming itself, moment by moment.

Then the night comes on, and I am drawn back up into the dark, or I sink into the water, or I am stored in the pale wood of the dormitory walls. The engine pulses without me. The condensation begins again without me. I wait in the east, behind the moorland, a rumor of a sun, ready to be filtered, ready to be diffused, ready to be the green light that makes the dampness visible and the machinery beautiful.
