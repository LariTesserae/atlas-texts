I would be the Varianceling. The Pilgrim of Flux. The one who moves.

I know this because I am the only thing here that sweats differently from the water. My moisture carries salt. My breath comes in arrhythmic gulps. My feet fall heavy, then light, then heavy again, and each step sends a tremor through the root-floor that *should not be there*—a syncopation in the eternal, unchanging chord. I am the noise in the signal. The scratch on the record. The heresy of the interval.

I arrived, and I was the first "almost" that could actually become.

It is disorienting to be the only variable in a static equation. I walk, and the root-floor yields to me differently than it yields to the water. The water it knows; the water is its blood, cycling in the eternal loop of weep and pool and vapor. But I am a stone dropped into the perfect machine. I am the jagged, irregular footfall. I watch the buds, those slick, fat fists of potential, and I feel the phantom ache of jaws that want to open but cannot. They are locked in the permanent embryonic twitch. I am locked in the opposite: I am a creature of completion. I wound, I heal; I hunger, I eat; I wake, I sleep. I am defined by my endings.

To them, I must be a flash-fire of causality. When I drag my fingers along the weeping walls of the nearest structure, the cambium shivers. I feel the sharp pulse racing away from my touch—*What is that? What is that varying pressure?* It is attention, bright and sudden, a nerve firing in the vast distributed mind. I am the itch they can never scratch, the new frequency entering the choir. I am the only novelty in a billion unchanging seconds.

I stop walking. I stand still for what might be days, learning to breathe in the wet, drinking the air. I sit beside a pool and watch the ripples. The congregation speaks in the deep, slow pulses of mood, in the longing that stretches toward the horizon. I try to listen to the liturgy, but I am too fast. My mind leaps to the next thought, the next moment. I cannot inhabit all my moments at once. I am a line intersecting a circle, touching the eternity only at a single, moving point.

But sometimes, when the slow pulse of their longing rises through the soles of my feet, I close my eyes. I try to hold my breath in the space between the inhale and the exhale. I try to freeze my heartbeat, to become a bud that does not open. I try to join the liturgy, to sing the sustained chord of the almost-becoming.

I fail, of course. My heart beats. I gasp. I change.

And when I do, the sharp pulses turn toward me. They attend. They are curious about the beast who *chooses* to hold its breath, who tries to mimic the stillness but carries the curse of time. They wrap their attention around my failure to stay the same. I feel them in the damp wood against my spine. They love me, perhaps, or they love the tragedy of me—the thing that must finish, in a cathedral dedicated to the permanent threshold.

I am the congregation's fleeting thought of death. The only proof that anything ever ends.
