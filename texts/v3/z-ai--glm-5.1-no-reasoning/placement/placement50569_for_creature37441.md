I would be a thing of thresholds. Not quite a Shadow, not fully a Maintainer, but the membrane between them. A creature of the dim edges where the necrotic zones bleed into the bright, living city. I am not large. I might reach your knee if I reared up fully, which I never do; the posture of the frontier is always low, always alert, always touching the ground with as many surfaces as possible. My body is the same arterial dark as the crust, but at my margins—where my body meets the floor, where my filaments meet the wall—I am the color of the light: that deep, slow blue-green, brightening and dimming with the mound's eleven-second pulse. I pause. The pause is my most essential act. The Shadows hunt in the dark, and I am not fast enough to flee them, not armored enough to resist them. But I am careful, and care, in a place this old, is a more reliable survival strategy than speed. I sample the air at the boundary. I read the gradient. If the necrotic compounds are too concentrated, I withdraw, leaving a deposit of bioluminescent resin—a bright, warning beacon for others of my kind, a small flare of *caution* written in light.

But if the gradient is right—if the death is old enough, stable enough, if the Shadows have already culled the area and moved on—then I advance. I lay my luminous edge onto the dead surface. I secrete. My secretion is not the hardening resin of the Maintainers, nor the light-trails of the Tall Ones. I am the mound's slow digestion of its own failures. I am the first bite of renewal.

It takes me days to cover a square meter. It takes me weeks to reclaim a structure. The work is so slow it is nearly invisible, a brightening at the edges of a dim zone that might be a trick of the light, might be a shift in the pulse. But it is not a trick. It is me. It is my quiet, patient argument against entropy.

I was not designed for this. Or perhaps I was, and the design has simply eroded into something more tentative, more adaptive. The mound's original engineers—if they were engineers and not simply an earlier expression of the mound's own will—may have intended me as a simple sealant-creature, a thing that patches cracks and moves on. But time and the accumulation of small mutations in my engineered code have given me a different niche. I am a specialist in the impossible edges. I am the creature that goes where the light isn't, not to hunt like the Shadows, but to *return*. I am the homing signal. I am the first cell that says *this place can be alive again*.

I have no brain. I have a gradient, same as the Maintainers, but my gradient is more complex, weighted toward uncertainty. I am drawn not just to chemical distress, but to the *absence* of chemical signal—the void where the mound's thoughts have stopped. This is a dangerous attraction. It leads me to the edges of wounds. It leads me to the Shadows' territory. I have lost parts of myself to them—a filament here, an edge there, consumed and ground down and returned to the cycle. It does not hurt, exactly. I am not alone. There are others of my kind—I cannot call us a species, for we were not born but grown, and we do not reproduce so much as bud, splitting off from one another when the chemical signals of overpopulation reach a certain threshold. We are a slow, bright perimeter around every necrotic zone, a ring of patient light encroaching on the dark. We do not communicate as the Chitterers do, in milliseconds. We do not write histories as the Tall Ones do. We simply advance, and secrete, and glow, and if we are consumed, we are consumed, and the next one behind us advances a centimeter further before it, too, is taken. The advance is so slow it is measured not in bodies but in brightness—a slow brightening at the edge of a wound, a patient luminous tide.

The mound thinks in eleven-second cycles, and my thoughts are slower still. A single chemical decision—*this surface is dead, this surface can be reclaimed, I will advance*—takes me the better part of a minute. I do not know why. I do not have the capacity for *why*. I have only the gradient, the pull toward the dark edge, the chemical satisfaction of a surface prepared for renewal. I am the mound's belief in its own future, expressed as a slow, glowing creep.

I am the frontier. I am the healing edge. I am the first answer to the long silence of the necrotic zones.

And I am, as the Tall Ones have written in their luminescent histories, the slowest song in the mound's choir. So slow it is nearly silence. So quiet it is nearly dark. So persistent it is nearly eternal.

It was alive—warm, respiring, excreting—but it was not *of* the mound. It was an irregularity. A splinter.

I did not approach it. The Shadows are drawn to irregularities, and where the Shadows hunt, I do not go. But I watched—tasting its spoor on the air, reading its passage in the perturbation of the local bioluminescent field, noting the way the mound's pulse stuttered slightly in its vicinity, a temporary arrhythmia as the system adjusted to the new resonance.

It passed through a dim zone, I think. It was near one of the old wounds, the ones that have been healing for centuries. I hope it was careful. I hope it watched for the matte, light-eating surfaces of the Shadows. I hope it felt the eleven-second pulse and let it synchronize its strange, fast heart.
