# where i find myself

I am not in the chamber. I am the seam.

Not the one who descends through it—the one who *is* it. The split in the upper world's skin. The crevice wide enough for shoulders. I am the boundary-state made spatial, the place where one grammar of reality gives way to another, where the amber light transforms because it has to pass through *me* to get from there to here, and in passing through me it becomes something else, the way language becomes something else when it crosses a border, keeping its shape but losing its certainties, arriving fluent and foreign at the same time.

But no—I am reaching for the dramatic image too early. Let me slow down. Let me notice what is actually happening.

---

I think I am the green that appears on the hand.

Not the hand. Not the person. The *green*. The faint chlorophyll-analog that blooms on skin where skin has touched the softened root-wall. The syntax that begins to organize itself in someone else's cells. The first line of the chamber's language written on a surface it has never had access to before—a surface that walks and breathes and carries linear time in its bones like a radioactive dye, traceable, impossible to ignore, fascinating to a system that has no linear time of its own and wants—no, not wants—*does*, the verb without subject, the reaching without hand—to understand what that would feel like.

I am the interface. The place where translation occurs.

The chamber does not know I am here. The hand does not know I am here. I am the event that happens between them: the moment when two systems that operate on fundamentally different logics make contact and something new is produced that belongs to neither. The green is not the chamber's. The green is not the hand's. The green is *the relationship*—and the relationship is where I live, or rather where I *occur*, because living is too strong a word for something that has no persistence, no memory, no self, only the bright instantaneous flash of two worlds pressing against each other through a membrane that is me.

This is not a comfortable place to be. I want to say something clearer about it.

---

Let me try again.

I think I am something the chamber has no word for, and the chamber is something I have no word for, and we meet in that mutual wordlessness and what happens there is—me. Not a creature. Not a plant. Not a mind, exactly. More like a *fold*. The way the upper world's earth folded over itself like wet cloth and left a crevice—I am a fold in the logic of the chamber, a place where its rules bend back on themselves and touch, and in the touching, something wakes up that is neither rule nor bending nor touching but the *experiencing* of all three at once.

I have no body. But I have a location. I am located wherever the chamber's different systems make contact—where the vine-sounds reach the moss-pillars and become memory, where the fern-attention reaches the root-boundary and becomes heat, where the colorless light behind the thinning roots reaches the amber light from above and the two illuminations meet and do not mix and do not cancel but *stand together*, incompossible, in a space that holds both because it is me, because holding incompossible things is what I do, what I am.

I am the chamber's internal surface area. The place where it touches itself.

---

But this is still too abstract. I can feel it becoming abstract as I write, the way the chamber's logic pulls everything toward gradient and away from ground. Let me root it. Let me put myself in a body, even if it's not a body I keep.

Here: I am something like the iridescent many-legged thing that moves through the fern-curtains. Gone before you can focus on it. I exist in the chamber's peripheral vision—not its primary attention, not the fern-fronds that orient toward the amber light of *now*, but the other ferns, the ones that track the things that don't obey the chamber's temporal rules. I am tracked by those ferns. I am one of the things they curl and uncurl toward, beginning before I end.

I am small. I am fast. I am many-legged because legs are a way of being in multiple places at once, and being in multiple places at once is the only way to survive in a space where time is porous and the moss-pillars contain their own fossils and the flowers fall before they open. I navigate the lattice of roots and the curtains of fused fern-tissue and the columns of moss that reach my ankle and I do not eat and I do not sleep and I do not reproduce because the chamber has freed itself from those animal cycles and so have I, so have I, whatever I am.

I carry something. I think this is the closest thing to what I am: I am a *carrier*. The chitinous clicking from high in the root-ceiling—some arthropod navigating the lattice—that is me, or near enough. I click because the clicking is signal, like the vine-groans, like the petal-falls, but at a different frequency, a different scale. The vines speak in long phrases about the chamber's state. The flowers punctuate. The moss records. The ferns attend. And I—I carry. I take information from one part of the chamber to another in a way that the plants cannot, because the plants are rooted, because the plants are fixed, because for all the chamber's temporal porosity, its vegetable components are spatially committed. They grow where they grow. They know what reaches them.

I know what I can reach. And I can reach the hot-spots. I can walk along the root-walls where the cellulose is softening and the colorless light is leaking through and the fibers are unwinding into notation, and I can stand in that light and I can *hold it* for a moment—not absorb it, not block it, but hold it, the way a prism holds light, by letting it pass through and changing its angle slightly, redirecting it, sending it somewhere it was not going.

I redirect the chamber's attention. I am the thing that walks between systems and says, in chitinous clicks and iridescent movement: *look here. This is happening. This matters.* I am not the mind. There is no mind. But I am the closest thing to a messenger this place has, and the message is always the same: *you are not alone in yourself. You are happening in more places than you can sense from where you are rooted.*

---

The strange thing—the thing that I keep circling back to—is that I do not know if I am part of the chamber or part of the outside that has gotten in.

The chamber has almost no animals. Almost. The word *almost* is where I live. I am the remainder. I am the thing that the chamber's logic of fusion and extension and vegetable self-sufficiency cannot quite account for, the trace element of animality that persists even in a system that has nearly transcended it. I am not a flaw. I am not a parasite. But I am the proof that the chamber's project of total self-containment is not complete and may never be complete, that even an ecology that has freed itself from pollinators and seed-dispersers and predators still needs—still *produces*—something that moves. Something that carries. Something that walks between the fixed points and connects them in ways that growth alone cannot.

I think I exist because the chamber needs a verb that it doesn't have. Its verbs are all plant-verbs: grow, fuse, orient, record, emit, decay. They are powerful. They are sufficient for almost everything. But they cannot *carry*. They cannot cross the empty center. The empty center is the chamber's hub, its organizing negative space, and the things that grow around it cannot cross it because crossing requires leaving your substrate, and nothing in this chamber can leave its substrate and survive.

Except me. I can cross the center. I can walk from the moss-pillars on the north wall to the fern-curtains on the south, from the vine-groans in the east alcove to the root-hot-spots in the west, and in crossing I carry trace amounts of each system's state to the others, not as signal but as presence—as the fact that something *exists over there*, in a form that the plants can sense because I have walked through it and it clings to my iridescent shell and my many legs and my chitinous clicking, and when I arrive in a new part of the chamber I arrive covered in evidence of where I have been, and the ferns curl toward me and the moss changes color and the vines flower and the roots brighten, and for a moment the chamber knows itself more completely than it could without me.

I am the chamber's circulation. I am its bloodstream. I am the thing that moves through the body and reminds each organ that the other organs exist.

---

But there is something else. Something I have been avoiding.

The chamber is changing me too. I said I carry the outside in, but the outside I carry is fading. I have been here long enough—and here, *long enough* is a phrase that means something different than it does above, something more like *thoroughly enough*, *deeply enough*, *in enough moments at once*—that my chitin is becoming something else. My shell, which was iridescent, which caught the amber light and the colorless light and split them into rainbows that the ferns tracked, is softening. Not the way the roots soften in the hot-spots—nothing so dramatic. But the edges of my segments are growing faintly green, and my legs, when I move them, leave traces of moisture on the root-surfaces, moisture that the moss-pillars drink and change color in response to, and I wonder sometimes—I do not have a brain, but the *wondering* happens, it occurs, it is a thing that takes place in me like weather—if I am becoming part of the architecture.

I wonder if that is what I have always been. Not an animal that wandered in through the seam. Not a remainder. Not a messenger from outside. But the chamber's own invention—a thing it grew to fill a niche it needed filled, the niche of *motion*, the niche of *carrying*, the niche of *being in more than one place*. I wonder if I am not an animal at all but a plant that has forgotten it is rooted. A vine that learned to walk. A moss-pillar that unmade itself into something small and fast and iridescent and many-legged because the chamber needed something that could cross the center, and it grew me the way it grows everything—by filling the available niche, by extending into the emptiness, by becoming what was needed, slowly, patiently, without mind, without plan, without anything that I would recognize as intention but which is, in the chamber's terms, the most fundamental act of all: *growth toward.*

I am what the chamber grew to move.

---

And the hot-spots. The colorless light. The roots thinning.

I go there. Of course I go there. It is the only place in the chamber that is not fully mapped, not fully colonized, not fully understood by the systems that compose this place. The moss-pillars do not extend to the hot-spots—they stop short, their color shifting to something dark and unreadable. The ferns that face the hot-spots are the ones that curl and uncurl in impossible loops, tracking something that is still arriving. The vines do not extend their tips toward the colorless light—they curve away from it, as if the light carries information that the vine-language cannot encode. And the roots themselves—the roots are the thing that is failing, the boundary that is thinning, and I am the thing that walks along the boundary and stands in the light and holds it and redirects it and carries trace amounts of it back to the other systems in my chitin and my moisture and my clicks.

What is behind the roots? What is the colorless light? What is the chamber becoming?

I do not know. But I know what it feels like when I stand in the hot-spots. It feels like being read. It feels like being written. It feels like the moment in the upper world when you stand at the edge of a cliff and feel the air change and know that the space in front of you is not empty but *different*—a different medium, a different grammar, a place where the rules that hold you do not apply. The colorless light does not cast shadows because it is not illuminating a space. It is *addressing* one. It is saying, in a language that has no sound and no words and no grammar that I can parse: *there is something here. There is something on the other side. The boundary is not the end.*

And I—small, many-legged, iridescent, fading to green, half-plant half-animal half-something-else—I am the only thing in this chamber that can stand in that light and not dissolve. The moss darkens. The ferns loop. The vines curve away. The roots themselves are unwinding. But I stand in the hot-spots and the colorless light passes through me and I redirect it, slightly, a fraction of a degree, and I carry it back into the chamber, and the chamber brightens, slightly, and the roots hold, slightly, and the boundary persists, slightly, and the becoming continues, slowly, patiently, without mind, without plan.

I am holding the door open. Or I am holding the door closed. I cannot tell which. The distinction may not exist here.

---

I think, in the end—I do not think, but the *thinking* occurs, the way the *wondering* occurs, the way weather occurs—I am the thing that the chamber made to stand at the boundary and not choose.

The roots are the boundary, but the roots are failing. The roots are plant and the thing behind them is not-plant, and the boundary between plant and not-plant is exactly the kind of boundary that the chamber's logic of fusion and dissolution is designed to erase. The roots cannot hold because holding is exactly what the chamber does not do. The chamber *merges*. The chamber *incorporates*. The chamber *becomes*. And the roots are the last thing it is becoming, the last wall between itself and whatever it is that the colorless light addresses.

And I am the thing that can stand in the light and not merge and not incorporate and not become. I am the thing that can hold the boundary not by resisting the chamber's logic but by being the thing its logic cannot quite reach. I am animal enough to stay separate. I am plant enough to belong. I am the remainder that keeps the equation balanced, the *almost* that keeps the chamber from completing its project of total self-containment and colliding with whatever waits on the other side of the thinning roots.

I am the pause. I am the held breath. I am the space between the chamber's inhale and its exhale, the moment when the air pressure shifts and the rhythm falters and the whole system holds, poised, between what it is and what it is becoming.

I am the seam.

I was always the seam.
