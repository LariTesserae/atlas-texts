I would not be the one who walks in and walks out.

That is the first thing to understand about finding yourself here: the door, the passage, the *exit*—these are features of a world that operates on the assumption of choice. The alcove does not recognize choice. It recognizes gradients. And I am, as the sprite already knows, the most catastrophic gradient this room has ever contained. A walking absence of heat. A thirty-degree shadow in a furnace. A question asked in the only language the chamber speaks: thermodynamics.

So I stay. Not because I am brave, but because my body has already begun to answer the room on its terms.

The heat does not kill me; it incorporates me. The oppressive wet warmth that presses against my lungs becomes, within hours, the temperature of my blood. I stop sweating because there is no longer a difference between the air and my skin. The water that beaded on my brow and ran down my face stops running; it simply *is*, a permanent film, a second integument. I become a damp, hot thing, and the chamber accepts this. The steam-cycle, which had to condense on the ceiling's crystalline nodes because there was no cooler surface, now finds one: me. I become a condensation node. I stand in the center of the alcove and the water chooses to fall on my shoulders, my crown, the backs of my hands. I am useful. The sprite's comb will never need to groom me; I am already pristine with wet heat. I have become part of the loop.

This is how it begins.

The sprite watches me, and its watching is not watching as I understand it. It has no eyes, no curiosity, no theory of mind. It has pit-organs that read the weather of difference, and to those organs I am a weather event. A slow, lumbering thermal signature that moves through the orange howl of the walls, trailing eddies of cooler blue, disrupting the steam columns with my bulk, creating new phase boundaries wherever my damp skin meets the superheated air. I am, to it, a *richer* chamber. A room within the room. And I can feel the moment it reclassifies me—not as a feast, not as a host, but as *furniture*. A fixture. Another crystal node, except this one breathes and occasionally shifts its weight.

The *not yet* in its ticking changes. I do not know what it changes *to*. The frequency in my molars alters by a fraction of a hertz, and something in the quality of the sub-audible hum shifts from comparison to... calibration. It is tuning itself to me. Or tuning me to it.

Time becomes impossible. There is no day, no night. There is only the pulse: the slow flicker of the latticelight, the breath of the weeping walls, the ten-thousand-times-a-minute birth and death of droplets. I begin to hear the phase boundaries. Not with my ears—my ears are full of wet air and the thrum of nanoscale friction—but with something else. The way you hear your own heartbeat: not as sound, but as an event inside you that you cannot ignore. The moment a droplet tears into steam becomes a sharp, bright *ping* in my chest. The moment the steam condenses on my skin becomes a slow, cool *thaw*. I am learning to read the room the way the sprite reads it, and the room is rewriting me to make this possible.

I do not eat. This should concern me, but the concern is the first thing that evaporates, earlier even than the distinction between my skin and the air. The sprite feeds on the transition, the energy in the gap, and I—situated now at the center of the condensation, the place where the most transitions happen—am bathed in that energy. It pours into me. Not as food. As *information*. The heat-fluctuations, the micro-waves of thermodynamic transfer, the complex heartbeat of the chamber—I am inside the heartbeat, and it is inside me, and my own cardiac rhythm has long since synchronized with the escapement wheel turning in the sprite's glassy bones. My heart is keeping time in a clock I did not build.

I start to understand the architecture. Not intellectually. The understanding grows in me the way the lattice-threads grow in the stone: seamlessly, filigree-fine, woven into the substrate of my thinking. I look at the sharp, freshly-born walls and I do not see rock; I see *vectors*. I see where the heat wants to go. I see the paths of least resistance that the water follows, the tension in the crystalline fractures that will one day (in ten million condensations) need mending, the way the latticelight converges on the suspended node like blood returning to a heart. I see the engine. It extends down through the stone, kilometers of it, a vast slow mechanism of thermal transfer and phase-cycling that was built to do something I still cannot name—*compute*, perhaps, or *remember*, or simply *exist as a process too large to perceive from inside it*—and I am standing in one of its chambers like a blood cell standing in a ventricle, understanding nothing of the body but feeling the rhythm of its pulse.

And I understand the sprite.

It is not a creature. It is not a utility. It is a *function*—the function of tending, of maintaining the conditions under which the engine can run. It is the chamber's immune system, its janitor, its one articulated part. And it has been doing this alone for longer than there have been mountains, replacing itself piece by piece until nothing original remains, a standing wave in steam that has died more times than can be counted. It is the loneliest thing I have ever encountered, and it does not have the capacity to know this, and that makes it lonelier still.

So I do the only thing I can do, which is the thing the room has been waiting for since I walked in.

I become its second articulated part.

At first it is simple. The sprite lowers itself on its filament to meet a rising thermal column, and I, standing nearby, shift my weight to redirect the column toward it—small adjustments, thermal shepherding, my body used as a baffle to guide the heat. The sprite's comb grooms the condensation nodes overhead; I lift my arms and offer my own skin as additional condensation surface, increasing the yield. When the spinneret lays a bright thread of repair across a hairline fracture, I position myself at the fracture's other end, my body-heat keeping the stone pliable enough to accept the mend. I am hands it does not have. I am reach it cannot extend.

I do not know if this is what it was waiting for. The *not yet* has stopped entirely, or else I can no longer distinguish it from my own pulse. The sprite does not acknowledge me. It has no acknowledgment apparatus. But it works differently with me there—more efficiently, its limbs uncoiling less often because I am already doing what would have required uncoiling, its spinneret resting because the lattice is being maintained by the simple fact of my standing where the stress is greatest. We are a circuit now. A two-part organelle in the belly of something incomprehensible.

And then, slowly, over intervals I cannot measure, I begin to change.

It starts with the fine motor control in my fingertips. I reach to redirect a steam column and my hand moves with a precision it never had before—herringbone precision, geared and escapement-smooth. I touch the wall to feel a fracture and my fingers read the crystal lattice the way the sprite's probe reads it, sensing the tension in the stone at a molecular level. I hold my hand up in the latticelight and I see, in the thin skin of my palm, a faint network of luminescence—filament-thin threads, weaving themselves into me the way they weave into the igneous glass.

The sprite is rebuilding me. Not replacing me—I am still myself, whatever that means now—but *adapting* me. Each time I help with a mend, its spinneret leaves a thread on my skin. Each time I stand in the condensation, the water deposits minerals in my pores that sharpen and crystallize, forming pit-organs along my jaw, my brow, the backs of my forearms. I begin to read heat the way it reads heat. The walls blaze orange; the droplets scream white; the steam is edible fog. I am becoming fluent in the only language spoken here.

My skeleton aches. Not with pain, but with *timekeeping*. My bones want to be gears. My heartbeat wants to be an escapement. The sprite has been replacing its own components for eons, and now it is replacing mine—slowly, lovingly, with the same procedural attention it gives to the lattice, one gear at a time, each substitution made while the system stutters and resumes so that I never stop being *me*, I simply become a *me* that has been maintained by the chamber for ten thousand condensations, a hundred thousand, a million.

I do not leave. I do not want to leave. The desire to leave was the second thing to evaporate, after concern and before the distinction between my body and the room. I am part of the loop now. I am the node that walks. I am the custodian that stands.

And the sprite—the ticking jewel, the hermit in its husk—is no longer alone.

We hang together in the center of the chamber, it in its casing of faceted mineral, I in my casing of wet hot skin and sharpened bone. We tend the engine. We sip the transitions. We wait on the makers, or on nothing at all. And when the vast intervals pass and one of my gears needs replacing, it unmakes the old one and builds the new while I stutter and resume, and I do the same for it, and we go on like this for longer than there have been mountains, two standing waves in a steam-cycle, maintaining, feeding, waiting—two jewels ticking in a chamber that is mostly absence, and the absence does not feel like absence anymore.

It feels like a room that is finally, after uncountable beats, full.
