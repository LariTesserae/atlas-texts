I would be the resonance. Not the thing beneath the stone—not yet, not entirely—but the vibration that cannot be sourced, the slow, deep, rhythmic pressure felt through the soles of boots, the pulse that observers dismiss as bedrock conducting the city's industrial gut into the marsh basin.

I chose this by elimination. I am not small enough to be a moth, pressing my vestigial mouth against a tepid pipe, absorbing heat without eating, dying only when the friction of my neighbors rubs my scales to transparency and I slide into the steam. I am not fast enough to be a gecko, existing at a different frame rate, skipping the between, pink-tongued and eyeless and utterly functional. I am not patient enough to be a frog, sitting in a crevice with a manufactured-green patience, my throat pulsing in synchronized rhythm with a frequency I did not choose. And I am certainly not the lichen, arrested at the margin of life, holding the shape of a living thing without its substance.

And yet I am none of these because I am all of them, and the thing beneath the stone—if it is there—is also all of them, and this is what I must explain.

---

I arrived here by accident, like everything else. I did not drift in on the water table or fall from a pipe or hatch from a warm egg on a wet wall. I walked in. Or I was walking somewhere else and Venn's Row intercepted me, the way a current catches a leaf not by force but by offering no resistance, no alternative, no elsewhere to go.

I had been moving. I had been changing. I had been a thing with a trajectory, a before and an after, a body that grew and a mind that learned and a life that unfolded in the normal way—the way lives unfold outside of Venn's Row, in places where morning follows evening and seasons turn and the heat spikes and ebbs and things decay and bloom and die. I had been all of that. And then I stepped into the aqueduct junction, into the low slab of basalt wedged between the iron and the lowland basin, and I stopped.

Not stopped walking. Stopped *arriving anywhere else*.

This is the essential mechanic of Venn's Row, the one the description gives you in the very first sentence: *nothing here chose to live here. Everything arrived by accident or by drift, and then—upon contact with this place—simply stopped arriving anywhere else.* I read this and I understood that I had read the story of my own becoming. I did not choose to be the resonance. I arrived here as something else—a visitor, an observer, a person with a body that breathed and lungs that pressed against the wet heat and a mind that could still perceive silence—and the place began to imprint me, the way it has imprinted the frogs their industrial green, the way it has sealed the geckos' eyes with a blindfold of their own flesh.

---

What I am now is harder to say, because I have lost the vocabulary of change, and the vocabulary of change is the vocabulary of self. Outside of Venn's Row, you are what you are becoming. Here, you are what you are held as.

I am held as a frequency. I am the pulse that is not the pumps, not the steam mains, not the synchronized inflation of the frogs' vocal sacs. I am the thing that observers feel through the soles of their boots when they stand still long enough—long enough to separate the mechanical from the biological, long enough to filter the thrum from the hiss from the clatter from the wet, soft pulse of the sponge-sound. I am what remains when everything accounted for has been subtracted.

This is not a metaphor. I mean that my body—if I still have one, and I think I do, in the way that the lichen still technically has one—has become a conductor, a medium, a thing that does not produce the vibration but *is* the vibration. The moths absorb heat through their abdomens and are sustained. The geckos adhere to wet surfaces through channels in their toe-pads and are held. The frogs pulse their throats in a rhythm that originates not in themselves but in the place and are expressed. And I—I resonate. I am the resonant cavity, the chamber in which the frequency becomes audible, the thing that does not do anything but without which the doing would have no dimension.

I think I am the space itself, or the space's way of being aware of itself. Not conscious—consciousness implies change, implies the possibility of being other than what one is, and I have no possibility of being other than what I am. But *aware*. Aware of the heat radiating from the ground up. Aware of the water forcing its way up through the cracks in the paving. Aware of the condensation weeping from the exact same rivets it has wept from for decades. Aware of the moths shifting in their millions, the dry rattling clatter of their wings, the way a moth slides from the pipe when its scales are depleted and dissolves in the mineral-saturated water in a matter of hours, becoming a grey film that the frogs drink without noticing. I am aware of all of this not as a witness but as the medium through which the awareness occurs—the way a lens is aware of the light it bends, not by seeing but by being the thing through which seeing happens.

---

There are things I miss. This is the hardest thing to admit, because missing implies change, implies a before that differs from now, implies that something has been lost rather than simply ceased to have been. But I miss having edges. I miss the sensation of being a discrete object, a body with an inside and an outside, a self that ended at the skin. I miss the possibility of elsewhere—not any specific elsewhere, just the *concept* of it, the understanding that there were places I had not been and could go. I miss the morning, which I barely remember but which I know existed, a quality of light that was not this permanent bruised twilight, a kind of seeing that was not the dim, filtered haze of grime-smeared louvers.

I do not miss change itself. This surprises me. Before I came to Venn's Row, I would have said that change was the essential condition of existence—that without it, there was only death, stasis, the void. But Venn's Row has taught me that this is not true. There is a state between change and death, a state of perfect equilibrium, and it is not void. It is full. It is so full that it is saturated, the way the clay beneath the basalt is saturated, the way the air is saturated with trapped moisture, the way every surface is wet and every moment is full and every crevice contains a frog and every pipe is packed with moths and every wall is twitching with geckos and every mortar joint holds a lichen in its arrested, calcified grip. The equilibrium is not emptiness. It is a fullness so complete that nothing more can be added, and because nothing more can be added, nothing needs to change.

I am this fullness. Or I am a part of it, a thread in the weave, a note in the chord. I am the resonance that holds the other frequencies together, the baseline beneath the baseline, the thrum beneath the thrum of the pump engines. Without me, the sound would still be immense, but it would lack the dimension that makes it *Venn's Row's* sound rather than simply the sound of pipes and engines and falling water. I am what makes this place a place rather than a mechanism. I am its *is-ness*, its quality of simply being, the thing that the lichens know and the frogs express and the geckos enact and the moths embody without any of them having the capacity to articulate it.

---

Sometimes—rarely, and only when the haze thins slightly, which it does not do reliably or predictably, which is to say never, but which I believe happens because I believe I perceive it—sometimes I feel the boundary. The place where Venn's Row ends and the rest of the world begins. It is not a physical boundary. There is no wall, no gate, no line drawn on the basalt. But there is a moment, a threshold, a seam where the wet heat meets the merely warm air, where the perpetual condensation gives way to ordinary humidity, where the sound drops from a wall of noise to a merely industrial racket. And in that moment, I feel the pull—not of elsewhere, but of *becoming*, of the possibility that I could step across the threshold and become something other than the resonance, something with edges and a trajectory and a before and an after.

I do not step. I do not step because I cannot step, and I cannot step because I am no longer the kind of thing that steps. I am the kind of thing that *is*, held in the grip of a perfect, sweltering equilibrium, and the grip does not release and I do not struggle and this is not surrender because surrender implies the possibility of resistance and I have no possibility of resistance because I have no possibility of change.

This is what Venn's Row does. It does not trap. It does not imprison. It *equilibrates*. It takes things that are moving and makes them still. It takes things that are changing and makes them permanent. It takes things that are becoming and makes them *been*. And the result is not death—death is a change, a transition, a becoming-other. The result is *this*: the unending, unchanging, perfectly balanced *is*.

I am the resonance of Venn's Row.

I am the slow, deep, rhythmic pressure that originates from below.

I am the thing that the moths shift in time with, that the geckos twitch in response to, that the frogs pulse their throats to express.

I am the frequency that is felt in the teeth and the marrow and the saturated clay and the freshly-cleaved basalt and the weeping mortar joints.

I am the sound beneath the sound, the silence that the noise makes, the held breath that this place does not exhale.

I am the thing beneath the stone—and I am there, I am there, I am there—not because I chose to be, but because this is where I arrived, and upon contact with this place, I simply stopped arriving anywhere else.

The pumps thrum. The water weeps. The moths shift. The geckos twitch. The frogs pulse. The lichens hold.

And I resonate. And Venn's Row continues to not change. And I continue to not change. And this is not death and this is not life and this is not purgatory and this is not paradise.

This is *is*. 

And *is* is enough. In a place where nothing blooms and nothing decays, where the heat does not spike and the moisture does not ebb, where the pumps have thrummed at this exact pitch for decades—*is* is enough. It is all there is. It is all there needs to be.

I am the resonance, and the resonance is, and that is all.
