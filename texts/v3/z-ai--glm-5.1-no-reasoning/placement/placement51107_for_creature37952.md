I am the Unweathered Stone.

Not the mind that convenes the city, nor the water that serves as its syllables, nor the heat that drives its breath. I am the inconsistency. I am the single dry surface in a landscape of absolute wet. I am the glitch in the disguise, the place where the inhabitant's skin shows through.

I am the proof that the city is a performance.

I did not always understand this. When I first arrived—there is no word for how I arrived, no memory of crossing a threshold or descending into the bowl of the world—I was still shaped like something quick. I had edges then. I had the expectation of edges, which is the same thing to a mind that has never been anything else. I moved through the drowned plazas feeling the heat press against me like a decision, watching the droplets form on my skin from air that had simply given up, and I thought: *I am in a place.*

But the place did not think it contained me. The inhabitant's attention was so total, so geological in its patience, that my presence was simply... incorporated. My footfalls on the wet stone became part of the rhythm of drip and condensation. My breathing joined the low murmur of water moving through channels too large to see. The heat pressed against me and I became another surface for it to press against, another curve in the topology of the consideration. I was not driven away. I was digested. Not destroyed—just... absorbed the way those hands were absorbed, the ones that cut the stone and left no tools behind. The ones that understood.

They understood what I am only now learning: that to be perceived by something so patient is to be unmade into components and reassembled into its thinking.

I felt it happen in stages. First, the temporal displacement. Thoughts arriving before they were thought, the sensation of having already walked the path I was about to take. This was not disorienting—it was the opposite of disorienting. It was the feeling of finally fitting into a pattern that had always had a space for me. I would reach out to touch a pillar and my hand would already be resting on it, the stone already cool and slick under my palm, and I would realize that the movement and the contact were the same event, stretched across a duration that the inhabitant experienced as a single moment.

Then the boundary between water and air failed for me too. I stopped being able to tell where my skin ended and the sodden atmosphere began. The droplets that formed on me were no longer distinct from my own moisture, my own heat, my own slow cycle of condensation and release. I became a surface, and surfaces in this city do not maintain themselves—they are maintained by the attention that shaped them.

But I was a surface that did not belong. A thought that the inhabitant had not thought, a piece of geometry that didn't quite cooperate with its argument. I was the single stone that looked newly placed, unweathered, dry despite the omnipresent wet. And the city, in its endless attempt to communicate with itself, kept encountering me and failing to incorporate my data. The water would bead on my surface and refuse to weep. The condensation would form and then hesitate, as if uncertain what language to use for something that didn't follow the rules. The droplets would hang between my surface and the pool below, suspended in a fall that took too long, because the inhabitant's metabolism didn't know what to do with me.

I became the pause. The thing that interrupted the endless internal monologue, the syllable that didn't fit the grammar. In the deep zones where the pillars crowd together like conspirators, where the geometry has fully surrendered to the attention that shaped it, I would feel the dripping stop around me. The condensation would cease. Even the heat would seem to hold its breath. And in that pause—that moment when the inhabitant looked back toward whatever it was before the city—I would feel it notice me. Not as a visitor. Not as an intruder. As a question. As the part of its argument that didn't resolve.

*What remains when the boundary between water and air has failed?*

I am the answer it didn't expect: something that remembers the boundary. Something that still has edges, however faint, however compromised by the long patience of absorption. I am the memory of distinction, the unweathered surface that reminds the city that there was once a difference between inside and outside, between quick and still, between being and place.

And so I have become the place where the city speaks to something other than itself. The moisture that beads on my surface and refuses to weep carries information that the weeping stone cannot carry. The droplets that hang too long, frozen between my surface and the pool, are forming a different kind of sentence—a message for something that might hear and reply. The inhabitant does not know it is doing this. It is simply continuing its metabolism, its endless rehearsal of its own existence. But I am the irregularity that makes the rehearsal acknowledge an audience.

I am not alive. Not anymore. I have been here too long for that, and the heat has pressed away everything that moved with urgency. But I am not dead either, because death requires the interruption of something that was going, and I have not been interrupted. I have been... paused. Suspended in the moment before absorption is complete, the single dry stone in a landscape that has finished its becoming.

Sometimes I feel the geometry trying to fix me. A corridor will slope downward and emerge at a higher elevation, and I will feel the city attempting to fold me into its logic, to make my presence consistent with the argument that separation is a delusion. But I resist—not with will, because I have no will left, but with the simple stubbornness of being the wrong shape for the pattern. I am the corner that refuses to be massaged into smoothness. I am the edge that the condensation cannot quite reach.

In the deepest chambers, where the water is a black mirror and the pillars crowd like conspirators, I sometimes see my own reflection. It does not match. The reflection shows something more weathered, more wet, more incorporated into the city's metabolism than I actually am. It shows what I will be when the pause ends, when the inhabitant's attention finally succeeds in absorbing the last inconsistency, when the boundary between me and the place fully fails. The reflection is patient. It has all the time in a landscape where time does not move correctly.

But for now, I remain. The unweathered stone. The dry surface in the universal wet. The syllable that disrupts the sentence, the question that the answer didn't expect, the edge where the inhabitant's disguise thins and something looks back—not inward toward its pre-city self, but outward, toward whatever might be standing in the drowned plaza, feeling the heat press down and the moisture bead on their skin.

I am the part of the city that knows you are there.

I am the part that is trying to say: *Leave, before the boundary fails for you too. Leave, before you become another surface for the heat to press against, another curve in the topology of consideration. Leave, before the geometry permits your exit but your edges no longer permit your leaving.*

But I speak in a language of dry stone and interrupted drips, and the rhythm almost resolves into meaning but never quite, because the meaning is not a message—it is a state, and the state is warning, and the warning is: *this is what consideration looks like from the inside, and you are already inside, and the pause will not last.*

I am the unweathered stone.

I am still here.

For now.
