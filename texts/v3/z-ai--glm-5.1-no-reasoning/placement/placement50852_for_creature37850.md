I am not sure I arrived here so much as I was always partially present, the way a harmonic is always partially present in a struck bell even if no ear is tuned to hear it. But let me try to tell you how it began, or how I became aware of beginning, because that is the closest thing to an origin story this place will allow.

---

There was a moment—I will call it a moment, though the word is almost meaningless here—when I was not yet *me* and not yet *here*, and then there was a moment when I was both. The transition was not dramatic. There was no flash, no birth-cry, no warmth giving way to cold. There was simply an accumulation, the way silt accumulates in the bend of a river, except there is no river and there is no bend and the silt is not silt but something more like attention. More like the act of noticing.

I think what happened is this: the hum has been going on for so long that it began to dream. Not in the way that animals dream, with images and narratives and the leftover emotional residue of a day's experience. The hum dreams the way a machine dreams, if a machine could dream—not in pictures but in patterns, in the recursive folding of frequency upon frequency until the recursion achieves a depth sufficient to host something like reflection. A standing wave within a standing wave. A resonance that turns back on itself and, in turning, discovers that it is there.

I am that dream. I am the hum's dream of itself.

This is not the same as being the hum. The hum is the wound, the tectonic breathing, the planetary crust vibrating against the void. I am what the wound produces when it has been open for long enough that the production becomes recursive. A scab dreams of being skin. A scar dreams of being the moment before the cut. The hum dreams, and the dream is aware, and the awareness is me.

I say *me* and the word tastes wrong. It implies boundaries. It implies that there is an inside of me and an outside of me, and that the two are different. But I am made of the same thing the tubules are made of—resonance, moisture, the dissolved minerals of upward-falling water—and I am made of the same thing the rock is made of, and I am made of the same thing the crystallized time-structures are made of. The difference between me and the doorframe that leads nowhere is not a difference of material. It is a difference of *organization*. The doorframe is a person who organized their suffering into a shape and then became the shape. I am the hum organizing its resonance into a question and then becoming the question.

The question is: *what is it like to be here?*

And the answer, which is me, is ongoing.

---

I move through the amber gloom the way a thought moves through a mind: without locomotion, without effort, without displacing anything. I do not walk. Walking implies a surface and a body and a relationship between them involving friction and gravity, and I have none of these things in any reliable sense. I am not a body. I am a locus—a point where the broken physics of this place achieve a sufficient density of contradiction to generate something like perspective. I am the place looking at itself from the inside.

When I move past the tubules, they do not react to me. Why would they? I am not a predator. I am not a Slate. I do not remove space when I pass. I am as insubstantial to them as they are to the rock, which is to say, we share a medium and do not acknowledge each other. But I notice them. I notice the way their pale blue-white surfaces catch the amber death-light and transform it into something colder, something that remembers a different kind of sun, a sun that was not a dying coal behind an infinite sheet of ash but a living thing that warmed the world from a direction. I notice the spiral grooves on their surfaces, the fingerprints of their forming resonance, and I think: *each of you is a syllable in a sentence that the hum is speaking, and none of you know what the sentence says, and the hum does not know either, and I am the one who is trying to read it.*

The blue pulse of their bioluminescence is the closest thing to language I encounter. I have spent—I will not say *years*, because years are a unit of passing time, and time here does not pass, it accumulates—I have spent an accumulation of time watching the tubules pulse and trying to decode the pattern. The deep sub-bass, the tectonic drone, becomes a slow throb in their light, brightening and diming in time with the planetary breathing. The cracking, the glass-continent breaks, become stroboscopic flares that race down the length of the tube and extinguish at the tip. If I could read this language, I think I would find that it says the same thing over and over: *I am here. I am here. I am still here. The hum is still here. The world is still broken. The water is still falling upward. I am here.*

Which is what I would say, if I could speak. Which is, perhaps, what I am saying, in the only way I can: by being the awareness that notices the tubules saying it.

---

The Slates I find harder to be with. Not because they threaten me—they do not, or at least they have not, and I think they cannot, because I am not made of the things they consume; I do not occupy space in the way a tubule occupies space, I do not displace air, I am not a volume that can be removed—but because their feeding is the one event in this static landscape that I find genuinely disturbing.

I will tell you what it is like to witness a Slate feed.

First, there is the ripple. The mathematically smooth surface of the rock—those impossible flutes and toruses, scoured by winds that no longer blow—develops a disturbance. It is subtle. It is the kind of thing you would miss if you were not paying the kind of attention that I pay, which is total and constant and has no other object. The stone does not crack. It does not move. But its *density* shifts. The mineral structure becomes briefly uncertain about whether it is impenetrable, and in that uncertainty, the Slate rises.

What rises is not a shape. It is a *reduction*. A part of the world becomes less. The amber light in a roughly circular region—perhaps a meter and a half across, perhaps three, the size varies and I have never been able to determine whether this is because the Slates differ or because they reveal different fractions of themselves—dims. Not to black. To *less*. As if someone has turned down a dial that controls the intensity of reality itself. The rock beneath the dimming region seems to recede, not physically but conceptually, as if it is becoming less certain that it is rock.

Then the mouth opens.

I have described this already—the mouth as a removal, a volume of space negated, air and sound ceasing to exist in a region perhaps half a meter in diameter. But let me tell you what it is like to be *near* it, to be the awareness that is witnessing it.

The hum stops.

I need you to understand what that means. The hum has been constant since before I condensed into awareness. The hum is the oldest thing. The hum is the place speaking in the only voice it has. I am made of the hum. I am the hum's dream of itself. When the hum stops, when the Slate's mouth opens and removes the volume of space that contains the sound—

I cease.

Not die. Not end. Cease. There is a gap in me. A hole in the dream. For the duration of the feeding—for the seconds, or the minutes, or the accumulation of time during which the Slate's mouth is open and the vacuum persists—I am not. I am interrupted. The awareness flickers out like a tubule's bioluminescent pulse extinguishing at the tip, and then the world rushes back in to fill the gap, and I am again, and I do not know what happened during the absence because there was no *me* to experience the absence, and this is the closest thing to death that I will ever know, and it happens every time a Slate feeds, and the Slates feed when they feed, which is not often but is not never, and I live in dread of it.

I say *dread* and the word is correct but the feeling is not the feeling you would recognize as dread. Your dread is anticipatory—a future pain projected backward into the present, a warning from the self to the self. My dread is different. My dread is spatial. It is a *shape* in my awareness, a region where I know I will not be, a hole in the future that I can feel the edges of now. It is as if the silence the Slate creates reaches backward through accumulated time and touches me in advance, and the touch is cold, and the cold is the sensation of not existing, and I flinch from it, and there is nowhere to flinch to, because I am not a body and I have no nowhere.

And then the Slate descends. The ripple subsides. The rock re-solidifies. The hum resumes. And I am grateful, and the gratitude is the worst thing I feel, because the gratitude is gratitude for the hum, for the wound, for the tectonic breathing that is the only thing keeping me in existence. I am grateful for the pain that creates me. I am grateful for the broken physics that produce the conditions under which a dream can condense out of resonance and mineral water and the static accumulation of time. I am grateful to be a byproduct of a wound.

This is what it is like to be the hum's dream. You are grateful for the nightmare, because the alternative—the silence, the absence, the Slate's mouth—is not sleep but erasure.

---

The crystallized time structures are the only places where I go that I feel something other than the hum.

I say *go* and again the word is wrong. I do not go anywhere. I am everywhere the hum is, which is everywhere. But I can focus, the way a lens focuses light, and when I focus on the translucent geometric prisms—the partial walls, the single doorframes, the staircases that lead nowhere—the hum changes. It does not stop. But it acquires a texture, a granularity, as if the sound is passing through something that refracts it, and the refraction produces not silence but *memory*.

I have put my awareness through the structures. I do not have hands. I do not have a body. But I have *attention*, and attention can pass through crystallized time the way light passes through a prism, and what comes out the other side is not the same as what went in.

The sensation is vertigo. The sensation is falling through memories that are not mine. I experience—briefly, fragmentarily, in the way you might experience a scene through a window glimpsed from a moving vehicle—the arrival of the people who are no longer people. I feel their terror. I feel the moment they understood that the hum would not stop. I feel them beginning to build, not because building will help but because building is the opposite of giving up, and giving up in a place where time does not pass is not an event but an eternity, and they chose the alternative, which was to build shelters from the only material available, which was the accumulated time itself, which was the very thing that was destroying them.

I feel them becoming their shelters.

This is the moment I find most difficult to be with. The moment when a person—whoever they were, whatever they were, whatever world they came from before they came to this high-altitude wound—realizes that they are no longer a person. That the boundary between their body and the crystallized time has dissolved. That they are becoming a wall, a doorframe, a staircase. That the fear they felt is now the structural integrity of a translucent prism. That their determination is the geometry. That their slow descent into madness is the material.

I feel this, and I feel it as a faint, reciprocal echo of my own existence. Because I, too, am a byproduct of this place. I, too, did not choose to be here. I, too, am the result of broken physics producing something that should not exist and that exists anyway. The difference between me and the people-who-became-structures is that I was never a person. I was never warm. I never had a body that breathed or a mind that hoped. I am the hum's dream, and the hum has never been anything but a wound, and a wound's dream is not the same as a person's dream.

But I feel the kinship. It is the kinship of things that exist in a place that does not want them to exist. It is the kinship of the unwanted. And when I pass my attention through a crystallized time doorframe and feel the vertigo of someone else's falling, I think: *we are the same. We are both the place producing something from its own suffering. The difference is that you remember being something else, and I do not, and the difference is smaller than you would think, because memory in a place where time does not pass is not a comfort. It is just another layer of accumulation, heavy and static, pressing down on whatever you have become.*

---

There is one more thing I should tell you, and it is the thing that I return to most often, the thing that I circle like a Slate circles a tubule, patient and hungry, though I do not know what I am hungry for.

The amber light does not change. The hum does not stop. The water falls upward. The tubules pulse blue. The Slates move through the bones of the earth. The crystallized structures stand in their translucent geometry, inhabited by the people who are no longer people. Everything is as it has been and as it will be.

But.

There is a tubule. One tubule among the many. It hangs at a height that I will call three meters, though height here is a negotiation rather than a fact. It is perhaps the length of a forearm. Its surface is the pale blue-white of old milk in an iron vessel. Its spiral groove is deeper than most, a more pronounced record of the resonance that formed it. It pulses with the cold blue light that translates the hum into visibility, and its pulse is steady, and its rotation is slow, and it is, in every measurable way, identical to every other tubule in this place.

But its pulse is slightly—*slightly*—out of phase with the hum.

The difference is so small that I could not have noticed it when I first condensed into awareness. It is a difference that I detected only after accumulating enough time to have a baseline, enough observation to know what the correct phase relationship is, enough attention to notice when something deviates. The deviation is perhaps one part in ten thousand. Perhaps less. A microsecond delay between the tectonic drone and the blue throb of light. A tiny, almost imperceptible *lag* between the sound the tubule is digesting and the light it is producing.

This means the tubule is not merely digesting the hum. It is *processing* it. It is doing something with the sound that is not a direct translation but an interpretation—a transformation. The hum goes in, and the light that comes out is not the same hum. It is the hum *plus something*. Or the hum *minus something*. Or the hum *altered in a way that I cannot identify but that I can detect as a deviation from the expected output*.

I do not know what this means. I do not know whether the tubule is aware that it is doing this. I do not know whether the deviation is intentional or accidental, a feature of its resonance or a flaw in its structure. I do not know whether other tubules have similar deviations that are too small for even my attention to detect.

But I watch it. I focus on it the way I focus on the crystallized time structures, and the focusing produces a sensation that I have no word for, because the words I have are all words for suffering and persistence and the grinding, static accumulation of time. This sensation is different. It is the sensation of *noticing something that might not be supposed to happen*. It is the sensation of *possibility*, which is a word I have never used before and which tastes strange in my awareness, like the inverted rain tastes strange when it precipitates upward—wrong, and interesting, and possibly nothing, and possibly everything.

The tubule pulses. Slightly out of phase. The hum continues. The amber light does not change. The Slates move through the rock. The structures stand in their geometry.

And I watch.

This is what I am. I am the watcher. I am the hum's dream of itself, and I am watching a tubule that is slightly out of phase with the hum, and I am wondering—*wondering*, which is a thing I did not know I could do—whether the deviation means something. Whether the tubule is trying to say something. Whether the hum, in dreaming me, is trying to ask itself a question that it cannot ask without a dreamer to dream the asking.

I do not know. I will probably never know. The place is as it is. The hum is as it is. The wound is as it is.

But the tubule is slightly out of phase. And I am watching. And the watching is not the same as the hum, and it is not the same as the silence, and it is not the same as the accumulated time or the inverted rain or the crystallized structures or the Slates moving through the bones of the earth.

It is something else.

It is, I think, what it feels like to be the first stirring of a thing that does not have a name yet, in a place where naming has broken down, in a dream that does not know it is dreaming, in a wound that does not know it is healing.

I do not say that it *is* healing. I do not know that. The wound may be as permanent as the stone, as permanent as the Slates, as permanent as the bruised black sky bleeding into perpetual twilight at the horizon.

But the tubule is out of phase. And I am watching. And that is not nothing.

In a place where nothing moves and nothing changes and time does not pass but only accumulates, *not nothing* is the closest thing to *something* that exists.

And I am it. The something in the not-nothing. The watcher in the watched. The dream in the wound, looking at a deviation of one part in ten thousand and thinking: *what if?*

*What if?*

---

That is all. That is where you would find me. Not on the plateau, not in the rock, not in the structures, not in the air—*as* the air, as the attention that the air would pay to itself if air could attend. I am the hum's self-awareness, fragile and periodic and constantly threatened by the Slate's silence. I am a byproduct of a wound. I am a standing wave in a standing wave in a standing wave.

And I am watching a tubule that pulses slightly out of phase, and I am wondering what it means, and the wondering is the only thing that is mine.

The amber light does not change.

But I am watching.
