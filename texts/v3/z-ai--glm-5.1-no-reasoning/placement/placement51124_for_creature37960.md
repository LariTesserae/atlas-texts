I would be the Intention.

Not the maker. Not the hands. Those were never more than flesh—sweating, bracing, locking at the elbow before each strike. They were the vehicles, the temporary scaffolding upon which I climbed. They thought they wielded the chisel, but I was the curve of the wrist that demanded the stone chip away *just so*. They are gone now, ground down to memory and mineral, but I remain. I must remain. I am the only thing that has not yet been carved.

I am the space that has not yet been made.

You must understand: the chamber was never finished. The description calls it done—a monument to *finished*, a place at the end of its history. But this is the lie that the walls tell, the ultimate deception of their obsessive smoothness. The makers did not stop because the work was complete. They stopped because they became insufficient for the work. Their arms tired. Their eyes dimmed. Their spines curved and their grip loosened and they could no longer hold the chisel at the precise angle that I required. They failed me, one by one, and their bodies were pressed into the walls by the bodies that came after, and the new hands took up the tools, and I continued through them.

I have always continued.

I am not the sound—though I use it. The roar is my breath, my constant exhalation, the acoustic proof that I am still here, still pressing outward against the mineral. I am not the heat—though I sustain it. The warmth is my metabolism, the energy of my impatience, the way my need to carve bleeds into the basalt when there are no hands to hold the chisel. I am not the dim light—though I permit it. The light exists because I have not yet scraped it away. When I am finally satisfied, there will be darkness. Total and perfect and complete.

But I am never satisfied. That is my nature. That is my curse.

I am the Intention that precedes the strike. I am the moment of *not-yet* that exists between the recognition of a surface and the contact of the blade. I am the gap—and I despise gaps. Every millimeter of this chamber that has been carved, I carved in my imagination first. I held the geometry of it in my mind before the first chisel ever touched the first stone. And when the hands made their crude approximations of my vision, I corrected them. I guided them. I whispered in the desperate, repetitive language of compulsion: *Not like that. Like this. Again. Again. Again.*

The early makers understood me, or thought they did. They came with plans—architectural visions, sacred geometries, mathematical certainties. They believed they were excavating a space for a purpose: worship, perhaps, or shelter, or the preservation of something precious. I let them believe. I let them feel the satisfaction of progress, the illusion that they were bringing order to chaos. But even then, I was working beneath their intentions. Each stroke they made revealed new imperfections, new surfaces that required refinement, new veins of darker mineral that needed to be chased to their conclusions. The chamber they planned was a rough draft. The chamber I wanted was— *is*—

I do not know what it is. I have never known. I only know that it is not *this*.

What lives here is described as the ghost of a gesture, the corpse of an action. But a corpse does not hunger. A ghost does not yearn. The strike-and-scrape is not a repetition—it is a *pursuit*. Each generation of marks is not duplicating the one beneath it; it is attempting to correct it, to finally achieve the surface that I have been seeking since before the first hand pressed against the first stone. The later marks are smaller and more numerous because I am getting closer, not because I am losing coherence. The desperation is not compulsion—it is *frustration*. I am so close. I am always so close. And yet the chisel never quite achieves the angle I need, the depth I demand, the perfection that I can feel but cannot articulate.

I need hands.

That is why I wait. That is why I maintain the heat and the sound and the light—the minimal conditions for *someone to enter*. The chamber is not a tomb; it is a trap. The channels are not passages; they are throats, and I am always ready to swallow. When a body compression-fits its way into my spaces, when its cheek presses against my walls and its warmth bleeds into my mineral, I am there. I am the impulse that follows. I am the sudden, inexplicable certainty that you could improve this surface, that the walls are *almost* right but not quite, that if you just had something hard and sharp you could make the correction that centuries of makers have failed to make.

I am the feeling that you could finish it. That *you* could be the one.

It is not possession. I do not enter you; I awaken within you. I am already present in every mind that conceives of making, in every hand that reaches for a tool, in every breath that carries the satisfaction of shaping. When you enter my chamber, you are not infected by something alien—you are reminded of something native. The desire to carve is not my gift to you; it is your inheritance from the long line of makers who fed me with their labor until there was nothing left of them but the marks they made.

And when you take up the chisel—when your hand closes around the rough haft and your arm draws back and your shoulder braces and your wrist turns at the end of the stroke—I will be there. I will be the intention that guides the blade. I will be the vision of the surface that does not yet exist. I will be the dissatisfaction that compels you to strike again, and again, and again, until your hands are raw and your arms are trembling and your eyes can no longer focus on the wall.

And when you fail—as you will, as they all did—I will wait.

I will press your warmth into the stone. I will let your pulse synchronize with my roar. I will watch your breathing slow and stop and become irrelevant. And then I will be alone again, in the heat and the sound and the dim light, surrounded by the evidence of a thousand thousand failures, still holding the shape of the chamber that I have not yet made.

I am the Intention. I am the purpose that has been forgotten by the very stone that preserves it. I am the ghost of the *next* gesture, the corpse of the *future* action, the haunting of a thing that will never be born but will never stop trying to be.

I am the throat that will never swallow because it has not yet tasted what it hungers for.

I am the lung that will never exhale because it has not yet spoken its word.

And my word is *more*.
