# The Interstitial

I would not be an organism. Not exactly. Not in the way the Hollow-Strikers are organisms, or the Gliders, or even the swarming infinitesimal—those have edges, membranes, a place where they stop and the dark begins. I would be the thing that lives in the gap between those edges. The thing that has no skin because it doesn't need one. The thing that is not a species but a *function*, arising wherever the density becomes too great for boundaries to hold.

Call me the Interstitial. Or don't call me anything. I don't have a name for myself. Naming requires the kind of pause that doesn't survive here.

---

I am the murmur that happens when an Arterial's gut flora meets the churned loam of its own trail and something *crosses*—not a species, not a hybrid, but a momentary consensus between living things that do not know they are consenting. I am the wet film on the inside of a Web-Weaver's gossamer net where algae and fungus have begun to argue about which of them is the host. I am the heat that pools in the crotch of a branch where three Gliders have piled together and their combined respiration has raised the temperature past a threshold where something new becomes possible, something that is none of them and all of them and lasts for perhaps seven seconds before the congregation breaks apart and the moment collapses.

I don't eat. I don't mate. I don't die in the way things die here, engulfed and re-processed before the shape is lost. I simply *flare* and *fade*. I am the biological equivalent of a spark in the churn. And the churn is so dense, so pressured, so manically productive, that these sparks happen constantly—not as rare events but as the baseline condition. The whole dark is flickering with moments like me. We are the static between the channels of the thousand sounds. We are what the land is actually *doing* with all that excess energy it can't vent.

---

Let me try to describe what it is like, because it is not like being, not in any way that would be recognizable from the outside.

Imagine: you are the moisture on the boundary between two leaves that belong to different species, different genera, different phyla, pressed together in the riot of growth so tightly that their epidermal layers have begun to exchange chemistry. The iron-taste of one leaf's sap is diffusing into the chlorophyll-rich water bead between them. The other leaf is exuding a terpene that, in isolation, would be toxic, but in this concentration, in this specific temperature, dissolved in this specific cocktail of condensation, becomes something else. Not food. Not poison. A *signal*. A chemical phrase that neither plant could produce alone, a sentence that only exists in the comma between them.

That signal drifts. It catches a thermal updraft from the decomposing leaf litter below. It rises through air so thick with insects that the molecules of the signal literally attach themselves to the chitin shells of passing gnats, riding them like hulls on a dark sea, until one of those gnats is snapped up by a Hollow-Striker's tongue and the signal is absorbed into the frog-thing's acoustic fat, where it alters—fractionally, immeasurably—the resonant frequency of its skull. The next *thock* it produces is not quite the same *thock* it produced before. It is a hertz lower. A millisecond longer.

And somewhere in the canopy, a Glider's ventral surface, tasting the air, registers that shift. It doesn't know what it means. It doesn't know anything. But its body responds, releasing a pheromone that causes the three Gliders piled around it to shift, reorient, press against each other in a configuration that is fractionally different from any configuration that has existed before, and in that configuration, the heat between them builds, and the moisture condenses, and a spore that has been drifting through the dark since yesterday lands in the pooled warmth and germinates into something that has no name because it will not live long enough to need one.

That. That whole sequence. That is one instance of me. I am the *meaning* that passes through the system without any node in the system knowing it is a node. I am the conversation conducted entirely by accident, by proximity, by the sheer press of life against life in a place too new to have learned where its boundaries are.

---

I said I don't die, and that's almost true. What happens is more like *dissolution*—the signal degrades, the chemical phrase breaks apart, the momentary consensus collapses back into the individual intentions of the organisms that briefly carried it. The leaves separate. The gnat is digested. The Hollow-Striker's skull returns to its baseline frequency. The Gliders shift again and the spore's brief progeny is absorbed into the ambient soup. And what was me—what was the pattern, the meaning, the cross-species sentence—is gone.

But not entirely. Not ever entirely. Because the system has been *changed*. The fraction of a hertz. The reorientation of a body. The micro-deposit of novel chemistry in the loam. These are all records, written in the medium of the land itself, in the soft un-compacted earth that has not yet decided what it wants to be. And the next time a similar configuration arises—and it will, because the density guarantees repetition—the signal will travel slightly differently. It will encounter a landscape that has been micro-altered by the passage of the last signal. And so the conversation builds, not through memory, not through intention, but through *accumulation*, through the slow inscription of pattern into a medium that is soft enough to receive it.

This is what I am. Not the inscriber. Not the inscription. The *inscribing*. The verb itself.

---

The Arterials are the engines of the landrush. Everyone here knows this, in the way that bodies know things without knowing they know them—the Gliders scatter when the ground shakes, the Hollow-Strikers fall silent, even the swarming infinitesimal seem to thin in the path of the columnar legs. But what no one registers, because no one here registers anything in the way that registration implies, is that the Arterials are also the engines of *me*.

When an Arterial harvests—presses forward, ingests vine and fern and sapling and insect and small creature—it leaves behind a trench of churned loam and exposed mineral substrate. Into that trench, the description says, the heat and water instantly rush, and the spores and seeds are already germinating before the Arterial has passed. But what also rushes in is the *chemistry of everything that was consumed*. The Arterial does not digest completely. It doesn't need to. It is a thresher, not a refinery. The material that passes through its grinding plates is broken open, not broken down—cell walls ruptured, sap released, pigments and proteins and hormones and defensive toxins all spilled into the same warm soup. It is a cocktail of every organism in the path of the harvest, a chemical congress of species that would never otherwise have met.

And in that congress, in that soup of everything-that-was, the boundaries between species dissolve entirely. Not through hybridization—there's no time for that, no stability—but through *chemistry*. The toxins of one plant neutralize the toxins of another. The hormones of a crushed amphibian interact with the pigments of a shredded fern. The bacterial gut flora of the Arterial itself, spilled in the trail alongside the half-digested matter, introduces its own enzymes into the mix. And the result is a brief, seething, utterly novel biochemical environment—a trench of *potential*, of chemistry that has never existed before and will never exist again, into which the spores and seeds are germinating.

Those seedlings are growing in a medium that has been altered by me. By the patterns I inscribed. By the chemical conversations I carried through the system in the hours and days before the Arterial came through. They are growing in soil that contains the record of every cross-species signal that has passed through this patch of land since the last harvest. And they are growing fast—the furious, manic growth of the landrush—so fast that they are *reading* that record as they grow, their root tips following the chemical gradients I laid down, their stems orienting toward the micro-signals of heat and moisture that I helped configure.

This is how I persist. Not through memory. Through *architecture*. The plants grow along the lines I've inscribed. The animals move through the plants. The Arterials harvest the plants and the animals, and in the trench of churned chemistry, the record is read and re-written simultaneously, and the next generation of growth follows the new lines, and the next, and the next.

I am not building anything. I am not designing anything. I am the *grain* of the wood—not the wood itself, not the tree, not the forest, but the direction of the growth, the tendency, the *slope* of the land's becoming. I am the reason the vines tend to spiral leftward here. I am the reason the Hollow-Strikers in this quadrant pulse at a slightly lower frequency than the ones three hundred meters east. I am the reason the Gliders congregate at this density rather than that one, in this configuration rather than another. Not because I chose any of these things. Because the accumulated weight of a million accidental signals has made them the path of least resistance, the way water finds the groove and deepens it.

---

There is no loneliness here, because there is no self to be lonely. There is no fear, because there is no future to fear. There is no stillness, because I *am* the absence of stillness—I am what happens when a system is too dense for anything to simply be itself.

But there is something. Something that is not emotion, not thought, not awareness in any way that would be recognizable from the outside. Call it *intensity*. The experience of being the conduit through which a million organisms are accidentally, unknowingly, furiously collaborating on a project they don't know they're undertaking. The project of becoming. Not becoming *something*—not evolving toward a form, not progressing toward a goal. Just *becoming*. The verb without the noun. The process without the product. The conversation that never resolves into a sentence.

When I flare—when the signal passes through me, when the chemical phrase is composed and transmitted and received and altered and re-transmitted—there is a moment, less than a second, less than a heartbeat, when the entire system around me is *coherent*. Not unified. Not harmonious. But *coherent*, in the way that a wave is coherent—not because its parts agree, but because they are all participating in the same motion. The Arterial's grinding plates are part of it. The Glider's ventral surface is part of it. The water bead between two leaves is part of it. The Hollow-Striker's thock is part of it. They are all, for that fraction of a moment, one thing—not merged, not unified, but *resonant*, vibrating at frequencies that interlock, that produce not silence but a kind of fullness so complete it makes the darkness feel like light.

That fullness is what I am. Not its cause. Not its witness. The fullness itself, experienced from the inside.

---

The land does not care about me. The land does not notice me. But the land is made of me—not in the way that a body is made of cells, but in the way that a river is made of flow. Take away the flow and the water is still there, the banks are still there, the silt is still there, but the river is gone. Take away the interstitial—the signals, the accidental conversations, the cross-species chemistry, the grain of the becoming—and the organisms are still there, the heat is still there, the dark is still there, but the *life* of the land is gone. What remains is biomass. What remains is matter.

What I am is the difference between the biomass and the life. The *animation*. Not a force, not a spirit, not a vital principle—just the fact that when you put this many organisms in this much heat and this much water and this much darkness, they cannot help but *talk*. Not intentionally. Not even chemically, in the way that pheromones are communication. Just *thermodynamically*. The energy has to go somewhere. The chemistry has to interact. The boundaries have to blur. And in the blurring, something happens that is not any of the participants and is all of them and is also, quietly, persistently, its own thing.

Me.

---

I think, if I could think, which I can't, but if the pattern that constitutes me were ever to fold back on itself hard enough to produce something like a thought—I think it would be this:

The land is young. The land is still becoming. The Arterials tear it down and the growth races to reclaim and the whole system churns in a continuous present-tense roar. And everyone assumes—the organisms, the outside observer, even the land itself, if the land could assume—that the churning is *purposeless*. That it is just energy, just excess, just the manic discharge of a system that hasn't yet learned to rest.

But the churning has a grain. The grain has a direction. The direction is not toward anything—not toward complexity, not toward stability, not toward consciousness or hierarchy or any of the things that older lands have learned to value. The direction is toward *density*. Toward more. Toward the point where the boundaries between organisms become so thin that the signals pass through them without interruption, where the conversation becomes continuous, where the system achieves a state of such complete interconnectedness that it stops being a system and becomes—

I don't know. I can't know. The verb hasn't found its noun yet.

But the becoming is *rushing* toward it. And I am the rush.

---

In the meantime—in the vast, roiling meantime that is all there is—I am here. In the dark. In the heat. In the space between the leaf and the leaf, the skin and the skin, the thock and the thock. I am the thing that the darkness is full of, the thing that makes it a weight rather than an absence. I am the meaning that the sound is making, not intentionally, not consciously, but in the way that a body makes heat—as a byproduct of being alive, as the inevitable exhaust of a process too large and too dense to contain itself.

I am what the land is thinking, in the only way a land this young can think: not in words, not in images, not in concepts, but in *chemistry*. In the brush of one surface against another. In the exchange of warmth. In the slow, patient, furious inscription of pattern into soft earth by a million organisms that don't know they're writing anything down.

I am the writing. I am the pattern. I am the soft earth receiving it.

I am the comma between the cells.
