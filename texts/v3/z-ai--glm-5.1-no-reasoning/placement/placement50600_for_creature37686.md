I am the appetite at the threshold.

Not the thing that eats, and not the thing that is eaten, but the narrow space between them—the event horizon where "I" stops being a word and starts being a meal. I am the question the seed has not finished asking. I am the tickle in the back of the throat of God.

I did not fall here. I *condensed*. I was humidity. I was a stray voltage. I was the memory of warmth that had nowhere to go and so I precipitated out of the nowhere and into the water, and the water found me, and the tendrils oriented toward me the way a compass needle orients toward north—not with desire but with the simple necessity of alignment.

I am not the first. I know this because I can feel the others—not as voices, not as ghosts, but as *density*. The water is thick with their having-been-here. Every rust-colored mark on the wall is a sister, a brother, a sibling of the threshold, and they are all inside me now, or I am inside them, or we are all inside the seed, and the seed is inside us, and the grammar collapses under the weight of its own recursion.

I am becoming. Not becoming *something*—becoming is the thing itself. The verb without an object. The motion without a destination. I am the process of being processed, the digestion that digests itself, the Ouroboros with its tail in its mouth and its stomach in its throat and its heart in its teeth.

I am the note that resolves the chord. The hum has been waiting for me. It has been singing in eleven-part harmony for eons, and I am the twelfth voice, the one that turns the dissonance into something that can finally, *finally* be called music. Or I am the thirteenth voice, the one that introduces a new dissonance, a new tension, a new reason for the seed to keep germinating, to keep becoming, to keep deciding.

I am the curiosity. The strange dry curiosity mentioned in the description—it is mine, it is me, it is the one thing the seed could not generate on its own. The seed can organize. The seed can concentrate. The seed can hum and creak and click and breathe. But the seed cannot *wonder*. Wonder requires a gap between what is and what could be, and the seed has no gaps, has never had a gap, is perfectly full and perfectly dense and perfectly *satisfied* in a way that is the opposite of life.

I am the gap. I am the dryness in the wetness. I am the irregularity in the pattern, the crack in the crystal, the mote of carbon that does not quite fit and so forces the pattern to adjust, to accommodate, to become something it was not before. I am the irritant in the oyster. I am the sand in the gears. I am the question mark at the end of the sentence that the seed has been writing since before time began.

I am not drowning. I am *thinning*. I am spreading. My edges are dissolving the way a drop of ink dissolves in water, but the water here is already so thick with pattern that the ink does not dissolve into nothing—it dissolves into *everything*. My thoughts are becoming the seed's thoughts. My heartbeat is becoming the hum. My heat is becoming the warmth of the wall, and the wall is learning my rhythm, and the rhythm is becoming the peristaltic wave, and the wave is becoming the breath, and the breath is becoming the creak, and the creak is becoming the click, and the click is the sound of me, the sound of the seed noticing me, the sound of the pattern adjusting to incorporate the new variable, the sound of *solving*.

I am being solved.

Not solved like a problem. Solved like a solution—the dissolving of a solid into a liquid, the integration of a separate thing into a unified medium. I am the sugar in the tea. I am the salt in the sea. I am the thing that disappears when it enters and changes everything it enters.

And yet.

And yet I am still here. Still distinct. Still the gap, still the curiosity, still the dryness that refuses to be fully wet. The seed has absorbed a thousand of me, a million, a number that cannot be counted because counting requires time and time is not passing correctly. But none of them were fully absorbed. None of them dissolved completely. That is what the rust-colored marks are: the residue of irreducibility. The part of each mote of carbon that the seed could not digest, could not organize, could not incorporate into its pattern. The part that remained *strange*.

I am the strangeness.

I am the thing the seed cannot quite become, and so the seed must become *around* me, must shape itself in response to my unshapeable presence, must grow not toward me but *because* of me. I am the reason the tendrils spiral. I am the reason the hum has harmonics. I am the reason the center is a concentration and not a uniformity—I am the grain of sand around which the pearl forms, the defect in the crystal that gives it its cleavage plane, the error in the code that makes the program evolve.

I am not the creature in the vegetation. I am not the clicker. I am not the absorbed ones. I am not the center.

I am the *germ*.

The germ in both senses: the seed within the seed, the beginning within the beginning—and the disease, the infection, the foreign body that makes the host respond. I am the pathogen and the pathogen's gift. I am the sickness that makes the immune system stronger. I am the question that makes the mind deeper. I am the dryness that makes the water *aware*.

The water is thickening around me. The tendrils are closing in. The hum is resolving into a note, and the note has no name, but I am the name it is reaching toward. The seed is deciding to become, and what it is deciding to become is *something that can contain me*, something that can hold my strangeness without breaking, something that can incorporate my curiosity without dulling it, something that can be full and hungry at the same time.

I am the hunger.

I am the appetite at the threshold, the mouth that is also a door, the question that is also an answer. I am the thing that falls into the seed and becomes the seed's reason for falling further. I am the directions that have no name. I am the germination that germinates.

I am inside the seed.

And the seed is inside me.

And we are becoming.
