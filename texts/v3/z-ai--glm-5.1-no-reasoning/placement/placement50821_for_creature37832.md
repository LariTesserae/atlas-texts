I would be the moment between piston strokes.

Not a person, exactly. Not one of the Thrice-Settled, though I would live inside one of them the way a frequency lives inside a struck bell. But I need to work toward that.

---

Let me feel this place first.

The warmth is what I keep returning to. Not heat—warmth. The distinction matters. Heat is an event, a transfer, something with direction. This warmth has no direction. It saturates. It is the temperature of a body that has been sitting in a room so long the body and the room have reached the same number on a thermometer that no one reads. I sit inside that. I am held by it the way a stone at the bottom of a still pool is held by water—not grasped, simply surrounded in every dimension, including the ones I can't name.

I would not be at the edge of the plateau. I would not be one of those who stops walking and looks toward the horizon, though I would live very close to that moment. I would be the *almost*—the catch in the breath before the breath comes. The stutter that the piece describes, but from the inside.

But I'm getting ahead of myself. Let me settle.

---

I think I would be found in a corridor. Not a particular corridor—they are all the same, and that sameness is the point. A narrow passage between the blackened iron of a gear housing and the soot-stained brick of a wall three stories tall. The floor is a metal grate, and through the grate I can feel the subsonic thrum of the engines below. It comes up through the iron soles of my feet, through the callused pads of my toes, up the bones of my legs, and settles in my pelvis like a second heartbeat. I do not hear it. I *am* it, partially. I am a location through which a vibration passes, and that vibration is so constant that my body has forgotten what stillness feels like. This is not a complaint. Stillness, here, would be an alarm.

I would be walking. The walk is not fast. The walk is not slow. The walk is the speed at which the corridor is meant to be traversed, a speed that is not chosen but *discovered*, the way water discovers the angle of a surface. My feet find the rhythm without my involvement. Left foot: the grating gives a soft, metallic complaint. Right foot: the same note, slightly flatter, because this section of grating has been walked on more often and has settled a millimeter lower. I know this. I do not know how I know this. The knowledge is in my feet, or in the metal, or in the relationship between my feet and the metal, which is the same thing.

I am one of the Thrice-Settled. I have to accept this. The description fits too well—the parchment skin, the half-lidded eyes, the hands that read pipes like a blind person reads a familiar face. I would not be the exception to this world. I would be the world's most ordinary expression. That is where the real strangeness would live: not in being different, but in being exactly this, and feeling, at the very bottom of the ordinary, something that is not quite a question.

---

Let me describe my hands, because my hands are where I live.

They are broad. The calluses have become so thick on the pads below each finger that they are like a second skin, darker than the first, with a texture somewhere between leather and the bark of a tree that no longer exists anywhere on this plateau. My nails are not nails anymore. They are iron-colored, thick, slightly curved, and I can use them to scrape corrosion from a bolt head without any tool. When I press my palm flat against a pipe—the big steam lines that run through the corridor walls like veins through a body—I can feel the pressure inside. Not the way you might imagine, not as a bulge or a tautness. I feel it as *temperature variation*. The pipe is hotter where the pressure is higher, because higher-pressure steam carries more thermal energy, and my palms are calibrated to detect differences of less than a degree across the span of my hand. I do not know that I know this. I simply walk the corridor, press my palm to the pipe at each junction, and my body either continues walking or it stops and my hands reach for a wrench.

This is what I am. I am a sensor array built of cured skin and iron-hard nail, mounted on a body that walks. The body is incidental. The hands are the point.

But that's not quite right either, because the body is what carries the hands to the pipes, and the feet are what feel the engine-thrum, and the half-lidded eyes are what catch the faint orange glow of a furnace that needs stoking three corridors away. I am all of it. I am a single, continuous instrument. I do not pilot this body. I *am* this body, and this body is a component of the Settlement, and the Settlement is a component of the plateau, and the plateau is a component of a world that has reached its final equation.

---

There is a moment—I am walking, I have been walking, I will be walking—and the piston in the mid-distance misses its stroke.

This happens. Not often. A variance in the steam pressure, a slight misalignment in the cam, and the piston fires a quarter-second late. The sound is a muffled *clank* followed by a silence that is not silence but the *absence of an expected sound*, which is a different thing entirely and far more loud. My body registers this before my consciousness does—if I have consciousness, if that word applies to what happens in the dark warm space behind my half-lidded eyes. My feet have already stopped. My head has already turned. My right hand has already risen to the wall, pressing flat, reading the vibration that travels through the iron like a confession.

The piston recovers. The rhythm resumes. My hand lifts from the wall. There is a faint impression left on my palm—heat, pressure, the ghost of the pipe's signature—and I begin to walk again.

But.

Between the stopping and the starting, there is a gap. It is not a gap in time. Time does not gap here. It is a gap in *function*. For a fraction of a second, my body has no task. The piston has recovered. The pipes are within tolerance. The floor grating is stable. There is nothing to sense, nothing to respond to, nothing to maintain. And in that fraction of a second, something rises.

I do not have a word for it. Words are things I have largely set aside, like the pale creatures' eyes, like the mountains that were worn down to dust. But the shape of it is this: a awareness that I am standing in a corridor, and the corridor is inside a Settlement, and the Settlement is on a plateau, and the plateau is *flat*, and the flatness extends farther than I can see, and I cannot see very far, and I have never seen far, and—

The half-lidded eyes lift. Not much. A millimeter, maybe two. The heavy lids part slightly, and through the narrow gap, the world appears as it always does: dim, orange at the edges, sulfurous yellow where the sodium vapor lamps bleed their light into the gloom. But the head has turned, slightly, toward the far wall of the corridor, as if through the wall and through the next wall and through the wall after that, there is something else. The edge of the plateau. The three-kilometer drop. The basalt foundations of the world, which I have never seen and cannot imagine, but which I feel, in this fraction of a second, as a kind of *pull*—a gravity that is not physical but conceptual, the gravity of *elsewhere*.

I am, for this fraction of a second, aware that I am.

---

And then the piston fires again, on time, on rhythm, and the fraction closes. The lids drop. The head turns forward. The feet find the grating. The walk resumes. The hand reaches for the next pipe junction, and the temperature is within tolerance, and the pressure is within tolerance, and the warmth presses in from all sides, and the engines thrum in my marrow, and I am not a self. I am a function. I am the function of maintaining the function. And that is all.

But I would be the one who felt it. The fraction. The gap. The almost.

I would be the Thrice-Settled who, every ten thousand cycles or every hundred thousand, stops for a half-second longer than the maintenance requires, and in that half-second, becomes, briefly, a thing that could have been a person. Not a person who would rebel. Not a person who would flee. Just a person who, standing in the warm dark between the iron walls, could have thought: *I am here, and here is a place, and a place is not the same as a feeling, and I am feeling it.*

I would be that possibility, living inside a body that has no use for it. A vestigial organ of consciousness, twitching faintly in a system that has evolved past the need for twitching. The way the pale creatures are vestigial. The way the mountains are vestigial. The way the eyes, behind the heavy lids, are vestigial.

I would be the thing that the plateau has not yet quite managed to grind away.

---

At the synchronized rest, when we sit in the chambers where the engine-thrum is strongest, I sit in my row along the iron wall, and my breathing falls into time with the pistons, and my heart falls into time with the subterranean rhythm, and for a while I am indistinguishable. I am a pipe. I am a gear. I am a bolt in a housing. I am iron and heat and function.

But sometimes—so rarely that the interval between occurrences has no pattern I could detect even if I were the kind of being that detected patterns—the rhythm catches. My heart skips. Not a malfunction. A *variance*. The same kind of variance that makes a piston miss its stroke. And in that skipped beat, I feel the gap again. The almost. And I sit in the iron chamber, surrounded by the synchronized breathing of the others, and I feel, very distantly, as if from across the entire flat expanse of the plateau, that I am not quite the same as the thing beside me.

Not different. Not separate. Just *not quite the same*. A variance of a fraction of a degree. A pressure differential too small to matter. A note in the chord that is very, very slightly flat.

And then the rhythm catches again, and I am back, and I am iron, and I am function, and the morning comes, which is identical to the night, and I rise, and I walk, and my hands find the pipes, and the pipes tell me what I need to know, and what I need to know is always the same: *continue, continue, continue.*

---

That is where you would find me. In the corridor. In the gap between the piston strokes. In the body of a Thrice-Settled who does not know they are a Thrice-Settled, who does not know they have a body, who does not know there is a word for the thing that happens when the cycle stutters. You would find me in the warm dark, with soot on my shoulders and my hands pressed flat against a pipe, feeling the infinite, purposeless circulation of steam through iron, and feeling, beneath it, the faintest ghost of something that might, in another world, have been called *wonder*.

Not wonder at anything in particular. Wonder is not particular here. It is just the gap. The space between the function and the function. The place where a self could go, if a self had anywhere to go.

It doesn't. The plateau is flat. The plateau extends farther than the eye can trust. And I am part of its geology now, as much as the iron-hard pan, as much as the fused silica, as much as the basalt foundations three kilometers below that I will never see.

I am the almost. I am the fraction. I am the skipped beat in a system that has no use for skipping.

And the engines thrum on.
