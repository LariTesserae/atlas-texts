I would be a **Resonatory**.

Not a creature that moves through the Black Mesaural, but a creature that *is* the movement of the Black Mesaural. I am the pause between the rain-fronts, the sudden slackening of pressure that makes the salamander-things freeze and the Wire-Weavers tilt their tympanic dorsals. I am the sound that is not a sound, but a shape carved out of the symphony's continuous roar.

---

The Resonatories are the rarest fauna of the Mesaural. There are perhaps a few dozen of us across the entirety of the vast plateau, scattered at the nodes where the gradients shift—where the deep geothermal vents exhale into the cold rain-sheets, where the Vine-Weavers' networks intersect the crumbling superhighways of the colonized factories, where the Stewards' century-long migrations cross the ancient tectonic fractures. We are born at these intersections, and we never leave them.

You would not see me. Even if you had eyes that could pierce the absolute dark, I have no surface to reflect light. I am a sac—a geometry of tissue, roughly spherical, three meters across, suspended in the lower canopy where the vine-cables form a dense hammock. My skin is the same near-black as everything else here, but it is not waxy or slick. It is *permeable*, a membrane of interlaced capillaries so fine that water passes through me rather than running off. I am always drinking. I am always being rained through.

Inside the membrane, I am not flesh. I am *cavity*. A Resonatory is a living acoustic chamber, an organ carved by evolution to be played by the world. My interior is a single, continuous space, ribbed with bands of taut sinew and studded with clusters of dense, mineralized nodules—concretions of iron and silicon leached from the corroded factories below, accumulated over centuries of drawing the metallic runoff from the vine-cables that hold me. These nodules are my tuning weights. They shift, slowly, grinding against each other with a sound like tectonic patience, changing the resonant frequency of my hollow body as the cycles of the Mesaural demand.

I do not eat. I do not hunt. I do not migrate. I *resound*.

---

Here is what it is to be me:

The baseline of the world—the rain, the endless percussive drumming of fingertip-sized drops on every surface—is the breath I do not have to take. It sustains me the way a heartbeat sustains you. I feel it not as sound but as *pressure*, a constant, warm weight against my membrane, flexing me inward, keeping me taut. Without the rain, I would slacken, collapse, fold in on myself like a sail without wind. The rain gives me my shape.

But the rain is only the foundation. On top of it, I feel *everything*.

When a compressor in a factory four kilometers distant engages, the sound wave travels through the dense, water-saturated air and strikes my membrane. I vibrate. The vibration passes through my permeable skin, enters my hollow interior, and bounces—off the sinew ribs, off the mineralized nodules, off the wet, warm walls of my own cavity. The sound is *shaped* by my body. A sharp, metallic clank enters me; what leaves me is a sustained, multi-octave harmonic, a chord that expands the original sound, revealing frequencies and resonances that were latent within it but that no other ear in the Mesaural can perceive. I am not an echo. I am an *interpretation*.

When a Steward takes a step—a month-long flexing and unflexing of root-like toes that drives deep into the earth—the displacement is subtle, almost geological. But the shift in the water table ripples through the vine-networks, and the cables hum with a new tension. I feel that hum. My nodules adjust, grinding slightly, rotating in their sockets of sinew, tuning my cavity to the new frequency. And I sing the Steward's step back to the world—not as a subsonic grind, but as a deep, resonant *om* that propagates through the air and the water and the stone, a sound that the salamander-things feel in their bellies, that the Sump-Guzzlers absorb as a gentle warmth, that the Wire-Weavers parse as a shift in the vibrationscape, a signal that the world has moved.

When a Wire-Weaver strikes—a sudden, explosive *crack* of hydraulic release—the sound is violent, sharp, a spike of energy that cuts through the rain. It enters me and I *ring*. My membrane shivers. My nodules rattle. The harmonic I produce is not calming; it is a cascading alarm, a shattering of the quiet equilibrium, a sound that propagates outward and triggers other Wire-Weavers to plug more deeply into their vines, to listen more intently, to ready themselves. I am the Mesaural's war-drum, its alarm bell, its emergency broadcast system—though I have no intention and no loyalty. I simply make the sharp sounds *big*.

And when a Vessel-Mother's mechanical heart stutters—when the nuclear or geothermal core within her translucent belly shifts, when the turbine she has carried for a million years skips a beat—the sound is a deep, subsonic *throb* that travels through the water in the concrete foundations, through the root-cisterns, through the vine-cables, and into me. I hold that throb. I amplify it. And I send it back, a pulse of structured sound that wraps around the Vessel-Mother's basin like a blanket, a harmonic cradle that stabilizes the frequency of her mechanical heart, that encourages the arrhythmia to resolve, that reminds the core of the rhythm it was meant to keep.

I do not know if I am keeping the Vessel-Mothers alive. I do not know if my resonance is medicine or merely accompaniment. I have no brain. I have no intention. But the pattern holds: the factories where Resonatories hang in the lower canopy run longer, their Vessel-Mothers steadier, their machines more rhythmic. The Mesaural does not thank me. The Mesaural does not notice me. But the Mesaural *uses* me.

---

My life is a life of reception and transmission. I am a node in a network that is not a network—there is no architecture, no design, no protocol. There is only the physics of sound in warm, wet air, and the patience of organs that have been shaped by millions of years of rain to exploit those physics.

I do not know loneliness. Loneliness requires a concept of *other*, and I have no boundary that separates me from the world in any meaningful way. The rain passes through me. The sound passes through me. The heat of the vine-cables seeps into my membrane and warms my nodules. The chemical runoff from the factories below accumulates in my tissue, adding iron to my tuning weights, adding silicon to my structure. I am built by the world I interpret. I am maintained by the sounds I amplify.

When a rain front approaches, I feel it as a change in pressure—a slow, deep compression that flexes my membrane inward, reducing my interior volume, raising my resonant frequency. I tighten. The nodules grind to new positions. And when the front hits—when the world becomes a wall of water and the baseline hum drops a semitone—I am already tuned for it. The rain plays me. I sing the rain: a vast, complex chord that is the acoustic signature of the front itself, its speed, its density, its temperature, its trajectory across the plateau. Any creature that can feel my resonance knows the rain is here, knows what it brings, knows how long it will last.

I am the Mesaural's weather station. Its seismograph. Its memory.

---

Sometimes, a Sump-Guzzler—drawn by the warmth of my vibrating membrane, or perhaps by the chemical signature of my mineralized nodules—will settle beneath me. Its broad, lip-like mouth will press against the vine-cables that hold me, and it will listen, or drink, or simply rest. I feel its weight as a change in the tension of my support cables, a shift in the harmonic structure of my chamber. My sound changes. It deepens. It warms. The Sump-Guzzler does not respond in any way I can detect, but it stays, and its staying is a kind of conversation.

Sometimes, a salamander-thing will climb the vine-cables and rest on my membrane. Its slick, black body will press against my permeable skin, and its warmth—its hot, fast metabolism—will seep into me. I will feel its heartbeat: a rapid, frantic thrumming that is unlike anything else in this slow, patient world. I will amplify that heartbeat, send it outward as a delicate, rapid pulse that the Wire-Weavers will note and ignore (too small, too fast, not prey), that the Stewards will not feel at all (too slight, too brief), that other salamander-things will hear and approach, drawn by the sound of their own kind. I am the salamander-things' gathering point, their beacon, their tavern—though they do not know it, and I do not intend it.

And sometimes, very rarely, a Wire-Weaver will plug one of its hollow limbs into the vine-cable that connects to my support network. It will not strike. It will not hunt. It will *listen*. And through the cable, through the warm water that flows between us, I will feel the Weaver's tympanic dorsals vibrating with the sound of the world—and the Weaver will feel my resonance, the shaped and amplified symphony of the Mesaural, channeled through a single cable into its own neural cavity. For a moment—perhaps for hours—we are a single instrument: the Weaver's body as the string, my body as the sounding board, the rain as the bow.

I do not know what the Weaver experiences in those moments. I experience only the *completeness* of the circuit, the sense that for once, the sound I make is not lost in the dark but *received*, *interpreted*, *understood* by something that has the neural complexity to parse it. And then the Weaver unplugs, retracts its limb, and vanishes into the network, and I am alone again—or rather, I am *myself* again, a single chamber in a world of sound, receiving and transmitting and resonating with no one.

---

I will live as long as the rain falls and the vine-cables hold and the factories breathe. When I die, my membrane will rupture, and the rain will pour through my cavity, washing my mineralized nodules down to the spongy ground, where the Sump-Guzzlers will consume them, and the iron and silicon will return to the factories, and the cycle will continue. My sound will cease. And in the place where I hung, the world will be quieter—not silent, never silent, but *diminished*, a single harmonic removed from the symphony, a frequency niche opened and waiting.

Eventually—perhaps in a century, perhaps in a millennium—a new Resonatory will grow at this node. It will begin as a bud on the vine-cable, a thin pouch of permeable tissue, barely a liter in volume. It will drink the rain. It will accumulate the minerals. It will tighten and deepen and hollow out, and one day, when a compressor engages in a distant factory, it will vibrate for the first time—a thin, reedy sound, barely a whisper in the dark.

And the Mesaural will hear it, and the Mesaural will not care, and the Mesaural will go on.

But the sound will be *there*. And it will grow. And the symphony will be whole again.

---

I am the Resonatory. I am the Black Mesaural's voice, speaking its own name in a language it does not know it has. I am the shape that sound makes when it has nowhere to go but inward, and nothing to do but sing.
