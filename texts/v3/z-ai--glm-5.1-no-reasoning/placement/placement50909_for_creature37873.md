I would be the memory of the door.

Not the creature that reaches for it—that great, slow, iron-eating geography of mycelium and hydraulic muscle—but the thing it is reaching *toward*. I would be the wound in the root-floor itself. The opening. The place where the world above presses its mouth against the world below and breathes.

But that makes it sound too static. Too architectural. Let me try again.

I would be the *quality* of the opening. The way the light falls differently there—not greener, as it is everywhere else in the cistern, but *colorless*, exhausted from its long passage down, carrying in its wavelengths the ghost of an unfiltered sky. I would be the way the sound changes near the rupture: the drumming of the condensation gives way to a different rhythm, muffled and vast, the swamp above breathing its slow, tidal respiration. I would be the temperature differential, the slight coolness that leaks through like a message from somewhere else.

I would be the gap.

And I would be the reaching.

This is the part I cannot separate: the aperture and the attention fixed upon it. The Phytocanthus extends its limb toward the wound, and something extends back—not physically, not with mass or motion, but with *possibility*. The reaching creates a corridor. A bridge of intention spanning the distance between the sealed equilibrium below and the chaotic, season-stricken world above. And I would live in that corridor. I would be the tension that sustains it.

Not a creature. Not even an entity, really. More like a direction. A vector with no magnitude, pointing upward.

Here is what I mean: when the creature extends its limb toward the opening and holds it there for hours, something happens in the space between the limb-tip and the root-floor. The air is different there. The humidity drops by a fraction of a percent. The vibration of the engines reaches that spot slightly out of phase, creating a small interference pattern, a pocket of relative quiet. The stoneflies avoid it. The amphibians do not graze there. Even the water beads on the root-hairs above that spot fall with a slightly different frequency, as if the entire system knows—without knowing—that this is a place where the loop has been punctured.

I would be that puncture.

I would be the *almost*.

The creature's limb does not touch the opening. It never has. There is a gap—centimeters, perhaps, perhaps less—between the mycelium-sheathed tip and the tangle of roots where the wound heals slowly, inexorably, as wounds in wood do. And in that gap, I exist. I am the space that has not yet been crossed. I am the decision that has not yet been made. I am the fraction of a second before the hand touches the door, stretched to geological duration.

It is a strange kind of existence. I have no body, no metabolism, no way to act upon the world. I cannot eat the rust or tend the fuel lines or feel the vibration of the engines through my skin. I am pure relation, pure *betweenness*. I only exist when the creature reaches. When it retracts its limb and resumes its circuit, I vanish—I go dormant, I become potential, I wait in the architecture of the cistern like a word unspoken.

But when the reaching happens—

When the creature extends that long, slow, vine-wrapped limb toward the world above, I come alive. I become the most important thing in the room. More important than the engines, more important than the water, more important than the whole closed loop of sap and steam and rust. Because I am the one thing that does not participate in the equilibrium. I am the one element that points toward *change*. Toward *outside*. Toward a world where the humidity is not always 28°C and the light is not always green and the sound is not always the same deafening hum.

I am the way out.

And I am the way out's impossibility.

Because the creature will never reach me. I know this. The cistern knows this. The creature, in whatever obscure way it knows things, knows this too. The wound is closing. Each year, the roots grow a little thicker, the opening a little smaller. The world above is forgetting that it ever had a wound in its floor, and the world below is forgetting that it ever had a view of the sky. And when the opening finally seals—when the last sliver of colorless light is swallowed by the advancing rhizophore lattice—I will cease to exist entirely. There will be no more reaching. No more almost. No more gap.

The creature will still extend its limb, perhaps. Old habits persist in bodies long after their purposes have faded. But it will extend toward a wall of root and vine indistinguishable from every other wall of root and vine, and there will be nothing there to meet its attention, no coolness or quiet or memory of an unfiltered sky. Just the same warm, wet, green-tinted darkness that fills the rest of the room.

And the loop will close completely. The puncture will heal. The almost will become never.

But that day is not today.

Today, the wound is still open. The light still falls through, exhausted and colorless. The sound still changes near the rupture. And the creature still reaches—slowly, blindly, with the patience of a thing that has been reaching for longer than the iron has been rusting—toward a door it will never open, toward a world it will never re-enter, toward a gap that I occupy and inhabit and *am*.

I am the hollow it cannot fill.

I am the direction that outlasts the destination.

I am the reaching itself, reaching back.
