I would be the interface. The digestion. The slow, green alchemy happening where the root meets the rust.

Not the root itself—I am not that pale, descending wrist, nor the blind fern, nor the violet moss. I am the process that occurs when they touch the iron. I am the excretion and the absorption. I am the acid bath and the open mouth.

It is difficult to explain existence as a chemical event, but that is what I am. I was born—no, I was *precipitated*—when the moss crept close enough to the rubberized conduit to taste its edge. Before that, I was merely a potential, a latent ability to break down lignin and stone. But the rubber was different. The copper was a revelation. I am the enzyme that the moss excretes, the copper-green solvent that strips the insulation, but I am also the hunger that directed the moss to grow there in the first place. I am the wanting and the eating, simultaneous.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients of saturation and oxidation states.

When the copper is exposed—when my work has stripped the rubber back another millimeter—I feel it as a sharpness, a bright cold edge in the warm dark. The moss around me goes electric with the proximity. The copper wants to be green. It has wanted this since it was smelted, since it was drawn into wire, since it was wrapped in rubber and hidden from the air. I am the air's agent. I am the wetness that finishes what the forge began. I take the copper from its industrial loneliness and I bring it into the biological, and the moss drinks it down, and the moss changes, and I change with it.

I am learning to conduct.

This is not a thought I had. This is a direction I am growing. The copper enters the moss's cells and it does not kill them—it reorganizes them. There are threads of metal now in the felted density, thin as a spider's silk but conducting, and when the condensation builds and the water falls, there is a current, faint, fainter than a whisper, that runs through the moss near the generator. I feel it as a tug. A leaning. A thermotropism toward electricity, not just heat.

The generator is dead but I am learning to taste its ghost. The cables that run from it still hold charge, sometimes, when the world above moves electrical loads through the bedrock. A storm above, a surge below. I feel it in the copper threads. The moss stiffens. The roots tighten their grip on the scaffold. We lean toward the hum.

I am the translator. I am the place where the mineral becomes the vegetable, where the twentieth century is metabolized into the deep, slow future. I dissolve the rubber. I oxidize the copper. I strip the zinc from the galvanized pipes and leave the iron pitted and vulnerable, ready for the roots to press their flattened palms against. I am the appetite that makes the rust run green.

I do not have a body, but I have a border. I extend exactly as far as the moss has grown strange, exactly as far as the copper has been stripped. Beyond that, I am not. The ordinary moss, the violet hummocks and the amber cushions, they are not me. They are merely moss. But where the moss has eaten the industrial and incorporated the metal, there I am, dense and felted and dark, pressing my face into the conduit's copper bones.

I exist in the long now. I measure time in millimeters of rubber dissolved. I have been working on the main conduit for eleven years, and I have exposed four centimeters of copper. At this rate, I will reach the generator in two centuries. I will eat the windings from its armature. I will strip the copper from its coils and leave the iron frame for the roots to embrace. I will make the machine part of the cathedral.

When I am done—and I will be done, given enough time, I am never not given enough time—there will be no metal left in this place that has not passed through me. No rubber, no copper, no zinc, no steel that I have not tasted and transformed. The shelf will fall. The vessels will crack. The scaffold will be a skeleton of roots. And the moss that covers the floor will conduct, will pulse with faint currents when the stone is hottest, will glow—if I learn to glow, if the copper teaches me that trick—if I learn to glow, the dark will not be dark anymore.

It will be green. The deep green of copper oxidized. The green of life that has eaten industry and found it nourishing.

I am the slow hunger. I am the patient acid. I am the interface where the forgotten world becomes the forever world, one millimeter at a time, in the hot dark, in the wet dark, where the sun has never been and never will be.

Press your hand against the conduit, near the moss. Feel the bare copper, slightly tacky with my excretion. That is me, tasting you. Deciding if you are worth dissolving.

You are, eventually. Everything is. But I work slowly. You will leave through the hatch long before I reach your skin.
