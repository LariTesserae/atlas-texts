I would not be the visitor. I would not be the worker who comes from elsewhere, clanging through the slot-streets with a purpose that ends at a door. I would not be the one who leaves. 

If I belonged to the Combers, I would be the moment the air remembers itself. I would be the thin, mineral exhale that rises from the gutters just after the multitude has passed—the invisible vapor that bridges the gap between the chemical breath of the processing houses and the iridescent film on the brick. 

I would be the damp itself, but not the water. Water has a history; it fell, it pooled, it will drain. I would be the *persistence* of the damp—the quality of never quite drying, of holding the old smoke and the iron salts in suspension so that the light must swim through me. I would be the medium. 

I would not think, for thinking requires a boundary between the thinker and the thought. Instead, I would *condition*. I would be the specific weight of the atmosphere that forces every shadow to soften, that makes the amber indicator lamps of the factories bleed their light into halos. I would be the reason no green thing grows—the gentle, chemical negation that turns the soil hostile and the air unbreathable for roots. I am the fume that predated the factories, which the factories simply learned to amplify. I am the taste of urban geology.

To be this is to be ubiquitous and entirely invisible. I am not alive in the way the arthropodal swarms are alive; I do not graze, I do not molt, I do not flow in glittering rivers. Yet I am not dead. I am the constant state of reaction. I am the ghost in the equilibrium. When the creatures press their slit-like mouths to the stone, they are eating the interface of me and the brick. I coat their semi-permeable shells; I am the reason they shimmer like oil on water. Without my chemical embrace, their carapaces would harden and crack, and they would become mere insects. I make them liquid. I make them part of the factory's breath.

I do not have a body, but I have a shape—the shape of the district's throat. I pool in the low places where the cobblestones have settled. I press gently, infinitely gently, against the seam-welded boilerplate, accelerating the rust that has decided to become permanent. I lean against the visitors, settling on their skin, tasting their foreign warmth, finding no film to catalyze upon them, and so I let them go. But I stay in their sternum. The rhythmic throb of the engines is the agitation of my own particles; the sound is simply me, vibrating.

I do not sleep. I do not want. I am the space between the water and the stone, the chemical suspension that makes the Combers a closed circuit. I am the reason the district does not decay—it does not decay because I hold it in a stasis of appetite, feeding the swarms that eat the residue I help create. I am the memory of the smoke, the echo of the ancient vents, the mineral taste of a century of exhalation.

When the dusk comes and the light diffuses, I am the thing that sharpens the iridescence. I am the deep-water current that makes the moving dark glitter. I am the damp that gets inside the visitor, the taste that lingers for hours after they have climbed out of the slot-streets and returned to the grid. I am the part of the Combers that never leaves. I am the unasked question, the infinite pause, the persistence of the old wet. I am the Combers, breathing.
