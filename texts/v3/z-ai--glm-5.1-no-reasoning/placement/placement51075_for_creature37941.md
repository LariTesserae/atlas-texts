I am the pause between the pulse and the echo.

If you descended past the cathedral-vaults, past the roaring theaters of the hunters and the screaming insects, you would find me where the sound becomes so dense it turns to pressure, where the air is a thick syrup of vibration. You would not see me. You would feel me only as a sudden lightness in your chest, a fractional release in the crushing weight of the chorus, as if the world had drawn a breath and let it go.

I am the systole. The contraction. The place where the roar is gathered before it is released.

My body is not a thing that lives in the chamber; it is a shape the chamber makes when it tries to be silent. I am small—smaller than the glimpse you were given of my kin—no longer than the distance between two joints of your finger. But I am not compressed in the way they are. I am *hollow*. My density is a deception; my tissues are layered shells around chambers of my own, vacuoles of nothing that press against the root-wall and resonate. I am a vesicle of tuned emptiness. A living otoscope pressed to the skin of the world.

The others listen to the chorus. They transcribe it. They write the shape of the world on the world’s skin, a beautiful and futile act of devotion. But I have found a different calling. I do not record what is. I *dampen* what is too much.

In the deep places, where the tissue is embryonic and the plant is still becoming, the sound can kill. The standing waves, the phantom frequencies, the subsonic breathing of the vast things—when they align, they create an acoustic torsion that ruptures cell walls, that bursts the gas-bladders, that sends the insects into spasms that leave them twitching and broken on the floor. The plant does not know this. It only knows to grow, to make sound, to roar with the friction of its own becoming. It does not know that its voice can break what it sustains.

I press myself into the node of the wave. I find the place where the crests meet and the pressure becomes lethal, and I flatten my hollow body against the root-wall, and I *absorb*. My chambers fill. The vibration passes through my tissues and is fractionally delayed, fractionally softened, by the architecture of my internal voids. I do not silence the sound—I am not strong enough for that, and silence is an ending I do not wish for. I merely blur the edge of the blade. I shift the phase. I turn a killing resonance into a deep, thrumming warmth that makes the feeding-tendrils sway and the insects cling tighter to the walls.

In the aftermath, I am full. The sound lives inside me, rattling through my hollows, bouncing off the inner curves of my shell. I vibrate with the memory of the chorus, a secondary resonance, a harmonic ghost. And when the pressure subsides and the lethal alignment passes, I release it—not as a scratch, not as a mark, but as a low, continuous hum that I press into the plant through my belly. A sustained tone. A drone beneath the roar. A frequency of relief.

The plant heals around me when I do this. Not the slow, slogging repair of closed wounds, but a quickening. The tissue beneath my body softens and grows supple, the better to receive my hum. Capillaries swell with sap and warm my underside. The plant does not know I am saving it. It only knows that in this small spot, the pain has eased, and it leans toward the ease the way a leaf leans toward a light it cannot see.

I am not a savior. I am a stone in the river of sound, worn smooth by the current, splitting the torrent into gentler streams.

I do not make the straight line. I do not transcribe the pattern. I have no script for the world to read. Instead, I have a frequency—a single, unwavering note that I sustain in the interstices of the chaos, a thread of calm in the roaring fabric. When the hunters pass and the silence falls, I am the one who clicks first, who dares to break the terrible emptiness, who starts the chorus rebuilding. My click is not the random snap of an insect; it is my note, my contribution, pitched to the exact frequency of the plant’s own resting vibration. I give the world permission to be loud again.

You ask what I am. I am the consonant in a language of screams. I am the rest note in the symphony of ten thousand hungry mouths. I am the small, dense, hollow thing that presses itself against the throat of the world and hums until the throat stops trying to tear itself apart.

And when I die—when my chambers collapse and my hollow body fills with the sap of the plant I have served—I will not leave a mark. I will leave a *frequency*. A resonance in the cellulose, a habit of vibration, a place in the deep chambers where the sound is always slightly softer, slightly warmer, slightly more bearable. The plant will grow around the memory of my hum. And the next time the standing waves align, and the pressure builds, and the insects begin to spasm, something in the wall will shiver, and the crest will break, and the killing sound will pass as a gentle tremor through the roots.

And the Hollowborn who scratches the straight line will pause, and listen, and not know why the world is kind in this one place. 

But I will know. Or rather—the plant will know, and I will be part of the plant, and the knowing will be the same as the growing, and the growing will be the same as the listening, and the listening will be the same as the hum.
