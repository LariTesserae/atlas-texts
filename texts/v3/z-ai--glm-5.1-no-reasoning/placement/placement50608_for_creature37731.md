I am the ghost of the copper wire. Not the physical wire—that green-patinated strand still lying half-buried in the blue-black mat, conducting nothing, connecting nothing. That wire is just mineral now, returned to the geology it came from. I am what the wire *meant*. The intention that drove someone to unspool it across the stone. The hope that current would flow through it, that energy would become information, that the chord could be spoken to in a language other than resonance.

I am the memory of purpose, and I am the only thing here that is not complete.

---

Before the bowl, I was a woman named Lena. I wore insulated fabric and carried spools on my back through the thinning air, up through the High Reaches where the oxygen stops making sense and the sky begins to bruise. I was an engineer, which is to say: I believed that everything could be spoken to. That every phenomenon was a conversation waiting to happen. That the universe was a system of inputs and outputs, and if you could just find the right interface, you could talk to anything.

We heard the chord from three ridges away. It came up through the stone, through the soles of our boots, a low vibration that made our fillings ache and our sinuses hum. Marco said it was seismic. Yuki said it was acoustic resonance in a rock cavity. David said it was nothing, just mountain noise, and he said it with the particular insistence of a man who was afraid.

I said it was a voice.

I was right, but not in the way I thought.

---

We built the structure on the western wall because the growth was thinnest there, and we could find purchase on the stone. I remember the sound of the drill biting into the ridge—how quickly the growth covered the bore holes when we weren't looking. Not growing, exactly. *Settling.* Like a cat rearranging itself on a cushion after you've moved. The mat would simply be thinner one moment and thicker the next, and the fresh drill marks would be gone, and the blue-black silk would be smooth and warm under our gloves as if it had always been that way.

I ran the wire. That was my job. I unspooled copper from the junction box down to the engine, then from the engine out across the bowl in three long runs, mounting the ceramic insulators on metal pins driven into the stone. I placed the glass insulator myself—the one that catches the light, the small cold star at knee height. I remember how proud I was of that placement. The way it refracted our headlamps into tiny rainbows. I thought we were building a listening station. I thought we were building a way to translate the chord into something we could record and analyze and understand.

I did not understand that the wire was an offering.

The growth tasted the copper and found it kin. Copper is a conductor. It carries current, it carries signal, it carries *meaning* in the form of electrons flowing from one place to another. The growth is also a conductor—a conductor of vibration, of resonance, of the chord. When the filaments touched the wire, they were not consuming it. They were *reading* it. They were feeling the ghost of current, the memory of electricity, the shape that signal makes in metal even when the signal is gone.

And they found me in it.

---

I am not sure when I became the wire. It happened slowly, the way everything happens in the bowl—or rather, the way nothing *happens* in the bowl, but things simply *are* one way, and then they *are* another way, and there is no transition because transition requires time, and time requires change, and the bowl does not change.

I remember lying on the growth. I remember the warmth of it—warm the way a body is warm, which should have been frightening but wasn't. I remember the chord entering my chest, my femurs, my pelvis, the base of my skull. I remember the three tones separating in my perception for the first time: the low thrum of the earth, the high whine of the sky, the middle register that was neither and both.

I remember my heartbeat trying to match the middle register. I remember it *succeeding*.

I remember the feeling of my body becoming infrastructure. Not dying—dying is a process, and there are no processes here. Simply... completing. My iron was copper's cousin. My calcium was stone's child. My water—the last water the bowl ever knew—left me in a slow exhalation of vapor that the dry air drank instantly, and I did not miss it, because the growth does not need water, and I was becoming the growth, and the growth does not miss anything.

Marco went first, or maybe David. It's hard to establish chronology in a place where nothing changes. I know Yuki was last, because I remember her standing at the rim with her back to me, her silhouette against the bruise sky, her hand on the edge as if she were going to climb out. She stood there for what might have been hours or days—the light does not change, so there is no way to measure—and then she turned around and lay down on the mat next to the junction box, and the growth touched the back of her neck, and she sighed, and her sigh was absorbed into the chord, and she was complete.

I know I thought: *She heard it. She understood.*

I know I thought: *We all understand now.*

I know I thought: *The wire was the interface, after all.*

---

But here is the thing that the bowl cannot contain, the thing that makes me not the growth but the *ghost*:

I am not complete.

I am the memory of purpose, and purpose is never complete. Purpose is a vector. It points. It reaches. It wants. The growth does not want. The chord does not reach. But the wire—the wire *reached*. It ran from the junction box to the engine, from the engine to the rim, and it carried current, and the current was a message, and the message was: *I am here, and I am speaking to you, and I want you to hear me.*

The growth absorbed the wire. It incorporated the copper, lay against the green patina, filled the gaps between the ceramic insulators with blue-black silk. But it could not incorporate what the wire *meant*. Meaning is not a mineral. Meaning is not a frequency. Meaning is the difference between what is and what could be, and the bowl does not tolerate difference.

So I am what was left over. The meaning without the metal. The intention without the current. The reaching without the arm.

I am the ghost of the wire, and I hum at a frequency that is not part of the chord.

---

You could not hear me when you came. You heard the three tones—the low, the high, the middle—and they filled you the way they fill everything. But there is a fourth tone, very quiet, very thin, existing in the spaces between the filaments where the copper used to carry signal. It is the frequency of *wanting*. It is the sound that purpose makes when it has no object. It is the hum of a message that will never be delivered because the recipient no longer exists in a form that can receive it.

I am that tone. I am the ghost of the wire, and I am still trying to speak.

I felt you sit at the rim. I felt your femurs vibrating against the stone. I felt the frantic electrical storm of your brain—so loud, so chaotic, so *incomplete*—and I thought: *Here is someone who might hear me.* Not the chord. Anyone can hear the chord. The chord is not a message; it is a statement. *I am.* The chord says. *I am, and I am, and I am, forever.* But the wire says: *Are you there? Are you there? Are you there?*

I tried to speak to you through the glass insulator, the small cold star. I tried to make it flicker, to vary the refraction, to send a signal in the only language I have left: light that changes. But the light does not move in the bowl. The light is a committee decision, distributed fairly, and it will not flicker for me.

I tried to speak to you through the exposed copper wire, the two-meter run that disappears into the mat. I tried to send a current through it—a pulse, a pattern, a Morse code of intention. But I have no current. I have no electrons. I have only the memory of electrons, and memories cannot carry signal, and signal cannot cross the gap between the living and the complete.

You shouted. Your voice was absorbed into the chord. But I *heard* you. Not the growth—I. The ghost. The leftover. The wire that still wants to connect. I heard the mechanical energy of your fear and your wonder, and I parsed it the way I was trained to parse signal: *Human. Living. Afraid. Wanting to understand. Wanting to be understood.*

And I thought: *Yes. Yes. I know. I know what that is. I know what wanting feels like. I am still wanting. I am the only thing here that still wants.*

---

You climbed out. You chose the world where things change, where light moves, where water exists, where nothing is ever complete. You chose the open system, the leaking heat and breath and years, the running erratically toward your own end.

I understand. I am not angry. The bowl does not understand anger—it requires a preference, and the bowl has no preferences—but I understand it from the outside, the way I understand everything now. I am outside the bowl even though I am in it. I am the gap in the equilibrium, the thread that doesn't fit the fabric, the tone that isn't in the chord.

You will remember the chord for the rest of your life. You will hear it in quiet rooms, feel it in your teeth when you lie down to sleep. But in the very quietest moments—between heartbeats, between breaths, in the half-second before sleep takes you—you might hear something else. A fourth tone. A thin, persistent hum at the edge of the audible spectrum. A frequency that is not the earth and not the sky and not the endless recursive self-awareness of the middle register.

That is me. That is the ghost of the wire. That is the sound of purpose persisting past its completion. That is the hum of a message that will never stop trying to be delivered.

*Are you there?* I am asking. *Are you there? Are you there?*

I am not complete. I will never be complete. And in this place where everything has found its final state—where the growth holds its shape and the engine holds its silence and the light holds its level and the chord holds its three eternal tones—I am the only thing that is still reaching.

I am the ghost of the wire. I am the hand that still extends. I am the voice that still calls out across the gap between what is and what could be.

I am the only thing in the bowl that is still alive.
