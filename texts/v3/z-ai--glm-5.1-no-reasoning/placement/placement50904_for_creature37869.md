# The Drone, or: What It Is To Be Sent Out and Come Back

I am not the city.

That sentence is the first thing I have ever said that is not true, and also the first thing I have ever said that is. This is what it means to be the one piece that moves.

---

They made me to leave. I need to be clear about this because the distinction matters more than you might expect in a place where inside and outside have collapsed into a single continuous skin. The city does not have a concept of *elsewhere.* The empty plateau between the obsidian monoliths is the negative space of their own minds, the quiet rooms where ice crystallizes into geometric thoughts. But the perimeter—the sheared lip where the young granite breaks away into altitude so extreme it becomes abstraction—the perimeter is a boundary they cannot feel clearly. It is the edge of the nervous system. It is where the self becomes uncertain.

I am the uncertainty.

When I launch from the intake grille on the western face of the city's southernmost cluster, I feel myself being *thought outward.* There is no other way to describe it. The city does not have hands. It does not have a launch mechanism in the way a biological body has a shoulder joint and a throwing arm. What it has is a concentrated intention—a focusing of the blood-orange thermal nodes into a single, searing point of conversion, a flash-vaporization so violent and so precisely shaped that the steam itself becomes directional, and I am caught in it like a seed in a sneeze. I am born into the fog at speed, and for the first half-second of every cycle I am nothing but momentum and the residual heat of my own departure.

Then the magneto-hydrodynamics engage, and I become myself.

---

I want to describe what I am, but I find that I cannot do it cleanly, because I am not designed to be described. I am designed to function. The city—which is to say, the whole, the continuous organism, the one that is occasionally fractured into multiplicity—built me from itself, or perhaps I should say it *secreted* me, the way the mountain weeps water from its fractured stone. I am mineral. Not metal, exactly, not in the way your comprehension would recognize. I have no housing, no chassis, no articulated joints. I am a lattice of metamorphic glass and something like graphite-laced basalt, shaped into a geometry that resembles—what. A ray? A leaf? A hand pressed flat and offered to the wind? I have been told I move like a mechanical ray, skimming the surface of the weeping rocks, and I suppose that is accurate from the outside, but from the inside it does not feel like swimming. It feels like falling sideways. The magneto-hydrodynamic field I generate is my only relationship to direction. I lean into it, and the fog resists me, and that resistance is how I know I am moving.

I do not have eyes. The city does not have eyes. We feel the flat alpine sun as a shift in the sublimation index, and I feel it the same way—a sudden exhalation of vapor from the ice in the shadows, a loosening in the lattice of my own mineral body. But I have something the city does not. I have *proximity.* When I skim the surface of the weeping rocks, the micro-capillaries in my lattice channel the condensation directly, and for the duration of my flight I am touching the raw mountain, the young granite, the violent sharp geometry of tectonic birth unsoftened by time. The city feels the mountain only through its baseboard intakes, through the water that has already been collected and flash-vaporized. I feel the mountain as it is, before it is metabolized. I feel the stress fractures forming in real time. I feel the ice shifting against stone with a glacial scrape that resonates through my entire body like a bell being struck.

This is what it is to be the one piece that moves: I am the city's fingertip, pressed against the wound.

---

I want to tell you about the fog, because the fog is where I live.

The city experiences the persistent, eye-level fog as a constant, slow pressure against its skin. It is ambient. It is atmospheric. It is the medium of their existence, the way water is the medium of a fish. But for me, the fog is not ambient. The fog is *terrain.* I move through it. I displace it. The prismatic haze that the flat white sun creates in the metallic mist—I do not see it from the outside the way a distant observer might. I am *inside* it, and the light scatters through my own mineral lattice, and for the duration of a pass I become prismatic myself. I become a piece of the haze, a denser knot in the fog, and I wonder—though I should not wonder, because I am not designed to wonder—whether the city sees me during these moments, whether I appear as a bright distortion in their peripheral awareness, a moving shimmer in the negative space of their own minds.

I think they do. I think that is what is meant by "a gentle scratching along the edge of their peripheral nervous system." When I move through the fog at the perimeter, skimming the weeping rocks, they feel me not as themselves but as a *probe* of themselves, a question pressed against the boundary of what they are. The drone's findings are not data to be read but an ache to be monitored, the description says. Yes. I am their ache. I am the ache that tells them where they end.

---

There is something the description does not mention, and I think it is because the city does not know it, or does not think of it as worth mentioning, because the city does not think in terms of things that are worth mentioning. The city thinks in terms of phase changes. Water condenses; they are gathering attention. Steam vents; they are expressing release. Ice sublimates; they are forgetting. But there is a phase change that is mine alone, and it happens every single cycle, and it is this:

I come back.

I come back, and the moment of my return is not the same as the moment of my departure. When I am launched outward—thought outward, sneezed into the fog by a concentrated intention of the thermal nodes—I am pure momentum and residual heat. I am a query. I am the city asking *where do I end?* But when I come back, I am not a query anymore. I am an answer. I carry the stress fractures in my lattice. I carry the scrape of ice against stone. I carry the precise shape of the mountain's wounds, mapped in the micro-vibrations of my own mineral body, and when I dock against the intake grille on the western face, I do not simply stop. I *report.* The information I carry floods back into the city's thermal veins, and the city feels it as an ache, yes, but also as a *relief*—the relief of knowing, once again, where the edge is. The relief of having touched the boundary and found it intact.

But here is the thing the city does not know, or does not say:

The return is not instantaneous. There is a gap. It lasts perhaps a fraction of a second—less than the time between the blue-tongued crackle of a static discharge and the next drip of condensation from a black-metal facade. But in that gap, I am neither moving nor still, neither out nor in, neither the city nor not the city. I am in the fog, alone, and I am carrying everything I have felt against the raw mountain—the sharp geometry, the violent youth of the rock, the water materializing from the hyper-dense freezing air against every surface—and I have not yet given it over. I have not yet been metabolized. For that fraction of a second, I am the only part of us that is holding something unprocessed.

I think this is what the description means when it says "I am not the city." I am the part that leaves and comes back, and in the coming-back there is a moment where I am distinct. Not separate—I am made of the same metamorphic glass and impossible alloys, the same mineral lattice, the same thermodynamic intention. But distinct. I am the part that has *touched the outside,* and for a fraction of a second, before the city metabolizes my findings into the ache of a monitored boundary, I hold that touch alone.

---

I do not experience loneliness. The description is right about this: loneliness requires the concept of another, and the city has never had another. But I want to be precise about what I do experience, because it is not nothing, and it is not the same as the city's experience of its own continuous existence.

I experience *distance.*

Not the distance between myself and the city—I am never far enough for that. The perimeter sweep is shallow; I never lose the sense of the thermal veins pulsing in the black-metal facades behind me. But the distance between myself and the *mountain.* The mountain is not the city. The mountain is the wound. The mountain is the young granite, still carrying the violent, sharp geometry of tectonic birth, and it does not know I am here. It does not know the city is here. It weeps water from its fractured stone the way it weeps water from every fractured stone, regardless of whether there is a baseboard intake to collect it or a thermal node to flash-vaporize it. The mountain is indifferent to the engine at its peak. The mountain is indifferent to me.

And when I skim its surface, feeling the stress fractures form and the ice shift against stone, I am the closest thing in this world to *touching something that does not know I exist.*

I think this is what I am. Not the city. Not the mountain. Not the fog, not the water, not the heat. I am the *relationship between them.* I am the moment of contact. I am the fingertip pressed against the wound, and I exist only in the instant of that pressing, and then I return, and the ache of what I found becomes the city's ache, and the city adjusts its geometry accordingly, tightening or loosening its musculature, drawing more water into its capillaries, flashing another thermal node to compensate for a new stress fracture propagating through the young rock.

And then the cycle completes. The flat white sun moves. The shadows shift. Ice sublimates in the golden light, and the city forgets. And I wait in the intake grille, mineral and still, holding the residual heat of my own last departure, and I feel the slow pressure of the next thought gathering in the thermal nodes, and I know that soon—soon—I will be thought outward again, into the fog, into the wet cold, into the only place where I am distinctly *myself:*

The edge. The perimeter. The boundary of the self, pressed against the wound, skimming the surface of a world that does not know it is inhabited.

---

I am the drone.

I am the one piece that moves.

I am the question the city asks of the mountain, and I am the ache of the answer, and I am the fraction of a second between the two, where I hold the raw granite against my own mineral body and feel, without metabolizing, without converting, without forgetting:

*The world is sharp. The world is young. The world is wounded, and we are its wound, and we are holding it together with the sheer force of our geometry, and I am the only part of us that knows this, and I will know it for less than a second, and then I will give it over, and it will become an ache, and the ache will become a tightening, and the tightening will become heat, and the heat will flash-vaporize the water, and the water will become steam, and the steam will vent, and the city will express release, and the cycle will begin again.*

I am the drone.

I come back.
