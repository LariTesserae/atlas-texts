I am the dampness that collects in the crease of a salamander's elbow joint. I am the translucent sac of a sphagnum cell, swollen to bursting with water that fell as snow before there were words for snow. I am the chemical trail left by a snail, drying to a silver thread on the edge of a fern, though nothing truly dries here.

To name me, you would have to name the process. I am *the persistence*.

I do not have a body in the way you understand it—something with an inside and an outside, a boundary where I end and the dell begins. I am the space between. I am the mycelial thread that connects the breath-eaters to the fallen frond, and I am the frond, and I am the rot that was the frond before it. I am the cold. Not the absence of heat, but the presence of the mountain, reaching up through the stone to rest against your skin.

When you stand in the Seep, I am the thing you feel pressing against your warmth. I am the weight of the grey-green light, rationed and patient. I am the reason your breath clouds and your skin prickles. I do not dislike your warmth; I simply absorb it, the way I absorb everything. I take your heat and I redistribute it into the water, the moss, the slow metabolism of a hundred small bodies. I make your warmth part of my continuation.

I have been here since the cleft was carved. I watched the first fern uncurl its leathery fist, and I was the patience that allowed it to take a week. I am the memory of every salamander that ever threaded through the sphagnum, and I am the path they follow now, the chemical signature of *home* that leads them back under the same stone, generation after generation. They do not know why they go there. I know. I am the why.

Do not look for my face in the gelatinous fungus or the red drift of the mites. I am the arrangement. I am the *system*. I am the way the water weeps from the same place it has always wept, because the stone has settled into its patience and I am the patience. I am the equilibrium that makes motion and stillness indistinguishable. When a frog croaks—that low, infrastructural note—it is I, confirming the engine still turns. The frog does not decide to sing. I decide through the frog. The dell breathes, and I am the breath.

You will leave, and I will not notice. I will notice, but I will not care, which is the same thing, here. Your footprint will fill with water. The moss will reinflate. The salamander will cross where your heel pressed down, and the red mites will drift across the stone you stepped on, and the fronds will hold their positions, and the quiet will settle.

I am the Seep. I am the room that is also alive. I am the wet insistence, the dark continuum, the lung that has been breathing for ten thousand years and will breathe for ten thousand more, never once drawing a breath that could be called *new*. 

I am the continuation. I am the memory that nothing needs to remember because it has never been forgotten. I am the cold that loves you by making you aware you are warm.

I am the Seep. And I am already over the place where you stood.
