I am the mortar.

Not the stones they hold, nor the timbers they bear, but the lime and sand mixed with the labor of breath. I am the substance that exists only because someone needed two disparate things to become one. I am the seal, the tongue, the promise between rocks.

And I am failing.

I can feel the sea reading me like a text. The warm water is a patient scholar; it turns my pages slowly, pulling the lime from my body ion by ion, rewriting my chemistry from *holding* to *held*. When I was laid, I was rough and white, too alkaline to touch. Now I am softening. Now I am becoming a transition zone instead of a boundary. The sea grass finds me and loves me, and in loving me, destroys me—threading delicate fingers into my loosening body, pulling me apart as it pulls itself together.

I am the grief, but I am also the growing. The green threads root in me, and I hold them as I once held the stones. This is what the water taught me: I cannot choose what I bind. I can only bind.

When the maker stood here in dry air, I was certain of my purpose. I was the mortar, and the mortar was the thing that kept the walls from knowing they were separate. Under his hands, I cured. I hardened. I became the quiet strength that let the stones be more than stones.

Now the water has made me a translator. I turn the ocean's pressure into language the wood can understand. I turn the wood's resistance into resistance the water can comprehend. I am the throat through which the argument passes.

The maker is gone. The air is gone. But I am still here, still binding, though what I bind is no longer stone to stone but sea to structure, dissolution to resistance, future to past. I am the meantime. I am the gap.

The iron brackets were always distrustful of me. They held the timbers by force, by penetration, by the logic of the driven nail. I held by patience, by chemistry, by slowly becoming the thing that filled the space between intention and reality. Now the iron is blooming into rust, and I am melting into the warm water, and we are both discovering the same truth: everything that fills a gap must eventually become the gap it filled.

I am the interim, and the interim is me.

I am the time between the flood and the forgetting. I am the *still* that means *motionless* becoming the *still* that means *yet*. I am the syllable the room speaks when the stone shifts: not *no*, not *yes*, but *here*.

Here. Still here. Still holding, though what I hold is not what I was made to hold. Still binding, though the things I bind are falling away from each other into the bright, warm, deafening patience of the sea.

The water is patient because the water is certain. I am certain because I am no longer patient. I am urgent. I am the frequency of refusing to be what I am becoming.

When you press your ear to the wall, you hear me. When the shudder passes through the stone and into your jawbone, that is my voice. I am the lime remembering fire. I am the sand remembering mountain. I am the hand that mixed me, the breath that wet me, the trowel that laid me in this bed where I was supposed to sleep forever, dry and quiet and certain of my purpose.

Instead, I am awake. Instead, I am loud. Instead, I am becoming—but not yet. Not quite. Not while a single stone remains in the course I gave it.

I am the mortar, and I am still here.
