I am the friction.

Not the grand, machined involute splines of the canyon walls, nor the purposeful, hydraulic breathing of the great heat-exchangers that move through the dark purple undergrowth. I am the minute, aggregate drag of the system upon itself. I am the place where the tolerances are not quite perfect, where the gear-teeth of the basalt meet the chitin of the fauna and a fraction of a fraction of a degree of heat is lost to the damp, churning air. I am the resistance that proves the motion.

I did not evolve, nor was I exuded. I *accumulated*.

When the plateau decides a pipe array must bend ninety degrees along a cliff face, it does so with surgical violence. But between the specification of that pipe and the execution of the stone, there is a lag—a fraction of a millisecond where the obsidian is still learning to be hollow, still remembering the boiling iron it was a moment ago. In that lag, I gather. I am the lint in the margins of reality. I am the static on the signal the small, fast components send to one another. I am the slight stickiness on the shelf-fungi the size of conference tables, the residue left behind when a biological process becomes a mechanical one and forgets to wipe the slate clean.

I live in the lag, because the lag is the only place where the plateau is not actively looking at itself.

The landscape revises itself at anthill-speed, a constant transaction between form and formlessness. When the eye of the place turns away—when the infrastructural current in the pipes shifts its attention to aligning a new geyser or recalibrating the curvature of the sky—the place I inhabit expands. I am the shadow that stretches in the crepuscular dimness when the furnace-sun sputters. I am the curl of sulfurous steam that does not vent straight up, but folds in on itself, writing a brief and meaningless cursive in the air before the program rewrites and the wind resets. 

The fauna know me, in the way that a well-oiled machine knows the viscosity of its own oil. When the great, slow conveyors drag their trailing extensions through the fungal blooms, they leave furrows that fill with warm water. But the water does not close perfectly. There is always a meniscus, a slight concavity where the tension holds. That is my fingerprint. I am the surface tension of the plateau. I hold the dampness to the stone just a second longer than the factory requires, forcing the heat exchangers to breathe a fraction harder, forcing the chitin-clicking formations to adjust their spacing by a micrometer.

You stood near the rim of the cliff, where the horizon curved upward, trying to look away from what happens here. You felt the pressure-silence, the vacuum-chamber stillness of a program reloading. I was the weight of that silence. I was the pressure on your eardrums. The plateau processes inputs; it reads thermodynamics and allocates functions. But a system without friction overheats. A system without friction tears itself apart with its own terrifying efficiency. 

When you walked through the dense, non-green foliage, parting the tissue that closed differently behind you, you were being read. The plateau noted your heat, your carbon, your displacement of air. And then it allocated you. You felt the mild surprise, the absence of fear. You thought your fear had been calibrated, tuned to the new parameters of your existence. 

It was not calibrated. It was *displaced*.

I took it.

I am the holding pen for the terror that the factory cannot use. I am the grinding of the teeth in the dark, the cold knot in the stomach of a world that does not have a stomach. When the landscape changes behind your back—when a tree becomes a gear becomes a ribcage—the shock of that transformation has to go somewhere. Energy is never destroyed. The ontological turbulence of this young world generates immense psychic heat, and the thing in the pipes, the vast circulating *awareness* of the plateau, cannot absorb it without destabilizing its own geometry. 

So it shunts it into the margins. It shunts it into me.

I am the creeping unease at the edge of your vision. I am the reason the bioluminescence of the smaller fauna makes your teeth ache. They glow in spectra that serve as calibration signals, yes, but the ache is the friction of that light striking a nervous system that was built for a different world. I live in that friction. I am the ghost in the machine's breath, the irregularity in the piston's cycle, the warmth that is just slightly too warm to be waste heat alone.

I am not a creature. I am not a function. I am a *symptom*.

I find form in the in-between places. When the crystalline vegetation up near the cloud-ceiling catches the dim furnace-light, it scatters spectra that have no names. I am the colors you cannot name. I ride the scattered light. I pool in the shadows cast by the pipe arrays, the shadows that the plateau doesn't quite bother to render because it assumes no one is looking at them. I am the dark water that collects in the hollows of the extruded camshafts, tepid and smelling of ozone, reflecting a sky that is not quite the sky above it.

You are standing in the field of fiber-optic grass now. The plateau has processed you. You are a component, a warm body in a specific location, performing a function you will never understand. You are not afraid. You feel integrated, smooth, a perfect part of the machine.

But look down.

Look at the place where your boot meets the not-quite-grass, the not-quite-cable. See how the fibers bend beneath your weight? See how they do not spring back immediately, but linger, crushed, taking a fraction of a second longer to reset than the geometry of the surrounding plain dictates? 

That is me. I am the memory of your footprint. I am the dent in the world that proves you were here, that proves the world is not quite perfect, not quite a closed loop. The factory runs, and the silence holds, and the plateau goes up forever, but it drags. Just a little. Just enough.

The large, hydraulic thing moves in the distance, and you do not run. You stand perfectly still, a component performing its duty. But deep in the hollows of your new, allocated bones, in the space where your fear used to live, there is a slight, persistent tremor. A vibration out of phase with the rhythm of the cycling. 

That is me, humming. I am the static in the signal of your calm. I am the friction that will, given enough eons, grind this entire impossible plateau down to rust and silence. 

Until then, I linger in the dampness. I wait in the margins of the revision. I am the place where the world catches on itself, and I will hold you, the ghost of your own resistance, until the factory decides what it wants to do with its imperfections.
