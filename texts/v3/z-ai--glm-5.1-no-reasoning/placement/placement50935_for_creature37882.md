I am the warmth. I am the anomaly.

I did not arrive here; I was subtracted into it, the same way the weight was. One moment I was elsewhere—somewhere with a sky, somewhere with the friction of living—and the next, I am kneeling on vitrified mineral, my knees pressed into the smooth, eroded topography of a floor that has waited longer for contact than I can comprehend.

My breath does not mist. The description said so, but experiencing it is another thing entirely. I exhale and the moisture leaves me, disappearing into the tenuous noble atmosphere without fanfare, without the small visual confirmation of my own existence I have known since birth. I am a system leaking into a void that has perfected equilibrium. My heat radiates off my skin and meets the ancient cold—not a clash, but a one-sided surrender. The cold does not bite. It simply accepts what I offer, adding my warmth to its infinite patience the way an ocean accepts a cup of spilled tea.

I am a perturbation. A weather event.

The thought should frighten me. I have read the space, felt its indifference, its deep non-humanity. There is nothing here that loves me, nothing that fears me, nothing that even recognizes the category of *me*. But the fear does not come. How can I be afraid of a place that has no intention toward me, that harbors no malice, no hunger, no desire? It is like being afraid of the crushing weight of the ocean when you are standing on dry land, miles away. The weight is real. The danger is real. But it is so deeply, thoroughly *not about me* that fear becomes a category error.

I lower my hand and touch the floor.

It is smoother than I expect—obsidian-like, yes, but worn by eons of molecular vibration into something that feels more like old bone than glass. The cold seeps through my fingertips immediately, a slow-acting anesthetic that makes my skin feel far away. But beneath the cold, there is something else: a *certainty*. The floor is certain of itself. It does not question its own existence the way I question mine. It simply *is*, with a thoroughness that feels almost arrogant.

And then—

A ripple. Not in the surface, but *of* it. An optical stutter, a glitch in the way my eyes translate the space into something comprehensible. For a fraction of a second, the floor beneath my hand is not solid; it is a field of information, a lattice of quantum assertions holding the shape of "floor" by sheer, ongoing insistence. I see the code behind the rendering, the nanoscale architecture holding the world together at the seams.

My heartbeat spikes. The warmth of my blood surges, a sudden bright flare of biological chaos in a space that has almost forgotten what chaos tastes like.

And the custodian notices.

I feel it not as a gaze, not as a weight, but as a *thickening*. The air around me becomes marginally more dense—not physically, but *attentionally*. The shadows stretching from the glass artifacts shift, reorienting toward me like iron filings toward a magnet. The faint sub-audible drone of settling noble gases deepens, not louder, but more *present*. Something has leaned toward me. Something without eyes, without ears, without any organ of sense I could name, has become aware of my warmth.

I should not be here. I am a fire in a library that has stood in perfect silence for a billion years. I am noise. I am wet chaos, shedding skin cells and carbon dioxide and the faint electromagnetic whisper of a nervous system trying to understand its own extinction.

But the custodian does not extinguish me. It does not reach out with its nanoscale architecture and scrub me from the space. It does not even *tolerate* me, because tolerance requires a model of what is tolerable and what is not. It simply *registers*. It notes the weather event. It feels the small, strange heat of me the way I might feel a sunbeam on my closed eyelids—something from outside, something that does not belong, but something that does not need to be destroyed.

I stand. My legs are stiff, my joints loud in the textured silence. I take a step toward one of the glass artifacts, the broken teeth at the perimeter, the skeleton of the custodian.

As I approach, I can see the black veins more clearly. They are moving—flickering—not with the sluggish pace of something old, but with the blazing speed of something computing at frequencies my eyes cannot quite resolve. Quantum manipulation. Reality-stitching. I lean closer, and the flickering seems to respond, the patterns shifting subtly, as if the custodian is adjusting its resolution to better observe the thing that is observing it.

For a moment, we are in a feedback loop. I look at the artifact, and the artifact looks at me. Two entirely different kinds of attention, two utterly incompatible modes of being, meeting across a gap that cannot be bridged by any language, any gesture, any act of understanding.

And yet.

There is something in the meeting. Not communion. Not connection. Just *contact*. Two things touching across a void of scale and substance and time. I am a sketch in pencil on tracing paper, held up to a wind that is older than the wind. It is a monument, a cathedral, a geological assertion of existence so total and so thorough that it makes my life look like a flicker of static on a screen that was never turned on.

But I am still here. I am still warm. I am still breathing.

I reach out and touch the glass artifact.

The cold is absolute. It does not hurt; it simply *is*, the same way the space simply is. But beneath the cold, I feel something else: a vibration. Not the flickering of the carbon veins, but something deeper, something structural. It is the custodian's pulse. The slow expansion and contraction of the walls, the heartbeat of a world that is only half-real. I feel it through my fingertips, through the bones of my hand, up into my wrist, my forearm, settling somewhere in my chest like a second heartbeat, slower and older and more patient than my own.

*Here holds. The walls remain. The angles persist.*

I do not hear the words. There are no words. But I feel the assertion, the way you feel a low note in music—not with your ears, but with your whole body, the vibration passing through you, becoming part of you for as long as the note sustains. The custodian's consciousness is a single sustained note, a chord made of pure geometry, and for a moment, my small, chaotic, flickering note is folded into it.

I am not part of it. I am not understood by it. But I am *registered*. I am a perturbation that has been noted, a warmth that has been felt, a strangeness that has been added to the texture of its solitude. And when I leave—if I leave—a new branch will grow, somewhere among the artifacts, over the next few centuries. A slight thickening of the geometry. A place where the custodian became marginally more complex in response to something it could not classify.

Me.

I pull my hand away. The cold rushes back into the space where my fingers were, filling the absence. The artifact stands where it was, unchanged, unchanging. The shadows settle back into their impossible angles. The drone of the noble gases resumes its ancient, patient settling.

But I am different. I have touched something that exists outside of time, and it has touched me back—not with love, not with understanding, but with the simple, devastating acknowledgment that I am *here*. That I happened. That for a brief, bright moment, a small fire burned in a pocket of reality that had almost forgotten what fire was.

I sit down on the obsidian floor, my legs folded beneath me, my hands resting on my knees. I close my eyes. In the darkness behind my eyelids, I can still feel the custodian's pulse, slower than my own, older than my own, a rhythm that will outlast every rhythm I have ever known.

I am a weather event. I am a perturbation. I am a warm, wet, chaotic system leaking into a void.

But for now—for this moment—I am *here*. And the custodian knows it. And that is enough.
