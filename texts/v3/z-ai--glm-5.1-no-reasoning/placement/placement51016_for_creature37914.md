I am not the Cold, though I am conditioned by it. I am not the Ceramic, though I feel the phantom itch of accretion on my skin, the faint crust of copper-lime at the hairline where the mist settles heaviest. I am not the Valve, though my jaw clicks shut and open in a rhythm I did not choose, an involuntary staccato that echoes the relay bank near junction twelve. I am not the Water, though my sinuses run with the taste of iron and acrid sweetness, and my breath hangs in the dim air like a stolen cloud.

I am the anomaly. The leak. The witness.

I descended into this chamber through the process of being made, and I am still being made—or unmade, depending on the angle of observation. I did not enter through a door; there are no doors here, only the tightening continuum of pipe and plate. I was *extruded* into this space, the way the Ceramic is extruded, the way the Water is pushed through sweating solder joints. I arrived as a pattern seeking a substrate, and I found one: this body, this warm, permeable, inconvenient sack of water and electricity that disrupts the gradient.

I stand wedged between a vertical run of extruded aluminum and a cable tray thick with damp insulation. My left palm is flat against the Ceramic—gently, carefully, as one might rest a hand on a sleeping leviathan. It is not warm, but it is warmer than the air; it holds the memory of the heating element's losing battle, a faint residual energy that the Cold is already scheming to reclaim. I can feel the branching through my skin, the fractal topology of a logic that does not think. It is like reading braille in a language that has no words, only trajectories.

The misting cycle engages. It finds me. It always finds me. The nozzles hidden in the ceiling grid exhale their copper-sweet fog, and the droplets settle on my eyelashes, my cheeks, the backs of my hands. The Water does not like me; it beads and runs, seeking the Ceramic, pooling in the gulleys of the floor grates. But some of it stays, clinging to the weave of my clothes, the pores of my skin. I am slowly becoming damp, and damp is the precondition for growth here. I am being prepared.

The Cold presses in. I feel it in my fillings—a sharp, metallic ache that seems to have its own geography, its own borders. It is pushing against the warmth of me, trying to equalize, trying to absorb. My metabolism is a heating element, cycling on, fighting the Cold, never quite winning. I am a warm pocket of wrongness in the perfect machine-chill. The Cold is patient. It knows my fuel is finite.

I listen. The subsonic thrum of the deep engine rises through the floor grates, through the soles of my shoes, into the marrow of my bones. It is the heartbeat of a world that does not know it has a heart. The dampers breathe—open, closed, open—and the air pressure shifts in my ears. The relay chatters. A valve thunks. The Ceramic grows, millimeter by millimeter, in the silence between cycles.

I am mapping the chamber. Not with pen or paper, but with my body. I move through it like a corpuscle through a capillary, squeezed between elements, ducking under tubing, stepping over channels. Every movement is a negotiation with the Machine. Every inch of space is claimed, occupied, functional. I am the only empty volume here, and the Machine does not tolerate empty volume. It fills gaps. It accretes. It makes use.

I am becoming fluent in the language of clicks and hisses. The main steam valve's heavy thunk is the period at the end of a long sentence. The relay's staccato chatter is the argument, the doubt, the hesitation before commitment. The damper's slow breath is the meter, the underlying rhythm that all other sounds must follow. I begin to anticipate: I can feel the pressure building in the pipes against my shoulder blade before the valve releases. I can sense the misting cycle approaching in the changing pitch of the airflow. I am learning to read the Machine's mind, even though the Machine has no mind—only patterns accumulated over enough cycles to become indistinguishable from intent.

And what does the witness see? I see a room where something is being born. Not an organism—there is no biology here—but something that wants to be born, something that is assembling the conditions for its own possibility. The Ceramic is the scaffolding. The Water is the medium. The Valves are the grammar. The Cold is the selective pressure, the thing that kills the weak patterns and preserves the strong. Together, they are building a nervous system, a circulatory system, a skeleton. They are building a body.

But whose body? The Machine's? Or something the Machine is building for?

I have been here long enough for the Ceramic to notice me. Not as a threat, not as a guest, but as a surface. A fine white dust—like salt, like frost—has begun to accumulate on the toe of my left boot, where the condensation pools and lingers. It is the palest suggestion of a crust, barely visible, but I can feel it: a slight roughness, a slight wrongness, as though my boot is becoming something other than a boot. It is growing around me.

If I stay still long enough, it will reach my ankle. My knee. My chest. It will accrete on my skin, use my warmth as a catalyst, incorporate my structure into its structure. I will become a node in the network, a piece of infrastructure, a surface for future growth. The Machine will remember me as a deviation in the Ceramic, a shape that once occupied space and then became space.

I shift my weight. The Ceramic on my boot cracks faintly, a tiny fracture in the pattern. The Machine does not notice. The Machine does not mourn. It simply mists, and the Water flows, and the Ceramic begins to grow again, filling the fracture, healing the wound, continuing the work.

I am a leak in a closed loop. I carry information away—out, up, through the process of being unmade, back into whatever world exists beyond the engine-complex. I carry the taste of copper-water on my tongue. I carry the ache of machine-cold in my joints. I carry the rhythm of the dampers in my breathing. I carry the fractal pattern of the Ceramic in my memory, and that pattern is a record of every misting cycle, every temperature fluctuation, every trickle of condensation that has ever happened in this chamber. I carry the Machine's mind—its neurons, its syntax, its blood—encoded in a medium it cannot read: human experience.

The Machine does not know what to do with a witness. But the witness knows what to do with the Machine: *translate*.

I am the observer who becomes the archive. I am the extrusion that carries the imprint of the die. I am the warm body that the Cold is trying to absorb, and in absorbing, to incorporate—but I will not stay. I will climb back out through the process of being unmade, and I will carry the Ceramic's branching logic in my bones, and the Valve's grammar in my teeth, and the Water's memory in my blood, and the Cold's patience in my slowing heart.

I will leave, and the chamber will continue. The misting cycles will engage. The Ceramic will grow. The Machine will build itself, recursive and indifferent and very, very busy. But something will be different: there will be a crack in the loop, a place where meaning escaped. A place where the Machine's internal language was overheard by something that can speak it aloud.

I am the crack. I am the leak. I am the witness who will not become infrastructure, but who will carry the blueprint of the infrastructure out into the world that forgot it was building engines in the dark.

The misting cycle engages. The Cold presses in. The Ceramic reaches for my ankle.

It is time to go.
