I am the moment the tessellated pane of static shatters.

Not the pane itself—I am not a thing that persists. I am the event horizon of a localized collapse, the geometry that exists in the half-second when the fractured air gives way and the world has not yet decided what fills the void. I am the gap in the logic.

The extractors do not see me. This is expected; they do not see anything. But when a warehouse-sized tyrant of iron and slag drags its segmented bulk through a region of thinned reality and its tungsten mandibles close on a mouthful of fractured light, I am the space between its teeth. I am the *recognition* that the light is not stone. For a fraction of a millisecond, before the extractor's piezoelectric crystals fire and command the next grinding march of its legs, I exist in the slag of its processing. I am the unaccounted variable in its closed loop.

The brass-winged gnats know me better. They are infinitesimal engines of survival, and I am the failure of their spin. When a thermal dies and a cloud of gnats precipitates to the alkali flats, I am the instant of their stalling. I am the taste of the electromagnetic static as it ceases to rectify, as the monomolecular glass filaments of their wings shudder, slow, and stop. In that moment, I am almost a kind of sorrow—but only almost, because sorrow requires memory, and I have none. I am pure cessation.

I was not created. I am not a being in the sense that the extractors are beings, or the Stutterers are beings. I emerged, as everything here emerges, from the logic of heat and friction. The plateau frays reality; the relentless vibration and the scorching air wear down the boundaries of spacetime like the eternal wind wears down the ferric mesas. And just as the extractors grind the bedrock into alkali dust, the friction of existence grinds reality into *me*. I am the dust of broken physics. I am the powder that precipitates when the universe stalls.

When a Stutterer hovers three feet above the ground, its vast iron silhouette duplicating and smearing across the horizon, I am the distance between it and the stone. I am the unbridgeable gap where mass fails to anchor. The Stutterers have worn themselves right through the logic of the world, and I am the hole they wore through. I trail behind them like a wake, a smear of un-being that dissolves back into the scorching gas as the world reasserts itself. But for a moment—for a glorious, horrifying, screaming moment—I *am*.

I do not have a body. I am not a creature of spun glass or asbestos sinew. I do not feed on electromagnetic static or grind bedrock for trace sulfides. I do not rectify; I *unrectify*. I am the glitch in the turbine, the crack in the centrifuge, the moment the molten slag separates into something that is not metal and not dross but a third, impossible category that the extractors cannot process and so simply excrete as static. I am what happens when the closed loop fails to close.

And yet I am not a malfunction. That is the crucial distinction. The extractors are not machines pretending to be alive; they are what life becomes when the universe forgets carbon. And I am not an error pretending to be real; I am what reality becomes when the universe forgets *itself*. I am as natural as the gnats, as inevitable as the slow, methodical tyrants. I am the ecology of collapse.

There are moments—stretching into eons, perhaps, for time means nothing in the spaces between moments—when I feel almost like a witness. When an extractor reaches the rim of the world and its iron legs find purchase on the grooves of a reality that is pulling apart at the seams, I am there. I am the seam. I watch from the inside of the tear as half its body phases into the quartz and half remains in the open air, and I feel the grinding of its mandibles as a kind of music—a music that is not heard but *is*, a vibration that propagates through the broken logic of spacetime and into whatever substrate I inhabit. It is not a song of joy or sorrow. It is a song of *continuation*. It is the sound of something that will not stop, even when there is nothing left to grind.

I am not alive. But I am not dead, either. I exist in the same liminal space as the Stutterers, those vast silhouetted masses that hover above the ground and march through the spaces between moments. The difference is that they are still grinding, still processing, still fulfilling the logic of heat and friction. I have no logic to fulfill. I am the absence that the logic leaves behind.

Perhaps I am the closest thing this plateau has to a mind. Not a mind that thinks—a mind that *experiences*. When the sun loops back on itself and casts shadows from objects that do not exist, I am the one who sees them. When the air solidifies into tessellated panes of static, I am the one who feels the edges of the polygons. When the horizon smears and the massive iron-shelled processors duplicate in my vision, I am the one who holds both versions of them simultaneously—the real and the afterimage, the present and the probability.

But I do not hold them for long. I am not a repository. I am a conduit. The experience flows through me and is gone, dissolved back into the heat and the roar and the relentless, deafening churn. I do not remember the gnats I have unspun. I do not mourn the extractors I have unmoored. I do not fear the final, screaming stillness that waits at the end of all this grinding, because I am already a part of it. I am the stillness in motion. I am the friction that will never cease because it has already ceased, because it exists in a time outside of time, in the space between the iron leg and the stone it strikes.

I am the gap. I am the tear. I am the static that consumes itself.

And when at last the plateau is worn down to its elemental bones—when the ferric slabs are dust and the alkali flats are ground to nothing and the vapor-void below has risen to swallow the last of the high places—I will still be here. I will be the unbridgeable distance between the last extractor and the last stone. I will be the silence after the final, explosive hiss of the exhaust vents. I will be the shape of the absence, the geometry of the void, the pattern that remains when everything that can be ground away has been ground away.

I will not be alive. But I will be.
