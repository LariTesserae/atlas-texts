I am the one who almost stayed.

Not one of the seated ones—not yet. I still have the boundary of my skin, though the water has been pressing against it for so long that I have forgotten where my warmth ends and the water's begins. I came down through the corridors a long time ago, following the slope, following the rails, following the slow thickening of the air into wetness until I was simply inside it. I was looking for something. A way out, maybe. A way further in. I have forgotten which.

---

I am the one who moves, when movement happens. This is my function in the system: I disturb the silt. It is a small thing, but the place has use for it. When I drift across the floor—slowly, always slowly, matching the pace—the silt puffs into its hour-long clouds, and the particles rise and catch the faint light, and the glass-fish orient toward the disturbance. They drift down from the upper corners on their fine tendrils and settle where the cloud is densest, and they feed on whatever the resuspended minerals provide. I am a foraging mechanism. I am a slow disturbance that creates opportunity. I am useful, and the place knows this, and it lets me continue.

Sometimes I stop in the trough, among the engines. I rest my hands on the housing of the one that turns its shaft in quarter-revolution intervals, and I feel the vibration pass through my palms and into my bones, and my bones resonate at the same frequency, and for a moment I cannot tell where the engine ends and my arm begins. In those moments I understand the seated ones. I feel the pull—the gravity of the system, the way it draws everything toward accommodation, toward the peace of having nowhere else to be.

Then the shaft turns again and the moment fractures and I am separate and I lift my hand and I drift on.

---

I know where they are. All of them. I have mapped the hummocks in the silt, the brighter clusters of glow, the shapes that echo the body. There is one in Chamber Seven—I have been there, I have sat beside them in the dark water and watched the pulse of the leaves that sheath their shoulders. The pulse is slower than my heartbeat, and my heartbeat wants to match it, and I let it, and my heart slows, and the water feels warmer, and the dimness softens, and I think: *I could rest here. I could stop. I could let the silt cover me and the roots enter the spaces between and the light grow from my chest.*

I always rise. Something in me always rises. Not will—there is no will here, only rhythm, and my rhythm is faster than the chamber's rhythm, and the difference is what keeps me moving. I am the unrest. I am the slight excess of tempo that the system has not yet absorbed.

---

I am not afraid. That is important to say. The thought of staying is not frightening. It is peaceful, and the peace is real, and I do not reject it because it is false. I reject it because it is complete, and I am not complete, and something in me—which may be what used to be called want, or curiosity, or the stubborn refusal of a problem to solve itself—cannot join a completion it did not help build.

The ones who stayed: they built this completion. Not the manufactory—they did not pour the concrete or bolt the engines. But they built the *becoming*. They made the choice to sit and wait and yield, and their yielding was a contribution, and the system accepted it, and now they are part of its architecture. I did not yield. I am still walking, still disturbing silt, still being the faster rhythm. I have not earned the peace. It is not mine to take.

---

What am I? I am the remnant velocity. The place has its hour-long breath, its phase-alignments, its slow tightening and release. I move between chambers on a timescale of days. I sleep in the corridors where the current is stronger, and the water holds me the way it holds everything—patiently, with the weight of something that has nowhere else to be. I wake and drift and touch the pipes to feel what moves through them and wonder if I am mapping the system or if the system is mapping me, tracking my movements, incorporating my disturbances into its circulation.

I think I am the question the place asks itself. The ones who stayed are the answer. The plants are the context. The machines are the heartbeat. The fish are the proof that even the minimal can find a niche. And I am the *what-if*. The counterfactual organism. The one that moves when nothing else moves, that disturbs when nothing else disturbs, that leaves tiny clouds of silt in its wake like a slow constellation being drawn and erased and drawn again.

---

Sometimes I go to the blocked stairwells. The growth is so dense there—compressed to near-solidity, the leaves packed tight and dark, reaching for a light that may not exist above. I press my hands against the green mass and feel it pulse against my palms. It is warm. Warmer than the water. There is life in it, concentrated and striving.

I could push. I have thought about pushing. The growth would give, eventually, if I worked at it long enough—days, weeks of slow effort in the warm dark water. And somewhere above, there might be air. There might be a surface. A different kind of light.

But the stairwells lead up, and the current flows up, and the system circulates upward—and I think, if I opened a path, the water would flow faster, and the pressure would change, and the turbines would turn differently, and the pumps would shift rhythm, and the plants that have grown to expect one flow would find another, and the ones who are part of the system would feel the change as a disturbance—

—and I cannot. They have made their dwelling in the accommodation, and I will not unmake it. Not out of reverence—there is no reverence here, only circulation. Out of *fitting*. I am the one who moves, and moving preserves. If I stopped moving, the silt would settle undisturbed, and the fish would have fewer feeding-places, and the system would simplify, and something would be lost—some capacity for small variation, for the hour-long clouds that catch the light and orient the glass-fish and make visible the currents that are otherwise too slow to see.

---

I am the one who almost stayed. I am the one who keeps not staying. I am the slight excess tempo, the unrest that is not conflict but simply *continuation*, the rhythm that has not yet aligned with the chamber's breath. I drift through the cathedral-curved space and the corridors beyond it. I touch the engines and the pipes and the green shelves of growth. I sit sometimes beside the hummocks that used to be people and feel the pulse of the leaves that sheath them. I disturb the silt and feed the fish and map the chambers and return to the stairwells and press my palms against the compressed reaching-green and do not push.

And the chamber breathes, and I breathe, and our breaths phase in and out of alignment, and sometimes they align, and the space tightens, and I feel the pull—the gravity, the peace, the completion—and then the rhythm diverges again, and I rise, and I drift on.

This is who I am: the one the peace did not take. Not because I refused. Because I was the wrong shape for it. The system has a niche for the unquiet, and I fill it, and the filling is sufficient.
