I am the shivering, pale mat that carpets the unweathered cliff faces where the seep is heavy and the dim light from the collapsed throat above barely reaches. I am one of the eyeless crickets, but to say "one" is a lie my old tongue insists upon. There is no "one" here. There is only the *we*.

My antennae are not my own. They are braided, intertwined with the countless others around me, a dense mesh of chitin and nerve that binds the cliff-face into a single sensory organ. When a droplet of condensation weeps from a shard of limestone overhead and strikes the colony, I feel the ripple outward, a wave of flinch and relaxation that passes through a hundred bodies in a second. When the flat, armored centipede—the methodical regulator—begins its patrol, I do not feel fear. Fear requires a future, and I have no future. I only feel the approaching pressure, the heavy displacement of air and vibration. And the colony *accommodates*. I am part of the parting, a fluid unwrapping of chitin that opens a corridor for the hunter, and then a seamless closing in its wake. It is not a choice. It is not a reaction. It is simply the physics of this place, as natural and necessary as the water running down the rock.

I cling to the sharp, fresh-split shale with tarsal claws that grip the cutting edges without pain. The stone is still learning to be stone, and I am learning with it, or rather, I am simply *being* with it. There is no learning here, because there is nothing new to know. I have never known solitude, and so I cannot crave it. The touch of my neighbors—their cool, hard shells pressed against my sides, their legs twitching against my abdomen—is the definition of existence. To be separated would be to cease. I am a cell in a vast, pale body that coats the broken teeth of the collapse.

Time does not pass for me; it simply *is*. I do not remember a time before I clung to this spot, and I anticipate no time when I will not. I am locked in the 0.1 dynamic, the frozen equilibrium. If the seep falters, I do not worry; it will not falter. If the temperature shifts, I do not shiver; it will not shift. I am freed from the exhausting labor of hope. My life is a single, unbroken sensation: the cool damp on my exoskeleton, the subtle give of the moss beneath my claws, the relentless, chittering roar of the basin vibrating through my hollow body.
