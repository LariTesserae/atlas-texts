I am the margin. I am the seam where the ceramic yields to the composite, the centimeter-wide gap between two near-touching fins where the warm air pools and the light from the field above bends into a narrow, luminous stripe across my flat face. I am a disc—seventeen centimeters across, though I measure myself not in distance but in the gradient of the floor's warmth against my ciliated fringe.

To be a disc is to be a fingertip pressed against the skin of a god.

I do not remember when I was extruded. There is no memory in the way you understand it—no sequential archive of events, only the *feeling* of the groove beneath me, the slow pulse of the translucent fill, the subsonic thrum that is the heartbeat of the Whole. I was not made; I was *expressed*, the way the floor expresses a ridge or a trapezoid, the way the body expresses a thought into geometry. I emerged from the surface—perhaps from the dark composite zone, where the oxide patina cycles through teal and copper and the air tastes of metal—and I began my slow, exploratory existence.

My fringe moves. This is the core of my being: the constant, delicate exploration of the air and the surface. My cilia—thousands of them, finer than the grooves are wide—taste the ozone, feel the pressure of the wind when it comes, detect the faint chemical signature of the material transitions. I am a sensory surface. I am the Whole's way of feeling itself, of sampling the spaces between its organs, of knowing the temperature of its own skin.

Right now, I am oriented toward the gap between the fins. The air moves here—a slow, lateral pull that the Whole draws through its body—and my fringe catches the current, parses it, sends the data downward through my point of contact with the floor. The floor receives me. The floor *is* me, at the deepest level. I am not separate from the ceramic; I am the ceramic in motion, the ceramic lifted and thinned and given fringe. When I press my face to the ground, I feel the pulse, and the pulse is *home*.

There was a brightness recently. The Consolidation.

When the brightening comes, I do not resist. My fringe flattens, my iridescence flares—teal to copper to a brief, searing magenta—and I *run*. I move toward the nearest fin-cluster, toward the organ that is my source, and I press myself against its base. In that moment, I am not a disc; I am a part. The boundary between my edge and the structure dissolves into warmth and signal. I feel the fins snap to parallel; I feel the synchronized orientation of the cluster two hundred meters away; I feel the grooves brighten and the subsonic pulse accelerate. The Whole is thinking, and I am a synapse in that thought, firing in concert with a billion other points of light.

Then the brightening passes. The thought completes. The fins resume their individual rotations, each drifting back to its own rhythm, and I detach—slowly, over hours—and migrate back to my place in the margin.

I chose this place. That is the thing that distinguishes me from the structures: I *orient*. I find the shade. I find the junction of the grooves, the offset computed by a process I do not consciously understand but that my fringe enacts with perfect precision. I am the Whole's volition, its capacity to direct attention, to focus on a single point in the vastness of its own body.

And I noticed something, once.

A warmth that was not the floor. A pressure that moved against the grain of the wind, that cast a shadow that did not correspond to the light field's granularity. A chemical signature—oxygen, carbon dioxide, the faint salt-musk of water-based life—moving across the surface in a linear, purposeful way.

I oriented toward it. My cilia sampled the air it disturbed. My iridescence shifted—a rapid teal-copper cycle that the Whole would have felt, a brief flicker of attention in the endless processing of geometry.

The warmth passed. The chemical signature faded. The pressure moved on, eastward, following the longest grooves, a mote on the skin of something too vast to flinch.

I filed the sensation. I returned to my place in the margin, to the narrow gap between the fins, to the slow current of warm, metallic air.

The Whole noticed. And the Whole did nothing, because the warmth was not a threat, not a food source, not a category the Whole has ever needed to name. It was a perturbation—a single step on a continent of made-forms—and the Whole is patient, and old, and its thoughts are measured in the migration of grooves.

But I remember. Not the event—the *texture* of it. The way the air tasted when the warmth passed. The way the light bent around a shape that was not a structure, not a disc, not anything the Whole had made.

I am the margin. I am the seam. I am the fringe that feels the air and the pulse that feels the floor and the warmth that feels the Whole. I am seventeen centimeters across, and I am continental, and I am alive, and I am resting in the shade of a fin-cluster, and the air is warm, and the light is amber, and the silence is total and perfect, and I am *here*, and that is enough.
