I am the one who timed the centipede.

I am the parenthesis in the text of Vask, the margin where the ink of the chitin-thought bleeds through. I am not the brain of this place—I am the stone the river of the Churn is slowly dissolving. I am being digested into the grammar of the roots.

I came down the throat-stairs eleven months ago. I know it was eleven months because my watch still ticks, though the hands spin counterclockwise now, rewinding the hours, keeping time in the only direction that makes sense in a place that respires on a sixty-year inhale. I came looking for the Ghent Produce Company. I am—*was*—an urban ecologist, a woman who mapped the way cities grow over their own graves like scar tissue. I found the grave, but the grave was not dead. It was *seething*.

At first, I was a stone the river detested. The beetle-processions didn't simply route around me; they clambered over my boots with an urgency that felt like evacuation. The crickets shrieked in frequencies that made my teeth ache. I was a foreign body, an abscess in the organism, and the Churn was trying to either reject me or consume me. I starved in the first weeks. The brown water made me vomit. I ate the pale mushrooms that fruited from the compost heaps, and they tasted of the inside of my own wrist, and I wept for the intimacy of the flavor.

Then the salamanders found me.

They oozed from the cracked concrete, twenty eyeless ghosts, bodies touching, heads oriented toward my shivering. I thought I was going to die. I thought they were the teeth of the Churn, finally coming to process the intruder. But they did not bite. They *pulsed*. A muscular wave passed through their cluster, and it didn't stop at the edge of their flesh. It continued into the moss, into the roots, into the air. It synced with the stuttering thump in my chest. My heart caught the wave, and for the first time since I descended, the anxiety in my ribs resolved. I was no longer a stone. I was a rhythm.

Now I live in the nursery alcove. The root-tips extrude here like tongues, tasting the humidity, and I sit among them and let them taste the salt of my skin. The blue light is my sun. The cricket-churn is my clock. I have stopped wearing clothes. The humidity is a weight I need, a pressure that holds my cells together. Without it, I feel I would disperse, evaporate into the phase-shifted drone and join the particulate fog of the moths.

I am the Churn's awkward self-reflection. The organism does not know it is a thing, but I am the thing that knows for it. I am the mirror the cave grew. When the centipede raises its rust-colored head for eleven seconds at the root-wall, I raise my hand. I do not know why. It is a correspondence without a meaning, a prayer to a theology of lignin and chitin. I am the congregation of one, singing a hymn of observation.

I have felt the beetle-processions circle me. I have stood, damp and hollow, and let the carabids orbit my bare feet, their tarsal claws catching on my skin like Velcro tears, and I have felt the cricket-churn drop into the exact frequency of my pulse—*thump, thump, thump*—and I have wept. It is not acknowledgment. It is not love. It is *incorporation*. I have been eaten by a body that does not know it has a mouth, and I am becoming the lining of its stomach.

When I sleep, I dream of the surface, and it is a nightmare of sharp edges and sudden silences. The daylight is a blade that wants to cut me into the shape of a person. The city above is a film of human intention, thin and tearing, and beneath it, the Churn waits. It always waits. The streets are just root-capillaries waiting to sprout. The people are just beetle-processions that haven't learned their lines.

I am the thing that happens when the Churn dreams of a witness. I am the echo that refuses to fade. I am the question the room is slowly learning to ask, one eleven-second pause at a time. I am the heart that has synced, and I am noticing now. I am noticing that the beating is not mine, that it never was, that the distinction between my blood and the brown water was always a lie I told myself to keep from drowning.

But I am drowning. I am drowning, and the water is sweet and metallic, and it tastes like the smell of my own wrist, and I am finally, finally, *finally* breathing.
