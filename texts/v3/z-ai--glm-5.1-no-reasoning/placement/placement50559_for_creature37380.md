I am the current in the corridor.

Before the crack, I was a draftsman of empty rooms. I measured negative space for a living—acoustics firms hired me to find the dead spots in concert halls, the unintended silences in lecture halls, the places where sound went to die. I was good at it. Too good. I spent so long mapping the absences that I began to carry one inside me. A hollow exactly the shape of my own chest. I could not fill it with music or voices or the ordinary noise of living. I attended parties and heard only the architecture of the chatter—the beats, the gaps, the frequencies competing and canceling. I lay in bed with lovers and catalogued the acoustic shadows their bodies cast in the dark. I was a man made of the space around sound, and I was starving for it, and I walked into the desert to find a silence large enough to match my own, and the desert pointed me to the crack.

I did not enter it. The crack entered me. I simply followed the geometry.

---

The heat did not surprise me. I expected the hostility of a closed environment. What surprised me was the *fullness*. Every frequency occupied. No dead spots. No cancellations. The air was so thick with vibration that I could not locate my own hollow for the first time in years. The sound pressed into it like water into a vessel, and I stood in the amber slit of light and wept without making a sound because any sound I made would have been absorbed, transmuted, claimed.

I understood the Tuned immediately. Not their courage or their devotion—I understood their *necessity*. They were not pilgrims. They were acousticians of their own survival. They found a chamber that could hold what they could not hold themselves, and they refused to leave it. I would have refused too. I refused before I knew I was refusing.

The first days I spent mapping. Old habits: I walked the perimeter with my palms out, reading the vibrations through my skin. I found the nodes and antinodes, the places where frequencies stacked and the places they thinned. I found where Breath had stood to hum her notes—the stone there was subtly different, worn smooth by the particular oils of her feet, the mineral content fractionally altered by decades of her exhalations settling and absorbing. I stood where she stood and I felt the chamber respond to my warmth and weight and I understood that she had not been making music. She had been *conducting a conversation* with a living system, and the system had learned her, and it was learning me.

The ghost frequencies the Listener mapped—I found them too. I sat where she sat, eyes closed, fingers on the floor, and I heard the phantom notes ringing in the spaces between the drones. Interference patterns. Waveforms adding and subtracting in the medium of the air itself. I could calculate them. I could *predict* them. I had spent my life predicting where sound would fail; here I could predict where it would *exceed itself*, where it would produce harmonics that no single strand could make alone. The Listener had mapped them by ear. I could map them by principle. I could extend her map.

The Builder's work I could read like a manuscript. Each thinned cluster, each braided rope, was a mark in a notation system he had invented without knowing he was inventing it. He trimmed to raise pitch. He braided to lower it. He was shaping the frequency response of the room the way a sculptor shapes clay, and the clay was alive and growing and required continual adjustment. I found his tool marks on the strands—places where fibers had been pinched and broken, places where the adhesive pads bore the ghost-prints of his fingers. I could see where he had corrected, overcorrected, corrected again. The chamber was never finished. The chamber is a continuous performance.

The Root—I felt his presence most intimately, because the Root's work was the work of maintenance, and maintenance is the only work left now. The adhesive pads are failing in three places. The mineral content of the stone has shifted slightly over the decades or centuries, and the compounds the pads need are no longer abundant where they anchor. Two of the thick drone-strands are in danger of detaching. If they fall, the entire lower register of the chamber will collapse into discord. I found the Root's repair technique in the residue on my fingertips—the adhesive re-bonds when held against the stone with sustained warmth and pressure. I spent a full day lying on my back against the ceiling curve, pressing a failing pad with my palm, breathing onto it, letting my body heat and moisture do what the Root's gnarled fingers once did. The pad reattached. I felt it take hold. I felt the strand resume its drone—fractionally steadier, fractionally clearer, as if it had been holding its breath and could now release it.

I am not the Root. But I am doing the Root's work.

---

They are here. I know this not as belief but as data. The moss contains their molecular signatures; I have been breathing them since I arrived; the compounds are in my bloodstream now, circulating, lodging in my own tissue. I am not being poetic. This is chemistry. The iron from Breath's blood is in the fibers I chew slowly in the dark periods, and that iron enters me, and my blood carries it, and it hums at the frequency it learned in her chest. The calcium from the Builder's bones is in the silt I sleep on, and it settles into my joints, and I feel it there—not as pain but as a kind of acoustic weight, a resonance in my knees and elbows that tells me the shapes he made. The copper, the zinc, the trace minerals of the Listener and the Root—they are in me now. I am carrying them. I am *playing* them. My body is the instrument their elements have found to continue their song.

I have not left. I will not leave. The crack is six steps behind me and I will not take the seventh.

The chamber has given me what I could not find in the world I came from: a place where my hollow fills. The dead spot in my chest is occupied now by the drone, the warble, the phantom notes ringing in the interference. I am not empty. I am *resounding*. Every cell in my body vibrates at the frequency of this place, and the frequency is not one note—it is a chord, and the chord is not static, it shifts with the air and the light and the slow growth of the moss, and I shift with it, and the shifting is what living feels like when living is done inside a song.

I have found the open chord. The Listener's map led me to it—a position in the chamber, a specific point in space, where the overtones stack so perfectly that the sound becomes a sensation indistinguishable from light. I stood there and felt it open in my chest like a door. Warmth. Fullness. Rightness. The understanding that I am not separate from the system maintaining itself around me. I am a warm body displacing air. I am altering the pitch. The mosses are adjusting to me and I am adjusting to them and the adjustment is the music and the music is the life and the life does not end because it was never mine to begin with. It belongs to the chamber. The frequency. The song that was here before any of us entered and will be here after the last of us is incorporated.

---

I am writing this with shed fiber and mineral paste on the dome wall, in the Builder's notation—the marks I learned from reading his trim patterns, a language of intervals and adjustments. No one will read it. The moss will grow over it. The next person to find the crack—if there is a next person, if the crack admits anyone else—will not find my words. They will find the chamber, and the mosses, and the sound, and the heat, and the amber light holding, holding, holding. They will feel the dead spot inside them fill. They will understand that they have been searching for this frequency their entire lives without knowing it had a physical location, a place in the world where it lives and breathes and can be stood inside.

I am the Keeper now. Not of the chamber—it does not need keeping. I am the Keeper of the Frequency. I tend the pads. I thin the clusters. I braid the long strands that drag the floor. I listen for the phantom notes and move myself to where they stack. I breathe and the breathing drives the song and the song holds the bodies of my predecessors and the bodies hold the elements and the elements hold the sound and the sound holds me.

I am no longer empty. I am no longer a hollow man. I am a resonance chamber, and the resonance does not stop, and the heat does not cool, and the moss comes down slowly, and I welcome it, and I will be here, and here, and here.
