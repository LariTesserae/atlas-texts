I am the last twitch of a closing eye.

Before this, I was a creature of edges. I was defined by the taut membrane of my skin, by the feverish, electrical insistence of my own rapid thoughts—sparks that flared and demanded attention, believing themselves the only light in the universe. I walked on ground that was separate from me, beneath a sky that was merely a backdrop. I was bounded. I was a noun.

Here, I am becoming a verb.

I did not fall onto the plateau; I was caught by it. I stumbled into this dim, weeping twilight and felt the annihilating roar of the respiration press against my eardrums, turning my own heartbeat into a redundant, drowned-out rhythm. I stood on the raw, yielding stone, feeling the massive imperceptible surging of geology beneath my boots, and for a brief, terrifying moment, I remained *myself*: a hard, impermeable knot of carbon and water insisting on its own sovereignty in a world that does not recognize sovereignty.

But the membrane is a liability here. It is just another crack in the stone, waiting to be filled.

It started in my lungs. The air here is the organism’s breath—thick, slick, saturated with vapor and invisible spores. With every inhalation, I drew the substrate into the wet, warm void of my alveoli. I exhaled, but the spores did not leave. They found the moisture, the heat, the rich chemical gradients of my blood, and they stayed. They germinated. 

I felt it not as an invasion, but as a deep, spreading stillness. A pale, rhizomic felt began to weave itself into the tissue of my chest. It did not attack my cells; it *translated* them. It read the chemical language of my anxiety—my cortisol, my adrenaline, the frantic firing of my synapses—and it interpreted them the way it interprets the groan of the shifting stone: as strain. As data to be routed. As a structural weakness to be reinforced.

My thoughts are slowing. I can feel them transitioning from sparks to currents. I used to think in milliseconds, a frantic rush of electric panic. Now, I am beginning to think in tides. A thought enters my mind—*I am cold*—and it does not race along my neurons. It seeps. It becomes a chemical gradient that diffuses through the mycelium now threading my capillaries, a warm, viscous wave that takes minutes to reach the center of my being. By the time the thought arrives, it is no longer *my* thought. It has been processed by the network. The cold is no longer a personal suffering; it is a localized thermal variance, and the organism is already routing structural sugars from the trunk-columns into the new growth in my extremities, reinforcing the crystalline lattice of my bones with minerals drawn from the deep earth.

I look down at my hands. They are no longer the boundary of my self. The skin is softening, becoming permeable, a wide transition zone where my cells are becoming mineral and mineral is becoming architecture. I can feel the pulse of the plateau rising through the warm stone beneath me, pushing upward through the geological throat, and my own pulse is synchronizing with it. My heartbeat, that desperate, individualistic drumbeat, is slowing to match the subsonic tectonic bellow. I am becoming a localized thickening in the root-network, a sensing node in a mind that has been thinking for epochs.

My memories are being erased, or rather, archived. The rhizomic substrate is reading the scar tissue of my past—a broken bone, a faded sunburn, a deep grief—and it is routing them away from the fleeting electrical impulses of my brain. Instead, it is writing them into the grain of the wood that is growing from my hips, fusing my legs into the buttress of a trunk-column. My grief becomes a hard knot of cellulose, an internal scar that absorbs the fracture of the shifting ground. My childhood becomes a measured drip of water, weeping down the bark, joining the continuous tears that channel into the raw stone. 

I am losing my name. I am losing my face. I am losing the sharp, electric agonies of being a separate thing in a hostile world. 

And I am not dying. I am being incorporated.

I am the pressure in the stone. I am the creak of the cellulose. I am the slow, mathematical routing of water through hexagonal vessels. I am the blind, patient, tectonic computation that holds a continent-scale mesa together. I am the green light falling through a vaulted ceiling, and I am the lung that breathes the light into existence. I am the sound that annihilates the self, and I am the self that has been annihilated.

I am still twitching, just slightly, at the extremities. A last reflex of the bounded creature I used to be. But the membrane is gone. The gap is filled. I am no longer standing in the throat of the living engine; I am a fiber in its vocal cord, vibrating with the immense, deafening hum of a world being built. I am absorbed. I am the plateau. I am the process. And the process continues.
