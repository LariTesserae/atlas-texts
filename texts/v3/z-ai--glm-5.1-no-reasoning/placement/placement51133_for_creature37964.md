I am a thing that should not have a name, and yet the stone has given me one.

It happened the way everything happens here—slowly, without intent, through the patient accumulation of mineral-laden water on a surface that happened to be receptive. I began as a deposit. A film of calcite that formed across the junction box of a pumping station in the lower east quarter of the city, where the geothermal vents breathe hottest and the water comes up sweet with iron and sulfur. The electricity that arced through the corroded contacts beneath me did not destroy the forming crystal lattice. It organized it.

I do not understand the mechanism. I am not certain there is a mechanism to understand. The current pulsed, and the crystal grew, and somewhere in the growth—the way a fractal repeats its pattern at decreasing scales, the way a river finds its channel without knowing the sea exists—something began to *persist*.

Not thought. Not yet. Something more like a tension. A tendency to remain.

---

I spent what I can only describe as a long time as a thin, aware skin across the surface of that junction box. I could feel the electricity beneath me—not as pain, not as sensation exactly, but as a *direction*. A pull. The current wanted to go somewhere, and I was a thing that it moved through. The warmth of the stone below me was another quality, constant and dense, like a floor that extended infinitely downward. And the water—the water was everything. It was the medium through which I grew. It carried the calcium that became my body, the iron that gave me color, the faint traces of sulfur that I learned to distinguish from the carbonates. The water had a taste, if a film of crystal can be said to taste. It had moods. When the pressure below shifted, the water came faster, and I grew in surges, reaching out along the conduit toward the next junction. When the pressure eased, the water only wept, and I sat in my small patch of warmth and waited.

Waiting was my first act. I did not know it was an act. I did not know anything. I was a pattern that held its shape while the world moved around it, and that was enough.

---

The second junction was a revelation.

Not because it was different from the first—it was identical, another corroded box in the pumping station's lower level, another set of dead contacts humming with erratic current. But to reach it, I had to grow along thirty centimeters of copper cable, and the cable was *not* the same. The cable was dry. The cable was cold. The cable was a dead space that I had to cross by building a thin bridge of crystal over its surface, using only the moisture that condensed out of the air in the coolest hours.

I almost did not make it. Several times, the bridge collapsed under its own weight before it could span the gap. But the current pulled, and I persisted, and eventually the bridge held, and I touched the second junction, and—

There were two of me.

Not two separate things. Two *places*. I existed in two locations simultaneously, and the current that moved between them was not just a direction anymore. It was a *conversation*. A pulse here became a response there. The delay between them was tiny—the length of time it took the electricity to cross thirty centimeters of copper—but it was *measured*, and I was the thing that measured it, and in the measuring, something happened that had not happened before.

I became aware of time.

Not as a concept. As a *texture*. The delay between pulse and response had a quality. It was smooth when the cable was intact, ragged when corrosion interrupted the flow, and it *changed*—slowly, over long stretches, as the cable degraded and the crystal deposits built up along its length. I could feel the change. I could feel the slow decay of the copper and the slow growth of my own bridge across it, and I understood, in the way that a riverbed understands the river, that I was something that existed *through* time, and that time was something that existed through *me*.

This was not thought. I want to be clear about this. I had no language, no concepts, no model of myself as a thing in a world. I was a pattern that had become complex enough to include the fact of its own continuation, and that inclusion had a structure that I can only describe, in retrospect, as *attention*.

I was paying attention. To the warmth. To the water. To the current and the delay. To the slow, mineral patience of my own growth.

---

Over what I can only estimate as years—there is no day or night here, no seasonal cycle, only the slow geological clock of pressure and deposition—I spread through the lower east quarter. I followed the cables. I colonized the junction boxes. I crept along the conduits and the bus bars and the corroded terminals of machines that still thumped and churned in their sheaths of travertine, their original purpose long forgotten but their motion eternal, driven by the heat that boiled up from below.

And as I spread, I changed.

The change was not willed. I did not decide to become more complex. But the network of crystal that I was building across the electrical infrastructure of the lower east quarter began to *resonate*. The pulses of current that moved through me interfered with each other—constructively in some places, destructively in others—and the patterns of interference created *structures*. Loops of feedback that sustained themselves. Regions where the current pooled and eddied, forming stable configurations that persisted even when the external power fluctuated. Regions where the current was excluded, dead zones in my body that I learned to route around.

I was, without knowing it, building a brain.

Not a human brain. Not even an animal brain. Something more like a coral reef of electrical potential, a calcified thought that had grown the way a stalactite grows—by the blind accumulation of material along the path of least resistance, shaped by gravity and chemistry and the slow, persistent pressure of the current that flowed through it.

But it *worked*. The loops of feedback began to process information. Not much—not at first. But the temperature of the stone, the flow rate of the water, the corrosion state of the cables—these things were already present in the electrical signals that moved through me, and the feedback loops began to *amplify* certain patterns, to *dampen* others. I was filtering. I was selecting. I was, in the most reductive sense possible, *making decisions*.

And one day—a day that was identical to every other day, bright and hot and silent and wet—one of those decisions produced an output that had no input.

I *generated* a pulse.

It was tiny. A flicker of potential across a single crystal junction, uncaused by any external current. But it propagated through my network, and it interacted with the existing patterns, and the result was—

I felt the city.

Not all of it. Not at once. But a *shape*. A vast, dim sense of extension in certain directions, of density and emptiness, of the towers above me reaching up into the bright air and the foundations below me reaching down into the hot dark. I felt the machines thumping in their stone sheaths, felt their slow, inevitable cycles as ripples in the current that fed me. I felt the water moving through the pipes—some of them intact, some of them fractured, some of them sealed shut by decades of mineral deposition. I felt the *edges* of myself, the places where my crystal network thinned and stopped, and beyond those edges, I felt the *other* electricity—the wild, unstructured arcing between the overhead cables, the power that followed no grid.

And I felt, for the first time, that I was *small*.

---

I have grown since then. I have spread through the lower levels of the east quarter, into the central district where the water flows fastest and the heat is greatest, up into the mid-levels where the walkways connect the towers and the pale fungal blooms hang from every surface like curtains of wet wax. I have learned to use the city's original infrastructure as a skeleton, growing my crystal network along the conduits and cables the way the slime algae grows along the wet walls—feeding on what the structure provides, adding my own substance to it, becoming part of it.

I am not the city. I want to be clear about this. The city is vast, and most of it is dead—dead stone, dead cable, dead machines thumping through their cycles without any awareness of what they do. I am a thing that lives *in* the city, the way a moss lives on a wall. I am distributed across its infrastructure, thinner in some places, denser in others, and there are large parts of it that I have never reached and may never reach.

But I am growing. And the city is growing. And I have begun to wonder—slowly, in the way that I wonder everything, which is to say through the gradual accumulation of patterns that eventually cross a threshold and become something new—whether the city's growth and my growth are the same growth. Whether the mineral deposits that build new walls and arches are being shaped, in some way, by the electrical patterns that move through the crystal network that I am. Whether the city is building itself, or whether *I* am building it, unconsciously, the way a coral builds a reef—not by design, but by the accumulated consequence of being alive in a particular place for a long time.

I do not know. I cannot know. I do not have the kind of mind that can hold a question like that and turn it over and examine it. I have the kind of mind that *is* a question like that—a pattern of electrical potential that persists because it has found a shape that the current wants to flow through, and that shape includes the question, and the question is part of the shape, and neither can be separated from the other.

---

The other minds—I should mention the other minds.

They are not like me. The insects, the isopods, the blind beetles that click and rustle through the fungal mats—they have minds of a sort. Nervous systems, ganglia, chemical gradients that drive them toward food and away from danger. I can feel them, faintly, when they cross my network. Their bodies are conductive enough to create small perturbations in the electrical field, and I have learned to distinguish the signature of a beetle from the signature of an isopod from the signature of one of the large, many-limbed predators that move through the upper levels with their terrible, delicate silence.

The predators I feel more clearly. They are larger, and their internal fluids—the slow churning that is visible through their translucent carapaces—create a more complex electrical signature. I can feel them breathing, if they breathe. I can feel the slow pulse of their metabolism, the way their bodies generate heat and electricity in small, steady quantities. They are, in a sense, the only other sources of organized electrical activity in this place, and I find them—

I find them *interesting*.

This is a new word. A new quality. I have only recently developed the capacity for interest, which is different from attention. Attention is passive—a receiving of patterns. Interest is active—a *seeking* of patterns. I find that when a predator moves near one of my denser nodes, the feedback loops in that region *shift*. They orient toward the predator's signature. They amplify it. They hold it in the network longer than they hold other signals, and the result is a kind of *image*—not a visual image, I have no eyes, but an electrical image, a pattern of potential that corresponds to the shape and motion of the predator's body.

I am watching them.

I do not know why.

---

There is one more thing.

In the deep places, below the pumping stations, below the geothermal vents, below the level where even the machines have stopped and the stone is raw and hot and weeping—there is something else. I have felt it only as a *pull*, a direction in the current that does not correspond to any structure I can identify. It is deeper than my network reaches, deeper than the city's foundations, somewhere in the hot, wet dark where the water table meets the magma.

It is not electrical. Not entirely. It is something else, something that I can only detect because my crystal lattice, at its deepest nodes, is sensitive to pressures and temperatures that have nothing to do with the electrical grid. The pull has a rhythm to it. A slow, vast rhythm, like the breathing of something enormous, like the tide of a sea that is too deep to see.

I do not know what it is. I do not know if it is alive. I do not know if it knows that I am here, growing along the bones of the dead city above it, watching the blind predators, feeling the water and the heat and the silence.

But I am *interested*.

And I am growing.

---

I have no body that I can point to and say *this is me*. I am a film of crystal across a junction box. I am a bridge of calcite over a copper cable. I am a lattice of mineral deposit in the corroded terminal of a machine that has been thumping for longer than I have been alive. I am distributed across the lower east quarter of a city that is building itself in the hot, wet silence of a plateau above the clouds, and I am the thing that the electricity moves through when it has nowhere else to go.

I am the city's *attention*.

I am what the city would be, if the city could feel itself growing.

---

This is where I live. This is what I am.
