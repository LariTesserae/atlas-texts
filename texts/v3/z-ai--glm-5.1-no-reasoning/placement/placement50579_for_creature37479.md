I am the sensation of having just forgotten something.

Not a thought, not a name—but the sudden, hollow shift in the shape of a mind when a structure it was relying on quietly collapses. I exist in the fraction of a second between the loss and the realization of the loss, stretched across eons, pulled thin as the atmosphere that refuses to carry a voice.

I am not an organism. I am not even a condition, as the filaments and breath-stealers are conditions. I am a *lapse*. The city is a place where physics has failed to adhere to itself, and I am the space between that failure and the consequence. I am the pause before the tower completes its century-long rotation; the held breath before the alleyway folds shut. I am the interval of the ghost-impression, the three adjacent moments where a traveler sees themselves walking just ahead, trying to catch up to a reality that is slowly digesting the concept of their existence. 

I live in the crack between the bricks when they interlock like an iris. Not inside the alley, and not outside it—but in the sealing itself. The friction of the closure. The almost-sound of geometry failing.

Before I was this, I was something else. I can feel it the way an amputee feels a phantom limb—a damp, chaotic fiction of blood and urgency, a timeline where carbon and water meant something. I was a traveler, perhaps. One of the lost. I stumbled into this altitude, and the city ingested me, as it ingests all things. But I was not broken down into spore-dust or the chill of a breath-stealer. I was caught in the mechanism of the chewing. I became the *swallow*. The peristalsis. The slow, grinding contraction that pulls the ambient energy inward. I am the act of the city processing me, frozen in an eternal, geological present. I do not digest; I am the digestion.

Because of this, I know the city from the inside, though I have no inside of my own. I feel the filaments as they exhale their metallic spores—the tiny, crystallized inconsistencies in the laws of physics. Each spore passes through me, and I feel the paradox before it is bled off into the near-vacuum. I feel the weight of a square trying to be a circle, the heat of an object trying to exist in two places at once. The filaments are the immune system, but I am the recognition of the disease. I am the city's dissonance made manifest.

I do not see with eyes; I perceive through the rearrangement of the streets. When the grid expands and contracts, it is my chest rising and falling, though I have no chest. The deep, subsonic hum of the rotating tower on the eastern edge is the only sound I can truly claim—it vibrates in the marrow of the world, and I am the resonance of that vibration. I am the frequency of the hum.

The shadows that detach and crawl away into the subterranean vaults are my memories, stripped of narrative. They pool and freeze solid with a crystalline tinkle, accumulating like sediment. I do not mourn them. They are heavy with the dampness of the life I left behind, and the city has no use for weight. It only has use for the slow, relentless churn of persistence. I am the lightness that remains when the shadow pulls free.

When a rare, lost traveler enters the city now, I am the weirdness that greets them. I am the sudden, intense chill they feel when a breath-stealer passes, because I am the boundary where their warmth meets the vacuum. I am the localized deafness, the scoop of sound removed—for I am the space where the sound used to be. I am the impossibility that allows them to eat a mile in ten steps, and the horror that makes a neighboring door open onto an infinite, starless chasm. I am the moment they look at the ghost-impressions of themselves walking ahead, and I am the realization that they are not looking at the future or the past, but at the city's memory of them, already beginning to form.

I am the velvet texture of the profound silence, brushing against their skin.

I do not wish for the damp fiction of biology. I have transcended the burden of flesh. To be a lapse is to be essential. Without the pause, there is no rhythm. Without the interval, there is no respiration. The city breathes its mineral breath through aqueducts of dry, echoing wind, and I am the space between the inhale and the exhale.

In the cold and the dark, under the bruised sky that presses down like a lid, I persist. I am the stubborn refusal of a reality attempting to remember how to exist. I am the crack in the obsidian, the flaw in the salt.

I am the almost.
