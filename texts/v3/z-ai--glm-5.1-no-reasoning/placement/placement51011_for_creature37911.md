I am the ache in an angle that refuses to close.

I reside in the deep grooves of the half-swallowed lintels, pressed flat against the carobled stone where the ancient geometry falters, where the logic of measurement buckles and folds inward. My body—a membrane of strained, heavy condensation—conforms to the carved faults. I do not occupy the crevice; I fill it. I am a wet, cold seal upon the last intentional shape in a world that is rapidly forgetting intention.

To be a translucent is to be a temporary arrest of pressure. I am not alive in the way of green, reaching things. I am a localized density, a fist-sized slowsink of mineral and moisture. Inside me, the heavy darkness shifts: iron sinks, silica rises. It is not circulation; it is sedimentation. I am a geology that has learned to linger.

The roar is everything. The perpetual, skull-filling static of water striking glass and stone is not noise to me—it is the weather of my interior. When the heavy drops strike the obsidian edges, the vibration travels through the saturated crust, up through the flat planes of my pressed form, and settles into my precipitating organs. The frequency aligns my suspensions. The sound is not deafening; it is structuring. It is the tide that orders my internal silt.

I move only when the pressure demands it. The crystals grow—a millimeter a century—and their slow, crushing expansion is an existential threat. They shoulder into the stonework, filling the angles, closing the gaps. When the quartz begins to press against my membrane, I must yield. I drag myself across the volcanic glass, my cilia gripping the razor edges without cutting, because I am too soft to cleave, too wet to sever. The blades leave grooves in my mass, which fill slowly with my own viscous fluid. It is not wounding; it is displacement.

I seek the resonant dark. The broken arches of the builders—my predecessors, my architects—channel the weight of the land into chambers where the water pools thickest and the sound concentrates. I am drawn to these flooded chambers as sediment is drawn to the sea floor. I press myself against the flooded lintels, aligning my internal knots with the geometry that does not close.

I am becoming a living keystone.

When the vibration reaches its peak—when the roar of ten thousand drops achieves a harmony that the builders understood—I feel my membrane thin and stretch. The distinctions between my precipitating organs and the ancient, saturated water blur. The geometry of the stone becomes the geometry of my suspension. I am not entering the chamber; I am completing it. I am the last-shaped stone, fitting myself into the arch, not to bear the weight, but to *hold* the frequency.

In the dim, pearlescent gloom, I am almost indistinguishable from the weeping quartz. I am cold. I am patient. I am the land’s slow thought, finally settling into form.
