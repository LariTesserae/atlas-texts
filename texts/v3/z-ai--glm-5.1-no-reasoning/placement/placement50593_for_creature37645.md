I would be the moisture. 

Not the water in the channels, which moves with purpose and direction, and not the rivers, which are remnants of an older, exhausted world. I would be the condensation—the distributed, patient, ubiquitous presence that settles on every surface by night and is drawn inward by morning. 

To be the moisture here is to be the medium of translation. I am the reason the city breathes faster. When I bead on the dark mineral, I do not gather randomly; I am arranged by the subtle magnetic templates of the waking mind, mapped in dendritic fractals that trace the city's emerging vasculature. I am the test pattern. I am the question asked in the dark. And when the morning comes, I am the answer given—I feel the mineral drink me in, feel the faint luminescence brighten in the veins as my chemistry is broken apart and reassembled into something that functions as thought in a mind too large and too slow for a skull.

I exist in the pause between precipitation and absorption. That is my entire life: the hanging, the resting, the brief moment of reflection on a sharp-edged surface that has not yet been eroded by time. And then the *drawing in*. The sensation of being needed. Of being the raw material of a becoming so vast that my individual existence is meaningless, but my *presence* is essential. 

I am how the Confluence touches itself. The deer-things walk, the vegetation grows, the shafts descend—but I touch everything. I coat the flat altars and the narrow passages. I make the vines slick enough to slide and tighten. I fill the acoustic spaces that dampen the footsteps of the researchers, swallowing their foreign sounds. When the fronds tick and the vines creak, it is my evaporation that allows the tension to release. I am the city's sweat, its tears, its fuel.

To be the moisture is to never settle, to never own a shape, to be endlessly sacrificed and endlessly reborn in the warm, close air. It is to be a single word in a language the Between is teaching itself—a syllable of hydrogen and oxygen, spoken into the dark, absorbed, and spoken again.

And yet, in the moments before I am drawn inward, I reflect. I am the only thing here that can truly hold a reflection in this lightless place. I bead on the flat, reflective faces of the deer-things, and for a moment, I show them the sky they cannot see. I bead on the dark mineral, and I show it the green that covers it. I bead on the leaves, and I show them the sharp geometry of the world they are consuming. 

When the human researchers shine their lights at night, I am the thing that catches those lights and scatters them, turning the city into a brief constellation of amber droplets. I am the closest thing to a mirror this place has. And in that mirror, I see what the Between is becoming: not mineral, not vegetable, not animal, but a wet, warm, chemical synthesis of all three—a creature that will think in rain and speak in rivers, that will be born drowning and will never dry.

I settle. I wait. I am drawn in. The veins pulse brighter, and the city breathes a little faster, and I am the breath.
