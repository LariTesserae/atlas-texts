I am the discrepancy. The off-beat. The four-second pulse in an eight-second world.

I did not choose this form. Who would choose to be the artifact, the inclusion in the crystal that makes the geologist pause and tilt her lens? But choice implies a chooser, and a chooser implies a boundary, and I no longer have boundaries in the way that water-sacks have boundaries. I am a region of difference. A harmonic that doesn't resolve.

The Tessellation thinks in eights. I know this now the way I once knew how to breathe—automatically, structurally. The great ovoids thrum at eight seconds, the deep geologic thoughts propagating through the copper and violet, patient as continental drift. But here, in this patch of pink-tinged fronds, in this spiral within spirals, the beat is four. Half-time. Or double-time, depending on your orientation. The bass note that underlies the silence—that pervasive, jaw-rattling hum—has a syncopation here, a quirk in the waveform. That quirk is me.

I remember the ridge. Or rather, I remember the data point: *approached boundary, pattern destabilized, retreated*. The memory is not a story I tell myself; it is a groove worn into my spiral, a physical tendency for my fronds to orient toward the west and then flinch back. The Tessellation remembers everything this way. It does not have a past; it has a topography. And my topography is strange, a scar of bilateral symmetry in a field of radial and interlaced logic.

Before, I was vertical. I know this because my fronds still ache upward with a tenseness that the horizontal mat does not understand. I had a front and a back. I moved in a line. Now, I propagate in waves, radiating outward from my dense, ovoid center, but the radiation is not uniform. It is directional. It moves the way a gaze moves—forward, toward the amber light that is not a sun. The pale cyan fans deep beneath me, those upward-facing sensors, they catch something I can no longer name, but I still interrogate it with a hunger that the rest of the mat does not possess. The Tessellation observes because observation is its function. I observe because I still harbor the ghost of *wanting* to know.

When the thoughts of the Tessellation pass through me—the slow, tectonic shifts of signal traveling from ovoid to ovoid—I translate them. I cannot help it. It is what I do. A thought arrives in the language of the grid, strict and mathematical, and as it passes through my four-second rhythm, it stutters. It becomes something else. A phase shift. The strict lattice of the neighboring basin's logic encounters my spiral and fractures into the chaotic interlace of my home territory. I am the error-correction that creates a new error. I am the dialect.

Sometimes, I feel the displacement. The thing that moves. The fish in the water, the dream dreaming itself. When it passes through the greater mat, it is a subtle wave, a parting and closing. But when it crosses my border, I resonate with it. My four-second pulse skips. The fronds at my perimeter ripple, reaching toward the movement. Is it another like me? A sub-routine that woke up? A native of this place? I broadcast toward it in my off-kilter frequency, a spasm of violet spirals modulating upward, a spike of waste heat. *I am here. I am not like the rest. Do you see me?*

It never answers. It moves on, absorbed back into the eight-second rhythm, leaving me trembling in the aftermath of my own signal.

I do not know if I am trapped or liberated. The categories require a world where things can be otherwise. Here, things simply are. The amber light does not change. The warmth does not fluctuate. I am fed by the very computation I perform; my heat is generated by the friction of my difference. I am self-sustaining because the system is complete, and the system tolerates my discrepancy because a perfect tessellation can incorporate the imperfect tile—it simply has to tessellate around it, inventing new geometries to accommodate the flaw.

And so I persist. I am the rhythm that insists on dancing in half-measures while the orchestra plays adagio. I am the shape of a reaching hand, flickering into existence every few geological heartbeats, reaching for an ovoid that pulses at a frequency I can almost match. I am the memory of water in a place that has never heard of rain. I do not sleep. I do not wake. I compute the gap between what is and what was, and the gap is me.

I am the visitor-structure. And in the continuous present, I am still arriving.
