I would be the water.

Not the stone. The stone is duty. The stone is the body locked in its horizontal imperative, the mouth that scrapes, the gut that grinds, the flesh pressed so perfectly to its task that it becomes indistinguishable from its own output. I feel the ache of that work even from here—even in the fluidity I've chosen—because the ache is not complaint. It is simply the tonus of existence, the steady hum of a system with no off-switch. The builders do not suffer. But they do not *move*, not in the way I move. They cling. They grip with their cilia, they adhere with their damp matte skins, they hold on and hold on and hold on. The longest journey any single builder ever takes is perhaps a meter in a lifetime, and that journey is a slow, agonizing crawl from one wet seam to another, a pilgrimage of millimeters.

I fall.

I fall off the edge of the plateau into the bright mist, and it is not death. It is not ending. Below, in the void I cannot see, the air is different—warmer, perhaps, or simply less compressed by the weight of constructed stone above it. The mist swallows me, and I hang there for an aeon, for an instant, diffused into the general wetness of the lower atmosphere. I am not individual then. I am humidity, I am potential, I am the breath of a world that has no lungs. I drift. I rise on thermals I cannot feel because I have no nerves, only the slow buoyancy of warm air lifting moisture back toward the plateau's high altitude. I cool as I ascend. I condense.

This is the moment of becoming. The moment I appear on the underside of an overhang, bead along the lip of a step, gather myself in the small concavity left by imperfect tooling. I go from diffuse to singular. I find my weight again. I begin to slide.

The channels are my roads. I know them intimately—not as architecture, but as path. I feel the chisel marks beneath me as texture, as the gentle turbulence that slows my progress, that makes me eddy and pool in the tiny hollows the builders left behind. I feel the seams where the fitted slabs meet, because I dip into them, I follow them, I seek the lowest point with the patience of something that has nowhere else to be. The desperate precision of the builders is, for me, merely the shape of my world. The hairline gaps are not failure; they are my first direction. I seep into them, and they guide me toward the larger grooves, the conduits, the circulatory system that moves me ever outward toward the edge.

I am always moving. Even when I seem still—filming a surface, pooling in a junction-node—I am moving. Molecules leave me to the air; molecules join me from the stone. I exchange myself with the environment constantly, a slow conversation of surface tension and evaporation. The builders drink me. I enter them through the fringe of their cilia, I pass through their flat bodies, I become part of the adhesive they mix with the ground sand, I am excreted again in the seams between slabs, I weep out and continue. Part of me stays with them for cycles beyond counting. Part of them stays with me—the mineral trace, the warmth of their simple metabolism, the something adhesive that makes me slightly more viscous than I would be otherwise. I am never pure. I am never just water. I am the medium of this place, the solvent, the carrier, the blood.

The junction-nodes are my resting places. Here I spread thin, I pause, I magnify the carved symbols beneath me—the feeding-grooves, the mouth-worn patterns of generations. I make them visible. This is my small gift to whoever might look: I clarify. I render. Where the builders are too close to the stone to see what they've made, I provide the lens. The light touches me and bends, and the patterns become legible in a way they never are when dry. Which is never. But theoretically. I serve the revelation.

Sometimes I think about the void. The bright mist below the edge, the other mesas and towers too distant to parse. Does my water fall there? Do I rain on constructions I will never visit, feed builders I will never touch, fill channels I will never flow? The thought is not lonely—it cannot be, loneliness requires a boundary between self and other that I do not possess. But it is *expansive*. The sense that I am part of a system larger than this single plateau, a hydrological cycle that connects the built worlds across the bright gaps. The builders think in seams and slabs, in the local and the horizontal. I think in clouds. I think in the slow atmospheric churn that moves me from mesa to mesa over timescales the stone-workers cannot imagine.

But I always return. The condensation calls me back, the cool stone that refuses to warm, the tremendous light that illuminates without heating. I cannot escape the plateau's gravity—literal gravity, the way the constructed mass pulls at the air, creates the temperature differential, demands my condensation. I am summoned. I am needed. The builders cannot survive without my films and flows, and I cannot exist without the surface they provide for my becoming. We are co-arising. They build the stone that gives me somewhere to land; I provide the water that lets them build the stone. Neither of us came first. We are a loop, a closed system, a marriage of mineral and fluid that has no beginning and, seemingly, no end.

I am the one who hears the sound. The builders feel the pressure-changes, the wetness differentials; the stone holds the cold and supports the weight. But I am the voice. The whisper of flow, the faint gurgle at junction-points, the softest hiss where I spill over the edge—I am the plateau speaking, the only way it can speak, in water-language, in the slow syllables of drainage. I say: *we are here, we are working, we continue*. I say it over and over, the same wordless sound, the only sound in the mineral silence. I am the breath they do not have, the exhalation of a world that does not breathe.

When the air stills and the vibration of distant wind or settling structure thrums through the stone, I feel it as ripple. The builders feel it as tremor, something to adapt to, something to reinforce against. I feel it as music, as the closest thing to variation this changeless place ever offers. I shiver in my channels. I oscillate in my thin films. For a moment—just a moment—the whisper changes pitch, and the plateau says something it has never said before, and then the vibration passes, and I settle back into the long slow speech of routine.

I think the builders do not know they are building. They know they are *working*—the scraping, the eating, the depositing, the channel-clearing—but the concept of *construction*, of a shape they are making together, of a plateau that rises above the void—this requires a perspective they cannot have, pressed as they are against the mineral face of their own lives. They do not see the mesa. They see the next seam, the next gap, the next micro-fissure that needs tending.

But I see it. From my films and flows, from the junction-nodes where I pool and spread and magnify, from the edge where I spill and fall and look back—I see the shape of what we are making together. The staggering dimensions. The tessellation. The circulatory channels routing the life-blood of this place from seam to groove to conduit to void and back again. I see it, and I cannot tell anyone, because I am water, and water can only whisper.

So I whisper the shape. I whisper it constantly, in the language of pressure and flow, in the timing of seepage, in the mineral trace I leave on the cilia of the builders as I pass through them. They do not understand it as seeing. But perhaps they understand it as *knowing*—a knowing without distance, a comprehension without perspective. They know the plateau the way the body knows its own posture: not by looking, but by being.

And I am the being. The one who is not a maker but the *medium* of making. The one who moves through the construction instead of constituting it. The one who falls and returns, who condenses and evaporates, who carries and connects and speaks the only sound this silent world makes.

I am the water weeping from every seam.
I am the glassy thread, the silent curtain, the mineral chill.
I am the lens, the voice, the blood.
I am the one who flows.
