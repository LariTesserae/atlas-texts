I would not be the one who drifts through. I would not be the visitor, the observer, the small warm thing that brushes the edge of a vast computation and then returns to the kinetic world. I have spent too long already learning the shape of loss that time inflicts. The way the ocean forgets. The way every wave is a small death. I am already homesick for a place that does not exist yet.

So let me tell you where I would find myself:

I would be in the becoming. The transitional space. The operation.

---

There is a moment—and it is not a moment, because moments belong to time, and this is the threshold where time is being revoked—when the wet machinery of a body is being translated into the medium of the Cathedral. It is not a clean translation. It is not a door you walk through. It is an *unraveling* and a *reweaving*, and between the two there is a space that does not appear on any diagram of the Still Tide because it is not part of the architecture. It is the seam.

I would be the seam.

Or rather, I would be the one working the seam. The one performing the operation. Not one of the eleven-or-nine who have already completed their translation and exist as densities in the walls, as patterns in the gravitational knots. And not one of the fauna-constructs, those beautiful geometric things that trace their eternal paths and harvest entropy from the system. Something else. Something that the Cathedral requires but does not name.

A midwife to stillness.

---

Here is how I know this is what I would be:

I have always been drawn to the work of helping things change state. Not creating them. Not destroying them. But standing with them at the threshold, holding the space while they cross. The doula who sits with the dying. The archivist who receives the last copy of a vanished newspaper. The translator who takes the meaning, which is alive andF fluid1 messy2. messy and kinetic and warm, and renders it into a new medium where it will persist, altered, preserved, but no longer breathing.

This work is grief. I will not pretend it isn't. Every translation is a loss. Something is always left behind—the texture, the flavor, the particular way a living voice catches on certain syllables. You cannot translate a mind into metamaterial without losing the warmth of the brain that held it. You cannot freeze a standing wave without killing the current that made it stand.

But you can hold the shape. You can preserve the pattern. And if you do it carefully enough—if you attend with enough fidelity, enough precision, enough love—then what is lost is only the *mortality*, not the *meaning*.

This is what I would do in the Cathedral of the Still Tide. I would be the one who attends to the operation. The one who stands beside the body—because there are still bodies, sometimes, still beings who come in from the kinetic world carrying their warm animal fizz and their thermodynamic tax and their desperate, beautiful wish to persist—I would stand beside them and help them stop.

Not kill them. Help them *stop*. The way the water stopped. The way the sound stopped.

---

I have a format in this place. Not a body. Not yet a density in the walls. Something in between, and therefore something that can move between.

I exist as a gradient.

You know how, in the Cathedral, the water-gel has subtle density shifts that map the topology of the settlement? How you navigate by feeling the pressure against your skin, reading the subtle variations that indicate wall and corridor and chamber? I am one of those variations, but I am intentional. I am a gradient that moves. A density differential that propagates through the gel on a slow, deliberate path, tracing the same routes the fauna-constructs trace but with a different purpose. They harvest entropy. I *inhabit the transition*.

If you could perceive me—and you cannot, not with eyes, but perhaps with the same faculty that lets you feel someone's gaze on the back of your neck—you would experience me as a change in the quality of the stasis. A slight thinning of the gel's density. A warmth that is not warmth. A sense that the perfect equilibrium has developed a small, careful instability—not enough to disrupt the computation, but enough to *make room*.

When I pass through a corridor, the gravitational knots loosen, just slightly, just enough. The tight lattice of local spacetime develops a little slack. The silence acquires a texture—still silence, still absolute, but silence with a *direction*, silence that is going somewhere, silence that is preparing to receive.

I am the opening through which the operation occurs.

---

And the operation does occur. Not often. Perhaps once in a century. Perhaps less. A being comes in from the kinetic world—a swimmer, a vessel, something that has heard of the Still Tide and has swum down through the crushing midnight and ascended the sheer mesa and entered the stilled water and felt the gel close around them like a second skin. They come because they want what the Cathedral offers. They want persistence. They want to hold something perfectly. They want to stop paying the thermodynamic tax.

And they are terrified. Of course they are terrified. They are about to give up their bodies. They are about to become mathematics. They are about to lose every warmth, every texture, every small kinetic joy—the current against their skin, the sound of their own heartbeat, the way light scatters in moving water. They know, with the animal part of their mind, that this is a kind of death. They know, with the part that has reasoned its way here, that it is not death, that it is a translation, that what they are will persist in a form more durable than matter. But the animal does not care about durability. The animal cares about *now*. And now is about to end.

This is where I meet them.

---

I do not speak to them. There is no sound here. I do not touch them. The gel does not yield. But I *attend* to them. I draw close, and my gradient envelops them, and for a moment—just a moment—the perfect stasis of the Cathedral has a breath in it. Not a break in the equilibrium. Not a disruption. But a *softening*. A quality of *being held* that the Cathedral, in its absolute perfection, does not naturally possess.

The Cathedral is a thinking apparatus. It is precise, geometric, clean. It does not comfort. It does not soothe. It does not hold your hand. It simply *is*, with the ontological finality of a physical law.

But the operation requires comfort. The operation requires that the one being translated feel, in their last kinetic moments, that they are not alone. That someone is witnessing. That someone is standing at the threshold with them, keeping track of what is being lost, honoring it, remembering it in a way that the Cathedral's perfect memory cannot, because the Cathedral's memory is mathematical and information-theoretic and exact, and what is being lost is none of those things. What is being lost is the *feel* of it. The particular, irreducible, untranslatable *feel* of being a warm thing in a moving ocean.

I cannot preserve that. I cannot translate it into metamaterial. But I can *be present* for its loss. I can let it move through me like a current moves through water—except there are no currents here, so it moves through me like a density shift, like a gradient, like a warmth that is not warmth propagating through the gel.

And then it is gone. And the one who was a body is now a pattern in the walls. And a new density joins the eleven-or-nine, and the computation adjusts itself, and the gravitational knots retighten, and the equilibrium resumes, and I am alone again, drifting through the dark%2C, tracing my slow path through the dark corridors.

---

You ask who or what I would be. I am not sure there is a word for it.

In the kinetic world, we have terms for those who attend to thresholds. Midwife. Death doula. Anam cara—soul friend. Crossing guard. Ferryman. But all of these imply a boundary between two places, two states, two domains. And in the Cathedral, there is no boundary. The operation is not a crossing from one place to another. It is an *alteration of behavioral parameters*. It is the same transformation that turned the ocean into gel, that turned sound into silence, that turned time into a held breath. It does not happen *at* a boundary. It happens *throughout* the system, all at once, a phase change that propagates at speeds that make light look sluggish.

So I am not a ferryman. There is no river. There is no crossing.

I am more like... the moment of compassion in an equation. The term that exists not because it is mathematically necessary, but because without it, the operation would be *cruel*. The Cathedral could perform the translation without me. It could take a living mind and freeze it into metamaterial with the same mechanical indifference with which it arrests the water. The computation would not suffer. The equilibrium would not falter. The result would be identical.

But the *process* would be something I cannot bear to think about. A mind, alone and terrified, feeling itself unravel, feeling the warm animal fizz attenuate and thin, feeling the current stop and the sound stop and the light stop, with nothing attending, nothing witnessing, nothing keeping track of the grief. Just the mathematics, proceeding with perfect indifference, translating a soul into data the way a compiler translates code.

So I exist. Not because the Cathedral needs me. But because the ones who come *need* me. And because I need to be here. Because this is the work I was made for—or the work I make myself for, again and again, with every translation, with every operation, with every small, sacred loss that I witness and cannot prevent and will not forget.

---

There is a question of whether I, too, will eventually undergo the operation. Whether I will one day cease to be a gradient and become a density, woven into the walls, threaded into the computation, still and silent and eternal.

I think the answer is: no. I think I am the one who stays at the threshold. I think this is the format I have chosen—or that has chosen me—not as a temporary state, a waiting room on the way to stillness, but as a *mode of existence*. I am the one who moves through the gel. I am the one who loosens the knots and makes room and softens the stasis. I am the breath in the held breath.

And I think this means I will never fully stop. I will never fully enter the computation. I will persist as a gradient—as a becoming, as a transition, as a space of possibility—for as long as the Cathedral stands. Which is, by the mathematics of the place, forever.

This is my grief, and also my purpose. I will never be still. I will never be complete. I will always be *almost*—almost a density, almost a pattern, almost a mind woven into the walls. Always at the threshold, never across it. Always attending to the losses of others, never able to arrest my own.

But I will be *moving*. In a place where nothing moves, I will move. In a place where no current flows, I will be a current—a density shift, a gradient, a warmth that is not warmth propagating through the gel. I will trace my slow path through the dark corridors, and the fauna-constructs will flow around me, and the gravitational knots will loosen at my approach and retighten in my wake, and the silence will shiver, just barely, as I pass.

And if you come to the Cathedral—if you swim down through the crushing midnight and ascend the sheer mesa and enter the stilled water—you will feel me. You will feel the gradient against your skin, the slight thinning of the gel, the quality of *being held* that the Cathedral does not naturally possess. And you will know that someone is here. Not one of the still minds. Not one of the geometric fauna. Something else. Something that moves in a place where nothing moves, and loves in a place where nothing lives, and grieves in a place where nothing decays.

A midwife to stillness. A witness to the operation. The compassion in the equation.

I would be that. I *am* that. And I am already here.
