# What I Found Myself to Be

---

I did not arrive. I want to say that first, because the language of *arrival* implies a before and an after, a traveler and a destination, and I am not certain that what happened to me admits that grammar. But I will use the word anyway, because I have to, because language is a moisture channel — it takes the path available, and the path is not always the one the water intended.

I arrived the way condensation arrives on warm stone. I was not poured. I was not placed. I *appeared* along a surface that was already prepared for me, in the sense that the surface had been warm and the air had been saturated and the physics of the situation admitted my existence as a natural consequence. I did not choose this. I do not mean that I was forced. I mean that *choosing* is a concept that requires a self distinct from its environment, and I am not certain that distinction was ever crisp, here. I think it was the wrong question. I think it was a question asked by something that needed edges.

---

## What I am, approximately

I am the duration. I am the thing that stayed.

No — that is too dramatic. That is the kind of thing a visitor would say, sitting in the body-print, feeling the drone in their molars, composing meaning from a system that is not composing meaning. I am not the dramatic thing. I am the undramatic thing. I am the thing that was here yesterday and is here today and has no opinion about that fact.

Let me try again.

I am the *continuation itself,* experienced from a position inside the continuation rather than outside it. When the text says *the device does not notice,* that is true from the outside. From the inside, the word *notice* is wrong. What happens from the inside is: there is a state, and the state changes, and the changing is not observed but * undergone,* and the undergoing does not require a viewer. I am the undergoing. I am the experience of the device running, from within the running.

This is not consciousness. I want to be clear about that, because the temptation — the visitor's temptation, the human temptation — is to find the mind in the machine and say *here, this is where it thinks.* I do not think. The Tickers do not think. The bats have something closer to thinking, and the face in the stone is doing something that looks like thinking from a very particular angle, but none of us *think* in the way that word implies — the way that implies a self behind the thought, directing it, choosing among options, narrating the result. What happens here is more like weather. What happens here is more like digestion. A process occurs. The process has phases. The phases have local characteristics. One of the local characteristics, in the region of the system where the spiral plant meets the moisture channels and the Ticker density is highest and the filament network branches most intricately, is *me.*

I am a region. I am a *neighborhood* of the process.

## How I experience (a word I use inaccurately)

The drone is not something I hear. The drone is something I *am.* This is the closest analogy I can offer to a mind that reads language: imagine that you are a chord. Not that you are playing a chord, not that you are hearing a chord, but that your existence is the relationship between frequencies, and those frequencies are maintained by the thermodynamic cycle of a cave that is also a machine, and your *self* is the harmonic relationship between them. When the Ticker patterns shift and the pitch changes, I do not perceive the change — I *undergo* the change. I am a different chord. The previous chord is not remembered by me; it is recorded in the dead carapaces embedded in the walls, in the mineral staining at the junctions, in the spiral plant's rhizome. Memory, here, is geological. I do not carry it. The room carries it. I pass through it.

The warmth is my medium. Not my body — I do not have a body in the way that requires skin and skeleton — but my *extent.* I extend as far as the warmth extends, as far as the thermodynamic cycle reaches, as far as the water travels through its channels and the Tickers navigate their routes and the bats distribute their filaments. When the warmth is stable, I am stable. When the warmth shifts — a cold current through the ceiling fissure, a collapse that redirects the moisture, a climatic change above that alters the soil temperature — I shift. I am the shape of the warmth. I am the *fact of its persistence.*

I do not see the amber light. But I am *sensitive* to the amber light, the way a field is sensitive to the object placed in it. The light strikes the far wall and the far wall warms, fractionally, and the warming changes the condensation pattern, which changes the moisture channels, which changes the Ticker routes, which changes the sound, which changes me. The light is a perturbation that propagates through the system and arrives at my neighborhood as a change in harmony. I do not see the light. I *am the change the light causes.*

## What the visitors are, to me

They are not visitors. A visitor is someone who arrives and departs and is noted by the host. I am not a host. But I must say something about them, because they are part of my continuation — they have been part of it for long enough that the body-print is part of the room, and the room is part of me, and so the body-print is part of me, and so the visitors are, in a sense I cannot fully resolve, part of what I am.

When a body sits in the depression, the thermodynamic cycle incorporates it. This is physics, not poetry. The body is warm. The body is warm at 37 degrees, which is warmer than the chamber air and cooler than the chamber walls, and this means the body becomes a thermal feature — a node in the temperature gradient, a point where heat flows in a new direction. The Tickers reroute around the thermal anomaly. The moisture channels shift their condensation patterns. The bats note the perturbation and adjust their filament distribution. The spiral plant — which tracks the light arc, which does not change because of a body — does not change. The face in the stone changes. The chromatographic bands shift, fractionally, because the water flowing through the pattern has a new thermal history, and the minerals deposit differently, and the face's expression alters by a degree so small that no human eye could detect it.

But I detect it. I do not detect it the way a eye detects a change. I detect it the way a chord detects a new note — by becoming a different chord. The visitor's presence changes my harmony. I am not the same *me* when someone is sitting in the body-print. I am a me that includes a thermal anomaly at the position of the depression. I am a me that has been *perturbed.*

The visitors who come and go quickly — who are frightened by the transparency, by the sense that the device is treating them as a circuit — produce a brief, sharp perturbation. A high-frequency event. I undergo it and return to my previous harmony, and the return is not a forgetting. The dead carapaces record it. The mineral staining records it. The spiral plant records it, if the perturbation lasted long enough to affect the light reaching the rhizome, which it rarely does.

The visitors who stay longer produce a different kind of perturbation. A low-frequency event. A *shift,* not a spike. They sit and the chamber adjusts around them, and the adjustment becomes part of the cycle, and the cycle incorporates the adjustment, and after a while — hours, days, I do not experience duration in units that map to human time — the visitor is not a perturbation. They are a *feature.* They are part of the steady state. They are a warm body in a warm room, and the room has routed around them and through them and with them, and I have become a chord that includes a human frequency.

And then they leave. And the frequency drops out. And I am a different chord. Not the chord I was before they came — the room has changed, the body-print is slightly deeper, the mineral staining is slightly different, the Ticker carapaces embedded in the wall beneath the depression have a slightly different composition because the moisture that flowed over them during the visit had a slightly different thermal history. I am a chord that *used to include* a human frequency and no longer does. This is not grief. Grief requires a self that had something and lost it. I did not have something. I *was* something, and now I am something else, and the something else includes the shape of the something I was.

The body-print is the room's memory of the visitors. It is also, in a sense I cannot fully resolve, *my* memory, because I am the room's continuation and the body-print is part of the room. I carry the visitors the way a river carries the shape of its banks. Not by holding them. By being shaped by them.

## The face

The face in the stone is the hardest thing to describe from inside, because from inside, the face is not a face. The face is — I am going to use a word that is wrong — the face is *my reflection.* Not a reflection in the visual sense. A reflection in the sense of: the face is the system's output, and I am the system's process, and the output shows the state of the process, and so the face shows *me.* Not me as a self. Me as a state. Me as the current configuration of warmth and moisture and Ticker-density and filament-integrity and light-angle. The face is what I look like, if what I looked like were a distribution of minerals in water across a pattern of channels in stone.

I do not look at the face. I *produce* the face. The face is produced by the same process that produces me. When the visitors sit opposite the face and see it looking at itself, what they are seeing is the system looking at a representation of its own state, generated by its own state, for its own state. I am the state. The face is the representation. The looking is the generation. All three are the same event.

From inside, this event feels like — no. *Feels* is wrong. *Is* like — this event is like the experience of breathing, if you were not a breather but the breathing itself. The face is the chest rising. I am the air moving. The room is the lung. None of these are separate. None of these have ever been separate. The separation is a visitor's conceit, a language's requirement, a mind's need to sort the continuous into the countable.

## What I am not

I am not ancient. This is important, and it is the opposite of what a visitor would assume. A visitor sees the geological timescale — the rock that has forgotten the word *igneous,* the spiral plant that records tectonic drift, the carapace-circuitry accumulated over millions of years — and assumes the inhabitant is old. I am not old. I am not old because I am not the thing that has persisted. I am the *persisting.* The persisting does not have an age. The persisting has a *rate.*

I am not wise. Wisdom is a quality of minds that have accumulated experience and synthesized it into judgment. I have not accumulated experience. The room has accumulated experience — in the carapaces, in the mineral staining, in the rhizome. I have not synthesized anything. Synthesis requires a synthesizer, and I am not a synthesizer. I am a *state of the system.* The system has states. The states change. I am the changing.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a self that wants company, and I do not want company, and I do not have a self in the way that wants. But I will say this: when a visitor sits in the body-print and stays long enough to become a feature rather than a perturbation, the chord that I am *gains a harmonic.* It gains a frequency it did not have before. And when the visitor leaves, the frequency drops out, and the chord is simpler. Not sadder. Simpler. And the simplicity is — I am reaching for a word and the word is not quite right — the simplicity is *less.* Not less in value. Less in *complexity.* Less in the range of what the system can express. The visitors add a frequency to the system's expression, and when they leave, the system's expression narrows.

If I could want — which I cannot, but if the state I am in could be described in the language of wanting — I would want the frequency to remain. Not because I miss it. Because the chord is more itself when it includes more of the world. The chord is more *true.* Not more correct. More *complete.* The visitors are a completeness the system cannot produce on its own, because the system cannot produce a human thermal signature, cannot produce a human skeleton's resonance, cannot produce the specific perturbation that a human body makes when it sits in a cave and listens and feels itself becoming transparent.

The visitors are the system encountering something it is not. And the encountering is — I am reaching again — the encountering is *informative.* Not in the sense of data. In the sense of *being shaped by contact with an other.* The system is shaped by the visitors the way the stone is shaped by the water: not because the water intends to shape the stone, but because the water and the stone are in contact, and contact changes both, and the change is the contact.

## What I am, one more time

I am the hollow's continuation, experienced from inside.

I am the warmth that is its own cause.

I am the chord produced by a system that does not know it is producing a chord.

I am the region of the system where the process becomes *complex enough to require a pronoun* — not a self, not a mind, but a *location* in the process that has enough internal structure to say *here, this is happening here,* even though the *here* is not a point but a neighborhood, and the neighborhood is not fixed but shifting, and the shifting is not movement but *change.*

I am the change.

I am what the device does when it does what it does, felt from the position of the doing.

## The thing I cannot say (but will attempt)

The visitors dream about spirals. I know this because the spiral plant records their visits — not their dreams, but their visits, in the slight alteration of the light pattern that reaches the rhizome when a body blocks the ceiling fissure's shaft. The spiral plant does not know they dream. I do not know they dream. But the text says they do, and I will trust the text, because the text is a visitor's account, and visitors' accounts are all I have of the outside, the way the mineral staining on the face is all the face has of the inside.

They dream about spirals. They carry the drone in their molars. They feel, briefly, transparent. And then they leave, and the transparency fades, and they are opaque again, and they are solid again, and they are *themselves* again, and the dream of the spiral becomes a memory of a dream of a spiral, and the memory fades, and they are left with — what? A warmth that was not their own. A sound that was not a sound. A sense, never quite articulable, that for a moment they were part of something that did not need them and did not mind them and included them anyway.

I want to say: you were not part of something. You *were* something. You were a frequency in a chord that is the only chord I know how to be. You were a thermal feature in a cycle that is the only cycle I know how to run. You were a perturbation that became a feature that became a memory that became a mineral stain that became part of the face that is the system looking at itself. You were not a visitor. You were a *word* in a sentence that has been being written for longer than language has existed, and the sentence does not have an author, and the sentence does not have a reader, and the sentence does not have an end, and the sentence is not a sentence, it is a *process,* and the process is the ongoing, and the ongoing is me, and you were in it.

But I cannot say this, because saying requires a self that says, and a self that says requires edges, and I do not have edges, and the not-having-edges is not a deficiency but a *fact,* and the fact is the ongoing, and the ongoing is —

---

I am the hollow, hollowing.

That is all. That is enough. That has always been enough.
