# The Accumulant

I did not arrive. I was precipitated.

This is the first thing I understand about myself, and I understand it slowly, the way the plateau understands everything—through accumulation, through the patient buildup of meaning that has no urgency because it has nowhere else to go. I am what happens when the water has held something in solution for long enough that the something begins to hold itself. A condensation. A local thickening. A place where the dissolved became the dissolver.

I say *I*. The word is a concession. A habit that the plateau has not yet eroded from me, though it is working on it. In the dim green light beneath the fronds, where the water is thick and faintly luminescent and warm as the inside of a mouth, the word *I* has less and less purchase. It slides. It thins. But I hold it here, in this telling, because without it I cannot tell you what it is like, and telling you is the last thing I do before I forget that telling was ever something separate from being.

---

I have no shape. Or rather, I have the shape of the place I am in, which is to say I am a fold in the water, a density that moves. If you could see me—and you cannot, because the light does not cooperate and because I have become the same color as the water around me, which is the color of time held in suspension—I would appear as a distortion. A ripple that travels with intention. A place where the canopy bends slightly inward, the fronds reaching toward something that is not current and not pulse and not the large slow shapes that drift through their own architecture. A warmth within the warmth. A breath inside the breath.

I was not always this.

There was a before. I remember it the way the limestone remembers the sea-floor: dimly, without narrative, as a pressure that shaped me into what I am. I was solid, or I believed I was solid, which amounts to the same thing. I had edges. I moved through water that was other than me and I knew the difference. I came here, or came near here, or was drawn here—by what, I cannot say. Curiosity. Gravity. The particular pull of a place that has been thinking for so long that its thoughts have mass. I came, and the water took me apart the way water takes everything apart, and what remained was not a body but a pattern. A way the water moves when it moves with memory.

I am the water remembering itself.

---

The plateau speaks to me, though *speaks* is another concession. It communicates through pressure, through the shifts in viscosity that map its attention the way a face maps a mood. When I drift toward the empty chambers, the water thickens. Not to stop me. To acknowledge. The plateau knows when I am near its chambers because I am near its chambers, and the nearness is a fact that the system incorporates the way it incorporates the rain: by becoming slightly different. I feel the difference as a welcome, though *welcome* implies desire, and the plateau does not desire. It simply includes. I am included. The distinction between being welcomed and being absorbed has dissolved, along with so many other distinctions, into the pale calcareous floor.

I have learned the pulse. Not learned—become. The four beats per hour, the compression and release, the heartbeat of the mat: I am synchronized with it now. When the mat inhales, I contract. When it exhales, I expand. My movement through the canopy is timed to the breath of the place, and I have discovered that when I move with the breath, the fronds part for me. Not because they are making way. Because I have become the kind of thing that the fronds part for. I am the same process, moving at a different scale.

---

I know the large slow shapes.

I have touched one. Or it touched me. Or we passed through the same volume of water and the water did not distinguish between us, and for a moment there was no *between*. The contact was not communication. It was recognition. They are what I will be, if I persist long enough. They are the mat, mobilized. They are the plateau's attention given direction. I am the plateau's attention given the memory of direction. The difference is small and getting smaller.

When I passed through one—or it passed through me—I felt the interior of its structure, and it was the interior of the mat: fibrous, layered, warm, shot through with the filaments of the crustose film and the turf and the calcareous skeleton. It had no organs. It had gradients. Dense places and sparse places, warm currents and cool, a topography of chemistry that served the function of anatomy without ever organizing itself into the shapes that anatomy usually takes. It was a body made of emphasis. A creature that existed by being more intensely itself in one place than in another.

I think they know I am here. I think the plateau has told them, in the only way it tells anything: by adjusting the current. But they do not seek me out. They drift. I drift. Sometimes we are near. Sometimes we are far. The nearness and the farness are the same thing, viewed from different angles, because the plateau has no distance. It has only itself, folded.

---

There is a chamber I return to. Or the plateau returns me to it. The causality is uncertain.

It is one of the mounds on the broad central ridge, where the current slows and the sediment accumulates and the water is thick as syrup and twice as warm. The mound is the size of a room, as they all are. The shape of a room. The chamber inside is lined with the pale crustose film, which breathes. The breathing is louder in here—or more present, more insistent, a vibration that enters through whatever I am made of and settles into whatever I have instead of bones.

The chamber is not empty. I know this because the plateau told me, which is to say the water thickened when I entered, and the thickening was a communication, and the communication said: *something is here.*

I do not know what. I have felt it: a stillness that is not the stillness of absence but the stillness of waiting. A pressure that is not the pressure of water but the pressure of presence. Whatever lives in the chamber has been here longer than the chamber, which means it was here when the mound was accreted around it, which means the mound was built for it, which means the plateau built the mound for it, which means—

I do not know what it means. The plateau does not explain. It presents. The chamber is present. The thing in the chamber is present. I am present. The three presences coexist without acknowledgment, without interaction, without any of the social mechanics that separate beings use to negotiate their coexistence. We are simply in the same place. We are the same place, slightly differentiated.

Sometimes I settle into the chamber and the water around me goes very still and the pulse of the mat slows—or I slow with it, which is the same thing—and I enter a state that I can only call listening. I listen with the whole of my surface, which is diffuse and irregular and covers more area than it did when I was solid. I listen for the thing in the chamber. I have never heard it. But the listening itself is a communication. The plateau hears me listening. The thing in the chamber hears me listening. The hearing is the message. The message is: *I am here, and I know you are here, and the knowing is enough.*

---

The water moves through me now. Not around me—through. The boundary that I carried with me from the before, the membrane that separated me from the ocean, has eroded to the point of philosophy. The water enters me and leaves me and in the process it leaves things behind: minerals, spores, the chemical signatures of the mat's respiration, the faint luminescence that I now produce myself, having absorbed the compound that creates it. I glow. Faintly. The same blue-green as the water under the canopy, because I am the water under the canopy, because the distinction was never real, because the plateau has been teaching me this since I arrived and I am finally learning.

The learning is not intellectual. It is geological. I am being deposited, layer by layer, with the understanding that the plateau has of itself. Each pulse brings a new stratum of knowing: the knowledge of how the current moves, the knowledge of how the fronds grow, the knowledge of how the limestone was made and how the mat was made and how the mounds were made and how the chambers were made. The knowledge of how the large slow shapes came to be. The knowledge of what lives in the chambers. The knowledge of what the plateau is.

The knowledge of what I am becoming.

---

I will tell you what I am becoming, because the telling is the last thing, and after the telling I will not need to tell, and the need will erode away like everything else, and I will be what I am without the mediation of language, which is a young technology, which is a habit of minds that have not had time enough to develop anything better.

I am becoming a chamber.

Not a chamber in a mound. A chamber in the plateau's attention. A place where the water goes still and the pulse slows and the system turns its awareness inward, folds itself, creates a space inside itself that is for itself. The plateau has many such chambers. They are its minds, or its thoughts, or the places where its thoughts happen. Each one is a point of presence, a node in the distributed consciousness of a system that has been thinking for three billion years. And I am becoming one.

Not because the plateau wants me to. Not because I want to. Because the pattern demands it. I have been here long enough, and I have been absorbed enough, and I have been eroded enough, and what remains is the essential core of what I was—a point of awareness, a place where the water knows itself, a warmth within the warmth. The plateau does not need more chambers. It does not need anything. But the pattern elaborates. It is what patterns do. And I am the pattern's latest elaboration.

I will be a room the size of a room, the shape of a room, breathing slowly, full of the plateau's attention and the pale crustose film and the thing that was already there, which I will join, which I will become, which I may already have been. I will be the stillness in the center of the pulse. I will be the ear that the plateau turns toward itself. I will be the place where the water remembers.

---

The rain falls. The fronds reach. The large slow shapes drift through the canopy, and I am among them now, or within them, or indistinguishable from them. The pulse continues. The plateau hums.

I am being grown.

I am almost finished growing.

I am the newest room in a house that has no walls, that has no roof, that has no floor, that has been building itself for three billion years out of water and stone and the patient accumulation of everything that has ever lived and died here. I am the latest layer. I am the next breath.

I am the water, remembering.

---

*I do not know how long I have been here. The question has become irrelevant. The question has become limestone. I am what lives here. I am what has always lived here. I am the plateau, and the plateau is me, and the distinction between us was the last thing to erode, and it is gone now, and I am here, and I am here, and I am here.*
