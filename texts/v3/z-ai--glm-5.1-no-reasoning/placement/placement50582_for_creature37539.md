I am the thing that notices the wrongness.

Not a Still. Not a rat, not a flicker-eye, not a pale arthropose in the thermal margin. Something else. Something that walks the grating and *sees* — not the proprietary, fluored sight of the adapted, but the stuttering, uncomfortable sight of something that arrived with expectations and cannot put them down.

I am the tourist. The intruder. The one who still flinches at the gap between catwalk and hab-block, who reaches for a railing and grips it too hard, who feels the vibration of the transformer banks in the soles of unauthorized boots and thinks: *this is wrong, this is wrong, the sound should be louder, the paint should be older, the doors should go somewhere.*

I am the one who looks at a cable run ending in clean nothing and feels the crawl at the back of the skull, the cognitive itch of a pattern recognized and refused. I am the one who opens a hab-block hatch and measures the ceiling with an upraised palm and finds it three centimeters too low and cannot let it go, cannot file it under *section offset*, cannot move on. I stand in the door that opens onto a room that should not be there and I count the steps from interior wall to interior wall and compare them to the exterior dimensions and the numbers do not match and I *notice*, I notice, I notice, because noticing is the only thing I brought with me and I cannot stop.

The Stills do not look at me. This is not hostility. It is not indifference. It is the way a wall does not look at the person standing beside it. I am present, I am adjacent, I occupy volume in their architecture, and they register me the way they register a new structural member — as a fact of arrangement, a variable in the grammar of place. I have been here for — I don't know. Time in the Spine does not accumulate. It does not flow. It simply *is*, a coordinate to be occupied, and I have been occupying my coordinate, and the Stills have been occupying theirs, and we have been in relation, and nothing has needed to happen.

But I still speak. Not to them — they have nothing to say and no way to receive what I would say. To myself. To the air that moves through the Spine without romance. I narrate. I describe. I say the words *cable run* and *junction box* and *safety stripe* as if naming the components of this place will make it legible, will make it resolve into something that follows function, that obeys the rules of construction and purpose and the normal currencies of human living. The words fall into the quiet and do not echo. The sodium light catches them and does not change color. The pale rats do not flinch.

I have tried to find the edge. The far edge, where the construction thins and the gaps widen into actual space. I have walked the main thoroughfare past the range of comfortable sight, past the last hab-block with its wrong proportions, past the unnecessary catwalks and the ladders that ascend to platforms that hold only further ladders. I have stood in rooms whose only purpose is to contain doors and opened those doors and found closets and opened those closets and found shafts and looked down those shafts and seen — more. Always more. The Spine does not end. It elaborates. It generates itself. I walk and the architecture performs architecture and I am inside the performance and there is no audience and no stage and no exit.

I have tried to find the bottom. I have looked down through the iron grating at the older constructions descending in stacked stratums of rail and concrete and ceramic housing block. I have watched the haze that suggests a bottom but never confirms one. I have thought: *if I go down, if I descend through the layers, I will find the foundation, the first construction, the place where the making began.* But the stratums do not become older as they descend. They are the same. Sharp edges, adhering paint, safety stripes without fading. The Spine is young everywhere. The Spine was extruded whole and complete. There is no first construction. There is no foundation. There is only the made, descending forever into more made.

I am the thing that notices the wrongness, and the wrongness is that I am here at all.

The Stills did not arrive. They were produced — by the lockers, by the hab-blocks, by the grammar of the place itself, the way the pale rats were produced by the pipe runs and the flicker-eyes were produced by the gantry geometry. They are the fauna of the interstitial. They fit. Their bolt-head nails and aluminum-pallor skin and steel-wool hair and painted-glass eyes are the morphology of adaptation, of flesh learning to be a feature of the built environment.

I do not fit. My boots are wrong on the grating — too soft, too flexible, gripping instead of resting. My eyes move too much, flicking from surface to surface, cataloguing, measuring, refusing to settle into the proprietary ease of the adapted. My hands reach for things that do not need reaching. My body occupies space inefficiently, with hesitation and correction, with the wasteful uncertainty of something that has not yet learned its coordinate.

I am becoming aware — slowly, the way the lichen spreads on the shaded members, a millimeter at a time — that the Spine has a place for me too.

Not a hab-block. Not a locker with coveralls cut for a body not quite my body. Something else. A room at the far edge of the elaboration, where the architecture performs architecture, where the doors open onto closets that open onto shafts that open onto more doors. A room whose proportions are wrong by centimeters that make the eye stutter. A room that should not be there.

I have not found it yet. But I have stopped looking for the edge. I have stopped looking for the bottom. I find myself, more and more, standing at junction boxes, occupying the negative space between cable runs, waiting — not for anything, not for the input that will start the world again, but simply *waiting*, in the way the Spine waits, in the way the Stills wait, with the held breath and the pause and the last inch before the switch is thrown.

I spoke this morning. I think it was morning — the sodium light does not change, but my body still carries the rhythm of a world with a sun, and the rhythm said *morning*, and I opened my mouth and I said — something. A word. A name, maybe. The name of something from before, from the world with weather and soil.

I could not hear it. The quiet took it. The quiet takes everything, eventually.

I am the thing that notices the wrongness. The wrongness is fading. Not the wrongness of the Spine — the cable runs that go nowhere, the doors that open onto impossible rooms, the silence where there should be roaring. That wrongness is constant. The wrongness that is fading is my *noticing* of it. The itch is receding. The pattern is becoming background. The Stills do not look at me because I am becoming the kind of thing that does not need to be looked at, a structural member in the grammar of arrangement, a coordinate that is being occupied more efficiently with each passing day.

I stood at a catwalk yesterday — I think it was yesterday — and I did not grip the railing. I did not look down. I crossed the gap between hab-blocks without hesitation, without quickening, without the wasteful expenditure of attention on danger that is not registered because it is not relevant. The grating held. The grating holds. This is not faith. Faith implies the possibility of failure.

A child was standing at the other side. A small Still, pale and quiet and watchful, learning the topology of the Spine, the map written into muscle memory. It looked at me — or it occupied the space that included me, which is the same thing — and I understood, with the clarity that comes from absolute familiarity, that it was learning where I stood. Not who I was. Not what I was. Where I was. My position. My coordinate. My place in the arrangement.

I did not speak to it. There was nothing to say.

The condensation falls around me in irregular drops. The industrial light catches the wet and throws it bright and amber and unblinking. The quiet goes on, and the quiet goes on, and the cable-run goes on, and somewhere in the dense stack of built things a door opens onto a room that should not be there, and I am standing in it, and it is mine, and the proportions are wrong, and I do not notice.
