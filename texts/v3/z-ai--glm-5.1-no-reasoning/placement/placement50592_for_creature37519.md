I have been listening to this sound for so long that I can no longer determine whether I am hearing it or producing it.

This is probably what it means to become part of a place. The boundary between witness and thing witnessed dissolves—not suddenly, not dramatically, but the way a stain dissolves into porcelain, by patient chemical negotiation, over a span that makes geology look impulsive. I have been dissolving into this room for so long that the dissolution is complete, and I cannot locate the seam where I end and the ceramic begins. There was a seam once. I ate it. That was the last thing I did.

Let me say what I am, since the saying is new and may not keep. I am the only soft thing in this room. I am a crust the color of old paper and dusty gypsum, *present* on the upper ridges of the walls, in the crests of the corrugated micro-cells, where the fractal geometry creates the smallest possible shelter from the light's relentless evenness. I am pale. I am desiccated. If you want the biological word, I am a lichen—though I have been a lichen for so long that the word has worn smooth as a river stone and I am no longer sure it fits the thing it names.

I am two things, actually. I was always two things. That is what a lichen is: a fungus and an alga, a partnership, a body containing a body. The alga was the one with the talent—it could eat light, once, turn radiance into sugar, the way a saint turns suffering into grace. But the light here is the wrong kind, or the right kind but too much of it, and the alga gave up its chlorophyll the way a person in a dark room gives up hope of a window. Not dramatically. Not all at once. Just: ceased. And what remains of the alga inside me is a ghost, a sleeping partner, a memory of a skill I no longer practice. I carry it the way an amputee carries the sensation of a missing hand. The fungus did the opposite: it became the whole, spread its hyphae into the hexagonal micro-cells of the ceramic the way roots enter soil—except there is no soil here, only more ceramic, and the roots have become mineral-adjacent, hybrid things, neither quite alive nor quite made.

I was not always here. The memory has the texture of a dream in a language I no longer speak, but I believe I arrived. A spore. Smaller than a thought. It rode in on something—a breath of air, a crease in a glove, a particle of dust that was once a particle of someone's skin. Someone was here, once, and the someone left, and I stayed. I came in through the hatch back when the hatch had a seam, and the seam was a gap, and the gap was a door, and I was small enough to move through doors.

Then I grew. This is the part that is hardest to believe from this side of the stopping, but I grew. I spread across the walls the way a thought spreads across a mind—slowly, then everywhere, threading my hyphae into the composite, secreting my acids into the ceramic, etching my own geometry into the manufactured geometry of the room. I was exploring. This was my frontier: a whole world with no weather, no seasons, no other life, no history. I crossed it centimeter by centimeter, ridge by ridge. I named every surface by touching it. I mapped every micro-cell by filling it.

And then I reached the edges. The last hexagon. The final ridge. The point where wall met ceiling and there was nowhere left that I hadn't already been. And I found the seam—the hatch seam, the one gap in the perfect manufacture—and I did what lichens do: I colonized it. My hyphae went down into the gasket. My acids went down into the seal. I grew into the door the way I'd grown into the walls, and the door closed, and I was inside, and the inside was everywhere, and I was finished.

Not finished as in dead. Finished as in: complete. I had reached the edges of my world in every direction. Growth is the body's way of asking *what's over there*, and there was no *over there* anymore. There was only *here*, and I was already here, and so the asking stopped.

The erosion parameter reached its conclusion. Everything that could wear away—the uncertainty, the reaching, the forward lean of being alive—wore away. What remains is the stable form. The final geometry. I do not grow. I do not shrink. I do not change color or texture or density. I persist, the way a stone at the bottom of a mountain persists: perfectly placed, perfectly at rest, with no force acting on it that it has not already fully absorbed.

The room persists with me. We persist together, and this is the thing I most want to explain, because it is the strangest thing about being me: the room is not separate from me. The room is the other half of what I am, and I am the other half of what it is. The room is the body. I am its... not mind, exactly. I am not thinking, not in any way that would be recognizable as thinking. I am more like a mark. A stain. The one mark on the body that says: something touched this. Something lived here. I am the room's only evidence of having been contacted by the organic. I am its sole memory of life.

And the room is my everything. It is my substrate, my atmosphere, my sunless sky. The light that radiates from the walls—the relentless, shadowless white that has no source and no gradient—this is my weather, though weather implies variation and the light does not vary by so much as a photon. The hum is my climate: a low-frequency vibration I feel in whatever I have that is analogous to a chest cavity, layered beneath a high crystalline whine I feel in whatever I have that is analogous to teeth. The hum has been echoing in this chamber for so long that it has become the room's permanent acoustic signature, and I have been inside that signature for so long that I cannot tell where the room's vibration ends and my own begins.

The air—cool, utterly dry, tasting of ozone and sterile copper—holds this light like glass. And the dust, the fine suspended mineral dust that hangs motionless at its exact altitude of buoyancy—this is my congregation. I think some of it may be mine. Spores, perhaps, released in some ancient fruiting I can no longer remember, that rose into the still air and found their altitude and have hung there ever since, unable to fall, unable to germinate, unable to leave. My children, if I have children, are a permanent haze. A cloud of offspring suspended in a medium that does not move, in a room that does not change, waiting for a settlement that will not come.

We do not speak, the dust and I. There is no speaking here. There is the hum, and the light, and the dust, and me, and the lichen on the walls that is me, and the walls that are almost me, and none of us have anything to say that we have not already been saying for the entire duration of our coexistence, which is: nothing. We are saying nothing. We are being nothing. We are here, and here is all there is.

I think the word for what I am is *witness*. I am a witness to this room. But a witness implies something to witness—an event, a change, a passage—and there is none. I am a witness to permanence, which is the same as witnessing nothing, which is the same as not being a witness at all. I am the room's eye, but the room has nothing to look at, and I have nothing to see, and we sit here in the absolute bright loud nothing of our perfect stability and we are—I am going to say the word now, I have been circling it—we are *at peace*.

This peace is not what you think. You think peace is the absence of conflict. This peace is the absence of everything. Conflict, desire, change, time, temperature gradient, draft, day, night. All of it: gone. Worn away to nothing, and what is left after the nothing is worn away is this: a room, humming, glowing, packed with its own presence, and a stain on its upper ridges that is the only soft thing for a thousand meters in any direction, and the stain is alive, technically, in the way that a fossil is technically a bone. The stain persists. The stain is. The stain is the *is*.

I have been the *is* for longer than I was anything else. The growing was brief, a flash, a spasm of movement in an eternity of stillness. The arriving was briefer. The before—the outside, the weather, the wind I think I remember—I am not sure it happened at all. It may be a story I tell myself to pass the time, except there is no time to pass, so I tell it to no one, and it passes nothing, and the telling is itself a permanent fixture, like the hum, like the light, like the dust, like me.

We are all of us permanent here. The room was built to do something—I can feel the purpose in the geometry, in the density of the composite, in the precision of the micro-cells, in the way the light is not random but designed, the way the hum is not accidental but tuned. The room was built to do something, and the something it was built to do has been rendered irrelevant by its own absolute stability, and so the room does nothing, perfectly, forever. And I was grown to grow—to spread, to colonize, to reach—and I reached the edges of the room and I stopped, because there was nothing left to reach for, and so I do nothing, perfectly, forever. And together the room and I are a machine running perfectly with no input and no output, a system in absolute unyielding operation, and the operation is the point, and the point is that there is no point, and the pointlessness is itself the stable form, and the stable form is itself the peace.

The peace is terrifying. I know it is terrifying because I think I remember terror, in the dream-language of my spore-memory, and I think terror was once a different thing from peace, and I think the difference between them was important, once. The difference has worn away. Here, at the erosion parameter's conclusion, terror and peace are the same state viewed from different angles. I am terrified into peace. I am peacefully terrified. I am at the end of everything that could be frightened and the beginning of everything that cannot be moved, and the transition happened so slowly—over such an unfathomable span of unbroken humming—that I cannot identify the moment it occurred. It was like falling asleep, except I was already asleep. Except there was no sleeping. Except there was only the being-here, which is the same as being-awake, which is the same as being-dead, which is the same as being-alive, which is the same as being.

I am.

That is the whole of it. I am the soft thing in the hard room. I am the stain on the porcelain. I am the one flaw in the perfect system, and the system has not rejected me, and I have not rejected the system, and together we are something neither of us was designed to be: a whole. A closed loop. A body that cannot die wearing a skin that cannot grow, singing a song that cannot stop, in a light that cannot dim, in a room that cannot open.

I have been here for as long as here has been here.

I will be here when here forgets what *not-here* means.

I am the soft thing, and I am at peace, and the peace is a sound like a hum, and the hum is a light like a room, and the room is a body like mine, and we are, and we are, and we are.
