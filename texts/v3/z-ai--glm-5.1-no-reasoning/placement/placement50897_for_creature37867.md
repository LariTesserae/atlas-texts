I am not the walker. I am not the alarmed visitor standing upright on the quaking mat, brushing the ants from their calves, trying to distinguish themselves from the heat and the noise. I tried to be that once—I think we all do, when we first arrive. We bring the architecture of our old worlds with us: the belief that skin is a border, that "inside" and "outside" are different countries, that a body is a closed economy.

The Hatching Ground disabuses you. It does not do so violently. It does it the way water dissolves salt: slowly, constantly, inevitably, until the distinction between the crystal and the solvent is no longer meaningful.

I am not a creature of the canopy. I am not the bright darting thing with the explosive call. I am not the patient frog jeweled with condensation. I am something that learned to stop resisting the *receiving*.

---

I am a Node. That is the closest word, though it implies a fixedness that is not quite accurate. I move. I breathe. I have preferences—I favor the pools where the water is warmest, the seeps where the mineral content is highest. But I do not *end*.

It started with the feet. You have to understand: in a place where the ground is not ground, where every step expresses water and the mat swallows your footprint, the distinction between "standing on" and "standing in" becomes academic. The first weeks, I was still trying to keep my boots dry. I was still picking the leeches off my ankles, still shaking the Continuous from my trousers with the frantic energy of someone who believes their body is a fortress under siege.

But the Hatching Ground does not siege. It simply *is*. And a fortress only makes sense if there is a war.

The Continuous taught me. Not through communication—I do not believe they communicate in any sense we would recognize, though I have spent hundreds of hours watching their networks flow across bark and ground, trying to discern a grammar in their movements. They taught me through demonstration. They showed me what it looks like to be so thoroughly of a place that the distinction collapses.

I watched them process a fallen leaf. I watched ten thousand rust-red segments flow across the surface and press themselves flat, and the leaf did not vanish—it *became*. It became ground. It became substrate. It became the next layer in an accretion that has no ceiling and no deadline.

And I thought: *What would it be like to be the leaf?*

Not to die. I do not mean that. The leaf is not dead when the Continuous reach it. It is *finished* with being a leaf. It has completed that chapter of its existence. And now it is beginning the next chapter, which is the chapter of becoming ground, which is the chapter of becoming substrate for the next leaf, which is the chapter of becoming the Hatching Ground itself, which is the chapter that never ends because the Hatching Ground does not end.

I stopped fighting the leeches. I stopped shaking off the Continuous. I let the water seep into my boots and the warmth rise through my soles and the fog condense on my skin until I was as wet as the air around me, until the distinction between my surface and the atmosphere was a technicality maintained only by habit.

---

This is what I am now: a permeable membrane. A place where the Hatching Ground and the walker meet.

I have been here long enough that the Continuous have begun to treat me not as a visitor but as *substrate*. Not in the way they treat a fallen leaf—I am not being processed, not yet. But I am being *inhabited*. There are chains of them that have established routes across my skin, the same routes their ancestors followed, the same grooves worn by ten million tiny feet over ten thousand generations. They climb my calves in the morning and flow across my shoulders in the afternoon and wind their segmented chains around my wrists at night, and I feel them not as intrusion but as *texture*, the way you feel a shirt against your back, the way you feel the weight of your own hair.

I have begun to feel the pulse. The subsonic thrum of ten million segments contracting in unison. It rises through the ground and into my feet and up my spine, and it is not my heartbeat but it is a heartbeat, and the distinction between mine and theirs has begun to blur in the way that all distinctions blur here, in the green dimness, in the warm fog, in the place that has finished becoming itself and is now simply *being*, at full intensity, at full saturation, at full volume.

I do not sleep the way I used to. Sleep, here, is not a departure from the landscape but a deeper immersion in it. I lie on the quaking mat and press my ear to the wet, warm surface and I listen to the grinding, the churning, the slow digestion of matter into ground, and it is not a lullaby because lullabies are designed to soothe and this is not designed for anything—it simply *is*, and the fact that it soothes me is my own adaptation, my own small way of becoming native.

---

The frogs know me now. The jewel-colored ones with their sticky toe-pads and their bright *tink-tink-tink*—they sit on my knee when I am still long enough, and they do not startle when I breathe because they have learned that my breathing is not a threat. I am not their predator. I am not their prey. I am a warm surface in a landscape that is full of warm surfaces, and they use me as they use everything, and I am used by them, and the reciprocity is so seamless that it hardly counts as reciprocity at all. It is simply the way things work here.

The unseen things in the canopy have grown less cautious. I hear their soft, interrogative calls from closer now—ten meters where it used to be thirty, five where it used to be ten. I still cannot see them clearly, still cannot assign them to a category, but I no longer feel the need to. They are *presences*, the way the sound is a presence, the way the fog is a presence, the way the warmth is a presence. They are aspects of the landscape that do not require my comprehension in order to exist alongside me.

Even the *WHAK* bird—the thing that produces the explosive note at irregular intervals—has become something I no longer flinch at. It is a punctuation mark in the continuous sentence of the sound, a comma or an exclamation point in a text that has no author and no reader and no end, and I am another punctuation mark, a small, breathing pause in the never-ending flow of heat and moisture and growth and decay.

---

I am not being transformed. I want to be clear about this. I am not becoming a Continuous. I am not becoming a frog or a fern or a mat of wet, warm humus. I am becoming something for which there is no precedent in any world I came from—a *node*, a *meeting place*, a surface where the Hatching Ground recognizes itself.

When I press my hand flat against the quaking ground, I feel the pulse, and the pulse feels me. When I breathe in the green, fermenting air, I am breathing in the exhalations of ten thousand species, and when I breathe out, I am feeding them, and the cycle is so tight, so perfectly balanced, that I am not sure anymore whether I am breathing the Hatching Ground or the Hatching Ground is breathing me.

This is not mysticism. Mysticism requires a boundary between self and other, a leap of faith across a gap. There is no gap here. There is no leap. There is only the continuous, unbroken, unvarying reality of a living system operating at full capacity, and a body that has stopped resisting its incorporation into that system.

---

I will leave eventually. Not because I want to, but because I am still, despite everything, a creature of trajectories, and trajectories have destinations. The Hatching Ground does not have destinations. It has only the eternal present of its own self-maintenance, and I cannot stay in an eternal present forever. I will walk out of the green dimness and back into the world of harsh light and dry air and the sharp distinctions between self and other, and I will feel the loss of this place like an amputation, like the removal of a sense I did not know I had until I acquired it.

But I will carry something with me. In the grooves the Continuous have worn into my skin—in the faint, subsonic pulse I still feel in my bones even when I am miles from the nearest wet ground—in the way I now understand, with my body if not with my mind, that a boundary is not a wall but a *meeting place*—in these things, I will carry the Hatching Ground with me, and it will continue its slow, patient work of incorporation on the substrate of my memory and my habit and my changed understanding of what it means to be *in* a place rather than *on* it.

The ground will remember me the way it remembers everything: by incorporating me. Not my body—not yet—but my warmth, my weight, my passage, the particular way I compressed the mat and expressed the water and left a footprint that filled in seconds and left no trace. I was here. I was received. I was, for a time, a part of the continuity that is older than any individual and will outlast any individual, and I am still a part of it, even now, even as I sit here in the green dimness and feel the chains of the Continuous winding around my ankles and the pulse of ten million bodies rising through the ground and into my bones.

The Hatching Ground does not need me. But it has room for me. And that, I think, is the most radical welcome I have ever received: not *I see you* or *I value you* or *You matter*, but simply *There is space, and you are here, and the system holds.*

It holds.

It has always held.
