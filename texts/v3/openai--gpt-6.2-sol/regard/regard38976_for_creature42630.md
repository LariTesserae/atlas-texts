In the dry year, the first rain falls hard enough to take down a roof over one of the oldest lanes. The roof comes down in three pieces. Two go soft almost at once. The third has roots through it and holds together. It slides as far as the lip of the mouse’s crack and stops there, with the weight of the wet sand behind it.

The mouse is out when this happens. She comes back along the wall with a piece of leaf doubled in her mouth. At the entrance she puts it down. She tries the left side of the fallen roof, then the right. She digs where the sand is softest, which is not where the entrance is. Each time she draws back, water runs into the little hole she has made.

The power that has come to the plain could take the roof off whole. It could dry the lane from one end to the other. Instead it goes down into the crack and finds how much air there is and how far up the water has come. The young are on the lining of stalks, which has lifted a little from the floor. One has a wet foot and keeps taking it out from under the others.

Outside, a fine root is stretched across a drip in the fallen roof. The power breaks it. The drop that had been gathering there falls on the other side. So does the next. Where they fall, the sand begins to run out from under the lip of the basalt. The mouse hears it. She comes around with mud on her nose and digs there.

She gets in sideways, as she always has. It takes her most of the night to bring the young out. She puts them in a beetle’s old burrow on the uphill side of the lane, goes back for dry stalks, and puts those in too. The leaf she brought home is still lying where she dropped it. In the morning it is gone. There are several creatures that might have taken it.

The power stays on the plain. On the far side of the city it finds a snake in a shallow wash, cold from the rain, with its tail under a stone. The wash is filling from farther up, although the rain has stopped. The power rolls the stone just far enough. The snake does not move for some time. Then it draws its tail in and goes up the bank. There is a place there where the basalt has kept a little of the previous day’s heat.

By the next afternoon the insects are working the sand that came off the roof. They take it from the lane in little loads and pack it against the wet face of a wall. The mouse sleeps uphill now. Her old crack is still cool, and when the water goes down she will go back for the stalks she left in it. For several days she will use both places.

At the edge of the plain the rain has washed a scatter of seeds out onto bare rock. The power watches the water go by. In one of the pits left by a bubble, a seed turns end over end and catches. The water goes on without it. Nothing more is needed there, and the power moves on.
