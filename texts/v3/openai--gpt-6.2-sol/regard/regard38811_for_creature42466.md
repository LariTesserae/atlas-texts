The power listened below the fans. There were men working in the pits, and the air that came past the woman’s ankles had still to reach them. It left the fans running.

It went under the ice. The large springtail had got a foot through a hole in the leaf and could not draw it back. The power could have lifted the ice, but the ice was keeping the water from going the way of the air. It softened the edge of the hole instead. The springtail pulled its foot out and went on eating.

There was a greater weight to attend to. Behind the ledge where the woman had lost her pencil, water had got in above a slab. There was room enough now for the pencil to turn. The roots would hold the slab a while longer, but they would not hold it through the winter. When it went, it would take the ledge and the path below it. On Tuesdays the woman stood on that path to read the last gauge.

The power waited for her to read it. When she turned to write the number down, it drew the pencil out of the rock and laid it across the open book.

She knew it by the tooth marks. The paint had gone at the wet end and the wood there was hairy and swollen. She looked up at the ledge. Then she shut the book and went back the long way.

At the top of the stair, the man she told said the pencil could have washed out. She said she supposed it could, and asked him to come down and show her where the water had run. He put on his coat. The power had done nothing to him. He was a man who would come and look if asked twice.

They closed that length of path and put the gauge where it could be read from the stair. In February the slab came down. Nobody heard it go. It lay across the old path with a torn mat of green on its upper face. By Tuesday a drip had found the low corner and there was water standing there.

Several leaves had come down with the slab. One was wedged edgewise in the stone, halfway out of the water. The power turned it over. There was nothing living on it yet. It was only that the soft side would keep longer underneath.
