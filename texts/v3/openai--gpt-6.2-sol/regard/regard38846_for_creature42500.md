Under the overhang, the young animal began to take in water. It had settled far enough back that the water it let out tended to come in again. There was food in the pit at first. By the second day there was very little.

The power followed the water out over the sill. Beyond it, a slow current passed close enough to the hollow to bend a strip of algae at the outer edge. The current had once come in through a notch in the sill. The notch was still there, but a flat pebble lay across the bottom of it, with sand banked against its inner face.

There were things living on the underside of the pebble. The power did not turn it over. It slid the pebble a little way along the notch, keeping the same face down. Sand spilled into the space it left, but not enough to fill it.

For most of the day there was no change. Then the water outside the hollow rose, and a thin layer of it came in through the notch. It went along the wall rather than across the floor. A strip of algae lifted at one end. Under the overhang, water that had been through the young animal once went past its mouth and did not go in again.

The power waited until the water fell. The flow through the notch reversed, and a little of the sand that had banked against the pebble went out over the sill. The pebble stayed where it was. It would not need to be moved again at the next tide.

From the middle of the hollow there was still nothing to see under the overhang. On the far wall the two grains lay at the ends of the short line. The low note went on. At the foot of the sill, on the side that could not be seen from inside, a few gray grains settled among the gravel.
