The power did not make the rain stop. It had noticed, though, that the woman who kept the school key had twice taken a second cup down from the shelf and twice put it back. That morning it let her remember the first time someone had asked her in out of the rain. She had been carrying a sack of potatoes. She had said she couldn’t stay, and the person had said she could put the potatoes down anyway.

She found the woman by the bank.

“I’ve got the kettle on,” she said.

“My coat’s in there.”

The woman with the key looked at the low stones. She had known where the other woman slept since November, but this was the first time either of them had had occasion to look directly at the place together.

“Will it be all right there?”

“Yes,” the woman said. Then, after watching the fern a little longer, she went up to the school.

There was a cardigan on the back of a chair in the room where the kettle boiled. She put it on without being asked. It had a hole at one elbow. She put her finger through the hole and said she could do something about that, and the woman with the key said she’d rather have it done in the same colour if there was any to be had. They drank their tea and talked about which shop might still have a card of that particular brown.

When she went back, the coat was where she had left it. The bird had gone out. She could see the green end of what it had been carrying, caught in the dry pocket behind the fern.

She took the coat and the blanket. She meant to leave the pebble, but from the entrance it looked, as it sometimes did, like something too large to carry. She went back to settle the matter and put it in her pocket.

At the school she knocked, although the door was still open.

“Could I leave this here till evening?” she asked, holding up the blanket.

The woman with the key moved a box of chalk off the top shelf. There was room for the blanket without moving it, but she moved it all the same.
