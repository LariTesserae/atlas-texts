At one of the broken pairs, the fallen post had kept its socket dry. The socket was deep, with a little shelf on one side. Whoever had put the posts there had stood each one on its shelf while driving wedges in below. Two of the wedges were still in the hole. A third lay under the far end of the fallen post.

The power that had taken notice of the summit lifted the post and stood it on the shelf. It did not drive the wedges in.

That afternoon the dust came down narrow for the first time in a long while. Before it cleared, an animal came with a pebble. The stone was too low. The animal took it away, came back with another, and in passing put a forefoot against the post. The post rocked in its socket. The column widened at the foot, and the tuft came apart.

The animal was back the next day. It went around the post twice before setting its stone down. This time, when the post moved, it put the stone into the gap at the base. The post still moved, but not as far. By dusk there were six pebbles in the socket, none of them doing much good. The animal had gone home without a thread.

It might have been enough to drive the wedges in then. The power waited. It had seen how long a young animal could spend opening one hair.

On the third day the animal brought one of the old smudged pebbles from another pair. It set it down at the center, where a stone ordinarily went, and left it there when the dust failed to come. In the morning the pebble was at the base of the post. By noon it had worked itself so far into the socket that only a pale corner showed. There were threads running from the corner to both sides of the hole. When the post moved, the threads on one side went slack and those on the other drew tight. The next morning the pebble was gone. In its place were three grains of dark stone, each wedged in a different crack.

The post no longer rocked.

For the rest of that season the power did nothing at the other broken pairs. It watched to see what the renewed column took from the sky, and what happened to the plants that had gone so long without a bite. Some of the oldest leaves never opened wide enough for a mouth. The animals fed at the cracks beyond them, where the same plants had put out leaves no bigger than new ones. Under the lip, on a ledge that had been bare, a seed caught in a shallow bite and held.

Years later, a post fell in the night. It was not the one the power had raised. In the morning two animals went along it from end to end. One went away. The other brought a smudged pebble and set it down beside the empty socket.

The power had not known that a stone so small could lift a post. It stayed until dark to see how the stone would begin.
