The place was still warm. She moved her hand a little and found the edge of the warmth, and moved it back.

Something had been listening from the other side of the wall. When she had tried to hold the seam shut, it had put a finger to the outside of the sheath. It could have held the two edges together. Instead it had taken its finger away and waited to see what she did when there was room.

Now it took the chipped pebble between two fingers and turned it in the dirt. It turned it just far enough that the next report from the seam went into the stone. The seam opened as much as it would have anyway. She saw a hair on the brown sheath spring upright. A little grit fell outside, and she heard that.

She waited with her teeth apart. Another hair sprang up.

She put her bare arm against the wall. The pebble was there, but the chip was gone. In its place was a broad face with a slight hollow in it. When she fitted her palm to the hollow she heard the root going on through the dirt. She took her hand away and heard her sleeve settle. She did this several times, leaving her hand off longer each time.

Then she knocked the heel of her bare foot against the leaf. It made a soft, unpromising sound. She knocked again. The second was a little louder, so she tried a third, and got it nearly the same as the first. She lay for a while considering which of the two she liked.

Outside, the two seed leaves had parted enough for a grain of dirt to fall between them. The power let the pebble go. The dirt would keep it at that angle until the root reached it, which would not be tonight.

Before she slept she put her hand to the hollow once more. The root had got through whatever had been in its way. She could hear it going easily now, with long intervals in which she could hear nothing at all. In one of those intervals she gave her little *tack*. Nothing followed. She had not known how softly she could make it.
