By the next morning the green lip had grown down over the animal’s back. Water still came out on either side, but the middle of the fan was dry. Under the lip the animal lifted a section of its rim and set it down again in the same place. It had been doing this all night. There was room to lift, but no room to reach.

The power that had been watching the valley put a finger under the lip.

The wood was warm. On its underside were the beginnings of roots, soft white points that bent at the touch. The power lifted only as far as those points would allow without tearing. This opened a space about as wide as the slit through which water enters a sleeping hollow.

For a long while nothing used it. Water ran down the finger and fell from the knuckle. The sun came over the upper terraces and shone through the lip, and the shadow of the finger lay among the shadows of the veins. Once the animal took hold of it. The power could feel the whole weight of the animal then, though nothing on the face of the cleft appeared to move. It held still until the animal found wood again.

Toward noon the animal was clear. It did not go up to the socket where the other one had gone. It went down a little, to a spot where one of the narrow streams crossed a patch of old brown root. There it began to feed. The first mark it made was hardly wider than the stream itself.

The power took its finger away. By evening the lip had met the wall. Water collected behind it and worked out through a socket lower down, where a young cleft animal, still creased from the wood, had been lying in the dry. It took the better part of the night for the water to reach it. By then the power had gone from the cut face.

Out on the rim, the two new leaves still did not touch. The farther one had tipped enough that the next drop to land on it ran straight off. The little animal came out as before and went to the edge in several places. At one of them it stayed a while, with its front end over the drop. Then it went back along a vein to where the leaf was thickest. There was a little water there, too, not enough to see from above unless the light was right.
