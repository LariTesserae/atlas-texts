The power came in the quiet hour and stayed through the noise. It wanted to know what the place was like when it was difficult to love.

By midnight the sister had put a corner of her sleeve in each ear. Her brother was asleep. Every so often his hand moved on the mat as if he were feeling for the jar.

Beneath them the white animal had not eaten for three days. The power could feel the silverfish coming down the wall, stopping where the fresh powder gave way to felt. It could have brought the two together by the smallest movement of the wood. It waited. The silverfish went down another way. A little later a beetle, going about its business, fell into the animal’s mouth.

Toward morning the power went looking for the water that had once come to the jars. There was less of it now, but not so much less as the brother supposed. Some went into the nursery. Some ran inward along a root that had grown since his mother cut the gutter. It fell at last into a pocket of gray wood, where it stood a finger deep until the heat took it. There were no mouths in the pocket yet. It had only opened the previous summer.

The power loosened a dead fiber at the bottom of the pocket. It took some time to do this without disturbing the live fibers beside it. The first drop went straight down and was lost. The next followed the dead fiber to its tip, hung there, and fell clear of the ledge.

In the morning the brother heard it strike a dry leaf. He lay down to see where it had come from. Then he got up and took the worst of the jars, the one with a piece missing from its rim, and wedged it under the drip. His sister helped him find a stone to keep it level. By the time they left there was enough in it for each to have a swallow. They had been planning to go down by the short ravine, where the footing was bad. They took the long way instead.

The power stayed until the heat came up. It had hoped to see the old female at the head of the file, but she had gone into a bench that did not open to the sky. For a while it followed the slight settling of the leaves above her. Then even that ceased, and it could not have said where she was without looking in a way it had decided not to look.

By the next visit there were eggs in the new jar. The brother tipped it a little to see them. He and his sister went back to washing their eyes with a cloth. On their way home they talked about bringing up another jar, and about whether they knew anyone who was coming up from clay country with room to carry it.
