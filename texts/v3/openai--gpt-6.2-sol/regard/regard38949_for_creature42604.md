The power came to the deep cut during a stoppage. The last roll had gone, and the lip was still cool. There was time to take it for a dead thing before the warmth reached it.

The power waited.

By the time the first bud rose, the power knew what had stopped the shop. A leaf in the bed below it had curled as far as it could, and the leaf that should have taken its place was caught under the edge of the shaft. It would come free. There was already a pale thickening where it bore against the wood.

The power could have freed it sooner. It could also have held it there until every bud on the lip had opened. It did neither. When the shaft turned, two buds stayed up. The smaller was in the path of the next roll, which took off its tip. What remained was too low to mark the stock.

Farther down, beneath the oldest of the ledges, the power found a leaf whose root had entered a conduit just as the conduit was going dry. The leaf had made a little shelf. Another flake had settled under it and put out a root of its own. Neither root could reach the oil still moving in the wall. Between them and the nearest warm seam lay the squared end of a dead trunk.

Here the power did something. It drew a little heat out of the trunk. The wood contracted enough to open a crack at one corner. No oil came out. The crack was dry its whole length, and the nearer root would have to grow the width of a thumb before it found anything.

The power stayed until the root passed the corner. Then it went up to the avenue.

The runner at the turn had begun to take the weight of the freight. Its rib was higher at one end, and now and then a stem slewed a little as it crossed. There would be work for the catches. There might, after a long time, be a bend where there had never been one. The power followed the old curve on foot, putting its weight where there was room.

At the rim it held a hand out over the dark. A flake came up, turned, and struck the back of a finger. It would have been easy to cup the hand. The power kept it still, and the flake slid off into the strip of cool air. It touched a ledge and came away. It touched again some distance farther in.

Below it, out of reach of the hand, the root in the crack had begun to flatten on one side. There was room to go on that way.
