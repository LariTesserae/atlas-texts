One afternoon the old female came out and found that the rain spot had dried before she reached it. She lay down anyway. When she got up there was no likeness of her on the stone.

The power had been watching long enough to know that this happened. It had also watched her eat a cricket that had sung through the better part of three afternoons. It could not give the cricket another afternoon and give her that meal. It could, however, do something about the way she had to lower her chin after six steps. There was a little pain in her shoulder that did no work for her anymore. It had once kept her from putting weight on the foot while it healed. The foot had been healed for years.

The power took away the pain. At the next pause she put her chin down as usual. Then she picked it up and went on.

There was something else. The slab over the entrance looked as settled as the rest of the slope, but at its far end, well back from the light, it was bearing on a flake of stone no thicker than a cricket’s wing. With each warm afternoon the slab moved a little on the flake. It would come down in the night, when almost everything that lived in the hollow was inside.

The power held the slab up while the stone cooled. At the far end it drew two faces of rock together until they met. The flake fell. It struck the sill and broke, and a young lizard turned its head at the sound.

Nothing had been done to the pegs. The drop still went sideways. The old female, coming back in, took the shiny way around and met a young male coming out. He put one foot on her back and then the other. She waited for him to get off.

That evening she went a little farther down the slope than she had been in some time. There was no water there. There was a place where the stone dipped just enough that, lying flat, she could get all four feet out of the wind. She stayed until the heat went out of it. When she came back, the smallest lizards were at the lip. One climbed onto her head before she had got all the way in. She stopped where she was, with her front feet on the inside and her hind feet in the last of the warmth, and let it cross.
