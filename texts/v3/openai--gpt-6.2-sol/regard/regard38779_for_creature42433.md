The power came in a dry year and at first mistook the beating for a request.

It put a little rain into one of the dishes. The threads under the dead leaf straightened. Toward evening the animal struck, and a flake of mica turned over in the clay. The power was pleased with this until it saw the snail come over the rim. The snail had spent the afternoon under a curl of bark. The rain had brought it out.

After that the power stayed through a number of seasons without doing anything it could remember having meant to do. It learned that there were places where a root was killed and places where the same root, had it grown a finger’s breadth to the left, would have been washed clean. It could find no rule that would have told the alder where to send the root. The alder sent others. In time one of them grew thick enough to lift a piece of the bank, and a dish that had always spilled east spilled west. There was a good year for the little ones on ground where there had never been any.

Then men came in winter and set white stakes from the river to the fallen tree. They had had water in their houses twice in three years. There was a brown line across the plaster above the beds of their children. They meant to cut a ditch straight through the marsh.

The power went upstream. There, under a road, it found a culvert with an old gate that had been shut and forgotten. On the other side lay a stretch of low pasture, empty since the roof of the farmhouse fell in. It took the rust off the hinge. It did not open the gate. In the morning a woman who had come to measure the water leaned on it, and it moved under her hand. She went home for boots and walked the pasture as far as the old house. When the men came back they pulled the stakes, except for one they could not find among the reeds.

The pasture took water that spring. It drowned three apple trees. The woman had known those trees since she was a girl. Before the water reached the lowest branches she went out and cut some shoots, and two of them took on a tree beside her house.

Years later, in another dry spell, the power came back to the alder. There was a vole chewing somewhere close by. The power could have put rain into every dish and held it there until the young were ready to spill. It listened instead. When rain did come, it lay on the fallen trunk and ran off both sides, a little more to one side than the other.

The first blow came from beneath the wood. The wait was longer than the power remembered. It stayed for the answer.
