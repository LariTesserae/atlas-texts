The power came first to the space between the two groves. It could have kept the hanging grove from taking root. By then there was a young animal under a leaf in each, and it was too late to do that without taking something away.

It waited through the cold. The adult crossed seven times. On the seventh crossing it missed the branch and went on rowing after there was anything within reach. The power lifted the tip of the branch. The adult caught it with one forefoot and hung there for a while before climbing.

When the first young unfolded, the adult went with it. The power stayed by the other leaf. It had seen how the adult steadied a branch: not at the tip, where the young would feel the pull, but close to the fork. When the second young put down its foot, the power held the wood there. The foot slipped anyway. The young caught itself with the other three, drew the first one up, and tried it on a different part of the branch. For the rest of the day it went no farther than that.

The power did this at other crossings. It did not give the walkers better limbs for swimming. Their feet were good for the work they had, and the power liked to watch them lift those feet a little early where the fans grew thick. It let the cold come through the roots, and the leaves fall, and the white mouths hang loose in the water. It had at first thought the mouths were waiting to be saved. Then it saw the new leaves rising.

At the upstream end of the shoulder it watched a brown animal set a stone. The grains gathered over the stone and then over the toes that held it down. The animal waited. There was a short interval in which the stone would bear the current but the foot could still be drawn out. This time the interval did not come. As soon as the animal lifted its foot the stone began to turn.

The power put its weight on the stone.

The animal went back along the shoulder, nosing the ground as it went. It returned with a thin stone and pushed it into the opening on the downstream side. Then it went for another. After the third, the power took its weight away. The animal kept one foot on the stone for a little longer, though it was nowhere near coming loose.

For a long time the power supposed that the two walls inland were the beginnings of the three by the warm rim. It could have gone ahead to see. Instead it spent a night beside the round stones, watching the three animals take out a section that had stood perfectly well the night before. They put it back with one stone turned so that a shallow cup faced upward. Before they went in at dawn, the smallest animal came back and pressed its chin into the cup. The next night it did the same thing. Nothing collected there that the power could see.

Years later, when a front lay over the rise, a hanging grove took root above the enclosures. One of its lowest branches grew down toward the hollow. Each night the largest of the brown animals came out and pushed it aside with its head. One night the animal did not come out. The other two went past the branch easily. They worked until morning, came back, and waited at the hollow. At last the small one went in, leaving the silt rough behind it.

The power did not draw the old animal out. It stayed where it could feel the water moving through the hollow, and after a time there was no movement there but the seep from the rock.

At dusk the two animals came out again. They went to the inland wall and began to work at one of the round stones. It had always taken three of them to move it. The smaller one put its head underneath; the other braced its feet and pushed. The power waited until it could feel which way they meant the stone to go. Then it took a little of the weight.
