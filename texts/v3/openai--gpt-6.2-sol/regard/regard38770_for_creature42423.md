The power came during a lifting. For a long time it did nothing that a fitter would have entered in the log.

It watched a finch drink above an empty saucer. It watched a woman warm her hands on a tub and then turn them over, though there was scarcely enough heat in the enamel to warm one side. At the house by the far rise it heard a hem drop onto the mat. The woman in the kitchen heard it too. She had been about to go into the passage, but she stayed where she was until she heard it drop a second time.

The power had thought of warming the whole basin. Now it went back to the tubs to see how much warmth was wanted, and when.

Near the end of the lifting, the night fitter found that the bench outside the engine house was warm. He put his dinner tin down and went inside for a contact thermometer. By the time he came out, a hem had settled at one end of the bench, and there were two young ones working their way up its back. He took the temperature at the other end. It was a little less than blood heat. He checked the wall behind it and the bolts underneath. Both were cold.

In the log he wrote down the temperature and the time. Then he ate his dinner on the bench. The young hems tried his sleeve once, when he reached for the lid of the tin. He waited for them to get off.

The bench has been warm on cold nights ever since. It is a long bench, made to seat six, and there has so far been room. On the coldest nights the fitter sometimes finds someone already there when he brings out his dinner. They move along a little. No one moves all the way to the end.
