The power came in the clear season, when the red corner of the tile could be seen. It put no light of its own into the opening. For a while it watched the animal underneath the leaf. The animal had come to the oldest of the cast skins, the one with the grit printed in it. It ate the skin in narrow strips. Where a grain had come away with the skin, it worked around the grain and left it on the leaf.

Outside, the water was beginning to lift a pebble out of its bed. Each time the pebble lifted, a little clay went out from under the end of the step. The mortar there was sound, but the tile had cracked behind the part that showed. When the last of the clay went, that end of the step would come down. It had very little way to go. It was enough.

The power waited until the pebble lifted again and set it just beyond the hollow it had made. The current took it a little farther and laid it broadside against the tile. Silt began to collect behind it. The first grains went through; then a shred of dead leaf caught, and the grains began to stay. By the time the water clouded over, there was something under the end of the step again.

There was also an egg the power could have opened. It could feel the young one put its mouth to the shell. It waited through several of those movements before it understood that the mouth was not yet pulling. It was opening and closing on the same place. The shell there was a little thinner than it had been the season before.

The power went away without touching it.

Much later, when the red corner had gone out of sight under the silt, the shell split where the mouth had been. The young one put two feet through and caught hold of the root that had grown between the dead branches. It came out with a piece of shell on its back. At the next fork the piece caught and came off. The young one went back to it and ate the rim, which was soft, and left the rest wedged in the fork.
