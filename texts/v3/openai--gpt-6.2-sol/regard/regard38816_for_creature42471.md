The power waited through a cold flow before it touched anything. It had thought the cups were failing. When the first leaf folded at the knock against the lintel, it saw that the plant had already begun to make use of what the cups had become.

It waited through the still water too. The first leaf to close was not the first to open. That one stayed folded for nearly a day after the others had spread, in the thin wash that came through the doorway. There was no injury in it. It was a different leaf in a different place.

At the saddle, the power found a road stone lying flat beside the hole it had stood in. A white tip had grown almost to the hole. The water pushed it down into the hollow and drew it out again. There were two broken tips in the joint behind it, worn blunt at the ends.

The power set the stone upright. It took two tries to find the way it had stood. On the second the stone went down a little farther, and a dark seam in it met the same seam in the paving.

Then the power went back to the house. It did not free the cups from the stem. A little ridge held the cord where it entered, and on either side of the ridge there were new leaves. To take out the cord would have been to take out something living. But the cup that struck the lintel had worn a shallow groove there. In the groove the power turned the cup a finger’s breadth, until its thick foot took the blow instead of its rim.

At the next cold flow it made a duller sound. No leaf closed. The power had got it wrong.

It stayed. In the long quiet afterward, as the leaves opened, it watched the stem swell back around the cord. That swelling turned the cup a little further. At the next flow the foot struck the wood and the rim struck the cup beside it. The leaf by the door folded. So did a leaf that had not folded at a sound before.

Far down the valley the green went out on one shelf and began on another. At the saddle, behind the upright stone, the white tip lay still enough for a grain of silt to rest on it. The power had meant to see whether it would reach the next joint. Before it did, a second tip came out on the sheltered side of the first and went back the way the plant had come. There was a crack there, too fine to see until the tip entered it. The power left the stone where it was.
