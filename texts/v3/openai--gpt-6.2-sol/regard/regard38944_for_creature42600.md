The power came on a clear afternoon and waited until the swallows had gone. From the ledge where the fox slept it could see the whole town. It could also see, which the fox could not, that a flat stone halfway down the new slope had nothing under its lower end but a little wet sand.

By morning the sand would be gone. The stone would slide across the shallow run and come to rest against the dam, just where the old beaver would be working. The power saw where her forefoot would be.

It did not hold the stone in place. There was no good reason for the stone to stay there, and once it had fallen it would make a deep, still pocket where the minnows could rest out of the current. The power waited until the old female had gone upstream for her cutting. Then it lifted a grain of sand.

For a little while nothing happened. A second grain rolled into the place the first had left. Water ran over it. The stone settled without a sound, then slid the length of itself and struck the dam. A wren came out of the roots and flew low over the pond.

Upstream, the old female stopped chewing. She stayed still long enough for the piece of bark in her mouth to soften. Then she ate it and brought the cutting down.

The stone had left her a gap of a different shape. She tried the cutting across it and tried it endwise, then took it away and came back with a forked one. While she was gone, a minnow found the still water behind the stone. It ran forward into the current and was carried back, and did this twice more before it found the place where it could hold.

The power stayed on the ledge until the fox came down. It moved over to give her the dry part. At that hour yesterday’s rain was still coming through the rock. The fox nosed a drop where it hung from the lip, caught it on her tongue, and lay down. Below her, the old female had got the fork to take. One of the kits came out and put its front feet on the stone, which was not a part of the town it knew. For a while it stood there with the water running between its toes.
