The power came in the afternoon, when the underside of the seat was warm.

It found that one of the wedges had split. The split began in a strike mark and ran inward almost to the end of the wedge. On a cold night the two halves moved apart. In the sun they did not quite come together again. Before the winter was over, the lower half would work out and the slab would turn a little on its supports. It would not fall far. The seat would still look like a seat. But the space on the uphill side would close.

There was a chip on the floor that would take the weight. It lay white face down among several others. The power waited until two springtails had gone from its underside into the seam, then turned it over and fitted it beside the wedge. The slab did not rise. Under it, the spider drew in her forelegs at the scrape of stone. After a while she put them out again.

The power stayed through the evening. The bird came and drank. A fly walked over the new chip and went on down between the stones. In the cup the first ice formed against the hard knot and grew outward, leaving a narrow ring of water at the edge.

By spring the broken half of the wedge had come out. It lay close enough to the support that a person might have picked it up and tried to put it back. The white face of the chip had gone gray except for a corner the drops did not reach.

On the first afternoon warm enough for flies, the spider came out and stood in the old cut. The cut was shorter now. One of her forefeet rested beyond it, on the chip.
