The first thing the power did was put back a stone it had taken for loose. It had lifted it no farther than the thickness of a leaf when every animal in the sleeping row opened its eyes. It put the stone back very slowly. The eyes closed, beginning at the far end.

For a year it came at noon and sat on the bench. It came to know the weight that arrived beneath it, and where that weight had been the moment before. Once, when the slab was late in moving, it followed the weight down through the mountain. In a seam well below the garden there was a little white patch where two faces of rock had only just begun to touch. Each noon a few grains fell from it. The apparatus had been made when there was room there for a man to put his hand.

The power waited for winter, when the low light reached the back of the seam. Then, a grain at a time, it wore the two faces apart. It kept each grain until it knew where to put it. Most went into the pale wall, which had been losing mortar on the side nobody could see.

On the first noon after the work was done, the bench gave its usual knock. A leaf turned over and six animals came out of it. One landed on the bench and drank from a twig that had caught in the seat. The power had thought the knock might be a thing it could spare them. Watching the animal drink, it was glad it had done no more than it had.

There remained the old one with the yellow foot. The power watched it drop an empty packet three times. On the fourth attempt it saw that the animal was trying to push the packet past the brass button, not up against it. There was room enough beyond the button, and oil enough still in the folds to be worth getting there. What was wanted was a place for the bad foot.

The power had one grain left. It pressed it into the face of the lower block.

The old animal came back late in the day with another packet. Its foot slid as far as the grain and stopped. It stood there for a while with its mouth full, looking, so far as could be told, at nothing. Then it pushed the packet in. The fine roots stirred, and the button turned a little, showing a crescent of brass that had not seen the light since the coat was worn.

By then the bench was in shadow. The power stayed until the animal had gone back under the lip. It could hear the small commotion there as the old one backed into the row, and after that it could hear only the garden.
