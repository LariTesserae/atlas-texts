The power came to the door while the keeper was coming out. It stepped aside. There was room to do this, though afterward it could not have pointed to where it had stood.

The keeper had the crooked tooth in its hand. It went to the rim and held the tooth with the root outward. Far below, the long mouth opened a little farther. The keeper put the tooth away. It had done this before.

The power could have brought the animal up. It looked for a while at the places where the plateau would have to break.

Then it lay down on the stone and put its ear over the edge.

What it heard was the tongue going back to the place where the tooth had been. It did this once during each breath. The interval was long enough for the keeper to cross to the house and fetch the awl, but not long enough, after that, to thread it.

When the keeper came back, the power asked if it might go down. The keeper took the tooth out again. It showed the power the notch, using its own thumbnail to show how the tongue had gone into it. Then it closed its hand. The tooth was not to go down.

The power went without it.

From the rim there was nothing to see for a long while. The keeper went back to work on the door. It got the point of the awl through both thicknesses of hide. Usually, by the time it had done that, the light in the hole was gone.

This time the light stayed. The keeper drew the thread through. It pulled the stitch tight, put a finger on it, and made another.

Below, the power had put a hand against the long jaw. It did not hold the jaw open. It waited until the tongue came round and let the animal feel the back of its hand. The tongue went away and came back. On the third pass it went a little farther, to where the wrist was warm.

The breath went out at last. Before the next one, the keeper had time to put the awl down and rub its left shoulder.

The power came back by a way that did not cross the rim. It had picked up a small piece of hide from the floor on its way out, and now asked for another. The keeper cut it one. Together they made a pad for the low shoulder. The keeper tried it in the doorway, shook its head, and moved it a little toward the neck. The power held it there while the keeper sewed.

At the next breath the old animal lifted its chin. The one beneath it shut its eye and kept it shut. The keeper stopped with the thread between its fingers. After a while the old one put its chin down on the stone. It had to do it by degrees. There was a place for it there.

The power stayed until the keeper had finished the seam. Then it took the awl to the sill and set it beside the pebble.

In the bowl, the room was empty. The keeper went into the house sideways, as it always had. A little later a hand came out from behind the door and drew the two worn boots farther in, where no one coming to the house would trip on them.
