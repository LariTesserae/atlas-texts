The power watched the young one through three strokes. The first time the bolt head rolled, the animal went after it. The second time it was already waiting on the side to which the bolt head would roll, but the water took it past. The third time it held on to the brick and let the iron go. Then, in the lull, it went after it again.

There was a white chip showing in the bank near the doorway. The concrete had worn back from it on three sides. The power loosened it on the fourth.

It fell flat. The first knock did nothing to it. At the second it slid the width of a brick and stopped with one corner in a joint. There was room under it for the young one, but not much water went through.

For most of the lull the animal stayed by the bolt head. It had found a crumb with one straight side and was trying that side against the iron. Twice it set the crumb down and went around to feel it from the other side. Then one of its feet touched the chip.

It went under. A little later the two feet came out again. They fetched the crumb and fitted it beneath the free corner, straight side down.

At the next stroke the chip lifted. The corner in the joint held, and the water ran through the opening all the way to the back. Something came out of it that might have been a little cloud of powder except that it drew in sharply from the edges when the flow slackened.

Down in the intake the eel took a fish. The loose conduit hit the ceiling three times. Under the old cover, at the turn in the path, the larger animal was still passing grit to the ends of its fringe.

When the powder settled, it began to fill the joint around the white corner. In the morning the young one would have to clear it. By then there would be something to eat in it.
