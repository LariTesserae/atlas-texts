The power came in the morning, when the ring in the bowl was easier to see than the bowl itself. It stayed through a day on which the drop did not fall.

Toward evening the old animal knocked once against the rim. The young one in the open unfolded its feet.

Above the hollow, a little way uphill, rain had worn a path around a flat stone. The path was dry now, but there were grass seeds lodged crosswise in it, all pointing the same way. The power lifted the stone. Under it were a white thread of fungus and a beetle with its legs drawn in. The beetle began to open them. The power put the stone back.

Farther up the hill there was another stone, set on edge, with nothing living under it. The power leaned it a little into the slope. Most of the rain would go on down the old path. A little would run along the underside of the stone and into the earth above the root.

Then it waited for rain.

When it came, the first drops on the hill made larger marks than there was water to fill. For a while nothing reached the hollow. The long shadow came over the roof and stopped where it usually stopped. The root bent, and a drop fell before the old animal had got its throat in place.

Another followed while it was drinking. That one struck the back of its head. It backed into the nick and lay there, blinking.

By morning there was a pool the size of a plum skin in the deepest bowl. The amber creatures had to go a little higher around the rim. One stopped at the clear lip, although there was water all the way around it, and drank there as before.

The power stayed long enough to see that the middle bowl remained dry. Then it went away.

Late in the next dry spell, when the pool had drawn down below the old dark ring, the young one that lived in the wall came out. It had grown too broad to come through headfirst. It put one hind foot into the water, then the other, and stood that way for some time, with the rest of itself still in the hill.
