The power came in while Iona was trying to get the mirror out from under the reeds. She had put it down flat to free both hands, and the bed had come down just far enough to catch the handle. She could see a piece of her own sleeve in it.

“Would you lift this?” she said. She thought it was somebody from below.

The bed rose a little.

“There,” she said. “No farther.”

She got the mirror out. Then she put it back in, at a better angle. There was the root she had been looking for all afternoon. It went through one hole in the tray and came back through another, and in between it had put out a fine white brush. The brush was what kept it from drawing back through either hole.

Iona sat back on her heels. Whoever was holding the bed had not shifted their grip. She could see both ends of the tray now, and there was no hand at either end.

“Can you keep it there a little longer?”

“Yes,” said the power.

She took out her knife, but with the weight off the root she could see there was no need for it. The tray was made in two pieces. She had always thought the seam was welded; from below, with the mirror turned, she saw the heads of three screws. She fetched the driver from beside the door. The first screw came out red all the way to the tip. She set it on the slat beside her knee.

The swift went by.

Iona looked up. There was nothing above the parapet but the cloud, which had thinned enough that she could make out a roof below. She reached for the second screw, then took her hand away.

“This might be a while,” she said.

The tray stayed where it was.

She went and sat on the edge of her panel. Water from one of the coping holes came down a vine and fell from the lower of the two leaves at the bolt. In the time she waited it fell three times. Then the swift came back, a little below the top of the wall. For a moment she could see the pale underside of its throat. It turned out over the drop and she saw the whole of its back.

By the time she went back to the bed she had forgotten where she’d put the screw. She and the power looked for it. The power found it in the fold her trousers made at the knee.

They took the two pieces of the tray apart and laid them back with a narrow gap between them. The root hung through it without touching either side. When the power let the bed down, Iona watched it settle, then waited through a whole filling to make sure the pieces didn’t shift. One did. She drew a line on it in chalk so she could see how far.

Before she went to bed she took the fault card off the door. On the front she wrote, *Reed tray. Needs a better fastening. Bring two people; one to hold the bed.* She left the card loose in its clip so it could be turned over without taking it down.

In the morning she heard two pairs of boots on the stair. One stopped to let the other catch up. Iona put her feet on the floor. She had time to pull on both socks before they knocked.
