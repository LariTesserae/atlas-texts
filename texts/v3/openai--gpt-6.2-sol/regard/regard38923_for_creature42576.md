The power came at night and waited on the step of the water office. There was a crab there already. It moved a little to one side, though the crab would have been just as happy to climb over it.

By morning it knew what the white fringe under the middle house was doing. The roots had reached the edge of a drain. They grew a little way into it every afternoon when the water stopped, and the first rush afterward tore them off. The house had been growing the same inch of root for most of the summer.

There was room on the other side of the drain. The power could have carried the roots across, but it did not know what the house would make of the ground over there. There had once been a house on the east street that put up a pantry in the middle of its own stairs.

In the morning Iva found the frog in the shoe. She took the shoe outside and laid it on its side in the gutter. Her son came out in his socks to see. They waited until it became clear that the frog was in no hurry, and then Iva went back in to get dressed.

That afternoon, when the water stopped, the power held it still a little longer.

The heron caught its fish. The younger brother finished his egg and folded the shell into a piece of newspaper. The tram came and went. In the middle house a white root lengthened into the drain, and then another did. Neither reached the other side. The first one touched the bottom and turned along it. The second found the first.

The power let the water go.

One root came off. The other lay flat in the current, so flat that a little crab went over it without breaking stride.

At work the next morning Iva found the floor of the middle house as low as it had ever been. She knelt to paint the line and put her free hand down in the place where she usually felt the floor give. It held. She shifted her weight onto that hand, then sat back on her heels. From there she could see out of the window at about the height the woman must have seen out while she was sewing. The bottom pane was green at the edges. A long way down the west street someone was shaking out a pillowcase.

Iva painted the mark at the height the water had actually reached.

For several days the elder brother went on putting his good ear to the floor at three. He had heard the water come back late and wanted to hear it do so again. His brother said he ought to use the interval to put a hand on the cupboard. On the fourth day he did. He felt nothing at all, though afterward his hand smelled of the orange his brother kept in the top drawer.

The frog left the shoe during breakfast. Nobody saw it leave. The boy brought the shoe in and set it beside its mate, and Iva, finding both of them dry that evening, put them up on the shelf for somebody smaller.
