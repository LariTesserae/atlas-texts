The power came on a night when the room did not stop at the red marks.

It held the two faces of the fault apart. It held them no farther apart than they had been the night before. Below, the shopkeeper heard the gutter run and went on counting her change. Under the pavement, the thin surfaces came out one after another and took their dust.

The power could have gone on holding the mountain. Instead, during the next inspection, it let the woman with the pad feel the weight that had come to rest against its hand.

She was kneeling to lift the conduit strip. She put the strip down again and fetched a gauge. Then she fetched a second gauge, because the first one said that the room was narrower at the front than at the back, and this was not something the room was supposed to be able to do.

There followed a dispute over which district owned the back wall. In the meantime she came down every evening to read the gauges herself. She noticed that the warm square went cold a little sooner each night. She also noticed that, if she sat on the pavement, it stayed cold until she got up. From then on she waited standing in the doorway.

She drew a shallow pocket beneath the old curb on the back of a work order. It would give the creature somewhere to withdraw when the room narrowed. The water office sent the drawing back with a blue line through the pocket: at that depth it would catch the night vapor. She drew it again, entering from the side, with the lip left whole.

They cut it by hand. The shopkeeper came up to see the work and put her palm in the trough the bad wheel had made. She remembered that her father had owned a good wheel, too. He used to say he would put it on when the bad one wore out. She had been ashamed of that wheel as a girl; you could hear him coming before you could see the stall. She told the woman with the pad, who had been about to smooth a chipped place in the trough and now left it alone.

For eleven nights nothing moved into the pocket. On the twelfth, the back of the bench cooled and a patch of the wall beside it grew warm. Two nights later there were fine violet grains on the lip. The creature had found a place from which it could reach the fault without having to put all of itself in the way of the closing stone.

That evening the power took away its hand.

The mountain moved. The woman watched one needle go past the mark she had penciled on the gauge and stop. From the floor came a tick. She waited with her coat over her arm until she heard the other one.

They left the red dashes where they were. Beside them the woman fixed a little brass plate with the date and the depth of the pocket, so that nobody would fill it in.

By then the power had gone down to the lower street. It watched the shopkeeper pull down her shutter, step out of her shoes, and stand in the gutter while she looked for her keys. The water covered her toes. When she found the keys she stayed where she was a moment longer. There was no one waiting for her to move.
