The power came to the turn in the road while the driver still had his hand on the horn. It waited for him to take it off.

There had been a machine under the hill. Two of its wheels were still there, nearly a mile down, where the men who took out the hill had cut the shaft and left the bottom works in place. The wheels had not turned for a long time. The little animal stood with three feet on the road and one on the place where the hill had been. It lowered its belly as far as it could without sitting down.

The power could have turned the wheels. It went down to see what else was there.

One of the old animal’s feet stood in the cut above the machine. For years it had been taking its weight off this foot, a little at a time. There was nothing wrong with the foot. But a length of flat iron, left from the shaft, lay across the place where it would come down next. At one end the iron was loose. At the other it went under the foundry wall. If the foot came down on it, there would be a noise in the pattern shop and perhaps a crack in the wall. The animal would lift the foot again. It had done so twice.

The power did not pull the iron out. A small black animal was wintering in the space beneath it. Instead it went to the far end, under the wall, and laid a finger on the iron. When the foot came down, the loose end sank into the dust. Nothing moved at the other end.

Under the north awning the porter heard the foot land. He thought at first that the sound had come from the gate, but it went on after the landing, a soft grinding sound, and he could follow it across the square. He had never heard anything cross the square before. He stayed in his chair and turned his head.

At the turn, the driver had started uphill again. In his mirror he saw the little animal get all four feet off the road. It stood for a while on the bare ground. Then it went down onto its belly. The driver did not see what it did after that.

It was nearly a year before the old animal took another step. By then there were three little ones at the turn. They came on different nights. There was no machine running there, but the weight in the rock changed as the old animal leaned, and on some nights there was enough of a tremor to feel through the belly. The little ones did not all like the same part of it. One went back to the works.

The porter still moved the second chair on the first of the month. In the spring he found he had been moving it after the foot had gone. He took his lamp out and stood in the print. Across the square, under the awning, he could see both chairs. He set the lamp down to brush the dust off his trousers, and heard the glass touch stone from where he stood. When he picked it up and tried again, there was no sound.

The power had taken its finger away. By then the iron was bent.
