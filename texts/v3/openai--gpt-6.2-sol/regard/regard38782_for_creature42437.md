The power first puts a finger under the gray foot at the back, thinking to ease it out. The eye opens. On the other side of the seam the five pads flatten until their edges are almost too fine to see. The far wall draws inward. A grazer loses its footing and hangs by one foreleg over the slit.

The power takes its finger away. The pads spread again, and the wall goes back far enough for the grazer to get a second leg onto a vein.

It stays at the entrance for several days. From there it can feel the oldest animal take up its slack. It can feel a foot lift three rooms away, where there is as yet no water. Once, when the foot lifts, nothing comes. The grazers eat underneath it anyway.

On the last night, the drop hanging between the lips begins to freeze. It freezes from both sides, leaving a clear strip down the middle no wider than the ribbon. The young animal reaches for the soft rim and finds it hard. It reaches again a little farther down. Each time it draws back, a thread of itself stays on the ice.

The power puts its finger into the drop, not under the foot this time. The water nearest the finger stays liquid. All around it the ice thickens, taking the shape of the two lips. The animal finds the soft place. It feeds there for a long while, its gray foot wrinkling and smoothing in the dark. Once the clear pads let go, all five together, and the power thinks it has misjudged again. Then they settle lower on the far wall, where there is still green to hold to.

By morning the lips have met. The animal has eaten a dark line between them. When the power withdraws its finger, the little water left in the line runs down into the slit. It falls for some time before there is any sound.

Later, when the ribbon is gone, a bud opens and lays its afternoon across the gray foot. The eye has closed for good. The foot lifts all the same. A grazer that has spent the morning going around it walks straight through the dry oval, stops halfway across, and begins to eat.
