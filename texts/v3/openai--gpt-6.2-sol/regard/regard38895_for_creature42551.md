The power listened for several nights before it did anything. At first it took the beat of the two pumps for a fault. Then it heard Nell, coming down the stairs with a bag of onions, stop at the second turn because one of the pumps had missed half a beat.

It left the beat alone.

There was a bearing in the smaller pump that would not last the winter. The power could have made it last. Instead, on Tuesday, while Bassi had the casing open, it let a little oil run off the worn place so that he saw it in the light of his torch. He fetched Nell. She wiped her thumb on her trousers and put it where he showed her. In the tin that evening there was a part number and, below it, in Nell’s writing, *Get two this time.*

The power followed the order as far as a desk upstairs. There it found a sheet on which the cost of the bearing had to be entered against the cost of a night callout. The box for night callouts was empty. In another drawer it found the agreement under which Nell had come to live behind the switch room. It was for six weeks.

On Thursday the woman who approved the orders came down to find out where the new bearing went. June was having lunch, so Nell turned over the bucket for her. The woman sat with her knees almost level with her chin and asked, among other things, how often Nell had to get out of bed.

“Only when it changes,” Nell said.

The woman waited for a number. Nell thought about it.

“I couldn’t tell you how often I wake up to see if it has.”

The woman wrote that down. She went back up carrying the old bearing in a rag. What she did with the sentence took longer than what she did with the bearing.

By spring there was a second dry chair and a night rota with four names on it. Nell’s was still there. The first evening she took off, she went to the allotment with two milk bottles. Someone on the rota had asked her what the lower tank ought to sound like when it was nearly full. She had started to write an answer, then crossed it out and taken him round to hear.

She watered the onions and sat on the boards at the edge of the plot. It was too early in the year for the shadows in the photograph, but she stayed until the shadow of a fence post reached the toe of her boot.

Below the yard, a drop struck the tin an inch in from the hinge. Nothing had been done about that. The lid was good.
