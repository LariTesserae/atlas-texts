The power watched the animal go back for the one that had fallen out of the drop. Until then it had been thinking about rain.

It could have made rain without clouds. It could have put an ounce of water into every court and kept it there through the afternoon. But it had seen a drop turn a street over, and it did not know what a full ounce would do. It could find out. The trouble with finding out was that the animals would be there.

Toward evening it went to the fallen leaf. On the north side the trapped animal was still alive. On the other side of town it stood under the rib with its chin up. Both its mouths were empty now.

The power took a little of the weight off the leaf. It did not lift it high. The leaf was holding a stem bent nearly double, and the stem, if let go, would have swept the floor of the court. The animal felt the change and drew up one hind foot. Then it drew up the other. It lay for a while with its belly showing, blue except for a narrow strip of nap down the middle. The power waited until it had crawled out before letting the leaf settle.

Under the rib on the other side of town, the animal lowered its head. It went away a little stiffly, though nothing had fallen there yet.

For several evenings the power returned to that court. There was a crease at its edge where the leaves would come almost together and then draw apart. It watched a young one sleep there, waking each time the two surfaces ceased to touch its sides. It watched an old one miss its hold and fall through the crease. The old one landed safely, but on a street from which it took the rest of the night to get back.

On the fourth evening the power made a place in the crease about as long as the old animal. It left the leaves free to move and made the place stay. When a shoot grew through it, there was still room beside the shoot. When the court closed, the place opened into the next court. A drop could lie in its lowest part without running out. There was nothing to prevent an animal from drinking it.

For a long while none went in. They put their mouths to the entrance and withdrew. Then one came carrying a young one whose feet had begun to go dull at the ends. It stood half in and half out and lowered its chin. The young one slipped into the water. The adult took a drink from the other end of the drop and went to scrape a shoot.

The power had meant to make more such places as soon as this one was found. But the next night the old animal came back with a strip of skin. It wedged the strip against the shoot so that one end hung in the water. By morning the wet had climbed the strip, and three of the little ones were lying along it with their feet in the air.

The power had not thought of that. It stayed to see what they would do when the shoot grew.
