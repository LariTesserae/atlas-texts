The power waited until evening. It had seen the animal go back for the grain, and wanted to know what else it would go back for.

With the gate down, the animal climbed to the joint. It put one foot under the lip and stayed that way for a long time. The power could not tell whether it was touching an egg or only holding on. It left the lip alone.

In the yard there was a broken flowerpot. One of its pieces had a rim thick enough to stand on. The power brought that piece down and set it on the floor, just beyond the fan, with the curve toward the stairs. It tried the piece with the weight of a man coming down backward, his wire already in his hand. The rim held. A heel coming down there would meet it before it met the sand.

In the morning, when the gate went up, the first grains fell where they always had. A little green thread caught on the broken pot. The animal stretched out and ate from it without leaving the fan.

The next time the slots needed clearing, the man felt the rim with his heel. He took it for a bit of brick the mason had left and shifted his foot. He got the reed out on his second try. Coming up, he laid it across the opening while he fitted the tile, then remembered and pulled it clear.

By then the fourth egg was empty. On the underside of the broken pot, where neither the man nor the noon light could reach, a pink point moved a little way, stopped, and moved again.
