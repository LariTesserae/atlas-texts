The power did not quiet the mountain. It listened until it could hear, inside the low note, a third sound. It was the sound a flat stone makes when it is almost, but not quite, bearing its weight on another stone.

The third wall had been built over a place that moved. One of its slabs had slipped down into the opening and lay flat there. Each time the thing beneath the saddle shifted, the slab went with it a little way and caught. The shallow impressions in the grit were where it had tried to put its weight without touching the edge.

The power waited for the high note and turned the slab over. There was a pale worm on the underside. It turned the slab a little farther, so that the worm was underneath again.

For three tides the third wall was still the last to settle. On the fourth it settled second. The thing beneath the saddle had found a position in which it could rest. A fish came to the ridge and hung there for a while, opening and closing its mouth. Then it swam over the top.

Under the overhang the little animal took a breath. The power could have made the interval longer. It could have held the water up until the animal had eaten its fill. But the animal had already let down its thread, and when the bead went sideways the power saw that it was following a crease in the returning water. It let the water return.

It did look for the other animal. In the course of looking it found stones that had gone out of the current years before they broke from the ridge, and a fish that had not yet come to the saddle. It did not find the animal. When it came back, it did not touch the place where the rock dried first.

By then the limpet had its foot over the rim. It reached down to the clean place and drew back. The power had taken this for fear. Now, being close enough to see the underside of the foot, it saw the grit come away on it. There was less grit each time. The limpet had been cleaning a place to stand.

The power stayed until the next coincidence of the notes. The foot came down, spread, and held. For a moment the shell rested slantwise on the rim, its whole weight on the small patch of bare rock. Then the shell came over.

When the water returned, it swept the bowl clean. The six empty shells came back one by one. The limpet was still outside, holding on.
