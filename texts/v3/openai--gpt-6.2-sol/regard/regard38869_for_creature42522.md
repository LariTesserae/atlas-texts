The first thing the power did was wait for her to come out.

It had taken the hollow under the snow for a place where she had been. Then one of the dents moved. A foot came out of it and went down a little farther on, and the whole drift settled without breaking. She stood beneath it for another hour. The power had come at noon and could have stayed no longer without making the day longer, but there was no need to stay in the light.

In spring a woman lifted the tread. She had come to find out why the gutter had begun to tip twice in quick succession. She put a leather bag over the keyhole so no one would drop anything through it while she worked. In the sudden dark the insect went on pressing an egg into the fold.

The woman found a chip of stone under the stop. It was smaller than any stone she would ordinarily have bothered to pick out of the sole of her boot. She took it out with the corner of a rule, tested the gutter, and wrote down how far it traveled. Then she saw that the fold had opened at one end. She fetched her pliers.

The power could see what would happen if she closed it, and what might happen if she did not. The fold was not coming apart. It had been that way on the day the gutter was carried up the stairs. There was still a pencil line inside it, beyond the reach of the insect’s head.

While the woman was getting the jaws of the pliers around the metal, the power let a drop fall on the back of her hand. She took her hand away to wipe it. In that interval the insect backed out of the fold.

The woman held still. The insect stood with her forelegs on the very edge of the gutter. The gutter tipped once, cleanly, and she drew both feet back.

The woman put down the pliers and took up the rule again. She measured the opening, and beside the figure for the travel of the gutter she wrote, *End of fold open 1 mm. Sound. Leave as found.*

By the following spring the pencil line inside the fold was gone. The note was still there, in a book in a drawer two streets away. It would be seven years before anyone needed it.

The power went on coming at noon. Some days there was nothing behind the tread that needed doing. On those days it listened for the ice to fall.
