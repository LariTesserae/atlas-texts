The power came on a Sunday, when there was no washing and the trams ran far enough apart for the rail to cool between them. The mother was halfway across the gravel. She had put her left foot down four times without moving forward.

Under the square, water was passing through a pipe that had been laid before the laundries were built. At one of the joints it made a faint, uneven beat. The power followed the pipe to the place where it passed under the old curb. There was a space between the pipe and the stone no wider than the blade of a knife. It pressed a pebble into the space.

The curb began to tremble. It was not enough to stir the grass growing over it. The mother put a foot on the stone and went along it to the alders. The next Sunday she took the same way without stopping.

In September the slip fell. It broke into three pieces, one of which went farther than anyone would have expected and lay almost at the foot of the platform. There was nothing in the hole. A starling stood in it that afternoon and pulled out a pale thread of root, which it dropped.

The power did not put the slip back. It waited through the first rain that lasted all night and saw where the water went. Most of it ran down the new face of the bank and out through the gravel. Some followed the buried edge of a cobble and dripped into the place where the three animals slept. They moved a little farther along. By morning the smallest was asleep with its mouth resting on its mother’s foot.

When the city sent someone to look at the square, the power went with her. She had been asked whether the depot could store its spare rails there. She measured the gate and the distance from the gate to the pump house. Then she had to wait for an extractor to stop before she could hear what the man from the depot was saying about the drains.

In the quiet she heard water running below the curb.

“Is that on your plans?” she asked.

It was not. They lifted the lid of a chamber just outside the fence. The pipe was still doing work that both of them had supposed was being done by a newer pipe in the road. It would have to stay accessible. In the end the rails went on the loading platform, which had been built to take a good deal more weight than that. The men set them down on blocks, leaving the space beneath as it was.

The power stayed until the first frost. There were things it could have done about the frost, but it watched the smallest animal find a snail in a bolt hole and carry it to the curb. The stone was warmer on the side that faced the laundries. The animal braced the shell against it and struck once with the outside nail of its right foot. From the broken bank came the soft tap. A moment later, from under the cobbles, its mother answered.
