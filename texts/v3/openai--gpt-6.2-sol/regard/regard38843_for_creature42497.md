The power came while the old woman was working the stem into the wall. It waited. She had got the angle wrong the first time and had to draw the stem out and turn it. On the second try it went in almost to her thumbnail.

Below her, the lizard with the missing toe had found a place where the warmth would last until morning. Below the lizard, water moved through a seam in the mountain. It passed within the length of a man’s arm of the lowest goat room, then turned and went down into rock that had never been warm.

The power followed it down. There were roots in the seam, white at their ends, and farther down there were blind things no longer than the nail the old woman had used. It took some time to see how much water went past all of them.

That winter, in the goat room, a drop gathered on a spur of stone that Maret’s father had been meaning to knock off because it caught the hay basket. The oldest goat licked it. By the time she came back from her feed there was another.

Maret’s father put a cup underneath. In the morning there was enough in it to wet his mouth, which he did before it occurred to him that somebody else ought to see how much there was.

For a week people came down to look. Those who had seen the cup empty were more impressed than those who had seen it full. The mason lay among the goats with his cheek against the floor and watched the drop form. He said not to touch the spur, not even to smooth it. He made a little shelf for a jar, cutting the bottom to fit a rise in the floor.

The water did not amount to a well. It amounted, in time, to one less jar on a pack animal. Sefa asked the bearers to put wood in its place, and that winter she kept the kitchen warm a little longer after supper. Her brother took off his sandals earlier and earlier in the evening. She sent him to the west end just the same.

In spring the date put out a second leaf. By then the old woman couldn’t get down the last stair, even on her hands and feet. Maret asked if she should bring the plant up. They both knew she could get a knife under it if she didn’t mind what happened to the root.

“No,” the old woman said. “Tell me which way it’s leaning.”

Maret went down to look. From standing up it seemed to lean out over the wall, but when she put her face near the paving she could see that the new leaf had come up on the town side. The old leaf was the one that leaned out. She called this up, and the old woman asked how far apart the leaves were, and Maret held two fingers between them and called that up too.

There was more water in the mountain. The power could have brought it all to the surface. It stayed through a second dry season instead, to see whether it had reckoned rightly about the roots and the blind things. It had been wrong about one root. It moved the place where the water divided a little farther down.

No one in the goat room noticed. For part of an afternoon the jar filled more slowly. Then the drops came at their old intervals, and the goat, who had been listening with one ear turned toward the shelf, went back to her hay.
