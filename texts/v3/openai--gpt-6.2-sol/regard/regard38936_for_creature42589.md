The first thing the power did was put back a stone. It had lifted it to see how the dust stayed dry. While it held the stone, a young wall-tender came up on its knuckles and reached into the space where the stone had been. Its wrist went over the edge into the water. The power put the stone back and waited. The young one felt all along the inside of the loop before it found the mark it wanted. Then it went away at a good pace, with one finger out of its fist.

The power had meant to find out how the walls worked. After that it watched from farther off.

There was a place on the plain where two of the low waves sometimes met. Most days there was nothing to distinguish it. The amber gathered there in broad folds, and the wall-tenders went out with empty hands and came back holding the folds against their bellies. But when the waves met, the water stood up between them. It stood no higher than the knee of a wall-tender. That was enough. They could keep their footing in either current, but for a little while there was no footing that would serve in both.

The power watched one of them set its load down and unfold its hands. It was carrying a child. The child had just learned to tuck in its fingers and kept them tucked even after the water got under it.

There was no stone nearby to lift. The power put a little of its own weight into the plain, and the plain took the weight and dipped. The top of the standing water tipped into the dip. For three breaths the child lay in water shallow enough to get its hands down. The grown one took it up by the loose skin along its back. The amber went on without them.

In the morning there was a hollow there, about as long as a wall-tender lying down. Water collected in it. A three-winged creature struck the water with its short wing and was surprised to go nowhere. By evening there were grazers at the edges. The power saw what it had done and did not fill the hollow in.

The wall-tender came back two days later without the child. It went on its knuckles around the hollow, putting its chin down now and then to look along the stone. At the end nearest the feeding ground it laid a small, flat pebble. The next evening there were two pebbles, far enough apart to set a load between them. After that there was a short wall.

The power could have made the two waves miss one another. It followed them back far enough to see what that would mean. One passed over the crack where the pale animal fed and brought the water to its mouth. The other went through a reach of old rushes and broke the stems that had not opened by themselves. So the power left the waves their long approaches. Where they met, it held the hollow open. As the stone wore down, it let the bottom down with it, no faster than the wear.

A long time afterward the child, grown now, brought a child of its own to that wall. It set the little one between the pebbles while it took a tough leaf and split it with a thumbnail. The little one reached down and found water on both sides. It kicked once, experimentally. The grown one, which was looking at the leaf, put out a hand to steady the stone.
