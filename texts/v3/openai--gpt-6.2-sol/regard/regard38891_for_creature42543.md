High on the wall, behind a row of houses, one of the trees had begun to die. You could see it from the rim once you knew which tree to look for. It still had all its leaves, but after a sheet of water passed over them they stayed bent in the direction the water had gone.

A root of that tree carried the tap from a corner below the square to a room on the far side of the basin. There were other ways to get to the room. A young one could do it between the two feedings of an old one, provided it knew which side of the white root to pass on. But there was no other way to send that particular tap.

The power that had been watching the city followed the sound into the wood. In one place, where the root went under a boulder, the living part had narrowed to a strip not much wider than a pad. Each time a drop struck the flap, that strip moved. None of the rest of the root did.

The power could have made the tree well. It went up to look at the crown before it did anything. Three younger trees had come up through it. One had already laid a branch across the outer room of a house. Another was growing into the open air with nothing under it, its leaves small and close together on the side the water struck. In a pocket of soil between two of the old tree’s roots there was a fern with a new leaf wound tight as a fist.

The power went back down to the corner.

A resident came out near dark and put a pad against the flap. It held it there a long while. When it let the flap go, the tap came back at the corner but did not reach the room across the basin. Sometime in the night the last strip of the root had come away from the stone.

The power put a finger in the space where it had been. At the next drop, something in the far room stirred. A little later the tap from that room ceased. It was a very faint one, and the power had not noticed it before.

For a while this was all the power did. There were mornings when neither resident came to the flap. There was a whole day when one held down its tap and the other went on sounding. Toward evening a body came around the corner carrying a green stone. It went into the room without stopping at the flap.

The work of replacing the root began some distance uphill. The residents brought round stones to the base of one of the younger trees and laid them in a row, leaving a wet gap between the row and the wall. It wasn’t possible at first to see what the gap was for. A fine red root came down into it. Twice it found its way between the stones and twice someone came and moved a stone so that the water ran along the wall again. By the time the root reached the corner, some of the young that had watched the first stones being laid were too broad to go through the gap where the work was done. They brought smaller ones and showed them where to put their pads.

The new root did not go to the old room. It ran into the root of another tree and came out under the steps of a house two doors farther down. No one moved the flap. Instead the resident in the old room began to go out after feeding and lie across those steps. The householder there made room. In time there was a shallow place along the threshold where both could lie without either having to keep a pad braced against the wall.

The power waited until the new root was thick enough. Then, one morning, it took its finger away.

The first drop made a different sound at the steps. The resident who had come from two doors up lifted an edge. The householder beside it did the same. They lay without moving until another drop struck. Then the one who had come from the old room went down to the corner and put a pad over the flap.

There was quite a long wait. From where the power stood, it could see the householder feeling along the underside of the step for the place where the new root came through. At last it found it, and pressed.
