The power came low over the gravel, and the gravel began to dry.

It went up again at once. From higher up it could still see the thin collars of water where one stone touched another. The young animal had learned to turn the stones with the side of its nose. It was getting better at this, though by now most of the stones it turned were ones it had turned before.

The doe had gone no farther than the next shelf. She stood with one forefoot raised. In the old days she would have put it down hard enough to call the young one. Now she put it down as softly as she could.

All across the plateau there were places where a little water would have made a difference. The power could see them from where it was. It could also see what a long rain would do to the animals in the grit, which were waiting for dry air to open their wings. It went out over the rim instead. There, where the warm air came up, it cooled a piece of bare rock about the size of the doe’s back.

Water gathered on the rock. Most of it ran down the face of the escarpment, which was not what the power had intended. It tried again a little farther inland.

There was no stream to speak of. A wet line came down a shelf and disappeared into the gravel. But at the foot of the shelf it passed over a stone the young one had nosed aside, and a little water collected in the place the stone had been. The young one found it by putting a foot in it. It drew back, lowered its head, and drank.

The power stayed until the doe had come back and drunk too. Neither animal took all the water. Later the little hunter came down off the shelf and put its forefeet in the hollow the young one had made. It drank with its eyes shut. For as long as that took, it made more noise than either of the animals it had been following.

Toward morning the doe started into the wind. After a while she stopped. There was no second sound now, not even a faint one. She stood there long enough for the young one to come up on her left and brush the wet hair of its muzzle against her leg. Then she went on, leaving room on that side.
