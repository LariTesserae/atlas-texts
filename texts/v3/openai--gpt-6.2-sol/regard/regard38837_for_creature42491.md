The power came on a dry evening, when the snail had been under the brick for six days.

It knew the snail was alive. It could have known this from the film across the shell’s mouth, which was still whole, but it knew it also from the snail’s side of the film. The space in there was smaller than the space under the brick. The snail had withdrawn as far as it could go. There was nothing to be gained by going farther, and nowhere farther to go.

The power stayed until morning. A woodlouse came to the underside of the brick and tried two hollows before settling in a third. Above it, the leaf the snail had not eaten turned a little on its stem. The last of the water behind it was gone. From the lane came the sound of a broom, which approached the entrance and passed it.

There was rain that afternoon. The first drops struck high up and left round marks that dried almost at once. Then water began to come off the roof. At the missing tile it fell in a broken stream, struck the clean clay, and ran toward the old foundation. There it met a ridge no higher than the thickness of the snail’s shell. The ridge turned the water under the last brick.

The power moved one brick crumb. It was a flat crumb, pink on the broken side. Set on edge at the end of the ridge, it sent most of the water through a break in the buried course. The black earth took it readily. Enough still went under the brick to darken the clay around the snail.

It was a long time before anything happened there. The fern lowered its fronds under the weight of the rain. A line the spider had laid came loose and drew up against the dock. By the time the shell moved, the roofs had stopped dripping.

The snail put out one upper feeler, touched the clay, and took it back in. When it put the feeler out again, the other came with it. It crossed the wet ground and went up the wall to feed. The power went with it as far as the second course, at the pace the snail went. There was a place where the green band narrowed to almost nothing and began again on the far side of a joint. The snail searched the bare mortar for a while before it found the new edge.

In the morning the pink crumb had fallen flat. A little black earth lay on the clay where the water had gone over. An ant came along the ridge, stepped onto the crumb, and continued down the other side. Under the brick there was a dry space just deep enough for a shell. For the time being it was empty.
