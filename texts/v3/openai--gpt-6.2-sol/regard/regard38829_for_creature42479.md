The power stayed until the eggs hatched. It had meant only to look in, but it could not see how the young got from the ridge inside the pot to the floor. In the end it saw one let go. The current took it through the handle hole and set it down two steps below. It was smaller than any of the grains it would later carry.

By then the power knew about the roof. One of the ribs had split where it entered the wall. In dry weather the split was white and scarcely visible; after rain it held a black thread of water. The rib would go at that end first. Three slabs would follow it, and the lowest would land across the outlet. It would take very little red grit to stop the water after that.

There was a woman who came twice a year to clear leaves from the inlet. She did this from outside with a bent wire. She had never been in the room. The next time she came, the power let a drop fall from the split onto the back of her hand just as she reached in for the wire.

She took her hand out and looked up. Then she went home for a ladder.

She brought her brother the next day. They lifted the three slabs and put in a new rib. The brother wanted to mortar the join while they were up there. The woman had spent the first evening on the ladder, trying to see where the old rib ended in the wall. She had seen the flies go out. She put two fingers under the last slab while he bedded it down, and he went along the join with his trowel, scraping out what squeezed through.

One of the old slabs broke as they were lowering it. A corner went into the water and lodged against a step. The woman reached for it with the bent wire, but it slipped beyond her reach.

For a few nights the current scoured the brick around it. Then the corner settled, leaving a shallow space underneath where the water ran more slowly. The first larva to find it went in headfirst and had to come out backward. By the end of the week the clay under there was green.

The power did not wait to see what would happen to all the young. When it left, the fish was lying in the gutter and the shrimp were coming down toward it. Above them, on the new rib, a fly was walking along the pale stone toward the gap the woman had left. At the edge it stopped to clean one of its feet. There was room to do that without folding its wings.
