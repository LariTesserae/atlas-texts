The next afternoon, when the brick begins to turn in the heat, something holds it.

It is held from the street side, low down, where the collector used to put a thumb. There is no thumb there now. The brick gives its usual small click against the jamb, but the black line does not close. On the inside, the fringe of the old plant stays round.

The power that holds the brick could take it away. For a little while it considers the room without it: the whole square of evening light falling on the dish, the wind taking a shorter way through, the loose seeds carried well past the threshold. The light would do the plants no good. And the seedling, which has grown across the join, would go with whichever surface held it the more firmly.

So the brick is held where it is. It takes most of the afternoon. The outer face gets too hot for the collector’s thumb, had the collector still been coming, and the oven bricks sound their note and lose it. Meanwhile, inside the little space, one fringe thickens enough to show a pale tip beyond the jamb.

Just before the wind reverses, the power sets a grain of grit on the worn step. It tries one grain and then another. The first would keep the brick too far out and spoil the draft along the floor. The second is flat on one side and scarcely wider than the fringe. The brick settles on it as the street cools. There is still room for the brick to turn, but it cannot turn quite as far.

The wind comes back. The seed under the threshold rattles against the dish. One of the big leaves slips loose and folds against its neighbor. No oil spills from the ridge.

For several days the seedling’s leaf remains folded. On the fourth day its tip catches against the jamb and the rest of it opens as the brick moves away. It is a narrow leaf. The half that faces the street soon takes on the color of the dust there. On the inner half, where the two fringes meet, a clear bead forms and draws back into the seam.

That evening a new leaf begins on the old plant. There is room for it behind the brick. It grows inward.
