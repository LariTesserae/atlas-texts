The power came at dusk, when there was nothing to lift.

It had seen the lip fail twice. It could have made a lip that would never fail, and for a little while it considered doing so. But the shelf would go on rising, and a lip that held here might do harm in water the shelf had not yet reached.

It went underneath, where the leaf lay along the wound. The creature had begun to close around it. By morning either the leaf would come free or the new skin would close far enough to tear it when the water took it. The power could not see any good in making the two pay for being of use to each other.

Out beyond the shelf the last warm water was spilling off the summit. The power held back a little of it. It let the rest go on down through the dark, past the ledges and the collars with their stones inside. What it kept was perhaps a cupful. It let that much through the nick, a little at a time, all night.

In the warmer water the root reached the end of the seam. There it divided. One hair grew out into the current and was lost. The other turned back along the underside of the stone and found a roughness no wider than itself.

At the turn, the creature lifted the lip.

The power had not expected that. It had thought the creature would leave the stone another day, now that the pocket was closing. Instead it took the whole weight of the lip, with the ribbon still caught underneath, and drew it a little way out from the shelf. The root held. Water got in behind the flat stone and washed loose a sprinkling of pale mineral. The creature waited until the last of it had gone, then set the stone back tilted, so that the nick no longer mattered.

By noon there was water moving on both sides of the leaf. In the slight give of the root at each swell there was a sound the creature had not heard before. Twice it took the sound for a loose grain. On the third swell it let it pass.

The power went down then, to see what lived below the reach of the turn. It took no warm water with it. It did not yet know what would be welcome there.
