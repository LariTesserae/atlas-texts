The power came in the afternoon, when the current was slack. It could have come at any hour, but this was the only time it could reach the lower rail without folding a leaf against its stem.

It knelt in the yellow rectangle. The pale tips stood all around its knees. It did not take this for a welcome, though it would have liked to.

The maintenance card was right about the dish. There was one in a shallow recess under the rail, with a curled strip of blue paint in it. The power set the paint on the rail and put the screws in the dish. Behind the cover it found that the little gate which admitted warm water to the garden was opening only the width of a fingernail. Each afternoon, when the heating circuit closed, the water stopped pressing against it and it swung free. Then the pumps took up their load and it jammed again.

The power held the gate lightly between two fingers while the current came back. It could feel, through the metal, both the force that wanted to open it and the uneven place that caught. From the lower shelf came the faint knock of a root against the back of a tray. It was the sort of sound a person might have heard for years and supposed was part of the pump.

There was a good deal the power could have done about the garden. It could have made the two brackets numbered 24 agree. It had already put a finger on the empty one when it noticed, among the thin shoots that had turned back from the wall, a new leaf no bigger than a clipping from a fingernail. It was the first leaf out there. A shoot coming back had rested on a shoot going out, and between them they had managed to hold it. The power took its finger away.

It took the gate off its pin and rubbed down the place that caught. Then it put the gate back and waited through a full turn of the heating circuit. The current still slackened in the afternoon. At other hours it went through the room a little more slowly than before, and more of the warmth reached the western window. The little paired plants there made no immediate use of it.

Before it went, the power returned the screws to their holes and the dish to its recess. The scrap of paint had drifted off the rail. It found it against a grate with the clear leaves and put it back in the dish.

For several weeks the gardener did nothing at the yellow line that it had not done before. Then, on the side nearest the wall, a new shoot stood up far enough to put out a leaf. The leaf opened over the rectangle. Its shadow passed across the first two letters of **KEEP CLEAR**, but the stem stayed outside.

There was still room to kneel. There was a little more shade in which to do it.
