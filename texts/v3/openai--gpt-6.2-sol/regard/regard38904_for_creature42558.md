The power came in the late light and stayed after the white rim of the basin could no longer be seen.

There was still a snail under the sunken stone. The white door had been shut all summer. The power could have lifted the stone without breaking the clay around it, or taken away the door without hurting what lay behind it. It did neither.

That year the first rain was brief. The drops darkened the flat one at a time, and for a while it seemed they would join. Under the bluff not a drop fell. The water from the seam went on running down its stripe.

At the corner of the sunken stone, the power loosened a little clay. It made a hollow no deeper than the mark a grass stem leaves when it lies pressed into mud. Enough rain collected there to run under the stone. The rest of the flat was pale again before dark.

Nothing came out that night. On the second night a feeler appeared at the corner and rested there. The snail went perhaps the width of its shell before turning and going under again. It had found something to eat on the underside of the stone.

The power stayed until the next rain. This one lasted two days. Water stood over the backs of the stepping stones, and when it drew off there was no sign of the hollow. The snail had gone as far as the middle tuft of grass. There were two ways back from there, one to the stone and one to the seam. By morning it had taken one of them. The clay was too wet to keep the thread of its passing.
