The power came in the dry weeks and waited through two rains before it did anything.

In the first rain the drop fell where it always had. In the second, water came down the root in a thread that did not break. It ran under the middle stone and across the dust. The gecko came back from the slope while it was still running. She went in by the usual gap, lifting her belly clear of the wet, and later came out the same way.

By morning the dust had settled to a dark crust. The water had stopped a finger’s breadth from the shelf. The eggs were dry.

Outside, tangled in the grass above the steps, there was a dead fig leaf with its stem still on. The power took the stem and caught it in the fine roots just above the middle stone. It left the loose end pointing into the litter. Then it waited for rain again.

The first bead hung where it had hung before. As it swelled it touched the stem, and a little of it ran sideways. The rest followed. The leaf tipped under the weight and let the water go among the old leaves. By the time the fig leaf had softened and fallen, a finer root had grown down along the stem. Water took much the same course without it.

The next cricket that fell by the deep cup went to the frog. The gecko had better luck that night on the slope. She came back with a moth, stopped under the middle stone to swallow it, and went in.

When the first egg opened, the little gecko spent most of the morning on the underside of the shelf, next to the shell. It came down after the light had left the bush. At the gap it found the two deep prints, put one whole foot in one of them, and stopped. A drop gathered overhead and went down the fine root into the litter. The little gecko stayed where it was until a springtail crossed the stone. Then it went after it.
