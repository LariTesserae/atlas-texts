Below the lowest winter shelter there is a fork with a smooth sliver hanging in it. It has been there so long that the cord has grown into the branch. People sometimes replace the sliver, thinking the notches have worn away. The oldest one anyone remembers was smooth too.

There is a way down. It takes three days, and at the end of it there is a broad shelf of living wood with nothing growing on the outer edge. Something lives under that shelf. In the brief light season you can see where it has worn the bark pale by putting its head out. The rest of the year you can tell it is there by bits of husk coming up past the edge. It eats with its head down and blows the husks out through its nose.

It has six feet, of which it uses five. The sixth is caught in a fork that was wide enough when it put its foot there. There is no wound. The wood has grown around the foot so slowly that the foot has gone on growing too. In winter the being lies with its belly against the fork and keeps both itself and the wood warm. In the wet months it reaches far enough to drink from a cup that fills only when it rains hard.

The power that came to the high country found it in a dry spell. It could have opened the fork. But there was a nest on the underside of the shelf, and a store of husks pushed into a seam where the rain could not reach them. The being had young. When it slept, they came out and walked on the back of the caught foot.

So the power brought water first. It brought it down one leaf at a time, along a route that would still be there when it left. This took some doing. A drop will go a long way along the underside of a leaf, but there is no persuading it across even a small interval of air. The power bent the tip of one leaf until it touched the next. By morning the cup had an inch of water in it. In the afternoon the being drank, and two of the young came along its neck and drank with it.

Then the power loosened the wood on one side of the foot. It did not open the fork. For a while the being was only able to turn one toe. It did this in its sleep at first, and later when it was awake. A month passed before it pulled its foot clear.

It put the foot back almost at once. The fork was a good place to brace itself while it washed the young.

By the time the new leaves turned edgewise, all five of them had been out on the shelf. The smallest liked to go as far as the pale place and lie there with its nose over the drop. The old one would gather it in with the foot that had been caught. That foot was broader than the others, and not so good for walking. It was very good for this.

The power stayed until it saw the being leave the shelf. It went no farther than the cup. There was a way around the back of it where the footing was good, even with a broad foot. On the way home it stopped to eat something it had never been able to reach. It ate for a long time. Then it took some back.
