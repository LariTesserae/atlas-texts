The first thing the power did was hold the screwdriver.

It had worked loose at the thick end of a stone. The camera would have shown it hanging by the last ridge in the red plastic, with its shaft pointing down. It would have fallen straight, like the washer, and gone into the fissure between the legs. The power put a finger under it. There was no need to put it back. When the resident woke, it came down the nearest brace and pushed the handle in with the end of its body. Then it fetched a smaller stone and wedged it behind the first.

The power had meant to do something about the noise. It had heard it a long way off. Now it went under the ledges and listened where the white animals kept their eyes. In each hollow the note was a little different. A young animal in a shallow hollow and an old one worn halfway into the rock opened their mouths at the same instant, though there were forty metres of cliff between them. The power went farther down, to the empty hollows, and heard the differences there too. It did not turn the noise off.

At the third governor, the resident went around touching the uprights. At the last one it stayed a while. Then it went out to the crack and took out a stone it had put there some years before.

The stone began to tremble as soon as it was free. On the journey back, the resident stopped twice and set it down. Each time the trembling ran out of it into the ridge. It was a thin grey stone with a white seam along one edge. The resident carried it up to the top of the rail.

Across the drop, at the bent corner, one of the white marks lifted.

The resident turned the stone and struck it once against the steel.

The sound went out into the basin. It went a surprising distance considering how little of it there was. But at a certain distance it was no longer distinguishable from the sound that was there already.

The resident waited. Then it struck again.

What the power did next would not have registered on any of the crew’s instruments. It did not make the stroke louder. It held the small difference between the stroke and the rest of the sound, all the way across the water, and let it go against the inward side of the bent rail.

There was a long interval. The resident laid the stone flat. It had started down toward the insulators when the answer came, two strokes so close together that the second caught the steel still ringing from the first. The power had not had to carry those. The bent corner gave them a note of their own.

After that, the power expected a great many strokes. There were none. The resident went down among the legs and moved two jaw tips to make room on the shelf. Then it brought the grey stone down and set it in the space it had made.

Toward the end of the day a dark animal came up beside the deck. Its skirt was full of young, all mouth and no jaw. The resident went to the edge as it passed. For a moment its free end hung out over the water. Then it let go of the deck and caught the skin between two of the young.

The animal went out over the drop. It did not go toward the other governor, not directly. For a long time it seemed to be going nowhere near it. The resident had settled into a fold that opened and closed as the animal swam. At each opening there was less of the ridge behind it.

The power stayed at the third governor. It had seen how a thing could be gone a year from this place and leave nothing a camera would know to look for. It kept the shelf as it was. Once, when a six-footed animal came up the brace, it let it pass. There was nothing on the shelf the animal wanted.

Much later, from the other side of the drop, there was a single stroke at the bent rail, a pause, and another. The power did not answer. It went down to the crack, where the thin stones stood with water on either side, and listened until it could hear, under the full weight of the sound, the last of the ringing.
