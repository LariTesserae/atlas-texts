The power came in a year when the cloud reached the rim.

There was no wind at first. The snow went out of sight, and then the far shelves, and then the lip of the shelf nearest the saddle. Water stood on top of the leaves. The six-footed animals came out to drink and kept putting their heads underneath.

By the second night the water had found a break in the rock. It ran down the break and into a seam that had never been wet from above. On either side of it the living margin began putting down a wall. The work went well for a while. Then the water rose behind the wall and spilled into the next seam, and the leaves on three shelves turned silver at once.

The power had been there long enough to know that this was wrong.

It followed the break down into the mountain. In one place the living margin had gone around a wedge of dry stone many times. The oldest wall there was scarcely thicker than the newest. The power stayed at that place through a discharge and felt how long it took the living part to begin its work again. It was a little longer than it had taken the last time.

On the shelf above, a hunter had come home without a glider. Its thread was wet and lay in a loose heap beside it. A six-footed female was carrying her young up the slope, one at a time, to the warm crossing. Except at the crossing there was nowhere left to set them that they would not slide. The hunter watched her pass. By the time she went back for the last one it had risen to its feet.

The power could have taken the cloud away. It could have held the hunter where it stood. For a time it did neither. It was looking for where the water would go if it were let out of the break. The first way led down through the roofs of the grubs. The second would have taken the warm crossing with it.

A long way down, below even the place where the hunters waited, there was a slab with nothing growing on it. The frost had lifted one end of it a little each winter. The power put a finger under that end and raised it just far enough. Water came out between the slab and the mountain, slowly at first, brown with material that had not moved since the shelves were made. Then it fell free. Far below, where there was weather every day, it became an ordinary waterfall.

The hunter took one of the young. The mother came back with the last one and nosed the empty cup until she had pushed it off the stone. She lay down with the two that were left. For a long time the power stayed close to her. She slept, then woke and combed the underside of a leaf with her throat. There was still plenty of water there.

Toward morning the discharge came on the highest shelf. It stopped halfway along. In the old break, the living margin had reached the edge of rock it had never touched before. There was water on the other side, but it was running away now, leaving a thin wet surface behind it. The margin divided. One part went on laying down the wall it had begun. The other went a short distance over the new stone.

The power had meant to lower the slab when the cloud passed. It left it as it was.

Years later, in dry weather, a little water would still come that way. It did not always fall clear. Sometimes the wind laid it against the cliff, and a dark patch would appear on the bare face and be gone by noon. A glider struck the slab one night and worked the edge of a wing into the narrow space beneath it. Nothing came of most of the eggs. But on the underside of the slab, where a drop could hang all day without being seen, a fern put out its first curl.
