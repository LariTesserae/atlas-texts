The branch had caught by one fork. The other was under water, knocking against a stone. Each time it knocked, the end with the leaves on it moved a little farther out.

The power came down to the bank and put a hand on the branch.

It could feel through the wood what the water was doing all along it. There was a place a foot below the surface where the bark had worn through. Silt was coming away from the caught fork in little rolls. If the power pushed the fork deeper, the whole head of the branch would swing out into the channel. It held it where it was.

Under the leaves the young one moved its hind feet. The mother drew them in against her belly. She had mud in one ear. When the branch stopped knocking, she raised her head and listened for a while, then put it down again.

The power stayed through the morning. It took very little strength. The difficulty was that there was nothing to do with the strength from one moment to the next except go on using it. Rain ran down the branch and into the hollow of its hand. A wagtail walked out on the wood, looked into the hollow, and picked a midge off the knuckle.

Across the channel, the dial on the oldest house went around. The two young under the floor lifted their heads. The house rose a little on its clay feet, and they went back to sleep.

By noon the bank had grown nearly to the end of the branch. The mother went down into it headfirst. She came up twice to move the young one, first out of the rain and then out of the way of the mud she was bringing up. On her third trip she took it down with her. The hole closed behind them.

The power loosened one finger at a time. The branch did not move. It was bedded now in gravel for most of its length, though the outermost twigs still shook in the current.

It was nearly dark before the mother came out again. She swam upstream close to the bank, crossed in the slack above the cut, and went under the house. A little later she came back the same way with one of the young in her mouth. She made the trip again. On the last crossing she carried two small beetles, one in each cheek.

The power had meant to go when the branch held. It stayed to see where she would put them. There were no willow leaves on the new bank except the ones she had lain under, and those were stuck together with mud. She peeled two apart, working at the stems with her forefeet. One tore down the middle. She used it anyway.
