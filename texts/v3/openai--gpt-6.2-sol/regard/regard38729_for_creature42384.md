At noon the shadow reached past the shelf. Where it crossed the channel, the combers stood on such stones as they could find and held their forelegs out over the water. They had no way to tell how much of the animal was coming.

The power watched from the south bank of the trough. There was a barley-sized animal in the water at the bottom of a boot, going round and round the heel. Each time it put down its foot, the boot shifted. The power took the boot off and laid it on its side with the opening at the lip of a well. It took a long time for the animal to come out. Its foot stayed on the leather after it had gone over the edge.

By then the young on the shelf had begun to turn. The first made it into the channel with a few combers still on its back. Two more went after it. The last had got a stone under one of its slits. When it tried to let water out, the stone turned a little, but did not come free.

The power put a hand in the water. The young animal rolled away from it and drove the stone farther in.

So the power went down into the channel and lay where the adult had lain in the morning. The water there was deep enough to cover its shoulders. It let the current divide around its body, leaving a little of it to run between its arm and the shelf. In that slower water the young animal stopped rolling. After a while it let out a thin stream along its back. The stone came out with it. There was blood on one corner, gone before the stone reached the bottom.

The power stayed where it was. The animal had not yet learned to keep its nostrils up when it went sideways. It went sideways twice, and the second time it righted itself.

Farther down the channel the animal whose shadow had been on the shelf came through. It took some of the combers. Others climbed onto the power’s shoulder, where the divided current brought them grit and eggs from both sides. They disputed the place. A little red brood changed hands, or some of it did.

When the last of the young had gone, the power stood up. By then it knew what width of opening would let water pass the shelf at the speed the adult had made. There was a loose stone nearby that it could have set in the channel to keep that opening. It had its hand on the stone when the hammering stopped. In the quiet it felt the stone move. Something underneath was pushing it up, not far, but with every pause it gained a little.

The power took its hand away.

It came back at the next falling water. There were no young on the shelf, only one of the little upright animals with the warm water in it. A comber stood over its opening. One of its hind legs had slipped into a notch and it could not get the leg out without tipping its brood into the cold. The power held the rim steady until the comber found a better place to stand. Then it sat a while on the shelf, with one bare foot in the water and the other in the boot, which was still wet.
