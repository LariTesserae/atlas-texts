The power came to the mountain at the hour when the small animals turned their bellies to the west. It had meant to stay until dark. It stayed long enough to see the herd come round twice.

On the first passage it saw an old grazer leave the others and go down to a seam below the last houses. There was nothing there but a pale crescent. The grazer put its mouth to the stone, then stood over it for much of the afternoon. Two young ones came and stood under it. When the wind dropped they all went on.

The power could have made the seam violet again. It could have made the crust grow as fast as the herd could eat it. It looked a long way along that possibility and saw, among other things, an animal born in a year of abundance walking to the end of a slope with nowhere else to put its feet.

Up near the summit it found a slab that had parted from the mountain on three sides. The crack on the fourth was full of grit. The slab would come down in the warm part of a day, when children used the passage behind the bakery. There was no advantage to anyone in its coming down then. The power held it until a quarryman, looking for a place out of the wind to eat his lunch, sat on it and felt it move.

The quarryman marked the slab with chalk and went to get his brother. They took it down on a sling. Part of it became a bench in the covered square. The rest lay in the yard for years.

There were some things the power did that nobody made anything of. At the bottom signal, on a night of copper dust, the sister’s basket caught the last step. She had put her wrist through the loop of the line to free a hand for her key. The basket took its weight and the loop opened. She sat down hard on the step. One of the things she had bought was a cup for her brother, whose cup had lost its handle. She unwrapped it there to see whether it had broken.

Her brother came to the door after he showed the light.

“Did you hear me?” she said.

“No,” he said. “I was coming anyway.”

In the years between the passages of the herd, the woman above the houses grew unable to carry her glass to the upper slopes. On clear days she took it as far as the quarrymen’s bench. From there she could see a seam across a cleft she had never been able to get around. It had always looked bare to her. The power waited for a late afternoon, when the woman had the glass out and was cleaning it with the inside of her sleeve. Then it let a little dust settle.

There was violet on the far side of the cleft. Not much: a strip no wider than the edge of a hand, running down into shadow. She watched it until the light went and came back the next day to see if she had mistaken a shadow for it. On the third day she brought paper. At the top she put the year, and beside it, in smaller writing, *first seen*. She did not know how long it had been there.

When the herd came round again, one of the young went down to that seam. The woman was dead by then. Her sheets had gone to a niece who kept them flat between two pieces of kiln facing. The niece had never seen a grazer close to, and when she got word that they were coming she went up too early. She waited through the warm part of the day, ate what she had brought, and nearly went home. In the evening she saw the young one come away from the seam. For a little while there was violet on the underside of its mouth.

The power left the seam as it was. It stayed until the animal found the others.

Below, someone at the lowest signal put a cup down and went to the door. There was a basket coming round the bend, its line just visible against the stone. The power could have brought it the rest of the way without a hand on the line. Instead it went down beside it at the pace of the person coming home.
