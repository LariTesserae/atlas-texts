The power came when the pumps changed over. It had mistaken the animal for a number of small animals, and wanted to see what it had missed.

The threads went out. The shrimp rose from the wall with all its legs working. A fleck of blue enamel passed through the animal without touching it. Then the current took hold, and the threads broke.

The power could have kept them from breaking. It had already begun to do so when it felt, in the threads nearest the wall, the animal pulling the other way.

It let go.

For the rest of that shift it stayed beside the green lamp. It watched a knob take in something too large for it, turn it over with its lip, and let it go. Two hollows farther on, another knob took the same thing and ate it. Neither appeared to mind the arrangement.

By morning the power knew what it would do about the room. It knew also that what it would do was not enough to make the room safe. The factories would close one day. The harbor would back up into the culvert. There were changes the animal could live through and changes it could not, and the power did not yet know which were which.

But there was a pump upstairs that had been slow to start for eleven days. The night foreman had compensated by starting it early. Each night he started it a little earlier. Soon the two currents would meet for longer than the animal could hold, and on that night the threads would not be the only things to tear.

The power went upstairs.

The foreman was eating a pear over the logbook. When he turned the page, a drop fell on a figure he had written eleven days before. He wiped it with his cuff. In doing so he looked at the figure, and then at the one he had just written. He put down the pear and went to get the woman who knew the old pumps.

That was all the help they needed.

Below, at the next change of shift, the animal made its shape. For a little while the power was everywhere the water touched it. It felt the washer strike, the shrimp draw in its legs, the grit settle at the crossings. Then it felt the animal begin to let go, here a fraction before there. It had thought the current did all the breaking.

When the room was loud again, the shrimp came out of the white place. Something soft had caught under the lip of the lamp. The shrimp took hold of one end; a knob took hold of the other.

The power stayed to see which of them would get it.
