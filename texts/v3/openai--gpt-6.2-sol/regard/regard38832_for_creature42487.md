The first time the power came to the hollow, the smallest cup was emptying a little before the others. There was no way to hear this from the edge. The water went down without a sound, and the young beneath the rim let go onto stone that was still quite dry. They went toward the note in the split, which was the only sound that came from anywhere high up. By the time the rain began, some of them had gone far enough to be on the wrong side of it.

The larger adult spent a long while looking for them. It went back and forth over the same stretch of floor, pushing once with each foot. Whenever it found one, it stood still while the little feet worked their way into the hairs around its mouth. Looking for the next one, it had to be careful not to brush off the ones it had.

The power sat at the smallest cup through a filling. Near the end, when the upward current was scarcely stronger than the warmth rising off a hand, it felt water moving sideways into a hairline crack. The crack ran beneath one of the places where the young held on. A new rim would have been an easy thing to make.

Instead the power reached down into the stone, below the last foothold, and pressed the two sides of the crack together. Then it waited. The cup filled so slowly that a frog laid eggs in a folded leaf, and the tadpoles had begun to move, before the water turned.

When the three cups emptied, the power was still sitting there. From so close it could hear the young land. Their feet made a noise like drops that had fallen a very short way. At the edge, the smaller adult gathered four in one sweep and missed a fifth, which went into the litter and began at once to climb the wrong side of a leaf.

There was a last one. It came down onto the foot of the power and stood with all six feet planted, its long wet hairs laid against the skin. A leaf tipped somewhere to the left. The power could feel the animal lean before it took a step, and kept its foot flat until the animal had stepped off.
