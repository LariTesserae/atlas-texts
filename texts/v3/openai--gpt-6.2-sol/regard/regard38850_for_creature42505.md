The power came at the hour when the old animals brought grit to the empty bowls. It went down the slope by the way they went, stepping wide of the pebble. From below, the plateau was no quieter. Each knock put a little crease in the dust at the bottom of the bowls.

It watched an old animal lower its head. There was nothing in the bowl but the grit it had brought the evening before. It waited until the grit in its mouth was warm, then let it fall. On its way uphill it stopped once and turned its head, though there had been no sound behind it.

The power followed the young one that had left that bowl. It had gone a great way on its first leg. The others had come after it, except for one hind foot, which was caught between two stones. The stones were a stride apart on the plateau. Where the foot was, there was not room for it to turn.

The power did not move either stone. It put a finger down beside the foot, on the side where the leg could bend. The young animal drew back at the touch. A little later it tried again, bent the leg the other way, and got free. It stood for a while with that foot off the ground. Then it set it down, and the knock went out through the basin.

The old animal was halfway up the slope. It stopped. The power could have brought the young one back, but already it was putting another foot down, farther off than the first. The old animal stood until the second knock came. Then it went uphill.

Toward morning the power returned to the western rim. A round stone there had begun to turn. One of the small white animals lay on its back in the groove, walking in the air. Over it a long foot hung so close that its shadow was no wider than a tooth mark.

There was room to put a finger in, but the power kept its hands still. The small animal caught a leg on the edge of the groove and rolled over. It climbed the stone and went down the other side. The foot came down.

By noon there was nothing to show where the power had been. In the basin the old animal tipped a fresh mouthful of grit into a bowl. A clear head lifted to meet it. The old animal held back the last grain until the little mouth opened again.
