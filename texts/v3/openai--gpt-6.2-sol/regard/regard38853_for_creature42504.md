The pot held leaves cooked down with a little salt. The man had made enough for the mother to eat when she woke and for the children to eat at once. He had not reckoned on the old woman, who had eaten at noon, but she took a spoon from the drawer and went round the pot after them. There was a good deal left in the corners.

By then the man had the baby. He walked with it as far as the door and back. At the door the floor was cold through his feet; at the window there was a draft. After several trips he found a place halfway between the two where he could stand still.

The mother lay down in the next room. Almost at once she called out.

“He’s here,” the man said.

“I know where he is,” she said. “Did he just swallow?”

“No.”

A little later she asked him again. He said no again, but this time he first looked down at the baby. She heard the interval.

The power that had been attending to the plateau could hear the baby through both walls, and through the floor, and underneath the sound of every motor on the street. It could hear the little muscle in the mother’s ear draw tight in expectation. It could have put her to sleep. Instead it came to the door of her room, where she could see it without lifting her head.

“If you like,” it said, “I can tell you which sounds are his.”

“Can you tell whether he needs something?”

“Yes. So can the man who’s holding him.”

The mother considered this. “Sit where I can see your feet,” she said.

There was no chair. The power sat on the floor.

For a while she asked about every sound. One was the baby. One was the lid settling on the empty pot. One was a noise the power couldn’t find anywhere outside the room, though it had heard her hear it. It said so. She pulled the blanket up over one shoulder. After that she didn’t ask about every sound, but only the ones she couldn’t bear not to ask about. Then she slept.

In the other room the man’s arm began to go numb. The old woman showed him how to put one foot on the low sill and let his knee carry some of the weight. It was an awkward position, but the relief was immediate. In this way they got through the better part of the evening.

Toward morning the mother woke and lay looking at the feet on the floor. She could hear the pump through the wall and somebody rinsing the pot. What she wanted, she discovered, was to go and get her baby. She put both feet down.

From the doorway she could see that the man had fallen asleep in the chair and that the old woman now had the baby. The old woman was looking out at the avenue. There was nothing on it yet. All the same, rather than call to her from the door, the mother went and stood beside her for a while to see what the light would reach first.
