The power came in a year when the row on the plain was buried. It found the animal by following a scrap of food into the ridge. The scrap took most of a day to pass through the crack. At the far end it went not to the nearest branch, which had already begun to die, but past it to the branches that were still working their way up.

There was a point of stone inside the crack. The gray thread lay against it. Each time the current lifted the body on the plain, the thread tightened, and the point entered a little farther.

The power could have lifted the ridge. It would have had to lift the sponges with it, and the brittle stars under the sponges, and the mud in which the animal had found a purchase. There would still have been the question of where to put it down.

Instead it waited for the current to turn. There was a short time between the pull from one side and the pull from the other when the thread went slack. In that time the power took the tip off the point. It took off less stone than there was in a flake of mica. What remained was still narrow enough for the thread to take hold.

That winter another fold of mud came down. Several of the old branches died. On the plain, one reached the inside of a shell and followed it the wrong way, toward the buried rim. It was a long time turning. The power stayed through this without turning it.

Near the end of winter a lip opened through the hinge. The first thing it caught was a grain of stone. It held the grain for a while and let it go. Later a scrap came down low enough to catch, and some of it passed through the ridge to the side that had fed the branch all winter.

By then the power had gone. The shell was small and lay almost flat. In the next heavy current it rocked a little on its hinge, and the lip drew in until it was still.
