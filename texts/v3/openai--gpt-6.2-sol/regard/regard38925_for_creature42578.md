The power had been there when you put your heel to the crust. It had heard the chip come free. It did not put it back.

Now it watched the youngster nose at the bright edge. On your side there was a thing you could turn over in your hand. On the youngster’s side there was only a place where the light went out.

The power gave the edge a little purchase on that side.

It took less than the making of a tooth on one of the ridges. The youngster could feel it with the pad of a foot. It pressed, and the chip tipped in your palm.

For a moment you held it level by tightening your fingers. The small foot pressed again. This time you let it go.

The chip lay dark side up. You could still see the light that fell on your hand and on the notch at your feet. After a while the chip rocked. A thin bright line showed along one edge, and through it you saw the youngster lying on its belly, working a claw under the lip. It got the chip up just far enough to look at you. Then it let it down.

You set it beside the notch, where it had been before you picked it up.

Some time after you had gone, the brown animal came back. It put its head low and looked through the narrow leaves. In one gap it could see the adult’s flank. In another it could see the youngster at the chip. It turned its head a little to judge the distance. The youngster saw the broad face in the sliver of light and took its foot away. The chip fell flat. At the tick the adult folded its flap and stood, and all three young went under it and out through a fork in the cane.

The brown animal came as far as the notch. It sniffed there. It was still thin. The power stayed with it as it stood considering the empty grooves between the ridges, and when it went on, the power went with it.

Later, one of the young came back to the chip with a piece of blade held crosswise in its mouth. It propped the edge up with a forefoot. There was nobody on the other side. There was the white notch, and farther off a brightness where the crust lay bare. It held the chip open long enough to draw a single plate from the blade. Then it took its foot away, not in haste this time, and finished eating where it was.
