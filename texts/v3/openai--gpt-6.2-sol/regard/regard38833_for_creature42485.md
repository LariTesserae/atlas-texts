The power came while the young one was still settling. It had been drawn by the noise, which could be heard a long way off in places where there was no rain and nothing to move. It expected to find something trying to get out.

During the next stroke it went under the stair. It found that the old animal had not merely drawn back from the edge. A little way inside, it had braced itself against the stone. Water went past the young one on both sides. Now and then a grain struck the old animal and stuck to it. The space in which the young one lay was nearly still.

The power stayed through a number of changes in the water. It saw a white button fall well out on a slab and be left there when the slab went dry. It saw another catch on a washer and make use of the damp underneath. For a while it considered keeping water over the whole plain. But on the underside of the platform, where the high water would have reached, there were buttons so new they had not yet turned white.

At the middle shed there was a pipe that had not carried water for a long time. Its mouth was closed by a disk of iron, and a pale stem had grown in the wet around one of the bolts. From there the pipe ran under the slabs and came up in the angle behind the bottom tread. In the years when it had been used, water had come out of it hard enough to round the edge of the stone.

The power did not take the disk off. It made a hole through the iron no wider than the stem beside it.

On the next stroke, nothing came out behind the tread. It took most of the night to wet the inside of the pipe. Then a thread of water came down the back of the stair. It carried a little rust at first. By the time the exposed slabs had dulled, the water ran clear. It was not enough to reach the young one, which was already in a good place. It went along the foot of the wall and filled two empty impressions where washers had been.

The power waited for the next low water to see whether it had done harm. The stem beside the bolt bent a little toward the hole. Otherwise there was no change it could see.

Years later, a part of the old animal came out below the bottom tread. It crossed the thread of water and attached itself on the other side. Under that small bridge the water spread. There was room beside the wall now for a young one to fall a little farther out and live.

The power had gone by then. It had left no light on the plain. When a light came on in the middle shed, the thread showed for a short way along the wall, and you could see where it divided around a grain of grit. When the light went out it went on dividing.
