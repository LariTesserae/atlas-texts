On her last day the woman ate her sandwich standing up. The stool had been washed and set upside down on the second basin. She had told the new man she would leave it, and he had asked twice whether she was sure.

At the end of the shift he came to the door with the keys in his hand. She was already across the bridge.

“Wait there,” she said.

He looked down at his feet. One was on the slab and the other was still inside the washhouse.

“Am I in the way?”

“No. That’s where you want to be.”

The powerworks went on. A tram drew up across the street and nobody got out. Under the bridge the sole put its head into the current. The man bent a little, and the keys slipped along the ring. At the sound the head went back.

“Will it come out again?”

“Usually.”

It did not come out while she was there. She had a tram to catch, and when it moved off she could see him from the window, locking the door. She had wanted him to see how broad the animal had become. It was an odd thing to have wanted so much, and she found she was still put out about it when she got home.

What the power did was farther down, where nobody was looking. There was a stone in the return channel that had turned a fraction in its bed. At low water it made no difference. At high water it sent a thin tongue of pond water back into the mountain run beneath the washhouse. Each year the tongue reached a little farther. In another summer it would have reached the quiet strip.

That night, as the level fell in the top pond, the stone settled back. It made a small sound, quite distinct from the sound of the works, though there was nobody there to hear it. Nothing downstream went dry. The snails on the stone did not have to move.

The new man took his lunch in the strip because that was where the stool fitted. For the first week he kept his keys in his pocket at the door. Then he forgot why he was doing it, and took them out on his way from the basins as he had always done.

In late summer a girl came to the washhouse after closing with a shirt she needed for the morning. He let her in and turned the tap past shut and brought it back when she was done. At the door she stopped so suddenly that he nearly walked into her.

There was a little pale thing in the run. It had got one foot onto the stone and the current had taken the rest of it sideways. The girl shifted the wet shirt to her other hand. Neither of them knew whether the thing was having trouble. They stayed where they were until it got its second foot down. Then the girl crossed, and he held the door open a while longer, in case there were any more.
