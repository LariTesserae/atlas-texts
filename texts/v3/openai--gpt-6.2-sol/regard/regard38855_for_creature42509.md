The power would go first to the lower end of the fork.

There is no water in it yet. There is a root no thicker than a hair, and on the root a bead which has taken most of the evening to gather. In another hour it will touch the wall. The wall will darken, and the dark will run ahead of the animal into the place where the fork widens.

The power would hold the bead on the root until morning.

There would be no need to hold it longer. By then the weight of the new bank would have brought two stems together below the fork. The root would be pinched between them, and what came down it would go another way.

The animal comes on backward. At the widening it puts three feet on one wall and two on the other. It tries once with the foot it no longer has. Then it turns, and eats a little from the wall that is now in front of it. It has not eaten there since it was young. The leaves are clearer than they were then.

The power stays through the rain. There is much else to attend to. On the high flats a stem has split, and in the split the young are moving their feet. The power follows the first of them down. It lands on a leaf without a tear. For a moment it is still unfolded, and all six feet go on working. The power does not tell itself that the slope needs what will become of it.

Farther down, where the new bank ends, last year’s leaves have been laid across a hollow. The rain is striking them flat. There is air beneath them, but no way in. The power lifts one edge. A heavy drop catches it and tears it as far as the stem. Three of the young fall there. One catches. It goes in folded and leaves the tip of its tail showing, as the old one did forty years ago, in a place now several feet above the ground.

By daylight the bead is gone. The old animal has eaten past the widening and come upon a root tip hanging in the dry. It puts its mouth to the tip, finds nothing it can eat, and goes around.

Above it, the twin leaves are still standing apart. Rain runs down their stems. A little of the thin light gets between them and falls on a leaf that has not seen light since it was green. The power stays to see that, too.
