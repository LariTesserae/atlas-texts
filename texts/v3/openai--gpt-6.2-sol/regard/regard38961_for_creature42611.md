The power came down the public stair on the side that people kept clear. It had no more weight than the others who came at evening, but it made the same mistake a stranger would have made: at the bottom it put a foot on what appeared to be a paving stone. The crab got up and went away. The power waited until it had settled again before going on.

It spent three nights in the town before it did anything. On the first it followed the drinker who dragged a foot and found that the foot did not hurt. On the second it went as far into Long Pocket as the warmth went. On the third it stayed with Sella’s brother while he shook the flour out of his bread cloth and into a cup. There was enough to thicken soup, if there was soup.

That evening the last drinker put its foot beside the bowl. The water left in it was too shallow to reach. The bowl moved a little on the paving as the drinker tried to tip it.

Sella came out with the cup she kept beside her bed. She poured half of it into the bowl. Then she stood there a moment and poured the other half in too.

Up on the rim there was an alder with one root laid bare along a shelf of rock. On rainless nights water gathered on the underside of the root, just as it did under the roofs. It ran to the end of the shelf and fell down the outside of the mountain. The power went up and put a finger to the stone. It did not open the mountain, though it could have. It made a shallow groove, no wider than a stalk, from the root to a seam that ran down the inside wall. Before it took its finger away it followed the seam to the bottom to make sure there were no eggs in it.

In the morning there was a wet patch high up on the cistern wall. Sella’s brother said it might be a leak. Sella said if it was a leak it was going the wrong way. They stood on the stair long enough to see a drop gather and fall, and then went home because the bread needed turning.

The groove did not give much. In summer it gave about a pot between dark and breakfast. There were nights when Sella still had to carry water down to the market with both hands. But she could leave a cup by her bed, and her brother stopped waiting at the door with flour on his hands.

The power stayed one more evening. After drinking, the small one came back to the middle of the square and stopped. The power could have found out what it was waiting for. Instead it sat down near the place where the woman with the apron used to stand. All the way across the square the newts began to come out, and still the small one did not move.

After a while the paving took the weight of another foot, close beside the first. There was a pause, and then the two sets of prints went on together. One of them dragged a foot. The power had seen that one go past the bowl much earlier and had supposed it was in a hurry.

In the morning Pell found that his chip had split. He brought a broader one from the bench and lay down to fit it. One of the newts came out over the back of his hand. When he got up, he left the broken chip beside the road, where it would be easy to find if somebody needed a smaller one.
