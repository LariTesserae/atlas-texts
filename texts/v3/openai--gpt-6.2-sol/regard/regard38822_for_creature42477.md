The power came at the hour when the bakery washed its boards. It could have come at any hour, but it had been listening to the room and had begun to look forward to that smell.

It found the little animal on the fern stem. There was water between the stem and the bed, and the animal had put out a foot over it. The power, thinking to help, lifted the stem by the thickness of a leaf. The foot drew in. It lowered the stem. After a while the foot came out again.

The power waited on the bench.

It had expected the larger animal to come back. Instead, the little one worked a grain of grit loose from a notch in the stem. It held the grain under the water for a long time. Then it let go. The grain went down and did not go far. The animal put a foot on it. The grain rolled, the foot came away, and the animal began to work at another grain.

This took most of the night. Once, when the little animal was nearly across, the floor opened and took away the three grains it had got into place. It went back up the stem. By then the power understood that it could carry the animal to the bed, but that the animal would still have to cross tomorrow.

There was a shallow pit in the rock beside the fern bed, just above the level of the returning water. The power ran a finger from the pit to the base of the stem. Where its finger passed, it took the polish off the ceramic. It did not make a bridge. It left a strip on which a grain of grit, set down, would stay.

In the morning the animal was under the ferns. Two grains lay on the strip, a little way apart. The power did not know whether the animal had used them.

Before it left, it went looking for the piece the builder had carried off. It found the lunch tin in a break room two streets over, upside down on a rack. The piece was no longer there. There was a faint track down one of the rack’s legs and along a pipe to the place where the pipe entered the wall. The power followed it as far as a patch of damp mortar. On the other side was earth, and something in the earth was taking its time.

The power had meant to bring it home. It sat a while in the break room instead. At six the builder came in and turned the tin over. He had brought a pear with a bruise in it, and while the kettle boiled he cut out the bruise and ate it first. The power stayed until he had finished his breakfast.
