The power came at the hour when the woman usually turned the clay. It stood on the other side of the wall, where the bricks were hot, and asked what she would do if she did not have to come back that evening.

“Come back for what?”

“For the brick.”

She drew the cloth away from one corner. The clay there was darker than it had been the day before. She had wrapped it too wet.

“I’d go with him,” she said.

Her brother had been going to the far edge of the plain. Every evening he took a breadfruit and went a little way toward it, and every evening he came back with the breadfruit uneaten. She knew he would get there sooner if he stopped waiting for her. He knew it too. Neither of them had said so.

“I can keep the clay,” the power said.

She considered this. “You haven’t seen me do it.”

So she showed it how to lift the clay with the cloth instead of taking hold of the edges. She showed it the hairs that came off the cloth and how to roll them free under the pad of a finger. The power had trouble with this. Its first hair went deeper into the clay, leaving a small pale hook.

“Leave that one,” she said. “You’ll make a hole.”

She told it about the cup last. It was not enough to leave the dust in it. If a person came and wanted to wet their hands, the power must let them. It must not ask them to wait until she came back, even if it thought she would be sorry to miss them.

By then her brother had come for her. He was holding a breadfruit in the crook of his arm and turning it, looking for the place where a bird had got at it. She took it from him and put her thumb on the place at once.

The power watched them go. At the seventh garden she looked back. It had sat down with the board across its knees, just as she did, but it had neglected to move the cup out of the way of its feet. She almost went back to tell it. Then her brother said something she could not hear and she hurried to catch him.

No one came to the wall that evening. The power turned the clay and tucked the cloth. When it was done, it sat a while longer. The bricks gave off their heat against its back. On the lip of a tile a bead of water grew heavy, and the power put one finger beneath it. It waited until the bead fell.

In the morning the woman found the cup where she had left it, with the dust settled on the bottom. The cloth was a little loose, but the clay was good. On the board beside it lay a strip of rind she had never seen before, yellow on one side and white on the other. She had given it to the power on her way back in the dark, when she thought it might like to taste what grew beyond the step. It had eaten the flesh and kept the rind because it did not know where to put it.

She took it to the sixth garden and showed it where she put such things, in a chipped pot behind the tree. Then, since it was there, she showed it where she slept. From the fork you could see the far edge quite well once you knew what to look for: not a break in the glare, but a little raggedness low down where the leaves came over.

Her brother was still asleep. She set one foot on either side of his and stayed where she was. There was time before the cloth would need wetting, and now there were two of them who knew how.
