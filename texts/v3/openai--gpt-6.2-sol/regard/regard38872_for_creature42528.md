The power came in a dry year and waited for the drop.

It had thought, at first, of bringing water. There was water enough above the hollow, moving through a seam the roots had never reached. But when it followed the course that water would take, it found the pale felt lifting off the shelf in one piece. It found the cup full through the first freeze. The leaf at the entrance, which had taken a winter to turn, lay flat under the current.

So it waited. When the drop came, it followed the fold to the hinge. By the time it reached the root, there was scarcely enough of it to see.

The power went up through the limestone to a place where two faces of stone touched. On one face there was a grain that had been working loose for a long time. In another hundred winters it would have fallen out. The power took it out now.

The next drop came no sooner. But it came a little fuller. After the leaf had lifted, there was still a bead on the root. It traveled as far as the hair-brown join at the entrance and hung there until morning.

In the following years the cup still dried. The leaf at the winter waterline still rose in the thaw and settled into its groove. One of the new leaves at the entrance grew past the edge of the stone that would have given it a shape. Its hinge hardened in the open, and the leaf came out nearly flat.

There was a root at the back that took no water from the change. For a while the power considered making another opening. Then, in the wet part of a year, it found a tip from that root laid across the face-down tile. The tip had not reached the tile by the shortest way. It had followed an old vein in the felt, even through the place where the vein doubled back.

The power left that way as it was.
