The smallest child has let his line go slack. When he draws it in, it comes a little way and stops. He puts his arm down to the elbow, then lies with his cheek against the leaves to get it farther in. The woman sees the heel of his hand turn up. He has found the hook and is trying to work it loose without pulling. She had shown him how to do that last summer.

Under his ribs the ridge begins to open. The two sides do not sink together. One rolls inward, bringing with it a sheet of roots that would take his arm between them.

There is a power that has been attending to the woman since she put her hand into the cold water. It takes the weight of that side of the ridge.

The woman hears the other side go. She puts a hand flat on the child's back and tells him to leave the hook. He says he has nearly got it. She tells him again, in the voice she uses when there is a pot behind him, and he lets it go. She takes him by the wet elbow and backs with him onto the growth beside the tree. The others come after her on their knees. One brings all the lines but his.

The power waits until she has counted them. Then it lowers what it has been holding. Water comes up through the leaves, carrying a pale chip of wood. The child reaches for the chip, but it goes down again.

By the next morning there is a gap the length of the woman's body. On the far side, the path rises out of the water just as it did before. For several days people come down it and stand looking across. They have to go back a long way to get around. When they reach the woman's house they tell her, with some feeling, what they were carrying when they found the path gone. One man had been bringing her a pot with a lid that fitted. He still has the pot.

The children begin a crossing lower down. They drag branches to the bank and push the thin ends in first. Most turn in the current and come to rest along the side they started from. The smallest child finds a branch with a fork in it and calls the woman to see. She comes down and sits with him. The fork is too narrow to catch on anything they can see, but she puts her foot on the thick end while he works the branch out over the water. After a while he feels it catch.

The power could keep it there. Instead it lets the child feel it slip, and feels with him the little knock when he gets it in again. This time the woman feels it through the sole of her foot. She shifts her weight onto it.

“Bring another like that,” she says.
