In a dry year the water withdrew from a joint a little inland from the rim. The small ones went first. The largest had grown around a corner in the rock, and when it drew itself out, the part that had lived beyond the corner would not straighten. It crossed the pool with that part trailing. At noon it reached a fin with a dry sunny face and stopped.

The power had been there since before the water failed. It knew where the creature was going. On the other side of the fin there was a joint still wet far down, with room on one wall. The creature had found it once as a young thing, when it could pass through a slit that would not now admit the narrowest part of it. It was going the long way round.

There was a loose flake halfway up the sunny face. In wet weather it did nothing. Water came down on both sides of it and met again below. Now the only water that reached it was a bead at a time from a seam no wider than a hair. Each bead hung a while and fell clear of the rock.

The power turned the flake over.

The next bead ran down the underside and touched the stone. It left a dark line no longer than a finger. For much of the afternoon the creature did not move. Then one of its lobes spread into the line, and the rest began to follow. By evening it had got around the fin. It took another day to get down out of the light.

The grey bird came while the drought still held. It found a strip in a joint farther along the rim and braced one foot against the stone to pull. A piece fell. The power did not catch it. It watched until the piece came to rest on a ledge with a little water left at the back.

In the first hard rain the flake washed loose. It went down into the bed of broken rock, struck twice, and lodged among stones much larger than itself. The creature on the wet wall lifted its edges at the second blow. After a while it laid them down again.
