The drop stands up, but the hook does not catch it. There is a fleck of clay inside the bend. The hook comes down on the drop and slides off, and the animal puts its foot back where it was.

Under the print a builder has begun to take its weight off the paddle. The inlet narrows. The next pulse comes a little way into it and stops.

What has been watching the plain could lift the tile. It has lifted heavier things without leaving a mark. But there are mouths on the pad beneath it, and farther along the same seam a walker has put down one foot to wait out the weight of its passengers. To lift the tile would be to lift the bank.

A little water remains in one of the whorls. It is not enough to stand on. What has been watching bends close and draws that water out in a thread. The thread touches the inside of the hook. For a while nothing moves but the fleck, which darkens as it takes up water. Then it comes away and goes down the inlet. The builder receives it on the paddle without knowing that anything has fallen.

The animal holds out its foot again. There is no drop there now.

For a long while the watcher does nothing. It had thought the animal would put its foot where the water had been. Instead the foot goes higher, and higher still, until it finds a drop hanging over the ridge. The hook takes hold. The other foot stays down. In that posture the animal is quite still, with its clear flesh stretched between the tile and the rain.

The watcher could go on. There are places on the plain where whole basins are turning over. But it stays until the second foot comes up. This takes longer than the laying of a tile. It takes long enough for a mouth on the builder’s pad to finish drinking and let go.

Then the paddle comes away. The heel of the print sinks a little, and the inlet closes. A drop that had been caught under the ridge rolls free and travels along the joint. At the bank it comes to rest against the foot of the waiting walker. The walker lifts that foot, not to climb yet, but to set it down on the water instead of the clay.
