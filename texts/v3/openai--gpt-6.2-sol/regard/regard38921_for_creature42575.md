The power came when a block beneath the great stair had begun to move. It had moved before, a little at a time, making the sound that traveled farther through the stone than through the water. This time, when it moved, the tread above it would go down at one end. The nick would close.

The power put a hand under the block and held it there through a turn of the current.

A crab came out of the joint and felt along one of the fingers. Finding no edge it could get a leg behind, it went back in. A little later seven of the young came down through the same joint, so thin that the dark of the opening showed through them. Six went on into the stone. The seventh caught on the crab and was carried out into the water, where a fish took it.

The animal on the stair raised its edge. There was nothing in the nick yet. The power had put its wrist in the way of the small return of water that brought food there. It shifted its grip. A skin with two legs still attached went round the nick twice and was drawn under.

It would have been easy to deepen the nick. There were hollows under the lower treads where food lay thick at slack water, and the power considered making such a hollow here. But when the current came back the other way, it saw what the shallow nick did. The first push of water took the grit out of it without getting under the animal. A deeper hollow would have filled, or the animal would have had to move. The power left the nick alone.

There were fallen stones at the foot of the stair. It tried one beneath the block and found that it shut off the joint the young had used. It tried another, which fit at the back but rocked. At last it took a flake no longer than the crab’s leg and set it on edge well inside, where the weight would come straight down on it. There was room to pass on either side. It withdrew its hand slowly. The block settled the breadth of a grain and stopped.

The power stayed until the water had come from both directions. On the second turn the animal began to move. It crossed the tread with its feet showing, went down the rounded edge, and continued along the next step. The place it had occupied was paler than the stone around it. Near one end of the pale place was a small heap of grit it had carried away from the nick, a little at a time, on its feet.

By then the water over the stair was dark. The power went down a step beside the animal. It did not know whether the animal would come back. There was no need to know before leaving the stair sound.
