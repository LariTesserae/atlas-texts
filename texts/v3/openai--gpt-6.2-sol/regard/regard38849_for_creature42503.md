The power came after dark, when the wedge was taking up the slack in the root. It put a hand beneath the wedge and lifted, no more than the thickness of one of the pale sheets.

All the ivory cups in the last tree went still. Farther in, among the groves that could not be seen from the rim, the spindles went on turning. The collar at the foot of the tree lifted from its ridge and did not settle on the next.

The power took its hand away. The wedge drew the root taut. The collar settled. In the outermost cup a salt crystal went over the lip.

After that the power came without touching. It watched the morning give the root back its slack and the evening take it up again. On the coldest nights a new scar began as a pale line across the underside. On warmer nights nothing happened that could be seen at the rim, though in a fissure half a mile inland a sheet would gather salt along one edge. The swelling grew by so little that the old scars were a better measure of its progress than its tip.

There was a ledge below the wedge. From above it looked wide enough to receive whatever grew down to it. The power went below and found that the ledge was the top of a flake with a clean break behind it. It would bear a little weight, and then none. The stone behind the flake was sound.

The power waited through the warm part of the day, while the root hung loose. Then it took away the flake.

It did not bring the sound face nearer. For a long time afterward the blade pointed down into a gap wider than the one it had been growing toward. There were nights when it seemed to make no movement at all.

Then, in the last of the evening light, the white line along its edge parted. One lip of the blade curled out. The other stayed where it was until the wedge cooled; then it curved inward, toward the stone that had been behind the flake. Neither lip reached it.

By morning a pale sheet hung from the inner lip. Its free edge was turned not toward the face of the rock but toward a little hollow in it, still a good way off. At dusk a salt crystal came down through the root. It caught there, in the thin edge, and stayed.
