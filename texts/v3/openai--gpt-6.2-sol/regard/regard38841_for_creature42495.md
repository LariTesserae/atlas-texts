The woman with the two cups was halfway across when the stone ticked. She stopped. The little diggers went on eating. Either they had not heard it through the flake they were working on or, being new, they did not yet know to stop.

Under the shelf, water was running out through a hole no wider than the stem of a cup. The hole was getting wider. In a moment the water would fall away, and the woman would hear it and turn. The shelf would go down first at the end she had come from.

The power put a finger in the hole.

The running stopped. The woman waited. One of the little animals let go of its flake and sat back on its folded legs. She had never heard water stop quite like that. She went on to the higher end of the shelf, set the cups down, and came back a little way to lay a stack of chips on its side. Then she took up the cups again.

The power kept its finger where it was. The diggers ate until the slick began to dry. One by one they went down to the wet edge and into the pool. The last had got hold of a good flake and was still trying to turn it after the others had left. It kept at it for so long that the woman had time to fetch the water and come back by the older path. From there she saw the little animal give up and walk into the pool with the flake balanced on its back.

Only then did the shelf go. One end sank slowly enough that the woman could see the water climbing it. The stack of chips slid a short distance and caught against a rough place. For several days the top chip was visible through the water. People who had not been there when the shelf went took it for a marker and wondered who had gone to the trouble of putting it so far out.

That evening, at the shelter, the door slab slipped while the woman was shaving a peg. She got her knee against it, but to reach the peg hole she would have had to let it go again.

“Hold that,” she said. She thought her niece had come up behind her.

The slab lifted a little.

“Not so high.”

It came down until the holes met. She pushed the peg through and tapped it twice with the hammerstone. Then she took her knee away. The slab stayed where she had put it.

Her niece was down at the pool, rinsing the cups. When she came back, the woman asked her to try the door. The girl pushed it shut with one bare foot. For the first time in weeks it went all the way without catching.

“Will it stay like that?” she asked.

“Until the floor moves again,” said the woman. She put the shavings in a cup. There were two pieces big enough to use the next time.
