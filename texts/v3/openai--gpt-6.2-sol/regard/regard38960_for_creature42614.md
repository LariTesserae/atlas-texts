The first thing the power did was take its hand off the slab.

It had meant to lift the stone a little, only at the bright edge, so that the people would have more room. Then it watched the sun go in under the bridges. The doors opened one after another, and in a room at the far end of the road a woman moved a bowl half the width of her thumb so that a sleeping child would have the last of the light on its back.

The power took its hand away.

There was a loose piece of limestone on the plateau, a long way off by the reckoning of the town. In a hard wind it rocked. One day it would come down against the thin edge of the slab and break it. The power turned the loose stone in its bed until the wind went over it without finding a purchase. A beetle that had been living under it came out, walked around the stone twice, and went in again on the other side.

That was all the work the power found to do that night. It stayed anyway.

In the morning the young man came back. He scratched at the wall and Siv told him to come in if he could do it without putting his foot in her supper. She had set the bowl out of the way the night before and forgotten it. He moved it to a ledge and lay down beside her, with one shoulder in the road.

The first of the pale animals came out. It put its feet on the edge of the leaf. The next came up close behind it and stopped. After a while the young man whispered that he thought the first one was stuck.

“No,” Siv said. “That one went yesterday.”

“How can you tell?”

“I can’t. Move your shoulder. Somebody wants to get by.”

He drew his shoulder in. A miller passed, dragging a thumbnail along the wall, and a little air came into the room behind her. The leaf rose and settled. The first animal let go. It went the length of the bed without opening its wings, and then opened them so near the floor that the young man put out a hand. It flew between two of his fingers and went out under the door.

The others stayed where they were.

Siv reached up and took his wrist and laid his hand flat on the stone between them. They waited for somebody else to go by.
