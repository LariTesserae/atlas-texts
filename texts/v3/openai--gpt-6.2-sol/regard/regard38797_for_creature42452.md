The power would come before the machine.

It would spend some time under the stair. There would be no need to stop the motor. It could hear, through the motor and the water in the main, the small dry sound of the young roach pulling its foot free. It would wait until the roach had somewhere to put the foot down.

Upstairs, a woman would be checking the delivery against a drawing. The power would draw her attention to a figure she had already checked twice. There would be nothing wrong with the figure. It would be the little arrow beside it, showing which way the floor was supposed to drain, that would make her put on her boots and go down.

She would take the stairs too fast and put one foot in the water. On her way back up she would call for a wet vacuum. Then she would go down again, this time with a light, to find out where the water was coming from.

The power would not keep the shallows. There would be people working on their knees beside the machine, and it would be a poor thing to leave them kneeling in water. The woman would have the drain cleared and the insulation finished at the elbows. She would find that the water from the rock could be led the last few inches to the drain without sealing the seam. This would take less time than sealing it. She would have to show the man with the trowel twice what she meant.

Some of the larvae would go out with the water. The centipede would lose the little flies under the drum and go hunting along the conduit instead. The spider would keep its web until the day the motor was serviced, when a man who did not see it would take it away with the back of his sleeve. The power would see it.

It would do one thing for the female under the stair. Where the stringer met the wall, it would take out a loose grain of concrete. There would be room after that for her to pass without turning. She would turn anyway the first few times. Then one night she would come to the place carrying nothing and run straight through.

On the morning of the delivery, the man with the pallet jack would see the seedling because the woman had propped the door open and the passage light was on it. He would fetch a paper cup from upstairs and lift the plant with more grit than root. He would mean to take it home. At the end of his shift he would forget it on the low wall outside, where it would get rained on that night.

The machine would fit. The man guiding it would put his hand on the rail and look up, just as he would have done. When he took his hand away the rail would hold a little of his warmth.

After the men had gone, water would gather at the mouth of the seam. It would follow the narrow groove the trowel had left, except for a little that would stay behind on the stone. Below the lamp, green threads would bend in that much water. Farther in, where there had never been light, the film would go on growing against the grain. The power would stay there awhile, where there was nothing left to do.
