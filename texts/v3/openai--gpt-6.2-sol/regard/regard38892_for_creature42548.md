The power would wait through the next rain.

It would see the seven springtails climb into the nick, the last one going in backward because there was no room to turn. It would see the young crustacean on the underside of the slab stop feeding when the current quickened, then start again. It would leave both places as they were.

At the height of the rain, when the water ran uphill, a flat grain worked loose below the mother’s seam. The current lifted it on edge. Had it fallen one way, it would have lodged across the narrowest part of the opening. The power let it fall the other way.

That was all it moved.

Two nights later the mother came down the wall to the seam. She put her feelers through and drew them back. This time, when she went in on her side, there was room for her legs to bend. She stayed there a long while, with her head in the lower passage and the last of the young still under her belly. Then she went through.

The power did not follow her at once. The young one under the slab had come to the edge of the film it had been eating. It felt ahead over bare limestone, found nothing, and backed up a little. There was more to eat in the shallow pit beside its left feet. In time it found that too.
