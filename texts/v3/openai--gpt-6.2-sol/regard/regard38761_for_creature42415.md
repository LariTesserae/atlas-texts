The next evening there is no square beyond the sill. There is the inside of a room with a low ceiling and a ridge of dust along one wall. The adult puts its knuckle down anyway. It waits until the smaller young one begins to pull at the skin behind its knee.

What has come to attend to the city waits too. It has seen a stair lengthen between a mother and her young and has seen the young one make a game of the new tread. It has seen a doorway turn aside from a falling stone. It does not make the stairs keep their length or the doors keep their rooms.

On the third evening the square comes back. The animal on the far side is already at the bottom of the steps. It has brought something down with it, a small white lump that it sets on the paving before it knocks. All the light goes out of the room in the narrow house, as it did before. The adult takes its hand off the sill.

Then the answer comes through.

There is still no square to be seen. The sill is cold, and when the larger young one puts a finger past it, the finger meets stone. But the adult knocks again, and this time there is scarcely a wait at all.

After that, a knock given in answer can get through a place that has closed. Not everywhere: it must be the same stone, though there may be half a mile of it between two knuckles, and the first knock must have reached the one who answers. For a while the long ones go on lifting their hands when the light goes. It takes them longer to learn otherwise than it takes the young.

The white lump turns out to be feldspar. The animal on the far side has carried it down on several evenings, and once has had to leave it halfway up the flight. One morning the back room opens on the square while there is still light in the streets. The animal is sitting with the lump between its feet. The adult from the narrow house goes out to it.

They sit a little way apart at first. Then the adult turns its back. The other breaks a piece off the lump and crushes it under the heel of its hand. It works at the pale patch until the adult jerks its head and strikes the paving. It stops, takes up a little more powder, and goes on with less pressure.

At the sill the smaller young one has got a rope out of the corner. It lays half of it in the shallow recess by the door. The larger one eats it. The smaller one watches this happen and puts the next rope farther back.
