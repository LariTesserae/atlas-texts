The power came on a day when the white stone did not come back.

A strip of lead had worked loose from a window and gone down the street in the evening turn. It caught at the curb. The stone rolled over it on the way out and lodged against it on the way in. By morning there was a little ridge of sand behind the stone, and the cup was on the still side. Its thread came out twice and found nothing to fold.

The power could have put the stone back. It waited through the next turn instead, to see what the water would do. The lead bent a little under the returning current. One end of it had already begun to wear a score in the white stone. In time it would cut through the hollow where the cup lived.

Farther along the curb, the loose end of the lead trembled over a joint. The joint was full of packed mud. The power took out a piece no bigger than a fingernail.

At first the only difference was a brown line in the water. Then the current got under the lead. The strip lifted, struck the curb once, and went on down the street. The stone did not follow it. The ridge of sand had grown too high.

But now a little water came through the joint. It ran along the still side of the stone and out past the hollow. For most of the day it was hardly enough to move a grain. At the evening turn the cup put out its thread, and the thread streamed straight for the first time since the stone had lodged. It caught a flake and doubled back on itself. A second flake went by while it was folding the first.

The power stayed until morning. It did not clear the rest of the joint. There was a white cup in the mud there, so small that the brown line had passed over it without breaking.

By winter the stone lay on its edge, with one face in the ward and one face out. The old empty cup had filled with sand. In the other hollow there were three red threads. One of them had found the curb. When the stone rocked, that thread went slack, and when the stone rocked back it drew straight again. There was room enough for both motions.
