The rain was already coming. The power did not put a shelf over them, or send the herd back.

It had been watching the old female for some time. At each crossing she had waited until the others were over before she tried it. This was not because she was afraid to go first. When the younger animals saw her stop to feel for a foothold, they stopped too, even when the stone under them was hot. It was easier to let them get ahead.

There was a way down from the ledge. The calf could have taken it already, by putting its soft feet into a seam in the wall. For the old female there would be a better way when the stone at the bend turned. But by then the ledge she stood on would have tipped far enough that she could not get to the bend.

The power held the ledge level a little longer.

The rain fell. The old female drank with her eyes shut. Beside her the calf kept opening its mouth too wide and getting water up its nose. It shook its head each time and raised it again.

At the bend, a slab turned outward. There was the usual small report. The old female lowered her head. A dark thread of water ran along the new edge of the stone, and she followed it as far as the bend, where she stopped and tried the first step with the bare skin above her hoof.

The calf started down the seam. She touched its flank. It came back up, knocking a few chips loose, and followed her the longer way.

By the time the ledge tipped, they were on a broad shelf below it. The herd had passed that way while the stone was still dry. The old female put her nose to a place where several of them had stood and called once. Farther along, out of sight, an animal answered.

The calf started toward the sound. Then it stopped to work at something in a crack. The old female took two more steps before she noticed. She stood with one forefoot ahead of the other, waiting while the calf got the last of a flower out of the stone.
