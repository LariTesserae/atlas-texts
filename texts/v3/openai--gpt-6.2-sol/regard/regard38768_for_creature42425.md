At noon the foot comes out again. There is room for it now, but the rock beyond the shadow is hot enough that the foot draws back before it takes any weight.

The power that has been watching could cool the whole face of the mountain. It sees, though, that the heat is what draws the wire. It can follow the wire farther than the second glint, to where it lies in the groove in the gray belly. There the wire moves a little with every breath. The groove is old and smooth, except at one end, where the skin has begun to grow over it.

The power puts no hand there.

It waits through the afternoon. Twice the foot comes out, and twice the belly tightens before the foot touches stone. The third time, the power takes the heat out of a patch of rock no bigger than the foot. It leaves the light alone.

The foot comes down. For a while it rests without weight, as it did the other times. Then the toes spread. One of them finds a ridge in the mica and closes over it.

Far down, the belly gives a small jerk. A wire goes slack. The mountain draws it out as usual, and there is the click, but only three leaves turn. The fourth has the weight of the little one on it now.

By evening the whole face is cool enough to stand on. The power does nothing to prolong the light. The little one works its way back into the shadow, missing the ridge once and finding it again. Down below, the parent rolls over. For the first time since the power came to look, all four wires hang loose at once.

After dark the jointed toe appears under the old leaf. It sets down one pad and then the other. The next grain is a long time coming. While it waits, the toe settles a little farther into the green, and stays there even after the grain has gone by.
