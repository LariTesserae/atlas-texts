The power came to the western ditch because it had heard the woman laugh. The last of the young sows had put a foot in the cup after all and had stood there drinking with the foot still in it.

It stayed to hear what else she might do. She did very little. When the animals had gone she lifted one end of the plank, let the water run off, and dragged it into the hut. Inside, she sat down to rub the heel of her left foot. She had been doing this out of the rain for some years.

The power could have mended the heel. It did, a little. By the time she stood up, it hurt no more than the other one. She took both shoes off for the walk home.

There was a child two watersheds inland who wanted to know why a plank on a rising river did not go out with the river. Her uncle had told her about the rope. This was not quite what she wanted to know. She had held the rope on a ferry and knew what happened when the water got hold of something broadside.

The power did not put the woman in mind of the child, or the child in mind of the woman. It let a culvert that had been backing up under the inland path clear itself in the night. In the morning the uncle found he could take his old way to the rim with a load of smoked fish. He asked the child to come because there were two baskets.

It rained the whole way. They sold one basket and most of the other. While the uncle was trying to sell the last three fish, the child heard somebody turning a plank over behind a hut and went to see.

“It does go out,” the woman said when she had heard the question. “This is the fourth.”

She let the child put it back. There was a low place at one end where it fitted against the bank, but the child found a better place a little farther down, where she could get her fingers out from under it without skinning them. The woman felt along the new footing with her bare toes. It would do.

They sat on it to wait for the uncle. The child asked if a river had ever come early while the woman was asleep.

“I expect so,” the woman said. Then, after a while, “I used to sleep here.”

She took the child into the hut and showed her the shelf that was wide enough if you lay on your side. There was a nail at one end for hanging a wet coat. The woman had forgotten the nail. She put her hand on it and remembered lying with her ear against the wall, listening to the woman before her wash two bowls in the rain barrel.

The power heard that too. It could hear the bowls as plainly as the water in the ditch. It did not give the sound back to her. She had enough of it to tell the child why there were two bowls and only one spoon, and the child, who had been hungry for some time, asked what they had eaten.

A long way below them the falls took the water that had been rain when the uncle set out. The power went with it for a while. When it came back, the woman was in the hut, feeling on the shelf for a second bowl. The child was outside beside the ditch, calling to her uncle to come and feel where the plank went.
