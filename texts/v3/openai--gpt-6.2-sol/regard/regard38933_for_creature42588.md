The next time the man came, he brought a pencil and a folding rule. He meant to measure the stain. It had spread past the mark he had made on his last visit, though the mark itself was gone.

He opened the door and felt in his breast pocket for the pencil. It had worked through a hole in the lining and lodged at his elbow. He kept one boot against the door while he unbuttoned his cuff.

The power did not still the pumps. There was water on the other side of the ridge that had to be moved, and people below who had every reason to expect it would be. It did not warm the room. It held back, for the time it took the man to get his hand out of his sleeve, the cold breath from the shaft.

The air at the foot of the ladder had never gone anywhere before. Now it moved along the underside of the rung, carrying the smell of the green and the wet metal out into the passage. The fly turned into it, then turned again. It walked to the end of the dry strip and flew.

It did not go far. It landed on the outside of the doorframe, in a recess where a screw had been driven a little too deep. The man found his pencil and stepped inside. The door shut, and the fly stayed where it was.

There was a light at either end of the passage. One whitened a patch of wall beside the stairs; the other shone through the wire guard over a drain. After a while the fly went to the drain. Under the guard was a curl of brown leaf that had come in on somebody’s boot. It was wet on one side. The fly stood on the other.

Inside the room the man set the end of his rule against the ceiling. A drop ran down the rule and split at the brass hinge. He took his measurement twice. At the foot of the ladder, the larva unbent from the grain of sand and went on feeding. The power stayed until the man had written down the number and the fly had washed both its front feet. Then the damper opened.
