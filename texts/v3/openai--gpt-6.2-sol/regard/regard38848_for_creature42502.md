The power came at the turn of the water. It did not bring light. There was enough to go by in the way the water moved.

It followed the low call of the fish into the chamber next door. The smaller fish was there again, its belly resting on the floor. When it called, the larger fish lifted its chin. In the street, where either of them might have gone looking, a loose valve made more noise than both.

Between the chambers was a plugged opening. It had been the mouth of a shell once, before another shell grew across it. The power felt along the seam. A worm had fastened its tube to one side, and on the other a clam no bigger than an egg had set itself in the silt. There was room below both.

When the tide turned, a single grain rolled out of the seam. The water took a second grain through the space the first had left. By the time the flow steadied, a little water was passing between the rooms. The opening was too small for either fish. The smaller one put its mouth to it and called. The larger one backed out into the street.

It went the wrong way first. It had always turned that way on leaving its room.

At the court the young shrimp still held the underside of the lip. The power could have stopped the water there, but the same current was bringing food to the mouths along the wall. So it let the water run. It waited beside the lip until a husk came by, turning end over end. The husk caught on the rough shell just upstream of the shrimp. In the small shelter behind it, the shrimp let go with two legs, then with the rest, and walked farther under the lip.

The husk washed away. There was nothing to show where it had been.

Later, the larger fish came back along the street. At the mouth of the smaller fish’s room it stopped. It could not have got its head through the opening. It rested with its chin on the sill and gave its call there. This time the answer came out into the street. The shrimp that lived opposite the brooding shrimp paused halfway into his room, his long feelers laid back along his sides. When the sound passed, he went in.

The power stayed through another turn of the tide. It had thought the young shrimp might need the shelter of the husk again. But when the water gathered under the lip, the shrimp came forward on its own and put two legs out into it. A scrap too small to have caught on any of the fans came within reach. The shrimp took it and drew back to eat.
