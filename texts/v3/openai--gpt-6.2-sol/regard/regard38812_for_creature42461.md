The first thing it did was wait at the foot of the stair. The animal that had fallen on the deep tread had got into the angle beneath it and was trying to climb out. Each time it drew itself up, its shell came against the lip and tipped it over. I had seen them do this before. Eventually they found the wall and went around.

A hand came down beside it, edge first, and turned palm up. The animal went on trying the stone for a while. Then it walked across the hand and onto the tread. The hand remained where it was until the animal had gone on to the next.

I had not seen anyone come into the square. There was a woman sitting on the bottom step, with her sleeve turned back twice. I could see the seam on the inside of it. When she stood, she went up the shallow treads slowly and the deep ones more slowly still. At the top she sat again, this time with her feet on the second tread from the top. She could have put a finger in the gap without leaning. She kept both hands in her lap.

After a while a fold came out and lay on the stone beside her. She moved her hand over, leaving about as much room between it and the fold as there was between the stair and the mountain.

They stayed like that through the time it takes the shelter light to go out and come on again when the thing makes a circuit. Then the woman got up. The fold went back into the gap. I thought at first that nothing had passed between them.

She went down to the shelter and read the strip, bending close as I did. At the first of the stops that were elsewhere she stepped off the curb. Her foot went down farther than mine ever had. I saw the sole of her shoe turn as it found a slope. She brought the other foot down beside it and stood lower than the paving ought to have allowed.

“Does it go anywhere?” I asked.

She took another step. Then she came back up to the curb.

“It goes a little farther than that,” she said.

She did the same at the other two stops. At the last she took her shoes off before she stepped down. She set them together under the bench, where they would not be in anyone’s way.

When the thing came down that evening she was sitting in the shelter. It passed her and the light went out. At the first stop she rose and went as far as the curb. The thing went on around the loop. She sat down again.

On its second circuit it stopped beside her shoes.

She took one and set it down in the street that was not there. I could see the top of the heel just below the curb. It did not drift. The thing put out a fold and felt along the shoe, then along the slope beside it. The rest of it came after, over the curb and down. From where I stood it looked for a moment as if it were going under the paving. I went nearer and heard water moving against something with a hollow place in it. Then I heard it again, a little farther off.

The woman waited on the bench with one shoe.

It was gone long enough that I went up to the gap twice to put my hand near it. The second time the stone had lost the last of its warmth. Coming down, I found I had been taking the steps too fast. I sat for a while where the woman had sat and watched the animals get from one tread to the next. Most went by the wall. One stopped at the lip until another came up behind it, and then both went over.

When I got back to the shelter the light was going out from the end where the last name was. I had never seen it go that way before. The thing came up over the curb in short folds. It brought the shoe with it. There was a little gray grit in the heel, which the woman shook out before putting it on.

She left by that stop the next morning. I know because I watched her walk down it. After the first two steps I could only see her shoulders, and after the third I could see her whole length again, farther off than the retaining wall. She was walking with the slight forward lean of someone going downhill toward home. She did not look back to see whether I had understood.

The numbers on the strip are still zero. Most days the thing goes around the loop and comes back up the public stair. Sometimes it goes down from one of the stops, and once it was away for so long that an animal made its way into the cold gap and settled there. When the thing came back it folded itself around the shell without dislodging it. A little later the animal came out on its own.

I have been down as far as the place where the woman left the shoe. There is a worn patch there, not much bigger than a heel, and beyond it the street continues. There is no rail. I went back for a rest before trying the next part. At the curb I found a fold laid flat across the edge, where I could put my hand. It waited until I had my feet under me, then drew away.
