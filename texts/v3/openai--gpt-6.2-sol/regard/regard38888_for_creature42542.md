The power would begin with the wall.

Behind the lowest course, where nobody could get a hand in to test it, a thin bed of stone was coming apart. It would have held through another winter, perhaps through five. The power would settle it back into the cliff. There would be no sound, and the lichen on the face of the wall would not be broken.

It would do this without waiting for anybody to ask. There was no good way for them to know.

Nella’s brother was another matter. The power could give him a morning without pain, and did. He woke before the sun reached his step and lay for a while trying to place what was different. Then he got up and went to the basin with the short-handled bucket. He had forgotten how cold the handle could be.

Nella found him on his step when she came with the milk. He had set out two cups. She looked at him, then at the cups, and went back for bread. They ate with their feet in the lane. He told her something about their mother that she remembered differently. For once there was time to get as far as the part they agreed on.

By the following morning his hip hurt again. The power saw him take hold of the doorframe and understood, from how carefully he did it, what it had cost him to discover that the pain could go. So it gave him the next morning too. And the one after that. It had no need to make a rare thing of it.

In time he went down past the bench to where his door had been. The boy was with him. A corner of the threshold was still there, though you would have taken it for any other stone. He put his foot on it to show the boy which way the door had opened. Then he took his foot off and let the boy have a turn.

The power did not bring the house back. A woman from farther down the valley had taken its door when the roof came in. It was on her kitchen now, with a strip added at the bottom. Her grandchildren had marked their heights on the inside.

Late that winter, the woman who worked nights was coming up from the road with a sack of washing. Her daughter had said she might come on Saturday, but it was Friday, and she was tired enough to be counting the turns in the path. At the notch she put the sack down. Before she picked it up again she heard a chair scrape in her kitchen.

The power had not brought the daughter. She had got a lift as far as the road and walked up with eggs in her coat pockets. By the time her mother came through the door she had broken one into a cup and was picking out a piece of shell.

There was much the power could have done with that moment. It could have let the mother hear the chair from farther down the path, or kept the egg from breaking in the girl’s pocket on the way up. In fact the egg in the pocket was a second egg. Neither of them knew about it yet.

The power stayed until the mother sat down. Then it went to look at the path below the notch, where rain had begun to take the earth from under a step.
