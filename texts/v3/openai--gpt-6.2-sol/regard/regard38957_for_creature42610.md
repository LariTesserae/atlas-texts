The power came to the plain in the forty minutes between noon and noon at the horizon. There was time to look under a lifted leaf before the light that had lifted it reached the farthest factories.

It looked first at the white roots. From above, there were places where the plain might have seemed badly laid. From below, in the air that took the spent light, those same places could be seen to make room for the backs of the old animals. The power waited under an arch while one of them got through. Afterward it waited a little longer, while the gray animals came down to the bare strip. The old animal could have moved on then. It did not.

In the oldest passageway the power found the two rosettes on the landing. The patch of shade between them came from one of the bundle’s leaves. Though the bundle had lain flat for years, that leaf still turned a little in its fold as the light went down the stair.

The next time the walker got up, the power went beside it. On the fourth step the walker put its foot down three times. The first two places were smooth with use; the third, where it found a hold, had scarcely been touched. Farther up, one of the small leaves opened against the wall. It stayed open as far as the width of its own edge allowed.

The power could have put the wall back. The old line was quite clear: a row of pale ends where the masonry had once stopped. But the space behind it was now the breadth of a nest, and five young animals lay there with their feet against one another. It could have taken the walker out into the open country, where all the leaves would have opened at once. It had seen what a distant sound could do to a runner.

It went back down the stair and picked up a worn corner from the foot of the wall. For a while it held the corner in the light, turning it until a little light went sideways from the worn face. Then it set the corner on the fourth step, at the third place where the walker had put its foot.

At the next bright period the walker used it. A little farther up, the leaf that opened against the wall received the light from the corner on its inner face. It drew back, folded the other way, and opened into the space above the stair. The walker stood with one foot raised. Usually by then it had put that foot down.

It took the rest of the steps at its usual pace. At the landing it lowered the bundle, but its feet did not spread at once. The leaf was still open. There was very little light on it now, just enough to show a second fold, running crosswise to the first. The leaf bent there, and the patch of shade moved off the landing and onto the next stair, where there were no worn places at all.

The power stayed until the walker put its foot there. Then it went down again, past the nest. At the fourth step it left the corner where it was. In time a black stem would find it and fit it into a bud. For now there was a little light left on it, and one of the young animals, awake before the others, had put a foot out to feel the warm edge.
