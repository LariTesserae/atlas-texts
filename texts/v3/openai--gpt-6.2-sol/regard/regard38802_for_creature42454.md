The power came in the dry months, when the fish were running out of room under the shelf.

For nine days it did nothing. On the fourth day a calf went into a pool too deep for it and came out on the other side, which was shallower than the side it had gone in by. On the sixth, one of the grey animals died under the floor. The others stopped sleeping in that part of the building. By the ninth day a snail had climbed onto its tail.

The one the power had come to see lived at the fall. It had no place there that was dry. When it drew near the surface, the water took on the look of water running over a hand, though there was no hand to be seen. The fish fed all the way up to it. They had had a long time to learn where it would be.

There was one thing it did that the power at first mistook for a difficulty with the fall. Every evening, when the shadow of the west bank reached the returning shelf, it brought a bead of stone up out of the current and set it on a ledge. By morning the stone was always back in the water. The power watched for a night and saw that nothing dislodged it. Just before the frogs began to call at the pass, the being reached out and put it back.

The next evening the power sat down beside the ledge.

“Who did you do that with?” it asked.

For a while there was only the sound of the fall. Then the being brought up another stone. This one was not round. It had a flat side, and on the flat side were two shallow cuts, close together.

“She couldn’t reach the water,” it said. “Not at the end. I brought them up and she picked one.”

“Did she keep them?”

“Until morning.”

The power had seen the woman's grave on its way into the valley. It was in one of the roofless buildings, under a tree that had grown around the wall so thoroughly that a stone from the wall was now a little way up the trunk. There was no need to tell the being where she was.

“Would you like me to pick one?” the power asked.

The being drew back into the fall. A fish that had been feeding beside it moved into the space it left. The power thought it might have made a mistake. It stayed where it was, with its feet in the water.

After a while three stones came up. One was so small it was difficult to tell from a drop of water. One had a thin white band that did not quite meet itself. The third was the stone with the two cuts.

The power picked the one with the band.

In the morning it brought the stone back. The being accepted it and held it for a moment where the current was smooth. Then it let go.

“Did you want that one?” the power asked.

“No,” said the being. “But I wanted to see which one you would take.”

The power had intended to be gone by noon. Instead it went down to the shallows and sat where it could watch the brown birds with their snails. Before dark it came back to the fall. This time, before any stones came up, it told the being about a young bird that had struck a snail eleven times and then carried it, still whole, to a different stone. The being wanted to know whether the second stone was any better.

It wasn’t. The next day they both went to look.
