The power came on a night when no rain was due. It stood in the square while the grazers went past and moved its feet when the little animal came with its leaf.

It had come for that animal. It waited until the leaf was in place and the animal had gone in at the eleventh door. From inside came the sound Len had heard once before, of something climbing. After a while there was a little scrape very high up, and a pause, and the scrape again. The power put a hand against the jamb. The climbing stopped. It took its hand away, and presently the climbing went on.

Above the square, rainwater lay in a fold of the canopy. It had been there since the last shower. The fold was deep enough to cover a hand, and blind white creatures lived at the bottom of it. When the water rose over the fold, most of it went down the seam to the steps. A little ran the other way, along the underside of a pipe, and fell into a drain.

The power followed the pipe. At the place where the water let go, it found a thread of root hanging a finger’s breadth short. It lifted the root until it touched the pipe. The root was too stiff to stay there. The power held it while the works turned through the night, and while Ada shut down the flywheels and stood with her lamp lowered, waiting out of habit for the last call. By the time she went home, the root had curled around the pipe. The power let go.

It rained upstairs the following afternoon. Two days later the animals came to the steps in their usual order. After the last of them had drunk, a drop fell into the fountain. It ran down the wall and stood against the lip. Then another fell.

Len brought his chair out that evening. There was room to put his feet where he liked, but he put them under the chair. The small animal came late, carrying a leaf broad enough that it had to turn its head to get past the end of the bowl. It stood for a long time where it had put the leaf the night before. Then it walked on and set this one a little farther round.

Ada found Len there on her way to work. He held up his comb to show her it was dry. She bent over the bowl. Three fine tracks came down the wall and disappeared under the water. On the other side, where the bowl was still low, three wet tracks came out. The last was on the paving, beyond the place her lamp could reach without her getting down on her knees.

She went on to the works. When she came home in the morning, Len had left the chair out. In the seat was a cup with a saucer over it to keep the moths from falling in. She took it indoors and drank it. It was water from the kitchen tap, which he had run long enough to get cold.
