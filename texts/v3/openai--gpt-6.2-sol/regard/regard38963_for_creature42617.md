The power came in the cold and stayed through the first warm afternoon. By then it knew that what looked from the pass like a hillside slipping was, among other things, how water got to the far end of the valley. It had nearly put a hand out to steady it.

On the fourth day it found a bird working at a notch. She carried each strand a little way uphill before she let it go. This seemed a waste until the power saw what happened to the strands she dropped beside her: the moving leaves took them straight back into the opening.

Behind the notch were two young birds. One had its foot against the roof. It kept taking the foot down and putting it back in the same place.

The power could have lifted the plate. But there was water standing behind it, and lifting it would have sent the water through the nest. So it held a single root, well back from the opening, while the slope finished its afternoon turn. The root drew thin in its grip. A little water came out of it. At dusk the bird went in with food, and the power let go.

It did this again the next day. On the third day one of the young came out while the mother was away. It got both feet onto the face of the crest, lost one, and hung there with its head down until the leaves turned. Then it ran. The other did not come out until nearly dark. By then the power had been holding the root for so long that a small gray animal had settled in the drop at the end of it.

There were other nests. The power did not find them all in time. At one it found a parent still bringing food to a closed seam. It stayed there until the bird went away. There was no work for its hands.

In the winter it went back to the first notch. The opening had narrowed to the width of a stem. It could have made a door that would never close, but the nest was dry because the plate lay so close over it. It left the roof as it was. Where the lip met the lower plate, it took out a single dead fiber. In spring, a root tip reached the space the fiber had left and turned along it. Another tip followed. There was still no door, but the next bird had less to carry uphill.

The power stayed for another year, and then another. Some days it did nothing. Once it watched a cream-colored animal spend most of a morning getting around a root it could have moved with no effort at all. On the far side, the animal began to eat, and before evening the first water went through. The next morning there were three clear drops under a leaf that had always been dry. The power turned the leaf back the way it had found it.
