The power came to the room with the fallen slab and stayed through a turn of the tide. At first it thought the animal was keeping the doorway clear. By the end of the tide it could see that the doorway was keeping the animal fed.

It went down into the old timber spaces, where there was just room for the gray tissue and a little water to pass. Here and there a part had been cut off from the rest. Some of these parts were dying. One, no longer than the pin that had hung the door, had put out a mouth at each end. The two mouths faced opposite ways. On the ebb one fed, and on the flood the other did.

Under the next house a brick was settling. When it came to rest it would close the last passage into a room where the animal had laid a net. The power watched a flake catch there. There was not much coming through, even now.

It could have lifted the brick. Instead it took a loose chip from the rubble and stood it on edge beneath one corner. The brick came down on the chip. A little clay squeezed out and drifted into the room. For a time the net took nothing else.

There were other bricks settling in the basin. The power did not go from house to house propping them up. In one place it watched a wall give way and open a drain that had been stopped for centuries. The first water out was black. Later it ran clear, and a thread at the mouth of the drain began to stir where none had stirred before.

It returned to the room with the fallen slab. The clean band on the stone was narrower than it had been. A twist of waste had caught at one end, and the current was wearing it away a grain at a time. The power left it there.

Long after the chip had sunk out of sight under the brick, a thin gray loop came through the space it had held open. It passed over the chip without fastening to it. On the far side there was a shallow hollow where a builder’s thumb had pushed wet clay out of a joint. The loop settled in the hollow and grew a mouth. It was a poor place to catch anything on the ebb. On the flood it did well enough.
