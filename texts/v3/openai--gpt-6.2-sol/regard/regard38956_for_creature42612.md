The power came in the dry part of the year. At the second turn in the roof passage there was a white mark on the underside of the brick, and no water. One of the animals hung with its mouth against the mark. It stayed after the brick had cooled, and after it had warmed again.

Far down the tower, on a ledge no wider than a finger, a bird had pushed a stem of grass into a joint. There were other stems laid across it. The bird had been dead a long time. Its nest was gone except for that first stem, which had swelled in successive rains and drawn a little of the mortar with it as it shrank. Water that had once passed down the face of the tower now went into the joint and came out warm, three courses lower. In a wet year there was enough left to reach the hollow above the second turn.

The power took out the stem. With a grain of lime it closed the opening the stem had made. It did not close the smaller opening beside it, where a root finer than a hair went in.

Then it waited for rain.

The first drop to reach the turn had grit in it. The animal put its mouth to the drop and took its mouth away. By morning there was another drop. It drank that one. Coming down, it stopped where the low sound met the passage and opened its mouth. From one of the slits at the far end, a head came out.

There was a young one on the path. It had got as far as the smooth brick and lay there with its feet spread, though there was nothing on that brick for them to hold. The power could have taken the sound away. Instead it waited, as it had waited for rain. A grown one came up from the shelf, set its jaw twice against the brick, and took the young one by the loose skin behind its head. On the far side it put it down facing a corner.

Before leaving, the power sat in the flat place by the entrance. It kept both feet off the path. The hollow at the back of the shelf was dry. There was no need to fill it now, but the power remembered the person who had, and poured a little water there, no more than the hollow would hold.

For a long while nothing came. Then a small jaw touched the shelf below the hollow. The power kept still. When the animal had drunk, it went down by the seam in the jamb, which was just wide enough to take the crease in each foot.
