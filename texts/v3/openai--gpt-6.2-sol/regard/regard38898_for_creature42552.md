The first thing the power did was wait for the large animal to go out.

It had mistaken the red grain for something caught in a wound. Outside, when the animal turned, the power could see the skin take the weight of the grain without tearing. There was a hollow under it, smooth and red on the inside. The grain had sat there long enough to wear a place for itself.

While it waited, the power followed the warmth down through the ridge. In one of the old basket corners, a crack had opened across the weave. Each time warm water came through, a flake of clay moved. When it came free, the water would take a shorter way out. The room would still stand. Its floor would be cold.

The power held a finger against the flake for a while. The warmth came back up through the bricks, and the flat animals opened the mouths nearest the joints.

It could have closed the crack. But there were small mouths in the crack too, on the side that had never been warm until it opened.

Farther down lay a piece of a slab that had once been part of a step. The power lifted it and set it beneath the leak, a little out from the ridge. Some of the water still went on down. Some ran along the underside of the slab and entered the clay again, two baskets higher up. The first time the flake moved, the slab rocked. The power took it out and set it back with its heavier end inward.

After that it waited through two greater pulses of warmth. On the second, the flake came loose. It fell past the slab without touching it.

Nothing in the room lit up.

Much later, a flat animal came to the edge of its seam and put three mouths over a joint that had always been cold. It rested there. By the next warm spell another had come alongside it.

The large animal went out that time. A little one caught hold of it at the sill and rode down past the first step, farther than it had meant to go. Its legs kept walking until the animal turned. Then it let go.

The power put out a hand beneath it. Before the little one came near the hand, two of its feet found the slope. The rear feet went on searching for a while. One by one they found places to stand.

The power took its hand away. The little one went up so slowly that a white animal at the edge had time to empty both cheeks before it passed. Where the grit landed, the new thread of warm water came out through the stair. The little one stopped there first. Then it went on toward the door.
