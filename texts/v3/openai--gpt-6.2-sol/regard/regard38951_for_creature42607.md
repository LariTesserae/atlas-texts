The power came down the ramp after the keeper had gone. At the back wall it heard the small toes pass the perch and go on around.

It could have lifted the young animal down. Instead it waited to see how the old one did it. The foot came up, the four toes spread, and the fifth stayed folded against the ankle. The young one reached until its belly lay flat on the rim. Twice it drew back. On the third try it found the foot, and the foot went down so slowly that for part of the descent it did not seem to be going down at all.

In the morning the power went up to the road and found the potter who had made the replacement pot. It asked whether there was any clay left over. There was a little under an upturned bowl, enough to make a knob. The potter wanted to know what the knob was for, since that would tell her what shape to make it.

“Something to put a foot on,” said the power. “A very small foot.”

The potter pressed the pad of her little finger into the clay. When the knob had been fired, the power set it against the pot, a little below the place where the old animal’s foot had reached.

That night the young one went around the rim three times. The old foot came up as before. The young one stepped on it, then found the knob on the way down. It climbed back up at once. It did this several times, and on the last trip the old foot stayed where it was on the floor.

In the morning there were prints going both ways. The keeper bent to brush the dust off the side of the pot, saw them, and brushed the brick beside it instead.
