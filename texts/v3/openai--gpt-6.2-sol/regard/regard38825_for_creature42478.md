The first thing the power did was put a finger over the joint.

The low note stopped. Inside, the lid of the second jar rose and stayed up. The woman, who had been halfway down the stair with the empty bowl, came back at a run. Before she reached the landing the power had taken its finger away. The lid came down crooked. Two of the young went over the rim in the rush of water, and the woman spent the rest of the evening getting them out of a tuft of roots with a bristle.

After that the power watched for nine evenings. On the fifth the woman did not come. Her hands had split at the knuckles and she was sitting three flights down with them in her armpits. The water froze on the stair. In the morning she had to use the bowl with both hands to break it loose, and one piece slid past her and went over the edge. She listened a long time for it to strike something.

There was a brick on the mountain side of the stair which had been laid with its good face inward. The power turned it over. On the face that had been hidden there was a hollow where a pebble had come away in the firing. It was not much of a hollow, but it was below the joint and a little way back from the wind.

That evening the woman heard a sound on the stair she did not know. She thought at first it was the bowl cracking. Water was striking water in the hollow of the brick. She put a finger in. The bottom was still warm.

In the morning there was a skin of ice over it. She lifted it out whole and found a shell underneath. This one she did not warm in her mouth. She held the bowl up to the joint and waited, and when the water in the bowl tipped toward the wall of its own accord she brought the rim a little nearer. The shell went in sideways. She heard it touch a leaf.

She still had to carry the trays up. There were still mornings when a jar gave less than it had taken. Behind the first pool the root hairs began to come back braided in threes, and neither the woman nor the power could see what use this was to the thing that braided them.

But some evenings the woman let the bowl stand empty beside her. She could finish a bank without looking over her shoulder at the stair. Once, after she had pushed a tray home, she left her thumb resting in the clay. From under the bluff the snail came out of its shell. It took so long to cross the pool that her arm began to shake. She put her other hand under her wrist and held it there.
