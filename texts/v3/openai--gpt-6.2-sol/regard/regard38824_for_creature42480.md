The forefoot found nothing it could rest on. The paint was slick at the edge, and each grain she touched went down with the water. She drew the foot in and laid it against the nearest egg.

The power had been there long enough to know what a drop was worth. The worm under the washer would not have lived through the last stoppage without one. It let the pipe go on sweating. But at the low edge of the paint, where the runoff took the sand with it, it set a grain on its broad side. The next thread of water divided there. Some went under the washer. The rest spread into the sand a little way short of the eggs.

In the morning she came out and turned the grain over. There was nothing to eat on it yet. She left it on its broad side, perhaps because that was the side on which it would lie.

By noon one of the shells had opened. The animal that got out lay with its legs gathered under it, as she had lain after leaving her skin. Another shell did not open that day or the next. She carried it when the river came up, last of all, and in the morning carried it back.

The power stayed through the shift change. It could have looked into the bright strip to see where she went. Instead it watched the shadows. Hers stopped halfway across, and a second shadow, so small it might have been a notch in hers, went on a little farther. Then hers moved again. At the far edge she stepped out with sand on her back. The little one came out behind her, quite clean, and stopped to wash a foot.
