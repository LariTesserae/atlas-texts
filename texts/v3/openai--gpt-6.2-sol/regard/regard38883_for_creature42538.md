The power came in the interval between the first dent in the egg and the second. It could have taken the dent out. It waited until something inside the shell took it out from the other side.

There was a tick due before the young one’s feet would harden. The power attended to it. Moving it even a little would leave one of the stool legs without its old hollow, and in the lean years the old animal would have to go farther than the thumbprint to eat. Nor could the sound be taken out of the tick. The old animal had been listening for it with the flats of its feet. It would know when to lower itself.

The power looked instead at the crease. Each time the old animal deepened it, it put the egg down again with the same care, turning it so that the softest part of the shell faced the loose dust. But the grains it dug out had begun to pile up on the side from which it approached. When the shell opened, the young one would have to cross that little bank. It could do so. Its mother had crossed a higher one at the same age. The power saw how long it had taken her.

It took one grain from the bank and set it at the bottom of the crease, flat side up.

When the egg opened, the young one put a foot on the grain. The grain tipped, but not as far as the foot expected. It put another foot beside the first. By the time the old animal came down from the stool, there were two tracks going away from the shell, one on either side of the groove made by its belly.

The tick came in the dark part of the chamber. The old animal flattened. The young one went under it so quickly that a piece of shell still adhering to its back came off against one of the scars. For some time neither animal moved. Then the young one put out a foot and brought it back in.

The correction had run a ridge through the thumbprint. On one side of it lay the piece of shell. On the other lay the grain. The old animal found the grain the next time it went out to feed, turned it in its mouth, and swallowed the part that was worn.
