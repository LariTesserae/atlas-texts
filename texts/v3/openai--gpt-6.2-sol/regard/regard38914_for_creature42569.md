The power came first to the rain. It stayed there long enough to see a bird come down.

The bird put both feet forward and sat down hard on the water. Its belly, which the girl and her brother had discussed so often, turned out to be the least of it. The girl missed the arrival. She was below the cold seam with a bowl, trying to get an egg off her thumb without losing the eggs already in the bowl.

It would have been easy to lift her through the surface. The power let her finish.

Down in the range there was an old lobe whose wall hurt when it drew taut. The hurt was not the mountain's warning of a break. The wall had been mended a long time before, and a little living thread had grown across the seam where there was no longer any use for it. At every filling the thread was pinched. The power waited for the lobe to empty and laid the thread down on the other side of the seam. The next filling sounded like all the others. Two people with their hands flat on the wood went on with their conversation.

At the green fall the power found the old woman gone. It knew where she had gone and could have fetched her. It could even have fetched her on a morning when she still had all her teeth and was angry at the boy for leaving with her comb. But the woman who had died had been very old, and when her hands began to fail she had spent the better part of a year teaching herself to let other hands do things. The power did not mistake that work for a thing to be undone.

It watched the granddaughter on the outside of the household. The child who had come loose was not in danger. He had an elbow in her ribs, and when she caught his wrist he pushed at her in his sleep, annoyed to be held. She moved her hand down to his ankle. He settled.

There was one thing she wanted. She could remember the old woman filing a bowl smooth but not the tune she used to sing while she did it. She had tried several tunes over supper that evening and each had turned into a tune she knew. The power knew the missing one. It had only four notes, and the old woman sometimes got the third wrong. The power could have put it entire into the granddaughter's mind, with the mistake and the scrape of the file.

Instead it went down to the people among the pumps, where a very old man was teaching a little girl to file the teeth off her first bowl. He had learned from the same woman. His hands were busy and he was not singing. The power drew no sound out of him. It waited until he put the bowl down to show the girl where she had missed a tooth. Then, in the long quiet before the next filling, it let him hear a fish rasp at the wood. It was the little rasp the file had made when the old woman took it away from him to show him how.

He sang the tune to himself. The girl asked him to do it again.

In the morning at the green fall the granddaughter took out the two combs. The old one was better for a child who hated having his folds opened: where the teeth were missing, a knot could pass without pulling. She used it on the boy who had come loose. He complained anyway. Then he asked to do hers, and she turned her back to him and held still.
