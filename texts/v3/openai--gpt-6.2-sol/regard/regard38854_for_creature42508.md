The power came in the afternoon and sat with both feet flat on the stone. Under the nearest lip the fringe drew back. The power waited. By the time it came out again there was no sun in the hollow, but the left face of the notch was still lit, and the power could see why the animal had never closed that last opening, though it had closed the one in the rock.

There was something the power could do about the thread. It would have been a small matter to draw it out whole, even to give it back its blue. But the living seam had grown through the twist of it, not merely over it. The power left it where it was.

Farther up the mountain, beyond the low tree, a loose stone lay across a crack. For most of the year it made no difference. In a hard rain the stone turned the first rush of water into the cleft that fed the hollow. The next such rain would move the stone a little farther in, and the rain after that would seat it. Afterward the hollow would get very little water. There would be several summers in which the animal still reached for the upper pockets, though none of them would ever fill again.

The power went up and lifted the stone. Under it were two small white roots. It set the stone down on its side, clear of the roots and far enough from the crack that the water would have to find its own way there.

Then it came back and sat for a little while longer. A bead gathered beneath the lip. In it the power saw a knee, a patch of sky, and, at the very edge, a fern tip moving in air too slight to feel on the skin.

The bead grew until it touched the fringe. It did not fall.
