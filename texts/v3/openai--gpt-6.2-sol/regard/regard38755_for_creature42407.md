The power came at noon and put a finger into the hollow in the step. The stone slipped from its hand. It had not expected the hollow to be so smooth.

It tried again. A child sitting on the top step took its heels off the bottom one without looking up from the basket she was mending. The power lifted the stone and heard the garden. It held the step up until a root broke, and in the interval heard three gardens answer. One was directly below. One was a good way off. The third was so faint that the power waited for another root to be sure of it.

Behind the cupboard were three young. The smallest was asleep with its mouth against a groove in its mother’s belly. The groove was empty. The power could have filled it. It was considering this when the mother put out a foot and drew the youngster closer without waking.

On the other side of the board, the woman took down her flour jar. The shelf dipped. She took down a bowl as well, then stood for a while with her palm flat on the wood. A nail had worked far enough out to catch on her sleeve. She had a new shelf ready, a stout one cut to fit flush against the brick. She meant to put it in that afternoon.

The power went into the kitchen. It did not make the woman fond of an animal she had never seen. It set one of the animal’s discarded foot skins on the rim of the flour jar.

The woman took it for the husk of a grain until she saw the toes. She put it on the table and went to fetch the mason.

He brought a chisel. She made him leave it on the table while they took the bowls out. With the weight off the shelf they could ease the board forward a little. There was a bed in the space behind it, and a pale streak where somebody had been keeping the bed clean. The mother flattened herself over two nail holes. A foot stuck out of the third.

The woman slid the board back herself. Then she held it while the mason fitted a pair of blocks underneath. He put the nails where she showed him, well clear of the holes. The new shelf went above the old one. It was a little high for the flour jar, but it turned out to be just right for the bowls.

That evening the mother went down to feed. On her way back she stopped at a place where the mortar had been dislodged by the work. She put her left hind foot out twice and brought it back. The power laid a grain of limestone in the joint, and she went on.

It stayed until the smallest of the young came down on its own. By then there were fresh marks on the wall of the passage and a dark ring on the new shelf where the woman, finding that she could reach after all, had set the flour jar. The youngster came to the inside edge of a leaf just before noon. It had eaten; there was yellow under its jaw, and a little dust still clung to one wrist.

When the root broke, the power heard the same three gardens. It could have made the nearest one louder. It listened instead.

The youngster shifted one foot toward the farthest sound. Then it shifted the other, and let go.
