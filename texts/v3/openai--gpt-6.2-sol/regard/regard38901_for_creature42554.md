The power came in the afternoon and stayed until the light went out of the water. It had seen the old one reaching. It wanted to bring the two terraces together, and had already put a hand beneath the farther one when it felt the small mouths working along the rim.

It took its hand away.

That night it went under the stair. The young were asleep with their feet laid lengthwise on the roots. Some of the worn places were empty. In one of these a root had died back, leaving a little white hook. Water ran down to the hook and fell clear of the stair. The power held a drop there long enough for a waking animal to find it. The animal drank, put one foot on the hook, and went back to sleep.

In the morning the power went to the far terrace. It did not touch the ribs. It found a root coming out of the underside of one of them, some distance back from the clear tip. The root had been growing straight down. The power bent it toward the valley. It bent a root from the valley toward it. There was still a little air between the two ends, but when the wind came up in the afternoon they brushed.

For several days nothing held them there. Sometimes they were a foot apart. Then the fine hairs on one root began to catch in the fine hairs on the other. Water stood in the place where they met. The roots went on growing, each a little way along the other, and the seam between them got harder to see.

The old one found the crossing while it was eating. It went down the root until its weight brought the far end into a shallow curve. At that it backed up. The young one climbed off its back and went a little farther, not as far as the seam. It drank there. Coming back, it had to step over the old one's foot.

The power stayed until it saw one go all the way across. This was neither of those two, but a grown one with a shred of green still hanging from its mouth. It found a place to eat on the far side, ate for a while, and came back the way it had gone.

From the high rim the gap looked as wide as it had before. The pale lips went on shedding their fine green skin. At the end of the stair, water fell over the missing riser. Underneath, where there had been no way back except the way you came, a drop hung from the lowest part of the crossing. Two of the young had found it. They were drinking from opposite sides.
