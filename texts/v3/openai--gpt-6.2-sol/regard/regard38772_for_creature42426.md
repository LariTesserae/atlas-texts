The power first noticed the young one by the cut. It saw the upright draw back and knew where the other three stones had fallen. The flat one was still whole. It lay a long way down, warm side up, on a shelf that would not move again.

It would have been easy to put it back.

The power waited through several of the young one’s rounds. The upright was not in the same place each time. It was moving by a little more than its own width in the course of a round, though at the end it always came back to the old inclination. Below it, where the cut had broken through a bed of thin plates, the young one was trying to pass a load from one plate to the next. The upright was the only piece it had that could take the weight while the first plate rose. It could keep doing this, but the plates were cooling. Soon it would have to let one of them go.

There was warmth deeper in the wall. The power could have brought it straight to the plates, but then the young one would have had to find out what to do with a wall that no longer cooled. Instead, far down in the cut, where there was nothing living on either face, it moved a fallen block just far enough that one corner touched the opposite wall. Warmth began to cross at the point of contact. By the time it reached the thin plates there was very little of it. It came late in the round, after the upright had begun to take the weight.

For three evenings the young one did nothing different. On the fourth it let the upright go a little sooner. One of the plates came up under its load. On the fifth it did not use the upright at all.

The stone stood for a long while after that. At last a tremor laid it flat. By then the young one had reached a part of the valley from which the cut was not even a line in the floor.

The power did not go about making bridges at the foot of every cut. There were creatures that used the cold of those depths, taking weight off a stone just as it contracted and nowhere else. It had not seen them at first. They went so slowly that the first one it found had been in the middle of a movement since before the young one’s three stones fell.

It stayed to learn their pace. Once, as it watched a new reach open, a clack came from well behind it. It knew enough by then not to turn. Ahead, under a slab still faintly red at the edge, a small weight came on and stopped. The stone that received it had never borne anything but the stone above it. For a while it bore both. Then, without any help, it passed the second weight on.
