The power came to the landing after the two of them had gone down. It put a finger under the stem, in the place where the girl had put hers, and felt the three roots holding. It did not try to lift it.

All through the fallen rock there were little stores of summer water. Most had a way out. One did not. It lay behind the block the stem had reached, in a seam that narrowed to nothing. When the cold went deep enough, the water would push the block outward. The roots would hold on to it as it went.

The power followed the seam down through the rock. At the bottom there was a grain of stone no bigger than a grain of salt. It eased the grain out. A thread of water ran down the back of the block and came into the town beneath the top step.

Nobody saw it happen. In the morning Mara found a little ice under the step and put a handful of grit on it.

The power stayed through the winter. It had meant only to see whether the water would keep running, but there was a great deal to see while it waited. On the coldest night, one of the sisters put a baked potato on the saucer and pushed it through the wall. The other sent it back with half the skin still on it, folded over to keep the soft part warm. They had been eating potatoes together for seventy years and still disagreed about the skin.

By spring the stem had thickened enough to cast a shadow of its own. Edrin’s daughter brought up a stool and set it on the landing. From that height she could see straight across the valley to a patch of snow that stayed white after the rest of the opposite slope went brown. She fetched Mara to look.

Mara sat down. After a while she said, “No higher than that.”

The girl made a mark on the rock with a bit of charcoal. It was only a short line. There was as yet nothing to put on either side of it.

The power left that spring. Below the top step the water went on coming out, and in time a root found it. It grew down beside the trickle, leaving room for it to pass. Years later, when the two stools were in place and there was a wall around the window, you could sit with your feet on that root. It was a little warmer than the floor.
