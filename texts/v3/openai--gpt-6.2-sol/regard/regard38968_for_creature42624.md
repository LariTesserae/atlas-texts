The power came to the marsh in a year when rain fell on the slopes below but seldom reached the saddle. For forty days the outflow did not reach the brink. The hollows in the sill held their water a little longer than the flat stone between them. When the last hollow dried, there were three black snails in it.

The power had not come because of the drought. It had been watching the broad animal for some time. It liked the way the animal, having combed a root clean, would leave its foot there while the fish came out. There was no advantage to this that the power could see. The animal might simply have been slow to think of taking its foot away. The power liked it all the same.

In the drought the animal took to lying at the lower end of a channel beneath the cane. There was still a trickle of rainwater there from deep in the scree. Every night it went a little farther up the channel to feed, and every morning it came back. One morning it came back with a young one following it. The young one stopped often and put its head into places the old one had passed.

A bank had fallen in during the night, leaving a narrow opening into a pocket fed from below. A few larvae hung from a root just inside. The young one went in after them. When it turned, the loose bank came down a little farther, and the root, which had bent to let it pass, caught it behind a foreleg.

The power could have cooled the whole pocket. There were things living on its walls for which the water was just warm enough. It could have lifted the young one out, but the last thing to seize that animal from above had been a bird, and it still bore the marks. So the power held the soft bank in place and loosened one grain of stone where the root was wedged. The root rose. The young one backed out badly, with its legs going in several directions at once, and lay in the trickle beside the old one.

By evening it was feeding. For the rest of its life it would enter a narrow place tail first. This did not save it from everything. It did save it from that again.

The power went down to the sill. The snails there were dead. In a crack below the brink, beyond where anyone standing on the sill could have seen, there were others of the same kind in water scarcely deep enough to cover their shells. The power stayed there through the afternoon. It had no need to be still, but there was very little room on the wet stone, and one of the snails was making its way across.

When rain came, the old animal returned to its usual root. The current had pulled the brown skein out straight. It took the animal a while to gather it back into a bunch it could comb. Halfway through the work a fish came out and took a larva from between its toes. The animal drew its foot up sharply. Then it put it back.
