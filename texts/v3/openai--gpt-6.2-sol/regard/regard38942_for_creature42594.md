The power first took notice of the town when the two children held the cup against the wall. It stayed after they had gone. In the dark there was still a little chalk on the side of the cup, and a little on the boy’s sleeve.

For several days it did nothing. It learned how the town sounded with work going on at both ends, and how it sounded when somebody called down a light well for a person who had already come up. It watched the widower take out the stones. It watched the baker come to her door at noon and stand just inside it until she could make out who was speaking to her. When the man with the bad back was given two empty cups, it saw him go back and fill them. He had had enough of being managed, but he wanted the water.

Under the public stairs there was a bed of stone no thicker than a loaf. It had been thick enough before the last room was cut. Now, when rain came, water stood in a seam behind it, and a little more of the weight of the stairs came to rest on the narrow end.

The power held the stone. It did not wait for anyone to be on the stairs.

There was a way out for the water through a nearly closed seam at the foot of the wall. The power opened it as far as the thickness of a hair. In the morning the man who cut stairs found white grit fanned across the bottom step. He swept it off with his foot. On his way back with his breakfast he found it there again.

He set his bowl down and went to get the man who knew how to listen to stone. The man brought his son, and the boy gave him the hammer before he asked. They spent some time at the foot of the stairs. Then they went up to the road and carried down two lengths of timber. By supper there was a rope across the top step and a notice that sent people the long way round past the baker’s. People complained about this for most of a week. The baker sold a great many small loaves.

The power kept its hold until the new pier was in. It felt the weight pass into the pier a little at a time as the men took out the timbers. None of them could see anything happen. One stooped to pick up a shim he thought he could use again.

Before the rope came down, the girl whose father had hurt his back dragged the baker’s stool a little way into the street. She meant only to see if the baker would call her back. From there she found that she could tell the baker who was coming while they were still out in the glare. She got two names wrong the first day. The second day the baker brought out a knife and a heel of bread and divided it between them. After the stairs reopened, the stool stayed where it was, except on mornings when people had a door to carry through.

The power went eastward with the cutting. There were other places where it put a hand to the stone, and many where there was no need. The low shelf came out a little crooked. At one end the chisel had followed a soft place and taken more than the mason intended. The power could have made it level. It left it as it was.

When the family moved in, the girl’s brother brought the cup from the old house with two others nested in it. He put them all on the high shelf. That night, after he had gone to bed, his sister got up and moved hers down. In the morning she found that she could reach it without getting out from under the cover, just as she had thought she would. She went to fill it and came back to bed to try again.
