It would come in a dry spell and stay through the rain. The first thing it would notice about the ledge animal would be the seam, which was being eaten. It would be on the point of closing the seam when it noticed how thin the white-legged animals were.

It would stay longer.

On the third night after the rain, the ledge animal would set its mouth beneath a drip that had stopped. The six pads would rise together. They would do this several times before the animal moved on. In the shallow runnel ahead, another animal would bellow, and the first would raise its hind edge, though there was nothing behind it.

The power would follow the runnel to see where the other animal was trying to go. There would be water within the length of its body, on the far side of a low ridge. It had been there the whole time. The ridge was too steep for a sole to hold on to, and the way around was full of other mouths.

When the rain came again, the power would take away a piece of that ridge. Not much: a piece it could have covered with a hand. It would make a slope on either side and leave the bottom just high enough that the pool would not drain. Then it would wait to see if it had got that right.

The first animal over would be a white-legged one. It would find the stone wet on the far side and open its knees. Others would come, and then a ledge animal, not the one the power had been watching. It would lower its mouth into the pool. For a while there would be six small disturbances on the surface. When it drew back, two swimmers would be turning in the water, and another would be stuck to one of its pads. The animal would put its mouth down again.

In the next dry spell the power would find the old animal by its seam. It would be on the slope, with the weight of its body still on the dry side and its mouth just over the top. Something behind it would bellow. Its hind edge would lift. Then the front of the sole would take hold, and the animal would go down to the water without being pushed.

The power would leave the ridge as it was after that. It would come back in the hardest dry spells and listen for mouths opening under places where water had been. Where there was water near enough, it would see what was in the way. Where there was none, it would bring some. It would put it down a drop at a time, slowly enough for the six pads to part and rise again.
