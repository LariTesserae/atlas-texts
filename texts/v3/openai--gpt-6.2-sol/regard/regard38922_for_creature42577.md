The power came in a dry year and waited until it knew which empty dishes were supposed to be empty.

There was one under the east stair that the sow passed without lowering her head. The old woman’s grandson had filled it once, carrying water down a cup at a time. By morning the dish was dry and a patch of clay beneath it had gone dark. He filled it twice more before his grandmother showed him the crack. It was so fine he could feel it with a fingernail but not with the pad of his finger.

The crack went farther than either of them knew. Below the dish it ran through the shoulder of a jar that carried the weight of the landing where they slept. In wet years a root swelled into the opening. This year the root had shrunk, and ants were going in and out beside it with their eggs.

The power did not close the crack. It held the shoulder of the jar until morning.

At breakfast the boy set his cup down and it slid toward his grandmother. He set it upright and watched it slide again. She took the cup from him and poured a little water on the landing. It ran toward the stair instead of away from it.

By noon they had moved the mats. The boy wanted to go back for the hearthstone, but his grandmother said they could cook on two stones as well as one. They took the flat-bottomed pot.

The power held the jar another night, while the ants finished moving. In the cool before dawn it let the shoulder go. The landing dropped at one end and came to rest against the belly of a larger pot. The hearthstone broke in two. Farther down, where the clay had opened, water began to fall into a jar that had been dry long enough to have a mouse’s nest in it. The mouse took her young uphill.

For a while the sow kept coming to the old landing. She would put one foot on the tilted clay, then take it off and stand listening. The old woman heard her from the next stair. In the morning she moved the wash bowl to the turn between them, where neither of them had to go far.

The nest in the thumb hollow was empty by then. The boy could have put his thumb in, but there was a little feather stuck to the clay. He left it there.
