The power came in the cold, when it could hear the mountain breaking. It listened for a long time before it moved anything. Most of the breaks were no wider than a hair. In one of them a moth had got out of the wind. In another, water was working its way down toward the cup.

The old animal came around the far side. It had to go sideways now, not because it had grown wider but because it could no longer bend well in the middle. It set the pointed chip against the rim and pushed. The ice gave a little. It pushed again and drank.

While the animal was drinking, the power looked at the way back. There was a flake there that tipped under the animal’s hind feet. It had tipped the day before, too. The animal had got across by putting its weight on its forefeet and pulling. The power could have made the flake steady, but there were eggs on its underside, in the space the flake would close if it settled. So it went a little way into the mountain and found a bit of stone with nothing living on it. It brought the stone out and wedged it under one corner.

On the way back the old animal leaned forward as it had the day before. The flake did not tip. It took another step and went under.

There was enough warmth in the power to take the ice out of the cup for the rest of the winter. It could feel, a little farther in, where the same warmth would wake the pale runners before there was anything for them to eat. It let the cup freeze. But it stayed until a bead of water came down the seam, and saw that on the far side, where the old animal drank, the bead ran straight onto the ice. There it spread thin and froze. All night the beads would come that way. By morning the old animal would have to push harder than it could.

The power turned the next bead aside. It did this by taking away a grain of stone. The bead hung for a while at the place where the grain had been, then dropped into the cup through the small hole the animal had made. The bead after that did the same. The water fell often enough to keep the hole open, but only just. A bird could have stopped it with a feather.

In the morning a young animal came to the rubbed patch and felt for the pointed chip. It found the broad one and pushed it uselessly against the ice. After a while it heard the drops on the other side and went around. Its belly dragged over the flake. It drank with the broad chip still in its mouth, until it discovered that it couldn’t, and put it down.

The old animal had not come out yet. The power waited. When it came, the young one was in its way. The old animal nosed it once, without much effect, and then stood with its chin resting on the young one’s back. Presently the young one moved far enough that both could drink.

The power left by the upper end of the cleft. A bird coming in folded the wrong wing first, struck the wall with its breast, and caught hold. The power turned at the sound. The bird had a little wool in its bill and had not let go of it. It climbed the rest of the way.
