The power came on the night the seventh shell washed in. It could have set the shell back in the groove before the water turned. It waited, and saw what the animal did with it.

For several days it watched the foot feel along the wall. There was a place just beyond its reach where the stone was rough enough to hold. The power could have put a little of that roughness nearer. On the fourth evening the animal came out, took a grain of gravel from beneath the shell, and pushed it in again broad side up. The shell tilted. The foot found the wall.

Far out on the flat, a dead branch had lodged across a runnel. On the next high tide it would swing free at one end and sweep a ridge of mud against the sill. The water would still find its way out, but not before it had risen to the roof at the back of the basin, where the young spent the day.

The power lifted one end of the branch and let the tide take it. It went out turning slowly, with a little mud on the part that had been buried.

Water came over the sill that night. Some of the pale gravel changed places, and a snail at the mouth drew itself into its shell. In the morning a young animal put two feet into the thread between the ledges, drew them back, and went around by the long way. At dusk it tried again. This time the thread sagged almost to the water before the animal got a foot on the other side.

The power stayed until it had pulled its last foot through. Then it went on, leaving the branch where the tide had put it, on a bank where there was room for it to rot.
