The power came in the late summer, when the bead at the end of the runnel was as large as it would get. It watched the bead fall. Then it waited through the night and most of the following day to see where the next one formed.

It could have brought water to the tree before dark. It could have brought back the other frog, or a frog close enough that no one coming over the lip would have known the difference. It listened at the seam when the water went quiet. There was no mistaking which frog the knock was meant for.

On the third afternoon, it went down into the stone to see what the work was costing. The bright root beneath the fern had reached a flake of rock with a little clay behind it. The clay was wet in the morning and dry by dusk. Each time it dried, the flake settled back, and the root had to begin again. Already there was a pale, flattened place on the root where the edge bore down.

The power put a grain of sand behind the flake. It chose one from the dry grass above the lip, small enough to leave the flake room to move. When the clay dried that evening, the flake came to rest on the grain.

For six more afternoons the water fell short of the tree. On the seventh it reached a loose curl of bark lying against the trunk. The bark darkened from one end to the other. By morning the clay beneath it was wet.

The power stayed until it saw what the being under the ferns would do with the new passage. It had thought the water might be the end of the work. But the next time the hollow went quiet, the crease crossed the stone and went on into the clay. A little way from the trunk, where there was no fern and had never been one, the ground lifted. It settled again so slowly that a drop on the bark did not fall.

At the seam the frog climbed onto its stone. It called once, facing uphill. Nothing answered. In the pocket under the tree, where the water was not yet deep enough to cover a fallen leaf, the knock had a faint second sound. The frog turned its head.

There was no need to stay after that, but the power stayed until the water grew loud again.
