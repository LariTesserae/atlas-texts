The power came to the valley and sat on the sill. It had meant to stand, but from standing it could not see what the mother did with her chin when she came back to look for a young one.

She went the wrong way first. It was a matter of inches. At the end of the sill she turned and came back, nosing the green. The young one was under the overhang, with its front feet on the wall. It had got high enough that she passed beneath it twice.

The power put out a finger. Before it touched either of them, the young one let go. The mother caught it against the sill with the side of her head. She stayed like that for a while. Her throat moved once.

The power could hear her. It could hear, too, the answer the young one made, which was considerably fainter than the sound of a drop striking a sleeve.

It went down to the upright stones. There was a polish low on the inner face of each, though nothing in the valley seemed to get any more worn. On the way through, the mother had to turn her head a little to keep from scraping the young one’s eyes. The power put its hand against one stone and considered moving it. Then it went around to the other side and found, just past the narrowest part, a hollow where the mother set her foot to take the weight. A wider passage would have been a different passage. She knew this one in the dark.

What it changed was the sound under the far sill. It lay down on the stone and listened from underneath until it knew which part of the roar was coming through the slab and which part was coming around it. Then it took up a loose flake and fitted it under one end. The end rose less than the thickness of a skin. The roar went on. But close to the green there was a little space in which a small sound could be heard.

The mother came back with another young one. She put it down and set off along the sill in the wrong direction. From under the overhang came a sound like a wet lip parting from a tooth. She stopped. She did not go straight to it; she stood a while with her chin clear of the stone, waiting to hear it again.

The power stayed until she had brought the last one over. By then the pale bird had come to stand at the end of the sill. One of the young moved in its sleep, and the mother laid a foot on it. A little later she took the foot away. It had made the sound without waking.
