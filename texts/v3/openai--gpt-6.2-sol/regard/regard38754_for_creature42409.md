The power waited through a shift of the haulage. At first it thought to steady the two flakes that chattered. But when the last loaded car went over, a crumb caught between them broke into three pieces. The broad mite took one. Two smaller animals came out after her and took the others. By then the flakes were still.

It followed the broad mite down from the lip and watched her lay her egg. When she backed away, the grain settled a little farther than she had allowed for. The egg gave under it. There was no mark on the shell, but the animal inside would not have been able to turn the hooks far enough to break out.

The power took the weight of the grain. It did not lift it high enough for the mother, coming back that way, to notice a difference underfoot. It held it there through the cold of the night and the next day’s haulage, and through many days after that. The mother fed at the lip. Once she passed so close to the egg that the rim of her back brushed it.

When the time came, one hook appeared and then went in again. For a long while nothing else happened. Then both hooks came out together. The young mite got two feet onto the glass and pulled. The shell came with her for the length of her body before catching on a point and coming off.

The power let the grain down. The shell folded flat beneath it.

At the lip the young mite found the clear part of a fly wing and worked at it until a long animal came up from below and took it away. She stood a while with her hooks extended. Then she went a little farther out, where the joint was still lodged against the glass.
