The power watched through a season of high water before it did anything.

It had thought at first to give the animals a bank that would stay dry. It had seen an old female come up three times in one afternoon, each time a little farther along the same shelf, and each time settle her belly only to have the water lift her. But on the fourth occasion she went out into the middle of the basin of her own accord. She hung there with her feet clear of the bottom. The fold in her belly opened, and the eggs moved separately in the current. The power had mistaken getting up for being driven off.

There was one thing it saw happen only once. A young animal came up under a fallen leaf. The leaf rose with it, and the animal, feeling the air must be just beyond that last thin weight, kept pushing. It could have gone sideways the length of one of its toes. It did not know this. The power raised a corner of the leaf.

The animal took in air so sharply that its back struck the underside. A snail let go and fell past it. For a little while the two went down together, the snail closed tight and the animal spreading its toes for the clay.

After that the power paid particular attention to what it was like not to know which way was sideways.

When the low water came, it found a shelf where the little fish were left in hollows. Most of the hollows filled from beneath. One did not. The old animal that had made it had rested there long enough for the soft clay to close around her toes. When she pulled free, the holes went only a little way down.

The power let a thread of water through.

A bird came in the evening and took two of the fish. The third stayed on until the basin rose. The power did not count the first two as a mistake. It had watched the bird go from one dry hollow to the next, lifting its feet clear of the clay with an effort that made the feathers at its knees shake.

At the far end, where the first leaves were opening, there was a place where the water ran off a hump in two directions. Most days the division was no wider than the back of a snail. The power loosened a little clay there. It did not cut a channel. It made a low place where a channel might begin, if the water came that way often enough and the roots did not get there first.

The roots got there first.

The next year a female laid an egg on one of the stems they held. As the young animal grew, the stem bent toward the little crease. The water was going down at the time. All afternoon the stem followed it, sagging by a distance almost too small to see. Just before dark, the skin split. One pink foot came out and felt along the stem, then reached past it into water.

The power had not foreseen that. It stayed to see where the other feet would go.
