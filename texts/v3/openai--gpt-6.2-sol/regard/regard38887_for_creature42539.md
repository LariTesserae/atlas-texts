The power came in the evening and sat on the turf above the square, where it could hear the drops fall from the mint. It stayed there until the masons had gone and the young hen came out from under the bench with a root.

That evening the cloud stopped a little short of the stair. The hen leaned out until the bare skin of her breast touched the stone. Something took hold of the root, but when she let go there was a scrape underneath, and the root fell. The hen stood looking down for a while. Then she went back for another.

The power went below the stair to see what the trouble was. The stone there was smooth except for a seam that ran the width of the tread. Something in the cloud had got a grip on the seam. It could hold on or take the root, but for the instant it took to do one it could not do the other.

There was new stone coming up under the stair just as there was under the bench. The power laid a hand against it and let it come out sideways a little. A mason would have called the result a bad place to put your foot. It was not level and would not take a sandal. But it would take the weight of what had been hanging there, and from the town it could not be seen.

When the hen came down again, she passed the cup and put the root over the edge. This time, after the pull came, the root lay slack against the stone. She did not let go at once. She had learned to wait for a second pull. It came, and she opened her bill.

The power stayed until she had gone back up. It had meant to go then, but below the stair there was the small, uneven sound of earth being picked off a root. It sat and listened. After a while the cloud rose and covered the bottom tread.

In the morning the child found the cup tipped against the bank. A white root lay beside it, neatly stripped of earth, with the little crown of green still on it. She had been leaving that part on because it gave the hen something to hold. She put the root back in the cup with a little water, and when she went home that evening, she took it with her to plant.
