The young deer stood with the ferns across its neck until the air came through again. This time it felt the movement on the skin inside its nostrils. It drew back, sneezed, and went after its mother.

She had put a foot through the turf. The hole was the sort she had made a hundred times, but under this one a loose stone had turned when she pulled, and the narrow end of it lay across her foot. She would pull twice and then stand still for a while, with her head low. The young deer came up on the side where her ear was split and began to feed.

The power that had been watching the plateau went down into the hole. It found the stone by the warmth of the foot beneath it. The stone had a thin edge. It could have broken there, but the broken edge would have been sharper, and there was room, just, to turn the whole thing back.

The power waited until the doe stopped pulling. Then it took the weight of the stone and turned it a little. Mud came into the space as soon as there was space to come into. The doe felt the change and lifted her foot.

She held it off the ground. The young deer reached for a blade of grass under her chest and she moved out of its way, putting the foot down without quite meaning to. It held. A little farther on she stopped to feed. She still favored the foot, but by the time she reached the pool she could stand in the water on all four.

The power stayed at the hole. In turning the stone it had closed a joint where a thread of water had been running. It could feel the water backing up against the mud. There was a root along one side of the joint, alive to the very tip, and the power eased the stone away from it until the thread went through. That took longer than freeing the doe.

By then both deer were out of sight. The bird had gone to roost in the thick grass above the ferns. Air came through the bank once more in the dark, lifting a drop that had hung all evening from the end of a frond. The drop landed on a lower leaf and went on down.
