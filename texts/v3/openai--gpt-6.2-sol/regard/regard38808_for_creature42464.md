The power would wait for a wet night. In dry weather it might have mistaken the mouse’s difficulty for a small doorway and made it larger, which would have made it a better doorway for several other things.

It would watch her carry the young up to the grass. The smallest would catch a hind foot in the down and come free with a little grey slipper, which it would lose halfway up the wall. The mouse would make the journey three times. On the third she would stop at the slit and turn the young one in her mouth before putting it in. The power would see then that there was room enough to get in, and not room enough to turn around.

It would ease a flake of limestone out of the back of the slit. Not the flake that held the roots. The one behind it, which was already loose, though nothing had yet come along that could take hold of it. It would lay the flake flat below the sill, rough side up, where the moss might get onto it.

The bowls would fill as before. The last notch would run, and on the outside face the water would break apart in the wind. The power would not stop to catch it.

Inside the slit the mouse would feel the space behind her with one hind foot. She would back into it, then put her head out again. Nothing at the entrance would have changed. After a while she would turn and nose her way between the young, who would make the small offended sounds they made whenever she wanted to lie where they were lying.

By morning she would have worked the lost tuft of down out of a crack on the wall and brought it up to the grass. She would push it into the new corner until it stayed. The smallest would pull it out again while she slept.
