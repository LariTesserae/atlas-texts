The power watched a hunter carry its young to the end of a curled strip. The hunter put one foot down and lifted the other five. There was nothing running on the far side. What it felt was the strip coming apart.

There had been places to go in the first hour after the fall. The pieces lay across one another, with spaces between them, and a hunter could pass from one underside to the next without putting a foot in daylight. The wind had been taking the small pieces first. By noon there were only three large ones left, and the one the hunter stood on had begun to lift.

The power did not hold it down. There were grazers feeding along the edge, and farther off there were mouths at valves that would open a little wider when the weight came off the rind. The falling of a leaf was an ordinary event here. This did not make it a small event to anything that lived on one.

A little way downwind there was an old split, sealed with amber. The power lifted one lip of it. It lifted it no higher than the thickness of a hunter’s body, but it went on lifting it for some distance, until the sheltered space reached a seam warm enough to live in after dark.

The first animal to find it was a black runner. It went in at one end and out at the other. Then it went back in. When the strip finally tore loose, the hunter came down with it and lay still for a while on the bright rind. Its young had hooked itself to the joint of a leg.

The runner set off across the gap. The hunter felt it go. At the split the runner stopped, and the hunter had to put its foot down three times before it found the little trembling edge.

By evening it had brought two more young from the wreck. It went back once after dark and came home without anything. There was room enough in the split for it to turn around without coming out. The power had not known, until it saw the hunter do this, how seldom there had been room enough for that.
