By the third morning the posts are in line for most of an hour. People stop on the old trail to watch the last pane come in. It is wide enough that, while it is clear, they can see the bronze fan and the three young behind it. One of the young puts a foot out. A child on the trail puts out a foot too, though there is nothing under it but air, and has to be taken by the back of the coat.

The pane comes down to within a finger’s width of the leaves.

Something that has been listening to the pass listens a little longer. There is the weight of the people waiting to go over, and the weight of the water that will gather there toward evening. There is also the small pull of three roots in three cracks. Until now the stone has been coming in as if the wall were bare.

On the old shoulder, well back from the edge, a warm bead works its way out of a crack. Instead of rising, it runs along the stone. It goes farther than a bead of that size ought to go. The clear pane follows it, turning so slowly that the people on the trail notice the turn only when they can see the sky in it. Then the far corner settles into the old shoulder. The near corner comes down a little uphill of where everyone expected it.

The report is no quieter than the others. The animals bolt. When the dust is gone, there is a shallow bend in the trail, and under the bend a space that a person could get a hand into but not an arm. The leaves have room to open all the way. Rain still comes through, though not everywhere at once.

The first walker takes the bend without looking down. The second sets a cup on the post with the hook, tries it there, and moves it to a level spot on the stone. By evening somebody has put a little water in it.

Underneath, in a place where the new stone was meant to close against the old, a moth walks to the edge of a patch of water and back again. The white-toed animal has come to look at the gap. It reaches across, takes hold, and brings its hind feet over one at a time. From there it can get to the bronze leaves without going out on the trail. It goes back the way it came and does it once more.
