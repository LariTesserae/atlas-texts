The power turned sideways to get in. It could have come through the stone, but the opening was there.

It sat on the stool. For a long while there was nothing to do. The smallest animals were still clear at the thighs. One had put a foot down on another’s back, and when the foot was at last dislodged, there was a vein exactly where it needed to come down.

From the stool the power could see something the person who made it had never stayed long enough to see. When an animal came out of the last ring, the mother kept the seam open until it took its weight off her. Then she closed it. Later, at the sound of the leaf, the seam opened a little. It did this even when the animal had been off her back for days.

From where she lay there was no view of the leaf. The root came down to the stone between them.

The power took the piece of mica from the rim of the dish. It held it by the edges and turned it until the young leaf appeared in it, very small. Then it set the mica in the salt where the mother could see it by moving the last ring a little way out of the hollow. Where she usually rested she would see only the root.

The first time the mother moved that far, the mica showed her a bare stalk. An animal had just got all its feet onto it and stood without moving. Then it lifted one foot. The foot beside it stayed where it was.

It took the animal most of the visit to reach the fold. Twice in that time the mother withdrew into the hollow. The third time she came out a little farther than she needed to, and the mica showed her only the ceiling. She drew back until she found the stalk again.

When the leaf opened, the red was no bigger than a grain in the mica. The mother stayed out until the animal came around the edge of the leaf and stood on the side that had been hidden.

On its way out the power steadied itself against the wall with an open hand, clear of the flake. It left the stool where it was.
