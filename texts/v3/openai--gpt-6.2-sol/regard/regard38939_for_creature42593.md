When the fern went under, the loose end slipped twice.

The animal could get its front feet onto the stone, but there was nothing there for the hooks. Each time it drew the thread tight, the frond lifted a little in the current and took the thread back. The empty shell knocked against the rim. By then the animal had been working long enough for a shrimp to climb into the shell and begin feeding.

The power came down to the seat. It had noticed the animal some days before, when the shell was still hanging clear of the water. What had caught its attention was not the nest but the way the animal went out every morning and put both hands on the shell, as if to find out what the night had done to it.

There was a stone on the outer sill with a shallow groove in it. The power had only to turn it over. But when it lifted the stone it found a snail underneath, its whole soft length laid out against the cool face. It put the stone back.

Then it reached into the pool and held the fern.

The animal went still. It had never known the frond to be still when the water was moving. For a while the only thing that came out of the nest was the shrimp, walking backward with something white in its mouth.

The power waited. The pressure of the frond against its hand was slight but changed all the time. It took some practice to hold it without making it more rigid than a fern ought to be.

Presently the animal came out again. This time, when it reached the stone, it went farther along the rim than it had before. There was a fine lip there, where the stone had worn away unevenly. The animal got one hook under it and then another. It passed the thread beneath itself, changed feet, and passed it back. From the seat the whole business looked like an animal trying to get something off its leg. It did it four times.

The power let the fern take its weight a little at a time. At the first pull the animal backed up and put both hands on the line. It stood that way until the frond had risen as far as the knot would let it. Then it went home.

By morning the water had fallen enough to leave the shell in the air again. The wader came back and found the shrimp gathered, as usual, where the fig root entered the pool. On the underside of the fern the animal was adding a second line. It had found another place to tie it, farther along the stone, which the power had not seen.
