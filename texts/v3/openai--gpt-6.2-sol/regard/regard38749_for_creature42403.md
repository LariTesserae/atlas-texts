The power came at the hour when the drivers changed over. It found the basin under the shelter bench and put it back where the feet of the bench had left four pale squares. Someone had moved it to sweep. There was a little water left in it, with a track of grit where an animal had stood.

All night the power listened to the foundations. It could have stopped them. It could have held the addresses in place itself, without a sound, but then the room in the middle of the crossing would have closed, and the calf would have had to learn to drink some other way. Nor did it like the idea of making the city depend on its staying.

Toward morning it heard a foundation miss a stroke. The survey lamp above it stayed red. A long street gained the width of a finger, and in a room on the open stone a sleeping man took his hand away from his ear. On the next stroke the street drew in again. The man put his hand back without waking.

The power went to look where the blow had gone. Far below the foundation, a root had grown between two slabs. It had flattened as it grew, and for the length of that one stroke the slabs had rested against it instead of against each other. There was a clear drop on the underside of the root. The power held it there until the next stroke came and saw that there was room enough for the root to thicken, if it grew sideways.

In the morning a foundation keeper found a faint wet line running out from under the casing. He fetched a lamp. His apprentice, looking in over his shoulder, asked why they shouldn’t leave the root where it was and see what happened. Ordinarily he would have said that roots were the reason they had inspection covers. He was near the end of his shift, though, and the apprentice lived on the open stone. He gave her the lamp and went up to check the survey marks.

They left the cover off for a week. The street kept its length. In the three nearest rooms the people could hear a cupboard shut in the next room, which none of them had known was possible. The woman with the folding bench came to see whether the little tread behind the station had gone. It was still there. She sat on it to eat an egg.

There were foundations where the same thing could be done and foundations where it couldn’t. At one of them a trial sent the upper note straight into a nursery on the other side of a garden. The keeper and his apprentice put that one back the way it had been. By winter they had quieted half a dozen streets, and in one of them the landlord raised the rents. At the next public meeting, the people who came to talk about roots stayed to talk about that.

The power remained until the grazers crossed again. Last season’s calf was nearly as tall as its mother. It went past the shelter without turning its head. After the herd had gone, one of the hunters came out from beneath the bench, put both forefeet in the basin, and drank. There was still a long reach of bare stone between that shelter and the next garden. In a seam not far from the curb, the power found a seed the herd had left. It waited for the rails to cool. When the first bead reached the end of the rail, the power drew it a little farther, until it fell into the seam. The next bead fell in the same place without help.
