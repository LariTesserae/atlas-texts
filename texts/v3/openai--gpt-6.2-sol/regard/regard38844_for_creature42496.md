The power first noticed the city by its noise. There was a place, well below the lowest boring, where two layers had been rubbing for a long time. They were both alive. The lower one was putting on wood as fast as the upper one wore it away. At the mills they would have taken the shaft out, but there was no shaft here, and nowhere to put one if there had been.

The power lay down between the layers. It could make itself as thin as the water in a joint. For a little while the upper wood went on moving and the lower wood did not have to grow.

This happened in the evening, while the old woman was in her kitchen. She had filled the pan from the tap and was waiting for the pipes to stop knocking. After they stopped she heard a foot on the draining board.

The calf was standing where she used to keep the bread. It had come in through the gap at the top of the window. There was no room for it there, but she knew better than to move anything until it had got itself down.

It put one foot into the pan, then the other. It was not the calf she remembered. This one had a dark place on its nose, and both its hind feet were on the floor.

She sat down. The calf stood in the pan until the tram came, and when she got up to turn the light out, it lifted one foot and set it down again.

“Go on,” she said. “I don’t need the pan.”

At the card table she found they had dealt her a hand and put a spoon across it to keep it separate from the discards. She told them there was a calf in her kitchen. One of the players asked if she meant the old one.

“The old kitchen,” she said, and picked up her cards.

Under the city, moisture had gathered in the space the power held open. The lower layer took it up. By morning there was a narrow band of new wood, softer than the wood on either side, and the power drew away to see whether it would hold.

The next time the layers moved, there was still a roll beneath the streets. It stopped for a breath in the middle. In that breath a fitter heard an apprentice on the other side of a press ask for a rag, and handed him one.
