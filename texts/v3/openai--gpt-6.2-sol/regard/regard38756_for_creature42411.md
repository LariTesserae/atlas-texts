The power came while rain stood in the courtyard. It stayed until the courtyard was dry, and through the afternoon, when the room was driest, and through the next rain. It wanted to know how much of the water reached the cup. Less reached it than the darkening of the chalk would suggest.

There was a way to bring in more. The power could have made a crack in the tile so fine that a person walking over it barefoot would not have felt it. But directly below the place where the first drop would gather was the flake of glaze. A drop gathering there would fill the pit on the raw face before it fell.

At the back of the cup, where the bare clay touched a length of chalk, the two surfaces leaned together without quite meeting. The power moved the chalk. It moved it less than the distance the wall had moved in the last winter. A little powder went down into a joint.

The next time rain stood in the courtyard, a dark line came out of the chalk and travelled along the clay. It stopped short of the handle. Under the overlapping edges, where the living chambers were, it stayed until well after the chalk had gone white.

The mite came out and fed at the old ragged edge. It went back by the same route. The power had thought it might go farther and had waited to see, but that was all it did.

Much later, there was a green chamber on the near side of the notch where there had not been one before. The notch itself was still there.

In the afternoon a child in the courtyard put a hand on the white tile. The power could have let her hear the whistle. Instead it let her feel the faint rough note that anyone might feel who held still long enough. She lifted her hand, then put it back. With her other hand she felt her wrist. For a little while she tried to count the two things separately.
