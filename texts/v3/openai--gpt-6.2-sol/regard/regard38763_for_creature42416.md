The power came on a morning when the click had not yet caught up with the rain. It waited for the click before doing anything. By then the larger animal had crossed under the drop and was pulling at the root hair. The hair came down against its back. It ran away and came back.

There was no need to make the light come from somewhere. The seedling, which had been turning for nine days, had put out a root on the side away from the seam. It had found what it needed without settling the question.

But the fine root was dying. The power followed it up through the clay to where a flat stone had shifted, pinching it against another stone. It lifted the flat stone just enough to let the root thicken. In the pocket nothing moved. Several days later a white tip appeared on the root hair the animal had been pulling. The animal tried it, found it as unmanageable as before, and took a thread of fungus instead.

The power stayed through the smaller animal's next shedding. This time the larger one was there when the skin opened. It put its mouth to the empty head first. Then the smaller animal moved one of its new feet, and the larger one came around to where it was. For a while neither could do much. The new feet were too soft to stand on, and the old skin was still attached at the tail. The larger animal got two feet under the smaller one and stood that way until the clay dried beneath them.

At the blister there was a morning in which this took less time. There was another in which the smaller animal did not get out of its skin. The power saw the larger animal at the rim, with its head down. It could have closed the blister. But a mite had gone in that morning with an egg, and was still walking, stopping now and then to shift the egg to the other side of its mouth.

What the power did was to take the worn-out shadow from beside the root and lay it along the inside of the blister, on one side only. The next mite to go in kept the shadow on its left. After a long time it came out with the shadow on its right. After that there was a way back, though it was not always a short way.

The larger animal did not go in. It went to the seedling and began moving the broad grain off the old husk. The power had thought it meant to put the new husk beside the old one. Instead it carried the grain to where the smaller animal was resting and set it down at a slant, one edge on the clay and the other on the stem. The smaller animal walked under it. There was room for the larger animal too, but it stayed outside, eating a thread of fungus by little jerks. When it had eaten enough it put its head under the grain. Two feet came out and took hold of its face.

The power left the grain as it was.
