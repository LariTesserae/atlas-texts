The power came while the bridge was apart. The little one had put its hook through the hole in the toe and could not get it back. Each time it pulled, the skin tightened. The parent held the heel open, though there was no longer any water in it.

From the two walls there was nothing to see. The power could see the crossing from the middle. The overturned young lay with their feet drawn up. A little way off, a black head waited with its mouth open. A white swimmer had caught in the hinge of its jaw. The swimmer was dead, and the head could neither swallow it nor let it go.

The power loosened the swimmer. The head swallowed it and shut its mouth. It would feed again at the next joining.

Then the power looked at the foot. It could have torn the toe, but the little one would need the skin when the water came back. It could have drawn the hook in for it. Instead it put a finger against the outside of the toe, just where the skin folded over the hook. The little one pulled twice more. Then it stopped pulling. It pushed a little farther out, which was the thing there had not been room to do before, and turned its hook. The skin fell slack.

The parent began to take the foot away. The little one caught the heel from inside, and for a moment neither moved. Then the parent let go.

Up at the notch, the last running water froze. The power stayed to see whether there would be another joining. It had seen the heat coming through the stone and knew there would be, but it stayed all the same.

When the water met, the foot filled so quickly that the toes sprang straight. The little one went down the same toe again. There was still food in it. This time, when it came to the hole, it put out its hook and caught the edge from the other side.

The parent went back to the jaw. It found the notch on the first pass. There was still one egg behind the tooth. The power had not noticed, until the parent laid a hook against it, how much of the egg was clear now, or how regularly the small mouth inside it opened.
