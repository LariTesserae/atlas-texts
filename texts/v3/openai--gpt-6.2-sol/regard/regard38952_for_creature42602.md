After dark the large animal got up and went as far as the end of the east street. It put its chin on the lip of shale. The step was not too high for it, but to get its forelegs up it had to turn its head sideways, and then it could not see what it was climbing toward. Twice it tried. The second time it stood with one foot on the rim for nearly a minute before drawing it down.

The power that had been watching could see the road beyond the lip, and the ditch beyond the road, and a long way down the ditch. There was water in it, running among roots. It could also see the way back to the culvert. The animal had left a wet track most of the way from there to the curb, though by now it was dry in the middle.

The power did not know which way the animal would go. It knew a great many things about the animal, including how it had got through the culvert, but not that.

A flat piece of shale had worked loose on the road side of the lip. The power drew it out and set it at the end of the street. It had to lift the piece again almost at once. Three of the seven-footed animals had come to see what was being put down, and one had got a foot under it. When they had gone on, the power set the stone a little farther out, where there was room to pass behind it.

The large animal was still there. It smelled the upper face of the stone, then the underside, which had not been in the air for a long time. It set both forefeet on it. From there it could look over the rim without turning its head.

A truck came by. It was going slowly enough that the animal could hear the loose end of a strap striking the side. For a little while the lights showed the ditch. The animal watched the water move in the opposite direction from the truck. When the lights were gone it climbed all the way up and stood with its front feet on the road.

Then it came down again. It went back to the weighhouse, where the mother was lying along the door with one ear folded under her. The young one had found a place between her forelegs. There was no room there for the large animal, but it lay near enough that when the young one stretched in its sleep, a foot touched its nose.

By morning several of the small animals had gone under the new step and out the other side. A fitter on his way in put his boot on it and rocked it once. He fetched a chip of shale from the empty lot and wedged it under the low corner. After that it did not rock. The power left it to him.
