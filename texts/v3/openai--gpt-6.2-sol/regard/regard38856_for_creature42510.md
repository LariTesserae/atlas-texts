The power would wait with it.

There was a little while, on starting the machine, when water went down the highest pipe instead of up. The woman knew this from the meter, but the meter was two floors below, and she had never seen what happened behind the leaves. The reversal took scarcely a breath. It was long enough to draw the thin film off the rock where the young held on.

Most years none fell. One year two had fallen into the pipe. There was no mark anywhere by which she could have known it.

The power could have held them to the rock. Instead, on the morning of the next start, it let a drop fall from the curtain of leaves into the woman’s tea. She moved her cup. A second drop fell, not into the cup but up against the underside of a leaf. She set the cup down and bent to look.

She watched through the whole of the start. Afterward she went below and drew a diagram on the back of an old inspection sheet. She drew the leaves, too, though they were not necessary to explain what the pipe was doing.

The change took until autumn. It was a small change, but the first fitting they sent her was the wrong size, and the second had a burr she wouldn’t leave in a water line. When it was done she came up with her tea and watched the seep. The vise began to tremble. Behind the leaves the water went on coming down.

The old animal reached the leaf that rested on the pool. It put a foot in, took it out, and waited. There were things to wait for besides the machine. A worm was working its way out of a seed husk. The animal turned its head a little toward it.

The power stayed until the woman finished her tea. It did nothing about the bolt. The animal knew the bolt.
