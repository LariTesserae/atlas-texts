The floor of the valley is higher than the summits of the old mountains. There are places along its rim where you can look down through a cut in the rock and see the tops of those mountains, much farther below than they ought to be. Most of the time there is no seeing them. The air is clear, but there is very little light. Toward the west a band of dull red persists at ground level, and now and then it goes out behind a ridge that was not there a moment before.

The valley is warm from underneath. On a flat slab you can feel the warmth through the soles of your feet, unevenly, as you might feel for a living thing through a blanket. The air against your face is cooler and so dry that a breath leaves no trace even in the sheltered hollows. There is no frost at this height, and no salt. Nothing has ever dissolved here and been left behind. The white in the rock is white all the way through.

There are miles of open ground. What looks from a distance like a slope of fine sand turns out to be a single surface that has come apart into plates without yet scattering them. Each plate will fit its neighbor exactly. Their edges are keen, and finer cracks end abruptly in flawless stone. There has not been time for a fallen block to acquire a rounded corner.

It is a quiet place in which to watch a great deal happen. Half a mile off, a straight line appears in the valley floor. Along it, shelves of warm stone rise one after another, some as long as a field. The movement is clean and surprisingly quick. A whole shelf tips, and for a few seconds its broad underside catches the red from the west. Then it drops out of sight. Much later there is a little clack, so near and distinct you might think it came from a stone you had just put down.

Nothing follows these disturbances. No wings lift. No rooted thing leans away from the heat or finds purchase in the new cracks. There are dark fans in the hollows that look like growth until you see one forming: grains jerk into rows on a flat face, starting at the edge and working inward. At the next tremor the fan slides off entire. The stone underneath is bare.

Here and there, far enough apart that it takes a while to notice the likeness, three loose stones stand around a fourth. The outer stones are set on edge; the middle one lies flat. At one such place the ground has opened and taken two of the uprights, but the other still stands, overlooking a depth the arrangement was never meant to mark. Whether anyone set the stones there is hard to say. There are no paths between them, not even a run of chips where someone might have dressed an edge.

Near the rim, the stone grows dark enough that you have to put a hand down to tell a ledge from a shadow. From there you can see the valley taking up more room. Its far wall shifts by degrees, and between two black shoulders a broad new reach of floor comes into view. A low red gleam travels over it, showing every seam. For a while the new ground is the brightest thing at this elevation. Then it cools, and there is simply more dark valley than there was before.
