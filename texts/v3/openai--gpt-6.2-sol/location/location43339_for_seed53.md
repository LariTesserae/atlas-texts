The hollow is thirty paces long and sits just below the highest point of the ridge. From inside it you can see almost nothing of the mountain. The near wall cuts off the summit; the far lip cuts off the drop. There is only a band of sky, very dark above the stone, and sometimes the tops of clouds passing from one side of the opening to the other. Their shadows are already on the country far below.

The rock is gray in the mass and nearly white where it has been rubbed. Long weathering has taken the sharpness off the larger forms without making them smooth. The walls have shallow scoops in them, each a little offset from the one above, and the floor is crowded with flat stones standing on edge. There is room to walk, but rarely room to put a whole foot down. In the narrow spaces between the stones there are smaller stones, and then chips, and then a flour so pale it looks dry even where it is wet.

Most mornings a cloud brushes through the hollow. It leaves beads on the underside of the projecting ledges and a dark seam at the foot of the wall. The sun comes in steeply afterward. Drops fall for perhaps half an hour, always from the same few points. One strikes the corner of a flake and divides; half goes down a cleft, and half creeps back along the flake's underside until it is too heavy to stay there. In the deepest scoop there is usually enough water to wet a knuckle. By evening it has shrunk to a bead under a skin of ice. There is no stream out of the hollow, only a stain that gets a finger's breadth farther down the wall in a wet year.

In that light, a damp patch can be the darkest thing in sight. Even the shaded stone gives back a little of the glare from across the hollow. Nothing grows where the water falls. Beside one of the scoops is a rusty fringe that from a standing height looks like a fine, dry moss. Up close it is a row of mineral plates, with grains of white grit lodged between them. It continues under a ledge where the sun never reaches.

Someone once made a seat in the lee of the higher wall. It is a broad slab resting on two squat supports, with small wedges driven under the lower end. Strike marks remain on the exposed ends of the wedges. The seat is too low to see over the lip from, but in the afternoon the sun reaches it while the floor all around is in shadow. A little farther up is a cup pecked into the native rock. Whoever made it stopped before taking out a hard knot in the middle. Water gathers around the knot in a ring. There are no other tools or marks, and on the approach there is no path to lose: just bare stone all the way up.

The wind is often audible from the seat without being felt there. It goes over the opening with a low, steady note. Now and then a loose flake taps against its neighbor. Toward dusk the cooling wall gives a click that is hard to locate, and a few grains come down through the crowded floor, ticking against one edge after another. In the morning there will be a sprinkling of fresh white dust two handspans from where they started. With the first drops it darkens and is no longer distinguishable from the rest.
