The valley has a floor but no ground. At the edge you can kneel and lift a corner of it: a thick green sheet, warm on top, slick underneath, with white roots running out into the air. The corner settles back into its seam when you let go. It fits so well that water passes over it without catching.

Everything here has been made in much the same way. The broad steps down from the rim are layers of folded leaf, their treads filled out with pith. The low walls are stems bent over and grafted into themselves. In the middle distance stands a long arcade with nothing under it. Its columns are spaced far enough apart that you can look between two of them and see a whole mile of shining floor. There are no chisel marks, no fastenings. Where a curve was needed, the little cells divided at an angle. Where a span had to meet another span, two soft tips touched and thickened together.

The place is open enough to take in at a glance and too large to judge by eye. Some of what look like puddles are basins a hundred paces across. Their green bottoms are so shallow and uniform that you can see the shadow of a passing insect travel all the way across one. Here and there a single upright leaf rises from the floor, taller than a person. Each has the whole light to itself. Nothing grows in the spaces between them because the spaces themselves are growing.

Water is seldom more than a finger’s depth away. It beads up through the walking surfaces and collects in long, level runs. At the rim it pours through slots in the folded leaves. The drop is considerable, though the valley is only high enough to look out over the tops of a few low clouds. From below comes a continuous, full-throated wash. Nearer at hand, drops strike the hollow walls and make them ring. You can feel the notes in the floor through the soles of your feet. It takes a while to notice that the loudest sound is coming from a part of the valley that looks perfectly still.

The light is ordinary afternoon light. A wet wall is bright on one side and dull on the other. The warmth is enough to bring out a smell like the inside of a split pea. In a sheltered fold you might find a small translucent snail, and once in a long while something narrow-winged goes rattling down the arcade. Neither seems to have anywhere in particular to get to.

At the far end, the floor stops in a row of pale rolled lips. Beyond them is a wide gap, and beyond the gap the first few ribs of another terrace are laid out against the sky. The ends of the ribs shine with sap. The whole place has that look of work left for the day: a seam still open, a buttress soft enough to take a thumbprint, a stair with one more riser needed before it reaches the landing.

The thumbprint fills in while you watch. The stair never gets its last riser. A thread of water runs over the same unfinished edge, but the edge is as sharp and tender as if the leaf had unrolled that morning. In the basins you can see how this works on a small scale. A green film creeps out over the water, almost too slowly to see; at its outer edge the film is already coming apart. The two motions keep the margin in one place.

From the high rim the unfinished terrace is the clearest thing in the valley. You can see where every rib is meant to go. A little water gathers at the tip of each one and falls, leaving the tip bright enough to look, even at that distance, as though it had just begun to grow.
