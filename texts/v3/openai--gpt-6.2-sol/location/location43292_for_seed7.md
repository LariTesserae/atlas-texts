The room is only twenty paces long, though in the dark it takes some time to establish that it has another end. A handrail leads down into it from a hatch in the hillside. The rail is warm from the first step. By the last, the air is hot enough that the sweat on your arms no longer feels cool.

The floor has split quite recently. On one side of the split, old concrete shows the shallow impressions of the boards it was poured against. On the other, bare rock has risen through it, shouldering a slab up at a slight angle. The broken edges are still sharp. There is white dust under the slab where the damp has not reached it.

Most of the room is bare. All the green is gathered in three deep seams, and in those seams it grows extravagantly. Thick, pleated leaves lie over one another, some large enough to hold the print of a whole hand in their dust. A root as broad as a wrist has followed the joint around a pump housing rather than crossing the iron. Between the seams there are yards of empty floor. Nothing grows on the low plinth in the middle, though water sometimes stands in the bolt holes.

An amber inspection lamp burns behind a wire guard near the ceiling. From the steps it looks as if the lamp has gone out; only when you pass directly beneath it can you see the filament. The plants nearest it have turned their leaves edge-on, exposing ribs the colour of celery. Farther away, along the hot wall, the foliage is thicker. Those leaves have a dull copper underside. In the small hours, when the hillside cools, they turn over one by one. By morning there will be beads of water on them, and by noon there will be none.

A return pipe enters the rock at either end of the room. There is a pressure gauge, a drain cock, and a little shelf with the pale outline of a cup on it. Whatever drives the water is a long way off. You can feel its passage with a palm against the pipe, but you cannot hear it. Now and then a drop lets go from the ceiling and strikes a leaf. It rarely gets as far as the floor.

At the far end, where the uplift has opened a narrow gap behind the concrete, a new leaf is coming in. It has grown flat against the rock with its upper surface facing inward, where there is no light at all. Behind it are the remains of earlier leaves, each pressed thin enough to show the grain of the stone. The oldest are folded into the break itself. The newest has not yet hardened. There is just room to slide a finger behind it, and the rock there is cooler than anything else in the room.
