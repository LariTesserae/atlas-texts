The flat animals are not all flat in the same way. The young are thin from nose to tail and can turn around in a seam without coming out of it. The old ones are thick at the shoulders. When they hang under the lip, their hindquarters sag, and now and then they have to take a fresh hold. There is one with a white toe that always uses the same foothold. The stone there has a little polish to it, though there is no telling how long a toe takes to do that.

In the rain each animal has about as much room as it can reach with a forefoot. They keep to these patches quite sharply. One will let a moth fall past its nose if the moth is coming down on the wrong side. The young catch mostly wings. You can find the wings stuck to their chins afterward, opening and shutting as they breathe.

The white-toed one is not among the dozen that go across. It comes as far as the place they went around and puts its long foot down. Then it lifts the foot and waits. When the reflection lifts its foot, the animal strikes. It draws out something narrow and white that winds twice around its wrist. It takes the thing back under the lip alive.

The bronze leaves are where it takes it. By day the fan looks as if it grew in a poor place and turned its back on the light. Now the animal gets its nose between two leaves, and the whole fan tips out from the wall. There are three young behind it, pressed into a space hardly deep enough for the white toe. They don’t reach for the food. They put their feet on the old one’s wrist and hold it still while they eat.

Later, when the saddle is quiet, the leaves open a little farther. Each has a pale root going into a different crack. The young move out along them, one to a leaf, and wait with their mouths open. The wall is dry at this hour, but the roots give water. It comes down the center of each leaf in a fine dark line. One of the young is too far out and the water rises from the tip before it can drink. It backs up a little. The next bead catches under its chin.

In the morning the cap is gone. There is a round dry place where the cup stood, and the first walker puts a boot over it without looking. Most of the animals have moved to the far shoulder. The old one with the white toe has gone back to its foothold. It hangs there while the boots go by, lifting its feet one at a time. Above each foot, a little dust works through a seam. The animal waits until the dust has passed before it puts the foot back.
