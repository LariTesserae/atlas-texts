The biggest of the cockroaches is a female with a brown egg case showing at the end of her abdomen. When the door shuts she goes under the stair with the others, but she has to turn sideways at the place where the stringer meets the wall. The case catches there. She backs out, feels along the joint a little higher, and goes in. In a few days she will leave the case behind the steel plate, in a thread of old caulk soft enough to take an impression of it.

The white ones are not a different kind. One has climbed onto the back of its discarded skin and is standing there while its legs harden. The skin is nearly whole. Even the feelers have come out of their old sleeves. Later there will be only a bit of the back left; the smaller roaches eat the rest.

They have more to eat than the floor suggests. In the open end of the insulation at an elbow there is half a cracker that went in with a workman’s glove. Along the wall below it there is a run of paste from a paper label. The label itself has slipped into the water, where it has broken into islands of white fiber. The young roaches go out onto it from the wall. If it shifts under them they stop, holding their feelers up until an edge touches concrete again.

The centipede on the junction box can reach the wall with its front legs without moving the rest of its body. It does this now and then. There is a little fly that breeds in the wet dirt under the drum, and when one comes up the wall the centipede takes it so quickly that the legs seem to close on nothing. Afterward it spends a long time drawing one leg at a time through its mouth. It has lost a leg near the middle. On that side there is a space in the fan just wide enough to see the screw in the lid of the box.

In the shallows, the mosquito larvae feed on things too small to distinguish from the water. The floor looks clean where they feed. Close up, there is a cloudy skin on the concrete and a scattering of dark pellets caught in the grain the finishing brush left. The water is no deeper than a finger there. One of the older larvae has stopped eating and hangs curled at the surface. When a drop comes down it tumbles to the bottom and lies still for a moment, then rises without quite straightening. In another day or two a mosquito will stand on the split back of that little curled skin, on water that cannot yet bear the weight of its wings.

There is a spider above the motor door. Its web takes up the space between a pipe hanger and a fold in the insulation jacket. Most of what the web catches is lint. The spider cuts the larger pieces out and lets them fall, and leaves the little ones where they stick. Once in a while one of the flies from the drum gets as far as the warm air moving along the ceiling. There are three dry husks in the fold behind the spider. When the motor stops, the loose edge of the jacket stops trembling, and the web goes still from the pipe hanger down.

The seedling at the door came in on somebody’s boot. There is a little hook between its two leaves that will be the first leaf with a shape of its own. For part of each day a narrow band of white light lies across the threshold from a fixture in the passage. It reaches the seedling but not the loose seed coat beside it. A roach has been over both and has eaten the softer end of the coat.

At the rock face, the green threads make a slippery patch no wider than a palm. There are cells living farther into the seam, beyond the reach of the lamp. They are not green. Some grow in a film on the grain of the stone, in water that moves so slowly a pocket of it can stay there through a whole stopping and starting of the main. Where the water comes out, the film and the mineral crust grow through each other. A fleck breaks off now and then and goes down into the shallows. Most of the flecks come to rest at the painted line, where the brush left a ridge.

When the machine arrives, its feet will go down on either side of that ridge. Someone will sweep the grit away from the door so the pallet jack can come through. The man guiding it will put his hand on the stair rail and look up to see whether the load will clear the pipes. Beneath his hand, under the tread where the new concrete meets the steel, a young roach will go on working at the last joint of an old leg.
