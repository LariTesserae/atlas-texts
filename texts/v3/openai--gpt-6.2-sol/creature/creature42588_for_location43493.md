A little gray fly lives on the back of the ladder, between the lowest rung and the wall. Seen from the grate, it could be a burr the galvanizing left. It has broad, hairy wings folded over its back. The rung is wet on top and wet at both ends, but underneath there is a strip of dry metal just wide enough for the fly to walk along without dragging its wings.

It didn’t come in as a fly. A female came in when the door was propped open for the men fitting the wired glass. She laid her eggs in the crease of the rubber seal, and most of them went out on a rag the first time somebody wiped the sill. The ones farther back in the crease hatched.

There are still larvae there. In the green at the foot of the ladder there are others, though you have to get your face low enough to see them against the bulb. They are narrow and whitish, with dark heads. They feed at the edge of the green, where there is a clear, soft film that lifts a little when water runs over it. One of them has got under a shaving of sealant. The shaving rocks for a long time before it goes anywhere.

The fly spent its first morning standing beside the empty skin it came out of. Its wings were pale then and stuck out at different angles. By the time somebody opened the door, they had darkened and folded. It flew toward the opening, met the colder air, and landed on the inside of the door. When the man pulled the door shut, it rode back in with him. He had come to look at a stain on the ceiling. He stood with his head tipped back until a drop went down the neck of his shirt, then put his hood up and went on looking.

During the pump change, the fly lifts one foot and puts it down again. Farther along the rung a bead has formed on a rough place in the metal. It grows heavier through both beats, stretches, and falls. The fly walks to where it was and lowers its head to the wet spot. Its mouth moves there for some time. Below it, the middle channel fills, and the water takes the sealant shaving off the green. The larva underneath stays where it is, bent around a grain of sand.
