Under the dished inspection cover lives an animal with a soft back. It has been under the cover long enough that the iron has left an orange stain on it. If the cover were lifted, the animal would not come up with it. It would stay on the bricks, flat and a little wider than a hand, with the stain showing the shape of a thing it no longer had.

It can move the cover, though not far at once. The edge nearest the north wall rests on a little pile of brick crumbs. During the lull the animal sorts these with the tips of two clear feet. It takes a crumb from the bottom and puts it on top, takes one from the top and works it in underneath. There is usually a prawn on the cover while this is going on. It takes a surprisingly large adjustment to make the prawn step off.

At the first knock the feet disappear. At the second, water gets under the raised edge and lifts the iron a little. A pleated fringe comes out all the way around it. The fringe is white, except at the tips, where the food it has caught gives it the color of dirty wool. Most of what catches there is grit. In the lull you can see the animal working it loose, passing it along the fringe until it falls. The brick crumbs it keeps are no bigger than the rest of the grit. It handles each one for a long time before deciding.

The eel eats the young ones. They come out in the warm water near the ceiling, no bigger than the fish in the gauge, and have to sink through the cold water to find a roof. Some get as far as the loose conduit. You can see them there if it is light enough: little white skirts drawn up tight against the metal. When the conduit clatters, one or two let go. The eel can catch them without coming all the way out of the intake.

The one under the cover has got as far as the turn in the old path. There the bricks are worn into a shallow cup, and the cover fits down into it so well that the animal has had to build its pile quite high. In the lull a fish sometimes goes in at the raised edge. It comes out at once, backward. Even in the green light you can see the little marks the fringe has left in the scales along its sides.

There is a young one under the bolt head nearest the doorway. It doesn't have much iron over it, and each time the flow starts, the bolt head rolls. Afterward the young one goes round it on its clear feet, feeling for the place that will be underneath next time. The whole distance it has traveled since it came down from the conduit is there on the bank: a scatter of tiny props, most of them overturned, leading from one brick joint to the next.
