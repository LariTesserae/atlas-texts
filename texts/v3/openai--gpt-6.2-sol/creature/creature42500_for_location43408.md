On the far wall, in one of the pits just large enough to see, there is a mouth. It is the color of the limestone around it. When it closes, it leaves a short line with a grain of sand at either end. The grains do not move.

Most of the animal lies inside the rock. Its body is soft and wider than the opening, though it came through that opening when it was young. It takes in water through one lip and lets it out through the other. A particle too small to see may go in and not come out. At this rate there is enough to eat, though not enough to grow much. The mouth has been the same size for several years.

There are others along the seam. They did not settle at regular intervals. Two are so close that the water leaving one passes the mouth of the next; then there is a length of bare stone. Below the sand the seam goes on, with old chambers in it in which nothing lives. Once a chamber is plugged, there is no way for the animal to reach another. In time even the little hollow it occupied fills, first at the mouth and then farther back, with grains finer than those on the floor.

The machinery makes no difference to the feeding. The mouths opened and closed before the note began, and on the occasions when it stops they go on opening and closing. The change then is in what can be heard: now and again a grain drops from the underside of a shelf and strikes the shelf below. There is no answering sound from the far wall.

Once, in water that moved fast enough to lift the ends of the algae, several of the mouths let go of young. They went out over the sill with the water. One came down on the upper surface of the flint, where for a day it moved about on a covering of fine hairs. There was room on the flint, but nowhere to withdraw. Before the water stilled it came off again. Another lodged under the finger-wide overhang, in a pit that could not be seen from the middle of the hollow. It lost its hairs there. For a while its whole body would have fitted between the two grains at the mouth across the sand.
