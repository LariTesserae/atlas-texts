I would start under one of the lamps. From above, the light looks as if it reaches all the way to the floor. From below, there is a black crescent where the rim of the reflector cuts it off. A fish can be quite still there and watch the little animals go by.

I could be that fish for a while. But I think I would go up to the old shore, where a set of steps comes down into the water. The bottom step is new concrete. The upper ones are stone and worn a little hollow. On a falling basin the hollows stay full after the rest of the step has dried.

Beside the steps there is a steel pipe with a cap on it. The cap has been painted blue, including the head of the bolt that has to come out before you can lift it. Inside the pipe is a float, and I think that is what I would be.

Water gets into the pipe through a hole near the bottom. A wave can slap against the steps hard enough to wet the top one, and inside I hardly move. But if the waves keep reaching a little higher, and the last wet step doesn’t dry between them, I rise. A wire goes up from me over a brass wheel. In the box beside the pipe a pen moves across a roll of paper. In the morning someone opens the box and puts a thumbnail against the place where the water began to come back.

Once a shrimp gets in. I know it first by the little knocks it makes against my underside. There is nothing to eat on me, but on the wall of the pipe, just below where I have spent most of the summer, there is a soft band of growth. The shrimp works its way around that band. At the seam it turns and works its way back.

Toward evening a gate shuts far down the basin. The pipe rings faintly. For a long time afterward the pen goes on drawing the same straight line. Then the water there begins to come here. I rise past the band on the wall, and the shrimp, which has been holding to it all day, lets go. For an inch or two we go up together. Then it finds the wall again.
