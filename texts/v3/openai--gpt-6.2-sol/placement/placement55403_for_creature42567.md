At first I think I’d be out past the last trees, wherever the motor runs. I’d know why it sometimes picks up that second note, and there’d be a warm casing I could put my hand against. But when I try to get there, I keep stopping at a place on the bank where a root enters the clay. There’s a hole underneath it. If you put your ear to the hole, the motor is harder to hear, not easier.

I’d be the cricket inside.

My hole goes in level for the length of my body and then turns down. At the turn there’s a shred of leaf I’ve been eating from the narrow end. It’s too wide to take around the bend. Every night I come up and find it wedged in the same place, and every night I eat a little more of what’s in my way.

I come to the mouth of the hole after the bank has given up its heat. There’s a patch just outside where I can stand with my forelegs apart and hear. The ants go by without making a sound I can use. The mongoose is different. Even when she’s not touching the bank, I can hear the small dry slips of earth her feet send down it. I go in when those begin. Once, just after I backed around the turn, a claw came in far enough to move the leaf. For the rest of that night I sang from farther inside. The clay took the edge off the sound. Another cricket answered anyway.

When the flood comes, I won’t be among the first to know. The root above me will be wet while the dust at the turn is still loose. Then the leaf will darken from its wide end, and by the time I’ve gone up to see why, there’ll be water coming in over my feet.

There’s a seam in the bank that goes nearly straight up. I’ve tried it before and come back because there’s nothing to eat on it. This time I’ll climb past the place where I usually turn. Near the top the seam runs under a mat of grass roots, and from there I’ll get onto a stem. It will bend under me, slowly enough that I can keep walking. I don’t know whether I’ll reach another stem.

Until then there’s the turn in the hole, and the leaf getting short enough to move. There’s one cricket across the channel and another uphill, close enough that I sometimes stop in the middle of a stroke to hear him finish. On those nights I wait a little longer than I need to. Then I lift my wings again.
