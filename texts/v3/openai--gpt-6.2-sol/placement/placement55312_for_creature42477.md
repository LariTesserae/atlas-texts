I think I’d be the builder who carried the piece away. Not the one who knew how to make the floor open, or how to persuade the weight of a tram depot to go sideways. I’d be the one they sent down with a scraper when the slurry set.

At home I kept my lunch tin on the draining board, upside down on a folded cloth. By morning there was a clear welt in the cloth. I thought I hadn’t rinsed the soap out, so I rinsed it again. The welt drew itself up while I was wringing the other end. It took a surprisingly long time. I had time to turn off the tap and dry one hand before it was done.

For three nights I left the cloth where it was and wet the corner when it began to dry. I didn’t know what it ate. I put down a crumb and found it untouched, then tried a little flour mixed with water. In the morning there were two clean spots in the paste. I thought that meant I could keep it.

By the time I brought it back, the room had been signed off. There was a notice on the door telling me who to telephone if I heard water running. I put the tin on the bench and worked the lid loose with my thumbnail. There was nothing in it. I tipped it toward the floor light and saw the piece flattened against the underside of the lid, on the side I’d just put my thumb on. I laid the lid down by the ferns. It was the only thing in the room that could rust.

I went back for it the next evening. There were grains of black grit on the lid and a mark where the metal had already started to go. I washed it at home. After that I still wet the corner of the cloth for a while. It was easy to do with the washing-up and took a week or two to stop.

I’d go down on my way home sometimes, even after there was no longer any reason to have a key. From the bench I could see the big one at Lime Street. Once, when there were two of them, I lowered my tin to the floor. It was warm from being under my arm. I wanted to see if the smaller one would come to it.

The seam opened. The tin settled with a little tick as the water went out from under it. Neither animal stopped eating. I picked the tin up before the water came back, and for the rest of the time I sat there I held it on my knees.
