I think I’d be just inside the doorway, where the worn bricks are still almost level with the bank. Not under the bolt head. In the joint it keeps rolling across.

I’d be a mussel there, one of the small ones with a pale edge to its shell. I’d have come loose from somewhere higher up and lodged so far down in the joint that for my first season I’d open onto the side of a brick. There would be enough water coming through to live on. I’d grow out crooked.

The north pipe wouldn’t sound like a pipe to me. The first knock would come through the brick I was fastened to. The second would come through everything. There’d be a moment after it when I could feel the pull on each of my threads separately.

The bolt head would hit me often enough to wear a dull spot on my shell. Then one day it would stop there, tipped a little, with its lower edge in the joint. The young one would go round it as usual. One of its feet would pass over the place where my shell opens, and I’d shut. It would go on feeling along the brick. In the lull it would put a crumb in the gap between us. I’d push that out the next time I opened. It would put another in, farther back.

For a while I’d know it mostly by what it left where I needed the water to come in. Then, on a day when the pipe knocked and the bolt didn’t strike me, I’d feel its fringe come out. The fringe would touch my shell and draw away and touch it again. Neither of us would move far enough to stop it happening.

I would never see the pale rectangle across the room. A little of the green from the shaft would reach me when the water cleared. Less of it each year, though not because the shaft was getting darker. The bolt would still rock on the second knock. By then my shell would have grown up around enough of its rim that it could rock without going anywhere. In the lull, the animal would still put out a foot and check.
