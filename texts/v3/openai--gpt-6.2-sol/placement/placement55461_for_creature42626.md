I would be a little higher than the animal, in a blind branch of the old pipe. The branch turns up behind the electrical conduits and ends in a screwed cap. When the room flooded, it caught a thimbleful of air. I think that is where I would begin.

There would be no green in there. When a pump came on, the water would press me farther into the cap. When it stopped, I would push back. A grain of sand might make that trip with me for several weeks, going up one side of the pipe and coming down the other, until it found a thread in the iron deep enough to stay in. After that I would have a way of telling if the water had risen higher than usual.

I would know the animal by the things it sent up. Most of the threads would pass the mouth of the branch on their way to the throat. One would come in often enough to make me expect it: a short thread from high on the wall, with a crimp where it had spent the day folded. It would straighten against the water under me. Once it brought a midge wing. The wing lay flat on the surface, and when the current took the thread, the wing stayed.

I don't think I would remain the air. Air goes into water a little at a time, even there, and I would have less room at the end of each shift. The wing would rise with the water until one of its corners touched the iron. Then it would tip, and I would find myself at that corner, where the water went up the wall a little higher than it did anywhere else.

For some time I would be that small difference.

Eventually the wing would soften. The next time the short thread came in, it would brush the iron and take the wing away. It might carry it back as far as the wall before the current turned. I like to think the shrimp would get it. A wing is not much to eat, but the shrimp has learned to hold a thing in its narrow claw and go over it with the other one. I would be wherever it found something worth going over twice.
