I think I would be under the public steps, though there is no under to them in the usual sense. They are solid down to the bottom of the ridge. But late in the afternoon, when a new tread comes in, there is a little while in which its front edge has joined the stair and its back edge has not. There is room then for something the thickness of a nail.

At first I thought I would be the thing that lived in that space. I would travel up and down the flight without having to climb, feeding on whatever got caught between the old stone and the new. But the stone fits too well. I would have to get out every evening, and I would need somewhere to go.

There is a recess just inside the narrow house. That would be my place. From the doorway it looks as empty as the others. If you lay on the floor and looked across it at dusk, you might see a little rise at the back, where I have brought in the things I cannot eat. Most of them are the clear skins of the creatures that live in the dust. I bring them in with the creatures still in them and take a surprisingly long time to learn that a skin with nothing in it is not worth going back for.

I would have a hard back, I think, and a soft rim all the way around me. The rim would let me hold on to a nearly vertical wall and tell me, well before the dust came, which way the wall was going to face. This would be useful but not infallible. Some mornings I would wake to find the house open on the other side of me and go out the wrong way. I would get as far as the corner before the light on the pavement told me I was on a ridge I didn’t know. The difficulty then would be getting back without crossing open ground. I am about the size of the thing the gray animal caught in the square. I can go a long way around a very small obstacle.

The adult would know I was there. Once, when the young were small enough to lie entirely behind its knees, it put a finger in my recess to feel what had accumulated there. I drew my rim in. It drew its finger out. Since then it has left the recess alone, except on mornings when I am late getting back and it puts its hand down for me to go under. I don’t know whether this is for my benefit. Its fingers reach almost exactly from one side of the doorway to the other, and make a good place to wait while the young are coming past.

At dusk I go as far as the back sill. I can tell when the far square is there because I can feel a step being tested that has no weight of stone beneath it. This comes through my belly as a faint lifting. The first time the adult knocks, I pull away. It is a hard blow at that distance. Then I put myself down flat again and feel what the adult, with only its knuckle on the sill, may not: a little rubbing, a pause, and another rubbing. Something over there is shifting its weight to free a hand.

The young one lifts its foot at the answer. I am just beside the foot, close enough to get under it before it comes down, which I have been known to do when startled. This time I stay where I am. The light is already gone, but for a while the far animal is still on the steps. I can feel it resume its descent. It takes two more steps, and on the third there is that little catch I know from the public stair: stone coming in at the front before it has joined at the back.

In the morning I go out to my own stairs. The gap won’t open until late, so I spend much of the day going around the edge of a single tread, putting my rim down here and there. Once in a while something puts its weight down on the other side. I have been taking it for the stair settling. I begin to wait a little before moving on.
