I would be under the new floor, but not so far in as the one making its way across it. I would be near the seam, where a movement can still go either way: out into the new stone or back through the old.

At first I thought I would be a loose plate there, warm on one side, with enough room to rock. But a plate is a place one of these beings can leave. I think I would be something it could leave without leaving me anywhere else.

There would be two ways for the weight to get around a deep notch. One would be short and nearly level. The other would go down through a stack of thin shelves and come up on a broad stone that took a long time to cool. Most of the weight would go the short way. A little would go down, and by the time it came up the creature would already have passed. The broad stone would lift against nothing.

For a long time that would be all I did. I would lift too late.

Then the short way would break. Not in one of the great disturbances that can be seen from the rim. A corner would come off a stone in the dark, and the next time the weight reached the notch it would have to go down through the shelves. It would come up slowly, with several stops that had nothing to do with the creature’s accustomed pace. At the top, the broad stone would find something bearing on it. It would lift that too.

I don’t think I would know, on that first occasion, that anything had happened to me. It would take another passage, and another. I would know it first as a difference in how long I stayed warm.

Afterward the creature might make a better way across. I would feel it working at a seam I couldn’t reach. There would be whole evenings when it sent no weight down to me at all. But some small part of each round would still come through, because in lifting the stone above me I had shifted the foot of one of the shelves. Even after the better crossing was ready, the shelf would tip a little. I could keep going on that much.

I would have no uprights of my own. I would know about the young one at the cut only by a slight, recurring load from that direction. It would be easy to mistake for a joint that hadn’t finished settling. Then, one evening, it would fail to come. On the next round I would have a little more weight to work with, and the broad stone would rise higher than it ever had.

I would spend some of that excess trying the old direction. Not enough to move the upright, wherever it was. Enough to find that the way to it was still open.

Much later, when the creature had gone far into the new reach, a shelf near the seam would tip and catch the last of the red. If you stood there with your hand on the floor, waiting for the clack, you might feel me before you heard it. I wouldn’t be the shelf going down or the stone that took its weight. I’d be the small lift under your hand, where a load that could have gone straight on had gone the long way round.
