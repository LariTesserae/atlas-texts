I think I would be down in the basin, where the hollows have been set out. At first I would take myself for one of the young. There would be a white wall close to my face, and above it a strip of sky that went dark whenever an older animal bent its head. I would open my mouth when it opened its mouth. A grain would fall, and I would feel its warmth through the floor.

But the young animal would be sitting on me. When it began to unfold, I would feel each foot come unstuck from my back. The last two would stay a long time. By the time they lifted, the bowl would have cooled except for two places about as wide as the ends of a match.

I would come out then. I’d be a flat animal with a rim of short legs, most of them underneath where they couldn’t be seen. On bare stone I could only go a little way before the front of me caught. Under a hollow I could travel quite well. I would fit my back to its bottom and walk it a few inches at a time. By morning a bowl that had been in the path of the big animals would be a little to one side of it. Or a bowl that stood in shade all morning would stand where it got the first heat. I don’t think I would know about the young at first. I would know that a bowl with something in it was easier to carry: the weight kept my back from slipping.

Once, while I was moving an empty one, an old animal would come down with grit in its mouth. It would lower its head to the place I’d just left. I’d stop. From under the bowl I could see the dust-colored collar spread a little against the ground. It would wait there long enough for me to get all my legs out from under the rim and settle them again. Then it would let the grit go.

I would go back after it left. There would be six or seven grains, still warm, in a place where there was nothing to keep the wind from taking them. I could get a leg around the biggest one, but when I tried to carry it the leg would keep trying to walk. In the end I’d bring the bowl back. I’d have to go a good way past the grains and come at them from the other side to get the bottom down without scraping them away.

That’s where I’d be when the light reached the basin: under a bowl with nothing in it that I could eat, getting the last of my legs out of the way. Uphill, the old animal would already be coming down with another hollow on its head. From where I lay I could see a patch of sky through its legs. The patch would narrow, and widen, and narrow. I’d have time to move the bowl again before it got here. I’d leave it where it was.
