I would be in the kitchen, in the grout between two tiles. At first I think I would be the joint itself. There is something to be said for being the place a thread finds when it needs to put its weight down. But the joint is there all day, and I don’t think I would be.

I would be a flake of paint from the inside of the letterbox, lying blue side down.

When the water turned I would lift with the other flakes. I’d get as far as the underside of the table, where the mouth would take the soft brown stuff off my back and let me go. I would come down a tile farther from the door. This would be a considerable journey for me. The next evening I might not lift at all.

If someone left a lamp on the draining board, there would come a time when I could see myself still falling. The flake in the light would turn over once, showing a blue you could no longer find anywhere on the door. I would be down in the joint by then, with the animal’s thread laid across me. It would be the lightest thing that had ever held me in place.

After a few evenings, I think I would stop watching the flake. I would watch the thread. Before it found the joint, its end would pass over the glazed tile, where there was nothing to catch on. It would go a little too far, draw back, and try again. From where I lay I could see the small distance it had still to come. There would be nothing I could do to shorten it. Then it would touch, and I would feel the animal’s weight, which was less than the weight of the grit that had settled on me while I waited.
