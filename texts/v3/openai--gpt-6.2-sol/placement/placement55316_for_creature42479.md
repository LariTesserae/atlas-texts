I would be the bit of white glaze at the wrong end of the case.

On the pot I was part of a line that went all the way around. A thumb could follow it while the other hand poured. I don't know what the pot held. I remember the thumb, if the little place where the glaze is worn thinner can be called remembering.

The larva puts that place outward. It fits my rough side against a red grain and fixes me there. All evening, while it tries to turn itself over, I see the ceiling, the sill, the ceiling. I am one of the reasons it is having trouble. When the other larva climbs onto us, I go around so fast that the line of sky seems to run right down into the water.

Under the sill, after the fly is gone, the case comes apart from the mouth. The neatly fitted grains go first. I hang on by a thread of silk that has worked into a crack in my rough side. In the faster water I knock against the brick. Between knocks I can feel the thread draw me back. This goes on long after there is any animal at the other end of it.

When it breaks I don't go out. I go sideways into one of the old chisel nicks. The current pushes another grain in after me, and that is enough to hold me. For a while my white face is level with the floor. Then the green coat grows over it. A larva clears the brick on either side and can't get at the middle.

I would have thought being uncovered again would mean the water had risen. Instead the dam gives way. There is the frog noise, and then, right over me, a tiny dry sound. A foot has come down where there was always water before. It tries the green, slips, and finds the worn place in the glaze. For the length of that step I am not part of a pot or part of a case. I am a place to put a foot.
