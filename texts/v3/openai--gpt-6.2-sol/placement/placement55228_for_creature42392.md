I would come in with the rain, I think, and be wrong about where I fell. I’d remember a white animal below me and expect to find myself in its coat. Instead there would be grass overhead and, through the spaces between the blades, the animal walking away upside down.

For a while I’d think I was one of the silver backs. There is a certain pleasure in being carried downstream with all the others, turning when they turn. But they catch on the grass and I go on. In a pool no wider than a hand I stop, though the water keeps going.

It takes the first ice to show me what I am. As the pool closes over, everything in it grows a pale edge, except for one small place beside a stem. There the water stays black. I am the little bit of warmth going out through the living blade.

That would be a good place to spend a winter if I could stay there. But what I am keeps leaving. I go up the blade and am lost to the air; more of me comes from below. On the coldest nights there is hardly enough to tell the blade from a dead one. Then the groves begin knocking in the west, and after a long while water moves among the roots. The dark space opens a little. Nothing comes out of it.

The animal passes me over and over. It has business at the ceiling. Sometimes I feel the water it displaces as it goes down for a mite; sometimes one of the young drifts against the stem, belly up, and lies there until it is pulled away. I would like to be warm enough for it to notice. I am warm enough that it doesn’t stick.

Late in the winter the bubble comes toward the stem. I feel the animal’s mouth close on the blade and expect it to bite, but it is only taking hold. The young slip past in the thin water. Then there is a tug that bends the blade almost to the bottom. When it straightens, the animal is on the other side and one of its feet is not.

All night I go out through the blade beside that foot. By morning there is a narrow seam of water between the foot and the ice. It would be easy to mistake that for something I had done for it.

In the thaw I lose track of myself. There is warmth everywhere, even in the rain. The young run over the top of the pool and the blade they passed in winter turns out to be a very short blade, with a yellow end. Below it the adult takes a root in its mouth. The root is longer than the animal. As it eats, the green tip travels slowly over the bottom, lifting a little each time the animal swallows. For a moment it rests against the blade. Then it moves on.
