I would be just below the ledge, where the passage is wide enough for water to go around me in both directions. At first I would take that little stir for something I was doing. All afternoon I would feel it quicken on one side and slacken on the other. If I moved at all, it would be less than the width of the mark I was making in the stone.

I think I would be one of the loose grains, though not yet loose enough to go anywhere. One end of me would still be caught in a socket. I would have a smooth face from before the break, and a rough face that had never been in water until this year. The rough face would be the one that held.

The young crustacean would pass me after dark. It would catch a foot in the space between me and the wall and pull. For a moment it would be able to hold itself there. Then I would turn in the socket, and the foot would come free. The animal would go on down. I would stay where I was, with the smooth face out.

Much later, when the pool began to empty, the water would take me in the same direction. I’d go a surprisingly short way. Under the slab there would be a place where the current spread enough to set me down. I’d land on the film rather than on stone. For a while it would hold me at an angle, like a crumb on something soft.

When the animal came to that patch, it would eat right up to me. It might push me aside with a forefoot and eat what I had covered. After that I would lie flat. It would be the first time since the break that water couldn’t get under me.
