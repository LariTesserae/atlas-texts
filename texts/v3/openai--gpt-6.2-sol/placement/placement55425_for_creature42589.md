I would be a wall-tender, old enough to be sent back for things. Someone has left a child asleep at one of the straight walls while they gather rushes. Someone else has put a good white stone in a loop and cannot remember which loop. I am sent for the child. I bring the stone as well, though it turns out to be a different white stone, and I have to take it back.

There is a loop I visit even when no one has asked me to. As a child I could get my whole body inside it with only my heels in the weather. Now I have to lie on my side to put my wrist down, and my knees stick out. The wall rocks when I lean against it. I have known this for some time. It is one of the things I come to fix.

The stone that rocks is broad and easy to lift. Under it are two prints in the dust. One is mine, from the last time I set it down. The other is so small I take it for the print of a rush until I see where the finger bent. There is a smear beside it, then a second print farther in. A child has been here trying to do what I do. I put the stone back badly, with the edge a little high, so there will be room to get a finger under it.

By then the dry margin has gone on. I can see it a long way off by the sudden darkening of the water on either side. I could catch it if I went straight across, but the small ones are playing at the next loop and I stop there. They have their feet out in the rain. One has tucked a foot beneath the other and is looking around to see whether this counts. I lower myself beside them and put my feet out too.

The rain is warmer than I expect. I had forgotten that part. I had remembered the effort of keeping still and the relief of pulling my feet in. For a while there is no effort. Then a ripple comes the length of the loop and lifts the water between my toes, and I pull both feet in at once.

They kick so hard a stone falls out. We put it back together. There are more hands than the stone needs, and for a little while I leave mine underneath.
