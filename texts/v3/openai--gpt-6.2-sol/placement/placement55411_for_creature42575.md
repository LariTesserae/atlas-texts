I would begin as one of the old animals at the doorway, the one on the flats. From there I could put the end of my body into the socket without coming out of the groove. I would know the other animal by a small pressure that came and went. For a long time I might take it for something the water did.

But there is a joint under the great stair where I think I would end up. Not on the tread with the animal that has the nick. Under it, where two blocks meet a little badly. A grain can go into the joint on the ebb and come out on the return. A grain a little larger goes in and stays.

I would be one of the young there. There would be room for me to go down folded, and farther down there would be room to straighten. I would grow past the size of the opening without ever having been through it in the other direction. Now and then a fish would pass over the joint. I would not see its shape, only a brief interruption in the light, and then another, as it backed up to look.

The block above me would settle by degrees. For a while there would be less room each time it moved. Then one night the far end would come to rest on something, and the end over me would lift. A crab would put a leg in, find nothing it could get hold of, and go away. By morning a fine white stream would be coming through a place that had never admitted light.

I think I would go out a little way and feed. Not far enough to see the piers or find out what was on the other side of the valley. I would get my forward feet onto the tread and leave the rest of myself in the joint. The animal above me would lift its edge. Some of what passed under it would come to me.

Then the current would turn. For the first time I would feel it on both sides of my body, and I would have to find out, with all those feet, which part of me was still holding on.
