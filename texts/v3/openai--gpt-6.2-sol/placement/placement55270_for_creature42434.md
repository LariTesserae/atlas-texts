I’d be under the street, a little downhill from the room with the fallen door. You could lift the paving directly over me and still not see me. You’d have to reach into the drain and feel along its roof for a piece of tile that gives a little under your finger.

I wasn’t made for the drain. I have a rolled rim and, just below it, a stripe of green glaze. The stripe disappears into the mortar at either end. Whoever wedged me up there had to try me both ways; there’s a scrape on my glazed side where I went in wrong.

The mortar on one side is gone now. When the tide comes up, I lift. When it goes out, I come down. There’s a grain of quartz beneath my loose corner that keeps me from coming all the way down. I’ve been coming down on that grain for longer than I was anything with a green stripe.

Some of the water I let through goes up a cord hole into the room. Not much: enough to turn the free end of a broken thread. The rest runs on beneath the wall. On the falling tide a little of it comes back with the room’s leavings, and a white fringe grows on the underside of my rim. It gets long enough to catch under me. I come down on it and cut it off. The next tide carries it up the hole.

Once, after a knock, the grain of quartz rolled out.

For a while I lay flat. Water still went down the drain, but none went up the hole. The animal in the room didn’t die. It took in its threads, and silt settled on the clean band above the old sill. From below I had no way of knowing this. What I had was a small, continuous pressure on the glazed side and nothing on the other.

It took a winter tide to lift me again. Something slipped beneath my corner before I came down: not the quartz, but a curl of fired clay, thin at one end and thick at the other. I came down crooked. Water went up the hole in a short rush, carrying the dirt that had collected in it. The first thing the animal caught, when it had a thread across that little current again, was a piece of my white fringe.

The curl under me has a stripe of green on it. I never touched the part of the drain it came from. There must be another piece of the rim farther up, doing something else by now. Mine is to rise just enough for the curl to tremble, and come down before it gets away.
