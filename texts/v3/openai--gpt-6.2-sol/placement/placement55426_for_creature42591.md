I think I’d come in on somebody’s boot. Not the boot that finds the soft place in the roof, but the one that goes ankle-deep at the creek edge and comes out cleaner than it went in.

I’d be a bit of red brick, small enough to have lodged in the tread. Once in the pocket I’d look like something the bank had let go. It would take a thumb rubbed over me to bring out the red, and there isn’t much call for a thumb down there.

For a while I’d lie beside the pebble. I wouldn’t get the drops it gets. I’d be on the low side, where the water reaches it first, and on an ebb I’d be one of the last things to dry. A snail would cross me and leave a track that stopped at my edge. There wouldn’t be anything to eat on me yet.

I like that the shrimp would be gone before I arrived. I’d have no occasion to see it carrying its eggs. The shell would already be lying white side down, and I’d take it for another pebble. Once, when the water was coming in fast enough to move us both, I might knock against it and hear, through the contact, how thin it was.

By then I think I’d have stopped being a bit of brick. Not altogether: somebody breaking open the bank a hundred years later could still pick me out and say brick. But a root would have got into the little hole where a grain had come away in the firing. That would be where I’d find myself, a little way in from the mouth, on the side of the root that stays wet. When the mill stopped for the evening, I’d still be warm from a narrow patch of sun that had reached me at low water. The creek would take the warmth before it took the light.
