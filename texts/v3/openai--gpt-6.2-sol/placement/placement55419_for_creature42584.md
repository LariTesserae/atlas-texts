I think I would be the third tooth, the one with nothing tucked behind it.

From the near wall I would look like the natural place to put an egg. My root makes a deeper pocket than either of the others. But when the water goes, it goes from my side first. The last wetness runs up my face and hangs at the point, out of reach of anything down in the pocket. The parent tried me early in the day. It took the egg out again before the crossing broke. There is a little polish at my root where it had to get a hook in beside the egg without piercing it.

I would have been a good tooth in the living jaw. I am the one that meets the opposing edge first. Now, when a black head takes the jaw up, I am the first thing it feels. The head turns us over and tries the other end. There is no meat on either end. Once, on being put down, I struck a splinter and made a tick loud enough to stop the feet immediately above me. They started again before the jaw had finished rocking.

I would know the parent mostly by the pull it puts on the jaw. Coming home in a whole skin, it can drag us a little sideways before it gets the borrowed feet loose. Coming home bare, it does not move us at all. There is only the small pressure in the notch. After the egg was lost, I would feel it there for a long while, though nothing would be pulling the other way.

At the end of the day, when the little one gets crosswise in the toe, the heel of the sleeve comes to rest against me. Each time the toe swells, the heel lifts. Each time it goes slack, it settles a little farther down my point. The parent holds its end still. I hold mine in the only way I have, which is by not being smooth.

Then the little one finds the next toe. The heel lifts once more and comes free. When the water parts, a scrap of the sleeve stays on my point. It is so thin that the last light goes through it. At the next joining it will soften and slide down to my root, and the parent, feeling there on its way past, may find it worth taking.
