I’d start on the east stair, looking for a place wide enough to set down what I’d brought. I think it would be a basket of pots. I’d have packed them with straw at the market, where the ground was level, and by the time I reached the first occupied landing I’d have a fair idea which two had been knocking together all the way up.

I’d be a pot seller, then, though not a particularly good one. I’d bring too many of whatever had sold the week before. I’d have to learn to sit through a whole conversation about a lid without trying to sell the pot that went with it. The first place I’d sleep would be on a landing where someone had bought a bowl and told me I could stay till morning. I’d put the basket on the uphill side of my mat. In the night I’d turn over and put my arm into it, and the straw would still be warm.

I don’t think I’d set out to live there. There would just come a week when it made more sense to leave the basket and go down to market without it. Then I’d start taking broken things down as well as bringing new things up. Most couldn’t be mended, but a woman at the market could put a new handle on a good lid. She’d ask me to bring the pot next time, and I’d have to go up and ask for it while somebody was cooking beans in it.

My place would end up a little below the houses, around the turn where you can hear people on the stair before you can see their feet. There’s a jar deep enough to sit in with my legs out. It stays dry except in a driving rain, when I have to move everything, usually in the middle of the night. I’d keep the lids there. People could come and try them without waking me, though they’d wake me anyway, setting one after another down on the clay.

One evening the sow would come up while I was sorting the basket. I’d draw my feet in, but she’d stop and put her nose deep into the packing straw. I’d hold the rim so she couldn’t tip the whole lot down the steps. For a while neither of us would move. Then she’d find the apple I’d forgotten I’d put there. She’d eat it right over the basket, getting juice and bits of apple onto three clean bowls, and I’d sit there holding the rim until she finished.
