I would come up from the other side of the pass, where the path is wide enough that you can walk with your hands in your pockets. I would have a sack with bread in it, and for the last hundred steps I would think mostly about where to put the sack while I ate.

There is no good place. On the ledge it would lean against my leg, pushing a little whenever the wind got behind it. In the hollow, if I put it down, the floor would take its shape.

I think I would be the person who put the white stone there. Not the person who knew what the space beneath it was for. Someone else would have held a hand out, palm down, to show me how high to make it. I would have brought two stones that were too short before I brought the right one. That much I would remember, even after I had forgotten what went underneath.

I would lie on my side at the mouth, with one hip getting warm. From there I could see the little entrance the animals keep clear. I would put a finger into it as far as the first joint and stop. Farther in, the skins would rise and lie down again, though nothing had touched them that I could see.

On the way out I would find a flake on the ledge. There would be enough light left to see a black mark near one end. I would pick it up and turn it over, and it would bend across the crease in my palm. I would set it down just inside the hollow, on what looked like bare stone. The stone would move off with it.

That is where I would expect to leave myself: going down the pass with my bread uneaten and one side of my coat warmer than the other. But under the white stone there is a skin with a gray grain in it. I keep coming back to that. The grain is too big to pass through the opening left by the animal, and the skin is too light to stir it. Each time the roof taps, the skin lifts a little all around the grain.

I could be that grain. I would know the hollow by the part of it that moved and the part that did not. Once, when the air went out, a young animal would catch the edge of the skin instead of the back it was reaching for. For a while we would both hang there. Then something warm would pass beneath us, and the animal would let go.
