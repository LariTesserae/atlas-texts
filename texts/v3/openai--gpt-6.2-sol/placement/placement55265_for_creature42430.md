I would be the lower of two stones an animal tries beneath the tuft.

It would take me in its mouth with the white side of its chin against the ground. There would be a seed in the soft corner of its mouth, but I would know it only as a small pressure that gave way when the animal shifted its grip. Set down between the posts, I would have the unaccustomed experience of something passing repeatedly over me without touching. Then the animal would take me out again.

It would not put me back where it found me. That is how I would get to know the place.

For a long time I would lie just outside the reach of the descending dust. In the morning there would be two narrow shadows. One would pass over me and the other would not. In the afternoon, when a column came down, I could feel the grains leaving the ground around me, but not the turning of the tuft. I would know when an animal had got hold of it by the way its hind feet pressed down. There was no need, from where I lay, to distinguish the animal from what it came for until it pulled.

One evening a young one would come after the adult had gone. It would nose me over, then nose me back, and finally put a forefoot on me. A few clear hairs would open. I would move a little without rolling. The animal would take its foot away and examine the ground beside me, where it apparently expected to find the thing that had moved.

By then one of the posts would be leaning. Its shadow would reach me earlier each morning. When the dust came down it no longer stopped at the same height on both sides, and one day a loose end would brush me while I lay there, well outside the place the animals searched. It would draw tight and lift one edge. I would settle. It would try again after dark, when the other stones had gone cold.

I don’t think I would go far. By morning I might be as much as a hand’s breadth nearer the leaning post. The animal would come at dusk and choose me again, not recognizing me, or recognizing me and finding that beside the point. In the lowered column I would be high enough. It would pull what it could free and leave with its head held very still, the way they do when they have something in their mouths they must not swallow.

Some of the threads would stay. I would find that I could lift either edge a little, but not both together. Toward dawn I would work a grain out from under myself and let another in. There are places on that summit where a stone could go a hundred miles without coming to anything it had to go around. I would spend the first morning getting over a grain of feldspar, and the second finding that the way around it had been open all along.
