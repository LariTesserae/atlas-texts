I would be the stone that fell from the third wall.

Before I fell, there was a thin place in me through which the low note came almost sweetly. The slab above rested on two points, neither of them at the thin place. For a long time this was all I knew of the other stones: which parts of me they leaned on, and which they spared.

Falling took most of a night. I went over at once, but the current caught me broadside and held me just off the bottom. A small fish hung in the same water. I could see the underside of its jaw. We stayed like that long enough for a white worm, which had lived between me and the stone above, to put out its whole length and find nothing. Then the fish turned, and I went down hard enough to lose a corner.

I had thought that landing flat would be like being mortared in. Instead, there is room under me. At the low note the grit presses into my underside, grain by grain; at the high note most of it lets go. Close to my broken corner a little row of grains stays pressed up for longer. The first few are so close together that I feel them as one. In the years since I fell, the row has moved the width of a grain.

There is a lively traffic through the space at the corner. A frond came in blade first and went out stripped to its stem. A piece of shell lodged there for half a winter. I came to know the shell by the way it knocked three times when everything else had gone quiet. Then there was a sweep that took the shell and brought back the corner I had broken off. I knew it as soon as it touched me. It came in with the wrong face uppermost, and for several tides I waited for the water to turn it.

It is still there. The part that fits me is down in the grit. The part that shows has a hollow in it which I remember as a blemish: a pinhole in the rock that opened onto a bubble no bigger than a seed. Broken open, it makes a good cup. In the dry intervals, it holds one bright drop after the rest of the saddle has gone dull.

I don't think I would see the limpet from where I lie. But I would know if it came as far as the chip. There would be a little pull on me when its foot tested the loose stone, and then, if it put its weight down, the chip would tip. The water in the cup would run into the old break. I have been dry there, off and on, for years.
