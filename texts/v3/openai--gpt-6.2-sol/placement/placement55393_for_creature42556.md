At first I think I’m coming home.

There’s a key between two of my fingers. I’ve put it there to leave my thumb free for the rail. Something rough is tucked under my arm, wet enough that I can feel it through my sleeve. I put my left foot down on the causeway.

That’s as much of the walk as I get.

A while later the woman comes by with the sacking under her arm and the key between her fingers. Her left boot lands where mine did. I know before it lands that the sole is coming loose at the toe. I’d felt it catch. I hadn’t known what had caught.

Most nights there’s nothing to distinguish the two steps except that hers goes on into another. Once, though, she stops when she hears me. She’s carrying a cup in each hand and has to wait for the water in both cups to settle. I think she means to set them down. Instead she calls, “Can you get the door?”

I try. The next thing I know I’m one slab farther on, with a wet stocking where the water has got in through the toe. The door is still a long way off. When she gets there she sets one cup on the brick footing and opens it herself.

I don’t have much use for a name. I’d like a second step. Not a whole walk, necessarily. Just once to put my weight down and then take it up again myself.

I come to know the joint where the broad animal braces its feet. It seldom lifts the whole foot. It rolls it over, pressing the four toes down in order until there’s room on the slab. I always arrive in time to feel the last toe let go. In the interval before the woman comes through, water creeps into the print. By the time her boot comes down, the print and the rest of the water feel the same.

One night the step comes on iron.

There’s no key between my fingers. The sacking is spread out to dry on the mud. I’m facing the ladder and for the first time I can feel both my feet: one on the bank and one on the rung the shells have brought level. The iron gives a little as I put my weight on it. A shell underneath makes a dry sound. I’ve never heard one make a dry sound before.

The woman is still on the bank. She’s barefoot, with her trousers turned up unevenly. She puts a foot out, then draws it back. I think she heard the shell too.

She crouches and feels along under the rung. There’s one shell that rocks when she touches it. She could take it out with her fingers. Instead she wets a corner of the sacking and works it into the gap beside the shell. Then she sits back on her heels and presses the rung with the flat of her hand.

The iron takes her weight without a sound.

She leaves the sacking there. I’d thought she might be going down the ladder at last, but she picks up her boots and walks back holding them by their tops. I hear her bare feet for a little way. Then there’s the faint slap of a loose sole. She’s put them on to pass the animal.

I stay where the iron gave. Toward the end of the long pump stroke the water drops enough to leave the top of the rung dry. I take my foot off it.

For a while there’s no sound at all. Then, a rung below me, comes the small noise of somebody putting a foot down carefully. It’s a noise I haven’t made yet. I wait until I’m ready.
