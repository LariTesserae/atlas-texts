I would start in one of the cups, though I don’t think I would be what lived in it. I would be the narrow place at its rim where the water went back into the leaf. The growth in the cup would reach me a little at a time. I would wear it down a little at a time. Neither of us would get much farther than that.

There is a place under the broad descent where two leaves lie almost against one another. The cup hangs from the upper leaf. Below it, the lower leaf carries a bank along its midrib. I would know the bank first by the way the cup drew in at one end. Its surface would flatten again so promptly that there would be no seeing it happen, only seeing that the growth had failed, at that end, to make a good fit.

For a long time I might take that small failure for my work.

Then the lower leaf would put on a new layer. It would come a little closer to the cup until, on one stroke of the bank, it touched. Not enough to spill anything. A bright join would run between the two leaves and be gone before the bank swelled again. Some of the water that had always gone back into the upper leaf would go into the lower one instead.

I think that is where I would begin to move. What passed into the bank would be hardly enough to wet a cut face. But one chamber would have a little more to give than the chamber beside it was ready to take. The difference would go on down the bank. By the time it reached the roots it would be possible to follow it without following any of the water that had come from the cup.

I would not be good at going round. I would get as far as a fork and spend myself on both branches. Or I would arrive at a chamber just as it filled and there would be nothing left of me to go on. Back at the cup, the leaves would touch again. For a while there would be a great many of me, all short, most ending within the thickness of the mat.

One would come to the place where an old traveler backs away from a closed passage. It wouldn’t open the passage. There would simply be one chamber to the side that yielded on the stroke when it usually held. The traveler would enter it and come out on its own bank a little farther back than it expected. On the next round it might do the same thing. On the round after that it might not.

I would like to be there long enough to find out which.

Eventually the lower leaf would grow past the cup. The join would cease. Whatever was left of me would have to live on the small differences already going through the roots: a chamber slow to empty where its wall was being renewed, a little water coming back from a traveler that had turned. I would be easiest to find where a route went briefly alongside itself, close enough that something taking the longer way could feel, through the wall, the stroke it would have been on.

Above me the cup would still hang at the same height. The growth would at last fit the end it had never fitted. From below, if there were anyone below, there would be no mark on either leaf to show they had touched.
