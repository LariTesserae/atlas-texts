I think I’d be outside, a few courses down from the room, trying to get a long piece of grass around a corner.

I’d have brought it up crosswise. In the open air this would have been the sensible way to carry it. Now one end would catch against the tower every time I turned my head, and the other would dip out over the drop. I could let go and find a shorter piece. There would be plenty of shorter pieces down where I got this one. But I’d have come a long way with it by then.

From outside, the tower would have a reasonable sound. It would be something I could fly out of and back into. On the lee side there would be a place where I could hear the grass scrape the stone. I’d work there, on a ledge too narrow to turn around on without opening a wing.

I don’t think I’d nest in the tower. The feet I’m imagining would be good for holding a ledge and bad for sleeping on one. There would be a tree somewhere below, forked low enough that a cow could rub its shoulder against the nest. I’d have lost an egg that way once. I’d go on using the tree. The long grass would be for bending around the fork, where the short pieces kept slipping through.

What would bring me so high with it, then, would be water. After rain, a seam on the roof would hold enough to drink from if I went early. I’d put the grass down, drink, and pick it up again. On a dry morning I’d follow the seam farther in, to where the roof passage made its first turn. I could get my head around that corner. The grass couldn’t.

There’d be a second turn I never saw. Sometimes, as I stood with the grass knocking against the entrance, a small white chin would lift away from a drop on the underside of a brick. The drop would be near enough that I could have reached it if I’d known which way to put my beak. I’d wait a while. Then I’d take the grass back down to the tree, where there was no water either, but where it was possible to do something with the grass.
