I would look for myself on the underside of a delivery belt, on the empty run back to the press. At first I’d think I was the piece of stem that broke off when the factory lifted. It goes around with its bud shut, held by catches that have dried to the hardness of the belt. I’d have the length of the warm shaft in which to open, and then the long cool run in which to lose whatever I’d gained.

But the stem is gone by the time I find the place. The bud opened just far enough to catch on a guide. What’s left is a tuft of torn grain and, on the guide itself, a leaf no wider than the torn end of the stem.

The leaf grows sideways out of the guide. Most of the belt passes without touching it. Once in a circuit the torn grain comes round and lifts it, and a little oil gets under its base. With each lifting it grows farther into the path of the belt. By the time the tuft wears smooth, the leaf is long enough to be lifted by the joint between two slats.

I think I’d be there, though not quite in the leaf. If you stopped the belt and cut the leaf open, you’d find a flap over the mouth of a narrow pipe. Farther in, where you couldn’t get at it without splitting the guide, a second flap would still be coming down from the last time the first one went up. The oil between them would be warm. It takes almost a whole turn of the belt for it to cool.

For a long time this makes no difference to the stock. Then the press shifts on its bedding and the belt starts coming back a little slack. Its joints lift the leaf twice in quick succession, once going down into the slack and once coming out. The second flap hasn’t seated. Oil gets past it into a thin part of the guide that has never taken oil before.

By the next stoppage there is a swelling there. It takes up some of the slack. For three or four circuits the belt runs straight, and the leaf is lifted only once at each joint. Then the press settles a little farther.

That’s where I’d be: below the loaded belt, where nobody putting a hand out to feel what was being made would think to reach. I’d get the motion a little late. I’d keep some of it after the belt stopped. And on a night when the press came down hard enough to pinch the pipe shut, the leaf would still rise at every joint. It would be several turns before the far end of me cooled.
