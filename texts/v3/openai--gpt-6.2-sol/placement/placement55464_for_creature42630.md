I think I would be a ground bird, though at first I took myself for the person standing on the old wall. I liked being able to see the dust front and the green patches beyond it. But I keep coming back to the bird pulling at the hollow leaf. From the wall I know there is nothing in it. Down here it makes exactly the sound a leaf makes when there is something in it.

I would work the seam above the mouse’s court. There is a place where I can put one foot on a stem and the other on the roof and pull without going through. I know this because I went through once. For the rest of that afternoon a little of the roof stayed between my toes. By the next afternoon the hole was closed, and the patch was a better place to stand than the roof on either side.

I would know the mouse by what she left. The white curls would be gone before I got there, but a leaf she had eaten would still have its good color on the shaded side. I would try that side first. Once, tugging on the skin, I’d bring the whole stem down and find two seeds caught in the fork where it had rested. After that I’d pull on several leaves that had nothing to do with the mouse. It would take me a while to stop.

Toward evening I’d go out to the little patches beyond the roofs. I wouldn’t see them as the beginning of anything. There would be one where I could feed with both feet in shade, and another where I’d have to stand in the open and reach. At the second one I’d pick up a seed too broad to swallow. I’d set it down to turn it, and it would slip into the fold. I’d walk to the other end and look in from there. The light would be better, but that wouldn’t bring it any closer.

The next year, coming to that fold, I might land on a roof where I’d meant to land on rock. I’d take a couple of steps to see if it would hold. Underneath, a mouse would be trying to get a stalk around the turn. For a moment I’d hear it scrape, and I’d bend down to peck at the place the sound seemed to come from. Then she’d get the stalk around the corner. I’d wait a little longer, with my head on one side.
