I’d start on the gravel, with a broom over my shoulder, on my way to the oldest house. I’d know which of the stones that looked loose would take my weight. At the place where the reeds met over the path I’d lower the broom and carry it lengthwise, so the handle didn’t shake a pint of cold water down the back of my neck. I’d have learned that yesterday, and several times before yesterday.

The broom in the house would be mine. The one I was carrying would be for the next house, assuming there was still a way to get to it.

I don’t think I’d know how the instruments worked. I’d know how to wash the dial without getting water behind the glass, and that if the door stuck you waited a little before trying it again. People would give me numbers to write down. I’d write them on one side of a ruled book. On the other I’d put things like *last firm step is six feet short of the door*. Sometimes the numbers would be the same for weeks and that side of the book would fill up.

At the new channel I’d put down the broom. From where I stood, the fresh bank would look like a place I could get to with one good step. I’d probably get as far as lifting a foot before I saw how fast the smaller bits of it were going by.

I don’t think I’d see the mother. I’d see the otter’s prints and then, while I stood there considering whether to go back for a plank, one of the prints would fill from underneath. A little farther up, a wagtail would land on the willow branch and make it bounce. I’d wait to see whether the bird went down into the heap of leaves. It wouldn’t. There would be plenty to eat on the branch itself.

By then I’d have missed the time when I was supposed to read the dial. I’d be cold from standing still. I’d go back along the gravel to the fork and turn the board that said THROUGH so the writing faced into the reeds.

That might be more nearly what I’d do there. Somebody else could have the book and the instruments. I’d be the person who went ahead of the people who said, “I came this way last week.” I’d turn the boards back when there was a way through again. Sometimes it would take me half a day to find one, and on the walk home I’d keep trying to think of a shorter way to describe it than *go past where the willow was*.
