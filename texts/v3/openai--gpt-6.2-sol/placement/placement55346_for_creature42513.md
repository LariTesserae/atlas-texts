I’d live in the alterations shop, in the screw hole nearest the edge of the cutting table. It would be a bad place on most days. The water moves steadily across the table, and the other five holes are deep enough for a little return current to roll around inside them. Mine has worn into a notch. Most of what passes over it goes straight out the door.

There’s a ribbon leaf that nearly reaches the notch. When the works knock, the leaf doesn’t move at first. Then its tip comes down and touches the table. The touch sends just enough water back into the notch for me to open.

I think I’d know the leaf first by the things it brought me. Lime, mostly, which I’d have to push out again. A shred of something soft from the gutter. Once, a black speck that moved up the thread faster than I could draw it in. I’d pull the whole fringe inside and feel the speck go on moving for a while after there was nowhere left for it to go.

The leaf would grow. There would be a long time when it touched the table twice at every knock, and a longer time when it lay flat across the notch and I had to feed around its edges. I wouldn’t know it had broken off until the next knock came. I’d open anyway. For a while I’d keep opening, a little later each time.

Then somebody would come down the ticket stairs and catch the cutting table to stop from drifting. It would rock on its short leg. All six of us would open at once, although there’d be nothing much to eat in that first rush except what had come off the person’s sleeve.

They might stay there a moment, getting their feet under them. I’d have my threads out in the narrow space between the wood and the heel of their hand. There would be a small return of water each time they loosened their grip. I’d feed as long as they stood there, and when they let go I’d get one last, rather good mouthful from the table settling back on its feet.

After that I’d live on very little until another leaf grew down. It would come from the same stand of weed and bend in much the same way. It would touch first at the far end of the table, where none of us could use it. Little by little it would reach the notch. By then I’d have grown around the outer lip, farther into the street than anything in the other five holes. At the first touch I’d open on both sides of the edge, and find that the water going out the door had things in it too.
