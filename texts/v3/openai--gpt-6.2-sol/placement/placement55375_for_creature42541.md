I’d come down with a new cup wrapped in a stocking. Someone upstairs would have said there was a cup down there, but it was broken. This would be an errand of the sort you give a person who has been getting underfoot.

The new cup would be blue, with a good broad handle. I’d set it on the stair and go over to fetch the old one. I’d get wet at once, in a place I had just seen the rain stop.

I don’t think I’d see the animal until I lifted the cup. I’d see the green stripe first and put my thumb in it to find out if it was as clean as it looked. Then something underneath would reach out, and I’d hold very still, with my thumb inside a cup I no longer knew what to do with.

There would be the two leaves to get back under the foot. The first time I tried, I’d lower the cup straight down and one leaf would stick out. I’d lift it again. It would be hard to see the low spot with the cup right side up, and hard to see the leaves with my head anywhere sensible. In the end I’d have one cheek against the ledge. From there I could see how to set the foot down on one edge and roll it gently into its ring.

I’d take the blue cup back upstairs. I might say I’d changed my mind about the crack. That would sound like me, and nobody would ask much more about it.

After that I’d find other errands that ended at the bottom of the stair. I’d bring the stocking to sit on. For a long time I’d look across at the ledge because that was where I knew something lived. Then one day, getting up to go, I’d put my palm flat on the tread and feel a knock come through before I heard it. I’d sit down again. It would take a number of visits before I could feel the little knock that came before the large one, and longer before I noticed that the shell under my heel had lifted at the little one.

I’d be somebody who lived upstairs, then, but could usually be found on the last tread, sitting with one shoe off because the bare foot was better at it. If you came down with me, I’d show you where to put yours. We’d wait for the knock. I’d have to learn not to say *there* so quickly that you missed it.
