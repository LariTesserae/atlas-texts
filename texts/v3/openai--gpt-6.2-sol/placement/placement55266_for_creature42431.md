At first I’d put myself outside, on my knees in the snow, with one glove off. I’d have got a fingernail into the seam by accident. The noise would make me snatch my hand back, and then I’d spend a long time trying to find the place again. I could be that person quite happily until my fingers got cold. Then I’d go down the ridge, and everything I knew about the room would fit into a story no one had much reason to believe.

I think I’d rather be the highest peg. Not the one with the grass on it, though that’s the one I’d have picked before I knew what the grass was for. I’d be the one that so far has done nothing but get wet.

There’s a cut in me where the knife met a knot and had to come out. The person who pared me turned me over and came at it from the other side. That left a little tongue of wood pointing into the room. In the first days it was stiff enough to catch a grain of stone. Now it lifts and lies down in the current. If I have a way of telling how hard the water is moving, that’s it. The blows themselves are harder to judge. A small one nearby can feel much worse than a big one on the other side of the room.

The female comes up to me once, on her way back to the leaf. She works around my shoulder, keeping the short side of her body against the wood. She stops at the cut. I think she might be resting, but she puts her head down and eats something there. Until then I hadn’t known there was anything on me to eat. She does not come back the next day. Whatever she found, she got all of it.

Late in the storm the long animal tries me. Its joint closes over my end and keeps closing until the pressure reaches the part of me that’s in the stone. The little tongue folds out of the way. I’ve had a long time to swell into my hole. I hold.

It lets go almost at once.
