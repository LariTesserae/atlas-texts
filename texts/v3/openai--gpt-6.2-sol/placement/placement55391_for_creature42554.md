I’d be under the second tread, where you have to lie on the wet step and put your cheek against it to see anything. Even then you’d have to wait for your eyes to get used to the green.

At first I think I’d be one of the young. I like the idea of having six feet and not yet knowing what will bear my weight. I’d try a root, my own back, the leg of the animal next to me. I’d put a foot into a drop and be surprised when the drop took nothing of me with it. But when I follow that life as far as evening, out to where the grown ones are feeding, I find I’m still under the stair. Something has gone off and left me there.

I’d be one of the worn places in the leaf.

It takes a long time to make one. The young do most of it in their sleep. A toe pushes into the same spot every time the little body settles, and in the morning there is a groove so fine you could mistake it for a vein. All day the leaf fills it in. The next night the toe finds the soft place and presses it down again. By the time the hollow is deep enough to hold a little water, the animal that made it has to tuck its legs to sleep there.

Mine would be the hollow of a left middle foot. I’d know that foot at any hour, although it would never occur to me that there were five others. Once, in the middle of the day, it would come down hard enough to hurt. A wing would rattle against the pith. Then the foot would settle, slowly, from the ends of its toes inward. Whatever had happened would be over.

The night it left, I wouldn’t know it had left. There would be the usual small lifting of weight, and the root would spring back a little. The unfamiliar thing would be morning: light getting all the way into me.

The leaf would begin to fill me in. For a few days I’d get shallower. Then rain would come over the tread, and a little of it would work around the root and stay. I’d find that water had a weight I could learn, too. Not much at first. Then enough to bow the thinnest part of the leaf.

One afternoon a young one would come down the root facing the wrong way. It would reach behind itself, put a foot in me, and snatch it out. A little later it would turn around and drink. I’d feel its other feet on either side of me, making dents of their own. None would be quite where the old foot had been.
