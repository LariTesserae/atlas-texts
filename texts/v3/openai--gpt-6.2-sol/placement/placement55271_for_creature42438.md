I would be the rest of the red tile.

The corner you can see in the clear season is the broken end. The rest of me runs back into the mortar, nearly the length of a hand. I was put there to stop the step from rocking. Whoever set it pressed down on one end, felt the other end lift, and took the stone up again. There are two tiles in the mortar. The first one was too thin. I lie on top of it.

My broad face has a shallow hollow where a thumb pressed the clay before it was fired. The mortar took the shape of the hollow, but when the step settled, the two pulled apart. Water got between them. For a long time there was nothing in the hollow but water. Then a root came in from the garden and divided there. The thicker branch went on into the mortar. The thin one went round the hollow and came out beside itself.

That is where I would first meet one of the animals. It would be small enough to go all the way around on the thin branch. There would be an old skin caught at the fork, with one end still fitted to the root. The animal would take the loose end and walk backward until the skin came off. It would have to go around the hollow twice before there was room to lay the skin out straight.

I would have no part in what happened at the far wall. From where I lie, the garden would be the few things that came out of it and the few that went in. A grain of silt would arrive in the notch between me and the other tile and lie there for years. The white shrimp would rest with one leg against my corner. Once, in the dark season, a root would grow out into the open water and come back with its tip rubbed blunt. It would take hold of the mortar just above the place it had left.

When the drop fell, I would hear it through the other tile. The sound a foot used to make on the step came through that way too, though a foot was heavier and sometimes stopped there. I would not mistake one for the other.

In the next clear season the young animal would come back, too thick now to follow the branch around the hollow. It would reach in as far as the dark knot behind its mouth, take what it could, and back out. The skin it missed would stay in the thumbprint. By the time another one came small enough to get at it, the root would be growing through it.
