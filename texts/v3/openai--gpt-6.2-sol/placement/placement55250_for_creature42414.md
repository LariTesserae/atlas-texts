I thought I would be sitting on the stool, with my heels on either side of the blade. I even knew how I would get up: left foot first, because there isn’t room to bring both knees forward at once. It took me a while to notice that nobody got up.

I’m the stool. I know the chamber best through three small places on the floor. The rear one takes almost all the slow stroke. At the front two I get the motor, and the little extra knock when an animal crosses the grating. I can’t tell how many bars it crosses. I can tell when it has something in its mouth. It waits behind my left foot without cleaning itself. An empty mouth is always finding some part of a leg to go over.

The person who sat here used to catch a thumb under my rim before standing. Sometimes they caught it and didn’t stand. Their heels would come off the stone and settle again. I knew that much about what they were deciding.

A roof animal came down once and stopped against me on its way to the switch. One of its hinges rested on the dull place in my glaze. I had thought that place would feel different when something touched it again. It did, but only because the hinge was so small. All that weight, and I could have covered the place where it bore down with the end of the person’s thumb.

There’s a slight hollow behind my left foot. The animal waiting there can stand with one pad on the floor and the other on my foot, out of the way of the slow stroke. It draws the grain farther into its mouth when the stroke comes. Afterward it lets the grain forward again. Sometimes it does this for hours.

From the entrance you can see the whole top of me. There’s room to sit. Down at the floor, one of my feet is occupied.
