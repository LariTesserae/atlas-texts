I would be under the fold of the leaf, on the side the mite uses. There is just enough room there for a bent knee, if the knee is very small. For a while I would think that was what I was: a knee belonging to something that had got the rest of itself out.

When she lays her hand where her shoulder was, I feel the floor come down. It comes farther under the heel of her hand than under her fingers. I push back, mostly to keep from being flattened. She takes her hand away. Then she puts one finger down in the same place, lightly this time.

It turns out I am a rib of the leaf. I run the wrong way. All the other ribs go down into the dark, but mine was caught in the fold while it was still soft, and the growing has brought it round until the tip points up. There is a little hook on the tip. It catches one of the loose stitches when she draws her arm back.

She pulls. I can feel the pull all the way to the place where I begin, which is much farther off than I had thought. A strip of the floor lifts with me. The mite, halfway around the fold, lowers itself flat. She stops pulling. With the nails of her bare hand she works the thread off the hook. It takes her a long time. Twice she has to let go and start again, and each time I think she has finished with me.

Afterward I don't go all the way back down. A narrow ridge remains, about as high as the gray roll she made with her heel. When she bends her left leg, her foot slips over it. On the next try she gets her heel against it and leans. I had not known a person could put so much weight in one small place without wanting to crush it.

She turns onto her side. Her sleeve hangs down instead of lying under her. For a while neither of us moves. Then she takes her foot away, and from somewhere along my length comes a small click.

She puts her heel back.

I can do it again. I wait until she's ready.
