I would be one of the two flakes that touch when the loaded cars go over the sheave. Not the one rooted in the shelf. The one with just enough play to strike it.

For most of the long pull there would be room between us. A mite could put a foot there. Once, a long one would feed its whole front half through and stop, its rear feet braced on my edge, while it worked a shred of something out of the gap. It would get the shred loose. I would know this by the absence of the little tug.

I think I would want to be the polish at first. There is so little in the crack that has been changed by being met over and over. But the polish would be on the side I could not turn to see. What I would have instead would be the blow, and then the interval in which nothing held me against the other flake.

On my farther face an egg would have rolled to a stop. When it opened, the young one would rub and rub and get three feet loose. With those it would row as far as the edge, where the glass was rough enough for the rest. I would be a bad place to hatch and, a little to one side, a good place to finish hatching. The animal would go on without having to make anything of the difference.

Years later the grain at the blind end would drop. The break would run through me so sharply that I would strike the other flake once, though the haulage had stopped for the night. Then I would settle at a slant. The two worn points would no longer meet.

I would not be in the new opening. I would be back among the eggs and the wing scraps, with one edge lifted a fraction higher than before. The broad animal at the lip would still have to back out when she was done. But one of the long ones, coming the other way with a scrap in its mouth, would find it could go under me without letting go.
