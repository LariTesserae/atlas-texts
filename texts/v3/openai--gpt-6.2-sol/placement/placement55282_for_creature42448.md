I would start on the bench, with both feet on the dry paving. I think I would keep them there a long time. The wet half of the stripe is close enough that I could put a toe into it without getting up, but from the bench I can see the road twice, and in the smaller road the flock is still light enough to count.

I would miss the first sound in the seat. At the second I would put my palm down, and at the third I would find that what I had taken for my palm was the whole soft underside of me.

I live in the bench, though not in the part that looks like an animal. At the end away from the bead, one of the bolts is a little too long. Between its head and the ceramic there is room for me to turn over. I am white on the side that takes the heat of the seat and darker on the side that touches the bolt. When I turn, which takes most of the dry afternoon, the dark side begins to whiten. I have no way of carrying the color with me.

There is a thread in the clearance with me. For a long time I would think it was mine. It comes out of a fold I can close, and when I close the fold I can feel it pull all the way along my body. But it runs down the leg of the bench and under the curb. Sometimes it draws a little through me while I am asleep. Once a small hard thing came with it. I held it all afternoon, working it back and forth in the fold, before I found the opening in it and let the thread run on. It was one of the square nails, hollow at the root.

From where I live I can see the bench animal’s near foot only when the light is low enough to get under it. The young are on the other side. I know where they are by the little loads they put on the ceramic. Early on there is scarcely a difference between one and the next. Later, one leans so far out that I can feel the parent take its weight. It does this often enough that I begin to wait for it, even on afternoons when the seat stays warm and nothing comes along the thread.

When the water reaches them, I feel the mouths take hold one at a time. The last one lets go and takes hold again. Then a length of thread comes through my fold so quickly that I turn over before the shaded side of me has finished going white.

The bench is dry at my end. It will be dry here for a long time yet. I lie with the unwhitened patch against the ceramic and the fold open, in case there is more to come through.
