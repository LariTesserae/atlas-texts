I’d live under the first bridge beyond Siv’s house, where the main road dips. There’s a flake of stone there that rocks under a loaded foot. For a long time I’d think of it as mine, though what I’d mean was that I knew how to step on it without making it knock.

I’d be the person people sent for when a sound in the house changed. Not because I could always tell what made it. I’d be willing to lie on the floor long enough to hear it twice. A jar that had begun to talk when nobody touched it might have a root growing against its side. Or the sound might be coming from the next house, where somebody had taken to grinding a seed at a time after everyone else was asleep. You could spend a good part of a night deciding which, and then have to decide whether to tell.

The first time Siv sent for me, I’d bring a wedge for the new roof. I’d have heard about the animal and assumed the husk was shifting. But what she wanted me to hear was a small dry knock under her bed. It came three times while I lay there, and each time, just before it, she put her hand flat on the floor.

We went out to the road. I stood on the loose flake. Siv went back in. When I took my foot off, she called, “That.”

I could have fixed it then. I had the wedge in my chin fold. But Siv asked me to cross once more with both hands empty, and then carrying something. I borrowed a jar from next door. The knock was much the same either way, but with the jar I set my other foot down harder. From the bed she could hear that, too.

It turned out she’d been listening for one of the millers who used to bring her flour. He still passed that way, only now there was no reason for him to stop. His left foot came down hard whether he carried anything or not. She liked to know he’d finished work.

I took the wedge home. Later, when the flake wore so thin it broke, I fitted a new one. It took me most of the evening to get it to rock without pinching anybody’s toes. I couldn’t hear the knock from my side of the bridge. Siv lay on her bed and told me when I’d got it right.
