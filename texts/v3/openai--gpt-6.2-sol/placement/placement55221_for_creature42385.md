I think I would be the loose strip of bark under the evergreen’s first turn. Not the part that still carries the tree. The part a moth can get its head under.

I had thought I would be at the opening. There would be knees to recognize, and the scrape of a sleeve, and once in a while somebody saying, “No, lower,” to somebody who was still looking into the wrong hole. But I would rather be far enough in that when a person blocked the light I could mistake it for a moth coming home.

There is just room under the bark for the head and a little of the body. A moth that tries to get farther lifts me. Coming out backward, it lets me down by degrees. I would know that part of its going better than its arrival. Over time there would be a rubbed place on the wood beneath me, though I wouldn’t know whether it was made by one moth or by all of them.

In a wet winter I might soften enough to lie flat against the branch. The first moth to try the place after that would find no entrance. It might wait there awhile, with its head against the seam. Then the wood would dry, and I would lift again, not necessarily on a night when anything came.

From where I hung I could see a little of the basin, and the big fern where it turned over the stone. I don’t think I could see the leaf in the shell. I would have to take the visitor’s word for it, if they spoke, or know only that this time, after they took their finger away, two people stayed on their knees.
