I think I would be in the tall house first, just inside the hole meant for a pipe. From the street you could see the lengths of sucker laid across it, but not the one that had slipped. It would be lying a little lower than the rest, with one end in the draft and the other in the damp. That would be me, or the part of me I would know about at first.

I would know the animal by the sides of its feet. It uses them gently on the eggs and not particularly gently on the wood. Each time it turns an egg above me, I shift a little farther into the hole. At some point my wet end takes hold. The next time the animal comes, it tries to put me back with the others and can't. It goes away and brings another length.

I grow down, as a root ought to, and come out in the cellar on the underside of a shelf.

For a while I would have no way of knowing these were two places. There would be the cold that dries me and the cold that doesn't. At one end, feet. At the other, mouths. I would put out a white thread into one of the hollows in the shelf and wake something there. It would take the tip of the thread into its mouth. I would grow another. For several nights this would be all we did to each other.

Then a drop would run along me from the hole in the house. It would come to the place where the wood passes through the wall and hang there. Usually the drops let go at that place. This one, finding the new thread, would follow it to the hollow. The animal in the hollow would drink it without waking. Its mouth would close a little harder on the thread, and high up in the house an egg would move.

That would be how I found out how long I was.

In the next hard rain the hollow would empty. The small mouth would let go; the shelf would rise a little as the adults took their weight off it. For the whole rain I would have nothing to do with the water. It would run down me and fall. In the house the eggs would scrape, and once, through the wood, I would feel the gutter hunter take one heel out of the sill.

By morning there would be a little animal on the shelf where the mouth had been. Not the same one. This one would have six feet. It would turn a splinter over, and then turn it back. I don't think it would have come all that way along me. I think it would have put a foot down where it hatched and found the shelf.

Later the cellar animals would come back. The first one under the shelf would put a foot on the little animal's back. It would go flat, not knowing yet what was being asked of it. I would be growing past them by then. There is a lip on the shelf where the water collects before it falls, and I would be feeling my way along that lip, putting out threads at intervals, not yet sure how many mouths there were.
