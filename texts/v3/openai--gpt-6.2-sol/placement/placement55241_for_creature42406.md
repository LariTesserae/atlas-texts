I would be on the uphill side of a runnel, if uphill is the word for a slope that lets a drop of rain travel the length of your thumb before it stops.

At first I would have legs. I’d use them all night and remember almost nothing of having them. There would be the black grit, which looked firm until I put my weight on it, and a white knee that came down across my back and stayed there while its owner rattled. I’d get out from under it when it opened the joint to drink.

The first fold I found would be full. I’d put my head in anyway. There would be room for that much, and with my head in I’d be able to feel the animal beneath me drinking: a small tightening that came at intervals and reached even this far back from the mouth. The thing already in the fold would bear down on me each time the skin drew tight. It wouldn’t have legs to push me with. It would push with its whole length, and I’d have to wait for the next drop to get a little farther in. By the time the drip missed, I’d be too far in to back out without its help.

I think it would help.

So I’d spend another stretch of the night on the stone, going in what I took to be a straight line, and arrive at the same animal farther down its flank. Here the skin would be ragged. The white feet would be working steadily below me. I’d climb until I found a pleat with an old dry shell caught in it. The shell would give less readily than a body, but it would give all at once. I’d go in headfirst, and the shell would stay outside, clicking against my legs as the ledge animal moved.

That would be my place. Not at the drip, though we’d spend days beneath one. Not in the tight dark under the sole. A little way up the flank, where there would be no reason to go if you were large enough to see the whole animal at once.

Later, without legs, I’d know the plain mostly by what happened to the shape of my room. When the sole came down over a high bit of limestone, one wall would press against me from below. When we drank, the walls would part. There would be long dry spells in which I could have fitted in the crease of the skin that held me and still left room for a grain of grit. Then rain would come and I’d swell, and for a while there would be nowhere inside the fold that I wasn’t.

One wet night something would put its head in. I’d bear down. It would wait. At the next drop I’d bear down again, but the drop after that would be a long time coming. The ledge animal would have begun to move. The little legs would slip on the wet flank and catch, slip and catch.

By morning the runnel we had left would be dry, and in the one we had come to there would be nothing yet but the sound of us. The legs would still be sticking out. From the stone you might take them for mine.
