I think I would live a little below the green fall, on the side from which you can see the fish go up but not see them come down. For a long time I would call it the quiet side. Then somebody from the crown would come to visit and ask how I could stand the knocking.

I would have grown up farther down, among the pumps. My sister would still live there. When she came to supper we would talk with our wrists out of habit, even with our mouths empty and the water quiet enough for speech. My children would watch closely. They would know the signs for *your end is crooked* and *leave it, I’m coming*, but there would be a joke my sister could make with two fingers that I would never be able to explain to them. It would have to do with a particular aunt, and the way she used to stop a conversation to feel the wall, not because a lobe was filling but because she had thought of something worse to say.

On the way up from the pumps there is a turn where you have to open both folds at once. It is an easy turn provided you have nothing in your hands. When my sister came with a bowl, I would go down to take it from her. After a while I would start going down for other people’s bowls as well. It would be a thing I did rather than a thing anyone called me. If I were late, a traveler might tuck a bowl under his chin and make the turn anyway, and I would find him at the fall with a fine white ring pressed into his throat.

One day I would go down for my sister and find her youngest coming up alone. He would have a bowl against his chest. I would put out my hands and he would shake his head so hard that one shoulder opened and turned him a little. I would expect to have to catch him. Instead he would open the other shoulder just as far. The current would take him up sideways, the bowl still firm against his chest, and for an instant his face would be level with mine. He wouldn’t be looking at me. He would be looking into the bowl.

There would be nothing in it. His mother had sent him up with an empty bowl to see whether he could bring it. At supper she would want to know how he managed the turn, and he would say he didn’t remember. I would show her with my shoulders. He would object at once: no, not that far. Then he would put his portion of meal in my hands so that he could show us both.
