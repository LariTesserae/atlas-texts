I would start in the hollow under the stool’s shortest leg. Not in the middle of it, where the dust is deep enough to lie still, but at the lip, where the small animal puts its mouth.

For a while I’d take myself for the click. There’s some evidence for this: I come back each time the animal eats, and when it stops to clean its mouth I have to wait with it. But there are evenings when it feeds in the other hollow and I hear the click from there. So I must be the bit that falls.

I have one sharp edge. The animal always takes me up with that edge pointing out of its mouth. It knows me well enough to do this without turning me first. There can’t be much left on my worn side, but there must be something. It has never taken me up and put me down untouched.

One day, on its way back from the egg, the animal steps in the hollow. I catch between two joints of a hind foot. It carries me all the way up the leg before it notices. It works at me with another foot and pushes me into a crack in the glaze.

From there I can see the hollow. I had thought it was an exact fit for the leg, but the leg touches only one side. The animal has been feeding from the side that bears no weight.

The next time it comes down, it passes over me without stopping. Its foot closes on either side of the crack. It has been climbing this leg since before there was a hollow, and there is nothing I can do for that foot.

The young one will come up differently. It will try the smooth face first, because that is what it can reach from the floor. It will get two feet onto the leg and slide until its belly touches the stone. The old animal won’t be able to show it where to step. Its own feet go to places too far apart.

By then I may be wedged far enough into the glaze that even the tick won’t loosen me. I don’t know what happens to a grain up here when the height of the room is corrected. But if I’m still there, I’ll be on the underside of the leg, a little lower than any of the old footholds, with the sharp side out.
