I would start looking for myself in the space they have left for the third wall. It is just wide enough to lie down in. The two walls on either side would take some of the current off me, and at night I could hear the little animals loosening the stones. I would get to know the sound of a stone being rocked in its bed, which is quite different from the sound it makes once it is free. For a while I think I would mistake that difference for the thing I had come here to listen to.

But the space between the walls is empty. I live farther upstream, at the end of the shoulder where the rock is coming together.

I am soft and about as long as one of the brown animals’ toes. Along my belly are two rows of cups. I can hold on to a stone with either row and reach the other out into the current. Most of what touches it is too fine to keep. A grain with any weight to it draws the whole row of cups shut. I spend the better part of a night getting such a grain from one end of my body to the other. There is a hard patch on my back where the grains collect. When I was small I could swim, but the patch has grown out over both sides of me. To turn over now I have to find a place where the current gets under it.

The stones the animals bring are good at first. They have corners, and there is a pocket of nearly still water behind every corner. I can let go with all my cups in one of those pockets. I do this to sleep. As the stone takes hold, the pockets get shallower. Sometimes I wake with the current running strongly along my belly and have to catch the first thing I touch.

This is how I came to spend a night on a foot.

There were four toes on it. The one I held was curled under, with its nail against the stone. Every so often it straightened a little and I could feel the water go through the space the nail had covered. Then it curled again. Toward morning it straightened all the way. The stone didn’t move. I could have let go then, but the foot went up and I went with it.

The animal carried me back to the walls. I had never been so far downstream. It walked slowly over the loose silt, picking up each foot higher than it needed to, and in the space between the walls it stopped. I thought it had felt me. I tightened all my cups. After a while it put the foot down sideways, very gently, and drew it out from under me.

There was a scatter of small stones there, none much bigger than the patch on my back. I settled against one. It shifted when I reached around it. I tried the next, and that one shifted too. For the first time since I had grown too heavy to swim, I could move something without having to go with it. By dusk I had drawn three of the stones close enough together that the water between them went quiet.

The animal came back that night. It nosed among the loose stones for a while and took one from the far end of the space. It has done that every night since. I don’t know whether it leaves my stones because I am there or because they are too small. There is room under them now to turn over. When I do, the cups that have been against the ground all day open into the water, and I can feel, before anything touches me, which way the grains are going.
