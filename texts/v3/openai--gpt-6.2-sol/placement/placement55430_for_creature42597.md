At first I would put myself in the drain cover. It’s where I could see the most, and I like the idea of knowing the pumps by the soles of my feet. But I’d have to be somebody standing there, and sooner or later somebody would come around the building to ask me a question. I’d step down. From where we stood talking there would be no pocket at all.

Then I thought I might be the drop that goes into the bolt hole. There’s a good long way down the siding, and at the end a shoot of moss just tall enough to change where I go. But I’d like to be there on the days the drops miss, too.

I think I’d be a springtail on the middle shelf. Not one of the ones you see jump. I’d be the one you were sure was grit until you looked at the grit beside it and found that what you’d taken for its shadow was gone.

Most afternoons I’d keep to the clay edge, where there is something to eat. I wouldn’t know there were two pumps. I’d know that at intervals the surface got difficult to stand on, and that there was a place below the washer where it stayed easier. Once a year I’d follow the receding edge down to the lowest shelf and find more of my kind there than I had any use for. The quiet would be nothing to me. The crowding would be the event.

Late in the summer I’d go out on the fallen moss leaf. There isn’t much of a journey in it. At the tip I’d have to bring my front feet back almost to my hind feet to turn. I’d come back to the clay and feed a little, then go out again. From the drain cover it might look as though I expected the leaf to reach farther the second time.

I don’t think I’d expect that. But there is a small difference between standing at the edge of the water and standing with water on three sides. I’d go out often enough to know it.
