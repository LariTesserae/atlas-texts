I think I would be a fish, though at first I took the place I wanted for a place to lie down.

It is under the second terrace, where a dead span is held up by two living roots. The grain has washed out of the span in long holes. In one of them the water runs just fast enough that, if I face into it and move my tail a little, I stay where I am. Nothing touches me. There is wood a finger’s width above my back and a finger’s width below my belly. I go there after feeding and let the current take the shrimp legs out of my teeth.

The hole makes a note when I am not in it. I know this because on my way in I hear it, and on my way out I hear it, but for a long time I supposed that the note was somewhere behind me and that the hole was simply a quiet place.

A hem lives along the underside of the span. The first time I felt it touch me, I shot out so fast I knocked my head against the roof. After that I would go in a little way and wait. It never touched me while the note was sounding. It was only when I went all the way in that the feet came loose, one row at a time, and some of them found my belly. They were so much smaller than I had expected. I could feel each one deciding whether to stay.

For a season I slept farther down, in a place with more room. When I came back, the hem had gone some distance along the root. Not far. In the whole time I had been away it had got past a knot I could have cleared without moving my tail. At the knot the dead wood curved away from the living wood, leaving a space too wide for the feet to cross.

I did not carry it across. I went into my hole, which was narrower than it had been, and rested there until I had to back out for water. The next day I went in again. On the fourth or fifth day, when I drew back, something held to me for an instant longer than usual. The hem had found the far side of the knot. By the time I turned around, it was lying against the root as if it had always been there.

I still go back, though I can no longer get my head into the hole. The note is higher now. Soon there will be no water going through it at all. There is another opening a little way below, in wood so new that the leaves brush both my eyes when I enter. I haven’t learned to sleep there yet. I keep turning around to see what brushed me.
