I would start on the far side of the bank, where the ray’s fish waits. I like the place it has found: the town has already eaten, and there is still enough coming over the bank for one small fish. But I don’t think I would be the fish. It can leave the bank and go out over the mud in the direction the ray usually takes. I would need to keep some part of myself against the shell.

There is a loose valve in the gutter that feeds the bank. At one end it is no thicker than a husk; at the other there is a heavy knob with two blunt teeth. The current lifts the thin end. The teeth stay where they are, and the valve comes down a little to one side of where it lifted. Over a tide it can walk nearly its own width.

I think I live on the underside. I am a worm in a crooked tube, with a fringe I can put out into the water. I settled there when the valve was still part of a wall. The wall went on growing after a crack opened behind us, and for a while the new shell held the old piece in place. I went on feeding. Then one night the wall was farther away than I could reach.

It takes me a long time to feed after that. Every time the valve strikes the gutter I pull in. A good deal of food goes by while I am doing this. Eventually there is one knock I stop pulling in for. It is the one the valve makes when it comes down on its teeth, a small hard knock with hardly any shiver afterward. The other knock, when the thin edge hits, can still put me all the way back around the bend of my tube.

A shrimp comes under the valve while it is raised. It is small enough to stand on the underside, which for the moment is a roof. It walks across my tube and stops with two feet on my fringe. I pull in, and it goes on standing where I was. When the valve drops there is still room for it at the toothed end. It finds this room before the next lift. Whether it has found a place to live or merely got out of the way, I have no means of telling. At the next turn of the water it is gone.

Much later the valve seats in the mud. My old opening is underneath now. There is water in the tube but nothing new comes with it. The other end of my tube, the end I made first, is up beside one of the teeth. It is blind. I spend a long time there with my head against the thin cap I put on it when I was small. There is grit moving on the other side. Each time I come back, I take a little more of the cap away.

The first thing through the hole is a grain too large to eat. It sticks. I work at it until the water takes it out of my reach. Then I put my fringe through, a little at a time. The valve does not lift anymore, but the water still quickens over it, and late in the tide something on the bank puts a foot down beside my opening. I draw in just far enough to let the foot pass.
