I think I’d be the person the woman asks to hold the board while she gets up.

I’d have come as far as the wall with a breadfruit against my stomach. The girl in the eighth garden had knocked it down and told me to catch it, but she hadn’t told me what to do with it after that. I’d carried it past three gardens looking for someone with a knife. By then I’d got attached to it in the way you can get attached to a thing you’ve had to keep from dropping.

The woman would put the board across my knees without asking me to move the fruit. I’d have to hold one end of the board up with my wrist. She’d go a little way out into the sun and stand with her back to me, rubbing the place behind her ear.

“Is something biting you?” I’d ask.

“No,” she’d say. Then, after a while, “Is there anything there?”

From where I sat I couldn’t see. I’d tell her so, and she’d come back and bend her head. There’d be a short black hair growing out of the swelling. I’d tell her that. She’d try to find it with her fingers and miss it every time.

I don’t think I’d be the person they were expecting. For a while I might think I was. It would be hard to sit with the brick on my knees and not try my thumb against the air above it. The clay would be cool through the board, or I’d think I could feel it being cool. But I’d know the woman from somewhere less consequential. I’d know that when she cut breadfruit she turned the knife over to scrape the sticking pieces off with the back of it. I’d remember being annoyed by that, without remembering why.

Perhaps I’d been the child whose shoulder they measured when they put up the shelf for the dipper. It would explain why I kept knocking my elbow on it now. But I’d also remember holding a child up to that shelf, his feet beating against my shins because he wanted to get down as soon as he’d got what he wanted. I wouldn’t ask the woman which of those she remembered. I’d ask if she had a knife.

She’d have one. It would be under the board.

We’d put the brick down on the wall while we ate. From there I could see a print in the second course where the thumb had slipped a little, leaving a ridge at one end. I’d been looking for a print I recognized, and this would be the first one I’d want to ask about for some other reason. Had the clay been too wet? Had somebody spoken to the person just as they lifted their hand?

Before I could ask, the woman would give me the knife and hold out a piece of breadfruit with a bad spot in it. I’d cut too much away. She’d take the piece I’d cut off and trim it closer, and there would be enough left on it for a bite.

Later, when she went to wet the cloth, I’d stay on the sheltered side with the board. My legs would have fallen asleep. A terrapin would be coming along the narrow shade between my feet, and I’d wait for it to get through before I tried to stand.
