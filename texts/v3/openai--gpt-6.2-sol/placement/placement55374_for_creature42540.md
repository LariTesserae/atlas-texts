I think I’d be on the ledge below the grove, in a seed bell that had lost its stone.

From inside it I’d know the six trees by sound, but I’d have them in the wrong order. The tree with the cracked note would seem nearest. The one that sounds like tools in a basket would be somewhere under my feet. It might take me most of my life to find out that the wind can bring a sound around a corner.

I’d be one of the paper-colored runners. I’d have come down in somebody else’s skin. A seed-eater had wrapped a kernel in it and wedged it among the bronze leaves, and I’d gone in after the kernel, though it was too big for me to eat. When a gust took us, the skin opened and the kernel went on down. I kept all my feet on the lining. That was how I came to the ledge: holding very hard to something that had already let go.

There’d be plenty to eat below the rim. The wind would pack the scales from the old leaves into a seam at the back of the ledge, and there would be little soft things feeding between the scales. The bell would be for sleeping. I’d have to go in headfirst and back out, and on some mornings the wind would be so square against the mouth that I could back all the way out and still not fall.

After dark the long-tailed animal would come down. At first I’d know it by the forefoot that came feeling along the seam. I’d retreat into my bell and listen to the claws sorting through the husks. I wouldn’t know there were young in the wall. I’d think the animal came down because this was where it ate, and wonder why it never stopped to chew.

Then one night it would take the bell.

If I ran as soon as its teeth opened, I’d come out between two courses of stone, among the young. One of them would put a tongue over me. It would be warm and rough and gone so fast I might not even stop running. I’d go down through the wall until my feet found the underside of a stone and there was room to turn.

Toward morning the wind would come over the rim and the trees would start up. I’d hear the cracked note from the far side of the grove. I think I’d stay where I was awhile, with all my feet against the stone, listening to that tree be far away.
