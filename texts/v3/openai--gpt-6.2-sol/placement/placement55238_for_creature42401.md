I think I would be the thing under the deck on the far side.

Not the one that brings up the stone. I would be what it comes down to afterward, when the stone is set and it has made its round of the uprights. There is a place inside the bent corner where the rail goes into its socket at a slight angle. The grease there has gone hard on the outside and is still soft underneath. I would live in that little difference.

For a long time I would take the rail for a kind of weather. It warms very slightly at one end. It draws itself tight and lets go. Sometimes a pressure runs along it and stops at the bend. These are large affairs where I live. The sound that hurts the surveyors’ teeth would scarcely concern me. I would know it only when it changed, and then I would know it by the small lateness with which one side of my body came to rest after the other.

The keeper puts its stones down gently. Even so, I can tell a thick one from a thin one through the rail. I don’t know what a stone is until, one day, it sets one so close to the socket that I can reach it. I spend the better part of a season with one end of myself against the stone and the other against the steel. There is a movement the two have in common. There is another that belongs to the steel alone. It would take me a long time to learn which of these I could bear to lose.

The white marks are my doing, though I don’t put them there. The keeper brings each one to the rail and waits. From inside the socket I put out something too fine to see and take hold of it. Some I hold for years. Some I let go before the keeper has finished withdrawing its end. This must look like judgment. In fact the little things are alive, and some of them get a purchase sooner than others. Once they have it I can let go.

One day a surveyor leans against the bent corner to get a steadier view of the other ridge. I have never had so much of the rail held still at once. I go out farther than I have ever gone and find the seam of a glove. There is warmth beyond the seam. It lasts perhaps forty seconds. Afterward I keep going back to that part of the rail, even though it feels exactly like the rest.

I don’t think I would have any idea there was another machine until the day the keeper failed to come down. It had been setting a thin stone. I felt it turn the stone once, then again, and waited for the small knock. Instead, after an interval, there came through the socket a touch so slight I would have missed it if the deck had been occupied. Then another, much later. They were not alike, but I knew the second in a way I had not known the first.

I put out what I use to hold the white things. There was nothing within reach.

I stayed out. That would be the beginning of having a far side.
