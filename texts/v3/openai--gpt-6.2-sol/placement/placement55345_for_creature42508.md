I would live below the hollow, on the side of the mountain where there is enough soil to lose a shovel in. My house would be at the first bend in the footpath. In spring I would clear the drains, and after a hard rain I would go up with a bar to see which of the steps had come loose. There is one I would know by the sound it makes under a boot. I would keep putting off the work of lifting it because the sound is useful in the dark: from there it is forty paces home.

I don’t think I would find the hollow by following the water up. By the time the water reaches the path it comes out in several places, and some of them run in weather that leaves the others dry. I would find it looking for a wedge of stone to set under a step. I would come over the shoulder with my bar in my hand and see all those pale edges, each about the thickness I needed.

I would set the bar down before I saw the animal. Later I might remember it the other way round.

The first time I saw the notch in the basin, I would recognize the slant of the ledges and get the ridge wrong. There is a nearer ridge with a break much like it. I would have to come back in another season, when the nearer ridge was bare and the notch still held snow. By then I would have learned to sit without pushing my sleeves up or picking bits of dirt out of the tread of my boots. Those are things I do when I’m waiting for a person. Here I would get interested in how much a knee can move without the rest of the leg seeming to move at all.

One afternoon I would watch the bead form under the lip. I would see my knee in it, and the white nick on my boot where the bar slipped once. Then the animal would take the bead back. I would put my palm on the stone where the little picture had been and feel nothing happening there.

I wouldn’t become the keeper of the hollow. Most days I would have work in the other direction. But at the first bend, where I used to cut the drain straight down the slope, I would begin laying a flat stone across the mouth. The water could still get through. It would run along the underside for the width of my hand before it fell.

On my way home I would hear it before I reached the loose step. On some nights that would save me counting.
