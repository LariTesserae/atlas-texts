I’d be near the door, but not on the rail. I’d want to be somewhere the birds pass without having to decide whether I’m alive.

At first I think of the thumbprint. There’s a little dirt in it, and in the ordinary noon light you could put your thumb over it and find out whether the last person to shut the door had a hand much bigger than yours. But the print is on the dry side of the channel. Nothing much happens to it.

Lower down, in the angle between the jamb and the floor, I’d be a carpenter’s pencil. The flat kind. Someone set me down to free both hands, and the next time the door opened it swept me into the angle. There’d still be a number written on the wall that I’d put there. I wouldn’t be able to see it.

For years I’d lie with my best edge up. Then the channel would deepen just enough for water to reach one end of me. Not the sharpened end. The blunt end, where the wood looks sealed until it gets wet. By late afternoon a fiber would stand up there. In the morning it would lie down again, a little less exactly.

The bird with the white toe would find it on a day when the bolt gave it nothing. It would try the fiber, let go, and try it from the other side. Used to the red filament and the way the whole nest comes with it, the bird would have its feet set for a much harder pull. When the fiber came away it would take one quick step backward.

I think that’s where I’d move. Not all of me. The part still shaped like a pencil would stay by the door, with the number on the wall and the old paint over one of its corners. I’d go up in the bird’s beak as a curl of wood so slight that it would have to open its beak twice to get rid of me. I’d catch crosswise in the mouth of the pipe. For a while I’d be no use there either.

Then a nestling, backing up to make room for another nestling, would push me in the rest of the way.

I wouldn’t hear its first call. I’d be under one foot when it stood up to make it. The foot would bear down, slip, and find a place. By the time the bird at the mouth of the pipe turned around, I’d have bent enough to stay.
