I think I would be behind the loose scale of clay. At first I would take myself for one of the mites. There is a great deal of stepping in the dark there, and some of it would seem to be mine. Then the rain would come and the mites would go out to the wet edges, and I would find that I could still feel each place where a foot had been.

I would be the scale itself, attached along one edge. Not much of an edge. At the root end of it there would be a little ball of clay that rolled whenever I lifted. It had been rolling in the same shallow socket for longer than the seedling had had leaves. I would know it by the small knock it made on settling, a sound too slight to be the rain.

The larger animal would come under me once, backing in with a fungus thread in its mouth. The thread would break while it was still backing, and its blunt head would come up against my underside. For a moment I would be held open wider than I had ever been. Three mites would walk out without having to turn sideways. Then the animal would lower its head and go on eating, and I would settle a little crookedly over the ball.

After that a bead of water could get in where none had got in before. It would take several rains to do anything noticeable. The ball would soften on one side. A mite would eat at the softened part. One morning I would lift as usual and there would be no knock when I came down.

The fall would be very short. I would land with the side the mites knew facing up.

For a while I would expect their feet. There would be feet, but mostly springtails’, arriving hard and leaving all at once. In the place I had covered, the clay would be paler than the rest of the wall. That would be the hardest part to get used to: a patch with my shape, which I could no longer keep wet or dry.

The smaller animal would come there after shedding again. It would have a strip of its old skin caught on one hind foot. It would walk over me, turn, and walk over me the other way. On the third pass the strip would catch where my edge had broken. The animal would pull for a while with all five of its other feet set down. Then it would go off with the sixth foot free, placing it a little too high at first.

The skin would stay on my edge until rain. I would feel the water take hold of it before it came away. By then there would be two mites underneath me again. I would know them by their feet, though I wouldn’t know whether they were two of the same mites. There would be almost no room under me now. To get in, they would have to turn sideways.
