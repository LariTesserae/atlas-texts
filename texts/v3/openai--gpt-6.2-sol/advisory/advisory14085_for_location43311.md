Pack for a short walk in warm darkness. Plan your return as if you were crossing a much larger place.

**Mark the way back before you move.** Set a steady, low light at your arrival point and bring a headlamp and a separate backup light. The hollow is shallow enough that your feet won’t tell you which way you came in. There’s no wind on your face to give you a bearing, and from the far shelf your entry point may look like any other patch of dark. Walk ten paces, turn around, and check that you can still see your marker. If you can’t, come back while you still know where it is. If your means of return is a doorway or a device, test it at the point of arrival rather than assuming it will work from the far rim.

**Bring what the hollow doesn’t offer.** Wear closed shoes with decent grip: the grit is thin, and it can slide underfoot on the stone. Carry your own water and keep the first visit brief. The channels aren’t a reason to go looking for a drink, and still air near skin temperature gives you very little warning of how warm you’re getting. If you don’t already know the air is breathable, verify that before you take off whatever you traveled in. The plants aren’t a reliable test.

Use your lamp low and a little to one side. A shin-high shelf is easy to miss in a flat wash of light, and the little plants are easiest to step on right where the stone looks like a convenient foothold. There’s room to go around them. At the taller plant, stop a step short of the rim. You can see the dead leaf from there without lifting it or putting a foot into ground you haven’t lit.

Give yourself a minute to stand still. The first thing you notice may be how loud your own clothes are. After that, you might find yourself waiting for something to answer the sound of your steps. Nothing in the hollow promises an answer, and nothing about that silence obliges you to investigate it. A good first visit can end at the taller plant: look at the leaf, turn around while your light is still in sight, and walk back.
