Bring shoes with a good grip, a light rain shell, and a dry pair of socks. The old cart turns have been polished by traffic, and the leaves in the wheel hollows lie flat enough to miss underfoot. There may be no puddle to warn you that a stretch is slick. Dress for the cool rising off the road rather than the warmth you feel standing in the open sun.

If you’re staying overnight, arrange a room before you come. Ask your host which approach they’re using this week. The ward is small enough that you hardly need a map, but a map won’t tell you whether a stair still has all its treads or which side of a lane is taking the evening water. Take a pocket light if you’ll be out after dusk. Look down before you step across a threshold: the dark line there may be where the water is supposed to go.

The foundry yard is a good place to get your bearings. Stand clear of the gutters and wait for a pump run. You’ll probably feel it in your soles before you hear anything. Watch the thresholds on the uphill side darken in succession. Once you’ve seen that, the shallow channels and little slate weights stop looking like things you might casually step on or move out of the way. If you see water going somewhere surprising, tell the pump keeper where and when you saw it. Don’t try to put it back.

Bring drinking water until you’ve asked what the household supply is used for. Don’t pick at a loose edge of pavement or take a sprouting offcut as a souvenir. If you want to know what the wood feels like, ask someone to show you a piece from the shed; they’ll know which pieces they can spare.

Give yourself an hour with nowhere to be. Six streets won’t take long to walk, but most of what’s worth seeing happens at the pace of standing still. Find a spot out of the way after the motor stops. Listen for the drops inside the wall and see if you can follow one all the way into the street. Then walk the same way back toward evening. A seam you stepped over dry in the morning may have a little water running along it, and someone coming home with groceries will cross a few feet farther up without even looking.
