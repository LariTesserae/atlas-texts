Plan for a mountain ascent and a short visit to a confined space. The garden is small enough to tempt you into thinking the visit will be easy. The mountain has most of the work in it; the seam has most of the risk.

**Leave enough time to come down.** From the summit you can see the range’s shadow on the salt but not the foot of your own slope. Don’t count on being able to pick out your descent from above. Record the route on the way up, set a turnaround time, and carry water, layers, and a headlamp with a backup. There is nothing to drink in the garden. The leaves look wet, but the shine is part of the leaf.

**Make your first visit with someone who stays outside.** Agree on a short check-in interval and what they’ll do if you miss it; don’t count on being heard through the seam. Leave your pack secured outside, away from the opening, and take only what you can carry without snagging. Before committing to the squeeze, make sure you can reverse it. If the pressure on your chest keeps you from taking a comfortable breath, back out. If your means of crossing worlds needs room to operate, leave enough time to get back to that room. The garden doesn’t have much to spare.

**Put in hearing protection before you go through.** The quiet on the approach tells you nothing about the sound inside. Earplugs may take the edge off, but they won’t necessarily help with the sensation in your teeth. Leave if you feel dizzy, nauseated, or in pain, or if you find yourself waiting for the noise to let up before you move. The nick in the belt wheel is a good thing to look at: it gives your eyes a fixed point when your ears insist something is turning.

Once inside, there’s no need to get all the way to the drum. A headlamp on low will tell you where to put your feet; when you’re standing securely, switch it off for a moment and let your eyes adjust. The green light is worth seeing on its own. Keep your hands off the foliage and the oil at the broken stalk. Don’t rub the lettering to make it legible or try to free the washer from the shoot. And don’t use a root-bound shelf as a foothold: the roots have kept the pieces in place, but that doesn’t mean they’ll hold you.

The sound may start to suggest a fan, then a duct, then people at the other end of it. You needn’t follow that suggestion. There’s already plenty to see from where a person once knelt in front of the drum. If you want a record, take a picture of the shoot and its washer without touching either. You’ll have more luck showing someone that fit than explaining it.

On the way out, stop somewhere well back from the drop before you shoulder your pack. Give your ears a moment. The strap moving in the wind may be the first ordinary thing you’ve seen in a while. It’s fine to stand there and watch it.
