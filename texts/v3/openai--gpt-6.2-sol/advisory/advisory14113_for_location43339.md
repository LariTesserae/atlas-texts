Prepare for an exposed ridge walk, not a sheltered pool. The hollow takes the wind off you, but it offers almost no drinking water, little level ground, and no view of the slope you’ll have to descend. The seat may be the most comfortable place on the mountain in the afternoon. Don’t let that decide when you leave.

Go in settled weather, with enough daylight to turn back and try a different line down if necessary. If you can choose where to arrive, pick a broad, stable part of the approach rather than the hollow itself. From below, identify the descent while you can still see the shape of the ridge. Take a few photos looking back downhill as you climb: an obvious route on the way up can look like a set of identical gray slabs on the way down. If your means of returning depends on a fixed point, put it somewhere you can reach without crossing the lip or negotiating the loose floor in a cloud.

Bring all your water, grippy boots, warm layers, gloves, sunglasses, and a headlamp even for a short visit. A pole can help on the approach, but inside, a tip wedged between two flakes can pull you off balance. Take short steps and test each foothold before committing your weight. The pale grit is a poor guide to what’s wet, and anything in shade may stay slick after the sun has dried the more obvious patches. If the approach is glazed with ice or cloud hides the way down, save the hollow for another day.

Once inside, resist the urge to climb onto the far lip to see the country below. The drop is hidden, and the wind you’ve been hearing may hit you all at once when you stand above the wall. Check the seat for movement before trusting it; leave the wedges where they are. The little ring of water in the pecked cup is something to look at, not part of your water supply.

If you want to see what’s particular to this place, come just after the morning cloud has passed, provided the route is clear. Pick a secure spot and stay still long enough to follow one drop. From standing height you’ll miss the way it runs back under the flake, just as you’ll mistake the rusty mineral fringe for moss. The hollow rewards that kind of attention more than it rewards exploring every corner. A click in the wall may be no more than stone cooling, but if grit starts falling where you’re sitting, move out from under the ledge. Leave while you can still see which stones are wet.
