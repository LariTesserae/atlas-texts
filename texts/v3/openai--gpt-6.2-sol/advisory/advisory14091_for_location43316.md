Go to the mountain, but plan to visit the crack from outside. The thumb’s width farther in is misleading: the mouth is scarcely wide enough for grit, and even the wider part is crowded. A mite at the feeding lip can stop traffic in both directions. If your means of traveling between worlds can also make you small, don’t count on that alone. You would still need a way out that doesn’t depend on turning around, getting past another animal, or finding the entrance unchanged.

Treat the approach as a mountain outing near a working mine. Get permission and find out where the haulage runs before choosing a route. Ask when the winch is idle, and aim to be in position before the brief late-day glint, with enough daylight left to get down safely. Bring layers, boots with good grip, and a headlamp for the descent. The frost outside tells you little about the temperature inside. Don’t put your face to the opening to see better; the sulfur smell is reason to give the escaping air space, not a way to judge whether it’s safe.

A macro lens on a stand set back from the glass is a better tool than anything you could slip into the mouth. Don’t clear the lip for a better view. The grains there intercept the animals’ food, and a grain that looks like an obstruction may be shading an egg. Keep your breath off the opening, too. In a place where a shift of air can lift a shed skin, blowing away dust is a substantial intervention.

With luck, the glint will give you a few minutes of the crystal shelf and little else at first. Let your eye settle on one flake. What looks like an edge of black glass may turn out to have legs tucked along it. If the haulage is running, you might see the animals flatten before you can hear anything from below. Don’t tap the face to make them do it again. The two polished points are a measure of how much the mine already reaches into the pocket.

The fresh strip at the blind end isn’t something to wait up for. It may open years after you leave, and prying out the wedged grain would make a different event altogether. A good visit might amount to seeing one animal give way to another in the glint, then walking down before the ridge freezes. Leave the grain where it is.
