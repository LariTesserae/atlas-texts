Prepare to visit the mouth of the hollow, not the hollow itself. There is room for a person in the dark only if you count the space the animals are already using.

The ledge is the first and probably greatest danger. Scout it in daylight, in dry weather and low wind. A bulky pack can catch on the overhang just when you need to keep your weight over your feet. Wear grippy boots, keep both hands free, and bring a headlamp and a backup even if you mean to be back before dusk. If crossing safely calls for rope protection, treat it as a climbing objective, not a hike made safer by carrying a rope. Have a companion wait at a genuinely safe spot and agree on a turnaround time. If your way between worlds is portable, leave it somewhere you can reach without crossing the ledge again.

Acclimatize to the altitude before attempting the pass. Dress for the cold outside, but don’t take the warmth under the overhang as an invitation to shelter there. You don’t know what comes out of the seam with the vapor, and the reversing draft means that even the air at the mouth can change. Neither a lack of smell nor the presence of thriving animals tells you it’s safe to breathe. Pick an observation point in open air, clear of the outward draft, with solid footing and enough room to turn around.

A low-light camera will serve you better than a bright lamp. Settle in and let your eyes adjust. Watch a single patch rather than trying to take in the whole chamber: the edge of a plate, the border of the red seam, the entrance beneath the white-veined stone. The best view may come when the draft turns and the pale undersides show overhead. If you can’t see that from a safe stance, let it go. Don’t lean in to follow the movement.

There is no bare patch worth waiting for. A thumbprint of exposed rock isn’t a foothold; it’s a momentary gap in an occupied surface. The cracks aren’t handholds, either. Leave the mineral crust, the shed skins, and the three stones where they are. Someone may once have put a hand under that lintel, but whatever they meant by it, the animals have a use for it now.

Come back with a record of what you saw, not a specimen. Before carrying your gear into another habitat or world, check the soles, cuffs, and seams for hitchhikers. At the pass you’ll mostly hear wind. It may take a while to realize that the soft sound like grit brushed from a sleeve is the sound of a crowded place making room for itself.
