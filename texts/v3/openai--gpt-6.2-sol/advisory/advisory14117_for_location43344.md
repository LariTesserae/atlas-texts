Prepare to visit the step, not to enter the garden. The opening is small enough to cover with your heel, and nothing about the roots suggests that making yourself smaller would help. For a finger, the far wall is close. For a root, it may be a lifetime away. You don’t know which distance the garden would give to the rest of you.

Go in the season when the water clears. If the step is within reach of a pole camera from the surface, use that; otherwise, treat it as a cold-water dive and go only if you’re trained and have a buddy. There’s no air under the stone, and no reason to trust the stone to bear your weight. Take a light you can aim along the opening rather than into it. Approach without kicking up the bottom. The water outside will carry the cloud away eventually, but the first leaves seem to keep whatever lands on them.

Look for the shallow wear in the stone and then for the corner of red tile. The tile is a good thing to have seen before you look any farther in. Someone put it there to stop a step rocking. You may find that small, ordinary fact useful when you come to the wall that is near to your hand and far from everything growing toward it.

Leave your hand outside. The break is sharp, and a glove thick enough to protect you would tell you almost nothing about the leaf against the grit. Don’t pry the opening wider for a better look. The space is so closely grown that moving one stem would mean moving its neighbors, including the dead ones. If the shrimp comes out, watch it for as long as your dive plan allows. It can spend an afternoon drawing one stalk through its mouth. You needn’t wait for it to finish.

Agree on a turnaround time before you go down. The garden’s cold may feel different from the cold in the open water, but don’t use that feeling to judge how long you’ve been there. And if you hear the drop, don’t go looking for the air it must have fallen through. Listen once, if you’re lucky enough to hear it. Then look back out. A grain of silt moving past the step in the current will tell you more about your way home than the sound will.
