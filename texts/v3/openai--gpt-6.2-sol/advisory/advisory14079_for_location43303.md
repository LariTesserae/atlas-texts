The first thing to arrange is the way back. The garden is only forty paces long, but the service walk ends where a stair used to be. Don’t count on finding another route down, and don’t assume a factory that stopped making things has stopped carrying power. If you arrive by crossing between worlds, make sure you can cross back from *inside the room*, without needing to reach a particular landing or hear a signal.

Go at noon if you can. The ordinary light is useful: you can see where condensation has darkened the floor and where the conduit crosses the room at shoulder height. Wear grippy, closed-toe shoes and bring your own water. The drops on the bolt heads are factory condensate, not drinking water. Leave the figs for the residents, and don’t lean on the troughs or the old handrail. If you can’t establish that the grating is sound, look in from the threshold.

Bring earplugs and earmuffs, and put them on before opening the door. They’ll help with the roar, not with the vibration you feel through your feet and teeth. If you bring a companion, agree on a hand sign for *out now*. Plan on a short visit; there’s no prize for staying until the blowers ease. Leave if you feel dizzy, get a headache, or notice any change in the air.

Once you’re in, give yourself a moment before looking down. The room is small enough that your body will expect the floor to be close to the ground. Keep your eyes on the far wall until you know how you’re taking the height. Then, if you want to see the tower, look down without moving your feet. A cloud shadow traveling down the steel may make the distance harder to take in, not easier.

Watch one bird instead of searching for all of them. Follow the direction of its beak to the drain, and the others may come into view along the rail. The longer you stand still, the more the room separates from its noise: the leaf isn’t trembling, the air over the conduit isn’t rushing anywhere, and a drop has been gathering on the same bolt the whole time. You don’t need to hear it fall to have seen what you came for.
