Make your first visit from the step outside. There’s already a place to stand, and the cut handhold lets you lean far enough to see how much the room changes when you move your head. What you take for the far wall may be the edge of a blade a pace away. Before you try to find a route in, make sure you can still see the step you’ll need to get back to.

Bring a bright headlamp, hearing protection, close-fitting clothes, and boots with soles stiff enough for the grating. Bring someone who will stay outside. Agree on a return time and a visible signal; don’t count on hearing each other over the motor. Put the light on before you lean in. The violet glow is enough to make the insulator look like a landmark, but not enough to show you what’s between it and your foot.

Give yourself time to watch the stool. Once you’ve seen a few crossings, you’ll start looking behind its left foot before the next animal appears. That feeling of knowing what happens next is one of the pleasures of the place, but don’t mistake it for knowing how the chamber works. The crossings repeat, the bare patch stays bare, and the stalk at the bolt hole has opened a cup it can no longer pull back through. You don’t need to supply an explanation before you leave.

If you do step inside, go only as far as you can reverse without turning sideways among the blades. Keep your hands off the fresh edges and look up as often as you look down: the roof is as crowded as the floor, just with fewer, larger animals. Leave the grating and the cups alone, and don’t sit on the stool. Above all, leave the switch as you found it. The rolled lip tells you somebody cared about the fingers that would reach under it. It doesn’t tell you what the shaft is doing inside the rock, or what happens when it stops.

Pay attention to your footing and balance once the vibration is coming through your boots. If you feel unsteady, back out while the entrance is still close. The warmth and the tremor end so precisely at the threshold that the ordinary dusty step may be the strangest part of the visit. There’s no need to go twenty paces to find that out.
