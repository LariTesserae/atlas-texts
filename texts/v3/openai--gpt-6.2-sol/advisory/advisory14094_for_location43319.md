Prepare to visit it from the courtyard. “The size of a bread oven” describes the cavity, not the space available to you. Even if your way of crossing worlds can make you very small, ask how precisely it can put you down. The largest clear patch would fit under a thumbnail. You want to arrive *over* that patch, if you can do so without touching anything, and you want a way home that doesn’t require turning around.

On a sunny day, find the white tile and lay your palm lightly on it. Lift your hand, wait a little, then put it back. The vibration is faint enough to mistake for your pulse; it’s worth giving yourself time to tell the two apart. Keep your weight off the tile. Don’t lift it for a better look: it’s the roof and the source of the light. With it gone you’d have a view into a hole, but not a view of the room as it is.

If you can look in without opening the wall, leave your lamp off at first. The light is already strong, and it comes so evenly that you may have trouble judging how far one thing stands out from another. Look at the broken cup from two angles before you decide what you’re seeing. The notch in the middle tuft is a good test: follow it down to the matching notch in the clay. Don’t wet the tufts to see if they revive, and don’t brush the joints clean. The powder is part of the floor by now.

If your crossing lets you listen from inside, start with the sound attenuated. The quiet in the courtyard tells you almost nothing about the volume below it. You won’t feel a breeze to account for the roar. Listen near the floor for the low note, then, if you can change your point of hearing without moving anything, listen under the cup handle for the whistle. That little difference is worth more than a recording taken with the roof off.

Before you leave, look for the thumbprint. It’s on a face of the mortar nobody could press a thumb against now. Give yourself a minute with that: someone put a hand there when there was still room for one. If all you can manage is a palm on the white tile, count that as the visit. There’s no need to make another opening.
