Go at noon the first time. You can see nearly the whole square from the edge, and there’s no need to cross it until you’ve worked out where the depot ends and where you’re allowed to be. An empty loading platform doesn’t mean the depot is disused. If there’s no public way in, look from the street or take the tram past the exposed bank.

Wear shoes with a decent tread, long trousers, and a layer that closes at the wrists. The square looks dry, and most of it is, but the damp is concentrated in places you might otherwise use for footing: under the lifted cobbles, on the ferny steps, along the drip line below the pump-house pipes. Stay on the open gravel. Give the broken bank a wide berth; the clay looks cleanly cut, but there’s already a piece hanging from a root. Don’t go under the platform after the starlings or put a hand into a bolt hole to steady yourself. Half the life of the square is in places that look empty from standing height.

The main thing to get used to is how little the sound tells you about distance. A tram you can’t see may be the loudest thing in the square, while a thrush singing in plain sight makes no sound you can hear. Don’t count on hearing a tram or a warning over the extractors. Pick a spot clear of the tracks and the bank, and stay through one full cycle of the laundry machinery. When it winds down, the thrush may still be singing. It’s worth finding out what else was going on the whole time.

For the echo, stand well out on the gravel, especially if the nettles are high. Wait for a sharp sound rather than making a racket of your own. The first soft tap is easy enough to place once you know to listen for it. Give the second one time. It may still sound like somebody setting down a cup indoors, even when you’re looking straight at the clay. There’s no need to go around the wall looking for the room.

If you pass this way again in autumn, take the tram. Look for the little patch of brighter yellow where the slip of clay was. You’ll have only a moment to see it from the window, but you’ll know exactly where to look.
