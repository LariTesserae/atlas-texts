The Salt Hollow is not dangerous in any ordinary way. Nothing hunts there, nothing falls, nothing poisons. It is a small, dark, warm bowl of finished earth. Its challenge is that it is complete. You will be the only moving, unfinished thing in it. Preparation for visiting it is less about survival and more about becoming small, quiet, self-contained, and able to be still without leaving a mark.

## Before you go

If you are coming through some multiversal transition, do not enter the hollow until your balance, vision, and judgment have settled. Sit at the rim first. The place will wait. It has been waiting for a very long time.

Study the description until you can walk the hollow in your mind:

- It is only about forty paces across.
- The floor is one unbroken salt crust, pale bone-gray, stained rust and butter-yellow.
- The south rim is higher, about twice a person’s height.
- The north wall is lower, and at its base lies a single dark chert stone about the size of two fists.
- Eight or nine low blackbrush shrubs stand far apart, each with its own bare bowl of salt.
- There are no animals, no insects, no tracks, no water.

Memorize this geography. The dark inside will try to confuse your sense of scale and direction. If you know the hollow before you enter, it will hold you instead of swallowing you.

## Mental preparation

The quiet in the Salt Hollow is not just the absence of noise. It has texture. You will hear your own blood in your ears. You will hear the click of your swallow, the soft friction of your clothing as your chest moves. Some people find this unbearable.

Before going, practice sitting alone in a dark, silent room for two or three hours. No music, no phone, no light. Learn what your own body sounds like. Learn to breathe slowly without fighting the silence.

The darkness is not total blackness. It is a dry brown-gray darkness. Your eyes will adjust, and the salt floor will become legible as a pale oval. But that takes time. Do not expect to see immediately. Let your eyes adapt for at least twenty or thirty minutes at the rim before you step down.

The place has no morning or evening. Time may stop feeling real. Bring a silent watch or a timer that vibrates. Set a clear exit time. You may need it to remember that you are still inside your own life.

## Clothing

Dress in quiet, soft layers.

- **Upper body:** warmer layers, because the air above head height is cooler. A wool or fleece base, a soft shirt, and a warm jacket or vest.
- **Lower body:** lighter layers, because the ground is body-warm. But cover your legs. The salt crust and fine mineral dust can irritate bare skin.
- **Fabric:** avoid rustling nylon and crinkly shells. Use cotton, wool, softshell, flannel. The silence will amplify every synthetic scratch.
- **Footwear:** the most important choice. Bring clean, soft-soled shoes or moccasins with a simple tread. Heavy boots with deep lugs will leave crisp, long-lasting marks. If you must wear boots for the approach, carry a separate pair of clean camp shoes and change at the rim.
- **Hat or hood:** the air is cooler at face height, and a hood also softens the sound of your own head moving.
- **Gloves:** thin cotton or nitrile gloves. Not for warmth. They keep your skin oils off the salt and stone.
- **Face and breathing:** bring a soft buff or an N95 mask. The air is still and the dust is settled, but your own walking may kick up fine salt-iron dust. If that happens, a mask helps. If the air feels clean, you can leave it down.

Avoid scented anything. No perfume, no cologne, no scented lotion, no hair product that smells. The hollow has its own utter neutrality. Your smell will be out of place.

## Light

This is the hardest rule for most travelers: do not use white light inside.

The hollow sits below the surrounding flat and keeps the dark like a bowl keeps water. The darkness there has no shaft of light, no shadow. If you turn on a white headlamp, you will create shadows where no shadows naturally exist. You will also destroy your night vision for half an hour or more.

Bring:

- A small red LED light with a very dim, sub-lumen mode.
- Use it only before entering, after exiting, or in an emergency.
- If you must read a compass or a watch, use red light at the lowest possible setting, pointed downward.

A compass with a tritium or luminous dial is useful, because it gives you direction without adding meaningful light. A small digital watch with a silent vibration alarm works well.

Do not bring fire. No flames, no candles, no torches. Fire would be loud, bright, and alien here.

## Navigation

The hollow is small, but in near-darkness a small place can become enormous. You can become disoriented within forty paces.

Before entering, note your entry bearing. If possible, stand at the rim and memorize the shape of the hollow from above. The south rim is the high side. The north wall is lower and holds the chert. The shrubs stand far apart, each one a separate black fact.

If you are worried about losing your way, carry a thin, dark cord. Tie or anchor one end at your entry point, pay it out as you walk, and use it as a return line. Retrieve it when you leave. Do not stack stones, do not scratch marks, do not drop glow sticks.

Move slowly. The dust lies old and settled. Your steps will disturb it. A slow, deliberate pace keeps the dust down and keeps you quiet.

## Water, food, and waste

The Salt Hollow is dry. There is no water anywhere. But you must not spill water on the salt crust. A single drop will bead on the dust or dissolve a tiny dark star into the floor.

- Hydrate before entering.
- Carry water in a sealed bottle or hydration bladder with a bite valve.
- If you must drink inside, do it carefully, with your head over your own clothing or pack so a stray drop does not fall.
- Do not eat inside. Crumbs are a permanent import. Eat outside the rim, before or after, and pack out every wrapper.

Waste is the most serious problem. Bring:

- A WAG bag for solid waste.
- A urine bottle or absorbent urine bags.
- Unscented hand sanitizer.
- A small ziplock bag for used wipes or tissues.

Do not dig a hole. The salt crust is not soil. It is a delicate record of a place that has stopped. Do not urinate or defecate on the salt. Pack everything out. If you are unfamiliar with WAG bags, practice using one before the trip.

## Sitting and resting

Bring a thin foam sit-pad or a dark wool blanket. This is your base. It protects the crust from your body oils and gives your knees some cushion. It also marks a small, temporary place that is yours.

Do not bring a tent, stakes, chairs with legs that press into the crust, or any heavy furniture. You do not need it. The hollow is not a campsite. It is a room.

If you sit for a long time, the upper air may cool your shoulders while the ground warms your legs. Keep a warm layer over your upper body. The warmth from below is steady but it does not rise to your face.

## Inside the hollow

When you enter, do so at the lowest part of the rim. Step down slowly.

The first thing you will notice is the warmth. It comes from below, from the salt and clay. It is not hot, but body-warm, close, as if someone had been lying there. It is warmer at your knees than at your face. It does not move.

Then the dark will separate. Near the ground it may seem faintly amber. Above head height it turns brown-black. Above that, the sky is only a suggestion, a low gray-brown lid.

Stand still for a long time. Let your breathing settle. You will hear your blood. The silence will come back over you like warm air.

Choose a spot on the open floor, not under a shrub, not near the chert. Lay out your pad. Sit. Let the place close over you.

Do not touch the shrubs. They are alive, but so slow they seem like dark stone. Their twigs look polished, but they are still plants. Oils from your fingers could remain for a very long time.

Leave the chert alone. It is not a tool. It is an unheld possibility. The description says it would fit the hand exactly, but no hand has ever closed around it. That is the one piece of technology the place suggests. The best preparation is to understand that you are allowed not to pick it up.

If you cross the hollow, walk slowly. Forty paces will feel long. Use the shrubs as landmarks. If you become lost, stop, breathe, take a compass bearing, and walk toward the nearest rim. The space is too small for the lostness to be real, but the dark will try to convince you otherwise.

## Leaving

When your timer tells you it is time, rise slowly. Let your blood adjust.

Turn toward your entry bearing. If the dark seems changed, use your compass. Pick up your pad, your cord, your water tube. Check the ground around you. Look for any hair, lint, crumbs, droplets, or fibers. Pack them out.

Walk out the same way you came. At the rim, you may use your red light if you need it. The bowl behind you will settle back into its own dark.

## What it will be like

If you prepare well, the Salt Hollow will not resist you. It will simply be there, as if you arrived after the ending.

You may feel unnecessary. That is not an insult. The place is already complete. It is not waiting for you. It is not waiting at all.

You may notice the shrubs as separate facts, not a community. You may notice the wide salt floor as an open space that holds room for something that is not there. You may notice the warmth at your knees and the cool air at your face and the dark sitting between them without mixing.

The silence will not be hostile. It will be complete. When you stop moving, it will come back over you like warm air.

If you are attentive, you will understand that the hollow is not an emptiness waiting to be filled. It is a stable emptiness, finished and whole. The best preparation is to be able to receive that without needing to change it.

Take nothing but the memory. Leave nothing but the faintest, soon-settled trace of your presence, and even that, try to make as small as you can.
