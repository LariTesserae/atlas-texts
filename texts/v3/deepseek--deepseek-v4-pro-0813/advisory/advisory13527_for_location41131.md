The Kettle is not a place you can visit casually. If you are an ordinary human multiverse traveler, you must prepare as if for a high-altitude volcanic gas field, a sterile laboratory, and a wildlife reserve where the floor itself is a single living tissue. You are the wrong kind of animal here: too wet, too loud, too warm, too heavy. Your preparation should aim to make you a non-event.

---

## 1. Understand what you are entering

Before anything else, understand that The Kettle is complete without you. It is a hot, dark bowl high on an old volcano, roofed by darkness, filled with sulfurous air and living things that have never seen sunlight. The floor is not stone. It is a carpet of small white hexapods packed so tightly they behave like one surface. Hoodmaws move through them like slow currents. Kiln-keepers tend rings of black stone. Pale tubes breathe gas near the fumaroles.

Your body, by contrast, is mostly water and salt. To the life here, you are not a person. You are weather: a moving source of moisture, heat, vibration, and foreign chemistry. The preparation is therefore not just about surviving the heat and bad air. It is about not becoming a disturbance.

---

## 2. Remote reconnaissance first

Do not enter The Kettle without watching it from outside.

Use passive or remote observation: low-light cameras, thermal imaging, vibration sensors, or probe drones if available. Do not use bright visible light. Map the one bare ledge, the entrance, the positions of the kiln-keepers’ stone rings, the active fumaroles, and the paths the hoodmaws tend to take. Observe long enough to learn the rhythm of the place: when the fumaroles cough, when the hexapod carpet ripples, when the kiln-keepers shift their stones.

If you cannot observe remotely, do not go. The Kettle does not need your attendance.

---

## 3. Physical and medical preparation

The air is thin, hot, and dry. Even with breathing equipment, the altitude and heat will stress your body.

- **Acclimate to altitude** if possible. Spend time at equivalent heights, or use simulated altitude training.
- **Train for heat tolerance.** Spend time in saunas, hot rooms, or heavy clothing in warm conditions. Learn how your body signals heat exhaustion.
- **Practice slow breathing.** You must be able to breathe shallowly and quietly for long periods. Panic breathing will not go well here.
- **Build stillness.** Practice sitting or standing motionless for an hour at a time. The safe ledge is safe only if you do not move too much.
- **Get a medical check.** Anyone with respiratory, cardiac, or heat-related conditions should not enter.

On the day, pre-hydrate for at least 24 hours but avoid diuretics like caffeine or alcohol. You will lose water quickly even inside a suit.

---

## 4. Breathing and life support

Do not count on filtering the local air. The atmosphere is thin, hot, sulfurous, and immensely dry. You need a self-contained breathing system.

- **Closed-circuit rebreather or positive-pressure oxygen supply.** It should be independent of local air, with a carbon dioxide scrubber and oxygen supply sized for the full duration of your visit plus emergency reserve.
- **Sulfur gas protection.** If any outside air can enter the system, it must pass through multi-gas cartridges rated for hydrogen sulfide, sulfur dioxide, and acid gases. Better: use a fully sealed system with no outside intake.
- **Humidified breathing gas if possible.** The dryness will crack your throat within minutes if you breathe local air directly. A closed system can add some humidity to the breathing loop.
- **Emergency oxygen.** Carry a small independent bottle in case the main system fails.

Never remove your mask inside The Kettle. Not to drink, not to speak, not to wipe your face.

---

## 5. Clothing and suit

You need a full-body isolation suit. It should be:

- **Gas-tight** with sealed seams, gloves, boots, and helmet.
- **Heat-resistant**, rated for radiant volcanic heat and occasional contact with hot rock.
- **Smooth on the outside**. The small hexapods climb by contact; a slick outer surface gives them less purchase.
- **Light-colored or reflective** to manage radiant heat, but not so shiny it creates glare in low light.
- **Slightly positive-pressure** if possible, to keep alien dust and microbial crust out.

Underneath, wear a moisture-wicking base layer, then a cooling vest or phase-change packs. The heat is not uniform. Near the rings and fumaroles it can be intense. Your body heat will also make you visible to the kiln-keepers, which sense heat through pits. A reflective outer layer helps reduce your heat signature, but it must not trap so much heat that you overheat.

Your boots should be insulated and puncture-resistant, but treat them as emergency protection, not permission to walk on the floor. Wear smooth overboots or covers that can be removed and bagged later.

---

## 6. Hydration, nutrition, and waste

You are a bag of water. The Kettle is a place where water is wrong. All moisture must stay sealed.

- Carry water in a sealed reservoir inside your suit, with a bite tube that reaches your mouth inside the mask. Drink slowly.
- Use electrolyte solutions to replace what you lose through sweat.
- Eat before entering. If you must eat inside, use calorie gel through a tube. Do not open your suit.
- Urine and feces must be contained. Wear an external catheter or adult diaper that leads to a sealed collection bag. Do not void into the environment.
- Do not spit. Do not bleed if you can help it. Do not exhale moisture into the room. Your breath is already a foreign liquid.

If you feel nausea, take antiemetics before entry. Vomiting inside a sealed suit is dangerous, and opening the suit is unthinkable.

---

## 7. Light and vision

The Kettle is dark, but not empty. Fumaroles give a dull red glow. The hexapods carry two blue dots that flash when disturbed. The kiln-keepers shimmer with heat.

- Use night-vision goggles or passive low-light systems if possible.
- If you need artificial light, use a red or far-red LED at the lowest workable setting, pointed only at your own equipment. Never aim it at the floor, the walls, or the animals.
- Bright white light will be a violent intrusion.
- If you see blue sparks ripple across the floor, it means you have already disturbed the carpet. Freeze and reduce all movement.

The dark is not a background. It is the place’s skin. Treat it with care.

---

## 8. Movement, sound, and vibration

The quiet in The Kettle is not calm. It is alert. Vibration triggers feeding. Your footsteps, even on rock, may be the loudest thing in the bowl.

- Move in slow motion. If you shift your weight, do it in increments.
- Do not speak. Use pre-arranged hand signals or silent radio text if necessary.
- Do not cough, clear your throat, or sneeze if you can help it. Suppress coughs gently. If you must cough, do it inside the mask and keep it as soft as possible.
- Do not drop anything. Tether every object to your suit or harness.
- Avoid hard-soled footsteps. If you wear boots, cover them with a soft outer layer, but remember you should not be walking on the floor at all.
- The one bare ledge is your entire world. Stay on it. Do not step onto the living carpet.

If you must move from entrance to ledge, plan the route in advance. The ledge is probably near the entrance, but if not, you need a portable platform or bridge that can span from solid rock to solid rock without touching the floor. A collapsible plank or a few non-marring pads can work, but they must be placed with extreme care.

---

## 9. Staging on the safe ledge

Establish a small station on the bare ledge.

- Place a folding stool or padded mat on solid rock only.
- Anchor yourself with a full-body harness and a tether to a removable anchor outside the bowl or to solid rock above the ledge. Do not hammer pitons into the basalt unless absolutely necessary. The Kettle is not yours to scar.
- Keep the tether taut enough to feel but not so tight it hums or transmits vibration.
- Arrange your equipment so nothing can roll, slide, or tap against rock.
- Have a silent emergency signal: two clicks on a radio, three blinks of an infrared beacon, something that does not create sound in the bowl.

The ledge is safe enough if you do not move too much. That means you may spend hours in near-stillness. Bring knee pads, a padded seat, and enough warmth or cooling to stay comfortable without shifting constantly.

---

## 10. Behavior around the lifeforms

**The hexapod carpet:** Do not touch it, step on it, or reach into it. If a few hexapods climb onto your boots or suit, do not crush them. Gently brush them off with a soft silicone scraper or brush. They are searching for moisture and salt. If your suit is sealed, they will find nothing and may lose interest.

**The hoodmaws:** They are size of small dogs, with mouths that open sideways. They plow through the hexapods, not through you. If one surfaces near the ledge, freeze. Do not create vibration. They are not interested in humans unless you act like a disturbance. Let them pass.

**The kiln-keepers:** They are large, slow, and territorial. Their rings are structures, not ruins. They are not for visitors. Do not approach them. Do not touch the rings, the stones, the scraped grooves near the entrance, or the low cairn. If a kiln-keeper notices you, remain still. Do not retreat quickly. They sense heat, so minimize your heat bloom and avoid aiming your body directly at them.

**The pale tubes:** Do not touch them or breathe near them if your suit is open. They shed tiny young and sip sulfur gas. They are animals, not plants. Treat them as sensitive and possibly reactive.

**The sulfur crust and microbes:** Do not touch the yellow-gray crust. It may be corrosive or biologically active. The only liquid here is sulfur, and it is not for your kind. If you contact liquid sulfur, treat it as a chemical and thermal burn.

## 11. Psychological preparation

The Kettle will feel like being inside a living body. The hiss of gas is a long exhalation. The tick of chitin is like dry rain. The floor ripples and flows. Blue sparks move across the dark like a nervous system dreaming.

You may feel:

- **Panic** from the darkness, the heat, the tight suit, and the knowledge that the floor is alive.
- **Derealization** from low oxygen, heat, and the alien rhythm of the place.
- **The urge to cough, swallow, shift, or speak.** Every ordinary human impulse becomes enormous.
- **A strange awareness of your own moisture.** You will feel wet, loud, and foreign.

Prepare for this by practicing meditation, breath control, and stillness. Train in a simulated dark hot room with ticking sounds and restricted movement. Bring a buddy if possible; one person can monitor suit telemetry and the environment while the other observes. Do not go alone.

If you begin to feel heat illness or confusion, do not push through. The Kettle will still be there. Your body will not.

---

## 12. Emergency plans

- **Suit breach:** Leave immediately. Sulfur gases, heat, and absolute dryness can incapacitate you quickly. Retreat to the entrance and outside.
- **Fall onto the floor:** Do not thrash. Use your tether or winch to lift yourself out. Do not call out audibly; use radio if possible. The vibration will attract hoodmaws. Protect your face and air supply.
- **Heat illness:** Initiate cooling, move toward the entrance, and get outside. Do not open the suit inside.
- **Equipment failure:** Have redundant air, redundant light, and a silent alarm. Know the quickest route to the entrance.
- **Rescue:** Arrange an outside team with a winch or line. They should be prepared to extract you without entering the bowl themselves if possible.

---

## 13. Decontamination and aftercare

When you leave, you may carry particles, shed skins, or microbial crust on your suit.

- Do not brush anything off outside.
- Enter a sealed decontamination chamber.
- Use dry vacuum and mechanical removal first. Then use chemical decontamination appropriate for sulfur compounds. Water alone may create acids or slurries that could damage equipment or spread organisms.
- Bag and seal the suit and all gear until you can clean them properly.
- Inspect yourself for any hexapods, shed skins, or irritants.
- Get medical evaluation for sulfur exposure, heat stress, dehydration, and psychological strain.

Do not bring souvenirs. Do not take samples. Leave no trace.

---

## 14. What it will feel like

When you first step onto the ledge, the heat will press on you like a hand. The hiss of gas will be the loudest continuous sound, but underneath it you will hear the tick of a million small bodies shifting. The darkness will seem to move. A blue ripple may spread across the floor, like a thought passing through the colony.

Your mouth will want to swallow. You will become aware of your own wetness as a kind of noise. Your heartbeat will sound too loud. The kiln-keepers will shift a stone with a soft thump you feel through the rock more than hear.

You will understand, in that stillness, that the floor is not ground. It is a skin. The rings are not ruins. They are kept. The tubes are not plants. They breathe. The whole bowl is alive, complete, and indifferent to you.

The only correct response is to be very still, very quiet, and very small.

---

## Non-negotiable rules

- Do not stand on the floor.
- Do not speak or make vibration.
- Do not expose water, saliva, urine, blood, or breath to the open air.
- Do not touch the rings, grooves, cairn, or animals.
- Do not use bright lights.
- Stay on the bare ledge.
- If the floor ripples blue, freeze and reduce movement.
- Leave no trace.

If you cannot follow all of these, do not enter The Kettle. It does not need you. It has been there a long time, a hot dark room at the top of the world, full of life that has never seen the sun. You are the visitor. Prepare accordingly.
