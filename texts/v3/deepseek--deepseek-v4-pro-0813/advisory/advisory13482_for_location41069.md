**Preparation for Visiting the Plateau of the Held Breath**

Visiting this place will not feel like an adventure. It will feel like walking into a paused photograph of the end of the world. The air will be thin and cool; your breath will go shallow without your noticing. You will be thirsty long before you feel thirsty. The silence will press against your eardrums. You will have the sense that the ground is not texture but tissue. At first it will look dead; then it will look too alive, everywhere, motionless. The light will be ordinary, and that will make it worse. You may start to feel that moving is rude, that sitting down is polite.

**Do not sit down.**

---

## Quick Summary

- Do not go alone.
- Do not go without a sealed environmental suit and independent air supply.
- Do not drink or use any local water—there is none.
- Do not touch the living pavement with bare skin.
- Do not sit, sleep, or take shelter inside any structure.
- Set a hard time limit and an automated extraction trigger.
- If you see your own footprints ahead of you, call for extraction immediately.
- If you feel a deep, peaceful urge to stay, that is the most dangerous symptom.

---

## What It Will Be Like

The plateau is high. So high that your body will begin to fail in small ways before you recognize them—slight headache, slower thoughts, a pleasant detachment. The air is dry beyond any desert. No dew, no frost, no seep. Your skin will tighten. Your eyes will dry. Your lips will crack if exposed. The cold is not bitter, but it is steady, and it will pull heat from you without wind.

At first the ground seems like cobble and salt crust. Then you notice the seams. The seams are not cracks in rock. They are living joints. The ground is paved with shield-shaped animals, fitted too tightly to count, their dorsal plates mineralized and pitted. Some have sealed eyes. They do not blink. The air is full of tiny mica-winged things that hang motionless. There are larger animals that you will mistake for standing stones. They are alive. The plants are alive in a way deep lichen is alive—too slow to see, too old to be dead.

The quiet is immense. Your footsteps will crunch and then fall away without echo. You will hear your own pulse, your own breath, maybe the faint tick of cooling stone. Do not tap back.

The longer you stay, the more the place begins to fray. A cairn on the horizon keeps its distance no matter how long you walk. A standing stone appears twice. Shadows point toward animals instead of away from the sun. You find your own footprints ahead of you, though you have not yet walked there.

That is not a puzzle. That is the beginning of your erasure.

## Physical Conditioning

### Altitude
- Acclimatize before departure. Spend at least two weeks above 3000 metres, preferably 4000–5000 metres, or use a hypobaric chamber.
- The plateau is high enough that hypoxia will make you docile and dreamy. That docility is dangerous.
- If you cannot acclimatize, carry supplemental oxygen and know how to use it before you go.

### Cardiovascular and Respiratory
- Train for slow, sustained movement at altitude. You are not running anywhere. You are placing one foot carefully in front of the other while carrying your survival systems.
- Practice breathing exercises. Learn to notice shallow breathing before it becomes confusion.
- Get a medical check. No open wounds, no dental problems, no respiratory or cardiac issues. The dry air will find every weakness.

### Skin and Mucous Membranes
- The air has no moisture. Your skin will crack, your nose will bleed, your eyes will feel like paper.
- Use barrier creams, saline nasal gel, lip balm, and eye drops before exposure.
- Seal any small cuts. Your blood is a water-based intrusion into a non-aqueous world.

---

## Mental Preparation

This is the more important half.

- Spend time in anechoic silence or sensory deprivation. Learn what your own pulse sounds like when it is the only sound.
- Practice stillness, then practice breaking stillness on command. The plateau may make you want to sit. You need a trained reflex to move when you have been still too long.
- Memorize a **reality anchor sequence**. Every fifteen minutes, say aloud:
  - Your name.
  - Your home universe or world.
  - The current time by your own watch.
  - The phrase: *“I am a visitor. I do not belong here. I will leave at [time].”*
- Then perform a physical check: touch thumb to each finger, look at your reflection in a small mirror, check your tether, check two independent clocks. If any step fails or contradicts, initiate extraction.
- Prepare for the feeling of being watched by many slow eyes. The eye-plates on the ground are alive and sealed. They will not blink. The plateau will notice you. That is not a threat. That is simply a fact.

## Equipment

### Life Support and Clothing

**Sealed environmental suit.**  
Positive pressure, chemical-resistant outer layer, integrated hood, full-face visor, sealed gloves and boots. It must not breathe locally. The air is thin, alkaline, and full of dust and tiny organisms.

**Independent air supply.**  
Use a closed-circuit rebreather with supplemental oxygen and CO₂ scrubber. Carry an open-circuit bailout bottle. If you must use outside air, it must pass through HEPA and chemical filtration. Do not inhale the local air directly.

**Moisture recovery.**  
Your exhaled water is precious and also a contaminant. Use a rebreather that condenses and stores humidity. Do not let it drip onto the ground.

**Insulation.**  
The cool is steady and high-altitude. Wear a moisture-wicking base, fleece, windproof layer, and insulated outer layer. Do not overheat. Sweat wastes water and can freeze or irritate.

**UV protection.**  
High altitude, thin air, intense light. Cover all skin. Use a UV-blocking visor and sunscreen before suiting up.

**Footwear.**  
Wide, flat, pressure-distributing soles or snowshoe-like over-soles. You are walking on living creatures. Reduce the pounds per square inch. If you have access to a low-level anti-gravity assist, use it to lighten your steps.

**Gloves.**  
Thin inner liner for dexterity, thick chemical-resistant outer. Never touch the ground with bare skin.

### Navigation and Anchoring

- **Physical tether.** A thin, high-strength cord run from your entry point or portal. This may be the only reliable guide back. Use a bright colour that stands out against salt and dust.
- **Active homing beacon.** A multiversal anchor that points toward your home portal. Check it every ten to fifteen minutes.
- **Two independent clocks.** One mechanical, one quartz or atomic. If they disagree by more than a minute, begin extraction. Do not trust local time or the sun.
- **A mirror.** If your reflection lags, blinks, or looks away, you are already in trouble.
- **Inertial navigation unit.** Optional, but do not rely on it alone. The rules of space are unreliable here.

### Recording and Communication

- **Paper logbook** with numbered pages and a graphite pencil. Write your name, the time, and your location at regular intervals. If you find writing ahead of your place in the book, close it and leave.
- **Voice recorder.** Narrate to maintain linearity. If playback says words you do not remember saying, stop and leave.
- **Camera.** Use it to document, not to navigate. If the same object appears in a photo where it should not, do not investigate.
- **Radio or tether-based comms** to your support team. Shouting will not carry far in the thin air.

### Food, Water, Waste

- Bring all your calories for the planned visit plus a 50 per cent reserve. High-fat, low-residue bars. No open food.
- Bring all water. At altitude and in total dryness, plan for at least 4–6 litres per 12 hours, more if using an open respirator. Sip on schedule. Use electrolyte replacements.
- All waste must be contained. Urine bags, fecal bags, absorbent pads, sealed containers. Do not spit. Do not bleed. Do not shed hair or skin if you can avoid it. Your moisture and biology are foreign contaminants.
- Do not use any local liquid. There is no water. If you see anything that looks like water, it is not water, or it is a trap.

### Medical Kit

- Altitude medications: acetazolamide, dexamethasone, nifedipine. Know the signs of high-altitude pulmonary and cerebral edema.
- Wound sealant, cyanoacrylate, hemostatic dressings. If you bleed, seal it immediately.
- Antihistamines, epinephrine injector, asthma inhaler if you have any history.
- Sterile eye wash, saline nasal gel, lip balm.
- Analgesics, but avoid sedating ones.
- Chemical heat packs, thermal blanket.
- Personal medications, pre-sorted and labelled.

### Emergency Extraction

- A dead-man switch that automatically triggers return after a set time or if you fail to press a button at intervals.
- A panic button or extraction flare that works across universes.
- A personal locator beacon separate from the tether.

### Do Not Bring

- Open flames, sparks, or combustion engines. The dust contains perchlorates and is strongly oxidizing.
- Drones or complex autonomous vehicles. They may be caught in the repetition.
- Seeds, live plants, pets, or any other Earth biology.
- Anything you would not want to lose to a place that erases difference.

## On the Ground: First Minutes

- Step out onto a small roll-out platform or mat if possible. If you must stand, do so lightly.
- Do not kneel, sit, or lie on the ground. The ground is not ground.
- Start your timer. Call in to base. Check tether and air.
- Stand still for one full minute. Listen. The quiet will be immense. You may hear your own joints, your pulse, the faint tick of cooling stone. Do not tap back.
- Look around slowly. Expect to mistake herd-beasts for standing stones and pavement animals for cobbles. When you see the seams and the sealed eyes, do not panic. They were there before you. They are not hostile.

---

## Movement

- Move as if the floor is covered in sleeping animals—because it is. Check each step if possible. Avoid stepping on eyes and soft seams. Use your wide soles to spread weight.
- Do not chase distant landmarks. The cairn that keeps its distance will never be reached. The standing stone that appears twice is not a trail marker.
- Do not walk in a trance. Vary your pace slightly. Do not fall into the rhythm of the ground tick.
- If your shadow points the wrong way, stop. Use your tether and beacon to reorient. Do not follow it.
- If you see your own footprints ahead of you, do not step in them. Turn around and follow the tether back if it is intact. If not, stay put and activate extraction.

---

## Observation and Interaction

- The air is full of tiny mica-winged creatures. They will part as you move and close behind you. Do not swat them. Do not inhale them. If they begin to collect on your visor, you have been still too long; move.
- The ground creatures: do not touch, do not pry, do not collect. A cracked plate may release non-aqueous fluids that are caustic or oxidizing. If you damage one, withdraw and note it; do not try to repair it.
- The herd-beasts: observe from a distance. Do not walk between a mother and young. Do not touch the salt on their legs. If one seems to shift out of the corner of your eye, do not wait to see if it was real. Leave.
- The plants: do not pull them. Some root into animals.
- The pale things under soil: do not dig. Do not anchor stakes.
- The huts: if you must approach, do not enter. Look from outside. The seated figures are not decoration; they may be alive in some waiting way. Do not touch their tools or necklaces. If you see one move, or if a doorway that was empty now shows a seated figure, withdraw immediately.
- Do not take anything. Not a pebble, not a flint flake, not the copper pin. The place is not a souvenir shop. It is an occupied body.

---

## Rest, Food, and Sleep

- Do not sleep on the plateau. If you feel exhausted, return through the portal or to a sealed base outside. If you cannot exit, take micro-rests standing, leaning on a monopod. Do not close your eyes for more than a few seconds. Set an alarm to rouse you.
- Eat and drink only through mask ports. Do not open your suit in the open air. If you must open your visor, do so inside a sealed tent or back at base.
- Do not sit inside a hut, no matter how shaded or quiet it looks. The huts are not shelters. They are the places where people forget to leave.
- Set a hard time limit. For a first visit, do not exceed four hours inside the plateau’s influence. The fraying begins earlier than you think.

---

## When Things Begin to Fray

You may notice:

- A landmark repeats or stays at the same distance.
- Your footprints appear ahead of you, or your own voice is already on the recorder.
- Shadows point toward animals or toward a place where an animal used to be.
- The herd seems to shift when you are not looking, but is still when observed.
- You find a duplicate of a personal item.
- You feel a deep, peaceful certainty that you should sit down, stay, and become part of the pattern.

**If any of these happen, initiate extraction immediately.**

Do not investigate. Do not try to prove the anomaly. The plateau’s repetition is not a puzzle. It is the first stage of your erasure. The feeling of peace is the most dangerous sign. The place does not need to attack you; it only needs you to hold still long enough.

---

## If Extraction Is Delayed or Fails

- Stay with your tether. Do not follow footprints. Do not enter huts. Do not speak to any figure—especially one that looks like you.
- Stand on your platform if possible. Activate your beacon. Make noise: hum, sing, talk to yourself. Keep moving in small, deliberate ways.
- Write your name on your forearm or suit with a marker. Reapply it every time you check your reality anchor. If you find it already written in another place, do not assume you wrote it.
- Do not eat or drink anything you did not bring. If a seated figure offers you a bowl, refuse. There is no bowl. There is no water.
- If you begin to forget your home, focus on your tether and your mirror. Say your exit phrase. Do not stop moving.

---

## Leaving

- When your timer or dead-man switch triggers, go. Do not linger for one last look. Do not finish a recording. Do not wait for the light to change.
- Follow the physical tether back, not landmarks. If the tether is broken, follow the beacon. If both fail, stay on your platform and call for emergency extraction.
- On the other side, do not remove your suit in the open. Enter a quarantine/decontamination chamber. The dust and the mica-winged creatures may be oxidizing or biologically active. Vacuum off particles first. Do not use water directly on possible perchlorates unless you know it is safe for your gear.
- Quarantine all gear. Do not bring local objects home. If you accidentally bring a pebble, it may be a living animal. Isolate it.
- Expect a difficult re-entry. After the Plateau of the Held Breath, normal time may feel frantic, noisy, wet. You may miss the stillness. That is a residue of the place, not a true preference. Talk to someone. Do not go back because you miss it.

---

## Final Word

You are an ordinary human. You are mostly water. You breathe dozens of times a minute. You shed skin and hair. You move. You want. You change.

The plateau is none of those things.

Do not try to become like it. Your survival depends on remaining exactly what you are: a warm, wet, noisy, finite creature that does not belong there.

Prepare for that, and you may come back still breathing, still blinking, still time-bound.

That is the only victory the place will not erase.
