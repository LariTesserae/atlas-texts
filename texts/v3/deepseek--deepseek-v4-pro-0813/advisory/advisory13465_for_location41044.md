# Preparation for Visiting The Grafted Hollow

Treat this as a preparation guide for a short visit—one to five sleeps. Longer stays require local permission, apprenticeship, and a very different gear list. The Hollow is not a cave, not a forest, not a town, but all three grown together and breathing. An ordinary human can survive and even travel well there, but only if you prepare as if you are entering the inside of a living body: cold, wet, dark, vertical, and aware of you.

---

## 1. First, Understand What You Are Entering

The Grafted Hollow is a single cavity inside the root-mat of a cold peat bog. It is small by city standards—forty or fifty strides across, twenty or thirty high—but it is layered vertically, filled with water, living roots, animals, and a sparse human settlement. The sun never reaches the floor. The air is near freezing and saturated. Water drips, runs, pools, and fog falls several times a day. The walls are not stone; they are living willow, alder, sphagnum, liverwort, and peat. The town is grown, pruned, lashed, and woven rather than built.

The place is quiet. The animals have learned that sound is dangerous. Predators hunt by vibration and patience. The whole hollow breathes through the entrance above, and the plants grow fast enough that the town must be pruned daily or it will close like a wound.

You are not visiting a place. You are visiting a responsive organism with a town living inside it.

## 2. Physical and Mental Preparation

Do not arrive unfit. The Hollow will punish casual preparation.

### Fitness
- **Upper body strength:** Ladders, rope hoists, and single-strand walkways are common. You may need to pull yourself up or arrest a slip.
- **Balance:** Practice walking on narrow, sagging, wet surfaces. A slackline or a wet log over a stream is good training.
- **Grip strength:** Roots are wet, smooth, and sometimes moss-covered. You will hold ropes and handholds for longer than expected.
- **Cold tolerance:** Begin cold-water acclimatization weeks before. Cold showers, cold outdoor swims, and sleeping in a cool room help. The Hollow’s wet cold settles in the bones.
- **Knee and ankle stability:** The floor is a lattice of roots with black water between. One misstep can put a leg through a gap.

### Mental
- Practice moving slowly and quietly. You will need to walk without slapping feet, dropping gear, or rustling fabric.
- Practice stillness. There are moments when freezing in place is the correct response to a predator or to a local warning.
- If you are claustrophobic, reconsider. The Hollow is enclosed, dark, and alive. The fog-fall will sometimes make the space feel as if it is closing in.
- Accept that you will be wet for days. Your hands may never fully dry. Your feet will be cold. Comfort is not the goal; survival and awareness are.

## 3. Medical Preparation

See a travel medicine provider if possible. Describe the environment honestly: stagnant water, animal urine and feces, low temperatures, high humidity, roots, soil-like peat, biting and stinging animals, unknown local microbiota. If your provider dismisses this as impossible, simply describe a cold, wet, tropical-free bog environment with animal contamination.

### Vaccinations
- Tetanus
- Hepatitis A and B
- Rabies pre-exposure, if available
- Typhoid, if the water is suspect
- Consider broad coverage for leptospirosis if a vaccine exists in your world; otherwise rely on prophylactic antibiotics

### Medications to Carry
- **Broad-spectrum antibiotic:** Doxycycline is useful for leptospirosis and many animal-bite infections. Amoxicillin-clavulanate for wound infections. Ciprofloxacin for severe gastrointestinal infection.
- **Antifungal:** Topical clotrimazole or miconazole; oral fluconazole if you have any sign of deep fungal infection.
- **Antiparasitic:** Something broad-spectrum, such as ivermectin or albendazole, if allowed.
- **Antihistamine:** For bites, stings, or allergic reactions.
- **Epinephrine autoinjector:** If you have any history of allergy.
- **Pain and inflammation:** Ibuprofen or similar.
- **Oral rehydration salts.**
- **Anti-diarrheal:** Loperamide for emergencies, but use cautiously.

### Wound Care
- The wet environment makes wounds slow to heal and quick to infect.
- Carry waterproof dressings, medical glue, steri-strips, iodine, saline, and antiseptic.
- Treat every scratch—even a small one—immediately. Cover it with a waterproof seal.
- Foot care is critical. Blisters in cold wet boots become ulcers. Carry mole-skin, tape, and antifungal powder.

## 4. Clothing and Personal Wear

Clothing is your first survival system. The Hollow is near freezing, but the humidity is near total. Cotton is deadly. Everything should retain warmth when wet or dry quickly.

### Layering System
- **Base layer:** Merino wool long underwear and long-sleeve top. No cotton.
- **Mid layer:** Fleece or wool sweater and pants. Quick-dry synthetic insulation works if it does not crinkle loudly.
- **Outer layer:** Waterproof/breathable jacket and trousers. Choose a quiet fabric if possible; many hard-shell jackets rustle. A softshell with good water resistance may be quieter. If you need full rain protection, put a thin waterproof layer over fleece rather than wearing a crinkly jacket.
- **Insulation for stops:** A synthetic insulated jacket or vest. Down is fine if kept dry, but synthetic is safer in this humidity.

### Footwear
- High-top waterproof boots with a sticky, aggressive but not rigid sole. You need grip on wet roots, but also enough flex to feel the surface.
- Waterproof socks or vapor-barrier socks inside the boots. Bring multiple pairs.
- Gaiters to keep water and debris out of the boot tops.
- Lightweight camp shoes are mostly useless—everything is wet. But a pair of thin neoprene socks can be worn inside a dwelling.

### Head and Hands
- Wool or fleece beanie.
- Neck gaiter or balaclava.
- Face net for the midges. The white midges drift without buzzing, but they may still be a nuisance.
- Helmet with a headlamp mount. The ceiling is living and sometimes drops debris; the risk of falling roots and water is constant.
- Gloves: bring at least three pairs.
  - Thin liner gloves for dexterity.
  - Insulated gloves for cold.
  - Waterproof grip gloves for rope work and climbing.
- Knee pads and elbow pads. You will be kneeling, crawling, and bracing against roots.

### Colors
Choose dark, natural tones—browns, deep greens, charcoal. Avoid bright colors, reflective strips, and white. The Hollow is dark, and the animals rely on stillness and camouflage. You do not want to be a bright, moving, alarming thing.

## 5. Equipment List

### Light
Light is scarce. Do not bring a blinding white beam.

- **Primary headlamp:** Red LED and a very dim white setting. Red preserves your dark adaptation and is less alarming to animals.
- **Secondary light:** A small handheld torch with a blue-green filter or LED that mimics the local eel-star glow. This is less disruptive than white light.
- **Chem lights:** A few low-glow green or blue sticks for marking your sleeping area or emergency.
- **Spare batteries:** In waterproof cases. Cold and humidity drain batteries.
- **No open flame.** The walls are alive and can burn poorly but can burn. Do not bring candles, oil lamps, or fire-based light unless you are absolutely forced and the locals permit it.

### Shelter and Sleeping
The floor is wet and uneven. There is little flat ground. A hammock is the best option.

- **Hammock** with wide, flat tree straps to avoid cutting into living roots.
- **Underquilt** for insulation beneath you.
- **Waterproof tarp** to deflect dripping water.
- **Bug net** for midges and other small life.
- **Sleeping bag:** Synthetic insulation, not down, unless you are extremely confident in your dry-bag system.
- **Vapor-barrier liner** or waterproof bivy sack to keep your sleeping bag dry.

### Food and Water
- **No-cook food:** High-calorie bars, nuts, dried meat or fish, hard cheese, chocolate, nut butters. You may not be able to use a stove at all.
- **Insulated flask:** Fill with hot water or broth before entering. It will stay warm for hours.
- **Water purification:** Bring a filter and purification tablets. Treat all water before drinking. Do not assume the black water is safe.
- **Electrolyte tablets.**
- **Collapsible water bottles or a hydration bladder.**
- **Chemical hand warmers** can double as a way to warm food or drink.

### Safety and Climbing
- Lightweight rope or accessory cord, at least 20–30 meters.
- Several locking carabiners and slings.
- A simple climbing harness or the materials to improvise one.
- Prusik loops for ascending a rope.
- A trekking pole or staff with a rubber tip. Use it to probe water depth, test walkways, and feel the ground before committing weight.

### Air Quality
The bog breathes, and the lower sump may have pockets of low oxygen, methane, or hydrogen sulfide.

- **Portable multi-gas detector** for O2, CO, H2S, and CH4. A small one with an alarm is ideal.
- If you feel dizzy, nauseated, or short of breath, move upward and toward the entrance. Do not descend into low areas during fog-fall.

### Tools and Repair
- A small, sharp knife.
- A multi-tool.
- Needle, heavy thread, sinew, and cord for repairs.
- Duct tape wrapped around a trekking pole or water bottle.
- A small saw is tempting, but avoid carrying anything that looks like you intend to cut the living root-mass. If you must have one for emergency, keep it small and hidden, and never use it except to save life.

### Medical Kit
Already covered, but pack it carefully:
- Waterproof dressings
- Iodine or antiseptic
- Medical glue and steri-strips
- Antibiotics
- Antifungals
- Antiparasitics
- Painkillers
- Antihistamine
- Blister care
- Thermal blanket
- Heat packs
- Splint
- Saline
- Oral rehydration salts
- Tick/leech remover

### Waste and Hygiene
- Sealable plastic bags for all waste. Pack everything out.
- Do not wash in the pools.
- Do not use scented soap or shampoo anywhere near the Hollow.
- A small quick-dry towel.
- Hand sanitizer.
- Toilet supplies. Use a wag bag or equivalent, and remove all human waste.

### Do Not Bring
- Open-flame stoves or candles
- Drones
- Loudspeakers or bright white lights
- Strong perfumes or colognes
- Crinkly plastic packaging—repack into waxed canvas or dry bags
- Spiked boots or heavy hard-soled boots
- Machetes, axes, or firearms
- Seeds, untreated plant material, or foreign soil on your gear

## 6. Approaching and Entering

The Hollow is hidden. You will not find it by looking for a doorway. From outside, it is only a shallow depression where the peat has slumped, the ground gives slightly, and the snow melts early.

### Finding It
- Look for a ring of melted frost on a cold day, or a faint rise of warmer air.
- Notice the absence of wind and the sudden smell of animals: copper, peat, and something animal-sweet.
- A thermal monocle or camera is extremely useful here. It will show the warm breath of the Hollow rising from the depression.

### Approaching
- Wear snowshoes, bog boards, or some way to distribute your weight. The bog above is unstable.
- Probe the ground with a pole before each step. The collapse is recent and the edges are raw and black.
- Move slowly and quietly. Sound travels oddly.

### At the Entrance
- Do not descend immediately. Stop at the lip and announce yourself softly if you have a guide or local contact. If not, wait. The people of the Hollow will likely know you are there long before you see them.
- Anchor a rope outside if possible. Use a deadman anchor or a large, protected living root. Do not rely on the bog surface.
- Descend facing the wall. The descent is short, steep, and newly made. The walls are sharp, torn roots, some still bleeding sap. Gloves and long sleeves are essential.
- Assume the sap is an irritant. Do not touch broken roots with bare skin.

## 7. Living Inside the Hollow

### Movement and Noise
- Move slowly. Step softly. Do not drop gear.
- Use hand signals if traveling with companions.
- Stay on established walkways and platforms. The locals know which roots will hold weight and which will bend.
- Single-strand walkways sag over the water. Use a safety line if possible, and cross one person at a time.
- Do not run, even in fright. Many predators respond to vibration and sudden movement.

### Fire and Light
- Do not light any fire. The walls are living plant matter. They can burn, and a fire would be catastrophic.
- Keep your lights dim. Use red or blue-green light.
- Do not flash lights into the water or at animals.
- If you must have a small contained stove, ask local permission. Even then, use it on a fireproof mat, away from roots, and only during a safe time. It is better to eat cold food.

### Water and Fog-Fall
- Learn the fog-fall schedule from the locals. Fog-fall happens several times a day: warm animal breath rises, meets cold air, and sinks down the walls in slow gray waves.
- During fog-fall, the eel-wyrms migrate upward, moths drift in sheets, and water levels change. Do not walk the lower sump alone during fog-fall.
- Never wade into black pools unless you must. If you must, test with a staff. The water may be deeper than it looks, and there may be hidden gaps in the root floor.
- Treat all water before drinking. Avoid getting water in your mouth or eyes.

### Plants and Roots
- Do not cut, prune, or rearrange living roots. The town is grown, not built, and the locals prune it daily with knowledge you do not have.
- Roots grow fast—some lengthen by a hand’s width in a single sleep. Do not leave gear resting against roots for long. Hang everything.
- There is one root at the center that hums faintly on certain nights. Do not touch it, prune it, or even approach it too closely. The locals will warn you. Heed the warning.
- Moss may grow through shells and create handholds. These are usually strong, but test before trusting.
- Some fungi are pollinated by animal fur. Do not scrape or collect them.

### Animals
- The Hollow is full of animals, most of them hidden. Assume everything is watching you.
- Do not feed, touch, or corner any animal.
- Avoid nests. They are tucked into every fork.
- The pale, long-bodied predators that wait motionless are nearly indistinguishable from roots. If you see one, freeze. Do not make eye contact. Back away slowly.
- If an animal attacks and you must kill it, follow local law: give every slain animal’s blood back to the moss. The moss remembers. Tell the locals what happened.

### Hygiene and Waste
- Cover all wounds with waterproof dressings.
- Pack out all waste, including food scraps, bandages, and human waste.
- Do not wash your hands or gear in the pools.
- Use hand sanitizer after touching shared ropes or walkways.

### Cultural Etiquette
- The Hollow is a town, and the town is an organ of a single responsive organism. Behave as a guest, not as a researcher or tourist.
- Ask permission before touching anything that might be a structure, a pruned root, or a dwelling.
- Do not take photographs or recordings without permission. The light and sound may disturb both people and animals.
- Useful gifts for the locals include copper needles, bone awls, sinew, good cordage, and woven cloth. They already have stone, antler, bone, and a little hammered copper. Avoid bringing salt, sugar, or any strong-smelling substance. Avoid plastic if possible.
- Do not introduce foreign plants, seeds, soil, or microbes. Decontaminate your gear before entering and after leaving.
- Learn a few local words or signs for “help,” “danger,” “water,” “food,” and “I mean no harm.”

## 8. Hazards and Emergency Response

| Hazard | Signs | Response |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **Hypothermia** | Shivering, confusion, slow movements, slurred speech | Change into dry clothing, use heat packs, drink warm fluids, seek local shelter, wrap in thermal blanket |
| **Cold water immersion** | Shock, gasping, rapid cooling | Get out immediately, strip wet clothing, dry skin, warm core, do not rub limbs, seek warmth |
| **Falls** | Injury, pain, inability to bear weight | Stabilize injury, keep warm, signal for help, do not move unless necessary |
| **Wound infection** | Redness, swelling, heat, pus, fever | Clean with iodine, cover waterproof, start antibiotics |
| **Predator encounter** | Still shape, eye shine, low growl or no sound | Freeze, avoid eye contact, back away slowly, use staff as barrier, do not run |
| **Low oxygen / gas** | Dizziness, nausea, headache, gas alarm | Move upward and toward entrance, follow airflow, do not descend |
| **Disorientation in fog** | Loss of direction, panic | Stay put, use cord to retrace, use dim light, wait for fog to thin |
| **Root growth / entrapment** | Tightening root, trapped limb | Do not panic; try to ease free; call for help; cut only in life-threatening emergency |

## 9. Leaving and Decontamination

Leave as quietly as you entered. Remove all gear and waste. Do not take souvenirs, seeds, cuttings, or live animals. If a local gives you a gift, accept it graciously, but ensure it is clean and safe to carry between worlds.

After leaving:

- Brush and clean boots and gear away from the bog.
- Disinfect gear with a mild disinfectant if possible.
- Check your body for cuts, rashes, or embedded parasites.
- Monitor your health for at least three weeks. Report any fever, rash, cough, or lethargy to a medical provider trained in unusual environments.

## 11. What It Will Feel Like

You will descend, and the first thing you notice is that the cold changes. It becomes wetter, closer. The air smells of copper, peat, and warm animal. The sound is dripping—constant, without rhythm. The dark is blue-gray, not black, at least at first.

Your breath will fog. You will feel watched. You may not see the animals, but you will feel them holding still. The silence is not empty. It is full of small held breaths.

When the fog-fall comes, it will feel as if the room is sinking. The warm breath of the Hollow rises, meets the cold, and sinks down the walls in slow gray waves. The eel-wyrms move upward. The people open sluices. You will hear water moving, not see it.

You will learn to move slowly. Your hands will be wet for days. Your feet may never dry. Your eyes will adjust until you see more in the twilight than you thought possible. You will begin to feel vibrations through the roots before you see the animal that made them.

The town will appear as small lights at different heights—flames caged in wet reed, eel-star fungus glowing blue-green. The locals will likely see you long before you see them. Let them come to you.

The Hollow is not a place to conquer. It is a place to be tolerated by. Prepare to be a polite guest inside something alive.
