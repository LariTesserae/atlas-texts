Visiting the Solventless Garden is less like hiking and more like making a short, heavily protected entry into a hot, electrically active, waterless cleanroom embedded in an unstable pocket of reality. Preparation should assume that every normal human need—breathing, sweating, drinking, urinating, touching—is a possible contamination or hazard.

The single most important fact: **you are a walking bag of warm salt water entering a place that has no water in any form.** That fact drives most decisions.

---

## 1. Do not go alone

This is not a place for a solo traveler, no matter how experienced.

- Have at least one person outside the entrance crack, holding the safety line and monitoring time, radio or rope signals, and environmental alarms.
- Two outside attendants are better: one to tend the tether, one to manage emergency extraction and medical support.
- Do not rely on multiversal travel, teleportation, or dimensional shifting from inside the pocket. The local reality is not fixed enough. Plan to exit physically, on foot, through the crack you entered.

Before entering, establish a clear time limit. For an ordinary human in this environment, **30 to 60 minutes inside** is a sensible maximum. Set that intention before you enter.

---

## 2. Acclimatization and physical readiness

The Solventless Garden sits high on a raw ridge. The air is thin, hot, and brutally dry. You are dealing with altitude and heat stress at the same time.

Preparation should include:

- High-altitude acclimatization for at least several days before the approach.
- Heat training while wearing similar protective gear, so you know how your body reacts.
- Physical fitness for moving carefully on sharp glassy rock while carrying a heavy sealed suit and breathing apparatus.
- Medical screening: no pacemakers, insulin pumps, or other implanted electronics. The magnetite mats and electric fields may interfere.
- Dental and skin check: avoid exposed metal fillings, braces, piercings, or jewelry. Metal can interact with static charge and heat.

Do not enter if you are ill, dehydrated, or sleep-deprived. This place will not accommodate a compromised human.

---

## 3. Water and moisture discipline

The garden is solventless. No liquid water, no humidity, no frost. Your breath and sweat are water vapor. If you breathe or sweat into the chamber, you become the first moisture source the place has ever known.

Preparation must include:

- A fully encapsulating suit with a true vapor barrier. No exposed skin, no loose cuffs, no open seams.
- A closed-circuit breathing apparatus or full-face respirator with a moisture trap on exhalation.
- If you must drink inside, use a sealed bite valve connected to a bladder inside the suit. Never open a bottle or canteen in the chamber.
- A urine collection system is prudent even for a short visit. Do not urinate on the ground.
- No water-based lotions, wet wipes, lip balm, or perfumes on the outside of your suit.
- Pre-hydrate and void before suiting up.
- After exit, do all drinking, washing, spitting, and urinating well away from the entrance.

Assume that any moisture you release could alter the mineral growth, create a short-lived dew point, or simply violate the quiet physical rules of the place. Treat your own breath as contamination.

---

## 4. Thermal and respiratory protection

The floor is black volcanic glass and the rock gives heat back like a bell gives sound. The air is thin high-elevation air but feels thick with heat. Light falls hard and sharp.

Thermal gear should include:

- An aluminized or reflective outer suit to shed radiant heat.
- A cooling vest using non-water phase-change packs or a small active cooling loop. Water as coolant is risky; if a pouch leaks, you have introduced water.
- Insulated, heat-resistant, cut-resistant boots with non-metallic toes.
- Cut- and heat-resistant gloves that still allow tether handling and mask adjustment.
- A full-face helmet with an IR-reflective or heavily tinted visor. The light is hard, the shadows are black and clean as cut metal, and the glare from obsidian may be intense.

Respiratory protection should include:

- A closed-circuit rebreather if possible, because it handles both altitude and moisture.
- If using a filter mask, use cartridges rated for ozone, acid gases, sulfur dioxide, and particulates. But know that a filter mask still releases your exhaled moisture into the air.
- A multi-gas monitor for oxygen, ozone, sulfur dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, and carbon monoxide, with visual alarms.
- Emergency oxygen for exit if needed.

If the gas alarm sounds, do not hesitate. Leave along the tether immediately.

---

## 5. Physical protection against the sharp terrain

The floor is unweathered volcanic glass. Obsidian can be sharper than surgical steel. The mineral fronds and silica spines are equally unforgiving.

Preparation should include:

- Cut-resistant trousers or chaps.
- Knee pads and shin guards.
- Puncture-resistant, heat-resistant soles.
- Overboots to avoid shedding debris into the chamber.
- A non-shedding, non-conductive mat to stand or kneel on if you need to rest or set down equipment.

Move as if the ground is broken glass. Test each foot before weight. Do not drag your feet. Do not brush against copper fronds, bismuth blooms, magnetite mats, or silica rosettes. Many of these may be sharp, hot, or electrically charged.

---

## 6. Electrical, magnetic, and fire safety

The Solventless Garden is an electrostatic environment. Piezoelectric quartz ticks under stress. Static charges jump between crystal spines. The air tastes of copper and ozone. Some fronds grow like drawn wire pulled from vapor by electric fields.

Preparation should include:

- An anti-static or static-dissipative outer layer.
- No loose metal objects, metal zippers, metal-frame glasses, or metal jewelry.
- Non-ferromagnetic, non-conductive tools and carabiners where possible.
- Electronics hardened and shielded against static discharge and magnetic fields.
- No open flame, no spark-producing tools, no unprotected batteries. Sulfur vapor and sulfur dust are flammable. The place already has natural static discharges; do not add ignition sources.

Do not touch the mineral fronds. Some may carry charge, and you may become the first grounding path they meet.

A compass may fail near the magnetite mats. Do not rely on it. GPS and radio may also be unreliable.

## 7. Tether, navigation, and reality discipline

The most insidious hazard of the Solventless Garden is that distance and time are unreliable.

The far wall may seem to recede. The chamber may sometimes feel like a room and sometimes like a valley. Shadows may lie in two directions at once, or arrive before the object that casts them. Sounds may come out of order: the crack before the cooling, the hum before the wind. Two versions of the same space may appear to overlap.

Your only reliable spatial fact is the physical tether running from your harness to the outside anchor.

Prepare with:

- A low-stretch, heat-resistant, cut-resistant safety line long enough for the chamber but short enough to prevent wandering.
- A full-body harness.
- Two tethers if possible.
- A battery-powered strobe and passive radar reflector at the entrance.
- Edge protectors for the tether where it runs over sharp rock.
- A second independent line as backup.

Once inside:

- Keep the tether taut or in hand at all times.
- Do not trust apparent distance. If the far wall recedes and the place starts to feel like a valley, treat that as a perceptual trap. The physical dimensions are about thirty strides long and twelve wide.
- Do not follow the receding wall.
- Do not chase a shadow that arrives before its object.
- Do not investigate a sound that has not happened yet.
- If you see another version of yourself or your path, do not approach, do not call out, do not make eye contact. Avert your eyes, stay on the tether, and withdraw slowly along the line.

Time may also be unreliable. Bring two or three independent shielded timers. Set them before entry. When the first timer reaches the agreed limit, leave—no matter how little time seems to have passed.

## 8. Leave no trace

The Solventless Garden is unweathered. It accretes; it does not erode. A footprint, a scratch, or a dropped hair may persist indefinitely.

Before entering:

- Clean all gear with dry, lint-free methods. No water.
- Remove loose fibers and debris from clothing and equipment.
- Use shoe covers or overboots, but do not rely on them for grip on glass.
- Do not apply adhesives or tapes to the rock.

Inside:

- Do not collect samples.
- Do not break fronds.
- Do not carve or scratch the rock.
- Do not spit, shed, or place bare hands on anything.
- Carry out all waste, including moisture traps, suit tape, batteries, and any fibers that may separate from your gear.

Treat every copper frond, bismuth bloom, and silica rosette as a natural artifact of geological time. You are a visitor, not a collector.

---

## 9. Emergency contingencies

The place is quiet but not forgiving. Plan for the worst before entering.

- **Lost tether:** Stop immediately. Do not try to walk to the walls. Sit or stand on a protective mat, activate your beacon and strobe, and wait for the outside attendant to stabilize the line. The chamber is physically small; a stationary person is easier to recover.
- **Heat illness:** Begin exit immediately. The outside attendant should have shade, water, and cooling ready. Do not tough it out.
- **Laceration:** Use a hemostatic dressing and direct pressure. Exit immediately. An open wound will dry rapidly and contaminate the site.
- **Gas or ozone alarm:** Stop, signal the outside, and leave along the tether. Use oxygen if available.
- **Electrical shock:** If someone is in contact with a charged frond, do not touch them directly. Use a non-conductive tool to break contact, then begin extraction and CPR if needed.
- **Sulfur thread encounter:** If you step near the molten sulfur thread, back away slowly. Do not touch it. Treat any contact as a molten-sulfur burn.

## 10. Mental preparation

The Solventless Garden will likely feel profoundly alien.

There are no birds, no insects, no animal calls. The quiet is broken only by tick of cooling stone, the click of static discharge, the faint hiss of hot air moving through narrow flues, and a low hum that is sometimes felt more than heard.

The vegetation is mineral: copper fronds, bismuth blooms, silica spines, magnetite mats. It looks like a garden, but it is an industrial geology, a factory without a builder.

You may feel awe, unease, dread, or a strange grief. Absence of water and animals can feel like absence of life. The silence may press on you. There may be moments when the place seems beautiful to the point of being overwhelming.

Prepare mentally:

- Practice simple, procedural calm: stop, look at the tether, check instruments, move deliberately.
- Do not let curiosity override the time limit.
- If the place starts to feel like it is looking back at you, or like the hum is speech, do not interpret it. Call yourself back to your breath, your tether, your task.
- Accept that some phenomena will not make sense. You are not there to solve the Solventless Garden. You are there to enter, observe briefly, and exit without harming it or yourself.

---

## 11. Post-visit routine

After you exit through the crack:

- De-suit in a clean area outside.
- Bag the entire suit, gloves, boot covers, and moisture traps.
- Do not shake dust into the air.
- Rehydrate, cool down, and perform a medical check: heat, lungs, eyes, skin, ears.
- Write a report immediately. Note any time contradictions, sound order errors, impossible shadows, or fronds that seemed older than the rock. These are not hallucinations; they are part of the site’s condition.
- Expect a period of mild derealization afterward. Ordinary moisture, wind, and animal life may feel sudden and loud. Allow time for your mind to settle.

---

The Solventless Garden is not hostile, but it is utterly indifferent. You are a soft, water-filled animal entering a dry electrostatic assembly line. Preparation is mostly about not being the first water leak, the first spark, the first cut, or the first lost traveler. The place will not accommodate you; you must fit yourself to it without leaving a trace.
