# Preparation Guide for the Salt-Worn City

## Preliminary Assessment

You are not preparing for a wilderness expedition. You are preparing for a place that has outlived its own purpose and become something closer to a physical law. The city does not want anything from you. It will not notice you. This is the most dangerous thing about it.

The primary risks, in order of likelihood:

1. **Conceptual disorientation** — the erosion of your ability to distinguish real from remembered, present from past, here from there
2. **Dehydration** — the air will pull moisture from you without your noticing; you will not feel thirsty until it is too late
3. **Spatial displacement** — becoming lost in a geometry that does not obey continuity
4. **Exposure** — the cold is not dramatic, but it is constant and dry
5. **Psychological attrition** — the emptiness is not neutral; it is *complete*

---

## Mental Preparation (Do This First, Do It Thoroughly)

### Accept the following before you depart:

**You are not an explorer here. You are a visitor in a place that has no concept of visitors.** The city was not built for beings like you. It was not built for anyone you would recognize. Do not expect to understand it. Do not try to decode it. Your goal is to pass through, observe, and return with your mind intact.

**The city will not harm you deliberately.** It has no intention. But intention is not required for harm. A stone does not intend to fall.

**You will feel that you are being watched.** You are not. The city is empty. The feeling is a byproduct of scale and silence. Remind yourself of this often.

**You will feel that you are being forgotten.** This is closer to true. The city erodes memory—not yours, but the world's memory of you. You may find yourself checking your own name, your own face, to confirm you still exist. This is normal. Do not panic. Panic is a door.

### Train yourself in the following disciplines:

**Non-attachment to maps.** Practice navigating by dead reckoning in unfamiliar terrain. Then practice navigating when your dead reckoning is wrong. Learn to hold a direction without believing in it.

**Temporal patience.** The city's time is not your time. A walk that should take ten minutes may take three hours. A walk that should take three hours may take ten minutes. You cannot control this. You can only avoid making decisions based on expected arrival times.

**Shadow discipline.** Before you go, spend time in places with strong light and sharp shadows. Learn to notice when a shadow is wrong—wrong direction, wrong length, wrong darkness. Train yourself to *not look directly* at shadows that are wrong. Peripheral awareness only. Direct attention gives them weight.

**Echo skepticism.** If you hear a sound, do not assume it has a source. If you hear your own footsteps, do not assume they are yours. If you hear a bell, there is no bell. Accept the sound and continue.

## Physical Preparation

### Conditioning

The air is thin. Not mountain-thin—*old* thin, as if the atmosphere itself has been worn smooth. You will tire faster than you expect, but you will not notice because the cold masks exertion. Train for endurance at altitude. Train for walking on stone. Train for stairs—there will be many stairs, and some of them will not be where they were when you started climbing.

### Acclimatization

If possible, spend time in high desert environments before departure. The dryness is the key variable. Your body must learn to conserve moisture without your conscious intervention. Practice recognizing the early signs of dehydration: the specific headache, the specific irritability, the way your thoughts begin to loop.

### Medical Considerations

- **Skin:** The air will crack your skin. Bring a heavy, oil-based balm. Apply it to your hands, face, and lips frequently. The salt in the air will find every small wound and make it sting.
- **Eyes:** The dust is fine and constant. Bring sealed goggles, not open glasses. Bring saline drops. Do not rub your eyes with dusty hands.
- **Lungs:** The dust is not toxic in the conventional sense, but it is ancient and fine. A simple cloth mask is sufficient. You are not protecting yourself from poison; you are protecting yourself from the weight of geological time entering your lungs.
- **Teeth:** The salt will coat everything. Rinse your mouth with water when you can. Do not swallow the salt.

## Equipment

### Water

This is your most critical resource. There is no water in the city. None. You must carry all of it.

- **Quantity:** Calculate your normal desert requirement, then double it. Then add a third again. The city's dryness is not like desert dryness. It is *absolute*. You will lose moisture through your breath, your skin, your eyes. You will not sweat because the sweat evaporates before it forms. You will not feel thirsty because the thirst response is calibrated for a world with humidity.
- **Containers:** Use multiple small containers, not one large one. If one fails, you lose a fraction, not everything. Metal or thick plastic. The salt will corrode metal slowly; plastic is better. Insulated containers are ideal—the water will not freeze, but it will grow cold, and cold water is harder to drink in quantity.
- **Discipline:** Drink on a schedule, not on thirst. Every hour, a measured amount. Do not ration too severely—dehydration will impair your judgment, and impaired judgment is fatal here. But do not drink freely—you cannot resupply.

### Food

High-calorie, low-moisture, low-residue. You do not want to need to defecate in the city. The city will not mind, but the act of leaving organic matter in a place with no organic matter is... inadvisable. It creates a connection. It gives the stone something to remember.

- Dried meats, hard cheeses, dense breads, nuts, dried fruits. Avoid anything that creates crumbs. Crumbs are marks. The stone remembers marks.
- Eat sparingly. Your body will be cold and dry; digestion will be slow. Overeating will make you sluggish.

### Clothing

The cold is dry and constant. The wind is light but persistent. The dust is fine and pervasive.

- **Layers:** A base layer of wool or synthetic that wicks moisture (you will sweat, even if you don't feel it). A mid-layer of insulation. An outer layer that is windproof and dust-resistant. The outer layer should be light-colored—the city is dim, and you want to be visible to yourself.
- **Footwear:** Sturdy boots with good ankle support. The streets are stone, uneven, and sometimes not where they appear to be. Your boots should be broken in before you arrive. Blisters are marks. Marks are remembered.
- **Gloves:** The stone is cold and sometimes sharp. The salt crusts are crystalline and can cut. Wear gloves at all times.
- **Head covering:** A hood or hat that can be pulled low. The light, such as it is, comes from all directions and none. A brim will not help. You want something that reduces your field of vision slightly, that creates a sense of enclosure. This is psychological as much as practical.

### Navigation

- **Compass:** Bring one. It may or may not work. The city's magnetic field is... unreliable. But when it works, it is a fixed point. When it doesn't, it is a reminder that fixed points are not guaranteed.
- **Map:** Do not bring a map. If you bring a map, you will use it. If you use it, it will begin to disagree with the city. Then the city will agree with the map. Then you will not be where you are.
- **Marking:** Do not leave marks. No chalk, no string, no cairns, no scratches. The stone remembers marks and grows around them. If you mark a wall, the wall will remember you. You do not want to be remembered by the walls.
- **Landmarks:** Use the towers. They are the most stable features. But remember that "stable" is relative. A tower that looks ten minutes away may take an hour to reach, or three steps. Use landmarks as references, not as destinations.

### Light

The city is dim, but not dark. The sky is a permanent twilight, the color of tarnished pewter. You will be able to see, but not well. Shadows are deep and sometimes wrong.

- **Flashlight:** Bring one, with spare batteries. Use it sparingly. Light attracts attention, and while there is nothing to attract, the act of creating light in a place that has not seen light in an impossible span is... significant. It changes things. It makes you visible to the city.
- **Candle:** Bring one, as a backup. The flame will tell you things about the air—if it gutters, the wind is shifting; if it burns blue, the air is thin; if it goes out, leave.
- **Do not use light to look at shadows.** If you see a shadow that is wrong, do not illuminate it. Do not confirm it. Confirmation is a form of belief. Belief is a form of weight.

### Shelter

You will need to sleep. The city is cold at night, and the cold is not the kind you can ignore.

- **Tent:** A simple, low-profile tent. Not for weather—there is no weather—but for enclosure. The psychological benefit of a defined space, a space that is *yours*, is immense. It is a small act of resistance against the city's emptiness.
- **Sleeping bag:** Rated for cold. The temperature drops enough for salts to crystallize out of the air. You do not want to be exposed to that.
- **Ground pad:** The stone is hard and cold. Insulate yourself from it.
- **Location:** Sleep in the open, not in a room. Rooms in the city are not always rooms. Some are larger on the inside. Some are only the memory of rooms. Sleep in the street, in a wide space, where you can see the sky. The sky is the only thing that is reliably above you.

### Tools

- **Knife:** A simple, sturdy knife. Not for defense—there is nothing to defend against—but for practical tasks. Cutting food, trimming bandages, prying at things. Do not use it to mark the stone.
- **Rope:** Lightweight cord. Useful for securing gear, for creating a perimeter around your camp, for lowering yourself into places you should not go. Do not use it to mark your path.
- **First aid kit:** Comprehensive. The city will not injure you, but you will injure yourself. Twisted ankles on uneven stone. Cuts from salt crusts. Headaches from the thin air. Blisters from walking. Treat every injury promptly. An untreated injury is a mark. The stone remembers marks.

### Documentation

- **Journal:** Bring one. Write in it. The act of writing is an act of self-confirmation. It reminds you that you exist, that you have a perspective, that you are not part of the city's slow geology. Write in pencil, not ink. Ink can freeze. Pencil is also easier to erase, and you may want to erase some things.
- **Do not take photographs.** The city does not photograph well. The images will be wrong—not blurry, but *wrong*, as if the camera saw something other than what you saw. And the act of photographing is an act of framing, of selecting, of saying "this is what I saw." The city does not like to be framed. It will resist. The photographs will resist. Leave the camera behind.

## Rules of Conduct

These are not suggestions. These are the difference between returning and becoming part of the city's slow chemistry.

### 1. Do not enter a doorway that is darker than the room behind it.

This is the most important rule. The city has many doorways. Some lead to rooms. Some lead to other places. Some lead to the memory of rooms. Some lead to nothing at all. You can tell the difference by the darkness. A doorway that is darker than the room behind it is not a doorway. It is a threshold. Do not cross it.

### 2. Do not trust echoes.

The city's acoustics are wrong. Sounds arrive before their causes, or after, or not at all. If you hear your own footsteps, they may not be yours. If you hear a voice, it is not a voice. If you hear a bell, there is no bell. Accept the sound and continue. Do not investigate. Investigation is a form of belief.

### 3. Do not follow shadows.

Shadows in the city do not always point away from the light. Sometimes they pool in the wrong direction. Sometimes they linger after the light has moved on. Sometimes they have weight. Do not follow them. Do not step on them, if you can avoid it. Do not look at them directly. Peripheral awareness only.

### 4. Do not leave marks.

No scratches, no chalk, no string, no cairns, no fires, no blood, no urine, no feces, no footprints if you can avoid it. The stone remembers marks and grows around them. If you mark the stone, the stone will remember you. You do not want to be remembered.

### 5. Do not take anything.

The city has no artifacts. No metal, no timber, no glass, no rope, no writing. But there are stones, and salt crystals, and mineral growths. Do not take them. They are not souvenirs. They are parts of the city. If you take them, they will take something from you. You will not know what until it is gone.

### 6. Do not give anything.

No offerings, no gifts, no sacrifices. The city does not want anything from you. It does not want you. The act of giving creates a relationship. You do not want a relationship with the city.

### 7. Do not name things.

The city has no names. The towers, the streets, the plazas—they are unnamed. If you name them, you create a connection. You make them yours. They are not yours. They are not anyone's. They are themselves, and they have been themselves for so long that they have become a law of the landscape. Do not try to make them yours.

### 8. Do not pray.

This is not a religious prohibition. It is a practical one. Prayer is a form of address. It assumes a listener. The city is not a listener. It is not a presence. It is an absence, a completion, a mature form. If you pray, you will be praying to the emptiness. The emptiness will not answer. But the act of praying will change you. It will make you smaller. It will make you part of the city's slow chemistry.

### 9. Do not stay too long.

There is no fixed limit. Some travelers can spend days in the city and return unchanged. Others are lost after an hour. The difference is not in the traveler, but in the city's attention. The city does not pay attention, but sometimes it *notices*. If you feel that you are being noticed, leave. Leave immediately. Do not pack. Do not finish your water. Do not look back. Leave.

### 10. If you become lost, stop.

Do not wander. Do not search. Do not call out. Sit down in the widest open space you can find. Wait. The city's geometry is not stable, but it is not random. It shifts by inches and degrees. If you wait, the city will shift around you. Eventually, a street will open that leads somewhere you recognize. Or it won't. But wandering will only make it worse. Wandering is a form of panic. Panic is a door.

## The Creatures

### The Glass Loper

You may see one. You may not. They are rare, and they are not interested in you. They are tall, thin, pale, with too many joints and a body like a folded kite. They move slowly, deliberately, scraping salt blooms from the stone with a hard tongue.

**If you see one:** Stop. Do not approach. Do not make noise. Do not try to photograph it. Observe from a distance. The loper will ignore you. It has been ignoring things like you for longer than you have existed.

**If it approaches you:** It won't. But if it does, do not run. Running is a form of prey behavior. Stand still. Let it pass. It is not interested in you. You are not food. You are not a threat. You are not anything.

### The Quiet

You will not see one. If you see one, it is already too late.

The Quiet looks like a fallen column until it moves. It makes no sound. It is the only predator of the glass loper, and its hunts are not chases but relationships played out over years. It is not interested in you. You are too small, too fast, too loud. But if you see one, if you *recognize* one, you have drawn its attention. And its attention is a slow thing, a patient thing, a thing that will follow you for years.

**If you see a fallen column that was not there before:** Do not look at it. Do not confirm it. Walk away. Do not run. Running is a form of recognition. Walk away slowly, deliberately, as if you have not seen anything. As if you are not there.

## The Experience

You will not enjoy this. That is not the point.

The city is not beautiful in any conventional sense. It is not sublime. It is not awe-inspiring. It is *complete*. It has been itself for so long that it has become a law of the landscape. Visiting it is not an adventure. It is a confrontation with a kind of time that has nothing to do with you.

You will feel small. This is appropriate. You will feel insignificant. This is accurate. You will feel that you are being erased. This is the city's slow chemistry, working on you. Resist it. Not by fighting, but by remembering. Remember your name. Remember your home. Remember the people who love you. These are the things the city cannot touch. These are the things that make you more than a mark on the stone.

When you leave—if you leave—you will be changed. Not in any dramatic way. You will not return with powers or wisdom or secrets. You will return with a deeper understanding of emptiness. You will return with a new appreciation for water, for warmth, for the simple fact of being alive in a world that is not yet complete.

And you will return with a new fear. Not of the city—the city is not frightening. It is *indifferent*. And indifference, true indifference, is more frightening than any malice. The city does not hate you. It does not love you. It does not know you exist. And it will continue to not know you exist for an impossible span of time, long after you are gone, long after your world is gone, long after the plateau has eroded to a plain and the plain to a salt desert and the salt desert to a set of streets that no one walks.

This is the gift of the city. It puts things in perspective. It reminds you that you are small, and brief, and that this is not a tragedy. It is simply the truth.

- [ ] You have accepted that you may not return.
- [ ] You have accepted that the city does not care about either of these things.
- [ ] You have enough water. (You do not. Bring more.)
- [ ] You have no maps.
- [ ] You have no camera.
- [ ] You have no intention of leaving marks.
- [ ] You have no intention of taking anything.
- [ ] You have no intention of giving anything.
- [ ] You have remembered your name.
- [ ] You have remembered your home.
- [ ] You have remembered the people who love you.
- [ ] You are ready to be small.

## A Final Word

The city begins before it announces itself. You will see it from the western rim: a standing line of dark forms in grey half-light, too regular to be natural, too ruined to be alive. You will drop into the wide basin, and the forms will resolve into streets, towers, bridges, and plazas. You will understand that you are entering a place that has been urban for so long its urbanity has become a kind of geology.

You will be afraid. This is appropriate.

Walk in. Walk carefully. Walk with your eyes open and your mind closed. Do not try to understand. Do not try to remember. Do not try to forget.

Just walk.

And when it is time to leave, leave. Do not look back. The city will not notice your departure. It will not notice your arrival. It will not notice you at all.

This is the only mercy it offers.
