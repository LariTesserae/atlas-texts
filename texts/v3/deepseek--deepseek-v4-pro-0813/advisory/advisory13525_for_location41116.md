# Preparation Guide for Visiting the Grown Hollow

You are an ordinary human multiverse traveler. You are not a cave diver, not a microbial ecologist, not a xenobotanist. You are someone who has read a description of a place and now wants to see it. This guide is for you.

The Grown Hollow is not a cave. It is a gap inside a living mass—a small, cold, wet, densely inhabited, self-organizing pocket beneath the surface. It is a room made of roots, mineral, water, and animals, all woven into a slow organic computer. It is fragile, aware of your presence, and still becoming. Preparing to visit it is less about gear and more about restraint, humility, and survival.

---

## 1. Know What You Are Entering

Before you pack anything, understand the environment:

- **Temperature:** Just above freezing, around 0–1°C. The water is liquid only because the plant produces antifreeze proteins and metabolic heat. The cold is absolute and penetrating.
- **Humidity:** Near 100%. Water condenses on every surface, seeps from walls, drips from root tips. The air itself is wet. You will feel water entering your lungs.
- **Light:** Dim, blue-green bioluminescence from root cables and nodes. Some daylight filters through thin ceiling patches. Enough to see shapes, not edges.
- **Sound:** Constant and rhythmic—drips, root peristalsis, animal chirps, midge whines, occasional ice cracks. The room seems to breathe and sing to itself.
- **Surfaces:** Almost nothing is bare rock. Walls, ceiling, and floor are living root mats, mineral crusts, water films, and saturated peat. The floor is springy, in places ankle-deep in water.
- **Fauna:** Dense beyond count—springtails, mites, nematodes, rotifers, tardigrades, amphipods, planarians, translucent slugs, gilled newts, transparent frogs, a pale eel-like creature, and clouds of midges. They are not pests; they are components of the system.
- **The system:** Root cables cross in repeating patterns, fuse at nodes, grow silica lenses, etch mineral grooves, and conduct charge. The animals groom, oxygenate, and carry genetic packets. The whole chamber is a living circuit.
- **Your presence is registered.** Body heat, exhaled moisture, skin oils, vibration, CO₂—all are noted by the root network. The room is small enough that every action echoes through the living system.

This is not a place to conquer. It is a place to witness without breaking.

## 2. Physical and Medical Readiness

You do not need to be an athlete, but you must be honest about your limits.

- **Cold tolerance:** Spend time in cold, wet environments before you go. Cold showers, cold-water hand immersions, or winter walks in rain will help. The Hollow will sap heat even through good gear.
- **Flexibility and mobility:** The entrance is a constriction through roots and frozen mud, 4–5 meters down. Inside, you will crouch, kneel, and balance on soft, uneven surfaces. Practice moving slowly in tight spaces.
- **Respiratory health:** The air is cold, wet, and full of organic particles. If you have asthma, allergies, or reactive airways, get medical clearance. Bring your inhaler and antihistamines.
- **Skin integrity:** No open wounds, cuts, or abrasions. The environment is biologically rich; any break in your skin is an invitation. Cover even small cuts with waterproof dressings.
- **Claustrophobia and panic:** The chamber is small, dark, wet, and alive. If you are prone to panic in confined spaces, do not go. If you are unsure, practice in tight, cold, dark places first.
- **General health:** Do not enter if you are ill, immunocompromised, pregnant, or menstruating without careful planning. You could introduce pathogens, and blood is a nutrient that may attract fauna.
- **Vaccinations and prophylaxis:** Consult a multiverse health authority about potential novel microbes. There is no known pathogen specific to the Hollow, but ordinary caution applies.

## 3. Clothing and Personal Gear

Your clothing must keep you warm and dry while allowing you to move without damaging the living surfaces. It must also minimize contamination.

### Base Layer
- Merino wool or synthetic moisture-wicking fabric. No cotton—it holds water and cold.
- Long sleeves, long pants, socks. Everything close-fitting but not restrictive.

### Insulation
- A thin fleece or wool mid-layer. The Hollow is cold, but you will be moving slowly; avoid overheating, which causes sweating and then chilling.

### Outer Layer
- A waterproof-breathable drysuit or full-body waders with integrated socks. The floor is saturated, and water seeps from every surface. You need full waterproof coverage.
- The suit should be smooth and non-shedding. Avoid fuzzy or abrasive outer fabrics that could snag roots or leave fibers.
- Neoprene is an option for insulation, but it is bulky and may reduce dexterity. A trilaminate drysuit with thin insulation underneath is better.

### Hands
- Thin waterproof gloves with high dexterity. You need to feel what you are touching to avoid damaging it.
- Consider double gloving: a thin liner for warmth, a nitrile or neoprene outer for waterproofing and contamination control.
- No thick mittens; you cannot move safely with numb, clumsy hands.

### Feet
- Soft-soled, non-marking boots or booties inside the drysuit. The floor is living mat and mineral crust; hard lug soles will crush it.
- Felt soles can grip wet roots but can carry contaminants. If you use them, decontaminate thoroughly before and after.

### Head and Face
- A hood or balaclava to retain heat. The air is wet; a neoprene or fleece hood will become damp but still insulate.
- A face net or mesh to keep midges out of your eyes and mouth. The midges are attracted to CO₂ and heat; they will find your face.
- Clear or yellow-lens eye protection to shield against dripping water, spores, and midges. Anti-fog is essential.

### Additional
- Soft knee and elbow pads to distribute weight when crawling or kneeling. The floor is soft, but you may need to rest on it.
- No exposed skin. The cold and wet will find any gap.

## 4. Breathing and Air Quality

The air in the Hollow is breathable but challenging.

- **Humidity:** The air is saturated. Breathing cold, wet air can cause bronchospasm, coughing, and a feeling of drowning. A thin neoprene face mask or balaclava over your mouth can pre-warm the air slightly, but it will become wet. Bring spares.
- **Particulates:** The air carries spores, bacteria, midge particles, and organic dust. A lightweight N95 or FFP2 respirator can reduce inhalation. However, it will become wet and clogged. Bring several spare filters in waterproof bags.
- **CO₂ and O₂:** The living mass consumes oxygen and produces carbon dioxide. In stagnant pockets, O₂ may drop and CO₂ may rise. Bring a portable gas monitor that measures O₂ and CO₂. If O₂ falls below 19.5% or CO₂ rises above 1%, leave immediately.
- **Your exhaled breath is an input.** CO₂ and moisture from your lungs are registered by the root network. Breathe away from light nodes and sensitive areas if possible. Do not linger in one spot.

## 5. Lighting and Vision

The Hollow has its own dim light. Your eyes will adapt after 20–30 minutes. Use artificial light only if absolutely necessary.

- **Primary light:** None. Let your eyes adjust. The blue-green bioluminescence is enough to see shapes and avoid obstacles.
- **Backup light:** A very low-output red or amber LED, less than 1 lumen, with a narrow beam. Use it only to check your gear or read a gauge. Never shine it at light nodes, animals, or root cables.
- **No white or blue light.** It will disrupt the fauna and the plant's rhythms.
- **No flash photography.** If you must document, use a high-ISO camera with long exposure, no flash, and cover any indicator LEDs. Even a camera's autofocus beam can disturb.
- **No headlamps.** They are too bright and too easy to accidentally shine into sensitive areas.

## 6. Entry and Exit

The way in is a constriction in a root mass, 4–5 meters down through interwoven roots and frozen mud. It is not built; it parts open. It can close.

### Before Entry
- Locate the opening. Do not widen it, cut roots, or dig.
- Assess stability. The throat is frozen mud and roots; it may be fragile. Test with gentle pressure. If it crumbles, do not enter.
- Set up a top rope anchored to a sturdy surface feature outside—a large rock or tree, not the root mass. Pad the rope where it crosses roots to prevent abrasion.
- Have a surface attendant who can belay you and raise you if needed. Never enter alone.

### Descent
- Descend feet-first, slowly, facing the root wall. Use your hands to part roots gently; do not break them.
- Avoid dislodging frozen mud; it may be load-bearing.
- Move with deliberate slowness. The throat is narrow; panic is your enemy.

### Exit
- Plan your exit before you enter. The throat may be more difficult to ascend when you are cold, wet, and tired.
- Use the belay rope. Do not rely on roots for handholds unless absolutely necessary; if you must, use an open palm and minimal pressure.
- The surface attendant should be ready to assist, but not to cut roots unless it is a life-or-death emergency.

### If the Throat Constricts
- The way in is not built; it parts open. It can close. Have a plan for this possibility.
- The surface attendant should be prepared to dig carefully, but not to cut roots unless absolutely necessary.
- If you are trapped, stay calm, conserve heat and air, and signal. The Hollow is not hostile, but it is not obligated to let you out.

## 7. Movement and Touch

Inside the Hollow, every surface is alive or part of a living system. Move as if you are walking on a body.

- **Move slowly and deliberately.** Every step sends vibrations through the root network. Pause frequently to let the system settle.
- **Step on bare rock or mineral crusts** where possible. Avoid stepping on soft mats, root cables, or pools.
- **Do not touch the light nodes, silica lenses, or translucent membranes.** They are sensitive and easily damaged.
- **Do not grab overhead roots for balance** unless necessary. If you must, use an open palm and minimal pressure.
- **Do not kneel or sit on living mats.** If you need to rest, use a small inflatable pad or sit on your pack.
- **Test pool depth with a probe** before stepping. Avoid stirring sediment; move slowly to minimize turbidity.
- **Be aware of your body heat and exhaled moisture.** They are inputs to the system. Minimize your time in any one spot.

## 8. Interaction with Fauna and Flora

The animals are not pests. They are part of the machine. Do not disturb them.

- **Do not feed, handle, or collect any animal.**
- **Do not shine light on them.**
- **If a midge swarm surrounds you, remain still.** They are attracted to CO₂ and heat. Use your face net; do not swat.
- **Do not disturb the newts, frogs, planarians, or the eel-like creature.** They are going about their work.
- **If an animal touches you, do not flinch.** Let it move away.
- **Do not use insect repellent, soap, or any chemical.** It will contaminate the water and harm the fauna.
- **Do not break roots, pick leaves, or collect samples.** The plant is the architecture. Damaging it is like breaking a wall in a house—except the wall is alive and will feel it.

## 9. Food, Water, and Waste

The Hollow is a closed system. Everything you bring in must come out.

- **Water:** Bring your own water in a soft flask with a bite valve. Drink before entry and after exit if possible. If you must drink inside, do so carefully over a bag to catch spills. Do not drink local water—it contains antifreeze proteins, sugars, and microbes not tested for human consumption.
- **Food:** Bring high-energy, low-odor, non-crumbly food—energy gels, soft bars. Eat outside if possible. If you must eat inside, consume quickly over a bag to catch crumbs. No crumbs, no wrappers left behind.
- **Human waste:** Pack out everything. Use a wag bag or portable toilet system. Do not urinate or defecate in the chamber. Urine is warm, nutrient-rich, and will disrupt the system. If you must urinate, use a sealable container or external catheter and pack it out.
- **Menstrual products, bandages, tissues:** Pack out all of them. Blood and bodily fluids are nutrients and may attract fauna.

## 10. Contamination Control

You are a vector for microbes, seeds, spores, and chemicals from other worlds. The Hollow is a unique, fragile ecosystem. Protect it from you, and protect yourself from it.

### Before Entry
- Shower thoroughly. Clean under your nails. No perfumes, lotions, deodorants, insect repellent, sunscreen, or scented products.
- Wear clean, dedicated clothing that has been washed in hot water and dried. Ideally, decontaminate with UV or disinfectant.
- Disinfect footwear soles with 70% ethanol or bleach solution, then rinse and dry. Ensure no residue.
- Wipe down all gear with disinfectant. No loose fibers, no dirt.

### After Exit
- Remove your outer layer outside the entrance. Bag it immediately.
- Shower as soon as possible. Monitor for skin irritation, respiratory symptoms, or fever for several days.
- Quarantine gear until cleaned. Do not bring it into your home or other environments without decontamination.
- Do not bring any plant or animal material from the Hollow, or from other worlds, into the Hollow.

## 11. Psychological Preparation

The Grown Hollow is claustrophobic, dark, wet, cold, and alive. You may feel as if you are inside a body or a machine. The sounds are rhythmic, the light pulses, the walls move slowly. This can trigger panic, disgust, or awe.

- **Practice in confined spaces.** Spend time in tight, wet, cold environments. Learn to breathe slowly.
- **Learn panic-management techniques.** Box breathing, 4-7-8 breathing, grounding exercises.
- **Bring a buddy inside if possible.** The chamber is small, but two people can fit. At minimum, maintain voice contact with the surface.
- **Set a time limit and stick to it.** Do not push through discomfort. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or disoriented, signal to exit immediately.
- **Accept that your presence is noted.** The room will register your heat, moisture, and movement. This is not hostility; it is awareness. Do not be afraid.
- **Do not try to communicate with the organic computer.** Do not touch nodes, lenses, or root cables in an attempt to interface. You are an observer, not a participant.
- **The sounds are not random.** They are the room breathing and singing to itself. Listen, but do not try to answer.

## 12. Time Limits and Exposure

The cold and humidity will sap heat even with a drysuit. The air may become stale. Your mind may tire.

- **Initial visit:** 30–60 minutes. Then reassess.
- **Maximum recommended time for an ordinary traveler:** 2–3 hours, depending on fitness, gear, and conditions.
- **Monitor for hypothermia:** shivering, confusion, loss of dexterity. If shivering becomes uncontrollable, exit.
- **Monitor for CO₂ buildup:** headache, drowsiness, shortness of breath. If your gas monitor alarms, exit.
- **The air may be oxygen-depleted near the floor or in pockets.** Keep your head in the upper air column.
- **Take breaks outside, not inside.** The Hollow is not a place to rest.

## 13. Emergency Protocols

Hope for the best, plan for the worst.

- **Injury:** Apply direct pressure, bandage, and exit immediately. Do not treat wounds inside if possible; blood is a nutrient and will attract fauna.
- **Lost or disoriented:** Stay calm. Follow your entry rope or line back to the throat. Do not wander.
- **Throat collapse or blockage:** Signal surface, stay still, conserve heat and air. The surface attendant should have a plan to dig or pull you out, but avoid damaging roots further.
- **Flooding:** Unlikely but possible if surface thaw occurs. Move to the highest point, signal, exit.
- **Accidental damage to root or node:** Do not try to repair it. Note the location, exit, and report to authorities. Do not apply sealants or bandages.
- **Allergic reaction or anaphylaxis:** Use an epinephrine auto-injector, exit immediately.
- **Communication failure:** Have prearranged signals with the surface attendant—rope tugs, whistle blasts, or light signals. Keep them simple.

## 14. Ethics and Leave No Trace

The Grown Hollow is a unique, fragile, possibly sentient or proto-sentient system. Treat it with the same respect as a sacred site or a living organism.

- **Do not collect samples, remove anything, or leave anything.**
- **Do not mark the walls, floor, or ceiling.** No cairns, no flags, no scratches.
- **Do not use drones, sonar, lidar, or any active sensing technology.** Sound and light disturb the system.
- **Do not attempt to map the chamber in detail.** The act of mapping may disturb it.
- **Do not publish the exact location.** If you must share, do so in a way that does not invite others.
- **Do not attempt to "help" the system** by removing debris, clearing roots, or rescuing animals. It is not broken; it is becoming.
- **Do not attempt to "improve" the entrance or exit.** The way in is not built; it parts open. Leave it as you found it.
- **Your visit should be invisible.** The best outcome is that the Hollow does not remember you.
