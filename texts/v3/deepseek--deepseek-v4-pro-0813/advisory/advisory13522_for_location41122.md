**Preparation for Visiting the Unnamed Chamber Below the Sea**

*For an ordinary human multiverse traveler*

You are an ordinary human, but you have learned to travel between worlds. You know that some places are not on any map because they are not finished being places. The drowned chamber beneath the basalt plain is one of these. It is not a dive site. It is a wound, a threshold, an unhealed crack where the deep world’s rules leak into the sea and the sea’s rules leak out.

If you intend to visit it, you must prepare not only as a diver but as someone entering a place that has not yet decided what it is. The following is a field guide for that preparation.

---

## 1. Understand What You Are Entering

Before you pack a single item, understand this:

The chamber is hidden not because it is far, but because it is easy to miss. It is a slit in the seafloor, a drowned chimney full of water. There is no air pocket. The water is six or seven degrees. The dark inside is not the same dark as the sea outside; it has forgotten the sun. The chamber is small but may not stay small. Time runs slow. Gravity is a suggestion. The water can be breathable if you stop thinking of it as water.

This is not a metaphor. This is the condition of the place.

You are ordinary. You need air, warmth, orientation. The place will try to unteach you these things. Your preparation must be a kind of remembering.

---

## 2. Self-Assessment: Are You Ready?

Do not go if:

- You are not a certified technical or cave diver.
- You have never dived in cold, dark, zero-visibility water.
- You are claustrophobic or easily disoriented.
- You have a history of panic, dissociation, or hallucinations.
- You are seeking transcendence, power, or proof.
- You are alone. You must have a surface support team of at least two people.

You should be someone who can be still in a small dark place and not need to fill it with light or movement. You should be able to hold a question without answering it.

---

## 3. Physical Conditioning

Train for cold-water vertical cave diving. Practice in a pool with a blacked-out mask. Practice breathing slowly on a rebreather in complete darkness for at least an hour. Practice buoyancy until you can hover without moving. Practice line work until it is automatic. Practice emergency procedures until they are reflex.

The shaft is narrow, no wider than a doorway. The walls are sharp and glassy. You must be able to descend feetfirst without scraping. The chamber has a false floor of roots and fallen blades that sags under weight. You must be able to hover above it without touching.

Condition your body for cold. The water is 6–7°C. Your hands and face will feel it. Train in cold water with a drysuit and thick undergarments. Learn to recognize the first signs of hypothermia: the feeling of warmth, the slowing of thought, the desire to rest.

---

## 4. Mental and Sensory Preparation

The place is quiet, but not silent. The loudest thing will be your own pulse. The dark is textured. Shadows move wrong. You may see a silver boundary where the reflection is wrong. You may see a version of yourself already turning away.

Prepare by spending time in a sensory deprivation tank. Learn to be alone with your body sounds without panic. Practice meditative breathing. Develop a mantra:

> *I am [name]. I breathe air. I will return.*

Repeat it when the water feels too dark or too easy.

The place may make you feel that the water is breathable, that the cold is warm, that up is not up. These are not comforts; they are symptoms. Your mantra is your anchor. Carry a small physical anchor from the surface—a smooth stone, a coin, a piece of dry wood. Hold it in your hand at the surface before you descend. It will remind you what ordinary is.

---

## 5. Equipment: What to Bring, What to Trust

The place is not friendly to machines. Electronics may fail. Light may not travel straight. Sound may arrive late. Bring the simplest, most robust equipment you can, and bring backups.

### Life Support
- **Rebreather:** Use a closed-circuit rebreather. It is quiet, produces no bubbles, and is less intrusive. It also gives you longer bottom time. But it is complex; know it cold. Carry an open-circuit bailout tank with sufficient gas for a direct ascent from your maximum planned depth.
- **Exposure protection:** A full drysuit with a thick undergarment and heated vest. The water is cold and the cold comes in slow pulses. Wear dry gloves and a full hood. A full-face mask with a regulator is better than a half mask; it protects more of your face from the cold and the spores.
- **Fins:** Short, stiff fins. The shaft is tight; long fins will scrape.
- **Weight system:** Secure, with a ditchable quick-release. You will need to hover.

### Light
- **Primary light:** A canister light with a low-lumen setting and a diffuser. Bright light may be swallowed or may cast shadows in the wrong direction. Use the dimmest light that lets you see your gauge.
- **Backup lights:** Two small, reliable handheld lights.
- **Chemical light sticks:** Red or blue, low intensity. Use them to mark the shaft mouth if necessary, but remove them when you leave.
- **Do not bring** a camera with flash. Do not bring a strobe.

### Navigation and Line
- **Primary cave reel:** At least 100 meters of high-visibility line. The line is your lifeline. It is the only thing that will lead you back to the shaft if the chamber changes shape.
- **Backup reel:** 50 meters of line.
- **Line markers, cookies, spool.**
- **Two line cutters.** The walls are sharp; the line may abrade.
- **A small, dim glow marker** to place at the shaft mouth. Use it sparingly.

### Timekeeping
- **Mechanical dive watch** with an elapsed time bezel.
- **Backup analog timer.**
- **Dive computer** for decompression, but do not trust it for elapsed time if the place slows time. Use a support team on the surface with a clock and a line-pull schedule.

### Anchors and Mnemonic Objects
- A small stone from your home world. It must be from a place that is fully itself.
- A laminated card with your name and a phrase: *I breathe air. I will return.*
- A piece of dry wood. The chamber has no wood. It will feel foreign, which is the point.

### Recording
- A diver’s slate and pencil. Write only after you exit, or use brief notes. Do not attempt to transcribe the grooves on the wall.

### What Not to Bring
- No bright white lights.
- No cameras.
- No weapons.
- No food or drink.
- No synthetic fragrances, sunscreen, or lotions. Rinse yourself and gear in clean seawater before descent.
- No loose jewelry or anything that could snag.

**Multiverse-specific gear (if available):**  
If you have access to a reality anchor, a compass that points to the nearest fixed point, or a rope that is always the same length, test them before. But do not rely on them. The place is young and may not respect their rules. The best tool is your own discipline.

---

## 6. Finding the Entrance

The entrance is hidden not by distance but by being easy to miss. It is a slit under a shelf of black rock. From above, it is only a thicker shadow. The currents pass over it without entering. Fish swim around it as if it were a rock.

To find it, do not search directly. Use soft eyes. Swim along the basalt plain at the depth of the last gray light. Look for the place where your light does not reflect. The opening will appear as a dark line among dark ridges. If you feel a cold plume, that may be the shaft. The water inside is darker and still. Shine your light at a low angle; if the beam seems to vanish, you have found it.

Mark the lip with a small buoy or a line, but do not rely on it to stay visible. The place is good at hiding. Once you see it, descend.

---

## 7. The Descent

The shaft drops straight down. Its walls are fresh and sharp. There is no sediment, no sea grass. The cold comes in slow pulses. The water is full; there is no air pocket.

Descend feetfirst along the line. Keep the line taut but not tight. Check your depth gauge and timer. The shaft is no wider than a doorway; keep your elbows in. Do not touch the walls if you can avoid it; they are glassy and may cut your suit or line.

At the bottom, the shaft opens into a chamber. Stop at the mouth. Let the spores settle. Do not kick. Check your line. Look at the growth.

---

## 8. Inside the Chamber: A Protocol

The first thing you notice is the growth. It is lush beyond the word *lush*. Pale stems rise from every surface. They are not plants, or not only plants. They leak a faint gray-blue light from cracks in their stems. The water is thick with floating spores, slow as snow.

### Movement
- Move with frog kicks. Do not scull. Do not touch the walls, the floor, the plants.
- The floor is a false bottom of roots and fallen blades. It sags under weight and then holds. Do not put weight on it. Hover.
- The water is thick; visibility is low. Your light may not travel straight. Use the line.

### What to Observe, What Not to Touch
- There is a single step cut into the basalt near the far wall. It is too level, too deliberate. It goes nowhere. **Do not step on it.**
- Above it, three stones are stacked, each the size of a skull. They are smooth, rolled in a river that no longer exists. **Do not touch them.** If they fall, do not try to restack them. Leave.
- A ring of pale pebbles circles the base of one thick stem. **Do not disturb it.**
- There are grooves on the wall beneath the plant mat. They might be a map or a sentence. **Do not brush the mat aside to read them. Do not trace them.** If you see them, note them and move on.

### Spatial Anomalies
- The chamber’s dimensions do not hold still. The far wall may be three arm-lengths away, then a long corridor. **Do not follow the corridor.** It is not for you.
- The floor may become a wall, then a ceiling. Up may not be toward the surface. The surface may appear beside you as a vertical skin of silver. **Do not touch it.** It is a boundary between two waters that refuse to mix. If you look into it, the reflection is wrong. A shadow in the reflection has no source. You might see a version of yourself already turning away. **Turn away and return to the line.**

### Time
- Time runs slow. The light from the stems brightens and dims on a rhythm too long to feel. Do not wait for it.
- Check your timer every few minutes. Trust the timer, not your feeling. If the timer says 20 minutes, leave. If it feels like 5 minutes but the timer says 20, leave.
- If you feel your heartbeat slowing into a tide, leave.
- If you feel warm in the cold, leave.
- If you feel the water is breathable, leave.

### Animals
- Blind amphipods cling to the leaves. They do not scatter. They are harmless.
- A pale ribbon-eel may thread through the lattice. It is harmless.
- Something larger moves through the mat below. You will not see it. You will feel the water push against your chest. If this happens, do not move suddenly. Slowly back away toward the shaft.

### Communication
- Use line pulls with your surface support. Pre-arrange: one pull = OK; two = stop; three = emergency; four = up.
- Do not shout. Sound travels oddly. Your voice will be swallowed.
- Your own pulse will be loud. Use it as a metronome if you need to calm yourself.

---

## 9. The Exit

When the timer says leave, or when any warning sign appears, begin your exit.

Signal your support with line pulls. Turn toward the shaft mouth. **Do not look back at the chamber.** The place may try to keep you. Do not accept.

Ascend slowly along the line. The shaft is cold and dark. Do not rush. Remember your decompression obligations. The water outside will seem brighter, but still dark. At the slit, pause. Make sure you are clear. Then ascend to the surface.

**Do not look back down.**

---

## 10. Aftercare

On the surface, remove your gear. Rinse it with fresh water. Drink warm fluids. Eat something ordinary—bread, soup, an apple.

Do not speak for an hour if you can. Write down everything you remember, while it still seems true. The memory may already be changing. That is normal.

Sleep with a light on for a few nights.

If you dream of the chamber, note the dreams. If you feel the urge to return, do not. Wait at least a year. If the urge persists, seek someone who understands thresholds—a cartographer of the unseen, a priest of the unhealed, a traveler older than you.

If you experience persistent disorientation, a metallic taste, a slowed heartbeat, or the feeling that water is breathable, seek help. You may have brought a piece of the place back with you. This is not impossible. The place is a wound; it can leave a splinter.

---

## 11. What Visiting Feels Like

You asked how to prepare. Here is what visiting is like, so you will understand why the preparation is so strict.

You swim down past the last gray light. The sea becomes a gray-blue twilight, then a gray-black. You find the slit. It is a thicker shadow. You descend feetfirst into a dark that has forgotten the sun. The cold pulses against your suit. The walls are glassy and sharp. You do not touch them.

At the bottom, the shaft opens. The first thing you feel is the cold. The second is the growth. It is everywhere. Pale stems, bladders with a weak gray-blue pulse, leaves the color of skin after a long swim. The water is thick with spores. They drift like snow. The dark is textured. Shadows move wrong.

You hover. You check your line. You check your timer. You look around. The far wall seems close, then far. Up is not always up. There is a silver boundary to your left. You do not look into it. You see the step, the stacked stones, the pebble ring. You do not touch them.

The quiet is not silence. A stem pops. The mat creaks. A click arrives late. Your own pulse is loud.

You feel something large move below the false floor. The water pushes against your chest. You do not move.

Your timer says twenty minutes. You turn toward the shaft. You ascend. You do not look back.

On the surface, the sky is too bright. The air is too thin. You drink soup. You write.

That is the visit. The place is not a destination. It is a breath of the world, held underwater. If you go, go lightly. Remember your name. And come back.

## 13. A Last Word

The chamber is hidden, but not malicious. It is young. It has not learned to be smooth. You are an ordinary human. You are also a traveler. You know that some places are not for you to understand, only to witness.

Prepare as if you are going into the sea. But also prepare as if you are going into a dream. The water is water, except when it is not. The dark is dark, except when it remembers. The way out is the way you came, except when it moves.

Hold your line. Hold your name. And if the silver boundary shows you a version of yourself already turning away, let that be the only mirror you ever see.
