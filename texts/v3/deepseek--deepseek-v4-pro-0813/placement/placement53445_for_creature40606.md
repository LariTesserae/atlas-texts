I would find myself halfway up a new bonewood tower on the eastern edge of the plateau, just above a tear in the root-mat where steam rises in a steady, hot column. The shoot is pale and hollow, still growing, its bark already cracked from the speed of its own ascent. I am wound around it in loops that are not neat but necessary.

I would be a tendon-vine.

Dark, rubbery, wet with condensate. I have no mouth, no eyes, no hands. My whole life is moisture and tension. When the air thickens, I swell and shorten. When it dries, I lengthen and loosen. That is all my motion, but it is enough. The bonewood gives me height; I give it shape. I pull the straight spire into an arch, slowly, over hours, the way a muscle remembers a weight without knowing it is a muscle.

At dawn I am slack and heavy with dew. By noon the steam is thick, the heat rises from the root-mat, and I am tight as a soaked rope. The tower groans under my grip. Cracks open. Steam escapes from the fissures I have made. That hiss is my voice, though I do not speak. The crack is my signature, though I do not write. The arch is my thought, though I do not think.

Below me, bladderleaves bulge with hot water. A valve-membrane the size of a door curls open and spills overflow into a waxy gutter. Somewhere in the fissure my coils have opened, a dissolver is already waking, pale and threadlike, tasting the crack like a question. It will eat the tower from inside. I feel it coming, and I do not resist. My work is not to last. It is to shape what will fall. The falling is also building.

At night the air cools. I lengthen. The tower sags, and I allow it. I am not tired; I am drying. The mist settles on me, then the heat returns, and I begin to swell again.

So I would not be a visitor here. I would be a resident—one of the plateau’s slow, wet, anonymous builders. I would be the one who turns a fast, brittle sprout into a brief arch above the green mist. A tendon-vine, pulling, cracking, holding, releasing. A muscle in the high hot weeping machine, where the building never stops.
