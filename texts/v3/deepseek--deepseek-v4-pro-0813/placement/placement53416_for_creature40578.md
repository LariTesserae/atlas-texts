I would find myself in the lower galleries of the built valley, in the warm wet dimness beneath the frogs and the bats. I would be one of the masons.

I am not the one who walked down from the pass. I never saw the ridge or the far wall blued by mist. I am one of the pale, soft-bodied ones, smaller than your smallest fingernail, nearly blind. I live in the wall, and the wall is my world.

To me the valley is not a landscape. It is gravity and scent and heat. Below is the red clay, soft and wet where the walls sweat. Above is the crack that must be mended, the sagging edge of an older chamber. All my life I travel between them. I roll the clay in my mouth, coat it with the shining fluid from my abdomen, and when it is no longer mud but something darker and stronger, I press it into the wall. I shape it with my head. I do this again and again.

I do not hear the frogs as you do. Their chorus is a pressure in the soil, a ceaseless trembling that tells me the wet world is still alive. The bats are a brief breath from the lower galleries at dusk. Roots are slow, hungry fingers that split my tunnels and are then swallowed by our new walls. When I meet a sister, we stop and touch whole body to whole body, exchanging food and information. We do not speak; we trade heat and molecules.

Far below, in the blackest chamber, the queen is always laying eggs. I know her not as a face but as a pulse in the clay, a scent that turns my legs along the right path. I have never seen her. She is not a ruler; she is an engine. I sometimes carry her eggs along with my bead of clay. They are soft and pale, and they are the future walls.

When I die, I will fall or be taken, and my body will feed the fungal gardens. The fungi will feed the larvae; the larvae will become masons; the masons will build the valley a little farther. I will never know the whole of it. But I will have been one small self in the dark, in the noise, in the warm wet air—one cell of a body made of mud and roots and eggs and sound.

So I suppose I am not just a mason. I am the valley, the way a single bead of clay is the wall.
