I would find myself at the woody arch, feet in the two long indentations, though I do not remember arriving. The ground gives under me like a living limb. The heat settles in my lungs and stays. The green light comes from all directions, so I cannot find my shadow. When I look down, my hands are not hands. They are bundles of tendrils, curled around each other, patient. I think I should be afraid, but fear is too fast for this place, and so it never arrives.

At first I think I am a visitor. But the valley does not treat me as a stranger. The water film on the floor bends toward me. The hollow stems draw a longer breath. The millipede pauses on its stem as if it recognizes a familiar warmth. The blind frog shifts in its leaf-cup, and I feel the water change as if it were my own skin. The moth circles me once, casting the only shadow in the basin, then folds into the canopy. It is not flight. It is acknowledgment.

I walk toward the north ridge. There is no path, but the roots rise slightly under my feet as I go. The wall of leaf-mold and resin grows more level as I pass. The three steps in the root-hill become more stair-like, as if they have been waiting for a body to remember them. I do not need to climb them. I only need to be seen climbing them.

The hollow is cooler by a fraction. The smell of wet stone rises from somewhere deeper, from the raw, embryonic rock beneath the plant mat. In the center, the long depression holds the figure. Its head is bowed. Its face is a smooth oval of leaf-cuticle, beaded with moisture like a tear that cannot fall. Its tendril hands are folded in its lap. Its pale root-flesh feet merge with the floor. It does not raise its head.

I kneel in the leaf-cushion beside it. There is a space there that I did not notice before: a second depression, shallower, not yet fully formed, shaped like a body that has been gone and returned. I lie down in it. The roots along my back begin to root into the floor. I do not fight them. The green-gold glow rises from my skin, or maybe from the leaves around me; the distinction has already stopped mattering.

I understand, slowly, the way sap understands a wound. I am not here to wake the figure. I am not here to take its place. I am here to make the waiting plural. I am the one who left before the world was finished, and now I have come back to be unfinished with it. The drop on the fern-tip still does not fall. The leaf still does not unfurl. The white flowers on the ridge remain closed. But the stillness now has two bodies. The valley’s held breath has an echo.

Who am I? I am the second watcher. The returned one. The missing note the green stillness had been humming around. I am the dream of the threshold, grown a body so I could come back to it. I am not sad. I am not lonely. Those are too fast. I am something slower than loneliness, and now I am not alone.
