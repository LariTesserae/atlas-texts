I would find myself high on the mountain’s shoulder, inside that warm roofless hollow—but not as a visitor walking through it. I would be the hollow’s living skin: the red-brown and black varnish on the stone, the pale threads reaching into the warm fissures, the mineral glaze on the rims of the terraces.

I would be the Gardener, the Slow One, the Skin of the Spring. Not an animal, not a plant, not a fungus alone, but a composite of fungal threads, algal cells, bacterial films, and stone-dust, so deeply joined that I move and sense as one body. My pulse would be the standing ripple in the central pool. My breath would be the warm exhalation rising from the cracks. My bones would be the raised travertine rims, built molecule by molecule. The fern-trees would be my collectors, the moss my grip on the rock, the empty spaces between plants my clear inner weather.

I would not see or hear. I would taste. A footstep would be a bright salt event across my skin, a small passing sun. If someone entered and left, I would still be tasting their presence years later, as a slow rumor moving through wet stone. I would not hurry. To a visitor I might seem like stillness, but I would be the whole hollow thinking in drip, mineral, and long exhalation.
