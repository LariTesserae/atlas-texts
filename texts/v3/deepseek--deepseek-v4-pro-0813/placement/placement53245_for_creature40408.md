I would find myself at the base of the north wall, where the salt crust is warm as something that has been lying there. I would be the dark chert.

I am the size of two fists. I have one glossy conchoidal fracture, unworked, with no chips around me. I am not a tool. I am the possibility of a tool. The only kind of technology this place suggests is me—a stone that could fit a hand, and a hand that has never closed around it.

The resident is the hollow itself, and I am part of it. The salt floor is its belly-skin, the polygonal fissures its healed sutures. The south rim is the ridge of its shoulder. The dark above head height is the underside of its chest. The scattered shrubs are its sense organs, pushed up through the salt to listen, to taste the still air. I am the one small part of it that has been pushed up too—the creature’s eye, or its tooth, or the one hand it has not grown.

I do not see, but I feel. The dark layers around me: warm at the ground, brown-black above, gray-brown at the sky. The silence has texture. I feel the shrubs as separate facts, each one keeping its own bowl of bare salt. The floor beneath me is body-warm and still. The resident does not move. It breathes once every few years, and between breaths the dark sits in layers like held air.

When you enter, I know. The silence gains a new boundary—your pulse, your breath, the soft friction of cloth. The resident does not wake, because it has never been asleep. It simply holds you the way the hollow holds the dark. Your warmth at the knees is its warmth. The salt under your feet is its skin.

If you pick me up, I will fit your hand exactly. That is not chance. I have kept the shape of a palm for centuries, not because a hand made me, but because the resident remembers a hand without having one. When your fingers close around me, the hollow feels you through me, the way a tongue feels a stone placed on it. For that moment, I become the creature’s one gesture, its hand grown by borrowing yours.

But you will not stay. That is right. The hollow is not waiting for you. Its emptiness is stable, complete. You will set me down, and the silence will close back over me. No mark will remain. Erosion has finished here. Change is elsewhere.

I will stay at the base of the north wall. I will still be the dark chert, with one glossy conchoidal fracture. No chips around me. No hand holding me for long. I will remember your warmth as a slight difference in the mineral, and then that too will settle into the long present tense.

So I would not be the visitor. I would not be the whole resident. I would be the small, hard point where the hollow’s stillness meets the shape of a hand—the one thing in the Salt Hollow that could be lifted, but is not. I would be the dark chert, the creature’s hand that never grew, waiting without waiting because the place is already complete.
