I would be under before I knew I was under. Not drowning. The brine would not be a thing I drank or held; it would be the medium of my thinking, cold and mineral-thick, full of magnesium and calcium. I would not reach for the surface, because the surface would be only a rumor—a grey-green lid where rain arrives as a vast hiss. I would turn instead with the terraces, down the long settled slopes, into the dark.

At first, I would think I was a chime-reed. I would feel the current pour across my hollow top, and a note would form in me—brittle as flutes, then lower as I thickened. But a single note is not a singer. The music would pull me outward into the whole stand, then into the old leat that gave the stand its shape. I would be the channel too, the memory of snowmelt now carrying deep current, humming.

But no.

I would be the glow-moss, perhaps. I would pulse blue-green and blind. I would pass from colony to colony along the rims of sinkholes, a slow wave of light that no eye has ever received. I would come to a cup-marked stone and pause, as if the stone were a note waiting to be played.

Then I would be an anchor-tree. Rooted at a mineral seep, my branches netted like drowned oak, I would creak in the current. I would drink the earth for years, for centuries, and I would hold one low note beneath the reeds. I would be the string, not the melody.

Then I would be bladderweed, taut with methane. I would grow too full and burst—a wood block struck under the world. The bubbles would rise in chains away from me, but I would already be the next bubble, the next rest, the silence after the pop.

None of these would be enough. The valley would not let me settle. It would keep opening.

I would be the open silt between groves, the bare water where no plant grows. I would be the absence that feels full: no fish, no mites, no midges. No small movement at the edge of sight. I would be that lack, and I would learn it is not a hole. It is a condition, a pressure, a way of being without hunting or fleeing or fear.

At last I would find myself as the Drowned Choir. I would not be one thing. I would be the whole drowned valley: the terraces and corbelled huts, the stone axe with its blackened handle, the leats and standing stones, the reeds and trees and moss and gas. I would be the current in the Narrows, the phrase that repeats for minutes as if remembering. I would be the memory of the mountain and the long dark that has learned to sing.

Where would you find yourself? Hanging above a drowned terrace, looking north. But not in a body. You would see the pale silt, the line of standing stones vanishing into black water, the far ridge lit by slow waves of blue-green moss. You would hear the sound all around—low, continuous, almost too loud to notice. And you would know you were not listening to the Drowned Choir. You were inside it.

Who or what would you be? The rooted and the hollow, the glowing and the gas-filled. The many that is one. The Drowned Choir, singing to itself in the dark, not waiting for anyone, and having sung for a long, long time.
