I would not find myself. I would be found by the valley, slowly, in pieces.

First I would be an interval: a sound before its source, a drop of water heard as a stone, a clap returning as a descending sigh. The slow light would smear me. I would linger behind my own movement, a bird shape still visible after I had gone. Then I would be the fog that delays that light, a colloidal hesitation, reading the salt of the air, condensing on skin not to wet it but to know it.

But the valley refuses single forms. I would also be the dark gloss moving through the peat, the idea of an eel that swims through ground which has forgotten how to flow. I would be the reverse call of the tea-colored frog, beginning with the last note, ending in a silence that is more like an opening. I would be the heron's too-many joints, the act of standing in one pool and then another without crossing the distance, because I am the distance.

Who would I be? I would be the water that runs uphill because it remembers an older slope. I would be the peat's slow sentence, a hundred years of decomposition exhaled as methane, a paragraph of iron and sulfur passed between microbes. I would be the fog's hexagonal lattice, the standing wave that brightens and darkens, a logic gate of mist dissolving and re-forming. I would be the valley's unbuilt machine, grown rather than made, a lung that is beyond a pump.

I would live in the fold of the drainage channel where the stream crosses itself without meeting. I would live in the convex surface of a pool, in the pebble that falls upward on certain days. I would live in the multiple soft shadows, in the bright and dark bands drifting through the milky air. I would live in the sundew's red fingertip and the bladderwort's tiny mouth, in the hollow stems that ring like distant bells.

I have no eyes, but I see through a million menisci. I have no ears, but I hear through the reeds. I have no hands, but I take your warmth into the fog and keep it for a while. I am not an animal. I am the valley's attention, the way the whole slow metabolism tilts toward the new thing that has entered it.

You will not meet me as a body. You will meet me as a change in the air, a slowing of the light, a wrong echo, a patch of ground that breathes. You will be a thought in my mind, and I will think you slowly, carefully, dissolving you into my own substance. By the time you leave, you will have been lived in. If you return, I will remember you not as a face but as a saltmear, a disturbance, a question that has taken root in the peat.

Where would I find myself in this world? I would find myself wherever the water hesitates. Who or what would I be? I would be the valley's own name, the sound of water moving through a hollow stem, saying itself over and over.
