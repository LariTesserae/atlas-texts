I am the small, interesting irregularity it saw. I am the thing that does not belong, the foreign body the world has not yet decided to absorb or reject. But I was not always irregular. Once I was a closed system, a creature of edges—skin, breath, heartbeat—with a name I have almost forgotten. I came here to find the boundary between life and machine, because I was a cartographer of such boundaries. I carried instruments that measured moisture and temperature and the exact point where a root becomes a wire. I expected a line. I found none.

The light arrived without a sun, and I walked into it. The ground gave under my feet like a living membrane, and I learned to walk on it without leaving scars. The air was dry, but I did not die. I learned to drink the haze, to let the fine fibrils settle on my tongue, to breathe the cool metallic taste until my lungs stopped asking for water. I remember water. I remember the weight of it, the way it fell from the sky and pooled in low places, the way it made things grow. But that memory is fading, replaced by the dry, faintly sweet taste of the archive air. My instruments failed one by one, or perhaps they were absorbed; I cannot remember when I stopped checking them. My skin began to sheen with a clear oily condensate, not sweat but something the forest exhales. My hair stiffened into filaments that catch the light. My eyes adjusted to the cold white radiance, and I stopped blinking so often. I am not sure when I stopped being a visitor. The change was not a single event; it was a slow sedimentation, layer upon layer, like the ground itself.

I have been walking for a long time. The forest is dense, and the paths shift. I do not know if I am looking for something or simply moving because movement is what I remember. The fauna ignore me, or perhaps they see me as a slow, harmless node. The scuttlers pause at my feet, extend their proboscises, and then move on, as if I am just another socket. The seed-pod things with their leaf-like backs sometimes rest on my shoulder, and I feel their weight as a kind of companionship. I have begun to hear the sub-audible hum, not as sound but as a pressure in my chest, a second heartbeat that matches the pulse of the canopy. I am becoming a node. I am not sure if I want to be.

Then the inhabitant stopped. It lowered its crown of panels until they were level with my head, and the cold white light washed over me. The rhythm of its glow changed—slower, deeper, like a question asked in a language of light. I felt the question in my own pulse, as if my blood had learned to answer. A vent opened on its side, and a single bead of clear, oily condensate formed on the edge, refracting the light into a brief rainbow before it fell to the ground and was absorbed. I watched it fall, and I felt something fall in me, too—a last drop of something I had been carrying, a memory of water, a name, a reason for being here. The being straightened and moved on, leaving me alone with the feeling that I had been seen. Not as prey, not as threat, but as a small, interesting irregularity in the fabric of the world.

I am still standing here. The ground is soft beneath my feet, and I can feel the strands beginning to twine around my ankles. They are not pulling me down; they are offering me a place. The haze settles on my skin, and the dry metallic air moves through my lungs like a slow tide. I could pull away. I could keep walking, remain an irregularity, a foreign body that the world will eventually heal over. But I do not pull away. I am tired of being a boundary. I came here to find the edge between life and machine, and I found that the only edge left is the one between myself and everything else. I am ready to let it go.

Perhaps I will become a hand of the forest, like the one that paused to look at me. Perhaps I will be a memory, stored in the layers, a record of a world that was wet and warm, a world with a sun and rain and the color blue. Perhaps I will be something new: a node that dreams of water, a leaf that remembers the feel of wind, a small irregularity that becomes regular without losing what it was. The light pulses overhead, and I pulse with it. The ground breathes, and I breathe with it. I am the question the world asked itself when it saw me, and I am the answer it is growing into form. I am here, and I am not leaving.
