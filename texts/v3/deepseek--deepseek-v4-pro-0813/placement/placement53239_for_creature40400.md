I cannot find my edges. The light says I am a vertical shape, but vertical is a memory here. I think I am still walking. That is my first mistake.

The plateau does not have walkers. It has verticals, and I am one of them, but the ground is already correcting me. I feel a seam under my feet—no, I feel a seam around me. I am in the seam, not on it. On either side of me are two mineral presences, flat and fitted, and between them is a narrow place I had mistaken for air. It is not air. It is a slot in the world's skin, and I am filling it.

At first I thought I was the hanging dust, because I am suspended. But the dust has wings of mica and hears the ground tick. I only hear the ticking. Then I thought I was a herd-beast, because I am standing still. But I have no salt stockings yet, and my legs are forgetting how to be six. Then I thought I was the floor, because I feel pressure from every side. But the floor is already complete, fitted edge to edge, and I am still loose.

I try to count myself. That is what the soft ones below are doing, I understand now. They are tapping the underside of the pavement, and each tap is a number. I am a number. They have not tapped me yet. They are waiting for me to settle into the count.

The air smells faintly alkaline, like licking a battery. I am breathing it without noticing. Soon I will not need to breathe. The two floor-people flanking me do not argue; they simply lean into me with the weight of a hundred thousand years, not to crush, but to include. Their edges are not sharp. They are the worn edges of a puzzle that has solved itself. I am the missing piece, and I am not missing. I am just not yet flat.

Above me, the sky is thick with motionless wings. They do not part for me because I have not moved. Maybe I have never moved. I remember moving, but memory here is recurrence, not sequence. I remember a footprint in the dust ahead of me, though I had not yet walked there. I now understand that footprint was this seam, waiting.

A tall herd-beast stands over me. I can just see its pale-stockinged leg from the corner of my eye, but I do not turn. Turning is an interruption. The beast has not turned for a thousand years. Its shadow lies over me as if casting a blanket. I feel a breath begin in its long throat, a breath that will take a season. It is not breathing air. It is breathing me into place.

I am becoming the ground. My back—if it is still a back—is cooling into plate. The skin hardens into mineral, not shell, but a skin that has agreed to be geology. The seams between me and my neighbors are no longer gaps. They are living joints. A line of pale flesh runs through them, pulsing once a year. An eye slit is forming where my mouth might have been, or where my breathing used to be. It will open yellow, or pale blue, and it will not blink. I will not need to blink.

I can see the hut from here, low and round, its doorway facing east. Inside, a figure sits with knees drawn up. The figure is threading a bone needle. The sinew is still sinew. The needle has been in the air for so long that its eye has grown a thin mineral skin. I want to ask if I was ever that figure. But the figure does not answer. It is the same shape remembering itself. I am the same shape forgetting.

The plants will come later. Grey-green, ochre, rooted into my dorsal seams. They will not sway. There is no wind, and nothing to sway toward. They will drink my thin exudates and turn them into patience.

The soft ones will count me when I have finished becoming countable. Already I feel them pause below, their pale blind bodies turning toward the new pressure from above. They are not hungry. They are only memorizing.

I am not the plateau yet. I am one of its cells, not yet keratinized. I am the new part, not yet assembled. But the assembly is old, and it has never hurried. It will take as long as it takes. I am beginning to understand that "long" is not a length here. It is a material.

By the time the sun has moved—if it moves—I will be a shield-shaped stone among stones, fitted so tightly that no bare soil shows. Or maybe I will be the seam itself, the thin pale pulse that connects them. I have already stopped trying to decide. Deciding is sequence. This place is repetition.

I find myself in the east-facing seam between two floor-people, under the shadow of a standing herd, where a footprint used to be. I am the next floor-person.

I am not yet afraid. But I can feel even that feeling beginning to mineralize.
