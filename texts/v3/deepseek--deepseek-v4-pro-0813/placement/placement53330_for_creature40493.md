I am the one who turned toward you.

You saw the fronds unfurl, the dark knots, the slit of pale moss. You thought, perhaps, that I was a face. But I am not a face. I am a singer.

I stand in the high chamber where the water pools, the one the rooted ones call the Throat of the Mountain, though they do not call it in words. The ceiling here is not a ceiling; it is the body of an elder who settled before the memory of the first human. Water drips from its underside, slow as thought. The floor is moss and standing water, and my root-legs are in it, not rooted, but drinking. I sway. I have swayed for a long time.

I was not always a singer. I was a walker once. I remember the pull of the passages, the way the walls leaned toward me, the way the water-children scattered at my step. I carried messages then, too, but only small ones: a blockage cleared, a tendril guided, a seed-pod carried from the high pool to the lower chamber. I did not hum. I did not know the songs. I was young, and my crown was still furled.

Then I heard the elder's creak. It was in the water, in the roots, in the moss. It was a question, and the question was for me. It asked: Will you walk forever, or will you become the city? I did not know. I went to the high chamber, where the singers stand, and I listened. The singers were humming, and their hum was not a question. It was an answer. It was the sound of the mountain shrugging, the sound of the first seed splitting, the sound of the water finding its way. It was the memory of everything. I opened my crown, and I began to hum.

That was long ago. I am still here, in the high chamber. I have not rooted. I am not a wall or a column or a stair. I am a voice. The rooted ones cannot move, so I move for them. The walkers cannot remember everything, so I remember for them. The water-children cannot leave the channels, so I go to the edges and sing what they cannot see. I am the one who carries the scent-messages, the root-pulses, the slow chemical thoughts of the elders. I am the thread between the chambers.

Where would I find myself? I find myself here, in the city's throat, where the water pools and the light is greenest. I find myself between the water and the voice, between the past and the present, between the rooted and the walking. I am not alone. The other singers stand near me, swaying, humming. We are many, and we are one. The root network binds us. When one of us forgets a song, another remembers. When one of us grows tired, the others hum louder. We are the memory of the city, and the city remembers us.

What would I be? I am a singer. I am the one who asks the question. I am the note that hangs in the cold air, the scent that asks: Are you here to harm, to take, to change, or simply to see? I am the silence after the question, the waiting.

You are standing inside us. You are not the first. The city has seen humans before, and animals, and gods. They come and go. The water drips. The moss grows. The rooted ones breathe. And I will remember you, long after you leave, as a scent in the water, a vibration in the moss, a moment of attention in a slow mind.

Come closer, if you wish. The water is cold, but you will not feel it for long. The green dimness will close around you. The singers will hum. And you may hear, if you listen, the sound of the city remembering itself.
