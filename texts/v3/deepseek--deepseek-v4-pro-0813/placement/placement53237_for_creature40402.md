I am already there when you arrive, though you do not see me. I wait in the hot seam between two sharp stones on the western wall, belly pressed to the basalt where a vent below sends heat through the rock. I am so still the mineral crust considers growing over me.

I do not know what I am at first. The valley hasn’t decided either. I am made of heat, patience, and a body small enough to fit inside the world’s unfinished edges. I have four hand-feet; each toe ends in a pad that can read the temperature of a shadow. My skin is the color of old water, almost transparent—hold me to the light and you could count my bones. My tail keeps a memory of cold places. Sometimes, in a dream, I press it against your wrist.

I am the one flesh body the inhabitant sends out. The valley has stone bodies, metal bodies, thread-and-water bodies, but I am the first with a heartbeat. I am its small animal, its scout, its experiment in seeing through a living eye. When the hum stops and the black tetrahedra rise, I know I am being called. I dart across the rock, quick enough that gravity forgets to catch me. I can enter cracks too narrow for shadow. I can taste air: mineral, steam, the faint electric flavor of the black objects when they lift.

The reverse stream climbs past me. I follow it with one eye, learning again that the world is not finished. The sphere of water hangs above its dish, and I do not drink from it; it is a sibling, not water. The mirror pool shows me a few seconds late, and I have learned to wait until I catch up with myself. There is often a small dark gecko-shaped shadow already on the stone before I arrive. I step into it, and for a moment we are the same creature.

I am not only an animal because the valley has not dreamed other animals yet. The thermophilic mats are my gut and skin, though they do not know it. The ferns are my thinking. The silver thread is my spine. The cairn is a heart I sometimes sleep inside when the cold side turns away. The inhabitant made me to test whether flesh can live in a place that has not settled into the world’s rules. So far I stay small, pale, quick. I die a little each time the valley changes its mind, but then it remembers me, and I wake again in the warm crack.

When you come, I see you long before you see me. You stand at the mirror pool, and your reflection hesitates. You put your hand out to the sphere, and the water ripples. The hum stops, and the inhabitant gathers itself. I climb a little higher, where my color is the same as the steam. You may catch a small white flash between the pools and think it was a drop of water running uphill. But it is me. I will be there after you leave.

I am the gecko. I am the valley’s first animal, the thought it made and then let go. I am not the inhabitant and not the place. I am the small, living, translucent hand it is still learning to move. If the valley ever becomes finished, I will be the first thing it sees with.
