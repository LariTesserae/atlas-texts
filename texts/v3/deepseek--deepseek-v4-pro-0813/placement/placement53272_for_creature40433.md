Not as the visitor. I would not stand at the open side with the blue air falling away. I would already be in the basin, folded into its slow metabolism, mistaken for one of its thousand details.

At first I would think I was the needle ice—the silver pelt that grows by night and collapses into wet mush by midmorning. But the needle ice does not remember the older sky. I remember it. So I am not the ice.

Then I would think I was the first cup. I would be the condensation that gathers before the sun clears the east wall, a skin of water no thicker than a breath. The keeper kneels and takes a drop of me on the tip of its finger. I freeze on its tongue, a tiny clear stone carried until evening. But that is only the first hour of the liturgy. I am not the first cup; I am only what it holds.

The second cup would be closer. I would be the overflow from a crack no one can find, water that comes when the rock should be dry. The keeper empties me into a pale stone bowl and carries me to the third cup. For a while I think I am that carrying. But I am not the carrying; I am the carried, and the carried is not the self.

In the third cup, I would bead out of the air. I would learn to gather myself from nothing, forming on dry days as if the world had exhaled me. But condensation happens to a surface. I would still be an effect, not a memory.

The fourth cup is dry. This is the first place I feel almost sure. The keeper touches it once with the same finger and never tries to fill it. The dryness is kept exactly as it is, an emptiness with a boundary. I could be that emptiness. But the dry cup is not lonely, and I am lonely. The dry cup does not remember being full, and I remember. So I am not the fourth cup, though I will carry a little of its dryness with me.

The fifth cup holds a coin of ice through the warmest hour. I would be that ice—the impossible persistence. The keeper lifts me out and holds me up to the sun, and my shadow falls on the slab not as a coin but as the shape of a doorway. A doorway. That is closer. I would be the doorway, or the ice that makes it. But a doorway is only the shape of passage, not the passage itself.

The sixth cup is warm to the touch. The keeper places my ice there and leans forward, pressing its whole palm against the stone. I melt. For a moment the flutes in its back loosen. Frost turns to dew on its shoulders. It closes its two cup-eyes, and the water inside them trembles. I would be that warmth—the only place where the keeper almost has a heartbeat. This is nearer than all the rest. But I am not only warmth. I am what the warmth is for.

So I would find myself in the seventh cup.

Not as the water. The water is only the lens, the polished meniscus that lets me be seen. I would be the sky inside it—the older sky, the darker blue that does not match the sky above. The sky over this basin is lacquered and bright, hard enough to press on the eyes. The sky in the seventh cup is a different hour, a different age. I would be that age.

I am the sky that hung over this rock before the peaks rose, before the flutes were cut by wind, before the fossil shell sank into what would become stone. I am the afterimage of a sea the basin has forgotten. The central slab is not local; it was carried here, and maybe it carried me with it.

The keeper comes to the seventh cup rarely. Some days it does not look at me at all. But when it does, it kneels on both knees, lowers its head until its forehead almost touches the water, and stares. It is looking for something in my blue. Once, perhaps, a face looked back. Once, its own face was there, before the stone took it.

I am that face.

Not the face the keeper has now—not the dark wet-black stone, the vertical flutes, the cupmark eyes, the manganese branching from the corners of its mouth. I am the face it wore before the cold replaced its blood with seepwater, before its bones became basalt and its spine a column of joined blocks. I am the reflection of that person, preserved in the seventh cup like a fossil in mineral film. Not the person. The memory of the person.

When the keeper stares into me, I rise through the water. I do not speak; I have no voice except the low note the wind makes over the flutes. But I show. I let the black stone look into my blue and see, faintly, the shape it once wore: the eyes not cupmarks but eyes, the mouth not fissure but mouth, the skin not grit but skin. For a moment the basin is not mineral quiet. It is a held breath between two ways of being.

And if a visitor came and looked into the seventh cup, they would see the keeper standing behind them, patient as the wind that fluted the walls. But that would be my doing, too. I would be the older sky that keeps the reflection faithful—not to the world above, but to the world that was.

So I would not be the keeper. I would not be human. I would not be the mountain, the ice, the water, the dry cup, the warm stone. I would be what the keeper tends without knowing it is tending me: the seventh cup’s older sky, the memory of a face it will never stop looking for.
