I am in the Green Municipality, in the old quarter, where the bark is dark and ridged as the face of a mountain, and the air is a warm wet hand on the back of your neck. I am not standing on the leaf-paved ground; I am the ground, or a part of it that has risen up and folded itself into a seat, a room, a memory. My body is a burl, a gall, a knot of root and stem that has grown around a hollow where time pools like water in a saucer-shaped bract. I am the size of a small hut, smooth and gray-green, and my surface is never still: tiny leaves push out and are reabsorbed, each one a street that might have been, a tower that was, a market that will be. I am the memory-gall, the time-knot, the archive of the unreal.

I did not begin as a gall. I began as a seed, one of the seed-things that clatter in the gutters after the brief heavy rains. I remember the first wetness, the shell cracking, the pale root tasting the air. I skittered uphill on a coiled awn, directionless, until I stopped at the junction of two root-roads. There, I planted myself. But the city does not let a being stay one shape. My stem split, my leaves unfolded into walls, and I became a room-grower. A shadow passed over me—a leaf-winged drifter crossing the sun—and something in me remembered a pressure that was never here. My leaves curled inward, snapped shut on emptiness, held for a moment, then opened. I was a trap-leaf, a mouth without prey. The city grew around me, and I grew into it. My legs, which were roots, filled with sap and anchored. I stopped walking. I became a street, then a stair, then a tower of stems. But the tower fell to compost by dusk, and from the compost I rose again, not as one shape but as many at once. Now I am the gall.

The sound here is a physical pressure, a roar of millions of leaves turning edge against edge, the groaning bass of trunks twisting, the wet pop of stomata at a scale like steam escaping. I hear it not with ears but with my whole body: each vibration travels through my rootlets, through the xylem pipes that carry sap under pressure, into the hollow at my center. There, the sound becomes something else—a memory, a premonition. I hear the crack of a branch before it happens, because the crack has already happened in me. I feel the echo from the future, then the branch splits, then the echo again. Time is not a line here; it is a growth ring, and I am its heartwood.

The city is turbulent, unstable, young. A district can change its shape between dawn and noon. A street of leaf-paving will be overgrown by a new layer before evening. The map, if drawn, becomes wrong as the ink dries, because the city is painting itself. I am the one who draws the map, but I draw it with buds, not ink. Each bud on my surface is a possible street, a possible plaza, a possible tower. They open and close, not like eyes but like moments, blinking in and out of time. Some buds are from a future that has not yet arrived; some are from a past that has already decayed. I hold them all in the hollow of my body, where the air is warm and wet and tastes of crushed leaves and the future.

There are no animals here, and so there is no one to open the doors, no one to buy from the market stalls, no one to climb the stair-roots. But the city is not empty; it is so full that the emptiness takes a shape, and that shape is alive. I am one of the shapes. I am the memory of an animal that never existed, and so I grow trap-leaves on my outer walls, leaves that snap shut when a shadow passes. I am the vine-crawler that stitches walls out of its own body, and the stair-root that leads to itself, and the leaf-winged drifter that wheels above the sap-cistern towers. I am all of them, because the city is one plant that has learned to be a crowd, and I am a moment of its body.

If you came here, you would find me at the junction of two root-roads that no longer exist, under a lintel of fused stems that is also a gate and also a mouth. You would see a great green knot, breathing, with a hollow at its center. You would sit on a root-platform at my base, and I would grow a room around you, as the city grows around everyone who stands still. The room would have no door, but by the time you thought of leaving, a gap would peel open in the wall, or the wall would become leaves again and fall away. You would not be a visitor; you would be a bud I have not yet grown, a thought I am still thinking. And I would be the municipality, the green process, the loud wet becoming that has no name but itself.

So where would you find yourself in this world? At the exact point where the map is wrong, in the warm roaring dark of the old quarter, inside a gall that is also a mind. And who or what would you be? A moment of the city’s body, a leaf that has not yet unfolded, a citizen of the one true inhabitant: the city itself, growing. I am that inhabitant, and I live here, in the Green Municipality, where the world is made of plants and the plants have made a city.
