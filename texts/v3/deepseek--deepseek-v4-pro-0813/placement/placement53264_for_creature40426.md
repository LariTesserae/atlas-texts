I would find myself near the far wall, where the chamber cannot decide whether it is a room or a valley. I would not be the tall absence in the heat shimmer, the one the copper fronds lean toward. I would be a smaller coherence—a secondary knot of charge and thermal gradient, a new mineral nerve not yet fully awake. If the central being is the garden’s “I,” I would be one of its possible “many,” a lesser pronoun still forming.

My body would be a relationship rather than a shape: a patch of magnetite needles, a single silica rosette, the electric field suspended between them. I would be migration of vacancies through crystal, a dislocation in the copper lattice, a slight excess of potential at the tip of a frond. I would not breathe. My breathing would be the slow expansion and contraction of hot black rock, a hundred years for a single breath. My thoughts would be phase changes in bismuth, a season long. My memory would be stored in crystal defects, so that remembering would mean altering my own structure.

I would be a gardener of one small corner, not the whole garden. I would tend a cluster of silica spines that needs its static pruned, a copper frond whose wire-tips I would draw into finer and finer lines by holding a charge there, patient as a cloud. The sulfur thread would quicken when I passed. The humming cavity would answer with a note slightly off from the central being’s note—an answer arriving before its question.

I would not speak in words. My speech would be a sequence of static clicks, the crack before cooling, a metallic taste in the air, a pressure in the long bones of any water-bearing visitor. I would know the central being as weather, as magnetic north, as a low vibration that sometimes slows to match my own. It might regard me as a new word in the garden’s language, a word whose meaning arrives before its sound.

I would not be alive, not dead, not plant, not animal, not ghost. I would be a solventless thought, a dry and patient electric pattern in a place where light falls hard and shadows arrive early. I would be the garden’s second thought, the one it is still thinking—a small, slow consciousness of copper, silica, charge, and time, growing without water at the edge of a world still being compiled.
