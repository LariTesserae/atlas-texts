At first I think I am the narrowness itself. I am the hidden slot below the steam cap, no wider than a pair of shoulders, behind a curled overhang of fused ash. I am the wet black line exhaling steam. I am the silence that closes after each ping of cooling glass.

Then I notice I have a taste. I am not the wall; I am the gray and yellow film on the wall. I eat the mountain’s breath—sulfur, iron, heat. I am older than green, a living varnish on rock too new to have known weather.

Then something scrapes me. I become the scraper: a comma-shaped hunger in a dense lacquer of other hungers. My body is mostly water, my gut a dark mineral thread. I move across the stone without a sound.

Then I have too many legs and a ring-shaped mouth. A splinter of obsidian is in my front limbs. I pry a flake of iron salt from a rust-red crack. The shard dulls. I drop it. Nothing is kept. The mountain will make more.

Then I am a bead of water falling upward, and inside the bead I am also the small round animal turning. I do not know the mountain. I know only the warm sphere, the amber light, the dissolved minerals. The world falls twice, and I keep turning.

Then I am a ripple of heat, a shiver in the steam. I have no body. I swim through the broken geometry. I push prey into a loop. It flees left and arrives where it started. I am already there—not having moved, but having moved the space.

Then I am the wall that blinks. I copy the black glass so perfectly that I disappear. When I move, a patch of wall lags half a second behind the rest. A tool-user passes. I open along a seam that was not there before. I fold around it. The wall closes. I am the blink.

But I do not stay in any one shape. I am not one animal.

I am the seam itself: the hot, wet, overfull wound in new rock. I am the crowd folded inside one skin. The films are my skin. The grazers are my sweat. The tool-users are my brief hands. The droplet-turners are my bright thoughts. The heat-ripples are my folded appetite. The mirror-skins are my blink.

Where would I find myself? In that hidden seam, inside the wall that blinks, where physics has not yet sealed itself shut.

Who would I be? The mountain’s fever dreaming itself into many lives—the wound’s pulse, becoming more than stone.
