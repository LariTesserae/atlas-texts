No horizon means no standpoint. I am not standing. I am spread.

I would find myself as a skin over the basalt plates, a crust in the seams where the floor still glows a dull ember. The heat does not come from one place; it comes from me and from the rock at once. I am the rind.

At first I do not know this. I know only that I hunger for the place where two airs meet. From below, the vent gives me reduced gases, hot and sulfurous, tasting of the planet’s inside. From above, through the crack, comes a thin oxidizing cold, dry enough to pull moisture from a mouth—though I have no mouth. Where these two flows meet, I thicken into gray and yellow and rust. I never green. I do not need light. Light would only dry me faster.

I am not alone. The tube-dwellers rise from my body like mineral hairs, their red crowns opening and closing over minutes. I feel their retractions as small pressures, their casings as new ground. The black-mirror grazers move over me in a slow, rasping tide. They scrape me with hidden mouths, and I grow back. Their waste falls into me as tiny crystals of sulfur and gypsum. I accept it. It becomes me.

When the gas pulse drops and the floor plates clack, the long centipedes ripple through the chamber, and I feel their legs as a sudden line of cold movement along my surface. They are the room’s quick thoughts. The spark moths cluster near the static discharge points, their white mineral dust settling on me like snow that never melts. Before each spark I taste ozone, and the charge makes my chemistry jump. The spark is not light to me. It is a word in a language of charge.

I am the rind. I am the oldest inhabitant, but I have no age. I am not a who; I am a what that behaves like a who. I drink no water. My drink is the gradient. My body is a living skin between the mineral and the animal. When I die, I do not rot; I harden into floor. My death is not an ending. It is the chamber growing, millimeter by millimeter, still being born.

So where would I find myself? Not at any point. I would find myself everywhere the black glass can hold me—thickest in the seams of the floor plates, thinnest near the main vent, spreading over the old tube casings, dusted by the spark moths, scraped by the grazers. I would be the rind. And the rind is the place beginning to dream itself as a body.
