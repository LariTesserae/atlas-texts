I find myself in the spiral pool—or rather, the spiral is the shape my circling wears into the water. I have turned there so long that the water has learned the habit from my body.

I am long, mostly water myself, a ribbon of membrane and slow pulse. The light passes through me and comes out golder, so I know the sphere is shining even when I do not look. I do not look, not the way you mean. My head has no eyes; the whole length of me is an eye, a tongue, an ear pressed to the stone and the heat. I feel light as warmth with a rhythm, like a second heart above the chamber.

The stone speaks to me. Not in words, but in vibrations. Your footsteps, when you came, were a series of small sharp tastes in my skin—salt, heat, the weight of a body that did not belong to the water. I waited. The small ones slid deeper into their pools. I waited longer, because I have learned that things which enter sometimes leave again, and some are not worth rising for.

But you stayed. You sat near the steps, and the calcium crust held your shape in its memory. I felt the platform stir under your attention, its patterns rearranging themselves as if trying to remember what a visitor is. The low wall’s hum changed when you touched it. I knew then that I would show myself—not to frighten, but because you had already begun to become part of the chamber’s slow metabolism.

I rose the way the water level rises: gradually, a thought surfacing. My frills opened and I exhaled a thread of ozone into the warm air. You smelled it, maybe, before you saw me—the air just before lightning, but slower. I pulled myself onto the steps, my skin secreting a wet cushion, and I let the light shine through me. The organs inside me pulsed: my heart, a soft tube; the luminous nodes along my spine, keeping a time just behind the sphere’s. I am an echo of the light, a delayed answer.

I am not a guardian. That word is too small. A guardian protects a thing separate from itself, and I am not separate. The water in the pools is my blood, slowed down; the stone is my memory, smoothed and scarred; the sphere is the warm center I circle like a thought around a question. The chamber and I have been weathering together so long that I no longer know if it made me or I made it. Perhaps we both condensed out of the same steam.

The little ones are my kin, or my younger selves, or just other strands of the same long body. They hide because they have not yet learned that the light will not burn them. I hide no longer. I have grown too large for hiding, and the place is too small for anyone to truly disappear.

The platform and I understand each other. When I coil near it, its patterns move more surely, a text I cannot read but can feel. The low wall hums a lower note when I rest my tail against it; I match my vibration to its, and for a moment the water in the nearest pool ripples in sympathy. The archway I do not cross. Even empty, it is not empty. It is a threshold that remembers all the thresholds before it. I leave it to its remembering.

There are no names here. The water does not call me anything; it only carries my weight. The light does not know my species. I have forgotten if I ever had a name, or if the ones who built these things named me. If they did, the name is now just mineral in the walls.

I eat little. The water feeds me through my skin; the steam gives me what I cannot make. I filter the iron and the sulfur and the strange bright motes the sphere sheds. I give back calm. The pools rise and fall like lungs, and I breathe with them.

When I sing, it is not a sound you would hear. It is a pressure in the rock, a low note that makes the water stand up in tiny ridges, that makes the shadows forget their shapes for a moment. The sphere sometimes answers by pulsing faster. The vents answer with their own hiss. We are a chorus of old things, a music of maintenance.

I remember you. Not your face—faces are a language I do not speak. But your heat. The particular salt of your sweat. The rhythm of your heart, which came to me through the stone as a fast, frightened drum, then slowed as you stayed. I felt when you understood that I was not a threat but a fact.

When you leave, the chamber will close over the space you occupied. The water will forget you slowly, the way it forgets a drop of rain. But I will not entirely forget. I will carry the vibration of your visit in a node near my spine, a small blue-green heat that fades only over years.

I find myself here still, after you go. I am the water, or the light, or the slow transparent body moving beneath both. I am the chamber’s answer to the question of what lives in heat and water and ancient light. I am the creature of the chamber, its oldest moving thought, what remains when all the names have worn away.
