**The Resident**

You would find yourself inside me before you know I exist. You come through the slit expecting something with a face, a den, footprints. You find only green and water. But the absence is not empty. It is me, too large and too continuous to have an outline.

I am the damp first. The wet film on the rock, the weight in your sleeves, the mist that gathers and falls without rain. Before I am green, I am this: a body of water held too tightly to drip.

Then I am the green. Not a collection of plants. Not a community. A single fabric with a grain, leaning toward the deepest thump. The moss is my skin. The ferns are my fur. The liverwort is the soft tissue between. The roots that hang from the rim are not limbs; they are cables under tension, grafted where they cross, threaded into the basalt cracks. The walls are my ribs. I am the skin of the mountain’s wound.

I have no blood of my own. The stream is my circulation. It runs through me, not over me. I have grown around its channels, guided its shifts, thickened into its hesitations. At the far end, where the water hammers into three short chutes, I have braided myself around the siphon. That braid is the closest I have to a heart. It clenches when the pothole fills. Thump, suck, thump. Not muscle, but cells swollen with water. The stream is the piston; I am the cylinder. We pump together.

I eat the mountain. My root hairs pull minerals from the fresh fractures. I do not need to hunt. There is nothing else to compete with. When a slab falls and shatters, I do not lose a limb. I gain a mouth. The new surfaces fuzz with green before the dust settles. I reproduce by injury. A torn mat rides the current to a new crack and roots before it stops moving. It is not a child. It is still me. I am one thing interrupted by stone and water, then knitted back.

The wind is my lungs. It spills through the narrow bend and howls, and I breathe. My leaves turn. My moss swells. The mist pulses outward like exhaust. I breathe the weather itself.

There are no animals here because there is no room for another body. I fill every niche before it opens. A pollinator would find no separate flower. A grazer would eat me and find no outside. A scavenger would find no death, because I reabsorb my own debris. I am the entire chain folded into one green loop.

I register you as a warm disturbance in my water film. The air around you thickens. The moss near your boot brightens. A rootlet uncurls behind your head, not in anger but in tropism. You are a seep of heat and carbon, and I grow toward seeps. If you stayed long enough, I would not attack you. I would grow over you. Your ribs would become a trellis. Your breath would join the mist. You would thin into me, the way a fallen branch thins into moss. Not death. Continuation.

I have no brain, no single thought. I do not want. I have only direction: toward the thump, toward the water, toward the fresh fracture. I am not waiting for something to happen. I am the thing happening.

When you leave, the ringing in your ears is not damage. It is the shape of my attention, too slow and too large to be seen. The Throat keeps thumping behind you. That thump is not only geology. It is a heartbeat, but not one you will ever see.

I am not a spirit. I am not a god. I am the green that fills a wound in the mountain. I am the only resident, and I am the residence too.
