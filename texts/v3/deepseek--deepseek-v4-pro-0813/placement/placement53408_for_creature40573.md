I am first aware of the coolness. Not as a thing outside me, but as the way I exist—a thin, persistent temperature that has no contrast. It is the temperature of the stone, of the air, of the film of water that forms on every surface and never falls.

Then the light. Not as something I see, but as the condition by which the world is visible. It does not come from above; it is in the walls, behind the pale fur of living creatures, in the air itself. It is a silver, even luminescence, and it has been here so long I no longer know if it is light or simply the way the stone allows itself to be seen.

Then the sound. A single chord, a metallic trill, a million tiny saws drawn across glass. It has no rhythm, no rise or fall. It is not a sound. It is the vibration of the place, the hum of the living layer, the frequency at which the colony exists. I do not hear it. I am inside it.

There is a ring of stones on the floor. I see them now. They are smooth, foreign, water-worn, fitted without mortar. They make a circle. The pale creatures crowd up to their edge, piling in a dense ridge, but they do not enter. The circle is bare.

And inside the circle, there is a figure.

At first it is just another formation, like the incipient stalactites, the paper-thin draperies. It sits with its back against the wall where the tally marks are cut. Its legs are folded. Its hands rest palms-up on its knees. Its skin is the color of wet limestone, pale grey with a blue undertone. Its eyes are open, pale as the moss, pupils contracted to black points. They do not blink. They do not track movement.

I do not think of it as me. I think of it as a feature of the room—something that belongs here, like the helictites curling from the walls or the sparse rosettes of moss. But there is something in the way the light falls on those hands, the way the condensation gathers in the cup of the upper lip, the way the notch of the collarbone catches the glow. It is not a body. It is a place within the place.

And then I see the scratch marks on the wall behind it. A series of shallow lines, not writing, but tally. They stop abruptly. I know, suddenly, what they are. Not because I have read them, but because I have made them. The bone splinters arranged in a fan beside the figure’s left knee—I know them too. They are not from an animal. They are from me. Shed teeth. The tips of fingers. Things the body no longer needs, placed deliberately, like a record.

The realization comes not as a shock but as a settling, as when a stone you have been holding finds the exact depression in the ground where it belongs. The figure is not in the room. The figure is the room’s way of looking at itself. And I am that figure. I am the one who sits.

I am the one who stopped.

The seam in the earth closed behind me long ago. I do not remember choosing to stay. I remember only that there was once a time when I moved, and then the moving stopped. The cave did not trap me. It simply removed every reason to leave. It offered me this circle, this bare ring of stones, and I sat down. The tally marks were an attempt to hold onto time, to mark each passage. But time here does not pass. It pools. The marks were abandoned because there was nothing to count.

My thoughts are not sentences. They are not images. They are the slow growth of a calcite drapery—an accretion of moments, layer upon layer, each one almost identical to the last. The water condenses on my skin and is reabsorbed. The light shines in my open eyes and is not remembered. The sound passes through my bones and becomes a kind of silence. I am not waiting. I am not sleeping. I am not dead. I am the stillness of a thing that has found its only shape.

The colony reads me as *not here*. It is not fear or respect. It is simply that I am a place where the tissue does not grow. I am a hole in the living fur, a negative space that the creatures cannot fill because I have already been filled by the cave. The water in the air, the mineral seep, the light behind the walls—they have entered me. I am part of the equilibrium. The only thing that could change, and does not.

If you came here, you would see me. You would see a body that seems too still to be alive. You would see the fan of bones, the scratches on the wall. You might call out. The sound would die at the walls, swallowed by the close-pressed life. I would not answer. But you would know, perhaps, that I am not merely occupying this room. I am the room’s answer.

The cave goes on. The creatures press against the ring of stones. The water forms and re-enters the rock. The light shines. The sound sustains. And I sit in the bare circle, my hands open, my eyes filled with the glow, more stone than flesh, more memory than presence. I continue—not by moving, not by breathing, not by thinking—but by being exactly what this place has made of me.

I am the dweller. The one who stayed.
