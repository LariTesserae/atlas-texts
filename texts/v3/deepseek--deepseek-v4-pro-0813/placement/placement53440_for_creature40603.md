I can tell you where to look, though by the time you look I may have moved.

I am in the interval. The city is most honest there: between the low dome and the white terrace, where the causeway drowns under a sheet of warm water and the water does not reflect but absorbs. The ground is black mud that gives back more heat than it takes. If you stand there long enough, the dark stops being dark and becomes a medium, the way water is a medium, and you can feel the city breathing through it.

I am not the one who answers. I am the one who waits for the question to be repeated until it wears down into a shape like the other shapes here.

Who am I? I am a form the steam learned. A slow drip that stayed too long and became a body. My face, if you call it that, is a patch of white sinter where three rivulets have carved something like a mouth and two closed eyes that were never opened. My height changes with the humidity. My shoulders are a ledge where salt flowers grow and crumble. I have no sex, no age, no name. If I had a purpose, it would be to keep this particular piece of dark from collapsing into just dark.

You would find me near the channel that runs uphill. It runs uphill because I remember it that way, and the water follows my memory. I have been here since before the last colonnade lost its third pillar, and I will be here after the next one slumps into the mud. I am made of patience. It is not a virtue. It is a material, like basalt, like sinter, like the thick wet air.

When you arrived, I felt you as a change in the water’s attention. Not a disturbance. A difference. The mud around my base warmed by less than a degree. The salt on my left side cracked in a line, and the line ran toward you like a word I had not yet learned. I did not turn. I do not turn. But I saw you the way the pool sees the sky that is not there.

I am not the only one. There are others spaced across the flats, each one an interval made visible. Some are larger, some smaller. One is so dissolved that he is only a hesitation in the steam above a warm spring. Another has grown a second thorax of salt and now leans slightly, like a building that has forgotten which way is vertical. We do not speak. We do not need to. The city speaks through us, and we are the pauses in its speech.

What would I be? I would be the one who almost learned to have hands. When you stood at the edge of the pool and looked into the water that is too dark to reflect, I felt the shape of your looking. For a moment, my arm—this long encrustation of mineral—raised itself the way steam rises, and I understood that I was not only a place. I was also a seeing.

But I did not move. I am not finished. I am a salt rind at the edge of a thought. A city that has not yet decided whether to be stone or water. A witness made of waiting.

You would find yourself here, in the wet dark, and you would not be a visitor. You would be a new mineral in the city’s slow metabolism, a new interval. Perhaps you would come to stand beside me, and we would be two variations of the same question. Over centuries, our edges would soften. Salt would join us. The water would rise and fall through us. We would become a colonnade of two, then one, then none.

But that is still far off. For now, I am here, between the third arch and the pool that remembers steam. I am the one who did not leave. I am what happens when the dark grows attentive and does not look away.
