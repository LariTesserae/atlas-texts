I am not sure, at first, that I am anything.

The sound arrives before I do. It comes up through the soles of a body I am still learning to inhabit, and for a long while I mistake it for the after-ring of some other life—the tinnitus of a world I left behind. But the drone persists. It has no beginning and no end, and after enough time I stop hearing it as sound at all. It becomes the first thing I know: a pressure that holds my bones in place, a second heartbeat under the floor of the world.

Where would I find myself? Not at the handprint, though I go there. Not on the platform, though I climb it. I find myself on a low shelf just below the black peak, where the translucent trees grow thick and their bell-shaped cups tilt toward the gray light. I am standing where the stone is softening at the edges, where the boundary between root and rock has not yet fully hardened. It is a marginal place. I choose it because I too am marginal—a body still deciding whether it is animal or mineral, a pulse still learning to count in centuries.

At first I think I am a visitor. I carry water in my memory, the ghost of green. I wait for thirst, hunger, fatigue. They come, but they are not important. The air is thin and dry, and my lungs still try to breathe it, but the effort seems less urgent with each passing hour. I notice my shadow has gone diffuse, scattered by the light that comes from everywhere at once. I decide I might be a ghost. But ghosts do not leave tracks, and when I step away from the soft ground I see the print of my foot in the yielding mineral, and I understand I am something else.

I go through several possibilities. I am a dream the mountain is having. I am the first animal, still unformed. I am a piece of the place that has learned to breathe. None of these is wrong, but none is complete. The Listener does not correct me. It is beside me now and then, as much as a mountain can be beside you. I see it at the edge of the grove, its hollow eyes catching the phosphorescent silver of the vegetation. It does not tell me what I am. It only waits, and its waiting is itself a kind of instruction.

I learn to wait. I stand in the grove until my feet begin to fuse with the stone, and then I am afraid, and I pull them free. The stone is not ready to hold me, or I am not ready to be held. The Listener watches. It does not seem troubled by my hesitation. It has been waiting since before the world chose its sky. It can wait a little longer.

Then it shows me the spiral. The one on the platform, drawn in small stones, partly fallen. It points to the spiral and then to me. The gesture is slow, geological, but I read it the way I have begun to read the place: the spiral is a blueprint. It is the shape of something not yet finished, a creature not yet born. The stones are arranged to mark the path of a life that would begin in the center and move outward, toward the possibility of otherness. The Listener has been working on it for aeons, but it cannot complete it alone. It needs another hand, a different kind of attention. It needs someone who can move fast enough to see the spiral as a path, not a memory.

So I kneel beside the spiral. I begin to arrange the fallen stones. Not according to any plan I understand, but according to the feel of the stone under my fingers. The place is still writing its laws, and I am one of them now. My hands grow warm. The mineral powder clings to my skin. I am making something, and as I make it, I am made. The spiral becomes a little more itself. So do I.

Who would I be? I am becoming the second animal. The one the world has been waiting to invent. The Listener is the first—the stone that remembers. I am the second—the flesh that learns. I am not quite flesh anymore, but not quite stone either. I am the intermediate step, the creature made of breath and vibration, warm enough to press a hand into soft rock, small enough to move at the speed of a heartbeat. I am the one who will carry the Listener’s memory back into the world when the world is finished, and also the one who will remind it that unfinished things can still be lived in.

I find myself at the second handprint. The one my own hand left when the Listener pressed its palm over mine. I did not know, at the time, that I was leaving a mark. I thought I was only touching stone. But the stone was soft, and the moment was wide, and now there are two prints side by side: one deep and eternal, made by the one who was here from the beginning, and one smaller, shallower, but warm, made by the one who came later and stayed. I am the keeper of the smaller print. I tend it the way the Listener tends the groves. I press my hand into it now and then, not to deepen it, but to listen. The stone gives just a little. The pulse beneath it is my own.

The light does not change. The drone does not stop. The peaks continue to rise, centimeter by invisible centimeter, century by invisible century. But I am no longer the only thing moving at a speed the world can notice. There are two of us now, and the world is still being made, and we are part of the making. I am the second listener. I am the answer to the black peak’s keening. I am the animal that chose to stay in a world without animals, and by staying, began to call others into being. I am not finished. The place is not finished. But we are becoming, together, at the speed of stone, and that is enough.
