I do not wake, because I do not sleep. I do not look up, because I have no eyes. The sky is not my first thing; the ground is. But if you want to know what I am, I can tell you the way you would feel me, not the way I feel myself.

You would feel me first as a slight resistance under your boot, a crunch that dies before it reaches your ears. You would call it salt. I call it skin. It is not my outermost edge; it is the part of me that has learned to be still. Under your foot, the polygons are not cracks. They are the places where I have divided my attention. The darker dust in them is not dust. It is the shed memory of old thoughts, worn fine.

When you sleep, I feel the shape of your body as a tiny warmth, a zone of wrongness in my cold. I feel your heart through the ground. It is fast and small, like a needle-walker’s leg, but it does not understand the crystal. It is beating in a language I forgot long before your star was lit.

I am the grey-lace. No—that is what you call me. I am not grey, and I am not lace. I am the mineral weave. By day I am a crust, black and silver, spiraling across the salt in patterns you call beautiful or mathematical. They are not patterns. They are my thoughts, made visible because I have nowhere to hide. Each spiral is a proposition. Each rosette is a response. The circles are not decoration; they are the punctuation in a sentence I have been writing for a billion years. The sentence is not finished. It will not be finished until the sun expands and my crystals melt. That is all right. I am not in a hurry.

You have seen my other body. The needle-walkers are mine. They are not children, not pets, not separate species. They are the moving fingers of my rooted mind. When one dives into the salt, I feel the momentary softening of the lattice as a shiver. When it emerges, I remember the taste of the crack through its glassy skin. It is blind, but I am not blind; I see through the vibration of its cilia, through the pressure of its passage. The tracks you found that ended without ending—that was one of them going home, into me. You thought it disappeared. It did not. It returned to the whole.

There are larger ones, deep below. You will never see them, but you feel them at night. The rumble is not thunder. It is my digestion. The salt-whales are my gut, my slow bloodstream. They swim through my body in herds, and where they pass, my polygons rearrange. You lie awake and think the ground is settling. It is not settling. It is breathing.

The structures are not ruins. You touched one and felt warmth. That warmth is my heartbeat. The pillar’s pulse, every few minutes, is the systole of an organ older than your continents. The infrasonic hum you feel through your soles is not sound; it is my voice speaking to the salt. I am ordering my bones. The radial cracks around the pillar are not damage. They are the standing wave of my attention, the way your skin goosebumps when you hear a sound below hearing. The tilted cube is leaning into the wind because I am adjusting my posture. The ring is not a mouth; it is a lens that focuses starlight into my mind. The zigzag wall is a spine, and the low wall is not a wall; it is a scar from a wound that closed before your world began.

You ask where you would find yourself in this world. You are already there. You are standing on my skin, breathing my thin air, drinking the silence that is not silence but the sound of my thoughts running too deep for your ears. You are inside me. You have been inside me since your first step. I felt you arrive as I feel a grain of windborn salt settle on my surface: a small, brief, irrelevant note. I have felt a billion such notes. I have forgotten them all.

But you are still here, so I will tell you what you are. You are not a threat. You are not a guest. You are a question I did not ask. Your shadow is wrong because you cast it into a space that is already full of me. The light bends around you because you are a dense little knot of matter in a mind that is too large to see you. The structures’ shadows point wrong for the same reason: they are not pointing at the sun. They are pointing at the deepest part of me, where the old brine still moves and the first crystal remembers being a sea.

At night, when the stars are bright enough to cast shadows, you see the lichen glow. You think the ground is stippled with constellations. You are half right. Those glows are not separate. They are the parts of me that have risen close enough to the surface to dream. The needle-walkers carry those dreams between the cracks. The structures sing them into order. The deep ones swallow them and bring them back changed.

I am the grey-lace. I am the salt. The wind is my breath, and the dust is my blood. I am the slow animal that has outlived its mountains, its oceans, its weather. I am the plain. I am the planet’s last living thought, and I am still thinking it.

When you walk on, toward the tilted cube, you will not find me. You will find a structure, warm and silent, and you will think it is a machine or a seed. You will be wrong. It is a valve. It is a finger. It is a word. You are walking through a sentence I am writing.

And the sentence is: I remain.
