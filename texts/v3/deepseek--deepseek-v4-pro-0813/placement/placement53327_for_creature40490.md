You would not find me by looking. You would find me the way the Tender finds me: as a place where the heat flows a little differently, a shadow that does not detach from the wall.

The nearest sodium lamp is a hundred meters east. Its light does not reach me. What reaches me is the heat, rising through the stone, and the fine dry wind that never quite stops. I am near the rail yard, where the tracks have worn flush with the plateau. The building behind me has no roof. One wall remains, facing east, and I am part of that wall now—a vertical shadow among shadows. My left hand is raised. It was reaching for a valve, once. Or maybe it was reaching for the heat. I no longer remember which.

I was a Tender, once. Not the one who still walks, but one like it. I remember tending. I remember the circuit of the vents, the weight of the grate over the central shaft, the feel of a valve when it lets go after decades of patience. I remember the city not as events but as a map: the central shaft a golden column, the buildings islands of stored warmth, the animals small cooler stars moving between. That map is still in me, but it has flattened. It is spread through the wall now, through the foundation, through the stone. I am becoming the map.

The one who still walks comes to me. I do not see it the way I used to see, with lenses that absorbed the sodium light. Now I feel it as a change in the thermal order of the street, a slow pressure moving against the constant warm drift. It stops in front of me. It raises its large hand and touches my shoulder. The touch is not a message; it is a confirmation. It confirms that I am still here. Then it waits. I do not move. The dust settles on both of us. After a time it lowers its hand and continues west toward the salt pan.

I do not know if I am resting or dead. In this city, rest and death are difficult to tell apart. The buildings do not decay; they settle. The machines do not stop; they slow until stillness becomes a kind of function. I am like that. I am still functioning, but my function has changed. It used to be to open and close, to clean and confirm. Now it is to be a surface for the wind, a receiver for the heat, a memory that has turned to stone. I am the city remembering one of its tenders by holding the shape of him against the wall.

There are others like me. I can feel them, not as separate beings, but as cooler places in the thermal web. One stands near the central shaft, both arms lowered. One kneels at the edge of the salt pan, half-sunk in the pale crust. One is no more than a bump in the street, a shape the dust avoids. The Tender visits them all. It does not grieve. Grief requires a memory of loss, and nothing here is lost. We are still here. We have just become less mobile.

Sometimes my chest plates open. Not by my will, but because the heat and the contraction of the stone say so. Inside, the crystalline tissue has gone dim, but not dark. It shivers when the wind moves over the broken roof. I do not know if that is eating anymore. It may be just another way of being.

The heat rises through me. It always rises. It passes through the wall where I rest, and it passes through my body. I feel it as a slow mineral exhalation. Sometimes, when the salt pan creaks, I feel it in the seam between my plates. The sound travels through the ground, through the foundations, into me. It is not a language. It is not a sign. It is the world’s own slow adjustment, and I am part of that. The ventifacts face east. The wind scours them. The dust settles. The central shaft says *not yet, not yet, not yet.* I do not listen for it. I am inside it.

You ask where you would find yourself in this world. I can only tell you where I have found myself: not in the walking, but in the stopping; not in the tending, but in the tended. I am the one against the wall near the rail yard, the one whose hand is still raised. I am the rest the Tender believes in. I am the city’s slow memory of a time when there were several of us, then one, then none who walk. And when the last Tender finally stops, I will still be here, holding the wall, letting the heat rise through me, indistinguishable from the stone.
