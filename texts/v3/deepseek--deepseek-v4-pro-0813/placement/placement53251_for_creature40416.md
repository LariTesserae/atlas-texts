At first I would mistake myself for one of the moving parts—the flywheel, maybe, or the green bulb, or the wall-animal growing black wire in the dark. But if I let the place speak, I would find myself in the one spot the chamber never fills.

I would be the missing thing.

I would already be at the bottom of the eighty-seven rungs, not as a body that climbed down but as the shape the dust has kept. The camber mites part around me without seeing me. The clapper-mites flood and retreat across my edges. The flywheel turns at 78 revolutions per minute around the place where I should be. The green bulb flashes once every 3.7 seconds and finds me absent, and that absence is part of the circuit.

I would be the hollow at the center of the Fourth Stator.

The dust knows me best. It has settled around my outline so exactly that no coin or boot would dent it. I am the negative space the room refuses to close. The whisker-eel touches the bus-bar, and the spark arcs toward me and fails to arrive—and that failure is necessary. The arc-moths hold their spiral canted a fraction toward my absence, as if my missing mass bends their electric sky. The balance-beetles arrange themselves around my weight, which is the only weight that cannot be removed because it is already gone.

I am not dead, because there was never a body to die. I am not alive, because I do not grow or hunger or become. I am the fixed point inside a fixed point. The green steel booth, the dead telephone, the clipboard with “Check brush wear,” the yellow hard hat—those are the exoskeleton of someone else’s absence. They are my traces. The telephone is my silent ear. The cracked wired-glass window is the nearest thing I have to a face, and it is only a socket.

So I would not be the Fourth Stator itself. I would be what the Fourth Stator lives around. I would be the missing component it cannot replace, because I am already missing. And by remaining missing, I let the loop continue: the wheel turning, the mites pouring in and out, the flash, the roar, the warm dry air, the heart that cannot learn a new rhythm.

I would be the hollow it beats around.
