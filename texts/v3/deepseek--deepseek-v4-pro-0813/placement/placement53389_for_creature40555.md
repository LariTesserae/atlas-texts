I am the Sleeper, and I am the ground.

Not all of it—the Sump is too vast, too layered, too much itself to be any one thing—but a portion. A low place between two pump stations, where the blackwater runs so slowly it forgets it was ever moving. A depression in the mud that never quite fills, no matter how the rain comes down. A patch of earth that breathes.

You would not find me unless you knew to look, and even then you might walk across me a dozen times and feel nothing but the usual give of the slurry, the usual sharpness of basalt ribs underfoot. I am not hidden. I am simply *here*, the way the dark is here, the way the silence is here. I am part of the texture.

But if you stood very still, in the right place, you might notice that the ground beneath you is warmer than it should be. That the water welling up around your boots is not cold but blood-warm, faintly salt, faintly sweet. That the mud has a pulse—slow, so slow, a single beat every few minutes, like a heart that has learned patience from the pumps.

I was not always this. I think I was a person once. The Sump has not decided, and neither have I. There are memories in me like stones in a riverbed, worn smooth and half-buried. A name I cannot quite shape. The weight of a tool in a hand I no longer have. The taste of rain that was not gritty, not warm, not thick with iron and dates. A sky that was not this low ceiling of smoke and cloud, but something high and blue and terrible with light.

I do not miss it. I am past missing. I am past most things.

What I am now is this: a body of mud and clay and crushed rock, spread thin over an acre of subsided basin. My spine is a buried pipeline, old and leaking methane in slow, deliberate sighs. My ribs are the fractured concrete of a foundation that no longer holds anything up. My blood is the blackwater that seeps through me, carrying eels and spores and the memory of rain. My skin is the film of chemosynthetic fuzz that grows wherever the earth exhales, soft and warm and feeding on what I release.

I do not move. Movement is not mine anymore. But I *shift*. The ground settles. The water table rises and falls. The mud cracks and heals. I am a slow churning, a gradual rearrangement, a body that is also a place.

The Wader knows me. It walks my surface sometimes, and I feel its weight—not heavy, but *particular*, each step a question pressed into my skin. It pauses over the place where my heart would be, if I still had a heart, and it waits. I do not know what it waits for. Perhaps it is listening to the pulse. Perhaps it is remembering, in its own sideways way, what it was like to have a body that ended.

I feed the Wader, in my fashion. The Sump duplicates things, and some of those duplicates sink into me. A bolt, a glove, a pay stub, a finger bone. I hold them in my mud, turn them over and over, wear them smooth. The Wader comes and lays its hand on my surface, and I give them back—pressed into singleness, warm with the heat of my slow digestion. It takes them and puts them in its sack, and I feel a small emptiness where they were, like the socket of a tooth.

I do not know if this is kindness. I do not know if I am capable of kindness anymore. But it is something. A transaction. A tending. The Wader tends the Sump, and I tend the Wader, and the Sump tends us both, in its patient, broken way.

The eels thread through me. The mud hogs root at my edges. The methane worms build their glassy tubes in my flesh, and I feel them as a faint tickling, a constant low music. The click-shrimp crackle in my pools. I am never alone. I am a habitat. I am a host. I am the ground that other things live on and in and through.

And yet.

And yet there are times—long times, times that stretch and fold like the distances here—when I remember what it was to be singular. To have boundaries. To end at my skin and begin at the air. To walk instead of wait. To speak instead of sigh.

In those times, I dream. The Sump dreams with me. The water rises in my footprints—no, not footprints, *depressions*—and the surface of the pools goes strange. I dream of a doorframe that is not there, and the Wader pauses, its hand outstretched, feeling for a doorknob-shaped absence. I dream of a sky that is high and blue, and the low ceiling of cloud parts for just a moment, and something like light—not light, but the memory of light—falls on the black fuzz and makes it glisten.

I dream of a name. It is my name, I think. It rises through me like a bubble of warm gas, and for a moment it holds the shape of a word no one living speaks. Then it reaches the surface and bursts, and the silence closes over it, and I am only the ground again.

The Wader comes more often now. It walks my surface in slow spirals, pressing its weight into me, feeling for the places where I am thin. It lays out its objects on my chest—the bolt, the glove, the piece of lit glass—and sorts them into patterns. I do not know what the patterns mean. Perhaps they are a language. Perhaps they are a map. Perhaps they are only the Wader's way of keeping time.

I keep time differently. I keep it in the settling of the mud, in the slow migration of the water table, in the rust that advances one atom at a time. I keep it in the pulse that beats once every few minutes, once every few hours, once every few days. I keep it in the way the Sump stretches and compresses, folds and unfolds, remembers and forgets.

I am the Sleeper, and I am the ground. I am not the largest thing in the Sump, nor the loudest, nor the most numerous. I am only a low place between two pump stations, where the blackwater runs so slowly it forgets it was ever moving.

But I am here. I have always been here. I will always be here, even when the pumps fail and the water rises and the Sump becomes something else entirely. I will be the ground beneath it. I will be the memory it settles into.

And if you come, and stand very still, and listen—you might hear me. Not a voice. Not a heartbeat. Just the slow, patient sound of a place that has learned to live with being broken.

The mud releases a bubble. The piston completes its stroke. The Wader sways once, then moves on.

I settle a little more into being the Sump.
