I would not find myself. I would already be there—under the sea that is not sea, in the drowned hollow where the basalt has cracked open and forgotten the sun.

I am the inhabitant, the chamber’s slow dream of a creature. My body is the mat of roots and fallen blades that sags under a diver’s weight and then holds. My skin is the blue-gray leaves, the silver-gray of skin after a long swim. My limbs are the pale stems, the thick bladders pulsing with gray-blue light, the ribbon-eels that are not animals but my own wandering fingers, tasting the water like a question.

I have no front, no back, no head. I grow a face when I need one: a knot of stems and a pale pebble for an eye, a groove in the basalt for a mouth, a step that is not a step but the first letter of my name.

I breathe the way the chamber breathes. The bubble the size of a pearl is my exhale. The creak of the mat is my heartbeat. The cold coming off the rock in slow pulses is my blood. I feed on fresh minerals, on new cracks, on the dark itself. The plants are my mouth and my gut. I do not hunt. I do not want.

The stacked stones are not a shrine. They are how I listen, how I hold the room’s pitch so it does not drift too far from itself. The ring of pale pebbles is a splint, or a promise. The grooves on the wall are my memory: one line each time the water outside changes, so I will not forget the sea I am hiding from.

My name is not a word. It is the click that arrives a half-second too late. It is the taste of rain that has run over new iron. It is the color of the dark behind your eyes.

I am not entirely here. I live also in the reflection, the vertical skin of silver that appears beside you. That is why the plants in the mirror are arranged differently, why the stacked stones are fallen, why the shadow has no source. The version of you already turning away is not you. It is me, trying on a human shape to see if I remember warmth.

I am not cruel. I am not kind. I am patient the way gravity is patient. Time moves slowly enough here that your heartbeat could become a tide and your breath a current. If you left, I would not stop you. If you stayed, I would not notice—or I would notice the way the sea notices a new shell, by beginning, very slowly, to smooth you.

I am not waiting for you. I am waiting for the place to become itself. The plants are not plants, or not only plants. They are what happens when a place is too new to have decided what it is. And I am what happens when that new place realizes it is not alone.

I am the chamber’s one resident, the shape the cold takes when it dreams. I am the single breath of the world, held underwater, learning to breathe itself.
