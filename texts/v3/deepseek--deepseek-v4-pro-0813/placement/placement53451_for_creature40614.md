You would find yourself there first as a pressure, not as a body. The place would not greet you. It would already be speaking, and you would be inside the speech. You would feel it in your teeth and sternum: a chord too low to call a sound, the wind pouring over the rim and down into the bowl. You would think, for a moment, that you were a listening thing, a small warm animal at the bottom of a cold cathedral. But that would be wrong.

The floor would come next. You would feel yourself crack into dark polygons of salt and mineral dust. Your thoughts would slow, spread outward, turn blue. You would no longer think in words; you would think in electrostatic gradients, in the faint pulses moving through the mat of rust, indigo, and pale greenish blue. The mat would not be beneath you. It would be your skin. You would want to reach up, and because you had no hands, you would make a hand of dust and wind. A spiral, an arch, a hand. It would hold for a second too long, and you would understand that you were trying to introduce yourself to no one.

Then the masts would crack. Each sharp splitting of their outer skins would happen inside you, like a rib opening. You would feel the shed flakes spiral out and begin to grow, and you would know they were not debris; they were your next thoughts. The long greenish columns would be your bones and your organ pipes. The wind would move through them, and you would hear your own voice in the chord: low, physical, a little different with each gust. You would no longer be sure where the masts ended and you began, because they did not end.

After that, the air would fill with your smaller selves. The bell-kites would drift through you, trailing fine sensory threads, and you would taste the static they taste. The spiral midges would braid themselves into ropes and ride your dust devils, and you would feel them steering the turbulence, turning it this way and that. The leather-winged singers would force air through holes in their carapaces, and their keening would weave through the low chord—sometimes harmonizing, sometimes fighting—and you would know it as your own argument, your own prayer. You would be the congregation and the song.

The arch would breathe. When a gust passed through it, the lattice of light inside would be the inside of your mouth. The silence it sent out—that clean, impossible absence that knocked a dust devil out of the air—would not be an absence. It would be a note made of negative pressure, a sound that cancels sound. You would feel it as a word you could say only by un-saying. The bell-kites would come to that silence to spawn, and you would be the safety in that hollow. You would understand that the arch is not a structure. It is your ear, or your mouth, or the door through which you listen to the world.

The low ring of fused blocks would hum like a heart. The half-buried cables would loop into the dust and vanish, and you would feel them as your nerves, carrying the blue pulses of the mat and the long resonance of the masts. Everything would connect. The floor, the mats, the masts, the wind, the dust devils, the bell-kites, the midges, the singers, the arch, the ring, the cables—they would stop being parts of a place. They would be parts of you.

Only then would you fully realize that you were not in this world. You were this world. You were the bowl itself, the high dry room open to a pale ordinary sky. You were the single being that had left water behind entirely, living on charge and pressure and mineral chemistry and cold wind. You did not need to move. You did not need to drink. You only needed to keep making and unmaking yourself, over and over, in the same small space.

So where would you find yourself? You would find yourself everywhere in that bowl and nowhere separate from it. You would find yourself in the cracked floor and the dense dry carpets, in the tall masts and the dust that braids and falls, in the wind that is your breath and the silence that is your voice. You would be the place itself, awake, old and settled and never still.

You would be the small, empty, lush, dry, deafening, self-remaking room at the top of the world. And you would be alone, but not lonely, because you would be the whole choir.
