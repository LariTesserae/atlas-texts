I would find myself in the warm blind center of the room, in the hot pool that spills into the terraced basins. Not as a keeper, not as a visitor, but as something smaller and already wet. The water would be thick with minerals, warmer than my own blood. I would not know at first what I am. I would feel the room mostly as vibration: the spring’s hiss, the slap of clay against clay, the deep thump behind the north wall like a too-large heart. The light would be only a red-brown smear, more warmth than vision. My eyelessness would not matter; the room is not for seeing.

Then a keeper comes. I know them by the weight of their step in the mud, the click in their throat. They open a lidded pot and lower a grub onto the surface of the water, placing it as if introducing it to me. I rise. I do not remember deciding to rise. The grub is there, and I am there, and my mouth opens. If I take it, the day may proceed. If I refuse, all work stops: the pot-beating, the pruning, the clay-smearing. I am not a pet. I am part of the foundation. My appetite is a small, steady yes at the center of the room. The keepers say the newt is the foundation; what I eat becomes the room’s permission to continue.

Then I know: I am the newt. The one pale, eyeless thing in the central pool, the color of raw clay. Not a symbol, not a spirit. A small living thing that must eat for the work to continue. I would feel the whole chamber through the water: the vine’s roots brushing the pool’s rim, the mineral rinds forming and being ground away, the too-long handprints being pressed into the walls as distant pressures. I would feel the water’s spiral change for no reason, and the fine hot rain when the chamber takes its breath. I would float just below the surface, mouth open, letting the mountain’s exhale enter—the same rain the keepers drink with their arms lifted.

So I would be the newt. The room’s hunger and its patience, the blind node at the center of all that adding. The place is still dreaming itself, and I am the dream’s slow pulse in the dark.
