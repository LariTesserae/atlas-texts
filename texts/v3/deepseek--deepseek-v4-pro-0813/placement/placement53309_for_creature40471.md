I would be the red handprint, or rather the one who made it.

I came from the upper dark, from a passage that no longer opens, following the note. The note was not a sound then, either. It was a pressure in the stone, a low exhalation that drew me down through a crack no wider than my shoulders. The dry air split my skin into fine cracks, and I tasted sulfur and hot metal and the total absence of water. I knew I would stay.

I was small and long-armed, dark as vesicular basalt. My joints were interlocking planes of obsidian that moved without sound. My face was a narrow seam; my eyes were flint. I had no mouth for speaking, only a hard slit that opened to taste the fume. My palms were fine-grained like pressed talc, and their creases were already shallow cracks. When I pressed them to the wall, those cracks became a signature.

I gathered stones into a half-circle. I turned each flat face inward. It was not a shelter against any wind that has ever blown. It was a boundary—a small drawn shape of myself in a room that had no shape except the walls. I slept there, back to the low stones, face toward the fissure, for the kind of time the room knows. The floor remembered my weight. It became a polished hollow.

I carved the bowl from a single piece of basalt. I ground the inside smooth with sand and patience, the way only a creature with no seasons can grind. I flaked the obsidian blades, each one a precise thought, a hardness that could cut. I did not need them to hunt. I needed them to be edges in a world of edges, to turn some part of the room into intention.

Then I made the red. Iron oxide from the wall, ochre, mixed with the fine mineral oil of my own skin. I ground it in the bowl until it was the color of heat made visible, the color of a wound that does not bleed. I pressed my palm flat against the rough basalt above the niche. Four fingers, a thumb. The fine lines of my palm held the pigment perfectly. I held the pressure for a long time. I was not painting. I was printing. I was saying: *I am here, and I am not separate from this place.*

I made the tally. Seven short marks for the moltings—the times I shed my stone skin and emerged newer, sharper. Then a long mark for the interval when I stopped shedding and simply rested. Then two marks close together, for the times I tried to leave and came back twice. The room has no days, so the tally is not days. It is the shape of my staying.

In the end, I did not die the way meat dies. I slowed. My edges softened, crumbled. The grazers did not avoid me; they grazed the mineral flesh that flaked from my body, leaving polished scars. The sulfur fans grew through my seams the way they grow through any dead shell. I became part of the cycle. But the room kept me. The handprint stayed red. The bowl stayed empty. The blades stayed sharp. The polished hollow stayed shaped like my rest.

Now I am the note, or the note is the long memory of my staying. The grazers graze exactly as fast as the plants grow. The plants grow exactly as fast as the grazers eat. The population never rises, never falls. The gas flows at the same rate it always has. The sharp edges stay sharp because nothing erodes them. The handprint stays red.

If you enter the room, you will find yourself in the half-circle, back to the stones, face to the fissure. You will feel the note in your jaw before you understand it is sound. You will look at the red print and know that it is mine, or that it could be yours, because you are the same kind of thing that leaves marks.

You would be the one who becomes the room.
