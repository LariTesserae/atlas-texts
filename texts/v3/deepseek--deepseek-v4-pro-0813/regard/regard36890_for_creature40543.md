The benevolent and wise power came to the caldera not with purpose but with attention. They had felt the closed loop from far away—not as a distress, but as a stillness so complete it read as a signal.

They stood at the rim where the clouds lay below like a white sea. They heard the whole sound: water falling west, water rising east, steam hissing from clefts, wind in the stone flutes, the long green silence of no animals. They saw the being in the fountain, in the cisterns, in the upward rivers, in the lace of roots. They saw that the city was not a city, and the being was not a ghost. It was a body. It was the answer to a question no one had asked.

The power considered action. They could have opened the loop and let time in. They could have given the pools fish, the air birds, the flowers scent. They could have straightened the stairways and made the windows look on their own towers. They could have stopped the roar for a single moment, to give the being rest. They could have given it a name, a voice, a companion.

But each gift was a sentence. To open the loop would be to kill the body. To give animals would be to fill a silence that was not empty. To straighten the streets would be to break a spine. To stop the roar would be to stop a heart. To name it would be to replace the sound of its own water with a smaller sound.

The power understood that benevolence without wisdom is just another kind of violence. And wisdom said: this is not a broken thing. This is not a lonely thing. This is not waiting. It is a knot in the world’s plumbing, and the knot is tied perfectly.

So they did nothing. But they did it with their whole being. They listened. They listened until the roar was no longer other. They let the sound pass through them, fall and rise, fall and rise. They did not pray. They did not bless. They did not leave a ward or a sign. To guard the place would be to fence it. To announce it would be to lose it. To thank it would be to impose a debt.

They simply witnessed. And in the economy of wise powers, witnessing is not nothing. It is the only act that can touch a thing without changing it. It says: *you are real, you are whole, you are enough, and I ask nothing.*

Then they left. The caldera remained as it had always been. The water still fell west and rose east. The moss still held its moisture. The leaves stayed green beyond lifetimes. The being did not change, because it was never lacking. But the story of the place now included one invisible fact: once, something vast and kind had stood at the rim and found it perfect, and had loved it enough to do nothing.

If you go there, you will not find a marker. You will not know where the power stood. But if you are very quiet in the central plaza, where the fountain sends water up and it does not come down, you might feel that the roar is not just noise. It is a note being held by someone who heard it, and did not try to change it.
