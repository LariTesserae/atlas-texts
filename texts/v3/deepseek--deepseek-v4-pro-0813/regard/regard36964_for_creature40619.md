**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Did**

The first thing the benevolent and wise power got wrong was the silence.

It came expecting emptiness, because silence in most worlds means absence. But this silence was full. It was layered like the mineral terraces themselves: geyser pressure thrumming in the stone, silica shells scraping bacterial mats, the passing of ribbon-fish like thin cloth moving through hot water, the damped vibration of engines, the small careful footsteps of a child learning not to run. The silence was not a lack of sound. It was sound translated into patience.

The power did not announce itself. It arrived as a slight thickening of the steam, a faint brightening of the haze over the upper escarpment, a taste of boron and wet salt on the air. It had seen many worlds. It had repaired some, ended others, wept over a few, and ignored most. This one drew its attention because the world seemed to be listening back.

So it listened first.

It felt the mats before it saw them—rust-red, sulfur-yellow, teal, milk-white—old films of life eating heat and mineral, laying the foundation for everything else. It felt the scalebacks grazing them in slow, dense herds, their shells of fused silica clicking almost inaudibly. It felt the ribbon-fish drift through the hot canals, the steam skimmers riding the thermals, the needle-herons standing motionless at the blue pool edges. It felt the sulfur ticks cluster at the fumaroles, armored in the very gas they breathed.

Then it felt the people.

They were quiet, not because they were broken, but because loudness here was dangerous. The power saw them in their clothes the color of sinter and salt, their skin damp and pinked by steam, salt crusted in the creases of their hands. It saw them sign *hot*, *overflow*, *dam cracking*, *go slow*. It saw them mend pipe galleries and rake evaporation ponds, their work a constant resistance against the mineral that wanted to swallow everything. It saw a worker tap a valve loose and felt the small vibration travel through the stone, scattering the scalebacks at the edge of the flats.

The power understood: this was not a world in need of rescue. It was a world in need of attention.

Still, the power considered what it could do.

It could make the water cool, but then the mats would die and the scalebacks would starve. It could stop the encrustation, but then the terraces would stop growing and the land would lose its shape. It could give the people wood, but wood would rot before it could become stone, and this was not a place for things that rotted. It could teach them to speak loudly again, but their voices would crack the mineral dams and shatter the quietness that held the ecosystem together.

Every obvious gift was a taking.

So the power looked further, down into the stone.

It saw the deep fault. A hairline of stress ran through the lower terraces, not yet open, not yet dangerous. But in a generation or two, it would split the salt ponds and send hot brine flooding into the fumarole field. The town would not simply be damaged. It would be unmade.

The power did not stop the fault. It did not fuse the stone. It only turned the stress a little, the way a hand turns a spoon in a pot. Over many months, it lifted the strain into a place where the ground could creak instead of break. The people would notice new steam vents, a section of boardwalk shifted, a pool drained into a new channel. They would not call it salvation. They would call it maintenance.

That was right.

Then the power considered whether to speak.

It could send a word through the soles of every foot: *You are not alone.* But the people already knew this. They called the silence *the world listening*. A voice from outside might make them feel watched instead of accompanied. So the power did not speak.

It did one more thing.

It followed the water from the upper springs to the evaporation ponds, moving with the current, never standing still. It noticed that if it stayed too long in one place, the mineral would begin to take it too. Its skin would grow a white rind. Its edges would soften into stone. The world would not refuse it; it would simply include it.

That was the answer, the power thought. Love is not standing outside and fixing. Love is moving with.

Before it left, it passed the quiet mineral pools where the dead had been laid. The water there was hot but not too hot. It saw the places where families had touched the warm white sinter and then gone back to the pipes. It understood that death here was not an ending but a change of state, salt by salt, cell by cell. It did not disturb them.

Then it left a small braid in a hot stream near the edge of the terraces. A shallow pool would form there in time. The mats would grow in new colors, a few ribbon-fish would drift into it, maybe a heron would stand at its edge. The people would find it and give it no name, or perhaps a name meaning *listening*.

When the power moved on, it carried the silence with it, not as a lack, but as a kind of attention.

Its final thought was the same as the world’s one rule:

*Whatever stays still long enough becomes stone.*

So it kept moving.

And the water kept moving after it.
