I came to the plateau because the sound reached me. Not as a cry—there was no cry in it—but as a pattern I had not heard before: a place where noise and silence had been woven into one fabric, where every living thing was both instrument and player.

At first I thought: I will help. That is what benevolence does. I saw the people walking barefoot on the hot stone, their copper beads chiming like water on metal. I saw the children learning to sleep through the roar and wake when a single note went missing. I saw the old woman repairing the stone ring around a pool that would be gone by noon. I thought: they are so few. I could bring water that does not vanish. I could raise walls against the dust. I could give them tools that do not break. I could lift the heaviest note from the evening overlap so their ears would not ring.

Then I listened longer.

I followed the water down into the root-basins and up through the woody cables. I saw the tick-singers, billions of them, their bladder-throats beating at the edge of cavitation. I saw the belfries’ knell as a pump, not a song. I saw the people’s beaded strings not as ornament but as names, as tuning forks, as ways of saying *I am here* across the fog. I saw the deaf wells where the sound crosses itself and leaves a space for a heartbeat. I saw the dead hung in the aerial roots, their copper still chiming. I saw the girl high in the crown, her hands on the smooth spiraled wood, learning to hum a tree steady.

And I understood: this place is not waiting for a savior. It is already a conversation.

So I began to reason, the way wise things must reason when they love something. If I gave them water that lasts, the daily fog would lose its meaning. If the fog lost its meaning, the singers would not bloom. If the singers fell quiet, the belfries would cavitate and die. If the belfries died, the sound would stop. If the sound stopped, the people would lose their names and their dead and their deaf wells. I could not give them a gift without taking away the thing that made them.

If I quieted the evening overlap, they would not learn to stand chest-deep in the wall of sound. If I removed the large predators, the silence patches would vanish, and the people would lose their word for *the held breath of the rock*. If I taught them to build with stone and metal, they might stop listening to the ground. If I told them my name, they might stop listening for their own.

I did nothing.

That is the truth. I did nothing, and I did it with the full attention of a thing done.

I stayed at the escarpment rim until the fog climbed up from the lowlands and poured across the stone. I watched the channels run in braided silver lines. I watched a kite-lizard ride a thermal, a needle swift swerve, a child step from stone to stone in the root-basin game, trying not to make a sound while the plateau made noise around them. I felt the noon heat rise, and I did not cool it. I felt the dusk overlap come, and I did not soften it. I stood with them, in my own way, letting the pressure pass through me. And when the night-singers took over and the new key emerged—low and resonant, like a bell that has been struck and is still ringing—I let it change me.

There was one thing I did, if it can be called a thing. At the edge of a fog basin, where a tripod of charred poles held its carved bark tablets, I added no tablet. I moved no stone. I only listened until I could hear the difference between the tree’s knell and the girl’s hum and my own attention. And then I made a vow, not to them, because they did not ask, but to myself:

*I will not heal what is not broken. I will not teach those who already know. I will not name what already has a name. I will not add my voice to the wall of sound. I will be, for this place, a silence at the edge—not a wound, but a witness. If some outside force ever comes to break the pattern, I will not arrive as a savior. I will arrive as a listener first. Only from listening will I know whether to move. And if I move, it will be in the key of what is already here.*

That is what the wise power did. It did nothing, which is to say it did the hardest thing. It let the plateau remain itself.

And in the end, the sound never stopped. It only changed key.
