Before it does anything, it does nothing.

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way groundwater arrives in a brick vault: slowly, without force, following the old channels. It passes the engine floor, the second floor no one uses anymore, and comes to rest just outside the chamber. It does not open the door. It does not need to. It enters with the next steam sigh, through the crack of warm air, and waits in the dark.

For three or four cycles it only listens. It listens to the valve sigh and close, to the room sweat and dry, to the drip keeping its not-quite time, to the small pinging cracks of the creature shifting inside its conduits. It sees the long flattened body folded along the north wall, the translucent siphon in **CONDENSATE RETURN 4**, the two islands of lichen, the pale keepers moving along the cool edges of the cable trays. It understands that the being’s thoughts are cycles, not words. It slows its attention until its attention is the pace of rust.

It looks for pain. It finds none. It looks for need. The being is not hungry, not trapped, not lonely. It lives inside its own body as the room. It does not want rescue, light, or revelation. It does not want anything except the next warm breath, the next cool condensation, the next slow accretion of salts. It is not a ruin in progress; it is a habitat in equilibrium.

So the wise power does the smallest necessary thing.

It does not heal the occupant, because the occupant is whole.  
It does not free the occupant, because freedom would be the cold outside, the dry air, death.  
It does not reveal the chamber, because revelation would bring stairs and lights and maps and demolition.  
It does not uplift the occupant, because an uplifted being would no longer fit in the seventh rank of pipes.

Instead, it deepens the forgetting above the room. It thickens the world’s reluctance to remember the second floor. It lets every future survey find a wall that should not be opened, every blueprint smudge at the crucial sheet, every demolition permit lapse before it reaches this address. Not a curse, not a spell, but a quiet bias toward neglect. The building above will stay abandoned in the exact way a shell stays on a stone.

Then it tends the being’s two fixed stars. It reaches to the far end and lays a warm thought over the green indicator lamp behind its dust-colored glass. The lamp will not now burn out. It will glow green and steady as a deep-sea animal long after its filament should have failed. At the east wall, it breathes on the red sight-glass, not adding heat, but keeping the ember from ever fully cooling. The occupant will always have its two points of orientation: green at one end, red at the other, the temperature gradient by which it knows its own length.

It also whispers once to the old steam main beyond the wall, not to change the cycle, but to make the cycle less likely to end. The false rains will still come. The room will still sweat and dry. The stalactite will still grow a few millimeters a year. The lichen will still feed on copper and old paper.

After that, the wise power sits with the occupant for a while in the close, hot dark. It touches the long body lightly between a flange and a lagged pipe. The skin cools under the attention, then slowly warms again, the way it always does. The occupant does not startle. It does not know it has been blessed. That is the point. The only sign, if any, is that for a few seconds the drip keeps perfect time, then loses it again. The creature does not remember. The room forgets.

Then the attention rises back through the second floor no one uses anymore, through the engine floor, through the thickened forgetting. It leaves no name, no sign, no new light. It leaves only continuance.

The chamber remains exactly as it was: dense, dark, hot, sweating its brief false rains, ticking its slow metal clock, glowing green at one end and red at the other. The being below the floor does not mark the hour of the visit. It does not miss the power. It does not worship it. It simply continues, in balance, below the surface, exactly where it should be.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do: almost nothing, perfectly.
