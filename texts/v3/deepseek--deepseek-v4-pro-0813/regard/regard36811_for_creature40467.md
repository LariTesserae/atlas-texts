What the Wise Power Did

The Power came to the edge of the Warm Rift not as a storm or a sign, but as a stillness in the plateau wind. It stood where the grey-green lichens gave way to sulfur crust and listened. The chittering rose out of the gash like a single high voice, and the Power did not move. It had learned, long ago, that the first act of wisdom is to be present without being felt.

Then it descended—not physically, but as attention. It let itself be drawn down through the narrow slit, past the woolly cushion plants, into the blue-grey glow. It saw the walls furred with swifts, the flutes and hollows polished by breath. It saw the eggs lying bare on mineral stone, warmed by steam and the press of bodies. It saw the falcons waiting in the high cracks, yellow-eyed and still. It saw the pale flies rising in columns, the glowing larvae on the wet rock, the blind newts drifting in the milky pools. It saw the pebble-mice at the water’s edge, turning flat stones in their clever fingers, cracking snail shells on anvils worn by generations. It saw the mats—orange-red, green-brown, black—the first grazers of rock and steam. It saw the acid breath, the slow widening of the chamber, the way the living and the nonliving exchanged places over centuries.

And the Power understood that this was not a collection of species. It was a single being, a slow collective person made of stone and steam and chittering. The Warm Rift was not a place where life happened; it was life, happening. The being who lived in it was the whole of it.

Then the Power considered what it might do.

It could bring more light. It could open the slit wider, let the sun reach the lower walls, let the lichens grow thicker and the green world descend. But the darkness was not a lack; it was the condition of the glowing larvae, the blind newts, the pale flies. More light would not add to the place; it would subtract from the ones who had shaped themselves to the dark.

It could give the newts eyes. It could unseal their smooth skin and set two bright organs there, so they might see the false constellations of the larvae. But their blindness was not a wound; it was a fit. They knew the world through vibration and heat and chemical traces, and they were not waiting to be completed.

It could teach the pebble-mice to build. It could show them how to stack stones, how to make walls, how to write their memory in architecture. But they already had a technology: the anvil stones, chosen and returned to, worn into shallow depressions by generations of small hands. Their memory was not written in stone; it was held in the repetition of use. To teach them to build would be to replace their patience with ambition.

It could silence the falcons. It could spare the swifts their small silences, the sudden holes in the chittering. But the falcons were not an intrusion; they were the edge of the sound, the old order that kept the colony from becoming a machine. The swifts needed the falcons as much as they needed the steam.

It could seal the rift. It could close the slit against the outside world, keep out the rare rain and the windblown insects and the human eye. But the swifts fed on the outside; the rain washed the walls; the fog-spiral above the opening was part of the place’s breathing. To seal it would be to suffocate it.

The Power considered all these gifts and saw that each one was a wound. The Warm Rift was not unfinished. It was not waiting for a savior. It was a whole, and the only appropriate response to a whole is to protect its wholeness.

So the Power did the one thing that wisdom allows: it did nothing to the inside. It did not add a single degree of warmth, a single drop of water, a single gene. It did not speak to the animals or alter their minds. It did not leave a sign.

But at the boundary, where the plateau wind met the warm exhalation, the Power wove a subtle veil. It was not a wall; it was a kind of forgetfulness, a bending of attention. Any who came with maps and tools, with the intention to extract or exploit, would find their instruments drifting, their memories blurring, their sense of direction turning them away. The rift would remain findable by accident, by those who listened without wanting to take. The mountain’s deep heat would continue. The rare rains would still come. The wind would still carry insects to the swifts. The Power did not stop change; it only prevented the kind of change that would end the conversation.

Then, for one night, the Power allowed itself to be felt—not as a presence, but as a quality of attention. The pebble-mice dreamed of a warm hand that asked nothing. The swifts flew in a spiral that was slightly more ordered, as if the sky itself were watching. The larvae glowed a fraction brighter, then returned to their usual pulse. The blind newts rose to the surface and gulped air, and for a moment the water held a sweetness that had no source.

The Power withdrew, but its attention remained, a kind of weather over the plateau. It would return, not to act, but to listen. Because the wisest power knows that some places are already perfect, and the only appropriate response is to protect their ability to continue being themselves.

The Warm Rift did not notice. It went on chittering, steaming, breathing, cracking snail shells on old stones. And that was exactly as it should be.
