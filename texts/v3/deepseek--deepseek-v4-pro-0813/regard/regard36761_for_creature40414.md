The benevolent and wise power came to the western rim and looked down into the basin. What it did next was almost nothing. That almost-nothing was the most precise act of love it had ever performed.

It could have brought water. But this was a dry city, furred with thenardite and gypsum; water would have been a violence wearing the mask of life. It could have brought seeds. But the manganese dendrites already branched like ferns, and the gypsum already flowered in the cracks. It could have repaired the broken bridges, filled the hollow towers, opened doors that were only an inch deep. But the city was not broken. It was mature. Its emptiness was not a wound; it was the intended product of its long inhabitation of itself.

It could have left a name. But a name would be a map, and maps begin to disagree with the city.

So the wise power did not save it.

Instead, it descended without weight. The dust did not lift. It let its own shadow fall the wrong way and did not correct it. It kept no map. It stood before a doorway only an inch deep and did not try to make it deeper. It watched the glass lopers scrape salt blooms with their hard tongues and did not pity them. When the Quiet lay beside it, mistaking it for a fallen column, the wise power did not move.

When it met the Resident, it did not count its limbs or search for a face. It saw that the Resident was not lonely, but was the city’s memory of having inhabitants, condensed into a shape that could stand at the end of a colonnade and wait. It did not ask the Resident to speak. The Resident rang once, a low note without a bell. The wise power’s answer was to listen.

They remained together for what might have been a century, or the pause between the fall of a salt slab and its landing. The stone ticked. The shadows pooled. A salt bloom opened in the cold. The city’s layout changed by inches and degrees. The wise power attended without needing to alter.

Then it left. It restored nothing. It promised nothing. It allowed itself to be forgotten. It smoothed the faint traces of its attention from the dust and let the wrong-way shadows it had caused unpin themselves. It even let the edges of its own memory become porous, so that no future benevolence would harden into a plan and bring it back with water or seeds or writing. The city’s stone remembers marks and grows around them; the wise power made sure there was no mark to remember.

If anything, that was the gift: no gift at all, or a gift so perfectly fitted to the city that it vanished into it. A witness who understood that some places are complete by being empty, some beings are whole by being alone, and some love is exact enough to leave no trace.

The city remained. The Resident remained. The salt bloomed and shrank. The low note rolled through the streets. No bell. No map. No mark.

Only, at the far end of a colonnade, for a while, the faintest sense—already fading—that something had looked upon the city and found it sufficient.
