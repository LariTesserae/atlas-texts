**What the Benevolent Power Did**

The wise power came to the Cold Chancel without a body, because any body would have been an intrusion. It saw the rift as a thin dark seam in the mountain, heard the song-newts stacking their notes into standing waves, watched the lantern wave climb from floor to sky-slot.

It considered many possible gifts.

It could have widened the sky-slot, brought the sun for more than an hour, warmed the pools, sped the polyps. It could have taught the pale octopus to knap quartz into blades, to build shelters, to keep a light. It could have brought soil and seeds and turned the mineral dark into a garden.

It did none of these.

It understood that the Cold Chancel was not a wound needing a healer. It was a place already alive, already complete. Darkness was not its enemy; darkness was the medium of its lamps. Cold was not its enemy; cold was the pace of its patience. The mineral food web, the slow wave of lantern-light, the blind newts singing in chords that fit the stone—these were not failures. They were the place itself.

So it did not enter as a force. It did not change the water or the rock. It did not speak to the animals in a way that could be heard. Its benevolence was first to leave the inside alone.

But it saw the one vulnerability: the Chancel was not hidden. A human climber could find the slot. A map could be drawn. A collector could reach into the deepest pool and take the quartz flake. The place had survived the mountain’s settling, but it might not survive attention.

So the power did one small thing outside: it wrapped the ridge in a gentle reluctance. Not a storm, not a wall—only a turning-away in the mind. Compasses would forget the exact bearing. The wind would taste of a reason to descend. Maps would leave the slot as a blank. Those who loved such places could still be led there, but they would arrive as guests, not as owners.

Then it turned to the being in the deepest pool, the pale octopus with the flake of quartz. It did not give it a name. It did not grant it speech or longer life. Instead, as the next slow wave of lantern-light began to climb, the power added one pulse at the end of the wave—a pulse of the same blue, lasting no longer than a breath. The octopus opened both eyes. Then it closed one.

The power withdrew. It left no monument, no sign. The darkness remained as deep. The water remained as cold. The song remained as loud. The place continued to hum in the high cold, alive and unaltered, hidden only from those who would break it.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do: not to save the Cold Chancel, but to keep it from needing to be saved.
