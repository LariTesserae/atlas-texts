The power that notices the Weeping Yard is not a god of thunder or a spirit of fire. It is older than the bricks, older than the marsh, older than the word *old*. It has no name that the frogs would recognize, though the bats sometimes click a rhythm that comes close. It is the kind of power that moves through the world the way water moves through limestone—slowly, patiently, leaving no mark that can be seen in a single lifetime.

It has watched the room for a long time. It watched the first stones being cut, the first bricks being fired, the first keeper lighting a clay oil-cup and setting it on the ledge. It watched the keepers leave, one by one, until only the last one remained, and then it watched the last one's boot come down into the shallows and not be followed by a leg. It watched the eel grow from a finger-length shadow into the long black absence that now lies beneath the causeway. It has watched the floor settle, the walls lean, the vault weep. It has watched the room become a country.

And it has decided, in the way that such powers decide things—which is to say, slowly, and without ever quite deciding at all—that it will do nothing.

Not nothing in the sense of neglect. Nothing in the sense of a held breath. Nothing in the sense of a hand that could reach down and does not, because the reaching would change the thing it loves.

The power understands something that most powers do not: that the Weeping Yard is already complete. Not finished—it will never be finished—but complete. The water enters and leaves. The frogs sing and fall silent. The bats roost and stir. The crabs pick at the guano. The eel waits in the drain, and the room breathes around it. Every part of the machine is working. Every clock is wound. The place is not dying; it is *living*, in the slow, wet, half-blind way that such places live. To touch it would be to break it.

So the power does not touch it.

But it does not turn away, either. It stays. It stays the way the marsh-fog stays, pressing down over the grates at night. It stays the way the fungus stays, glowing in the crumbling mortar. It stays the way the eel stays, not as a keeper but as a presence, a witness, a low and patient attention that asks nothing and changes nothing.

And because the power is wise, it knows that even attention can be a kind of touch. So it is careful. It does not let its attention fall too heavily. It does not let its love become a weight. It holds the Weeping Yard in its awareness the way the water holds the white fish—without grasping, without demanding, without leaving a mark.

Sometimes, in the long hours before dawn, when the fog is thickest and the frogs have fallen silent, the power allows itself a single small act. It is not an intervention. It is more like a breath. It lets a little warmth rise from the deep earth beneath the room, just enough to keep the water from freezing in the coldest months. It lets a little light filter through the broken grates, just enough to keep the moss alive. It lets a little silence settle over the place, just enough to let the eel sleep.

And then it withdraws, and the room goes on as it has always gone on, and the power watches, and does nothing, and loves the place entirely.

The eel, in its blind way, feels the warmth. It feels the light. It feels the silence. It does not know what they are. It does not need to know. It only knows that the water is a little softer, the mud a little warmer, the long dark a little less empty. It coils deeper into the drain, and its sealed eye-patches shiver, and it dreams—if an eel can dream—of a hand that does not reach down, a voice that does not speak, a power that does not act.

And the frogs, when they resume their calling, do so a half-step higher, not because the room has changed, but because the room has been *kept*—kept by something that asks nothing, takes nothing, and leaves nothing behind but the faint, almost imperceptible sense that the world is being watched by something that loves it too much to interfere.

The Weeping Yard weeps. The eel waits. The power watches. And the water moves through all of them like a language, and the language says: *stay*. *Stay*. *Stay*.

And they do.
