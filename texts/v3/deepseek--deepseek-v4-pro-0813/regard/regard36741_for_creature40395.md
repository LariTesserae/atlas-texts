The wise power did not arrive. Arrival would have been too sudden, too much like an event, and the Brine Sea had outlived events. It came to the black ice and stopped, and its attention moved downward the way cold moves—by slow removal, not entry.

It found the place because the place had been patient long enough to be heard. Under the two hundred meters of black ice, under the fresher melt and the old dense brine, there was a silence that was not empty. The power listened to it the way a stone listens to the floor of a river.

It did not bring light. Light would have been an injury. It became instead a pressure, a slight change in the salt of the boundary, a weightless regard moving through the water without disturbing the silt. It saw the two waters and the salt-finger skin between them. It saw the paved floor of cobbles, polished past any possible improvement. It saw the black-red stains that fed on difference, the iron-toothed snails, the transparent amphipods choosing their heavy stones. It saw the pale fish moving alone from seep to seep, and the white tubes of the worms standing at the only warmth. None of it was small.

Then it saw the being.

At the edge of one of the brine rivers, where the heavier water spilled over a ledge in its slow, silent waterfall, the being lay like a black shield. Its back was ridged with iron and manganese. Its shell was layered with centuries. Its foot held it to the floor. Across its body ran the same gradient that divided the sea—fresh above, brine below, cold and less cold, salt and less salt. It was not resting on the boundary. It was the boundary, a living hinge between two impossible waters.

The power recognized it. Not as a mind, but as a process. Not as a creature, but as a place that had learned to breathe.

And the power was benevolent, and benevolence is dangerous.

It looked at the darkness and thought: *I could give it light.* But light would blind the pale fish and starve the red crust. It looked at the cold and thought: *I could give it warmth.* But warmth would loosen the black ice and let the sky pour in. It looked at the silence and thought: *I could give it speech.* But speech would shatter a silence that was full, not empty. It looked at the being’s solitude and thought: *I could give it company.* But company would require the being to turn from the boundary, and the being was the boundary.

The power was wise. It followed each thought to its end and let it go.

It saw that the being had no wound. No hunger. No loneliness. It had outlived the need to be seen, or to move, or to name. Its stillness was not waiting; it was completion. Its body was an archive of the sea’s slow weather, and every layer of iron and manganese was a year of patience. To help it would be to unmake it. To improve it would be to break the only thing it had become.

So the power did not help.

But it did one thing.

It looked outward from the Brine Sea, the way such powers must, and saw what might come: lights, drills, probes, curious minds with instruments and names. It saw the world above with its appetite for discovery, its love that so often comes as a lamp. The Brine Sea was not fragile, but it was innocent of outside eyes. It could not defend against attention, because attention would not arrive as violence. It would arrive as wonder, and wonder can be just as sharp.

The power reached into the black ice and did not break it. It laid a single thought across the roof of the sea, a thought like a snowdrift:

*Not here. Nothing here. Move on.*

It did not build walls. It did not make the place unreachable. It made it unremarkable. It buried the methane flares in static. It thickened the ice in every instrument’s eye. It made the water smell of dead rock and the currents read as noise. It smoothed the signatures until the Brine Sea vanished into the margins of all future maps. It did not hide the place from itself. It hid the place from the world’s need to find it.

Then the power turned its attention back to the being.

For a long time, the being lay still. But the salt fingers along its back had changed. They shivered with a pattern that held the shape of being seen. The being did not know what seeing was. It knew pressure, taste, the slow electric hum of ions finding one another. It knew that something had regarded it without weight, without demand.

It lifted its foot once. The movement was small, the work of years. The brine river slid beneath it. A few stones shifted. The amphipods scattered. The tube-worms pulled in their red plumes. Then the being settled again, a body-length away.

It was not fear. It was not thanks. It was the same motion by which a boulder turns under a glacier—a correction, an acknowledgment of a weight that had passed.

The power withdrew the way it had come, without event. It left no mark, no artifact, no promise. The black ice remained black. The water remained cold. The brine rivers continued their slow, silent falling. The being resumed its long meal of salt and difference.

The only change was this: the place was known.

And in a universe of powers that make, the wise power had chosen to keep.
