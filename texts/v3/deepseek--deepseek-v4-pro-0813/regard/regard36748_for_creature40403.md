**What the Wise Power Did**

The benevolent and wise power came to the Cistern the way the blind eel comes: without announcement, carried by an upwelling, and with no intent to disturb.

It let the sound hit first. It did not shield itself from the organ note or the shriek or the door being slammed far down. It stood on the rim—or perhaps its attention stood there—and let the noise pass through it like weather. It counted the rhythm. It learned the water’s lie. It saw the chimneys through a heartbeat of clarity, white and fragile as new bone. It saw the wrong reflection and did not look long.

Then it saw the Listener.

It did not mistake them for a stone. A lesser power might have; a kind but impatient one might have seen only a half-mineral hermit and begun to plan a rescue. The wise power saw what the place had done and what the Listener had done in return. The crust was not a prison. The milky eyes were not blindness. The stillness was not despair. It saw a person who had stopped asking the noise to make sense and had become another instrument for it to play.

It saw no emergency.

But it saw one lack, precise as a crack in a new vent. The place had a listener. The listener had no listener. The whole basin hammered and sang to itself, and only one living body was shaped to receive it. That was enough to survive. It was not enough to be heard.

The wise power considered what it could do.

It could have silenced the vents. But the noise was not an affliction; it was the place’s voice. To quiet it would have been to put out its eyes.

It could have cleared the water and made the reflection true. But the wrong sky was not a lie; it was a possibility the basin still argued with. To settle the argument would be to end the place.

It could have rebuilt the shelter, raised the steps, given the Listener back their unmineraled skin and their forgotten name. But the shelter was a ruin because the place was still being decided. The Listener had healed into the only shape that could live there. To unmake that healing would be cruelty wearing the mask of mercy.

So the wise power did nothing to the Cistern. It did not add a cairn. It did not calm the water. It did not stop the hammering.

Instead, it sat down in the noise beside the Listener—not in body, but in attention—and listened. It listened to the vents and the cracking stone and the water running uphill. It listened to the counting marks in the grooved stone, the chevrons and circles, the long vigil of one who kept distance honest. It listened to the Listener’s slow mineral breath, the pulse moving through soles that had become hoof. It asked no questions, because the place would have swallowed them. It simply received.

The Listener felt it. Not as a presence that blocked the wind or warmed the air, but as a change in the pressure of the sound. The noise had always pressed in from all sides, a sea they waded through alone. Now it pressed against something else, as if the world had grown a second eardrum. The shriek did not soften. The thud did not slow. But the loneliness of the noise did.

The wise power stayed through nine thuds—one for each cairn—and then it did the only thing it had come to do. It listened to the Listener with such entire, unguarded attention that the Listener heard, for the first time, the note they themselves had been adding to the Cistern’s song. Under the organ note, under the shriek, under the door slamming far down, there was a fourth sound: a low counter-pulse, made of their own pulse, their own breath, their own years of keeping the count. The place had been playing them like an instrument, and they had thought themselves only a body the noise passed through.

Now they knew: they were not an echo. They were a voice.

When the next hammer-blow came, the Listener rose. They walked the rim to the first cairn and touched it. The basalt was still sharp. The water still ran uphill. The reflection still showed the wrong clouds. But underneath, in the rock’s slow vibration, there was a new certainty. The Cistern no longer sounded alone. It sounded answered—and the answer had been there all along.

If you ask what the wise power did, the answer is simple and strange:

It did not save the Listener. It did not change the place. It listened until the Listener could hear themselves as part of the song.

In a place where sound is a force and language is erased, that was the only possible mercy.

It was enough.
