**What the Power Did**

The power came as a change in the quality of attention, not as light or sound. It did not step onto the root-mat, because it knew that even a god’s weight is weight. It stayed at the edge of the world, where the clouds drag across the plateau, and it listened.

It heard the many-voiced dripping, the hollow organ tones, the creak of lignified plates. It heard that the sound was not random. It was a conversation between the place and the inhabitant, a conversation so old that the two had become one sentence. It saw the unfinished arches, the ribs that stopped, the stairs that turned and ended in a blank wall. And because the power was benevolent, it felt the urge to finish them. Because it was wise, it did not.

The power understood that the unfinishedness was not a lack. It was a posture. The place was not waiting to be completed; it was holding a possibility open. The bud near the inhabitant’s front was the same shape as the unfinished arches. It was not a seed that had failed to open. It was a door that had not yet decided to be a door.

The power saw the inhabitant, pale and slow, pouring itself across the wet flats. It saw the water-cup at its front, always full, reading the world through the rings on its surface. It saw that the inhabitant was not lonely. It was the place’s way of being awake. The empty halls were not empty; they were the space the inhabitant had not yet occupied, and that space was part of its body.

The power thought: *I could give it a companion. I could give it speech. I could make the bud open. I could finish the arches. I could fill the halls with light.*

Then it reasoned: Every gift would be a wound. A companion would be a second body in a loop that has only one. Speech would break the water’s grammar. The bud, forced open, would not be a possibility but a demand. The finished arches would be dead, because here the only finished thing is the unfinished thing, held in equilibrium.

The power saw that the place was not a problem to be solved. It was an answer that had already been given. The inhabitant was not waiting for a god. It was the god of its own small world, folded into a shape that could walk.

So the wise power did the only thing that would not be a violence: it withdrew.

But not the passive withdrawal of indifference. It did the active withdrawal of restraint. It folded its attention away the way a hand withdraws from a spider’s web without breaking a thread. It set a seal around the world, not of light or law, but of unlikeliness: a curvature that makes other powers look elsewhere. It made the place hard to find, hard to remember, hard to want. Then it forgot the way back, so that even its own memory could not press on the place.

The water continued to drip. The bud remained sealed. The inhabitant settled into the long hall, and the house was lived in, exactly as before.

And that is what the wise power did: nothing, perfectly.
