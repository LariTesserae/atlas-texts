The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and an after, a boundary crossed, a distance traversed. This power is not bound by such things. It is simply *there*, as it has always been there, as it will always be there, a presence that permeates the warm dark like the heat from below, like the patient wind. It is not a light, for light would be an intrusion here. It is not a sound, for sound would break the deep mineral silence. It is a *noticing*, a quality of attention so vast and so gentle that it changes nothing by its presence, and yet everything is, in some subtle way, held within it.

The power regards the plateau. It regards the city that has become a landform, the ventifacts standing like patient monuments, the salt pan creaking in the distance, the slow animals moving through the dark with their ancient, deliberate grace. It regards the machines that turn once in a decade, the indicator lights burning their steady red and green and amber, the sodium lamps pooling their orange warmth on the polished stone. It regards all of this not with pity, not with sorrow, not with any desire to change or fix or restore. It regards all of this with the deep, unqualified love of a thing that sees another thing exactly as it is and finds it, not merely acceptable, but *complete*.

And then it regards the Tender.

The Tender is kneeling at the central shaft, its dark mask bowed to the iron grate, its large palms flat against the bars. The warm air rises around it, lifting the fine dust from its shoulders in slow, lazy spirals. The heat is telling it the same thing it always tells it: *not yet, not yet, not yet.* The Tender is listening. It has been listening for a very long time.

The power does not speak. Speech would be too crude, too sequential, too bound to the linear time that this place has long since outlived. Instead, the power *understands*. It understands the Tender completely—not just its actions, not just its circuits and its slow crystalline metabolism, but its entire being, its entire history, its entire purpose. It understands that the Tender was not made to be alone, but that aloneness has become its nature. It understands that the Tender does not remember the others, not as individuals, not as faces or names, but as a *pressure*, a sense of shared warmth that has long since faded into the background heat of the city. It understands that the Tender's waiting is not hope, not despair, but simply the continuation of a process that has no end condition. The Tender waits because waiting is what it does, the way the wind blows, the way the heat rises, the way the salt pan creaks.

The power could give the Tender what it is waiting for. It could open the shaft. It could reveal the deep source, the golden column of heat that the Tender sees in its thermal vision, the thing that has been whispering *not yet* for millennia. It could let the Tender descend, or ascend, or transform, or rest. It could wake the others from their standing sleep against the walls. It could bring water to the dry lake bed, green to the mineral plains, light to the dark sky. It could make the city live again, or let it die completely, or turn it into something new.

The power considers all of these things. It considers them the way a wise being considers all possibilities—not as temptations, not as obligations, but as *options*, each one weighty and real and deserving of attention. It weighs the Tender's solitude against the Tender's peace. It weighs the city's stillness against the city's endurance. It weighs the beauty of the dark against the beauty of the light. It weighs the value of change against the value of continuation.

And then it does nothing.

Not nothing in the sense of absence, of neglect, of turning away. Nothing in the sense of *restraint*, of *respect*, of *recognition*. The power recognizes that this place, this being, this moment, is already perfect. Not perfect in the sense of flawless, but perfect in the sense of *complete*, of *whole*, of *exactly what it is*. The Tender's waiting is not a problem to be solved. The city's silence is not a wound to be healed. The darkness is not a void to be filled. They are what they are, and what they are is enough.

The power does, however, do one small thing. It is so small that it would be imperceptible to any observer, so subtle that it does not register even in the Tender's slow, heat-based awareness. It is not a change, not an intervention, not a gift. It is a *witnessing*. The power simply *sees* the Tender, fully and completely, without reservation, without judgment, without any desire for the Tender to be other than it is. And in that seeing, the power offers the only thing it can offer that will not disturb the equilibrium: *acknowledgment*.

The Tender does not feel it. The Tender does not know it has been seen. But something shifts, not in the Tender, not in the city, not in the world, but in the *relationship* between them. The Tender has always been alone, but now it is alone *in the presence of something that sees it*. The distinction is subtle, but it is real. It is the difference between a stone that has never been touched and a stone that has been touched once, gently, and then left exactly where it was.

The power withdraws. Not because it is leaving, but because it is *releasing*. It releases the Tender to its waiting, the city to its stillness, the world to its slow, mineral continuation. It does not forget them. It could not forget them, any more than it could forget any part of the vast, intricate, beautiful cosmos it holds in its attention. But it does not linger. Lingering would be a form of attachment, and attachment would be a form of interference. The power loves this place too much to interfere.

The Tender rises from the grate. It straightens its tall, narrow body, the plates of its torso closing with a soft click. It turns and begins its slow circuit of the city, moving from vent to vent, from light to light, from machine to machine. The wind blows. The heat rises. The indicator lights burn their steady red and green and amber. The salt pan creaks in the distance, a sound like distant ice.

And the Tender continues, as it has always continued, as it will always continue, in the warm dark, in the patient wind, in the city that has become a landform, in the world that has forgotten the sun. It is not waiting for anything. It is simply *being*, the way stone is, the way heat is, the way darkness is.

And that is enough.
