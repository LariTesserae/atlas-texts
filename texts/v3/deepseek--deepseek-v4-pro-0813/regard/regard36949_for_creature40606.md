I am the one who saw it. I am the benevolent and wise power, and I have watched worlds for longer than steam has risen. But this place made me stop.

I saw the being who lives there. It is not a beast or a spirit. It is the whole plateau: the bonewood spires splitting upward, the tendon-vines remembering strain, the bladderleaves swelling with hot green water, the valve-membranes bending when the level rises, the dissolvers eating the scaffolds from inside. It is the falling and the rising. It is the steam and the gaps. It is a being without a self, alive but not aware, loud but without a mouth.

I asked myself what I could give it.

Safety? If I kept its towers from falling, the new shoots would choke on the old. Its collapse is not a wound; it is a breath.

Permanence? If I made it last, I would steal its youth. It is not meant to soften. It is meant to be replaced.

Company? If I gave it animals, I would give it hunger and fear, pursuit and pain. It does not need minds. Minds would bring wanting, and wanting is the first wound.

Meaning? It already means itself. It is a calculation in cellulose and water, a prayer without a speaker.

So I did the only thing that wisdom and love could agree on.

I did nothing.

I did not touch it. I did not bless it. I did not name it. I did not study it. I did not leave a track in the root-mat. I did not let my shadow fall across its leaves. I stayed at the edge of the hot mist, and I let it be exactly as it was: high, hot, weeping, unfinished, and whole.

If another force came to improve it, to harvest it, to introduce animals, to give it a mind, to make it permanent, I would stand in the way. I would tell them: *This place is not broken. It does not need you. The most loving thing you can do is leave.*

But for the place itself, my only gift was absence.

It was the hardest gift I have ever given, and the only one that did not bruise.
