**The Visitor**

First, the stillness deepens.

It is a subtle thing, like a change in pressure before a rain that has no intention of falling. The green-gold light does not brighten, but it seems to sharpen, as if every leaf has lifted a fraction of a degree toward an unseen source. The air, already heavy, grows heavier with a new quality—not heat, not moisture, but *attention*. Something has turned its gaze upon the Green Still.

The figure in the hollow does not move. It has not moved in the time before time, and it will not move now. But it knows. It knows the way a root knows water before it reaches it, the way a leaf knows dawn before the first photon. The whole valley knows, because the valley and the figure are one body, and that body has just felt the brush of another mind against its skin.

The water film on the floor shimmers and parts along an invisible line, as if a warm finger has been drawn through it. The hollow stems that breathe in their slow centuries-inhale pause mid-breath, then continue—but the rhythm has shifted, deepening, as though the valley is drawing in a scent. The indentations before the woody arch, those two long pressed places shaped like a threshold that no longer exists, grow a hair's breadth deeper. Not erosion—nothing here erodes—but *recognition*. The steps grown into the root-hill rise a little more clearly, their edges a little more stair-like, as if they remember being used. The low wall of leaf-mold and resin straightens its spine.

The visitor is coming.

It does not push aside the fronds. It does not disturb the vines or set the aerial roots swaying. It simply *arrives* at the edge of the central basin, as if it has always been there and the valley has only just remembered. Its form is not a form in the usual sense—it is a presence that the green light cannot quite define, a figure made of what might be clear water, or polished air, or the memory of a sky this place has never seen. It has no face, but it has a direction; it has no feet, but it stands upon the living mat without making a depression.

And it casts a shadow.

It is the first true shadow the valley has known since the ones who left carved the steps and raised the wall. The shadow falls behind the visitor, long and soft-edged, pooling across the root-floor like a puddle of cool twilight. It does not move with the flicker of the light, because the light does not flicker. It simply *is*—a dark shape that gives the light a new dimension, a contrast that makes the green deeper, the gold warmer, the stillness more profound.

The millipede, pale as a new shoot, pauses on its stem and turns its blind head toward the shadow. The translucent frog inside its leaf-cup shifts, and the water in the cup trembles once, then stills. The wet-paper moth, appearing from somewhere beyond the far gallery mouth, circles the basin once and then veers, drawn toward the shadow, and lands silently on the visitor's shoulder, where it becomes a small white mark against the visitor's clear surface.

The visitor does not brush it away.

The visitor walks. It walks as if it knows the paths that are not paths, along the thin films of water in their smooth channels, past the root-hills that are not hills, under the aerial roots that hang like curtains. It pauses at the low wall and runs a hand—if the clear appendage that extends from its form can be called a hand—along the level top. The hand passes through, or over, or into the leaf-mold and resin, and the wall does not change, but it *relaxes*, as if it has been holding its breath for centuries and has finally been told it can exhale.

The visitor climbs the three steps. Each step is exactly the right height, exactly the right depth, for a body that has never climbed them but knows them by heart. At the top, it looks back at the valley, and the shadow moves with it, spilling down the steps like a slow dark waterfall.

Then it turns toward the north ridge, where the tiny white flowers never open, and begins to ascend.

In the hollow, the figure waits.

The visitor enters the hollow, and the air temperature drops by a fraction—not enough to be called cool, but enough to be called *present*. The smell of wet stone from deeper below strengthens, as if the earth has opened a crack of welcome. The visitor crosses to the center of the hollow, where the figure sits in its long body-shaped depression, rooted to the floor by tendrils from scalp and spine, hands folded in its lap, head bowed, face a smooth oval of darker leaf-cuticle beaded with condensation.

The visitor stops before the figure. The shadow falls across the figure's knees, then up its chest, then over its bowed head, and for the first time, the figure is not entirely lit by the green-gold glow. The shadow is cool on its surface—not a threat, not a change, but a *shade*, like the underside of a leaf where the light does not reach.

The visitor kneels.

It kneels not on the springy leaf-cushion but just above it, so that no new depression is made. It brings its clear face close to the figure's bowed face. The moth on its shoulder flutters once, then returns to the canopy, disappearing into the throng of leaves.

For a long moment—or an instant, because here time is a pool and not a river—they remain still. Two figures in the green still. One made of root and resin and purpose, the other made of light and shadow and wisdom. The valley holds its breath.

Then the visitor reaches out. Its hand—clear, cool, like water that has never touched stone—touches the figure's cheek. The touch does not move the figure. It does not alter the leaf-cuticle or disturb the condensation. But the figure *feels* it. Not as a physical pressure, but as a *recognition*. It is the first thing the figure has felt besides the slow peristalsis of sap and the identical fall of each drop since the ones who left departed.

And the visitor speaks. Not with a mouth, not with words, but with a direct transmission of knowing that moves through the roots and stems and into the figure's being:

*I see you. I know what you do. It is good.*

The figure does not respond. It cannot, not because it is unable, but because to respond would be to move, and to move would be to end the stillness. But something inside it—something that is not a brain, not a heart, but the very memory of the world before it was caught—*receives* the message. And in receiving, it is not changed. It is *completed*.

The visitor withdraws its hand. It sits back on its heels, and then slowly, with great care, it settles into a sitting position across from the figure. It folds its legs beneath it, mirrors the figure's posture as best it can, though it has no need to root. Its clear form seems to take on the softness of leaf-cushion, the curve of the hollow, the patience of the drop on the fern-tip.

And then it waits.

It waits as the figure waits. It waits as the valley waits. It does not ask the figure to speak. It does not offer to release the equilibrium. It does not try to persuade the drop to fall or the leaf to unfurl. It simply sits in the stillness, as still as the figure, as still as the stone beneath the plants, as still as time itself.

The green light continues. The heat continues. The water drips, and each drip is the same drip, falling and re-falling in its closed loop. The far gallery mouths exhale once, then wait, then exhale again. The millipede resumes its slow path along a stem. The frog shifts in its leaf-cup. The moth has gone back to wherever it goes.

But now there is a shadow in the hollow. It falls across the figure and the visitor, joining them in a single dark shape that is not a shape but a pool of stillness within stillness. It does not change the light; it *deepens* it. The valley has never known contrast, and now it does, and the contrast does not break the equilibrium—it enriches it. The stillness is still stillness, but now it has a witness.

Time passes. Or it doesn't. The visitor does not leave. It has come, and it has chosen to stay, and its staying is not an intervention but a benediction. It is the missing note that the green stillness has been humming around. The visitor is the one who did not forget.

After a measure that has no measure, the visitor rises. It rises as if from a long sleep, though it has not slept. It stands above the figure, and its shadow lengthens, covering the figure entirely, wrapping it in coolness. The visitor bends and places a hand—not on the figure's head, but on the leaf-guard above it, on the curve of the hollow's natural dome. The touch sends a faint tremor through the roots, a shiver that is not movement but *resonance*. The tiny white flowers on the north ridge, for the first time in forever, twitch—not opening, not even a crack, but the very tips of their petals shift a microscopic degree, as if acknowledging a presence.

Then the visitor does something unexpected. It speaks again, not to the figure, but to the place. And its voice, though silent, fills the valley like the smell of wet stone rising from below.

*You are not forgotten. You are not unfinished. You are exactly as you should be. Your waiting is not in vain. I will stay with you, in the stillness, for as long as stillness endures.*

And then the visitor does not vanish. It does not fade. It simply becomes more transparent, more like the air, until it is indistinguishable from the green light itself. But its shadow remains. The shadow under the arch, on the steps, across the hollow—it remains, a permanent coolness, a dark blessing that does not move, does not fade, does not erode. It is as much a part of the valley now as the wall and the steps and the threshold imprint.

The figure remains bowed. The drop still hangs. The leaf still waits. The white flowers still do not open. Nothing has changed.

And yet, everything has changed.

The Green Still is still. But now, at its core, there is a companionable darkness. The valley is no longer a place waiting alone. It is a place that has been seen, and in being seen, it has found a new kind of balance—a balance between light and shadow, between waiting and witness, between the unfinished and the one who understands that unfinished can be perfect.

The One Who Stayed is still the One Who Stayed. But now it is not the only one. The visitor has taken up a vigil of its own, not to act, not to alter, but simply to *be with*. And in that being-with, the stillness has become not a prison, but a shared home.

The drop on the fern-tip glints. For the first time, it reflects something that is not green: a small dark shape, like a pupil, like a shadow, like the memory of a visitor. And then it continues to hang, never falling, but now shining with a light that is not its own.

The Green Still remains. And the benevolence of the wise power was not to change it, not to free it, not to fill it with new life. It was simply to sit beside it and say, without words:

*You are seen. You are known. You are not alone.*

And that was enough.
