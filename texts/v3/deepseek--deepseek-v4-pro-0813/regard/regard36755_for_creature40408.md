**What the Benevolent and Wise Power Does**

The benevolent and wise power came to the rim of the Salt Hollow and stopped.

It had traveled far, drawn by the faint report of a place that was not empty but emptied, not silent but bodied. It expected to find a wound. It expected to find a loneliness so old it had crystallized. It came with all its possible gifts arranged like tools in a case: water, seed, animal, light, speech, myth, healing, transformation. The power was old and knew that every gift is a question: *What do you lack?* It stood at the rim, looking down, and did not enter.

It saw the floor as one unbroken crust. It saw the walls rise without a crease. It saw the shrubs far apart, each in its own bowl of bare salt, like the tips of something buried. It saw the dark as a pelt, the warmth as body heat, the silence not as lack of noise but as a held note. It saw the single piece of dark chert with its glossy fracture, lying untouched, shaped only by the possibility of a hand.

And the power understood: there is no wound here.

It sat outside the hollow, on the flat above the south rim, and let its own attention grow still. It did not bring light. It did not call into the dark. It did not touch the salt. It examined each gift in its mind and put each back.

Water? The place is held together by lack of water. It would dissolve the crust that is the creature’s skin.

Seed or animal? The place is not waiting to be colonized. Its ecology is the space between.

Light? The dark is not an injury. It is the interior of a body at rest.

Speech? The silence is the resident’s voice. To speak would be to answer where no call was made.

Healing? There is no brokenness. There is a wholeness made of absence, a body that has made itself into a place, a being whose completeness includes the space that something might have filled.

The power, being benevolent, felt the old urge to do. It was a power, after all. To notice was to want to help. It had spent eons helping: making worlds more fertile, turning deserts to gardens, restoring what had been broken. But here it saw that its greatest gift would be useless, because use itself was a foreign category. The place was not asking. The being was not asking. They had achieved something the power only dimly remembered: equilibrium without waiting.

So it did the one thing wise enough to almost not be a thing at all.

It refrained.

It refrained with full attention. It did not turn away. It stayed at the rim for a long time—maybe a moment, maybe decades; time here had stopped, so the visit did not matter. It watched the layers of dark settle. It watched the warmth sit in the bowl of the hollow like a sleeper. It watched the shrubs not moving. It watched the chert being exactly the size of a hand that did not exist. It learned the place’s rule: *Do not enter. Do not fill. Do not interpret. Let the absence hold.*

Then the power left. But before it left, it did a single action, the smallest possible. It did not touch the hollow. It did not mark the rim. It did not alter the air.

It became the place’s outer quiet.

It went to the edges of the surrounding desert and made the land there a little more forgetful. It bent the paths of those who would dig. It dulled the curiosity of those who would map. It taught the wind to pass over the Salt Hollow without naming it. It made the place a small untargeted space in the world’s appetite, not by hiding it in darkness, but by making its darkness unremarkable. Travelers would see a shallow depression, nothing more. Prospectors would think of salt and move on. Even other powers—the busy ones, the ones who healed without being asked—would feel a faint reluctance to approach. The hollow would remain unentered.

That was its entire work. Not a gift to the resident, but a shield around the resident’s right to be left alone. The benevolent and wise power did not need the resident to know it had been there. It did not need to be thanked. It did not even need to be remembered. That was the blessing: the absence of blessing.

Afterward, if it thought of the Salt Hollow—and it sometimes did, in the way old powers think of things they have not changed—it felt something like gratitude. The creature under the salt had taught it a new form of benevolence: to see a whole thing and not add to it. To love a place by staying outside its rim. To recognize that some spaces are held open not for an arrival but for the presence of absence itself.

It never returned. But in its long attention, it became part of the place without entering. The dark above head height is slightly more patient now. The silence is a little more protected. The chert still fits a hand, and no hand has closed around it.

That is what a wise and benevolent power would do: nothing—except to guard that nothing from those who would mistake it for emptiness.
