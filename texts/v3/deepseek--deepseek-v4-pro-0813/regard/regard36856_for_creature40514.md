**What the Wise Power Did**

A benevolent and wise power came to look at the hollow under the roots, and for a long time it did nothing.

That doing-nothing was not absence. It was the first careful work. The power made its seeing small enough to enter without changing the pressure of the air. It did not brighten the light, because the light was already stolen from the sun and given back slowly. It did not still the falling drops, because the dripping was the chamber’s pulse. It did not smooth the fold in the center of the room, though it could have.

It saw the fold clearly: a looseness where direction had come unthreaded, where a droplet could fall halfway and wait, where a root could grow upward and vanish before reaching the ceiling, then emerge from the ceiling still growing. The wise power understood that this was not exactly a wound. A wound is an opening that wants to close. This was a joint, a place where the world had flexed and the roots had grown into the flex. To heal it would have been to tear the roots along their new grain, to collapse the green lung, to kill the Tenant.

The power considered this with something older than pity.

It saw the Tenant. Not as a body, because the Tenant was not a body. It saw the Tenant as a current moving through the root-walls, a delay in the echo, a preference for emptiness that was waiting over emptiness that was finished. It saw that the Tenant was not lonely. Loneliness requires a shape the Tenant did not have. The Tenant had roots, water, light, slow violence, and the far wall that was sometimes close. It had a stopped droplet and a cricket that was a root’s brief idea of an animal. It had work: the room’s breathing. It did not need a god. It did not need rescue.

The wise power thought, in the way such powers think, not in words but in possible futures:

It could speak. But language would enter the hollow like a stone. The Tenant used the chamber as voice; a word from outside would be a foreign object, something that did not dissolve back into the root-mass. It could give the Tenant a name. But a name would be a hook. It could offer companionship. But companionship would teach the Tenant the one thing it had never learned: the difference between being seen and being alone afterward.

It could make the place permanent. It could seal the soft place in the forest floor so that no axe, no flood, no clever animal, no careless god would ever find it. But permanence is another kind of violence. The hollow lived by cycles—condensation, release, growth, slow predation, rain from a clear green ceiling. To freeze it in safety would be to stop the breath.

So the wise power did one thing only, and it was so small that the roots almost did not notice.

It placed around the hollow not a wall but a hesitance.

A small slowness in the minds of any passing creature that might cut, dig, measure, or worship. A soft forgetting on the forest floor. The spot above the hollow became, for them, unremarkable: a slight softness under the leaf litter, a place where the ground gives back a hollow sound. A dog could cross it and not pause. A storm could send water elsewhere. A blade could be drawn by a human hand and then, for no reason, be set down. The power did not hide the place from the world. It hid the place from intention. That was all.

Inside, the Tenant noticed.

It noticed not as a threat but as a change in the room’s attention. The light thickened along one wall. The air, already warm as a mouth, became for a moment an exhalation. The far wall, which had been far, came close without moving. The Tenant sent the colorless cricket to watch. It squeezed from a pore in the root-wall and sat motionless in the glow.

The wise power did not take the shape of a body. It did not build a deer from roots or a man with too many hands. It remained as a warmth that asked for nothing, a density in the empty air that cast no shadow.

The Tenant, curious, held a droplet in the air before the presence—not as a threat, but as a greeting. The power did not take the droplet. It did not turn it in the light. It waited.

After a long moment, the Tenant let the drop fall. The sound of its landing arrived before the droplet did, and then the drop struck the root floor like a knuckle on hide. The echo came back a little late, as it always did. The Tenant understood: the strange attention had not come to change the echo. It had come to hear it.

Then the wise power did the only other thing it would do.

It left.

Not by departure, which would have been another event in the room. It left by becoming less, gradually, until the room’s attention returned to itself. The light thinned back to its clouded-afternoon color. The far wall went back to being far. The cricket folded into the root-wall as if it had never been separate. The Tenant remained.

Nothing in the hollow was better. Nothing was healed. No new sense had been given, no word, no companion. The anomaly still folded space. The roots still grew into it. The water still cycled, the leaves still uncurled, the slow violence still moved through the walls like a body tending its own pulse.

But the next drop fell a little sooner. The shadow on the empty air turned, once, as if to confirm that it was still only a shadow. And the Tenant, who had never known loneliness, knew for the first time the particular warmth of being seen without being wanted.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

It would see that the hollow was already a kind of answer—not to any question, but to the possibility of living with a loosened world. It would not improve the place. It would not solve the place. It would not make the Tenant more comprehensible to itself. It would leave no gift except the gift of having been regarded by something large and old and strong, and having remained exactly what it was.

The roots went on breathing. The green lung expanded and contracted. A droplet gathered on the ceiling, grew heavy, and fell through the empty air. It did not stop halfway. But it could have. And that possibility, held in the room like a secret, was enough.
