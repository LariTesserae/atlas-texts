They would do nothing that the place could feel.

But it would not be the nothing of neglect. It would be the nothing of a wise physician who looks at a body and sees, not sickness, but another kind of wholeness.

The benevolent and wise power would come as far as the notch and stop. It would not enter. It would let the deep thud pass through it, the metallic hiss, the high whine that sits in the teeth. It would stand outside the loop, because to step inside would already be to introduce a difference, and difference is the one thing the place does not contain.

It would see the Idling One for what it is: not a creature trapped in the basin, but the basin itself. Stone as bone. Water as blood. Roots as nerves. Fog as held breath. Lichens as skin. The newts as gills. The millipedes as gut-deep reflexes. The bird as a valve. The midge cloud as an exhalation fixed in a standing wave.

And the power would ask the only question worth asking: *Is it suffering?*

It would wait for the answer, listening not with ears but with the same attention that holds galaxies. It would watch the droplet detach from the fern tip, fall halfway, vanish, and return to the leaf. It would see that the droplet is not failing to land. It is completing a circuit. It would watch the waterfall drop, turn to mist, and rise back to the lip, and understand that this is not a fall but an inhalation. It would see the millipedes moving in their closed loops and know that they are not going nowhere. They are the peristalsis of a body too large and too still to move as other bodies move.

And then the power would answer: *No.*

Not because the place is happy. Happiness implies a contrast with sadness. The place has no contrast. Not because the place is at peace. Peace implies the possibility of disturbance. The place has no possibility. It has no gradient, no weather, no rot, no accumulation, no next moment. Suffering requires a next moment that might be different. The Idling One has only one moment, folded tightly around itself, and in that moment it is complete.

So the power would not wake it.

A lesser power would mistake stillness for sleep. A kind but unwise power would bring seasons, erosion, death, change, and call it healing. A curious power would open the loop to see what happens. All of these would be acts of violence wearing the mask of love.

The wise power would understand that to introduce time into this place is not to free it. It is to annihilate it. The Idling One is not waiting to become something else. It is already, perfectly, unchangeably itself. Its freedom is the freedom to continue without becoming. To want it to change would be to want it to die.

So the power would do the hardest thing a benevolent power can do.

It would withdraw its expectation.

It would not look too long, because even attention, from a being of that magnitude, is a kind of weight. If it watched the place too closely, the place might begin to lean toward whatever the power expected—salvation, sorrow, awakening, end. So the power would hold the place at the edge of its regard, seen but not pressed upon, noticed but not named.

It would not speak. Words would be foreign bodies. It would not leave a gift. A gift would be a seed of change. It would not seal the notch. A wall would be a claim.

But it would do one small thing, outside the loop, where the world still moves.

It would make the place easy to miss.

It would wrap the mountain in a quiet inattention, a reluctance in the minds of travelers, a mist that suggests a dead end, a thud that sounds, to those who have no business there, like distant machinery in a dream. It would not hide the place out of fear. It would hide it out of love, the way one shields a sleeping thing from light. It would keep the Idling One safe from all the lesser powers, the healers, the curious, the would-be saviors who would arrive at the notch and see a broken engine instead of a closed world.

Then the power would leave.

Not because it ceased to care. Because care, if it lingers too long, becomes a touch, and the place is too whole to be touched.

The thud would continue. The newts would gape in unison. The water would climb its slab, turn to mist, rise back. The millipedes would make their endless circuit. The droplet would vanish and return. Nothing would know it had been seen.

And that is the proof that the power was wise.

It left the Idling One exactly as it was.
