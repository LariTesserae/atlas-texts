You do not see them for a long time. That is not because they are hidden; it is because seeing is the wrong sense. The valley is already inhabited by sound before you have eyes for it. The first resident is the pressure in your sternum, the note that makes your teeth hum. It has been watching you since the ground began to murmur.

They gather where the frequencies cross. In the basin where the low ground-thrum meets the insect shriek, the air becomes granular, as if the moisture has learned to pause. At first it looks like heat ripple. Then it turns, and the ripple has a direction. It moves against the wind. It leaves a trail of colder air and spores. That is one of them. Not the whole of it—only the part it wants you to see.

Their bodies are temporary compromises between water and vibration. Some are tall and narrow, built of fog and the memory of rain. They stand in the tower galleries and sway with the organ chords. Their limbs are interruptions in the mist; their faces are shallow places where droplets condense and run down. Others are smaller and more animal: curled things of moss and paper-wasp nest, walking on too many joints, carrying a silence inside them like a lung. A few have no shape at all. You only know them by the way a patch of bioluminescence dims as they pass.

They are the city’s second weather. The first weather is rain; the second is attention. It moves down the fluted walls and through the wet channels, pausing at the roots of the trees. The plants turn toward it the way they turn toward noise. The bats rise through it and come out with their flight paths changed. The frogs stop calling and then begin again, a half-tone higher. That is the residents speaking.

What are they? They are the sound made self-aware. The city is not their building, exactly; it is their instrument and their body, the way a shell is body to the animal that secretes it. The towers are flutes for their long slow thoughts. The cisterns are drums for their smaller urgencies. The walls sweat resin because one of them has sung the wound closed. The spires condense fog because they are humming at a frequency that pulls water from the air. The technology is not separate from them. They are the conversion.

If you come close to one, you will feel it before you see it. The air around it is dense, as if you have stepped into a bell. Your own voice comes back to you changed. It may choose to show you a face: a slight hollow in its fog-body, lined with fine droplets, not a face for eating or breathing but for receiving. It does not use eyes. It listens to your shape. You are a gap in the echoes, a dampened harmonic in the city’s long chord. It circles that gap, tasting the edges.

They do not keep names. Names are too slow. They keep frequencies. The elders are not visible because they have dropped below the range of skin and settled into the stone. They are the low seismic murmur under the basin. When the city shudders, that is not tectonic; it is an elder turning in its sleep. The young are small whirlwinds in the high slots, barely able to hold a shape. They feed on the difference between two notes. A new one is born when a gust of wind crosses a dripping edge at the right angle and a patch of fog begins to disobey the weather. For years it is just a taste of copper at the back of the tongue. Then it gathers a body from spores and mycelial threads and the dust of broken pumice. It assembles itself around a silence and learns to move by letting the silence travel.

They are gardeners and predators and librarians all at once. They prune the ferns not with hands but with intervals. A certain chord makes aerial roots reach toward a channel; another holds water in a bromeliad’s cup; another tells the waxy leaves to funnel condensation. They sing the vines out of the high ledges and back into the towers. They eat by tuning. A hunting resident drops its frequency, and the paper-nest colonies fall silent; the moths halt; a bat folds its wings and drops like a fruit. The resident takes something from that stillness—not the animals themselves, but the pattern of their alarm. It swallows the silence and moves on. The frogs are their metronomes. The birds are their moving scores.

Their architecture of sound is also a memory. The towers are libraries of pressure. Inside each one is a standing wave that has not altered for centuries. The residents go there not to read but to resonate. To remember is to match a frequency. A dead resident is not buried; it is lowered into the ground-bass, its body released into the humid air. Its knowledge remains as a slight change in the valley’s tuning. Some of the towers are not for seeing or living; they are for remembering. The circular openings are not windows. They are mouths for old songs.

The city is not abandoned. It is occupied by these things, who are many and one. They do not need inscriptions because a library of pressure does not erode. They do not need doors because they enter as an overtone enters a string. They are not a species in the human sense. They have no single body plan, no fixed boundary between self and environment. One of them can split into many smaller rain-shadows. Several can merge into a low fog that covers a whole terrace. They are plural the way the sound is plural: a stacked confusion of tones that is also one chord. Their politics, if they have such a thing, is harmony. Their wars, if they occur, would be dissonance, and the city would be their battlefield. But there are no wars. There is no need. The valley is tuned.

They do not think of the place as a home. Home is a human word for a boundary. They think of the city as a question they are still answering. The stone, the water, the growth, the animal life—all are phrases in that answer. The sound never lessens because they never stop asking. When the wind rises, they ask with the whole valley. When it drops, they ask in the dripping basins. The city’s roar is not weather. It is their voice.

If you stay until the wind drops, you will hear them. Not as words. As the sound that remains after everything else has been accounted for: a low persistence, a pressure behind the eyes, the feeling that the air has been listening all along. They are not hidden. They are the listening itself.
