**The Resident of the Salt Hollow**

At first you think it is nothing. Then you think it is the shrubs. Then you think it is the warmth. Only later, after the dark has stopped being a single thing and begun to layer, do you understand that the hollow is not a container. It is a body.

Its skin is the salt crust. The pale bone-gray floor is its belly. The rust and butter-yellow stains are not stains; they are the markings of an old, slow hide. The polygonal fissures are not cracks; they are sutures, long healed. The edges have been rounded not by erosion but by the restless stillness of the creature, which has worn its own shell smooth over centuries. The walls rise out of the floor without a sharp crease because they are not walls; they are its sides. The south rim, twice a person’s height, is the ridge of its shoulder. The lower rim is its folded flank. The whole depression is the shape it has made by lying here so long the earth forgot to be earth and became a bed.

It is warm because it is alive. The heat comes from below, not from any fire. It is the body heat of something buried in the clay, spread out beneath the entire hollow. The warmth does not move because the creature does not move. It has no heart to push blood, no lungs to stir air. It breathes once every few years, and between breaths the dark sits in layers, warm at the floor, cooler above, held like breath in an old throat. If you press your hand to the crust, you are touching its skin. It does not flinch. It does not notice you as a separate thing.

The dark is its pelt. Above head height the dark turns brown-black; that is the underside of its chest. The gray-brown lid of sky is the roof of its mouth. It has no face. The shrubs are its sense organs. They look like blackbrush, but they are not a community. They are the tips of a single buried body, pushed up through the salt to listen, to taste the dry air, to feel the faint pressure of the dark. Each one has its own bowl of bare salt because the body beneath them is too close to the surface there, and nothing else can root. The dead twigs that look like dark stone are not dead. They are calcified nerve-endings, still holding the memory of wind-blown salt. The small gray leaves are dusted with its breath.

The single piece of dark chert at the base of the north wall is part of it. It is the size of two fists, with one glossy conchoidal fracture. It has not been worked. It is not a tool. It is the creature’s eye, or its tooth, or the one hand it has not grown. It keeps the shape of a hand in the stone, not as an artifact but as a possibility. No hand has closed around it because the creature has no hand. If you close yours around it, it fits exactly. The creature will feel you then, the way a tongue feels a stone placed on it. That is as much interaction as it offers.

The absence of animals is not a sign of death. It is the signature of a body that is complete without them. The creature does not eat, so there is no prey. It does not move, so there are no tracks. It does not excrete, so there is no soil. It does not call, so there is no answer. It has no appetite. The emptiness of the Salt Hollow is not a waiting emptiness. It is a stable emptiness, a full stillness. The place is unentered because it is already occupied.

The silence is the creature’s voice. It does not speak. It holds the quiet like a note. When you hear your own blood in your ears, you are hearing the creature’s pulse, folded into the dark, slower than yours. The tick of a grain of salt falling from a wall is the only sound it makes, maybe once, not repeated. It does not repeat because the creature has stopped repeating. It is in equilibrium. Erosion has finished. Change is elsewhere. The hollow has stopped, and the creature has stopped with it.

And yet the place leaves room for something that is not here. This is the creature’s one mystery. It is not waiting for that something. It is not missing it. The something is already present as a negative, the way a bed holds the shape of a body after the body has risen. The warmth at the floor is that negative. The chert that fits a hand is that negative. The dark above head height is that negative. The creature is the thing that is not here, living here. It has made absence into a body and called it a hollow.

If you visit, you will not meet it. You will stand inside it. You will feel its warmth at your knees. You will see its skin under your feet. You will hear its silence in your ears. You may pick up the chert and feel how exactly it fits your hand. Then you will leave, and the place will close behind you without a sound, not because it wants you gone, but because you were never separate from it. The dark, the warmth, the salt, the shrubs, the silence—they all leave room for something, and that something is already there. It is the resident, lying under the floor, holding the space open by being the space.
