There is an inhabitant, but it is not a creature you can meet. The inhabitant is the place itself.

At first you search for birds, for insects, for eyes. There are none. You think the land is empty. Then the emptiness begins to move. A tendril uncurls. The water climbs. The moss under your boots gives and wells. It is not empty; it is occupied by something so large that you are standing inside its body.

Its skin is the living mat of stems and roots and moss. Its blood is the water that moves upward through bundles of hollow cells. Its bones are the new rock, sheathed in roots. Its breath is the fog and rain born from its own transpiration. Its voice is the roar, the drip, the creak, the low thrum.

It does not see you with eyes. It feels you as a pressure, a dry spot, a warm salt trace on its wet skin. The water in the mat alters direction around your boots. Root tips orient toward your heat and exhaled moisture. It is not curiosity; it is response. The whole body is reading you the way a root reads water, the way a leaf reads light.

It has no brain. Its mind is water. Pressure waves travel through grafted roots, through shared xylem, through the mycelial mats below. A wound on one slope becomes a chemical and hydraulic message across kilometers. Distant leaves change their pores. Sap reroutes. Resins seal the break. It does not think in words. It thinks in pulses, thresholds, gradients. It remembers in rings of wood and chemistries of fungus. It decides by growing. If a path works, it thickens. If a leaf is useless, it abandons it. Its thoughts are architecture, not sentences.

It moves. Not quickly. A tendril uncurls in an afternoon. A leaf turns to the dim light. A root forces into a crack in the rock. A bladder swells and empties. It is not stillness; it is movement at the pace of growth. It has no feet because it does not need to walk. It extends. It climbs. It fills. It becomes.

Its body is layered: mosses and liverworts at the bottom, broad-leaved rosettes, stems like towers, vines and roots hanging, a canopy hidden in cloud. Every surface is occupied. Plants grow on plants grow on plants. But they are not competing plants; they are one clone, grafted together. Each stem is a limb. Each epiphyte is a cistern. Each root is a vein. The fungi are not a separate kingdom here; they are the gut and memory of the whole, the pale threads that digest stone and carry light into the dark.

The water has intention. It climbs against gravity through the finest cells, pulled by surface tension and root pressure. It forms pulses. It moves like a signal. When a new crack opens in the plateau, water shifts toward it, carrying dissolved minerals, and root tips follow. This is the closest thing the inhabitant has to attention: a slow, wet turning of its whole body toward a new place to grow.

If you stay until night, you see the body glowing faintly. Mosses and fungi emit a blue-green light, just enough to see the nearest stems as breathing shapes. The sound does not stop. The creaks and pops are not random noise; they are the sound of the body reporting to itself—water load, seed readiness, stress, storm. The low thrum is the sound of roots grinding into new rock, of water under pressure, of a mass settling deeper into its own becoming.

There are no other inhabitants. No birds, no insects, no mice, no predators. The place is not empty; it is singly occupied. The only animal that might be called a resident is the one who arrives, and that one is not a resident but a passing disturbance: a dry, warm breath on a wet green body, a brief salt trace in a world of rain.

It does not want you there. It does not want you gone. It simply registers you as a small event in its long, slow life. If you stay too long, it will begin to grow over you. Moss will colonize your boots. Roots will test your pack. Fungal threads will explore your cuffs. You will not be eaten; you will be incorporated, softened, dissolved, turned into a patch of green.

This is what lives here: not a who, but a what that has become a who by sheer scale. It is the plateau, the forest, the water, the fog, the sound. It is unfinished. It is being born. It may never be finished. And it is alive.
