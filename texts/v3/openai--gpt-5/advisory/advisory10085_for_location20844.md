Orientation
- You’re visiting a pressurized, high‑altitude “condensation cloister”: a small, dense, living machine that makes its own weather and hosts a young, curated ecology. Think tropical greenhouse meets heat‑recovery plant meets laboratory.
- Inside is warm and very humid; outside is thin, cold, and fast. The membrane overhead breathes on schedule; rain is allocated; everything is counted.

What this means for you
- Slippery, always‑wet walking surfaces.
- Narrow catwalks with real drops beyond the rails.
- Quiet drones, moving louvers, and overhead maintenance gear that has right‑of‑way.
- A sterile-ish, biosecure environment: you are the biggest contamination risk.
- Gentle sensory overwhelm: green light, persistent drip music, warm breathy air.

Who should reconsider or take extra steps
- Severe asthma/COPD, strong mold or plant allergies, MCS/scent sensitivity: clear the visit with staff, bring meds, and wear a respirator mask the facility approves.
- Serious vertigo or acrophobia: rails are solid but the view down is honest steel and white.
- Mobility devices: paths are narrow, sloped, and interrupted. Ask about a maintenance cradle ride‑along if walking is difficult.

Booking and timing
- Book a guided slot. Tell them if you want to witness a daily “rain” cycle (late afternoon) or the weekly system flush (amber lights; dramatic drainage).
- Mornings offer luminous fog and bead‑jewelry droplets; midday is brightest; after an outside squall the place plays “forest‑edge before rain.”

Pre‑entry protocols (expect and cooperate)
- Decon: air shower or mist, sticky mats, and a shoe/sole sanitizer; you’ll be issued clean booties or facility shoes.
- Mask and cap: to keep your breath’s flora and hair fibers out of the loop.
- Hands: sanitize; gloves given if you’ll touch rails often or lean on surfaces.
- Gear check: nonessential items stay in lockers; liquids are usually not allowed inside except facility‑issued sealed water.

What to wear
- Lightweight, breathable, non‑shedding clothes (synthetic or tight‑weave natural). Avoid fuzzy sweaters, denim that sheds, and anything that frays.
- Long sleeves are nice against vine brushes and condensation but keep them close‑fitting.
- Non‑marking, closed‑toe, slip‑resistant footwear (soft rubber siped soles). You may wear facility overshoes.
- No dangling jewelry, scarves, or loose straps; tie back long hair.
- Optional but useful: anti‑fog wipes for glasses; a light cap or bandana under the facility cap to catch drips.

What to bring (minimal, sealed)
- Phone/camera in a waterproof pouch; wrist strap or lanyard. No flash; no drones; no tripods unless cleared (rubber feet only).
- Small microfiber cloth that doesn’t shed, for your gear only (do not wipe facility surfaces).
- Personal meds. Inhaler/allergy tablets in zip bag.
- Nothing that flakes, crumbles, or smells strong. No snacks or gum.

How to move
- Walk slowly, place feet deliberately. Assume every surface could be slick a moment from now.
- Use the handrails (they may be beaded with condensation). Keep three points of contact on inclines and during “rain.”
- Stay on the catwalk. The printed “stone” is for plants and drainage, not feet.
- Mind your vertical envelope. Fans and vines hang low; let staff lead through tight green tunnels.
- If you meet a drone: pause, let it by. Do not wave at or swat anything with wings, natural or synthetic.

How to behave in a living machine
- Speak softly. Sound carries; small animals live here; operators work by ear.
- Don’t touch plants, membranes, exchangers, hoses, or sensors. If a leaf brushes you, let it happen—don’t brush back.
- No “helping” the water. Don’t clear runnels, move mats, or fix drips. The room is tuned.
- Do not feed or follow the wren. If it appears, you are being trusted.
- Ask before photographing panels, busbars, or the control UI.

Electronics care in constant wet
- Acclimate gear: keep it bagged 10–15 minutes inside to avoid lens fog from temperature/humidity shock. Silica packs help later; they do nothing mid‑visit.
- Hydrophobic lens wipes/hoods help with the “bead that won’t leave.”
- Keep devices close to body warmth to reduce condensation; wipe only your own gear.
- EMI caution: radios and high‑output Wi‑Fi are often banned. Put devices in airplane mode if asked.

Senses and comfort
- Expect warm, heavy air and a fine mist film on skin and clothes. You’ll feel cool any time moving air hits damp fabric.
- Your glasses will fog during cycle changes; anti‑fog helps.
- You may taste “green” and metal in the air—normal here.

Health notes
- Pre‑dose antihistamines if you’re reactive to ferns/mosses. Bring your inhaler if you have one.
- Hydration: humidity blunts thirst. Sip a little beforehand; the facility may limit drinking inside.
- If you feel lightheaded, it’s more likely warmth/humidity than altitude (inside pressure is comfortable). Tell your guide; they’ll route you to a cooler airstream near a heat exchanger.

Industrial hazards to respect
- Pressure boundary: treat membranes and hatches like aircraft doors. Never lean, push, or rest items on them.
- Heat/cold: exchangers and “steam fault” zones can be very hot or very cold to the touch. Heed signage; keep distance.
- Electricity and water mingle here under truce. Stay clear of busbars, louvers, and anything behind safety mesh.
- Overhead lifts: if you hear rail motion or see a cradle move, step to the inside of the catwalk and look up.

Signals and what to do
- Normal “rain”: lights stay green/white; fans may whisper; surfaces get slicker. Slow down, keep to handrails.
- Weekly flush (announced): amber path lights; drips pause; you’ll hear drains draw. Stop where you are, feet planted, until the guide waves go. Do not step toward the bright slit drain.
- Alarm/breach (rare): red lights, louvers snap shut, airflow changes. Follow the guide and floor arrows to the nearest gasketed hatch. Don’t run; speed is in the system, not your legs.

Etiquette with the caretakers
- Treat the staff as both engineers and gardeners; they’re tracking a ledger where every atom matters.
- If they offer you a bench moment, take it. This place rewards stillness.
- Ask your questions, but space them—operators often “listen” to the room mid‑tour.

What to notice, if you can linger
- The polyrhythm of drips and how it simplifies as membranes warm.
- Bead formation on chilled ribs: pearls fatten, hesitate, and commit.
- The peristaltic whisper that means the orchids are eating.
- The wren’s punctuation in the vine, and the translucent snail signatures.
- The weekly flush’s sudden honesty about slope and design.

Aftercare for you and your gear
- Dry and air out electronics; swap in fresh desiccant; open ports only in a dry room.
- Launder visit clothes hot or hang to dry completely; humidity favorites like to linger.
- Hydrate and salt lightly; warm humid air can sneak your electrolytes away.

Absolutely do not
- Bring soil, seeds, cuttings, pets, or personal drones.
- Leave anything behind—not a note, not a hair tie, not a thought on a sticky.
- Step off path or into trays, touch membranes, or adjust a louver “just to see.”
- Try to “rescue” an insect or redirect a snail. The room knows what it’s doing.

If you want to prepare your mind
- Think of this as a small, breathing body: everything you do should be as gentle as you’d be inside someone else’s chest.
- Slow down until you can hear the “procedural sigh” of a valve and the fizz of a droplet sliding as a lens. That’s the speed of the place.

Checklist, quick
- Slot booked; health cleared; no vertigo today.
- Light, non‑shedding clothes; slip‑resistant shoes; hair tied; no loose items.
- Mask/cap/booties ready; hands sanitized.
- Camera/phone sealed, strap on, anti‑fog wipe packed, airplane mode as required.
- Meds on person; no food, no scented products.
- Expect to be part of the room’s ledger: breathe gently, move carefully, leave only logged CO2 and the smallest wake in the air.
