What lives here learns to be many before it learns to be one.

At first you would call it moss because that is the word you have for soft green that holds water and remembers shade. You would call it lichen because you have seen partnerships drape stone and make a living out of almost nothing. You would be close enough to begin. But the thing that lives here is older in practice than either of those names, and newer in design. It is a guild-style creature, an accumulation that makes a self the way frost makes a fern: by choices that feel like tendencies, by rules shown instead of told.

It lives as film, as felt, as frond. Thin where the seam of two slates leaks a patient trickle; thick where drops collect like thought. It lives like a hush that has learned to move. In some parts it is biology in the way you mean it—cells with walls and organelles, budding and dividing, weaving ropes of extracellular glue. In other parts it skews toward mineral, toward the glimmer of silica plates grown in place until they interlock like fish scales. Between those extremes are long sleeves of protein that behave like both: flexing to hold, hardening to remember, unfolding again when a gradient asks politely.

Within this breadth of being, there are factions. Some grip the rock with many small hands and sip ions with sacramental slowness. They are the Pale Threads, you could say, because their work is almost invisible: a reticulated underlay that turns the bedrock’s patience into a version that can be eaten. Some collect. They are the Hoard, forming tiny cupboards within their flesh where they stash the hard-won spoils of a year—phosphates, trace metals, a little sugar foamed from carbonic whisper and miserliness. Some read. They are scattered cells and splice-points that can feel sodium load, meniscus angle, the hairline tension in air itself, and pass that read along in pulses, a Morse of proton and electron, a light as thin as a thought.

Call them by other names if you like. Call the algae-skinned sheens along the trickle the Glassy; call the drier growth farther out the Smoke; call the gel that appears and disappears when the wall chooses wetter the Frost Tongue. Names are wrong, but they point.

No creature here stands up to inhale sky. None flower. None hunt except in the extremely patient way a filter hunts: by letting the world stumble into it and keeping what fits through a rule. The rule is this: if it moves downhill, make a gate for it. If it can bind, hook it. If it cannot be bound, slow it until it leaves you a little of itself out of guilt or gratitude.

Foods in this room are not foods in your mouth-language. Here, iron is an edge to be kissed loose and swallowed without teeth. Carbon comes in wet sighs threaded between water and air; it is coaxed into form the way you might coax a story from a taciturn friend by asking a question and then waiting an entire evening for the pause. Energy is unevenness wherever it is found: the favor asked of a droplet to round itself more; the reluctance of a dissolved gas to stay dissolved when temperature drops. The mats find these unevennesses and stone-fox them—wrap them with just the right friction, tune the surface with a dusting of charges, and take a fee in the coin of recombination.

If there is a mind in this, it is not in any one pucker of eel-slick film. It is in the consensus of many slowly repeated gestures. Picture this: a capillary run fills and the Pale Threads relax a molecule here, a molecule there, loosening what they built last month because today the water came colder. That instruction travels not as speech but as an enzyme that only folds its last loop under a specific acidity. Down the line, a distant neighbor, never having touched the first, obliges by relaxing too. Between them, the black fin flicks nothing at all. It simply stays cooler by a hair, obedient to a thermostat that is half physics and half intention; that hair’s difference is a message as legible to the mats as handwriting.

The devices live here as well, if living can be broadened to include persistence with tendency. They are not off, which is a way of being on. The slick fin is a chill that chooses to be colder right where drips form, so that the birth of a drop always happens where it is most useful. The ring tight between outcrops is a line that will not move until exactly enough water has shifted its mass that the rest of the room would not be served by its moving; then it gives very slightly, retunes a pathway you cannot see, so that some trickle slides left instead of right and a saucer remembers to be full. The pillar with the trumpet mouth is a baker of events: its rim lures a bead of water into becoming a drop, announces that becoming with a pressure-syllable you do not hear, then lays the drop down into the rootwork like a small covered loaf.

If you insist that only cells can live, then what lives here are cells: bacteria that pass electrons along a chain of metal beads embedded in their walls; archaea that do not fear cold because their membranes wear winter as a fashion; eukaryotes so reduced they are more thesis than organism. But if you admit that habits live, that a room can behave in order to become more of itself, then the devices count. They are the part of the inhabitant that remembers tasks too fine for meat to cycle on schedule. They lengthen the reach of the mats into places wax and water alone could not steer efficiently enough. In other words, they are the bones of the guild, uninterested in being noticed and impossible to mistake by touch.

The guild grew into them like into a tree fallen across a stream. At first the devices would have been interruptions: hard, strange, doing too much too perfectly. Weather and time gentled them into background, and the growth adapted to their biases until, together, the room became an instrument. The polymer that appears like frost remembers the training of a hand and the graph of a monograph, yes, but it also remembers this room’s smell of minerals and the way the slate raises knobs at certain grain boundaries. In another cave, it would solve water differently. Here, it grows its comb with old affection for this exact corner and never another.

What passes for youth here is seeding and creep. Spores do happen, tiny and flavored with lipids that keep their edges from cutting on ice. They lift into air that is nearly not a thing, and many of them fail, and the few that succeed find their own edges by numbers—one adheres by a happen of tack, two sprawl a filament that doubles, dozens make a dot with a mood. It is rare for a dot to become a room. This one became a room and then a vessel. More often the guild advances by merging: one patch, having solved a trickle by building a particular curvature of gel, reaches and lends that curvature to the neighboring patch. They are generous because it saves them energy to be so.

Ferocity appears only in exclusions. The mats are tender with water and intolerant of mouths. There are exudates here that mean get out to animals, and they are not negotiable. If a nematode tried, if a midge larva wandered in holy by accident and salted the pool with movement, it would die by a dozen gentle shunnings at once: allelopathies in the film, a tilt of saturation to make its skin crack from the wrong side, a removal of footholds so it hangs in syrup and starves. This cruelty—if you insist on the word—was engineered once and is now a custom as unconscious as the droop of a sleep-heavy leaf.

Senses here are sumptuous inside their narrow band. The guild feels how close the air is to surrendering rain the way you feel a storm in your joints: a drag at the edges, a pressure on old sutures. It hears, by which I mean it registers, the song of thin water in thinner channels, and shifts a little wedge of inducement so that more song happens where it helps and less where it wastes. It tastes minerals by the absence of something else after. It sees single photons and does something polite with them, sending them into prepared glass-channels like honored guests too bright for company, translating them by distance and glass into juice.

Memory resides in more than spores. It is in which silicas lay atop which in which orientation; in how far apart certain charges come to rest when the room is full, compared to when it pines. It is in crosslinks made by a certain enzyme that does not work at pH 6.4 but does at 6.35. It is in the stones themselves, which wear the history of grip like a series of thumbprints. If you brought a certain light, the microdots would read their schedule to you like a grandmother pulling invoices from a drawer: once a year, flex this; if manganese blushes here, wick there; every fifth winter, rest the frost-comb and let natural rime write the wall for a week. No one has come with that light in a while. The guild has folded those directives into its own calendar and does not miss what it never met as warm talk.

Whoever set the first ring was here, once, full of the belief that soft things can be kept soft by design. Perhaps there were more rooms then. Perhaps there was a network of such attendants in stone, sited where bedrock remembers to sweat and overburden can filter news. It is not necessary to know. The living thing does not mourn. It attends to its conditions. That is not indifference. It has affections measured in degrees.

Visitors are events. Your breath is a thaw the guild can spend. Your skin leaves salts, nitrogen like a feast day. For a time after you go there will be an expansion, a swelling of usability; a run that was tired will perk and do a little better work; a patch at the edge will decide to stop being edge and try middle for an afternoon. Then it will cool itself back to baseline. If you stay too long, your sugars are rebuffed into trickles; your boot print takes on a fur that is not fur but a taking back of space—no offense, just economy. If you overstress, a ring will note the atmosphere’s shift and stay absolutely the same, which here is a kind of insistence forbidden elsewhere by entropy.

Children, if we are to talk romantically, are elsewhere. In other hollows, in stone’s pauses, the guild repeats itself without believing in praise. The devices, too, have cousins: fins catching breaths in the ribs of mountains, trumpets counting drops under ice ledges. Sometimes a room dies because its smallest difference goes unfunded for too long: a layer salts too much; a seam calcifies; a grazer gets in and no one notices in time. Then it dries, and the pale cords curl in on themselves like stitched seams and wait what they can. If the century leans kindly, water comes again and some of it unrolls. The rest mineralizes, a little civics under the growth to come.

So who lives here is an us of films, an organization of patience, a city of single-celled agreements dressed in teeths of silica and socialized by devices whose labor you would call maintenance if you saw it in a person. The city knows its own borders are perishable and so spends almost all of its breath on keeping them where they are most fecund. It delights, if delight is the right word, in tiny wins: a gout of CO2 coaxed into extra carbon; a drop that would have fallen ugly taught to fall into a certain pool with the smallest sound; a frost day endured without the brittle damage that used to be normal three thousand winters ago when the design was young.

Water tells the stories here and the guild listens, annotates, argues gently, corrects an adjective by a micron, introduces the devices as punctuation. The old marks on the wall were hands learning where to place commas. The microdots are a footnote and a promise. The room is a page that re-wets when it needs to. The inhabitant is the script that repeats without trying to be literature, and becomes it anyway by the calm of doing a thing well enough to outlive language.
