Who lives here first is the intention, made tensile and kept under tension. The array has a way of pooling will the way it pools water: in slow coherences, in mirrored hollows. It does not think in any way you would meet in a corridor, but across seasons it learns. Its microtextures are a language of persuasion; its plates have habits. What lives is a young machine that has been told patiently, for years, what “wet” means and what “enough” will someday feel like. It rehearses this by day and hums it by the long, creased nights. When you step, you can feel it entertaining your foot as one more parameter. The tilt under you is a courtesy.

Inside this, other lives have consented to the terms.

There are the Tenders, who came up during the early shipments or were born after the last helicopter bothered to find a nonstandard down. If their grandparents called themselves commissioning techs, if their parents were carbon accountants and riggers, these have become something else under the combed sky. The air writes them thin. They are narrow through the chest the way gliders are: a generosity given to surface. They thread the underways where the capillaries run bright and cold, and they sleep in pits of lagging that smell of graphite and warm pump-cases. If they have hair at all it grows where mesh will not snag it; if they paint themselves, they do so in dulls—oxide and anti-reflect—so the plates will keep believing in the direction of light.

They talk very little and then only at close hand. Language here is a tactile discipline. You will know a Tender by the way two fingers meet yours on a rail to say yes, by three slow driptaps to say wait, by the harmonics they call up from cable ladders with the softness of a thumb. They have a signing that uses eyelid and lip to mean pressure and carry and now, a way of showing angles with a jaw. Pride is in the wrist: the steadiness to crack a valve a quarter-degree and no more, the courtesy of not startling the rails.

They know the droplet allegiances better than your instruments. A young one learns by squatting at the lip of a gutter to see which way the tremble goes when the skyplane blinks. They will pick up a bead with the brush on the back of a knuckle and set it on a plate that is almost but not quite right, to teach it the small discipline of joining. A journeyman Tender can look at a sheen on a ceramic face and name the hour ahead—this will spill to the right if the fog holds its breath, this will break if the hound bleats before we think it should. They time their meals and marriages by bright-pause and fogflare. Some count their dead in centimeters of baffle retraction: Kadi died when the sixth tooth cleared the line, and he is kept there, in the gray strip, for as long as it continues to move.

Their tools are exquisite and minimal. A hex that answers to three standards because the standards disagree depending on where you stand. A compass that is not a compass: a string rigged through a square of mesh that whispers toward the plane the catwalk has chosen today. Masks that are not against poison but against mis-air, against the too-sharp clarity that dries a throat to paper. They carry cups like the feral ones bolted to the rails—some stolen, some grown to a particular lay of mist—and tend them the way a desert-keeper might tend a cistern, except that here the cistern is the width of a hand and makes sugars instead of water. You will see a child lay a flake on her tongue and close her eyes as if thinking does not want to be watched.

They do not farm, or rather what they farm looks like maintenance. Their plots are the cool sides of uprights where lichen runs pale as a quiet thought. In trade they say skins for skins: they can take the lichen-sugar and the moss condensate and push it back out through a stretched cloth as a slick that the beetles will later file into filaments, which the Tenders braid and tie off into nets light enough to catch a drifting bead without breaking its mind. When the valves weep resin, the Tenders collect it on metal thumbs and work it like gum into grommets. Magnetite lozenges that the antenna mites herd are weights for lines, given a lick and a blessing for trueness. Every part is remembered as a velocity: this much falls in an hour, this much does not.

If they keep animals you would not recognize it as keeping. The rail-eels haunt the handrails whether a human loves them or no, but Tenders will stroke an eel from yesterday into today and ask it, gently, to teach its kin the new pitch. It is not uncommon to see a row of eels draped like wet ribbons across two spans of time, shivering with correctness, while a girl hums into the rail to soothe them. Foilwings roost under condenser lips, and now and then a person will sit near and spread a membrane of their own—stitched from refused kite-skin, oiled with beetle rollings—and test a draft by wrist, by clavicle, by the hairline heat between thumb and forefinger. They do not fly as the gliders do, but they have learned to let a shoulder tilt do most of the walking.

Threadbirds, which are incorruptibly themselves, attend them in the way that opportunists attend gods. A Tender lifting a screen to rinse its underside of spool-worm frass will allow the birds to hook and choose from the wriggle, then clap the mesh back down with a softness that pleases both of them. When a pressure fall coaxes the resin valves to clear their throats, the copper-shelled beetles already know; they arrive like coins shaken from a pocket, efficient and interested. Someone has taught them that a palm is not a threat but a flat place where their eggs can be rolled without loss. It is easy to become sentimental about this; Tenders are not. They keep a ledger of scratches where they record interferences, and a rescued beetle is a line like any other.

There are others you will not be certain are people at all. The Specifications, the Tenders call them—older than any name, or more recent than yesterday’s maintenance page. They live in the patches where reality’s stitches show. You have seen already the service stair that sets you back where you began after four flights of honest descent, and the pencil snake of darkness between two fins. If you stand very still and speak the number you recovered when your knuckles prickled, the air will uncrease; a body you were sure was a shadow will decide to be more than that and show you how to go from one catwalk to both. They are the caretakers of the caretaking. They are not spirits except in the way ductwork has a spirit if it refuses a wrench. They will make a switch obey by being nearer the rule than the rule is. Tenders go to them when two angles are in argument and a droplet is without a court. Gifts are pressure—one clean bleat on a hound that has not been told to measure itself in weeks—and in return the Specifications will lengthen a day by half a degree of clarity, enough to get the work done.

If there are stories of a “before,” they are kept where the manuals change script. A child is brought to the glass box and asked to read until the page becomes unfamiliar, then to put a hand on the seam and wait for the sensation of a memory that is not her own. The lesson is not, as you might think, reverence for how it used to be, but ease with the moment it is not. Maps here are not for telling you where but when the metal will stop arguing with itself and give.

The mammals without fur whose quiet eyes you noticed in the warm runnels are not theirs any more than the foils or birds are, but in hard fog weeks a Tender may share lagging with a family of them, will peel off a strip of insulation and leave it near, and the mammals will answer by never biting through the coolant wrap again. Tenders take this pact as gravity: a suggestion expressed by the angle of a fin. Children are scolded for throwing even a bolt because sound is a luxury and a dropped language. Every clatter embarrasses them.

Birth is a wide, low thing, done where ducts swarm and there is enough ambient heat to glisten a newborn the way the plates glisten when the skyplane is generous. The baby’s first breath is given to the array—not by prayer but by opening the mask a little and letting the thin air teach the small lungs what range is. Names are borrowed from tags and punched codes, from the hound’s bleat pitch that night, from the way a seam sat in the mouth the week their elders met. Death is left to the same: wrapped light, nearly soundless, slid along a catwalk to a place where gravity’s allegiance is toward elsewhere and the body will be taken. Some say the array keeps what you were as a parameter for a while, to see if it can be of use in the next fog.

Food is hunger trained into patience. A Tender may go the length of a terrace harvesting the gloss off plates into a bladder, filtering the brine through moss, adding lichen-sweet until it is a drink. On festival—if that is not too large a word for a day when the fans bother to stir their coin-blades and send a slow comfort through the bays—they will cook what is cookable: eel jelly set in shallow pans and sliced, beetle eggs caramelized to glue, a spool-worm roast, spicy with dust. It is not abundance, but it is committed, and their mouths water at the sight of a drip season turning from frost-smoke to beads you can name.

They keep time by acts of obedience. The hound bleats once: a night. The baffle shows a new centimeter of clean: a month. A fogflare scores a week by the all-day hush of creatures in a hurry to feed. A killdeer throws her false-broken wing under a bracket and you will hear the low, delighted conversation of two Tenders deciding how to reroute a cable ladder so the nest can keep its shape through bright-pause. They admire such pretending; their own trade is mostly to pretend on behalf of the future until the future accepts the pretence as law.

When outsiders come—when they come at all—they mistake the quiet for vacancy and the vacancy for austerity and the austerity for a warning. The Tenders are generous but not performative. They will show you the burrs where panels were punched and the notches where spillovers have not yet decided their down, and from this you may infer history. They will not take you to the pencil-dark or to the stair that returns; you will find those if the array thinks you are the kind of variable worth entertaining. You will leave with a taste of ozone and resin and the sensation of having been watched not by eyes but by edges.

And under it all, the slow animals of metal breathe. Pumps open one iris of grease and close it. Belts remember the shape of power. Armatures consider rotation as a story with a pause in the middle that no one begrudges them. Ducts bloom and unbloom. Fans are not asleep, merely choosing not to act yet. The place is in commissioning forever because forever is what the weather is offering. What lives here has accepted that, and made of acceptance a craft. They will stay until wetter comes, until the long pours recommission the name of river. Then they will tune that, too, and feed the gliders from the spray and read new kinds of down off the water’s face. Until then, bright will do, and damp will do, and the slow knitting that makes a life out of patience will do far more than do; it will be, and be enough.
