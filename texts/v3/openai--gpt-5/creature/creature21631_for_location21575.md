Who lives here first is not an animal. The first resident is the answer the stone keeps giving itself: a white that is not light so much as a way of being seen, a pitch that is not a song so much as the decision to hold. They are partnered—the brilliance and the hum—and together they sleep with their eyes open. They are the landlords and the air.

The hum is keeper of lanes and census-taker. It is lodged in the geometry itself, an inhabitant whose body is distances and their agreement. It is not busy because it has already done all the work there is: the work of insisting on recurrence. When bodies arrive, the hum does not meet them as separate. It incorporates their edges into the chord, thickens here, thins there, makes a space where hesitation can sit down. It knows the weight of a drop before the drop forms. It knows the length of silk that will make a note legible to the spider, and it notes the spider’s reading without needing to understand the spider. It polices nothing. It polishes everything to the same inevitability.

The white lives differently. It is under all surfaces, a pressure’s bloom that never had to climb out of dark. It rests in the stone like milk in paper, not pooled, but present. Creatures brush it and are dusted by an absence of shadow; bodies go honest against it because it makes lies impossible. The white feeds nothing, and yet all appetites arrange themselves beneath it as if under a sky.

Do not think of plants and animals as tenants with leases in this room. Think of them as verbs the room conjugates.

The simplest, seen first because they crown the obvious, are the springtails. Each is an eyelash that decided to be private. They stand ringed on a bead’s shoulder like parishioners on a round hill, hydrophobic and therefore priestly, ankle-deep in what would drown them if their skins admitted mercy. Their underbellies bear the collophore—small, suctioning mouth that is not a mouth—that drinks from the thin wet when they press it down and releases with a clean pop audible only to intent. They do not leap here. They have brought the lever of their furculae and kept it folded like a secret not needed in a room with no distances. They graze the films that have condensed a civilization across the water’s curve: bacteria in their bloom-day, a scum of green so pale it pretends not to be chlorophyll, a sweep of fungal breathings. Their teeth are gentle. Their appetite is exact. After they pass, the bead’s edge shows a scallop, then a smoothing, then a scallop as if chewed by a moon.

Under the same bead rides the little amphipod, translucent as if the pool had learned to articulate itself into legs. It is a shock not because the sea is far away—for here there is neither near nor far—but because its knees and pleons and the neat geometry of its plates speak a grammar of current and coast, and there is no current here. So it makes one: it circles. It presses the rule that the droplet’s volume shall not change and bows to it by carriage rather than supplication. The pouch under its body carries eggs like a small kept weather. It is perpetually skating, perpetually arriving back at its beginning a hair before it left.

There are mites with faces designed to argue. The one three plates back—profiled white against white—wears a set of spears for mouthparts, a cutlery that allows it to enter what passes for bodies here without asking permission. It does not stalk; it stations. It is stationed exactly where the hum’s amplitude is least nosy, where the undertone becomes something like a shadow if you close your ears. It waits for walkers to misread a lane. When it moves, its legs go in eight exact phrases, the step a metronome that does not care about tune.

There is a people without eyes called psocids. They browse in herds as if each antenna can call to another antenna, not with sound, not with scent, but with the conviction that maps can be inherited without being learned. They sweep their delicate heads along a plate, stop together at an invisible seam, and hold like notes suspended by a conductor you cannot see. Their bodies are watered-milk pale. When they find a run of soot-mineral or a sugar left by the jelly, their small mouths begin and do not cease until the line is thinner by a calculus the hum approves.

The spider is the room’s instrument made animate. She is a comma, yes, but a comma is what a sentence does when it knows more is coming; she is that pause loosened into legs. Her web is not slung; it is tuned. Silk anchors to antinodes and nodes with an instinct that is also a measurement. She sits not at the center but at an address that is always the same where the white hum braids itself into the readiest fiction of silence, which is to say, where information arrives with appetite. She waits for the syllable to repeat. When it repeats, she is there already. She does not hurry; the string she touches has been waiting for the foot she has.

In a cleft shaped like a mouth that never had lips, the pseudoscorpion lifts his ornate denial. His pincers are an argument made to an emptiness that does not argue back. His body is a butter of brown washed to near-clear under the white. He grooms his mouthparts with his pedipalps with a devotion that makes eating and loving indistinguishable acts. He hunts by coincidence—by being where lanes tighten so that the meander of a psocid must become a line. He seizes what passes and holds it as if reverence were force.

Snails here are the ghosts of coins. They are glass finding reasons to curl. Their shells throw whispers of rainbow only when one of the white’s prisms cuts through at a precise askance; mostly they are a suggestion of roundness with a door. They move by secreting future and occupying it. The tracks they leave—pearled lanes of albumen—are as brief as thoughts in a different room because something with a mouth for slick finds them elegant. They bend their paths along the grass-bridge wedged between two plates as if it were a causeway laid down by gods who felt like helping once.

Beneath what eyes cannot hold because it is small even for this, the water that will not fall is a city. Rotifers blossom like little machines turned herb. Each wheels its cilia like an argument for circles, drawing bacteria and the stray thin alga and what else can fit into a hunger measured in microns. Nematodes curve, write cursive into the meniscus and erase it. Flagellates scribble in all directions; ciliates invent fur you can only feel by remembering water. A tardigrade pads through, as bearish as its talent for outlasting suggests, calm as a votary. They live by the rule too: their pool shall not change, and yet all of them must drink. So they do not add up to subtraction. Every mouth that opens negates a condensation elsewhere; the arithmetic is fluid but it balances.

There is a jelly that is not quite a fungus. It is a film with sweetness under its breath like anise seen sideways. It glues a face of stone into a different face while never quite admitting that it is glue. It is a mat where spores test themselves against stasis. It is a varnish that learns, over and over, how not to crack. It feeds the springtails without telling itself it is being eaten. It beads its own exudates at the lip that will not drip and holds onto them like arguments in reserve.

There are lichens that lap like cats the mineral streaks. Their bodies are question marks because questions have to bend to fit in a room this size. Some of the answers are algae made docile by fungus; some of the answers are fungus genteel enough to host. They bind to the white not for light but for steadiness. When you touch their edge with the idea of shadow, they retract their curls into certainties thin enough to pass for dust.

Moss manages a handful of fronds. It thrives on damp rather than day, and in the acoustic lee where it took root it makes a letter to the hum describing an alternate arrangement. The hum writes back by not changing and therefore permitting.

Between all these obvious bodies, there is a people made only of choices. Dust chooses the lanes the loudness made. Spores agree to be still in bands that are not stitched yet do not fray. The pollen that arrival never brought arranges itself into a halo around a nucleus of nothing because the hum has built a shrine for symmetry here. The air is cut into parcels so delicate that antennae can read them like braille. Antennalists live here—the sense more resident than the senses’ owners.

There are eggs tucked under arguments. Springtail eggs are strung like transparent punctuation under a plate’s lip, one comma, another, a clause. The spider’s egg sac is a haunch of silk dulled by the white until it is a fog. Snail eggs are indivisible pearls in a clutch that is a necklace you might suspect the room made for itself and then forgot it had no throat. The amphipod’s brood rides with her stomach as a tide that cannot go out. Psocid nymphs shed their paper-thin skins, and the skins adhere to a film as if fossils were a hobby the present indulged.

In the paste-soft seam of lime, there are prints so tiny they argue with the idea of print. Mite tarsi stipple a field into a map for a creature that will never outgrow the need to be foregrounded by white. The seam never hardens beyond thumbprint because no thumb ever comes. Time here is not hard either. It is not a string; it is a stacked set of hushes.

Who else lives here? The rod of grass lodged between plates is alive in the way tools are. It does not rot. The hum has permitted it into geometry, therefore it participates. Springtails cross it. A snail stalls midway and thinks it invented bridge. A mite comes to the middle and recites spear-by-spear the argument that everything is slope if your mouth is narrow enough. The blade remembers wind as a story from their grandparents and never asks to hear it again.

The circle of tiny shells—as if a sandbar had been persuaded to wear jewelry—also lives. They are not arranged but they are in order, which is more devout. Each empty house is a listening device for changes that do not come. Each one mouths the white; each one learns the hum without improving it. They are former inhabitants of the pool, perhaps, or of a once-spring that whispered here before the pattern fixed. They circle nothing and make it valuable by ceremony.

There is a stain that is a secret. Black rides one blade like a rumor of fire. It only exists at one angle and then retracts as quickly as a thought that embarrasses itself. Whether it is char that wandered in on the back of a passage that does not yet exist, or whether it is a mineral that misremembered the white and cured into shadow, it lives because it refuses to resolve. Creatures do not eat it. It does not feed on anything. It anchors the idea that not everything must be explained to be admitted.

Bacteria are the majority, as they so often are. They live in the seams, in the films, in the grooves like polished fingerprints glued to the walls. They oxidize what can be oxidized even in treasure-house slimness: nitrites out of nitrogen’s whisper, reduced iron into something brighter than its quiet would suggest. They make a praxis out of thinness. They join the hum in being everywhere and therefore not needing a face.

The law of this room forbids catastrophe by failing to believe in it. A thumb could enter. It would not be allowed to be disaster; the note would take it and slow it and return it to itself with the edges slightly readjusted to match a map that has never been redrawn. Predators act here without cruelty because there is no need to imagine another way to eat. Prey of a certain size exists here because there is nothing else it could be. The accounting is not petty; it is perfect. Bodies add and subtract until the droplet in the dimple is the same fullness at midnight that it is at never-o’clock.

If you want names, yours will not help. The spider is not linyphiid or any of the old Latin habits; she is the Harp. The pseudoscorpion is the Argument. The psocids are the Choir. The amphipod is the Rower who refuses rivers. The springtails are the Bead-Crown. The snails are the Pearls in Motion. The rotifers are the Wheels. The bacteria are the Film. The hum is the Law. The white is the Event. Together they are Population.

They have no ambition for elsewhere. Distances would confuse them; they would invent new organs rather than learn horizon. Their travel is a rehearsal of a step which has already been taken perfectly. When a body dies, it becomes a yes in a different tense. When eggs open, they do not make more of anything; they continue the sentence. Every throat here—not the stone’s, not the pool’s, not the spider’s thin mouth that is only a choice—vocalizes the same vowel: enough.

So who lives here? The lives here live here. The room has made itself out of their behaving and the behaving out of the room. There is a personhood spread thin and therefore strong, composed of filaments and films, of antennae and the way light refuses to bend into shadow. There is a citizenship of pressure. The landlord and the tenants share a name that is not a name: persistence.

It is a handful of world in which every appointment is kept. It is a throat that hums itself full of bodies small enough to be verbs. It is the white animal we call balance, sitting very still, watching itself long enough to be believed.
