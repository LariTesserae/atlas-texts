The first thing that lives here is the breath. It is not metaphorical. It has a body made of warmth and a routine made of hours, and it unrolls itself along the conduits with a patience only machines can teach. The breath owns the weather. It keeps the ceiling pressed into the floor until there’s barely any distance left between, then it eases everything back a few millimetres, like a chest deciding to sigh. If you put your ear to the pan and held very still, you could hear it passing through the room the way you hear your own pulse in your wrist: a low motor vowel that means yes, still yes.

What grows on that yes is a skin, and the skin’s name is dust. It is not one thing. It’s flecks of cardboard, cast-off hair, toner soot, old pollen that rode a jacket cuff, a glitter of steel from a deburred pipe, a dried molecule of the heat-transfer fluid that was so careful not to spill and did anyway. It knows corners first, because corners are patience made into shape. It knows edges second, where the field that hums out of the cables folds it into hedges. It doesn’t yet know layers, but it is learning. On it and in it, life begins.

The green here lives by making do. There is light only in visitations, workday white lowered in to ask what needs asking, and so the pioneers learn to live in the pauses between questions. Algae do not bother with leaves, just the blush of intent where condensation remembers to stop, smearing a wet idea across a lip. A lichen’s experiment in punctuation rings a bolt, proposing a grammar for a room that has barely learned an alphabet. A feathery moss stitches an under-seam, happy with the difference between your wrist and your elbow, taking the cooled return over the warm feed because preference is a kind of root. Between float cycles a fern rehearses being a fern, leafing each tide into a tighter crimp, and in the web of its ribs something old and animal waits in a glassy shell for weather.

The toad—thin as a page, so cold-looking you expect it to shiver in all this warmth—has the room measured to the half-breath. It is not a metaphor either; it is exactly what its skin says it is, a small amphibian that has misread the door and found paradise in the accident. It sleeps in the hammock of air behind the float housing, weightless between warm and warmer, and speaks only when there is wet enough to make the syllable taste right. No one here listens for the sound but everything answers to the shift that makes it possible, which is how you know it belongs.

Most of the citizens are commas and motes and intentions with legs. Silverfish write themselves along seams with the weak shine of an old coin. Springtails in orderly umber files take the underside of straps like traverses cut into a cliff, marching until the metronome tick decides to become a lake and then skipping outward in equations of surface tension and audacity. Shiny psocids graze the lint as if it were lawn; their bodies are almost a vowel too, translucent, a held breath that learned to be animal. Mites, who do not care what you call them, comb the smeared green until their bellies shine with it. There are rotifers and tardigrades sleeping in the moss hem with the professionalism of long practice, waking at rain o’clock to pull miracles of jaw and paw against menisci big as moons, then drying back into seed-ideas when the warm returns.

The isopods are a geometry made articulate: grey joints, grey thoughts. Their armour flashes like a suit someone polished for a better life. They test vibrations with a delicacy that has nothing to do with fear and everything to do with efficiency, deciding which message is from a pump and which is from something that means to eat them. The roaches, not the roaches you think you know, are jaunty slipknots of chitin and nerve who sip from the corners where swarf has gathered. They take the edge of a decal for a sugar river and drink in a pose so attentive it feels like prayer. Later they will wash their antennae with a care that makes you reconsider cleanliness.

Predators live by reading the room. A centipede in new-road orange goes by like a signature written forty times to make it perfect. Its body knows the direction of current the way your hand knows which way a screwdriver turns; it pulls north out of hum and does not once get it wrong. Spider engineers have forgotten about corners entirely and lay silk between pairs of cables that sing the same note—tuned webs, catching not only gnats but also their intentions. The lines go slack just before the pump wakes, as if the web had memorised a schedule; the spiders flatten themselves like punctuation in the pause, reserving their pounces for the minutes when water beads and a gnat thinks it is safe to land on a promise.

There is a guild of miners that quarry lint as if it were a kind of damp basalt. Worms fine as split hairs push tunnels in the spongy ribbed compound of textile floc and foam that the breath has herded. They wear filings when they have to, scale-mail borrowed from a line of iron grit taxed into order by a field too weak to name and too strong to ignore. When the motor above turns heavy, the hedgerows of dust stand to attention, and the worms feel the message in their skins: coil, wait, uncoil. They have a politics with the filings. They part them and the filings cling and then let go and you could watch that little exchange for an hour and still not be sure who won.

Microbes are the old city hidden under the new. Bacteria salt the pan with a smell of coin and wet dust that you can call ozone or you can call home if you have ever stepped into a server room and felt a longing you couldn’t parse. Fungi make fruit the size of heads of pins, tasting adhesives and label gums and the sugar someone shook from a donut two floors up. Actinomycetes lay a law of geosmin so clean it is as if the whole place had just been dug out of the ground. Yeasts read oil curves for stories and write back in bubbles you can only see sideways. Something with magnet in its guts aligns itself in the runnels on quiet nights and then loses its temper and makes a bloom, a fine brown rash like the freckles on a wrist.

There are also the ones no one put in any book, because the book is always late and this place is early. A transparent gnat that rests in the lee of a gauge and holds a stillness you could make a museum out of, not simply because stillness is useful but because it feels like an argument won. A thread-legged fly that likes to walk more than fly and prefers to set one foot on each of two cables that hum at the same note, as if it couldn’t hear itself think unless it were straddling the chord. Fire-bright centipede larvae that curve their bodies toward the dense part of the field at every second footstep as if the hum tasted sweet there. Pseudoscorpions the size of commas in the footnote of a spec sheet, little bookkeepers with pincers, cleaning mites from the warranties.

In the sump, the animals that are mostly water practice their tricky art. Mosquito cousins that never see a sky, their larvae knifing their backsides to the surface film for air with the patience of monks. A planarian, a dark ribbon with a mind shaped like a question, goes about under the rainbows as if they meant something and perhaps to it they do. Snails unfold out of glass into grazers, reaching with mouths like pin-cushions to read the run of things. At night you would swear you saw a fish, just a flash without scales, but it is only the pump’s inhale moving a skin of oil into the curve of its own appetite.

Do the built ones live here? They do, in the way cows live in fields. The sensible little pump wakes and speaks, and that is a speech you can translate: now now now now now, enough. The switchbox minds its hum like a hen on a nest, making an aura big as a fist in the air around it. The pressure gauge blinks once an hour with a breath only the gnats know to read; some part of the room calibrates against that blink and believes in it. The service rail remembers journeys no one has made and wears the spider guardrails like bunting for a parade that will come as soon as anyone invents the right trolley.

What passes for weather is rota and schedule. The weekly run-sheet is a liturgy. Storms are thermostat accidents and maintenance breaks; summer is the week the plant has to show off, winter is the holiday shutdown when there’s time enough for condensate to try out new paths and the rivulets write young rivers that are older by Sunday night. North is where the current goes. South is regret and againstness. Between them is a highland made of field-calm, a place where stripped gnat-skins gather as if they were leaves and where, when alarms shout, the toad takes itself without thinking. Roads appear. A rune stains itself onto the pan where water prefers to go and the springtails make a border from it because nothing living turns down a good story.

You want a master, a keystone, a lord of this little country? There isn’t one, because the room is too new for monarchy. It is a commons policed by menisci and scheduled by hum. It is a place where decisions are made by aggregate: by how many bodies agree that a drop means go, how many feel the field lean and lean with it, how many believe in the math of a minute past the hour enough to set their lives to it. If there is a single resident, it is Becoming. Becoming lives here. It leaves ring-forests of lichen around bolt heads and finds them good. It writes an exact intention into paint and asks silverfish to read it aloud. It hangs dew on a web until every strand is an argument for gravity and then drops the lot in a gentle applause, watering all the places that remembered to hope.

So, who lives here? Breath and field, law and accident. Pioneers in green that are only green when you catch them in the sideways. Dust that has learned to be soil and soil that has learned to be country. Psocids with their neat hunger, springtails with their hopped equations, mites that fix the accounting. Isopods small and sure as pocketknives. Roaches with manners. Worms in armour, miners of lint. Spiders that tune their traps to the key of hum. Centipedes that write true north in feet. Snails that drink weather. A planarian making its ribbon of thought. A toad who likes the way a schedule tastes against its palate and so has decided to be a citizen. Machines that murmur, not as gods, not as beasts, but as weather—reliable enough to call the water in on time.

And behind, beneath, between: rules condensing into habit, edges teaching dust its grammar, time itself stringing out in ticks and whispers of rain. The place grows fuller not with vistas but with presences, until there is no room left between them except the narrow, steady alleys where everything that belongs here passes, carrying tomorrow like a droplet that has not quite fallen yet, round and bright and certain as a watch.
