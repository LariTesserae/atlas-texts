Those who live here are specialists in the edge, heirs of the seam where heat and thin air barter over a film’s worth of water. They are not settlers so much as instant makers—arrangers of surfaces—taking what the mountain exhales and what the sky steals back, and turning that argument into flesh fine as a whisper.

Oldest, if age can be counted in hours in such a place, are the dark eaters of the skins. They are the emerald-makers, stooped chemists that graze the rock’s broken bonds. They win their bread from iron and sulfur, from the small rebellions of electron against mineral. Under your squint they look like mud, then stain, then a faint thready pile. Lean closer and they are ranks of flour-grain ovals, each wrapped in its own sheen, each glued into a congregation by slick exudate. They breathe iron and let out sweet-metal on their tongues. The color they learn to wear—brown gone green in the right slant of light—is not vanity but happenstance, pigments tucked in case of sun. Where the mountain’s breath slants steadier and the wet lingers, their army thickens and frets the light to jade. When passing heat thins the film and peels them, they slough. When sloughed, they are soup for everyone else.

Around, above, and through them, another people move by changing what water wants to do. Imagine a body whose skin is a border only because it can persuade the meniscus to bow. They are bubble-folk, tensioneers, living in the precise mathematics between drop and surface. A bead inches along a ceiling of glass the way a thinking animal would, not by muscles but by gently ruining and remaking its own curvature. It lays down a faint light scar as it goes, shaving the emerald monks nearly bald, and drinks whatever stirs in the wake. When two of its own kind meet, they do not so much touch as recognize a sameness in skin; union is a sigh felt in your teeth. When the overhang’s edge hooks them, you watch the mindless intelligence of geometry split them cleanly in two—two selves, still tense with purpose, each taking part of the harvested road.

Some of these tension-makers carry cargo: granules of salt riding inside the bead, flakes of cell wall, filaments folded into their water like tools stowed under a tarp. These possessions change how a droplet tugs at the world. A bead thick with certain salts will, at a crux in the rock, choose to flow where the gradient is steepest in heat rather than in gravity; another, seeded with oil-skins secreted by pigmented things, will stop dead and set a weir, catching its kin behind it and milking their contents across a tongue-thin lip into itself. Their bodies are economics: who can make the surface stiffer, who can soften it, who can tip the meniscus at just the winning tilt.

The skins’ stoics do not object; they are eaten as often as they eat. They have their own bright cunning. They knit mucous spindles that flash like hair in the right light. These are not stalks or roots, not plant at all, but extruded ladders quickly stiffened with mineral—iron glazed thin along slime, salt calendared into grit. Dew beads on these sky-threads and, under a tension gradient too fine for you to see, flows backward toward the anchor cell. The thread is abandoned the moment it has paid for itself with water. Another climbs. In an afternoon, a cathedral of scaffolds will appear and un-appear; all that remains are freckles of stain and three new green circles where the ladders once fed a group into bloom.

Not everyone here is a mathematician in water. There is at least one jointed thing, small as a serif, made of chitin angles and patience. You know it only by a flick of highlight when it moves. It toes along with hooks that find purchase on the mountain’s razor grain, dragging behind it a halo of cloudy motes that are not dust but a tame scurf of cells—its harborers and harvest. Its appetite is a preference written in taps. At a weakness where a traveling bead has shaved the emerald film to raw, the skater kneels and rasps, not to eat the gray, but to crease it—a gutter an eyelash deep. The next droplet that comes binds there and holds. Around the rim of that sudden pond the skater circuits, licking gradients you will never taste, harvesting sugars and iron and hints of sun. If it uses tools, they are of the place itself: a hook of salt lifted and set, a scratch, a membrane levered up and let fall. By the time you decide it thinks, it has already demonstrated it does not need to.

Where there are gatherers, hunters. You do not see some of them except as a moving absence. A bare nibbling crescent passes along a green, erasing, its lip so fine it counts as the idea of a blade. It is a thing that adheres to the barest surface charge and rolls that advantage ahead of it, a tide of adhesion scraping skim from stone. When it touches a bead, the bead startles, then goes blacker at its skin and collapses to a spot, its contents gated across some invisible border. Elsewhere, a collar-shaped body cinches around a droplet with a suddenness that is not motion but a changed equation; the ring tightens, and the bead is eaten from all sides at once, its last wobble the only eulogy. Then there are the snippers, jointed or not you cannot tell; they patrol the interstices where thread and thread cross, and in a single curt failure of tension, they cut. A chain-of-pearls of little spheres goes from traverse to tumble in that instant, three of the chicks sliding not down but toward a narrow, shining mouth in the rock, into ambush.

The light is a weather here, and there are those who have learned its schedule the way you would learn a city bus. Some of the emerald-skins keep tucked-in suns—pigments the color of old money and highland moss—that, when the lens-light paints the pocket in sudden caustic, bloom to ochre and mint. For a minute they are fields, flagrantly rich. Their grazers—sleek-lipped cells with cilia not for swimming but for combing the wet—posture low along the edge of that brightness and rake. Farther back, a family of feathery, glassy fans unfurls from where it has been plastered flat to the stone. Not plants, no: filters. They taste the air, which in that instant is fog, and clap shut again when the sky-beam moves on. Their closing drops a little rain of caught things onto their own mouths.

Heat is also weather, and there are those tuned to the mountain’s breath. The pressure in the little room swells and relaxes by a hair, the way sleep breathes. Some bodies are sensitive to that. Barophores, if you need a word: skins that, when the hollow sighs out, ripple and lay themselves flatter, gaining more meal from a given wet; when it breathes in, they ramp their lips and stop. When a hotter puff climbs from a capillary and covers a whole wall in mirror, mirrors popping as beads form—in that storm, entire neighborhoods let go like dry leaves and ride. On the new shore, they do not build permanent; they pencil themselves in and chew the margin. No one here is certain of more than an hour.

There are masters of salt. Not the slow-growth spires that garnish protected corners like ungrown teeth; craftsmen that precipitate and reap in minutes. One such lives in the green pocks that look like small bites taken by a tidy god. It writes in salts on the skin of the film, spelling a lace of whiter lines only visible when you tilt your head. That lace is a corral. Water runs to it. Nutrients stray there and are held there. When a predator by adhesion rolls up, the saltwork means the difference between vanishing and merely shrinking. The saltwright licks its own fences when they have done their work and starts a new piece elsewhere. You can tell where it has been by a peppering of hooked grains, placed not as a statue would place them, but as a hand places pins as it sews.

Visitors happen. Spores the size of irritations arrive, thrown into the stratosphere by fires a continent away, heliumed down the cliff’s face, decanted into this slit by a thermal that could not care less. Soot dances in, thinks to stick, and is lacquered in by a wet breath; it becomes furniture and then food. Pollen, deformed by the height and the heat into wrinkled shrives, lands and is mistaken for prey by a collar-thing, then thrown away in disgust, unswallowable. A blown insect scale comes to rest against a ledge and for a while is a sun itself, concentrating light into an arc where pigmented cells have never waited before; they learn, quickly. And there is you. You touch the lip, flinch, scrape a knuckle; your salt leaks in under the guise of geological fact. It is weather. It is a festival. It is erased, more thorough than shame, in the following hour.

Reproduction, here, is not oldest-daughter-taking-over-the-house. It is bursts. The emerald monks cast off cells that seal themselves against dryness with sugars and waxes, float bodyless until a dew blooms under them, and only then remember to be awake. The tensioneers divide along stress lines, forks of a thought that was all along a desire for more edge. The jointed skater leaves tiny white punctuation marks in the only consistent shade, narrow commas of egg that resist heat by being small. Later, there are instars almost too brief to count, ghostlets in the motes behind its hooks. Death is not an event but a gradient; bodies unmake into slick; slick turns green; green is shaved for energy by water with a mind.

Even the laws here are citizens. Capillarity has opinions. Cohesion makes enemies and alliances you can see if you come back ten minutes later and the same bead appears on two surfaces at once, joined through a crease that, studied long enough, defeats your sense of angle. Gravity is uncertain in a blink between mountain-breaths; some lives are ready for that and climb a little right before the fall resets their world. The rest do not complain—they are rewritten. This is a place where a choice made by a hill of glass a meter beyond the slit becomes a doctrine lived by twenty species in under a minute.

So who lives here? The stoics who make rainbows out of rust. The bubble-bodies who farm the meniscus. The ring that steals a drop whole and swallows light with it. The jointed skater that scratches a road to catch a drink. The threadwrights that pull fog backward along their own hair. The saltwright, writing with whiskers that will not last the hour. The grazers who memorize the cathedrals of light and attend their service on time. The snippers who unstring pearls of travel into meals. The opportunists of pressure who lie down when the hollow sighs and stand when it inhales. The visitors who are made into locals whether they plan to or not. The laws, pressed so close together that they seem to make intentions. All of them are more edge than bulk. All of them are built of decisions rather than plans.

They have histories, but not the kind you can stack. They are biographies of seconds, technics of touch and angle. Their memory is not held in any brain you would recognize but in habits of wet and the way a crease catches at a certain angle when the heat is thus and the light comes in sly from above. If there is culture, it is written in a scratch, a placed grain, a turned skin—gestures that last as long as hunger does.

When you go, the rumor of them will go with you: that sweet-metal breath, that sense of a sleeping pressure-beat that you will misread as your own blood. They will not have noticed you enough to fear you. They will have rearranged to use your salt and then forgotten it, the way a drop forgets a face when it runs. Above the soft world and under a slit-slice of day, their nation is the argument line itself, forever mobile and never loud, uttering its physics so quietly it counts as prayer.
