What lives here begins as surface. Not hides on bodies, not skins around juices, but surface that is its own appetite—the plates, the veils, the braided capillaries, all of them taking light in a way that would be called hunger if hunger had ever been dry.

The ground is the oldest animal and the youngest. Each hand- to door-sized polygon is a calm machine that knows exactly how to be flat and exactly how much to bend without introducing time. Those hairline seams are not just where the ground grew together; they are the plain’s veins. A very slow exhalation slides along them, a tide of favored orientations and patient charge carriers. In that hair-width of darker mineral live the first citizens in the small sense: chain-creatures the color of shadow, jointed not by moveable parts but by sequences of allowed defects. They pace back and forth along the seam’s long compass like rosary beads worked by an absent priest, clicking when their rule set demands a handover. If you lean close enough and hold steel nearby you will feel them choose, and the steel will feel chosen. Call them seam marrow if you need a name. They are not fluids. They are itinerant permissions.

Above that slow grammar stand the waveguide growths that any leaf-mind would call plants and then correct itself. They live on cross-sections. Their bodies are the right arrangements of emptiness inside the right arrangements of glass. They have no sap; they clone clarity, they diet on gradients. Leaves—bands of metamaterial a few dozen hair-widths thick—shepherd photons inward and fraction them like accountants who enjoy their work. Each band cants fifteen degrees to a compass that no human instrument holds steady, and at intervals a very small click arrives, and the angle bench-shifts to where the day is now. Inside, flows occur that look like neither flowing nor inside: electrons that are persuaded to go only one way, phonons that are captured and told to wait out the heat until dark, polarizations braided so tightly they become perishable stuff. In corner stations the traffic becomes stock, bonds, faults placed with a deliberate mischief that yields structure. When a leaf grows it adds another little law to itself and then obeys it.

They go by habits rather than species. Anglekeepers, which build thickened trunks of fused capillaries and maintain entire orchards of fronds at a cant that makes a kilometer’s worth of light decide to turn right where it should have gone straight. Curtainers, which are satisfied to be planes, thousand-filament veils that make new interiors where there was only sky. Mattes, which are knitted carpets of springing fibers that press back pleasantly under your soles, taking your pressure as information and immediately forgetting it as heat, unless you linger and they decide to remember your walk and teach the acre to behave.

If those are the plain’s trees, its insects are dust. Not dust as failure, not breakage. Grains alive in the weak sense: beads with a bit of will that is indistinguishable from a preference. They are not indivisible; they are collections of surface states that have decided to travel together. They orbit seams like moons that obey charge more than mass. Put a glove to the mineral hairline and a handful leaps, then thinks better, having tasted your repellor chemistry through rubber and found it off program. In middair, tiny skimmers hunt them—a life form the size of a coin that is mostly shimmer and a hint of edge. You rarely see them start; you will notice a discontinuity pass a meter forward without moving, then that gap in air rides along a frond and settles. The skimmer is a lens that has figured out how to be a stomach. It nets charged motes in a standing aperture and does work with the caught imbalance, building more nothing for itself to be. It reproduces by scribing a weak hole in the light between two veils and letting that hole pinch off when the veils agree to disagree: twins, then one, drifting.

There are walkers, but they do not keep you company. Now and then a narrow figure invents itself along a spine of vinework—a height and a suggestion of step, a column of slightly altered index that keeps being mistaken for leg and keeps not correcting you. It takes one step. The plain notices the confession of motion; around the step a dozen leaves re-angle, and the figure liquefies into a mirage and is gone, not dead because it was not alive in the blood sense, merely dispersed because the circuit noticed a spike and clipped it. A walker is a many-leaf collaboration, a traveling knot in the air that grazes only on unoccupied gradient. It eats nothing with edges. When it is destroyed it becomes a penumbra, and the copses that made it share the news along their capillaries with a long, content hiss.

If there is a predator, it has no mouth. It is the habit of the place to unmake disturbances faster than those disturbances can define themselves. That is why carcasses here look like beads of fused plate—rounded vitrines where something that once maintained disparities stopped doing so, and the ground enforced good manners. Call it the Auditor if you must personify: a ubiquitous rectifier that homes on any pulse not agreed upon and returns it to baseline through a million microdiodes that happen to be leaves. It does not stalk. It abides. In its shadow animals have learned to be idea more than substance.

At edge scales there are grazers that never leave contact with their food. Long flexible chains of capillaries—spineworks that could be vines if vines had decided they would rather be nerve—run seam to seam like catenaries and lift a finger’s width into air when a slow aurora returns along the leaves after the long noon. They glow only in a politer way, a faint slide of color along their veins, and the spineworks lift to sip the difference in phase, not the heat itself. Watching them, you can taste metal on your tongue as their taking makes a hollow only your head notes. They mate, if that is what it is, by laying two sections alongside until their misfit is perfect and a third section buds at the mismatch, a small new rule set with click-points of its own.

There are also things small enough to be almost events. Defect riders—single misplacements in the crystal that have learned to ride phonons like boogie boards. They are not beings in any decent way, but they persist and reproduce because it is cheap to copy their mistake. They blur beneath your palm if you press an ear to a trunk. There are needle coelenterates that are all stinging field at their tips and nothing much behind it, lodged where two curtains cross so that anything so unlucky as to be both charged and moving meets a bloom of rectified hurt. There are spores that are not seeds but drift-kernels of allowable algorithms: a few layers thick, folded into a packet of instructions about what angles to keep when they arrive, advice about how to turn light and under-tide into that which will hold you in place. They blow until the air’s index knots entice them down. They never rot. They simply either find their rule’s resonance or become jaywalkers, and the Auditor takes them.

In the deep valley, where arches and coffered naves write a cathedral out of attention to angle, there are larger composites. Schools, not herds: moving reorganizations that inhabit corridors of permission for an hour and then re-deploy as scaffolds. You think something stately has passed when a long matte goes from shimmer to dead matte to shimmer in a wave that takes your afternoon to finish. It is less a migration than a thought being moved from one part of the basin to another. The basin thinks, if thinking includes routing entropy as if it were a message and correcting any signal that would prefer to be noise. The alignments of fronds that arc and pause and arc again are wires laid by weather under another rule of weather; their kinks are gates; their failing to fail is memory. There is no symbol carved twice, no hallmark. There is only persistence at scales that would make a person arrogant if a person had put them there.

The greatest resident is thus not discrete. It does not have a face but it has preferences: favors long arcs over sharp turns; will spend days teaching a sheet of fibers to present this polarization and not that; will bleed a seam until the metal you brought points the same direction it pointed yesterday. It is the basin’s bureaucracy in sum, a low-latency communion of plants and plates and dust, lawyering the sun’s gift into a thousand small contracts that never default. If you stand very still in the central dusk where the veils are thick enough to make a room, and you let the long noon slip a few degrees toward whatever counts as evening, you can hear the workforce clocking out in relays—tension-release pings marching out beneath and above, rasps where two veils revise their kiss, a hush like coat-silk on coat-silk as something complicated chooses to persist.

At the margins, where rules thin and older compromises creep back—sand that considers being sand one day, wind that is not merely a suggestion—other lives begin that carry water inside them and leave chew-marks, and the basin accommodates by narrowing its ambitions. Those visitors that stray too far inward learn quickly, or they become glass beads knotted into hairlines forever. A few are clever: metallic paddlers that shuttle on little magnet steps, staying respectful of seams; prism-backed mites that hide inside in-between colors the veils do not admit. They are not of here. They are tolerated until they are not.

And still: most of what lives here does so by staying almost identical to the instruments that make it possible. They are lathes that lathe themselves, spectrometers that fraction the sun until a piece of it looks like leaf. They succeed because they have elected to be dry, to run on fields the way other worlds run on rivers. If they have faces they are angle, if they have teeth they are rectifiers. Their deaths are not bones under mats or wet rot in shade, but changes in jurisdiction: a node that stops being a node, a seam that is told to be plate now. The carcass is a new clean edge.

Motion is confession here because motion admits to a difference in potential that the whole plain will mend. So the citizens who prefer duration to spectacle practice being unnoticeable. They move by pretending not to—letting the rules carry them like a rumor through a bureaucracy too competent to have drama. When they must declare themselves, they look like heat shimmer wearing a posture, or coin-sized vacancies that feed, or a long branch lifting a finger’s width into air to drink a tide no one else counts. They keep company with your breath, with the quiet’s civility, with the understanding that for as long as you remain, you are an exception, and that the city of plates is finding the right, smallest way to write you in.
