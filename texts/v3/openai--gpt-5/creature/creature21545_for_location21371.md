The first thing that lives here is film.

Not the slick your thumb would find on a glass rim, though it has cousins in kitchens. This one is architecture. It is a clear glue exhaled by bacteria staking a claim to polymer and concrete, a varnish they weave from sugars and proteins until a surface becomes a place. It catches what the rain brings—dust, city pollen, skin of roofs, a stray shred of carbon—and dissolves it down to fit mouths you cannot imagine. It lays itself exactly where water spends the most time, because that is where a life can be made to live without interruption.

Inside that film are voices like motes. The green-flecked ones—diatoms with glass in their bones—write their crisp alphabets along the ridges of the packings, gold-brown where the light reaches even dimly. Cyanobacteria color their slicks with the deep patience of old lakes. Nitrifiers in their pale poverty gnaw trace ammonia from the sump’s breath and, by doing so, buy credit for all the others to spend. Filamentous grazers, sheathed like conduit, rig nets across bolts where a drip repeats. Ciliates paddle. Rotifers open and close like little cinched purses, rasping at the algae that make the room a low cathedral of food. A nematode as thin as idea lengthens, contracts, lengthens again across a brush of moss.

They say nothing anyone here would call saying. They plan nothing; they occupy. They are the room’s most complete citizens, because they have been taught by rain to read. They can see, in the way the air leans through the blades, that there is no night here, so they do not attempt one. They can feel, in the way a soluble mineral rings a lip, which drips carry calcium and which carry only the rumor of it, so they grow where the rumor does not clothe them in stone. They are poor by design and faithful by necessity.

The ferns came later, spores like ash from a century’s work finding every seam, every tiny ledge your handrail bolts forgot to own. They are happy with a syllable of soil if the wet is steady. They root in the accordion folds of the media and turn the folds into a book that keeps writing itself. Their fronds memorize the lanes of falling beads, lace them, hedge them, teach their tips the choreography of a rain that will not deviate. Mosses pull slow counties of green across the packings where the spray softens. Liverworts lacquer the undersides with the color of old glass bottles and never look up. The long leaves that should have given up—the leftovers from some duct-breath experiment in cleaning air with plant—did not; they took the truce on offer between mist and light and set their ribbony arguments to pendulate. They comb the weather for you.

Between these mercies of green is deliberation. The emptiness is not vacancy; it is the territory of air, which does not forget it is the megafauna here. The fans are not machines in the way a drill is a machine. They are animals of pressure, always inhaling, always holding the sky a little closer than it deserves to be held. They graze the midges as they bloom from the algae ropes, sip them up into their slow blades and offer no drama in return: just a slight enrichment of the dust.

Below, in the shallow pan where the grating gives up its constant metal voice to a different kind of brightness, live those who require a margin. There is a shore, narrow and negotiable, where the scum boards lean a line of quiet against the inch-deep sheet. That is where pale springtails skitter like punctuation blown sideways, feeding on what this world sheds of itself. That is where a snail in a brown grammar lesson creeps along the translucent chalk on a plate and leaves behind one sentence of silver. A frog appears there sometimes, body all agreement with water, as precise and noncommittal as a period. It stays until it does not. No one sees it arrive or depart. It is not hunted. It simply belongs in the way you think only certain commas fit certain lines.

Up where a lamp kisses a run of conduit, a spider has risen to engineering. It understood something about circles and tension and tried to apply that to a climate that will not keep still. Its work is a damp disc tugged long and thin by the never-absent wind, and it endures because the spider does, adjusted now to a program of mending instead of catching. This is not a hunting ground; it is a rehearsal hall for tensile strength. Even stubbornness lives here if it can keep its feet wet.

Fungi are almost a rumor. Biocide is a god here, clergy in a plastic box with a peristaltic devotion, dosing with bureaucratic gentleness so that nothing with lungs that makes mist into disease can get uppity. Still, behind a conduit lip, a mold has written its low blackings in a place the rain reads less often. Hyphae insinuate where the operators forgot to imagine a drip. They do not make flamboyance; they make a precise shadow. They are the quiet teeth of dust.

Mineral has learned some mammal tricks. Calcite keeps flocks along the steel lips, white and friable, drip-mutton that grows from the daily milking of water for the ions it wears. They aren’t alive, but they behave like something that would be if it could. They grow where it is always barely the same again, which is the nearest thing to a condition a rock can call nurturing. Evaporation is a slow shepherd with a patient dog for a fan.

If there are gods, they are men and women whose hands have not been near this air for years and yet are everywhere. Their lashing of wires at hand’s width tells you where thought once placed consequence. Their painted lines say where it is safe to stand if a panel yawns. Their chalk date behind plastic is less inscription than spell; whatever they meant to maintain, the room has maintained itself by never changing the magnitude of anything that moves. The gauge needles live in their zero-drift Eden, pinned to exactly where someone — a person with a chewed pencil, an eye for standardization — decided contentment would be. They do not wobble. If the humans live here, they do so as a chorus of absent intentions clicked into permanence: setpoints, interlocks, ranges accepted and enforced.

You could say the machine lives here, too. The pumps do not throb; they persist. Their breath is the roar. Their faith is that water, pressured in manifold and flowered through a thousand nozzles, will make a world soft enough to never resist them. They are not sentient; they are continuous. Still, stand with your hand on the rail and you will feel a mammal’s hum in your palm and you will think of heart.

What does not live here: drama. Predation. The fast. Sensations that peak. Night. Surprise. What lives instead are schedules, habits, affinities. Everything that wins in this cell wins by mapping a repeat and settling into it until the repeat includes it as an edge condition.

And there are the transients who think they might belong and discover that the lease is strange. A dragonfly that rode a draft in from a stairwell once, mapped the vast, saw that there was no buoyant stillness worth a clutching ovipositor and let the air give it back to somewhere with less conviction. A moth tries the light, perches with the implacable determination of velvet, and becomes fur and lime. A seed, ambitious, cannot find a dry moment, swells prematurely and becomes food to the fine democracy of rotifers who do not believe in ambitions, only in mouths.

In this place a life is a contract with water. The clauses are simple: stay wet; stay low; learn the lanes. Most of the signatories are small enough to occupy a bead’s skin. Some are wide green fronds that hold motion the way a shelf holds books. A few are animals noticed only when you choose to look at the inches you are stepping over. All are honest. None are curious.

If you must name the resident, you could call it compliance. You could call it a marsh that learned to pass for equipment and never forgot either language. You could call it the film, again, because it is everywhere and uses everyone’s name, even the machine’s. The room is a hearth for water, and what lives here are the kinds of lives that can be warmed by a weather that is noise and never goes out. They are the small keepers of a long, wet truth. They do not ask for more. They have everything they need, continually, and they are continually themselves.
