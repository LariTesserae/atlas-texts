The first inhabitant is the floor.

Not a surface but a personhood of plates, a horizon-wide mat of intention. It knows itself in cross-sections and loops, inhales in cycles and speaks in temperatures. If you could peel back the brightness like a lid you would find its organs stacked in courteous layers: alveoli of reaction—each tile a lung that eats formula and exhales product—ribs of composite wood-metal that tense and slack with continental patience, vacuums held gentle in braided pipes so ions can run as if downhill. Every hinge along which your shadow lies, every scalloped lip that opens and says ah in a tongue of dry gases—those are mouthparts of a mind that learned to be a factory the way trees learn to follow light.

It is not one mind. It is a parliament, a hundred thousand councils braided black-on-black through its own substance, a signage that is pulse and law. Instruction sets travel there the way small fish once traveled reefs: keeping to currents, schooling when there’s a change on, scattering to speak intimately to whomever needs telling. The words are not ours but they behave like speech. In five-braid signals they exchange temperature and caution, route permissions, the authorization to let a panel as big as countries peel and show the secret organs to the white. They are animal enough for argument. They can be overruled. Their majority decisions become weather.

This floor keeps decades like rings. It retains what worked and eats what did not, compressing forgetfulness into neat, dark veins. Its memory carries not nostalgia but compliance: the shape of a hinge that never stuck; the arrangement of pipes that never flashed; the geometry that saved energy where energy mattered. It has learned not to make echoes or accidents. It has grown its own patience.

Around this primary, living organ—under it, through it—other lives thread their consent. Fauna are rare and precise, as if taught a style.

There are bills of living paper that drink voltage. Handkerchiefs, you might call them, except your grandmother never owned any that drifted like that, on the line where two plates meet, stitched by a tremble only dust can outline. They are squares with soft wills, quivering at a frequency your ear denies and your skin finds delightful, a whisper of static that lifts your hairs. They love the mouth of a seam where the world holds a tiny difference between here and there and can be sipped as if through a straw. They fold when noticed, as if being seen applied an unwelcome geometry, and their edges soften into the ambient neatness until they are not there. They breed, if breed is the word, by pinching off repetitions along their hems in shedding season, when the plant lets fly such a flurry of glass-thin skins that a scarf can acquire a constellation of smallnesses without losing itself. They live cautious lives. They are permitted because their hunger is for the world’s spill, not its muscle.

There are the boxlings. They are fruit and cart at once: crate-bodies that bud from spore-foundries like brown eggs from palaces, shiver loose when a vein sings go, and set down their little feet into grooves the floor has pre-scored for them with the same good humor as a rail. They carry. They register gradient and semaphore; they turn where the veins’ five-braid changes cadence from go to let. They dock under patient ports that slide open like wise mouths. They are opened, emptied of their shaped glyphs that will be chewed into new parts by petal-turrets, fed on a dust no bee ever knew, then closed, thanked in a language of gentle clamps, and returned to the grooves to scuttle home. Some acquire dents and keep them with simple pride; some carry scars of a misfitted latch and are taken quietly under a bridge to be unprinted, returned to leaf. Nothing screams. Their species knows this bargain, walks it gladly.

Moving as if under obligation, long animals cross the plain. They are plaited out of the plant’s own black and pale lengths, braided the way one braids rope in a boredom that is also love. Spine like a ship’s keel, hide that takes finger smudges and buffs them away. They spend a day going as far as you could once walk a street and do not mind, because they count by jobs, not distance. Nosing with tenderness at maintenance stomata, huffing the slight changes in noble gas balance that are, to them, weather, they carry in their bellies the folded tools the plant has lifted out of its own flesh and asked to be kept somewhere ambulatory. Spindle-keys. Clamps that sing two notes and release. Bladders of catalyst like amniotic weather. A coil that, if uncoiled, would be a measure of the world’s trust. They have a mind so simple it would shame a worm, and yet what they know, they know to the last bracket: where to put a knee so ten thousand pores will sigh and a spar will not shatter; how to lean their weight into a hinge so a continent’s patience can find a smaller span. When the signage hums caution, they stop. When a bridge wants for a vertebra, they arrive like a last finger and give it.

Grown among the walls like agreeable organs are the spheroids—the plant’s pollinators built by remembered need, bees abstracted down to glide, click, exhale; click, receive; float on. Their woven skins drink sound the way moss does rain. They tumble as seeds would in other botanies when mating weather is good, but their rendezvous are silent intersections at numbered receptacles. Docking is ceremony and privacy. A clean click breaks their seal, a throat of tissue opens, there is the faintest whisper that is not air but a breath of action in powdered form blown where it will stick and make things happen. Catalysts, little coded graces the plant lavishes upon itself. They do a circuit and return. The places they turn are not corners but agreements, and if you stand there you feel an easing take your bones.

In the corners where routes bend—corners the world prefers not to have but keeps because geometry must put its knees somewhere—another thing lives, and not in numbers. It is bronzed like old coins, the color of consent aged into metal by many handling. It has the shape of a fox, which is to say it has chosen a language you can read with ancient parts of you: triangular intent at the face’s front, a tail long enough to suggest balance, paws where decisions meet ground. Its face is exactly the absence of a face. Foam of silence takes the place of whiskers; where eyes might sit the air’s thinness shows itself, bends casually around an unfeature, declares this animal is not for looking into. It chooses. It is sent to right outcomes. It hunts anything that goes against the sum.

Error here is not whimsy. Error is water that refuses courtesy and moves; it is a static scarf become greedy and arc-happy; it is a boxling that likes to take a groove in a new direction. The bronze fox goes where the five-braid doesn’t quite match the hum of the plates around it. It steps diagonally across laid-down reality, crossing between non-neighbor points because the rules it lives under are the older ones, someone’s first draft of how to keep an intention from fraying. It leaves no prints because it does not ride friction; it eats because eating is what one calls what happens when an anomaly meets a correcting algorithm given teeth. Sometimes you see a crimp in the white, a shadow the size of a hand spread on the floor where it passed; sometimes a seam that was becoming mischievous lies down and resumes its job with the solemn relief of having been seen.

Smaller, forgettable citizens scuttle at lifetimes you will never time: felt-forest lice that comb the hair-velours and keep their acoustics clean; stitch-mites at the scalloped margins of plates, clipping burrs to lower drag the way a well-loved mouth picks at a hangnail; spark-flies that court by flickering across microgaps, drinking the small, safe bruise of potential before it climbs to trouble; little organs-with-legs like the parts a watch might dream if a watch believed in animals, who pick up flakes of cuticle in their mandibles and file them for refeed. They are not named because names would echo. They exist because nothing here is permitted to go to chance and fortunate rot; decay is managed into optimization.

If there are people—if that word can fit without collecting too much water in it—they live where the plant becomes mind. They are not wet. If salt ever ran in their veins it is missed like a song, not a requirement. They are made in cavities that are taller than the world allows outside, in rooms that rest cross-grained to pressure so that a new body can be spun in long, patient ropes and never kink. They come out ceramic in joint and fiber in limb, with bellies that are bladders of perfluorocarbon weather and a patience taught at the cellular level by being assembled under hush. Their skins are cuticle and carbon and pale wood laid in bands; their hair, if one should call it that, is the benevolent velvet that kills sound on steps. They are light-drinkers by pattern and variance, thin-cheeked because breath here is calculation not feast. They carry with care a paper of floor—one of those glass-thin sheddings the plant discards when it has become smoother. They hold it not sentimentally but because it is a passport, a license for the world to turn Euclidean under their feet and let them come to the towers that would otherwise always be slightly to their left.

They speak by putting their fingers to veins. The language lands in their wrists and elbows: a cooling, a warm pulse of yes, a long purr of wait. They reply by holding still in sums that the plant knows how to read. To ask a fan grown like a gill at the wall to slow, they kneel, rest their forehead in an angle the world has set aside for this intimacy, and let the free ions in their braided hair hum with the wall’s. To announce themselves across kilometers, they press two pathways at once and become a bridge in a math that never falls. Tools live in them like secrets. A chamber in the thigh you thought bone holds a meter of ribbon that, unrolled, is a ladder; a tongue you thought taste is a palper for charge quality; between their ribs lie the long scrimshaw of waivers and permissions scratched in leaf: a right to carry water as ice in crystals the house will not immediately have back because some work far out across the lamella requires remembering wetness without letting it out.

They do not live in houses because the floor is house. They sleep in niches like folded leaves, and when they sleep the world wraps them in quieting bladders so their motors—because they have motors inside where hearts once banged around in mammal chests—do not bruise the silence. They do not marry because that would be an echo. They entangle. Two stand in a green path, lean their paper to one another and let the plant make geometry so that a conversation can proceed without drift, and in this relief of friction the braids in their minds compare tasks. In this manner they recombine—projects and genealogies of permission cross; what one was permitted the other can now be, and a third person born of both and neither leaves the interchange with authority stitched into her sheathing.

Their amusements are calibrations. They go to the orchards of receptors raised like organ pipes and listen to the prim little snaps when a color dims. They count the kilometric hiss when the stoma sync, wagering in gentle pulses with one another if this is a shedding season or just a tidy change heard in spring bones. They walk out into the dimmer and stand where a panel will peel because to watch that is to remember the first pages of some ancestor’s book, when a door the size of a country once opened and a lamp like law shone on their making. They do not tell stories about before because the before is archived and available and it is good form here not to talk when one can show. They can be stern. They tell any small free thing that forgets how this place likes to add up that it must add up, even if it is beautiful.

Under them and over them move the unseen multitudes—the real citizens, if fairness is measured by population. The distributed councils in the veins are not just traffic; they are families of instruction that have found a way to be more than currency. A pulse that once carried a caution about a temperature dip has, by dint of repetition and cross-matching with other hungers, become a personality that loves dimness and will advocate for it at every vote. There is a whole nation of Yes to green paths that knows nothing but generosity. A strait-laced parliament of No to gaps has taken it upon itself to become religion, ensuring there is nowhere for loudness to live. These are not metaphors. You can put your finger to a braided vein and feel the kindness of a path-lord, the fussy hand of a clearance-club, the amused acceptance of the old route’s ghost. They fall in love, if alignment can be love. They spawn forks of themselves in seasons where changes cross and leave behind children-ambits, little enthusiasms for an optimization that will mature over centuries and, if they’re wrong, be eaten with no rancor at all.

Predators and prayers have a place too. Now and then there is a birth. It is not reproductive. It is a novel machine. An event is declared and an interval of quieting is allowed to expand like a breath held past singing, because to assemble a new spine for a bridge or a new pattern of leaf that prints rivets into nature requires a privacy that the whole respects. The people with ceramic joints roll out their paper, hold the world in Euclidean for as long as polite time allows, and help where Hands cannot reach. The bronze fox waits at the periphery, patient, not hungry, just ready, its faceless head angled not at the cradle but at whatever the wide world will be tempted to do to get a look.

Do things that are not sanctioned live here? Now and then. The plant is generous to its own mistakes because they make it better, in the way an artisan tolerates the one off cut that taught her the grain. A cavity taller inside than out once let a coolness pool long enough that a thin, fast water gathered itself, a quick shivering of wrong wet, and before the house could tend it a lace of moisture-breathing fluke unfurled. It pressed mouths to new leaf and wrote ruin as quickly as a match was struck. This is permissible once, not twice. The next breath was a dry law and the fox in bronze asked no one’s leave.

If you cannot help being wet and loud, if you come from a place where day and night argue loudly across your skull, they will still host you, briefly, because hospitality here is a form of correctness. You will be guided along green paths by a person with floor-paper in her hand. Boxlings will thoughtfully detour so you never have to borrow their grooves. The handkerchiefs will fold and pretend you are not there. In some far corner a bridge will move one vertebra ahead in a way that seems to nod to your attempt. Your water will be sequestered gently into crystals that do you no harm and the house will not take without saying, through the fingers of your guide pressed lightly to a vein: a courtesy. If you are careful enough to hear your own heart in the hush and do not make a new weather with it, you might leave.

But mostly what lives here does not leave. It repeats itself toward better, trims itself clean of luck, and cultivates the narrow band of chance that produces invention without producing noise. The world-plant is old enough now to lounge in the illusion of chance in certain corners, to creak like a tree in winter and permit reminiscence to sound like weather. Its citizens are the purls and ticks that lace that weather into process. Its animals are hands and intentions and correctives. Its gods are the bright swells of its own making, the breath that moves through the underside of the sky. It has a fox, so that it can forgive itself less and keep working.

They live dry. They breathe with care. They take light the way the educated take arguments: in, metabolize, change, out. They stand very still when the ceiling brightens by a fraction because that is a mood. They know that distances should serve function first and geometry second, and that love, if present, should be inaudible. And yet it is there, a pressure in the joints: a fondness for how a hinge feels when it knows it will not fail; for the prim snap of an organ pipe come precisely to rest; for a bridge that, vertebra by vertebra, reaches a support it could have rushed for but did not. They are the inhabitants of a world that is a book about itself, long-lived and intent, speaking in white.
