At first you will call it empty because the residents do not step out of themselves. They are not shaped enough to flee, nor rare enough to be glimpsed as individuals. What lives here has chosen the interval between wet and warm as its body, and once you stop looking for silhouettes you begin to notice how many of those bodies there are.

They show as skins first. In every seam that weeps without stopping, a dark nap of organism clings as if velveted onto the stone. You would call it lichen if lichen were allowed to give up on light, or fungus if fungus learned to eat charge and mineral instead of wood. It is closer to chemistry than to your old categories, but it keeps house like a plant: carpeting flow, smoothing edges, tuning surfaces until water behaves the way it prefers. It does not own a color so much as a depth; where it lies thickest, the wet gleams blacker, and the ground around reads like an elevation map in tone.

From those margins, other citizens extend in the slow vocations. There are the chalk-pale pads along the raised courses, spaced with the regularity of lanterns because each one poisons a little circle of air to dissuade its neighbor. They open and close to the breath of humidity the way flowers open and close to light, though light is a story they’ve heard in the pressure of the lid above. Their mouths are not on their faces but in their skins; they drink vapor, salt it away, bleed brine when the pulse rises, and feed quietly on the redox served at the contact of wet stone and air. They farm their own dew by exuding films that nudge beads to settle and hold, then they sip them thread by thread. They look like ornaments, and in another climate that would be their fate, but here they are cisterns with a will, positioned at intervals that help teach the lanes below which way to carry themselves.

Between skin and pad a whole guild of veilmaker lives, fabricating the trembling skeins you can see in that one district where bridges of water span gaps that ought not to be spanned. They engineer hydrophily the way a mason chooses mortar, conferring on a particular lip a lick that changes its mind about which way is down. Look long enough and you will see them adjust a contact angle with a secretion so spare you would not know how to count it, then wait while a string of beads concedes and begins its sideways walk along a cornice where a pipe used to be, though there was never a pipe. Their craft is capillarity; their pride is continuity. One of their old sayings, expressed as a habit in how their films cure, is that a structure is still a structure if you can teach it to hold itself together even when the stone gives out. When the skeins snap, it is not failure so much as reset; the filaments retract their chemistry, the stray drops find new cues, and the veilmaker bends back to the work as if time were the only currency that mattered.

Hidden inside the stone, strata of electrogenic folk run their cables through the city’s bones. They are not fibers you could lift in a tweezer, but chains of cells that share an appetite for transport at a distance. They live on the difference between what the brine offers and what the scale denies, and one of their chosen tricks is to string electrons along themselves the way others string water, tolerating lengths that would be ridiculous if they were bodies instead of functions. It is their habit to settle along the stress lines that glow mutely when the crystals complain, because where the wall lights—faint salad of color to your eye—they read a route for energy and the promise of fresh fracture, fresh mineral, fresh work. A hand brushed across such a wall is a storm to them. A whisper-breeze inside an arcade is a feast.

The glowing green that stirs at the back of a baffle when thunder rolls in the lid above is someone too. They are fungal in temper and adaptive in verse: a colony that answers the change in field with a wash of bioluminescence not because another eye is watching but because it smooths their metabolism to release the excess as light. They do not speak with that green, but they arrange with it, and in time the other films in that quarter have learned to use the pulse as a cue, ripening a generation of propagules just before the city flushes itself so that the contraction rush will carry their children farther along the remembered grooves.

If you want names, you can sort them by how and where they stand on the phase boundaries. Edgelings live in the scalloped drip-cups under arches, in rinds where a film turns brittle then plastic again on the next breath. They reproduce on the hinge of change, spitting out spores into the exact footfall of the next droplet to grant them a free fall longer than they could crawl. Archivists hold court in the slow plazas, writing and erasing on the calcareous faces with an alphabet of texture and wetness. They do not write ideas, they write futures: deposit here a hair more, and twenty cycles hence a lane will choose left, and because it chose left, a certain basin will stay full long enough for a child-skin to settle. Their script looks like ornament to you—scales, ladders, chevrons—and that is the joke; there is beauty because constraint repeats, not because anyone needs it.

Drykeepers attend the negative plazas—the ones at the center of wet squares that remain stubbornly unslick. Their lives depend on forcing a dry island inside the rule, an exhaust for the city’s heat where moisture is encouraged to surrender, sink back, go thin. They are chemists of hydrophobia, smearing the stone with molecules that teach water to despise it for a time. When the hotter cycle walks through, they stand like small altars, feeling the radiative push as a kind of prayer answered: edges chalk, beads shrink from the open, lanes lighten, and for a little while every unit of work in this valley goes toward uncoupling. When the weeping returns—and it always returns—they open filigreed umbrellas a few microns from the surface so the first beads skid past rather than settle, preserving the hole in the rule for the next time the balance demands it.

Breathers cultivate gas down in the patient bowls. They herd fizz. They ride the little eruptions where bubbles insist on joining the rest of the air, and in the seconds or minutes between such events they pull what they can from the gradient the bubble left—a little sulfide here, a shred of ammonia there. There is no mouth in them, only a hundred thousand pores facing a film, but pores are mouths enough at the right temperature and the right patience. They set the pulse that reads like a city thinking. You hear them at the knuckled bench above the basin’s seat: an intermittent pock when a thicker drop lets go and disturbs the film, the almost-audible whisper of gas persuading its way through gel.

Some of them have learned to be architecture, and those are the closest this place comes to bodies you would recognize. The low bunkered roofs at the horizon that swell not upward but outward are colonies that persuaded mineral to grow them a shelter with a hundred blind little mouths on top. Each cup is a rest for water, a holdfast for a long drink, a nursery that does not need seas. They thicken rather than reach because this sky punishes height; to live here is to understand what not to do with a verticality you are offered. They do not build alone. Water is their co-worker and their tool: veils to span, films to hold, sprays to seed. Erosion is not their enemy but their ledger. The undercut line on the faces of the embankments is a clause in their contract, the knuckle at the bottom of a slide the punctuation where a paragraph turned.

There are fewer kinds than there are roles, and roles swap frequently. Today’s archivist dissolves into tomorrow’s edgeling; a lantern pad’s rim sprouts hair for once and plays veilmaker to protect a neighbor in a hot hour. The city’s only predators are its own revisions and the viruses that fuss at everyone’s genome between pulses, adding and erasing phrases. When the far weather changes in the lid and a new rhythm writes itself into the pressure of the air, they take what they are and adjust. Every citizen here is an improviser on an old theme. Their language is timing. Their law is repetition. Their humor—yes, they have humor; it lives in the way a runnel will feint at splitting, split, and then join again at the same interval every cycle for fifty years as if pretending the choice were ever free.

They move, when they move, by altering what the world does to them. A person would call it patience. A bead slides sideways along a stone that has been taught to let it. A cornice grows a whisker and a life rides that shade one body length farther along a wall that did not used to exist. A skein breaks and a thousand microbes take their one jump, land, and begin exuding again as if nothing had happened but a breath. A spray pops from a triboluminescent seam and what to you is a faint blue is to them an event: a hundred photoreceptors, repurposed proteins from a lightless ancestry, report the world is thinking stress, and somebody’s colony three arches over turns on a pump because this is how you borrow a little from the crack’s expense.

Reproduction is geography. No one goes far in the usual sense; they go wide through water’s habits and deep through stone’s acquiescence. When the city flushes itself, they fling children into that windless gust: spores into the convection’s brief climb, neonates glued to the backs of beads, DNA roped into filaments that can be read by the next film when they meet. When the breath turns in, they retract, they bank, they sleep awake. Sleep here is not absence; it is a narrowing of attention to the hour-long task, the day’s fraction of layer, the ribbon of surfactant that must be repaired before the drip loses the beat.

They do not see you. Or if they see you, it is as a report of salts and temperature. You are a soluble event. The air you carry with you is lighter and emptier than this air, and for a moment very small things like that. Your hand across a crystalline wall is weather that obliterates and primes. Your sandals, if you make it as far as a plaza, are a ridiculous example of a foot, and the lanes will take your tread as instruction and as trespass, both easily forgiven. You are not unlike a hot pulse, another one of the reversals, and like the reversals you will go, and they will smooth where you were.

It is easy to suppose the city had builders. The fact is simpler and stranger: the city is the animal, and what lives here are its tissues, its flora, its habits, its immune system, its librarians. The citizens are tendencies with biochemistry attached. A film on a wall that inscribes chevrons is a person to the degree that a beaver’s dam is a beaver, a coral reef a coral; but the analogy fails, because coral do not make the ocean remember which way it ran last time. Here, memory is the citizenry’s proudest art. Gravity is mostly a consensus, and so are history, drainage, thickness, home.

There are no bones except the ones grown from solution and softened by drip. There are no mouths except the million faces that touch water. There are no eyes, unless you count the runes on the condensation walls, which do in fact learn and, having learned, hold. There are no voices louder than the next drop and the next and the next. But life is loud enough if you know to hear desire in behavior. What lives here desires continuance in a place that has made a pastime of unmaking. It desires one more layer placed, one more bridge spun, one more plaza taught to stay dry at its heart against all persuasive weather. It desires—softly, without body—the long exactness this valley has promised it, and every hour, in heat and in weeping, it earns a little more.
