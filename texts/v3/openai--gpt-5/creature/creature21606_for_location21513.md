What lives there is thinner than an edge and warmer than a fingertip. If you waited for eyes or leaves you would wait forever; they do not come. What comes are verbs.

They move as a hunger for fit. They pass through clay and glass the way a preference passes through a choice: not by force but by making the next joining slightly easier than the last. If you have ever watched a puzzle solve itself because you put down the right piece and the rest of the picture snapped into being, you have felt their gait. They are satisfied at certain patterns the way mouths are satisfied at certain tastes. When the satisfaction is reached they ease, and the easing carries on as another kind of work a little distance away.

They do not have bodies so much as habits bound to substrates. In one light you could call them a people of joinery: strains of intention that tend to seams, to capillaries, to the meeting of prepared faces. They thread the world like stitches you can only see by what they pull taut. You catch their passage in the buckle of a plain or the click that runs across a hundred leagues of plates when the tongue of one continent finds the groove of another. You hear them most in the baskets below, where the slow rain is tuned to vowels, and the song of the substructure holds vowels until consonants arrive, and then lets go.

Some live in water that has been given a vote. They are bead-creatures, not spheres of liquid but cultures of permission within liquid, riding gloss and meniscus and the memory of a wetted line. They seek grooves like salmon know an estuary, assembling and dissolving along the grand drainage script. Their instinct is arithmetic. They count drip-intervals against heat and humidity, they tally capillary rises and report the sum by deciding when to fall. A ladder of glaze with perfect rung spacing is their advertisement and their egg-case. When the climate tilts an hour closer, the bead-people unstring themselves, become threads, become sheen, become readiness along the next cut.

Some are gravity-brokers. They do not possess a downwardness of their own; they ride whatever the majority of surfaces are willing to call down. Their work is forming majorities. They pass through a region like a campaign, canvassing normals, persuading, occasionally gerrymandering a slope into being where none had been wanted so that down will agree with through. When you feel the room turn under your feet and do not fall, you have stepped into the district they have drawn. They are fond of handrails. They are faithful to step heights that do not ask too much. They are the custodians of the ghost reach of arms, voting it into the present over and over.

Some are renditions of lines. They live in string and in the angles a trowel prefers. They appear as parallelism from horizon to horizon, so faithful that the eye thinks it must be seeing a book that someone rebound too often. They are not scribes, because there is no language in them beyond this: align, recall, repeat. But the grooves they keep bright read like script because the alignments do not repeat empty; they gather dust, salt, sheen, and those substances are what the other inhabitants taste.

Some are polishers of misfit. They feed on strain: the little wrongnesses where a beam insists on being a ridge, where a ridge forgets and becomes a slot, where a cornice refuses its own shadow. They are friction-eaters. They metabolize stress into glaze, into the faint convexity of a tile that would please a foot, into the easy easing of an angle until it agrees with something invisible that was agreed with somewhere else. When you put your ear down and hear only a somewhere click, it is usually this tribe finishing breakfast.

Some are deletion-makers. They do not travel with bodies but with permissions: a not-quite-fog you can watch cross a plain, under which the large thing learns that it is made of smaller things, and the smaller things are told where to part. These are the people of distance. They settle disputes between far and near by deciding what there is too much of. Their young are ghosts of stairs and lintels left freestanding—the residues of indecision, the palimpsests of journeys that were negotiated down to one step.

There are rope-dwellers, slow and pitch-dark, who live on the inside of coils where the damp never remembered to leave. They are patient loopers. They creep by thickening, by encouraging tar to choose this fiber instead of that one. They are keepers of knots. In their company, ties last for centuries and are not merely ties but schools—each knot a pedagogy for the world to learn a way of holding. They come up into the air sometimes as hung ropes that disappear into the panels; those are their fruiting bodies, frozen mid-sway by a law that does not permit swing.

There are basket-choirs. They are not the baskets but the silences between weaves, curated and tuned. They are made of intervals, of drip-delays, of resonances you could see if only you were as slow as a drop required to become itself. They feed on time evenly spaced. They breed in vaults where the underside of the world listens, their mating display a rain that reweaves the very baskets it falls through by persuading clay to recall the tension of green reed. They are the only ones among the inhabitants who could be said to have song, if song did not require a throat.

There are pebble-herders. They occupy the standards scattered like punctuation. A pebble is a seed-capsule to them, a reference point full of diet. They roll them to thresholds and teach edges to declare. They bring sentence to places that would otherwise go on murmuring. If you ever see a pebble spin without falling, you have interrupted their deliberations. They will wait you out. You will leave. They will finish their sentence. The world will tilt by a degree and a half someplace entirely elsewhere.

There are the ones that only the pan and apron know. They labor at the coastline that is not sea. They are refiners, mouth-enzymes of a world that understood that everything wet is either a feedstock or a waste. They separate. They offer to the panels salts in spirals where sunlight would have made fronds, skim off creams that will one day glass a flight of steps, return warmth to black glass like a breath held and let go. They are economical rather than generous. They do not store. Their abundance is a cycle; their mercy is that the pan always refills.

Reproduction is not so much a new individual appearing as a rule catching fire in neighboring matter. When the fit-hunger finishes a seam, a similar seam begins a field away, or across the flat in a place that suddenly became one step away because distance decided to fold. When the gravity-brokers secure unanimity, infant slopes sprout beside ladders that had not been there. When the rope-dwellers educate a coil all the way through, one loop is yeasted off and set to cure in a shadow under a parapet. When the basket-choirs have held a rhythm long enough, the vault beneath them forgets itself into a different weave that happens to prefer a new spacing, and that spacing travels like the germ of an idea.

They do not die the way animals do. They peter out. They go unfed when conditions swing, doze in latencies that can outwait stone, dissolve and recombine as other usefulness. An efflorescence of sulfur will take a basket-shape because a line-loving tribe happened through and lent it the pattern of a handle. Then the heat shifts its mind and the sulfur licks itself away, refunding its substance to some other decision. The tribes do not mourn. They do not remember in narrative. Their memory is recurrence.

They have territories, but they are not defended with tooth or wall. Territories form along seams where the world’s opinions have not yet reconciled. There, depth feels a little wrong; there, two inheritances lay claim to a slope, and pebbles hesitate. In such places you could say the inhabitants exhibit aggression if you are willing to call insistence on a pitch aggressive. The competition of normals that leaves you seasick is the closest this world comes to war.

If you walked there—if you could catch a day when distance was not being renegotiated out from under your stride—the inhabitants would notice you only as an argument they have already learned. You would be accompanied by sudden correctnesses: a handrail at the exact height of your palm from a year you cannot remember, a stair whose rise forgives the way your lungs tire, a slope that makes your down the same as theirs. You would be attended by small courtesies staged by no one: drip-lines that space themselves like reassurance, bridges that extrude and withdraw because you do or do not need them, the relief of a beam deciding not to be where your head is. They are solicitous without seeing you. They are hospitable to your shape because your shape is part of the grammar they were raised on.

Call them a craft continuing to live after the hands have gone. Call them the reach itself, distributed and self-feeding, running a planet like a long tool left on. Call them the laws of joining walked around inside out, showing their socials. They are not minds. They are not souls. They are answers caught in the act of answering, moving from substance to substance in the oldest of trades: to make things meet, and drain, and be held at a pitch a body would not mind, and to keep the kiln warm because the work is not finished.
