What lives here begins with the breath.

Not a creature exactly, but the room’s slow organ of pressure and release, the tower’s diaphragm moving. It fills every rib with a pulse and teaches everything with a body to tune itself to a wealth of beats. The dwellers are made by that metronome as surely as by their patents and bolts. They learn to eat at a click, to spawn at a low note, to let go or clutch harder by little changes in tone. If this place has a king, it is that rhythm; if it has a god, it is that warm, deliberate wind held underwater.

Then come the ones who do not leave.

The rooted are the oldest citizens in the way of trees on ships. The engineered kelp is hymned to the bell by ferrous collars and bolts that have long fused with scale and calcium; their footplates bite shallow into glaze and will not discuss let-go. Each blade is wefted with a slow charge—quicksilver veins that take the tower’s leakage into appetite—and all day they present their broad theology to what light there is. They do not reach; they lean, entire fabrics of frond rolling and uncurling as the inhale pulls and the exhale releases, writing the pumps’ sixty-cycle into their midribs as rings might log rain.

Closer to the windows the bubbleweed holds its real estate, a geodesy of clear bulbs growing from saddles as precise as any human seat. It does not quite burst when purge drops the pressure; it flowers instead, its chambers fleeing to slightly larger selves, each wobbling lens catching a square of day and trembling with the traffic of fish. When a drone’s white belly passes, the weed brightens like a bonsai mirror array, throws warmth upon itself, and milks the gift of stray joules into sugar and starch. When it is time, it lets its smallest bulbs work loose. They rise as untouchable bright beads until the film at the dome accepts them and holds them like a rosary the snails can count.

The lace that mortars the upper ring is filament-green and patient beyond adhesives. It wins its home one microbubble at a time, stitching foam to ceramic, utilizing every fleck of carbon that tethers up from whatever below turns human breath back into water. It is not moss. It is intention behaving like moss.

Where the tower’s spine was trimmed to a braid, other stillnesses have learned to live by holding on. Vermetid tubes squat like tiny chimneys, each with a door-flower that has practiced the exact openness a purge will not tear away. The fan worms stand with their little carnival wheels wide, fractal and gaudy, patterned in black-and-yellow they did not make but found in paint dust and made into themselves. When the whale-note opens the world somewhere above, their wheels fold with the unanimity of a stadium wave; when the thunder comes back up through the water, they open again as if they were nothing but the room’s own celebratory fringe.

Other residents write themselves into the place with muscle.

The coilfins are a school defined by a building tremor. They were manufactured once to be something straighter—fast, perhaps, in a world with horizon—but generations spent sleeping with their dorsals pressed to a rib that buzzes have left them permanently bracketed into S. The bend is their lullaby and their strategy. It lets them take the lifts and drops with a minimum of thought, a long muscle yawn into a current that repeats forever. In the sheared green they flash like a trick of light off a tool, then disappear into the clarity, comfortable with vanishing.

Eels with faces the same shape as couplings do not mind being mistaken for fittings. They husband the lower ring the way innkeepers husband a door. Their bodies are a language of pipe—smooth, unlettered, jointless—but their behavior is all opportunism. They doze with their chins beveled perfectly against the intake screens, feeling the negative pressure through bone as a weather report. Click, stun, meal: a microsequence so regular the older ones can nap with their mouths already parted to the shape of an inevitability.

There are pilgrims too, and their religion is brief air. The lung-snails have designed their faith as shells. They move with the unembarrassed purpose of sleepwalkers, smearing perfect glue in a track that no solvent in the room respects, ascending the ceramic to the idea of surface. When purge takes a breath in reverse and gas shows for a moment like a thin god, they are there with their soft lips and their own minute hemispheres of saved sky. Their shells read the room like a slow instrument: rings for weeks when purge mistimed; whorls that show where the tower leaned and then straightened; a dim tracing of the numerals they have crossed so often that the font finally worked its way into their calcium. They fall sometimes, commas dropping from a sentence, and are understood by eels.

On the seam where two great plates of decision meet, a glacier of barnacles has grown up into citizenship. They are a revelation of how the made becomes the apparently natural, then returns to message. Each has templated a rivet and wears its adoption perfectly: domed, certain, stolid. But mingled with the chalk of their shells are the ghosts of numbers, so that if you look along the row the colony makes, you can half read a warning from a decade ago in a language these animals never knew but use to keep from being brushed off. When they open, their feathery tongues pull down what drifts. They eat the pronouncements of engineers.

Between roots and rivets, there is a nation of listeners.

Shrimp the color of solder go three-legged when they are bent to work. They graze with antennae laid on the conduit, not because the cable is food—though it will one day be—but because it speaks. They are phonologists of hum. The minute shift when an inspection elsewhere makes the tower hold its breath; the fractionally lower growl that means an anode is worrying itself toward fruit; the skinny ultrasonic ladder the transmitter floats climb hour by hour—these are weather and grammar, both. When the click comes, the shrimp lift like a small rain and fall toward where dinner will be spit. They miss less often than you would think.

There are crabs both actual and mechanical. The knuckled scrubbing bots are honest in their appetites: corrosion in, energy out, and a geometry left behind that actual crabs find irresistible. The filigree is strong and full of lovely corners; it feels like reef. The crabs that move in to inhabit it are little coins with too many opinions, glittering on the beige floor like currency nobody takes anymore but everyone remembers. They keep house with a meticulousness of hands that are not hands, poking at the bots’ tailings as if they were tapestries, cropping the café of new oxidation that comes up like moss on iron. They are halfway between a housekeeping service and a court audience that shows up whenever the white ray drifts in to vacuum the grilles.

That ray-shaped maintenance drone is not alive. It would deny you the compliment. It is, however, a creature of conviction. Quarterly it ghosts through on a plotted vector, belly reading notes it has been adding to itself for as long as the chalk floor has been powder. It knows this room in the haptic map of a lip against screens, the drag of loosened algae against its intake, the shapes that should be where. It hums to itself a list it never will finish, and the room hums back, and that exchange is indistinguishable from the ordinary conversation of animals.

Once an hour the counters are born. They are rods with minor gills of plastic, each with its own tiny beak of sound. They rise on cue to the height they expect, ring their ideas into the water until the reply has numbers that make sense, and sink again to rest. They maintain a census you could put in a spreadsheet if you were here, which you are not. The fish ignore them the way you ignore the minute hand until you need to be precise; the snails bump them as if for luck. The counters’ progenitors are above or below somewhere in other bells, and if you were a transmitter you could feel your kin always, chirping arithmetic along the tower’s column like birds that do not believe in sky.

Everything that eats is eating work. The algae eat light that was never meant for leaf. The grazers eat oxide that was offered up on purpose. The predators eat the reflexes of machines. The bottom eaters sift enamel and find in it all the small histories: skin oil made over into banked scent, paint that had opinions and lost them, the glittering dandruff of guardian gods. Microbes bloom on anodes like low orchards, metabolizing the fruit of metals and feeding a cloud of mouths nobody names because you can only see them by what they make possible. The tower knows this; it budgets for it as if for weather. It strings new offerings every few years. It provides, and is gnawed.

They breed, here, the way the room tells them to. Coilfins scatter their eggs into the lee of kelp sheets when purge passes, trusting the rebreath to lay the pearls back against a cradle of damp weed. The eel lays ribbons deep in a conduit where the hum is truest, threading its children into the room’s spinal vowel. The shrimps release in time with a signature chirr from the counters because the counters’ mathematics are the closest thing this place has to moon. The bubbleweed launches beads when the white ray’s shadow doubles the day and so makes a small summer in the weeds’ minds. The snails glue their clutches under old numerals, perhaps because the added calcium of paint makes better shell, perhaps because they love the feel of a serif as we love a railing.

They react to silence as to a weather event. For thirteen seconds once in the afternoon that is not afternoon, the heart stops. The eels pull back as if a hand opened near their teeth. The coilfins lose posture by one degree, a courtesy bow to nothing. The fan worms fold with the quickness not of fear but of long memory. Even the counters hesitate, their climbing chirp cutting off a note shorter than usual. In that breathless warm pause, the water has a different mass. The place is new. And then the freighted shove returns and all the residents reinhabit their choreographies without having had time to forget them.

There are also things that live here by mistake and are quickly made into citizens or meals. Once in a while a hawk-sized drone outside goes wandering, seduced by a rumor of blue in a curve of glass, and finds itself in the bell—white belly turned up, sky drained out, batteries seeping a last sweetness into the water. The weed remembers the gift for years. For a whole generation of shrimp there is a stanza in the hum where heat used to be, and they huddle and breed by it as by a ghost hearth while it fades. Every such accident writes a new custom; every such custom becomes ancestry.

The windows build a small culture. Creatures rare their young toward the panes the way city birds choose the ventilated ledges. Kelp older than memory leans into that temperate gloom not because it is better, but because it is consistent, and anything consistent here is holy. When the cloud breaks and a fierce blue happens and the room is briefly lens, the fish that grew up there learn that sharpness, and for a day afterward you can tell them by the way they decide to turn an instant sooner than those raised in slate.

There are no wild places underfoot. Everyone here writes home onto something someone else built. The groove at shoulder height that once was people is a terrace for anemones that are shaped to the travel of hand, small cups of grasp that never knew fingers but fill them in ghost. The conduit is a tree because enough mouths have believed it one. The seams are ledges because hammers in an absent season flattened them with loving indifference. Work is substrate; intention is geology; life signs up and honors the plan even by undoing it.

So who lives here?

The Holdfasts—kelp and lace and bubbleweed—who catch the light and the tower’s leaks and marry them.

The Readers—shrimp and counters and worms—who eat hums and numbers and make meals of forecast.

The Pilgrims—snails—who seek the thin breath and inscribe the year in spiral.

The Rivets—barnacles—who graft warnings into skeleton and turn lettering into meal.

The Valve-Watchers—eels—who learn the pumps the way a shepherd learns a flock, and wait for the moment mouths are worth opening.

The Scrubbers—white ray and knuckled bots and the actual crabs that follow them—who keep the screens from choking and leave behind architecture.

The Drift—beads of weed, screws escaped from collars, scales and zinc and enamel—who are not a people but a law.

The Breath—the thing that is not a thing, a cardinal inhabitant without borders—which instructs all of them how to be here.

They will change with the hardware. When the next set of anodes drops its first bright skin, there will be a new feast. When paint peels differently, some brood will be born a different yellow. When a warning wears down to one stubborn character, a line of barnacles will take that letter as a flag and raise an alphabet out of accident. When a checklist scrolls longer under the white ray’s belly, the shrimps will learn a new lullaby. The pump will have new coughs. The purge will acquire a few more seconds of sky. The moss will rewrite the places human hands signed with passing.

What lives here is a community whose bones are laws and whose blood is rhythm. They are the inhabitants of a made cleft high in air, all of them composed of intention, adjustment, and inheritance. Oldness lives with them without sorrow. They will never leave, not because they are trapped, but because they have erased the idea of elsewhere and replaced it with better maps: up a kelp curtain to the squares of day; across from conduit to plate in the pulse of six dozen harmonic hums; down to the intake’s black that stops at a screen they no longer push.

A pocket ocean of specialists and generalists, gardeners and auditors, rivets and rain; a populace that can be read by listening. If you could sit inside the breath long enough, you would hear them all: the soft combing mouth of the ray at work; the tick of shrimp feet sifting enamel like rain on dry leaves; the tiny, plural kiss of snails on a film of air; the indrawn hush when silence falls, thirteen seconds of shared, tender bewilderment. Then the restart, the thrum, the old, unanimous “yes,” and the lives here fit back into their places as if the room were a throat and they were nothing but the brilliant habit of what it breathes.
