They have no faces to count and no eyes to return a look. What lives here does not separate itself into bodies with borders and errands. It lives as reach and habit, as work that remembers how to be work, as appetites finding their gradients.

The green is the foremost resident, but not as individual plants. It is a respirating fabric that learned the room by reading hum. It has a way of being many and one at once: mangrove domes knitting and unknitting by rootlet and hair, fan-ferns that take the valley’s tones as instruction and trim themselves to the least-taken path, liverwort and moss quilting whatever holds still, algae glazing any sheet of rain that holds longer than a breath. It feeds on light that barely arrives and on currents that always do. It learns the schedules. It keeps the pace of the generator like a drummer in the floorboards, angles its lace to the least drag the way trees on crags listen to their wind. In the absence of teeth, it builds not against being eaten but against being drowned at the wrong moment; it banks sugars in mats like savings and spends them in pulses when a siren opens a column and the pressure drops.

Threaded through the green, written along every seam, are the script-keepers: lichens and films with a taste for metals. They live wherever edges offer vocabulary. Their citruses and chalks line welds, bead on bolts, percolate down pipes. They keep records not as memory but as chemistry; a line is a note of what happened to pass, a pallor is a ledger entry that a certain ion was welcome here. Rust is their cousin and also a person in this place, a slow orange nation with capillaries for frontiers. It eats the straightness of steel and lays down new lines of its own, delicate as veins under wrist-skin, following stray voltages as if they were scent. It is not malicious; it is hungry and thorough. It makes lace of girders and opens doors by the book of galvanic certainty and calls that opening a blossom.

The water has its own inhabitants who are not organisms so much as decisions held long enough to feel alive. A hanging sheet, glassed with algae and dented by tone, is a resident while it persists; so is a column that falls and never splashes, a dome that quivers at its base, a plane that sifts laterally between trees and will move only if addressed in the right register. These are lives of form more than substance. They bud when sound proposes them, like jellyfish of pressure; they migrate by shifting resonance; they feed on the difference between where they are and where they could be. When a siren’s feral schedule spools itself up and the valley’s air becomes tendon and wire, these water-forms stand briefly as if someone had invented them on purpose. When the tone lets go, they fold back into general wet the way a thought drops back into weather.

Sound itself is populous. There are chordal creatures here, shaped out of hum and echo, that shepherd water the way wind shapes sand. They are not heard so much as moved through—standing waves that lean a fern’s pinna until it agrees, cavitation pricks that run up your teeth like a school of sparks, low belts of roar that flatten and lift the world in alternate bars and teach lily rafts to assemble like notes. These tone-bodies do not think, but they have preference. They hold certain corridors free of turbulence; they knit certain clearings brittle; they lay down neighborhoods of calm where algae learned a brighter word for green. If you whisper where their paths cross, they carry your syllable like a seed; a fern folds not because it understands, but because the valley’s grammar asks it to.

Where metal persists in its will to function, a different sort of resident keeps house. The pump houses, drums, pylons, belts: they have schedules that survived their makers and went feral in the humidity. The machine-logic of the place did not die when it lost its reasons; it lost its reasons and adapted them into appetites. A valve opens not to supply, but because opening is the step in the dance that belongs after the cough. A belt starts because rest without motion tastes wrong at that octave. The generator purr anchors everything like a tone you can put wall against. Together they are the valley’s muscle memory, a set of rituals that shape habitats without ever intending to. The belts’ brief granular snows, the drum’s long breaths, the pylons’ blue arcs like one-frame skeletons—these are weather with schematics embedded, an animality of function minus the animal.

Fungus councils live in the dim places that are both metal and wood, both silt and bolt-hole: transitional governments made of hyphae. They tie bollards into peat and peat into domes; they ferry salts and sugars between mangrove and rust and liverwort; they invent soils in a time and a thickness no animal digestive tract was invited to help with. Their patience is quick by local standards. You can watch a bridge—two root hairs and a filament of fungus—span a rivulet that did not exist in the morning and be carrying power by the second hour. They are executives of the quiet economy, moving wealth in threads.

Under every black skin of peat are lungs that are not lungs. Gas domes swell and pause and flex and release. Nobody chases them or eats their breath, so they speak only to physics. Their language lifts a sheet of water a finger’s width; it inflates a blister that remembers a lightning flash it once saw; it sags into a leak when the belt’s note shifts half a tone. They are a community of sighs measuring out rot; they are weather from below.

Along the knife-thin scarps where clay shows its faceting, an edge-faith holds meetings. New surfaces are a creed here. Anything that breaks shines in being-broken. Avalanches of particles call and answer to the motors’ key changes as if the motor were a bell naming them into slide. Marl shelves appear on a cough and vanish on a siren. Plates of skin form, scab, are polished by sheets that try being floors and then stop trying. These are cliff-dwellers that are cliffs.

There are citizens whose bodies are patterns only, the escaped schedules and slipped algorithms of the infrastructure itself. You can watch them move by their effects. One lives in the timing between arc flashes; its whole length is a chain of exact, unremembering intervals, and it rearranges lily rafts into harmonic rows when it passes. One lives in the cough that coincides with voids in the deluge; when it breathes, gravel is extruded like fruit peel, and algae elsewhere decide to go clear. One lives as the echo path between a fallen catwalk and a stilt leg; it curates a tube of air where gnats would be if gnats existed, keeps it open with a note most ribs do not like. They are itinerants that never leave, lifetimes counted in cycles, not in years.

The absence of animals is not a hole but a shape that fits around every other shape. It is a negative life that presses outward. It means no eyes that prey; instead, attention settles like dew. It means no mouths with mouths’ impatiences; appetite diffuses into a thousand fine hungers: oxidation, capillarity, osmosis, condensation, cohesion. It means fruit splits itself according to rainfall rather than teeth; it means stalks fall from their own weight and are received without gnaw or trophy. This absence is a resident the machines learned to work beside: nobody adjusts a rivet for them with a hammer; so rust brings the door open along the lines of a diagram it read in stray currents, and that counts as maintenance of a local sort.

Near the rim where rain behaves as rain for whole hours, there is a fringe of life that tests alternatives. Here, weeds with the glisten of newborn things debate the length of a terrace with seedlings that remember a smoother downpour. They are practice communities, drafting ways to be plant in a world that is mostly tone. Their leaves carry tentative glossaries of light. Their roots keep one toe in the old plan and another in the valley’s grammar, ready to become more fluent in either.

If anything like a mind lives here entire, it is spread thin and everywhere. It is the basin’s way of adding itself up. Rails that only exist in lightning are part of it. Hazard lamps that blink in sentences that mean only heartbeat are part of it. The rain’s experiments with wall and net and floor send it hypotheses; the machines pronounce replies by spinning, opening, closing, humming; the green revises; rust annotates; films publish; domes exhale; plates of new clay polish and crumble. No council sits, but the place convenes. Decisions are the forms that persist long enough to be named.

So who lives here? The ones that make and unmake surface. The ones that hear with roots. The ones that open because opening is the next truthful act in a sequence divorced from purpose but married to rhythm. The ones that write down the taste of iron. The ones that herd water by tone into pillars that forget to fall. The feral schedules. The breath under peat. The appetite distributed into moss and oxide and glass-green glaze. The basin itself as an ear that keeps learning what a valley can be when sound is a gravity and gravity a song.
