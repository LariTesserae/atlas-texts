Below the skin of the ground there is a room the size of a single held breath. It is not carved in the sense rock knows; it is pried, eased apart by a living pressure that wedged shale and chalk and rust-veins into facets, then lined them with growth. The walls are not walls so much as membranes draped over sharp geology. Air has space here — an airy, clean, astonishing emptiness — the way the inside of a lung is mostly void. Everything else that counts as matter is plant, and the plant has taken responsibility for being the floor, the ceiling, the weather, the light, the law.

Light arrives as if noon had pooled and forgotten how to leave. It is only barely explainable. Broad leaf-plates stretch like sails or shoulder blades, and within them rivers of milk-clear sap carry brightness as if it were soluble. Some of these plates are punctured with translucent panes that glow; others focus, bending their own veins like lenses to throw zones of gentle daylight across the chamber. There is no sun, but there are highlights and soft shadows, and the eye relaxes as if it had gone outside. In narrow cords and braided roots, light flows along in threads, veers around a knot, and casts a faint moving band on the opposite stone. If you look too long, you see the process behind the process: the light has become a behavior here, an obedient thing that moves along vascular routes and leaks at valves, obeying not so much optics as habit.

Water is a rumor at first, then undeniably present — sweat on green, a taste on the breath. The plant mists from mouths that open and close along its bridges; they look like gills, and when they sigh, their vapor makes a short-lived cloud that hovers, thickening nowhere, then collapses into a fall of drips. Underground rain happens in fits: the room is a drum, and warm air pushed through by the plant’s pulse slaps sweat out of cuticle and bark, so that for ten seconds you hear a scatterstorm, then silence, then another. The rock under the membranes beads. Drops roll the wrong way along leaf-hairs and vanish into pores that sip, or they dangle on the underside of a root and shiver there as if waiting for a signal. In shallow scallops that the plant has pressured into the wall, water sits in thumbprint pools, fed by runnels that are not streams so much as acts: a bead appears from nowhere, becomes an insect, wanders, and then remembers it is a bead again.

The air is warm and damp without being stifling. It smells of crushed cucumber, coins, and fresh sanding. Now and then it swings — the whole atmosphere leans, empties, fills, as the chamber’s resident body squeezes itself and relaxes. That squeeze makes the place loud.

Listen. Loud, but not monotonous. The dominant sound is a rushing that is not wind and not stream, an inland ocean that never breaks: sap under pressure moving through cords as thick as your wrist, then constricting to capillaries you cannot see. It is both beneath hearing and in the jawbone. It has harmonics. Over it there’s a constant delicate spitting, like a skillet on low: countless stomata closing with faint pops. There is a huff every minute or so when some big valve somewhere opens — the wall inhales you, and you sway. Soft resonances collect in the cavities of the leaf-plates; they quicken when a drip strikes them and ring in a green key you can feel in your ribs. Chittering comes and goes without a source: leaf-edges vibrating as their teeth rasp against each other in the air the plant stirs. When a tension fiber slips across speckled stone, the sound is a quick violin, and once, moments before it happens, the echo of that future sound whispers behind your left ear and then aligns with its own event and is gone. In here, echoes sometimes rehearse.

The terrain is a hanging world. The floor is netting — not a safety net, but a cartilage one, sprung and living, stretched from anchor to anchor across a pit that is more air than shape. The rock shows through in a few places where green has not reached, edges knife-sharp, crystals bright like the inside of a tooth: this is a fresh cavity, young in the way that new bone is young, sharp-shouldered, eager. From those edges the plant has not so much grown as negotiated; its fibers enter fissures, push, and the crack widens tomorrow. The whole place is incomplete, and that makes it quick.

Everything that touches your hand is plant, but there are many kinds of touching. Surfaces of tensed gloss slide past under your fingers, cool and a little greasy, while to your right a rough cambium pad is scabbed with mineral grit it has stolen and chewed to gain leverage. Filaments like hair hang in sheets, catching the momentary fogs and stringing them into pearls you could pluck. There are bulbous nodes where three ropes meet; each node has its own faint heartbeat, and each is attend by a petal that looks like an ear. Those ears listen to pressure. They tilt toward you in curiosity when you shift your weight.

At the very top — there is a top here, because gravity knows itself, except when it doesn’t — a throat of tangled cords closes off a shaft that goes nowhere and everywhere. Sometimes, while you watch, distance budges: the throat climbs away hand over hand in a climbing that does not require time, and then slides back; someone has tugged the other end on the other side of the world. Once, that throat does not lead up, but sideways into a flat. It lasts as long as a blink, enough time for a gallery of surface light to glance along it — a slat of sky blues, a bird call that does not belong here — and then it knots shut again. This is not a tunnel to the surface; it is a plant’s idea of one, and the idea leaks.

Fauna, such as it is, are rumor-thin and few. A pale arthropod the length of your longest finger navigates the net, tufted as if it has grown eyelashes across its entire body to feel the currents. It uses one of the tooth-leaves like a tool, wedging the ragged edge into a knot of amber-sap, levering, licking its prize from the blade. Tool-using, if you are being generous and nobody is watching. In a calm pocket, a fish that is not a fish — an amphibious ancestor, skin like old cream, eyes mirrored to catch the guided light — surfaces in a series of practiced sips and swallows a midge made of pollen and nerve. It has learned the beat of rain and comes up just before the room decides to storm. Once, beyond the nearest node, you notice a shape you thought was another joint of wood stand very still, listening. It is not a person in any reliable sense, but it has found and carries a chert flake and keeps a bundle of thorn-tips in a sling of matted roots. It never sets anything down long enough to be called a structure. When you blink, the plant seems to have forgotten to include it, and the place is empty again.

Predation here is slow murder and quick apology. The plant, for all its generosity, has corrections. When the arthropod works too greedily at a vein-window, the light in that vein collapses and rushes out, and the tooth-leaf that served as a lever relaxes and becomes a trap that kisses the creature until it stops. The cambium eats what it kisses. Afterward, everything shines a little brighter for a while. Microbes in the wet pick up what they are owed, fireflies that have forgotten they know how to flash; their glow is chemical honesty, not the plant’s trick with light.

Cycles run. The plant breathes deeply every hour; you can set a clock by the room’s exhale as it spills warm air into deeper rock and drinks in a new draught that tastes of stone and old shells. There is a sap-tide that swells from the leftmost arch to the rightmost, taking twenty minutes to arrive and fifteen to recede, and if you keep your palm on the vein-wall you will feel heat chase cool as it goes. With the tide comes exudate: beads of sugar, a lick of oil. Grazers arrive in the minute before the exudate forms. The room is an orchestra of beats nested in other beats: leaf opening, stomata stutter, resin tear, heartbeat node, respiration pulse, drip burst, and, wider and stranger, the weekly attempt the plant makes to rewrite a corner of physics toward more convenience. On that day, in that hour, the floor gets a little nearer to the ceiling, and the echoes stop coming from the future and behave themselves.

Rules behave differently here, but they have their own consistency. Light behaves like fluid if you pour it through leaves; sound behaves like a seed — it is planted and later emerges; water climbs where hair asks and falls where stone refuses; gravity can be loaned to objects the plant trusts, such as its own ropes, which hang sideways for an afternoon while they ferry minerals; time is sticky where sap is sweet, and if you think here for too long in one place, you feel remembered. The handful of creatures who know this pocket abide by these revisions unconsciously. A tool is a leaf used long enough to be called by a new name. A shelter is a promise to return to the same branch-knot again; nobody here makes such promises.

There is no architecture except the plant’s insistence that certain spans be held, certain bells be taut. Still, the place knows rooms. Here a balcony of leaf holds you above a pool; here a throat opens into an apse of soft panels that take your sound and give it back gentler; here a sheer cut of stone — a facet that would slice your palm — is lidded in moving cuticle that thickens and thins as needed. The rock’s youth is visible in flakes razor-thin at the base of one membrane, in splinters trapped like insects in amber within the plant’s grip, in the way the ceiling peels back, one layer after another, in a slow peel you can hear.

Over long sitting, your inner ear sets to the room’s hum and you learn its weather. You can tell when the next rain of condensation is about to release because the hairs lift across the net like the coat on an animal before a storm. You can tell when the strange throat at the top is going to try its sideways again because the light in the smallest panes dims to the color of the surface sky at evening, a sky you have not seen, but which the plant has memorized. You can tell when the fish will rise. You can predict the minute of sweet. In a place where reality breaks politely and often, patterns are a kindness.

On leaving, if leaving is something the pocket allows your idea of yourself to do, the afterimage is of an emptiness filled with organized will: a hollow kept warm and bright by a green mind that keeps its own heart in its hands and squeezes it on schedule. Outside, with proper gravity and reticent echoes, light will seem lazy, water stubborn, and breath too quiet. Down there in the hidden room, all three were aspiring to be more than themselves and, for the most part, were succeeding.
