You have to press your cheek to the hotter-than-sun stone and squint into a slit no wider than a knifeblade to find it. The mountain’s face is young here, a fresh-broken, high black wound that still shines like cooled syrup where flows froze in the act. Beyond the fissure there is a pocket, the size of a held breath. It is a room that never meant to be one: a vesicle bubble in volcanic glass, snapped open when the ridge tore itself taller, now perched within wind’s reach twelve thousand sky-meters above anything soft.

Inside, the rock weeps from every angle. Water is always about to be water here—film more than droplet, glaze more than stream. The hollow’s walls are slick, their curves feathered with razor steps where crystals had been stretching when the mountain broke them. Heat lifts out of the stone in steady exhalations that make the air shimmer; the world beyond the slit is thin and cold and pulls at the moisture, so that everything in the pocket is the argument line where steam becomes liquid again. The light from outside is normal daylight, but slid through translucent skins and veils; it enters as spangles and rainbows and pale tallow glows, confounded by oil-thin layers of condensed water stretched across facets.

The walls are black glass, with green-tinged olivine eyes peeking from the matrix and hair-thin veins of iron ochre beginning to brown where the wet lingers. In protected corners, the first salts are dusting in delicate whiskers, not yet long enough to be called stalactites. Every edge is young and mean. Granules the size of a pepper grain are boulders here. The mountain’s height is palpable: the little space sighs with the pressure difference, a pressure that beats like a sleeping thing—minute, regular, just enough to make the skins tighten and slacken in place, so the shapes shift between heartbeats even when nothing appears to move.

No plants, none that carry leaf or stem. The nearest thing are skins of chemistry, clinging wherever heat and wet strike the right bargain. They are matte and brown-black and stubborn, less organism than rumor of one, feeding on the hot iron and the broken bonds in the wet. Where a film has had a minute to thicken, it frets the light into emerald. Lean nearer and the emerald is an army of flour-grain ovals anchored by slime: archaeal stoics, stooped like monks, inhaling stone, giving the pocket its faint, almost sweet metal breath. When they slough, they lay a slick that the rest of this place will eat.

Everything else moves. Overwhelming is relative; in a space not much bigger than a fist, there are more lives than your pupils can resolve, each with a route and a hunger. They are not familiar. There are no legs, no feathers. There are beings with boundaries made from tension and adhesion—bodies that are bubbles until they are knives.

A bead the size of a sesame seed crosses the underside of a glass overhang upside-down, defying gravity by riding a thin bridge of water that clings stronger than its weight. It pulses as it goes, changing the angle of its meniscus at the front and back like a swimmer flexing shoulderblades. It leaves a paler streak as it passes: the archaeal film shaved nearly clean by that moving curve. Two smaller beads adjust to meet it, merging with it, vanishing into its surface with a sigh of a sound you feel more than hear. The new, larger bead arches itself higher along the curve. A sharper edge of rock reaches for it. For a heartbeat the droplet hooks over that edge and becomes two, division clean as a thought, one flinging forward and clinging, the other left behind to be eaten by something faster.

Faster is not louder. The pocket is quiet to the point of rudeness. There is no wind, only its memory in the steady draw through the slit. There is only the click of a cooling crystal giving up the last of its heat. There is only, sometimes, the sound small as an eyelash brushing a page: a brittle filament of salt breaking under the weight of a passing body. Most of what happens here happens by silent surface forces—surface tension, van der Waals quiet—the hush physics makes when it’s doing the heavy lifting.

Not everything is liquid. Something jointed—but so small you notice it only when the light flashes along one edge—skates along by snagging silicon-knife ridges with hooklike points, dragging behind it an entourage of cloudy motes. Watch long enough and the motes change what you think is ground color; they were never dust, but a pale feeding, a scurf of cells. The jointed thing stops at a patch of weakness where a bead has just shaved the film and, with delicate, repeated taps that show a preference rather than a mind, it rasps a shallow gutter into the biofilm that will trap the next traveling droplet into place. When the bead arrives and sticks, it shimmies around the rim and licks at the invisible gradient of food that pools there. If a word like tool fits, it is made of moment and mineral: nicks in slime, prised membranes, not structures but intentions in passing.

You would think heat would chase life from here, but hot is different at this scale. The rock’s exhale keeps the pocket in a perpetually tropical fever: bright, damp, 45–60 degrees to the touch from ridge to ridge, with gradients so tight you can map them with your breath. Hot is energy, and energy stacks bodies. In every corner where a curve cups a palmful of humid, the air is stratified: fog at the top of a hair’s breadth, clear below, then slick liquid gleaming on surface angles. Things inhabit their own layers. In the thin fog, filaments like hairs on which dew condenses and flows backward—skyroots, if you like poetry, but they are not plant; they are extrusions of extremophiles, threads of mucous scaffold extruded and immediately set with mineral, a scaffold they climb and abandon, climb and abandon. Down where the glass is dry between sweats, the jointed ones scratch trails. On the skins there are round glyphs of paler green as if something hunted them in meticulous bites; turn your head and the glyphs aren’t circular at all but wrinkled lines that make sense only when the light strikes from a very specific place.

Peculiar physics knits this hollow. Turn away, then turn back, and you see the same droplet on two faces: a single curve of water that threads between opposed planes and shows different halves to you at once. It is true; the space is folded so tightly that one path can touch itself in more than one direction. Surfaces meet at angles that do not add up if you try to lay a protractor across them in your mind. When the mountain sighs and the pocket’s pressure blinks minutely lower, gravity’s vector hesitates. Some beads climb. A rivulet will shudder, pause, then take the steeper route uphill because the ones behind are pushing harder than the pull below them.

There are predators obvious only by effect. A bare patch appears in an emerald film in a shape that moves, rim first, erasing as it goes—a tide line sliding along rock with nothing to tide. When it passes a suspended bead, the bead beats once as if alarmed; then its surface darkens, and collapses, and what remains is a wet scar that still moves on, the hunter under-control of adhesion and charge. Something else gives itself the shape of a collar and slides around a droplet, cinches, and steals it, leaving only a wobble in the skin. A hen-and-chicks of spheres makes an excursion across a steeper gap, trailing a thread as thin as mislaid hair; a jointed skater whips past and snips the thread with one deliberate stroke, and three of the kernels fall—fall sideways—into the mouth of a crease.

Occasionally an event from elsewhere touches. The mountain sends a hotter puff up through some capillary you cannot see, and a whole panel fogs, tightens into a mirror, and then blooms with micro-popping as the mirror nucleates off beads. With each pop, a microbiome relocates; with each relocation, a pattern changes. Every few minutes a clean beam of daylight sneaks in at an angle that strikes some curve like a lens, and the entire hollow throws a caustic of brightness along one arc. In that minute, bodies that drink light rather than heat—pigment flecks embedded in slime—brighten to ochre and mint. Their predators learn the timing and wait. Even the passive physics is learned by those who use it.

Outside this tiny planet, the drop is real and immense. A meter beyond the slit the cliff breaks into cloud; the light the pocket takes is honest, the sky’s, unstrange but worked upon. Inside, heat hums to itself. Water condenses no matter the hour. Newly born drops feel out pores in the rock like tongues. The space saturates with presences that mostly ignore you because you do not much exist at their speed or their scale. When you flinch you scrape a knuckle, and your salt becomes a weather pattern. Minute later, the pattern is a feast, then carved away as if it had never been.

There is no built history. Nothing is laid that means to remain. If there is technology here, it is the kind that passes in an hour and lives entirely in the muscle memory of adhesion. A hook-shaped salt grain, dragged and set. A cut in slime. A way to face the lens-light when it comes.

Young stone means edges everywhere, and edges mean choices. The hollow keeps making them; it is active even in its silence. You can stay until your breath fogs the entrance and the world inside runs hotter, crowds closer, becomes more numerous in its intentions. But you will never hear it crescendo. When you withdraw and the slit falls back into ordinary rock, everything that seemed impossible returns to being a rumor high on a ridge. Only that faint sweet-metal scent lingers, and if you wait long enough, even the mountain’s pressure-beat recedes into the heart you mistook it for.
