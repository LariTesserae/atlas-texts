Lift the square of painted steel and the world exhales. Warm air rises from the void beneath the floor, a breath steady enough that you could set your watch by it. The light you lower in—plain, workday white—finds edges and right angles first: galvanised ribs, a molded-polymer pan, a run of conduits strapped tight as vertebrae. There is no horizon here. It is the size of a large cupboard, or a small shipping crate, or a shoebox if you are springtail-small. Scale jitters with your eye. Everything is near and all of it is occupied.

The ground of this place, if you can call it ground, is slightly canted toward a notch where a sump sits with its own bright eye of a float switch. The floor is not old. Its paint is still unscuffed in long planes between drain lips, its scratch marks crisp, its stamped serial numbers unblurred by time. At the low lip of each bolt, clear water beads into a perfect circle, menisci curved like tiny glass domes. Under the conduits, condensation threads along the cable ties, gathers, hesitates. Every few heartbeats a droplet commits and falls, ticking like a polite metronome against the pan. The ticks arrange time here; when the bigger unit upstairs cuts out and its fans wind down, the drip accelerates into a brief whisper of rain along the cable trays. When it surges back, warm air glides through again, and the little laugh of rain recedes.

There is an economy of green. Film algae veneer the dampest corners in a furtive shimmer, more a suggestion of color than a thing with leaves. Lichen’s first scouts ring a single bolt head in pale punctuation, and a run of feathery moss—with the tenuous optimism of a new idea—makes a thin hem under a seam where the condensation never quite dries. Down in the sump, a curt immigrant fern has arrived via somebody’s boot tread, biding in the warm draft and the predictable little tides called by the float switch. Here and there, a grass seed, cheerfully misplaced, takes root in the felt of dust caught in the corrugations around a fastener, sending up a pale blade that leans toward the service lamp as if toward a world.

If plant life is sparse, animal life is the conversation of this room. Silverfish move like commas in the margins where warm meets cool. Lines of springtails—shiny as graphite and nearly as deliberate—traverse the undersides of conduit straps, then loosen into a skipping scatter when a droplet lands and becomes, in their scale, a sudden lake. A transparent gnat, all wing and lens, hangs in the lee of a gauge housing as if it knows what numbers mean. Two isopods (grey, articulated, each ring bright where the light catches) shelter in the seam-shadow, antennae tasting currents made by machines as much as by air.

A centipede, as orange and precise as a new road marking, takes the high route along a cable tray’s lip, tracing with body and feet the faint field that hums out of the live bundle. Its segments seem to agree that north is wherever the power goes. There are others here who agree: threads of damp dust stand to attention along the cables, iron filings gathered and aligned into quivering hedgerows by invisible force. Inside those hedgerows, thing-like worms with glossy bodies as fine as hairs have quarried tunnels in the packed lint; you can see the openings where they part the filings with a furtive push and the filings cling to them for a moment in stippled armor before twitching away. When the big motor above kicks, the filings shiver as if alive. The worms follow the shiver like a map.

Built things appear sparingly, as if embarrassed to be found out. A pressure gauge sits canted on its bracket, its cloudy lens etched by a decade of tiny condensing and evaporating breaths. A sensible little pump sleeps by the sump and wakes only when the float button rises; then it murmurs to itself and sends a thin silver thread off into the waste line. A switchbox—beaten, competent, its legend plates worn smooth by hands—minds a circuit that hums in sympathy a half-handspan away. The service rail, a single-rail track for a trolley that never seems to come, makes a shallow arc along the far wall and out through a chink where the plenum opens into a darker run. On the rail, fine webs are strung like guardrails at ant-knee height; those webs are always wet, loaded with dew until they bow and then, at a certain mass, empty in a simultaneous patter that waters the little, lucky places below.

The noise of all this is mostly absence, a quiet padded by ducting and design. You hear the hum, a low vowel made of electricity and balanced loads. You hear the regular tick of drops. You hear—only when you listen like another creature might—the soft articulate murmurs of many small bodies changing direction, halving then doubling their speed in response to the weather that is engineering and schedule. Occasionally, far away on the other end of the ducting, something like surf: the plant takes a gulp, changes pressure, exhales.

Edges are young here, and they command attention. Dust has only begun to learn the room’s grammar. The rivulets that run down the inveterate low-sides after a shutdown have not yet cut their grooves; they wander, their paths getting a little more certain each night, writing in silt a script that springtails can most easily read. Rust is not, yet, a color you notice before the grey. Paint flakes are as rare as moulted skins. There is a place where the epoxy floor has been skimmed by the corner of a crate; the mark is white and abrupt, not softened by traffic. The bolt heads still hold their geometry. It is recent enough that you can feel the confidence of the tools that made it, the way their teeth fit perfectly and left an exact intention.

Down at the sump—sealevel for this microcountry—you can see a topography that isn’t meant for you. Oil film curls into rainbows with every breath from the pump. The fern’s frond stirs barely, and on its wire-fine stem three translucent snails wait out the dry movement hours; when the unit cuts and the little rain comes, they wake without leaving their spots, extending ciliated mouths to taste the run of things. A small toad, vellum-thin and the color of coveralls, rides the warm air as if in a hammock behind the float housing. It will speak later, briefly—one liquid note that confuses the echo into a chorus—and the gnats will make a cloud and the centipede will turn, not because of the sound, but because the room has shifted toward wet.

Predators and prey choreograph to these shifts. Spiders here have lost all interest in corners and instead throw their silk between any pair of cables that hum alike, the web catching not only flying things but the steady draft itself, which trembles with the approach of a pump cycle. Their anchor lines are thick with magnetised grit; the silk is stained as if by graphite. The roaches are not your idea of roaches; these are narrow and quick and sip from the sharp angles where swarf has packed, then lick their antennae clean with patient, precise gestures. Sometimes a droplet severs its commitment to a cable too early, hangs fat and swaying above a pan, and then falls away with a softness that masks its force. In that moment, under that small rain, a sheet of dust avalanches and reveals beneath it—flash of mother-of-pearl—a clutch of eggs pinned to the pan with a thread of plastic fiber.

Rules accumulate like condensation. Here are some. North is with the current, south is against it. Storms are thermostat accidents and maintenance breaks; seasons are written into the weekly run-sheet and the shift supervisor’s sleep. Fresh water appears at reliable minutes past an hour and at those minutes predators flatten themselves like punctuation in a lull. Gravity is strict; but surface tension is a kind of kindness that lets the smallest bodies cross vastness without sinking if only they remember to tap the water quickly and believe. Smells matter more than shapes: ozone and sweet cutting oil and the breath of fungal fruit.

And weird, in the way that close attention makes anything stranger than you expect. Those hair-fine worms in the magnetized lint build fences of filings around their burrows; when a fan reverses they scrunch into coils and the fences lean, then sway back as if saluting. The moss—where it has gotten bold—seems to prefer the cooled returns from the machine to the warmer feeds, although the difference in temperature is barely the difference between your wrist and your elbow. A rune appears on the pan under the glow of the inspection lamp: really just a stain where water always chooses to go, and yet the springtails treat it like a road and never cross it except when rain accelerates, as if the line itself knows where they are and demands a toll. There is a spot where the cables cross and the fields interfere into a place of quiet; it is always littered with the stripped skins of gnats, and the toad goes there when the plant alarms go off, no matter what else is happening.

This small, low, new place does not care for vistas. It accumulates presences instead, until there is no room left between them. A cable tie becomes a cliff with a ledge route worn into its side; a bolt head is a mesa with a ringforest of lichen and a wet-season lake atop. The light is the light of a workday that never ends, steady and unforgiving and exactly bright enough for what needs doing. The built world drops through it in scattered hints—a forgotten decal, a serial plate, a handle that expects a hand—and the living world gathers around those hints with a competence both opportunistic and orderly. The water comes and goes, the heat breathes, the materials speak their edges, the noises keep and break their promises. It is quiet, except for how alive it is. It is warm, and damp where damp insists. It is recent, which means it is still busy becoming itself, laying down the rules in glossy drops and the little, deliberate movements of things with a stake in being here tomorrow when the unit cycles again.
