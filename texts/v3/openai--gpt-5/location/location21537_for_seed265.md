Dark here is not an absence; it is the medium. It presses softly against the skin and beads on every surface, warm enough that breath never turns brittle. The air is saturated and patient. You feel, before you know, that the land is low—the way sound falls away without echo, the way fog lingers and collects its own weight, the way your step draws up a seeping that was waiting just beneath a thin skin of soil.

The plain extends farther than sense can catch. There is no horizon to shred the world into near and far; instead, distance is measured by delay and dampening. A pump’s tremor, very far away, arrives through the ground long, then the faintest hum finds the ear, then the hush of the ground absorbing it again. The field is open in all directions, but the open is full. It is crowded with bodies, frameworks, veils of condensation, thorn-length stalactites of drip. Everything is within an arm’s length of something else—wire next to slick reed, lichen film next to cast-off carapace, the ribs of a pipeline curving just behind a taste of salt on the air.

The terrain feels new to touch. Edges are unsoftened, as if drawn recently and still drying. Fresh fracture planes stand like pages left fanned by an invisible hand. In places the bedrock is not quite rock as you know it but a cooled sheet with pores, and from every pore a thread of water. Water never decides into torrents here. It sweats, it clings, it waits. Shallow basins keep their meniscus for months. The ground learns you by the weight of your step—a thin crust fractures, a vellum of silt lifts and floats back down without dust because there is no dust, only damp. Where small gullies run, they are crisp with little shelves, no rounded stones, no tumble songs: a newly placed alphabet for future erosion to learn.

Vegetation is sparse and purposeful. No canopies, no lushness, only things that commit to the plane of rock or the upright of a post. Long ribs of sedge spaced like the teeth of a broken comb, each blade slick and heavy with dew. Ropey, pale tassels that are not plants the way you remember, but filters: open-mouthed sponges that lift their pores to the thick air and drink what the air cannot carry any more. Crops of sheets—a living varnish—spread across overhangs, metabolizing what leaks: iron, sulfur, a rumor of electricity. Anything that grows high grows hollow, as if to lighten itself against the drag of the always-wet.

Animals abound, but they have learned the volume. They are present like humidity is present: layered, omnipresent, hardly noticed until they brush you or stop. Slow grazers the height of your hip stand in clusters, velvet faces lifted, tongues unfurling to collect condensate, then roll it back with the film of microbe that rides it. Thin-bodied swimmers move through the soil, not water—a ripple of grain, a flex below the crust, a mouth that is more of a hinge felt through the boot. Ceiling creatures cling to the undersides of ledges and human-made frames with plates and feathery sensory fans, sampling the pressure tides for prey. You hear their little valves opening and shutting, the soft click of matched shells. There are coursers with limbs like knotty reeds; they don’t run so much as unspool, their bodies a bundle that stretches and gathers. When they move, the world’s wet silence absorbs them whole. Predators here are listeners. A cough could feed a family; so few living things cough.

Between them and among them, and just at the edges of touch, installations stand—it is an industry that learned manners. Scattered pump-houses on pylons lift their bellies out of the saturation. Their walls are skinned with mute panels, the surfaces textured for condensation and shedding. Pipes rise from the ground with the inevitability of reed-beds and lie back down again like sleeping animals. They are wrapped in materials that never ring—no clangs, only a low-breathing warmth. Every few kilometers, a condenser field leans into the sky it cannot see: tall frames whose meshes are tight as cloth, drawing liquid out of a sky that prefers to carry it. Sometimes there is a flex through those meshes, a microscale avalanche of drops, and the gathered water staggers through sluices to sumps cut deep. On rails scarcely wider than a palm, service crawlers go their rounds like beetles, feeling more than seeing, nose-sensing the trace of oil and chemical signature of worn bearings, and then they stop for an hour, a day, to listen for the next instruction to arrive through the ground.

Electricity is made here the way heat sleeps: differences just big enough, every surface an exchanger. No lamps. Lamps would die. Light is a thing that comes here and does not survive a meter. Photons spend themselves warming the wet until there is no name left in them, and so anybody who needs to find what is in front of them learns to do it with hands and echoes. The factories hum, but not all the time. They work by taking very slow breaths from the planet, and they exhale slower. The marks they leave are careful: a chalk of mineral here, an alignment of drilled holes there, a net of cables resting on mushroom-colored insulators. Scattered yet scaled: each solitary node is a whole, patient organism of pipes and fans and slabs, with miles of unseen interdependence humming through the ground.

Rules of the world, so far as anybody can tell them in this dark:

- Sound carries, but not like you expect. It leans. A note sent to your right arrives at your left and late, and then again, holed and slow, from the ground. The terrain is new and has not yet learned to reflect. It prefers to swallow, to practice being a body rather than a bell.

- Water rises as much as it falls. It beads on undersides and in the folds of things. Steps draw it up, heat draws it down; no rain, but raining anyway, all the time, in a way that never makes you flinch.

- Light does not last. It converts to heat so eagerly that any torch is a wet hiss and then a warm stick. In this place the night is not a cycle but a state, and the state has a hunger.

- Distances are not reliable when unobserved. A place will keep being where you last touched it. If you try to cross by memory alone, your elbow will reach before your hand. Surveyors mark with ropes you can smell and textures your knuckles remember. The fauna, of course, have maps printed into their bodies; they do not get lost unless they mean to.

- Time is laminated. What you do stays for a while as a phantom: you lift your foot from a pool and the ripple continues to hurdle around the basin long after it should have found a shore. Engines turn slowly because fast things slip out from under their own cause, and the repairs required to pull them back are tiresome.

- The ground declines to be purely horizontal; it keeps a small personal slope beneath every living thing for drainage and courtesy. You never stand truly still; you stand in a slow, private river that pretends to be stillness.

You are always near rock. It is not old rock. It is sheeted and sutured as if from yesterday, with slicks where fault faces kissed under pressure and then let go. From these planes the water weeps. In cuts made for pipes and in sinkholes where the weak layers collapsed, the section shows a simple story: only two or three layers brought into being in a single event and not yet complicated by time’s handwriting. The margins of pools are straight where they should be scalloped, right-angled where wind would curve them, because wind here is a mingling, not a shove.

Where the built world touches the beast world there are agreements. The condenser frames are latticed with empty niches the size of a palm, and in these niches small mottled creatures wait and sweep. They are allowed. In exchange, they keep the meshes free of slime. The sumps have ramps in two sizes; the larger for service, the smaller for the herds that descend on schedules nobody taught them to drink from the slow, warm drip. The rails that guide the crawlers make a faint tune when stroked, and the coursers sometimes run the line with their long, careful feet, touching each sleeper and changing direction when the pattern in their pads says turn. This place is populated like a city is populated, only the city is quiet and the roles never speak their names.

Where there were once trees—or what would be trees, if this world liked uprights—there are instead racks and spines. Some are human-made: gantries that never found their true height, half-assembled conveyor skeletons fossilized by budget or by custom into permanent reach. Some belong to the place itself: calcareous combs grown in the upper drip zones, each tooth aligned to coax the weeping water into a continuous film. Stray figures appear by touch: a chair sunk to its knee in a mud’s slow refusal to carry it; a partition wall that divides two identical patches of nothing. The plain keeps the debris of trying like mementos and absolves the hands that tried by forgetting their intentions.

Despite the abundance, movement is measured. Seasons are not of temperature—warmth here is steady, barely inflected—but of wetness’s mood. There is a time when the weeping eases and the sumps become advice rather than law, and animals cross the flats in greater numbers, leaving lines that hold because nothing scours them. There is the return of the heavy drip, and things fold themselves back into the creases of rock and scaffold. Predators do not chase. They place. They find the angles where the rules of distance bend and then lean into them, so a victim walks into a mouth that was somewhere else a moment ago. It does not hurt more for that; it is simply efficient.

If you stand under a condenser, your hair becomes threads for water to climb. You feel the mesh above vibrating minutely with the burden you cannot see. Somewhere, a valve cycles; it takes a minute, it takes an hour, there is no hurry. A single train of service crawlers passes at a pace that could put moss to sleep. One of the grazers sidles close, huge and harmless as furniture, and you feel its exhale on your wrist. The dark has weight, but it is not a threat. It is an element, as simple and unarguable as ground.

Far off, the sea is not seen but understood as a limit, a pressure dome, a band of salt in the air’s taste. The plain tilts almost imperceptibly toward it like a body leaning toward sleep. The young rocks and the newborn gullies will be taught in time by lapping, by grinding, by the tasteful insistence of waves, but not yet. Right now the place is the first page of an atlas, the outlines inked and drying, the legend being invented as needed. The machines write in small type at the margins. The animals annotate in grease and in quiet gestural notes. The water underlines it all, wetly, continuously, the script that makes every letter shine for no one.
