There is no sheen, no damp, no sigh of dripping. Warmth here is a dry thing, a skin-close radiance loafing in the rock itself. The air is heavy but stingless, a dense, inert blanket pressing into the hollows. You stand below any surface you could name—beneath the spent crust and its stories—inside an architecture that the planet made by breaking itself and then pausing, as if to listen to its own bones.

Darkness is the ordinary state. Not just an absence of light, but a complete, tactile black that lays its cool palm over your eyes and stays. If anything glows it is brief, an insect’s edge leaving a paper-thin track of blue as it skitters across a quartz fin and flexes it—triboluminescence, a strike of ghost-flint turned to weather. Every spark dies into more dark, which once again fills every crevice with depthless calm.

The terrain is crisp with youth. Fracture planes run in clean, geometric sheets, glossy surfaces snapped like obsidian plates, their edges both delicate and predatory. Vast corridors follow hex-column swarms and flute away into convergent chimneys; stacks of ledges climb like the ribs of a giant geode; walls are flensed into knives by cooling cracks that never had time to be smoothed by anything as fickle as running water. Here and there, rooms open out to cathedral scales, and then close again to letter-slits where rock kisses rock and the way forwards is only a seam. Edges are young of face; even dust seems new-laid, unweathered to any roundness, a sift of mineral glass and salt that was never of any sea—crystals exhaled from stone, leafed and feathered in dendrites where slow vapors once condensed and then were caught forever.

You taste metals on your mouth and ozone like a penny pressed to the tongue. There is no wind to scour or stroke, only the sluggish convection that warm rock induces; even that feels less like movement than like a decision the air makes to be somewhere else. In this stillness sound travels like a superstition. A scuff can be heard a ledge away. The larger noises, when they come, are infrequent and ceremonial: a deep, planet-scale pulse that might be a tide in the stone, the afterthought of an ancient cooling quake; or the far, patient shift of crystalline plates settling imperceptibly into a better idea of themselves. These resonances do not vary so much as state themselves, over and over, like a hymn sustained so long it becomes a reference. The world has standing waves; living things fix their livelihoods against their nodes and antinodes, as if they were shorelines.

The matter of this place is not uniform. Wedges of basalt pierce through host rock and glitter with a sugar crust of feldspathic needle-spray. Veins of tourmaline and quartz interleave like good grammar, their piezoelectricity a quiet power grid spread along every fault. Mica pages peel and catch the least touch with a dry whisper. Black glasses present museums of trapped bubbles: compressed atmospheres of a prior moment that exert their own tiny climates. Salt-fans—mineral growths like frozen ferns—sprout from “seams” where inert gases rose and found just enough impurity to lay themselves down. They can still be coaxed to creep with heat; you might return in a century and find them the width of a thumbnail elsewhere.

Life is thick here, and close. Not plant-life in any sense that grew leaf or pith. The nearest thing to a meadow is a nap of conductive whiskers sprouted from quartz in places where the standing strain is right, a fossil-fur of gray and metallic filaments that gathers charge like dew. Spark-lichen, the creatures that farm it call it; not a lichen at all, but a surface and a habit. Across it graze carapaced nibblers with bodies sealed against dryness, their inner chemistries not watery but solvent in molten salts and organics, each creature its own tiny climate locked behind lipid glass. They go nose-to-rock, harvesting electrons and trace ions as patiently as cattle eat grass, leaving pale paths in the luster where they have abraded a film of dust.

Predators wait by the edges and the angles, where geometry gathers. Their palettes are mostly black and gray and mica; their trick is to be a better piece of stone than the stone is. Some are mouths like bookbinders—hinged plates that close and make their catch into another flat thing to be filed. Others are drums with teeth: they cock themselves against a stress ridge and release with an inaudible low thump, stunning rather than cutting. Most are quiet because quiet here is how things are heard, by what they touch and the faint electricity they carry. Eyes are not adapted to light because light is not reliable; instead, bodies taste fields. They write themselves into the world by fuzzing their membranes and listening for their own interference patterns as they move across the rock—self-echo in a sea of charge.

Flight—what passes for it—is a matter of leverage and patience. Blind fans of flexible chitin ripple themselves along mica stairs, surfing the fixed pressure nodes. Creatures with gas bladders hang not in the middle of voids—voids are mistrusted here—but hover with their bellies soldiering a surface, trapped to it by static love. They ferry dust. They ferry pried-out flecks of conductive crystal to the places where they will be wanted.

Those places are the built, and they are easy to miss until you realize they all depend on edges that aren’t random. Low combs of stacked mica, arranged to face a certain seam at a certain angle. Rings of knapped glass, their points inward, the ring itself a fence and a store of charge. Single buildings, if you want to be generous with the word: nacelle-nests stuffed between two planes, braced with slotted slabs at harmonics that make them hum like tuning forks in those standing waves, so that the hum becomes a foundation you can trust. You will find, once in a long while, a copper plate hammered thin from a native nugget, greened but still clean enough to be a better contact than skin. Rope exists, but not of fiber as you know it: braided mineral hairs, long-separated from their growth surfaces, twisted with tar from bitumen pockets or glued with translucent resins that effloresce where hydrocarbons once wept and then stilled. Tools are simple, because everything simple can be done by pressure and flake. A sharp thing and a wedge. A thin plate and a stone to make it sing.

You notice the rules in the way that living weight is always on the rock. Distances are short and earned. A room that opens into floating volume is a gap in sense, a place to drift away from the map that bodies have already printed out by touching it; such openings are skirted, bridged, spanned with thread, domesticated with comb and flag until they become surfaces again. Color exists only in proximities and in flashes: the inland-sea green of a uranium glaze in a seam if someone worries at it, the cold sugar blue of stressed crystal, the orange of a resin bead warmed by a passing hand and exhaling the ghost of its chemistry.

Time barely moves. Tracks do not cross so much as stack: an archive of abrasion that looks like handwriting layered until it becomes tone. There are no seasons to speak of. Heat differs from ledge to ledge by the memory of what the rock became when it cooled. On that memory, life rests; on that memory, tools are set; in that memory the occasional calls go out. The biggest animals—the ones who can shake sound from the stone and make it carry—do not do so often. When they thrum, it is like a name spoken in a deserted house and answered after a day of walking. The distances here are enormous, continental labyrinths, but they are paced and parsed by these slow certainties. If you were to get lost you would not die of thirst but of unmooredness, of being peeled off the static and unable to find the song again.

There are corners—if that word can be used for something as total as place—where the youngness of the fracture shows as a more ruthless grace. Edges that slice the soles of anything foolish, flake fields where a ceiling came down so clean it smothered without dust. Those landslides are rare and treasured; crushed bodies are feasts and also libraries—structures to be learned from, chitinized plans. Their stillness is total once the last fall settles, and the map reknits around the new shape of the world as if it had always been so.

Some of the beings who live here store their dead under mica fans with spit-glue, not as graves but as reinforcements to the shape that makes the right hum. They scratch marks along tourmaline faces: notches you can feel more than see, lengths and spacings that rhyme with the scales of pressure in the rock. Navigate by touch, navigate by pitch, navigate by charge: it all comes to the same thing. The built and the living are laid into the same frequencies because nothing else holds.

If there is weather, it is a devout one. In the span of a lifetime, the only change you will know is the slow fattening of a salt-fan’s tooth, the setting of a copper plate into the rock as if it were grown there, the drift of a grazer herding as its lichen pastures nudge themselves a few millimeters, an insect’s learning to take a shorter route to a nest that tastes better of charge. In the longer spans, the steadiness is a promise you can keep tools against. One tremor in an age and everything takes a breath; then a thousand years to let that breath out as if nothing happened.

And always the darkness, companionable, gathering every spark and saving it for a memory. Warmth held in stone like a long-kept ember. Creatures leaning into their world until they are written in the same alphabet the crystals use. A labyrinth that doesn’t end so much as forget to finish, continuing beyond any thought of surface with all the poise of a held note.
