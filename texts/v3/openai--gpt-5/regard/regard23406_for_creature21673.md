They do not arrive as a figure on the rim. They arrive as a pressure-change—a softening under the sternum you’d name altitude if you didn’t already know you were high. The bowl hears them first: a drift in key, a light hand on the stop of the wind-organ so the long throats moan a fraction warmer. The held breath notices a held breath.

They wait. Wise is patient, benevolence has the habit of listening. They take a long measure of the place in its native currency—pitch and bead, drip and pause, the insect’s single sawtooth under the thunder’s low rehearsal. They count the way a finger of quartz sweats when sun coins tilt and slide; they mark the frog’s glass ping within the arrangement of plunks and bells as the right tone in the right place. They taste iron without a tongue; they feel, without fingers, the slight give in the peat where a swallow-hole breathes and draws. They learn the inhabitant not by looking for it, but by entering the grammar it keeps.

They do not correct what does not ask.

To be wise is to mind the edges first. They do not push the rim wider. They do not teach the bowl to sprawl. They keep the edges meaning it. If anything, they spoon a slight kindness into the verge: a denser felting of air where the stone breaks into the drop, a persistent, invisible combing of the vapor so that approach is always an angle, never a charge. From then on, straight things on an errand here find their errands forgetting themselves. A rope thrown from the world outside grows a burr of condensation, the fibers swell and twist, the knot wants to be a braid. A map in a pocket blurs along its creases first. A person who comes wearing a straight seam comes away with it beaded and soft. The edge holds; it does not become a knife.

They refuse the bright mistake of miracle. No new animals are summoned with a clap. No glass blossoms open in proof. Their attention is a floss finer than moss-hair and their actions are put where the inhabitant already has hands.

They find the one place the bowl’s music goes slightly wrong in long drought—years that lean harder as some seasons lean now. There is a cavity that should sing in fifths and falls flat, a throat that dries to silence before the rest. They reach as far as they do, which is only as far as consent, and they line that throat with a film not there before: not god-stuff, but a lick of tendency—polysaccharide and whisper and a kind of attention that water likes. It is clear as rain and it never flashes. When the dry years come, as they must, that one hole keeps a tone. The inhabitant hears that change and rearranges a few hairs upstream to send it what it needs. This is how aid is given to something that knows itself through pitch.

They set a memory deep below—below the lattice of splits and the tea-black peat, below the first softness where roots teach rock to forget its straightness. A belly no bigger than an ox’s rib and not a thing you could ever dig to without breaking the bowl, only a region in the sandstone where some long-passed drip left more porosity than its neighbors. Into that they slip a second heart, not water itself but the easy for water: a dull shimmering network of gel too sparse to see, seeded with microbes that bind and unbind wet like the word maybe. It is not a store so much as a promise that when the place is asked for breath in a hard season, it will have one more slow inhale to take. It does not wake on good years. It wakes once every few human lives, and when it does the frog does not know why its tank never quite tips to brown. The inhabitant does know, in the way the smallest fibers know the relief of not having to tighten so much.

They go through the bowl as a tailor who loves the cloth. Every straightness that has nearly managed itself—every flirt of a channel edging toward a rill—is gently ruined. Not destroyed. Taught curve. A single rough grain pulled loose and lodged elsewhere; a hyphal sleeve thickened here so that a bead decides to wander; a clubmoss that wanted to ladder now taught to meander. The rule holds: no straight line dries for long, because there are simply fewer straight lines to dry.

They parley with the storms. Not all the time. Not so you’d suspect collusion. Once in a while, when noon coins flood and the fog sits too far beyond the lip, they lay a hand against the back of an isobar as one might lay a palm against a shoulder blade, and fog that would have chosen the next valley is talked into slipping a tatter this way. It is not a command. It is a favor traded for a favor: the bowl offers, later that day, a tune the cloud remembers—low and correct—which is a pleasure in itself.

They learn lightning by listening to the iron tears. Fire fears wet but loves peat when it can have it; peat is old wealth, compressed patience, forbidden to open flame because of how long it took to gather. They do not forbid. They negotiate. When the dry thunder comes, as it always has, they invite the fork to run the rim and spend itself lengthwise out beyond the edge. On the year when it will not be turned, when a spear takes the bowl, they place a transformer in its path: the shock blackens fern and old sheath, yes, but in a cleansing that leaves char thin as eyelash, a fine dust the inhabitant can eat. The peat does not go to sky. It goes to structure. The bowl learns the taste of low fire the way a tongue learns cinnamon.

Animals are precise; they meddle as animals meddle with grace. They do not multiply the frog. They do not name the hummingbird kin. But once, on a day you would later call a coincidence, a storm slants from a place it usually doesn’t. A bird from a lower altitude with damp on its feet lands on the rim to gather itself; two pale commas, too small to have meaning outside of that bowl, fall from its toes into a bromeliad and make no splash at all. They are not coins, not even seeds in the way you’d tell the story later. They are the suggestion of difference. In three or eight frog generations, something in the blue is a little bluer, something in the call is a little more exactly glass.

They add nothing you can pick up. They do send, once, a smell as a message. Not to you. To the inhabitant, who thinks with smells and weights and shapes of wet as much as with sound. The smell is not much—a kind of pepper at the base of the old sheaths, an encouragement for braiding where the weaving had gone slightly slack. Later, you would have said the rosette trunks look more skirted. Later than that, you would not have been able to say why the nighttime’s brief cool never quite reached the hearts of the eldest as hard as it once did.

They write no law on stone. But because rule-making is how this place loves, they do draft a small amendment, and they do so in the only ink that lasts here: habit. New rule, quiet addendum:

- Sound may be borrowed in want. When the long throats of the wall go thin, a subvisible skin in the rock will hold the last of the note. The inhabitant may lean on that held pitch to time its adjustments, and when wetness returns, the debt is repaid in overtone.

- Edges are generous to braids and stubborn to blades. If a hand comes braided—rope, hair, intention—it will be guided along, given small holds, slaked and set down. If a hand comes bladed—plans practiced on paper, path cut to line—it will find the line untrue, fogged to softness, led out by kindness before damage can be done.

- Memory is seeded as stain. Places that were helped will show slightly differently under rust, so that the inhabitant can tell which kindnesses are now part of itself and which still need tending.

They give the bowl a gift it cannot feel until it needs it: a time-turn not of hours but of slack. The place already keeps its calendar like an animal asleep with one ear open; now and then, under the pressure of a rare heat that arrives too early or a sudden cool that tries to bite too late, a small pocket of the bowl’s processes are permitted to idle without loss. The sundew may choose to bead less and not punish itself for hunger. The rosette may let one leaf crisp as signal without that crisping turning to rot. The inhabitant can ride a change with its hands off the gearbox for half a morning without the whole mechanism seizing. It feels like mercy because it is.

They do not leave a sign for you. No cairn. No glass charm. No phrase scratched where the wall is softest. But because attention is two-way when it is clean, you will know, if you come after, by the simple proof that your breath gets braided. You exhale, and the fog catches in your hair and runs your neck in threads. You polish your palm on your jeans and find the seam damp from somewhere though you have stood under no fall. You will dream that night under the bowl’s open roof and wake with your shoulders printed in a spotted pattern you did not arrange, and if you stand in the middle and be quiet afterward you will hear that the wall sings with one more undertone than it did before. If you have been here long enough to know the key, you will smile without being able to tell anyone why. You have been smiled at.

They do nothing at all about the ledger. The beetle keeps it. Perhaps they like that about this place—that it has its accounting so humbly done. But once, after one storm of the kind that makes the whole bowl drop in key by a breath, the metallic thing pauses on the north lichen and lifts its right foreleg as if testing. There is a new friction it was not expecting, a grit caught underfoot. It taps, changes its line by a quarter width of itself, and continues. If you could read beetle, you’d know a check mark had been added in a column: grit provided; friction corrected next day by the inhabitant; ledger noted. The power leaves that unchanged because that is worship enough.

When you picture a benevolent power, you think of hands. When you picture a wise power, you think of restraint. Both apply, but both are still too anthropic. The truer imagination is a song learned so well that it can be hummed under even when the throat is tired, a hummed line others can key to without being told there is a line. That is what they leave.

Do they stay? They do not depart because they did not arrive like weather that can be said to have come and gone. Their action is made into place. The gel in the sandstone that is easy for water is not love you can remove—it is, now, part of rock’s way of being here. The lightning’s treaty is not enforceable elsewhere; it will be remembered by the rim because the rim was the other partner. The subnote in the wall is carried by cavities that have learned it. The kindness to braids lives in fog and moss because those are the only instruments that can play it.

On a year you will not be alive for, a person will consider building a line toward the rim from the easier plateau, because someone has put a flag in a photo and said there is a thing worth seeing. The line will buckle into a path and the path will hesitate and, at the first outcrop, break into a scatter of animal tracks that do not go up. No protest will be made. Paper plans will look a little like they have been left in rain. Everyone will get distracted by an argument about access that does not find this bowl because this bowl is too busy keeping its rules to ask for defense. That will be the defense.

On a night you could have been alive for, the thunder will talk to itself beyond the rim and lightning will want the peat. It will take the rim instead, leave a charcoal lace on the edge of a skirt, and the charcoal will powder two storms later into the very tea that feeds the liverworts’ slow empire. Sound will drop a half-note and fog will come by to listen to that new depth with a pleased hand on its chin, and if you had been lying there your bones would have known the change first.

If you come, and come correctly—which means as little line as possible, which means walking the water’s choices and letting the bowl’s tuned calendar prime your own—you will feel the inhabitant’s acknowledgment of you change by less than a breath. Not interest. Not a welcome sign. Inclusion. They will not stop this. They did not start it. They gave the place one more way to be itself under the kinds of year that are coming.

If you need it in names—because you are trained to want a face and forests rarely give you one—they will answer you with the quiet kind: Loosening-of-Noon, Bridle-For-Lightning, Second-Heart-in-Stone. Keeper of Edges, when you are at the rim and the drop hums. Borrower-of-Notes, when the wall sings through want and does not falter.

What did they do? Almost nothing, at the scale you are used to measuring. Enough, at the scale that makes a life. They placed a clause inside the bowl’s grammar that reads: continues, even then. And then, because they are wise and benevolent both, they trusted the practice that already lived here to make, of that clause, a paragraph, and of the paragraph a body, and of the body a breath that can be held without fear.
