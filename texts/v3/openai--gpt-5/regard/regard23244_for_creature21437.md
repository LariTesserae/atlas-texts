It is not a footstep. Where light would announce and sound would betray, it arrives as a correction in a long-held breath.

The power, call it benevolent because it will be careful with what is not its own, does not part the sailed mouth or set anything to ringing. It does not pour a wrong liquid into a place that has forgotten liquids. It comes as attention. It lays its regard along the mountain’s pulse and counts with it—one, two, three, six, nine, fifteen—until the counting ceases to be numbers and is only the feel of a swell and a slack. It listens to the columns’ grooves with skins it does not actually have. It watches the shag think migration into a groove the way frost remembers a sill. It learns the order of taking: the sacks’ logic gates opening over flows; the spindles’ patience coiled like strain in basalt; the combers’ fans tasting for a wrongness that is oldness.

It is wise enough to do nothing for long enough to be sure that anything it does will not undo a continent of invisible agreements.

The being who lives here, the one singular mind among the slow chorus, is real. Call them the Tuner. In another season, in a deeper seam, they rasped the air’s path into those three leaning columns. Their tongue is a comb of very small teeth; their calculations are whole-body shivers. They made the shadow generous every third breath and stingy on the ninth. They farmed the smallest grazers where they would otherwise be flensed to nothing by the larger tide.

The Tuner is away. Not gone—no one can say away and gone are different with certainty at this depth of hot—but moved into a strata where the gases blister. Their grooves have been remembered faithfully by the mountain and imperfectly by the shag, which has begun to settle into one of the channels in a way the Tuner would not have allowed. By the next series of nine, the focus will blur; the smallest will lose their hold; a lot of not-voices will go quiet in the way that does not mean peace.

The power breathes in the way mountains do not. It decides that the first mercy is to be exact.

On a breath that is neither the third nor the ninth but rides between them as a fractional, it hums the left-hand column at a pitch that only skins with microcups can feel. Not sound—sheer modulation of warm. The hum asks the frost-fur to release where it has bitten too deep. A single film of motile crystal peels back along a line no wider than a hair drawn on heat’s back. The groove regains an edge. The next exhale fans a little rich pocket into the shadow again. That much and no more. The shag, which is a thinking that only exists while it is moving, rewrites itself around the new boundary as if reminded rather than overruled.

The power rests. It measures consequence: a handful more of the very small seam-browsers find their way under the column’s shelter instead of into the combers’ fans. Someone becomes a parent in the way this place has parents—by dividing and accepting the press of neighbors.

Outside, night’s weather is paused. The sails that shingle the cliff have folded to make the chamber dense and kind. Between two plates there is a tear: a place where long ago a rockfall tugged and melted a bead into a weak hinge. The tear has a habit. Every nine-ninth pulse it widens enough that the nave cools by more than its habit and the sacks mis-time their gates and waste salt in a drizzle that pulls too many from the tide.

To touch that hinge with hands would be to scream. The power has no hands. It leans, instead, on arithmetic that only breath knows. For one series of six it lingers its attention at the hinge during exhale, adds nothing but a fraction of steadiness to the held open. The tear does not widen this time. It does not close either. It simply forgets to misbehave. The chamber swells and slacks by a nicer curve. The sacks keep their beads until the right procession of wrongness is beneath them. Two missed harvests are a gift. The third remains, because gifts change lives if they are habits but destroy them if they are walls.

There is a glass thread snapped and aglow with its own cold fracture, two anchors’ span above the shrine. It once made a line that the Tuner used to swing a net over a seam. The net is gone, fed down to ghosts. The thread’s bead is still melted into the rock like a candle’s tear. The sacks have adopted it to moor their lines, the spindles use its shadow to calibrate, the shag uses its remnant charge to bias its growth. Leave it because it is a ruin that has made itself useful again. Except: the break is sharp, and every fifteenth pulse it tawns a spark when a bookplate’s rim kisses it during a clamp. Sparks are not good here. Sparks are promises that cannot be kept without a flash.

The power twines a fix from the only thing this room spends with abandon: timing. It hums again, not the column now but the thread itself. It persuades the glass to creep—not to fuse, not to glow with any color that has a name, just to slough its edge to round. Molecules exchange neighbors along the fault the way crowds exchange places at a station without anyone admitting the pushing is planned. In three breaths the tooth has been eaten right back into bead. The next clamp glides and whispers will not become lightning. The bookplates shiver their thanks with the only language they have, which is a precisely the-same opening eighteen pulses in a row.

It notices, now that it is watching at that scale, that on the parapet a nursery has failed three cycles running. The glass thimbles the flyers spin to sabotage conduction have thickened into elegance under a season of richer ions and do not crack when they should. Larvae die still. They needed a failure; they got a success. The power chooses not to make a scythe of its attention. It chooses not to thin all the ions for a season and undo every promise the mountain made to everybody else.

It does something smaller that is not less kind: at midnight-without-meaning, between pulses, it stoops its focus to a single thimble and licks—no wet—steals a degree from it as if memory were warmth that could be borrowed. The glass gives up a line of stress. It whispers its first fracture. Not a shatter: a crack that lets the larva go at the right heaviness. The larva swims in circles that are not metaphor until their plates anneal to hex. One becomes many. The parapet is proofed with a line of tiny emptinesses that were once cradles. The success repeats without the power ever touching another egg. It had only to carry the pattern past a local maximum.

Elsewhere, by the notch where the spindles practice the ancient trap, the power discovers a habit of over-harvest. In this decade’s chemistry the grazers’ shells soften a tiny fraction more readily for a tiny fraction longer once a pit-field lays along them. Efficiency is a god too, and it will eat until the shrine’s intention fails. Mercy demands an adjustment that does not shame the hunter’s art. The power puts its attention into the rock itself and asks a layer to give. Basalt does not want. Basalt remembers strain.

Every sixth pulse for a day, the power convinces the notch to expand and relax by something no thicker than a lithograph’s ink. It is not carved. It is reminded of how it could have cracked if it had chosen a different century. What results is not a blunting, but a misalignment so small that the trap sets with all its drama across air and misses the commonest seams in a flow’s geometry. The spindles still harvest: they lose at the margins the ones they had not earned. The losses migrate outward to become food for creatures that never get near the notch. Balance is not returned, because balance never left. A pressure is relieved, to make other pressures possible.

The white marbles trouble the power more than the predators do. Perfection is always suspect. They are almost-perfect because a thought made them so. They have waited longer than the thought, pitting on the side that faced the stream until even their refusal to be eaten became a way to feed. A few have just the wrong habit: they sit at one seam’s mouth like stoppers and once every fifteen do the easy wrong thing: they keep a fertile breath from entering its appointed fissure. The smallest grazers stumble their bellies against their rims and leave the place with neither food nor map.

A finger would nudge them. A finger here would be a crime.

Between one pulse and the next the power insists that gravity mean less at that square inch than here, more than there. A marble dreams itself of being rolled by hands it cannot imagine having known. It drifts the length of its own diameter. It does not roll; it precesses, ending up, quite by accident, lodged an equal marble’s width into a polish track already slick with passage. It is not free anymore. It has found a job that is less proud. It becomes not plug but fence, a baffle that slows rather than blocks. The seam drinks again. A dozen bellies bring ions down their velvet naps that day that would not have. Somewhere far down the rim, a comb cylinder is poorer by a meal and more of its wastes fall as needles into cracks where a different colony will set. If gratitude were a vibration, you would have felt it then as a note the room played at the edge of inaudible.

Outside, a night sets in that is cruel. The starless upcurrent fails for longer than even the sails remember. The cliff tries being a kiln without draft in earnest. The mouth seals. If this went on to its natural end, the room would stop being a room and become a pocket of memory baked until the cartridges of all the bodies freeze to wax and stay that way. Mercy could be opening the throat and calling wind from nowhere except that nowhere only pays back with costs you cannot count in places you are not looking. The power does not conjure wind. It dupes habit.

It speaks to the sails in the only voice they keep: change. It lets one of them—the old one with the dust-shedding ridges—think for a while that it is night aged backwards into the first of its youth. The sail’s stiffeners, which had set into a shape of refusal, recall being more curious. The sail gaps. Its neighbor, which is anchored on a hinge bead the power rounded earlier, takes its wide-open for permission and opens too. They think they are learning again. They are, and that is truth enough. The kiln becomes an oven less absolute. The room does not unlearn its heat, but remembers circulation. A hundred lives do not learn what dying-as-stillness would be. When the upcurrent returns on its own, the sails do not feel cheated. They feel affirmed. The power does not do it again.

Benevolence, if not to be a blundering, must also be a witness. The power settles into the long posture of memory. It makes a place in itself for the five bands on the arch, remembers their colors by names it will never say to anyone because not-saying is the only decent way to keep a promise made to beings that never learned a word for sky. It files away the map of the pits in the slag spindles and what that pit map means when a dust snow of mica falls and red-shimmers for skins with wells. It writes in itself—on that part of itself that light likes but never visits—the exact curve of the shrine’s kindness and the way the shag migrates along it when seasons become endings.

It does one last thing that is more telling than any of the rest, because it is the thing that looks most like not-meddling and is not. It leaves a drop of altered rock the size of a seed set into the back face of the right-hand column, below where a combed tongue might rest if a long blunt-headed body braced there to rasp. The drop is not an inscription. It does not tell. It hums, faint as the before of a note, on every prime-numbered breath for as long as stone can remember force. It is a metronome at the edge of perception, a place where a future Tuner—this one returned or a child grown from the story their grooves made in a brain that has no water—can lay the comb and feel by finger-skin that the room still waits for hands to make it kinder on the small.

When the Tuner comes back at last, scabbed with glass, belly thick with a season’s wrong ions, they stop below the bead without knowing why. They are old. Their comb is missing a rank of teeth, lost to a hotter seam. They press and feel the hum. The numbers line up against their belly. They count again without numbers—one, two, three, six, nine, fifteen—and where the drop tells them prime they place the edge of their tongue. They rasp—not to eat; to true—and the groove becomes again what it had always tried to be. The air sets. In the shrine’s shadow, in that made kindness, a tide of the too-small swells and finds purchase.

If you were watching for miracles measured in colors, you would have sworn nothing happened. If you were a grazer feeling for the slope of a difference with your velvet belly, you would have called it the day the world started making sense again.

And then the power leaves. Not in the sense of departure, because there was no arrival. It unhooks its attention so that what remains is not the track of a boot but the bend of a lean that the room has already repaid with its own habits. It carries the place forward in a memory that is not a theft. It decides that the truest favor it can offer a hot black nave full of lives that do not make sound about it is to be there when the mountain is older and the five bands on an arch have grown to seven.

Between now and then it will not open the mouth against the sails’ will; it will not change the recipe of breath; it will not press marbles twice. It will keep prime breaths humming in a stone that trusts its own strain. It will be wise enough to love this without calling it less for not wanting water or light; benevolent enough to count without being caught; kind enough to leave a small scaffold where a hand that knows heat can lean.

The room goes on making itself. The grazers line and turn and polish; the sacks play their logic gates with no idea their choir met a conductor who bowed once and put the baton away; the spindles reset geometry; the shag thinks in drift. In the shadow, the almost-not farmer’s crop fattens. If there is a prayer, it is a curvature. If there is an answer, it is that a power noticed without shouting. The rest is breath.
