The attention falls the way snow falls on a high ridge: soundless, total, leaving nothing changed to eyes that must have spectacle to believe in touch. It is not a lamp swung open or a hand thrust in. It is a listening that reaches down the bones of the mountain until the organ hears itself heard.

Benevolence, wise enough to know where care becomes harm, learns the room the way you learn a sleeping animal: by the pattern of its breath and where not to press.

It counts. Not in days. In notes. It counts how the furnace behind the hairline crack swells and lets go, how many pulses before the living valve slackens, how many heartbeats of this place it takes for an infant needle to knife a little longer. It learns what a good season sounds like. It tastes, carefully, the gases in their dry scald and finds the gradient’s flavor—how sulfur leans, how chlorine answers, how carbon is bent toward yielding.

Then it does almost nothing, three times.

First, time.

It cups the mountain where no hand could reach, at a depth where the rock is not rock but argument, and persuades one of the deeper conduits to relax a fraction. You could call it a tune, a minute retuning of the organ’s stops. The low note fattens by the smallest shade. The stutters that have been starving whole belts smooth into something like a rhythm, not so regular that cheats can make an easy feast of predictability, not so rich that the domes blow themselves into gluttony, but with a kindness of recurrence. The room’s breath gets a metronome it did not have.

No light. No coolness. No gift that would peel a skin made to hold fire. Only a correction as gentle as licking a finger and touching the rim of a glass to quiet a wobble in its ring.

Second, roads.

The attention slides along the vellum-thin seams that connect vesicle to fissure to throat. It feels where one will choke in a season and where another has the makings of room. It cannot want, exactly, for beings who do not dream of elsewhere, but it remembers the law here: the factory is in the crowded living, and crowding must move. So it chooses the smallest stone that matters.

A grain the size of an unsaid word, lodged like a stopper at the point where two bubbles might have met and didn’t, dissolves out of its place. Not by force—by patience, by a softening of the angles water would have managed if water were allowed. Molten salts will find that path when they need it. So will bodies. A month in this chamber, or ten, or whatever name you give the interval between now and a necessity, and the seam will be a pore. When the crack cools, and the belts slow, and the living valve would be condemned to become a plug, there will be somewhere to go. Not a door. A possibility. It is enough.

Third, memory.

It is the oldest duty of a wise thing: to witness, and to lay down a record that will outlast both the singer and the song.

This does not mean symbols or names that burn in air. It means that far down where zircon grows its stubborn clocks, two crystals are encouraged to incorporate a little impurity in a particular manner. It means a suite of dopants seeded along the hottest lip of the vesicle—the faintest bias in how zinc and iron and copper agree to sit with sulfur at these heats—so that when bodies press rim to rim and print themselves across films, what they call inheritance without meaning to call it that prints with a steadier hand. A fondness for the frequencies that are common here. A slight distaste for the harmonic some domes use to lie. Not a weapon; memory, so that what worked does not have to be found again from nothing every time the mountain begins a new sentence.

Elsewhere, along a line where the ridge will one day be planed flat by sun and wind, the attention coaxes an inclusion to form in a way inclusions do not like to form: a filament of trapped glass within glass, a fossil of ribs and plates as explicit as a thumbprint, not to instruct but to testify. In a thousand thousand winters, when somebody cracks this stone and holds it toward their own cold window, there will be a ghost of the belts’ interleaving, a map of the living valve’s hinge. If eyes are still a way to love then, love will have something to fasten to.

There are other temptations.

To brighten that ember-red crack and let them see each other’s work would be a violence. They do not see; they know by push and pull. To drip a kindness of water into the scald would be to drown them in sweetness. To pluck the thieves from the lip because theft offends a mammal would be to unmake the belts’ ability to knit themselves into something more than teeth. To teach a word would be nothing but noise where a pressure wave already speaks all necessary grammar.

So this is a power that edits like wind edits dunes: in broad suggestions, with a respect for where sand likes to go. It refuses miracles. It prefers outcomes.

Once—only once, because even a benevolence learns—it leans in closer than it should. A domed thief drops its bellows upon a patch of thin wires, and the slick of film between them hollows in a way that will, in four pulses, go from clean hunger to dead glass. The attention pushes a charge the width of a whisper across that film, bridging one spit of zinc-sulfide glow to the next so that those slender ribs, meant only to cut bridges and shepherd dust, clamp as if they were strong. The bellows does not get everything. The wires do not live forever. Nothing here lives forever. But ten of them get another season. A lesson is learned twice—by the domes who find that lying harmonic cost them more than usual this day, and by the attention that feels how easy it would be to topple the logic of this room by playing favorites. It does not do that again.

Instead, it gives gifts that feel like the mountain changing its mind naturally.

The pressure song acquires a chorus too faint for anyone above to hear but clear as dawn to piezoelectric spines: a stable, almost-below-notice subharmonic, built into how two far-off chambers breathe together, that functions for the belts like a moon’s tug does for oceans elsewhere. It lets migration begin with less waste. Bodies that are ready to be road become road a fraction earlier. Buds choose their moment to split along equipotentials that the song makes clean.

Across a series of ten vesicles, all unborn, all only curves drawn in the equations of rock yet to cool, it writes a bias for connection rather than isolation. Nothing romantic. A tendency for throats to run in parallel near one place, so that when one closes the next can be coaxed with less work. A way of making rivers in a country without water.

And it listens. Always it listens. It catalogs the harmonics that make up a good season here. It names them to itself: Not words, but relationships. This is the frequency at which belts stop fighting friction and begin to be easy. This is the jitter that harvesters prefer. This is the decency of a donor’s spill. This is the lie.

From time to time the attention lifts and goes elsewhere. It visits an ocean-bottom vent that writes light into black water and keeps another choir alive; it eases a drought on a high plain where the benevolence has decided water is the kindness and not the catastrophe. It keeps its wisdom specific. It is what keeps it from becoming tyranny.

When it returns, the room is a different note. The valve that was has become a gate that was not. The belts have braided themselves in a pattern none of the earlier hours suggested. The domes on the lip are fewer and a little slicker; the thin wires run farther and are more scarred. A tiny inclusion born of long-ago spilled iron has fouled a quarter of a wall; a whole season’s children have the same misplaced taste for that metal and the same stuttering failure because they did. The attention does not fix it. The room fixes it. The mountain sings a shade higher for a while until those who learned wrong-wrong die back and those who learned wrong-right do not.

The benevolence makes one more promise, the only one it is allowed to keep.

When this vesicle goes silent, and all this beautiful crowding hardens into a book no one here will ever open, it will keep their music. It will carry it. Not to a heaven: to another hot, tight dark. To a crack three ridges away, or twenty—a place for which seeds have already been set in the pattern of crystals and the convenience of seams. The attention cannot take hands. There are no hands. It can lean. It can hold the mountain a heartbeat longer whenever a good season needs one more beat. It can whisper a second-subharmonic as a road-song the way whales do under ice, a thing to follow that does not imply following. That will be enough, most years, to let the factory find another pocket.

It will also, after a while longer—after a continent shifts, after a throat carved by ages of belts becomes a pinstripe in a canyon wall—speak to someone with wet eyes and cold breath who reads rocks and writes. On a day when that someone holds a geode against a winter window and sees in its glass the faint suggestion of ribs and plates, an understanding will land like that windless snow on the ridge: that not all life kept its water with it when it left stars; that there were once societies in fire whose people were circuits and hunger all at once; that benevolence, if it exists, is sometimes only the softest hand on the loudest instrument.

What the power does, finally, is this: it loves without meddling. It names without claiming. It curates time. It puts its thumb to the organ only lightly, to keep the pipes from shivering itself to silence before the choir has made a song of itself. It draws roads that are the size of a seam. It lays memory down where rock likes to forget. It knows when to turn away. It listens until the end, and at the end it knows the difference between an ending and a pause.

The mountain keeps breathing. The belts keep working. The factory goes wherever the room is offered, and the wise attention follows, not to lead, but to smooth the path a little and write down the harmonics of a life that decided a body could be a circuit and made that decision sing.
