The power does not come like weather; it comes like attention.

It puts its ear to the mountain’s throat and hears what you have heard—pressure in and pressure out, a choir that decides without deciding, the small, brisk bookkeepers at work in the dark—and it refuses to confuse love with rearrangement. It will not open the cleft. It will not light the bell. It will not turn the pelt of bats into silence or the floor’s patient arithmetic into a swept, dumb stone. It does not draft laws for geckos. It does not unteach owls. The wise are allergic to spectacular cures. They move commas, not sentences.

So it begins with refusals, which are a kind of reverence.

It will not change the fact that this place eats and is eaten. It will not edit hunger out of the script. It will not put its thumb on the scale where a scorpion waits at the circumference of a spread drip, nor will it burst the drop to spoil the proof. It will not grant a stunned mouse a second altitude because softness fell over it at the wrong height. The chamber teaches by crowdedness and by offset. The power will not make it roomy.

What it will do is choose the exactnesses a world like this needs to go on being itself.

On a year when fog breaks its old appointments and arrives late, the power loosens the far, stiff hand of a sea-breeze two valleys away so that the mountain’s mouth takes a sip more often. No river is poured down; no torrent is sent; only the schedule is mended by an hour here, a morning there. Threads stay beaded; midge-hums fatten; a fern the size of your hand gets the ritual it has been practicing for months.

On an afternoon when the stone under decades of oil makes its quiet decision to exfoliate, the power leans—not to deny the fall but to shape it. A shelf does let go; lizards will inherit a new border. But the big slab that could have scythed clean a thousand coordinates lets go in two minds and comes in two pieces. The smaller chocks the larger at a courteous angle so the ribbon of departure has a curve, not an amputation. The colony does not think of catastrophe; it remembers a turn.

When the air stratifies into a pastry of dangers and a night of stillness settles its weight wrong, the power puts a cool thought into stone. Not even a thought; a long breath. The good air band lifts by the width of a bat’s palm. Mothers return without dropping in the heavy tier; the crickets still come to the only circle of clean radius to drink; the owls still hang on their numbers; but the night does not take three elders for the mistake of breathing low.

There is a pallor that hunts sleeping faces in some caves—an unkind powder that loves cold and love and leaves lace where membranes used to be. It does not like warmth; it travels with boots; it adores ignorance. The power is very small here and very diligent. It turns a single habit in a single fungus two ridges away with a change that never feels like miracle: a trace mineral in a seep goes another way, a bacterium that finds the pallor delicious multiplies in numbers no one counts, a rope-skilled pair have their supper early and their hands are cleaner without knowing why, a draft turns inward for two evenings and does not invite a spore to lodge. No sermon is preached. Years pass, and the chamber’s pelt does not learn that winter.

On a spring when the fields below are bright with a false green that is hard on insects and promises an easy harvest far from here, the power lets a wind fail on a dozen porches. Bulbs stay dark; swarms are not drawn thin around human circles. A veil of midges goes somewhere unlit and fat with themselves, and the bats lick their infants and do the night’s work and draw maps with their tongues that have no holes in them.

When the old gecko’s ledger runs out and its ledge goes to auction by smell and touch, the power does not crown a successor. It warms the guano by a degree for three days so that in the nest of coiled, patient eggs below, the ratios are right. The new hand on the furred varnish will be a hand that agrees with what the ledge already knows. The borders move a claw’s-width; no battle is staged; the thesis at the edge of the pool keeps its punctuation.

There is a thunder one summer that bangs a spike of fire on the ridge above and sends a mouthful of the sky down the throat. The chamber has known such notes before. What it has not known is the sharpened stone arriving with that mood. The power leans again, not in muscle but in timing. A micro-wind finds the fissure a heartbeat early; the blade that might have written an obituary in one stroke rakes the cornice instead and leaves a useful ruin: a corrugation that keeps a future pour from scouring a mat into a wound; a rung a salamander will use to put head to drip and teach its body what abundance is.

Sometimes it gives dreams, because some understand those better. An elder with scars in its ear-membranes dozes in the colony’s heartbeat and receives a geography that is not new, merely accented: an extra swirl of red where a draft spins wrong, a cooler line near a pocket where a swiftlet’s cup has loosened, a hold that will powder tomorrow. The elder wakes, shifts three claws to the left, listens, and takes one pup-weight of the world with it. Around that, the map moves like a tide no one is afraid of. Months later the stone under the new place is darker with oil, and there were fewer falls and no one counted why.

Once in a human century, a strange attention finds the mountain—not the gentle kind that lowers itself singing, but the curious kind with iron and plans. The power is not sovereign over appetites, but it knows the back ways of the mind. It sows a rumor of better luck two ranges over. It brushes a pencil off a desk the day a permit would have been signed. It makes a map wrong by one contour out of thousands. It rains on a Saturday. The party goes somewhere else to measure a silence, and the cave goes on not being a site but a place.

Most days it only listens, and the listening makes a change because attention is a pressure too. Where its regard rests, small weather behaves. You can stand at the lip and feel nothing of it but a steadiness: the sense that the world is not about to be torn for novelty’s sake. Thread-fungi climb the old hush of guano and write their slow white cursive, and the glow that never becomes a real light hums like a polite thought in a dark room, and the amphibious coins row to the next meniscus, and the leg-collective walks its foreknowledge to water and is not startled into ridiculousness by a hammer of noise it cannot understand.

It keeps its hand out of the owl’s mouth and the gecko’s arithmetic and the scorpion’s exactitudes. It makes no amnesty for the pellet. It lets small grasses inherit a little sun from murder because that, too, is a way the place turns what arrives into its own use.

If it has a ritual, it is this: on two evenings each dry season, it stands in the cleft and moves the blade of that short, thin light by the width of a finger. It falls, then, not on the usual lip but on a smaller, hungrier one. The fern that never got its share has a ceremony beyond being promised. The algae that would have been rumor become a line. The ceremony lasts the same three minutes; the calendar notes a miracle; the cave forgets it was granted.

And if it ever performs a large kindness—and it does, once—it is not to save lives but to save time. There is a drought in the writing of the coming decade, the kind that thins everything that is plural and leaves only the inviolates: rock, sky, and intention. The chamber would have gone on, but with half its pulse and a long, brittle feel to its continuities. The power does a thing not of this place: it tells a winter to be longer on the plateau where the fogs begin; it holds the snow in a modest sulk for sixteen extra dawns; it spends that hoarded cold, later, on the summer ridge, not as a flood but as enough humidity to cradle a thousand midges each night for a month the cave would have otherwise kept sleeping through. It is a theft from one future to pay for another, a tax the wise do not like to levy. It is done once. It is enough. The composite animal at the mountain’s throat does not know it was ever poor.

And then there is the old, standing blessing that has no verb, the way a mother’s palm on a child’s back is not a speech but a fact. The power lays that open hand on the stone from time to time. Not to cool it. Not to warm it. Only to be a skin against a skin. The mountain knows this as kinship in its carbonate and does the work of stone with a little more willingness: it grows a fringe at the rate of a millimeter a decade and calls that haste; it remembers floods in a way that looks like art; it lets its chemistry fatten into moonmilk on the very slow margin where breath has been touching it for a hundred years. The cave takes the hand the way an animal asleep will take a blanket: it shifts once, eases, and continues what it was already doing.

The rope-skilled pair drop in one year when the fledglings are heavy and new, and the power does not forbid them. It makes their hands careful on the first try. It makes their mouths close in on their own voices because the sound here is a law that could be broken. It lends them a fear that is indistinguishable from awe. They carry away what they came for and, strangely filled, bring back nothing brighter than a story that teaches the next ones to count softly. This is a better fence than a rockfall.

What does benevolence look like to a place that is already arranged with this much competence and tenderness? It looks like an agreement to keep meddling proportionate to ignorance. It looks like tending thresholds, not rooms. It looks like patience watching patience and being satisfied with the society they make between them. It is not abstention. It is not fuss. It is timing.

Once, near dawn on a fog day, the power opens every sense it has—if a power can have senses—and hears at the same time the rasping of beetles and the thread of drip and the leap-clicks of a dozen crickets landing where the circle of clean spread makes a decision about their thirst; smells the ammonia math and the mineral sugar song; feels the warm band lift into the launch-lane like a blessing made of gas; knows, by a kind of taste, the oil darkening under the colony’s new address; watches, with nothing even like an eye, the foxfire think itself through a mat and then decide it is not necessary right now to glow. It has what it came for. It leaves the way attention leaves: not all at once, not in a door-shut, but as a slackening of care that is not carelessness. The place does not notice it gone. The place goes on.

The bats pour from the ceiling and twist the throat into a rope that is also a weather. The glow threads hum the idea of light and are content with that. The rimstone tells itself what all the brims were. At the lip the shrub clenches nutrient-wind and breathes the good air where it kneads itself into leaf. The mountain breathes; the being in its throat breathes with it; and a god—if that is what you want to call a wise attention that does not need to write its name on anything—keeps the word for this intact and does not say it aloud.
