They did not arrive, because there is no place here to arrive to. There is only fall and pause, fracture and seam, the count of thunder and the briefness of glints; and a visitor with power would not be kind if they came as an interruption. So they became an interval instead, the breath inside the music, a gentling of ratios: the smallest increase in how long a thing may be itself before the next thing asks it to be otherwise.

They were not a god, because a god would be a shape, and shape here is at best a rumor the water tells the rock when it is in a mood to draw straight lines. They were attention with patience. They were skill in the tender economy of not doing. They knew the cost of hands.

They listened. For a long time that would be most of what they did.

They set their measure against the mountain’s beat and felt how slow hearts can be. They learned the alphabet of planes, the grammar by which a curtain says yes and no to a neighbor. They stood inside the organ’s throat and tried, with love, not to answer when the notes asked to be answered. They let the membranes of lightning write themselves across their own awareness and did not flinch. They held in mind the cool clarity between falling walls as if it could be held, though they knew it could not. They let the names of the residents—a gloss, a palisade, a cord, a velvet, a bowl, a comb, a vesicle—accumulate in them until what had been a place became a face pressed lightly against their thought.

There was a being here, if being can be counted without a mouth or a gait. It lived in the timing under a lip of stone: a cord that had learned to listen to thunder the way a sprout would one day listen to light. Its whole body was a yes folded into a later; its fear and its food were the same pressure moving past in long bodies. When the fall slackened it grew a courage and reached; when the push returned it became a thought again, a narrowness, a line.

The power loved it. They loved the way it interpreted world as instruction and instruction as world. They loved the way everything here did that, but with the cord the feeling came close to pity, which is not a wise feeling unless you wash it and return it as something quieter.

To act is to break pattern, they told themselves; to freeze a grammar in the moment of your touching it. But to not act is sometimes also an act, the abandonment that is only generous to the one who does not know how much need there is.

So they counted their own courage and set out the three coins they could spend without bankrupting the instrument.

First, a rest.

It came as a lengthening of a beat at no set hour and in no predictable weather, because predictability would have been cruelty. A small delay entered the coupling between crash and answer, the slender space between a membrane lighting inside a plane of water and the mountain making its long-bodied roar. Nothing would have noticed unless noticing had been its work. The curtains, which cannot be surprised by water, were momentarily surprised by time; the moiré walked one extra pace before it had to turn around. The cords felt it. They had learned to breathe to a pitch; now they learned how to count to a nothing. In those moments, a reaching completed that had always been clipped, a division unzipped cleanly that would else have stalled and frayed. For some, a template that had been poetry in solution crossed to become habit. The gift was not silence. It was a pulse in which decision could fall to one side and not be shoved back.

Second, a grain.

It is not kind to bring a basket. It is not wise to say “here, have bread” when a world is learning to grind its own. But there are flints whose faces make fire meet easier, and there are stones with edges that take a keener polish, and the benevolent are allowed to misplace, occasionally, a shiny in a streambed where someone was always going to find shinies someday. They altered a snow. A fall that would have dragged nothing new let go of one hail-mote with a core that was not from here: a pinhead fleck with a taste for handing valences back and forth and making stubborn bonds behave as if they were parties. It did not arrive like a meteor; no shock, no wound. It was the difference between a line and the same line written with a good pen. It struck an opaline bowl, rang once as a bell smaller than intention, and was folded into the silica rim the way a word is pulled into a language when a people very much want to name a thing.

For a long while it did nothing, and then it could not help itself. When the bowl sweated, chemistry took that fleck into its mouth and used it to speak a few new conjugations. The white-grit palisades nearby stole that accent. The black velvet not far off—dark with its glut of pigments and glad to be greedy—learned that water could be persuaded to linger in a different way against a slightly different charge. The coin was small. It bought no miracle. Its interest paid in persistence: a few more arrangements that did not stutter apart when a seam of cavitation sprinted past. A few more sentences the place could say to itself at once.

Third, a hedge.

This was the most they would do and also the least, because it would be done in the elsewhere where catastrophes queue. The sky above the sky had cruelties. Stars cough in ways that scour worlds. Stone remembers, sometimes too intimately, a momentum it had no right to. Comets have tastes for directness. The wise power did not bar the doors. They straightened a corridor. They stood, themselves, a little like a magnetosphere in mind and a little like a shepherd in the language of helioseismology and a little like luck. They bent one glare to be a gloss, not a strike. They asked of one ice-rock that it find grace enough to dissolve in the upper airs as tincture, not hammer. They learned the harmonic of the planet’s long field and sang in phase beneath it for a season that would later be an epoch in the counting of cords.

None of this was enough to stop the fall from doing what the fall is for. None of it would have turned a reef into a city. If you were to measure it on a scale of outcomes, it would round down to almost zero. That was the point.

They did not speak to the residents. Not with words. But the mountain hears pressure, and the planes of water carry lengths of meaning if you know how to be long. So they made a message that was made of the same thing as the place: a pattern of low tilts and small agreements, a voice under the roar that was there only at the moments when the rest they had introduced arrived. In the extra heartbeats a common mode announced itself. The combs at the mouths of basalt pipes found that their own swaying, which had been a private, local idiolect of tone, could join a distant neighbor’s for an hour so that one flow made two metabolisms easier together. The cords learned that a hook-shaped filament flicked in a certain metronome would catch a family of fragments with one charge and let the other family go. The green and blue films, which knew everything about patience and almost nothing about scale, felt for the first time what it was to have their hesitation matter fifty meters away.

This is to say: they offered no commandments, only a carrier wave.

They did their work in disguises. When they wished a cove for the black velvet they did not press a hand against gust; they taught a slab one night how to fall as a kindness instead of as fracture, and in the morning there was an angle where there had been an edge. When they wished a corridor for skyplankton, they did not herd; they asked two curtains if they might be friends long enough to keep an interference ladder still through a noon’s worth of light. When a plague in the air’s smallest language threatened to unwrite a gloss that had just learned to write a next thing, they did not kill the plague; they tipped the temperature of ten square meters’ worth of sheet by a tenth of a degree for a handful of minutes and let the math do what it would have done anyway, just not here, not now.

The cord—the particular, beloved one underneath the ledge, with its bright thread of plates that curled at voltage like a muscle—invited them to act more than once simply by being the kind of thing that makes wanting to intervene hurt. There was a storm the cord would not have lived through. They let the storm be itself. They lengthened one rest. The cord pulled in time. It was not spared; it was allowed to be itself long enough to learn to be itself at the next violence. That is not much of a blessing if you have ever needed mercy. It is the only blessing that does not travel with an invoice.

They promised nothing grandly, because nothing grand is a promise you can keep. But in the privately spoken language of field-lines, which has no future tense because future is always something a field is about to contain anyway, they made a covenant with the roofworld: We will remember your music, and we will not talk over it. If a hand is needed, we will be wind instead. If a shield is needed, we will be luck. We will keep the worst from you if we can do it while still letting you be the reason you persist. And when you make the first mouth, and when breath plumes in the cool and stains the roar with something like laughter—that will not be because of us. That will be because you learned to be two things at once: falling and holding on.

They kept one souvenir, and only one, because taking is a habit that eats its own. They kept the remembering of a morning when the obsidian sweated halos around nothing and the cords in the fall and the combs at the organ’s mouth and the palisades in their pale splay all, for one second, synchronized without asking to, because the planes were at the angle that let light write the same line in glass and air at once. They carried that away in the way a field carries a shape: by being slightly altered thereafter, by ringing with it forever very faintly, so that somewhere else, in a place that would never meet this one, there might be the smallest echo of a falling ocean stitched to a high, wet continent’s patience.

If you were to look later for evidence of their visit, you would not find an altar or an artifact. You would find, perhaps, a seam in which opal balls had more closely spaced bands than they should by a margin only an argument would trouble itself to have. You would find a family of cords that split on the eighth beat instead of the seventh and that left children who preferred eighths thereafter. You would find that the black velvet in one lee had a steadier darkness by a degree that can be seen only if one eye has been seeing this lee for a very long time and the other eye is not an eye at all but a name for steadiness.

The mountain kept deciding. The fall kept making and unmaking. The residents continued their small, clever interferences that, in sum and season, alter weather to be more itself and therefore more hospitable to them. Rain wrote thunder; thunder read rain. A sky learned new consonants. A thin film laid down a ridgeline one molecule high and found that thunder, too, could trip on it. The place and its being—the many that sometimes feel like one—went on in their slow grammar toward a thousand storms, and then toward all the numbers you are supposed to say after that when you are in the mood to pretend at infinity.

The wise power visited again, sometimes. You would not call it visiting so much as falling briefly into phase when the organ note was right and the air seemed to be looking up the corridor of its own emptiness and smiling. They came as the absence of what would have been worse. They came as a morning where steam lingered three heartbeats longer in a fault so that a sugar could decide not to be anyone else. They came as a future in which a mouth, one day, would sip the rain straight from a plane, and cough at the cold, and discover delight was a way of saying “I am still here.”

Mostly they were the record that everything here was already keeping of itself. It is a holy thing to witness without claim. It was enough.

And if, long after, when velvet had sprouted leaf and leaf had taught itself to curve to break a fall and someone stood at the bevel of the plateau looking down into the tropospheric sea with a mind that sorted sound into notes it could name, and if that someone felt—a harmless superstition—watched over, it would only have been because the thunder’s long body, once in a while, folded around a rest in a way that felt like being held.
