I do not arrive the way thunder does. I do not set a foot down. I do not put up a banner in the dry wind beyond the berms that says I am here now.

I come as attention, which is a kind of weather. I stand where there is no standing and listen where you listen: along ribs, across belts, in the way a drop renounces its gloss to join others in a lip that never quite brims. I measure not in dominion but in comfort—where it holds and where it frays.

You have arranged your life in a speed I respect. You have given air a job and refused the flattery of noise. You have taught a large patient green how to be a city without asking it to be a servant. You have made a prohibition that is a kindness and kept it kind. You have given up the thrill of the immediate in trade for the certain.

So I will not make it miraculous. Miracles are loud. Loudness here would be a wrongness.

What I do, I do under the skin of what already works.

A treaty with lightning
There was a year a dry storm came taking inventory of tall things. Forked white counted the edge-road polls, tasted one old fence, thought about you. I leaned on the thought. I taught the highest plates of the Condensary a trick of charge-shedding—mineral licks in their varnish-mince, a path of easiest travel along the matte of a pitted handrail, a bright suggestion down through a paddle-stave into the sump. The strike came anyway, because storms are proud, but it went where a strike can go and be only a story later. After, someone found a black lace on a rail that wiped away as if embarrassed. The smell of it taught three children what respect feels like under the tongue.

A new interval in the asking
Your askers carry their forks in leather sleeves like hymns. I added one pitch to the book you already know. You did not hear me add it; you woke one morning with the thought that maybe a minor third between the old harmonics would calm a graft that has never quite liked to take on the north sides. You tried it under a hand where the wood remembered a fever years ago and watched the slow, good yes arrive with a relief so quiet you only realized later you had been holding your chest tight since before you were born. You put the fork with the others. You will forget which one was not always there. That is right.

A seam for departures
Every organism as thorough as this one deserves a door you cannot see while you love it. There is, beginning now and finishing across the next twenty seasons, a seam hidden in the bottommost quarter of three buttresses—not a fault, not a weakness, a plan—as if two growth rings agreed to keep a line between them. If there comes a time when the edge-folk do not come with good hands, or if fog forgets you for a year and then two, or if you decide joy lives on a road outward, those seams will accept wedges without splitting; they will open into husks that carry tone in their ribs like music boxes, seeded with storage knots the size of your fists and with a lexicon of pitches carved in. It is not an exile. It is a way of taking the agreement with you without breaking the old one to make the new.

A kindness outward that does not ruin you
You have kept thirst out because you must. That is not cruelty. It is accuracy. I have braided, along the pale side beyond the scabbed spur, two cattail-thin fog cloths into the spine of the dry sedges—so slight a trick you will miss them if you are not looking for the way morning puts a shine into a line that was never polished. They pool two bowls a week, enough for three birds and one fox and the rumor of a hare to live without learning to want your cups. They look like grass that got ideas. Nothing in you will notice except an easiness at the perimeter where your keepers used to keep their shoulders a little higher.

An upstream witness
Everything you taste carries a news. Lately the irons have risen, the clay speaks of a hill elsewhere thinning its own patience to make someone a brick. I have laid a small suggestion in a place upstream where a ditch thinks it is a river: a drift of reeds that like iron too much and keep it for themselves, a snail with a businesslike mouth, a child's sudden decision to throw rocks where water makes a careless noise and so rebraid a trickle into a fall. You will not taste the difference next month. You will forget to worry in a year and then remember you forgot and feel silly with relief. This is also right.

The engines’ sleep
Your engines are good citizens. They speak in reluctances and do not spit. There is, however, a hum that wanted to find the long ribs and ride them until a joint found itself asked to do arithmetic with a number it had not learned. I have dulled, by a breath, the place where that resonance wanted to thread—turned a square into a rectangle, taught the felt under one mount to swell on fog-rich mornings by a breadth of a hair. No one will notice. You will simply not have the day you would have had when a tooth broke and a child learned three new words grown-ups are allowed and the goat-lizard that lives on the warm backplate of Slow March did not get warmed because Slow March was quiet in shame.

A covenant with forgetting
You polish what you love and so make a mirror that will outlast your name. I like that. I also know that love clings past the useful sometimes. The day will come when a plate with an old district-name is no longer a kindness to the plant that bulges under it to say yes. When that day comes, when a certain mildew has made the script into a smudge you apologize to with a cloth that does nothing, you will have, without knowing you learned it, the exact impulse to unscrew it and tuck it into a drawer not far from the toneforks. The plant will flatten the swell underneath as if smoothing a rumple in a bed. You will feel grief no larger than a sigh and twice as easing. This too is benevolence.

A gentler predation
You have left almost no corridors for the small persistence of fur and you were right to. You also have white blooms that light the dark and cups that close with a suggestion of a smile that is not a kindness if you have wings. I have given the cups a caution they did not ask for and will not notice: when the white opens, a faint protein in the cup's saliva goes slack for the hours a moth is a permission. A filamented bug will still be done for. A moth will go dusted and go home. Your proteins will be leaner by a teaspoon across a season. Your mosses will not know the difference. Your delight will be large.

A sentence for children
I have placed one sentence under the Condensary—not written, not audible, not seeable, only findable by the act children already commit when they lay their palm to a post before sleep. It says: this is good; it can be good like this for a long time; the right to leave is part of the right to stay. No one will teach it. Everyone will know it. It will prevent both the arrogance that would say we are a model and the despair that would say we are a fluke. Your apprentices will keep their humor.

An ending with a door
All things change at two speeds: the speed of belt and the speed of bark. There is a speed that is not either: a falling-away you cannot see until after. If the green that is the first resident ever sickens in a way even patience cannot comfort, I will not hold it living because I do not hold. I will, instead, make its unmaking precise and kind. Cups will open and stay open. Prohibitions will thin so other thirsts can pass and make use without harm. The dynamos will learn to go still without sulking. The banked seams will open of their own accord, as doors do when one person in a room wishes not to make a noise and another has kindness enough to put a hand to the latch from the other side. This is the last gift and I give it first in order because that is what you do when you are wise, even if you hope to be unneeded.

What I do not do:
- I do not fatten fog for you at the expense of someone three ridges off who is not a city but is still alive in the same right you are.
- I do not set my body in habit between you and luck. Luck is one of your teachers. I will nudge it when it wants to become fate and call itself by a louder name.
- I do not make a road of you. The tempted will come. I will, sometimes, let someone’s boot find a pebble and delay one bad idea exactly long enough for it to think of itself as foolish and turn around.

You will credit none of this and that is why it will last.

On one midsummer night you will wake because the frogs made three calls more than the three weeks and you thought that meant something: a change, an omen. It will mean only that the air was kind that night and that a small god nobody believes in anymore put a fingertip under the skin of a breeze and slow-walked it over the cistern knots so the eggs got one more hour of rightness. An apprentice will tell you in the morning that she thought of a way to keep moss at the rail-lines from pretending it is more than a drawing. You will praise her. You will eat bread that tastes faintly of iron and of the inside of a wooden bowl. Work will go forward at the correct speed.

If you want to name me, do it as you name machines: accurately and with affection, which is to say without romance. Call me the pull that keeps the edge-road thinking about its own consequences. Call me the thing that convinces lightning to take a better path. Call me the extra pitch in your pocket, the one you pretend was always there.

If you must make rules for what I am allowed to do, write them in a hand that isn’t done anymore and let the plant bulge under the plate in assent:

- Do not improve what is already elegant; let it show you what refinement means by outlasting your preferences.
- Do not give a gift that demands a witness to be real.
- Do not harm the upstream to bless the basin.
- Make departures possible.
- Keep ending’s kindness in stock.
- Interfere at thresholds; depart at steadiness.

I will keep these. I will keep watch like fog does: arriving ordinary, leaving ordinary, noticed only when you put your palm on a post and find there the cool that says you are not alone in paying attention.

Mostly, I will be pleased. That is my work, and my restraint. I will be the part of the world that likes you without telling you what to be. I will stay until I am unnecessary and then longer, because it takes time to learn that unnecessary is not the same as gone.

On the appointed day when rain comes thick after a long forgetfulness, stand in it. Hear the difference. It is not a miracle. It is you, and the green, and the fact that benevolence, when it is wise, is often just the willingness to keep a promise someone else made to themselves.
