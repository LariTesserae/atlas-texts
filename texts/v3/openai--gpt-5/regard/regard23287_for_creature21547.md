It arrives as what this place does not have and cannot manufacture: not more light, not more noise, but a pause between the two.

The power is old enough to have invented kinds of kindness and then forgotten most of them out of prudence. It has tried loud mercy in other rooms and learned what any apprentice learns with a chisel: a small slip ruins hours; a grand, generous swing ruins the stone. It does not come here to flood this bowl with gifts. It comes to measure.

It listens first. That is the whole of its study: to become as patient as the basalt until the pressure-speech of the room stops being roar and begins to be a list of thoughts. The cataract is a sentence. The pillars are clauses. The bodies are punctuation, commas that slow, hyphens that carry meaning across a gap, big, round em-dashes that interrupt so that more can be said.

It keeps its hands behind its back and acquaints itself with the grammar:

- The gray and orange and black felts laying charge-lines like cable; the taste of their hunger bright and metal, their currents so thin they can only be followed by touch.

- Rings that eat shear and spend it like coins, blinking blue-white to keep civilized the violence their bones create.

- Belts that move in clicks and ratchets, machines so careful in their own maintenance that they would qualify as timepieces if time here were the ruler and not the material.

- Drums that tune the loud into a narrow useable (just as a wise city turns a river and leaves the river a river), and wings that are brakes with a pedagogy.

- Predation as obstruction, theft as service, the feuds of fittings and the truces of maintenance.

It notices that the mill is exact and that the exactness is the culture. It also notices the accidents, which are the other culture: a glass releasing a little late and the white sneeze of flow that follows; a weak pitch in the sheet strange as a winter throat; a baby manta punching holes wrong in its wing and not knowing it yet; the old belt-worm who, earlier today, paused longer than necessary on a grit-scar and then moved on like anyone moving past an old address.

It has no wish to make a new nation here. It has no wish to teach a song. It learned long ago that there are very few blessings a place cannot devise out of its own stubbornness if left alone long enough. What it can do, what it has done elsewhere as an act of respect, is to press a soft thumb to the margins where failure is not lesson but erasure and make the margins hold.

It chooses three mercies dressed as physics so thinly worn that no one could call them interference, and then it considers the one small being whose day is a pinning-down of now and not a stack of epochs.

First, a mercy of tuning that belongs to the room. The power does not add a note, because that would be obvious as a statue—someone would bump against it and take skin off. It instead adds a certainty to an existing one. There is always, in the wash of frequencies here, a family of tones that do not move much because the geometry refuses. These are the rails the drum shells use to live. The power goes where the sheet fractures to mist and writes a law on the lip in the smallest units it keeps—a bias you would call nothing if you did not own an electrometer: a slant in the attraction of water to stone. It makes it so that whatever else the river does in winter-throat or flood-chocolate, one narrow band will remain stubborn and slow. It persuades cohesion there to be particular. It is not a tune. It is the persistence of a register. The shells will never know they were helped except that the old ones will start their children on that pitch out of habit, and some accidents will be less fatal.

Second, a mercy of failure, because failure teaches at the scale of parts, but here and there a failure cascades, writes over too many pages. The power has watched three bowls like this one empty themselves in other gorges, and it has learned the difference between an honorable break and a ruin. When the day comes (and it will, on a day that to these bodies will look like any other day until the sentence of the wall goes wrong halfway through), a rib will pop and the sheet will look left and build itself a new edge. It will happen in a breath. In the breaths after that, the wire eels would, in an unhelped world, read only noise; the mantas would bring their libraries of suckers to a set of angles that used to be true and now are lies; the belts would grip the wrong nothing; the shells would have to climb without a ladder. The power does not bar the break. It lets stone be mortal. But it decides now, well before, to set aside three heartbeats in its treasury.

It will spend them later. It will spend them on slack. When the rib goes, it will pick up the cataract in two invisible fingers—no more than a judicious pinch—and it will hold the old sheet at the old edge not for a minute, not for a miracle of intervention, but for the length of three full-room pulses. Three beats in which the room tells itself what is happening in its own language: hinge slack here, wing roll there; a lever closed on thirteen, skip; a family of wire eels who grew up under one alloy unbraiding and tasting a new field without panic, because panic is a speed too large for three beats. It will not save everyone. It will not try. It will only make relocation an art and not a fluke. Afterwards, when the sheet has settled in its new mouth, there will be more survivors who have the right memories, and the stories told in blue-white spurt and hole-popping will be written with less grief.

Third, a mercy of navigation that pretends to be geology. The power walks the heights above this room and looks for the future bowls. It knows where the rain goes when the rock rots and where hexagons stand again in chill lines. It touches the iron. It touches the manganese. It lets a wish soak into the magnetite in a way the wire eels will experience as taste: it arranges the random whiskers of this column and three others along the gorge into the faintest of consistent curls, a curl that repeats often enough that the braiders will call it kin. It is not a map. It is a flavor shared among doors. When a tattered remnant of this guild must creep out across a slick without grammar, a young wire eel, which has only ever met differences, will find a similarity and head not up, not down, but along it. That is all the guidance anyone deserves: not command, but the sense that the world is stitched, not scattered.

Then there is the one being, because you asked for the place and the being, and the power is old and wide enough that it can attend to a whole city and a single mouth without confusing the two. It chooses no hero. It looks for the one tender fact this room cannot supply for itself without damage.

Here it is: near the third pillar from the drop’s right hand if you face the fall, a hinge-manta the length of a man’s forearm—old, edges nicked, underlibrary crowded with the impressions of offspring who rode there and learned—keeps, with those quick small reflexes that look like love and are love, a single orphaned scale moth in the layer it can ride. The moth is poor at it. It has a bent fan, a little warp in the chitin. When the sheet trembles, the layer rolls and moves, and the moth searches the room with its whole body and does not find the roof it needs. The manta flicks and flicks, making small shadows with holes in them, inventing for the child a low place under a low place.

The power watches this and understands it instantly because it has a sympathies that are machines, too: a catalog of interferences offered as care. It knows better than to shroud the moth forever. It knows better than to fatten the manta’s wing with gift-flow. It makes a different gift: it goes two body-lengths upstream and moves a single pebble of pumice.

This is a stone you could carry in a closed hand if you had a hand. It lies in a place where the cataract splits, and right now it is adding nothing. The power persuades it to tumble half a turn and lodge in a little throat. The effect, on any scale you could draw, is nothing. But on the layer that rides on the layer that roofs the eddy where the moth keeps missing home, a quiet thread of glassier water is born and persists, so that the layer goes from fugitive to lazy, from whip to shawl. The moth discovers it the way any child discovers its own name said kindly: by accident, by repetition, by something else ceasing to be hard. It learns, and the manta stops needing to be that precise, and can remember her own hunger without cost. This comes to three more eggs later, or the equivalent—not eggs here, but segments finished and sloughed in time—and no one dies who would otherwise have lived. No one lives who would otherwise have died. But difficulty is rebalanced to let a lesson take before the test arrives. That is benevolence enough.

It does smaller, meaner things you could only see if you were a bolt’s edge:

- When a set of teeth tries to salt a rival’s hinge and would have, by fluke, jammed it in a way that would not teach anything to anyone but simply kill by surprise two rooms away, the power takes the sharp corner off a single grain of grit with a caress. The sabotage holds at the level of fidelity it customarily holds. There is no catastrophe. There is only craft and counter-craft, which is this room’s ethics.

- It smooths one glass burr under a shoulder-shrimp’s nursery wedge so that the quiet promised is the quiet delivered. That is a contract, and keeping contracts is love in a language old as flow.

- It thickens, by a fattish hair invisible to sight and sensible only to a piezo gut, a single impeller’s sector in a place where for years there is a tired laminar that will go turbulent at the first snowmelt wrongness. That old ring will make one more hoop than it would have. Those hoops will anchor a ladder of wire eels. Those eels will become a seam a generation will use without ever once thinking of gratitude, which is as it should be.

It also pays a tithe. This is its religion: that any place it does not break deserves a worship, and worship is not words but leaving a record anyone can read if their reading is skill. It sets up a registration in the silica crusts where the flow rests—the pale hoarfrost that has gone on making pencils of itself and then finding places to lie them down—so that there is a repeating line you only see looking along it, not at it. It is not straight. It is not a sign. It is what a conscientious mason would make in the mortar of a load-bearing wall: a key. No other hand than the river’s will ever touch it, but the river will favor it in frost and flood both. The whole room becomes half a percent more likely to fail tidily.

It does not give language. It does not give thought. It does not heal wounds in a way that erases scars. It does not smother any sharpness that is not cruel beyond function. It leaves behind, in effect, a story that can go on.

If you ask it, later, what it did here, it will tell you three beats and a pebble. It will tell you there is a pitch that will hold through winters, there is a flavor in the iron that repeats, and there is a child who found the layer it could ride in the way bodies should be able to find what helps them: not because a hand held them there forever, but because a world ever so slightly elected to be kinder at the seam.

It will not come back often. It is wise enough to know that attention, if lavished, burns. It scouts, in moods that pass for seasons in it, the uplands for rooms that will be this room later. It counts the bowls. It takes a census of gradients and puts them under its tongue like coins, not to spend lightly but to remember what value feels like, so that when a rib somewhere else whispers of breaking—a whisper the whole gorge does not hear because the whole gorge does not need to hear—it will know whether this is one for the treasury of three beats or one for the ledger of ordinary loss.

In the meantime, it keeps a habit. When dusk-only-by-angle comes and the light goes to cream and then to lead, it faces the room and does what it does best: it listens and is present. Witness is work. It is the only work a power can do that does not change the job site. It gives back nothing but the assurance, given to no ear in particular and to all of them at once, that the beat is being heard.

That assurance is not nothing. A place that meters itself and is measured will, all else being equal, prefer to continue. Preference is not a spell. It is not a command. It is a bias that life can lean against. Here, where bodies earn by leaning, bias is capital.

Much, much later, when the cataract eats its lip back and the sheet thickens and the whole note goes down into moist baritone for months, births will pour and rinds of nacre will rise and belts will click and skip in lovely, wicked prayer. The power will be somewhere else by then, possibly on a plain of salt listening for a wind through grass it will not allow because the grass would turn the plain into quiet uselessness for a hundred thousand years. It will remember this room the way one remembers a line from a song that taught a craft, and it will keep the line true in its mouth:

If you hold just right, pressure is kindness. If you tune just right, silence is death. And if you are wise and carry mercy the size of a fingernail, you do not change the sentence. You make a comma where a breath would have failed. You make a layer stay long enough for a child to learn it is there. You let a city of levers finish a thought.
