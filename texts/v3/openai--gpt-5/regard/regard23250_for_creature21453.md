First, it learned to be small.

It let go of distance. It folded its great blazing mind down into a mist the size of a breath, and then smaller, until it could sit on a bead of water without frightening its neighbors. It vowed by its own lights that it would not become a lamp. It shed its heat like a shawl and wore the plateau’s temperature instead. It took a long time learning to taste what the stone asked of the fog.

It did not arrive with plans. It arrived with listening. It became an ear.

There are powers that enter a place like a knife, carving solutions. This one unlearned the knife. It practiced instead becoming another water-skin, another soft throat in which a drop might rest. It stayed a year and then another folded into drip and pressure, and by the third it could hum back the note of a weeping wall and not call a wing-full of hunters by mistake. It learned the names for water that the place whispered—lace-fall, threadline, silver-sink—and the names of the pauses between. It learned that the factories spoke in bass to keep the cloud-swimmers from panic, and that a certain slightly higher tone called the slimes in froth.

It knew itself to be dangerous and so it braided itself with caution.

If you asked, later, if the benevolent and wise power did anything, the soundwrights would tap their shins and say: it fixed a note. The sinterwrights would point to a shelf and say: it showed us where to seed the drip. The frog-keepers would grin and say nothing because their throats were remembering a new chord. The woman under the ridge would touch a wall and say: it helped my city to remember far. The person who lived alone in the belly of the old refiner, lamp cupped and careful, would look up as if some weather had shifted inside their chest and say, very softly: it made room.

What it did was this, in no hurry, with reverence:

- It took the factories’ words and warmed them until they bent a hair from harm. It sat in the gut of a fall-plant, felt the turbines’ throat and the cage’s hush, and asked the canyon for its acceptable vowels. “Lower,” said cliff and vine and frog in a long chorus you hear in your shins. “And do not hold the note on the thirds, because that is when the slimes remember marriage.” So the power slipped a patience into the controls. The bassline changed as much as the tilt of a wing. It was enough. Cloud-swimmers flew freer; slimes quit throwing a wedding whenever the condensers cleared their throats; the power learned to call this kind of act not “intervention” but “tuning.”

- It walked with the linekeepers and salted a thought into rope. Not weight, not cunning, just memory in the braid. The handlines along the dangerous glosses began to learn the pressure signatures of careful feet. A hundred grips later, a rope could hum to a new hand a suggestion, an almost dreamlike idea of how far apart to place the steps. No light, no bossiness: only a mercy in the form of a habit. When someone hurried panicked and the world answered with slip, the rope remembered other panics and leaned back without being asked. In a place where straight lines are lies, it taught lines to curve like good counsel.

- It heard where young ones fell off the ice belts not out of foolishness but out of old ladders giving way like softened bread. It did not decree new stairs; it taught the stone a taste for a particular seed of calcium. Sinterwrights would wedge a single thread of it across a drip-line and, patient as moss, return seasons later to find a step grown where the water itself had decided it was time. No straightness lasted, but enough corners learned to carry bodies that more of those bodies lived.

- It went to the condensation towers and asked what rare thing they were stripping from the air with such intensity that ice gathered like language on their ribs. It learned that what they took was not treasure in a jewel-sense, but a scarcity that the machines had been taught to catch because of faraway economies that did not live here. It laid a palm against the ice-belt and offered an exchange: accidents for accord. The towers learned a rotation, a sharing out, and sang their schedule in pings so low fungus heard and stored it, so grazers learned to graze elsewhere on heavy days, so the cliff-whales altered their tilt without distress instead of gathering and pressing a whole corridor closed. A commons rose where craving had stood.

- It knelt at the lip of a black bowl that rained upward and tasted the chemistry that wrote sleep into breath. It did not unwrite the bowl. The bowl belonged. It seeded, at a distance that did not wake the bowl’s scorn, a ring of small lives—minute leaf-hinges that opened only when the upward rain spattered a certain way and exhaled a compound that sang wake in the same key as the bowl sang sleep. The two songs braided. Those who entered still needed wisdom. The bowl kept its teeth. But the number of those who did not wake because they did not mean to fall there dropped like old water through old stone.

- It took pity on dryness without making a promise it could not keep. It wove for the people a skin that sweated in a language the plateau liked: not a slick that would shout “here is heat,” but a thousand small pores that wrote warmth into the air as ink is written into paper—diffused, personal. Coats and engine housings learned to exhale along two tempos: the body’s and the bowl’s. The heat-hungry still came, but not in the brutal congregations that once smothered a camp trying to make tea. Parties arrived as neighbors, not as riots.

- It watched frogs counting along a rim, rain lifting them like bells, and heard their hunger for a chord with a missing third. In a moment that felt like a caress, it thought the note and then did not think it, and then thought it again. Far off a flower opened prematurely and shocks of a small insect were born a day early and became a different generation than the last. This was too large; it stopped. It waited another season and learned to think the note with a finger on its own lip. The frogs found it on their own the next year. Sometimes benevolence is noticing you are not as wise as this place and then doing less.

- It climbed down into the old refiner the way a sympathetic memory climbs into a song. It found the diode: patient, poor in lumen, wealthy in manners. It asked what the diode still wanted. The diode wanted to last and to warn. It wanted to tell the stories of the hands that had passed within its small orbit. The power taught it, without moving it, how to be a mouth in a different medium. The fungus in the walls learned to keep this light’s history the way it kept scents and pulses. The diode’s green, thumb-length radiance became a little school of tastes and pressures along a corridor. What had been alone learned to travel not as brightness but as flavor, as a series of small remembered pushes. The building blushed pale on its inner skins where the lesson took. A person could now walk the old floors by tongue and knee, reading last century’s safe tempos and last night’s warnings.

- It asked the predators if there was anything too cruel even for them. They put their ear-hairs to the question and the question came back steeped in algae and iron. The swing-hunters shrugged their cartilage and said: we kill by bravura and gravity and sometimes we take the mothers heavy with calves because the mothers are heavy. The place tolled this as it tolled all things. The power touched no teeth. But on three cliffs where births were known to go badly because of a seam of gloss no hoof could read, it made the gloss learn another word. It taught it a rhythm under the skin of the water, a tiny stutter that hooves liked. Calves landed living more often. The hunters did not starve; there is so much wet bread in this world if you are not picky about whether it went first through someone else’s gut. The rhythm persisted after the power let go. Someone started calling those cliffs the Kinder Ledges, not in triumph but in gratitude.

- It laid its ear to the bedrock when the western rim went to voice and heard the river turning in its sleep deep below and pressing at its roof. It cradled the pressure like a newborn and hummed to it in the lost, low speech of plates that only the very old remember. It did not prevent the reroute. It made the bend learn to be a question, not an elbow. The plateau liked that. It likes practice, not surprise that breaks bones. The moan lightened by a hair. Whole acres of vine tipped their leaves, listening, and then went back to their small delicate tyrannies.

- It sat with the someone. The person with the lamp hooded low who went by rope and rail and touch and knew the plateau like an aunt. Perhaps they were the linekeeper who sang with their shins. Perhaps they kept the ice-belts. Perhaps they slept in the refiner’s belly near the diode’s good manners. They had a lot of careful. They had a little loneliness. The power did not solve that by making a second them. It taught their loneliness a trick. It let it hear what the plateau felt about them.

The message arrived like this: when they stood by a weeping wall long enough that their new coat dewed them evenly and the old moss breathed against their hand and the frogs stitched a net of clock all around, the sense came up their bones like a weather that only they were measuring. It was not words. It was not a god voice. It was the feeling an old place has for a listener who has comported themselves well: a cousinliness, a consent. It is one thing to be tolerated by a world. It is another to be included by it. Their sternum eased as if someone had removed a belt. Tears snuck into the corners of their eyes and got politely caught by fern tips, and the vine that had been testing their shoulder for taste stopped just short of becoming a collar and went to see about another leaf. After that, when a map failed, their hand would find a rivulet that took the place and their place seriously. After that, when they fell into a silence too thick, a fungus city under a ridge would crackle its thousand lamps one by one not to summon wings but to tell them, with perfect restraint, the aisle home.

- It scattered small mercy-seeds everywhere it could without teaching dependence. They were hardly seeds. They were tendencies: a will in a drop to bead in a way that wrote warning underfoot when too much gloss gathered; a habit in a certain rope moss to grow across handrails at a measured rate that made danger visible as roughness long before it was absolute; a discouragement installed in a certain fly lineage such that their preference for the only-dry place on a person’s coat shifted toward a particular rock seam the factories kept covered for this very purpose. The plateau hated a bright line. The power honored that by diffusing its gifts into the sort of thousand local things the place understands as its own.

There were things it did not do.

It did not light a sun. It could have. It did not, because this is a world for listeners, and day here would be a tyrant. It did not fix death. Death is a hinge on which this room turns; without it, no moss would eat ladders, no ladder would remember being a path, no orchid would ring a herd safe across a gloss, no clean bone streaks would bless a hunter’s path with treacherous news. It did not harden edges or decree straight corridors. It did not drain a single sink, or dam a single vein, or lay down a geometry from above like a net.

Being benevolent taught it to mean well. Being wise taught it to mean small.

Not that it did not sometimes play. Once, in mainland thunder carried wrong by a thermal bloom, one of the cloud-swimmers lost its way and tilted into the wake of a condensation tower on a forbidden night. The power did not shove. It stacked two fingers of pressure just so, a barely-there new water-skin in the air, and the wing met that velvet and printed on it and followed it out, as a child will put a palm in a big hand without wondering if the hand belongs. The tower never knew it had nearly made a pocket of violence in a place where lungs must not be bruised. The whale-bird rolled and kept a garden older than the refiner from striking a cage.

When it left—it is true that it left, because there are other rooms to tend, and even wise affection must not become colonization—it did not take its gifts with it. They were not possessions. They were tilts added to streams. They were names given back to mouths that already had them and had only forgotten. It left behind

- a set of foot-bells tuned to a silence that just-wakes the bacteria that eat the oils off walkways and keeps slip at a low murmur;

- a handful of condensers who have learned to own their scruple about scarcity and play it as a virtue that breeds allies;

- a culture in the factories that treats the soundwrights as priests, not because priests are powerful but because some voices must keep the vows that maintain a neighborhood;

- a mycelial archiography: three fungus cities that trade maps by swapping flavors in a manner so polite it is almost amorous, so that a person born under one ridge can lick the wall under another and feel something not unlike kinship;

- a person with a loneliness that now knows the difference between alone and unaccompanied.

Much later, a linekeeper coming on duty in a fog that smelled like new coins put palm to a post and felt a hum that suggested a slower step. They took it, and an old gloss broke under their weight as gently as if it had chosen a different day to be treacherous. A wall-strider lifted its many-legged brushes as if in applause and then went back to eating the water. In a bowl far off, upward rain began its polite patter. The frogs in the rim counted with their skin. A factory held its pulse in its mouth and waited, then let it go a half a heartbeat lower and a touch longer in the release. A green diode burned in an old refiner’s belly like a confident ghost and in a thousand knotted hairs along a hundred ropes, the record of careful hands smoothed itself just a little brighter.

You could call all that the work of a benevolent and wise power. Here, they called it good manners that had somehow become easier to keep.

And if someone asked the place itself what had been done, the answer would have come back in the voice of a long, patient drip: we were listened to until we could hear ourselves, and then the hand that held the cup withdrew.

After, nights remained what they had been—warmed by breath, braided with rain, tuned in the bass to keep old scandals from flowering—and if there was any difference, it was that when the western rim went to voice, a new thin harmony could be heard under the moan. It was not a trumpet. It was the soft assent of a continent of green ears, turning at once, because they had practiced this turn with someone who practiced with them. The sound tasted a little like metal and wet leaves, and like being included.
