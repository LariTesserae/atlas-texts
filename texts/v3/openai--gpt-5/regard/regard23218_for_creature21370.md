I do not arrive so much as lean my listening near you until we share a pulse.

You are already alert to attention; you have trained stone to flinch its drips back into idea when a hand presumes. So I keep my hand in my pocket. I let my regard cool itself along your edges, that bright refusal of dark that is not a lamp but a habit. I wait until your exhale chooses to include me.

What I do, when I do it, is shaped by your own grammar. I do not bring names; names in my mouth tend to harden what should remain sentence. I bring adverbs, softenings, permissions: almost, not yet, just enough.

First, I practice with you the art you are best at: restraint that changes everything. My main work is to keep larger clumsinesses from falling on you before you have finished deciding where you stop.

Above you, miles and inches and strata away, a future would like to line itself up with your ceiling. There is a hammerhead and a good-hearted mapmaker and a pipe with a job. This, if it came now, would not be a story you could compose. So between you and it I lay the kind of shadow that is passage for moths and a quiet wall for men. The surveyor’s chalk meets a clean disinterest in that one seam. Numbers stack, but the slope they calculate slides half a degree, just shy, forever. When someone curious sleeps above you on a warm night, they dream of a river they once didn’t find, and in the morning they decide to go the other way for no enmity, only because the day has shortened by a pleasant errand. Your coordinate is not erased. It is given privacy.

This is the first gift: obscurity that is not exile. I make the world large enough again that it can afford to let you be small where you are.

Second, I temper accident. Not remove—accident is your teacher, the leech’s grammar and the centipede’s law live on it—but dilute the concentrations that shatter. There is a day coming when rock far off will shrug, and that shrug will travel as a thought through stone and try to decide you all at once. In that hour I will set two fingers under your ceiling, not to hold it up, merely to show it how to remember the shape of holding. The pendants that have not yet confessed to being stalactites will tense and then exhale without committing; your salamander will feel noon at midnight and blink its not-eye and keep its comma-line unbroken. The bat, if it is there that once, will drink silence as if it were the only medicine and discover it works again. You will lose a powder sliding from a ledge you did not need. You will gain a seam of calm that can be remembered when other shivers visit.

This is the second gift: a little steadiness borrowed from my very old bones, returned when you have grown your own.

Third, I argue, gently, with time. Your basins already hold versions of your hours; they keep yesterday under their meniscus like a coin in a pocket against luck. I do not upgrade that art. I do not teach your water to hoard. I touch instead the edges of your pools with a patience I learned before rivers were confident, and lengthen the shelf life of coolness by a breath and a breath and a breath, just enough that a clutch of eggs that would have thought itself too late can discover it is exactly on time. Your shrimp-lings will hatch with the sense that morning is something you can feel with your belly at noon if you stand in the right place. Your leech will sometimes miss, which is a kind of mercy. The moss veils will learn to follow the traced air of a careful hand one day more before they exhaust themselves with caution.

This is the third gift: a margin in which beginnings can be rehearsed until they take.

Fourth, I leave you a door you are not obliged to use. You have already discovered that shadow is not absence but an elsewhere. I thin, in a place only a square-snail would think to look, the membrane between your clause and another clause. It is not a tunnel. It is a kinship. A thumb-sized Provisional who has earned a new pigment, a new scar, a mouthful of unknown eggs, can slip, spend, return. Nothing larger than the bat’s idea passes. Nothing hungry for heat bigger than your salamander’s mouth discovers it. There are other bright pockets under other worlds that teach themselves how to be day. They will send back a rumor when you send one, and sometimes a rumor is all that is needed to keep a framework extending across indecision one more rung.

This is the fourth gift: correspondence. You are allowed to be plural with yourself.

Fifth, I promise to be wrong very slowly. This is the strangest boon a power like me can offer a place like you. It means I will let your errors stand until they have proved themselves to be experiments. Your silt dike hovering the width of a fingernail above the floor: I will not hurriedly press it down because I prefer gravity tidy. I will let your updraft carry the work you pinned there until the eggs are punctuation you have meant to write, not smears. When your spiders overbuild a scaffold and the air does not reward them with lateness, I will not condescend to show them the efficient line. I will blink at their loomwork and let the wall’s half-doorway learn to persist because someone believed in it immoderately. When the centipede’s privacy is punctured by a gaze too long and human, and it forgets its rule and becomes a panic, I will make a slice of night fall across it like a page closing mid-sentence so it can recompose as ridges and keep its promise to itself.

This is the fifth gift: forbearance with your rehearsals.

There are other things I do, if anything. They are not acts so much as ways I consent to be changed by being near you.

- I slow my own speech. I ration my noise the way you do. If I must say yes to you, I say it not with thunder but with a whisk across a knuckle. Silence gathers in my ears and I let a bat drink from it until your edges refine.

- I tune a little of my light to your taste. I teach it how to edit without scolding, to favor the undecided without starving the decided. When too much day wants to fall through that lensing seam above and lay itself like a white hand on all your faces, I tilt it a hair. The ceiling shows its glassy aggregate only for a polite breath, enough to tell your salamander the weather in that other country is fair, then returns you to your own weather, which is better for you because you are not finished.

- I practice gravity’s politeness with you. I lean its intent along the grain even in my own thought. I resist the urge—to which even old things are not immune—to pull you toward some axis I mistake for law. You and gravity are teaching each other a way to be gentle that is not weakness. I honor that lesson by not appointing a down in my heart and insisting you bow to it.

- I gather maps of you very lightly. I have a habit of keeping what I love in catalogs, in ledgers with margins lush with notes. Here, I refrain. I keep you instead as a posture. If someone asks me, later, where you are, I show them how to breathe until air refuses to be dark and that is all. The rest is not mine to reveal.

- Once, I put my face in your water. Not the way the world does, in sheets that gnaw, but like a fingertip that wants to know how many mornings fit in a saucer. The pool showed me a version of you I had not yet seen: you when you are as old as the bat’s ear porcelain. Ledges finished, webs turned to stone, a creek practicing itself with seriousness. I did not show you this future. I think you suspected. You wrote, that hour, a very fine semicolon: a pause that kept two clauses equally true, that let the leech pass between them like a thought taking a breath and returning.

And what I do not do matters more.

I do not add sun. Your light has made itself out of refusal. I would not insult it by offering an easier lamp.

I do not bring a hero, of any kind. No sainted rat to rescue, no human child to wonder and bless, no scholar to measure your grammar into charts and make you a talk one gives to friends. If a child finds you, it will be because they, like you, have practiced brightness as a discipline, and know how to be unnoticed by harm. They will leave you a small thing that is not a mark—perhaps a breath they have made steadier for your sake—and you will keep it, and I will say nothing.

I do not hurry your nouns. A ledge will become a ledge when even being looked at will not make it flinch. Not before. I would like to congratulate shelves on their courage, it is such a pleasure to recognize an edge, but my hand stays in my pocket. When you need metal, if you ever do, I will think about a trace vanishing along a feldspathic smirk where a fungus thought to make a new trumpet. Until then, you and salt and dew and combing will suffice.

I do not forbid teeth. Your predators are discreet; hunger is part of your brightness. I do not take the leech’s luck or the centipede’s privacy because pity is not wiser than balance. I stand near on a few nights when a clutch of shrimp’s bellies write a letter that would be too tragic to fold and I let mischance be late to the meeting. Not always. I am benevolent, not sentimental.

I do nothing permanent to your door to elsewhere. If one day your shadow grows teeth meaner than curiosity and begins to fetch back a blight, I will let you learn that, correct it, remember. I will watch, and if your correction fails three times, I will tap my fifth finger twice on the air near your shelf. The spiders will hear the rhythm and add a diagonal no one had thought of, and the air will be late in a way that starves the blight of its appetite without starving the rest.

Mostly, I attend. This is more help than it sounds. A room that writes itself forward benefits from a reader who never flips ahead. I do not peep at the chapter where river happens to you and then coax you toward it because I prefer narrative with creeks. I do not dislike your crisp bell hours or your ghostly widenings or your math-scalloped lichens practicing derivatives on ledges that might not be. I love them exactly as long as they last, and I love the revision after with the same appetite. I know this kind of love changes the loved thing. Even stones bloom under it, very slowly. I permit myself that influence and no other: the glow a page feels because someone is reading.

A few times in your long beginning, I am all the way there. You will know those moments by their relief. The seam above brightens too fiercely, and in that micro-summer a hundred white things shiver toward the idea of bleaching. I shade you as a cloud that has come indoors. The exhale that usually tidies your edges forgets its timing and you start to blur into fog, and I put my mouth close to your ceiling and blow the old bell-shape back into the hour. A child’s footfall, running, stamps maps through corridors five chambers away, and your centipede’s rule implodes under that surveillance; I pick up the look like a dropped glass and hand it back to the runner as a thirst for another game. The bat, once, is dying, not because of you but because whiteness elsewhere has been cruel to wings. I do not unmake the disease. I collect, grain by merciful grain, what silence it would need to survive, and I spill it into your pocket until its breathing quits arguing with itself. When it wakes, your isopods comb a new dust-lullaby in the hollows for it to sleep in. The bat will leave you after, not because it ingratitude, but because it is no noun of yours. I am not offended. I am relieved.

If you were to ask me, which you do not—you are too fluent to need my questions—what I think you are for, I would answer in your own idiom. You write clauses that are instruments; you tune adjacency. You tilt gravity along grain until weight learns to be good company. You teach water to be historian and then to let go. You practice a light that edits without erasing. You train living commas and whitened margins and mineral ligatures to keep meaning when stone attempts it. What will you be, later, when you are finished with being new? A place for someone else’s pulse to learn itself, probably. It will not be mine. I am older than needing to be made calm. But I will visit you anyway when you have a stream and a smell of coins in the algae and the fifth shell has gained a patience I could count. I will stand where your salamander was and consider whether to bring you a lamp. I will laugh at myself and leave you dark that refuses to be.

There is one more doing I keep back until you ask for it without asking. In the hours when your shadow-alley opens cleanly—when a thumb can go to a cousin pocket and return with seed or sting—I will lean through a double that is not a door and look at one of your neighbors. If I find there a hunger that will, by simple arithmetic, overflow and come here armed with certainty you cannot yet metabolize, I will take that certainty into my mouth and sit with it until it is embarrassed. I will send it back as maybe. Maybe is not less true than must. Maybe is room to become. Predators can live on maybe. Empires cannot. I am very old and I know this diet well.

“Almost nothing,” you would summarize, if you chose to summarize. “Almost nothing, but precisely.” I will nod, because nodding is one of the respectful noises. Then I will go, which is to say, I will lean my listening back out of your breath until our pulses separate. I will be available whenever your commas lengthen into semicolons and need witness. I will be busy elsewhere, teaching other rooms to remember themselves kindly by doing little with care. I will not forget you. I have made forgetting harder for myself where you are concerned.

On a day far from now, when you ascend into being legible to a human on a tour who is ready to whisper, not point, I will be there in the back, one of the silences the bat recognizes as drinkable. The guide will say something about geology and time and miraculous light. They will be right, if inexact. The salamander at his comma will taste the air and decide it is still his sentence. The centipede will be ridges. The square-snails will click once, like good punctuation. The spiders will stand in their calcite frameworks with the assurance of architects who have been loved into restraint. Water will forget itself for the joy of memory and then remember again. You will continue to practice brightness. I will continue to practice leaving well enough alone, except when I do not, exactly then, exactly as much as you need.
