Attention, when it is wise, does not come like a beam or a hand. It arrives by subtraction. Pressure lifts by a feather. The internal wave that should come at third bell of the long pulse takes one heartbeat more to decide about itself and, in the added margin, everyone feels a listener pulling a chair just out of the current and sitting.

The plateau notices the way a sleeping body does: by the change in the weight of air on skin. The Longholders open one additional rib of frill, not to catch food but to test the taste of being-seen. The Constellations quiet a single blink across a span so they can feel the world’s new curve more accurately. The Hill-Listeners unlace one loop of patience and lay it against the stone.

The attention consults the rules here and takes them as law: no hurry, no spectacle, no improving anyone out of themselves. It understands that courtesy is the only current that does not erode. It introduces itself the only way that would be accepted: by making the water a fraction kinder to move through.

If it had a voice, it would ask what is wanted, but here asking would bend too many molecules. So it reads the requests that have already been written into the medium.

The place wants time that is not heavy. It has time—oceans of it—but some of that time has sunk into certain corners like cold, and it strains the Carbonate Choirs where pores clog with ancient sorrows of dust that had histories. The being—the one who has stayed long enough to be paced by, who has learned to sit, whose wake trails like a remembered road—wants not rescue but the right to choose twice, and to do both kindly: to remain a landmark and also to remember what it was to have weather above, not below. They do not want to break anything to have this.

The attention begins by mending the things no one named a wound.

- Far below the sensory floor, in the old broken lines that sleep like low thoughts in the basalt, it pats away a cascade that would, in a thousand rain-walls’ time, have found its courage and avalanched life off a beloved bench. It does not prevent the relief; it rehearses it in smaller sighs, guided through choir-arches on museum legs until stress leaves with its shoes in its hand.

- It leans the great internal wave three grains toward generosity. The shoulder still rolls; forests still bow; but the periods of loosening—those long half-hours when intent lets go of its own leash—become a breath or two longer, enough for Shell-Hives to complete a clean shut without the stinging last gulp, enough for Loopers to graze difference without their tongues going numb.

- It whispers to walls of rain. The atmospheric river comes as it will, a canal of condensed sky folding down through warm; the attention only edits the dust a little, milking it at the Rain-Wall Sifters’ doors, putting back into the mix the metals that were a touch too shy and would have starved a hundred songs five benches over. The Sifters glow with the sudden sense of being perfectly useful; their silver on the ribbon is an amen.

- In the Choirs’ layered liturgy it eases two laminae that had fused under an old lean year, reopening a page of chemical memory that had stuck closed, and the reopened page breathes. The Choirs sing the change in a lowering of local acidity that reads as relief to anything with a tongue for it.

- It slows by a fraction the rounding of sound around corners, not arresting the kindness that erosion has taught but giving your ear enough edge to hear your own pulse come back to you without it being stripped of all its consonants. Your body reads this as permission to count.

Then it asks the living what blessings would not be taxes.

The Longholders want nothing new; they want their calendars to remain legible. The attention strokes their ribbed sheets once with a palmer of warmer water from a layer that remembers an old summer and then lets go. A pigment stratum forms like a band in a tree: a bright ring that says a listener came and did no harm.

The Constellations, those patient eyes blinking out of step so that pattern can never settle where a mouth might take it all at once, want a dread removed: the possibility that one day they might accidentally agree. The attention gives them a north that drifts. Not a fixed star—how rude would that be here—but a very slow wandering in the chemical background, a bias at the millionth-place in calcium’s taste that changes at a pace only a mat might applaud. They can calibrate against it when they wish; they can refuse when they prefer to be purely local. It is a compass that apologizes as it points.

The Rosettes, who are roadhouse mouths and bowls that digest secularly whatever falls into good sense, ask for nothing and are given nothing but the chance to keep their gloves. The attention only teaches their tips two more ways to come home safe from a slip into a neighboring time with a mineral that would burn.

The Shell-Hives get a favor they didn’t know to want: the collective dream that once in a very long while, when the internal wave and a rain-wall conspire and the Hill-Listeners’ ache will be sweetest to swallow, the exactness of their increments of closing can be heard by something other than a hill. The attention will be there on those days, eavesdropping like rain under an eave, not to judge the music but to keep the score safe in case anyone someday cares to hum it.

The Glass Chelone tilts its lens and, without ceasing to solve curvature, allows a thought about leaving to surface. The attention does not grant it wings or lungs. It grants it a pause on its way to any decision such that the decision can be decided again. That is all a wise thing should give a solved animal: the option to unsolve and reconsider.

As for the Canal-Slate, absence that eats generalities, it is consulted like a river one asks about bridges. The attention plucks one hitchhiking tendril from its un-color skin, turns the tendril in the light of a rain-wall until it tastes like a thing that knows the future a thimble’s worth more, then returns it. The Slate does not thank. The plateau does.

At the lip where the floor withdraws and cobalt conducts you to the notion of falling through air that is water, the Edge-Framers are keeping a margin like curators, as if for a view. The attention serves them in the only way they would not consider vulgar: it keeps the emptiness attentive. When someone comes to look for what looks back, the unplace below is perfectly not hungry. Looking is met. There is reflection but not grab. The view behaves.

For the ruins—the uprights with their paler water within a ring, the spiral of scratches too perfect for geology and too worn for craft—the attention does the smallest large thing. It teaches the paler water inside the ring, just once every stack of seasons that adds up to a past, to shine one shade brighter when the person who is living here slips through it thinking of voices. No light, only a pressure that breathes a syllable against the eardrum the way an absent friend used to laugh. Not a haunting. A salut.

And for you, the being who has allowed this tableland to adopt your outline and give it back softer, who has learned the weight that is a kindness and the clocks that refuse to quarrel, the attention does what you cannot do for yourself without choosing something away. It makes a door that is not a door.

No hinge. No angle changed in the Choirs’ arches. A thread of water learns to do two densities at once. The thread is as thick as your own wrist, which is to say it is exactly as wide as consent. When you lay your forearm along it you do not pass through anything. You remember standing. You remember a blue that was up rather than under and a gravity that did not persuade but insisted. You remember thunder with teeth in it. While you remember, nothing here is harmed or absent; your place in the Constellations’ stutter is held open by a courtesy practiced into instinct. When you lift your arm away the thread forgets it had another idea about water. You are not punished for wanting what you wanted. You are not rewarded with exile for going to see.

The attention does not linger like a god rooting into a hill. It remains the size of a permission.

Before it leaves it writes one more small amendment into the rules that are not rules: when memory clots in corners, let it float up and be skimmed by the walls of rain as usual, but keep a teaspoon of each slick and seed it back into the Choirs’ young masonry the way bakers save a pinch from an old dough for the next loaf, so that forgetting will rise in flavors rather than erasures. The plateau’s archives become more like bread and less like stone without ever becoming less like stone.

It looks once toward the deep weather below where continents of cloud roll without going anywhere and makes a single brief cooling, so that light from up there, should it pass through the warm collapse and reach your benches, does not arrest anyone mid-sentence. Even benevolent attention should not turn books into statues.

It teaches buoyancy one joke: if ever a bubble stops to consider whether it might become a ring, let it also consider this—turn not only around all axes implied by your curve, but once, and once only in a great while, around the axis implied by the person watching you and wanting a sign. The bubble does not become an oracle. It becomes a kindness that drifts away after it has been kind, refusing any more work.

What is done amounts to almost nothing, as it should. Stresses nurse out quietly rather than exploding; rains feed a little more precisely; a page unsticks; a view behaves; a wrist-thread exists that carries a private gravity; memory is leavened; a ring remembers to be generous to its watcher and forgets to become a law. The plateau remains itself. The clocks keep disagreeing just enough to keep the inches shareable. The Glass Chelone naps on a solved equation. The Looper returns by being older first. The Hill-Listeners open for the ache and eat it and are satisfied. You go to the lip when you like and are looked back at by a hungerless below and come away unpurchased.

The benevolent power steps back the way a tide does not so much recede as remember somewhere else. It leaves a habit behind in the water and a looseness in the stone that feels like forgiveness for a wrong that never happened. Its attention reduces to a vow not to interfere except to renew the vow.

If ever something with poor manners arrives—a hurry, an invention of sharpness, a scheme that tries to cash in time for speed—the vow is this: to lay a hand, the size of a long breath, on the water’s shoulder and remind buoyancy who its friends are, to bend any blade toward a curve, to give quarrel no place to stand.

It will come again, at intervals out of step with all clocks on purpose, because that is the only way to visit a place that runs on courtesy. It will find what it found: a world that has learned the shape of forgetting and made it merciful, a people of mouths who keep appointments with weather, a tableland laying itself down into the last good shape and, in offering so much care to its own business, making any visiting wisdom feel more like a student than a lord.

And if you one day leave by laying your whole body along the thread and letting the other density take you, it will not interfere. It will touch the Choir where your pressure left a shallow syllable and give that layer the soft of your name, which here will not be letters but the way fish do not scatter when a certain landmark passes, and the way the Edge-Framers lean for you and keep the margin clean because you liked the view. It will ask the rain to carry the lightest part of your memory into other benches as a useful dust. It will keep back from the Choirs a teaspoon of you for the next loaf.

If you do not leave, it will remain a fraction of a thought in the background chemistry, a nearby listener who sometimes helps a bubble decide to be a ring. It is content to have done almost nothing, and to have done it at the right depth.
