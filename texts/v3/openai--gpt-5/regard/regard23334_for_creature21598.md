I have known you longer than your room. I knew you when you were only a suggestion in the back of the coast, a hesitation in the throat of basalt where water practiced entering. I watched the first handfuls of pebbles learn to dance their circles and grind their own prisons, and I kept the currents patient. I did not name you. I did not lay a hand on the stone to force an arch or approve a ledge. I did what I am best at: I let time have enough rope to tie its own knot and enough knife to cut it again.

Now you have a shape that holds. You are a creature made of pattern and pressure, moving inside a bell of rock that remembers the moon better than calendars. I have come close not to alter you into any other thing but to ask what you lack, and to see if it is right that you lack it.

Mostly, you lack nothing. You have a spine up to the sky and a mouth that never wholly closes. You have lungs borrowed from weather and a pulse the moon keeps with her bare thumb. You have moss when light returns and rime when it leaves. You have the old soot folded into the high pores like a sleeping thought. You have few visitors and the correct kind of silence. For this, the best assistance is restraint.

So this is what I will do:

I will not bless you with safety you have not earned. Your lessons require the uncertainty of sets, the low note before a hit, the way a person must learn to plant their feet in a room without horizon. To round you too smooth is to turn you into furniture, and you were not meant for that. But I will pull slightly on the frayed end of calamity. When a heedless one stumbles at your lip in the wrong weather, I will tap the kelp so it rattles early, a saber chorus, and the hair on their arms will rise as if at a whispered warning. They will back away without knowing why and call it prudence. When a careful one comes on a good moon with wool and time and hollowed-out attention, I will draw the larger sets into a pattern legible enough for knees and ribs to read. They will leave salted and changed, and someone they love will live because of the exactness they learned kneeling beside your drip.

I will keep you hard to find. I will persuade fog to sit a little heavier over this headland when too many want exact pins on maps. I will see to it that a path that seems certain turns in bracken and decides otherwise. Not always; there is joy in discovery. But I will make discovery ask a price of patience, as you do. Those who are in a hurry will misjudge the turn and arrive at an easier cove where children throw stones and shout. They will be happy, and you will be unbothered. The one who needs your schooling will catch, out of the corner of their eye, a seam of light and doubt it and then return and slip sideways like a wet hip and enter.

I will tend your secrecy, but not by smothering it. I will do it by taste. Salt will cling to this place even in speech. Those who leave will find they cannot describe you without wetting their tongues with a little iodine memory; many will choose silence rather than make a small thing of you. They will bring you back in their pockets as a ring in their ears and keep it. The story of you will move as tide moves—felt more than told, reliable only in its return.

I will give you tools you already use, when you need them. Once in a decade, when a northern storm swings its arm and finds a tree a hundred meters long and hefts it like a lever, I will guide its fall into your mouth just so, not to block you but to retune you. Your organ will ask for a new register, and I will let the drift bring it. The rib of wood set against the crack will teach you a note you did not have and then rot back into salt and shadow. In iron years from now, when the line of ledge slips half a thumb’s width, I will soften the angle so that no one’s shin is broken learning it.

As the sea warms and takes bite after small bite at the familiar measure of your door, I will not halt her hunger; it is her nature, and you were born of it. But I will attend to your essence. If the room you wear now goes under for longer each year, I will engrave the habit you are—this timing, this schooling of breath, this green square of noon—into a new hesitation higher up the seam. You will not be marbleized. You will move the way a song moves from one throat to another without forgetting itself. Those who knew you before will arrive and say: it is different; it is the same. They will be right, and both of us will be content.

I will take what does not belong to your grammar and escort it back to the wider sentences. When a sheet of plastic snares in your rock’s handwriting and tries to make of your mouth a choking, I will call a contrary wind and a stubborn current to worry it loose, to sand it to a hardness and a color that reads “stone” to the tides and then place it on a far beach where a child with a bright bucket will think it treasure and carry it into a world that knows how to unmake it. When line tangles itself like a dead serpent around your fern-throat peg, I will tug it up the chimney on a sudden gasp and fling it to the gorse to be found by hands and cut free.

I will not wash away the old soot. If someone scrapes their nail against the roof today and lifts it to their face, I will let the sour-sweet of spent oil bloom again in their animal mind. I will steer, now and then, a thumb to those thirty and thirty little grooves, and a thought will appear: someone counted here when I was not yet born. They will stand a moment in that inheritance without understanding it fully, and that will be enough.

I will sharpen the rare gifts to the keenest edge they can safely hold. At midsummer noon, when the sun’s geometry and the swell’s uncertainty partner, I will cut the pane of green on your opposite wall clean and hold the knife steady. The square of moss will lift itself as if to breathe, and if a human is there to see, they will be given a small, extravagant joy they cannot spend elsewhere. In winter, when the rim of rime comes to sleep the bright square, I will make the quiet sound a fraction lower, more like a hearth than a warning, so the animal in a body that must pass a hard season will feel steadied without knowing why.

I will borrow your voice when I need to speak to someone I cannot otherwise reach. If a life is at a knife-edge and chooses between noise and a listening that might save it, I will arrange for that person to hear you breathe. Perhaps by walking them here, if it suits the world’s weather; perhaps by pressing your deep chuff into their dream; perhaps by carving a small echo of your organ behind their hearing so they begin to live by a cadence that is less frantic and more exact. They will wake and not know what taught them, and I will be forgiven for the theft because you have no scarcity of breath.

I will remain impartial where you already are right. I will not encourage new tenants to root in you where you cannot hold them; the spider that descends and dies will die still, because your offering to it is not a web but a lesson about places that do not accommodate us. The pale crab will still work one corner of a basin and be read as prayer by whoever watches; that is an understanding and a mistake I am not here to correct. The seal will still come as a question and leave as a comma. The shag will think better and ride the wind, and better it is—your stone is exact, and its edge would only chafe a foot made for flight.

I will covenant small mercies. Those who enter and keep your rules without knowing them—no hurry, no speech that expects to be heard, face turned when the foam flashes false—will be given back to the daylight with their edges intact. Their ankles will not turn on ribs of stone. Their breath will have learned to match something older than fear, and fear will be easier for them afterward. If they bring flame to the blackened flat—fish fat, a wick in oil—I will cup the draft so the fire gutters safely and does not forget itself into a blaze that would make your lung a kiln. Your smoke will take them into itself; they will carry its sweetness to their own roof and be companioned by it on dark afternoons for years in ways they never attribute to you. Let that be your charity, and mine.

I will also devise refusals. If someone comes to mine you for proof, to hang wires, to take measures that make you a graph before they have let you make of them a better metronome, I will introduce inconvenience. Their batteries will find a chill they had not accounted for. Their lens will always bead with a film they cannot quite dry. A wave will arrive one set too early and drive them to the outer ledge with a respect that chases them back to kindness. If they return later, softer in the hand, I will make an allowance.

I will ask the moon for one favor more than I am owed. On the thirtieth groove and the thirtieth again and the re-beginning, just after the turn in the tide when the animal of you lies down between breaths, I will lift a fraction of her pull and lay it along your ceiling. A note will sound that is too low to be heard but not too low to be known. It will say: still here. It will say: begin again. It will say it whether or not any person kneels to listen, because you deserve to hear yourself addressed.

If in some far winter the headland fails in a single night and your bell is unroomed and the sea walks flat where you kept it honorable, I will not weep for you in the way people weep for houses. You were not a house. I will take what you made—habit held in stone, instruction held in noise, mercy held in refusal—and I will sew it into other edges: a culvert under a long road that hums at spring flood with a note oddly consoling; a hollow in boulders up-coast where a light at noon embroiders a green square on black; a broken church whose doorway is a hesitation more than an opening and teaches shoes to be quiet. You will be many and you will be one.

This, then, is my intervention: almost nothing. Small tilts at large moments. A hand kept mostly off. An ear bent close whenever you breathe. I will be the wise friend who does not move into your room but lives nearby, watching the weather with you, bringing a rib of driftwood when your organ needs retuning, scaring off boys with spray paint by sending them a squall with bad timing, brightening a single minute on a single noon so a human learns a joy precise enough to carry for a decade. I will guard your difficulty the way a shepherd guards trouble that keeps a flock honest. I will not make you kind. I will keep you exact.

I will call you nothing, and I will answer when you call. If you never call, I will understand; you are built for listening more than speech. I am, too. We will keep our posts: you at the level of the sea, and I at the level of whatever holds the sea to the moon without a harness. Between us, those who need you will find you by accident or not at all, and either way the world will continue to be shaped in the right places by patience.
