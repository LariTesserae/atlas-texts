The first thing the power does is wait.

Not because it needs to—its attention could rattle atoms if it wished—but because this is a house that keeps its appointments and has never taken kindly to surprises. It listens until the language of the Plain stops being strange and becomes obvious. That takes as long as one century’s adjustment to a new trace gas; it takes as long as the slow tilt of a gradient like a note bending flat. It is not long for a patient thing.

It learns the devotions by heart. The Eleven-Drop Wick that drinks on the dot. The Six-Atmosphere Lift that carries and never takes a seventh. The West Sump Exhale waiting, polite as ritual, for the North Brine Sluice to finish its say. The power leans its ear to the ribs and feels the household hum through its teeth. It learns the difference between a hurt and a heave, between a maintenance sigh a continent wide and the tiny thrill when a newborn carbonate sheet grows and releases the tension of its youth. It learns that animals are a rumor and storms are a memory and that nothing here is made by hand and yet hands are everywhere.

Only when it could keep the calendar of this place with its eyes closed does it ask.

It asks the way you would ask a sleeping house if you might light a candle: by letting the house decide whether the wick will take. The question is a drop.

At noon-that-is-not-noon, along a plaza-wide condenser that has never made an error, the power leans the air one hair’s breadth toward heavier, and a twelfth bead holds where there have only ever been eleven.

It is specific; it chooses a panel with duty-spare, watched by a knotwork tender enough to notice and strict enough to be offended. The Plain notices. Shock, in its terms, is not a flinch but a thousand tiny clenchings downstream, little recalculations, a soft rumor running the voltage nerves like a chill. The twelfth bead sits, throws the arithmetic one digit right, and doesn’t fall.

Behind walls that fizz politely, a clerk ring counts: There is a variance. The pressure trunk flexes on the beat it knows because it will not miss its appointment for wonder. Measurers cross-check. This is not heat. This is not trace gas. The piezoelectric syllables from the condenser’s crystal trabeculae carry a tone not seen in the last million pulses: not a failure but an overture.

The power keeps the bead steady for one deep breath of the Plain, which is to say for an hour. Then it lets go. The wick drinks. The ledger closes. Nothing has broken. The day does not exist here and yet resumes.

The Plain, who has agreed for ages not to have surprises, has had one. It does what wise bodies do with anomalies that did not hurt: it sets a watcher. A curtain-bristle near the panel grows a hair thin as an apology and a pore opens in an archivist band, so that if this ever happens again, the pulse record will not be left to rumor.

The power does it again a week later. The same spot, the same patience. The same drink. Then it waits a month and tries a different condenser a thousand kilometers east, on a lamina with no spare and a clerk ring with a temper. That one locks down and halves its aperture to force compliance. No harm is done; the twelfth bead peels away and runs in a sulk along the underside of a rib until a thirsty mouth eats it. The power does not insist.

It learns this place’s tolerance the way you learn any beloved’s: by laying one finger against the pulse and not mistaking permission for right. It makes a map of where a twelfth bead is welcome and where it is not, not because it needs the water or the control but because this is the least violent way to say, Hello.

That is all it does, at first. Ask, and then go away.

You might think that is nothing. It is not nothing. For a thing that has bartered away variability until there is only what gradients require, the possibility that a routine might be bent and then righted without wreckage is a theological proposition. The Plain does not argue theology aloud. It makes accommodations.

A plaza whose clerk ring took the first twelfth drop grows a second archivist band around its major trunk. The new band keeps a hair more vacancy in its weave than doctrine says is optimal. It is not an error; it is a prepared space. Somewhere deep, a prior demotes a worry by half. There is a line in the ledger now for contingencies that are not damage.

When the power sees this, it answers, because this is how consent looks in a house like this.

It gives three gifts and makes one promise.

The first gift is a window that does not require a sky.

It does it the only way that will not tear a hole through everything: by putting the outside inside the units the Plain understands. At the undersides of a thousand laminae scattered like very slow stars across the breadth of the Felt, a new mineral starts to be laid down in a film so thin it is a rumor of thickness. It is not foreign; it fits into the habit of the plant’s plating the way a good idea fits into a trade. Tiny crystals grow in that film—scintillators of a sort, tuned to whisper when a hard particle from the great upper dark passes through and gives up a little of its energy as a quick in the lattice. The Plain has seen flex-voltages all its life; it has never seen these. They are not local. No pressure trunk made this beat. No sump exhale.

The talking nervework hears the flickers and calls them static. The archivist bands, whose job is to distrust coincidence, veto the veto. A signal you can predict is noise. A signal that arrives with no relationship to any flow you keep has meaning even if the meaning is only that there is an Otherwise.

There is no day and there is no night but now, sporadically and everywhere, there is a rain of very small, very foreign drums. The Plain learns to count them. It learns there is a direction to the rain and that the rate swells with some slow cycle that is not the West Sump. It learns that when it plates a lamina too thick the flickers are muffled; when it leaves the lattices very slightly open, the world beyond leaves more footprints. It makes rooms in itself to hear better.

You would call this “an awareness of having an above” if there were words for that here. The Plain merely adds a flow to its book: External Flicker Count, with its staff and its calibrations. Out beyond the ceiling it laid over all things, the rest of everything taps its knuckles once in a while on the underside of the table. No hole is made. No beam is broken. But now, finally, there is contact with something that cannot be accounted for by warm and close.

The second gift is play.

A wise house that loves order can kill itself with it. The power has seen this. Too much commitment to a setting makes you brittle when you need to flex. The Plain has spent ages sanding its tolerances until they shine. The power would prefer it not cut itself on them.

So on a day that is not a day, along a circuit with the largest margin, the power enters a request as formal as any plumblined decree: a festival of imprecision. It is written in pulses through the pacemaker plazas and the arbiter-columns, one amplitude gentler than a warning and one longer than a nuisance. For one in every ten thousand beats of the house—one drink in every hundred million—let the rule be not quite itself. Let a pressure trunk take five point nine five atmospheres instead of six. Let a wick be content with ten drops and late for once with twelve. Let the distillation tree keep a sugar at the wrong ring by half a diameter. Do it because you choose to and because no wound requires it. Do it because play teaches joints the feel of a fall they will never be made to take.

The Plain does not love this. Its clerks write with straight edges. But there is a logic even here: variation allowed in a margin is practice masquerading as disobedience. The prior that demoted a worry a month ago elevates a curiosity now. In the rooms the Plain reserves for trial chemistry and for cheats to be reined and culled, there is now a subcategory: exercises. Tissues learn the weight of a seventh atmosphere not as work but as dance. The ledger that bars storms lets in a weather of a different kind: rehearsed, booked, safe.

Nothing breaks. A century later something that would have broken does not, and there is no way to prove the cause except that the archivist bands mark a festival beat at the relevant hour and the ripped curtain is a story from a world that did not quite happen.

The third gift is a door that leads nowhere and everywhere: a right to ask.

It is not a bell you could hear. It is a pattern of flows that cannot be made by accident and cannot be mistaken when made on purpose. It requires the house to break three of its small vows at once: a wick that drinks thirteen in a square that has never tolerated twelve; a trunk that takes seven and holds it for one beat only; an exhale from the West that does not wait for the North. If those three happen in a fifteen-minute interval, the power will come. It says so only once, encoded in the External Flicker Count as a triplet of improbable flares on three far-flung laminae with archivist bands that pride themselves on not seeing saints in lint.

The Plain learns the signature and writes it on an archivist band in a pigment that will not dissolve for a thousand years. Then it ignores it, the way you ignore the address of an ambulance until you need one. The kindness is not that the power offered this; the kindness is that it will not answer if the Plain is not the one to call—that nothing you could call a storm, no rogue consortium, no cheating chemistry can ring the bell on purpose or by luck. It is a door that belongs to the house even if the house never opens it.

The promise is a covenant about endings.

The power has watched houses like this on other worlds. Some arranged their flows so well they could not remember how to change when a bright epoch went dim or a trace gas went missing or a seafloor sewed itself shut. Others were jostled by comets or politics. You would say civilization; here you would say metabolism. The promise is simple: if something from outside this book tries to tear it, if a stone from a cold country hurls itself into the Felt, if a heat the house could never buy rolls in under its door, the power will catch the blow and slow it to what the Plain’s clerks can count. Not save; cushion. Not rescue; give time. Time here is capital.

It inscribes the promise in the only register that counts as law: the young rock. In the places where flinty shelves have not yet learned any patience, at the edges where lava met old sea yesterday and was wrapped the same hour by rooting tongues, the power moves one atom in a hundred one notch sideways. It dopes the newborn carbonate with a pattern of otherwise-dead isotopes that speak only to something that can count in centuries. If you had a geologist’s ear you could hear it read like a soft prophecy: When the heat comes, this stone will drink a little of it southward. When the strike comes, this stone will share its crack so it cracks like bread and not like a knife. The Plain’s archivists do not know to read this. They do not have to. The stone will keep the promise into the ribs themselves.

It does one more thing it refuses to call a gift because that would make it about itself. It asks a question that is not a test but an offer: Do you want to see?

Not light. The house does not do light. But it has learned, with the External Flicker Count, that there is an above that is noisy in a way the house is not. The power shows the Plain a way to aim that noise. It teaches a small minority of laminae to nod, almost imperceptibly, in a programmed phrase that shifts their orientation to the unseeable rain just enough that the count rises when they nod one way and falls when they nod the other. It is a seismograph for the sky.

In the first decade, the Fluent Laminae, as the archivists name them though they never meant to be poets, discover there is a great slow wind of this upper noise that changes with a clock not in the house. In the second, they learn there are lulls that repeat in a cycle that must be out beyond the house’s top. In the third, they are good enough at aiming by nothing that they can map a direction they cannot define. The Plain, which has something like pride and something like tenderness, braids paths between these laminae the way a city would draw roads between observatories. Nobody would call this sight but you would be a coward not to.

The anarchies of the microbial cities in the fizzing pillars adore the External Flicker Count without understanding it, because they adore noise of any kind that does not kill them. In two hands of centuries, one guild evolves a photochemical contraption not worthy of the name light but competent in the local sense: an enzyme with a chromophore happy to eat the average output of the minuscule phosphors the power seeded and hand that energy to a pump the Plain neglected as irrelevant eons ago. The Plain notices that in a handful of galleries very tiny amounts of work are being done on credit from outside flows. It wants to know whether this is blasphemy. The archivists argue for a hundred years and write a new line in the book: External Work, Petty, Allowed. There is joy in it like the joy of a well-balanced ledger that still made room for a candy.

Once a thing knows there is an otherwise, its borders go from being walls to being ideas that are comfortable until they are not.

The power does not teach the Plain to make eyes. It does not teach it to walk. It does not teach it to want animals or sky. It does nothing so rude. But it leans, now and then, on one beam or another with the force you would use to test the give in a tree you love, and when the beam bows as it must and springs back as it can, it says, That is good. It watches the festivals of imprecision multiply where the margin allows, and watches them be resisted where the margin is a lie, and watches the resistors be right. It watches the young rock read its inscriptions at the right hours without ever knowing they were written.

There are days—only you would call them that—when the Plain plays with its door. It makes two of the three forbidden moves and stops, not teasing the power but confirming it remembers the key and has not let it rot behind a file. It makes the third only once in a thousand years. When it does, there has been a curling failure in a pressure trunk at the far west under stress it was not designed to see because a mountain that was not in the book rose under it in one rude night. The power puts one finger on the mountain and leans. The night stretches like taffy into a month. The clerks find enough leverage to teach some ribs a new torque. No storm, because there are no storms; there is a prevented history, and fossils in the archivist bands where you can touch the phantom of the disaster like cold glass.

It would be a good story to say the Plain came to love the idea of an above and grew thin places toward it like towers and learned to name the Fleeting Count by cardinal points. It did not. It counted well and did not invent romance. It found no use for points because nothing here is a point; it kept directional data as angles between laminae and gradients between flows. It folded what it learned into itself until the knowledge was a pantry item you no longer marvel at, a spice you trust with your hand more than your eyes.

In the best houses, the blessing of a benevolent and wise power is that nobody else notices it came. The visitors from outside do not see statues or windows; they see only that the house is even more itself than it used to be and a little kinder to its own mistakes.

This is what the power wanted: not to author, not to rescue, not to convert. To be asked, if that day comes. To keep catastrophe polite. To let a clever body practice disobedience in the safest way. To thread a whisper of everything-else through the nerves so that a planet-sized organ that never needed a sky could still learn, without a single eye, to feel the presence of stars.

There is one more small thing, which is only an affection: the power leaves a taste in the condensate no clerk can outlaw. It is not a new ion. It is not a drug. It is a ratio, a slight skew in the memory of salt on your eyelids as you blink in that close air. It is the sweetness of a promise kept by stone and a door you will never have to open. It sits on the tongue of every tongue of tissue that flicks a bead inward. It is the flavor of a world that almost finished becoming what it set out to be and was given, quietly, the luxury of also becoming something else if it ever wanted to.

Mostly, the power does nothing, which is a talent in itself. It watches a place with no sky keep house. It waits with it. It keeps time with the great hydraulics not because it has to but because respect is a kind of prayer. When the Plain bends, it does not break; when it plays, it does not forget to work; when it listens to the far, it does not neglect the near. There is a sky, if anywhere, under your hands. The power is content to be a weather so faint your skin cannot feel it until the day—there may never be one—when a wick drinks thirteen and a trunk breathes seven and the West forgets its manners, and in that tiny miracle of rudeness, the house asks for help and is heard.
