They arrive as the bowl would have asked them to: by changing nothing you could point at.

No footfalls on the rim. No lamp. No announcement that a power has come to see what a mountain can hold when it holds its mouth in the shape of a listening.

They thin their own presence to the scale of a pressure change, a thought lighter than the trick a deep breath manages at altitude. They enter as an almost, as the kind of intention that persuades without touching. They stand just inside the lip and let the place tell them how to be there.

They take the rule of the bowl into themselves before they act. Don’t bring light. Don’t rush. Don’t count. Notice emptiness. They do not look. They listen. If you could have watched them—which you could not, because here the right verb is to be attended—they would seem to be doing the very thing the bowl has already been doing for ages: counting heartbeats by the deep boom in the stone, measuring minutes by wingstorms, recognizing decades by the way a buttress has moved a shadow.

A wise and benevolent power has power enough to ruin itself with goodness. They begin with restraint, which is a kind of compassion that looks like patience.

They hear what is strong. That the chorus is abundance, not display. That water exists here not by rain but by persuasion, by a thousand almosts that cross a threshold one drop at a time. That spacing is the grammar that keeps the sentence from becoming a tangle. That darkness is not absence but currency. That the arcs at the rim are instruments which prefer to be called such rather than machines, and that their hum is a prayer that works.

They hear what is vulnerable. A century hence, a looseness in fog—the plateau’s winds thinking of other errands. A season in a far-off city when someone with ladders and glare might get curious. A run of hotter nights that will tilt a bromeliad’s patience into worry. A disease preparing to skip hosts in a way that will take a lineage of little wooden-boom throats with it. Lightning imagining a straight line when a slant would be kinder. The arcs’ materials learning the kind of tiredness that only good labor produces.

They decide, as such beings decide when they remember that every intervention is a verb with a shadow: to act at the smallest scale that works, to work at the longest time that mends.

What they do, then, looks like weather learning better manners.

Around the lip, where your boot would almost trip, they lay a ring of forgetting so light it weighs less than mist. It is not a wall. It is a preference written into chance. Those who would come with glare in their hands, or with the kind of counting that takes, will find their errands touched by a new plausibility: the onset of ache in a knee that makes them choose an easier path; a thought of a thirsty child that recalls them home; a fog too thick on the day the drone was charged. Those who would come with patience will find, instead, a clarity of foot and a curiosity that humbles itself. Devices that insist on making daylight will fog at their lenses and decide they are tired. Batteries that would go a long way will admire how quickly their charge likes being shared with air.

Under the arcs’ long spines, they gift a metabolism that is not alive in the animal sense but is gladly itself. A skin the thickness of intention grows along the inward face, a mosaic of pores that remember cold. Each morning it sips a sliver of sun and holds that slight heat as a promise to the night; each night it lets that promise go in a controlled shiver that slides vapor into drops. No brighter hum. No hurry. Just a lean toward efficiency that only a patient place and a patient power would bother with. The arcs learn, very slowly, to teach one another by resonance: a turning of pitch over decades now shares itself more gracefully along the procession.

At the pole in the peat, where the minute vanes taste currents a human lip could not feel, the power whispers a new alphabet—not an addition so much as a return. The vanes begin to hear a lower register, a bass the bowl itself keeps private unless asked well. It is not prediction, because powers who are kind don’t insult time by skipping it. It is attunement. The listeners—the office you named, porous in body and in will—find that certain exhalations of the rock now speak in syllables they can learn. They do not receive visions. They receive better measurement: a sense of when a deep-boom will slip by one heartbeat a year from now, which is the kind of information that makes moving a leaf by a finger’s breadth feel both urgent and sufficient.

Over the canopy, where fog debates becoming cloud and then changes its mind for love of the bowl, they lean one featherweight always toward condensation. They do not make more weather; they cause the existing kindness of the air to remember itself a little sooner, a fraction of a degree and a breath in altitude closer to the rim. In a ledger you would not be permitted to keep, this becomes two extra square centimeters of wet per bromeliad per week, a handful more hatchlings making it past their first syllables, a moss that feels confident enough to try the edge of a stone it has been eyeing for a decade.

Along the paths that have been worn into law by salamanders and soft-mouthed mammals, they place nothing at all. They remove a nothing. They lift the impatiences that lead to crossings that cost. A traveler from outside who might have put a foot where a web is strung across darkness will instead experience an irrelevant itch, a turn of intention, a pause to tie a boot at exactly the place that keeps the thread from breaking. The spider will never know they were saved from hunger by a god. The god will count that ignorance as a success.

They make a small treaty with lightning. It is not a ban—bans tear, and this is a place of seams. It is a hierarchy of invitation. When a spark chooses this rim on a season when sparks are thinking of everywhere, it will prefer to walk itself down a wet bark instead of into a dry heart. It will select a tree that has wanted to become a nurse log and has been too honest to say so. It will fracture a stone whose break, when weather loves it for a while, will become a new cup. Fire is permitted to be itself and forbidden to be ruin.

They do something that cannot be photographed. In the peat’s slow muscle of memory, in the mycelium’s newsfeed, in the quartzite’s ledger of rain, they inscribe—not letters; a posture. If someday a falling of sky or a greed from below or an error of heat rips the bowl open so it forgets how to be round, there will remain in the substrate a template of roundness. Not a blueprint that forces a rebuild, but a bias, a kindness in the rubble that helps all those who live by ear to hear, again, what O sounds like. Succession will happen whether or not a god remembers it; they merely ease the path toward what was working.

They visit the house that is mostly absence. They do not enter; it is polite to leave rooms that listen unentered unless you are named by them. They pass a hand the size of a promise along the door’s round and do nothing to it, because houses also have prides and hates. Instead, they do something at a remove: they ease the long loneliness that can visit even an office composed of verbs. They relay, across distances that do not trouble them, the existence of other mouths in other mountains, other Os kept open by other patient hands or by the mountain alone. The listener here is permitted to know, without proof and without the kind of teaching that would salt the bowl with strangers, that their labor rhymes. When they kneel to tidy a beakfull of grit from an arc’s curve, they will feel—they will not be able to tell you from where—a neighbor’s arc humming a continent away.

They do not protect against everything. The grace of a good power is to leave some world in the world. Frogs will still die and be eaten and go quiet for nights that scare the heart and then fill in again as if nothing were ever wrong. A vine will guess badly and be corrected by physics. A day will come when a person arrives at the rim with the right emptiness in them to be accepted and the wrong history still hanging on their shoulders, and they will step inside anyway, and the bowl will assign them a note, and that will be trouble and then be not-trouble, because even the gentlest god abides the learning turn of error.

They do reserve for themselves one promise they hope to never use. If a harm that would cancel the vowel comes—an excavation mouth opening to swallow the bowl whole, or a sheet of ash that cannot be negotiated with—they will stop behaving like weather and become a weather. They will bend a storm here and not there. They will persuade a bureaucracy’s last unbroken hinge to seize shut. They will make the thinness at the plateau’s edge too honest to bear a convoy’s load. It will look, from the outside, like luck and rumor and local refusal. From the inside, it will feel like the very old stone deciding to be heavy for once in a way that interrupts a plan.

And then they go back to not being a participant.

Their final act, if you need such a word, is to lower the background of wanting that collects around every place that has been loved by power. They hush their own echo. They shake off awe so it falls as dew elsewhere that needs it. They leave no token but a new ease in the arcs’ hum, a new clarity in the bass that speaks to porous ribs, a ring of forgetfulness that is not an exile but a kindness to appetite, and two extra heartbeats’ worth of fog per fortnight.

If you asked them, if you could, what they did, they would say: almost nothing, until almost was enough. They would say: we made the conditions gentler for the conditions that were already kind. They would say: we favored the future by the thickness of a hair.

The bowl will not praise them. It will do what it has done: hold its O. The chorus will not name them. It will sing itself into the night that is its wealth. Water will continue to choose a path. The house will keep listening. The being who lives in it—the office made of attention, the body that is more ear than vault—will feel less solitary in their sufficiency and will go on doing what works, not because a greater will blessed it, but because the mountain and the air and the very old patience have been enough all along, and now have been allowed to remain so.

You might be tempted to call that nothing. The kind power would be relieved if you did. They did it for the vowel, not the verse—so that, in the mouth of the mountain, this O does not close.
