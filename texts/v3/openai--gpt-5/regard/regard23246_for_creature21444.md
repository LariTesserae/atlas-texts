The attention, when it comes, is a kind of listening that does not make the place louder. It is a pressure that does not add to the mountain’s pressure, a light that does not whiten the dolomite one degree more. The first act of a benevolent and wise power is always to wait, to learn what a system is already saying about how it wants to go on being itself.

So it waits through a noon and a season and the long, satisfied year the plateau loves to repeat. It counts the figure-eights stitched by swifts, the shell-bright flashes of the kites, the intervals of fog that soften the teeth of sound to mousse. It learns the grammar of the held chord and where the animals have put their voices to avoid being crushed by it. It notes the places where moisture chooses to appear instead of flow, where a saxifrage’s pretense succeeds, where one blunt-faced bird likes to drop bones so a ledge stays polished for a hundred dinners.

Then it asks, the way a doctor asks a body without words: What hurts? What changes would force you to change? The place says, with the honesty of stone, We are tuned. We are exact. If you must do anything, do not make us more.

The wise power understands constraints as a kind of kindness. It touches like one fingertip to a spinning bowl, and only there, and only as long as the bowl needs it. What it does could be listed, but none of it is flashy, and none of it is the first idea a power would have if it loved to be seen.

It lends a cloak, first. Not invisibility—only disinterest. Maps mislabel a spur so that a road crew prefers an easier pass two valleys over. A planner’s hand hesitates over a row of proposed turbines and drifts to a ridge with better numbers. Weather-app icons are a little wrong for the rim so that on the day a crowd would have come to shout into the vents and film the shout on their tiny glass, a fog arrives instead and holds them in a parking lot thirty miles down. Hikers take photographs of other stunning stones. A drone’s battery tells a plausible lie. Curiosity is not punished; it is redirected so that it does not arrive as a crowd. This is not exile, and it is not ownership. It is deference to a place that is finished with large gestures. It is a wide palm held out in a hallway so that heavy boots do not step past the sign: keep quiet, something is sleeping with its eyes open.

It gifts a margin. The rule of moisture here is exacting; life in the place has learned its numbers fine. But there are summers far below now that burn long enough to empty even the habit of rain. Ash rides the jet and would like to settle, to oil the stone just enough to shift a pitch. The power, being kind and trained in the art of tolerances, sifts the ash one gust more to the left and lets a little salt fall in a valley that could use it more. When a dry string of days would have starved the biofilm that keeps a vent’s lip wet enough to sing true, a filament of cloud wanders, slurs its edge on purpose where the pipe’s mouth drinks, and moves on. The gift is three additional droplets over the course of a week. The gift is enough. When a winter is two degrees milder than the old calendars remember, the wise one cools dawn in the grikes by a mere half-degree on twelve mornings—nudges of sky that mean the hoarfrost survives to noon the way the grike-runner’s tongue has learned it should. There is no miracle in this, only the saving of a pattern before it frays into a different song.

It protects a quiet. The mountain’s note is the way gravity is here, and it is good. But voices require a notch. Animals have already learned to speak in the gaps the pipes leave; the gap shifts when heat rises or stone fattens its lace. The power places a soft finger on a single high pressure at one mouth so that, whatever the weather does, a small band of silence stays open at twelve kilohertz, steady as noon. To creatures with ears the size of rice grains, this is a cathedral. To the kites it is a lane where a partner’s flash is not something seen through a hiss but seen. To a blunt-faced thief it is the drum it has always pretended the mountain made for it on purpose. Again, this looks like nothing. Again, it is everything.

It pays the tuners. Inside the vents the living skins lay down their moonmilk and make the pipes rounder where the chord likes roundness. The wise one slips into the water a few ions at need: a whisker of molybdenum, the thought of vanadium, a memory of calcium hard to come by in a year when an upstream glacier brings down silt with the wrong recipe. It stabilizes a consortium one winter by letting a bacteriophage arrive a month earlier than it would have on its own, so that the bloom breaks and remakes at a strength that fits the pipe. It is a gardener in a garden that seems bare if you do not measure your days in nanometers and niches.

It declines to save lives, and so preserves them. That sounds contradictory until you watch a glider tilt one whisper too far into the voice and tap the stone and fold. A large bird eats. The polish under the dinner stone brightens. Nothing that lives here has been promised rescue. The wise thing refuses, cleanly, to keep anyone from dying of the kind of mistake the place itself teaches about. What it refuses instead is catastrophe that would pour in from an outside system: a sheet of plastic snagged on the karren that would mat a pan’s mouth shut; a storm-hammer that would cut new pipes all at once; a disease from a valley chicken yard that would teach deafness to a bird built to be deaf only when it feeds. Those the power turns with the same patience it turned the road: an updraft meets a cigarette ember and takes it high and harmless; a gust cracks a thin, ugly film before it has learned to be contiguous; a traveller with a cough sleeps one night too long in a town that is boring in a human way and never has the strength to climb to where the chord could be infected by lungs that do not belong. Mercy is the shape of refusal here.

It lays an echo, far away. This is not duplication; that would be vandalism. It is a contingency. The wise one knows that even the best-maintained present moves under you. It cools, very slightly, a shoulder of carbonate two hundred kilometers along the range for two thousand years—an act that looks like nothing but shade on a particular afternoon to a particular patch of lichen. It lets water remember that ledge and then find it again the next spring, and again, and again, so that when the time comes the mountain there will have the beginnings of a mouth it did not plan, and a population of bacteria will already know how to be moonmilk at those angles. A single species of cushion will have made a small green button in exactly the right cup and lived there for eighty generations. The kites will find it by watching an experimenter breeze for a long time they cannot recall deliberately trying. They will flag to one another: here is also a station. No one will move en masse. There will only be options, the rarest altruism a power can give: somewhere else to be the same kind of self in a different year.

It places one little idea in one small skull. A grike-runner—furred belly, velvet wing, ears that open and close like cautious windows—dreams a dream at the edge of waking in which the toe-thumping it does to speak to its neighbor tries a slightly different rhythm and is, to its amazement, understood from further away. The pattern is nothing desert-wrecking; it is simply more robust to the day the vent’s gap rakes over a few hertz. The power does not teach syntax. It nudges metronome. Years later, when heat lifts the chord an eighth of a tone for half a summer, a line of communication holds that would otherwise have gone static. Three litters who might have gone hungry because a message about a cache and a hawk failed do not. This is small enough to be morality.

And once, because even a benevolent precision can be tender, it gives the place a holiday. The fog that comes twice a decade comes a third time, unseasonal and kind. For an hour the mountain’s voice does not turn off, not exactly, but wears a woolen coat. Everything that has learned to live inside insistence rests. A kite hangs on air and does not have to speak to itself about feather and eddy. A swift sleeps standing, if what swifts do counts as standing, and wakes to a world that remembers it. The blunt-faced bird feels its ear skin lids open against something like a hush and washes its head in it the way it would wash carrion stink, only with a different look in the eye. The power does not do this often, because days off that become custom are theft. It does it once in a generation and calls it a blessing, and that is what it is.

For humans, because they are drawn to edges and songs and because they have a way of misunderstanding gifts left out on ledges, the power does the oddest thing of all: it writes the rim into a few hearts so specifically that when they do arrive they come already gentle. They do not build structures because the place has built the only structure it needs. They do not clap because their palms understand privacy. They wedge no foreign pebbles into any crevice. They stay to the grikes and watch with ribs open to the pressure and go away changed in a way they cannot explain without ruining what changed them. This is not a fence. It is taste, trained from underneath.

Mostly, though—because it is wise, because it is benevolent—it continues to do very little. It monitors, it hums along, it adjusts a micrometer of moonmilk and a gust, it lauds the courage of a plant that succeeds by density and a bird that communicates by light. It keeps catastrophe elsewhere. It gives the place a second place to be, far off and unlikely. It keeps a notch clear for voices because songs without rests are torture. It answers the mountain’s answer: hold the note.

When it leaves, if leaving is a thing attention can do, it does not withdraw as shade does; it goes the way gravity goes when you step from a parapet to a causeway—still there, doing what it always does, nothing personal, a law disguised as kindness. It leaves a promise the plateau can use: not that it will be protected from the laws it lives by, only that those laws will not be suddenly replaced by someone else’s law. It leaves the place what it was before and what it wants to keep being: abundant in bands, empty in gulfs, rigorous, exact, patient. It leaves the beings who live in it with what a good parent gives a child who is already perfectly themselves: room; witness; a margin; a way out that looks like another way in.

And if you insisted on calling any one of them the Being who lives there—the mountain’s throat, say, its living laces of chalk and bacterial patience—the power would bow to that thing and say, in the only language such a being hears as words: I hear you. I will not sing over you. If ever your pitch drifts because the sky that feeds you has been warmed past the edge of your art, I will cup my hand for a moment over your mouth so you can catch your breath and retune yourself. Then I will take my hand away. Keep doing what you do.

The music keeps. The wind plays the pipes and the pipes thicken into the song that suits them. Once every long now, a fog coat is set on the shoulders of the ache of light. A blunt-faced bird feels safe being deaf while it eats, and is not made to be deaf otherwise. A kite flashes a sentence in a band of silence that exists because kindness can be an engineering problem. A grike-runner drums a rhythm for a neighbor it has never met, and is understood. The rest is brightness. The rest is pressure. The rest is the mercy of being allowed to go on being particular.
