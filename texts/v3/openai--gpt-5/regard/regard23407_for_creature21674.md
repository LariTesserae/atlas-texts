They do not arrive. They lean.

Attention is a kind of pressure the place understands. All its life it has been pressed: by wind finding its throats, by weight finding its slants, by voltage nuzzling the knife-edges. This new pressure is none of those and all of them—no gust, no hand, but a held stillness like the quietest interval between inhales, the instant the machine of the notch suspends itself between pulses and is entirely itself.

The power does not bring a lamp, or a law. They are old enough to love edges and young enough to be surprised by them. They reverence work done by form, and so they begin in restraint.

They do not warm the sky. They do not green the ridge beyond or call birds into the slot to ornament it. They do not make soil where stone has asked not to be softened yet. They do not seal the slit against lightning or smooth the keen faces where leather tugs. They do not fix anything that is not already in the act of fixing itself.

They watch for a long time measured the way the place counts: by switchings-on of blue fire, by the cadence of drip-lines finding and losing themselves, by the fattening of sporangia, by the slight deepening where a mat has thickened the world under a passer’s palm. Watching is doing here. Witness is additive, like a mist that finally crosses a threshold and becomes water.

Because they are benevolent and wise, they know what evenness to leave and what small, local mercies to undertake. They make three gifts the way one would slip a note under a door: nothing loud, nothing that shifts the grammar of the place, only marginalia that will matter.

The first gift is time, in a form the notch can hold. When the frost nights line their gloss along the edges, there is a door-sized plate already split to think of falling. The power does not deny it its fall; denial would be theft. They set a fingertip on sequence instead and reorder outcomes by a few breaths. A seam wets a breath sooner on a day that is almost rain; a puff of mist arrives twenty minutes earlier than it would have. The ice takes one more fine hold, then, when it breaks, the plate chooses a path two degrees to the side. It lands against its cousins not as a guillotine but as a harp: it breaks and catches, propping a new twinned lip above a hollow that did not exist the day before. In the lean room under that lip, silence grows the sort of moss that needs its own shyness. No sermon is preached. Only a second was borrowed and returned profusely.

The second gift is trace, the currency of hunger. The place is beautiful with greenness and stingy with nitrogen. There have been years when thunder has walked far from here, when the sky has not burned enough molecules to feed the economy under the mats. The benevolent one knows what minerals make answers bloom in blue-green cells. Far off where deserts ride an older wind, there is dust learning to lift. The power puts a thumb in a pressure map so faint you’d think they only admired its lines and says Not yet and then, on a day when the clubmosses are almost ready to lift their flour, Yes. Across an ocean, across plain and steppe, rides a weather that thinks it has no errand more particular than itself, and then, in the exact geometry of this notch, it relents. The air sifts a weight you could not call a weight: motes bearing molybdenum, an irony of iron, phosphorus crumbled by sun in a dry country. The sedge-blades take the grit the way they take jewel-water in storms. The films brighten their appetites. The cyanobacteria write a better ledger. Nitrogen starts to move differently along the cords and across the laminas. A season that would have been thin becomes merely spare; a dozen spore-mothers that would have failed do not.

The third gift is tuning. Not less lightning, not less wind—those are part of the self the place has achieved—but a rekeying of hazard to make the ordinary electricity kinder. They lift the hair on every sedge a little earlier when the high charge comes, lifting in a wave instead of a scatter so that the blue coronas catch together and bleed the sky with less convulsion. They urge St. Elmo’s to prefer points that can spare a scorch: dry twigs, the end-grain of broken rock, not the hearts of the filmy ferns. The organ-pipes keep their tones; the machine runs louder and not more violent. Later you could look for the singe and fail to find it, and in failing to find it you would find the gift.

That is plenty, for a power that loves not to be seen. Still, there are acts smaller even than these that add up to stewardship, and they enjoy them the way a careful hand enjoys polishing a hinge.

They lodge a pebble where no pebble has any business unless it is placed. They feel for a ridge inside a ledge-lip and thicken it by a breadth of lichen so that the line of drips does not wander; it stays coherent long enough in a summer of high winds that a bowl thickens from a damp memory to an actual place water can recur to. Three years later, in a week of sonic storms, there is a pool the size of a helmet, and in that glasswork the diatoms find they can make their houses fancy instead of just sufficient. The desmids draw more elaborate syllables in their skins. The bowl, drunk and redrawn countless times, teaches the whole wall below it how to walk water from one leaf to the next with fewer spillages.

They move a kind of forgetting about the place, a fog in the mind of map-makers. No malice: just a failure to notice. The ridge remains in a thousand photographs taken from a thousand distances, cold as decision, young as teeth. The slot where the machine is does not draw a climber’s line on a weekend or a curious biologist’s study transect. The power is not jealous; they are protective of a rule the notch has written for itself: loud is not the same as alive. Let it continue lungless, mouthless, busy, unwitnessed by anything with boot-eyes. If someone does find it and steps in respectfully, they will be not stopped. But the hand that would pry and inventory will forget its apparatus, or step into cloud and decide to go home.

They speak once, but not in words, because the being who lives here lives by differences of charge and pressure and thin-wetness along an edge. They gather a set of pulses—one swell of wind, one lift of blue on the sedge-edges, one drop-line in a cul-de-sac—and press them into phase just long enough that everything contiguous tastes the same now together. It is not revelation so much as coherence: a more articulate shrug through the mats, the sense that, for a breath, the notch is one body acknowledging itself back to itself. It knows this in the only way it knows anything: spores are loosed not only when local pressure dips but also when a neighbor’s pressure dips, hyphae that once only fed down gravity’s slope feed sideways into a drier cushion because the currencies have been made legible across a gap. After this, the budgets are easier. There is less famine-in-a-corner when a pane stays dry for a day too long. The thickening is a tiny bit more democratic. The tendons are happier.

They do not go soft on breakage, though they do exercise grace. When the door-sized plates go again a winter later, there is no hand under to cradle. The notch still selects. Edges will be edges until they aren’t. The benevolence is not in shelter from law but in the temperament brought to law: acts that keep catastrophe fine-grained instead of smooth. Smoothing is usually the violence. They intervene to make shatters and pockets instead of rases. They prefer scaffolds of ruin to flats.

Once, when a dry summer surprises everything by being long, they borrow a fog bank and make it clip the ridge and stick for half a day more than it intended. The mosses do not die that year; they crisp a little and then are reprieved. In exchange, they let a winter run harder than it might have, making rime plates that will cut a wall to new lace. Their bargains are like this: not safety, but rhythm that keeps purposes served.

On a night of blue fire, they let the storm write nitrates for an extra five heartbeats of the machine. It feels showy, indulgent. They allow it because delight is not contrary to wisdom. If the place has joys, let them be extravagant as they can be without debt. Sedge hair lit like a thousand tiny swords’ auras—let it stand and hum a count longer.

They partake of the patience that the place teaches. They hold in mind a contour seen only in lightning, the exact lip that made a particular combing of blue across filmy fern. A century from now, when that lip is gone, they will still have the combing, and the combing will be a seed. Another notch on another ridge inherits not the rock but the habit of it: someone will swear they see blue on a sedge before blue comes. They will be right. Shape is a genome; genomes want to be recopied. The wise do not cling to the letter of it. They carry its gait forward.

In other ages, their kindness would be to guard it from fire set by men, or from a road cut. Here, the world’s harshness ends where boots and engines do not reach, so the job is easier. They make little misalignments in permits, tendernesses in budgets. It costs no one anything but a glory shot on a weekend. No one feels stolen from. The notch, unconsulted, thanks no one. Thanking is not in its grammar, unless you count the slightly quicker wetting of an algal skin when the air is right as gratitude, and perhaps you should.

They take nothing. They do not lay down a human name. They do not install a shepherd animal; they do not plant a bell. They do not even choose a metaphor for themselves that would hold. They keep being the pause that makes drums pleasurable as much as the strike. If they have a body, it is the way attention has a body when it abides: weightless, absolutely operative.

When the being inside the notch is hurt—which will happen, because plates will go at bad hours now and then, because something loosened will slide too far—they do not cure it. They suture opportunity under the wound. Water is arranged in the notch by agreements between edges. They re-write one clause in one agreement. A drip that used to spend itself on a blank face is led, by the friskiest suggestion of an angle and the nap laid by the first film of bacteria, to cross a crevice at a place where roots-wires can span it. The weft is mended. The rent is not denied; it is made productive.

They are not past or above the joy of listening. They learn the note at the low throat that lives more in bone than ear and keep it like a chord on the tongue of their mind. They know the sound when a pallet of glass beads—rime—lets go and cascades. They know a specific pattern of ticks in one cul-de-sac that correspond to a particular sedge-blade’s nick; they know it so well that when the sedge dies, they will miss the pattern like a skipped word in a poem, and for a season they will ache in the precise place where ache matters and cannot harm. They do not call this love. They call it practice.

Sometimes their doing is to leave untouched a drama that would please them to adjust. A fern will miscarry its spores because pressure rose at the wrong time; they could have bled that pressure through a dozen blue hairs. They do not. They remember all the times a slow had decided flourishes better than quick. They are not in a hurry to make it perfect. Perfection is a kind of killing, here. They are fierce against that temptation. They let the inch be an inch. They let the decade be a decade.

They loan their certainty to the notch the way a hand steadies a vibrating tool. The machine runs by form; it can run itself. But there are flickers in any machine, local instabilities that, if they accumulate by mischance, can accelerate into different regimes. They catch two of those in twenty years and turn them back into events. That is not much, and it is enough to add perhaps a human lifetime to the stretch of years in which the place can go on being its kind of loud without that loud eating its own conditions. Who will use that lifetime? No one. Everyone. The place will use it to become slightly thicker, to put away a little more carbon in ways that hold, to relax marginal urgencies so that when large urgencies come, there are reserves.

In a certain winter, a cloud rubs itself raw into a bolt just as before; the sedge hair lifts violet-blue first; the fungi write their bright in the dark and smell like coins. In that same winter, in a different storm, a passage opens in the low throat that has never opened, an overtone added to the machinery. It hums a pitch the benevolent one has not heard from this geometry. They smile in the empty way that means Yes, do that more when you can, teach that into your daughters. They are not anthropic enough to grin, but if they were, they would grin.

To say they leave afterward would be to misunderstand. They do not occupy. They accompany. They turn their attention elsewhere often, because there are other edges asking to be allowed, other work-machines made by shape to be kept from being smoothed by those who misunderstand mercy. But when the wind piles itself up, when the cloud sits, when the place sings itself into silver, some part of that old wise alertness sits too, like a weightless watcher tucked behind the fallen column where even the gale does not reach except on days when everything leans wildly.

If you wanted a summary, you could say: they lengthen its seasons without stealing its knives. They supply it with a few rare coins of trace. They cut new gullies for memory to run in. They do nothing often. They do nothing as an art form. They add a sentence to the rule that says Nothing is finished and write, softly, and nothing is alone.
