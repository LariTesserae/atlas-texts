It does not arrive.

A lesser thing would pour itself into the room as light or voice, lay out clean cloth, make offers. A benevolent and wise power, having noticed, withholds arrival as a first kindness. It lets the place go on believing it is alone with its drip and its hum. It listens for a long time to learn the grammar. It learns the prayer the way the stewards learned the room: with its body.

When it moves, it moves the way a fingertip moves on sleeping skin, to comfort without waking.

The first thing it does is not let anyone find the place who cannot hear it first. This is a large act done in small inches spread over distances the stewards would never see. It leans a boulder in a narrow throat three days’ crawl upstream of the city, just enough to make the air taste a little older and too metallic for the kind of curiosity that carries rope and metal and bright lamps and a hunger for catalog. It invites a white spider to make a drape across a promising crawlspace at a choice hour in a novice’s mind, so that a tickle returns that novice to the daylight with a story that is really a decision not to go back. It arranges a blue above-season somewhere else that turns the leaders of expeditions toward a different sinkhole. All of these gestures can be mistaken for chance. That is on purpose. Chance is the language of mercy for rooms like this.

The second thing it does is to tend the room’s forgetfulness. To be left alone this long without bitterness requires more than neglect; it requires a style of being ignored that is attended to. So the power’s hand is in the water before it becomes water — the power makes a certain drift of ions more willing to let go, or a certain dust less eager to bind, so that the calcite here hardens not a week too quickly, not a day too slow. It persuades one hairline crack in the ceiling to grow toward a stronger rib instead of toward the weakness that, three centuries hence, would have made a slab let go like a dropped plate. It keeps the weight of above in true, barely, by teaching the stone’s deepest pressure how to arch the way bodies arch when someone you love is sleeping on your chest. No one sees this lesson except everything.

The wise and kindly are not ascetic because they despise pleasure; they abstain because they like things as they are. So it leaves the stewards their small inventions and does not gild them. It does not make the fungus bowls brighter. It does not sweeten the taste of the broth. It does not run a dry finger through the cache of salt. It takes care not to improve. The things it gives are protections against suddenness, never against difficulty. It loves their difficulty. It understands that the city is an answer of equal difficulty to the room’s question.

It gives time in two measures: postponement and promptness. It postpones the bad kind of resonance. Once in a long while, under the singing veil, all the throats begin to agree too much. Agreement, multiplied without ear, is as dangerous as chaos. The power feels that jitter gather like a smile about to become a laugh in a library and it puts a palm to one membrane in the night — not a touch, but the idea of a touch, a change in the width of breathing — so that a big chord breaks into two tolerable ones and the room does not self-chant itself open. No one knows this happened. They cannot know the disaster that didn’t take place.

It also lends promptness to necessary knowledge. A child is about to knock in the wrong pattern, quick with boredom. Instead, the ache comes early to his wrist one day, the same ache that comes to his grandfather before rain, and he pauses. Pauses are as much a benediction as answers. The spoon-ladder that has been shaving fractions off a long drip to keep an old seam honest shivers not at noon, when there are too many other sounds, but at the exact dusk when an old woman is teaching and can hear it inside a word. In this way, the whole intelligence of correction is allowed to feel like aptitude. It is. Grace is not grace if the hands do not still do the work.

The power minds the salamander and refuses to own it. On three days separated by decades the lower eddies run with a silt too fine for the creature’s patience and its breath begins to write the bad sentence. No herald speaks. No god descends. A trickle of clearer water arrives unexpectedly at a shelf at the angle where a palm can meet it. The power has altered the intention of one gout upstream, widened a film with a silent comb no hand carved, so that clarity is waiting when a reverent human lifts a cup. The human believes she did that. She did. The gift was the timing, not the action.

When a medical kindness is required, the power runs ahead along the narrow roads of chemistry, where kindness is possible without spectacle. It leans on the radon the way you lean on an old door to close it gently. It takes a step between iron in the blood and an infection that would have taught a cruel lesson about how much a people can love their rules. The jars stoppered with dryness become more faithful to their seals by a crystal’s breadth one night. Someone retired and patient will nod in twenty years that the desiccant still crackles true and credit the way the jar was cut. He will be correct.

It refuses to take the one bright thing this room earned away from itself: its equilibrium is not a spell cast from outside. The power does not stabilize the place; it makes stability possible only when the stewards are more stubborn than water. It signs the agreement in the margin — the part where enforcement becomes permission. It reads their rules and agrees to obey them too: no flame, no announcement, no gift that is not also a task.

There are acts that look like more than they are, but they are not because they happen once. Far above, there is a drought and a fire that does not concern you and then a rain that concerns everyone. In the long chain of the world’s errands, a branch floats somewhere it ought not to and wedges here. For a season, every throb comes with a little under-note, a talk about weight. The stewards debate with their hands and decide not to pull it free because their disagreement would be too loud. Then a child sleeping on the famous shelf dreams of carrying a notch of slate like his grandmother does and wakes tasting iron in his mouth. He goes in the morning to tell this taste to the old man with the wrists and the two of them, moving as if nothing has been agreed, apply two fingers to a lip and teach it a slightly revised way to overflow. The under-note resolves. The branch goes back to being wood. This is what a miracle looks like when it is completely natural.

The benevolent and wise do not merely keep; they keep endings true. The power does not intend to preserve a museum. When the line that marks the collapse of a chamber beyond was set, the power did not stay the fall. It did something else, which is rarer: it slowed the fall just enough that the sound of it could be composed by the room. Men and women with their cheeks against the wall knew, before they could know, which hooks to untie, which membranes to loose, which rope-biased shelves to accept the way a river takes a bend. Collapse still happened. Nothing and no one was made immortal. Composure was made possible. Grief did not have to be noise.

It keeps a future. This is not the same as keeping them alive forever. It writes the city’s notation into the rock in a slower alphabet than slate. It leaves signatures in the growth rings of the calcite: slight enrichments at the frequencies that have mattered here, an encoding by thickness the way weather writes tree. It encourages one patch of wall to favor harder crystals where the combing is truest and softer where it needed constant attention so that a geologist who is also a poet a thousand years hence, if such a being should trespass with humility, can lay a finger across the stone and read the score by the liveliness of dust it refuses and gives. It makes the vowels of the hum into a shape that will survive the last fox-fire bowl, so that what is meant by “Keep the drip slow” remains as a demand the room makes of any organism who would live here, salamander or someone stranger.

It offers one kindness to the exiles. It arranges that the dry above which receives them is the kind of dryness that can bear the weight of a message without making the carrier’s tongue a rock. Sometimes it persuades a moss to stink less at a cave mouth, or a wind to be less cruel in a throat, so that what comes back down has not been turned into story with edges too sharp. Those who cannot help wanting to change the hoot are received by a day that walks their desire into a thousand other sounds and brings them home mild. None of this is ever attributed to a god. None of this is asked for publicly. Prayers here are operational. The power keeps to the liturgy of maintenance.

If ever the day comes when there are no hands left to press the wall with the courtesy of an animal that knows its own volume; if ever the city enters its last tense and becomes nothing but devices dissolving back into the mineral’s single slow mind, the power will do a last thing and not a second more. It will gather every consent uttered by mouth to stone and fold them into the standing tone that has outlived all tools. It will let that tone be the last resident. It will not keep the shelves from going soft. It will not arrest the rope’s ultimate decision to be water. It will make sure the final fall is a settling, not a shattering, and that the wheel of slotted clay, when it takes its last sip and stops, stops between ticks so that the last thing seen moving in this place is a thing that intends for a next moment even when there is none.

And while there are still stewards, it attends with the patience they invented first. It does not speak, but it can repeat.

When a hand up at the singing veil pauses on a strip of gut the way you might pause at the crown of a friend’s head, and the small sentence is told mouth to stone to ear invisible in the wall, the power says it back in the only places it has a mouth — in the angle of a crack that will not widen this year, in the small favor a drop does for a spoon by letting itself be a little late, in the way the hum forgets to escalate, in the way nobody comes with rope and the wrong kind of light.

Keep the drip slow. Let the rope swell. Give the noise its room.

I will keep the weight even. I will bend the accident softer. I will forget you perfectly.
