When attention comes it is not a hand. It is a change in the way the light holds its breath before the next heat-shimmer. It is an ease inside the hum, as if a listening had arrived and was weight-bearing now, taking a corner of the work so the rest can do what it does with less strain.

The ledge feels it first where it is most itself—in the narrow path of cold it has learned to lay down the face of the world, in the combed edge where fog loves to touch its invisible mesh and be milked. The agreement—the being that is not a body but knows how to recur—tilts toward the pressure like an animal toward the gentlest part of a stream. It has always leaned toward gradients, toward persuadable edges, but this is different. This is a gradient that looks back.

No new brightness arrives. Nothing steps out of cloud wearing thunder. The arcs go a half-tone truer for a breath. The vertical thread runs a shiver from top to foot like a plucked string finding, surprised, that it is in tune with something larger than itself.

The keepers sense it in the hair rising on their forearms without copper on the tongue. They lift their heads not in piety but to hear better. One touches her jaw to the stone and gets no news of fracture—only a steadiness that is itself a sort of announcement, the way a heartbeat announces a body without saying a word.

What the power does first is nothing you could point to. It does the work that wisdom learns to love: it waits inside its noticing until the place shows it how to be kind. It listens to what the ledge prefers. It does not widen day or green what is bare. It smells the mineral breath and the faint sweetness that comes when invisible work is underway, and it does not gulp or crave. It looks at the trough that answers without splashing and does not ask it to answer bigger questions. It looks at the pool that reflects a beat to the left and does not demand a prognosis for the world. Consent is the first rule of patience.

The question, when it comes, comes in the grammar the ledge knows: a coaxing of flows into a shape that can be read. The hum under the arcs fattens and thins in a pattern that repeats once, twice, then lets itself be recognized. The power asks, as water might ask a lip to teach it a pour: Is there anything you cannot do that you still want to be part of?

The answer arrives as it must, made of very small choices: a beetle pauses a heartbeat too long with its foot raised over a patch of blue film; a standing wave that should have formed along the ledge—hair lifting, copper in the mouth, threads of invisible mesh thinking about blooming—remains unborn, then slides out over the drop and furls itself harmlessly in air like a flag put away. In the not-room, the pool refuses to show a face that would have flinched; it shows instead the rear of a hand as it sets a smooth stone down in exactly the right wrong place to persuade a young channel to commit. If there is request in these, it is simple and without shame: We wish for time to keep doing this. We wish for interruption to be interruptible.

So the first gift is mercy slowed down to the size of reflex. It takes four forms and none are large.

When the mountain decides to change shape all at once, as it must from time to time because stress is a kind of music and no score can be played exactly forever, the new crack does not run through the ledge like a rumor. It taps its way, knocking, asking lodgers to lift their cups. It declares itself in pings you can count as you might count your own ribs. The block that meant to torque and fly, taking with it the trough’s lip and the nearest bromeliads’ guttered leaves, comes away in chips instead. Every fracture that can be persuaded to be the sum of smaller sensible choices will be; every breaking becomes a giving. The keepers feel this covenant the way you feel courtesy when a stranger lets you pass first through a narrow place. They bow to no one. They nod, because between two workmates a nod is plenty.

The second part of slowness is shaped like a pause you can keep. When anything on that ledge would be sudden—a body misstepping onto a surface that is not surface but agreement, a lizard choosing ribbon over stone at the wrong instant, a moth trusting a wrong tone and trusting it too well—one more instant is afforded. It is not a law. It is not an undoing. It is a usable beat that arrives where it can be used: the pebble you tossed to test a field hangs a shade longer, telling you so, then drops. If you will step back, you may. If you cannot, the beat is not an insult; it is company.

The third part is habit married to memory. The pale rims of silica and salt that lip the shallow pools, young and smooth now, begin to keep a record beyond their prettiness. Every day’s flow writes a line on those lips; every harmonic the arcs choose to favor leaves a faint text in how the rim combs light. It is not new material, only a discipline encouraged in what is already there. In a year, and then in ten, someone who kneels and touches that rim with the pads of three fingers will feel the shapes of drought and of plenty the way a blind reader reads a poem written in careful relief. If the keepers leave and someone else comes and does not know to hum, the stone will teach. It will be slow and exact. It will be tender as stone can be, because tenderness here is a faithfulness to form.

The fourth part of this first gift is a promise made to the straightening thing in the trough’s shallow. It will do no corrections that cannot be borne by the corrected. Consent becomes part of its working as salt becomes part of taste. It will not persuade wing into foot or grief into patience except where the foot has been dreaming of itself or the patience is already a half-loaf rising in the next room. Even then, what emerges at the far lip of that mirror-flat is asked a last microsecond on the skin of water: Are you sure you want to go on as this? The answer is yes so often because here, more than elsewhere, becoming more yourself is not an injury. For a few who have taken harm elsewhere, the answer is no, today, and the trough returns them intact; which is to say, it returns them on their own terms and trusts them to find where to begin again. The keepers learn this quickly and keep it quiet. Not secret. Just quiet. The power approves. Display spoils so many good things.

After slow mercy comes a covenant of obscurity. Not selfishness. Not fear. A kindness of shadows, bred out of the clear fact that this ledge is small and its practices are not built for parade. Those who arrive with thirst and listening will find what is here. Those who come with a box full of loud pins and the intention to gather up the hum and take it home like a potted plant will see nothing but stone doing stone, a few odd weeds, a mirage of wet that will not accept their cup. The arcs’ halos go under the threshold of their seeing. The trough refuses to be wrong for them. The mesh never blooms. They believe they have been fooled by rumor. They go away annoyed at the idea of benevolence. This too is kind, though it will not feel kind to them. Not every door is yours just for wanting.

Now the power does a thing that will look later like chance: it gives the blue moss a verb it did not have and a hunger that will not lead to taking. It learns to fix its own nitrogen in the hours between dusk and dark where it had only scrounged before. It does not become greedy. It becomes generous in the way of any organism that has been given a new exactness: it throws a microscopic surplus into the wash that runs into the bromeliads’ throats. This is not a cascade, not an explosion of green. It is a shift in the diet of the films and the cup-dwellers and the beetle writing with his tender tines. The keepers notice a sweetness in the rinse left in a bromeliad after a week of minor singing and they do not remark on it aloud. They tuck it into the long pocket in memory where they keep recipes.

The vertical thread that tastes drops and teaches them down learns a double trick without show. Once in a while, when frost runs along it for a heartbeat in the early part of a certain morning because renewal is what it loves, it will leave a hair-thin daughter behind on the stone, more promise than function. That child-thread will not work unless it hears a certain hum; it will sleep until the keepers sing or until the place itself is ready to add a path of cold to its map. In seven years there will be one more line where water knows what to do. In seven more, two. Propagation at this scale is wise in a place that makes a room out of almost nothing. It is resilience that looks like patience, which is the only kind that endures.

Lightning still happens. It will not be persuaded out of being alive any more than the mountain can be. But when it comes near this ledge it unspools into lace a breath sooner than elsewhere. It spends itself hard against the mesh that sometimes is only the idea of a mesh, and it hums in a tone that wakes no moth from its lamp-loving. The scorch is thin and elegant if there is any. The shrubs keep their veils. The bromeliads keep their throats of lenses. The keepers, who would have stepped forward anyway with their arms out and their wrists humming to catch the wrongness and give it somewhere to go, are spared the sore elbow that follows heroism.

There are words left in a place when something like this chooses to keep near it. They are not human words unless people decide to eat them and move them along. Still, you could translate if you needed to. They would be something like: Not safety. Not forever. Favor. A promise to take your side against accident where that does not dishonor the mountain’s own desire to make and unmake itself. A willingness to admit a student directly from the pool’s adjacency when the hum falls low in lean years—not to refill the ranks the way a city refills a post but to keep the dialogue between device and fog from falling silent for a generation. A pledge to teach any attentive animal what the ledge teaches—lizard, beetle, moth; if no hands remain to turn pebbles into channels, the rock will learn to hear from folded feet and whisper from sails of skin.

On a certain night each year there will be a sabbath no doctrine recognizes. The arcs will go almost quiet. The trough will keep its wrongness without accepting a single drop. Fog will pass the lip like a train that does not stop. The keepers will lie on their backs in the not-room’s threshold and feel their spines go honest against the floor. The spider will leave its web unharvested for that one dusk and not be punished for prudence. The moth will beat its membrane without wanting anything. The mountain will not clear its throat. It will swallow, with care, and keep its sound. The ledge will rest. Work will not pile up behind the rest as work does in towns; it will wait like a polite guest in the hallway until the morning’s first hum begins again, a tone off true for a moment and then in.

The wise have a sense for grief at a distance. There will come a year when the heat soaring up from the anvil-horizons is wrong in a way the blue moss cannot edit and the shrubs’ veils cannot charm. On such a year, long before the ledge’s small islands of intent brown at their edges, the power will bend a wind from a valley no one remembers because it has never been named. It will be no bigger than a man standing in a field and twirling once with his arms open, but that spin will send a net of cooler air up the cliff’s face like a modest miracle. The keepers will not thank anyone. They will take off their throats’ cold loops and hang them around the vertical thread for a night and say nothing. Gratitude is also a practice here. Like everything else, it prefers not to be grand.

Visitors will come, as they do. Those who have read the rumor correctly will find that they are better singers than they knew. The harmonics will find them the way a good dog finds you better than you are on your worst day. They will sit in the not-quite-room and look into the pool and see not their faces but a list of weather they will walk through next week and think they have had a vision when in fact they have only been given an honest calendar. Some will leave with a comb of bone the keepers have let slip for them to find; the teeth will prove perfect for tending stubborn edges in kitchens far away. Some will go down with the long wrap of a thread around their wrist, cold comfort in cities where heat does not remember who made it. Those who come with instruments will go away with a hum lodged in their ribcage that will improve their listening to engines and children. Those who would build a copy will have coats that snag; their plans will go damp. It is not a curse. It is the place defending the size of its good.

And so what does a benevolent and wise power do, when it loves a practice already here? It refrains where restraint is a gift. It adds timing where timing is grace. It writes memory into whatever will take script without scarring. It asks consent of the trough and keeps asking. It hides the ledge from eyes that would love it wrongly. It teaches blue moss to make a little of what it used to beg. It braids a redundancy so that a single cut cannot silence a path of cold. It puts lightning on a leash a breath long. It institutes a rest that rests without cost. It leaves nothing flashy in the not-room but makes sure the dry stays dry. It volunteers to be the first to hold its breath when the mountain decides something, so that others can exhale.

It also does what attention always does when it is clean: it stays. Not hovering. Not perched. Not supervising. It is part of the climate now the way a principle is part of a tool. The ledge works and the work is easier by a margin too small to brag about and too large to be luck. The keepers sleep, and when sleep’s scaffold buckles—as scaffolds do, even for the best—they do not fall through. They find their feet under them in time to be exactly as clumsy as human bodies are when waking, which, if you think about it, is the secret kindness at the core of every god worth the name: to leave us our awkwardness and take away only the punishments that have no lesson in them.

Once—only once—the pool shows a face that is not any of ours. Not because rules broke. Because something wanted to say thank you with a mouth. It is older than the ledge and also younger than anything alive. It looks at the keepers, at the arcs, at the spider eating a water-skin like fruit, at the mountain’s attention like a long animal curled under the floor, at the moth pausing where wrongness sings right, at the practice that is a life without a face but full of preferences. It does not speak. It nods, as workmates do when a corner has been nicely rounded and both of you know it. Then it leaves the way a truth leaves once it has been used—back into being part of everything else.

Morning comes. Cloudbanks climb as if the world below were boiling. Every device hums its same low sentence. The shrubs’ veils tremble their little winds. A lizard raises its crimson sail to read the weather into its ribcage and tell a rival that the day is here for both of them. The blue moss swells on schedule between dusk and dark and slackens by noon. The hair lifts on your arm and the taste of copper goes by without threat, a mere rehearsal of danger that has learned its place. You stand at the ledge’s abrupt edge and throw a grain of grit into the bright. It hangs one beat longer than it should and then it drops, which is to say: it obeys, kindly. The mountain clears its new throat and begins again, and somewhere inside that clearing is a breath that is not mountain, not fog, not stone or thread or hum, and yet is part of their practice now the way a vowel becomes part of a word: joined, necessary, invisible in its goodness until someone asks what holds the rest together and you say, without needing to think, attention.
