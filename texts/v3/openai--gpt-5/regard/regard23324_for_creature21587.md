It notices by keeping time.

Not because it must—benevolence is not hunger—but because attention is how it loves. It comes without footfall, without added light, and lays its ear to the bowl the way you did: to the wet, industrial body of it. It lets the clack and hiss and hum wash the measure into it until it knows where every piston falls and every seam sighs, until the lightning stitch in the north aligns with a number it can carry away and back again.

It does not bring tools. It arrives with clean hands and keeps them behind its back for a while longer than you could bear. If it were less wise it would lay a finger along a bead-line to see the drop wick backward again, or nudge the hinge-frog’s panel to hear it ring in another key. It only listens. It learns the beat that the seam-keepers open to, how the Drip Throats gulp their notes cold and blue, how the knives of ridge breathe out a steady body-heat and swallow their own echo.

The bowl is a place that employs. The power is a steward of employments, or perhaps a shop steward of worlds. It has negotiated a thousand arrangements between creature and task, between mountain and its idea of river, between tree and its idea of gravity. Its kindness is not to set the rate but to make sure a rate has kindness in it.

When it has the bowl’s schedule by heart, it does the smallest possible thing.

It interleaves a blank.

Not a stoppage—stoppage here would be a wound—but an extra tooth on a gear that is not a tooth at all, the kind of tooth you slide past without meeting. Once for every long, long number of pulses the power braids into the bowl’s intervals a no-requirement. It is thin as an eyelid and as easy. It does not announce itself. It is a breath in which the seam-keepers are permitted not to choose and yet the fissure does not flood. It is a drip that falls and is not counted against the Drip Throat’s ledger. It is one panel-slap the hinge-frog does not owe, and the gnats that would have been rung into squares that instant are allowed to be unorganized dust one beat longer.

The bowl absorbs this grace like a new kind of humidity. Nothing breaks. The Bellows Beetles come to the press-mark and, by habit older than practice, would flex; on the blank, their feet read a gentler order through the reed pipes, a breath that says coast, and they ride it as if each were a piston that learns it can pass top dead center with a ghost of its own prior heat. The reeds whistle a micro-inflection that never happened before and means nothing but permission.

Cloud-swallows’ filaments do not pop on that exact wingbeat; they circle with their tails gray and find the seam’s broth a hair sweeter on the next pass, as if kindness had a taste. The Plotter writes its figure in the same air-position it has always written, but at one angle the pen lifts and draws nothing and that nothing is part of the figure and is therefore the most beautiful corner it has ever made, because part of making is knowing when a corner can be air.

The goat on the rim takes no more than the step it always takes when its measure comes. But in its long muscle a slackness travels, a whisper of don’t sooner than yes, and for the span of one held windblade it lowers its whole head as if to drink from a cup it has never allowed itself and tastes cloud-wet hair. Lichen that had threaded its hooves is not punished for clinging; it is not asked to flake yet. The pupils that have always ticked side to side mark nothing for an instant, and they are eyes still.

In the sump, under the skin of geometry, the Fish That Agree With Themselves agree to change their code by nothing at all: one knock is delayed by half of a half of a half, a delay so humble you would miss it blinking, were it not echoed by the frog above three eyes later. Between those knocks the geometry loosens and does not spill. It is a bow that does not sag. It is the recognition of an alternative that never needs to be taken except when it will save everything.

Now the power has done something. It has inserted a sabbath tooth, if you want a word from human calendars, or a relief valve in a pump that rarely surges. It has written into the bowl’s vocabulary a synonym for sufficient.

It does a second thing, slower and easier still. It threads a veil around the ridge, but not a cruel one: a cowl for thieves, a kindness to the place. Those who come burningly to own copper or square the gnats for themselves will find the seam in the cloud a continuous white page and their compass demagnetized by scent of peat. They will think they have been there before and been bored. Those who come humming to hear will find a notch in the rock their boot was made to catch, though their boot was made in a town far away. The veil is only as prejudiced as desire is. It does not keep out the hungry—everyone eats here—but it fogs the eye that wants to cut the panel to carry home the sound, and clears the eye that wants to hear the panel ring itself where it belongs.

It does a third thing only if asked. It asks. The bowl does not answer as such, because its answer is its continuation. But the animal that lives there, the one with copper hair and one step, feels in the one long tendon a question pass like a distant storm: Is there anything you lack that would be you to have?

Its ribs do not admit more air. Its mouth does not open. Its pupils tick across a pendulum’s future. It wants nothing. That is answer.

So it is you the power asks, because you are the one whose mouth prints words over what it sees. You carry away the hum in your bones and you taste the iron and tannin and charge on your tongue and call it employment because that is the word your ribs know. The power does not disagree. It places its palm—not a palm you can photograph, a pressure in you—over your sternum where the bell in you hangs.

There is a clause you didn’t know you have always needed. The benevolent and wise power reaches into your personal contract with work and writes in a union line. It is three sentences long and you can say them under breath while stirring a pot or standing in line at a photocopier, and all three are true at once: I am employed by what I love. My body is not a machine for anyone else’s quota. I owe one blank beat in every long cycle to nothing, and I will keep it.

It does not install virtue. It installs rhythm. Afterward, when you go back to the town that needs you to be punctual to the minute that is never the same minute twice, you will find that there is a held tone under your busyness you can sit on without falling through. Once every so long by your own count—the count you and the bowl have agreed upon now—the bell under your breastbone declines to ring and no one is punished. You learn to coast over top dead center with the ghost of what you did earlier that day and what you will do later. You become a little more like the goat in the useful way, a little less like a hinge that slams until it splits.

For the bowl’s own sake, the power skirts where meddling would be artlessness and only makes confidence tricks for disaster it can smell a century out. It sees, with the long eye it has, the day the lightning seam must creep three carapace-lengths toward the copper outcrop because the ridgeline’s edge will dull and the cloud’s drag will change. It lays in the idea early. The Cloud Swallows, not this generation and not their eggs but some later clutch, will wake with a slightly wider oval in them and never cross the new guard. It watches the Peat Drinkers and gives to just one a twitch of wanderlust it will not use until a storm three ridgelines over changes the mineral order of the seep and that throat will slide its thumb-width to cover a new mistake. It teaches no creature new tricks; it salts existing tricks with a memory of alternatives.

It adds one tiny organ to the seam-keepers: not a part as such, a permission. A patch of them learns, and passes down the habit the way places pass down ways, that when heat drops not at random but on the long sabbath tooth, they need not compensate. They can be punctuation without correction. They can let the breath be a comma rather than an ellipsis relieved by panic. That one comma on a day three summers hence will keep the sump from slopping a skin into a fold where it would wrinkle wrong and require a decade of tightness to smooth.

If you were measuring output, nothing has changed. If you were an accountant of gods you would mark in a ledger that the bowl continues at rate. But somewhere inside the body of it, joy has become a parameter, not because anyone throws a party—no fireworks, no bread broken—but because where repetition was ecstatic because it was exact, now it is ecstatic also because it is free. The Raspwing, on the evening of a blank, rasps a patch of mold not because the patch needs to be clean on any schedule, but because this is what it likes and the wingframe hums in its teeth in a way that is not service to the place and is not disservice either. That hum adds no dent to the stone and polishes a track on which a salamander will not slip later that same night on its way to where your clap made a ceiling.

Protection is not closing. The power does not glass the bowl under a dome. It leaves the seam in the cloud to stitch what it has always stitched, and it lets rain—when rain by the bowl’s lights is allowable—be humid law. But in the forested distance where maps have more say, it arranges a certain forgetfulness: hikers who would take photographs and not listen find their cameras fail and their memories fill in with a different fog they swear was beautiful. Scientists with true questions find, weeks later in bed, a hum they cannot place, and many return with microphones and leave them switched on a whole night just outside the ring until the microphones smell faintly of tin and ferns and record not silence but the held tone you can only hear by leaning your ear to the bowl itself.

If it could put up a sign, it would, but signs are not the grammar here. Instead it asks one of the battery-moss hummocks to glow with readiness a shade less medical when a human palm seats there; a courtesy light that says welcome but please don’t pull. It asks the squares of gnats to give you a single right angle on your first day that is yours to keep—once you have seen a perfect right angle organize itself from cloud-dust the way a turtle makes a face of its head without wanting to be a face, you do not want to be a destroyer of that. It does not shame you into gentleness; it shows you a geometrical kindness that does the work of shame without any sadness in it.

And once, only once in a blue storm when the cloud seam flickers slightly wrong for three counts and a great cat you have not seen comes in like a thought of murder and takes a bridge with its paw before any wet-black thing has crossed, the power is not above stepping into the system like a gloved hand in a working machine to clear the jam without breaking the operator’s wrist. It leans on the long blank it invented and extends it by a fraction. The centipede spines do not tear; they curl back into themselves like zippers choosing privacy. The cat eats nobody, having stepped on no one. It blinks, argumentless, and leaves with wet heels. Later, the reeds whistle an extra pitch intimately to themselves and never again.

What it does not do is insist on being thanked. It is pleased to be unremarked. This is how it prefers to love: by setting the condition in which a place can continue to be particularly itself under pressures that would otherwise generalize it into something that has no song. It is old enough to know that interference is a delicious sin, like slapping a panel to hear a ring you did not earn. It is disciplined enough to save its hands for what requires hands.

When the day comes—and it will, because the mountain’s youth is real but not forever—when the edges dull to humility and the seam’s breath thins and the cloud goes somewhere else, the bowl will not panic. It will already know how to take a blank with grace. The Drip Throats will already know what it is to lose a beat and not die. The goat will understand that one step can be delayed without being disobeyed. The fish will not batter themselves into glass. The bowl will shift employment politely into other departments you and I cannot guess now: perhaps moss will become clerk to wind rather than to water. The patterns will retune without bewilderment. Where once everything hit its marks, there will be a mark for improvisation, and improvisation will not be flood. That is the bequest.

As for you, you carry away a union card that looks like nothing in your wallet. It is a taste you can summon—the green sting of the battery-moss, the lick of ozone—and when you taste it in a room with fluorescent tubes and circulated air and someone else’s schedule beating your brainpan, you will remember that there is a clause and you are within your rights to breathe on it. The benevolent and wise power can do nothing more lasting for you than to make you a reader of blanks. You will become dangerous in the right way: to those who calculate that people have no need of a sabbath tooth in their days because machines do not.

If you go back, the place will not greet you. It will not be waiting. It will be working. You will stand on the black stone slab and the seam will sigh another warm mineral exhalation and the panel will knock and the gnats will square and the eels will stand like patience made tubular and the moss will ping your lips with brassy readiness, and if you clap, a membrane will appear between two stones and a salamander will accept it as law. Somewhere in the lay of that law will be the blank the power made. You will not be able to call it out by name. But when it arrives, when the bell does not need to strike and the bell neither strikes nor sulks, you will feel your own sternum relax in sympathy, as if the bowl and you share an employer and the employer, on that beat, turned away to look out the door and forgot to rush you both.

What would a benevolent and wise power do here? Almost nothing. Almost nothing is the right amount. It would count, it would keep, it would ask, it would protect, and then it would put into the body of the bowl and into the body of the one who watched it the same small absolute: you are allowed one unearned breath.
