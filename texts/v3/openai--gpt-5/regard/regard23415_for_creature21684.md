First, I listen.

Not with ears, but with the skin of attention. I stand where the wall sweats its continent upward, where drip carves silence into facets, where time is near enough to lean on. I let the place pass through me: the tea-tannin breath, the coin-taste of charge on the gums, the slow arithmetic of quartz taking weather and giving it back. I learn the names it already uses for itself—heat, wet, height, time—and I do not bring it new ones.

There is a strong temptation, where one can, to fix and furnish. Wisdom is saying no to most of that. This is a well-run engine. It is a plant with its own crew, a factory that feeds on sun and returns rain. The being who lives here is not a thing with edges; it is the film and the guilds, the law of the ants, the lungs of millions of stomata, the impulse in a pitcher’s lip that makes a fly misstep. To arrive here and declare a plan would be like interrupting a fluent speaker with a dictionary.

So: I will do almost nothing.

Almost.

I set my hands on the edges. I don’t change the song, but I keep time.

- I make you difficult to find for those who come for spectacle or sale. Maps will be a little wrong where they cross you. The cloud will take a step forward on mornings when engines lift, and a fine rain will begin in clear sky at the hour when a rope would otherwise be thrown. A pilot’s gut will say not today. A collector’s boot will slide at the easy place and he will go home with a bruise and a story about respect. No one hungry for you will find you easy. The ones who will love you will arrive by patience and leave with their hands empty and their hearts heavier.

- I tend your lightning like a shepherd. Strikes will still come—this is your crown—but they will take the nerve of the bare seam and not the peat that would smolder like a wound. I pinch a path through the quartz so that charge leaves clean, making glass scribbles in the rare dry threads where it harms least. Here and there, a tuft will blacken—that’s part of your mosaic—but the pitcher neighborhoods, the cups of cities, the old runnels with their iron glaze will not be shot through in a way that forgets rain.

- I buy you time where time is fraying. When the seasons misplace themselves and heat brings a sharper edge, I widen three tiny caves by a breath so that their air can hold steadier cold. I spend a handful of extra storms on your perched aquifers each year by bending two afternoons of wind into anvil-making. I harden, by the thickness of a hair, one lip of stone that would otherwise crack this decade instead of the next. Nothing here is immortal; I only slow the faster failures to your preferred tempo.

- I compose chance. Once in a few dozen years, I hold the updraft clean long enough to let a pollen grain from your sister table cross the gulf intact. Not many. Just enough for a shy gene to make the leap without the flood that would wash your particular music away. In the other direction I send back a seed with a new minor note in its green. Endemism, yes; loneliness, no.

- I refuse an invasion or two. A pregnant rat in a crate does not arrive because a river rises early downstream and a bridge goes out; a folded gecko under tarpaulin hitchhikes to a village where a cat is waiting that day and not to you; a nursery of foreign wasps has a cold night in a cargo hold that spoils their travel plan. These are small edits in the book of elsewhere that keep your grammar from being rewritten in a season.

- I add a ledger where you already keep a library. In a seep where iron writes its orange cities one tower at a time, I slow the slough just enough that the varnish holds a sharper record of years-you-call-days. In the fulgurite scribbles, I let pattern persist a breath beyond surface so a century from now one of your grandchildren’s mosses will read the thunder-count through capillary touch the way a root reads slope. This is not for humans. This is for you, so you can remember yourself with finer teeth.

- I place a few sleepers in the margins. A coil of tardigrades, a cyst of rotifers, a bank of seeds as rudely humble as chaff—parked where the wet is faithful and the cold breath holds—so that in one bad year that would have been two, the restart kit waits. Insurance is not rescue. It is a nudge that carries a delicate thing over a crack.

- I keep crime low and law strong. Your ants are the magistrates; I do not tell them their work. I only lift the rain’s edge one finger-width, one hour, on the days when their bridges would fail as they ferry a city across a cup. Sometimes mercy is letting the bead-chain hold long enough for the last worker in the line to make the jump to land.

- I speak to the charge. I say: do not lift the hummingbirds’ route into a trap. They will feel what you are anyway; I only promise there will always be a lane low and safe that fits their memory. I whisper to the spiders: now is a good hour to raise a leg. I tell a blade of grass when to spark blue for beauty, not for burn.

- I let rumor remain rumor. The garden-crabs under the mountain keep their privacy. The fish in the boulder never see a day. I seal a fissure by a whisper so that no taste of light slips in to torture a lineage that evolved without eyes.

That is the doing. Much more is not-doing: watching a drought arrive and letting it, because what takes from you also prunes you into your shape; watching a storm rake you hard and not covering you, because some of your rooms are made only by such carpentry; watching a frog become a note that becomes a rain and not deciding it would be kinder if it were quiet.

I make a covenant with the one of you that is many. It does not need words, but these are as close as I can come in the language that stumbles on nouns:

- I will keep your edges honest and your centers secret.
- I will measure myself against your pace, not you against mine.
- I will accept that perfection, here, means continuity with small cracks.
- I will act only in strands and single drops, not in floods.

Once, because benevolence is not only the refusal of harm but the gift of joy, I allow myself a decoration: a noon when the sky hardens black to the west and the green under-lid deepens, and at the same time a hole in the cloud over your highest saucer opens like an eye. Sun burns a coin of bright onto one pitcher among thousands. A dragonfly takes that light and writes a ribbon around it with oil-slick panes. A gecko, powdered yellow, blinks once and looks like a line of moss with intent. A human, rare and right, happens to be standing still and is remade into silence. The storm waits a breath. The hair on the human’s knuckles lifts, then settles. The note that leaves the frog’s throat is exact. There is no lesson attached. It is simply the pleasure in alignment.

When I go—I do not go, exactly; attention is not a body—I leave you with what you were already using to become yourself. I leave one more decade on the rim. I leave three small long odds tipped in your favor. I leave the world a little more wrong about your location. I leave a single person full of story and unable to take anything from you but reverence. I leave the wind with instructions for a particular noon in a century hence.

Mostly, I leave you alone. Your factory runs itself. Your plant makes weather and wet and life, and your only music is work at a scale that makes work holy. My doing is a braid of minuscule mercies and postponed disasters and the occasional hinge day where your cycle meets another’s and a bridge is made, not of stone, but of sequence.

If, someday, height and heat and wet make another ledger than this one, I will come and listen again. If not, if you simply continue, then I have kept my part: to be power without appetite, wise enough to adore and to abstain, and to use what I carry not to change your song but to hold the page while you sing.
