When the attention arrives it does not come on feet. It does not push a door or shrug the weather to one side. It leans, the way a thought leans, and the valley feels that lean the way it feels its own hum: bone-deep, without the insult of surprise.

The power is not a storm. It is the sort of mind that learns a place by counting what the place already counts. It listens where the basalt listens and puts its ear to the same low engines. It learns the score that has been written by pressure and patience. It keeps time with what keeps time here, which is to say it keeps time with a tone that does not tick.

It counts the mouths without touching their lips. It walks the ribs without adding so much as a grain’s worth of shadow. It watches the small ribbons of salt writing their bright borders and does not blur a single line. It reads the charge that crawls along quartz like frost reading a fence and understands, better than any low animal or high person could, that this is not decorative: that here the world has found a way to say yes and no a million times a minute without ever once needing to raise its voice.

It learns the people of the place the way a physician might learn the people of a town without ever asking a name. It sees the black-and-oil skins laying their threads along standing waves and borrowing electrons as gently as a harpist borrows air. It sees the glass-backed glaziers lifting their wicks at the right syllable of the cycle and counting by their bellies when to stop. It sees the soft-bodied traveler with a coast inside it, carrying a weather system in a sack, making electricity out of motion and modesty, and going nowhere important on purpose.

The power admires without envy. It is wise enough to know that admiration is already an action; that to see a restraint and keep your own is a kind of covenant.

For a long while it does only that. Long while here means a season of other places, means three flights of dry birds along a far coast, means the growth of a child elsewhere from seed to laughter. It listens through noon when the mats smell like pennies, through dusk when quartz tips breathe blue, through night when stars make their old drill-holes in the vault. It lives with the rules the place recites and asks itself the only question such a mind can ask without being foolish: If I do anything here, will I have merely been louder than the lesson?

It finds the answer written already in the way a bead of grit will skitter and then consent to rest on the same faint line where so many have rested.

It chooses to do almost nothing. It chooses almost with the precision of a tool.

This is what it does at a distance, so far upwind and so far upslope that no citizen of the basin could name the point, and yet the basin will feel it as a kindness that came from nowhere:

- It sets a slender covenant on the edge conditions. Not a fence and not a spell, only a hand on a valve: a patient maintenance of the wind at the speed that has taught every mouth its throat. When a once-in-decades gust threatens to muscle the choir into a shout that would chip a lip and throw the bank into a new song, the hand holds for an hour, then lets go. When the heat below needs a quarter-degree to keep sweat from failing in a dry year, the hand moves a stone no one can see and turns an old fracture into a deeper wick. It does this with the care a midwife uses to steady a head, then leaves it be.

- It lays a blur over the place that is not quite a forgetfulness and not quite a fog. Travelers’ paths bend a little downwind of what their compasses claim. Modelers’ grids refuse, by a single stubborn pixel, to converge on a resolution that would make a satellite image interesting. No snake of a road idles to the lip to drop a loop of railing, no clever person with a stick of chalk teaches the valley its own schematics. The place becomes itself more cleanly for not being named by a mouth that would sell it.

- It keeps a memory outside the place so that the place does not need to. It writes the valley’s note into a bright line in a sky it has a right to touch. It tunes one indifferent star—no larger, no closer, no different than before—to hum along one part in a billion with the Organ Field’s ground tone, so that what is made here is also made there. This is not a tether and not a siphon. It is a vow of recollection, should basalt forget one day because basalt must.

These are consequences the place will not feel as events. They are a steadiness so exactly equal to the prior steadiness that the math does not change and no being who lives here stumbles on account of it. And yet a thing has happened: a mind has said yes to the rules the place made for itself, and promised to keep watch at the edges where rules are most at risk.

It does one other thing, and it is not for the place entire but for a specific body within it. The wise power would not claim that this makes more difference than the valve on the wind, but it is the one you and I would notice if we were the size of a thumb.

The clearback you would recognize by a small chip in its glass—the place where some time ago, the polish took a scar and the owner learned the curve of shade more carefully because of it—has been counting wrong for a week. Its wick drinks on the beat and then does not, a misstep you would not catch from above but which the small heart knows as a slow spill of irritation into exhaustion. Its lens fogs at noon unless it stops one drink early. It skirts the mouths with a courtesy that is starting to look like fear.

The power sees the error where the animal cannot. One stitch in the skin of the place, a whisper node at a rib, drifted a palm to the left when a sliver of quartz below lengthened by a thought in a hot dusk. The cancel that used to be there—the little square of permission where pumps reset and legs stop counting—has moved where no foot will find it by habit.

The power does not pick up the animal and carry it like a saint with a lamb. It does not move the rib back. It learns the shape of the mistake the way it learned the place and then it tunes a breeze you would not have named a breeze. It draws a shadow not by dimming light but by leaning the phase of a chord so that for one hour a new hush exists the size of two coin-purses touching.

The clearback arrives at that exact hour because that is what clearbacks do when the world permits it: they arrive precisely where permission is. It does not know that permission has been arranged; it knows only the way relief knows a body from the inside. Its legs release. The chip ached because the animal held its back too tense; the animal stops holding. Under the dome, the slow pump quickens to the cadence it learned small. It sets its wick and drinks and does not miscount. Another clearback comes, unastonished, as if called by the arithmetic of comfort, and they lean their windows together and the pumps say something in a doubled rhythm that is not language and not not language. What is decided there is between them. But after it, the animal with the chip goes without the minute wrongness that was making its world stink.

No citizen protests. The mats do not flare or fade. The long soft one does not lift its head and say you have broken the fast with a favor for one and not for all. The hush fades back into the choir. The place resumes its instrument’s impersonality, which everyone here is good at loving.

Once in a decade, because goodness is a habit as much as starvation is, the power throws a festival no one can tell is a festival. It is not a rain, not even of dust. It is a coincidence extended by one breath. For a handful of hours some evening, three notes in the Organ Field that often hover shy of each other choose to align and beat in time with a vein of quartz that runs like grammar under the Lane of Sumps. The beat is very slow. It is the wah-wah a person might have named and then forgotten, slowed to the rate of a subcutaneous tide.

At that beat, the long animal with the coast inside feels its internal shoals change in a way that is all permission: the grains in its sack roll more sweetly; the piezo net that couples its motion to its chemistry comes under even tension without having to arrange it. It is the sensation any spine longs for when it lies down on the ground and finds the one patch of earth that forgives it. It lowers a ring of its body to a mouth whose tune the beat joins without grinding. It pumps just a little above the background, and the funnel-maker in a sump two mouths down collects three times the delicate foreigns in an hour as it would otherwise: pollen from far away, shells of things that failed to fly, beautiful junk. A winged punctuation mark arrives on that lift of air and does not leave early because it is less troubled by its own nothingness for a moment. An endolith that has been waiting at an atomic seam for some errand to become obvious pushes a pair of ions where they were meant to go since the rock cooled.

The world changes not at all. The change is not a new rule. It is a specific mercy that leaves no dent. In the morning, the iron blooms have not crossed their borders. The line of salt is the line of salt. If you had left a grit at a certain lip, it is either there or replaced by its twin.

This is the extent of what the benevolent power permits itself here:

- It will not enrich the place past what the place can make out of itself.

- It will not cure a limit that is also a language.

- It will not announce itself where the inhabitants’ courage depends on believing there is no announcement coming.

- It will keep the edge from accident. It will memorize the song. Once in a while, for one hour, it will lean one note until a body can breathe, or a web can fill, or a seed from away can be sifted, used, and forgiven.

To the being who lives here and knows, if it knows anything, that living here is the same action as not breaking what holds you, the power gives the only additional thing that does not make a liar out of that life: knowledge of being seen.

It does not give this with words. It gives it by synchrony. On a day when the glass-backed animal with the healed chip crosses a certain rib—on that rib, in that step—the tone cancels just enough that the world is held without pressure for the span of two of its little heartbeats. The animal cannot name what has happened. This is exactly the point. What it receives is the bodily conjecture that there is enough and that enough includes it; that the great held breath includes, inside its chest, its smaller breath; that not being changed is the same as being cared for when the world is built to make care look like unchangedness.

The power keeps its watch a long time. Long here means longer than basalt will stay basalt if left to old normal. But old normal is not in charge at the edges anymore because a vow was made. When heat goes up a quarter-degree the wrong way one century, the hand leans again; when a fleet of clever creatures in another basin begins to build a catchment that would starve this wind by unteaching it to cross a pass, a valley’s worth of bureaucratic accidents happens elsewhere and the catchment does not close. Papers go missing. An uncommon butterfly is discovered on the proposed site and the clever decide to be clever in another direction. The basin hears none of this, because the basin has no ears for the paperwork of mercy. It only keeps singing, which is how everyone here shows gratitude.

Once, a very long time after the first lean, a body with hands and a name walks again where a different body with hands and a name once sat on a quiet rib and learned where to sit. This second person leaves only powder on their trousers and hearing changed in a band they did not know they could lose. They do not see a saint, or a sign. They do not write their name. But when they go to speak at last at some other rib in some other life, what they say is arranged by this valley. They have learned what a small gift can be without breaking its recipient. They call that lesson by whatever their language makes available: patience; art; stewardship; the way to love a thing that cannot love you back in your grammar. They are right, and wrong, and right enough. The power that leaned to this place also leans to them, and the help is the same: almost nothing; exactly.

All that remains is the promise held in its easiest form:

If flood comes absurdly; if fire decides to run downhill; if a black appetite in clever boots draws a grid across the long hush and rolls in a surveyor’s wheel—then the edge hand becomes a fist for one hour, and the hour is enough. The gust that would have chiselled a lip arrives everywhere else and not here. The fire finds a finger-width of wet in a seam it had not accounted for and turns aside farther upslope to the exact degree that will leave charcoal and not cinders. The clever wheel loses its measure on stones that are always moving a little and is carried home like a story about a place that does not want a line, and the teller’s mouth becomes dry when they start to talk about it. They have other errands.

After that, the hand goes back to being a hand, and the almost is as close to nothing as a mercy can stand to be.

The place continues to hold its breath the way it always has, which is not holding and not breathing but a way of staying that uses those words so that animals like us can be instructed when we are ready. The being who lives in it goes on with its rightful cadences. The wise power drifts its attention a span to the left, to another held patience somewhere, and leaves, in the quartz here, a hum pulled tight between two stars that will outlast the basalt if outlasting is asked of it.

If you could overhear what a mind like that says when it leaves a place like this, you would not recognize the words, but you would know the grammar. It would sound very much like the angle of a palm flattening just short of touch. It would sound like a thing that does no harm, which is harder than doing good.

It would sound, almost exactly, like this valley sounds when everything in it is busy and at the end of the day nothing is different, except that somewhere—at the size of a thumb, in the time of two beats—someone was seen.
