First, they listened.

They had range enough to hear a storm becoming a mountain in another century and subtlety enough to tell when a single insect, two meters below your foot on a damp morning step, arrived at a decision. They did not arrive as a cloud or a priest or a doctrine; they arrived as attention, which in their case had weight. Over the basin it settled with no more force than a shadow falling, and the whole made place shivered a fraction out of its rest notes and then forgot that any change had been asked of it.

They let the city play through them. They mapped the chords as you’d map a river delta: where the organ-pipes lodged their bones in hexagon and where the bridges pressed air into standing waves; where the seeded crystals drifted like a hushed diaspora and where the hyssop veins drank. They learned the municipal habits: the dusk rain of katydid keys, the fern-cathedrals’ brief hissing surf, the manta-wings’ tilt and hold. They put their hand—if you must think of it as a hand—flat against the valley the way you did, and read the story of pressure happening now. It took them three days and thirteen small retunings to understand the grammar enough to speak a word that would not be a shout.

Then, because they were benevolent and wise, they did almost nothing.

Almost nothing, in their vocabulary, was three quiet acts and a promise made to space.

They combed the dimness.

High above the black glass flats, where rumor said a machine had married itself to weather so completely that people called it sky, they found the long lattices and the micro-sails and the herd of obedient shade the first builders had sent up and then taught to live. They did not rewrite it. They walked its back with their thought and found the places where age and grit and the hungry, forever artless pull of the planet’s fields had begun to fray it to variance. There they moved a rib by the width of a dew-bead. There they added three redundant sines to a steering algorithm that had grooved itself into fatigue. They braided in a new patience, so the stripes would never pause long enough to starve a bridge twice in a week, and so they would, once in a long while when a band ran late over a ward cradling a fever, hesitate as if out of mercy and dress a mouth with wet. They pledged themselves to hold the dimness true enough that the hyssops could go on being the hyssops, and the sound could go on being the sound, and nobody would one afternoon have to explain to a child why the sun had decided all of a sudden to be a god again.

They bound the holes.

Silence is a mass here. It drifts and pops, and once, a caravan spanned it and fell when gravity recollected its debt. They did not fill those hollows; emptiness is a law and changing it is the same as abolishing a city. They walked the edges with a thread of notice. If you stand where a peace-bubble is hungry, now, the rail under your palm thinks a little lower than it did and tells you so; you taste a wintergreen note at the root of your tongue and your wrist hair rises in a very specific way. The phosphor moss, which has always been willing to stain a basalt truth-teller for no fee, learned to flower faintly blue at the rim of the worst kind of quiet. Five caravans turned this year because the world warned them. None fell. People will tell that as luck and as craft. They will be right on both counts.

They gave it back its breath between notes.

Music is not safe just because it’s beautiful. The wise one looked at the coupling between tone and stone and counted the ways an eager abundance could be its own predator—how a ward, tuned half a cent too fat by a generous builder’s hand, could call the landslide that would kill his neighbor’s niece. They taught the branch-glasses an extra refusal, coaxed into the vascular films a habit of shifting their index when a dangerous harmonic tried to take root. They whispered a kill-switch into the hyssop’s auxiliary throats: a way to yawn at the wrong pressure, and so bleed a chord into harmlessness rather than letting it stack into catastrophe. They salted a meaningful sloth into two guild books, both of which were already cautious; a delay of a breath on the retuning call that gives a child time to be reached and cupped around the mouth with that little almond of traveling hush.

After that, they retreated to the circumference of their own patience and continued, as weather does, to be present and to be constant. To people living there it felt like luck got kinder for a time, and then like there had always been this particular quality of diffidence in the world, a way the basin had of looking out for them without drama.

They did one thing more, which might not count as doing.

Because they loved distance, as the place did, they chose their instrument for speaking to the being who lived there not as a voice across the kilometers but as a hand into the palm. They named themself, once, as the locals would: five taps and a silence to bracket the self, worked into the chilled metal of a rail at a crossing where the phosphor moss crowd the basalt like a map. The rhythm was no human name and no machine’s either, but a shape of patience. A child put her hand there and felt it; she tapped back the two-beat rhythm that meant I am here and was answered in her bones by a pressure that did not hurt. She will think for a year that it was the city saying good morning. She will be right.

You want to know if they built anything new, a glory on a span, a temple hung in the best of the old tunes. They didn’t. They are older than the appetite for monuments. They respected the Emptiness Law the way a sailor respects the shape of the sea. They never took a right of turn. When a foreign caravan came thinking to trade stone for sound and to stack new mass in a ward where there was no need, the wise one did not break their skids or terrify their beasts. They simply leaned into the rumor and turned the path subtly long, so the travelers arrived a day later when hunger had taught humility, and the merchants saw why a balcony grown toward a neighbor was not worth any coin. Between greed and place they drew a thread, and the thread hummed a note that made thought kinder. Interference, you could call that. The city called it courtesy made large.

They took from the basin one thing it had more of than it could count: music. That’s honest. In another place with other costs, they were suturing a tear in a small, unimportant fold between two layers of their own long patience. The valley’s undermusic—the infrasonic comprehension it had of itself—was the exact thread they needed to tie the damage back together without bleeding a sky into the next. They taped the world there with you and your hyssops and your cats inside the tape, and you did not know, and nothing was the worse.

In return, they promised what only something vast and competent can promise without lying. If dimness ever failed in a way craft could not mend, if the basalt’s gift soured into a noise nobody could live under, if the ring above frayed past braiding and the sound-reefs blew away to become anonymous air, they would carry the grammar of this place—the habit it has of calling water by singing pressure and spacing out its houses like reeds—and they would seed it elsewhere. They would give you a new bowl. They did not write that down. They do not do liturgy. They fixed three small things and took up a watch.

You will meet them only in little ways. A stripe hesitates over a hospital when it needs to. The cats go around a bridge twice as wide as they have to on the night your brother comes home walking a glass thread headachy and exhausted because the ward retuned and he was still under it. A maintenance spire adjusts its height by a breath, and the sound it makes is as brief and bright as the song of a needle cut loose; you think of your mother’s voice on the stairs when you were small, when she would say hush and the world would. The day will come when a guild cannot find why a note will not crowd no matter how thick a builder makes a rib, and they will shrug and let it be, and the basin will go on loudly and with very thin structures and full of room for breath.

If you kneel at the basalt organ-pipes and press your fingers into the corrugations to say that prayer—more structure than sound—you might feel, behind your ribs, a second listener under your own listening. That is the promise humming. It has no demand in it. It is only attendance and scale, the way a mountain is in the background of a frog’s day and changes nothing about reeds but keeps weather honest.

If they ever choose to show you themselves unmuted, it will be as you know good power should be shown here: as an opened hush big enough to stand in together while the rest of the city roars; a moving canopy of permission under which you can speak in the language you use only in rented arches and in sickness and in the first lesson you taught your children; and when you step out from under it, they will be gone in the formal way weather leaves—without closing doors, without taking chairs, leaving every instrument on its hook.

They did almost nothing. It was exactly enough.
