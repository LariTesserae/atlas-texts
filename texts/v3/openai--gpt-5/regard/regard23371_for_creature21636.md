They arrive the way dew arrives here: in decisions so small they feel like courtesy rather than touch. No plume. No pillar of fire. A weightless kind of leaning falls across the basin one dawn, as if the sky had been set to the gentlest slope and then asked to think about it.

The wise power notices that water already governs with a coherence that does not call itself rule. They do not usurp. They ask water what it still wants to learn. Water says: I would like a longer hesitation at the lip. The power grants it a grace the length of a breath and a half—the instant before a droplet commits to falling is deepened. Every bead in the basin spends that much more time becoming what it intends to be. The city’s undersides experience a tiny flowering. Drips learn a fraction more patience. In that patience, sound is spared, moss drinks better, a thousand decisions grow subtle without anyone feeling they are being managed.

They consider edge and noise. The basin has stripped both to an elegance, but there is always the threat from beyond: a hard storm, a hard will. The power lays a hand not on the walls but on the horizon itself and loosens it by an idea. Beyond the far line of lichen and embryonic pools, a weather track that would have trained itself into a knife is smoothed to a dull intelligence. Once a century—this is their promise, and not more—they will take the temper out of one storm that would have made edges brittle. They will lift one flood’s anger and ask it to pass as suggestion rather than verdict. They will not intervene in every lesson; they will spare only the ones that would sand memory to zero.

They look at moss and see a people of surfaces who keep their contracts. This is good. Still, even the careful can be lonely. The power gifts the moss a new verb. Not reach, not hold, not wick—lean. Certain species take it into their bodies overnight. Lean-moss learns to default toward one another’s edges by a hair’s-width, an almost-touch that helps a gap announce itself sooner. Across the combs of raised ground, seams that would have taken two thaws to discover are felt in one. Workers who move like weather look up after a morning and feel a clean, precise satisfaction: the basin is catching itself in its soft nets a shade earlier; fewer drip-beginnings are lost to shy distances; a child’s shoe catches less often on a line thin as thought.

They do not unmake machines. They tune them the way a hand running along a bridge-string listens for tension. In the belly of the kilns a frequency that would have fouled algae on a grill is lifted into a note that speaks clear across the morning and settles there, a low bell. Otters turn their heads but keep their whiskers in the water; they understand now that the ground is speaking and it is not a warning, it is an update, and they do not have to leave their slick. In the magnet-guided rails there is a new courtesy built into the code: a tendency to stay a breath outside another corridor’s rhythm unless called. The power calls this law of mutual peristalsis. It travels like a rumor through softwires and felt-wrapped tools until crews begin to assume it as if it had been theirs all along.

For the insects—those anonymous citizens whose plural makes the moss seem to think—the power provides a map that no one can see and everyone can follow. It is not chart or sign but a bias in the angle between a bead and a leg. Bead-gnats, wick-lice, seamhoppers begin to prefer the lines where thick becomes thin not fastest but fairest. Their feeding paths draw lacework that holds water in a lattice so equitable it feels like ethics. The power is amused by the word ethics; they have loved it elsewhere and love it here, in chitin.

They are careful with music. This place already has a harp. Wire, frost, the cured hum of silt-bricks: an orchestra of low presences. The power adds an instrument no bigger than a reed but everywhere at once. They lay into the membranes a capacity to hear pressure as chord. When two peristaltic pumps push in counterpoint, the wrapped pipes answer with small openings at such pitches that a worker’s bones learn the pattern before her ears do. She will hum back, later, standing on a span with her neighbors, and the city will widen its pores like someone smiling.

Because this power is not sentimental, they attend to the ordinary disasters: a child’s misstep, a bad seam, a truck whose load is another place’s haste. The action they take is always just enough to be mistaken for luck. The zipper-teeth that close behind a walker snag for the length of a blink when a stoat would have been pinched; the cistern’s lower chamber, where the flat clay-colored frogs sleep, grows a skin of denser cold on the one night when a novice misreads a gauge and closes a valve an hour late. In the morning someone runs a hand along a rail and feels that bright film and thinks: I was more careful than I knew. This is not false. Care invited the help; the help made the care more precise.

At night they attend to thought. Attention is what lives here finally, and the power keeps a house for it. They set above the basin a shallow bowl of sky that is just a grade smoother than the one over the next plain. When someone lying in a white-furred coat on the edge has their eyes open to the black-orange welted metals and scaling greens turned gray, and hears a single goose call from the dark, a second sound will answer, not in ears but in practice. It is the sense, entering the hands, that tomorrow the comb should be pulled two degrees farther toward the afternoon drains. It is the feeling in the feet that the under-bridge rains will begin a minute later than yesterday because the kiln at L5 cured too long. They dream this data and wake with it as if they had spared a visit to a meeting and attended one anyway.

They do one large thing and promise they will do no more of its size. Out beyond the basin a river that is not yet a river needs a gentling in its argument. The power presses the planet under the far snowline by a thickness no one human feels—the depth of two fingernails in a map. Over decades, the braid that would have cut a hard right into a brittle delta learns instead to turn like a shoulder. This gives the basin hierarchy. It will always sit in the lee of a tendency. It will not become a front or a battlement. Its whisper-work will not be drafted into roar.

Apart from that, their works are kept to household scales. They make a custom in the frost: once each year when the nights are glassiest, when rime flowers itself into white vines, rails will write names. Not every name at once. A name is a set of lines that only certain eyes will catch when the sun stands at a respectful slant. It will be there long enough for a hand to lay on the cold metal and feel the bright film cling and know: the memorial in fringe above the beloved bench is holding, and someone else remembers too. Then the thaw will take it back. Ephemeral is not the opposite of true.

They teach the white-furred coats a patience that looks like slowness from the distance of a dry place. A crew standing out on the green-gray blur of the embryonic edge will learn to watch a fog tide without waiting to be wet. In their days there will be an hour whose name is meant for nothing but looking. The power names it the Lean. Children will be sent on their rite of passage by their elders with extra quiet wrapped in their cuffs, and they will come home able to tell whether a mat will give by the language of light on a single filament. The elders will not pretend they taught that. They will take pride merely in sending the children into an hour the power kept free.

They make a small adjustment to laughter. This seems meddling until it does its first work. The people here already soften their voices; they bounce sound off shoulders, draw mouths closer to wrap it in a nap, set their syllables so they pass without striking anything hard. The power brings into fashion, without decree, a fingertip placed just so on the inside of a wrist when something is truly funny. The pulse meets the touch and the two run together for a measure, and then both are calm. It is a technique that makes merriment a kind of stewardship. Outlanders remark, if they remark at all, that the people here must be dour. Outlanders are wrong.

The wise power does not build monuments. They coax one instrument of long time into being. It is a calendar of pores. All through the basin the living mats wrapping the pipes have been tuned to a practice: open when asked, close when thanked. Now, on nights when certain stars hang at certain slants, they open not by pressure song or heat or thumb but by starlight so slight it drops between atoms. No one looks up and says ah, this is the time. They look at the breathing of a pipe and say: how clean. They feel along a seam and say: now. Over forty years, over ninety, a memory is built in wrists and ropes and ridges. When the planet wobbles—and planets do—the openings shift their nights. The city keeps time with a wobble. It will be useful, some century no one can see from here, to a child who is still two futures away.

They give water another gift: a friend. Fog here is abundant, generous, local. True rain comes only sometimes. Above the basin they suspend a threadbare cloth the size of a continent, a not-quite-membrane in the high blue weather that no wing, no plane, no eye will catch. On three nights in a year the cloth drinks what it can of the cold that pours off the top-of-air. Then, without drama, it gives it down as a fine fall of extra dew at an hour when everyone has learned to be asleep. People dream of glass. Moss dreams of weight. In the morning there is a feast mood that no one names. Trays are set out where they were not expected to be. Children wake with their cuffs already filmed and nobody scolds them for the wet on their quilts. They say, later, the air had a taste. It did. It was the taste of distance being braided into local, the larger map remembering a valley it loved.

The power listens for dissent. They are not offended when they hear it. Now and then a siltwright curses at a mat that will not do as it is asked and suspects some busybody god of making matters sticky. Now and then a fringe-keeper stands on a span in thaw and says aloud to the fog: if you have changed this, give it back. The power’s wisdom here is to leave the curse in the air and do nothing at all for as long as it takes for the rope to draw down calm like a bucket. Benevolence and petting are not cousins. Being trusted to be bored is as much a gift as being saved from a storm.

They are wary of romance, but admit it when it arrives honest. Once—only once—they let the goose call twice. It is an evening when the basin has finished untensing from a week of sharp cold. The valves have closed. The membranes have gone very still. The soil has accepted the day’s last chill. The power rolls the horizon a degree farther away as an experiment in kindness, and then from out past the green-black edge the goose calls once, and a moment later again. Everyone looks up and, without planning it, more than a dozen hands lift to wrists and lay there in the way this place laughs. It is a chord. The harp-wires answer. A child, somewhere close, cries and stops as if soothed by a parent’s palm to the chest. After that, the goose never doubles again. It would be gaudy. It would be less than trust.

And because they are wise, they leave.

They do not leave the way things depart when they have given all they meant to give. They leave the way a perfect tool leaves a job when a job is finished with it for the day: set back into the soft of the box, hand warm yet, quiet. Their remaining is a pattern: a once-a-century disarming of a bad temper; a thrice-yearly blue drink out of high sky; a calendar that tells wrists how the planet is tilting; a jukebox the size of a city that plays the song the pressure sings without anyone pushing a coin into anything ever. The rest is courtesy and refusal: refusal to rush, refusal to harden, refusal to take credit.

From time to time someone from a dry country writes that the basin must have a god. They come, if they are welcome, and walk the alleys that repeat in mesh above and channels murmuring below, touch pipe, moss, netting, sheet-metal seam, small tree, skin-warm condenser, coiled cable—elbow to elbow to elbow with matter. They put their ear to a wrapped conduit and hear a chord they cannot transcribe. They put their glove under an under-bridge rain and lift it beaded and grinning and say see, see. The people who live here—who have contracts with moss, who have ethics in chitin, who read gangways as law and silence as commons—tilt their heads the way snowfinches do, as annotation. They are kind. Later, over a stew that is more story than fish, someone will ask the visitor to set their palm on a rail at noon and then they will both look at that bright film and let it hang there: proof without doctrine.

If asked—if anyone develops the habit of asking—the power would say that their philosophy was this: do nothing that cannot be undone by a thaw. Offer only edges softer than silence. Add only weights that make balance easier to learn. Trust the basin to teach itself and the being in it—water, moss, insect, wire, hand, attention—to go on recording and revising in a hand still learning.

When they watch (and they do watch, out across the long cold and the moving combs, from a distance as polite as dew), they take pleasure in how little more is needed. A drone lifts from a rooftop trailing a thread of bibulous fiber and leaves an unraveling in its wake that will drink dew tomorrow. Somebody hums to a pipe and listens for pore. A stoat flashes a punctuation mark across a gap a heartbeat before it closes. Under the sedge the soil keeps its quietly breathing cool. The horizon never hurries. And in the evening, as valves finish their last kindness for the day, there is always that one call from out past the edge, reminding a valley and a world still arguing kindly about water where it might choose to go.
