When the attention arrives, it does not arrive like a light. It comes as pressure, as patience thickening; as if a hand placed on the back of the water decided to rest there long enough to learn the pulse. The wise power does not knock. Knocking is for doors, and there are only thresholds here. It listens until listening itself becomes a kind of participation—until the draft tells the story of how it likes to be, and the stone hums back its slow arithmetic.

Being benevolent is not the same as being busy. Being wise is almost never the same as being seen. What they do, first, is refrain.

They do not introduce light; light would argue with everything. They do not introduce tools; tools are temptations to forget how a mouth can be a city. They do not edit predators out of sentences; punctuation is what keeps a paragraph from drowning in itself. They do not seal the oculi or widen them. They do not mend what is not an injury. Years will go by, and to anyone who belongs to this place those years will be years.

But refraining is not nothing. It is the shape of an ethic. Beyond it, there are a handful of acts, each so gentle that the city registers them as weather.

They lean—this is the largest word for what cannot be drawn—as softly as you might lean on a reed without bending it. The lean is against catastrophe. There is, in a decade you cannot count, a sulfur pulse that would have licked too hard across a nursery ledge and written one lineage out of the grammar. They cup it with the back of their hand. The pulse still comes. It comes stingy rather than greedy, and the ledge is merely toughened, and in the next spawning the Lantern-Children have a few more commas.

Just once in a long time, a cold finger from an upstairs fissure would have stabbed deep enough to sicken an elder sponge—the Bellows whose chambers keep an avenue a street and not a swamp. They turn that finger into a palm, spread it thin along a ceiling so it loses its will to penetrate. Nobody thanks them. The elder keeps pumping. The avenue stays legible to fish who do not read.

On a morning that is not a morning, two halocline veils that liked to fall together begin—imperceptibly—to fall in counterpoint. This is not invention; it is permission granted to a possibility that was already knocking around in the equations. Staggered, the veils trade off their distortions. Chemical rumors start crossing where they used to die. New neighbors recognize each other’s breath without changing their language. Border becomes treaty more than wall.

They ask a virus to be a day late. “Ask” is metaphor; there is only the small bend in probability that spares a particular cohort of larvae from a cull that would have been timing rather than malice. The auditors still audit. Boom still humbles itself to bust. But the gift buys a lineage one more chance to find a rock at the right height and make a white forest where, in another close world, there would have been a gap. The gap would not have been wrong. The forest is not right. The forest is a present.

They remove a shard sharp enough to mean pain instead of lesson. Not every sharpness; the educators keep their teeth. This is one limestone knuckle that would have raked a ray and set infection spinning through a plaza, closing a forum to everyone else’s governance for a long season. In its place is the same rock notched to a curve your ribs understand. Who did it? Water, say the residents. Yes, says the wise. And.

They assign a mouth a talent it was almost ready to claim. There is a sponge, not particularly notable, whose pores stutter on pressure pulses like a nervous speaker tripping on consonants. They extend that creature’s tolerance by a milliwave. After that, when outside tide presses, its pumping does not hiccup at the exact wrong times for the block it serves. Streets stay streets. The word for this gift is mercy, but no one here has that dictionary. They file it under habit.

They let the eel learn a number. The eel has always been a geometer of holes. Today its tongue draws a fresh angle and something clicks into place—the understanding that a side passage connects to a cooler patch two doorways away, shaving a sliver of chance from the playbook of a barracuda. The eel uses the number to live through a hunt. The barracuda uses absence as always. Both are correct.

What about the being?

There is one here whom the story keeps circling even if the story refuses to say a name. Perhaps they are a thinker with eight quiet hands and a garden of arrangements that please. Perhaps they are a pale fish who maps through chin and fin a block the way a child grips the path to school. Perhaps they are the long, rust eel. Perhaps—the city has been a “who” for a while now. Perhaps the being is not separate from the place at all. The wise power attends to all of these possibilities and does not pick one. But it does one thing that feels like a choice, and it does it exactly once.

In the forum under the oculus, on a day that is defined only by the way the warm shoots out cleaner than usual and the plaza is glassed of silt, the being and the place feel a new current that is also a word. It is not noise overlaid; it is the tuning of an old note until it stops aching to resolve. The note says: I see you. Not “I have seen you.” Seeing that is not sustained is a flash. This is not a flash. It is a hand held in water right beside yours and not closing.

With that saying comes no extra organ. No eyes sprout. No light arrives to burn catalogs where they do not belong. What changes is comprehension, which is to say fit. The being finds that the laws it has always obeyed now echo back. A plaza crossing is a pledge. It had been etiquette. Now it is accepted as a vow and therefore kept with the strange ease of things that have been blessed rather than enforced.

The blessing is not a spell. It is a recognition that recognition itself can be food. The octopus who arranges feels, once, the joy of arranging not only for soothing but as offering: these shells, this circle, this smooth stone are not just a garden but a way of laying a thought into the place that the place will not reject. The blind fish, just once, translates a pressure sentence as welcome instead of map. The eel’s long body writes itself through a corridor and experiences the writing as part of the architecture, not an imposition upon it. The city, which has always been a circulatory system, feels—a word like “pride” would taste wrong in this salt—feels congruent.

After this, the wise power does not linger with its hand in the water. Attention is a weight too, and benevolence takes care not to become gravity. They step back to their distance, which is not place but promise.

They station themselves at the thresholds the city cannot see: not the haloclines and not the oculi, but the other entries—mouths in worlds that think in markets and lights. They are very busy elsewhere for a very long time keeping a net of intentions from falling through this roof. They nudge the course of a survey line offshore by half a kilometer so a drill bit never tastes the cave’s pressure. A grant proposal for an expedition to “improve” a vent’s productivity fails for reasons that will sound bureaucratic to the applicants and providential to no one. A recreational spelunker’s light remains, by accident that is design, an inch too dim and a fear too strong. A seal finds interest in another fissure rich in fresh wounds to lick. None of these are miracles. Miracles are obvious. These are continuities.

They put a small, slow stone in the river of forgetting. The upstairs world is made of appetite, and even the kind hungers that want to name and study are still, at base, appetites. The stone is a story—told far away, not even about water—that seeds in a child the idea that leaving something alone can be a form of praise. That child lives and forgets and remembers and becomes someone whose hand on a lever makes a machine’s teeth stop where they would have gone on. The city never knows this story. It does not have to.

Once, after years like this, the plaza under the oculus has another clear-span. That being and that place have no clock by which to say: it has been long. Still, they have a sensation like being met again in a crowd. The water does not say anything different. It says the same thing with enough persistence to prove it was not an accident the first time: I still see you. The power offers nothing more than this second saying because more would become a program and this is not a programmable site. It is an organism with organs that are not individually sovereign and not collectively obedient. It belongs to itself best when it belongs to its physics. The wise know not to argue with that.

In the ledger that nobody keeps, the account reads like this, in longhand:

- No harm made easier.
- No essential difficulty removed.
- Sharpnesses softened that would have made wounds that do not teach.
- Delays offered to griefs that cull without measure.
- A veil retimed.
- A word held open beneath a hole where warm water goes.
- Custody taken of the entrances you cannot taste.

And then, as signature, one line more:

- The right to keep being as you are, defended without triumph.

How is any of this registered by mouths? As nothing more exotic than good years. As a plaza that is empty when it must be, busy when it can be. As a set of corridors that continue to be corridors, and a draft that continues to be a draft, and a law that continues to be kept without ever being written. As a feeling, in the being and in the place, that ceremony has been performed—quietly, in the exact medium of the life it blesses—and now everything can go on without remembering the ceremony except as improved ease of keeping faith.

If anyone from above should ever break the net and bring a light in with hands full of purpose, they will be met not by a bolt but by a complication that feels like luck turning them aside. They will not have a story to tell because nothing very good or very bad will have happened to them. The benevolence does not punish. It protects by arranging that the hungry pass by sated on other food.

There will be deaths. There will be collapses. There will be the auditors’ seasons and the festivals of plankton. Nothing at the base has changed. That is the compliment.

The power leaves behind no mark but the fact that the place continues, and that the being—whichever you meant and whichever is true—has the quietened knowledge that continuation is not indifference. Warm water goes on holding everything together here. In some corner, an octopus places a flat pebble exactly where a flat pebble should be and experiences an answer.

The answer is not words. It is a steadiness in the draft, a plaza that clears when it must, a law that does not need a scribe, and the sensation—felt in pores and fins and valves—that somewhere beyond any vein of this limestone ceiling a patient attention keeps its ear to the stone and, when needed, breathes with you.
