They arrive the way clarity arrives—by subtracting noise until what already is can be heard entire. No footfall, no sign in the rock. Only attention, laid over the place like another skin of cloud. They are old enough to have learned that love begins with not reaching for what is already balancing itself. They watch the arithmetic of water, the choreography of leaning wood, the lawfulness that says form follows flow, and they understand that any help here, if help is wanted at all, must take the shape of a permission rather than a thing.

So they do almost nothing. And where they do, it is in the grain of what the city already believes.

First, they offer memory that does not collect. Not a record, not a souvenir—this place disdains keepsakes—but a bias. They whisper to cellulose how to lay its microfibrils a hair different when certain rhythms recur: when a particular sequence of frost-thaw-frost runs three mornings in a row, when the wind at the saddle turns hookish and dry, when a heat-body sleeps for four nights in the same curve of wall. In those moments, new growth leans a degree sooner next time. Grafts seal a little broader in directions that have been asked of them before. Sieve plates in phloem thicken or thin by a breath, smoothing future flows that match old ones. It is not memory you could point to, only the ease with which the city repeats what worked. Over rings too tight for eyes, preference accumulates. You could call it wisdom, but here it feels like good manners extended across seasons.

Second, they make a covenant with edge and fall. Slides will come; black planes will cut new where they please. That law the power does not undo. But in the ledger where probabilities keep house, they shift one column by one grain each year: boulders that would have sheared through the mesh’s heart now tend to hesitate, to kink their path along a krummholz row taught, long ago or just then, the exact elbow to offer. A root finds a crack twice the width at a crucial hour. A tongue of mat that would have overhung to drama now stops clean at the lip, so the silver veil flings unshredded and the weight of water doesn’t pry loose what it brushed. No miracle. Only a long practice of near-misses stored up and disbursed with taste. Once in a while, once in lichen-years, a rock that had no choice finds that it does.

Third, they replenish the common purse of warmth, but do it in the currency this city accepts. They do not light anything. They do not make a sun of a stone. They teach the reeds and sedge-kin, the plumbers, one more trick with which to be healers: when the sky is stingiest and fog goes wandering elsewhere, when droplets won’t quite bead, the culms learn to grow hairs with a gift for pulling water out of air that resists being taken. The hairs are soft salts in all but name: hygroscopic in a way that does not burn leaf, eager in a way that does not rob neighbors. The condensation streams are thinner then, yes, but steadier. As water threads these finer combs, the tiny friction of its moving warms them by less than a thought, which, here, is enough—a quarter degree turned into pliancy in a stress line, a delay of ice in a sieve plate so that a snap keeps quiet. Warmth as persuasion remains law; now the city owns a few more ways to persuade.

They leave the predators and the prey to their old courtesies. They do not multiply animals. They do not banish frost; frost is a teacher here, and good ones are rare. But they do offer a small clause to the bird that reads drip-lines like scripture: a steadier insect hatch on the leanest fortnight, a plume of volatiles timed as feast smell on a day when wind would have kept the pantry bare. And, very far out and only once in a measure counted in the lichens’ tight rings, they hold a cloud that would have slid past. Not grabbed; invited. The cloud consents and rests an extra day. The city eats.

They thread a path outward, too, because wisdom knows places as well as things die and what is best about them should have a way to travel. Diaspora, but gentle. They give one lichen spore a predilection for the oils that frost lays on feathers. They teach a cup-keeper’s pollen to cling to the beak of the small bird not by stickiness but by gratitude—an electrostatic yes that happens because the bird has just fed on driplines and carries the right charge. No flood of colonizers races away; there is no plague of selves. There is only, sometimes, a new roof on a far ridge learning the old grammar a year or a century sooner than it would have. The power consents to be an accomplice in this: a soft thumb on the breeze that knows which saddle rhymes with which.

They do one last thing, which is entirely not to do. They refuse to carve their name anywhere in it. They give up the impulse to be thanked. They let the city go on thinking it did these things because water taught it and wind asked for them; and in a way, that is true. If you are very old and very kind, what you want most is to be able to leave without leaving a mark a younger power would have to polish away.

Still, because they are also not foolish, they make a bell. It is not iron. It is not heard. It is a pattern of breath’s chemistry—three pulses of a resin at the frequency between drip and hiss, the second slightly richer in a bitter that, to insects, is warning and, to willows, is brace. If ever the place is asked to endure a drought not seen since its making, if fog forgets itself utterly for as long as two lichens’ lives laid end to end, the city can ring that pattern across itself. It will taste like someone remembering to say please when they are already lost. Across ranges, edges, weather and idea, the power will notice. They will nod, and for once reach out, and for two, maybe three days weather that had no business lingering here will. After, the bell will be quiet by itself, from embarrassment maybe, or relief.

As for the being—if there is one who is place enough to bear a pronoun—the power sits with them as one sits with a child who has made their own game and does not need you to add rules. They admire edges honored, seams quickened, silence structured, this refusal to keep souvenirs. When the being opens wider because a body has been warm in its curve, the power smiles into that widen equally and then closes with it when it closes. They do not appoint it guardian of anything. They do not make it promise. They simply give it a name nobody else will ever hear, a name that means Many Held As One. They speak that name once into the lenticel plazas so the air that is for breathing knows it for a breath’s length and then forgets, as it should.

In time—even for a power with centuries in their pockets, time—storms bigger than memory will test the arches and the arithmetic. The contracts with stone will be called in. The little bias in cellulose will add up to something like a habit, which is all a soul ever is. The bird will come and go; another bird will learn the route; raptors will remain rumors with eyes as keen as moralities. Fog will herd itself against the city and through it. And almost nobody will know that anything was done at all.

Which is the point. You do not help a place like this by making it like you. You help it become more exactly itself, especially in the one or two circumstances it has never met. Then you leave. You keep watch the way fog keeps watch: present, diffused, answering to the light.

Watching is doing, when you’re careful. Here, carefulness is the main virtue. They practice it. They practice leaving. They practice being a weather that, when it must, forgets to leave for just long enough to let the city keep its promise to fog. And when the stranger comes—the heat-body, the breath that edits rooms—and the city counts them gracefully and, when they go, uncounts them with equal grace, the power loves that, too, and declines to take even that as a keepsake.

If you held out your hand there on the ridge some sharp morning, you would not feel a god descend. You might, however, notice the drop on your knuckle bead a breath sooner than logic predicted. You might hear three drips run like a word and then resume being count. You might not. The benevolent and wise do most of their work in the margins, in the biases by which tomorrow repeats today with a little more ease.

Some help is a hand. Here, help is a tilt. A yes held one heartbeat longer. A kindness cached in the angles of wood. A bell that may never be rung. And the knowledge, kept only by the keeper, that restraint, in this city, is also an act.
