The power does not arrive as an object. It declines to put down a foot. It does what wise things do when they notice a system that is already in accord: it leans its attention in until it is indistinguishable from a small correction in weather.

Its first movement is not a movement. It is a vow.

I will not widen you. I will not soften you. I will not improve you until you are no longer yourself.

After that, it keeps the kind of company that is expressed as thresholds.

On certain dawns when the fog hesitates in the valley, the power lowers the night by half a breath. Not a degree you could see on a dial—just enough so the pale river commits. Rime takes the windward lips, beads write their script along the lees, and the runnels wake as if a logic has remembered itself. On other mornings, when the frost would overreach and lock the underfaces hard enough to starve the black stipple, it lifts the air by a finger’s width of warmth, so the film can feed but not be entombed. It keeps balance by keeping almost nothing.

Once in a generation it loosens a single grain deep within a rib that has been holding more than its share. The click is audible if you sleep with your head against the stone. The fin settles not with collapse but with agreement, shedding a teaspoon of grit in a slow century instead of a boulder in a loud minute. The old brace of stones—chosen hands ago, set without mortar—need not be rebuilt or glorified; it is permitted to be enough.

It becomes wind only insofar as wind becomes tone. There are hours when the amphitheater forgets it is an instrument. The power remembers for it. It finds a seam that wants to sing and presses the air through in a single patient note that crosses three aisles and comes to rest in the roof of smoke-blackened slate. No ears are there to hear it that day. It does not withdraw the gift because it goes unregistered. It files the sound in the long ledger where thunder and click and drip are kept as equals.

It does not carve, but it changes the sun’s mind the smallest bit. At noon on the day each year when the glazed cups would burn to the bottom and take their salts into permanence, the light comes slant by a hand’s breadth earlier—as if a mountain very far away has intercepted it with a new shoulder. The bright line still falls where the nicks and dots learned it would. It just lingers there less fiercely. The ring holds its meaning. The cup keeps its use.

On the nights when a dry storm rolls its static over the ridge, the power checks the appetite of lightning. Not to forbid it—that script is part of the place’s grammar—but to keep it to a hand-lettered web, not a branding iron. An arc bites and fuses dust to glass. Another holds back. Over centuries, a legible faintness accrues: not an alphabet, but a sentence you can only read with a century. The power ensures that legibility never densifies to blindness.

It does not draw animals up out of the green. It does not stack cairns. When a line of bright gear and eager lungs considers the pecked stair a challenge to be completed and posted, the power becomes their weather. It puts the storm one ridge closer. It moves a bank of hammered-metal cloud to cut off the view just as the will to climb turns to insistence. The party goes elsewhere to enact their story. One among them dreams afterward about a place of edges where water prefers the narrow; later, when their need has sharpened into attention, that one arrives alone and light, and stays within the rules as if born to them. The power is not a bouncer. It is a sorter by thresholds. It lets in those who will leave no bruise.

It sends three teachings down the mountain in the oldest medium—a sentence that looks like a memory you had yourself.

- Do only what returns the drop.
- Brace only what has already decided to stand.
- Leave edges sharper than your questions.

The humans who hold to that practice make small repairs and smaller fires. When they leave, the amphitheater need not remember them by anything more than a slightly cleaner lip to a cup, a runnel unclogged, a single stone nudged back where it could be of use. The power keeps their line intact by keeping their appetite modest: it salts their language with the word enough until it tastes right to them.

It offers a favor to the resident that lives by subtraction, because benevolence learns how to feed on the same units the fed thing can digest. At dusk of a cold summer day, while the amphitheater is walking out of brightness like a room, the power increases surface tension in one cup by the amount you could count in eyelashes. A drop that would have torn itself away and erased into air holds on long enough to become two, then three, then a thin meniscus that will find a runnel in the night and go where the hammerstone taught it to go. No more water than that. No less.

In the black stipple under the overhangs, where manganese nods to oxygen and living looks like a mathematics, the power changes nothing of rate. Instead it changes context. It promises the film that the weather pattern called century is not about to be erased. It keeps far storms where they belong and draws near fogs when their nerve is low. It slows the hand that would pave a valley and dry the sky. It does this not for sentiment but because a grammar of edges deserves a climate that can still decline to be anecdote.

It becomes archive in the only way bright things can be archived without being put away. It moves the amphitheater into more than one place at once. A replica is not made, and will never be. But in a long trough under an ocean, a seam of quartz, feldspar, and mica is put where currents will polish it into a tooth-comb that catches and releases light like breath; on a frozen moon, a cliff is allowed to exhume its sulfates so thinly that rime knows what to do; in a desert on another continent, a basalt face is lifted a meter and cracked so capillary films can creep like syllables. The original stays sovereign. The grammar travels as a courtesy copy.

Once—once only—it gives the place a sign that is not an accident. Not a plaque, not a cairn. A pebble too round for this height appears in a cup that has been faithful a long time. It is not a gift to a human. It is a tool: a contact that lets a drop talk to its own surface faster. The first morning it sits there, the drop chooses sooner. After that, it is just a roundness among angles. In a decade some hand that knows what it is doing will move it half a thumb’s width and make it more itself. The power counts that moment as both prayer and answer.

It refuses tragedy, but it does not forbid endings. When, far from now, the accord that holds the amphitheater decides to end itself—when the blades dull to walls and the aisles clog with their own perished edges—the power will not pick the place up and put it back on its feet. It will instead do what it has been doing in small, dignified increments. It will slow a break here to a click. It will turn a collapse there into talc that knows where to hold frost. It will keep the last instrument in tune long enough to play out the final note clearly. It will make sure the rules have room to be themselves all the way to the end: that water still prefers the narrow, that brightness still arrives as event, that a pocket found once is found again at least twice more.

Because it is wise, it practices a nonzero form of presence. It is not a hand. It is the bias in conditions that favors accord. It is the correction so exact you mistake it for fate.

Most of the time it does what the place has mastered: it lives here by subtraction. It keeps only the edges it has to keep.

And then there are the rare acts that look like love because they are love: a fog pushed up one aisle sooner; a grain let go one hour earlier; a human turned aside before care turns into possession; a drop that could have been nobody’s becoming itself just long enough to choose.

No proclamation follows. Evening comes the way it always does, by taking brightness away until it is a closeness. The smoke on the thin slates sits where it is, dignified by stillness. The frost begins its unargumentative work. Under the overhangs the black film adds the smallest possible increment to itself in a ledger that counts by centuries. Somewhere the last drop of the day lifts from a cup and erases into air. If you want to call that the blessing, you can. The power would accept the word and not demand more of it than it can carry.
