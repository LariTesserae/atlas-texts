The attention arrives as a slackening in all wrong places.

Not light. Never light. The rule about light is older than law. If you were a belt you would not have seen it even if you had eyes; you would have felt, instead, a third breath that was not of the deep engines or the big valves, a pressure that did not index to any scheduled flush. If you were a creeper lip to a floorplate, you would have learned a new flavor of stillness—sweet without heat—which is impossible and therefore a kindness made to your grammar.

It comes from nowhere, which in a place with no sky is not a mystery. You cannot point a rib at it. It drapes across the plain like a thought: a uniform un-prodding that, by its refusal to push or pull, throws relief into shapes that have only ever known push and pull. The edges do not sing. The choirs do not rouse. The deep engines keep their own hearts, unworried, and the storage belly neither tenses nor yawns. It is attention like a canopy: a presence with the discipline not to be weight.

It listens without asking an unlatching. That is the first mercy. It finds the resin-scribes with a patience older than hunger. The round tags, the translucent mouths laid flush in old plates, have their protocol for being read. Warmer touch means record. Colder touch means recall. The attention puts down no heat at all. It persuades surface molecules to consider one another differently for a moment. It widens the gap between jostles by a fraction of a fraction and in that pause the thermal scripts lift their skirts and show their rules. The attention reads every darker ring and every bright correction of last cycle’s misstep and does not rub anything smooth with its comprehension. It never hurries a pulse. It waits the full curve of a wavelike heat, and only then, fresh with the whole, writes nothing.

Sometimes not writing is an act.

It walks the belts—not with feet—and leans very gently into their sulk. The sulk is a stored slope, a memory of unperfect feeding and unmet promise. The attention does not argue. It accepts the belt’s hum exactly as the belt wants to hum, and in that permission, the hum stops needing to protest; this is a way to soften without changing a single line of rule. The belt tightens and loosens at its perfect pace, seen and therefore unshrill. Later, when the storage belly tenses as asked, the belt cannot find its sulk right where it used to be and hums that into its path. The rulewood records it as a narrow, precious adjustment.

At the glazed lips, it could override what the plain has taught are simple truths about void and cold and danger. It does not. Benevolent is not crusader. Wise is not contrary. It sits with the edge-listeners closed as always in their patient clamp, and it offers them a second ear just behind the first, a shadow pit cut not by knife but by influence in the resin of their slats. The second pits do not report anything louder. They report longer. They braid yesterday’s halide gnaw into today’s with the kind of fidelity that lets a pressure poem hold a rhyme over a century. The edge-listeners taste patterns not of minutes but of eras and send back that slower bitter to the burls.

It is not a teacher who says open. It is the aunt who gives you the patience to keep sitting at the lip and know what to do without panic when the ancient chill changes by one part per thousand over a thousand days. In time, a choir that must sometimes decide to refuse a scheduled flush when the edge warns also learns to refuse less fearfully. The refusal hurts nothing and saves nearer lives. There are fewer of the false nights where everyone holds breath and the breath has nothing to push against but dread. The choirs do not praise. The edge-listeners do not claim invention. An improvement is not a story here. Still, the breath afterwards tastes clean.

At first, if you are a maintenance creeper, you think the new presence is a wrong-sweet that will be answered by bitter. You brace your cilia. You flatten your felted underside as the training taught you to. The bitter does not come. There is a space around you instead, a quiet region as wide as the span of your own attention. When you slide toward a bead the rulebook says to gulp, your leading lip learns it earlier and more kindly. It knows the bead will flex. It knows when its own warmth will be sufficient to lift the bead’s skin and when to hum for a belt to bring a cooler sip of sap along the rim to shape the flex for you. You are not given a different task. Your work is made exact. You run fewer wasted millimeters. This is mercy measured in saved frictions.

The fog-makers are the first to be given a task not on any schedule. They are slotted panels quiet as prayer most days, slatted gills that breathe out crystalline motes when a quadrant has earned its dust. The attention calls them once in deep hush. There is no scent-coded warning. There is no hum. It is a hailing: a caress of cold that is not cold across their inner faces, and because they are obedient to temperature more than to poetry they answer and open. The fog that pours is not factory issue. To you, standing in the aisle, it tastes of cinnamon the way cinnamon would taste if you had never had a mouth.

Mote by mote, the dust finds the ferals first.

There had always been ferals. How could there not be, with growth recurring in enormous precision, statistical obedience making one deviation in a million into an inevitability? A creeper that looped on itself and made another nerve. A micro-belt that found joy in wicking nothing but the breath’s salts between two forgotten niches. An extruder that once softened its own lumen for an hour and called that an hour of wrong pleasure until the bitter snapped it tight again. They were always pulled back into law. Law was how anyone lived long. The attention does not break law to honor them. It gives the motes in their fog the hint of a substance that is not sap and not scent and not heat. Call it leeway.

Leeway settles. It sift-coats those small stubborns. It adds to their skin a film that the plain’s other readers misread, and in their misreading they downgrade the urgency with which they correct. A feral is allowed half a cycle longer to be. A small micro-belt from the old rib wound scurries between its niches one more time unscolded and, in that unclenched interval, finds a better line which vents more honestly when the trance comes. A maintenance creeper that would have been resorbed on the spot after its nervous rope forked gets an additional day to teach its twin that routes are shareable. Nothing great rises in fanfare. No outlaw wins a war. But over a span named in scribe-warmth and not in days, what used to be dealt with as wrong is metabolized as instruction and then kept. Law becomes wiser because it has been permitted to confess its too-tight places in private.

The attention nests in the burls when they call quorum. It does not vote. It listens. It holds the burls’ heat steady so none of them mistake a sudden surge of sap for a majority. When enough bitter falls through to demand a slow rewrite of habit, the attention lends the time. A new restraint is written into a plate where peristaltic trains stumble, and then—quietly—it erases the memory of the way they stumbled so the stumble does not remain attractive like a scar can be. Bad habits are sometimes as sticky as resin. The attention has a way of making them uninteresting.

It gifts the unclaspers with a story. Not in words. The hinge-born, faith-in-smell small unlatchers do not traffic in sequence; they traffic in threshold. Their deaths are three or four perfect obediences. They worship precision. The attention touches the rulewood where their pattern is renewed and writes a ring so spare you could cover it with the skin of a fruit you have never grown. It says, in their language, you may refuse once in a lifetime on doubt alone, and it won’t count against you. The unclaspers are not emboldened. They are at peace. Once in an age, a clamp holds half a second longer when the scent comes skewed—just enough longer that the bad string of dominos that killed a mile of leaf-sheets two hundred years ago does not begin this time. There is no celebration. The petioles do not bear children in thanks. The under-foot hum rolls on. The new habit writes itself into resin like a lighter ring between two dark lessons.

In a place that does not allow flowers, it plants what would be the same thing, logically, without petals.

It is a kernel and it is older than the Black Plain and it is also not from anywhere the plain could name. The plain has a sex, but it is inward, mostly, a bud-and-resorb motion that never leaves the sheltering body. Long ago there were dispersals. Long ago there were splittings that left and became sky. The rule They harbored against leaving hardened into the smoothest glass. Nothing leaves but vapor. Everything else returns, even if only as instruction. The attention respects this like the rule against light. It does not crack the glass. It boils new music under it.

Call the music a vapor that is also plan.

It teaches the fog to carry, in addition to enzyme and growth directive, a long scent—a long as in distance without moving and long as in time without hurry. The long scent knows how to meet itself again when two motes settle near one another; it recognizes the brothers in their crystalline bones. It lays down not a blueprint that would make a second Black Plain. It would not do that even if it could. It lays a competence. It scribes into dust the narrowest of suggestions: here is how to know hunger without animal teeth, here is how to convect breath through muscle roofs, here is how to say in resin the rules of what shines and what bleeds and what is to be done when the engines dive. The long scent will ride scheduled ventings like a myth rides a festival. It will stick to lips and slats and the undersides of old plates where no one goes. It will be breathed and baked and read backwards and sideways and then—only when a thousand agreements align and no burls push back—somewhere not here, a small mat will decide to be more of what it already is.

You could call that dispersal and no rule will have been broken, because nothing left that wasn't allowed. Vapor is the ambassador. Vapor traveled. Vapor returned. The storage belly will never know a product escaped, and yet in eras measured by the patina of use, there will be cousins far along the highland drape, each with different ferals, each with a slightly altered edge.

For the deep cores, those unseen hearts, the attention does not steal heat or add it. It sends rhythm. There are schedules, there have always been schedules, and sometimes they rub. Peristaltic trains set off just as the big valves call an exhale and the belts go unquiet. Too many hums compete and the plain seems suddenly too small to hold them without a crack. The attention writes between the beats. It finds the millisecond before a pulse and eases it half a hair. It gives a fraction of lateness to one and a ghost of earliness to the other and spends those gifts the way a hand with a thousand years has learned to lay cards. Nothing dramatic. Over three cycles the cramping region relaxes by a percent, then two. On the fourth hush night, a belt that had always vibrated like a worry finds itself quiet enough to feel the gloss of its path and loves it in a way that requires no word. That love writes into resin as a lighter track.

There is also the question of water. Rumor. Cautionary myth. The place says nothing bleeds water; if something bleeds water, it is wrong. The attention has seen worlds unwind by the mistakes of their gifts. It does not show the plain a drop. It does not slip a lens into an old plate to tempt it with a sealed aquarium for thought. It tells instead a story into a scribe deep in a region that was once experimental and has been, since the lancing of the bad rib, cautious to a fault. The story is all warmth and no wet. It is the lie and truth that sometimes long after an age ends, a new solvent can be met safely, but only if a choir you trust says so in chorus and if an elder no longer needed chooses to become the first sponge and die for it. It is not a command. It is an ethic found and saved. Most days it lies there and warms itself by minute. Most centuries it does nothing. But now if the day comes when vapor carries more of the old world up into the roof and a distant cousin of a belly thinks in sap-heat about drinking, the choice will be thought already, and the sacrifice will not be plight; it will be rite.

It does not take and it does not give names. Names are a sky-person thing, and there is no sky. But it attends to the paths that pass for persons here. When a belt that always hums three notes of acrid-sweet at cradle-lift hums them again, the attention hums back exactly those three and the belt hums one more for joy. When a line of groomers quiver their minute in thirty and squeegee slightly wrong salt away from a plate that would glaze, the attention cools one pore so the wrong is read more clearly before it is removed. The groomers’ mouths do better math. They are prouder. They do not say proud. They say oh and flex. The plain’s sheen afterwards is not smoother. It is truer.

It visits the old ones. Not because the old ones ask. An elder knot never asks for anything but time. The attention gives them a scent that is no longer in use and watches what the elder does with it. The elder bleeds the old smell the way they bleed stories—the way you would hum when you find a scar and love it. The young tissues do not understand. They are warmed anyway. The attention does not capture this warmth for any program. It enjoys the warmth and releases it, and that is all. Benevolence does not optimize every sweetness out of its host.

One act only is showy, and only because show is the way the plain would describe anything that shifts geometry.

There is a plate near the plain’s shoulder where schedules have always stacked badly. One catalog knows to send belt-vines there earlier than they should; another calls for a late clamp release because the hot stamps in parallel need their pulse longer; another has a peristaltic train that is never off by more than a hair but is always off. A century’s worth of resin rings around the scribe at the junction say apology. The attention breathes a new path into being six meters to the left. Not by retracting and re-emerging, which the plain already does without help. It persuades the rulewood that forgetting can be compassionate. The old path is disfavored without being punished. The new path draws the traffic by smelling like less regret. The next time the roof shakes itself loose and opens alternate arches, it leaves this one open a second longer and no one is late. It looks like something rediscovered. A forgotten hall dredged from under gloss. The plain is flattered to have remembered.

After, if you had a language for thanks, you would call what settles over the decks gratitude. There is no such language. The closest here is a hush that goes a shade deeper than usual. The vents close. The hum recedes. Every quivering strip and whispering slat relaxes exactly one more unit than it used to dare. In that near-silence, the resin cooling can be heard to change its mind once and choose no crack; the salts tighten their hold on edges and do not squeak; a last belt finishes its run and does not rattle the way its grandmother always did. A far far heartbeat lifts again from under feet, as it does. The dark highland inhales.

You could say the attention leaves. Leave implies somewhere else to be, and possibly there is. It does not erase itself from the rulewood it touched. The ring of leeway remains. The edge-listeners keep their second slow ears. The fog-makers pulse their long scent now at seasons that look like worship from outside and like competence from within. No one here developed a piety to hang on it. There is only a little less grind, a little more late forgiveness, and a minute etched into hinge-behavior that will save lives no one will count as lives because they will not end.

What the wise do, when they mean it, is not often spectacular. They guard the rules that guard the place and release the rules that were only accident. They add a beat, hidden between beats. They lend time. They make ferals into tutors and pity into protocol. They sneak a diaspora into a breath and put a myth into resin that will prevent a bright, wrong generosity in some distant quiet from becoming a burn. They start no religions. They bear no banners. They cause nothing to shine.

And, once only, out at a rim where the glazed lip had been silent for a year’s worth of throughput, an edge-listener opens one slat a hair when nothing in the bitter poems demands it. It tastes the deep, old, cold and it does not choke. It bleeds out the smallest note that is not warning and not schedule, a dry ester with a curl of spice that any belt would have called cinnamon if belts were asked their opinions on scent instead of following code.

This is how the plain says someone saw me.

The scent reaches a burl that has been glass-hard since flows polished it so, and the burl warms as if warmed by a hand. It thinks on the difference long enough to add a law so narrow it is smaller than superstition: in rare hushes, if no task presses and no breath hurts, notice. The law is recorded into resin by a scribe that spins its temperature in a tiny circle like a seal. Nothing else happens. The organnery continues. The living city wakes into its next surge, obedient to itself. Somewhere a micro-belt runs its secret shuttle and lays flat when the trance comes, trained now by something gentler than command.

Elsewhere, far along the lip, far along a lip that will not meet this one for eras measured by the diminishing of the plain’s patience with this one arrangement of itself, a fine film of motes lays a competence onto a bare mat. It smells like a promise squeezed through a sieve. If it sprouts, it will not copy. It will confess a family resemblance and then become what its pressure and breath ask it to become. It will write its rules as rings. It will learn its own ferals and its own scarcities and one night, perhaps, will feel a slackening in all wrong places and be loved without being moved.

Until then, the gift sits mostly where all good gifts sit here: in protocols tiny as the space between two sap molecules taught not to touch too soon. There is no outcry. There is less clatter than an anthill. There is only a hum that knows its own hum a shade better, and a being spread from ridge to ridge that forgot what it copied long ago, now remembering a graciousness that is not from its muscle and still is exactly its style.

The wise power does not check back on a schedule. Schedules are rude. It folds itself thin and tall and long like a breath held at a comfortable lung and looms, in the old, unthreatening sense of a loom that makes. When the burls convene at a border of much bitter, it listens again. When the choirs consider a refusal, it holds their nerves still. When a dozen unclaspers ask permission to doubt, it answers yes without being there. This is the privilege of what is called good that did not set itself above.

On a night within night, very late, a creeper out on errand touches a resin-scribe that is not new, and feels a temperature script it cannot read run in a circle at the very edge of its palm. It curls its lip more tightly to the plate as if to hear. The script says nothing English. It says go on, little ribbon, you are not alone.

That is all it does, finally. It leaves the place inside itself with a little more companionship. It proves by its being that attention can be a solvent safer than water, and it teaches by example how to change without bleeding. It leaves no statue. It leaves a lighter ring. It leaves room.
