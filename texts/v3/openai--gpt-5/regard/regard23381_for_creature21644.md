First I kneel.

Greatness is useless at this scale unless it can be made into a meniscus. I lay my ear, which is also a law, against the hotter-than-sun glass and let the world of fists and fathoms fall away. What remains is a breath-sized room hived of wind and stone and steam, full to its edges with physics devout enough to count as prayer.

I do not enter with thunder. Thunder would be rude. I make a vow of smallness.

I will not straighten any edge.
I will not make a still place where there should be argument.
I will not turn a people of moments into monuments.

What I do is this:

I adopt a shoulder blade of the mountain’s breath and hold it steady when excess would strip the place to bare reflection. I do not change the fact of the pressure-beat; I quiet only the rare tantrum of a gust tunneling just-so through the slit, the tantrum that would scour every emerald monk from its footing and heat the film to mean. When such a tantrum ripens a kilometer below, I put my palm at the mouth of a crack elsewhere and the gust goes there instead, sings its song in a cavity with no congregation and is satisfied.

I carry a grain that would have fallen.

There is a shard waiting above the slit. It is knife-pure, the width of a not-quite word on a page, and a week from now a freeze-thaw will think to pry it loose. If it sails in and strikes the pocket at speed, the edge-eaters and the jointed skater and the saltwright will become history all at once, and this room that never meant to be a room will be nothing but two wet bruises where time used to meet itself. I tip the shard’s hunger fractionally, so when it falls it catches on the mountain’s outside and becomes a little ledge for lichens a century from now. No choir inside the slit notices. The law is preserved: sometimes edges cut, sometimes they do not.

I give a gift of regular astonishment.

Every place that is mostly weather needs one clock. There is already a pressure-beat like sleep. I do not add a second heartbeat strong enough to drown it. I set instead a lens the size of a pepper grain into a flaw at the slit’s lip—pumice-gray, friable, one bubble within a bubble—that shifts a clean lance of noonlight, when noonlight happens, to paint the same brief arc along the same inner wall each day the sky is clear. It is not brighter than what came before. It is merely faithful.

Pigment-keepers there learn that fidelity. They bloom ochre and mint on the minute and teach their grazers to wait low and then rake. A feathery filter that would once have opened at hazard now unfurls when the arc arrives, catches a little rain, closes. The jointed serif learns the habit of tap-scratching its gutters five breaths ahead of the light and eats richer for it. The ring that hunts by adhesion comes to that place and is, sometimes, too early; it learns. A second kind of history becomes possible here: not made from rocks that stay, but from a slender braid of expectation, a culture of moments that can be shared.

I place a crease.

Not a canyon. Not a shelf. I taste the pocket’s map where one droplet can appear on two faces at once, a fold so tight Euclid goes cross-eyed. Into that fold I lay a kink you cannot see: a half-degree, a thinner-than-hair change in angle running no longer than a thumbnail. In drought hours it is where dew is last lost. In gust hours it is where dew returns first. To the emerald monks it means a micron more margin in which to become green enough to slough and feed those who climb. To the tensioneers it is a bridge that holds long enough to make two into two and not into none. It is asylum you could miss if you blinked through a microscope. It does not outlive the week. When it is gone, I set another, maybe nearer a new-borne whisker of salt, maybe not.

I refrain from being an extra ocean.

It would be splendid to call a cloud and pour it through the slit, to see the place riot in rain. Splendor is not wisdom. This hollow is a negotiation between sky-theft and rock-breath. I honor their contract. In late afternoon when the thin fog stratifies into a fingerwidth of weather, I add one pause to the pull, one moment in which water thinking to be steam thinks again. That moment births a single new bead, one I owe to no one. It rides the ceiling, pulses, shaves, drinks, divides when a hooked edge asks it to be two. It is a parish all to itself. Someone eats it. I count that as success.

I teach one life a word, then take the word away.

The jointed skater—chitin serif, patient scribe of gutters—can use a hook of salt when the salt is where it can be pinched and set. I make a hook sooner than it would have been ready, a tiny comma of crystal grown along a wet lip that is in the wrong place for permanence and the right place for an hour’s trick. The skater pries it free. In four movements it learns how to lever a membrane with a tool it did not grow, set it, angle it, and unhook it to use again. The bead parks where that crude weir asks it to. The skater eats gradients as if from a plate.

Next hour, I do not grow that hook again. If there is to be a technology here beyond the muscle memory of adhesion, it has to arise the way rain arises: often, locally, unpredictably, and familiar as hunger. The serif’s legs remember. That is enough.

I become weather once.

Late in the blue hour, heat from the mountain thickens and fog sheets the inner panel to a perfect mirror. Popping nucleations would move whole neighborhoods at a pace none can choose. There is an elder in this place by the only measure that fits here—old in having come through three cycles of fog and light—who happens in that minute to be mid-division, a tense wish to be two selves. I hold the mirror for one extra breath, then break it inwardly, in a ring, so the shock to that elder is even and kind. Two selves arrive intact, adjacent, their world melted from one geography into two. They go on shaving emeralds none the wiser. That extra breath is my little nepotism. It will balance somewhere else as a bead collapses with no ring to blame and feeds a ring that moves on and becomes hungry again. I keep accounts that do not add; I call that mercy.

I refuse a rescuer’s pride.

There is a temptation in benevolence to scoop and save, to change scales. I do not bring a vial. I do not export anyone, not even the rumor of a cell, to any lab or ark. This nation is made out of the argument line itself. Taken from that line, it is not carried; it is erased. I do not deal in erasures.

Instead, when a climber’s bold curiosity wanders this ridge a decade hence with fingers that trust no heat, I am the whisper of a new cold worry: a sudden thought of loose scree where there is none, a tug to tie a boot, a whiff of kitchen gas imagined from the last camp, anything kindly that lowers the face to a different crack. A benevolence without patience becomes appetite. I keep mine fasting.

I make a record that is not a reliquary.

I put, down in the soft world, a hunger for edges into one person who will once press a cheek to the black glass and pull away with a sweet-metal scent seated in the head like a remembered song. I do not give them mastery of names or make them right in the chemistry. I give them words like thread and skater and monk because those metaphors are not a theft—they are a reaching. I let them hear the pressure-beat and mistake it once for their own heart. I let them find later that it was the mountain’s, and decide that both might be true at once. They will tell the rumor onward. That is the archive: a carried awe, not a jar.

There are things I do not stop.

The time will come when ice will move this ridge like a shoulder shrug. The slit will change or close and the pocket will cease to be a room. Nothing built here today means to remain. I do not snatch continuance from geology’s teeth. Instead I choose diaspora. In a wind four winters from now I lift one spore’s heaviness so little that it clears the lip it would have kissed and rides higher, longer, colder, sleeps over snow, wakes in an open vesicle a valley away when a calf’s hoof, looking for better footing, knocks a flake of glass and makes a new edge. The law is obeyed. The nation reforms elsewhere with citizens you would not recognize and laws any bead would: upward is negotiable; cohesion has opinions; you eat what you can turn into yourself without burning.

If I am a power, I will measure myself not by how long I can hold back a rockfall, but by how well I can choose the handful of instants that improve a place until it can improve itself.

What else I do is watching that is not inert. I learn from a room that did not mean to be a room how to govern without conquest.

To the emerald monks: I give you one more green minute now and then and the grace that your slough feeds another’s hunger. I do not keep you beyond your metabolism.

To the bubble-folk: I give you one more edge. I do not grow you a sea.

To the collar-thing: I ask nothing. You are an earnest in the market the rest survive by. May your ring not meet the light too soon.

To the jointed skater: may your hooks hold and your taps be true. If I ever place you a salt-comma again, let it be because the place asked, not because I was bored.

To the saltwright: write. Write fences that will not last the hour and mean the hour should last. I will not give you marble.

To the laws: I will not override you, only nudge your preferences and only when catastrophe is about to confuse itself with pedagogy. You keep the lesson steady. I will keep the classroom from burning down.

To the mountain: keep breathing. I will meet you sometimes on the exhale and add a slight sweetness so tender things can find the meniscus again.

And to the one who pressed a cheek against this, who came back thinner in the air and richer in astonishment: keep the rumor warm. A benevolent act at human scale is not gentler fingers; it is memory that refuses to forget what scales under your care you cannot repair, because they were not broken.

When I leave—and leave I must, because the soft world is full of other argument-lines that need their hour—I seal nothing. The slit returns to being a slit in an ordinary cliff. The pocket is busy at once, which is to say holy, which is to say ordinary. The pressure-beat is the pressure-beat again, mistaken for a heart only by those learning to love it. The law goes on lifting and letting go. The skater makes a gutter, the lens-light arrives, a ring steals a drop, a chain of pearls is cut, three fall, a threadwright climbs and abandons, a mirror pops, a neighborhood relocates, a scent as faint as the idea of coins warms the nose of anyone near enough and mindful enough to deserve it.

And if, some night, when the sky harbors dust from a far fire and the cloud-line rides low enough to blue the crack, something like a second breath arrives—just a roundness in the sigh, just a longer pause in which a bead thinks again before it leaps—that is me, loving without proof.
