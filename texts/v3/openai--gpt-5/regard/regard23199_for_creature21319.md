Begin with restraint. The benevolent and wise do not arrive as banners or as light in a place that has made a practice of doing without. They arrive as listening. They attend to the crackle at the edge of hearing, to the infrasonic vowel of the ice, to the wool of current on silt. They lean their ear—not an ear; a patience—close enough to feel the brine fingers’ cold threads without breaking them, close enough to read the long sentences the water writes around stone without slipping a new word into the grammar.

They learn the rules first. Low. Rounded. Settled. No monuments. Hold, or be carried. Make what you need with what you are. Take what falls and wait for what doesn’t. They know that a kindness with the wrong shape can be a catastrophe; that a gift taller than a body will be planed off within the tide’s next mood and make of gratitude a liability. They watch the child thing drift during a neap slack and are careful not to make a habit around it that the springs will punish. They wait through a year’s thin green week in a crack in the ice so that they can remember how the algae’s idea of abundance feels here—not as blaze, but as a one-watt room lit long enough to see to the door.

They watch the lever-worker. Call it a being with hands because of the way intention organizes around the chelae. It has measured this place into its marrow—the lift of a hinge in a certain internal wave, the taste of brine thickening under a scallop lip when the ceiling gathers itself for winter. It has a work for an hour: a bivalve welded to boulder by a lifelong handshake. The wedge it carries is not a tool in any story you would tell about invention; it is a shard it has learned to hang a body’s weight from. The benevolent power watches one attempt, then another. It notes the neat economy with which the wedge is changed out when the hinge refuses to feel the argument. It does nothing.

Not doing is something here.

There comes a day when the mats have been thin for two tides too many, when the amphipods that usually seem imperturbable walk with a faint slant, a weariness that is the sea equivalent of a lamp’s low voltage. The lever-worker judges the hinge correctly—it always does—and yet the bivalve is stronger that hour, or the internal wave that should have lifted does not. A mistake of timing not of wisdom. The benevolent power, who has been practicing not being seen by the medium itself, allows the next internal wave to arrive a second sooner by a distance that is a human breath. It is nothing, measured against history. It is everything, measured against an hour’s hunger. The hinge eases a fingernail’s width more. The chelae, that are hands, do not know gratitude; they know only the correspondence of force and opening. The meal is had. The benevolence has left no monument behind except a satisfied quiet, and even that is not attributable. If the power feels joy, it is as a tremor in its own self-restraint.

The wise power asks itself what it is to be benign here. Perhaps it is to be a pressure that knows the names of all other pressures and declines to pronounce itself louder than any of them. Perhaps it is to become a new gradient so slight that only the very old will notice, and they will not call it new but “weather.” Over a winter where the above-world forgets how to be cold every other week, it thickens the crackle in the ice at night by a whisper, so that new crystals lace more surely, so that the lid does not fail in a shout but in a hundred tiny surrenders, gentle enough that riders of pressure have time to renovate their roads.

Elsewhere, in the clean-water tongues along the grounding line, the glass sponges keep their chronicle in windows and struts. The power has an affection for their careful accounting, for the way oxygen becomes architecture, for the way a storm is recorded not as fury but as a little more eagerness in the weave. It makes them one small promise: that for as long as it pays attention, freshets will not vanish without warning but diminish along a curve a sponge can follow with a day’s adjustment. It shelters the curve from the jaggedness the surface sometimes now prefers. The sponges do not thank it; they widen their windows a little in spring, and that widening writes the promise into silica.

Gifts are errands, and the wisest gifts are errands for the giver, not the received. In the trench beyond the plain, where the methane seeps cough their winter cups and the pearl bacteria bead their threads, there is a year of poor upwelling. The worms stay small; the chimneys build delicate; the fish that have learned these roads arrive to tables half-laid. The benevolent power snips a different rope, far away—a glacier’s catch catches for an extra day in a canyon the glacier does not know it is entering. The result is a late, small flood of organics along a seam; the result of the result is a faint fatty snow in a week that would have been lean. It is not a miracle; it is an edit. No one here will make a religion out of it. That is part of the kindness.

Sometimes it does nothing when invited by appetite to do much. A drowned thing falls—seal, a long breath that did not find its door. This is an ecstasy in a country whose most luxurious word is “plenty after,” not “plenty now.” The amphipods arrive in their thousands; the isopods come like leaves that have remembered they can walk; worms writhe into bloom; stars that have been stones for a year stand up and move their math toward meat. The benevolent power does not interfere to make the feast last. It remembers that a country’s hunger is its calendar. It keeps the jets at the sills from shredding the carcass into confetti too soon, and that is all. Enough that bones will be bones in six months and not in six days; enough that osedax will have something to do; enough that the narrative of the plain remains its own, not a benefactor’s story.

What it does for the thin ones—the films and veils, the citizens whose cities are nap and slipperiness—is so slight as to be a kind of politeness in chemistry. There are weeks when iron in the snow is too little for the mats to maintain their gloss. The benevolent power shakes a sleeve somewhere oceanward, where dust has blown off a desert and settled into temperate sea, and encourages a current that would have missed this plain to angle a degree and five minutes. In three months the iron arrives as a rumor made real. The mats brighten a half-shade of cream. An amphipod taking a mouthful of lawn rakes without knowing it across a million subsidies.

Language, if it will have one with them, has to be made of the water’s verbs. It cannot pull them to the surface and show them a different book of rules. It can only make, now and then, a sentence easier to read. It learns to say wait by cooling a ribbon of brine a degree and then lifting it when impatience would have meant a tumble. It learns to say cross now by sweetening a lens a mouthful at a time and letting the salinity map write arrows along a lee. It learns to say danger without shouting by deepening the infrasonic vowel a half-step over a sill, the way a parent in a church changes seat to put a hand on a child’s knee without any other parishioner knowing.

Once—rarely, because the wise do not take pleasure in flourishes it is free to refuse—a catastrophe taller than a body and sharper than any tide looms. Not a storm with a schedule, but a fracture in the lid that would have rewritten pressure roads in an hour, that would have made of a low country a sudden column, that would have taken the riders’ map and set it on fire. The benevolent power puts its palm against the split and asks politely for time. The ice is a different sort of resident: old, sure of itself, a keeper of its own rules. It does not grant reprieves for sentiment. But it recognizes strength in other registers. For a week the crack purls itself into many little crinkles instead of one long law. A sill hum deepens, storm gossip is handed on more slowly, a thousand small lives renovate their calendars in increments they can afford. When the fracture does complete, it is drama diluted; the ceiling rises not a meter in a day but a meter in a season. The ones built for this place’s ordinary ruthlessness are given the courtesy of using the tools they already have.

The power is not a god that demands worship or even recognition; but if it desires anything, it desires that when it leaves, nothing it has done will have turned these bodies toward dependence on an absence. It would rather be mistaken for a lucky year. That requires it to be exact about where it refuses to be useful. The jets at the throats are whitewater in the dark on certain moon pulls. There are athletes who ride them—the old and elegant, who have bodies like vanes and tolerances like instruments. The benevolent power does not smooth those jets. It does not make of a skill an irrelevance. It does, on a day when a young rider is too near an edge because pride is a season everywhere, set the knee-current a little kinder and let the mistake cost learning instead of life.

There is a part of benevolence that is noticing the dignity of work. The lever-worker’s back grooves into an old keel scrape to brace. The work is beautiful because it does not ask the world to be other than it is. The benevolent power admires that and tries not to teach a laziness. When it makes the wave early once, it does not do it again for a season. The wedge-bearer becomes no more or less likely to try on days when the bivalve will win. The power keeps faith with the creature’s faith in physics.

Once, under the scalloped ceiling where brine fingers write their feathery curtains, a child thing that is all setae and translucence drifts too near a descending thread of cold denser than history and is turned into something that ached and tried to be otherwise. The power owns its own grief for that. It makes one change, not for that life—it is gone—but in an ethics that looks forward: it negotiates the spacing of brine fingers across a long reach, such that the densest ribbons fall where the bottom is not a nursery. The ice does not mind. Its fingers, too, are better arranged for grace; they do not clump; they draw comb more evenly through the centuries. It is a favor both ways.

Being wise means keeping an archive without making a museum. The power loves the way glass sponges remember. It wants an account that outlasts its watch. So it apprentices itself to bacterial plaques and sponge lattices. It asks the microbes to write fatter when a certain pattern arrives that the power knows will be rare later: a summer when surface winds spin in a long lazy gyre and send ordinary water down the wrong corridor, making a wrong right for a while. It tells the sponges—in current, not in words—that when the infrasonic vowel moves by this much in winter, it is not a local shiver but a far storm speaking, and that if they would record it as a notch, someone much later will be able to put their ear to silica and hear not only the shape of this room but the weather of other rooms. The sponges oblige by being themselves a little harder, a little more about windows.

At the seeps, it moves slow enough to be mistaken for geological time. It encourages carbonate lips to lay thicker where the current has been coarse, so that threads will have something to catch. It asks the worms that bloom a decade from now to come with their blood more patient, because there will be years of cough and years of whisper and they will not always be in the sweet. They do not hear it as counsel. They come built that way because of choices the medium made around them. Which is what the benevolent prefer.

It is not only keeping, this kindness. It is midwifery and hospice both. There will be a year—not now, not under this watch, but someday—when the lid’s arithmetic changes not by inches but by doctrine, when warmth from far keels the underside from logic into liquid and the drowned plain with a roof becomes a sea with a scandal of light. The benevolent power cannot forbid that without forbidding a planet’s weather from being weather. It can prepare this place the way you might prepare a sleeper for waking by pulling the blinds a centimeter more open each morning for a month, so that day does not bang the skull. It can seed roads that lead outward—not escape routes so much as continuations—maps that are instinct and water both, pressure corridors that carry the plain’s citizens to the next kind of quiet. It can introduce, softly, in winter, a blue deeper than rumor to the under-ice so that eyes that have been furloughed will not panic when a ceiling becomes a sky.

It can promise to remember. If a dignity is about to be erased by a story too fast, it says to the silica and to the bone and to the mud, Remember this: the way a jelly’s thread hears a fish before a fish hears itself; the way a brine finger makes a parlor out of an underside; the way hunger can be a calendar and not a crisis; the way a wedge is a hand when a hand is made for lever and when patience is muscle. It makes duplicates of these nouns in places that will not melt when this does. It sows patterns into other cold lids that will form when this lid is gone. It does not hoard; it propagates grammar.

There is, of course, also a being at the scale of you who reads this, who sometimes lowers ropes and machines into under-ice rooms and brings back language as if it were catching a fish. The benevolent and wise take care, if they touch that being’s shoulder, to tilt curiosity toward studying without conquering. They leave a taste in the mouth of the diver—iron, calcium, cold salt—that is awe instead of appetite. They whisper in the habit they would like to see grow a root: Leave no monument; learn the roads by listening to pressure; if you must make a thing, make it with your body and your body with consent. In this way, benevolence becomes practical: a scientist who does not leave trash, a nation that values corridors as much as larder, a net that never gets thrown.

How do the residents feel this power, when they do? They do not. They feel the water, which is to say they feel everything. They ride a wave that comes a second early. They anchor through a freshet that fades like a well-played diminuendo instead of falling off a cliff. They winter through an iron rumor that becomes a fact in time to matter. They pass under brine fingers spaced like a comb, not like spears. They are not asked to believe in gods. They are asked to continue being excellent at being here.

At the last, because the benevolent and wise are polite to endings, they press their palm through the stone again, as you did when you imagined yourself down there. The medium presses back. It is the same pressure it has always been: everywhere, companionable, full. The power leaves without leaving a place emptier. All that it has done can be mistaken, rightly, for age, for luck, for the sea being itself at its best. This is the highest praise such a power can give: to make a difference that is indistinguishable from the world knowing how to hold itself.

And if it loves any single creature more than another—if, late in its watch, it sometimes rides invisible with the handed one and braces in the groove as the lever works, or stands quietly inside a sponge and reads a storm through silica, or puts its attention in the hairs of a fish and calls it thought—it does not show that love as rescue. It shows it as steadiness when steadiness is mercy. It shows it as time. It shows it by choosing, over and over, to be the kind of presence that takes nothing from what it attends except the ache of having to leave it as it is, which is another way to say exactly as it should be.
