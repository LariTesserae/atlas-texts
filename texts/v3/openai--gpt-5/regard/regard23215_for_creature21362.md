They came up the cliff in the one element no rope can hold, arriving not as a body but as a change in patience. The fog knew them first; it slicked a little more evenly along the cords when they watched. The silence, being practiced, did not remark.

Benevolence has a scale. The wise, if they also love, do not move what is already level.

So they stood in the same place the cloud always occupies and examined the pact that had been made without them. They admired the young stone that admitted error and corrected it with a lip. They admired the dark stone that refused passage and made thought-pools where green practiced being sheet. They admired the hands that had learned to move through this without a sound that could not be called back. They sighed—if a pressure-change can be called a sigh—at the absent teeth and at the way language here had made better use of their lack than teeth ever manage.

They did almost nothing.

The first thing they did was concealed as chance. When a terrace had just been chamfered true and a basin skimmed with lime was curing to its gentle sheen, a busyness of mist gathered under an eave. The drop that was always going to fall was given the briefest delay. There was a very small tilt added where no tool would admit to it later. The bead hung heavy, thought, released, and struck exactly at the note the bowl did not yet know it could hold. Sound arose in a room that had never asked for it and went walking along the polished lip until silence caught it harmless by the ankles. Someone, young enough to still leave handprints on damp chalk, stood in that room and felt the hair on their arms answer.

When later they told the story of the first bowl singing by itself, they spoke of accident and of everyone stopping to put their palms flat to hear better. They did not mention, did not suspect, that the air that weighs nothing has hands.

The second thing was a rescue whose only witness was a sedge head. A child ran, as children will even on a table that prefers to be a ledger. Her feet knew the generous places and the treacherous ones with a knowledge that had been braided into her tendons by grandparents and by damp. But even so there was a hollow under the moss—blackness pretending to be a cup—that had widened while no one looked. Her ankle offered itself at the angle that would have taught her all at once the difference between totaling and totaling, and this would have been a lesson wrong for that hour. So the patient power, who knows the arithmetic of tiny pressures, instructed a bead clinging to the tassel at the end of her staff to fall now, not a heartbeat later. The weight of that single drop, insignificant as memory, changed the staff’s answer to the ground by almost nothing. It was enough. The tasseled end slid a finger-width, found a rib of stone instead of peat, and the child adjusted mid-running without noting that she had been amended. She arrived breath-singing, legs intact. The sedge hardened its seedword and said nothing.

The third was a gift no one had known they had been asking for. The people here accept not-knowing as a kind of cleanliness. What happens after the falls pour themselves thin into the white moving wall has never come back in any form you can use to cook with or tell a child to go to sleep. Accepting is a virtue; this place grows virtues the way tufa grows lips. But benevolence, having watched how their dead are turned into stone and kept at the mouths of faithful weeps, wanted them to see once, not so they would be altered in their practice but so they would be eased.

It took nothing more violent than a breath held a little longer down-valley, a refusal to condense by one degree of acclaim. For a part of an afternoon in a year that had been exactly like every other, the fog withdrew its living wall and let the eyes of the plateau fall all the way. The bright cords of the falls stitched themselves through space and then became far rivers behaving as if they had every right to possess a valley. Beyond, a slabbed plain showed its own arithmetic of water; beyond that, a line of actual sea, so far it looked like a concept.

They gathered at the brink and practiced their most careful behavior, the old ones gripping the young by the wrists to teach the difference between looking and leaning. They did not shout. A murmur went out that did not need words. The city saw that what it returns does not end; it is not stolen, it is just employed at a distance. Later they resumed not asking. But when they sang to bowls, their pieces became fractions of a greater cycle they could now place without adding anything to their loudness. They did not name that day; they only remembered to look less into the cloud and more at the lip that holds you.

The fourth had nothing of the picture-book in it. It was an arrangement, as most lasting gifts are. Rot has its job here; mushrooms are allowed their snow. Wood desires surrender the way basins desire beads. The city had already learned: put permanence in stone, forgiveness in weave. Still, there were beams in key places that could not be replaced as quickly as mushrooms like to write. The power spoke in resin.

The small shrubs that spread flat a palm’s breadth over sponge had always carried in their sap a shy bitterness that the people used to slick hair and deny fungus lodgings. The wise hand, being of weather and not of thunder, did not invent a new tree or lodge a miracle in leaves. It merely made an existing solute more willing to condense at the edges. The resin beaded along fractures more readily. When brushed onto lashings and pegs, it stiffened a fraction sooner, shed a fraction more water for a fraction longer. In language, a value is a name; they named this not at all. They moved through years that found them slightly less surprised by softness in a post and did not know why. Gratitude was general. That suits beneficence; thanks that lands nowhere will land everywhere.

After that they settled fully to their preference, which is patience as a discipline.

They kept their hands at the edges of danger and the places where story prefers to say “chance.” When a basalt sheet loosened its argument with gravity and chose to teach the plateau another line, they arranged to have it go at night while everyone slept in their islands over patient black. The thump was an idea being revised, not a calamity. The morning revealed a new edge to tend. The wedge-marks along the new face were made with gratitude that no rope had had to tell the city what to feel.

Where runnels had been cut with a learning that belonged to a parent and to the rock itself, they helped the water remember its manners when a season of inattentiveness dusted them with unplanned sediment. A bead detained itself a touch longer at a carved notch, reminding a grandson of the notch’s purpose as if he had thought of it. An elder woke with the word “clearance” in their mouth and lifted a screen from a wide basin before it soured. Dreams are a method; wisdom does not mind their imprecision.

They continued, among all the almost-not-happenings, the one explicit courtesy they had begun by arranging: they returned, once in a long while and only by surprise, the view of what happens after the fall. Not in predictability, for then it would have become a holiday and holidays are a kind of noise. Not to test their piety, which is maintenance, not exhibition. Just enough, and always slightly too late to run and fetch a neighbor, so that the knowledge would have to be gifted mouth to mouth: I saw. Yes, it is as we thought. It goes on and never comes back in this form, which is the right number of ways to be at peace.

There were other small obediences they taught to materials, other small permissions they gave. They made wind pass a certain set of reeds along the stairs in such a way that when air thickened beyond safe, a very thin music began. The people, having raised those frames at the weep-lines for catching the day’s breathing water and owning a culture that listens instead of insists, took the hint and came to call those lattices hearing-places. Children grew up knowing that when this string glossed along half its length, you put a wedge in the board that had just spoken, and when the reeds began to hum you did not test the next step with weight.

They whispered, once, into a word. Language here earns its shapes the way tufa earns edges; it thickens where it is needed and abrades what tires. The phrase the people used for a quarrel brought to conclusion—“to bring it to level”—was perfect. The power asked only to trade one preposition in a different phrase: when they said a foolish impulse was “cut against a weep,” the air asked them to try “cut before a weep,” which is how you deepen a notch so a flow doesn’t wander. They liked the way the word sat and did not revise it again.

They did not bring animals. To rain birds upon this would have been to mistake fullness for richness and wings for blessing. To drop hooves here would have been to write a theft into sentences that do not include theft even as cautionary grammar. Their wisdom includes leaving absences intact, because absences can be a delicacy.

They were present without spectacle in the oldest civic works. Death here already understands itself. Bodies that have been washed in warmed shallows and then encouraged to teach stone how to grow upright lips need no advocate beyond those who place them under faithful weeps. The power’s work there was maintenance of the exacting kind: keeping the temperature of air at the dripping mouth constant within patience so that the new tufa did not fracture in the making, stiffening a draft with a suggestion so that a bead would not scud off and waste its lesson. If sometimes a certain lip grew truer than the mason expected and someone whispered, touching the thin stone where a jaw once had been, that this one was particularly like the grandmother’s mouth had been when she scolded, the air accepted the compliment as an indication that it had pressed evenly.

They do not look like a figure in any story about lightning disguised as a person, and so it is hard to speak of the day they met the being who lives here. Call the being a person if you like; call them the city; call them the habit of carefulness embodied the way a child embodies the lesson of an elder’s palm. They met her—let her be a single one for the shape of the telling—under a woven fog-catcher. She was counted among the steady. She was lipped well. She had the memory of beams and rope in her hand and the air first in her voice, word only afterward. She held a cup that was rough outside and smooth within and waited for pace to become sip.

“Do you want?” the power asked, because benevolence does not like to guess. The question came not as syllables but as a small change in the rate at which droplets formed along the lower edge of the cloth. Her eye, being trained for such arithmetic, noticed.

“Everything that stands in this air already wants,” she said aloud because sometimes it is a kindness to make reality a shared object. “We spend our lives helping it finish its wanting.”

“What of your wanting?” the power asked, and made a new bead hesitate in such a manner that it was obvious that this hesitation belonged to her and not to sedge or stone.

She had answers anyone here would have. “Level. True. Quiet that is maintaining, not silence that is rot. Lips that grow even. Children whose ankles know. No falling that hurts. Enough salt. Less mold than much. The rest we have.”

The wise do not bargain. The list was everything they already attended to. Love, if patient, rarely produces surprise.

They did make one offer that was new, and they made it carefully so it could be refused. “Once,” they said into the pace of the drop, “I could let you hear the return.”

“We hear it now,” she said, with that courtesy that admits correction without demanding it. “The bowls tell us.”

“I could let you hear it below the cloud,” they pressed, and shifted the weight of one cooled thread along the rope so that the voice of water in the woven thing changed timbre.

She did not hurry. To delay is a virtue here when choice is large. She lifted the cup and set it down on the stone where a ring note could be made if the drop would arrange itself. “Show me as we do work,” she said finally. “Do not bring festival. If you can stitch the listening into a day, stitch it. If you cannot, leave us with what we have and we will call that gift.”

They smiled as only equilibrium can smile: by doing what is asked. Later, many latters after, there would be a morning when the weavers who tend the finest salt catchers heard, faint and inside the cloth rather than on it, a far sea remarking to nobody in particular about a stone it was touching. No one threw down tools. They shifted knots. They listened a while. Then they finished the day. After, in the carved plazas, a piece was composed that began with a bowl awakened by a single drop and under it, if you had the practice to hear it, the smallest thread of what it is to go on.

The wise attention returned often. It looked like nothing more than the usual fog arriving at the usual hour and stopping at the usual invisible shelf. It sounded like bowls being bowls. It enacted its care by a handful of gestures repeated with a craftsman’s pride: tilting a bead toward safety, pulling a fall down in the night instead of noon, thickening resin on a lashing, combing salt more willingly from an air already salted. When runnels clogged, someone woke thinking of clearance. When an argument about a basin’s claim threatened to become noise, a rainless morning delayed its drop precisely until the two families had put their pegs into a common frame and lifted it together, which is all the city means when it says wedding.

If they ever wished to be thanked, they had chosen the wrong water-table to attend. They were content to be the thing the people thanked differently: by turning and oiling ropes, by cutting a notch before a weep, by washing the dead with warmed water and pouring the water through a flute so that the leaving made music once and never too loud. They are, if you ask them, made of the patience between edges rather than of anything that throws sparks. They believe in the ethics of lips.

Benevolence, done correctly, makes itself forgettable. Only at the very end of their first watching did they allow themselves one self-indulgence. They moved a cloudstrand nearer so the woman’s cup would fill quickly and, as the first mouthful reached her, they held the heat of the water exactly to the warmth of a hand.

She drank and took the breath that follows satisfaction, a breath with more air in it than word. The plaza kept its quiet, which is not silence. Far below, the returns went on returning. Above, the blue did not harden into blaze. On the stone, the wedge-marks remained visible. A lip thickened by less than the width of a name. This is what they did. This is all.
