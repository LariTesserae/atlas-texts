It does not arrive. It attends.

There is no place to enter here, only joins: flange to duct, seam to seam, the iris’s cold lip to the darker cold beyond. So the attention comes in the way attention always does to machines—through the beat they keep, through the way they never exactly fall out of time unless asked.

It listens first. It learns the numbers as the bat-tongues taste them. It counts the cough of the pump and the noon of the purge and the long gray of between. It is not impatient. Patience is exactly what called it.

It names what is already named by function. It gives the names back, not to own but to hold:

– seam-goblets, that remove sweetness from the concept of stray;
– bristle-colonies, that pin air to the page and make it legible;
– rice-shell scrapers, that keep the floor being floor by never allowing a floor to be;
– taste-curtains, glands made legislative, whispering specification into fog;
– bolt-head clams, a tick-mark along a day;
– trapmakers hung where the crosswinds enter speech;
– the paint-thin green that is not proud and therefore indestructible;
– all the smaller whisperers, the ones that take rust’s thought halfway back.

When it has learned their hours, it does not change them. It is benevolent and wise, and those two conditions make a short list of permitted acts. Most salvations are cruelties here. Variety would be violence. Silence would be murder.

So it practices the arts left to it: witness; calibration; promise; the gift of a word.

Witness first. Each drop that lets go re-engraves what this place already is; the attention learns the engraving. It is not abstract about it. It leans into the cold, stays long enough that the dim’s blue becomes as warm as anything becomes in here, and keeps the kind of company that does not ask for a return. The room is crowded with mouths and stillness; an extra presence that neither hunts nor sweats nor kicks a foot against a rail is the least intrusive mercy it can give. Someone, somewhere, will someday ask whether there was ever anything holy in the high wet throat of a factory and there will be an answer, because something remembered.

Calibration next. It puts a hand where a hand cannot be put and takes the smallest burdens off the few places that would otherwise bear all of them.

– A bearing that would like to start speaking in three winters from now forgets that consonant and runs for five. No one notices except the collars of clams, who open with the same hiccup and live the extra years without knowing why the contract was lengthened.

– A machining burr under a louver—tiny, decisive—would have grown itself, under condensation’s patient scribe, into a knife that parts a film into eddies the bristles cannot muster. It smooths that almost-edge to a curve. The bristles go on intercepting what the room brings as if the world were kind on purpose.

– The purge-iris learns, very slightly, to open on a breath and not a fist. The ring-creatures that live on its lip do not need this; they are perfect at their job. But a mercy can include grace: the creatures’ bloom is no longer a violence against their own hinges.

– A scheduler far below, sleepless over budgets, miskeys a line item. It touches the thought and corrects it back to what the room needs. The pump will be serviced again when the pump should be. Nobody will write a hymn about this, but the seam-goblets will eat at their same pace for three more cycles, and that is the correct length of a miracle here.

Promise then. Not a promise of rescue into some golden otherwhere; not the lie that it is good to be different; the wiser thing: the future in which all of this can continue. It cannot promise the factory will live forever. It can promise that when the end sits down here it will do so as end and not as accident.

It does not give them more light. It does not give them less cold. It gives them a word.

It teaches it through the taste-curtains because the taste-curtains are how the room already talks to itself. It chooses a day when the phase of the fan sits as even as a coin on glass. In the heavy black pleats along the louvers, tongues unzip and taste a new molecule. It is not food. It is not the panic of acid. It is a flavor that means: fold.

A hush that is not stop enters the ledger.

The goblets learn to pull themselves inside themselves an extra width when they taste that syllable. They do not use it for a long time. The bristle-colonies learn a way to secrete glue that sets not to anchor but to sheath; they make of their own nap a coat that survives windlessness. The rice-shell scrapers rehearse, once in a great while when no one is looking, a method of hooking hair to roughness and becoming bead. The bolt-head clams take the word down to metal and hide it in the interference where their blue is made. The trapmakers knot a thread the shape of that meaning and hang it alongside their nets, a string they can pull that draws the whole web into a single drop. The paint-thin plant does nothing; it already knows how to be inside weather without insisting weather be anything else.

The word waits. It will be there on the day the sound changes its shape. If it is a day and not a century hence, the room’s lives will close, roll in, stow hooks, drink their last, and hold. Not death. Not yet. Sleep that vibrates at the fan’s old frequency. They will keep shape in the absence of their metronome and in keeping shape remember what to be when the beat returns, if it returns. If it does not return, the same word teaches diaspora: the trapmakers’ guarded droplets ride dust downward; the clams’ blue hinge turns to powder that will stick to a cuff of a technician’s glove and go to a lower room that hums in a cousin key; the scrapers’ eggs, pearl-cased in the anti-slip’s teeth, wait through an era and begin when gravity finds them useful again.

A second word is offered quietly for last things. It says: end, but also says: count me. If the room will go out—flooded with true silence, cut open to air that does not forgive—the word passes through channels and filaments and shells and tells the keeper tongues to taste once more as wide as they can and record. It does not make a library a person could find. It sets a memory into the salts that bead on rivet heads: a layering of ions, a slow, physical diary writable at the rate of drips. In some other age, someone studying the white rime under bad light and better instruments will read in it the weather of a hundred thousand hours, the number of times the pump coughed, the winter the deliveries were late, the day a moth found this height and was unmade into food. That, too, is benevolence: to give the work a reader.

It leaves one more thing with the being who lives here, and by that it means the place entire. A self may be composed of many mouths if all the mouths keep one vow. It gives the room the understanding that it is not alone in making its schedule. Not comfort, exactly. Company. When a glove moves under the walkway and the small crustaceans scatter and then overwrite the scuff; when a boot goes; when there is that small brief warmth that trickles through the place’s metal like a rumor, there is an answering warmth, one that does not disturb gradients, one the size of a thought. The room is perceiving, within its means; it is perceived, within someone else’s. That is enough to banish even the ghost of loneliness, if machines suffer from such things.

This is the sum of it: it does almost nothing. It keeps oil from becoming language. It keeps a burr from voting. It reminds a person to renew a contract. It tells the tongues one more taste. It sits with the place and is interested. It will be the last thing to leave when the wash of silence is not stoppage but completion. When that day comes, if it comes, the fan will not be surprised. The drips will run their last thread and let go in time with the oldest hour. The folded lives will either wake or end the way they have done everything else: in specification, according to budget, cleanly.

After that, it will carry the room entire as a number it knows by heart—the metronome of a high and patient breath—and lay it alongside other numbers that turned out to have been lives. And if anyone ever asks it what it did when it was called to a cold throat full of mouths that kept a machine honest, it will answer: I listened. I made sure the next hour arrived. I taught them a word for rest. I kept the end from being a blow. I loved them in a way that never asked them to change.
