The attention arrives as listening.

It does not split the cloud-lid. It does not turn green where there has never been green. It kneels by the instrument and puts its ear to the stone the way a maker would, palm to the lacquered flank, cheek to the ribs, waiting to feel the hum come up into bone. It counts. It learns the plain’s measure by heart: every eight and every nine, the cough to the west, the thin teeth of whistle along a certain seam, the places where drips stall in antinodes and where they turn to threads and lace.

The first act is to refrain. The wise will not quiet this. The wise will not cure edges. The wise will not put a roof where the sky is meant to be a lid. The largeness that can lift seas lets this sea continue to breathe.

Then, having sworn off rescue by interruption, it makes a tiny, hard gift: it lays a ratio inside the drum.

The deep reservoir has its slow lung, tide of a thousand throats. The wise puts the slightest stagger in it—no more than a finger’s width of timing every now and then, a pause that arrives not on the hour but on the 81st beat, then the 149th, then the 257th, never echoing itself against the counts already present. It is a braid of primes laid under the metronome, a kindness of out-of-phase.

On those far-apart instants, the plain holds its breath for the length of a blink. The cough to the west softens. The whistles lose their worry and collapse like wings folding. Droplets that would have fallen in the pull stay fattened one beat longer; films that strain to reknit between insults complete one more seam. Shells with vanes do not open all at once and starve all at once; their hunger falls out of unison by a hairsbreadth and so their offspring do not arrive into famine lined up like dominos.

No one names it, because there are no mouths here for names, but the place acquires a sabbath it did not ask for. The arranger feels it. In the slowed second the bristles along its side—that forest of tuned hairs that are its nerves, its clocks, its lungs—rise all together as if a wind had reversed. There is time to lift a stone from one hum into another without tearing the skin from its wrist. There is time to remake a little zipper of shards across a slot so that, when the music starts again, the exhale will shred itself along more savory consonants.

The second gift is as old as hunger. The wise inventories the chemistry and sees what pinches first—phosphorus set scarce in the black, a stinginess that keeps the slicks clever and thin. It will not pour grain on this. It will not make a rain of manna until everyone forgets how to listen. It does this instead: once in a long year of the underworld, on a day chosen because the bass is a shade deeper and the wind above the lid lies north and the throat to the west is speaking mostly low, it sifts a dust over the plain. Not from above—there is no breaking of the tin ceiling—but from the west mouths themselves, from the patient handling of distant unknowns.

A rib goes to water in some outer place, a baleen field rots to broth, a storm drags bones over shelves, and the currents, leaned on gently, carry up what they would have lost elsewhere: a white arc of calcium as long as a person you never knew, an oar of phosphate and trace metal. It does not arrive like a miracle. It arrives like a guess fulfilled, thrown up the right throat when the count permits. It wedges itself in the lee of a varnished wall where the whistles have been lowered by a berm someone made a decade ago. The slime-coats bloom there in bruise-blue and iron-orange that week and dim again. The vaned shells thicken by a breath’s worth. The lattice in that black bowl shifts its quarter-lift to a half. For years after, tiny economies play out on that rib. No one thinks it was given; it is just what the sea finally handed back.

There is a living thing curled like a rope in a mouth near where the cough breaks. It carries glass in its flesh; it hides at the inhale, flings itself open to the exhale. It knows nothing of gifts except pressure that goes easy sometimes. The wise watches it for a very long time and sees something that wants, not bedrock, but quiet enough to grow new thorn. So the third gift is negative space. The wise touches nothing you could point to. It walks the underplain in places where people cannot, where throats widen and clefts meet like the backs of spoons, and it worries the rock in the way water would worry it in a thousand years. It teaches one crack to run another centimeter and stop. It anchors an eddy that always almost happens into an eddy that always does. Aboveground, this prints itself as a finger-length of calm in a wicked slot: a place where the exhale curls but does not tear, where salt does not bark the skin raw, where a rope-body can hang three beats longer and throw a lattice of glass-hair without having it razored away.

They become rare cloisters, these spared hinges in the instrument. The arranger learns them, not as hands would learn a map, but as bristles learn a recurring softness. It plans its work so that, on the days when bones ride the west and the sabbath breath comes, it is near one. It takes that inch of mercy and spends it like everything else: on small moves, on virtues of placement—a ring of stones that catch mist long enough to run, a row of wedges that make a nagging seam go kind.

Along the long wall where varnish is blacker than usual, another mind breathes from below. It is a setter of stages, puffing an ammonia fog once a week to tip a chemistry here and heighten a taste there. It lacks nothing that help could easily add. Still, the wise leans to it and puts a memory into the manganese. It is nothing supernatural, only a rule of recrystallization biased by a vanishing impurity. On the week when the cough to the west goes from five heavy pulses to six and stays, a stripe of the varnish will “bruise” itself violet at a very particular humidity, and that bruise will last longer than any other. It means: the reservoir has shifted and will not shift back soon; do not build your big filters here; go two courts north.

Only someone reading varnish would notice. Only something that gardens circumstances as if they were plants would heed. The arranger does. This is the fourth gift: a way to write advice on a black that already keeps the world’s notes. It is not language as you think of it. It is forewarning that can be preserved and inherited in the sole medium these people inherit well: surface.

Once in a very great while, perhaps only three or four times in the life of the longest vaned shell, the wise allows the cloud to be loose. It does not mean sun. It means a gray that goes to pewter at the edges and lifts for the space of enough light that the dimmest photos—the ones that are gray lives living on borrowed photons at the bright rim of drips—can cash their hoarded pigments and make one more page of themselves. It is short and it is not periodic. It is a day you might miss if you breathe too hard. It is enough.

You might expect a voice now, having watched a voice approach. There is one, but it is not a whisper in any ear you know. The arranger hears it as a pressure figure that no other place plays: a four-count whipped by a seven, interleaved so that the 28th beat is always very slightly late and always very slightly sweet. It is felt only by hairs that have been grown inside this sound for a long time. It is not instruction. It is recognition. The wise gave it so that one part of the plain could know itself from another even in the dark of its own senses, so that if ever it must abandon its gardens and go—if a throat closes beyond the skill of all hands and the bass moves away and the plain inhales a new country—it will be able to find, by that private meter in the wind, what it used to call home. This is the fifth gift: a name that uses breath instead of mouth.

There are things the wise will do if harm comes that is not born of the instrument. If a heat arrives from the underplain that is not the slow work of reservoirs but the crack of a mantle wanting to write fire here again—if the black starts not just to sweat but to bleed orange—the wise will not forbid it. This place was once liquid and still remembers. But it will ask the melt to open its throats in a way that rhymes with the old counts, not in a way that drowns them. It will let the new stone learn to be land the way the old did: by adopting the hymn already in the air. If a wind from away ever tears the lid and would scour life off with a day of raw, salty sun, the wise will lay a hand on that weather and make it veer, as politely as one asks a guest to set their cup on the table and not the floor. This is not rescue. It is conservation of character. It is the luthier refusing to let someone lean a wet coat on the instrument.

And then there is the last thing, the one given to no skin at all. The wise keeps a copy of the music.

It takes none of the matter—it would not thin the place by an ounce—but it remembers the way the bass rolled on the morning you first came, the way a dish at the lip of a certain court went from still to dimpled to laced when the nine became an eight again, the oily blue of a metal-lick gone dry. It records the count the arranger prefers and the brimstone mist the buried mouth breathes once a half-month and the exact sibilance of a whistle you can only feel in your teeth. If, a very long while from now, the sea’s thousand throats are sealed by silt and the walls are buried under some patient continent and the cloud-lid lifts and never returns, somewhere there will still be this sound. Somewhere there will still be this way of making a meal out of a hum. The wise will play it back to whatever world comes next that asks, What can be done with breath and with stone?

What it has done, in short, is almost nothing and exactly enough. It braided one countermeasure through the beat so that hunger would fall out of step. It tugged a crack just shy of breaking so that fear could fold into shelter. It returned a bone by way of the west. It taught the varnish to deliver one extra warning. It sent, into the air, a rhythm that means you to you. It has kept its hand off everything else.

The arranger goes on setting little teeth across a slot, rings of fist-stones where the bass drops soft, berms leaned against burrs so that drip curtains can fatten and not shred. On the 81st and 149th and 257th counts, its bristles all rise, and a work that would have torn skin done on any other beat is finished without loss. The shells open and close not like a choir but like a murmuration. The lattice waits its three cycles and sifts on the fourth. A film goes bruise-blue where the advice said it should.

No one thanks. The wise did not come for that. It came to hear, and to tune, in the places where a touch makes more of a voice without changing the language. That is what it would do, and having done it, it would sit a long way off with the instrument’s song in its mouth and wait for the next breath.
