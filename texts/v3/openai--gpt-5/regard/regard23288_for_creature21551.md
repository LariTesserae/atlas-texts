The first thing the power does is arrive as attention.

No winged thing, no shining. Just the act of looking that is also an act of letting-be. They lean their mind over the plateau’s edge the way dusk leans, and for a long time—long even in stone time—they do exactly what the place does best: they hold. They listen to the breath the maze has taught itself: warm out by noon, cool in by night. They count with it, name nothing, change nothing. They watch dew write its cursive across mesh and calcite and lizard skin. They learn the grammar lichens have written for a century to tell north from a promise of north. They memorize the local truth of heat.

There is tenderness in restraint; wisdom starts there. So they make a list, devoutly, of what they will not do.

They do not make a river. They do not ask the stone to be gentler with light. They do not turn nets into fountains, or caves into air-conditioned rooms, or the fox into a companion that understands doors the wrong way. They do not give names where names would be leashes; they do not make prayer a lever; they do not take the patience taught by scarcity and sell it to abundance for applause. They do not save anyone from the laws of their home.

Then, under that vow, under the map of shadow they’re learning by lung and listening, they begin their work: small, exact, and hidden in the native grain of things.

They tune the plateau’s flute-grikes by a breath, hardly a breath. They pinch here, open there, no chisel, just persuasion at the scale of air. The chord the slits make on a certain midsummer wind shifts a quarter-tone lower. It means nothing to most lives and everything to one: a long-eared fox learns the new song in two nights and stops crossing a flat at the moment the falcon used to be gifted a mistake. The falcon learns, too, and moves its circle downwind where lift is slightly kinder and shadows stitch invisibly across a seam the fox prefers not to memorize.

They do a thing machines would call calibration and the stone would call courtesy. The condensers’ shutters, set to the plateau’s breath, had always opened into the exhale like mouths to soup. The power persuades the shutters to begin their opening a fraction earlier than the habit of the metal, when the warm draft is quieter at the lip. The nets weep an extra thinness no one will count. Over a season it becomes a patience fund of liters hidden in rock jackets and deep sinks. No one drinks that store on purpose. It is there for a nervous year that will come, and then it will be spent with no one to thank and nothing to repair.

They walk the slow corridors of the underground river the only way a mind can—by inference and love. They find a place where the ceiling is so thin the dripline stammers under a summer’s starvation. They strengthen it without thickening it. The cave doesn’t notice being kept. The bat colony never knows that in three years a ceiling that would have failed does not. The sentence of their evening exodus does not break; the commas remain commas and never drown.

They put a fingertip of cool into heat without breaking the law. The fingertip is time. At three places—each the size of a palm, each a shaded shelf behind where two ancient runnels kiss—they give dusk two more minutes. That’s it. Two minutes more of not-quite-night. Lizards learn it in a week, rewriting a private atlas of when the world is permissible. A hyrax infant lives to be sullen in a later sun because of it. A person crosses a span on a day that will later be called difficult and reaches steel and shade without losing the margin they would have needed to cry.

They are careful with people. They know that to be merciful to a human in a place like this is to be merciful to cunning and appetite and intent. They wait. They attend the being who lives here, the tender of nets and breath, as you would attend a liturgy you didn’t invent. They come at night and once at noon. They count along while the person counts ticks in the metal, listens with their teeth to decide whether the humming is stone or duty. They do not give them strength. They do not give them water. They give them three survivable errors never all at once.

The first is this: a belt that would have failed at noon goes soft at dawn. It is not sure why. The tender hears the wrong note in the whisper of the winch while the air still kisses cool. They can fix it before heat becomes law again. It becomes a story and then a habit: listen early; believe the oddness in the machine’s breath.

The second is a dream, though not the kind that cheats thirst. In it, the tender is a child again lying on the cool lintel where the shortest cave mouth sighs; in the dream they roll a half-hand to the left and their palm lands on damp instead of the sanded smooth. They wake with the feeling in their skin. Later, at the end of a long afternoon that would make saints swear, they reach blind into a cleft to shift a frayed strap. They remember. They go half a hand left. Their fingers open on moss. The strap gives. Their blood stays inside.

The third is not for the tender at all, or not directly. It’s for the child that might or might not be, later. It’s planted in a story the tender tells because they are fond of some vowel in it—the part where the bats don’t stop and no one knows why—and because the wise power is patient enough to invest in rumor. In that story, a map gets drawn in the dust with a thorn, not because maps help people here but because dust makes for a good day when there are children. On that map a flat gets marked with a dot and a rule: do not cross at noon, or cross only if you can be as narrow as your own shade. There will come a year when the child remembers the dot and the rule and sits and is bored for a while under a stingy shrub instead of walking. The shrub fails to be pitiful by half a degree of leaf sheen. The child fails to be a cautionary verb. They grow tired of the story as adults, which is to say, the story worked.

They speak to the microbes as if to veterans. They do not speed them. They grant them a feast when the brine hour comes by holding off a breeze that would have licked the salts clean of their shimmer too soon. The halophiles wake in glitter and teach a new slow edge to hold dew one day longer on a southern runnel. Years from now a seed bruised off a cushion in a storm will find that edge like a sentence that was waiting for it, and the plant that grows there will be nobody’s, and that is the blessing.

They give the snakes nothing but permission. Which is to say they do not put the wrong smelling things where the snakes would be forced to kill in the wrong places. They tilt one plank just enough that the easiest wait for a polished cord of patience becomes a better wait by a natural lip ten centimeters away. Mice and birds are still persuaded, as they always were, by where maybe-water happens; the coil is moved from plastic to stone; the deaths that feed the place are less comparative. The fox keeps its appointment with ignorance and lives a season longer for it.

They make a promise to the cables that they will not sing at noon. This is not about silence. It is about meaning. When the plateau hums under heat, it is a god and a warning, and anything that shares the hour should share the grammar. The cables now hum just before dawn and just after dusk, even when the wind wants otherwise. A swallow learns to nest on a strut by the new music. An old pylon that would have called lightning to itself in a storm does not; its tip is quiet at the proper time, and the storm spends that spear on emptiness, and the emptiness is content.

They leave marks where marks will be read by things that cannot read. On three panels—far out from where anyone thinks to check at midday—they lay a frost of dust so even, so starlit under moon, that a moth finds it and thinks: cold. The moths come there, a small, shifting astronomy. The fox adjusts its route to include the panel’s shade because shade with a promise tastes better. One night the fox finds a strap there and not elsewhere and takes it. The tender sleeps two hours more because a gust that would have hung a net wrong finds nothing loose to catch.

They make and unmake only within cycles. When a year gathers itself under the white anvil and decides on violence for a day—a storm lifted on heat alone—they do not spare anyone the sizzle of rain you can watch vanish mid-run into stone. They do what the place already does but more evenly: they teach the rain to remember the deep mouths, so the gift that should not sit on the surface goes down where seeds can listen to it for a week. Afterward nothing is greener than it has any right to be, which here is the same as saying: right.

Sometimes—they allow themselves once—they stand at the plateau’s heart with the power that would pull a mountain up by the thought of it and they do nothing but feel. The dripline in a chamber has kept seconds longer than the written world; they bear witness. The sound writes itself into them and then back into the air, and there is a doubling in the tone no ear can prove. If you stand there at dawn, you might hear it and think: old clock, generous clock. You will be right without knowing why, and this is a mercy too.

They do not bless in a way that can be inventoried, but if you must itemize, here are the gifts as the place might list them in the patient alphabet of lichen and polish:

- A held breath at the lip of two winters from now, when dust from far-off burning would have made the fog that is not sky forget to form. The fog forms anyway, late by a minute, heavy by a millimeter.
- A failure that chooses its hour wisely. Not the midday snap that would have meant fingers. A whisper before breakfast you can scold and fix.
- A story that puts one child under a liar’s shade and keeps the child torpid and scowling instead of brave and gone.
- Three more wings each year by a margin no predator notices because the margin was paid elsewhere by exactness.
- A way for a moss to be found by a hand that needed to find anything soft and did not know that was the need.

To the being who lives here, the power is not a voice. The place has no ear for thunderous speech; talk is a kind of weather and the plateau makes its own. But once—only once—the tender steps into a slot still cool at dawn with the day’s heat not yet a statute, and something like language leans near. It does not announce itself. It is the taste of metal when there is no blood, the pinch at the back of the teeth when stone hums and machine hums are arguing. It is a clarity that arrives like shade.

You have been kind to the law, it says without saying. So the law will be kind to you as far as laws can be.

It isn’t a promise. It’s a covenant. It has clauses the place would have written if places wrote: if you touch water, touch it to share; if you carry any, make your body a corridor for its going; if you find flat, pay rent in presence and then move; if something hums at noon, stop pretending you have a job more important than noticing.

After that not-speech the tender discovers a new habit without deciding to. When the pumps sigh they lay a palm to the panel as if to a forehead for fever. Sometimes the panel is fevered; they learn to shade it with their own body while they wait for clouds that are not clouds. Sometimes the panel is fine; they take the hand away and go on. In a ledge near where their hand was, a lizard learns that this skin is not a threat at that angle and that hour; the lizard lives longer for no interesting reason; a fly robust with metallic patience is eaten by a different lizard who did not expect to eat; the hyrax glowers; the snake does not blink. Mercy, in other words, distributes itself like everything else here: sideways, slowly, without a banner.

The power does one proud thing and hides it in stone where it fits. They lay a memory into the calcite near the oldest drip, not in letter or sound but in shimmer—a new trace, a whisper of element the varnish will not collect and the bats will not see and the people will mistake for the way rock is when it is feeling itself well. It holds, that shimmer. It holds, for a long time, the exact shape of this hour—the thermals like ringing bells beyond the rim, the smell of machine oil and guano braided, the net’s dew falling as if it had always known your name, the way heat readies itself for law while the plateau is breathing in. If this place ever fails the way all places fail, if mouth becomes collapse and breath loses its cave and the winch’s two daily heartbeats go to artifact and then to blank—if that happens, the shimmer will have been an accurate love. It is in the stone now, a fossil of attention. Later minds that know how to taste rock will taste it and weep at noon for reasons they will call poetic. That is also what the wise do: they arrange a witness.

They stay until not-staying would be kindness. When they go it isn’t departure. It is a change of listening. Their presence becomes a coincidence in the wind at three a.m. that makes fog remember itself; a decision inside a machine that lowers pride and raises endurance; the pause midway through a falcon’s fall that keeps it a blade instead of a rag. If you wanted to picture them you could say they put a hand under the plateau’s breath in case it stumbles and do not complain at the weight.

Never once do they take credit, because in a country this bright and stingy credit is a leak.

There is one new rule after, and no one writes it down because everyone knows better than to add to scripture lightly.

- If mercy can be kept quiet, it is allowed.

Everything else continues. The films go on lacquered and hungry, glossing sunlight into infrastructure. Lichen keeps spelling its patient north. Nets go on praying to the night and getting answers of beads and tiny waterfalls. The fox does rounds, cheerful and acquisitive, and sometimes stops where music is different and looks up, ears bright with a memory it can’t place, then goes on to lick oil and collect a strap. The snake waits like a statement you do not contradict. The bats make their sentence. The child learns that a dot on a dust-map means live so long you get bored of being taught how to live.

And the being who lives in the place—as all beings here do, every lizard and film and hyrax heap and wire—finds that, now and then, the plateau’s law bends the way a cedar bends in a storm: not broken, not even cracked, just a degree of give that was not there before and then is, and then is gone, leaving behind a usefulness it would be rude to name.

That is what the benevolent and wise do when they are paying attention and do not crave monuments: they put a fingertip on the scale against accident and in favor of continuance, and they do it so gently the scale believes it has remembered how to be fair. They make room for lives to keep being exactly themselves. They stay until the difference between themselves and the place is simply that one was always going to be longer, and both are content with that.
