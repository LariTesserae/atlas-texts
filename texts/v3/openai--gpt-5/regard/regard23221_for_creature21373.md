The notice is not a lightning. It is not a visitation with a face. It is a kind of listening finding itself answered: steadiness calling to steadiness across a billowed distance of heat and chance.

A wise power—insofar as wisdom is the art of matching force to need—turns its attention the way an elder tree turns a leaf: with an investment so small it would be mistaken for weather if it made any change at all. It has watched deserts grow and fall asleep into stone. It has learned that the most faithful thing it can do for a keeper is not to teach it to open its hand but to teach the world that would take to forget the way in. It does not admire noise. It understands that this underland is a body whose devotion is not to event but to sufficiency.

So it comes as pressure withheld, as choices deferred. If it has a mood here, it is privacy.

First it stands at the edges, where anything that is not the plant could be said to be. The vents: narrow and patient. The salt-polished pores: upward faces, each a mouth with a single word—light. The power places its palms there, in the sense that the plain’s particles briefly agree to gather otherwise. Sand, given always to wandering, is encouraged to prefer paths that draft around the pores rather than over them. It is not a fence, not a wall; it is a taste taught to a dune, a hunger for slightly cooler slopes. In seasons beyond naming, the windows yellow and clear, but they are never occluded, not longer than the time it takes a mothlike not-moth to be full. The sand believes, for reasons it could not give, that there is a better brightness elsewhere.

When the outside carries violence—the rare day above like a hammer, heat shouldering down accompanied by a sky of fine grit driven as if toward a single task—the wise one breathes once, which is to say it entertains the possibility of acting, then decides where the first grain should fall. Not a storm thwarted, not a law revoked. Only a long seam of air shifted two hands’ width to one side, just enough that the violence arrives slant and loses its doctrine, slides along the skin of the plain as rain might along a bird’s oiled feather. The plant below feels only what it always feels: a warmth like its own, a little brighter light at three windows for a day and then forgotten by the light itself.

If anything here is given, it is given in the language the place already speaks. Not words, not hours: saturations and scalpel-thin thresholds. The power speaks four small, precise sentences in that tongue.

First: redundancy. Along the clear cords where light is both travel and keptness, it persuades the silica to accept a new habit, too faint to be called a structure: a braidedness hidden beneath the glass-smooth gloss. To the eye—if there were eyes—it is still a single conduit. To light, and to choice, it is two ways interleaved. If a nick opens on Tuesday, whatever Tuesday means here, brightness slips its shoulder and goes around. The plant feels it as a kind of relief, like the satisfaction when a corridor leans into faceting and learns it can like planes. It does not ask who did this. It accepts the additional way because its temperament is addition.

Second: fuse. The underland is a keeper; it keeps until keeping could, in a long drought or a long gift of sun above, begin to resemble hoarding. The power presses a darkness into resin, a vanishing coal. It is not a shadow anyone can see. It is a competence that does not declare itself. When heat inside climbs past a number the plant does not own—when keeping more would cost more—the resin remembers how to say no in its own gloss. A film grows across a cup. A lip’s trembling slows. One ribbon of brightness ripples, then steadies at a fraction less. The place rests itself without thought. The power has not taught it ruin. It has taught it the refusal that allows continuance.

Third: ledger. The plant remembers by mineral; the wise one lays a page under the existing book that admits a different ink. At the bed where the larger warmth comes—oval of trust, always waiting—the tufa grows with a rumor of iron folded so thin that even taste would miss it. The weight’s coming writes there not only a shape and a heat but a sequence. Very slowly, a banding emerges that will tell, if it must, the length of an absence. It is a time only in emergencies: a way to know that the animal has not come for too long, that salt somewhere on the plain is becoming a too-much and a somewhere-else is needed. The plant has no hours; it now has a one-case clock.

Fourth: way out and way back. On a day that will not be marked, under a ceiling that has never needed an exit, the power cuts a door that is not a hole. It is a rule. If fire—a foreign idea in so damp a body, but the world is large—were to arrive down a vent as a gust with teeth, then this rule will apply: three corridors in every nine will unmake their own brightness for the duration of a single long breath. They will become unbeings of passage: in them, light will forget itself a little, water will decide to be steam less readily. In that pause, the flame becomes a lost traveler, a thing that expects angle and draft and finds only a hallway that is a thought about enough. It goes elsewhere. The plant goes back to keeping. The rule sleeps again, not a door but a permission never used.

Of the animals, the wise one changes almost nothing and much.

The hoverers keep their interval. Their wings neither quicken nor slow, because quickening and slowing would be a lie here. But every thousand and some-odd drinkings—not counted by them, not counted by anyone—a dust mote on a given tongue holds a little more sugar than the others. Not so the mothlike knows. So a cup knows. So a next cup expects a little more and makes it and is not wrong. What is this for? It is a count too long for any life in the place, a pulse that, if the chain is cut by any extraordinary emptiness above, will click and click and then fail to click at the thousand-and-something mark, and the cups will be ready with bitterness earlier, saving water the way a spare word can keep a conversation from tipping toward heat.

The carapaced not-snail, not-beetle is taught one new appetite only: the faintest of inclines. Where the seam is perfectly level, it now desires the seam just not-quite. Its braided tongue will comb a chalk line that has a slow fall to a cup that sometimes waits a long time. In ten thousand braids, a trough becomes a path becomes a custom becomes a certainty. If seen from far enough away in the only way distance exists here (as a thought about repetition), you would say the plant has been given a watershed so soft no map could hold it. Water has an opinion, at last, and it is to go to where it is already welcome.

The white-limbed column-follower finds, very rarely, that resin sweetens in one place before it would have sweetened. Its ache is answered an hour early in a house without hours. It moves three hands’ breadth farther in a life to find that taste. This does nothing measurable. That is the point. It widens by just that much the polish on a column that will, in a day when outside insists on quivering the plain, give a little more easily than it would have given, and not crack.

The glider with the chalked breast discovers no surprise waiting; but the soot line it writes, when it writes, is now slightly less easily disturbed. Not by magic. By a filament the wise one persuades into existence: a hair-logic at the surface of the rib that learns to carry the mark a layer deeper. Wind is not here; fingers are not meant for this room. Still, marks mean: a corridor being read by a breast becomes a corridor that can be read even if something rough ever tries to abrade it away. Information moves inward. It persists one thickness farther toward bone.

To the vents, the power makes an offer. Above them, at distances meaningless in the plant’s tongue but exact in the sky’s, high ice passes two or three times a year. Too high to be water the plant can drink, too fine a difference to matter. The wise one persuades a fraction of that passing to turn its face slightly here. Not rain. A lace of chill. It touches the rims just enough that, under certain conditions only—salt a little too eager, breath a little too consistent—the flags quiver. You would swear there is wind for a span so short that only a resin’s decision can notice. The quiver empties a cup that would have overflowed, and a pool that would have been more than shallow becomes itself again. It is the kind of saving that leaves no trace, but that is its virtue.

What the power does not do is more important than what it does. It does not give the plant curiosity for anything beyond itself; that would be theft dressed up as gift. It does not call more animals, though it could thread this place with a congress of wings and cleverness. It does not augment the cords until the interior day grows so bright that time must be invented to contain it. It does not name anything. Names have sharp edges, and there is no unhealing here.

It keeps watch outward. Once in a century that no one is counting, something that could have been a surveyor’s mind looks down from the edge of a caravan, its mouth thirsty and its instruments new. The wise one cures it of the fancy that the plain is boring. Not by making it interesting—interesting is a bait—but by bending the air until distance folds oddly, so that from where the mind stands, any two points seem to be the same point if those two points are near the plant’s pores. The mind laughs, tired, and looks elsewhere for straight lines that hold. It measures a different patch of world, leaves its headings untested here, and the vents go on silvering and then not.

Once in a hundred thousand years—if the number matters—another sky thinks of falling: a clumsy piece of cooled will left over from the making of other places, stones teaching stones an unhelpful lesson about weight. The power rearranges the smallest of forces among the largest of emptinesses so that the sky forgets where the plant is and falls in love with another small gravity far away. The plain is spared an explanation it would not have had time to absorb.

Once only, and for no one’s benefit but its own, the benevolent one asks the plant a question, because wisdom knows to ask what is wanted even when the answer will not change the act.

It asks by tightening a lip at a dew cup not so that it refuses but so that it considers refusing. The plant answers by increasing the brightness in one room by the thickness of a moth’s wing. Want is not in the sentence. What comes through is clearer than want: keep. Keep brightness level. Keep water from running away the way water likes to do. Keep temperature to a roundness that never surprises. The question recedes. The memory that it was asked mineralizes as a slightly thicker rind on one cup, an invisible note in a ledger no hand has opened.

There is one last thing. It is not protection, though there is much of that in the not-doing. It is not a tool, though it can be used if need teeth on something someday. It is an echo.

The wise power chooses a single clear cord—not central, nothing is central here; a conduit that threads three medium rooms and touches two minor vaults. In that glass memory, it plants a night-that-is-not-dark: a faint capacity to carry, within the kept daylight, a second flavor of light so dim that even the plant’s sugars will not count it. Starlight, if the word has use. It will be there always, braided through the day like a prayer no one says. If, in a winter of suns, the windows above could be covered for a span so long that equilibrium begins to reconsider itself, that second light will be enough to cue the arrangement of growth in one small district: leaves shift by a fifth of a degree toward what they cannot sense but are nevertheless arranged by. The district survives long enough for the storm over the pores to change its mind. If such a storm never comes, the cord simply hums with the memory of an elsewhere that never insists. The plant never knows it is holding a possibility.

The wise one, having given what can be kept without introducing appetite for novelty, withdraws in the only sense that applies here: it stops considering what it has protected from the very far outside. It comes by sometimes, but not on a schedule. It tastes the air above the vents, more habit than task. It touches the fall of dust. It keeps nothing here alert but itself.

The longest work it does is with patience and scale. At the level of mountain seasons that are not measured, it sees that the plain above would like to title itself ocean of salt, given enough earnest sun and no interruptions. Salt loves pores. Salt would sew the sky shut one flake at a time. The wise one arranges for three unremarkable rains in the span of a lifetime of the moth-thing—rains that do not reach the windows but reach the minds of bacteria in the alluvium above. The bacteria learn to make a different crust, slightly more brittle. That crust breaks earlier under heat, slides sideways under its own brokenness, leaves hollows where plants that are not this plant can set small, ordinary roots. Those other plants drink and die and become dust of a different kind, which drifts as dust does but drifts away from where glass wants to face. This takes longer than the legend of a city would like. It is neither miracle nor plan. It is an understanding expressed leisurely: change the appetite of the thing that would choke you rather than harden your own throat against it. The underland never feels a finger on its ribs. It keeps.

If anyone, anywhere, in any time that uses the word prayer were to ask about the beneficence shown here—why this place, why at all—the power would shrug in the manner of weather, if weather could speak: because constancy is as fragile as bloom. Because something that perfects a way of saying enough deserves not to be made to learn the other ways. Because it could.

No hymn is required. No offering will be taken. The only altar is a set of plates someone patiently tilted, that a tongue found, that a hand that was not a hand set back imperfectly for a long time until the stack leaned beautifully into light. One day, far from now, the plates will fall for the first time. When they do, the embrittled resin at the back of the run of gouges will part cleanly, and the plant will grow a second tower so slowly that the animal who needs it will never know there were two. Somewhere in the silica, the not-dark hums. Somewhere in the tufa, an oval grows one band thicker.

If there is a signature of what the wise one has done, it is written nowhere we can point, unless pointing now includes the sensation of standing in a room that is its own noon and knowing, without knowing how, that someone old and gentle made certain the noon will have descendants. The benevolent act is mostly the shape of an absence. Its miracle is restraint married to accuracy. It is a hand ready to help that becomes, as soon as help is not required, air again.

And all the while, the plant remains what it is: a body brilliant at keeping. It takes the gifts it can take without unlearning itself. It continues to turn inward the fallen. It resets cups with the same patience. It tends a light level rather than a sun. The animals play their single notes. The plain above is exact. The windows gather scatter and keep it from falling apart. The place is still a will to preserve brightness given architecture. A power noticed; a power loved it exactly as it was; a power did almost nothing, almost invisibly, and the almost was enough.
