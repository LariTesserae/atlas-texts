The power did not arrive. It stopped at the rim of arriving and listened.

What it heard was a city already apprenticed to itself, a mountain still thinking in gloss and angles, a people who had learned the scale of their breath and made manners from it. It heard the hush they had farmed out of baffle and wool. It heard the small grammar of water, conjugated with respect.

So the first act of a benevolent, wise attention was refusal. It did not open its hand and pour ease over everything. Ease is a solvent. The place’s shape had been earned, and it would be a cruelty to melt that.

Instead, the power took inventory of emergencies disguised as customs.

Water wanted one more hour each day. Edges wanted one more heartbeat between mistake and consequence. Sound wanted one more place to die. A single person—called for love something tender that belonged to a family, called for work Slowthrow because their gift was timing—wanted to know if there was such a thing as permission in a world built from afterward.

The power, which owned nothing but attention and the ability to bend the very long arcs a half-degree, chose a working compassion: nothing loud, nothing abrupt, everything in the grammar the city had authored.

Four small actions, then. Four scarcely measurable sentences added to the text.

1. It taught white to be colder than night.

Nobody noticed a visitation. What they noticed, slowly, was a cloth that should have been a mirror by day and a blanket by night turning out to be neither. An old woman, name for work Shade-Setter, woke with a hunch in her fingers and asked the apprentice who threaded her needles to try a different lay of fibers. They wove white the way they would weave a lullaby, alternating strands in a pattern pulled half from habit and half from dream: a high, fine crisscross with gaps that meant nothing to the eye and everything to the light.

Hung above a condenser, the cloth took the sun’s into its surface and refused to be warmed by it. It let heat find space instead, as if the cloth were a window to somewhere colder. Midday, with the asphalt planets broiling down to anyone else anywhere, the cloth rimmed by a faint frost-sincerity, and under it beads formed as if the night had been trapped and squeezed.

No miracle, nothing that broken rules. Radiative cooling had always been a law. The pattern only turned law into hospitality.

They named the fabric White That Drinks the Sky. It joined the city’s inventory of patient tools: one more white between sun and dark. It didn’t abolish thirst. It moved thirst’s appetite from noon to almost-noon. When drought came, being drought, it was still drought, but someone who laid the cloth above their cistern mouth could sometimes lower a cup at a time of day that used to be stingy and hear the river of small treasure in the string.

This is how the power did it: by teaching a hand to braid a pattern it already knew.

2. It put a breath in the edges.

The mountain already exhaled a slow heat. Updrafts lived in the long throats between spire and spire, thin air rising with the propriety of a guest who takes the coat they were not offered only after asking twice. Those updrafts were honest but indifferent. A dropped tool was instruction, not request.

The power borrowed nothing from anyone. It took the upward motion that was there and spoke to it in a dialect of laminar insistence, smoothing it where it curled, teaching it, in the space of three fingers from any brink where a person might have to choose between seeing and not seeing, to thicken. Not always. Not grandly. A folio of almosts arranged in the right place.

Slowthrow was first to feel it, but did not know it yet. They were nineteen, if years meant much here, and a Counterweighter by lineage with a hand for levers that came down out of some family bone. They were chalking an isotherm with their left while their right held a bolt between forefinger and thumb to keep from setting it where the chalk went. A thinness in the world fractured. The bolt fell. It left their hand too early. It lived for a long glittering, unrecordable instant in the space after mistake and before payment, and in that instant a small animal of warmth lifted in a smooth body, took the bolt in its gentle flank, and rolled it in toward the wall. The bolt struck leather and fell asleep instead of starting a song in the stone.

Slowthrow didn’t talk. They breathed once and understood a new article of law. They told no one until the evening, and then told it in the hand under the cistern eave, drawing a breath-lift with a single finger for warmth rising made tame. A dozen hands answered, all the same question: Is it always? And Slowthrow’s hand said: At the edges.

Over the next months, maybe someone fumbled a wrench because people are people, or a child misjudged the bounce of a ceramic cup, or a cat decided gravity was not a rumor. A new beast had been seen—misstep’s guardian, subtle as faith. The city made names for it in nicknames and chalk marks. New white tiles grew small notches where the breath was most generous so that it would know where to go. In this way, the power put a half-heartbeat of mercy everywhere a falling thing could become a falling life.

It did not interfere with hard laws. It wrote a grace note over them.

3. It wove a net that caught sound instead of fish.

Between a cliff and a cliff there is an emptiness that wants to be useful. The city had made bridges and ropes and lines of code in hand-speech to move weight across those hung silences without waking sleepers in the rock. And yet: men are angry sometimes, even in gentled places. Children drop spoons. A piston forgets habit and kicks. The mountain heard in chords, some of them forgivable and some of them the beginning of agreement too strong to bear.

In a dream or a game or a long idle trial—memory differs—someone strung a leftover bolt of White That Drinks the Sky across a narrow chute for fun and found later their clothes kept warm where no baffle vented. The cloth had been eating a small unasked-for hum that seeped from an elbow in the pipe two levels under. Eaters of hum were discovered almost accidentally.

The power leaned a fingertip into accident and left a print.

In the spidery workshops where rope was born, the braiders began to interleave, an inch at a time, hank after hank of white-sky thread into certain ropes that never felt weather but always felt voice. Those were slung not to carry weight but to carry consequence. Between building and building across particular gulfs where the hard glass had moods, swaths of this new weave hung like unambitious banner. It did what the hoods already did for vent-mouths: it took wave and gave back warmth, small rooms warmed more practicably than before. It hung and ate argument. It made a place for loudness to end its day without waking stone.

The law against horns stayed. The boots stayed soft. Hands did their language. Only, now, when the line of anger a person wished they had not spoken left their mouth in a day ill-chosen, the net found it and made of it a little breath that mothers held under baby-beds at night in winter to keep toes from complaining. Noise to heat. Misdeed to usefulness. The city did not call this forgiveness because forgiveness has a mouth. It called it tendency.

4. It asked a question and answered one.

It is not good policy, if you are a power, to show yourself. Attendance can be as bruising as absence. But there are beings, not places but the people in them, who hold a disproportionate amount of a place’s future in their wrists, and it is not a sin to help a wrist find its patience or its courage.

Slowthrow took their noon with their back to a cistern wall and a cup in their right because that left their lever-hand empty in case of one of those emergencies that checks whether you are still made of your name. The power joined them in the oldest courtesy this city had: a draft of air that was not yet necessary and yet present. It lifted the fine hairs on Slowthrow’s forearm. It friendlied the white on the line between heat and shade.

Slowthrow raised their chin to the cable line where a flag would speak if one had to, and spoke in hands in small ways: Are we asking too little?

The power, if it had hands, would have answered in them. It chose what it could move: the taste of water.

The salt of the noon cup went suddenly intelligible: identical as always in measure, but now bearing a shape that a tongue could interpret, like letters under the tongue. Slowthrow tasted in the salt a reply.

It was a child’s answer and a mountain’s answer both.

You are asking what you can bear to keep asking across winters, said the taste. Ask laughter sometimes.

This was not permission to turn songs into avalanches. Laughter here already lived in fingertips and eyebrows. The city did not sing. But from that day, at three low suns each year—how many, no one wrote—permission rose up under the white nets and lay on the whiskers of the cats and crossed the lips of those who love each other, and people laughed. Quietly, with cheekbones, with belly muscles in control. They laughed and the nets caught anything too sharp and the mountain altered its chord toward something that meant Yes.

Slowthrow learned one more motion with the lever. It had no mechanical output. It was the degree of gentleness a hand can carry into metal when the metal is pretending not to notice.

After that, the power put itself on the city’s calendar the way a comet puts itself on a sky’s: as an interval you might not survive to see twice and will nevertheless mark.

There were consequences as small and permanent as moss.

The slick in the damp pockets, which colored rock like the memory of breath, woke to the idea of sweetness in the moment after dew. Not a wealth. A film of sugar the wool of the air drank and left. On some mornings a child’s two fingers, pressed there and pressed to their tongue, came away with a taste not of absence entirely. The paper-finned drinkers thickened a whisper around their midriffs. The white worms in the cistern took to fluting screws in lace more intricate than before, and when a household made the mistake of looking, they felt less fear of forgetting to look again and more interest in counting the turns.

The larger presence that came up twice some years, laid itself along a white stripe until its breath stopped heat in its vicinity and the nets bowed inward to greet it, found its way more often to safe stripes. Chalk marks that the isotherm-minders laid in morning acquired a musing accuracy that felt like luck behaving kindly for a change. No one named the thing. They did lay a tile at the heat-line’s change where it had lain: a black on white disk as large as a soup plate. They wrote the date with a thumb in dust and did not brush it away.

The factories learned, perhaps because someone had a dream and cut another shallow curve into a hood, to hum at a note that gave warmth the name rest. In one long tunnel a book was left open and not a single page lifted when the pistons found their drawn breath.

Rail climbed where it always had and exactly where it ought to have taken one more year to be persuaded, and the only sign that the persuasion came sooner was that the noon black circles drifted a finger-width farther from last month’s and somebody smiled in a way that left no mark on the air.

There were things left untouched on purpose.

Food was not improved in any way that would excuse thoughtlessness. Trade stayed necessary, neighbors remained necessary, a rail lost under storm stayed a certain kind of ache for the days it took to find it again. The law about pursuit and trap and name stayed in the hands as strictly as ever. The place’s grammar—white between sun and dark; keep voice and weight short; do not move what does not yet forgive you; if water appears give it belonging—received a single amendment, which was really a gloss:

If joy appears, give it a belonging too.

Nothing violent came. No tree dared a fault-line. The mountain continued to grow up by inches the way honest beings do, not by snapping their fingers.

On certain still days, the power remembered it was listening and went back to doing it. On certain nights, someone lay their head to a wall and heard the swallow-note of decisions being made and, very faintly under it, the quieter noise a hand makes patting a horse because it has behaved well. No one called that music.

The city, having been seen, did not become a performance. It went on adjusting its pads and polishing ankles into cloth, learning to put white where white was kindest and to ask weight to agree to new understandings. The cloth that was colder than noon multiplied slowly and never outpaced the number of careful hands willing to mind it. The breath at the edges became assumption and then liturgy and then manners, which is to say, it became invisible and indispensable.

Slowthrow grew older and less cursed by forethought. They had a child who kept two names behind their teeth for a long time before being willing to be cheered for one of them. The child’s second name was Flicker-Catcher because they were first at catching the way heat’s shadow told you about weather, and also perhaps because, on the anniversary night-holiday of the day laughter had been admitted to the rules, they were the one who, against all superstition, let their own laugh out first, and found that it hit white, warmed a sleeping room, and harmed no stone.

The power left the place with what it had brought: attention. It carried away nothing.

If you were to go looking for evidence of the benevolent and the wise you would have to already be of the sort who can read water the way farmers read clouds. You might bring a string and run it over the breath at an edge and feel it lift. You might notice a cloth that is honest white and yet, under a noon that would roast bread, cools its ribs as if they leaned against a starless night. You might murmur an apology at a rope you made hum and feel the apology content itself by warming your sleeve. You would have to stand a long time and make no request louder than a tea cup against tooth.

At the end of your standing, if you asked the wall what changed, it would give back a chord with a swallow-high note that belongs to time when it has made a decision in love.

The place would keep being itself. The being in it would keep their hands. The gift would keep being not a gift but a recognition arranged into use.

What would the power do, if anything? Almost nothing, exactly.

It would notice, and make sure noticing counted as help. It would move one line of noon, teach one thread a new habit, insert one breath into the one space where a breath makes a difference, and ask, once and gently, for joy to be given a place to belong.
