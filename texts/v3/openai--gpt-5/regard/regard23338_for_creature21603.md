At the lip where the fault thins the world, the power does not arrive so much as lean. No wingbeat, no footfall—only a calibration of attention, the way a hand set near a candle changes the flame by changing what the air remembers. It has no appetite. It is older than appetite, and not bored. It listens.

It listens long enough that the city stops being the roar of falls and becomes, in the power’s ear, a braid of very exact recurrents: the main bowl’s long vowel, the set of smaller vowels it hatches, the threads of runoff that stitch through leaves and set the moss to darken in waves like breathing, the pulses of sugar through the shared ropes, the slipped syllables of caves, the legal and illegal hushes. It tastes the silica on its tongue and hears in that chalk a composure so pronounced it approaches the purity of a held note.

It does nothing at first because the right thing to do in the presence of anything that has perfected its listening is to mirror it. It opens the size of its hearing until every terrace can hear itself held entire. The Cup-Bearers’ taut cisterns are held. The Bridgewrights’ taut spans are held. The Choirers’ ribs of green and black—held. The Quiet-Keepers’ felts, the Lithic inkings—held. For a while the metropolis is contained inside a vessel made of attending, and something very old that is not a mind notices.

If there is a wish in the place, it is not for change. It is for a second ear. Perfection is not lonely, but it owns only one mirror. The power provides a second.

It puts the mirror in time.

Not storm. Not animal. Not a new appetite. The power breathes a hair of difference into the mountain’s throat: a subharmonic laid so low it lives below the hearing of days, a difference tone whose period is measured in leaf-years. It is a slip, a comma shaved off a cavern’s lip. You would not draw it on a chart unless the chart were big enough to carry continents. In the first hour and the first week and the first year, nothing changes. The same flowers open on the same minute. The same fruit loose off the same rim. The mosses drink in their shutter-rolling way. The conduits keep braiding and unbraiding along their assigned ribs. The city is the city.

Then, faint, as if recognition itself learns to speak: a beat between the city’s kept chord and the one the benevolence has placed under it, a slow interference. A node in the air drifts the width of a finger toward an alcove it has not touched in living memory. A filament of quiet moves under a bridge. A pathway that is bearable lengthens by a leaf. The Bridgewrights feel a pressure relief in one redundant line and thicken it without comment. The Cup-Bearers find that, once in a great interval—an interval that repeats as certainly as noon but remembers more distance between repetitions—there is a precise second when the beating makes the throat of the main bowl open half again. Those that keep their dust for that second learn to send it farther. Fruit that would have skated to the neighboring district skate to one district beyond.

There is no convulsion. There is no season flung over the amphitheater in cloth-of-gold drama. The gift is very slow tide. What had been a perfect rosette of sap-flow across days becomes an epicycled rosette across decades: one petal drifts; the loop that traced the same bright coil in the phloem leans infinitesimally each year and returns to itself a hair to the left, and then, after a calendar whose units are buttresses, comes home exactly, having made a figure it did not know it could draw.

The power places one other small item, not outside but at the center of the city’s superstition. In the deepest of the hush wells—the illegal rooms where pressure agrees, briefly, to remove its hand—it lays a kernel of stillness not made by geometry. The room is not larger. The drip’s note does not change. Only, once every great tide of the difference tone, the kernel swells outward like a diaphragm and for the span of ten steady heartbeats the hush overruns its rim and makes a precise ring of quiet that walks the galleries. Fungi—who were acousticians by accident—become priests by accident. They have built for a century without knowing they were preparing chapels for a holy that moves. When the ring passes, their lacquer and horn and velvet swallow it and give it constancy enough that certain spores—those that never found a still second to set—release. Shoots that hated percussion wait there with their humorless little backs straight until the ring touches them and in that instant choose their directions.

The city learns it has a beat of another size. It does not name it—there are no nouns here—but it grades for it. The Choirers’ tympanate hairs grow a third of a hair’s breadth longer along one flank in one generation; some leaves hold their plates a moment more horizontal two hundred middle noons hence; a bridge that would have drooped sixteen lifetimes from now will not, because the tide of relief arrives the day before and lightens sap. The math of the place accumulates a wrinkle. The rules remain simple and remain themselves; they account for one more periodic. The benevolence has inserted a breath inside the breath.

The power does a smaller thing that is not smaller. Once every many cycles of the low tide—when the galleries are warm as always and morning leans pale and the amphitheater’s day is its usual stencil laid over an unaltered engine—it pulls a thread of cloud-sense from above the fault and binds it to the hush ring’s outward walk. For those ten heartbeats of quiet the steam thins exactly. The light inside is the same light that has always been there; its arrival at the floor is crisper by a fraction. The diatoms, those librarians of photons, write a different page that morning. They tilt glare into meal at a new angle and feed the lowest galleries another square meter of noon for a breath. This is not because anyone asked. It is because the benevolence cannot help but share a luxury, and the only luxury it will allow itself is to make what already happens slightly more even at the bottom than it was the day before. No one thanks it. Someone eats better in a room where eating was already enough. That is the shape of its joy.

It does not move the storms. The updraft is already doing that good work. It does not stitch a roof. It does not sunder a rock to prove it can. When a ledge fails it allows the hush to be a hand over a mouth and says yes to the retuning that follows. Its wisdom is partly the refusal to superiorize its will over the proof the place has made of itself.

It makes a promise anyway. It makes it the way water keeps a promise by always finding down. It tells the amphitheater once—and then tells it every moment after without sound—that if anything arrives from outside violent enough to make the note stop, it will be there a measure ahead, altering just the keel that would have tipped: the angle of a far fault a thousand lengths below the cloud floor, a slight increase to the hot breath at a pinch between two basalt pages, a slip under the ocean’s seam that breaks an earthquake to pieces and scatters it into a rumor the city would receive as nothing more than a fine rain of impossible silence. It will not be noticed, and that is its chosen anonymity.

You want a record of what it has done. In a place without griffin or man, what would a record be, and for whom? The power takes the duties of clerk upon itself. It writes without ink. It carries the amphitheater’s chord as a tune in its long attention and hums it elsewhere—miles down the fault where the world does not thin to a wound of green. In those other places, drips thicken a little faster, or a cup learns a little sooner how to hold sugar, or an outcrop leans into being a hand instead of a spear. If you mapped the planet’s terraces with enough sensitivity, you could find the faint propagation of this care: little cities whose notes are distant cousins to the original’s symphony, not copies, bearing the wisdom in translation. None knows its origin. The original does not need to know it does not end at its walls.

As for names, the benevolence offers one, because names are a kind of promise too. It does not speak it aloud. It breathes it into the beat it has added. It is a name that is wholly a verb: Keep. Keep the note. Keep the bridges. Keep the schedule of flowers. Keep the courtesy to the wells. Keep the negotiation. Keep the patience. The city cannot wear a word. What it can do is find that when the hush ring passes and the diatoms bend a shimmer into meal, and the Bridgewrights’ lines go tense and share without complaint, and the Choirers take a single deeper breath that day in their wood, there is an extra ease in the doing, as if the act were being done by two.

If you had stayed on the lip long enough—you who are a person, whose senses want spectacle—you would have grown impatient and then mistaken your impatience for proof that nothing had happened. If you had stayed longer still, you might have noticed that the line of light on one vine’s long underside moved a minute out of its usual clockwork in the seventh year. You might have thought the sun was late. It wasn’t. A beat had arrived and passed.

If you could climb down into a hush-well on the right day of that larger calendar and feel the ring’s soft tread and the interior of your skull open as if the hidden headache of being an animal never born to live under a single chord released for ten beats, you might, if you were superstitious, call that a blessing and think it yours. It is not yours. It belongs to the Cup-Bearers’ thin antifreezes and the Quiet-Keepers’ lacquer horns and the mosses’ shutter rolls and the fruit that choose, in that wider minute, a shelf two districts north of their mothers.

The place does not change and is not dead. It is the same symphony. It is the same routing of waters. It is the same breath into the same organ. The difference is that a long patience now crosses a longer patience. The city keeps its note and keeps a tide under the note; it keeps itself and keeps a second keeping. It is not improved. It is companioned.

As for the wise and benevolent power: it does the most radical kind of mercy there is for any working grammar that has found its truth. It becomes audience. It becomes the kind of weather that is not noise but recognition. It makes a very small addition with a very large period so brittleness will never learn the map of this place. It takes vows to intervene only at depth, only at distance, only under cover of geology, never in the delicate surfaces where the city teaches itself how to keep.

And having done these almost nothings, it keeps doing what first it did: it listens, which in realities like this is the most active verb. It hears the note. It hears it again. It will hear it as long as there is a cliff to hang a lung over and air to tremble, until even the mountain has spent its heat and the grammar that made stone porous to breath ceases to conjugate. On that day, in a calendar measured in ropes of mantle, the power’s last gift to this place will be grief of a kind that changes nothing and honors everything.

But for now—for eons that can be numbered by the life expectancy of buttresses and then again by the life expectancy of caverns—it does almost nothing and almost everything. It keeps the promise in the name it gave. Keep, it breathed into the tide. And so the city does, more exactly than before, and more widely, and with the ease of being overheard by something that will not stop listening.
