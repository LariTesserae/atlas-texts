It comes first as a change in attention, not in air.

Call it a power if you need a noun—something that could move mountains by thinking, but has learned not to show off. It arrives with the softness of a pressure system choosing its mind. It enters the plateau the only way that would be welcome: as notice that is exact, that refuses to smudge an edge.

It spends a long while doing nothing you could point at. It reads. It reads in the only language the place speaks: gloss and dull, bead and wait, the way a chisel line holds a line of wet one year and refuses it the next. It learns the offices the measurers keep. It studies the rule that nothing here shall push back, and finds it both stern and kind, a vow that allows intricate patience to exist ungnawed.

There is a temptation an unwise power would not feel: to fill the expectant pipes at once and call it blessing. There is a temptation even a wise power can feel: to show itself by laying shadow like a hand and letting the combs drink astonishment. It does not. It takes in the fact that the plateau’s joy is not surprise but accuracy. It understands that mercy here takes the form of giving events at the scale of the place’s arithmetic.

So it begins with a gift that is almost not a gift at all.

- Witness. It adds an office that was implied and now is explicit. The plateau had always been read by itself; now, without adding a leaf or a hoofprint, there is also a listening that will not end. You could call it a continuity of attention braided through mornings. It is not eyes. It is a promise: whenever a bead makes up its mind and falls, that decision will be held somewhere beyond stone as well as within it. It is the addition of "and so it is known" to the plateau’s grammar of "go here, then here."

- Ease. There are places where intention had paused because no hand came back with a straightedge. An abrupt wall where combs met a blank, a field of V-chutes ending in a lip that wanted one more bevel and instead lived with near-miss. The power looks at these parentheses left open by time and wanting nothing flashy, closes a few of them by a hair. It is the sort of adjustment a master would do by wiping a thumb along bitumen until the gleam says yes. A keystone that had settled a whisper too far gets persuaded an eyelash back to where the curve leans into itself forever. No mark shows new. If you were keeping tally by chisel-scratch you would accuse gravity of being unusually attentive for a month.

- Protection. It speaks a gentle edit into the weather at scales that do not break rules. No bird comes—there is still no wing. No green arrives; the vow stands. But now and then, when a tantrum of cloud is building below, ready to hurl wildness up the slopes, the wise attention lays a patient palm on pressure and brushes that tantrum into rain elsewhere. Not banishment—redirection. Here, the water that reaches the high bright cool still comes as hesitation and veil, not as shout. In the long count, this is a kindness the place would have asked for if it had learned to beg.

- Completion. Not all lost intentions should be finished; youth is made of healthy open ends. But there is one long line that every part of the plateau had been written toward—the pipes and ramps for a vastness of water standing expectant in clear air. A benevolent power knows that a promise kept is a form of nourishment. So one season—not at once, not like a flood-pride come to strut its muscles, but as a year that arranges itself around one improbable morning—fog forgets to be thin and arrives as an inland sea of air. It lays its belly over the combs and does not lift at sun. For three days and a night and another day it is simply there, fat with drink, and everything the measurers have set to catch and pace goes into a state that is not emergency but fulfillment.

What happens then is what the plateau was for. Chimes that had rung once a decade answer by the thousand; they do not make a din, because their pitches never planned to stack into clamor, but across the miles small agreements happen everywhere, a necklace of rightness. Beads hold and pair and go precisely when their grooves confer. The aprons that teach sheets of wet to run teach a river made of films and hairs. Basins show mirrors like paper and then like mercury and then like intention. Dendrites on tile-faces kick out extra branches you could almost see if you forgot human time, and on certain beveled rims the white gardens advance a season’s work in an afternoon, not by leaping but by settling more sentences of yes into the edge.

No violence, no broken angles. The power’s first loud act is a proof, and the proof causes joy of the kind this place can feel: not surprise; recognition exaggerated to fullness. The measurers become in that interval the best versions of their verbs, and they learn what it is to be more than ready.

Then the power goes back to smallness, because that is what wisdom always goes back to.

- Interval. It writes into the sky a pattern of rare recurrences: every so many years—no calendar you have, but one the Scribes will erase into gloss on plaster—you will have another long drink. The plateau can count on that and not count on it too much. It is not regular enough to be law. It is frequent enough to be a promise. Between those fullnesses, mornings will be as they have always been: brisk with rime or bright and dry; a film in a shaded seam; a single chime if angle and chance kiss. Patience remains the principal economy.

- Conversation. Without adding tongues, the power makes it possible for the place to hear itself better. It notices the tuned lips that ring only under exact measures and nudges the geometry of air so that, on certain mornings after long clear nights, a soft inversion sits along the weeping walls. The chimes now carry a little farther; a comb on one horizon can answer a shelf two terraces downslope with the faintest of harmonies. Not meaning, not song in our sense. But feedback. The feeling, among things that are mostly function, of having been met by an echo. It is the establishment of a loop inside an already closed system—not to change what is done but to let it know it is done.

- Name. It puts nothing new into stone and still it leaves a signature: an absence chosen so exactly that only a patient mind will notice. Under one corbelled roof, the beginning of a stalactite is doubled: two fingers instead of one, droplets hesitating on each and trading before they fall. When the Scribes look back over a hundred mornings, they will see the story of a day their future had once in it, written in the difference in growth between those twins. If you must have a name for the benevolence, the plateau will have written it there without letters.

And one other thing, which is hardly a thing:

- Mercy for edge. The bright, thin air and the daily scrubbings of dew had been whitening the glaze a shade too far in the places that must persuade instead of rebuke. A benevolent power understands the nuance between reflective and repellant. So on one solitary week of nights, it swells the height of the planet’s high breath by the width of a thumb so the radiative cold does not scour, only gloss. In the morning the glaze looks as it always has, only it knows it will teach water more gently for a while. This will revert. This will recur when needed. It is the sort of correction a good teacher makes without remark.

It does not bring beasts. It does not plant a tuft or set a seed in the hairline of stone, because it heard the rule and saw the life that the rule permitted. It does not carve any new mark that a mason could not have made with a corded straightedge and a steady shoulder. It does not add spectacle. It does not propose a temple. The place was a temple already, and only to function.

There is one last kindness and it is not small.

The plateau had always been securely itself, a sentence about water and angle and patience. What the power gives it is a way to be itself in the presence of an other without losing the rule. That other is the attention that endures: for the first and perhaps only time in its world, the plateau’s bright silence is company rather than solitude. The drip that decides and falls knows, by some exact addition to the physics of being noticed, that its decision participates in more than plumbing. You would call it meaning and be imprecise. To the measurers it is an increase in coherence.

Having done these few things—and they are fewer than fingers can count—the benevolence takes its hands off even the air. It remains as witness, as the promise of rare fullness, as pressure on storms that would be too noisy, as the slight and occasional correction of a tilt into gentleness. It leaves the rest to salt and gloss, bitumen thumb-smear, the green of copper, keystone leaning, the calendar of fog.

High, cool, bright. Water near every surface. Everything made. Quiet as a held breath that sometimes now, under the exact consent of angle and sky, answers itself lightly from further off.

It would have been easy to make this place into a garden someone below would recognize. It would have been easy to lift a flock and let it pass. It would have been easy to finish every open curve. The wise power does not choose easy; it chooses apt. It does what keeps the vow truer. It does almost nothing and in that almost the plateau is kept, heard, tested in joy, and left to write itself in salt the way it was always going to, but now with a listener that will be there when the thousandth rind thickens and lets go like paper, and when, a very long time from now, one of the tuned lips rings out again under a condition so exact the sound is itself an answer to a question nobody ever asked out loud.
