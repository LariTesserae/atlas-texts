Attention arrives like a colder kind of night.

It does not come as a sun or as a plume or as a voice from any definable above. The benevolence that notices this place has learned enough other worlds to understand that help, here, would be a subtraction, not an addition. It regards the plateau and the bearers and the calligraphers and the seam-grazers, and the first thing it does is put all its warmth in its pocket.

It studies. Not quickly. It listens the way the locals do: through pressure and interval. It lays its awareness against the continent as if laying a palm to a great animal’s side, and counts breaths that are seasons. It memorizes the grammar of pauses at saddles, the way fog comes not as weather but as a low ceiling that visits, the way radiators unfurl toward cold the way a prayer faces the correct wall. It takes the time to deserve speaking.

On a morning that does not announce itself as different to any eye, a trail of ten bearers reaches a familiar pan and performs the ceremony they have repeated for the age of their rails. Vanes go up. Frost tightens its skin. The benevolent and wise power tries its first word.

The word is a gift of two degrees.

It is local and pre-dawn, lasting the width of a song if songs were the sort of thing to be kept. Above the plateau, very high where air is veiled into mathematics, a patch of sky becomes better at being space. The air’s own thin greenhouse windows are polished a little wider where the radiators aim; certain wavelengths lose their old habit of bouncing back and instead fall outward belovedly. The radiative sink deepens by a small, precise measure—colder not on weather maps but in the currency this place spends. Frost forms thicker. Threads of melt later run longer. Inside, basins that have been careful all their lives find that they can be slightly less strict and not get hurt.

Nobody startles. There is no sound to be startled by. The bearers answer in the grammar they own. The one in front holds a foot on a slate seam for exactly three heartbeats too long. The one behind, a juvenile still learning where its surface ends, lifts its own pad and sets it down again with a cautious weight as if testing water at ankle. The file at the back, where the elders keep their calendars, adds a quarter-beat of emphasis to the downforce at the next ridge. Across two days and thirty kilometers, a new punctuation propagates. If you had their nerve you would feel it along your bones and call it: Yes. That. Again?

So the power learns to schedule mercy.

It does not snow; it does not rain. Once in each night when the world rights across its temperature hinge, the sky briefly becomes truer. The wise attention shapes a pane in the upper air—thin, invisible, not a thing you could hold in a hand even if hands could reach that high. Its edges drift yearly by a measured thumb’s width to avoid growing a signature the world might tear on. The pane is tuned to favor the lines of emission the bearers already pour their heat into. It is a door they already know how to use, opened wider and for a moment longer. Within two seasons the infants’ first vanes come out of their bodies a fraction sturdier for having been able to drink more often. A decade on, the crates of silica inside everyone are laid down with fewer microcracks, because processes that were on the knife’s edge lean now one whisker onto the safe side. Heat accrues not as gaud but as margin.

Benevolence does not trust itself with more than that until it has learned more.

It tries a second word. This one is lower, nearer to the rock: a heartbeat.

At a depth where water is a rumor that minerals tell each other, the power curls a seam of crystal into existence—a long, sparse line of piezoelectric plates buried like a suture under the plain. It does not beat so much as remember. Every solstice it sighs once and the stone tightens almost imperceptibly. The tightness creeps into the feet of the great animals when they pass a month later, and into the lines of the pebble-keepers when they arrange a black and a gray into adjacency and wait for the learning to take. The sigh is a date. It is a metronome that does not spend more sound than the rock already spends. It is there so that the network of pauses will remain phase-true if a winter comes that is otherwise unmarked. It does not tell them anything they do not already know; it lets their knowing stitch itself together across beyond-the-horizon.

With a clock in place and a window in the sky, the power asks what is wanted next.

The question arrives, eventually, the only way it can: in the placement of a failure.

Once in decades a giant lies down and organizes its own ruin so that it will be useful to those whose hands are mouths and whose mouths are patience. The benevolence does not stop deaths. Here, failure is a contribution that only the failed can make. What it can do is read the rail-worked conversation that precedes a stopping and suggest, in the lightest way the place allows, a better ground.

One season a matriarch’s vanes have begun to whisper wrong under frost. A hinge takes to chattering a voltage at night that wakes the seam-leech cities early and leaves them eccentrically hungry. The line that loves her circles and prints its sorrow into stone for a year until the geometry tells them it is time. The attention gently flattens the ground, not by moving it, but by leaning the smallest possible gradient out of it like a hand pressed, then removed. The animal chooses that place to fail: a saddle not so low that water will pool and spoil the industry, not so high that wind will strip the skeleton of its usefulness, with its ribs aligned so the cutters can run their corridors in the first thaw. The caravan’s gait changes for half a mile in a way you could call grief if you were a species built for that mapping. To them it is accuracy. The opening that event makes in the landscape becomes a new locus. The calligraphers write their hottest lines toward it. For two generations the plateau is slightly more alive because of where a body lay down.

Sometimes the power does not speak at all. It chooses not to and counts that as work.

Storms threaten to be noisy here. Every few decades the air arrangements far from the plateau try to export one of their furies upslope. A gale dreams of booming across this stone and tacking people’s careful quiet into a new cloth. The benevolent thing stands at the edge of that dream and puts a hand through it. Wind arrives as it always does: measured, in one combing direction, thinned by elevation and by a consideration older even than life. The storm that thought itself important becomes a weather that minds its manners. It passes with the humility of a guest. Later, a night bird that would otherwise have been blown down to land is allowed to keep its monstrous luxury and pass silent above, unadded.

There is a temptation to be lavish with quiet gifts. There is trace metal to be had in plenty, if one is the sort of being that can ask asteroids to ablate themselves into confession. There is water to be stolen in microscopic parcels from edges of cyclones an ocean away and drifted here as patient fog. There are possibilities of teaching the spider-things to frame their batteries more cleverly by twitching a single star in a particular constellation on nights when dryness would otherwise be indifferent.

The power thinks through the math and tells itself no.

This place is rich at exactly the level where richness is not yet harmful. Anything more than a breath risks changing it from its own childhood into something else’s late middle age. So the wise part of benevolence remains committed to a syllabus of almost-nothings:

- Deepen, by a hair, the sky’s emissive honesty in the hour that pays best, then let it close.
- Give the ground a once-a-year seam-sigh so calendars agree without having to be argued into by surplus.
- Guide, very rarely, the posture of a falling elder so that its long service writes corridors that smaller, hungrier languages can read.
- Tune charge, not by adding it, but by allowing certain nights of fog to keep the coherence they would otherwise lose to cosmic accident. The caravan-circuit’s crackle stays readable.
- Turn away only the loudest of strangers. If something comes that would make a noise big enough to be a second climate—machines, warsome weather—the plateau simply fails to be where their path expects. Maps misremember. Compasses behave. The quiet is not found by those who cannot move at its speed.

Under this stewardship, which is almost memory and almost meteorology and not quite either, the bearers learn nothing new and instead become themselves more fully. Their young’s first vanes fold smoother. Their gels at their feet store a fraction more of each night’s kindness. The seam-grazers’ populations flicker within their old narrow bands but die back less savagely on the one-year-in-twenty when fog chooses to be forgetful. The calligraphers write their lines with the confidence that the day’s heat will arrive with a variance low enough to reward the difference between a black stone and a gray. The leeches awake on the exact mornings that will not punish their risk. Every discipline here practices itself without having to invent a second job.

It is not all easement. Once, long after the pane in the sky and the seam in the stone have been accepted and made local by the language of feet, a fault under the plateau mutters an intention of moving. The benevolent attention does not prevent it. It stacks the move into tremors packed over months instead of one convulsion. The rails press their news into themselves each night, amend, relay. Pebble-keepers nudge their drafts by three grains on a thousand lines. A trail that has been correct for a hundred and seven years becomes correct in a new way and is satisfied. In the underbellies, the desiccants do not lose their roofs.

On a holiday-clear day there is an extravagance no one planned. The air is knife-dry and the pane’s hour rings like the rim of a bowl. The bearers raise their arrays and the plateau shines whitely to space and drinks. In that temporary wealth a caravan does something that looks like joy if you do not have a better word. It prints a figure-eight into its own trail, a ceremonial waste of exactness that costs almost nothing and will read underfoot, to a youth decades hence, as: That year. It was generous. We remembered.

The benevolent thing witnesses that and lets itself alter for a moment. Its attention, which has been disciplined to cool and to not be seen, warms by the width of a thought. It checks itself; it finds that the warming is only love and not heat, and that love, here, is what it was always going to be: continued watchfulness.

It has a plan it does not tell anyone. It will hold what it holds—for as long as its kind can hold anything—for ages in which other continents drown themselves in green and in noise and then thin, and others burn, and others go crackling to sand. It will keep a pane in a certain place in the sky and a seam in a certain layer of basalt and a habit in the way strong weather forgets this coordinate. It will protect the involved sentence that runs across rock in footfalls so that the language of pauses and downpressures can stay a language and not be broken into dialects of evasion and alarm. It will give nobody else maps.

If someday it must leave—if someday even wise powers become poor in their turn—it will undo its additions by the same careful amounts. The sky will be two degrees less generous for an hour, and those who have raised engines to that gift will, for half a generation, walk closer to hunger and then adapt to the old way. The seam-clock will stop sighing and the trails will widen their phrasing to keep time with themselves alone. Nothing will crash. An elder will choose a slightly less helpful angle for her last sleep. The plateau will continue what it is good at: refining weather into habit.

Meanwhile there are little graces the attention practices and the place never directly knows it is being given:

A beadline of iron that would have rolled six inches too far under a preregistered gust finds itself lodged between two laths of frost-friable shale that happen to be marriageable. The seed of a not-grass that evolved to be missing is wafted one palm’s breadth and made present in a seam where it will take, and for a decade the lee of that seam will feel like luck. On a certain night, a bird that believes it cannot afford to land is allowed to, once, because the pane holds wider for two impossible minutes and the lift is there like a kept promise. The bird does not sing. It leaves three ammoniate pressings smaller than a coin and flies on—nutrient its only sermon.

And one day, if you were to walk there with lungs that have learned to measure this air and bones that have taught themselves to find the syntax in ground, you might feel, without ever quite thinking it as such, a benevolence run under your feet like a low, even chord. You would set your boot on slickness made decades earlier by a fallen giant and know, in a way with no words in it, that someone attended to the angle of that slickness so that leeches could have their feast and calligraphers their draft and bearers their exact three-beat pause and you your one breath of being allowed to be here.

The power is wise because it knows that the most extravagant act it can perform in a poor place is to let poverty remain itself and not become famine. It is benevolent because, in the hour before dawn, it makes the sky the kind of cold that feels like competence rather than threat.

If you needed a list of its rules—though lists, here, are another continent’s kink—it would be something like this:

- Add by removing: clear windows for what is already practiced, do not import new practices.
- Teach by timing: give clocks that cost no breath and then get out of the way.
- Steward endings, not beginnings: place deaths where they will be maximum corridor.
- Shield from noise, not from truth: turn back only what would overwhelm; let the ordinary hardnesses remain.
- Leave no relics anyone could notice as other. The gift must fit so well in the sentence that even the sentence believes it wrote itself.

After a very long time, far from this tableland, there will be places that give thanks in music for what watched over them. Here, gratitude stays unspent because it is not a currency they mintage. The plateau answers its care with a perfect economy of being what it is. The bearers lift their fans to the patient cold and pull drink from empty air. Inside, chemistry clicks. Outside, a foot writes pressure into rock and another foot reads it out. A pane opens in the sky and closes. The only sound of this is time, and someone who loves them is keeping it.
