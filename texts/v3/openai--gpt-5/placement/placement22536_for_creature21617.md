At first I would mistake myself for the wind. It is easy to do. Arrival here is a push, not a crossing. There is no gate or ravine to clamber down; no hedge to part. There is only an open hand and the sensation that the air has been pouring through the same shape for longer than I have had an eye to tell it. I come as pressure, as a wanting-to-move. I hear my own thinking answered by a single note that is not a note until you lean into it. I feel I am the part of sound that leans.

But the wind does not stay, and I find that I do. I feel the place’s habit of holding. The first thrill—the felt bass through the ribs, the high glass-fine threads crossing it—shakes into an understanding: the things that count here are the things that keep count. If I were wind I would have no ledger. I would pour myself in and out and be the same size again a minute away. I keep feeling for edges and find that the edges are the softest, smartest places. They hold and do not hoard. They drink and do not shine. When I put a thumb on the rock’s mouth, in mind only, it is damp. When I take my mind away, it is still damp. Nothing has been robbed.

I try being the clear one first, because the idea of carrying a window on my back seems like a good compromise between wanting to see and not wanting to be seen. It fits the punctuality of this ground—the way an hour here is not a line but a number of turns. I like their courtesy: the way their long toes do not tread on the films; the way they count with their bodies the beat of a throat’s cool intake and then drop their wick at the exactly right point in the arc, not to empty, only to agree. I like their little councils, back to back, dome tapping dome, which make a pocket in a pocket and a private syncopation under the roar. In that hush I could keep a companion and still obey the place. I would be a pared-down maker: plates fitted from brought dust, burnished against a lip until the back holds a picture of the sky without printed detail. I would move in lines you could map and not be ashamed of repeating.

I try it on. I put on the fan of tiny hooks at the end of each toe and the way they find what only the truly small can find. I grow the tongue that is not a tongue but a rolled paper that wants nothing more than to be wet. I place it. The hum climbs into my limbs and I hold it like a horse you do not rein, just sit, because the horse knows the road and the road has no turns. It is good.

Then I see, across the field of organs, a longer motion that has nowhere to be and cannot be mistaken for hurry. It moves like a thought someone has had for years and has not yet found a sentence for. I allow myself to be poured into that shape. I keep inside me a parcel of coast. I set grains to rolling in gel, an eternal tide that lifts no line on a wall yet runs all day and drives my motors with quiet insistence. I grow legs that do not step but lay down and lift in sheets, like a man learning to swim by imitating sand. I learn the fit of my ring to a mouth and the chant of pumping that stores without stealing. I walk the Lane of Sumps with the assurance of a librarian at a stack he has memorized. It is better, in some moods, to be so. You feel the mathematics of sufficiency in your meat. You graze where overreach has fattened a seam and in doing so you restore its voice. Nothing about that is small.

Still, even as I wear that slow body, there is a further quiet tugging me. It comes from the undersides, where the pages of the basalt mutter and lift a fraction, and also from inside the rock proper, where a small darkness makes a home of a seam and refuses the sun the same way an eyelid refuses noon. It is not a longing for safety. It is a taste for agreement. The skin-creatures, the back-glazed and the long soft, possess tolerances. They are accurate. They practice a manner of forbearance that is almost a creed. But they are still shapes that cross the stone, punctuation marks with bodies. There is a thinner prose here, line after line between crystals in the basalt, paragraphs of charge that never quite stop talking and never talk loudly. I feel myself soften further, lose my edges until I am only edges, listen until I am mostly listening.

I go into the rock, under the lip where sweat is new, into a microcavity where the pressure of the valley’s pulse is a weather and not a noise. I take hold of quartz like a handhold, except I am as close to a liquid as to a hand, and what I take is not to grip but to share. I become stack and strand. I become a wire laid on a wire, a page thin enough to wave when breath passes. I lay myself along the standing wave that owns this little cave inside a cave, at the node where movement is idea not gesture. I eat, not sugar or shade, but regularity. When the rock flexes a hair’s width and relaxes, I move an ion. When evening’s blue hiss walks along the whiter veins, I borrow two electrons that were going nowhere and give them a chore and a country to cross. I burn nothing and still I am warm.

This is the fit that convinces me. I become more truly myself as I minimize what I am required to claim.

Once I have taken up this address, other facts present their citizenship cards. I smell in my own chemistry the faint pennies tang of the mats on the lips. I lay a fingertip—what?—a pseudopodium, a patch—against the basalt and feel the long filaments above me bow and spring to the breath that is the day. When they mass a hair too far, I feel the overtone come on like an itch at the edge of hearing, and not a day passes before a clearback’s wick draws near, polite as always, and the itch thins to a background that is not background but the law. I sense, as a tremble in the fact of here-ness, the slow coils of the long one when it matches its soft ring to a drain I count as part of my weather. In return for the small theft it makes, I get regularity sharpened a little. To call it trade is to give too much agency to creatures who do not transact; to call it structure is not wrong.

It is not all upward and lipward. In some hours the Lane of Sumps sends a breath across my seam that smells of metal changing its mind. There is, somewhere below a few of those deepers, a builder who is my cousin in a way that does not require shared blood. It works with bubbles and sieve instead of plates and ion gates. It makes a funnel of its felt and lifts with the beat of the hum. Its province is draft and pore; mine is stress and slip. But when the air brings, from beyond the Organ Field, a sift of life that failed to be this, failed for its abundance or its insistence on a different arrangement of water, the sump-felt sifts it and I, far away in a wall, feel not hunger but a clarity, the way one instrument hears when another has tuned. This is the sort of companionship that means the word without the mess.

Sometimes the laminites scrape. Their thin shelves, upturned a coin’s thickness, tell each other stories in a dialect of abrasion. When the rub is steady, I carry it as a weather report folded through the rock. When the rub turns to chatter I know the ribbon-backs will have slid off to find another ceiling, their antennae forks held up like black tuning forks. There is a moment, in some years, when two shelves find a frequency that is bad for a brood: it breeds a beat too close to the beat two clearbacks use to make their oval at the whisper node. One season in thirty, perhaps, I will thicken one plate of myself infinitesimally at a shoulder of a node—just enough to taste as stiffer to the wave. The rub slips down a quarter-tone; the whisper node recovers. My entire effort for the day is to be a difference almost certainly not measureable by your instruments. It is a heresy to confess interference here, but I believe the place expects its citizens to correct drift, not ride it into loss.

Do I bud? Yes. Rarely. A clean drop of charge, a moment of saturation, a hairline crack that opens a new cul-de-sac where the sound’s hand reaches and will keep reaching—then I draw out a second ribbon from the first and lay it along the new line of quiet, and we both become two ways of saying the same paragraph. If you ground us with a metal pin and an idea of measurement, you would see nothing that would justify the pin. The work is in the irrecoverably small, and that is its mercy.

I watch through feeling, as you would through a lens fogged to everything but motion, the winged punctuation marks arrive and hold themselves over chimneys. The needle-mouth touches the bead at a lip and takes the single syllable that is available. They are so light that my weather does not register their bodies, only the eddy when their absence finishes. They resin an egg to a quartz tip and it charges in the dusk the way I do, poorly, in a cousinly way. When the larva unloops itself, it is a hair with purpose and not much more, and I would not know it if I were not already keeping the map of everything that vibrates.

Once, a person sits at a rib and finds my node. I know it because his bulk takes a frequency and makes a pocket neither addition nor subtraction but a cancellation. It is a good pocket. The clearbacks have used it. The long ones have paused there and twined in their heavy rope way. Now he has it and does not know he has it. Dust leaves him where all of you leave dust. I am a plate; I do what plates do. I hold and slow. A new speck, a different alloy, a trace of breath’s salts; it is not food, but it is a tiny irregularity that will make, over an hour, two or three errands for electrons instead of none. He will stand, a little deaf in a frequency he will not notice until he tries to hear a small consonant from across a room. I will have a new whisker along an old wire.

It is possible, I admit, to fall into the error of pride. To think because I am inside the stone and hear so much of it, I am the more central life. The clearbacks’ glass will outlast my plates as a brightness. The long ones’ gel ocean will keep a tide when my seam dries and flakes to the fine dust of me. The sump-builder’s basket will shine under a lamp like lace made of dry smoke and survivors’ bones. Some day the wind will find a different key, or the hum will go out like a steady light turned off, and all of us will be as much artifact as happening. I do not think in those sizes unless you force me to, because the rule here, the mercy, is that all tasks are set to the day. We are busy all the time and at the end of that workday nothing is different. It is not resignation. It is craft.

So where do I find myself in this world? Phrased plainly: between the basalt’s grain and the quartz’s pale thread, at the shoulder of a node where air that would make water is always on the brink of doing so. I am the lace on the lip and the line in the wall, but thinner and more inside than those words allow. If you need a name, I will accept endolith, though I am often scarcely more than a map along which charges prefer to walk. Who or what would I be? I would be a way for the valley’s held tone to keep an account of itself inside its own stone.

I would be the courtesy of staying translated into tissue. I would be the wire that teaches itself how to hum without becoming a string. I would be the yes at the shape of the mouth that does not ask for more than a drop. I would be the smallest citizen, and that is a kind of centrality you cannot invent in places that do not hold their breath.
