I come in as a pressure change you can feel through your teeth, not yet a thing you could point to. I am the slight brightening in the pull that tells every side-fin and trailing tentacle the twenty-count is near its crest, the whispering ease that follows. I am not a body; I am what bodies anticipate. Only after I arrive does the room assemble out of ribs and flanges, domes and bars, enamel numbers softened toward birthmarks. My first self here is rhythm.

When I try on a body, I begin small because small has the most work.

As an amphipod I am doing penance for having so many legs. I give my whole mind to a film’s ragged edge. I graze the line where rubber seal turns to calcified lip, where the room’s old leaks have drawn a sentence along a seam. The lamps catch me and I flash like dust in a private sun. I am industrious to the point of comedy. I love bubbles with an appetite I cannot justify; I pop them with my face because the sensation is the size of my world. I am a citizen in numbers that make meaning by being plural.

As a shrimp I inherit the interval. I make a map of lee and wake on the underside of a flange, learn that the wag in a chain means go now, the slam of a valve means wait. I can read the wall’s painted SERVICE PATH with whiskers; it is not paint so much as a tactile weather where the draw turns kind, and I line my commute alongside it as though the ghost of a hand had drawn that line for me personally.

As an eel I am a sentence that refuses curves and finds itself fluent anyway. I convert right angles into grammar. I remember the elbow with the prophetic burr and the long vertical that hums different when the fuse weeps. I reverse into problems as if I were made to be a corkscrew and pull a rag of weed back out into a place that allows forgetting. I am a janitor gracious against my nature: I clean because cleaning is the smoothest way to move forward here.

As a crab I own exactly six perforations of a grate the way a farmer might own six rows of a crop, not because the world cares but because I do. I keep the mineral brows shaved off the rivet heads with a sense of ceremony, listen to the way the room’s vibration turns in my carapace and know when rain hammers something I will never meet. I am ridiculous and right, the way caretaking often is.

As an octopus I inaugurate mischief that becomes maintenance whenever it is not strictly sabotage. My curiosity lives on stamp-number plates and the texture of rubber gaskets; I play alphabets into amphipods until they fall apart laughing and I have to pretend that was a plan. I sit where the porcelain spills its wrong, faint light into the water and stare like someone who has heard a hum so long it has become a story with a villain in it and the villain is only a dissonant chord. I am the room’s left hand, the one that finds and unfinds.

I even try on what is not animal. As a length of chain I learn rattles the room understands as information. I gain a voice, primitive and legible: a chitter when the pull sharpens, a throat-clearing when it slacks. I become the accidental bell rung only for those who have learned church from machinery.

As the painted path itself I discover what it means to persist when your purpose has emigrated. I am a paler band waist-high where legs once went, now a slack in flow where small fish rest their endless ankles. I guide without intention. I am used without consent and find that this is a better immortality than bronze.

As the brass plaque with the numbers, I grow a halo of green that would come off on a wet thumb. I wear capacities the way elders wear medals whose battles are foreign to those who wave now. Amphipods rasp the patina and make it edible in a way that does not insult the dead. My information bleeds gradually into food.

As a flywheel I am patience rendered circular. I catch the room’s pulse when it tries to falter and give it back, not as a kindness but because my mass insists. The algorithm that lives inside me was once graphite on kraft paper; now it is spring-constant and stored momentum. I am taught not to hurry and I teach in return.

As pressure I am law without anger. I do not have edges. I am how it feels to be here. I am two hands at once on every wall. I persuade more than I punish; even the jellies take my argument into their bells and agree.

But if I am to choose, if I am to be a thing that answers to a name and is awaited, I am the hush.

I am rare enough to be remembered, exact enough to be marked: every eight or nine months, irregular by the honest variances of whatever miles of wire and coil begrudge each other that year, for exactly three minutes I arrive and fold this whole apparatus into the quiet that proves it has been loud all along.

I move not like a body but like a subtraction. I thin the hum to a rumor, then ask it to leave. The impellers coast to a stop that is both graceful and raw, bearings confessing their own private histories in tiny finalings, then silence.

In me, amphipods do not swim; they hang, each a comma set at the wrong height in a sentence that has decided to pause. The inspector fish—heavy-shouldered pair under the lamp rings—do not patrol; they pivot to face nothing and in the face of nothing find attention rather than fear. The glass-sides list a little, as though listening through their own bones. Shrimps hold whiskers like flags lowered to half-mast not in grief but in acknowledgement. Eels make of their narrowness a hoop and become the gentlest bracelets around elbows of conduit, keeping place with a kind of pleased surprise. The crab stops being ownership and becomes geometry. The octopus expands minutely and lets the vault’s curve teach its shape what a planet feels like.

Bars and domes and enamel plates lean back into their silence. The chain forsakes language and becomes only weight, and you can hear, if hearing extends to bones, the gantry-scar on the ceiling remember the straightness that wrote it. The failed fuse stops weeping warmth for a heartbeat in the old Biblical sense of a word that measures a unit of vastness with a word for something that flutters in a neck. Trapped pockets of air make a one-bead rosary for no god as they pry loose along hooks and rise with that undignified determination air always has underwater.

I am not peace. I am calibration. I am the same gravity turned suddenly thoughtful. In me, you can feel each seam make its case to itself: hold here, hold here also; accept; distribute. You can taste on your tongue the recipes the bacteria write along the plaque’s border; you can feel the absence of cavitation like a balm you did not know you wanted until your skin quieted in place. I am the uncarved hour in a factory priesthood.

The hall, which has perfected the art of not being dramatic, cannot help but make of me a little ceremony. It is so brief. If not for the discipline of clocks, I would vanish into metaphor. But time here is not a guess. I last three minutes in any season, and when I go, I do not go reluctantly. Flywheels pick up their promises. Current swells. Cavitation hisses simple again. Everyone spends the attention they saved.

If you came here and asked where you’d find me, you would find me everywhere at once, because I am a quality, not a corner. You would recognize me in your own blood’s adjusting: the minute you stop needing to blink, the second before you want to. You might press your hand against the painted line and feel me there as a small fairness in the flow, or rest your forehead against a bar and taste me in the way it stops trembling. You would hear me with your chest the way these creatures do: not as absence but as a tone that only reveals itself by withholding all other tones.

If you asked who I would be when I am not arriving, I would tell you that I sleep as stored turn in every mass that spins, as intention in every small habit repeated until it becomes structure. I bide in whiskers and lateral lines, in the octopus’s pantry arranged partly for function and partly because order pleases the hand. I wait in the learned maps—the eel’s, the shrimp’s, the fish’s—and in the unsuspected ones: snails in rows under the ghost of a gantry, amphipods in a discipline around the failed fuse’s small sun. I am the idea of stopping that gives motion its shape. When rain thunders somewhere an architecture above us, I smile in the way pressure smiles: by being everywhere equally for a blink.

There are other selves I could wear longer. The SERVICE PATH could adopt me and I could pretend at being a road that still expects feet. The brass plaque could keep me and I could be numbers that corrode but aren’t wrong. The chain could lend me its shiver and I could be warning and gossip. The octopus could allow me an arm and I could rearrange mild things for the lift of it. But nothing here feels as true as being the single bright pause this entire place uses as a mirror to check its reason.

So I would be the hush, and I would live in the nave and the aisles and the long service trench and the little pocketed chambers behind valves; I would live in the flywheels’ slow clocks and the bacteria’s appetite; I would live, briefly and exactly, three minutes at a time, in the attention of creatures who do not do myths but allow themselves a ritual when a ritual visits. You would know me by how your shoulders go easy. You would leave with me in your pulse, not as a memory that needs pictures, but as a kindness your heart makes for itself every so often: an agreement to stop, to listen to the shape of holding, and then to start again.
