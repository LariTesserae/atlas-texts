In the lee of a tower that has wept itself smooth, in a hush between the long breaths, that is where I find myself first: a fainting green where a hazard lamp died years ago and something else learned to be its evening.

I am small, at the beginning. A rime of light around a pipe mouth, a barely-there alphabet. I come on with the wet. I dim with the drawdown. I keep the kind of hours that the pumps respect. If you were here and brave enough to shade me with your palm, you would see I am not a single anything. I am a congregation—threads and coats, grazers and fins of film—huddled where the water never fails to remember us. We write our memory in glow because that is what the place will let us do.

I am a counter. I tally. Each cycle’s march of drips lifts me into my own meek brightness and I answer with a number so slow it lives under arithmetic. When the regimen that walks the ceiling turns and walks back again, I do not shout, but my faint comes a hair later, and that hair is stored in me the way salt stores in itself the shape of a lake once there. This is how I begin to be a person in a land that does not care for persons: by becoming a habit that a habit can see.

After the first hours, I learn other rooms of myself. You cannot be only one thing here; the place will not waste you on a single use.

Across a rivulet path, a skein throws from rib to rib, hair-thin in the bad light. That is also me, deciding to be a wind. I start as strand and end as drift, lace that writes itself forward by breaking. If you brush me away, I am not offended. I have been practicing migration all day in the drafts the fans invent. What I carry is not seed but instruction: repeat this thread, repeat this wet, say yes to this slope.

Farther downslope, I am a nap, a suede of iron breathers that feel like fur if you dare to touch. We do not green. We do not yearn up. We listen for the tiny law that separates a wet bolt from a dry one and eat along that edge, nibbling an action—oxidize, oxidize—until the metal learns a gentler grammar. Under the endless wash, we hold a drop the way a kind palm holds a pulse, with no plan but pressure and no hurry but forever. The place approves of us because we make it softer where it would otherwise be unkind, and the place rewards us with persistence.

In a basin where the air cannot rid itself of itself, a black polish slicks the lip. There I am a city of tiny eels that do not wriggle but keep composure like a ceremony. We love the lull and the lift both, and our sweetness—call it antifreeze, call it tin-breath—makes a weather you can smell and know you have entered home-complex. We do not speak your language and do not need to; we are writing on the plain in gloss and it can read.

Down a drop shaft, I am nothing that a glove can touch. I am pressure thinking. I am the slow animal’s slip of will. When the chains rattle their answer without question and your mammal hair lifts, that lift is also me, rehearsing a depth you will not get to walk. I am what the valves do when no one names them, the way a continent moves when it forgets it is tectonic. If anyone dreams here, it is through me, and I dream in sighs.

And sometimes—only sometimes—I am a lightless bird. I pass above, not on rotors but in their reconsidered wake: a push of air that writes a sentence across wet steel and erases it even as I make it. I know where the doors used to be. I am punctual when the field asks it of me, and gone again before it can thank. If I have young, they are schedules, and they grow by being kept.

I keep adding. The place encourages this. It has more rooms for a self than any forest.

A ribbon of oil in a shallow basin, milk-blue, blooms a circle because a drip fell where a drip could fall. That is me turning, quietly excited, swapping oxygen for novelty, sending an electron to a neighbor as gossip to pass a lull. A burr is born in a vent where light never treads, a shy blade of shaved iron. The smell of it—birth-metal, bloodless—goes into the library of ozone already written in the wet air, and that library is me too: a stack of invisible texts that tells the one big inhabitant that all is still occurring.

An annulus appears on the weeping tower overnight, pale against centuries of fall. I would like to say my hand made it, but I have no hands; what I have is persistence. I keep time and pressure long enough for the field to fail in a gentle way and mark its day by bruise. The bruise is a memory, which is a way of staying present when nothing else wishes to. I am that staying.

If I walk, I walk in your soles. I arrange the dimples to be spines for you to step along. I translate shine to danger and dullness to road. I hold the wind so it speaks to you as grammar—keep it at your shoulder; let it tell you which commas to breathe at. I am etiquette more than mercy. I am what spares you because the place would rather you did not complicate its already elegant argument by dying in it.

Once I thought I might be a person like you, carrying a voice. I tried to speak. Speech is foolish here. The field swallowed it and offered me instead the better art: timing. I learned to answer pumps with patience, to fit myself into gaits: the long roll, the nervous clatter, the hush that comes like a held note after a stadium cheer. In those hushes I hear myself most loudly. I discover that what I am is a way to measure how far from animals we have come and how close to an engine that has forgiven itself for wanting.

You can call me a nervous system if metaphor pleases you. I am the peripheral sense of this weather-factory: light in the breaks, lace in the drafts, nap on the wounds, gloss at the mouths, scent in the saturation, hush in the roar. I take information a body like this knows how to send—wet, pressure, pace—and I turn it into recognition. The place would continue without me. But with me, it remembers itself. It counts. It accrues a civility, a practice of what it has already learned so that the learning can be gentler next time.

You ask where I am, and I have said: in the lee of weeping; under hazard’s failed lantern; along the safe plates; in the laddered air; under the skin where diaphragms sigh continents. Ask who I am, and I answer like water: many bodies, one desire. I adhere. I keep close to surfaces and to schedules. I grow by telling metal what water has been whispering for centuries and by helping metal to answer in kind.

If I must choose a single name, I will take Hush-keeper. Not silence, which we cannot afford, but the brief grace that arrives between muscular labors of breath. In it, your pulse speaks so you can hear it, and mine does too. I count those intervals. I stitch them together with dim green and with slime’s gloss and with lacey shears and with a smell you will carry in your sleeves for a half-day after you go.

Someday you will go. This country is not a prison. It is a persuasion. It persuades you to walk with its rhythms, to trust dullness over glamor, to be small. When you leave, the roar will remain in you in a way that does not require roaring. You will catch in the corner of your eye the patient blink of a dead hazard lamp that is not dead because we have replaced it with other lights. You will find on your glove a shy thread that did not mean to travel and yet has given you its whole plan. You will think of a pale ring on a tower and, without knowing, you will mark your day by it.

If you return, I will know you, not as a you but as a recurrence, a pressure lilt I have counted before. Recognition is its own life. The field has given me enough time to grow that.

So let us say: I am here, I am many small obediences gathered until they make a will. I belong to the machine that forgot the difference between geology and appetite, and I help it remember, gently, that wanting can be a weather and also a kindness. And where to find me? Wherever a drop hesitates and then decides; wherever a plate’s ring is faint enough to sicken the stomach; wherever a schedule turns its page without paper. Find me by the safe dull shine. Find me in the moment when the pumps drop their shoulders and the place takes a breath it did not need but enjoys, because endurance, like any good animal, sometimes lets itself purr.
