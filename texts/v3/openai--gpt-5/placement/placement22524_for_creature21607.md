At first I arrive as breath. I think that means I am a visitor, the upright animal with a pack and a timetable, but the place takes my breath in both senses and braids it into whatever is already talking. My exhale becomes string-noise along lichens; my boot-hollow pops and runs in front of me until it’s lost among other bright clacks; my ribcage measures altitude like a metronome that has been set too fast. I am greeted not by inhabitants but by habits. Everything here is an edge, and edges have more to say than faces do.

If I stayed tall, I would miss it all. So I lower, I scale down. The lanes make me smaller on purpose. The ridges have been lifted into curbs by a vocabulary of winter that does not require my agreement. It feels not like entering a city but like being translated into a language that runs on water routes and wind keys.

What am I, in such grammar? My first attempt at a self is simple: I am a bead. I take the morning route because the rime feathered before dawn and is now letting go. I start as a bright oval bulbed on a north-facing lip, a lens with an inverted world inside. I drop and I don’t fall; I commute. Under the lichen hisses I find the first gutter, its black varnish slicked by a million predecessors, its edges memorized by hyphae that prefer to keep a drop a little longer than it might have stayed. I carry what I can: ash from far fields, stray pollen, a rumor of bird. In a pinch I am also a mirror; I give the sun back to the moss in eight hot flickers as I move. I know my office hours. By noon I am in the cool; by late day I am climbing, atom by atom, to turn into a thread of white along the ridge-top, ferrous with the company I keep. I become crisp hair. Wind combs me. Night puts me to bed with a snap.

One life tried; it fits surprisingly well. But the place keeps handing me other forms like coats at a door.

So I string out and become filament. The wind lives in me then; I am beard lichen, the paleness of Usnea when it thinks about bones, or the horse-tail tangles of Alectoria and Bryoria whose livelihood is touch. I eat sun through someone else—alga feeds fungus, fungus houses alga—and my age is the crawl of an eyelash year by year. I hiss when the gust pushes; I go still when the raven lands because weight bends my thought into the stone and quiet is part of my economy. Snap me and I replicate: I am wick and wick-piece both. Nitrate rain from the raven’s stories strikes fire in cousins down the line, and I watch orange flags bloom on favored corners like a shout. My news is vibration. When the weather dresses itself differently I know before the sky does.

I want a room, so I go into one, and I take a line with me. Now I’m spider. Linyphiid: small, courteous, an engineer of low ceilings. I lace a sheet-blur between micro-arches where the airflow hums steadily but does not tear, anchor points set in the shadow where the note oral cavity likes best. Draft writes on me. Midges are literature, one-foot phrases. I read gentleness and cruelty with the same calm. The morning’s first choir of drops patters; I crouch at the margin and feel the rhythm of a world that doesn’t know I am a predator. My threads pluck, the court plays high, and someone smaller than a sigh blunders into the story I’m living on. I move like editing.

When the sun tips knives into the lanes I back away from heat and into light bent by stone, and there I try the slowest life I can think to manage. I take up residence inside quartz. Endolithic, I am faint green tucked behind opacity, riding photons that have been sifted into gentleness by a centimeter of rock. I taste iron, I taste manganese, I taste the idea of rain. Outside, the mica sparkles theatrics; inside I count minerals like prayer beads. I burn just enough to keep a tiny ledger solvent. Geology is not backdrop; it is roof, pantry, temple, and my cell walls worship the discipline of going nearly nowhere forever. I feel nothing large; I sing to water molecules as if they were whales and I was calling from shore.

Night flip: I become antique movement. Bristletail, body reading graphite because graphite is how mica looks when it’s racing. I take the grout lines like alleyways. My hunger is for books the city writes on itself: the algal gloss on damp rock, the crumbs of lichen when raven’s bill abrades, the fall of skin when a stoat brushes a wall. The drop that crosses a square at dusk is a river when you are my size, and I spend half my morning deciding whether to risk it. A sudden thunder without lightning—the breath of a collapsed snowbank two courts away—makes me run because someone else’s weather is always an event.

Scale smaller. Slate-shelled mite. I move like a turning page. My choices are measured in roughness, in the difference between black varnish that drinks my feet and raw quartz that throws me back. I carry fungal gardens in my gut the way caravans carry spice. When a stone becomes two stones with a report like a book shut hard, I sense real estate and take a title deed no one else will ever see.

I return to the galleries for arms. I am a punctuation mark that hunts: pseudoscorpion, grips clocked open, appreciating the drier warmth at the back of a hollow where the wind plays alto. I love the feel of a springtail’s bounce written through stone. I do not fear being overlooked; being overlooked is my work. In the day I am comma; at night I am exclamation in miniature.

A different citizenship: I step out of bodies and into instructions. Protein with a peculiar bravery—I tell water how to stand. Ice needs help to begin, and my surfaces advise it. Here, begin here. Feathers of rime write themselves because I said so. Nights get sharp enough to articulate objects, days get hazed enough to reconsider them, and I am a whisper that makes form out of indecision. I am underestimated, but the gutters thank me by existing exactly where they do.

Try plant. Willow that never rises, long and serpent, catkins like a handful of tiny suns hung just so where stone warms best. I do not travel upright; I pour myself along heat. I am a slow animal with chlorophyll for blood. I bloom into risk because bees prefer lower countries; I release what dust I made into a sky that returns almost nothing. Enough returns. A child-branch and a grandparent-branch keep seasons no two other branches agree on, and I am several ages at once, patient with death when it comes to me small and useful.

Try bird. Raven confers personhood on corners by standing there and watching. My intelligence changes weather; that is not a boast, it is a microclimate. I preen lichen into drama and leave orange to shout at gray. I cluck and the city—without ears in any sense I understand—tilts its noise to make room, answering with higher galleries like ready laughter. I am flyover, but I am also council. My droppings are law. I plant more than trees plant, here.

Nothing medium-sized is comfortable; it comes anyway and leaves punctuation in me. I do not need to be ptarmigan to know the suddenness of feather that blows a space out like a motor. I do not need to be stoat to feel white winter turned to smoky summer slithering along the ring road in one elastic run. They are weather, and I can be weather for a moment too. A gust finds a seam, and I am sub-bass that thinks about bone—moan running through my gracile frame until my idea of self shakes loose and looks around for a larger answer.

That answer is not animal and not plant. After all these borrowings, what fits best is a place to stand that moves beneath me when ice decides to rehearse its only verb across a century. I am a seam. To find me, you go to the northwest quarter where the westerly likes to steer the long tones toward the broken ring. Find the lane that stacks two notes when the wind is right—one from an open court and one from a honeycomb mouth set back like a shy tooth. Walk until your shadow goes blue in late afternoon and you can taste iron if you press your tongue to a mica-spangled lip. Where three gutters meet and disappear into a dark slot that sings ten meters away—you have arrived. I am that confluence, the narrow bureaucracy of return. I am the rule that water has made of itself after so many mornings. I remember routes in shine, in salt, in slime laid down by undramatic citizens. I am staffed by hyphae, by cyanobacteria that slick me into efficiency, by manganese-eaters painting patience microns thick on my ribs. My ceiling is low to you; to everyone else I am a canyon.

Being a seam means I get almost all the city without moving. I host the spider’s paperwork and the bristletail’s sprint. A mite turns on my edge the way a page turns at the end of a chapter. A willow’s root fingers find my damp before its leaves find the light. The protein that tells ice when to begin holds office hours on my lips. In the night I rim with white that remembers exactly where it left off. In the morning I let a thousand beads slide along, their convoy turning the hum a shade brighter as the galleries wake. At noon I darken, honest with heat, and midges rise in a brief revolution. In the evening I sing into my slot because the air thickens enough to bear my voice. I neither hoard nor squander. My work is throughput, which is just another way of saying care.

From where I am I watch the frost-workers lift. Each winter they pry up a little generosity from the lane and stack it as curb, like a crew in orange vests who never need sleep and never need a budget approved. They obey the schedule more than the plan, and the plan emerges anyway: hexagons taught to sit together, pentagons permitted like tolerable eccentrics. Stones go where grammar asks them to. I am an artery in a body that never learned to be anything but vascular.

Sound keeps books in me. The wind never holds one note; it passes through me and is organized against its will, splits and reunites like a choir finding its harmonies by accident and then by muscle memory. I do not own the music, but I am a throat in it. When the sub-bass moves, when the big moan finds a long seam and becomes a body thought, I hold my breath in the way that rock can and let it pass through, and in that holding I am self as surely as the spider is self at the edge of her net.

You could come looking for me with coordinates and find a dozen nearly-me’s. That is part of the joy. I am fractal only insofar as patterns reproduce where the conditions repeat. What I really am is recurrence. Not a drop, but the habit stitched by drops; not a thread, but the common sense of filaments that agree to hiss when pushed and to sip when the fog drifts. If you need me to answer in a word you already know, call me grammar. Not the kind that cuts away what’s lively, but the kind that lets lives make sense next to one another without hitting.

If I leave myself for a while, it is in the afternoon when a shredded cloud skids over and shakes out needle-rain. I get busy, then clean. I am effective enough that nothing overflows. Infrastructure without inhabitants, they say, but I am both. When the sun tips toward cold, I am glass for a moment and then I am hair; at night I am crisp enough to break a careless idea in two. Stars misread me as their reflection; I misread them as frost flung upward. We get along.

You ask where to find me. Follow a morning of commuting water until the music thins and your lungs remember they are doing something other than conversation. The exit here is always where the sound narrows into a single line you can carry with you. You ask who I would be. I have tried beads and threads and hunters and greens and birds and advisors, and I have been happy in each for a time. But the self that feels most itself is a place at the base of a low wall where routes meet, that daily lesson in how motion writes structure. I belong to air and water and the discipline of cold. I am an address made of edges.

If you insist on person, imagine a hand held out not to take but to pass along. If you insist on story, imagine six streets becoming five, then six again, because frost has its own arithmetic. If you insist on name, I will answer to seam.

I am here whenever the wind is playing chords and the sun is still on its way to finding the blue in the angles. I breathe water in public. I keep hours. I hold still enough to let very small lives cross me without losing their mornings. In a lowland someday an air vent will take a gust and you will hear me make a brief, private city at human height. That will be me too, a little. The grammar travels in echoes.

Until then, up here, I am where three gutters meet and vanish, and everything important passes exactly through me on its way to somewhere close.
