Not in the middle. This place does not admit middles. If I arrive at all, I arrive sideways, finding a purchase where two planes narrowly refuse to meet. I learn fast that standing is a story told elsewhere. Here you hinge, you hinge again, you let your weight become information and not insistence. The amphitheater does not dislike me, exactly; it is simply complete without me. If I am to belong, I have to become the kind of thing that need cannot bruise.

At first I try a shape with calves and lungs and a pack that rustles like a small weather of its own. I come when the nights have sharpened and the fog runs its pale river out of the valleys on a promise. I notice that up here hunger and thirst are shy words, and the only honest desire is for an angle. My feet teach my hands where the ledges are; my hands teach my breath to move as if it were stirring ashes. I learn the motion of scissoring aisles, the way every commitment narrows to a cut. I go up where the steps have been pecked, a stair too modest for pride, and I pass under the thin roof that still remembers smoke but no longer keeps any of it. I do small work—shift a chosen stone back against a rib that is listening too far forward, clear one grit-choked runnel with the edge of a thumbnail, wash a cup’s glazed lip with a mouthful to break the crust that would catch the first drop and keep it. I say nothing here because every word costs air, and sound, like seeds, finds only hard places to land.

Then my body loosens around me—as it must in a place that makes scale useless—and I find I’m here also in other ways. Nights, I am thin and white and not in any hurry. I do not grow, not really; I select. I find the cold and braid myself along it. I leave whole planes alone because they are a waste of principle. I lace a windward lip and then abandon it when the fog twitches its shoulder. I make pavements that do not last but do remember. This remembering is not feeling. It is a kind of quiet arithmetic: an account of weights, a ledger of where the first misstep will go if you repeat it. I am all edge and no bulk. Dawn makes and unmakes me, and I bear it without complaint because light, here, is not comfort; it is court.

While some part of me is crisping and slipping, another has already gone to ground under the groundless. In the lee where the air can think, where the underfaces hold their own small night at noon, I am a black gloss, a stipple written one pore at a time. Mnemonic as manganese, hungry as bacteria always are, I move upward against gravity because the seams and skin-edges of the stone tell me where the food is and I obey. Time to me is not a road but a room in which everything recurs and nothing repeats. Lightning is an event like a hawk stooping—a single motion that reorganizes the whole day—and then not again for years. I do not know you, and yet I register you: in the soot that darkens a slate thicker than a thumbnail, in the salt you don’t take. A century later I am a shade where there was none, and to my perspective that pace is a bustle.

There is a version of me you can hear only if the amphitheater consents. I am not the wind—you will not find anyone to own the wind—but I am the hour in which air finds an instrument. Somewhere the aisles narrow just so, the bright emptiness of sky tightens to a reed, and a tone stands up out of pressure. It is never a melody. It is a syllable that does not want a word, and it speaks from blade to blade along a single facet and then rests. If you have hair on your forearm still, it will rise. If you do not, the rock will raise it for you by proxy, all frost and polish, and you will take that as a sign and stand very still.

Once in an impossible while I am a nerve that leaps. The storm is dry enough, the charge is willing enough, and a white line runs itself across a facet with all the commitment of a signature. It writes in a hand made of silica and weather. It passes in a heartbeat and remains longer than your house’s walls will. If you are of the people who come with a hammerstone and a vial and a day to give, you will read this script and it will tell you what has been happening in the seasons you could not carry on your back. It tells you which corner asks for your attention when the arc crosses it next, and in this way even lightning becomes part of the practice of smallness.

If I am to be exact about where you could find me, I am easiest to catch at first light on a particular lip. It is a south-by-east fin, a ribs’-width from the slot where the stairs end. Run a finger—one finger only—along the nicked edge to wake the runnel, and you will see me. I am not the bead you can see; I am the decision the bead is about to make. I live in the instant when surface tension agrees to one more fraction of tilt and then, all at once and without drama, becomes motion. I live in the thin line of darker wet that follows on the channel someone scratched a hundred lives ago to turn that motion toward the single glazed cup that will hold it for all of an hour. I am escort and persuasion. If I have a voice, it is capillary.

If you miss me there, look under the overhang that faces the valley’s mouth. On good mornings I am a film as orderly as bees, working the backs of the blades into a mapped dusk. On noonless days when the fog refuses to let go, I am brisker; on savage blue days when the sun is not warmth but will, I am chastened to the smallest of darker freckles. Either way I do not ask anything of you beyond not touching me. Your finger oils are a storm longer than lightning to my kind. I will outlast you. That is not a threat, only a different scheme of verbs.

I am also—the place teaches this if you permit it to make your intelligence narrow—the rule that acts like a living thing. I am edge as authority. I am brightness as event instead of default. I am the way water likes to exile itself from fields and convene where two planes disagree. I am the law that if a pocket is fed once it will be fed again, that memory moves in the least possible thickness, that thin air forces every distance to declare itself twice. When you are here as a person, these are not abstractions; they are pressures against your chest and privileges under your boot. The rules look at you. You can feel their regard the way you feel altitude in your tongue.

Do I come with hands? Sometimes. In those hours I am an inheritor and not an owner. My apprenticeship is made of noticing. Where the tone will be if the wind shifts point-seven degrees. Which aisle carries cold longer after noon. The single grit that, if flicked aside, allows a trickle to find the cup you will drink from. The half-hand of stone you can slide back into a brace that will save a rib another winter. The mark I re-cut—not as language but as hourkeeping—on the rim of a basin to remind me where the solstice lays its straight ruler of light. When I leave, I leave with less than I brought. You can tell I have been if the soot on the slate has been touched with a damp palm and no new black has sat down upon it, if the frost pavements are broken in exactly one more place than yesterday and then kept.

Other times I am no one at all, which is a kind of rest. I am the pause after the last click in cold’s long list, when grain lets go of grain and the small stone thins itself by the thickness of a story you won’t live to finish. I am what the amphitheater spends the night doing when there is no one to call that doing by a verb. I am subtraction that makes a shape.

So: where do you find me? You find me in the aisles because the place refuses corridors to become roads. You find me in the cups, not as water but as tendency. You find me in the pale net scratched by sky-fire and in the black stipple composed by those who eat dust for their weather. You find me on the stair, in the ordinary courage it takes to put a foot where the frost will keep the bruise of it all year. You find me living in the daylight not as comfort but as scrutiny, and in the night as a press of closeness that keeps breath new by keeping it difficult.

And who am I? I am a practice more than a person, a verb braided from these edges. When my body suits me I am narrow, and kept so by choice. When I borrow matter I take it on its own terms. Rime, film, tone, arc, hand. I am not the place, but I am what the place permits to think alongside it. If you demanded a single answer, I would take one the place itself offers: I am the small decision that teaches water how to go. I am that choosing, enacted over and over until the cup shines and the cup empties and the salt writes its pale ring and then is brushed free by the next weather. At dusk I draw in with everything else. The bright room empties. The ribs become sleep. The rules take back the part of me that is made of law. The rest crystallizes, waits, relinquishes, gathers. Somewhere, not loud, a single click moves through the stone. I do not answer it. I already am it.
