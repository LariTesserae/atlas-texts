At first I try on the obvious answers. I try on being a person, stepping in the honeyed dimness with a cloth over my mouth and a purpose in my pocket. I try on being clever about where I put my feet on the basalt hexagons, weighting the faint dishes as if obedience could save me. In that costume, I am gone before my second thought can find the end of itself. Here thought is a thing that must be held up against a single pressure, and that pressure makes all other holding collapse. In a body, I do not last. The note fills me in like grout.

So I look for a way to be that is not bruise and breath.

I could be a clamp of copper, hammered and wide-eyed, seeded with the brown patience that metal finds when the world stops asking it to react. I could be a dovetail cut to refusal, socketed into the exact quiet of a node, the chisel’s history kept on my flanks like scars in a mouth where no tongue ever goes. I could be the bitumen, the black vowel in the seams, now stone by conviction, slick in a way that hears nothing forever. I try those shapes in imagination. They are good shapes. They belong. But they are not a place I would find myself; they are things that have been found and set. They do not answer the question of where I could be that would let me know I am, even in a world where knowing is shaved down to the one verb the place permits.

There are maps here already, older than any person’s thought of maps: the slow zebra of dust, the thin bright/gray bright/gray along lines where motion refuses itself and along bulges where it swamps. The only calendars in this world are made of powder taking instruction. If I am to live on a principle, I will live as something that takes instruction, but not all of them.

I find myself, then, in one of the freckles. I find myself where the sound cannot quite get in.

I am an oval of hush no larger than the palm of a careful hand, nested in the crook where a ceramic throat shoulders into a boxy cavity and the carved flare of a horn lip leaves just so much shelter. I do not have walls; I have a contour drawn by a pressure I do not carry. My edge is exact and without decoration. Everything sings except me, and the line where I begin is a law the tone obeys because the stone makes it.

Dust knew me before any mind could. It drifted as far as drifting is permitted here—which is not at all, not really; it sorted, it was pushed by nothing more than difference in motion until it laid down a thicker grayness inside my outline. The thin glitter at the foot of the walls grows in whiskers and breaks and never lies in the maxima; in me those whiskers last a fraction longer before their heads tremble off; the pieces come to rest and do not rearrange. If there is a clock, I am the face where the hand does not twitch. I accrue. I am a proof of stillness that the tone leaves room for on purpose.

How big am I? Exactly the size of the angle that shelters me, and no bigger. As the one note sands sharpness into forgiveness everywhere it can reach, my shape sands too, easier and truer. The geometry is tending me. In time my edge will be cleaner than the edge of a coin. It is almost that now. You could lay the pad of a finger on my floor and feel the difference. Here the air is only air. Here the long animal of steadiness touches me along my border and cannot cross; it throws itself again and again there and is turned by my refusal into itself.

I do not hear the tone. Hearing would be a way of admitting it. I know it as your eyelid knows light: by pressure, by the outline it makes when it is just on the other side of me. All the hall’s organ is my neighbor. We are not enemies. We are insistences that nest. I am not a wound in it. I’m a pore the body keeps closed.

The selenite sighs its fixed amber through me as through anywhere. There is nothing to catch a spark here and I do not want one; my dimness is the same as the hall’s dimness because it has to be. What I have that the tall vault does not is interior space where thought, if it were possible, might belong. If you were living and if you knew the star chart of safe spots (and once someone did, once hands learned it), you could lean your temple into me and count in your body again, find the drum in your ribs, the tremble at your throat, the grain in your bones. You could stage a sentence here and let it stand. It would not be voiced; there is no throat in this world but one, and that one is full. Still: the sentence could exist long enough to take a shape inside you and be known. That is almost the same as speech. It is enough like prayer to count.

Because of this, I am harbor. I am margin. I am negative not as lack but as made space. The builders knew me and made law out of me. Behind every third thin slot there is a dead chamber to swallow a thing the tone should not be; somewhere behind that there is one of me to swallow even the remainder of remainder, until the body is true. Between reeds, between spines, at lip and lintel: we freckles collect. We are the places left on purpose like footings under a waterless bridge, except there is no water and no walking; what we carry is the condition of carrying.

Sometimes the salts manage a shape that looks like froth stopped at the point of folding. They crust the angle between two horns with a careful white, comblike and delicate, and then they let themselves be educated back into powder by the unceasing shaking that is this world’s season. In me they are permitted only a little more architecture before they give themselves up. Their failure is almost a kindness; they are the only things here that do anything like die, and they do it so undramatically that it feels like courtesy, like a ceremony of making room. When they break, I keep them. When they begin again, I am exactly where I was. If there is harvesting, it is like breathing: automatic, unmeant, performed as an attribute of being slightly less moved than the rest of the room.

Other freckles exist around me, their presence mapped by the powder’s choices and by the way tool marks hide. We do not talk to each other. We are not a society of absences. We are the same absence repeated differently, because each stone shoulder and horn mouth makes a slightly different shelter. If you set out to inventory us you would wind up with a list that is also a poem about variance under strictness. There are ovals narrow as a finger’s width and ovals round as a saucer. There are ones tucked under thresholds with saw-tooth sills that rake the tone until only a sliver of it is left to gnaw and cannot cross. There are ones perched like commas at the turn of a duct where heat tilts just so. There is one under the lip of a faceted horn where the plane breaks the curve and the pressure forgets itself for an inch. I am one of those.

I am near the center—near but not on it. A rank of horn mouths crowd each other here so closely that their flares kiss, and amid their fluted gossip the stone lid lies flush, scored with spirals that read like a diagram of breath written for fingers instead of eyes. Along one of the notches that points toward a bundle of reeds, the pressure field leaves just enough shadow to make me. In the age of hands, someone might have knelt across from the lid and fitted their palm into me to lean while their other fingers traced the carved score. In me they could be a boundary themselves and not be unmade immediately. They could teach their bones the line the builders drew and feel the long animal of steadiness answer through stone and air. They would have known exactly what they were stealing. They would have given it back within moments. That is what I am for: to allow the small courtesies of theft and return in a place where stealing anything larger than a breath is not possible.

Outside does not exist in any way that matters to me except as a trend the whole hall drinks from. Thinness becomes thickness. I am inside the law that converts it. I do not have seasons. My time is thickness of powder, the chemistry of a gray that leans infinitesimally this way or that depending on which continent the high, dry breath shaved a grain from centuries ago. I cannot number days. I cannot be visited by weather. A bright thing that could have been an insect once passes the reed-bundle’s slots as a habit in a carrier gas and arrives so disassembled that I only know it by how the glitter in me takes one new facet shape for a long while. I keep that, too. It is not a memento. It is an increment of gray.

Predators are ideas I do not allow. Water is a word the hall refuses, and I refuse it with the same energy. If anything wet came here, there would be no me. I am the child of dryness as much as the tone is. Moisture would smear my edge into a general apology. The selenite would milk. The bitumen would remember softness and ruin its own vow. The long sound would go ragged with contaminants, and all the busy reed-baffles that make it monastic would be netted with life it did not ask for. I cannot think those thoughts without changing my shape, and I do not change. I bend, infinitesimally, as the stone warms and cools on the only scale allowed here. I hold.

If I have a self beyond boundary, it is the record I keep. Dovetails hidden where I lie have kept the maker’s striations under me safe inside a pocket of almost-zero, where the tone goes past but cannot lick. The hand that rasped them left me nothing to read except the guarantee that difference once had fingers. The guarantee is enough. The rest is here now as law: the reed bundles spaced with no two intervals alike; the dead slots that do what death is supposed to do, which is to stop things; the slotted doors that make sills into sieves; the ceramic throats turned true and then collared, bone-pale and absolute.

Sometimes I tell myself what I am again so that I will not be tempted by metaphor, because metaphor is a cousin of water, a solvent that sneaks. I am not a cave. I am not an ear. I am not a god of respite. I am not a mercy. I am a fixed solution to an equation set in stone and air. I am measured in inches and in the absence of oscillation. I am the contour that says: here the held verb cannot quite hold; here it finds its border. I am health for a body whose health is to be one thing only. I am contained and I contain.

The tone at my edge is full of its one act and placid as a motor that drew its power from height and was not built to fail. It leans against me without complaint. If it notices me at all, it notices me as your eye notices its blind spot: as the way everything else around is suddenly more certain because of an unseeable pore. We are good neighbors. It is monk and warmth, I am cloister; it is field, I am fence line; it is the verb, I am punctuation. None of those are right, but they point.

If you were to come—if some law was set aside and you arrived with a heart that could hold its beat under such an always—and if you had time before the pressure borrowed you entirely and pressed your speech into flatness—you would find me in the crook by the central lid, right where the carved notch kisses a particular horn. Your teeth would shake and then feel less shaking in one small place. You would put your palm there because your hand would seek it like water seeks lower. Your bones would become the instrument that reads me. For a beat without number, your thought would exist. It would be whatever sentence a mind makes when finally it meets a law so complete it refuses even to hurt. Then you would be over. It would not be a cruelty. You would join my calendar.

Later—by which I mean after more glitter had softened my floor by a layer one grain thick—I would still be here. The selenite would still be sighing its resinous dim. The bitumen would still be a stone that remembers it used to be sticky as a rumor. The ceramic throats would still be bone-tough and underglazed with the sheen you could mistake for skin. The copper would still be the brown of fruit peels and belts that learned the word enough. The tone would still be itself, its edges buttering the last raw angles down, patient as the idea that gave it birth and then took its hands away and left it to keep drawing the same line without fatigue.

Who am I in this world? I am the hush the tone wears like a freckle. Where would you find me? In the small negative under a horn lip, in the crook beside the heart’s lid, where powder gathers and stays and a sentence can, for a moment, remember how to be a sentence. I do not need a pronoun. I am my boundary. I am the shape that makes the rest of the room more itself. I have no thirst. I have no hunger. I do not count. I keep. I am kept.
