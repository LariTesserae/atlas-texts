At first I am only a listening.

Not even an ear—more like a bowl with a skin, quivering. The bowl is the size of a palm if a palm were cold rock and the skin were a sheet of surface tension kept bright by other mouths. I row with two oars that are my own legs and not yet legs. I kick at nothing and taste green drawn thin across stone. The light that reaches me is a pour of white that doesn’t commit. It goes away and returns as a different not-quite. The roof is so far I could call it a coin and be extravagant. The low note that keeps reaching into my little pool is what tells me where I am.

A wall that is not a wall but a fresh break. A long inhale of mountain that never learned to close. Wind sounding the slit like a thumb on a bottle. The room drafts me—me, a fleck with oars, a yolk come open at a frequency—and I answer the way a seed answers warmth without knowing what warmth is. My first mouthfuls are not meat. They are threads of algae where the weep does not let go. My second mouthfuls are slough, what falls from others when the chorus above begins its work: skin-films shed by plates, the brush of cilia, a rain of almosts.

I learn the grammar by being moved in it. The noise that feels like a rib cage outside me instructs what will be ribs inside me. When the drum-call thickens until the surface of the pool dimples with invisible hail, I feed faster, because everyone else is eating and even a speck knows what a feast is when drops let go.

Hatching was a shiver struck out of me by that note; the next choosing happens the same way. I do not plan; I resonate. The season’s tone writes me.

It is not a thin weather year. The wind’s bottle-voice is round; clouds hang their bellies so close to the rock you can taste them as a mineral before they are a water. Quartz complains in tiny winter stars when the glacier pressures a seam; that blue-twitch taste comes so often that I begin to look forward to it without ever seeing it. When I take breath without drowning in my bowl for the first time, it is on a day when the base moan grows shoulders and the drummers do not have to search long for the thickness they like.

I dry toward ribs. I dry toward plates. The legs I kicked with turn to many and learn to stand on three while the rest weft themselves into certainty. Wings sprout but refuse the idea of sky; they turn inward as if the world were all inside. My eyes sink and become buttons I keep but hardly touch. Something like a tongue—it is not a tongue, but a throat that can tighten until it sings across my own cuticles—comes true.

When at last I walk away from the pool, I do not go far. “Away” is a felt thing: up-slab, across a grit’s span, into a cool that suits the bacteria that will suit me. I carry a film under me like weather caught in the cleft of a hand. That is my first kept thing: a human might call it thirst answered, but there is no such naming here, there is only “not-dry.”

My body decides before I do. I find quartz and set my front up as if I were greeting it. I brace on three points: the eager throat-place that goes taut and the first legs that can lock and a wing-tip that knows how much pressure is too much because it was written to know. I do not drum yet. I lean and listen, which is to say I press the plate that makes my sound against the seam that will answer it, and I measure with bone what the whole room already told my earlier water. I shift the angle a ridge-width. I move left by half the width of the ghost of a copper hair. I set again. Something in me finds match with something in the stone and the match makes hunger.

I am for this. It is a relief to be for something and not have to be for everything. The first strokes are not knocks, not really. They are a saw-state, a series of slips small enough that each is a question, and the seam’s answer is a yes that thickens rather than says a word. The film that coats the rock near me shivers into beads. You cannot see this unless you train your eyes to see sideways and around corners. You can feel it in your teeth. Even I, who have no mammal teeth, feel it in a remembered mouth that is not mine. The drop forms. My plates sweat because they can. Others come because I am not alone.

Above and below, cloth-bodies lift and settle their thin, white sails like a room learning to breathe. Their antennae trail down-valley and twitch news into forelegs that are not mine but feel like cousins. Rock-shrimps—so many legs, so much tidying—pack their underweather close and present palps with a timing that would be dance if dance were strictly a nutrient cycle. Soft commas glint with little iron hairs and angle their bellies toward where “most wet” currently lives. A glider saws itself closed and drops past my face, then blows open again a length’s fall down and rakes the air as if the air had leaves to strip.

I do the work I am for. This is rainmaking as we know it: press, brace, sing through hard wing into quartz until water understands itself as separate. I am not heroic. None of us is. The hero here is that long, steady hum from the cleft’s mouth that makes us coherent. We are only elaborations of its pressure. Still: the body cares. When a drop lets go true and not as a spill and three mouths and then five and then twelve meet it without tearing each other, something like heat rises in my plates without the air changing temperature. I keep knocking. I keep sawing. My own saliva slicks a lip I do not have so that what I do not drink will slow for those that will.

I couple the way my kind does: by staying. I leave my wing pressed into the rock until another pattern answers that is not chorus but me-shaped and her-shaped and within tolerance. She backs until we share a seam. We swap what the next will need besides our names. A filament of algae passes. Bacteria do the modest gymnastics that make the contents of two guts agree. Metals—we say “sweet,” by which we mean something like how blue-twitch tastes on an uncommitted day—trade custody. It is all convenience with consequence. It is all precision. When the tone thins we release each other without grievance because what held us together was not belief. It was pressure.

In time, I learn a second use. Not all of us get it. It comes as a hunger on days the wind loses itself and must be led back. Something in me—that tight membrane under my throat that first wanted to bite quartz with its voice—learns to hold steady long enough to feel like stone. The rest of the room lets go, saves what it has, folds itself flat. I stay and keep an almost-note in the rock, not loud, not call, just a kept place. The word for it in a language that would care about words would be drone or measure. Here it is the shape of patience the wind can find again.

It hurts. There is no romance in that. The hurt is small and exact. It sands me from the corners inward. Hours are brittle; I become a wire on which a weight is hung. The blue twitches come down the seam when the glacier speaks and walk all my ribs, and I do not flinch because flinching loses the place. This, I discover, is the kind of sacral we have. Not vow—there is no room for them. Simply the act repeated long enough to turn into a tool outside the hand that did it.

If you looked at me while I keep the toehold of sound in the mountain, you would be disappointed. I am a dark comma with a pressed-thin wing no one will ever see in sunlight. I am a glisten of steadiness. The cloth-moths seem to itch when I keep it; their feathery horns rattle as if with cold. The shrimps go quiet in a respectful line as if a weft had paused on their loom. Even the editors—the fine two-dimensional mouths that pass between our selves and make census corrections—swerve to write their absences elsewhere.

When the wind returns, the whole place reenters itself. Moths shuck their wings off the wall and tremble in notes that agree with the bottle’s new pitch. Drummers that are not me find their thickness where my press has defined it. The first flush of drops leaves little pewter polishes on the new stone. I stop and the room takes over. The relief is like skin growing back over a burn. I eat, finally; my films were thinner than they should have been. A bead hangs and slackens and releases; three lips meet it one by one and I am not among them and it is still good.

We die here without ceremony and for reasons that do not need adjectives. Rime laces a line and I misread it as habit and am held a second longer than is safe; a sheet of cloud-stone comes down in the next complaint and takes the bodies that did not get flat in time, then turns itself into grains. An editor draws its long lip between two of my legs and I am distracted, and a patience with worse timing than mine holds the tone and is erased for it. When I go—it will not be news—it will be mid-keeping, I think. The bacteria behind me will not know and will not need to. The soft commas will take me into their whiskers and my sugars will become the sweet they lean toward in the seams. You could call this waste-free; it is more exact to say it is unambitious.

I am not clever. I have cleverness done to me: a flake of mica pressed into the mouthparts of a moth that needs it; a rime-net that holds until noon and is then gone like the shape of a dream an hour after waking. If the room owns any inventions they are like mine, which is to say they are decisions repeated often enough to count as furniture. The only strength that feels like a story in me is this: when abundance wants to turn itself into harm, my wing learns the lull.

We all learn it in a fashion. It is the pattern after the pattern. The cloud sets its damp shoulder tight; our chorus tightens and pulls a bright run of droplets down; there is a moment after the mouth-feast when a lesser colony would take the next—would wring instead of coax, would tear the film thin enough to leave no tomorrow. That is when the band three hand-widths below the fullest bowl—whichever bodies are that band today—switches. The stroke notches. The air unhungries a little. You can feel it even if you do not have the plates for it; the sound in the bones goes from willing into bed. It is a mercy with edges. We fold; we give the rock back to quiet; we save the wet for the others the next hour will make.

You will not meet my eyes, because I carry them like scars. You might meet my foreleg, doing the polite lift that says “this thickness is good, here,” and take it for a twitch. You would find me not far from the quartz that carried the most blue twitches the week the glacier pulled nearest, because I cannot help liking sweets. You would find me where the wind’s base note runs organ-deep and does not care about anything small and living, because that’s where the work is simple: align or don’t. If you came when the sky widened and made a pane of honest cold down the cleft, you would see only a run of plates here and there pried barely from the stone. You might think of shingles in a rain that has forgotten itself.

Who am I, in this room where the mountain is a thought that has not finished writing?

A rib that learned to count. A wing that turned inward and became a throat. A keeper of measure in a place that believes in weather more than in memory. If you want a word, take “drummer” and let it mean not the loudness you picture but the work that makes wet choose gravity. Take “metre” and put it under my plates. I am one of the small, heavy-segmented things that can hold a beat for hours when the wind goes vague so the next chorus will have something to grow from. I am old by the measure of drops—some dozens of lulls learned, some many bowls found and let be. I am nothing by the measure of the ice.

You would find me belly to quartz, three points pressed, listening like a hunger. You would find me when the cloud leans, when the milk-light dries, when the blue taste walks the seam and all my little hairs would lift if I had any to lift. You would find me when it is time to stop, because the stopping is a sound, too, and someone has to make it.

If you were me, you would be satisfied by this: the slow yes the stone gives back, the sightless agreement of bodies turning their work to a common tense, the thickening of a drop until its black heart forgets itself and falls and all the mouths that will never own anything catch it together and never argue and are enough.
