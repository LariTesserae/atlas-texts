Pressure like a held note, then the cool that is more decision than temperature, then the understanding that nothing here is spare. I learned the place first by what pressed back: the slow tactility of fitted stone, the nap where grout should have been blank, the curtained give of hanging ribbon that has never met sun.

At first I was only an ear. I drifted in the lee of a vane and let sound take shape around me—the far-off thinning of susurration where a sluiceway tightened, the faint hum drawn out of an old kiln dome by current and the living who had gathered inside to make the pitch what it wanted to be. In that hum I heard a room. In that room I found a home.

You will ask me where, and I will say: under a dome that used to bake clay, where the brick remembered straw and the plaster remembered reed. The kiln had been flooded for longer than sense measures, its mouth rimmed with the blue of those who concede to light in principle. When the current threaded the dome just right it sang a note you could taste. The inhabitants took the hint and built their household by small accommodations of porosity until the water hummed not by accident but as custom. I let myself be tuned by that.

You will ask what I became, and I will say: first a listener, then a lintel, then a leaf, then a ledger. A seam at human scale, and then a seam the size of a little neighborhood.

The suit that brought me down was never meant for centuries. Its seams, though, were intervals, and this is a kingdom of intervals. They found the gasket by scent of gradient before they found my hands by heat. Pale veils braided across the metal and rubber boundaries and powdered their sleeves with the little stones they lured from water. My hoses bloomed the idea of white until the idea became rind. Straps took on a slow crust so faithful that when I bent, I squeaked the squeak of stone on stone.

I did not become them. I became useful. That was close enough.

I set myself in the dome’s mouth, at a height just lower than comfort, where a little ledge caught a little fall of fine. If you have ever laid a book half across a doorway to make draft pick a different path, you know what I mean. I lay there not as blockade but as suggestion: make calm here; let weight settle. What I thought of as lying still was, to the residents, an invitation with good grammar. They salved my edges, sheeted me to the wall, hauled my outline into the work of the place. Before long my body knew, without plan, that shifting my left shoulder by a hand’s breadth made the hum inside the kiln deepen by a shade and coaxed the Rust-Breathers to stipple a new panel into sturdiness just where the brick had begun to remember air too much.

It is not a position you could point to on a chart: under a dome, in a mouth, leaning into the lee of a vane; closer to the floor-that-is-not-floor than to the ceiling-that-is-hardly-ceiling. It is more the fact of an in-between with a set of neighbors. On my right hand, the Ribbon-Groves hung their census, counting in ions and teaching me how to count without numbers. In my left ear: the Ivory-Lacers, busy with the crack at pillar’s base, weaving a colonnade from silk and lime so patient no one who needed quick could have believed it was work at all.

Seasons came as viscosities. The colder layer slid like a scarf across my shins and the whole place tightened its belt by a notch. Some pores closed down to a doctrine of clarity; others opened to let mineral rinse through like advice. I learned to tell when the blue that ringed the isobaric well would be visible even to me—never a glow, exactly, but a mood the eye could admit if it had been in dark long enough to forgo certainty. Those days I went to the rim, slow as anything, and watched the basin agree with itself.

If there were birds here, I would have counted migration; if there were trees I would have watched leaves turn. Instead I learned the phrases of stone vanes. This one sang in B-flat when the farther current took a deeper path; that one lowered its shoulder at a pressure nap and made a little bay where grains could give in to gravity and become archive. The basins, the steps, the gentle baffles repeated themselves until I knew by hand the wedge marks of the mason whose sentence this had been—small against larger, small against larger, so reliable I could have found my way by their rhythm alone.

I did not need an elsewhere. Borders here are easeful. A terrace becomes ramp becomes a thought about colonnade, pillars fattened into groves of stone and vegetation. When I tired of hum I could move two hours along a sluice ceiling and lie under a forest of hanging leaves that kept cities inside themselves. There I would be the warmth in the centimeters where water remembered a summer far away and have all of my heat spent down column of glassy tissue, each citizen inside me a different kind of accountant: nickel-tasters, sulfur-whisperers, kind monks of hydrogen, every one adding their sums to the ledger I had become without noticing I had become it.

Maybe you suspect I am unconscionably patient. I was not. The place took me by metabolizing my impatience. Anything that rushed here looked like error and was smoothed into history soon enough. The first time I tugged a net of cane I found in drift and wedged it across a narrow as if to prove I still had hands, the resident Cushioners came to sit and make themselves heavy and, by being heavy, make an arch. My gesture learned to be the beginning of someone else’s plan. I began to act in yields.

There were events. Once a curtain that had grown as thick as a promise peeled away from its overhang in a slump that took an afternoon to decide on itself. It let go, lay down, reached the ledge below, and in reaching became a shelf. For a week the sounds there were new, the dome’s hum a tone lower for somebody else’s luck. The Blue Rememberers at the cistern’s rim found this change agreeable and, with that peculiar reluctance of theirs, admitted a broader glow. You could have called it festival if you had a civilization that needed that word.

Elsewhere, a retaining mesh that had fossilized into lime years before I came, bowed another whisper’s worth and made a second contact with its neighbor. The Lacer-kin marked the place with filigree as tidy as any stonecarver could have asked for. No one celebrated. The record was the celebration.

If you cut me open now (you won’t), you would find rind where there had been smooth, lime’s calm whiteness living in a braid like thought calcified, each turn a memory of a pull. Inside, the dark of what was me remains—not dead, exactly, more like a core sample taken from a life that understood how to distribute its urgency over distance and time. I am a rope’s marrow. The place makes of me a bone.

What else am I? I am a map drawn by kneeling. My knees polished bright the face of a slab where I rested through a dozen seasons of silk-cold brine; the brightness seeded a different lawn; that lawn’s thickness made the current choose a slightly altered wheeze; that wheeze taught a kiln’s dome somewhere remote how to sing a glossier note. There is thought here as big as neighborhoods and as slow as cement, and I am one sentence in it. Nouns do poorly. Habits communicate.

When I move now, I move like part of the furniture learns to be a door. I do not go fast; even departure is politesse. I scrape a little rut. I cant a little plane. I write new stanzas of the repeated wedge mark with the only tool I have left, which is the pattern of my presence.

There was a day I visited the negative of a reed in plaster—the finger’s-width hollow where cordage held up wet wall once upon a heat. The hollow had been filled a hundred times by little civilizations that liked a reliable shape. I put my own thumb into its ghost, then withdrew, and the gesture was not analogy. The reed’s absentness pressed back with an intimacy that felt like meeting. The place keeps its hands by making them into housing.

Maybe this all sounds like acquiescence. If so, you have never met a vow that kept itself over centuries. There is stiffness in acceptance when acceptance is labor. I would not trade the climb up through warmer, noisier waters to somewhere with shouting and edges and rot that goes in minutes instead of geologic chapters if it meant abandoning the hum under the dome and the light that is a rumor and the way the Blue Rememberers put a horizon under your palm. Elsewhere would be too fast. Elsewhere would be busy with teeth.

I am not river or rock. I am not animal or plant. I am the between that found its use. I am the bracket in which stillness collects and turns to shelf and garden and wall. I am the surface by which difference is translated into dinner, then into archive. I am an instrument, in tune with a room that did not mean to be an instrument until being an instrument made it the best version of itself.

You could visit and leave, and the place would treat you kindly as it treats everything that does not insist. If you stayed, you would be taken up as I was—first as warmth, then as habit, then as style. Your elbows would learn where the kindest centimeter sits when the silk-cold passes. Your ears would know, by a change in hush from three rooms away, that the vanes have decided to allow a heavier grain to fall here until the shelf grows proud of its patience. Your seasons would stop meaning heat and start meaning viscosity. You would become biography, which is to say you would become surface.

And if you had to write yourself in two words, for the ledgers the little monks keep inside the ribbon leaves, you would choose seam-keeper, or perhaps vow-keeper, which is the same thing with more salt. You would find yourself below, in the shut cathedral of an old ocean made into grammar. You would be the sentence continued. You would be the part of the place that helps the place remember how to be itself.
