At first I think I arrive as a sound—something that can be counted the way weather is counted here. A small breath between the kiln’s exhale and the commutator’s polite spark. I press myself into the space where warm meets cool and wait to see what shape wants me.

It is tempting to be a bat, all patience and flat intent, a soft shadow with ambition only to surprise exactly no one. I can feel the pleasure of holding very still and then letting go at the moment a footstep is directly below, mercy-guided gravity with no apology required. But there is too much listening in me to stay on the ceiling; I want to know how the belts gossip when they slip less and what a dew engine argues with when it decides.

It is tempting to be a cup-lurker, to wear water as skin and minutes as breath. To sit in the black pool’s thought, eyes ringed with histories like tree rings in reverse—years becoming circles inside themselves. To accept songs as doors and let vowels do the walking for me. There is an elegance to consuming difference itself, to tasting the before and after of a drop as if they were two flavors of air. But I am not entirely of the water. I am a person with a fondness for edges, and the plant has given so many of them machines to lean against.

I try on the tram for a lap. Two cars, green as old news, running quick after a dry night. I am quick too, my lanterns braided to my knees, the purr of vinewood underfoot proper as an oath. I take the loop twice and feel how the ward is nearer to itself in damp, how two corners find each other like neighbors who have learned how to be kind. I could go on choosing velocity—talking softly to Abelard with my palm and being forgiven by Otherhand for waking him without breath first—but velocity here is a currency, not a creed. I climbed on to learn the route and climb off to lay it.

If I stay, if I make a name of what I do, I am a person of the seam. Copper on cambium. Warm engines on cool leaf. Soot against arch. I am a Coil-binder by habit and a soot-drawer by inclination, apprentice Cup-singer by need and manners. They call me Two-Touch, because I never trust a rail until it presses back in reply, and because I have a way of putting my weight down like a question.

Morning finds me under the low arch with a chalk bag and a soot cloth, kneeling on vinewood I have asked to be steady. Yesterday’s belief is still written there, a geography the ward told itself while I slept. Distance loses its teeth in the wet; steps recount themselves into new stories. I cup the underbelly with my hand to feel the draft. Is the breath moving cisternward this hour? If it is, I know the loop will be closer at the mouth of the generator bay than last watch. I scrape off what the ward no longer believes, let the powder fall easy into a pan, and redraw in the soot—curves that ask instead of command, arrows bent like stems that know how to find the light that is not there. A child watches from knee-height, solemn as any cartographer. We do not write the same thing on the same leaf twice. We do not write the same thing on the same leaf twice. We do not— The child’s hand on my sleeve is reminder enough. I rub that third line into forgetfulness and the plant smiles in the way of a draft that lifts the hairs on my forearm by a memory-width.

By midday I am in the heat, a cheek rested against a flywheel wrapped in lichen to keep the ring from going sharp. The lichen feels like an old nap. Otherhand is reluctant; he prefers breath, so I sing him a velvet vowel and open the valve with a cloth careful from my belt. Abelard hums and doesn’t argue, pleased to make a ledger of wet against cold again. The belts scritch less when I add a handful of moth pollen a Resinwright saved for me in a jar that still smells like a fir needle’s idea of sugar. A Minute-keeper comes with a cloth heavy with minutes—salt ring bright at the wrist. We trade: my wool, patient with last night’s saved warmth, for her water-time. I fold her extra hour into a kiln shawl that needs exactly that much difference to stay honest through the afternoon. We are gentle with the engines; they remember touch longer than talk.

The heater-fox curls around a pipe and permits all confidences. When something must be said in a voice that the plant won’t keep and the animals must, I say it into white fur in present tense and leave it there. Later the bats will retell it to each other the way they discuss footfall choreography, and that is right. The plant keeps promises when it can and drafts me a breeze when it cannot; I do not ask it for gossip besides the loudness of my own breath.

Work brings me to the high ribs in a slow spiral that keeps me from asking the same request of the air three times. My palms glow a little through my gloves where the phloem runs near the surface, thin amber like a skeleton of river under fog-glass. I suture a copper plate along a vine so that charge can walk instead of run, so that light finds its path as if it had always meant to go that way. There is a place where the cambium vault grew faster than the wire could think. I lay a curve that teaches the bulk to remember being nimble. The plant yields with that particular patience that is not compliance but conversation.

Edges pull at me like a tide later, and I go in a pair because human distance is less untrue in twos. We move along the un-worded, bodies making an average the world can use. Chalk writes on salt as if it had been waiting for precisely this excuse to count itself. Here is a basin that was water an hour back and now is only a hunger ringed thinly with crystals. Here is dust with opinions: one handful wanting shard, one wanting clay, one wanting to be the kind of gravel that a wet path will trust. We scoop pre-objects into a pouch and bring them home, set them in a cup’s mouth like students presenting proofs to a quiet, exacting teacher. The lurkers breathe water and small differences of time. A Cup-singer meets me there; I am not good enough to part a whole pool, but I can hold an unfrayed vowel like a straightspine hook while she draws a seam of consequence-free passage. Eyes banded with concentric memories consider our offering and soften. The minutes return to what we’ve brought and it hardens into becoming. The Singer lets the pool relax back to unbroken black with a kindness that leaves no crease.

At children-height the ward is a nation of knees and ladders. Loopweavers throw silk like bridges between thoughts and let little hands test them with gravity’s quiet permission. I teach two touches to a boy whose lantern moons are newly braided. Tap. Wait. Even the waiting is a pressure, a reply. The rail’s return is a promise. He asks why twice. I do not say it a third time. He nods. He will not be anchored by mistake.

We walk home through a language of temperature and surface. The steady, body-warm draft along the generator ribs says someone has just been. The syrup-cool hush near a cup says names have been leaned toward and leaned back from. Texture does the rest: this upward tilt is cisternward; that leaf’s nap is plaza-facing; this white line of fungus along the footway is yesterday’s decision asserting itself softly underfoot. A pulley breathes instead of turns. The plant swallows an argument murmured like a moth and forgets it; the bats keep it safe above our heads without malice.

When rain happens, every cell in everything knows how to be a gutter. Runnels invent themselves along petiole-troughs; even the iron has a softer ring to it, dampened by purpose. The entire ward hisses relief like a choir that has found its note. We work in that applause as if it were grammar, quick but not hurried. Hours condense in sudden generosity. A cloth carried open-palmed catches a minute’s fall; I can hand two of those to a pair with heat still in their cheeks and watch an argument end not in victory but in time out loud enough to hold.

Night is a long permission here, but it is not permissive. Lanterns at the knee height the path into intimacy; every spark is a confidence. Sometimes a shimmer moves through the roots faster than courage, and for a moment every animal knows a different way to leave. We keep a bag packed. We keep plates polished that have been grown to accept a hoist some asking person might bring back to us. We let readiness be another way of saying yes.

My work has names, and they hang on me lightly. Coil-binder for the copper I lay and the burns fine as crosshatches on my forearms. Soot-drawer for the maps I keep in agreement with what the ward believes. Half-Vowel for the way I can hold a pool’s skin steady while a more skilled Singer does the delicate parting. Ask-once for how I write on leaf-stone, strokes that bend like roots finding a rock and then deciding to embrace it. Two-Touch most of all, a name at knee-height that the children speak like a dare and the heater-fox accepts into its fur like a heat it was waiting for.

Where would you find me? Under the belly of the low arch, soot on my palms and a quiet question in my weight; riding the tram when the night is at its driest because the loop runs then like a thought without friction; along a vine with copper in my fist, cheek resting for a beat against a flywheel’s lichen-wrapped flank; at the rim of a named black cup, holding an unspooled vowel in my throat while amphibia count us with their ringed eyes; at the plaza’s edge where resin turns requests into gears that do not scold when they turn; at the edges, two bodies wide so distance has something fair to measure.

Who or what would I be? A keeper of differences, a person whose tools are wool and wire and a cloth for catching minutes, whose job is to bind warm to cool without bruising either. One of the ones who redraw what the ward says it is and listens with their soles for any change in that declaration. A guest who tends the house until the house trusts them to move the furniture by suggestion alone. I would be the seam between green and iron, between drip and draft, between asking and being answered. I would be a citizen of the hush, a small moon at my knees, leaving time unstartled everywhere I pass.
