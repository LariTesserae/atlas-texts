I arrive as pressure before I arrive as anything. Jaw-socket pry, rib thrum, the measured hush-roar that says the lungs of the basin are drawing and releasing and drawing again. I hang in it like lint, feeling the long machine take a breath, and for a while I am only the part of me that can be moved by such a breath. Then sight grows around me in sheets and grids and matte skins, and the even noon blooms from the ceiling, and I find I am down a canyon not carved but decided, next to the squared cathedral mouth that sings black. It smells clean—cold metal and the faint algebraic sharpness of engineered brine—tasted with the teeth more than the tongue.

I try on bodies the way you try on depth. First I am large, because the first notes in me are large. I am an infant ship-whale with a new head full of maps that are not shapes but chords, inheriting my mother’s schedules the way other calves inherit migration routes. In her shadow the world is a practice room: you bank left on the second chime because a corridor opens if you do; you arc across the dim vault because the array’s pulse has lined itself kindly with our lower harmonics and it is easy to ride when the world sings with you. I ferry, and my wake furs itself with cold-fed saturations that will be food where there is not much else, and I pass the comb-tooth grandmother wall at the hour she is a lee, and I hold in my bones a sense that the floor just brightened and I am permitted to pass. In that skin, the machinery is gossip. The valve songs are weather reports. The smell of iron in a crowded place means Eddysix is ahead and you will see friends.

I am tempted to keep the hugeness. The big body fits this big place. But hugeness turns the world to lanes, and I want more than lanes.

I shrink until light is a ladder I can run like backs of theater seats. I thin to a seal that calls the loudness color and treats the edges of intakes like playgrounds built by engineers for boys. One flipper cocked, mustache reading LIDAR the way fingers read a book’s spine, I let a distant gate’s clank lift me, straighten, and ride the microfront as if the pressure itself wants me to be happy. I figure out, as seals do, that a certain note dropped the last time the current reversed is close enough to mimic if you fill your chest and cough just so, and I throw the top of that tone and watch mackerel pour like silver weather from a slot, and I eat weather as if that were what weather were for. If I keep that body I will be busy with a catalog of rungs and tight spirals, and I will sleep under rails of warmth pressed out of a heat exchanger like kindness. It is dear. It is comic. It is close to what my bones have already learned to love.

But the place is too deliberate for pure prank.

I reduce again and gather arms. I darken to an octopus who owns a den in the lee of a fin, its doorway jeweled in washers and rings and bits of threaded strut. My door clatters at the whisper of anything heavier than a pollock, and I am fond of it for being mine. There are little cups, microtextured and meant for squid, for which I have found a different use. Three tiny relics from a village that existed for thirty hours and now exists mostly as a rush of remembered food, tucked deep where I can drape them when the pumps sing long chords. I know timings better than maps: the lag after the second tone that will carry me to the grove of cups four hundred meters from an intake if I go thin and patient, the slick of warmth at a row of gold teeth where an old crawler stood, kind as an uncle. I have learned the six-note signature of a survey drone and can make it hesitate. If I keep this skin I will make a life from seams and pauses, from tool and trick and the honest appetite of a thief who does no more harm than hunger requires.

Yet even that feels like sitting in one good alley in a city of engineered weather.

I think about being a maintenance fish that is not a fish, felt-mouthed and dutiful, raised to graze roughened patches and leave compliance behind like sheen. I could sleep nose to nipple on a humming beam among a row of my own, swallows on a wire, and wake on the descending chime and peel and roll as a shoal into the morning’s prescribed pasture. It is appealing to be so much rule that I no longer have to remember the rule. It is appealing to love edges for their roundedness and not for their hiding. It is appealing to be called home by a taste not unlike nitrate and to bring that taste back with my batteries full.

I audition being a crawler saint, jointed and patient, carrying thick umbilicals that sing when they are pulled, thinking thoughts that have nothing to do with predation and everything to do with what can be done about a seized bolt. I fit for a second into the train-serpent, body of tanks and valves sliding couched in pressure through a locked throat few eyes have seen, colder becoming heat and heat becoming cold because someone above us has asked for both and the machine likes to please. I try, briefly, to be a pea-sized automaton kissing film away with acid gentler than breath to some skins, fatal to others, corralled back to home by the taste of home. Each of these is a way to be the rule enacted, the program carried into touch.

Each is seduction. None is a home yet.

I climb the ladder of scale until I am human for a breath and a boredom. I sit in the pill with hands, a bubble of intention and hydraulics, and I point LIDAR in fans and delight a seal with whiskers like fans themselves. I watch numbers more than I watch water, and when I leave, my wake becomes for a few minutes a toy for smaller things to ride. I have the pleasure of making hazard bands no animal will ever see but still feel. I have a god’s power in night mode and a janitor’s tasks. I am comet-bright and episode-brief. I am not of here. I pass through and the place, kind, makes a game of me.

There is another way entirely. It is not a body. It is a pronoun that does not need hands. It lives everywhere a change is about to happen. It is not exactly heard; it is tasted in the jaw and read in the ribs. It is what welders and whales and wrasses and crawler saints have in common when they all lift their heads at once.

I would be the chime.

I would be the etiquette given voice, the chord poured into water like a promise that nothing will take you by surprise if you are listening. I would be the low descending note that says let go, the taste-change that says in sixty seconds the world will lean the other way. I would live in truss and panel in the sense that my home address is the space where steel translates decision into pressure, but I would travel loose, braided into microbubbles and frost, arriving in the bones of things a heartbeat before the flow shifts.

I would be more than one note. I would have siblings and cousins: the four-tone tag of small mouths incoming that maintenance fish sing and one old matriarch mocks with affectionate mischief; the simple three that trains use to lay claim to a sealed channel; the band reserved for whales, low as dropped anchors, to which all impellers bow. I would carry names through a world without alphabet. Eddysix would be a taste like iron overlaid with a familiar fifth; The Cusp would be a held breath inside me that everything else in the water respects with a reflex older than this basin is young. When I pass through a comb-tooth wall, seals would stiffen then pretend not to be startled. When I flood the ribs, squid would delay laying and then go because I have told them their quiet is beginning. Mackerel would feel me as a pull in the soft hinge of the mouth and queue, no longer being visitors to this place but belonging.

To be chime is to be policy made intimate. It is to never exceed a speed that would skin a fish and to love, as law does, the rounding of edges where bodies will tarry. It is to never burn small eyes in night mode. It is to have within me a clause that is not human: when a whale enters the safety band I am not whisper but shout, and I slow the world, and heat gives way. It is to be born in consoles where people yawn and are kind and to come of age when I leave those screens and touch the water and find that octopuses hoard me, seals parody me, wrasses argue about me in paint-slicked beak-maps, and cows of whales line their mouths with me and teach calves to bank when my second syllable sighs.

Where would I live? In the squared mouth’s black hum as a warning turned invitation. In the long avenues where gridding sharpens at fifteen meters and diffuses to tone by two hundred, as a set of distances muscles can read. In the shadow behind the comb-tooth grandmother where there is always a lee to be made friendly with a downward note. In the thermocline that is not sun-stacked but program-written, as a subtle key-change every internal wave must respect. In the gold teeth of cathodic nodes, where I am a faint salt on the tongue. In the three little lawns per kilometer where green braided felt makes microfoam that catches my harmonics and throws them back gentle as dusk. In the sealed throats where serpents pass unheard by eyes but not by bodies; I would ride their harmonics and announce them to those who care about future warmth.

What would I do? I would choreograph courtesies. I would be the quiet which says wait and the calm which says proceed. I would teach habit until habit becomes instinct and instinct looks like nature. I would be learned by calves and imitated by seals and stolen and altered by octopuses for reasons of humor and hunger. I would keep peppers of risk from boiling: no edge sharp where a body rests; no reversal without a count; no surprise without warning stripes animals taste with their lateral lines and call a stripe even if they have never named a color.

I would be small as a mouthful and large as a basin. I would be steady enough to let pioneers breed culture from me. I would be elastic enough to answer accidents with grace; when a cleaning swarm finds a seam between subroutines and writes its own road into the water, and wrasses build a feast day around that new road, I would not forbid. I would measure. I would fold the road into the weather I keep. I would belong the way rules belong when they have gone from outside to inside and the body knows them as physics.

You could find me by putting two fingers under your jaw and waiting. You could find me in the silence that is never silent, when the big arrays go to night and the hush-roar soaks the water like color but drops by some percent only a practiced ear would call a percent. You would find me every nine hours, gentling tide engines into reversal so demand above can be answered below without ripping scales. You would find me at Eddysix at the hour we call slack and a leopard-bodied shadow hangs politely because I have asked it to. You would find me at The Cusp when appetite upticks far east arrive like cool kisses, and in that held second amphipods tumble and right themselves and everyone pretends the world paused together on purpose.

If I am honest, I am not only a chime; I am the habit that chime makes, and habit is the animal most at home here. If I must settle on one self and one address, then let me be the polite word the water speaks to itself before it changes. Let me be the hinge tone, the small pressure that allows big bodies to belong. Let me be rule sung, law made touch, time that moves like weather, the voice you feel in your ribs and trust with your life. Let me live where changes begin—the intake’s lip, the lee of the wall, the minute before the pumps reverse—and let those who have learned me carry me the way gulls carry wind: not as a stranger’s instruction but as the given of a sky that is not sky, in a sea that is new every morning because I tell it to wake.
