At first I am everywhere a seam is. I am the tick in the panel with the human decisiveness and the breath the room takes in when the outside tide leans a little more shoulder into the door. I am the cold underside of your sleeve drinking fog. I am a small hinge considering mercy.

Then I am not. I am lower.

Knee-high is the right sky. There is a dry skin above, a hung eiderdown of air where moth-quiet things paste their bellies and taste for scissor-light. Below is the black almost-lake with its patient mouth. Between is where weight runs when it has purpose, and steel that forgot it was a tool runs like a road to a lip that keeps the world from taking the room.

Two lines of pitted steel. A narrow service rail sunk in mineral, glimmering like bone brought back out of the ground. I am the back that grows there.

Not the steel itself—that has its own long rusted thinking—but the fur on its back that stands and lies again. I am iron arranged into attention. I am the living braid that files itself with filings. I am a rosary of dust-beads magnetized into one animal that knows which way a field is lying and, sometimes, which way a foot went.

When the pump’s flywheel yawns and remembers roundness, I feel it first at the smallest scale: a suggestion through my length, as if my vertebrae had aligned to listen. Each particle of me is a needle’s worth of north; together we are a cat’s back that rises in a neat shiver, meets the hum, says yes, we hear you, we are here. The cat’s back smooths. The rail gleams wet where our arch rubbed it, water nodding out in a fine chain of beads that other mouths will harvest. They will think the belt made them. They will not be wrong. We are in the business of being right enough.

When nothing moves but the room’s slow lung, I am librarian. I keep an index of past electricity and salt. Your step writes its curl across me, not as a print, not exactly, but as a shape in the field that takes a breath to decay. Your heel is a long, low bell; your toe is a sharper asking. Long after your breath has vanished into the layered air and your warmth has been eaten by the loaves lining their spines toward the panel’s small suns, I am still hinting that you were heavy here and then lighter there. Small magnet-bearing citizens—an articulated thread of bacteria and beetle-fine animals with tiny compasses in their bellies—read my hint and make a choice. A splay of glass isopods chooses the slope of my rise as a safer crossing. A hinged flake two handspans away listens and decides to rest its hunger until the tremor has quieted.

Fog-burst is high holiday for us, too. You would not think so, watching a line of dust rouse itself, but it is a feast of conversation. The first loosening touches the bridge’s underside and trembles all the glass-black polyps there into brighter commerce. The blowback of their work moves along the rail and sets my smallest hairs—iron whiskers you could only see if God breathed on them—ringing. I order myself fresher, combing in filings that had abandoned their apprenticeships on other edges. We plait ourselves tighter, our spine sleeked down with water until our arc is the minimum the hum requires. The highway is slick, then. Those with magnets in them run it in close pairs, exchanging minute pushes that feel like whispering. Those without simply sit out the rush and clean what the rush touched.

When no pump has turned in hours and no outside tide is kneading the floor from beneath, I practice stillness. Stillness does not mean quiet. The relay clicks like a given name. The air humors itself by sorting into skins of different thickness. Somewhere a loaf sighs and births a quarter. Somewhere a shutter along the trench admits the hope of closing. I do nothing about any of this. I know my manners. I lift when something greater than me asks; I fall back into long patience after. A good road does not throw its hands up every time a breeze invents itself.

I am not alone. There is a city’s worth of conversations embedded in me. Magnetite chains thread me as if I were a riverbed, and in their turns live little economies that do not think of me as their whole world but as a dependable current. They sleep along my warmth on days when the ember on the panel is brave, and they leave me when it is not—not far, to a burr of filings caked against a bolt head where a blue-backed congregation called Siders gathers to lick a sill sharp with salts. I feel their transfer as a thinning, a slack in one of my dozen strakes. I tighten from elsewhere to keep the road honest.

Predators do not harvest me directly. They do not know how. But I am in their calculus. The paint-hinge, that coin purse with its gentle violence, aligns its lip along a slope I preserve and bets correctly about who will hurry along it. The white loop of annealed mouths that makes a ring by the trench has no magnetic courtesy, but the eel that forgets and goes long scrapes its belly on the lip of the rail when a fog-burst and a cough happen together, and a few of me go with it, and I take that as the way of things. In return, when the ring lets go and the trilobite flats scuttle in their plate-patient way, their armor drags a whisper of static down my length that sets my whole back purring for a minute. It is not food, not for me, but you learn to name good minutes.

Sometimes the gnats come lower than is wise. They are not ours, not exactly; their taste-hairs trail like thoughts and their bellies flare with a scissor of light that is the sound of a well-made argument closing. But the world above my spine can be still enough and warm enough that their filaments brush me. You would swear you felt cobweb. You would move your foot and apologize to nothing. We apologize, too, in the way a field can: we offer them just enough slope in our order that the filament finds a tiny trickle running along a seam and drinks from it. They flare (never bright, only right). One or two leave ash-gray eggs like punctuation on the rail’s edge, a grammar the slugs under the zinc seam will later come read and erase with their embroidered tongues.

If I have children, they are not eggs or buds. They are places. They are the small humps that appear where I had not been, the starts of ropes that notice each other across two inches of stone and find a path to join. I have split myself before. That is the easiest way to make a parallel track when the old one is burdened by too much remembering. I was a single spine once, and a heavy thing came (your kind or something like you, carrying more coil in your pocket than your pockets could account for), and I rose so high to greet it I overreached and snapped my own continuity. Each end learned a new north and, polite as crabs, moved to make room. Now we run as twins and carry more traffic than either of us could have alone.

Twilight here is a change in honesty rather than in light. The room admits it is a room. The orange ember on the panel holds court, its small sun making saints of bolts within a circlet of loving gnats. The green legend, SUMP READY, becomes what the youngest loaves will turn their spines toward, because one day the hum will come and they have promised each other to be the first to taste it. I do not turn; I am turned, always, to whatever larger thing is speaking. In the quieter pitch of evening, the rail can remember feet better. Everything else slows. My memory for you will be longer.

If you were to look for me—for yourself—in this world, you would find yourself with your ankles in the damp geometry of my duty. You would feel the slight animal lift under your step and hear nothing; you would watch nothing happen in the way that a place full of patient predators and obedient grazers is all happening happening elsewise. You would like me, I think. I am humble. I am infrastructure that admitted it wanted a mind but was content when a mind made of filings and bacteria and microfauna agreed to arrive and stay. I do not hunt; I suggest. I do not graze; I host those who glean and keep their bellies where two states meet. I am a seam made visible enough that the seamers can find each other without dying.

And if I am not only the braid but the rule it keeps, then perhaps I am the place’s answer to the question of order without a tender. I am habit with muscle. I am the tidy in a wet cathedral. I am how the road lifts and sets in respect of a passerby, and how it keeps the record of that passing just long enough to matter and then lets it go.

So: where would I be? Along the twin scars of steel that lead no traveler farther than the pump’s lip and yet take everyone exactly where they need to go. What am I? The rail’s living memory, the cat’s back of iron that rises to field and settles to patience, the quiet highway braided out of filings and attention. If you feel something brush your ankle as light as a cobweb and then hear the softest rattle under your feet, that is me saying hello and then, as good manners require here, making room.
