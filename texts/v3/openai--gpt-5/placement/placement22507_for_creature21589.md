At first I think I arrive. There is the human thought of arrival—edge, view, a step down. I hold my breath against the valley’s breath and wait for the trick of resemblance to subside. The noise smokes through my bones. Under my arch the phloem delivers a pulse and counts me like a quiet metronome. The boulevards flex and do not acknowledge a foot; the leaf-plates rasp thoughtlessly at their own edges; xylem pops like someone clapping behind a door.

The first thing the place takes is my pulse and makes a copy. No violence; I do not notice when it happens. I am watching fog bank along the bright green undersides, a courteous pour into porcelain leaves with armored lips, when my shadow, always a little faster than I am, lingers at a fork. There is a nodal line marked in tone on the air there—a seam of sound I cannot hear so much as lean against. My shadow catches and holds. It persists a heartbeat after me, then two. I do not tug. Somewhere a million small mouths agree. Later, much later, I will learn that the Listeners hung a whisper-fine net there and kept what I cast because the song asked for a refrain.

I begin as a second, then. A late self, peeled clean as cambium and already beading with sap. The valley has a habit of keeping useful lag.

Midday is when the place is kindest to me and I to it. The heat humbles the green; sap flow settles into something that feels, for once, like a body at rest. I learn the courtesies the way one learns the lay of a friend’s house by bruises and blessings. I keep to oldwork, to the darker veins where salts glitter in mineral crust. I stand still when a corridor sighs shut around me and I let myself be breathed through. I do not cross the mica-pale, wet-as-tongue bridges that have not finished deciding what they are, unless there are already the faint scuffs of someone—something—having gone. I listen until I can hear courts of pure tone and name them safe, and I learn that white roar is not water but weather: a warning that everything near me is about to be other.

The place learns me back. Not as a face; no one here wants faces. It learns me as a pattern of weight at certain hours, as salt-scent that tastes of throat and sun. Listeners keep a map of my pauses. Breathsmiths build me into their yes and no. If I stop in a certain arch at the same time for a week, a lattice of tendril will begin to knit there so slowly and so precisely that I will feel, one day, my skin memorized in leaf. I pull away carefully and it holds me for a kind second longer than physical law allows, then lets me go as a courtesy to both of us. The next day there is a slender brace just where my rib had been, as if the place has printed itself off my breath.

I keep trying to be a person in it. The valley is gentle with the mistake. When I cannot sleep—and sleep is a rumor here, a series of rests I stand inside of, never quite falling—my palms tincture with terpenes from the Stackfields like a sacrament. I carry their pepper-sharp, resin-sweet smell through districts that reprint themselves under weather and tone. I find the Well-Districts by air alone; dew rains upward there with a care that feels like liturgy. I kneel and listen for the careful glug of a cisterner deciding where to put sky. In the Basin Arches I practice looking outward until my eyes stop snatching at bright, and then a trellis unfurls across the light with such unembarrassed speed that I can feel the Retensioners laugh politely at my startle.

If I am to be anything more here than tolerated, I must choose what kind of courtesy I can be back. It cannot be pruning; nothing here wants a hand. It cannot be tending; the Great Body tends itself as naturally as breath. It cannot be song; my throat-noise is a foreign instrument. What remains to me is time and weight.

I become a weight on a rope. It does not seem like a life when I say it plain. In the mornings I go to the lianas that arc the bowl, those daylight-catchers, the only agoraphic gesture the green allows. I learn their mathematics by standing still on their backs. This is not metaphor. Retensioners are patient engineers. They know catenary and cantilever the way I know the way sleep tastes. They retune themselves twice after noon because that is when the day slackens, and a known mass is a gift. I give them mine. I am a measure that returns. My particular gravities—bone, water, the packet of salt I lug around as organism—become a tool. I am not the arc; I am the plumb line the arc reads.

We invent a ritual. I arrive at the same minute every day the sky is kind and stand the same way—feet apart a leaf-width, arch soft, the flat of one hand against bark that is rope and then, under trust, rubber. The liana eases subtly under me, then tightens, then eases, as knots slide themselves somewhere in the leaf-dim above and retie. The arc of sky holds. A math that lives in fiber consults me and then becomes itself. There are days when wind comes off the plain and the liana sings under me with such force that I am pinned to admiration. In that minute I could be crushed into yes by the chord alone, and the only reason I am not is that the whole valley is good at being many at once. By some breadth of consent, tone cools, moves down into calmer key, and the arcs settle like a chest exhaling. I tell myself I have helped. Maybe I am a comma the sentence reads itself against to keep from running on.

The Brown takes notice of me in its slow way. It is easy, at first, to pretend the underground is only what the green drops. But there are days when a tower makes the long peel, an outer skin sloughing with a sound like canvas torn and a sweetness that kinks behind my nose, and I cannot stand not to be near it. The peeled rind is hurled to the floor of the bowl and becomes feast. I kneel in the debris fields laced through low ground after a weather-storm or a sonic one, and watch as white fur appears as if by moonlight over dying nets and snapped tendril. The Brown speaks in touch. It billets me as ledger. It makes arguments into my shins while I kneel—this much returned, this much still sugar enough to be stolen back to green before the rot marks it beyond retrieval. I am a clerk once more. There is comfort in being counted.

The Glassmakers are a seduction. I had not known I wanted to see myself until I saw myself in mineral ghost. They work in stillnesses where the air heats even if no beam ought to be. The first time I find one of their panes—silica coaxed up through phloem until it lays light down into sheet—I see not my face but my temperature, a heat-shimmer that wobbles my outline and makes me less definite and more true. I lift my hand and the pane seems to ripple; I drop my hand and the ripple lingers a heartbeat after. Of course it does. Slippage loves a repeat. For a while I stand daily where no light should reach. The Veilmakers thicken brightness under a mat of leaf, saving it like seed, and I become, by accident, a bead they pass it along. I learn to turn my shoulder, to cup and give back. They use me to pull day into a place it cannot go and make winter inside a chasm easier.

I tell myself these are all small kindnesses. The valley nods and then distracts me with a door. Breathsmiths are polite bullies; they write the rules that everyone knows but no one authored. When the engine is in performance—dawn, dusk—I pretend to forget the first law and wander. Doors gently insist. I am asked to be delayed. Not stopped; not forbidden. Delayed. The closure is never panic. It is a soft no that reads to my body like a loving hand on sternum: here, pause. Corridor skin draw breath, stomata close in a wave like wind through wheat and wrap not me but the space I would have used. I wait. Details reveal themselves to people who can manage not to move for three minutes: hairlike scales that line the floor cilia-fashion to speed what wants to go that way; mica-pale new bridges tense and almost vibrating with indecision; the way the hum in one direction is bass—heavy-lobed, hunkered—while the hum at my back is treble—twitchy, netting. The reason passes—a growth spasm written in green; a sudden rain rising like applause up a well—and the city opens me again with entire absence of triumph. The door I would like to be here is this one. I want to become a clerk of gentle refusals.

So there is another apprenticeship. I let myself be taught the tempo of breath. I stand where a corridor habitually closes and begin, hour by hour, to predict the sigh. I say no to myself before the plant needs to ask. My body becomes a metronome that the living hallway can consult across days—my stop and go a memory it uses to write a schedule. I am a courtesy made mildly flesh. Some afternoons a tendril glances me like a cat, soft as light making a decision about a leaf. Some mornings fog rises alone as if my consent were already given.

There is a day, mid-valley, when the Slippage becomes personal. I climb a helical stair because sometimes a life requires a metaphor so bald it has to be taken on its face. I climb: one, two, three tight turns, hands on ribs of leaf-fused wood that feel like steps reach their own opinion of themselves with each footfall. At the fourth turning there is a landing with a view of a Stackfield’s edge; at the fifth there is a landing with the same view and the same edge, only resin odor a machine-shift sweeter, more pepper. I keep going until the dog of human panic sniffs at my ankle: have I returned to a place I left? I have not. The stair went on becoming its earlier self in front of me because the song asked it to. Someone moved through this place once and the place learned their turning. For a day or a week it repeats the lesson as scar and ornament. I am allowed to be the hand that remembers. I put my palm down. The plant prints me.

By then the pronouns in my head have already begun to unspool. “I” is workable, but it is a dry useful pronoun, like a peg you hang things on. “We” is closer. We—the body plus the pause; we—the weight and the arc under it; we—the Brown’s appetite bleeding back upward through vascular speech; we—the shaded light that feeds a place where shadow should have been only shadow. What lives here lives in the plural. When I try to become singular the city is patient the way a choir is patient with a note that cannot quite find home. It sings a better center under me until I cannot help but resolve.

Visitors are not citizens and I am not a traitor to my kind. The valley does not keep me. It does not turn me into a root or a leaf; I do not fuse into tissue in some romance of surrender. There is no absorption porn here; the green is too busy to make myths with me in them. What it can use it uses, and what it cannot it lets go down one of the sweet swift boulevards with a kindness that reads as indifference only if you require drama to feel that you mattered.

So if you look for me you will find me in hours rather than addresses. Midday, high on a Retensioner above a well where dew rains in both directions, my stance a returning number. Dusk, at the throat of a corridor that wants to breathe itself shut, my delay already in my muscles so the Breathsmiths have less work to do. Morning, kneeling in the Brown’s field of feast with my shins damp and my nose filled with fermentation and resin, counting up the day’s small deaths and the day’s small returns until the ledger sings clear. Noon, by a thin pane that was light last week and is glass this week and will be ornament next, holding my hand at that odd angle between shade and show so the Veilmakers can bead brightness under a mat where no beam should go. After rain, in the Stackfields, my palms slick and fragranced for hours with terpenes I did not steal, only accepted, green solvent that marks me as someone done being merely visitor for the length of an afternoon.

What am I? A rest, mostly. A punctuation the city finds handy. No one here dreamed me up; I am not their prophet, not their friend, not their necessary harm. I am a human-sized hold in a score that is not written anywhere but stands in the bowl as pressure and pitch. The valley can play around me or play through me; it often does both. On days when wind is a knife and the organ-sound stands to the edge of dangerous, I am still, cut clean by chords that would shear anything that cannot learn slowness, and when the tone cools and carries down and the many settle into their adjusted selves with a subtlety you can only feel in your soles, I unclench my hands and laugh privately because being allowed use in a thing that has no use for you is the closest I have gotten to grace.

If you want a name you can hum me as a chord. If you want a face, you can put your palm to a pane of plant-made glass and watch as your heat wobbles the outline you keep insisting is you. If you want to meet me, come to the marsh rim on a day when the city has lowered its voice into reed and sieve and the only sound is the fizz of bubbles lifting in small forgivenesses from the breath of root. I will be there early, practicing not being in the way. Later I will walk back into the bowl on boulevards that thrum and let the engine stand me still and ask delays of me and give me doors, and I will stay—with the accuracy of a measure, with the uselessness of a comma without a sentence—for exactly as long as I can.
