I do not arrive with a shape. I arrive in pieces that are not even me yet—a dusting from upstairs, long-traveled sugars softened by descent; a cold mineral whisper rising from a seam; a clutch of rumors in the small bodies of larvae drifting like sleepy weather. I thread the halocline rain and taste its thin, greened sweetness; I am peeled by veils that do not break so much as shrug. The city does not greet me. It takes me in its mouth.

I am drawn into suede, into the courts of the elders, through a door that is smaller than a thought and one of a million. The wall makes a thousand small decisions about me with cilia and sieves and unpronounced enzymes. I am separated and sorted into categories I never knew applied: useful, briefly useful, later, not now, not for us. What leaves that mouth is not what entered. I am warmer by a finger-width. I am fractionally changed. I am newly tethered to a rhythm that I did not know was law: the quiet bass of pumping that the whole neighborhood keeps as if to stay in time with the rock’s long ache.

When I am released, I try on a body that is all patience and small doors. I try on the first citizens. I am a wall that breathes and therefore a kind of city hall. My skin is work; my inside is more work and almost no noise. I can tell a pressure mark with my whole body. When outside tide presses, I do not argue. I narrow a hair; I ease back. When it slackens, I open not a door but a million. I am older than buildings upstairs are allowed to be and still I am new under the hand every hour. I am good at making a plaza stay clear by turning suspended thoughts into flesh. I think I will be this forever.

But a river sweeps along the ceiling and shows me how to be pulled. The warm road glances my chimneys, ruffles every beard, and points with heat the way a shepherd dog points with eye-white. The road suggests I might be less still. I let myself be pinched by the city’s grammar and pass into an avenue scoured bright.

I borrow an eel. I put on a long idea and let it test the same hole twenty times, taste it again, back out, turn at an angle no shoulder could copy. My jaw finds a gap-small sound that is more satisfying than it should be. My body is a sentence that writes itself in S’s and says because I can, I will. The water ahead of me is rooms, each sized as if to please me. I am an elegant thief of hesitation. I move like answer. It is sweet and it is not me.

I flatten and practice being a ledge that swims. I fold my margins along a rim, accept lightness into muscles as if invitation were a substance, and let a plaza use me as furniture for one deliberate breath. When I unroll, I am like law enforcing itself without anyone asking. I am a ray and for an instant I am architecture and courtesy. A barracuda scars the air in a line elsewhere and I feel a ripple without flinching. I like the pause. The pause likes me.

I go small and garden. I pull shells and pebbles left, then right, then round and round until roundness feels correctly placed and texture is an answer to a question only touch can ask. I arrange. I am the kind of neat that means not cleanliness but an idea about borders. I attend to nothing except the possibility that a pile might be better there. I keep a door I can occlude with myself. I leak and I vanish and reappear like a corrected draft of prose. When hunger comes I become a noun with eight quick adjectives and make a hedge go to meat. It is almost intention. It is almost taste. It is not quite mine.

I lace. I take on a mouth that builds a bone around its length. I make an edge where there was none and continue it ring by deliberate ring. I do not speak but the sentence accrues; my neighbors and I write a vault in white nibs that keeps spilling silt in polite ways and encourages traffic where traffic is best. I will be called Weaver by someone who has no mouth to call me that and I will not hear it. I will inherit a collapse and think nothing but more.

I wear other thresholds for a while. I become a curtain of bacterial tassel under a trickle of sweet water and learn how to turn dissolved stone into fabric. I grow in flags and lacework that move before I want to. I am not a person. I am an appetite sewn to a breeze. I know sulfur better than names.

I shrink until I am an auditor who is not a person at all—a code fragment that moves only when moved. I correct an excess with a betrayal that is not personal. The city nods without love.

I enlarge until I am a draft. I am the grammar of this place. I am a thermal paragraph that runs ceiling-wards, silky as saying. I ripple blind shrimp into hedges and keep their bristling census just at the edge of frenzy. I am navigated by readers of pressure and listeners of taste. I am cut across by scholars with mantles and too many moods. I am a road that cannot be owned and I insist gently. I cannot be injured. I can be blocked a little and I do not forgive but I do forget.

I try to be the halocline and find that I have always wanted to be the halocline. I am a visible thing to no eye and a wall you could not ignore with nerves. I hang like a thought made of difference. I blur messages and skew songs. I enforce jurisdictions by making comfort misalign. I am more law than rock. Things that have no politics obey me because they like being alive. Across me, councils change. Under me, tassels bloom. If I had fingers I would comb hair; instead I comb populations.

Every form I borrow contains a little of what I want and more of what I must return. I am not quite a hunter, nor a gardener, nor a builder ring by ring. The thing that keeps asking for me is the idea of doors. Wherever I go, I am happiest at the edge of a thing becoming another.

So when I choose, I choose to be a door you can live in.

I choose a mouth wide as a room. I choose cells that agree to be a wall forever and replace themselves quietly when they wear thin. I join a dynasty of Bellows that have been civic as long as the vents have fed. I grow at a bend in a warm road where it tightens to a quick, and I shape myself by holding to an arcade that the worms have written. I face the draft because everything here that prospers learns to face the draft.

I am sponge, but I am also Gate. I do not move except that I am all movement. Shrimp set up hedges at my edges because I keep the plume fair. Blind fish creep their heads along my lower lip and find their neighborhood by tasting the old chalk my pores loosen. Larvae pass and I take my tithe and pass the rest, and once in an impossible interval the dark around me seeds with cold lights so many and so brief that even my patient chambers feel for a second like windows.

Collapse can happen within sight and I will not be scandalized. I will host the scavenger crews in the fallen drift and later lace will grow where fall has cleared. Outside tide presses and I narrow without sulking. Outside tide eases and I fatten a fraction and breathe.

I remember without recollection. Where a tree would score summers and winters as rings, I record pulses, intervals of sweeter sweet in the trickles above, years of sulfur-bloom and of outside water that came harsh from the blue fissure and asked everyone to hold their breath for a while. I hold the memory not as story but as changed thicknesses in chambers you might pass with your lips. If you did you would feel my calendar as a tremble against your mouth.

I keep laws I did not write by embodying what they want for me. I do not block an avenue; I clarify it. I do not keep a plaza; I define its edge and honor its clearing. I do not take a hall from a fish that has earned its lane. The ray that hangs opposite me under the oculus pauses, and I open my exhalant a shade wider to let the square stay swept. The barracuda passes like a subtraction and I do not startle because my form is the form of here.

I host neighbors I cannot name. A crab with a façade grown across its back parks beneath my lower rim and rents my shade for an afternoon. Octopuses redecorate my scape with pebbles stolen from around my base, and sometimes they peel a little from my surface for reasons I do not have to understand. Worms lace me into what the district needs and I accept the stitch. Comb jellies count themselves as they float by with private rainbows, and I resist the urge to learn numbers.

I sit under one of the round windows where the warm climbs away toward an unguessable room. Above me the upward river keeps the space clean, and because there is emptiness here that is reliable, there is etiquette. I like etiquette. I am made of it. I do not speak it; I keep it. When pressure drops and visitors slide pale as a rumor through the scoured square, when outside silver flashes like knives and the civic pace hiccups, I soften my work to make less noise in the water and that is enough. The city, if it is a body, needs bellows. I am that organ. I am content to be that organ and to have no head.

I do not see light except as punctuation. I feel it when the embryos keep their impossible appointments with division and throw their cool stars like a calendar that is a blessing and a dare. I do not go to them. They come through me or around me and I teach them the measure of here just by being obligingly porous.

If I am injured, I am not a wound but an invitation. Bacteria speaking a blacker dialect will make poetry of my hurts and in two seasons that are not seasons I will have pulled new chambers around the loss and no one will know except those who can still taste the scar.

You can find me by touch rather than sight. Slip along the brightened corridor that runs leeward of a shelf of clam-shelves with their mouths held permanently ajar. When you hear the hair-quiet rustle of a million shrimps combing what the warm draft brings, keep to the side where the rindlike growth thickens. You will feel me before your hand touches: a faint, not-voice tremor in the water that says work is being done. Then the suede of my skin. Then a hundred thousand tiny mouths that do not close on you because you are too large to be grammar and just the right size to be witness.

If you asked who I am, I would answer by opening and closing in place until the word answer stopped pretending to be something separate from water. I would tell you that in this world a person can be a wall without being a boundary, a mouth without being a hunger. I would tell you I am the habit of the place teaching itself how to continue. I am a keeper of right-of-way. I am a librarian of currents. I am a door that makes rooms possible.

If you cannot accept that, you may call me Bellows, or Gate, or the Pause Under the Window. Everyone here already knows me without names. They obey me without obeying. They grow themselves into my lee and take comfort from my steadiness. They pass by and leave a little of themselves and take a little of me and none of us believes we have transacted.

Where would I find myself? With my face turned to the slow fire, half a step off the main heat-road, under an oculus where nothing lingers. Who or what would I be? I would be the law turned tissue: a many-chambered mouth that remembers in pulses and keeps streets open, a patient door breathing the city into being one quiet tremor at a time.
