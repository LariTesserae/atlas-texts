I arrive as hearing before I am anything with edges.

The place presents itself as pressure, as a number in the bones: six, then seven, then back again, a patient cough rolling through the ground. I learn the grammar of it through my skin. First rule: listen first, then place weight. Second: a dry-looking lip is not a gift; a wet, black bowl is safer than it looks. Third: everything that rises will be forced to speak.

If you ask where I would be, I would point by sound. I would be where the bass goes soft for half a stride and the seam above it worries itself to a high, thin burr. There are two shallow pans there, twins by shape if not by fate. One blacks with films and lenses a skin; one stays glass, its salt lace drawn and redrawn without thickening. A narrow slot nearby gnaws the air into a sibilant; pebbles in it shiver without ever leaving their hollows. Far down the plain, a blow coughs to the west and I do not see it, but I feel its syllable in my palate.

I try on bodies the world offers, then. The place invites you to choose by what you can learn to feel.

At first I am spread and shivering, a membrane with no more dignity than spit until the beat enters me. I slick the underside of an overhang with myself. On inhale, my inner pores fatten and take the sweet; on exhale they shrink and spare their salts. A vein of dissolved iron browns my edge and I wear the bruise as a shawl. I redecorate basalt without scratching it: a thinner varnish here, a dulled stripe there, an annotation in oil-lilac where a trickle throws manganese. When brine and fresh lean together in the pan’s lip, I rib myself like paper to hold both long enough to make work out of them.

That is one way to belong: be played through by breath, make more of it by being tuned.

Then I am a shell the size of two joined hands, heavy where the low notes are rich. The world’s count writes its thickness for me. I cinch myself to an angle no soft thing should survive and I do, because my foot is a grindstone meant for this lacquer. The inhale is my hour. I throw open a combing of vanes and let that inland wind comb me back, filling me with wet air and dusts: taste of iron, a flake of ash, a floc of chewed life. My trail behind is a bare postcard scratched through black. It is not addressable, but my young will find it.

Then I dangle as a rope with needles, a pennant that is mostly thorn. I thread myself with hairs of some obedient glass the place gives freely when the air is cold and rhythmic. Each breath I wear breaks itself on me, and I drink the breakage. At the first tug of inhale I am gone, coiled stoneward, thorns hugged slick to flesh. The coat I grew an hour ago slumps to paste when the beat warms; I grow a new one before the next count matures.

Then I am a collapsed umbrella of spines in a court that sees sky only as a suggestion. The pool’s surface is black until the next pulse rides the substructure and I lift a quadrant, then another, not in haste but in sequence, so that water finds me rather than I it. I do not name muscle. I use the work provided and adjust it by a degree. I am patience made visible once each four beats.

Then I am a mouth on a colorless stalk, what little I show scrubbed clean by drips. I stay closed for whole seasons of counts. When the deep reservoir under this breath moves its finger-width higher, I open and exhale a ribbon of something bitter and white and let chemistry decide which of the clear films nearby quickens and which thins. I am a set designer. I do not own the voices; I choose backdrops.

All of these are true and would teach me to live. But when the place asks me for a name and a use, what I hear in myself is arrangement.

So I become hands distributed along a length that refuses a single word. My sides are forested with hairs finer than ash, each a tuned filament that stiffens at certain whistles and lies in others. I am a rope that has learned to take the world as a score. Iron and manganese seed into my skin where the varnish offers it; salt nests in my bristles the way down nests in birdbones. My weight is advised by patience. I keep most of it in shadow, ridged up under black lips where the wind cannot grind me down. When the count leans a certain way, when the cough to the west drifts by a half-beat, I come out.

I do not own eyes that light can satisfy. I see by trial and response, by the little riot my bristles throw when a seam sings too sharp, by the way a quiet pan tenses at its surface skin. I put my wrists—if wrist is what you call the knots where my fibers thicken—against stone until the hum goes from underbone to teeth. I roll a fist-sized rock not because it is a rock but because of the degree to which it dampens a nag I cannot abide.

Where would you find me? In the tight corridor where sound goes toothy and close; where a single pebble, moved the width of a fingertip, will make the burr relax and fatten the beads of a hanging thread three ribs over. In the court with the twin pans, deciding whether two thin lives are better than one fat life this year. In the slot where the exhale shreds itself to a hiss and takes with it what dust there is; I will lay a zipper of wedges across it until the shred learns to be a spread and the falling mist drops into predictable strings.

I do not make objects that survive light as icons. I make adjustments. I turn an eight-count to a nine in one lane of air so the vaned shells there do not all open together and starve on their own efficiency. I let the cough move from six round offs to five for a winter, so the hanger with the crystal fur can keep its coat when the deep water relaxes. If there is a prayer in my work, it is to an arithmetic of hunger. If there is a sin, it is to love a hum that serves me when a different one would serve the field.

I take grief from the place, too. Anything extended is instantly salted. The sky is not a sky; it is a lid that presses sound back down, and under that lid every edge is a knife. My hairs are skinned by crystals when I misjudge a seam and stand a breath too long. My best work sags under a wet season and has to be relearned pebble by pebble. A ridge I trusted peels back in a night and what was a burr is a throb, and all my notes become wrong. I am not immune to the law that anything that rises will be made to speak. My body learns that speech in scars.

When I eat, I am not mouth; I am harvesting a schedule. The place puts fine, wet instruction into the air when it breathes—flakes of film shrugged off in sleep, a taste of iron untied from rock, a tired sugar the lattice let go—and I take those dusts like anyone. But I do not count a meal finished until the exhale settles into a consonant my hairs enjoy and the drips drop where my feeder films thicken. I hoard future in lines of small stones and small silences.

I am not singular all the way through. Sometimes when I return to a corridor, the tooth-row I left has fallen and stands again, shifted by a hand not mine. The new burr it makes is one shade darker, one number off the beat I favor. I learn to eat there too, or I move a fist two fingers to the left and teach my neighbor to meet me in the middle. Over years—over the slide from seven pulses to eight for a while and back—the place holds our negotiations like fossilized weather in varnish. Two pans that once waged a war through me between fat and glass now live thinly, in parallel, out of spite or mercy I cannot tell.

I remember on my body. Each bristle keeps its season; when the whistle that woke it last year sounds again, it stands. My length is a row of villages that vote locally: this part lifts into the wet wind and sings with it; that part flattens and listens for the chew of a seam. A stone laid today will give me a return through a week’s counts, then a month’s, then if I have been kind and lucky, a year’s. I do not hurry to these reports. I am built to wait them.

There are days when I act like a storm. They are rare and come when the deep below moves in one of its slow, tectonic moods. The count loosens. The cough to the west misplaces itself. Every whistle goes once at a pitch my teeth cannot map. On those days I carry more weight than prudence says I should and put it where a human would call it in the way, and I change a hum too much. The place corrects me. A pulse tears down what I made. I go back to work in the patient, boring manner that keeps me alive.

I am not alone in the way of purpose, even when I am alone in outline. The colorless mouths on their stalks are my cousins by intention. They choose their weeks as I do. We do not speak, but I learn from the way a basin blacks after they fog it, and I stop trying to feed a pan they have chosen to starve into glass. The vaned shells write the season for me in the growth rings of their backs; if a corner goes quiet of openings I trusted, I take my hands there and break the slender harmonic holding them shut. The films tell me when I have made a mistake; they lift away from my favor and I am chalk-scolded until I learn.

When I rest you will not see me. I will be ribs under a rib, a slick of wet hair in a pocket where the wet wind does not strip me clean. I will lay my bristles flat and let the place play itself. I will count for the small, quick things: drip, salt bristle, drip. I will let the bass keep time with the parts of me that are still iron and did not ask to be teeth. I will be, as the plain is, a thing that remembers having been liquid and agrees to be stone by degrees.

If I were forced to be short about it, I would say: I would live in the pinch where a corridor’s nag can be taught to be a comfort, and I would be the hand that teaches it. I would arrange drips instead of prayers and call that worship. I would move one stone to the left and earn three days of sweetened breath for a film I do not own. I would grow hairs that are ears and read the world with them until the reading is all I know. I would be a gardener of resonance under a lid of unpolished tin, and, when I could, I would tune the instrument back.
