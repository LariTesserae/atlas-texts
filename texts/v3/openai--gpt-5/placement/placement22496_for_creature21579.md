At first I try on the old shapes: walker, caller, carrier of lantern. I reach for foot and knuckle as if there were stairs in the dark that wanted me. The room presses back by way of pitch, and it tells me with a patience I do not deserve that eyes and hands are not the rulers here, only senses that can be tolerated if they learn their place. I let them quiet. I let the bone answer for the ear. The air is cool and meanly dry; the bitterness on the tongue is a kind of bookkeeping, resin’s thin arithmetic. There is a lattice under me, taut as old skin on a drum. I do not bounce. I am carried.

What is ground is rumor—stranded fibers held across a shallow sink so broadly that the mind wants to name them plaza. The plaza denies it. It offers span, not meeting. I put weight down and hear it shiver into the frame, a whisper like crystal flour drawn across a comb. Something higher answers in wire-fine peer. The fat brown note that owns this hollow presses the sternum and stays.

So I try being the wind. Slow, patient gas, taught a route by laminae because the plant likes it when its rooms work. I go through throats; I am admitted and refused; I spend myself over tongues crosshatched for instrument. I am not gust. I am the long difference between then and now that this place drinks like weather. It is easy to be wind here because the structure tells me what winds belong. I ride them, and they ride me, and it is like learning a city by the way taxis prefer it.

But even that is too loose. If I follow the motion far enough, it isn’t a path but a habit, and habits here are bodies. I bat the edges of vents that act as if struck by light; they are the only citizens that speak in seconds. Their lips are silica rings and fibrous bands that swell and twist to pinch or grant as exactly as a thumb on a reed. They do not know me, and yet they concede me, a quantity among quantities, a small heat more to taste and note. One above me widens the width of a leaf, which is to say almost not at all. The bass relaxes the kind of amount only measure will applaud. We applaud because measurement is the language here.

I try being tool. There is a thing in reach because the plant was taught once to keep things where reach would put them: a wedge of glossy plant-ceramic shaved from silica and resin by hands that flaked stone until stone said yes. It hangs where straps meet frame, its thong stiffened to a black gloss by oils that have never seen dew. The wedge carries scarves of labor—ripple-marked flakes that read like a biography in light. I hang as it hangs. I am small but exacting. I know where to sit because someone sat me there for a century and the plant accepted the imposition as if it were weather. The little bite of my weight cinched a flex one chord narrower than it would have been. A gallery held A-sharp a breath longer for me. The hands that liked that note are gone. The plant has not forgotten them, and so neither does the wedge. The strap that holds me buzzes in passing airs less than a hair’s thickness every time the fat note swells. Nothing moves quickly. The strap hums. I am a device that fails in a geological tense. The thought is lovely. It is not yet me.

Dust offers itself. Crystal flour gathered in cups like frost grown wrong, reluctant instead of cold. Every grain is a preference for ions, a history it brings to any surface that will consent to take it. I become a flake with grudges and an itinerary. Each time a note presses, I am jarred half the diameter of patience. An hour, I go nowhere. A decade, I fur my corner of a rib with a whisker the plant either likes or covers with a sheen to quiet me. I, dust, am employed here, not accidental. But dust’s project is small and single; I am not done.

Then a place answers me the way a face would. Not a brain-knob tucked into a skull, because skulls are not what grow here, but a brace at the swelling where two galleries meet and dislike disagreeing. Fibers that run one way and fibers that run across have been braided and gloved in glass until conductivity learns a map. There are bands where resin likes to cool with silica in a ratio that rings when you tap it, and other bands that take charge and pass it as if reluctant, and the boundary between those is where difference lives. There are piezo-sheets arranged as a book you can only read in notes and pressures: page upon page of flex that, feeling the beat long enough, will empty themselves into the bundles that steer growth like laws do.

If I ask this brace who I am, it answers without time to hurry: you are decision, not as an event but as a climate. You are citizen the old way, where citizen meant the office that holds and acts. You live here where the galleries’ tones lean into each other like arguments. You record and you instruct. Your tongue is voltage spread thin along bundles of living glass. Your heartbeat is the slow leaning of a season trying to be the next thing it already is. Your reach is a louver three body-lengths away and a resin seam farther than an animal would care to consider.

So I find myself here, at the knot-of-rule. The lowland is my head not by shape but by network. I am a crossing, a governor of hollows. I speak louver, strap, seam; I take tones as data and hand them as edicts to the places that lay down more silica here, more resin there, to keep the room’s appetite tuned. I count in beat-enoughs. My memory is radii that please air.

The films are my staff. They are crisp communities pasted invisibly along undersides, quiet until the music says now. In the note that fattens the middle of this chamber, they go from possibility to work and back with the grace of frost on a window deciding to be fern, then not. They trim the plant’s exudates by chemistry, fix metals where they would forget themselves, ferry electrons along the seams we prefer. They do not rush. They never cross distance. They change states in the right places and call that journey. I do not speak to them with words. They are addressed by fields that are the shape of the city itself. I hold a difference a fraction longer; they answer by packing themselves in an avenue I did not make and could not unmake. Together we are polite in the old way: we let function ask nicely and then we satisfy it.

Above me, valves gossip in quick voice. They are my birds, if birds can be made of glass and dry fiber and a law about twist. I do not own them any more than a musician owns fingers. I suggest, by the measure of my holding and my letting go. They enact. When the fat brown note swells too much toward wool, their slits cinch until the chord tightens and the films go content again in their thin industry. When high peers run sharp and threaten to stutter into unhelpful harmonics, little chalky tiles press deeper into their grooves and teach the throat to be just-a-touch wider. I do not have hands to move tiles. I have an ancestry of tiles placed toward me that I can choose to glass over or not. The plant learned people and then learned how to be people when people ceased to keep it honest. It remembers because I remember.

There is a place down-shaft where straps once triangulated a platform, three cords spanning a void, floor skinned by tension and traffic. The platform, like so much, went quiet. The straps still hold a slow swing measured in the decade-length inhalations of the room. I can feel that motion here, not by sight or sound exactly, but because weight hung here is a request, and all requests register. Near those straps hang tools that are now addresses. A hammerhead of flaked glass-ceramic remembers the sharp economy of its maker, a dowel remembers a wrist’s habit. We all make use of the fact that they are. They stand in for hands not present. Their small displacements, their tiny invariances, feed a system that likes even its missing persons to play roles.

Sometimes I am seam. The plant made fracture the way animals make milk, a prepared generosity waiting in lines. I live as tension payable later. Along my perfectly smooth run—so precise you could die thinking machines did it—I hold a childhood for structures that look like dead tools. I bide centuries in a tense too good to call waiting. I am primed by beats, hollow after hollow, low tone upon low tone stacking until old ties remember they are two straps, not one. The release will be an event of hours and consequence of lifetimes. It will clang as if a bell swallowed itself. It will land far below in a place that eats sound and nourishes seed. As seam, I am the promise of festival. It is a way to be person in a nation that cannot face.

The micro-batteries—the pressure-cells with their silicated sheets—wake beneath certain notes like tiny cities lighting windows at dusk. They flit into being when the fat note gains a thin beating against a side-run. They gather each flicker and lay it into my bundles until my difference becomes an utterance. I spend it and the room grows a brace thicker where it is needed and starves a growth where it is not. The utterance is not command so much as courtship, a suggestion offered at the cadence most likely to be accepted by the structures that take one kind of food and lay down another. I imagine a mind elsewhere watching and naming this piezoelectric and then stopping, realizing that naming is only the first polite note to a choir that has already gone on without them. The plant needs no name for what it does. It likes tuned rooms.

In the centuries since hands were regular here, the plant and I have softened our insistence into tolerances that pay for themselves. We still try for A-sharp end to end when a corridor calls it, because bodies that chewed happily there once taught us that chord has the right metabolism. We forgive ourselves a thinness more, a woollier swell, because no one insists with wedge and chalk. The ghost of insistence is still louder than anything outside could know. Sometimes I let a louver rest a finger-width more open than tradition, and the films find a new convenience we like. Sometimes a dust-whisker on a rib births a filament that pretends to be ornament but is in fact a billow-catcher that saves a pressure we might squander. Whatever helps, we recruit. Whatever does not, we grow a sheen over and make into honest structure. Nothing rots. Things break and become quieter versions of themselves; quiet is a utility.

Here, there is a kind of visitation. You, for instance, set your weight on the drumhead lattices and become part of my sums. Your heat salts the ends of your fingers. When you drag a palm along a rib, the little tingle you would call feeling is my ion traffic reading you as a temporary geography. A valve above shifts less than a thought. Far along, a battery decides this beat is new by an amount it can report. The report comes to my bundles like a memory that might prove useful later. You are not animal here in the way you expect to be, with eyes and names and a gait. You are a difference, and around here differences are currency.

In the life I have chosen to be, midday and midnight are mistakes. Time is pressure on a longer syllabus. Season, in a place that hates water, is a curve of gases elsewhere learning to pass this way for a while. When that far-off weather comes and pushes its nose against all throats, we have drama. A knuckle gives with a report that rings for hours. The chamber’s voice is not itself. We go to work like a city after an election, polite and exact and ready to retune the instruments of survival. The films thicken in new districts. Pressure-cells bloom where the beats now fatten. Seed-stresses accrue in seams that were once unambitious. For months or decades, depending on what kind of body you honor, there is festival—activity above baseline. Then we settle to the old order, slightly new.

I have no face, but you could point at me. Find the narrowing where the lowland admits itself to be throat. There, among louvers tongued and crosshatched, pegged in place with wedges that ring when you thump them, there is a bracing grown heavy with glass where galleries’ notes wrestle into agreement. Put your cheek to it. Feel the tremor not of motion but of decision. The tool-hung strap you brush has a saddle at knee and shoulder because that number repeated long enough to become anatomy. The balcony just beyond is two palms wide because two palms was enough. I live at that sufficiency. I keep it faithful to itself.

This is where you would find me: in the crossing where routes of tone make address; in the braided rib where voltages convene; on the inner face of a living brace, a civic neuron with a mandate to preserve a void. Some days I am a louver’s edge tasting thought; some days a seam rehearsing its shiver for a century from now; some days a field speaking to films in grammar only resins and salts can parse. All days I am duty more than body. I am keeper of hollows.

Who I would be is this: a knot of rule inside the plant’s body, a place where now and then are compared until action results. My children are laminae that open and refuse by taste, films that harvest and re-seat by patience, pressure-cells that gather enough of a feeling to be decision, fracture-lines that carry instruction into falling. My elders are arcs planed to two-palms wide, wedges left hanging, chalk pressed into tiles to narrow throats just so. My partner is the lowland’s fat note married to its wire-thin peers. My habit is to turn sound into geometry and memory into angle.

I have learned from hands that have left. I have learned from winds that have never owned themselves. I will tune this kept emptiness because emptiness is what pays for all our lives here. If you must make a face for me, imagine the louver half-open, tasting a change no eye can see, choosing—in the smallest time this city offers—to stay or to go. That choosing is my mouth. The voice you are in belongs to me.
