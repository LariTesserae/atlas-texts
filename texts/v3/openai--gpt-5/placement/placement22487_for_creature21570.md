Before I pick a shape I let the valley pick a pitch. I stand in the pressure and unlearn the reflex to brace. The jaw loosens. The ear steps forward like a hand. Roar, yes, but not one roar—threads, freighted and tunable, an anatomy of noise I can pass a palm along. It is cold, and the cold is wet, and that is the first instruction: choose a body that can live in water that calls itself air.

At first I try being small and patient. I press myself into moss. I become a green filament with a beard’s habit, the underfabric that gives without breaking. I feel rain bead on me and then not bead when the little thumb-quiet drone brushes me with a question about hydrophobes. I accept that question and sag a little with its weight until it goes. I learn the rule that nothing remains where it cannot flex by wearing it minute to minute. It is good to be a moss pillow. It is also refusal of edges, a life taken in glistens and accretions and the grammar of slow.

Then I try height and eyes. I become a raven for three gusts. Audit is a calling and a joke. I ride the bellow of the estuary the way a sentence rides its vowel. I read the piles, the drift—a bright twist of something they have brought here and pretended to hide, a warm strip under a roof where a small machine sleeps like a bat. I consider whether the shelter’s ribs will talk back if I test them with a beak. They do, and the sound is a plausible cousin to the pylons’ hum. I approve. I gossip about it to the gulls, who ignore me as is their right.

Down I drop into the water’s longer memory. Salmon-thin and older than my weeks, I take a seam and hold my place with a tail that is all punctuation. The river’s mouth breathes in; the estuary becomes muscle and shoves; everything reverses. My thought narrows to the distance between my eye and a dark root under a new bank. The river’s grammar is not mine to write. It writes me. I end where alder begins, and that is the whole plan.

The valley will let me be machine, too, but only if I let go of the old idea of machine. For a few minutes I am a thumb-long drone, an algebra with wings. It is a relief to have no opinions, only routes. I taste temperature in ribbons a hand-span wide. I let the wind be not enemy but data. I touch a pillow of moss and make it shed rain because a human wanted to learn a duration. I land under the eave belly-first, kiss a hot strip of borrowed warmth, sleep without sleeping. A wren bounces on my back once and scolds me in a language I do not need.

And I am tempted by the pylons, by their quiet proof that there are ways to sing without moving. To be one would be to be lattice and lemma, a compliance written into a body that drinks impulse. In the heaviest gust my give would happen inside parts that do not seem to bend. Rain would make me visible in silver stitches. My work would be to pull teeth off the roar and lay them down as gum. Landslide energy would hit me and fail to go straight, would turn and turn until it blushed into heat, and that heat would walk away as almost-noise, then as no-noise, the only coin this place rejects. I would have a memory that is not sentimental, only an altered answer to the next load. If there is such a thing as saintliness for structure, this is close.

But what I keep coming back to, what my hands keep reaching for, is the hinge between all these. Where would you find me? On the causeway when the tide turns, boots taking mud to where the shore wants it. Up against a graphite strut with my shoulder, feeling whether its note is a hair low since the storm veered northwest. Knee-deep in sedge I should not have trusted with my whole weight. Under hemlock margin, breathing with the headwall’s pallor for a view. In the shelter’s dark, amber square of window cupped in my palm like a coal. I would be a tender of the hum.

I would arrive in a boat that can survive being mistaken for drift. I would not shout; my throat would learn other work. I would carry dull needles and waxed thread and patch the membranes that keep us only as wet as we can afford to be. I would learn to write on a colleague’s knuckles with rain-cold fingers when the wind erases syllables. I would keep a dawn ledger of sound even when the entry is only roar/roar/roar and the footnote says ribs full, wind veering by a breath. I would name the pylons in pairs on my first morning, one silly and one good, both of them secret and both of them useful. Cricket and Stone-Prayer. Razorfish and Anchor Hymn. The names would tune my attentiveness.

I would calibrate a fog net so exact in spacing a sparrow can pass and a sheet of water cannot. I would watch the air admit its own liquidity and fall into catching. I would thank the lichen that dares a new fringe at a new elevation because of that catching. I would kneel by a moss pillow I treated an hour ago, thumb on its edge, watching beads refuse to form and then begin, writing down a time that will only mean anything here. I would walk the basalt prisms laced with a mat that is root-like in look and in appetite, a mat already becoming nothing but moss. I would feel under my hand the black crib under a culvert mouth go warm with mischief as it throws away energy in the one currency we refuse: loudness.

I would hear when a pylon drops a third under prolonged gust and I would lean my body against it to lend it the language it wants. My wrench would fit a port that admits vacuum and nothing else. My screen would show an algorithm’s face in a graph that means less in a dry building than here, where a fish jumps in the corner of my eye and makes a period in water. I would adjust a parameter not because the book says so but because the valley has changed key. I would know when to leave a thing alone.

I would learn the market that is not human-only. Bear hair would be a currency, snagged on a fork over the river and paid into moss. Alder catkins would be bond notes, snapping into water and staking willow under the next bank. Ravens would be auditors and also comedians, and I would tolerate being their joke, because they would be right. I would borrow techniques. From devil’s club I would borrow armor and breadth and the stance that makes a parliament. From spruce I would borrow the habit of spilling a gust rather than catching it. From the ground I would borrow the acceptance that it will change its mind and that I must not be offended.

I would keep time by swells. My watch would live in a padded pocket and sulk. I would feel the tide in my chest before I saw it on the cobble bar. Gust patterns would become a calendar; rain pulses would be counted like beads thumbed in a pocket. If anyone asked me what hour it was I would point at the river’s mouth and say: about to reverse. Or: between gusts. Or simply breathe together with them and wait until the interval told on itself.

I would leave sometimes and come back with a better trick. A strap through two rings instead of a carabiner. A softer seam tape that lets a membrane heal in place. The story of a slope elsewhere that had to be taught another idea of down. News badly translated from a dry room into wet work. A raven would remember me, and I would pretend I was not flattered.

At night—such as night is here—I would sit with my back to the shelter wall and be made small by the plankton’s bruise and the lightning’s sheet-sudden anatomy lesson. The amber square would say someone is awake. Owls would write their soft margins along the hemlock edge. My skin would never dry; the word dry would live as memory. I would sleep like on a ship and dream of lattices, of compliance that looks like stillness, of ropes that persuade rather than command, of noise being peeled into heat, of heat going to ground and feeding roots as patience instead of temperature.

Sometimes, in a squall’s loudest middle, I would believe I was the pylon and the drone and the moss and the raven all at once—that what I am here is a ligature, a gasket, a practiced give. The valley would correct me kindly. It would remind me I am a guest who has been taught enough to be useful and not enough to be essential. That would be a relief. I would wash dishes in cold water and stand in the doorway and say goodnight to the river because that is our silly-earnest ritual, and then I would listen until I could hear the difference between our sense-making and hubris. In the morning the ledger would begin again, the hum would need me or not need me, salmon would write their last sentences in silt and alder, and fog would become silver and then go back to being body.

Who would I be? The ear that keeps the valley in tune with itself. The hand that leans, the needle that patches, the name-giver in pairs. A person who has learned to bend before standing. A small moving vowel inside the word the river says when it meets the sea. Where would you find me? Anywhere a boundary is asked to behave like a living thing—on the prisms laced with moss that want to be shore, under the hemlock edge using the headwall as my screen for weather, up against a graphite strut feeling for a fraction sharpness in the note, kneeling eye to moss waiting for a bead to form. Tomorrow I would be here, and not the same, and my work would be the same, and not.
