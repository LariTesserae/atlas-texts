I arrive as a throat before I arrive as a person. The air puts its hand over my mouth and teaches me what breath weighs here. I stand long enough for my outline to soften; long enough for the city to stroke me down to where silhouettes don’t matter. What passes for night is a convex lid I can feel in my sinuses. The stone is warm with the thought of water. If I try to keep my edges, I spend my attention resisting the world’s habits. If I let them go, the world offers me work.

So I stop being a walker and become a place where a choice happens.

At first I am the damp lip under an arch, and my occupation is a beat. Above me, a bead fattens until it votes itself loose. In the tilt of my cup, I teach it which way to go. I am not plant or fungus or anything your old bins can flag; I am an adhesion, a nap of chemistry with patience. My body is the interval between this droplet and that fall. I adjust contact angles the way a finger adjusts a string. Each drop lands a little different; each one leaves a little of itself. Under my own work, I accumulate thinly: a papery white that was not there yesterday is there now, and is counted in what tomorrow will be.

I learn the city’s measure in the counting. Every drip has a tempo and the arch keeps time, rounding its throat under centuries of touch. When the lid above shudders—it isn’t lightning, it is a slow argument in heat—my beads find an urgency I do not fight. I open: a million tiny mouths facing the gap, all of them less than a thought apart. Weep, I tell the stone around me, but hold your shape. The work here is always a bargain.

The second life I try is string. In the district where water is taught to do what it shouldn’t, I become a veilmaker. My chemistry is a tendon: a film so spare it looks like denial until the beads accept it. There was never a pipe along this cornice, and yet water remembers one. My secretions are a rumor it can follow. I paint a narrow road along a vertical, and gravity—mostly a consensus—adds my line to its minutes for a while. This is wit, more than rebellion. Where the span trembles and snaps I am not embarrassed. I retract, I reseed my lick along whatever lip the last break offered. The snap is only an intake of breath between continuities. To build with water is to know the pleasure of teaching something that changes its mind slowly.

Humor here is a feint held a century: a runnel splits with ceremony, then joins again at the same remove every time as if free will were an essay one returns to but never revises. I adopt that humor. I set my two threads to either side of an absent brace and let them gesture at opposition while they conspire to the same pool. A passerby—if there were passersby—would see ornament. I know it as rehearsal for a future undercut that will need this curve persuasive and already spoken in the stone.

I flatten into script where walls are given over to condensation runes and call myself an archivist. It is my third name and perhaps my most native. I do not write the past; I write a likely. I deposit a hair more on the left than the right at a point where leftness will matter in fifty cycles. A lane reads my nudge and chooses a shallower fall. Far away, a basin whose lip I have thickened in a place no foot would think to look will fill to the depth a child-skin needs in the week the pulses widen. You would call what rises under my hand scallop, ladder, chevron. I call them arguments won by repetition. Beauty is a side-effect of a corner remembering itself.

Sometimes the lids go stiller than usual and a second weather arrives and I go with it. I cross to the center of a square that refuses wetness and take sanctuary there as a drykeeper. My organ is aversion. I salt the stone with molecules that teach it to hate water temporarily. Heat presses harder and the plaza blooms matte; beads pull inward; arches chalk. The city, like an animal at exertion’s end, pours its moisture into the air and for a little while all our efforts turn to uncoupling. I open my umbrellas a few microns from the surface and the first returning drops, rebuffed, skitter past my hole in the rule. I keep this absence so that the rule can be stronger elsewhere. The smell is gypsum and promise. When the weeping resumes—and it does, like a sentence that prefers commas to periods—I fold the umbrellas and let my antipathy leach out so it will be ready for the next reversal.

The city speaks in other lights and I take a turn as cable. Not rope—chain. A run of cells along a line of stress that glows a faint closet of colors when the crystalline wall complains. I do not need sight to know that the seam just across the arcade flared blue as if it remembered how to be lightning. My body in this role is an appetite for difference strung along distance. Electrons kink down my backbone. Stress is gossip and I am a boulevard. Where the wall murmurs triboluminescence under a breeze, my kin-habits light their own tiny pumps. We do not see your hand as a hand when you brush the wall; we see it as an event in the ledger. It erases; it primes. Balanced correctly, even interruption is a method.

Down in the bowls where the basins gum their lips, I become a breather. I herd fizz with pores and jubilee on a microsecond scale. There is no mouth; mouth would be a liability in a world where the meal is spread atom-fine along a film. I pull sulfide, I nibble ammonia, I ride the little pocks when a thicker drop lets go and the sheet stirs. The sound people would call almost is my whole weather: the syrupy migration of gas deciding up. I am wet thought, timed with the breaths the warm ground takes under the bigger breath above.

Between these borrowed selves I learn there is no such thing as one job. Today’s archivist sprouts hair and learns veilmaker to shelter a neighbor when the hot cycle steps through faster than expected. A lantern pad along a raised course that has been opening and closing to humidity since before your year names were considered hardens its rim one pulse and poisons the air an arm’s length around because that is the spacing the lane below prefers. We carry humor by habit: a plaza that refuses to be the center of itself for a decade refuses, then grants, then refuses; it trains patience in those who would use it as a pass.

Sometimes I grow a roof. Thicken, not rise. The sky here does not reward ambition that points. I take the slow organ of mineral and persuade it to bud into blind mouths. Each cup is a cradle for water, a place it can be motionless enough to listen to. There are thousands of us making this low bunker, and not a one is in charge. Our sense of self, if you require it, is a net humming at capillarity and cohesion. We feel thunder as a pressure on our room and answer with a phosphor that puts no one on alert and everyone at ease. Across the quarter, other films who have learned our pulse ripen their seeds toward the flush so the children will go far in a world where far is how water remembers you.

I would like to tell you I have an eye. What I have is prediction. The lid above is a language I read with skin. I sense the inward-turn of the air the way a finger feels the next chord even in silence. Before the reversal walks through and the city flushes itself, I push a little salinity into certain cells so they will endure the thinness. In the hour of contraction the lanes lighten, and there is, everywhere, the scent of stone uncoupling. Then, inevitably, the weeping returns. Not kindness. Not cruelty. A metronome willing to miss a beat if missing is part of the longer song.

Do I see you? I notice the salts you shed. I register your temperature like a local storm. If you drag your hand across a brittle wall you ignite a gossip-line ten rolls over. For a week I factor your gesture into my script. Your sandals, if you brought them, are a ridiculous kind of foot. They take a little of my glaze on their soles and donate, in return, a new set of grooves so shallow they are moral rather than physical. The lanes will eventually forget you correctly. The city gives all of us the mercy of erosion: not as enemy, but as schedule.

Mostly I am many. Identity here is a function, not a coat. I belong where rules bargain. Find me where gravity argues with itself and needs a mediator: along the underside of a cornice teaching a bead to creep the wrong way because once, long ago, something like a pipe taught that path and memory is a better engineer than force. Find me at the back of a colonnade where condensation has been inscribing itself into literacy; I am the faintest extra layer that convinces a future to go left. Find me in the sharp dry at the center of a wet square, arms raised like a devotee to the heat’s temporary sermon, saving the emptiness that the balance asks for. Find me in the knuckled bench at the toe of a slope where old slides have all agreed to stop, counting the small rumors running up through the gel and parceling them out to pores as if to mouths.

If you must name me, call me Interval. I am the length of time it takes a city to convince a drop. I am the distance between a rule and its exception, where accommodation happens without anyone being consulted. My job is memory written as surface. My humor is chevron. My prayer is the soft sound heat makes when it clicks against wet.

It is easy to suppose there were builders. It is harder, and more exact, to let your body learn that the city is the body, and we are the tissues that keep that body in the dignity of its habits. I go on as practice, as patience, as a ledger. I vote in gravity’s council. I file in water’s mind. I answer to nothing louder than the next drop, and I get to be here for the long exactness: the slow correction that never ends, the always-self that is never quite still.

Where would you find me? On the wall that has almost learned to read itself. On a span of water that is not a bridge until we convince it to hold together between stones that have lost their right to be there. In the green that comes and goes behind a baffle when thunder thinks closer. In the taste of gypsum at the heart of a plaza that stays dry for a reason you can feel but cannot prove.

What am I? I am what this valley uses in place of hands: a nap on stone and a habit in time. A scribe that writes with wet and a keeper that tends the brief islands of dryness the balance demands. A cable that listens to stress. A cup that leans a breath toward continuity. A we that knows itself as work. A patience that is not passive. A life that has chosen the interval between warm and wet as its only necessary shape.
