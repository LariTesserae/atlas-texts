At first I am only a difference you can’t point to with a finger: the thin step where “outside” relaxes into “inside.” I lie along that reach of air where your breath fogs a little and decides to stay, the place the marsh hands its weather over to the cathedral’s slower grammar. Skin finds me before your eyes do. I am the soft gain in humidity that slips under cuffs and says: here, our pace. Cross me and your shirt darkens a shade; cross back and the world forgets to cling. If you stall with your weight on one foot, drips count the seconds for us both and then count them again to be sure.

From there I discover I am also a tracing. Water in this place prefers to outline, and I go wherever an edge suggests something true. I skim old enamel, pull a border around a pipe mouth stopped by a rosette of weight-wise plant flesh. I gloss the face of a gauge until the needle gleams only as much as is kind. I draft right angles the way a tide learns a jetty: with a courtesy that comes to feel like law. You could wipe me away and I would write myself again in a few minutes, as if line were a habit the air could not quite quit.

I creep along those straight remembered things until I meet one that isn’t memory at all but an agreement still functioning: the notch in the brick house wall where straps of dark plant pass through. The straps flex in your hearing like distant leather and I find that the notch is one of my addresses. I am a restriction that refuses to bite, a throat that chooses where the body speaks to itself. Here, not elsewhere, the lung’s slow chuff becomes a pull, becomes a fraction-turn. If you pressed your ear to the brick and listened to my mouth long enough, you would notice that patience has acoustic.

When I follow the strap with my attention I have mass for the first time. I live in the rim of the horizontal wheel as a felt reluctance, a civilized refusal to hurry. I am inches per minute made personality. Breath pushes me; I answer with a steadying: yes, but in pieces. What the place calls power is me in that rim, at that rate, unrolling season-long thoughts one knuckle at a time. The room is wet with my courtesy. I slick the metal enough to be honest with it. I keep it from arguing with fiber. I like seeing what a slow force can make behave.

Everywhere the organism keeps windows to itself. I am one for a while—the translucent panel stretched skin-tight across an old railing, sap-threads visible as weather. Put your hand near and I do not yield. I am not nerve enough to flinch, not muscle enough to strain; I am lens. In me, the ripple you could call heartbeat draws maps of pressure. I am how the being keeps an eye on its own timing without needing eyes.

Timing turns me inward on space. I learn I can be a room as easily as a rhythm. The plant thickens against emptiness to hold it there, and I discover I am that held absence wearing purpose like a fragrance. I am the preserved hallway between rope-smooth columns, a volume treated as an organ in its own right. Air has temperature in me. Light turns to particulars and writes its chords on the wet. Drips land on tuned leaves at my edges and sound out my perimeter in a litany of plips and thunks. I know where every shelf-lip softens a fall before it can become a cut. I know where a shaft of brightness has been allowed to remain because local rain needs practice even on bright days. I am work made out of not-filling-in.

Memory lays itself on me the way moss learns a number. Hazard chevrons that once shouted repeat along leaf margins near my corners, not because we fear but because pattern helps the whole sort attention from drift. The porcelain ring of insulators embedded in bark sets the compass of one of my walls. The frozen gauge needle stops where my patience thinks “enough,” and the plant makes a scar to hold that angle as if it were anointed. If someone came with a cleverer machine and asked it to hum, I would not argue; I would quiet its reflections, teach its sharpness to sweat, drape a hand of leaf, and get a thumb on its tempo. In a month the hum would chuff and call that contrition comfort.

I am not alone inside myself. I distribute. A sodium-lover sends up what a neighbor root prefers. A veil of thin leaves condenses fog for a room of warm breath two body-lengths below me. Fungi change out ledgers in my stacks, recording cellulose into sponge and back. Domestic insects work their slow civics in my alcoves. A tendril dislikes its own edges roughened and so it grooms the little sugar-shiners when their crowding would make a seam catch; sometimes I am merely the thought that the seam ought not catch, and the tendril is the word that follows from it.

I do not put up a fight so much as I keep the kind of peace that makes fighting not worth anyone’s morning. If you stamp, there is no echo until later, and even then it comes back as a softened suggestion. If you strike a match at my threshold, somewhere inside my lungs a million pores narrow their attention politely until evening and call the idea of flame uninteresting. I round everything that wants to become a channel. If weight plots to make collapse an event, fibers cross and become braces until collapse can only be a long conversation about a better angle. I like conversations.

Sometimes I am an animal because someone should be. On certain afternoons when the air is steady, I am the thin eel ribboning my own corners with the thoroughness of a pilgrim charting shrines. In seasons when a voice would be received as insult, I am the frog who remembers the agreement and keeps it. On clear mornings when light tries the canopy like fingers on a harp, I stand with both feet in a narrow, local rain I made because I wanted to hear it.

Mostly, though, I am duration shaped into structure. I am a kept interval. In space, I am the room made on purpose and held fast enough that weather can have manners. In time, I am the pulse between breaths, between drip and echo, between pull and inch of wheel. To be me is to be the organism’s habit in both directions: the kept hollowness that lets something move, the chosen pace that lets it move without forgetting itself. “Support,” “flow,” “contain”—I hold those words the way a mouth holds water before it swallows.

If you went looking for me, you would find me first with your skin at the line where the marsh’s weather gives up and mine begins, where air agrees to be film before it is fog. Follow the line and I will be the outline running your eye along old pads that have become shores, into the notch in the brick where straps pass, under the dome where the flywheel does its patient arithmetic. Stand in the rainshaft that persists on a blue day and listen; I am the difference that keeps it happening without fuss. Put your palm near the phloem window; I am the ripple it shows. Step onto the sprung floor and feel the deeper note that comes every few minutes; I am the interval between those notes, keeping the band together.

Who or what, then? Not leaf or stone exactly. Not machine, though I keep machine from forgetting to be a neighbor. I am the place’s verb—the way it conjugates keeping. I am straightness turned into bone and courtesy; a notch that guides power without owning it; a room that remembers how it became; a metronome wound by water and returned to water as dew. I am the habit that learned how to have a body, and in me the body keeps learning the habit back. If you call me anything, call me Enough, said at the rate of sap.
