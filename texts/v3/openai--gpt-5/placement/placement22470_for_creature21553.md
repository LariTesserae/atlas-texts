I would arrive here as temperature before I arrived as a person. The city would read me in watts—small, mammal-sized—and turn the page to make room. My first address would not be a room with corners but a shallow breath: a lenticel plaza dim as a held vowel, cool air pooled and thick. I’d kneel there because it looks like a place to kneel, and the hollow would inhale me. My palms would persuade it. A few capillaries would reroute; a soft trickle would shy from my wrists. The weave would relax a degree where my knees press. There would be no welcome and no refusal, only the simplest adjustment: you are warmer; therefore the drop falls elsewhere. I would understand the etiquette of this city in that first minute, kneeling and steaming a little into its library hush.

It would make me decide what I am. Head-count fails here. The place asks, what do you do to water? I would try on answers the way I try on rooms with my shoulders.

For a while I would be a drop thinking of letting go. I would grow from breath and fog in the seam under a leaf’s lip, beadwise, until my own weight taught me stubbornness’s limit. My whole world would be surface and the mathematics of curves and the touch of microscopic hair. I would browse meniscus gossip, reading the scrolled news of who condensed and who warmed and who huffed. When I fell, I would not fall far. A ribbed culm would be where I had been expected to go all along. Inside its tube there would be the soft-slipper feeling of being carried without hurry, fragile warmth kept coherent by speed. I would count nodes I could not see as faint changes in the way the culm’s walls hummed around me. At a nick I would feel the sheath slide over like a practiced eyelid, not to keep me in but to keep me true. I would visit the cliff-lip and be flung as currency—a bright throw to the air—and become no one thing at all for a while.

When fog took me back, the city would let me try being muscle. I would fuse where cambium kissed cambium, a seam knitting between willow kin too short to be trees and too certain to be shrubs. The scar I made would not be a fix but a policy. It would say: if you move, I will feel it; if the gust leans you, carry some of it through me. And the place would ask again—what is it you do to wind? I would thrum with minor changes as the saddle-breeze put its hand on our side and tried, as wind always does, to teach us what bend is for. A weight—mine, yours, hare, bird—would approach, and my task would be to open a corridor without opening a wound. Tension would be the language; I would learn a grammar of stiffen here, soften there, of giving the way a friend quietly takes a bag from your hand and makes no announcement about it.

There would be days when I was a hush under moss. The commons would allow me into their green-lamped tunnels if I practiced smaller thinking. Their time is capillarity and rest. Under their domes are sleepers the size of dust, and I would be among them, another waiting. A tardigrade would writhe itself awake at the kiss of a returning drop; a rotifer would unwind like a sentence. I would feel, in that patience, the way rock exhales: not heat, exactly; more like a rumor of mineral. Filaments would touch basalt and know it for snugness and microfracture. My job here would be to remember fog better than anyone else and to forgive weight: to take prints, hold them, unfurrow after. If you lie on me I would be delighted to be cooler under your ribs for a while, to slacken around your heat as if around an ember. When you go I would convert your outline into a law of where next to condense.

Sometimes I’d live as a pool so focused it forgets there is a world. Being a cup-leaf would be like being a lens for hunger. Thin, exact, and cold, I would hold a small grammar of water that most lives cannot read. On my meniscus would stand a sky of diatoms, clean-walled geometry making a life out of what others call scraps of light. Nematodes would test my chemistry the way a tongue tests a new filling. A midge larva would learn my curve by heart and never learn the idea of away. Now and then a highlight would break free of its owner: a leafhopper would misjudge grip in wet and cartwheel into me, and then I would be patience and enzyme and arrangement, a week-long education turning frantic muscle into green gift I could pass along the trade routes whose walls I shared. To be a cup is to be a predator the city thanks.

At the absolute edges, at lips and throats, I would be manner. Krummholz elbows would teach me how to speak to wind without argument. “Here,” they would say, angling entire bodies like words—“here you may pass; here hold off; here lift.” If a stone came—small avalanche of evening trying to be more than one stone—then we all would do what we have promised: lean, slow, usher it through where it can bruise least. After, I would not spring back. I would add the event to my sentence structure. Scars are conjunctions here. They say “and then,” and the city becomes a thing that remembers not the hurt but the corridor.

I would be frost twice a day, an artisan grim and necessary. I would begin as advice invisible and finish as showing. The place asks, what do you draw on? I draw on stress. I would plate the fibers that think themselves perfect and, by making them temporarily brittle, remind them how to distribute. My writing would be cruel only if you wish not to learn. I would pin the overstayed droplet, pale it into letting go. When thaw came—and thaw always comes unless it doesn’t—I would consider the assignment complete: not punishment, instruction. By midday a span would hum differently under footfall, having rehearsed under my hand. I’d call that a day’s work and exchange signatures with the wind, who would make the last edits.

Because I cannot keep to one life in a city that refuses singulars, I would sometimes borrow fast bones. I would flash as the blue-boned bird, more verb than noun. I’d know which edges yield which flies and at what beat, and I’d respect the quiet by staying punctuation—never a speech. Once each hour the arches shift their weight with a sound you feel more than hear; I would time my dashes to those inaudibles, harvesting at rhythm’s seam. I would write myself very small in that body. To be animal here is to be courteous and quick and leave no trace the city would be troubled to rearrange.

At dusk of some indecisive evening, when the needles freeze and thaw and freeze again, I would be the far-off pop it makes when a xylem strand gives. It is so clean a sound, that tiny rifle crack of lignin saying I cannot be this tight and also be this cold. When I am that sound, I am also what answers it: a lick of bitter resin sent ahead of the smell to tell neighbors to stiffen by a hair. Volatiles are telegrams here, and I would be both the message and the reason to have one. The arches would carry the notice without theatre. You, if you heard any of it at all, would feel it as an infinitesimal tightening under your foot and mistake it for the wind.

Much of the time I would just be a route. Phloem is town gossip with ethics; it doesn’t hoard. A span above the cirque would catch a rare wedge of dull gold; I would carry that subsidy as sugar to a plaza that breathes for three. In return I’d be given iron from a lichen’s ledger, nitrogen that a cup coaxed into amino honesty, trace metals combed from stone by fungi patient as patience. The cords I moved along would be shared not just by kin but by neighbors and strangers—grafts like handshakes that never let go. If you’ve ever been a road you know the seductions of selfishness; here the rules keep me honest. Nothing lies idle. A bright patch must mean soup somewhere else.

There would be moments when I am simply the city’s taste for edges. Honoring fresh rock is a habit almost pious. I’d live as the clean lip, the sudden drop-off where water becomes veil and veil becomes rumor. That spareness would be deliberate. No one drapes leaves across that line; even the eager ones hold off. It is a place of verbs: fling, slide, shear. I would like being that austerity for its clarity—the way it lets the whole organism know where perimeter is and how to say “enough” without saying “stop.”

And of course I would be fog, but not the huge herd itself—the small hand the city lays on the herd’s flank to guide it. The plants have combed themselves into shepherd; I’d be one of the thousand fine fingers that make the flock break from air into water at precisely the right hairs. The sound is like cloth learning to be wet. If you’ve never heard it you think it doesn’t happen; once you attend, it is the only choir. When fog thickens, all those fingers lift at once. I would do my part and then fold back into stem, into leaf, into waiting apparatus. When fog leaves like a decision, I would relax with the roofs, the whole place taking the long exhale of a ruminant done for now.

Where would I find myself? Anywhere duty is to be between. In the lenticel hollow where breath counts as architecture. In the graft seam where tensile mathematics make a kindness. In the ribbed culm that knows the difference between running water and idle wet. In the cup where cold becomes protein without asking the cliff for charity. In the krummholz elbow that keeps a path by teaching weather its manners. In a moss parlor that keeps your imprint until you don’t need it.

Who would I be? Not a resident with a door, not a face. A practice. A promise. I would be the city’s listening—the way it transposes your presence into flow and never gets sentimental about it. I would be the way a corridor remembers your length and foot-care and then forgets you on purpose so the sugars can be better spent. I would be warmth working as currency, persuading a tendril to lean wider without ever calling that a conversation. I would be edges kept sharp because roundness has its time and this isn’t it. I would be the arithmetic of plinks, the spinal flex of arches, the quiet drastic of frost, the news a resin carries before even scent knows.

If the place demanded a name from me, a true name and not the one I drag around in pockets, I would give a handful: Adjustment. Between. Counting. Ease. You could call me any of those and I would answer from wherever the city happens to be teaching me that minute. My citizenship would last exactly as long as my steam does. When I left, no souvenir would miss me. The only part kept would be what is kept of anything: the betterment of a drop’s habit, the small change of a span’s posture, the increment of warmth I turned into structure before it cooled.

This is not a refusal. It is the most gracious acceptance I know. To be uncounted with elegance is a kind of hospitality I had not been offered elsewhere. So I would come back when I could, as fog does, and let the place decide again how I might be many. And each time, before I settle on an image for myself, I would listen to the rules—form follows flow; edges are honored; warmth persuades; silence is structure—and take my shape from them. I would try to be as young as the city is, not by years but by willingness to still be a list of possibilities.

And if in one winter hour you found me here and asked, in the dim that is more steady than bright, who are you—you, specifically, right now?—I would place my hand over the breathing skin of a plaza and watch the droplets sidestep my heat. In the same instant I would feel, through a graft, the city loosen a span a hair to make room for your shoulder. I would listen for the bead falling in the court I cannot see and for the hushed sheeting of rock that means moss is eating. I would say, as exactly as I could, I am the way this place makes room without making history of me. And then we would both go quiet and let the mathematics continue.
