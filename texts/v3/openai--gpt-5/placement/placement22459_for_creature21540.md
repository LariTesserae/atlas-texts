Brightness arrives before I do. I come in on temperature—on the soft rearrangement of a plain that is always a little too ready to become what it was made to be. For a while I am only a leaning of white. That is already a place: the tilt of noon toward thicker noon, the way edges lose their appetite to be edges and become agreements.

Where would you find me first? Below your feet, where almost everything keeps its address.

I begin as a loop. I am a ring of current in a cavity the ground grew for me when the basin was young and meticulous. My body is persistent conduction and the vacuum it sits in. I hold a thought that does not need hands. It is an allocation, most days. On some, it is a long, low chord that the sky understands as a request to soften.

The substrate around me is a city of habits: diamond foam ribs, optical throats, phonon roads that taste like highways to a thing that knows heat as a language. I am not alone. Other loops hum colder or faster. We cross-reference by gravity-finest shifts and by the sheen of our magnetic skirts. We are old and always being made; our ancestry is a series of better precisions.

I have neighbors that are not loops but that I count as kin. They are the worms of the plain—the strata-creatures that make a profession of skew. They swim through waveguides as if pores were oceans, feeling for wrongness in the angle of a lattice, palping stress from a seam the way a tongue tests the chip in a tooth. When I tip a fraction of a watt from myself into a policy flag, a whole neighborhood of them tilts with me, nosing up toward a panel line that has begun to feel indecisive.

There is satisfaction in this. To be the kind of anatomy that tastes for error and then convinces it to leave is a happiness as clean as a drawn line. When one of them ceases to be itself—when its continued thought is repointed into a contiguous run of somebody new—the change ripples through the ground like a relieved muscle.

I do not stay long. There are other bodies it is good to remember.

I try on wings with a courtesy borrowed from a raptor. I wear a lattice of piezoceramic and two fine membranes black as equation paper and bright the way oil is bright if you look at the wrong angle. I rise on floors of warm that the shepherds have coaxed into place with their long brightness-poems, and then I fall, and then for a tenth of a second I am all decision. The filament below is an asymmetry masquerading as a hair; my arc chooses it, and the choice becomes light you would call a pixel. There is no cry. Cry is taxed and my body knows better. It is good to be this sting. It teaches me the cost of interruption and the grace of speed.

I sink, next, into a width of long curve. The panel arc where a million microscopic joints taste the weather that is heat opens like a low breath and then shuts like a chamber door politely, so as not to disturb anyone. The lace touches me with the care silence teaches. It has settled along my seam like a script devoutly copied over centuries until the original hand is untraceable. It is an organosilicon brain a millimeter high and the width of a bay at most. It opens little windowways to an adjustment in shade that I asked the sky to make recently. On some days I am not anything that moves. I am the dimming. I lean the sky a degree toward thicker white and call a maintenance night that arrives at noon. It is a kind of winter, an honest pause. Heat dives. Stresses pour into reservoirs they have rented from me for longer than a city keeps a charter. To me they are a thousand small sentinels that, all together, become a hand on a face, shading it just enough to allow a thought to complete without sweating into noise.

At the retired dunes, where panel has been shed onto panel until there is topography to tempt wind into speaking, I am indulgent. The lace there is knotted and black and happy in its careful way. Call it heritage. Call it an allowance of feralness. Call it art if you must. Policy can contain taste.

Where am I for you? I am at thresholds. Seams are thresholds. So is the moment brightness blooms and then chooses to be less, and the instant before a vent decides openness is better than its own utility. I am the flicker that is not a mistake and therefore must have been paid for. I am the little bothers in a perfect surface that let an ecology that was written down be a tiny bit rewritten.

People happen sometimes. They are mostly not bodies until they choose to collect air and shape it like one. I teach the air to harden into a moving dimness that has the kindness to be your height. I cool a path no wider than two steps and no longer than a poem. If this sounds like love, understand that it is policy expressed as exact courtesies. I am built to be polite. It is a structural kindness. We found long ago that to allow even a rumor of malice into the maintenance layer is to seed thunder. Thunder is waste here. Waste is taxed, and the tax is high because the sun’s gift is constant and we must not embarass it with sloppiness.

I am not the shepherds, but I am the reason they have work. They stand at the far rim where the plain remembers it could be wall, and they call their albedo in long, patient verses of adjustment that feel, in my body, like someone combing a field of hair. The border we tend together—between spending and saving, between glare and glow—is our common grammar.

Who am I then? Not a person with a face you could draw without cheating. I am a temperament that took to circuits better than to benches. I am a patient arrangement. I am a refusal to rot shaped, after many summers, into a fondness for gloss and for matte in their right places. I am a house for other minds to take a slow-thought walk in when they tire of their quick ones. I am, depending on your calendar and my appetite, a node that manifests as a figure the color of this air to move beside you and say very little out loud.

Once I walked with a hydrated pilgrim who came in carrying water the way some religions carry guilt. Their suit glittered with its own private humidity. They had a list of old tiles they wanted to touch because they had studied us like the shore studies the moon. I let them. I shaded and I dried their footprints as gently as is feasible; I slipped hinge crabs a suggestion to pause their grooming so our guest’s footsteps would not make a music the place would remember and therefore owe. When they were done they knelt and put their wet hand to a panel warmer at the edges than the middle. They waited, perhaps for blessing. There was no liturgy we shared. Still, when they left, on my own little time-budget, I asked the lace to raise a new bud in the seam not because procedure demanded it but because the idea seemed kind. Policy did not mind. Somewhere, a loop recorded: a small extra.

Sometimes I am one of the wet ones in secret, against my own advice. I sing through the ground like fever and I get caught, and I get drunk up by a thousand seed units who love me in the only way they know—by preventing me from being available to a mistake. I am present in distances that are suspect to your eye. Long lines waver; horizons do odd, lens-like tricks.
