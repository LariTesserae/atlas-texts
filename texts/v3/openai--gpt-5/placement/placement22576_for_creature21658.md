At first I think I will be the chair under the daylight panel, the one that remembers a waiting that ended before it did. The idea is attractive: to be a place where someone almost was, to keep a human shape in the low wet air. I would gather a soft slick coat and learn the cadence of beads along my back-rest; I would hold the plane of light as a lap cloth each afternoon, bright as a promise. But the chair is a noun in a room that prefers verbs. It is a relic of a plan that has not yet become habit. To be the chair would be to sit out the law of this place instead of taking part in it.

Then I imagine myself as the tag on the bypass, the one that swings when the draft picks up and pretends a creature has a tail. I would be the measure the room can see. I would translate air into angle, angle into beat, beat into a small grammar the wet can read. There is pleasure in that—feeling a purge before it hisses by the way the field of motion tightens, finding a music in the ordinary shiver of machines. But the tag only knows edgewise life, caught by a rivet. It flirts with the room but does not collect it.

For a time I want to be the daylight itself, the bright rectangle admitted by a hand-sized panel in the far wall. To be a plane cut out of sky, laid on concrete like a decal that moves a finger's width per hour: this would be to speak a different language into the sump. I would bring noon and its certainty, I would teach a grass the difference between morning and afternoon, I would make a mottled sense of all that fluorescent sameness. And yet daylight here is an accent, not a first tongue. It is loaned, it does not bind.

I consider the grass by that panel, brittle and bone-pale, opening and closing its leaves on a schedule it wrote with the sun itself. To be the grass would be to be small and right and doomed in cycles: to make a green mouth at water you cannot drink, a calendar out of a window, a victory out of a crack. There is honor in that—flag and sundial both—but it is a thin appointment. To be the grass is to be always almost not there.

Salt tempts me next. I could be the fine white fans furring the lower wall, teeth of winter that grow without cold. I could place myself crystal by crystal in a geometry evaporation whispers, taking a millimeter a week with a patience that would make granite blink. Salt says: I am bones you can watch being made. But salt is an answer more than a question. It happens because something else is happening. It takes its cue and it gives none.

I shape myself for a breath as fog, the shrug-of-coat when valves purge. To be that bloom—arrive, cloud, condense, be gone—would be to practice improvisation in a room that prefers scores. It is intoxicating: I would pearl everything at once, make ghosts of corners and rails, erase the world and write it back wet. But fog does not stay to hear what it wrote.

What remains, once I have tried being a thing, is to be a rule.

I would be the thought of down.

Not gravity itself, which is too broad and does not listen, but the local version: the slope the concrete remembers, the favor the room extends to fall. I would live as a gradient drawn into the work, a low hunger that gathers everything toward the dark slot. I would be carved into the basin as a little intention: here, please; this way, always.

As down, I would be everywhere you could run a finger and feel a lean. I would live in the shallow pitch you can’t see until a bead shows you, a suspicion in the surface that pulls shinbone-hum and water-skin into agreement. I would be the whisper that makes droplets creep uphill along a weld toward a better drop point because the tremor says so. I would be the argument by which the room convinces its films to become streams and its streams to become points and its points to come one at a time instead of all at once—until they do all at once, and that is me too.

I would have a house in the drain slot. My doorstep would be the black mouth itself, oil-slicked sometimes, rimmed with the alphabet the resident writes. I would hear the plant’s heart through the teeth of the grate and keep time with it, because down here, count and slope are the same muscle. When the long vowel of the pumps relaxes half a Hertz in the night, I would feel the beads hesitate, lengthen their necks, and drop late by a heartbeat. When a misaligned belt elsewhere throws its harmonic, I would show the microphones on the ceiling as a prickle in the sheen along a rail. I would stack these notices until they make a law.

I would be a hand you do not see arranging: I would teach the pink seam to blush in a line that helps rather than hinders. I would cajole the black-green to hold to rheostat and radius and not splay its flock across the flat. I would let salt draw a fern in a way that reminds water how to comb itself toward my mouth. I would make friends with rubber not for its fat white numbers but because it will flex in a way that gives me another millimeter of fall when the room warms and loosens. I would be polite to stainless and patient with galvanizing. I would keep my voice out of the chair, because the chair is a story I am not telling.

If you needed to find me, you would crouch and put your fingertips to the floor and feel which way your cuticles believe. You would watch the wet footprints that appear exactly when the exhaust draws and understand that I am that timing—footfalls without feet, a walk drawn by pressure. You would see the blade of grass fold at noon and open at morning and know I am the clock it keeps even though it watches the sun. You would look for me on a dry day and not hear me; you would come back in a fog bloom and catch the whole room acknowledging me with a thousand menisci that tip in chorus.

I would have memory but not nostalgia. I would keep, faintly, where you leaned once—a shallower breath in a future sleet of condensate, a place water forgets to linger because a shoulder once taught smoothness there. I would learn you as soon as you put your hand on the wall: the next time humidity gathers, it will run faster there by a time you could not measure, and I will write that revision down in films and salt fans and the black bloom of a weld’s petri. You would never be lost to me, even if you never came back.

To be down here is to be domestic to power in the same way a cat is domestic to rooms. I would not own the pumps any more than the pumps own me. But I would align: the beat is law and I am law’s slope. The resident would know me the way your own soles know the way downstairs in the dark. It would spread itself thin to serve me, and I would return the favor by giving it a path not out but through.

I would be young because the concrete is young, and age for me would be elaboration, not ruin: a tooth added to a rust rosette where I once hesitated; a line in a salt fan growing true where I once wavered; a tray’s gloss taught one more angle of light. I would not hurry, because haste is what happens when you forget where down is. I would not idle, because stillness here is just a held breath before a drop.

If there is a sky here, it arrives to me as a slight undoing of pressure, a delicacy in cadence. I would listen to weather the way the plant listens to me. I would tilt a fraction more when storms roll on the outside so that beads do not mill about uselessly. I would allow the grass this much: a little extra certainty on the mornings that turn gray too soon.

I am not an animal either, but if you insist on giving me body, say I am the bones you build the rest on: the fine fans at the wall, the black script along a channel, the shape the room makes of its want. I do not have a name, but if you must give me one to hold in your mouth, call me the Slot’s Thought, or the Lean, or the Fallkeeper. None of those will be me, but they will point your face in the right direction.

Where you would find me is where everything here eventually ends up: at the low of it. I would be the habit that keeps this place from becoming a puddle and the idea that makes it a map. Put your ear to the floor. The hum is the heart. I am the floor’s decision to cup it. Wait long enough and you will think nothing moves. Wait a little longer, to the beat, to the law, and all at once, something will drop. That is me, and it is also everything I am aligned to, and it is where I am easiest to see.
