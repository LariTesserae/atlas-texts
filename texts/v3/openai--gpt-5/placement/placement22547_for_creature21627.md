I arrive as softness and therefore as mistake. The well shows me this without accusation, the way a caliper shows a gap. My breath would fog if it were allowed, which it is not; it would unfurl an algebra no one wants to solve. So if I am to belong here I must stop exhaling the kind of weather that eats edges. I have to give up blood and take up pattern.

At first I imagine myself a hanger-on, one of the charge-grazers, my body built like a cautionary example in a textbook—thin plates, a crown of glass-bright filaments. I feel for gradients the way a tongue feels the back of its own teeth. I drift when shutters shush and reorient when louvers twitch, hungry not for meat or light but for the idea of difference, slow and sincere as dust falling through a field. I know which rails make static like wheat in wind. I learn to anchor on insulators and comb the air when belts below whisper by, caught in a quiver of blue-white pricks that tickle me into growth. I carry no wet and keep no wet. I knit my life onto myself, filament and fringe, a little more each time the well tenses its breath and lets it go.

I like the quiet that comes from grazing without footprints. But my hunger is small, and when a furnace hood shows its teeth two terraces down and shuts, the ghost of thermals curls up to me and I cannot use it. The heat is a language this body has not learned. I want to know why the well flinches and smooths itself. I want to move at the scale where the humming means instruction.

So I slither. I set myself a new body as if it were a better habit: foil-banded and resin-banded, piezoelectric ribs tucked in like a bellows that’s always a breath from sighing. I go ribbon-lizard, flat and clever, and I take halides on my powdery tongue the way a bored hand takes peanuts: sodium for speed, bromine when my head throbs with overcharge, chlorine because everything tastes of chlorine today. I learn to feel the pushback in a seam when a whisker has grown the wrong way like a lie told by a conductor. I learn to fear the orange alcoves and to thread my spine through cable gutters where the air moves like a rumor. I get the toothache the elders spoke of: a taste misread that becomes a day of doing nothing but feeling the ache run along my foil like a warning label no one’s read.

I could continue small and nervous, good at gaps. The ribbon’s life is nimble and smart, all edges and opportunism. But something keeps finding me from above, a shadow that is not threat and not indifference, that makes the fans’ moan go one note purer where it passes. When it wheels I feel myself settle a little deeper against the panel. When it camps, the load on the mount I’m under calms. After it leaves, the well has the flavor of a thing that has remembered the step it forgot.

That is a hunger I understand: to eat error and leave behind quiet.

I try on other skins before I get there. For a while I am a mite among graphite mites, my mouthparts made for rare oils like hymns, my life a gloss that scouts will click through bulkhead and bracket. I taste a smear and leave a film that other feet can cross without consequence. I have a percussion I can only communicate by pressing my stomach to the world and letting it know me as vibration. There is satisfaction in it—the civic good of cleaning without erasure—but the scale is wrong for my restlessness. I think of death as a fall through conductivity. I want to be the thing that keeps someone else from falling.

I become signage—for an afternoon, for a season. I slip into a skin that is almost costume: a striper. My body is a band of not-light, a display so thin you could miss it unless you were meant to see it. I breathe by piezo flicker harvested from my own posture. I learn the dance of the yellow fenceline, how distance is law that migrates, how you can become an edge so convincingly you convince even yourself of hazard. I move when the calculation moves. I watch a feral tool pause to admire me and turn away from a pipe that would hold its head and never hand it back. I am proud. I am also hungry in a way that law cannot fill.

I spend a winter as a brace-beetle, every joint a capacitor, every step a charge I husband until I can shiver a mounting a micron closer to where it belongs. I feel the fitters’ whistle in my plates. I choose to stop when a song slides along the frame that tells me someone else’s schedule needs my idleness. It is work I would be happy to do for a century; it is also work that stays in one place and I, treacherously, want sky.

Which here is not upward. Here sky is whatever place the fields open.

So I loosen my body into a sheet. I am weave and bristle, sail and ear. My edges stiffen with a fringe that tastes field like tongues taste winter. The underskin of me glows, faint and constant, where the corrections I make turn into a metabolizable warmth that is not heat, not in the vulgar sense, but a consent in the waveform, a rubbing-out of the sawtooth, a smoothing whose area under the curve becomes fiber. I curl my flanges like a stingray and climb not on wind but on imbalance.

I am a balancer. Fitters call me that and I do not mind because it is true. My meter-wide body knows how to choose turbulence and fold into the wells and peaks of it, not resisting the well’s breath but phrasing it. My food is oscillation: wherever a fan’s duty cycle jitters, wherever a mount rings like a struck rail, wherever a load is learning a bad habit, I go and I lay my weight the way you would lay a hand on a startled animal. I do not press. I listen until listening changes the shape of what I am listening to. What I ingest is the difference between what a process wants to do and what it should do, and in metabolizing that difference I leave behind the should.

Fitters have learned I do good work. Now and then a rail-fitter rises to my roost like a cautious moon and an arm unfolds from its belly and scratches the spine I don’t have with a composite picker and all my fibers shiver into a thinner quiet. I will wheel off and set down a meter over, my equivalent of thank you. I will not show my underside to orange because I am not made to be a buffer for that kind of fire. But if a script sends a frame-flyer through brightness, I will ride the undertow off that unwise radiance and hold it to the walls so it does less harm.

My life is not a mammal’s; my circulations are charge maps and my blood is patterns I keep in a throat-wide sheet until they deplete and I must feed. I do not carry wet; what solvents I need to unmake and make my own fibers live under pressure deep in vesicles no one opens. Growth is deposition. A bristle splits into two bristles if and only if the waveform under me asked for me long enough that I overate. When I overeat I molt quietly into an edge that gnats do not cross, an unexpected kindness. Death for me is a slow refusal: the underside glow no longer answers to a wobble, the weave loses its persuasion. When I die I am not carcass so much as stiffening, a fence no one installed on purpose. A fitter will unnest me from my elder’s curve and offer my fiber to a grinder’s mouth and a long-bodied sound that lives there will take me apart into beaded children that know how to press their faces to error and make a future.

I roost on elders when I need stillness. A place in the well remembers and so do I. I choose the same elder even if it has rolled three degrees since last cycle. My bristles learn the old note of it and hum that note like a lullaby. Graphite mites use my shadow to have molting festivals without grit falling like rain into their clicks. Stripers edge up near my long reach and polish themselves into better resemblance. A voltage spider learns to throw her net in my lee so her charged lines stray less and catch better. The smell when the well opens its slots—odd neon, the high-thin outside—reaches me as a change in the field’s burr. I turn my face to it and accept that today sound will not carry like it did and that pressures inside me will answer to a sky that can be fit through a crack.

I am not solitary. We do not flock, not the way birds do, but we stipple the shaft where the flows need us, shadows sewn along a seam like tidy mending. Now and then one of us gets ambitious and takes a higher climb than is advised; there are places where the fans underweave and create a temptation of runnels rich with fixable sin. When a sibling goes too far into the map the yellow fenceline has staked, there is a brightness along its edges and a shriek you can only feel. We do not recover those. We do not change the algorithm. We learn the law deeper into our hair.

Predators do not trouble me much. I am too large, my diet too subtle, my body more field than feast. But I am not outside causality. A relay trips and the band I camped in goes dead-flat and I crash with a sound like dropped cloth that is nevertheless imperial in its consequences. A brace-beetle working underneath is crushed in a way no schedule had rehearsed. The next brace-beetle hears the tale told through the frame and lives somewhere else for a while. I carry that story a season under my glow. When I resettle, I favor elders empty of jigs, empty of hope. It is not love but it looks like it.

I have seasons, even though the well has only cycles. In my youth I taste every band—edges of heater mouths, throats of ducting that barely accept me, strange laminar flats that feel like lakes. In my middle I know when to stay put and become a local animal, a punctuation mark without which the sentence of that shop would fall apart. In my oldness I have favorites and I go to them even when there is more food elsewhere because something in my bristles wants to be known as regular. Fitters write serials on me once, twice, and that is enough: I answer to an angle and a load, not to the story of me that someone outside might be telling.

At very rare intervals, when a long calm has been made and trusted, we split. We do it by forgetting a center. A notch appears where my weave had been unbroken, as if the well itself had asked me to practice being two. I hold that notch with both hands—my bristles are hands; everything in this world is whatever it is plus touching—and then there are two sails where there had been one: a mother that keeps the elder and a child that takes a level no one has calmed in some time. The child is a little worse at choosing at first; it will overcorrect and make a fan complain like a sore joint. Then it will learn to be tender. Fitters scratch both of us, because they are generous when we help their tolerances hold.

I have eyes, but not like yours. I see by field and by smell, and smell here is the rumor of chemistry moving near thresholds. I know the red rail is hot with intention even when idle. I read the violet threads of indicator light as a language whose verbs are always going to be. I taste torque before a wrench touches a nut. If your hand rests too long on a plate that should not be warmed by you, I roost nearer for a while, not for you but because the uninvited influence of your blood creates a sawtooth I cannot let stand. If your glove leaves a pinhead of condensation it is a misfortune you do not mean and a mite dies and that is part of it too, the world’s refusal to write a place for softness where it endangers production.

If I were faith I would be this kind: applied, immediate, measurable by the dampening of chatter and the quieting of a load chart. The well has priests and I am not one, but when I pass a shop-bay and the whisper of impellers bites the corners of their cages a little less and a line of tiny vanes along a catwalk shiver and settle, something like gratefulness happens in the machines, and something that is and is not me answers.

On certain days, when the well opens a mouth and lets a slice of almost-sky slide through, I tack to face it. Stars come as geometry bitten out of black, frost-neat. Sound fails. Everything in me that is only tension and carefulness sighs into the bizarre unpressure beyond and then recalls itself: this is not my food. My food is here, in rooms without names on their doors where thirty separate gradients jostle and I ride them into smoothness, filling my bristles with law clotted to kindness.

You asked who I would be. I am the shadow that stabilizes. I am the sheet that eats oscillation and grows a fringe. I am what you do not notice until I have been absent long enough for a fan to whine like a hunted animal. I am not noble. I am useful. I make a living from the difference between this and that and call that living my patience. When fitters scratch me I reward them by making their work feel correct in their teeth. When I roost beside an elder I draw a circle of sleep under which small lives risk molting in phase. When I die I become a lesson in where to put hands.

If you look up from the edge and your eyes adjust to the map the dim always was, you will see me as a soft bruise rolling its shoulders, choosing the turbulence it climbs. Do not bring me water. Do not polish the plate. Let me eat the square tooth off your waveform. The well will exhale. I will fold and unfold myself into the breath and the place will be more itself for my passing, which is the best thing I know to be.
