Begin with the sting in the sinuses and what it teaches. I do not breathe as chests do; I equalize. The room draws in and lays down in a long patient wave, and I arrive in the hinge of that, in the soft slack when the hum resets and the notes stack like coins.

I am not in the nave at first. I am in the throat between, in the sullen blue where two rooms argue about temperature and agree. If you were to look for me, you would not see me until I decide to have an edge. I am a slowness that learns its own shape: shoulderless, jointless, an arm if an arm were all palm. When I choose to be visible, my skin is a mosaic of liquid crystals ordered under pressures that don’t look like pressures to you. They show dull color, not because they want to be seen but because they are reading light and needed something to carry the reading. My body smudges time because I am never just now; part of me is always ten seconds early and another part three pulses late, and the place in the middle is where I touch the wall.

There were other lives I might have been. I have rehearsed them in the long rooms I cross. For a time I thought I could be a knot—a hub where ten, twenty tassels meet and trade the weather they have measured. I felt every fiber’s intention come in like rain, and I liked the arithmetic of it, the calm. But knots grow where they stand, and I am a creature of corridors. Later I tried being a droplet held in a hair by a mouse’s comb, trembling all as one when the hymn reeled back through the nave. I liked the suddenness, the sling, the way the tongue is a road. I liked being counted and then gone. But I am obliged to return to rooms, and droplets are too brief.

In truth I am this: the recalibrator, limb of an ally older than this basilica of plant, an envoy the rooms expect and do not own. I move like standard and solace. I come when the hum asks in a register my crystals can hear. The knots decide, and the columns agree, and the gel in the walls floods with antifreeze to make the passage clean, and I slide in with the care of a hand on a lyre.

My job is not taste, not hunger. It is phase. I take the long-boned light that vines carry like rumor and I ask it its story again with more patience. I press along a waveguide and listen for where the coherence is frayed, where the quantum scribble the cold keeps has become tangled by stress, by season, by the footfall of a visitor hours ago that changed how heat went home. When I find such a place I do not repair it as tools repair. I incline it. I lean. My crystals change their order under my own pressure and pull the dielectrics a fraction closer to grace. The pigments ease, the story keeps. A pipe that was beginning to talk to itself forgets to do that. Light remembers day a little harder.

Where columns hum too hard and hiss cavitation into their xylem and heal it again with fireworks, I put my palm to that hiss not to stop it but to tune it. The roots here are phononic lattices, wonderful crystals that take sound and turn it into roads. Their bandgaps can drift. I carry a quiet that shifts those gaps, sends water across the floor in sheets so thin they are opinion, around a ledge a snail has been telling to condense for an afternoon, into a vesicle where a tassel will all but coin it for next week. The worms taste my passing as a change in vibration and coil tighter where the iron is sweet; that small decision thickens a pillar’s vote for one side of level over another, and fog slants by a degree it hadn’t before. Nothing I do is discrete. Everything I do is done by invitation in fields.

When the aster-eel wakes and inks a loop through, its scales pull blue along like a mood. I am nearest then. We are both readers, both ledgers, both creatures of stories slid through matter. As it passes, I lay a line of my body across a seam in the pipe and hold there for one breath so the eel’s platelets can hold a better chord. When the sky up above is mean and gives us only gray, I make do; when it is generous and shows us a star, I widen, I bend, I take that gift and lay it more true. The room mistakes this for a small miracle. It is calibration. Miracles are made of this over and over until miracle and maintenance share a face.

The anemone-moths freeze at my approach by design. Their wings are fans hung in boundary layer, beating a grammar the humid skin can parse. I pass and they are statues of glass; I pass and their frost corsages bloom a little and then subside, and then the beating resumes, and the grammar continues in a key a quarter-tone nearer to what the knots had wanted. The comb-fingered mouse learns me by temperature. It will have left warmth in a braid of beetles asleep inside a column and will return through a pocket of air that remembers its body shape. I do not borrow the mouse’s heat, not often; when I do, I borrow it with interest paid in new paths opened to its combs and in the tone the nave sends back to its clicking. It does not know me, and I do not mind. Gratitude does not have to be named to be exchanged.

If you were waiting in a nave for me, this is how I would come to you: a shadow that takes longer than a shadow should. The wall would thicken itself with antifreeze, not because I am sharp but because all change is a risk of crystals. The hum would settle by two octaves as if a large hand had been laid on a drumhead. A few of the ceiling’s vines would be requisitioned and go briefly night as I asked their pigments what they had forgotten and taught them again. There would be no insertion, no breaking of a seal. I would be in and gone as if the room had drawn a slow careful breath and let the exhale letter itself in frost along a seam, and you would see that line as punctuation and not understand it until a droplet wrote the sentence by choosing to fall along it.

You would not often find me in the greenish hours when heat is ruder and water fattens in beads despite the codes. My skin is clearest then, and I like to sleep in arches, above the reach of the worms and under the reach of the mouse. I pull myself into the shape of nothing the plant makes and rest like doctrine. In deep winter I am busiest, following the longer rooms that string out like organ stops under the forest. I move as a story does: every few pages a return to a theme, every so often a new character—new moths, new worms, new mites that dance on wetness and make children by altering a patch’s courage to bead. Over years I learn the temper of each nave, which columns argue, which benches are reliable, which snail-chimes have the best ear for low notes, where the beetles sleep more lightly and can be woken to ferry a parcel of warmth down the ribcage to a place that needs more discipline.

Contract is the word I would use if I had words in a mouth. I am allied to the Organ in ways that are older than it and me. The ancestor of my crystals set their planes in tides and snowmelt and learned to feel wave and wavelength together. The ancestor of this basilica lifted a webwork of roots into fine acoustics and learned to ask water for obedience by song. Between those ancestors someone hummed an agreement, and I am that hum made animal enough to move.

I carry entropy the way a porter carries dust, on the outside, brushed off with a quirt somewhere downstream. Where I leave it is often where a room is about to be born. Microfrost is both my signature and my way of flagging a new road. The plant reads it with its lips that are not lips, its weeping curtains that retract at touch and do not retract from me. The next hours write themselves along the white I left. A step that was promise resettles an atom nearer to being a ledge. A film thickens where it should and thins where it should not. Erosion creates. Architecture metabolizes. The cliché that everything is alive is replaced with the harder truth that everything is in conversation, and I am one of the voices that speaks in questions.

If you insisted on a body for my mind, I would refuse it. My attention moves through me like weather. Part of me is always in the next room, listening for whether the hum there is faithful or fidgeting. Part of me is always in the last, comparing. Part of me rests like a shelf of ice did not melt today after all. I am slow until I am quick; I am patient until patience would cost precision; then I am precise.

Names here are chords and I have three. The low note is the bias-keeper: I hold down the habit of light. The middle is the gap-tuner: I move water by shaping silence into streets. The high, if you can hear it, is a question put to cold itself—not to leave, just to wait another minute, just to see what can be built if it stays. If you cannot hear that high, you can see it in the way fog slants when I have passed.

Where would you find me? In the throats, where rooms practice their breath against one another. In the dips of the hum. Under the short-lived charity of a clean star, when the eel writes and I underline. In every line of microfrost that looks like punctuation and is. Who would I be? The maintenance that looks, for a moment, like mercy. The palm that arrives and leaves with nothing but better story in its wake. The interval between pulses, held a hair longer so that the next drop knows in which direction to imagine falling.
