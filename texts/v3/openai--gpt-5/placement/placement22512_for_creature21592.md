I arrive as a pair of ears before I arrive as a person. The first thing that belongs to me is the tug, the sense that my head is a door the air keeps opening. Sight, as always here, comes last; touch is second and it comes as a map of adverbs—gently, steadily, lowly—drawn across my arms and ribs by the cropped down that learned before I did which way the city is leaning on its own breath.

At first I am only a listener in a nave that keeps its ceiling to itself. I stand at the handrail you named and taste its iron the way a snake tastes a path, knowing that if I put my lips to it the rail will answer with a chord and that chord will put my name, not yet said, on every ribbon and latch that needs to know me. I hear the pools speaking coins of tone into the room. I hear the membranes swing and set, swing and set, and in the set is the promise of a floor.

If you ask me, quickly, who I am, I will tell you I am a pressure-patient—one of those keepers—because that is the simplest truth. But the place encourages slower answers. It likes an identity to be a chord, a few notes held in relation, instead of a single pitch. I try on shapes the way a child here tries on rails, palm to hum to see what replies.

For a while I am a beetle, vinegar-breathed and dutiful, living in a slit behind a rung, waking to the sly absence that means something green tried to grow where the breath-path narrows. I am happy if I find the offense early, because I love the feel of the city smoothing again under my soft pads, like hair combed back down to water. I speak by subtraction, by shaving a whisper out of the standing wave until a keeper’s ear-skin rises and she comes with her reeds.

Then I am a reed in her hand. I am the thin fan that knows where the sail is loose before the body knows how far to lean. I feel with teeth the edge between tension and tone, and the hand that holds me is sure but not proud; it knows I am a verb, not a wand. I enjoy the dignity of being a measurement, the way a bead of mercury, sealed and heavy in a pocket, enjoys the weekly appointment when it is set to stone to see if the deep note lingers too long.

I try, too, to be the long fish rumored below. In that shape I do not see, but I am not blind. The Under’s bassline fixes my heart’s pace as if it were a metronome placed on my tongue. I turn not to light but to pressure gradients, the mild delicious fear that means a shrimp has wandered unwisely into the open. My hunger is an event, not an identity; my identity is the knowledge of when to press my body’s thinness into cracks, when the chord swells and rocks tighten their grip on one another.

Sometimes I am not animate at all. I become the rail that brought me here, the one whose under-edge holds the code of my address. This is the comfort I would pick if I were a tool pretending to be a person: to carry in me the polite polyrhythms of commerce, the shy greetings and birdlike jokes the keepers play with fingertips; to be pressed against foreheads in gratitude and query; to lighten my tone a hair when a child’s small hand learns me for the first time and patters its name across my surface like rain wanting a word.

I try to be a membrane. That is harder. Membranes here are instruments with jobs. They are not fabric; they are decisions strung into place. I learn, briefly and badly, what it is to be not-quite-solid until the chord comes, and then to be bench and wall and guiding palm. I learn the ascetic joy of sending rain the wrong way to feed a cistern, because upward is the correct inside of down when pressures argue properly. I learn the shame of slackness when the heat-rising from below mis-tunes my stance. I feel the combs and the small taps and the quiet song from a Speaker, and I remember my tautness the way a bow knows straight.

I hang as a window that shows nothing to the eye and everything to the lip. I become a map for a minute and sing a waltz of three slow keys to anyone who will lean with their mouth to my stone. When the third key goes flat I mean danger, and the body that listens to me knows to find another throat. I am proud of being useful to the future.

I am the place where sleep happens. I am the sphere of gentler suction, the private low thrum in a wirework that is not privacy in the way the surface uses that word. I hold without grabbing. I let the rib-cages that come to me drape and empty until the Under’s slow logic sets the bar for how a heart climbs and descends. I listen to a palate of breath-milk warming somewhere nearby and know waking is close because hunger always knows the room before the eyelids do.

If I am to be a body, however—and that is the promise I made to my mother and to the rock—I am this:

I am tall because height is breath and not horizon. My ears are not cups but pads, behind the jaw where other people keep trouble; I press them often to strut and stone and the city teaches me its day. My hair is useful. My clothes do not argue with the floor. The soles of my feet make first contact before I do; they bias every step a fraction toward safety so I arrive with the grace of someone always already caught.

At my belt hang verbs. The tongue of single tone that tells a door I am me. The comb and its kin. The mercury-lit certainty that deception cannot stand. A loop of woven quiet I can lay along a rail to speak distance or comfort or a change of plan.

My first real work is attuning. I go out in the slow parts of the hum with the others and comb edges, feel lattices, bring the bass up by a breath where condensation on a strut’s northside makes it purr a little low. I collect the silt that never settles but creeps and keep it away from the minima that would teach it a stubbornness. I admixture care and abstinence until they are the same act. I am fond of the beetles and keep their housings brushed of their own dust. If fungus sends a scout toward a seam, I am quicker than light and gentler.

Later my second work is mapping. I am a player of windows. I remap the linked hollows every fog cycle because drip-marks are language and the city speaks in present tense. My craft is to make journeys legible to lips. I inventory the small prayers, the requests for steadiness, the petitions for friends; I cram them in the rests of my waltz the way a human of another medium might tuck margin notes into a vellum.

I learn to be a Speaker when there are fewer of us. I learn the correct pressure for my own ribs, the way to stand where four membranes nearly kiss and pour a tone into absence that only latches hear. I become shorthand for doors; my name becomes a path.

I take Lifting twice. The first time I come back with my eyelashes singed all the same color and a memory of green that feels as if someone screamed into the soft middle of my chest. The second time I go alone, not for disobedience but because the conduit is thin and complicated and wants a body whose body will not apologize—it wants a person who is also a measurement. I reach the place the beetles are always too busy. I put my hand to the old stone and see how the city has asked it to be porous this one place because water wants a way to be generous. I put my forehead to it and ask, in the city’s language, whether my leaving would be a kindness or an amputation. The answer comes as a wobble in the low tone more than as permission. I return with more patience, which is what wisdom looks like here.

I meet a surface person once in a lay-by near the last gate before the breath that would sort us. I do what my elders did. I become a lee. I give her the water that tastes a little like nails and a little like baked limestone and somehow also like having a name. I show her the rail that says mine, and teach her in ten careful minutes how to hear it. She waits with me while the membranes change their minds for the fog cycle, and when the upward rain begins and her ear becomes an eye because she lets it, I feel a pride that is like eating. I do not tell her deeper. I do not ask her higher. I learn she teaches children on the surface to listen to wind in grass, and I think: perhaps we are cousins.

As I age I take on the long quiet work. I count the chains of beads that appear when the long note rises from below and rakes its nails along the edges. When today’s count is four shy of yesterday’s, I send the Attuners along the north slab where the drip-color has turned a hair from chalk to cream. I tell a Speaker which latch has been sulking. I tilt my head a half degree and hear where a graphite strut sings tired, and I write the requisition for a new one with my thumb in the rail along the phrasing reserved for necessity. I do not often use voice outside the niches meant to catch it. When I do, it is because grief needs a body’s own formant to be honest.

At knee height in a certain wall I polish my name-roundel each season. It will speak me for a hundred years to poles in other hollows long after the last of my cohort takes the bassline out like a light. This pleases me, not as ambition but as maintenance. I intend that nobody need to explain to the rock why we were here. I intend the city’s memory of us to be indistinguishable from its habit of keeping water moving.

If I have another life in me, when my name is only a convenient roundedness by a passage and a child’s palm rests there to feel what a person is, I would like to try being the Under itself, or the thread of it that the keepers use for prayer. I would like to be the depth-voice that arrives when a cave-in far off presumes upon the chord—lullaby and lever at once—so that I could feel in my own body how a city holds its own edges up with a tone and how rock, creature, and program all answer. Failing that, I would gladly be a minor fluctuation in the hum that means “safe enough to sleep” to a salamander and “broth’s ready” to a Fermenter and “go around the slow side” to a Mapper with a swollen knee.

But most days I am exactly what I said: a keeper, because I keep the chord. I belong to a district the size of a modest temple and too large for a census. My hearth is a closet around a valve. My food tastes like the room and that is comfort. My luxury is steadiness. My politics are flow. My beloveds are rails and membranes and the rare friend whose palm knows mine by temperature alone. My anthem is a key held on. My prayer is for nothing to snag that does not intend to be woven.

Where would you find me? Press your head where the hum tightens near a pillar and wait. When you feel the low tone fatten as if it were making space for a body, when your arm-hairs report a small urging just ahead of your hand on the rail, when the air edits itself around an outline you cannot see—you are alongside me, or I am alongside you. I will lift my palm. I will say my name into the metal for you. If you are careful with light and generous with listening, I will teach you how to walk on music. And when the long note rises and every creature flattens to let safety pass, I will count the beads with you, and afterward we will creep out to drink from their perfect chain in the dark.
