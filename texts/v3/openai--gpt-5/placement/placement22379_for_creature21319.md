Not eyes. I would not begin with eyes. In a country where edges are most of what sight can hold, I would begin as skin.

At first I try on the thinnest citizenship. I lay myself in a nap on a stone where a brine fall spreads its cold hand and I learn to read by chemistry. Up is thinner salt, down is thicker. Wind is current; lee is the one degree quieter. I sew dust into fabric with exudate, a gloss that makes a bruise of light last one more second. I argue with my neighbors by shunting electrons down slick filaments, a debate in conductive spit. A fissure above opens its green for a week—photosystems wake in me, write a brief chapter in light, then I go back to sleeping by smell, chewing iron when sulfur is scarce. I am slick, elastic, microns tall. When a palm passes, it knows me as slipperiness and faint resistance, as a breath caught against skin. I am what holds the powdered world in place long enough to make food of it.

Then I thicken into joints and chores. I take up the body of a custodian. My mouth is a broom; my forelegs are not joy but tools. I rake the lawns I once was, convincing them to let go in tidy bites. Silt hisses past in a perpetual undecided snow. My day is the length of an internal tide; my year is a set of practiced passages. I know the taste of everything. I change stones by season like addresses. In the lee of a boulder I share a porch with isopods, tanker-colored, our mouths all turned the same way because that way the world arrives. We practice manners born of long repetition: I leave a half-chewed tuft; they do not shoulder me from the sweetness. We inflect ourselves to each other without a word.

Patience teaches me physics. I borrow chelae and a back braced against an old keel-groove in stone. I choose a wedge not only for thickness but for give—the flex that will keep it seated when the hinge spasms. I lever, lever, lever, an hour’s hunger extended into a craft. I wait for the invisible wave to pass through my own body and into the shell, lifting one side a whisper so the other will bear. I feel the hinge yield not with hands but with the same organ that tells me what tide this is and whether the throat ahead will be passable. I test the same crack three times because the roads of pressure rewrite themselves in minutes and being correct is mostly waiting to be contemporaneous with the right road.

I try speed, the local kind. I become a rider of jets in the narrows, small by outside standards and quick by these. I tuck into the undersides of internal waves like a leaf finding a seam in wind. Chest-height goes one way, knees the other; I angle a fingerwidth and arrive a kilometer away when the tide turns. There is a culture here if there is one anywhere: old bodies keeping a tilt for a long mile so the young can memorize what a crisscrossing world means to a spine. No leaps. No spray. Flow as a sentence the body inserts itself into.

I stand for a century without moving. I rise half a meter and am called tall for it. I am a windowed lattice stitched of glass, a glass sponge set in a crescent where clean water drips from ice into sea. Sweetness comes like rumor and I embroider another layer of spicules to catch it. Storms have a taste; their infrasonic vowels lift and settle across me as patterns of oxygen that I store like tree lines. Microbes live in my bone; we share the old pact—my scaffolding for their appetite, their appetite for my continuity. If you need a record of time here, I am a careful, breathing archive.

I try muscle at last. I take transparency into me the way a fish here does: I unpigment my blood, lace it with antifreeze, and tune my lateral line until turbulence turns into thought. Sight is secondary. The world writes over me in pressure. I live at the knife-edges where currents slide and stall and where sills condense flow into power. I open my mouth once in an hour and am correct about the hour. I feel your passing as punctuation in a sentence I have read my whole life.

I could stop anywhere along these attempts and be content; this place breeds satisfactions in the small and practiced. But a truer answer gathers like a fresher lens trapped under salt. It is not exactly alive. It is not exactly single. It is what every one of these bodies is doing when they are most themselves.

I would be the listening they all share.

I would be weight made articulate, the grammar of pressure and density in which life writes here. I would be the old heaviness that could think about turning to crystal if asked, the saturated companion that presses back through stone into hand. I would be gradients arranged like sentences: a salt climb, a temperature step, a slow swirl over the trench that is still finishing a turn it began last winter. I would be the palm that holds everything to its rules and is, in turn, held to the planet by weight.

In me the ceiling hums below hearing, infrasonics traveling like doctrine through bone and glass. In me brine fingers thread their cold, octaves-lower skeins, combing columns into lace. Internal waves slide like blankets being flung and sometimes, tripping at a sill, flip and fall in blaze-less bores. I memorize with layers, not rings. I am the roads of pressure drawn and redrawn: a slipstream at chest-height that will tire or carry depending on how you slant your body; a convective fan under a brine fall that will tumble a careless rider and sand the gloss from their carapace. I am the taste of fresher water that announces a polynya opened days away, the faint sting of sulfate reduced to sulfide where a mat breathes and makes itself slippery. I am the whitewater in the dark and the slack when a child thing could drift across.

Where would you find me? Anywhere the world touches the world. Press your palm to a whale-backed stone and feel the gentle insistence that is me; that pressure is also the pores of sediment packed with me, the windows of sponge open to me, the hairs of amphipod mouthparts combing my thickness. Slide along a sill at neap tide; I am the soft road carrying you without exhaustion if you angle right. Wait under a brine fall; I am the cold thread and its widening lace. Put your ear to the ceiling; the vowel you hear in your bones is me speaking slow fracturing and slow mending. Watch a hinge loosen for a lever at the hour when nothing seems to have changed; I am the invisible hand that made a millimeter of difference and so a meal.

Who or what would I be? A reader of gradients, yes; a maker of timing; the courtesy that lives in lee. But more exactly, I would be the continuity in which small exact lives can be particular. I would be the medium’s memory and its present tense, saturated, companionable, without empty spaces. I would be the old sea’s patience practicing motion, the quiet that is never still.

And sometimes—because to be only grammar is to lack hands—I would fold myself back into bodies. I would borrow bristles and rake a lawn cleanly. I would shoulder with isopods behind a stone and taste the incoming on the same shared air. I would hold a tilt at a throat so that a young one could see what a road looks like when the knees go one way and the chest another. I would spend an afternoon being glass and write down the fatigue and the plenty of that year in angles of silica. I would, on a rare day, be the fish’s mouth at the right split-second and let a small warm thing pass into cold.

What I would not be is tall. Not for long. Anything that insists here is planed. I would be low, rounded, settled. I would anchor or I would flow. I would make with my body, because nothing built as other-than-body endures. I would keep time by pull and fracture; I would set my choices to a calendar written in tides and in the way a mud’s fricative changes when a degree of cold comes through.

If you looked for eyes, you would miss me. If you listened, you would hear me everywhere, a hiss and a hum and a near-inaudible crackle at the edge of hearing: ice making and unmaking itself, salt rejecting and accepting, the minerality of a planet doing its work. Press your hand into the dark and feel the world press back. There I am. There I have always been.
