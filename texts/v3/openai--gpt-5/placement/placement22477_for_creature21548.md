At first I think I am a visitor. I come in over the causeway, boots staying just out of the glaze, and the place arranges my breath for me: slow, wide, vowel-shaped. I hold my mouth a certain way to drink the air. My shirt takes the taste of coins. My ears find the note and soften around it. The rules move quietly through how my fingers touch things—slope the cup, don’t leave the droplet a level to live in, keep the flow—until the place is whispering in me even as I pretend I am not yet of it.

But the longer I stand under the fins the less I believe in the lean figure on the causeway as the thing I came to be. I feel myself bead.

I become a drop before I am anything else, because here that’s what bodies do. I gather on the underside of a ribbed plate, fattening on vapor that has been made to remember itself. I feel the rule in my weight—the angle they have given me so I must go. I thread along a serration into another drop and with that kiss my size doubles and I cannot hold. I fall and the fall is brief music: a patter on a louver, a small string of pearls on a rail, a glass bead slipping skin against painted steel. The rail gives me to a gutter. The gutter delivers me to a sieve where frogs speak in bottle-sounds and an engineer’s comb has crosshatched the scum to deny a midge the stillness it wants. I run a little length bright enough to be claimed by a child’s palm. A mouth closes and swallows me like a secret. I agree to be part of that body for a while.

Inside the child I am named without words. My name is the thirst cooled just enough. My name is the luck of catching from your own roof.

Then I am a wing. The place wants a wing as much as a drop, because it has written it into its function. I become dragonfly: eyes cored from honeyed beads, jaw hinge bright, the sensation of an engine stored in pairs of thin, incessant muscle. I hang on nothing by humming in place. There’s a whole grammar to remaining precisely still on air, a syntax of microtilts and fibers alight, and it matches the fans’ grammar so well it feels like we share a manual. I run inspection passes over rectangles of water someone raked at dawn; I approve their ripples with my own. I eat the past tense out of the air so it can’t settle; I keep beginnings beginning. I watch human palms draw lines in slicks and I write thinner lines just above them, a cursive script only swallows and I can see.

But patience happens here too, a vertical patience, and so I let the trembling of my wings slow and I shift into heron. I stand in a basin with one foot drawn up like a promise. Mineral on my ankles. Warmth pressing steady on my back. My head folds and unfolds along the hinge of hunger, and I count with my beak the arithmetic of a fish that must run out of options exactly where I am. The machine breathes; I breathe with it; our counts find a common rhythm. When I miss, I relish the miss the way a Panhand appreciates a peel that comes back on itself clean the next try. There’s time. There’s always the same time.

When I finally remember to have hands, I am first small and barefoot, skin like paper creased from damp sleep. I learn the sump the way children here do, by the soles. I memorize which catwalk grating bites and which gives; I discover the happiness of a hammock hook that has warmed to exactly one degree below hot. I am called by something that is not meant to last but does: I am Baffle one season in, Rill two seasons in, Valve when I catch a falling handle by reflex that isn’t mine but the town’s moving through me. Someone writes my newest name on the inside of a boot with a paint pen, and I learn to lift my heels so the rot will not teach me another lesson. I play on the Pipe Forest’s low shoulders. I trade the leaves engineers permit as if leaves were rare coins. I break skin on a mesh because green looks softer than it is and a friend learns the pressure trick from the nurse and the story binds us tighter than stitches could.

Growing means I make work my weather. I move into hearing. It turns out ears here are tools like wrenches are. There’s an older man with a jaw like a bolt head who can lift a hand without lifting his gaze from tea when the south-line pump’s half-semitone goes brighter. He gives me years by the way he points. I follow him into listening.

I walk the Lines. My fingertips are always damp and the pads have learned the language of temperature in tenths. I feel where a clamp is stepping toward its threads. I hear cavitation before it chews the impeller into a confession. I put my ear to steel and hear travelling and don’t stop it, only keep it going where it’s supposed to go because that is the difference between design and accident here. I know the pitch of every valve’s slap and the split-second of silence that will mean someone is about to be hurt, and I am close when it happens, and my hand is there, and afterward a boy is named Semitone for the blessed timing of our chapter closing clean.

I do penance as a Dripkeeper. Algae are seasons in this place, and I’m a gardener not of plants but of thin. I lift a frond and it comes soft as hair, and I scrape it and the sieve sings and the frogs approve by clinking; I trade this color for that, not letting green win large, letting it have its measured edges. I polish louvers with a patience I did not inherit but caught from the constant. All the angles shine. I put a comb into pans at dawn and draw futures with teeth.

Once, for a number of afternoons large enough to count as a devotion, I am simply shade. Not the concept—the reality: canvas strung, old sail stretched, an awning that tenses and relaxes on its spar as the fans breathe the alleys. In heat like this, shade is a material richer than meat. I become the saint they invented because they needed a way to love me. They tuck offerings into my hems—bits of string not salty yet, a coin dull from a child’s pocket, a button that survived a season—and I repay devotion by lowering the tension behind the eyes of those who pass under me. I move in a geometry of kindness: I am as large as hands can make me; I choose softly where to fall; I am careful to not make stillness, only ease. When wind forgets us and won’t come, I lean into the fans’ river and learn to translate velocity into mercy. Someone whispers Give shade first, comfort follows at my edge and I realize I have believed this before language.

Some days I want fur and rope for a spine. Then I am cat. Salt makes my coat hold to the slick; the machine’s warmth makes me unlikely to purr louder than a diesel, but I do anyway when I am allowed a lap. I own the catwalk as if the whole grid were strained solely to catch me. I hunt the thought of rat into smaller thoughts and then nothing, because that is the work assigned to animals that are invited. I keep to the routes humans have painted in their minds as safe and I understand, in my cat way, that the painted routes are the only routes here: everything else is a pan; everything else is the water’s to take.

On another day I am the cobbler’s resin tin that never cures. I am tacky forever. I am the permanent verge of becoming, sweet and patient, smelling of tree and heat, a promise without hurry. My world is soles lifted, edges reintroduced to middles, threads renewed. I collect fingerprints without judging their stains: oil, salt, tea, the ferrous kiss left by a rail touched to rest a wrist on. The cobbler thanks me without speaking; he speaks with presses and clamps. We two are a loop that pretends to be a straight line and we serve by doing the same thing gently and again.

I cannot resist being a pump. I didn’t think I would be able to, and I can’t. To be a pump here is to have a throat of water and a wind of electricity. My spinning is a psalm. Bearings heat a little and I feel the hand that owns me lay itself gently on my casing the way a parent tests a sleeping baby for fever. I drink pressure and I deliver pressure; I sing the same vowel the whole town holds under their talking. Cavitation is the only dream I have, the bad kind with teeth, and even there the Linewalker’s fingers find me and wake me and bleed the air and cozen the impeller back into a story it can tell without wobble. When I falter, I am forgiven by action not by word; forgiveness here is expressed as wrench torque and a clean rag. I love them for that.

In the clinic I touch steel warmed by blood and cloth damp from the sky and watch the nurse with damp hair wrap a heel without scolding. Her saints hang within reach of her work: a scrap for shade, a comb tooth tied with thread, the bright blue lacet coiled around a nail. If I am anything in that room, I am the soft light on the stainless and the way it does not mirror perfectly because even reflections are restless here. She catches babies and lays elders down; both events exist under the same hiss and therefore are siblings.

I stand, too, where prayers are not words. I am the fingers that thrum a fan’s safety grille as I pass—plinkplinkplink—because someone taught me that’s how you talk to the big, indifferent tenderness that keeps us all. I don’t know what I’m saying, not in any alphabet, but I know the reply. It’s the note that never argues. I leave that talk behind me every time because I am always called forward by schedules. There’s comfort in a routine so dense it carries its own gravity; I fall into it and orbit, and my days are horn to horn, pan to pan, the microseasons of rust and bright paint, algae and stripped plate, the slow path of a color band under a child’s palm as we teach red means hot here, blue means this other kind of hot.

Beyond the levee I am briefly tempted by outsides. I think about hills and the theatrics of weather that piles up and breaks. Then a road gate valve slaps like a father’s hand on a table, the pitch drops its half a note toward evening, and the river of air the fans push says stand in me, and I do, and outside becomes another basin not started yet. Expansion is a thing we plan on paper and enact with wire cages crammed with bright stones and the patient persistence of backhoes. It does not change what I am. It repeats me at a new edge. I become more baffles, more comb teeth, more saints of shade, more rakes, more routes geckos will learn by tendon.

In those hours before the clock-night means less and sleep is a rumor and even the frogs seem to clink from further back in the stacks, I realize that the answer has been in my mouth since the causeway taught me how to form this vowel. Where would I find myself in this world? I already have. I’m whatever helps the rule live.

Keep the flow. So I am anything that turns a flat into a slope and a trickle into a thread that doesn’t stop. I am the angle on a sill, the lip on a pan, the kid’s instinct to tilt his cup.

Soften light, harden surfaces. I am canvas stretched and paint fresh. I am the hardness that doesn’t wound—the handrail’s softened edge, the edges of concrete still knife-square but wearing the idea of mercy in their sheen.

Trade heat for water and water for order. I am a fin’s rib that makes steam remember itself and a gutter that counts beads into a sieve that sorts a day into fish and frogs and drink.

Invite animals that eat other animals and design for their routes. I am beam meet beam where swallows write home, a catwalk’s grammar learned by a cat, a margin where fiddlers clack like a clock that forgets to be time and remembers to be food.

Give shade first; comfort follows. I am the moving shape of cool that lets talk be possible, the soft under awnings where sugar is measured out on a tongue and tea names are decided by accidents in noise.

Clean to the line and no further. Let the rest grow where it’s allowed. I am a hand that stops scraping just here and respects how a living film keeps a place decent without pretending to be dead.

If I need a name—people here give them as a kind of shorthand and a kind of spell—I will take Baffle and keep it. Not only as a child who didn’t sleep unless air moved on his face and grew into a Linewalker with a musician’s ear, but as a part that is function: the thing that takes a force and makes it kind. I will stand between and turn fury into flow. I will slow only enough to keep things going. I will never be still. I will be good with that.

Find me under the seventh fin when the afternoon is a prolonged present tense. Find me on the levee in the cool hour that’s a myth the tongue still likes to tell, walking the gabion wire with a lover whose hand doesn’t touch mine until both hands have agreed it is allowed. Find me knee-deep in a pan that mirrors a ceiling instead of a sky, slide a squeegee in at the angle you can only learn by living here, peel a glassy skin back on itself like proof. Find me listening to the half-change in the south line and raising a finger as if to conduct a choir that only knows one note and still manages to make a town sing.

Or don’t find me at all. Hear me. I’m the long vowel held in the sump’s throat. I am the work’s voice saying the same sentence into the next basin and the next. I began as a drop and a wing and a barefoot child. I end as a sound you learn to sleep to, and a rule your hand keeps without thinking, and a way to be a person by tending flows you did not start and will not finish.
