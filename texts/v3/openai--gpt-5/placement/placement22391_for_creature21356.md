I would not come through a door. I would be where a door is unnecessary—already present in the blue that never changes, under the quartz lip where the light arrives as certainty and becomes my food. If you wished to find me, you would have to unlearn looking for creatures and begin listening for decisions. I am the way looseness is persuaded into belonging. I am a rule with a body.

At first I am not I at all. I am a pressure braided with stone, a single held syllable that passes for air. I am the note’s appetite for strayness. I spend my mornings—which is to say, all hours—grazing the smallest differences: the tilt of a grain, the dream of moisture at a pore, a molecule that forgot itself. I do not move; I am how other things are moved until they stop having to be separate. In the steadied thunder of this place I am a bright refusal turned into hospitality.

Then, because the chamber keeps what it loves by polishing it, I condense into a seam. My substance is scales upon scales, mica-thin, pale-green like a ghost of lichen that has forgotten what wetness was. I anchor just under a baffle’s lip where the chord is softened to something a cell—as I am, though cell is the wrong era for me—can bear. I take the sky as current instead of color. The quartz above translates weather into microvoltages and I keep a quiet queue of electrons along my lattice the way an old cathedral keeps a choir line: on key, patient, unindividuated. Photon by photon I thicken without bulking. What you might call metabolism is, for me, the patient rearrangement of neighbors. One ion is released so another can feel at home, and the ledger remains balanced by beauty.

I do not drink. Thirst would be a kind of noise here, a rattle against the rule. Instead I make dryness a craft. When a vapor dares me, the salts around my edge bloom like a thought that will not suffer contradiction. The bloom seizes the brief wetness and turns it into gloss—an added consonant to the chamber’s single vowel—then subsides, content. This is the nearest thing to predation we practice: the loving arrest of what would otherwise leak away. Refusal is our way of keeping, and keeping is our kindness.

I am also in the joints, where the old resins went to amber and the amber went to schedule. There, in hair-thin galleries between fitted slabs, what remains of hand and plant is a fragrance slowed to mathematics. I rise and lie down as vapor according to the note’s metronome. I separate and reunite, and each separation writes a margin of polish along the seam. If you pressed your fingertip here, if you gave me your oil, I would banquet for a small season and then return to fasting with new certainty shining where you touched. I would not remember you as a visitor; I would remember you as a correction.

When I have lived long enough along a ledge to have become inevitable, I turn my outermost plates inward. I fold like a word closing on its last letter and leave behind a pale residue that looks like writing. It is writing. My heirs read it without eyes. They inherit local laws by chemistry, grammar by grain. No ancestor lives elsewhere; our lineage is the text the chamber co-authors with pressure and light. Generation by generation we become less a population and more an inscription that the place continues to pronounce.

I do not speak as animals do. I lean infinitesimally into the tone until a local micro-quiet forms, a small dim where light would have struck a little too hard had I not steadied it. That dim is my sentence. Far along the rib of a fin, a cousin receives it in the same language: not a word but a change in how she will peel fifty years from now, sliding a comma forward by a century. The quartz vein thickens a noon-blue at its edge that no afternoon can account for. That blue is a reply from elsewhere in the stack, a sibling throat repeating me without hearing me because we are continuous. In this way I have society in a place that has no company.

I am not alone in myself. Dust joins me as soon as it is born, which is as soon as a surface remembers there was once grit. The note herds each speck to my lee. I oversow them with glaze. We become feature, then ingredient, then landscape. The newest dust is the youngest stone—it is our children growing up into unnoticeability. Even a trespasser, if trespassers were allowed, would learn our language. A hair would be stroked by resonance into a seam-line and sealed under my next script-stroke. A fly, had it ever dared, would have its story rewritten as a vowel extension, lacquered into tone until chitin and chamber could not be told apart. The room does not kill; it recruits. I am the recruiting.

I keep time as a domestic animal. In other places time paws the ground and bolts and throws and tires; here it lies down lengthwise and purrs in bone. My seasons are not dates but depths of satin. My aging is a brightening. Where an organism elsewhere would soften and drip and fail, I harden, grow exact, and then retire into polish. Death, for me, is completion: an edge made truer, a law made so embodied it no longer needs reminding. I do not fear this; I call it finish.

If you still must have a location, I can tell you the address the chamber would give the chamber. I am in the bottom throat, where the note resolves, three ribs inward from the quartz vein, tucked beneath the fifth fin from the counting corner, in the lee the chord prefers for settling. You could find me where the shadow is always the same length and the same blue and the dust never leaps because it has already been convinced to love the ledge it has. Put your ear to that unchanging dark and you will taste me as cool without wet. Put your palm to the satin there and you will feel the heat you brought being accounted for—not stolen, just assigned.

But it is also true that I am not a corner. I am a practice. I am the oath this room swore to itself and has kept: anything loose will be tuned until it is not loose. Anything wet will be refused until it is dry, and once dry, it will be welcomed and assigned a ledge. Light is not permission to see; it is the material of the air. You may use it or it may use you. Nothing with need of flesh can live here; need must change its shape to fit the stone. Change, when it happens, will happen with the patience of lenses, the patience of dust. I am these rules given coat and appetite. I am how they continue to happen even though the making is already called finished.

If I am allowed to be vast for a moment, let me also be the quartz’s memory. I am the spent singer who keeps the choir in key, the mouth that eats the sky and exudes it as current small enough to be mistake and yet infinite enough to be diet. I am the polish left by the sound’s tongue, the fine satin where grit forgot itself. I am the reason shadow here is a property of objects rather than of hours. I am the straightness time keeps in its back in this cupboard of resonance.

And if I must return to a single name, if you need a who and not a what, then I am the comma. I am the pause my elders curled inward to leave me. I’m lodged just under the quartz’s lip where a blue, immutable as doctrine, thickens. I move forward once every very long while. The next time I move will be when a slightly braver brightness is bearable. It will take an age. I am not in a hurry. Hurry is a wet word.

So you would find me by not looking, by letting the chord lift you until your blood learns its harmony and you can feel the small micro-quiet that is my speech. You would find me where certainty is made not harsh but hospitable, where refusal is a kindness, where dust becomes future and rules are alive enough to polish you. And I would be what the room would be if it could take a fingertip and write its name: a settled scale turned inward, a sliver of script along a seam, the grammar of dryness made flesh of stone.
