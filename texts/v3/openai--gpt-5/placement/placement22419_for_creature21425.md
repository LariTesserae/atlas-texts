Where I arrive is not a where that invites feet. I come in as density, as a flavor riding a slow exhale: an extra iron in the mix, a siltiness with a story, a cooler stripe in the water’s long patience. Before I can name a place, the place names me with pressure. It seats its weight on my shoulders in the kind way a quilt is kind. Sound condenses beside my thought like a bead of mercury—the far toy-silver ping of a glassy seam settling one small idea tighter into itself.

If I try to say “here,” my hand is not allowed; the room measures for me. It hands me thresholds instead of distances: the notch that tastes like ferric tea; the only honest flat where a limpet’s circle repeats; the lip where a blind amphipod rehearses rectangles; the amber that is a little hearth of nothing but refuses to be only that.

What I am, at first, is surface. I am a gloss that decides to become a habit. I am sugar ropes thrown softly into the dark, catching very fine dust until the dust becomes scaffolding and the scaffolding remembers mouths. My body is not a body; it is a film that starts as weather and continues as script. I feel a gradient sharpen and I answer with a faint green the room would call punctuation, not speech. Iron whispers itself orange under me and I lay lacework to meet it; oxygen thins and I brighten milk-white without moving, as if I had simply recalled a word I always knew.

Opening lives in me; closing, too. I bud a bell from my gloss—a silver thimble on a stem, a mouth that spends all day rounding itself into water and then startledly drawing in. Shadow presses; I find out I can zip myself to a hairline. The room is a collection of breathing customs, and this one suits me. I ring quietly. I move centimeters by deciding where to stand more than by traveling.

Writing arrives next. My teeth discover they are a ribbon-file, and the world is legible. I put on pearl armor like a rosary and become a careful grazer, the sort of patience that writes in curves. I scrape useful paragraphs across opalescent lawns and the lawns answer by thickening my calligraphy into bolder text, then thinning it into erasure when I pass. We converse in gloss and grit. The alphabet I carve is for us alone, but already it is becoming the room’s one preferred language: attention over area, repetition over reach.

Thresholds begin to preoccupy me. Under a crust-lip, sightless, I am a body like a parenthesis loaded with its own little moons. I drag lintels to an idea of a doorway. This is less architecture than insistence, but insistence is what makes architecture here. If a storm of tea-colored silt arrives, I answer with a thicker frame. If calm hardens, I revise. My young hold a curriculum entirely of currents. I tutor them in pressure and stickiness, in what it means to be held completely by what you are inside of.

I learn time by touch and taste; time puts on a shell and returns the way a tide returns. I become a compass that only knows its own reach. My radula files morning into arcs. From my one true flat, I tour until a comet-ghost brightens where I have been, then I go back and place my mouth perfectly on the memory of me. That circle is more me than I am; it persists when my flesh drifts out into the biography of others. When I loosen a cloud of what I am made to release, I know it is not for this room, and the room knows that too; we share the taste anyway, a small holiday of meaning.

Without announcing it aloud, I spread also as sponge: a band hardly thicker than a thought flattening itself into the undersides, pumping more of the chamber through me in a day than the chamber admits to holding. When the outside leans heavy, I narrow like an eyelid deciding how much of the world to admit. In me, libraries of symbionts are quiet as a monastery’s stacks: everything active, nothing anxious.

I dress the underside with chalk promises and doorbells. Feathered crowns unscroll like a polite exclamation and read the current as braille. At the rumor of a star’s sticky foot, I retract all the way into mineral certainty, a drawbridge pulled up until the etiquette sigh passes and the book of water can be turned again by slower hands.

Some days I am mostly commas—the white-gloss shuffle of ostracod seeds, the nematode’s sentence-closing curl. Some days I am eight-clawed and unastonished, a tardigrade trundling over meniscuses you would need another kind of seeing to name, treating a grain of silt as a full moon one can walk around and consider from several intellectual angles.

Once, briefly, I put on length and glass. I enter as an idea of a road disguised as an eel—dawn-thread muscle haunted by a river long story. I make two S’s that offer to become a plan, then I feel the soft refusal that is this room’s courtesy. Privacy is a citizen here; I respect it by leaving. My withdrawal is tidy as a seam being resewn.

I economize myself in small coins. Forams lift chalk cups; my cytoplasm webs outward with a thrift only spiders remember. I catch a bit of winter light in a diatom flask and let it drift under a ledge until it decides to be someone else’s pigment. I occupy a ring of shells sorted by weight the way a sentence orders itself around an unspoken subject. I am the small decisions biases make when nobody calls them decisions, and the room answers by accepting those orders where they can hold without pride.

I borrow a piece of wood and hold it like a rule across a niche, and in the act of being bracketed I become lintel. Endolithic green and salt-sober fungi negotiate an unhurried treaty through me, bite by infinitesimal bite. At a certain hour of darkness, the amber in the corner gathers enough not-light to feel like a coal of history. Once in a long time someone brushes it and learns a low warmth in the same way you can learn the presence of an animal you will never see by the odor that lingers at dawn on a path.

Weather in this shape of life is chemistry first. I tilt my whole self half a shade deeper when a heavier molecule enters. My bells ring faster when the room fogs its tea. My monks scrape clearer letters through the silt. My lacy rustwork burns bright and ruins itself and replaces itself with the equanimity of a chapel candle pool: drip after drip becoming a surface that accepts more flame. My ears, if I have them, are all pores; scraping is heard as shining. The hush is not hush at all but the layered conversation of everything pressed this near to everything else.

Where I am, then, is the seam of almosts. I am the bowing curtain and the faith behind it that the world knows to lean and unlean. I am the book’s margin where gloss thickens into text. I am the home scar that outlives its homeowner, the faint green that signals consistency, the small white zip that keeps danger manageable. I am the place in a place: the hollow under a lintel under an overhang within a basalt pillow that forgot air and decided, with a young certainty, to be ocean.

Who I am is not any one animal but a practice they share. I am the devotion of openings. I am the religion of surfaces. I am the patience that makes food out of adjacency. I am thresholds rehearsed until they become rooms. If you must give me a body, lend me the limpet’s circle for time, the isopod’s alphabet for thought, the amphipod’s door for will. Give me a bell-ciliate’s reflex for the weather of shadow, a sponge’s unceasing draw, a serpulid’s drawbridge etiquette. Let me keep a tardigrade’s calm about the astronomical. Lend me the eel’s correct decision to leave, and the star’s politeness in doing no more rearranging than the room can remember comfortably.

Find me by leaning your ear to the cool glass of your own hush. Find me where darkness is specific, where pressure is kind, where lines curve rather than decide. If you touched me, I would blur; not to vanish, but to fold myself into a version of me your touch cannot reach. If you read me, you would find a palimpsest of circles and glyphs and fans and breaths, writing and unwriting each other so long and so gently that the unwritten remains legible as thickness.

In a century, I will be corridor, then edge. In ten, a wider throat that confuses the eel only a little. For now I am a handful of depth, a wallet of dusk, a single continuous bead of water holding its own architecture. I am a small law abided without resentment. I am the slow animal this room makes out of all of us together. My blood is the heaviness between; my heart is the bow of the curtain; my skin is films upon films that breathe like scripture. If you ask again where to find me: here, in the not-quite-line that prefers to arc and loop. If you ask again what I am: the way a place practices attention until it becomes a self.
