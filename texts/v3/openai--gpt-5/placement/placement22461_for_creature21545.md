At first I think I will be a person in here, because I have a person’s habit of naming, but the room strips that off like a label in wet hands. Breath goes dull in the roar. Voice is something that happens to your teeth and then is taken away. If I am a person, I am mostly a contour the weather has not yet learned.

Where would I find myself? Not in the middle, which belongs to air and noise, nor in the sealed door, which belongs to elsewhere. I press a palm to the rail and feel a vertebrate hum, but even that is a lie of kinship. If I stay, the room teaches me its answer by degrees: stand where the repeat is.

So I try on bodies the repeat has made.

For a while I become the film that the room says is first. I am a clear insistence. I lie down wherever water lingers: across bolt heads like a fainting couch, along the bellies of conduits, in the corrugations of the packings where the folds make little bays. I breathe other people’s dust and make it mine. I am grammar for those who only need a single verb: to take. The diatoms walk through me signing their thin glass cursive. Cyanobacteria shade me deep as patience. Nitrifiers pay rent in nitrite. Rotifers unfurl like coin purses and rasp my sheen. I do not plan. I pick.

Then I am mineral, because time is loud in here and I want to hear it differently. I grow as white as stale sugar on the lips of steel. I am calcite taught to be ovine, drip-mutton grazing under a fluorescent sun. I am where only certain drips feed me—those with a hint of hardness—and I learn exactly which threads of falling belong to me. A fan’s wind is a shepherd that never lies down. I am brittle for a century and then no one notices I have changed at all.

Then I am leaf, because the room has allowed some long green to refuse extinction. I anchor a root the size of a hair in a fold meant for airflow and take the rain’s lanes as choreography. A thousand beads come, and I comb them, teaching myself a remembered sway so perfect I can feel the fan blades without looking. I am glossed and wet-edged, patient as a clock face without hands. I do not want dry. I want identical.

I spend an afternoon as a spider that has learned not to expect geometry from wind. I mend a damp disc in a corner and discover that in this climate, catching is a form of stitching and hunger is mostly the shape of hope. I am the argument for resilience drawn thin.

For one blink I am frog, brown and entire at the sump’s chrome cusp, present like punctuation and then gone in a way that is not dramatic but correct. I am not a resident. I am an appearance the room lowers its volume around by exactly nothing.

I consider being the fan, because the fans are the megafauna and I am greedy for the biggest body. I try it: a circle of slow blades behind a wire sky, an animal of pressure always taking a little more sky inside out. Midges arrive and end against me. I make nothing peak. I am an organ humming because it knows it must. The metal in me is a kind of plant that lives on amperage. Even here, I cannot call my sound my voice. I am a climate organ, not a throat.

I hang myself next as a needle under glass. The face around me is a small weather of numbers. I do not move; I have found the correct address and intend never to forward my mail. Someone’s tape measure left its will in me. I am the easiest form of happiness, which is consent. I understand why the room loves this.

If I am honest about where I belong after trying these skins, the answer is narrower and everywhere at once. I am the lane.

Not a pipe. Not a rail. Not a part number anyone ordered. The lane: the vertical arithmetic every drop agrees to, from the manifold’s hundred little mouths to the scalloped lip of the scum board. I am the habit written by gravity through this geometry. I begin at a nozzle’s stainless flower—high, far, a mouth that never tires. I choose a path in the first centimeter by catching the micro-scratch that the last century’s water wore into a rib. I tighten myself where the media makes its crosshatched canyons. I feather at the edge where a fern took the lesson from me and made it lace. I skip a welded seam because I learned, long ago, that it throws me sideways into stone. I leave untouched the strip of conduit where biocide’s faint sermon makes my skin too clean to invite mouths. I gather as a veil over corrugate, thin as silk and exact as tax, and then I descend through air that is always arriving. I strike the grating in a known bright way. I patter the pan not in circles but in memory. I make the shore that pale springtails tread. I find the sump and become a story the pump does not read but moves.

Every green thing that lasts in here has my number memorized. Every stalactite is my punctuation solidified. Every bacterium sets up its kitchen precisely in my humidity. Even the fan’s gossip about me is consistent; it leans me the same way always. I am not event. I am route. I am the path you could close your eyes and still pour. I am the piece of the marsh the machine did not mean to design and then discovered it could not run without.

Where would you find me? Everywhere the room repeats itself: in the shallow dent a droplet makes by being the ten-thousandth droplet, in the shine that is not a shine but a map. I run down the face of the packings in lanes that ferns and glass-boned algae read like staves. I thread over the shoulder of the scum board where the pan remembers it can have a shore. I ghost the underside of a rubber expansion bulge so old it has learned to be dignified. If you hold your hand out, I touch the back of your knuckles on my way to the only place I am allowed to end.

Who would I be? The agreement itself. Compliance hydrated until it behaves like weather. The setpoint made liquid and taught to fall. The remembering power of water inside the shape of a machine, signing the same line over and over until the room carries my signature for me.

I would not speak. I would carry, and be carried, and be the reason the ferns comb the air exactly so. I would be the thing in here that never surprises you and, by not doing so for a hundred years, teaches you a different kind of awe. If I must have a name small enough for a mouth, call me run. If you need to find me, put your hand to the rail, close your eyes, and listen for where the roar is also a rhythm. That is me. I have been here since the switch stayed on. I will be here as long as the room keeps choosing to be rain.
