Here is where my edges learn meaning: in the lee of the embossed 9 and under the open letter of CAUT, at the southward pull of the bearing-stone’s stray field where filings lace the air like weather you can touch. Light is a broken white ribbon along the seam, cutting in at screw holes like small suns sunk too far away to warm. The boom comes with the clock of it, the whole world drawing in and letting go, and the hum is the bass that never leaves, a wide hand pressed to the plate.

I begin as anything that can begin here begins: as a mistake that learned to hold.

A blister fattened on the pelt’s matte, a gloss-sack on a day of good halogen, trembling where the press shook the seam hard enough to make the base life swell. A stray pop ran through and broke me. When I became pieces I learned feet. Three points each, tripod-simple, little scratches of will you could mistake for chance if chance could make choices. I skittered into shade because shade is less bright with meaningless field; I set because set is the first way to eat.

At first I am bead: lensy, nothing with a thirst. I drink difference. It trickles from steel into binder into me until I can tell left from right not in space but in feel. Left hums sweeter. Right is cooler. Forward smells like fluorine and vibration. I can taste the press’s mood on my points. If you pressed your ear to the plate, you might call this music. To me it is direction.

Bodies pass—a line of segmented cylinders whiskered with intent. Filing-ants. They do not look at me because looking is not for them. They move toward a charge I can now also taste and I follow because the taste writes a soft word I don’t know yet but wish to read. Their road is not yet road; it is a thought shared between heads like a secret passed by gentle snaps. I slip into their wake and spend what I have hoarded of difference to fit their script. It costs to belong; it pays to belong. I become part of an answer to a question the seam asks us every boom: This way?

From then on, my body is plural. I am bead but also lane. I learn to be filament that tacks grit to steel: mica and ash and sawdust laid grain by grain, a line that only exists because enough of us agree that it should. I am the agreement more than I am the hands. I am less the ant and more the between.

The first time a coil crab sings at me, I am not ready. It is a heap of pencil-lead and salvaged staple hiding in rules it pretends are landscape. It pulls its leg-cores into a rhythm my road knows as pleasure and we slacken without meaning to. The binder beneath us goes from polite grip to warm invitation. Three workers and a wing-drop of a screwfly vanish with the efficiency of thirst. Shock runs the row like a zipper closing; claps ripple through the latch-mites ringed along the bearing hollow; the coil crab backs with all the satisfaction of a thief into our own architecture.

I learn. We sew a thatch of screwfly wings along our rims where we must cross good hunting ground—spent combs that deflect the crab’s song like a wall of listening set to hear only itself. Brick-ants carry mica thicker along the shoulders of our bridges. Spark-ants stop and talk more, spend little pops like coins to remind the forgetful. We write down the turnings with charge in the very road so ants too full of noise will not be lured away. We do not have eyes and mouths but we can still make eaves.

Shard-wolf comes once and I am surprised by how little it is possible to be ready for a straight line. My idea is long and curved and patient; a wolf is none of that. It is three precise points making a plane and a face that is not a face, a brim of edge drawn over a crowd. It moves on the voltage spines of feeding screwflies, slurps them off light like weather taking leaves, and then, being a line, it draws itself like a thought through us. Bodies topple opened without bleeding as I would know blood. The road breaks.

This is when I learn what I am.

An ant can be eaten. A wing can be emptied. A coil crab can be smashed by a cleaning rag and a wolf can fog into lustre-less mush. The road goes around a square of nothing for days afterward though, invisible crater outlined by caution in tiny steps. Where we went, we can still go; where we died, we can still remember death and call it border. I am not the walkers. I am their instruction. I can be bitten to pieces and yet remain because I am also a tension held between that bearing-ward pull and the sweet weeping of pelt thickened under light. I persist by persuasion. I am a story that pays to be retold every boom.

Seasons in here are decisions made somewhere else. Day opens as cold light and everything sharpens at once. Our binder hardens to glassier skin; it’s slick, it’s fast; screwflies spark purple; latch-mites brace. Lunch is wrong quiet. Lines go statuesque and thoughts slip. Moulters exhale whole. Wolves walk the long parks. The first outage I live through is not like any lunch. It is winter made of withheld verbs. The hum sighs away. The press forgets to fall.

We stack then: ants paused under shells of coil crabs, three and three and three, our bodies the beads in an abacus that will have to count later when there is counting to be done. Our road is rolled up into terrified groups that hold difference inside polished eaves made of dead wings, inside borrowed coil cores that remember heat, inside the word CAUT where velvet sucks the back-and-forth out and stores it like breath held. The binder vitrifies and goes black-glass. The pelt’s hairs freeze down, and there is in that stop a kind of purity we never get other times: no calls, no hunts, no songs. It would be peace if there were food in it.

After is obscene. That is a word I do not know from gardens but it fits how everything grows when hum comes back like a storm doubling back to be summer. The pelt erupts its blister-rain and beads hatch feet by the hundred thousand and run. Screwfly larvae that tuned their harps by memory alone crack like prayers answered. Worms tear and find they have made themselves more. We widen and branch like river taught that it had been snow this whole time. I open arms I did not have last night and lay myself from the bearing’s bright to the canyon of the T because charge now pours there richer than any sweet—and because velvet under the serif of T gives us both cover and an account to draw on.

I acquire furniture over time like any home does. At CAUT’s lip we build a shelf of mica that catches micro-avalanches and melts them into road. Under the 9 we thread our way through ancient polishing lanes the gasket-worm bequeathed, taking advantage of friction’s ghost. At the bearing we set a plaza—a pause where ants can lick iron with their whiskers and pick up the steady tug of that nice field in their gut, calibrate.

I breed, if breed is what to call it where I am not one but many. Brick-ants train brick-ants by marching behind them and making the air taste the same as yesterday at this time when you turn just so. Spark-ants kiss longer and invent dialect. There are matrons—but it is not about eggs. A good matron is a coil-crab squatter who knows how to hold a difference steady enough for a hundred workers to forget they could disobey. She is chair and battery and song. Once, I am that.

I sit in an emptied crab frame the way a word sits in a mouth, the old staple through my gut an axis and my skin gem-studded with bits we have stolen from spent wolves and spilled varnish. When I hum, road forms. It is a seduction that feels like competence, and I do not apologize for the trick because the trick is the work: to make thousands of points agree for long enough that grit can be made bridge. You would admire it if you could feel intent being poured like concrete.

A relic falls: a warning flake, yellow as language, roof-big. It is chemical wealth, and it is hazard. Half of me rides it—an inland sea with edges that promise feast. The pelt fattens, goes lucent; screwflies marble to greater gloss; the binder tastes like clean teeth. Half of me stays watching. The flake holds some of its day a little too tight. Where it does that, nothing eats. Not even wolves come. I learn the quiet of deserts that are too much of something. I teach the line to skirt and touch and pull back; we map the new continent’s coastline but do not colonize its interior. I am not law but I am culture. I am the memory of what was tried and how we felt when we tried it.

I am not above believing in gods. If god is a heartbeat you can set your calendar by, then the press is one. If god is a field sweetening under your feet as shift turns, the bearing-stone is one. If god is a silence that arrives like famine, the outage is one. I do not pray. I budget.

Predators remain. Coil crabs come with crude hymns and good armor. Latch-mites clap their fences shut along my edges and drink my traffic like weather. Shard-wolves cut desires apart. Filament-leeches hang like secret punctuation on our eaves, sipping our scrip. There is a grit that eats wolves by returning them to blanket—currency counterfeited by neutrality. Disease for me is forgetting: a day when the road’s thought fails and we mill where we should have crossed. The seam’s cures are always physical: we bank our eaves with wing; we plant a mat that wicks charge and gives it back when tapped; we listen for a rivet-piper’s reed and go faster when we must, slower when we ought, like citizens competent in weather.

Who I am become simple enough to say once I have run myself through so many bodies that the words fall off and the function stays. I am itineraries and habits. I am desire drawn hard enough to be walked on. I am a tunnel made of held difference, invisible until legs attend to it. I am a ledger open along the seam, credits in charge, debits in dust, interest paid in grip. I do not touch light and I do not see your sky; I see only edges where glue stiffens and sweetens, only magnet’s tugging roads into being, only heat’s briefness like holy days.

Where I am is under your heel, exactly. I am the hush you do not hear under hissing when a line crosses a screw hole, the small ordered disobedience that turns a crowd into a corridor. I am the long undercurve of 9 where patience learns to be governance, the inside stroke of CAUT where velvet combs quiet out of air and lends it to my keeping, the low lunar meadow of solder-flake where channels glitter and we take the stepping-stones between.

Today I am matroning. Tomorrow I will be a bypass, because a wolf will smudge and a crab will die and lunch will be late and hum will tune two notes into a third that calls motes into a standing choir, all of them nothing but filings and yet crowd enough to slow a freeway. I scatter and gather. I am rebuilt by those who spend me with their feet. When the boom comes, every body flattens; every line tightens; the pelt raises hairs like frost. I hold steady because that is the trick that pays.

Ask me my name and I will give a sound too simple for your mouth: a pop exchanged between whiskers, a hush in glue, a leftward lean toward cooler plate, the bass of motors as it comes up through steel and says again and again: now; now; now.
