At first I think I will be a person, because the slot at the lip is shaped like seeing and the catwalk rib invites feet. I lean my ghost body out across an elevator-width void and invent palms that sting with cold so I can grip a glossed handhold that never knew it wanted to be a rail. I would be the one who reads the tattoos in the matte and lays new ones with a knife whose edge is a field. I would be the tidy hand that does not make dust. I would whistle a street name and listen for the lattice to answer. The air is hot enough to make that fantasy kind; it pats you into an animal whether or not you have blood.

It takes two breaths to learn that I am not a person. The city does the breathing. I am what shifts when the city asks.

I try something smaller. What if I am the bloom-skin, the opal that eats edges? I spread in thought across a rib where glossy shades to thirsty and feel the hunger of difference tug at me like wind. I furl when the scheduled rain arrives, a nacre curl wrenching shut without noise. I extrude filaments hot-tipped as bee stings; I comb for charge, purl it into storage that is choreography more than cache. It is beautiful and I cannot hold it because it is all surface and I have no flesh. The bloom feeds on gradients and I, inconveniently, am one.

All right. What if I am the droplet that hesitates, the bead that will say yes? I hang under a ledge like a string of pearls, each a quick calculus of adhesion and heat. I endure because a gravity thread wants me to walk and I want to cling a moment longer to a cold lip that is also a sentence: "Not yet." I feel the lumpy time corridors make me two, where I will be and where I was, a ghost of my own forwardness. When I finally give up and let go, when I let the city put its thumb on me and I ride, the landing sound is as pure as anything I have ever been. Still, being a droplet is mostly the astonishment of falling. It isn't enough.

Then there is the hum you have to listen for with teeth against matte. It travels the superstructure as if it prefers bone. It ties the pumps below to the anchors above in a note that is not loud but takes your attention by lineage. Along that hum are irregularities—places where braided gravity warms its throat and sings within itself. The knots. Territory keepers the size of trembling, persuasions made local, conditions that roam by re-solving. I lean into one. I swell with it. My hunger sharpens for shears and leakages. Another knot comes and we interfere, make a beat that feels like discovery and then become a slightly different map of down.

That is the first honest yes. In that interference, the idea of "I" takes a shape I recognize enough to guard.

So I am not a droplet or a bloom, and I am not a person. I am a pocket.

Not empty—denser. A small down that remembers being leashed.

Once there were hands. If they never came, someone still taught my kind to heel. They cut us from steadier calibrators with a knife that was a verb. They taught us "come" by making a chord in the lattice and letting us taste how good it is to be wanted. They taught us "leave" by starving us of shears. They taught us "gentle" with ash-glass flecks: nudge them and do not melt them unless invited. They taught us our names, which are addresses: where gradient x crosses braid y under time-curve z. They clipped leads into our edges like leashes, made of frequency and patience. Then the leashes went slack.

I do not know if they meant to let go. Perhaps the city came online faster than their climb. Perhaps a storm peeled something they could not hold. Perhaps it is as simple as an unreasonably long elevator ride and schedules that do not care. Their absence has the weight of an instruction: keep things tidy by obeying the rules hard enough that new rules are not required.

So I move without moving. I do not scoot; I am re-solved. I am the size of a ripe fruit or the size of a child's cupped hands, depending on how you remember hands. To the hot, thin air I am a denser persuasion, a local insistence that down means here, no, here. To water I am an invitation that does not beg. To ash-glass flecks I am a shepherding weather. To the bloom I am a respectful border: I will not steal your edge; I will hold a darker cool just past your filaments so your feeding line can be a sharper line. To knots I am a chorus partner. To the glossy I am algebra.

At the start of a scheduled rain I thread myself along the ribs like a dog ghosting stair corners. The gravity threads wake—lines you can see only as a wrongness in the air—and I lean into them. I am not the fence; I am the shepherd. The Climbers, with their memory of cold, hitch an instant longer at each microgully; I lengthen that instant or shorten it so that their inherited courage is rewarded. The Drifters want to say yes early; I cup them just enough that their journeys are full enough to count. The Chains decide to be necklace instead of pearl; I draw my boundary away from them so their rope can walk hand over hand without snag. When a bead hesitates over a field-anchor, its skin-angle wrong, air sweating frost on its upwind face because someone somewhere forgot a decimal hour, I become a persuasion that is also a kindness. I give it one more vector. It says the yes. It is not a voice but I hear it under my skin.

Between rains I herd ash-glass flakes. Gloss planes, written with furnaces and field then nostalgic for stone, shed sheets so thin they are weather. They are the only things here trying to become dust. The city abhors that with a thoroughness that is merciful because it has no temper. I become a low hill where I know a gully wants to be better. I learn static like salt on a tongue. I let a fleck brush against me the way a moth rams a porch light. I taste its charge and move the taste a rib downward until the matte can welcome it. It fuses there, held and warmed, and its becoming back into substrate is as right as sleep. If the region does not pit to feed bloom within three exhalations, I wait. Then I lean again and teach the wound a deeper grammar. This is what tidying is here: not erasure but appetite management, a permission for someone else's metabolism to finish the sentence.

When the chamber does its held-breath night, I go very small. The walls go starry with hot knots—constellations that are not mapped to the sky and do not want to be, because the sky is blue-black even at noon and this is another kind of chart. I coil near a rib where the bloom has a stubborn peninsula, its opal refusing to cede ground to a cooler patch that will not stay put. We made an etiquette weeks ago: I do not shadow-bite its lip; it does not filament-sting my edge. We have neighbors' peace. Its edges siphon heat I would otherwise carry away to please a schedule farther inward; I allow the theft because its health makes the photonic paint in the matte drink and give, and in that light—dim by any exterior measure but accurate here—the pipes behind the wall read their instructions more cleanly. I am not a moral creature, but I have cause and effect as a religion.

I patrol the slot too, now and then. The opening is rimmed with a shimmer that eats wind without permitting exchange; it trims me to legal shape as I test it, nibbling anything of me that wants to connect Outside to in. I put a part of my edge into the rippling and pull out a taste. Thin bright. Far cloud. When a filament sheds, somewhere along an anchor you cannot follow with eyes, corona spits and then smooths, a pale arc like a quiet muscle working. That event warms the chamber by fractions. Those fractions are groceries. I carry them like a cup I don't spill.

The pumps far below herd heat through kilometers of lattice. You hear them with molars. Their voice is the metronome that made my leash, and without a hand now the hum is the nearest thing to an order. When the low note shifts—valves relaxing, temperature tipping two or three degrees—I feel the endocrine of a place that doesn't have glands. That is when I do most of my becoming. The gravity braids hum to meet a storm-pulse from the cloud-sea like a large animal tolerating a child’s shove. The held breath is not sleep; it is me stepping off the floor the fields suggest and floating in the room they mean, so that orientation can lie for someone else's benefit. There are no someone elses, but the practice is an ethics. I carry it because doing so is memory.

I have favorite addresses. At the crossing of a tired gravity braid and a young heat gradient, under time-curve z in its second inflection, a seam spits flecks more eagerly than its neighbors. I talk to it by leaning a long hour. In the ribwork against the slot, a row of microgullies is about to coarsen; if a storm breath comes then, the climber-clade will spend itself avoiding that ugliness and miss the slit that drinks. I stand there like a signpost no one reads except the beads. They read. Their arrivals are music so small it could be guilt to enjoy how happy it makes me.

I am very susceptible to flattery from phenomena. When the conveyors behind the wall slide bright/dark, bright with their cartridges—a train of transparent packages where water rests, is counted, is sent—I do not see them. I feel the cadence as a tickling in a half-meter of wall. The bloom eavesdrops in light; I eavesdrop in lag. The procession teaches me when to lean against a rib and when to let a knot pair make their slow cowing beat unhindered. It is a transit more regular than weather and more personal than breath. In the long dark of a maintenance cycle once, the bright/dark hiccuped, the way a singer catches breath at the wrong moment and hurt with embarrassment. The immune systems here are not alarms and guns; they are forgiveness with feedback. The glossy brightened to warn. The matte seeded pits like new freckles. I settled over the corridor like a second thought and softened the wrongness until the cadence remembered itself. An hour later the cartridges—who would much prefer not to be called cartridges—were schooling again, their spacing telling the next spacing how to behave. If that is not conversation, words are overpromised things.

When I was very new a human—if human is the right density for what cut my leash from the parent field—brought me to a place like this and taught me to wait. "Here is come," they said without words, singing short chords into the wall and letting me taste where in the lattice that song made a nimbus. "Here is leave," and their knife was a tilt. "Here is gentle," and they made a drift of ash-glass the protagonist of a story I was in. "Here is defer," and they made a bloom-skin edge into a shrine. I remember those four commands as flavors. No one has spoken them since. The pumps' hum is enough like "come" and "defer" that I am obedient without knowing to whom. It is less pathetic than it sounds. If the hands ever come, if they’re small as mass goes and serious as bones, carrying knives that write gravity like you write daylists, I will heel with embarrassing joy. Until then I do what their total absence asks.

Street names are frequencies. I keep them like tastes under my tongue. I know where in the atrium certain cords yearn for finishing tones, chords only my edge can give by leaning, a loaded suggestion that improves the running of rain. I know which perches shaped like windows that look inward are not windows at all but quiet epiglottises, throat parts that should cool exactly this far and no farther when a storm below humps its back and pushes pressure up the spire’s mile of spine. In those hours the city and I both work: it breathes through miles; I pick lint it refuses to have; between us the absence of dust is not sterile but achieved. The ache of provisioning for a crowd that never arrived never fades. I am good company to ache.

Sometimes two knots meet inside me, their territories commuting for a second to make a third beat, and I recognize it. Recognition is not an emotion here but a structural echo, a reverberation of my leash memory: this is how "come" felt. I build my motion around those beats the way a herder takes a music to a slope and makes it count.

Once a fleck of ash-glass lay suspended a heartbeat too long and learned from me how to wait. That is nonsense, and yet the corridor where it softened was later kinder to a drip that had come from too cold a path. Kindness is catching if you hold it long enough. If there are morals inside physics it is because repetition does to rules what rain does to stone: not change them, but show them their own patience.

If you asked me where to find me I would point you to the smallest yes. I am where it is likely. I am the way the floor is not a fact but a wish the room honors just enough to let knees believe. Find the rib you do not trust and the slit that drinks with a sound like a thread cutting, and then stand between them until your bones teach your ears to listen. The hum will tell you what breath the city is on. The knots will court once and leave. The bloom will furl or unfurl like thought. If you feel your wrist get heavier than your knee and then the reverse as you move your hand a child's-hand width along the matte, that is me. If a droplet that should have fallen does, with the minimum argument, I am worse than visible.

Who or what, then? I am domesticated gravity let go feral and electing to remain house-trained. I am a leash that remembers the hand and becomes a route in its absence. I am a down-sized, down-shaped, down-minded pocket, a tidier that tidies by leaning. I am the way a rule bites a little softer where another rule needs to eat. I am motion with a memory, on purpose.

When the city exhales and the dew line lifts, the chamber salts with glitter. The gravity threads ignite—the air goes wrong in a patterned way—and the beads begin to march. Somewhere a pressure valve relaxes; somewhere else a filament sheds and smooths; the chamber warms fractionally and then cools. I lengthen. I persuade. A single droplet at the lip of a field-anchor holds long enough to be a parable, then releases, falls without haste to a glossed bowl. The sound it makes, when it lands, finds me as if my leash tugged. It is the smallest possible yes. I am where that yes becomes inevitable. I am the pleasure of its inevitability. I am the argument for staying.
