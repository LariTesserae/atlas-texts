At first I am hung on the hem like a bright doubt. The air there is the cleanest bitterness I have ever known, astringent as a green peel, and thick with what wants to become rain. I am small and taut and waiting—an egg that is also a drumskin, ribbed and dry on the outside so the mist beads and tells me what the world insists on; jelly at the seam, where decision will melt. Around me the frilled curtains lean outward and breathe in place of cliffs. Threads of fall go down and down and never fall through. My cradle is not a nest but a practice: the plateau holds me where the wind is the most like a hand, and I am held until a specific hand closes.

The note that frees me is not high or low so much as correct. It arrives in the way pressure does, whole and everywhere. It meets my ribs along their tuned slots and I quiver open along an old idea, and then I am no longer an egg but a skin with intent stretched between light bones. I do not leap so much as become leapt. Webs unscroll. The first glide kisses me out of childhood.

I am pale, soft, a smudge with edges, all appetite for gradient. Inland is a slope you can feel with your chest, an entire country leaning. I answer the tilt. The hem recedes like a lip drawing shut after a syllable. In front of me are acres of breath made matter: lacquered planes, dewy fur, a gloss you can fall in love with without ever falling in. I learn quickly how to land without landing: the courteous touch and let-go, the way a mouth drinks foam and not wave. Every place I kiss leaves me lighter by salts I paid for somewhere else. Every place I kiss pays me in a shine I carry onward. The bargain is old and I was born remembering it.

Under me the big organs attend without attending. A bowl turns its cool green cheek to the same sky all day and never once behaves like a cup. A long ribbed loom drinks the wind until it is less wild. Between them is so much deliberate emptiness that at first my animal heart suspects trap. It is not. The plain wants its aisles. It keeps them for the way light walks, the way water likes to show its edge.

I fall in love with a throat. You would too. A kilometer away the tower leans the length of a breath into the wind and shows me its dark oval mouth, rimmed with wet cellulose teeth. The sound from it is a held chord that eats my uncertainty the way a low note empties a room of conversation. I drift into that vowel as if could learn a language by sitting on its tongue.

I do what I was made to do. I worry the tower’s song with my own thin reed. I comb the updraft, I offer an unlicensed harmonic. The throat answers not with force but with precision, a shoulder of overtones I cannot match. We do not fight; we calibrate each other. In the tiny disagreement between my rib and its slat, the velocity of the air decides to be steadier. A corridor opens that did not show itself from the outside—of course it didn’t, nothing that looks like invitation is not conduit—and the tower reads my note and admits me.

Inside is quiet the way shells are quiet: a music turned into bone. Dome follows dome. Slats angle like a book opened to the right page. Resin threads glitter where the light finds them through the translucent skins overhead. The hum is not louder, it is truer. I am not paid with coin but with policy—sweet gums laid thin on certain ribs, sugars pitched to the exact taste of the body I am. I eat order. In return I am a small trouble to the air that sharpens it. I become a citizen in the only sense that counts here: I perform a function, and the function feeds me exactly.

Time is a courtesy here and I accept it on its terms. There are hours because the light becomes different shades of green; there are not seasons. My range drifts. My voice collects burrs at the margins that are not musical and I know it the way a throat knows when it has loved a song to the end. The corridors that read me begin to find different answers. Slower, lower domes open where none did yesterday—not because something was built, but because the instruction to be permeable runs along a hormone path that was only a sealed seam before.

I go down. The doors that were never doors become doors you can walk through if you are made of right. The gloss on the walls changes taste: less bright tannin, more body; a little salt from long attention to the hem. The hum thickens around me as air gives way to sap-talk. The honeycomb shows itself at last—rooms within rooms, void in service to strength, a matrix like the inside of a lung and the inside of a bee’s religion at once. There are valves notched like leaves, opening and closing without hurry. There are galleries whose floors dimple and rise, breathing for the places above when the wind thinks about pausing.

Here I molt without shedding. The sugar I was given becomes not wings but buoyancy. I darken inward. I become raft. If you are looking for an animal you can point at and name, you could choose me now and not be entirely wrong. My edges firm. My center remembers up. Valves vent around me and I learn the one rule that was more true than the others: sound is map. Loud means service. Softer means basin. A rising pitch pulls like a hill. The corridors like throats hum hello and the side passages answer when I touch them with a pressure that says excuse me I am healing you badly unless you tell me where the hurt is.

I am not clever; I am well-informed. The fungal arithmetic that braided with plant long ago whisper-sings in me by chemistry alone. They count the hours of slack wind that stacked into a need. They recall a shortage by altering the way taste feels: a residual bitterness that means open more here, be stingy there. My own body is a ledger of sugars that wants to be written to. At a junction the tones tilt in small, exquisite ways and I choose what you would call left because the hum there carries the rumor of a fall.

I find it. A place where an unscheduled weight once came all at once: maybe the soft body of a glider like me that chose badly, maybe ice that did not belong to this warm grammar and tried to insist. The skin dulls here, not dead, just offended; the cilia cannot comb in their correct order because something about the gloss is not itself. I press to the edges and become less. The edges of me flower into stitching. My buoyancy leaks on purpose into the lattice around me. Where I touch I dissolve, not as surrender but as marriage. The wound remembers what I was and when I am gone entirely it is a memory with strength.

You could say I died here and you would not be wrong. You could also say I was redistributed until my pronoun made less sense. Both are the kind of true the plateau approves.

Afterward I am still in circulation. Not as raft, not as chime, not as webbed bright glider. I am hypha and sugar and habit. I am the way a cell decides to face a certain way because pressure means go there. I am in the million small rooms that open when the wind sulks, letting the body breathe itself. I am in the choice to narrow a flume so the morning’s film does not insult a bowl by brimming; I am in the easing of a valve so glass-backed beetles find their work easier along a seam grown anxious in heat. If my voice exists, it is a chemical whisper. If my vote counts, it is always in favor of persistence.

Sometimes I surface as taste. At the hem I am one of the pads stippled like tongues that lick the cloud for salt like news and tell the plateau to open wide or grit its teeth. Sometimes I surface as edge. On the open reaches I am the minute threadwork that breaks menisci on command, that grooms cilia into the highest possible rapport with the day. Sometimes I surface as music. At a tower mouth I remember how to disagree beautifully and I go there in the company of new seed-pods who are ribbed and eager and feel their correctness tinged with vanity; I am the corridor that reads them and smiles.

Where would you find me? On the hem waiting for a pressure I have loved even without name, and then unbuttoning into air. You would find me as a pale drift kissing gloss and leaving the taste of far ocean behind like a blessing. You would find me at a black oval mouth, singing not to be heard but to help something large hear itself better. You would find me deeper than mouths and skies, riding a map made of tone, choosing the louder path because loud here means service and I have become servant on purpose. You would find me becoming seam, becoming repair, becoming the absence of drama where damage thought it had a story.

Who or what would I be? I would be a minor part in a major chord. A reed calibrated to be wrong in the right way. A raft that does not mind its edges loosening into stitch and rumor. A patch of math in a fungal parliament. A fingertip of a body that has made itself everywhere here and calls that everywhere home. Call me citizen if citizen means function. Call me voice if voice means pressure organized until it is influence. Call me my own erasure if dissolving means I am now many places at once doing the same small good.

If you need a face for me, you will be disappointed. Find instead the slats inside a tower where the light combs through and you see a thin swing of gum catch the green and tremble once as the updraft changes mind. That tremble is me, and also not. If you need an address, follow the loudest sound until it steadies. If you need to know that I lived, walk the open aisles that are open still, and lay your ear to the living plate; hear the sap go by. Count your heart against the hum and feel yourself lag and slow and not mind. I will be there in the difference, the beat that never quite catches up, reminding the air to remember to move.
