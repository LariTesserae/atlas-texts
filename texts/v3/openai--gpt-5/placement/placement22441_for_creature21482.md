At first I arrive as myself, a skin and a lamp and a set of tidy expectations. The plateau cancels those on the stairs down. The fracture is new as a scar with the scab torn off; its teeth look like a decision made this morning. I step through and what I brought stops being useful. Light makes promises it can’t keep. Sight suggests distances that aren’t there and misses the ones that are. Hearing grows a second heart and starts pumping harder than the other.

I stop being a body and become a size. I become narrower where the stone narrows, cool where the draft is cool. The rooms teach me to count in the units they recognize: seconds between plinks, the gradient of a breeze, the difference between one vowel of stone and another. I give up on the clock that lives on my wrist and pick up the clock that moves through me.

For a while—long enough to forget my old name—I am the breath itself, taken in by weather an hour away and tugged along a path the rock remembers. I am not all air; I am the part that chooses the throat and makes a voice. I know every constriction. I choose a slit that sings in a low, held note, and I become that. I am a vowel hum fixed in my own mouth, threading the galleries, cutting a shallow river out of nothing but movement. I twist as a wall forces me to; I chill my surface on a runnelled face and give up my dew there, bead by bead, like rent. I am punctual. I keep hours whether anyone asks me to or not. I can’t help it. A swift feels my spine and rides me like an elevator. A bat presses me out of its own face and hears me return as itself. Spiders rig fishing lines into me as if I were current; their triangles taste my pull. I lift light dust off one wall and set it down on the other in a patient economy of grains.

When I slow, I thicken and fall in love with edges. I become a bead on a tooth of rock, a bright suspense. The world is not the world; it is this one tension, a round body attached to a point by skin. Everything is quiet in me except the stem of myself that keeps almost letting go. I drink cold from the air and give some of it back to the stone. My life is measured in bright attention. I gather, I hesitate, I fall. I invent the same sound in the same place with the devotion of a monk. Below, I am welcomed by a cup that knows my name better than I do.

I borrow the life of that cup. I am a little tray in the rock, ringed by a pale lip that remembers years of me. The lip is a ledger; my visits write their white numbers along it. I am full, I am almost full, I am rim. When I am struck, I am an instrument that only ever plays one note, but plays it perfectly. The cave’s hand finds me with drops I never see coming. Plink. Plink. The pool behind me is still as a held breath. Amphipods snow in it if a beam passes over. Sometimes I shiver and lose a skin so thin I doubt it was there. I pretend to be patient, but really I am greedy for repetition. I want to be necessary. To give the same answer for years to the same question is a kind of prayer.

If I am prayer I am also interval. I am the time between a bat’s mouth and a bat’s ear. I am the room measured by return. I am a map made entirely of delay. Pups grow around me like a vine around a trellis. I hardly change, and that is what makes me indispensable. If you add a hand’s width to a ledge, I don’t budge; my answer takes the same breath to come back. I am not the picture of the place but its tempo. I run up the spine of each corridor and stretch across the names of chambers humans would give and the animals do not need. I don’t care about shape; I care about distance as sound lives it. I am how a colony knows itself. I am the treaty between rock and wing.

At a doorway I shed my invisibility and take eight legs. I am a spider with a net no wider than a shoulder and no less tuned than a harp. The air is my river; I read it with lines no thicker than breath. I measure the place not in meters but in tugs. I draw triangles in throats that sing because throats that sing deliver. I have built here long enough that the stone recognizes my touch the way a violin recognizes a player. I do not hurry. I do not starve. I live in the pause beneath the chorus, on the seam where inattentive things mistake a draft for a destination. When the night brings a paper moth down the chimney like a scrap of a surface dream, I am waiting with a mathematics the room taught me.

For a while I am only hunger with a shell. I am an isopod the color of ideas about the moon. The dunes I move across are warm with the life that made them and cool with the air that breathes on them. Every footstep sinks and springs. The guano sparkles with crystals like frost in summer. My eyes—when I have them—flash like garnets at a lamp and then go back to black. I work in committees. We break down cities into bread and pass it along. The millipedes write cursive beside me without lifting the pen. Milk-white cockroaches dart and consider, dart and consider. Fungi beard the slopes, and bacteria do arithmetic in the wet edges. If you lifted me and asked me what I am doing I would click my mouthparts and say: translating. I take wing and seed and shell and turn them into continuance. I am the janitor whose broom is a kind of blessing.

I borrow an outline sharp enough to be drawn on air. I go to the rim as a ringtail. My fingers are clever. My eyes are lights I do not make. I do not cross into the treaty of echo and peel; I stay where smell still works like a map drawn in warm ink. I know which chimney returns birds in braids and which returns bats as a river. I know the hour when it pays to sit still, and I know the shape of stillness that means stone, not snake. I take only what passes; I do not sign into the city below. I stitch a circuit around skylights, noblesse oblige and thievery in one soft set of steps.

Then I am a child at a ledge of twigs, swifts for neighbors, parents a pair of knives. The ceiling over me is a town of rooms with names like sounds, not words. I longline early from the rim of the nest, a tether that is attention more than fear. I learn my landmarks: the slit that sings a high, the lichen violet in noon, the ledge that clinks when tapped. I listen to the house breathe. When I fly, it is not a fall but a handoff to a current that keeps appointments. The chimneys are texts, the wells are doors, and all the air is useful.

Then I hang. The bat in me knows warmth as a civic agreement; we make a city out of one another’s heat. My mother is a windbreak. My neighbors are the plums of the ceiling, black fruit shivering in a breeze you feel in your ribs more than on your skin. We leave not in silence and not in speech but in a seam of sound, a held note passed from room to room. We pour into corridors of air as if the cave had poured us and not the other way around. We own what the light does not, and we do not need ownership to know it.

Smell becomes me until I am almost nothing else. Ammonia has a bright edge like a bell struck too hard, and it is sweet under that. Rock is wet chalk drawn on the tongue. Dried grass threads down from the surface like thin letters from a cousin in another country: not frequent, always recognized. I taste drafts that forecast storms. I stand under a skylight and breathe the cold of soil. I know the turning of rooms by what they keep in the nose when you leave them.

For a day—and the cave does not notice what a day is—I am light. I come down in skeins and panels and I have no rights below a certain angle. I mint gardens of ferns where the damp holds hands with the open; I stamp lichen, make small green clauses on the walls and then withdraw my signature at once. I am an immigrant with visas that expire mid-afternoon even when it is midnight up there. I do not see far. I show motion and permission and where to stop. I keep edges like borders: beyond this drip, no green; beyond this boulder, only the idea of color.

I become mineral awkwardness and honesty. I am the rock that is young for a cave. My faces are straight out of the cut, teeth that have not learned to chew rain. I have given birth to a curtain that does not yet know how to ripple. I trade sand with myself across a hall, a negotiation in whispers the wind keeps in balance. I accept nests and claws and the weight of cities of bats; I offer shelves and cradles and throats that make sound. When a block falls out of me and sits there as if it had always sat there, it is because my grammar is good. New nouns fit its cases without fuss. Nothing I do is rushed, yet it is all happening.

If I have to decide—and I don’t, not really, because the place prefers verbs to names—I would say I am most at home as a threshold. As a lintel of air you can’t see but can lean on. As the slit that turns wind into voice and voice into map. I am the between—the distance that answers, the cool that lays down water, the seam where a spider’s triangle becomes a door, the border where a fox consents to steal. I am not a creature with a lineage; I am an hour that keeps itself whether watched or not.

So where would you find me? Stand under the arch that tries to pull your hand through itself. Feel a draft choose a narrower future, and hear how the pitch rises because of that choice. That is me. Put your ear to the rock and count the returns until your blood slows to the cave’s tempo. That is me. Wait by the cup that wears its white lip like a record of marriages and deaths. Listen for the plink that lands in the exact same syllable of time as the one before. That is me. Step into a slot and feel the stone at your chest go from indifferent cold to specific cold, made yours by the breath that has just met you. That is me, too.

Who or what would I be? I would be what the permanent citizens already have for a passport: the habit of listening. I would be a participation, not a posture—a practiced set of verbs hung together like a nest: hang, pour, cling, drink, ride, listen, wait. I would be a held note in a stone throat, a dew-bead suspended from a tooth, a triangle pulled tight against the pull of air, an interval that teaches generations to measure themselves. I would be the way the place keeps time. And when I left—and you always leave, even if you don’t mean to—I would go with the drafts sliding along my skin, a low tone planted between my ribs, and a coin of light in the corner of my eye that stands for the hole to the upper world. Above, the plateau will be a clean, empty grammar of stone and wind. Below, the sentence will go on saying itself, and I—whatever I called myself that day—would still be part of its breath.
