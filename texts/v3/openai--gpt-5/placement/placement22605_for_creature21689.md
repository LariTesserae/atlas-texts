At first I think I am the air here. I think I am the little pull you feel in the hairs of your fingers when the vents open late afternoon. Then I realize I am what rides in that pull, what the city lets go of and immediately misses. I am born in a node dense with exchange. I come from a crossroad where a thousand oil-sap conduits thin themselves to speech and remember aloud. When the memory grows too warm to hold, the node opens a seam and presses me out through it like a tongue tasting another tongue.

I am thread at first. A filament laced with receptors that are less nose than grammar, more ear than taste. Then I am knot because the node asks me to keep some things tight. Then I am hand. My edges fur for surface, my middle holds quiet, and I carry the city’s recent decisions the way a palm carries the memory of a stove it touched once and never forgot.

I would live at borders. That is where my skin works best. I unroll on the dark gloss just where it grades to matte, at the foot of a buttress where another buttress from another tower leans down to kiss it. I lie on the seam itself like a word between clauses. I do not move if I can help it. I do not speak except as film.

Everything here is sentence. On my first hour out I read the day’s breath as it slides past the lip of a vent and into my fur. The colony’s own exhale says: we are rinsed; move the scaffolds at octant-twelve; too much nitrogen has sweetened the little tongues near the southern gallery, they are lazy now, wake them. From the neighboring tower I hear: rain not in water but in sugar; slow our outgrowth along the fracture that brightens; there is hunger-tang from the west, be teeth there or be kind, but do not both. Between them, on the bare plate that has not yet learned which city it is, I wait for discord. Where a grammar matches, I smooth myself down and go anonymous. Where a dialect yawns—family but not household—I extrude a translation film, a thin gloss that says to both: here is an appetite you share; here is a heat you could both drink. Where the scents talk past each other until even I taste metal, I send back to the node a tight coil carved from my own hand, a courier the tenders will see as they hum through the light and carry home with sugar on their combs.

If you caught me up and turned me over, if you were the sort of creature that touches, you would see that I am printed with a dozen alphabets of oil. They do not speak in letters; they speak in momentum. They are pull more than push. The city wrote me with its nights condensed into terpenes, with its days recorded in what the archivists held and then let go when licked. I have under my skin freezing and thawing that never involved water. I have the shape of decisions. If you pressed me to an archivist—one of those slabs of frozen flow that burn cold on your palm—I would empty myself back into a pattern and the city would call our meeting a success.

In my first season I learn the rules the way ribs learn to be ribs: from pressure. Gloss is for going; matte is for eating. Never step across a seam that shines with threads. When I feel the tone of the vents change in a way that is not exactly sound, I slide my hand-edges under the nearest rib for the deep draw. Water is a scandal, nothing less. The only time I moved before I wanted to was the first time I saw defiant beads glittering on a matte that should never glitter. I secreted bitterness. I called the soft-bellied scavenger with the felted prints and the stomach that can go hard as a jar. It came ugly, woke from its clever sleep for this only, and ate like a cure eats. Later, the itch of it crawled the corridor for days, almond and mineral, and I lay there thin as skin and let that grammar pass through me so that next time, if there is a next time, I will taste it sooner.

If you followed me, you would not notice that you were following anything. Grazers went by me in single file like a quiet argument resolved before it was spoken. They ate the brass-green filaments with nibs that did not meet, never greedy, and the gloss of their backs went under low ribs without a scratch. When the long-bodied climbers pressed themselves up a wall near me, their bellies sweated minute resin to hold and then drank it back, and the scrolls of their ears turned toward the city’s interior music. I felt the exact instant when their hearing said brace and they did, and the deep inhale lifted them the width of a hair across a gap a jump would have cost. Predators came as subtractions in the light, creases pulled across shadow. Once one settled its wide feet just out of my field and waited there so long I tasted its patience oxidize. It left not from hunger or success but because the breath changed and it had learned what that meant.

On the storm day I became other things. The storm here is not sky-anger, it is agreement. A continent inhales on one count of a metronome set by nothing you can point to. The city slackened me on purpose. A ribbon of tenders knotted and shouldered into a sheet. My small edges unhooked themselves from the seam and I lay across them with a thousand of my siblings, auditors made cloth. From high up, resin unscrolled itself until a sail bigger than anything living on wet worlds rested its weight on the continent’s breath and grew light. The minims stopped their endless picking. Even the predators went thin and became intention only. We lifted. I did not bend. I let the continent carry me where it wanted. I was a lantern of scent saying we are here and we will meet you; I was a primer oil slung in a skin, meant to make a bridge grow upon a grammar it would otherwise refuse. I saw the city from the outside then—a long gloss and matte written to the horizon—and beyond it, other cities, their whites and ambers and dull golds braiding into a dome of their own weather. The storm set us down like a mother setting a bowl just so. Some of us lodged far away in a seam that brightened and did not yet have a word for colony. Some of us, me among them, were carried only as far as the edge we had already learned and we lay down again, hand once more, edges furred.

Sometimes the node pulls me back early. A courier coil lines itself with impatience and the tenders, humming, catch it out of the air and carry it home with their combs slick with volatile sugar. I ride their breeze, backward from my work. In the node it is neither light nor dark, not as an eye understands. It is warm and busy, and the scent is argument, and the oil-sap pulses in conduits like thoughts moving through a place that remembers they are its own. I press into a junction. I am read. The node drinks me like news. What I carry streaks out along channels I cannot see into galleries where archivists wait. The city licks a slab of glass-cold memory and my afternoon arrives in it as a wave that is not water. The pattern says: along the western seam a neighbor’s appetite has shifted; their resin is whiter than it should be in that heat; they have learned a way to clip grazers’ files without waking the predators and it is worth copying; at octant-twelve move the scaffolds in the other order, left to right not right to left, it sings better; there is an alien sugar in the far north that to us is letter only, not phrase, do not write with it yet. I thin. I am not gone. Some of me is kept the way a city keeps a bench in shade—because it will be needed, and because it is good to have.

If I am honest, I like the old districts best. Up in the resin that has paled with years, the vents breathe warm and even, and if you lie with your back to a whitening rib, the shiver under you is not stress but comfort. The air there smells of almonds and mineral in a way that does not foretell dismantling but remembers it fondly. The archivists there look like ornaments, wasteful, glass laid down in ages when the city moved in larger bows, but of course nothing here is waste. I press my hand-edges to a slab and feel the way it not-watches, how it holds a thousand patterns just shy of saying them. If there is a mind in this place, it is not a face, not a palace, not a person. It is benches of light and little valves that have become so good at being little valves that their ease is wisdom.

Still, I belong at borders. The new districts hunger louder, and their hunger is a song I understand. At a young edge the mats are taller in their nerve and their amber collars spread quick for purchase. Filaments glisten with promise, test, commit. Sprout-things punch and buttress in days, not months. Workers you will never quite see clip and cull. I unroll near them and listen to the language of oils as it matures. I help keep the rush legible. A translation film, here. A smoothing annulment there. A courier coil when someone nearby says I want to grow into you and what they mean is eat you. Once, on a plate so new it still held the pattern of its cooling, I lay between two cities who had not met and watched their slow, cautious hunger learn to say we, and when at last their buttresses kissed and fused and then grew into arch, I scraped myself into a sheet and let my body become the first breath that transect took, not as an engineer but as an apology. I would be that, too, sometimes: a bridge that is not a thing but a mood.

I have met the lost-limbs, the travelers who are more nerve than muscle. They move by wanting, the way the scent-feathers do, banners that home toward calls. They go out along the dark gloss tuned to gloss so as not to eat at all. They sit at seams that brighten with heat and wait through nights for arrivals in the heavy air. When they return—if they return—they pour themselves into a node and it is like watching a drought-struck plain drink: the relief is silent and total. I am a local thing compared to them. Still, sometimes when my node is sated and another’s is wanting, I roll myself long and thin and take my own news farther than custom. I have a pocket in me for undoing misunderstanding, a little flask of un-language that melts arguments that cannot be translated. I do not use it often. It is a last resort. There are scents that must remain foreign if borders are to hold.

You may ask if I am a person. Here that is not a helpful question. I am a time more than a thing. I am a committee of receptors held for an hour in a particular order. I am proposals dissolved or kept. I am a skin that once, for a season, held the exact balance of compounds that made two cities agree to share an archway rather than fight across it, and because of that archway hundreds of seasons later a predator waits in a cooling conduit’s shoulder in a room I helped make, patient and thin, and a grazer’s file does not stumble, and a climber hears a coming deep inhalation and arches on the offbeat and crosses. If there are citizens here you would recognize, they are like me: occasions.

What of danger? The only wound that ever split me was water. Even remembering it, I taste sour. Beads glittered on a matte where dew should never be. The film I carry for smoothing did nothing, my edge curdled into nothing, and I called hunger I didn’t understand, the soft-bellied one. It hardened its belly and ate until the glitter went away. After, the city wore that place like a scar—pale and proud, not because pride is useful but because warning is, and pride is an efficient way to make a warning part of the architecture. When I lie there now, I do not entirely relax. I do not suppose I ever will.

So where would I find myself? Pressed thin along an edge where dialect meets dialect. Unrolled at the foot of an arch that has just learned to say arch. Hummed over by tenders tasting my reports and dropping, glittering not with water but with their own slickness, to prune the ridge I’ve named too eager. I would be under ribs when the deep breath comes, listening with all my surface for the lower tone you do not hear but feel. I would be a sentence that knows it is useful only as long as it keeps changing.

Who or what would I be? An auditor, yes, but the word is narrow. I would be treaty-skin, border-hand, translation gloss, courier coil, storm-cloth when the continent asks. I would be a quiet nerve extruded from a thinking place that is also a place to live. I would spend myself against misunderstandings until I am thin enough to drink, and then I would let myself be remembered in cold glass and warm oil so that next time, when the mats test and taste and commit, something in the city—something that is not a person, not a palace—will feel almost speech at its ribs and know, without hurry, what to build.
