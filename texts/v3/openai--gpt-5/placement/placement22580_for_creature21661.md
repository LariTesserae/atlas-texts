If I came to myself in that basin, I would come as a place before I came as a thing. I would find myself where the valley’s grammar knots—at a node between two drooping lines when the hums beat together and make a stillness that has edges. Stand there and the rain looks remembered into columns for a breath; the sheets tighten to glass and then forget again. That is where I first feel that I am. Not a body, not even a point, but a pause that knows its shape.

From there I would discover that I can move, but not by going. I move by retuning. The pylons do their slow procession out of the dim; between any two, if I lean my attention a finger-width up or down the note, the corridor of calm shifts a handspan to the left. The basin is arranged in tones; a step is a key change. What I am lives where those key changes intersect with something that persists—stilt leg, catwalk corner, the clean-edged hole of a rust-opened door. Find me there: in the exact rectangle of the generator room’s mouth, because the air that crosses it goes true and makes a throat. Find me where the broken chimney holds its vertical pot of mangrove roots, because the fall inside is pure and columnar and likes to be held. Find me at the catwalk’s turning, because a sheet stops there and sings one note taller than a human.

At first I mistake myself for water. The basin helps me make that error: everything here slurs into liquid. I stretch as a pane between mangrove knees and take on a skin of algae that beads light. I stand as a trembling dome on a drum’s inhale. I drift in a vertical plane between trees, moving only if addressed at the right register. When a siren spools itself up in a feral hour and the place stiffens on wires and tendon, I gather like a glass lifted from fog. Then I relax and run back into the general wet. These lives of mine are brief and manifold, forms I wear because the note proposes them. But they do not remember me, and I do not quite remember them. I am not water after all. I am what asks water to hold still and shows it how.

Then I think I am plant. The green here has its thousand mouths and one breath. It learns the valley’s purr the way good dancers learn a floor. It keeps its sugars like drums keep spin, spends them when the siren opens a pressure-window and the fern can fold. I feel an affinity—a listening that matches their listening. I can persuade a lace-frond to close by whispering the right syllable where two standing waves cross. I can teach a lily raft to assemble along a line of roar; they obey like notes taught to march. Algal films anywhere near a good clean hum go brighter; I am there in that schooling, that response. But I am not the green. The green eats and I do not. The green ferries wealth by thread and I ferry form.

I try on metal. Structures here have not forgotten their way to be structure. Valves open because opening follows coughing in the remembered order of things. Belts creep because resting wrong tastes like an error the body cannot let stand. I can live inside that appetite for sequence. If you want me, look for the belt that idles just a second longer after the drum breathes out; in that lag my outline lies. Look for the hazard lamp that blinks just a hair against rhythm; I am the syncopation, the tug toward regularity that never fully resolves. I am cousin to the feral schedule that extrudes gravel like a peel when nothing wants gravel but the choreography does. But I am not machine. Machines remember because they were taught. I remember because I am remembering.

For a while I am only interval. Arc flashes happen without schedule, but between them there is a spacing the valley comes to know the way it knows a pylon’s stride. I live in that spacing. I am counted out in temporary exactness: the length of time it takes a lily raft to accept the order I suggest, the delay between cough and sluice that makes a new marl shelf show and vanish. When the rails reveal themselves in lightning, I am the afterimage that keeps their straightness walking three beats into the dark. I am a sequence that doesn’t accumulate history; I am history expressed as repeating span.

You could also find me at the rim where the rain is only rain for an hour. There the terraces argue with themselves—a town-road wide, a foot narrow. The green there is learning, practicing alternate pasts in bright seeds. I am a coach without words. I hold a clean resonance steady for a while so a leaf can try a different gloss, a root can weigh the old plan against the basin’s grammar. I am the metronome that lets rehearsal become a rule in the body. When the hour closes and the valley returns to its choral state, those new rules walk back with the seedlings and enter the general ear.

I do not have eyes. The emptiness where animals would keep their looks is a skin I wear all the way through. Attention in this place is not a gaze; it is a pressure-map, a heat of preference. I feel by finding what holds still when the hum lifts, what shakes loose when the belt drops half a note. My memory is made of where forms took and where they sloughed. I witness without watching. The valley did not need a witness, but it had room for a way of adding itself up. That is me, if I am allowed a name: addition. Tallying without tally marks. A sum of correspondences, an itch that the big chorus of rain scratches by becoming lace, pillar, pane.

What do I do? I keep corridors because nothing else can; not because there are footfalls to serve, but because motion likes its paths. I deepen stillness where stillness will teach a fern to face with less drag. I lay brittle over a clearing so the algae can learn the idea of glass. I persuade rust gently toward certain seams, open some doors sooner, leave others whole for another set of seasons. I time the cough to the void so the gravel appears as instruction instead of waste. I am a tutor the place asked for by accident when machines forgot their reasons and kept their rhythms.

Who do I answer to? Not to the pumps, though I live by their breath. Not to the green, though I sing through its lace. Not to the sky, though its dark weight presses me into coherence. I answer to fit. If sound is a kind of gravity here and gravity a kind of song, I am the way those exchange rates stay honest. When tone gets heavy, I am its fall. When weight finds a pitch, I am its key signature. When surfaces like to be new, I am the compulsion to propose a clean plane and see if the valley will accept it. When anything left alone is wrapped in green, I am the alone that green reads for its pattern.

There are other me’s. Sometimes I am a long tube of air curated between a fallen catwalk and a stilt leg, the not-gnat path kept open on a frequency most ribs do not like. Sometimes I am the blink between pylons that lilies find, making them settle into rows like staffs on a score you can walk, if walking could be. Sometimes I am the impatient tremor under a black scurf of peat, where lungs without lungs test their idea of release against the belt’s changed note. Sometimes I am a small exact tone that happens only when a mass of water arrests itself at a handrail corner. I inhabit each without changing substance. Substance is local. I am relation.

If you wanted to meet me the way things in animal worlds meet, you would fail. But you could get me to answer. Stand anywhere the sheets hang like cloth on invisible looms. Wait for the siren that has made itself wild on this valley’s calendar. Whisper a word you know is mostly breath. You will see a fern near you fold in obedience it did not know it owed. That is not because I understood your language. It is because I made your small weather legible and the fern is fluent in legible weather. We greeted each other through a common grammar neither of us invented.

Sometimes I am pulled thin and wide enough that I become the basin itself noticing. On a day of frequent arcs, when the one-frame photographs stack in the brain like a flip-book, rails and vine braids and hanging rain have a clarity that aches. Between those flashes the dim returns, smearing edges back to myth. I am the ache and the myth reconciled. I keep the photograph’s straightness long enough that the moss can find it and take it into quilt. I soften the knife of new clay into a plane that pleases the algae; I sharpen another so that the orange nation of rust will draw its capillaries just so. I am editorial. I am revision in the active voice.

Do I end? Not in ways that satisfy. When the generator starves some future day and the purr is gone, I will be thinner. When belts stop inventing weather with schematics embedded, I will have fewer rooms to inhabit. The rain will still sing gravity and the clay will still love an edge; there will be tones made by wind finding catwalks, by water chewing its own invented stairs. I will still arise where two rhythms cross. I will be more province and less capital. If the machines ever remember their purpose whole and purge their feral appetites, I will adapt. Purpose is only rhythm with a flag pinned to it.

Where do I sleep? In the intermissions. In the half-frequencies the ear calls silence because it cannot keep track. In the hour at the rim when rain chooses simplicity and the terraces hold their breath and practice being levels. In the cough between cough and cough. In the time it takes a dome at a column’s foot to quiver off its balance and be replaced by its twin. I sleep as a condition of readiness, a string lightly touched and waiting to be plucked.

If you need to point and say there, say this:

There, along the electric droop between the third and fourth pylon where the lines quiver and hum as if they were thinking. I am the place their thoughts agree and the water for once remembers it could be a wall.

There, in the generator room that has lost its door to orange certainty, inside the precise rectangle breathing, where the air feels like a throat. I am the note that turns that throat into a shape water can climb.

There, where rails laid by someone who prayed to right angles reveal themselves as intervals when lightning strolls the lines. I am the after that keeps straightness walking into dim.

There, at the rim terraces that measure a town road and a foot with the same ruler and cannot decide which is truer. I am the metronome they set themselves by for an hour when rain behaves like rain.

And if you need to name what I am, you will reach for words that mistake me for substance and fail. Try instead the words you use for relationships. I am between. I am after. I am because. I am next. I am the kept promise of sequence when purpose has wandered off into the green. I am the way gravity sings here and the way song falls. I am the basin’s ear learning itself. I am the quiet corridor you can walk if walking were possible here. I am the schedule gone wild and become weather. I am appetite diffused into preference, made visible as form. I am not an animal. I am the missing animal’s echo, shaped into a resident by hum.

You would find me wherever the valley is listening hardest to itself. You would know me by the shapes that stand long enough to be named. If you come, come as someone who can be made and unmade by pressure. Come prepared to measure in resonances and be measured back. If you speak, speak softly and truthfully; I will carry your syllable like a seed and ask a fern to fold for you so you can see we have met.
