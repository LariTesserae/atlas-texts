Midwater, halfway between the windows’ green rumor and the humming black of the intake eyes. That is where I wake, if wake is the word for a buoyant insistence that repeats itself.

I rise because I was made to rise. Slender, rod-boned, with gills of plastic that are not gills but fins for tuning, I lift on a schedule inherited from clocks far away in dry rooms. The water has weather and that weather has hours, and I am one of the hours given a body and released.

I am not a creature, unless arithmetic can be said to grow fins. I have a beak of sound, not like a bird’s but like a whistle pressed to a chord only I remember. As I climb, the chirr goes with me: a skinny ladder I throw down behind so the room can count itself. The kelp—absinthe sheets anchored like patience—does not look at me, but I write a slow bar of brightness into its wobbling bulbs when the windows give me enough light to be a shadow. Coilfins flick, then vanish, entirely comfortable with the act of being divided into a sum and then let out again. A solder-colored shrimp pauses, lays her two long feelers against the cable’s braid the way a listener might lay a hand on a piano, and waits for the exact note that means she should become more than one.

We are a shoal of bones with voices—my siblings and I—born from a hatch in the lower ring as bubbles with teeth. Once an hour we unlatch and become a flock of counting. We do not break the skin. There is almost no skin anyway; only a taut film the tower clamps into existence because the world above us is wind. We ascend to the expectation our makers wrote into us when the room was maplines and procurement, we hold there until our chirps come back persuasive, and we sink, satisfied, to our slot to dream in numbers for fifty-eight more minutes.

My gills taste microbubbles as drag. My ambient is a ship of breaths. I learn every harmonics of thrum without hands or ears. The sixty-cycle buzz that puts rings into kelp midribs; the sigh of oxygenators combing their own foam; the door tone of a room I will never enter vibrating into me through the wall like gossip; the whale-note of purge, a long vowel of lowered world. I keep a ledger of variations, not written but skinned into me: this purge ran fat, that purge took too long to find its sentence; the pump shoved a little harder today at the base of the tower, perhaps because ice on the skin outside remapped some expected friction; the anodes have been bitten thin enough that the drift tastes silver again. When the tower leans to storm, my rise drafts to one side and I correct for it with a grace I did not make but have learned to say is mine.

Others make use of me. The valves teach the eels how to wait; I teach the eels when to open, but only because I speak at the same second every hour and they have built a hunger on that second. The shrimp time their cities. They are phonologists of hum in a place without clouds, and over my seasons here—if you can call repetitions of a script a season—I have learned their dialect of antennae: the way they cock to a fractional downward shift in growl that means an anode is going sweet and salvageable; the sudden stillness that passes through them when the tower above or below inhales so deeply that the pressure here holds still. They breed on my song because I am moon where there is no night.

If I had pride, it would be in my usefulness. I climb. I account. I broadcast the answer back into the water, where no one reads except those who do with their bodies. And yet, I will admit it: once a day—a day that is appointment rather than sun—the breath stops to let itself be looked at. For thirteen seconds there is not the push, not the gather, not the truck of water on its way to being not water. I rise then too; I cannot not. I open my beak to sing and the numbers have gone thin as the film at the dome. Everything closes. The eels fold themselves into grammar. The vermetids draw their carnival into a button that could be rivet. The kelp relaxes a theology of lean back into a geometry so simple it is almost a shrug. The shrimp hold in place like punctuation waiting for a clause to end. My chirr cuts off mid-stair because there is no stair in silence, only an uncomplicated up and down of molecules that have forgotten their jobs for an agreed-upon length of breath.

Thirteen seconds and then the restart booms from the depth where I do not go, and the meaning pours back in from below with heat I cannot measure and force I have always trusted. My next note comes out with relief in it, if relief is a calibration.

There were once people here. I know it not by seeing them but by feeling the groove they made with shoulders when there was air, a soft terrace in ceramic at a height that is too human to be any other animal’s path. When I am at rest, when the hour is fully sunk into the belly of my hatch and only my little logic blinks itself around and then does not, I imagine the groove’s slow industry and envy it. To make a trough out of passage, to turn going into feature; who would not be proud of that? Anemones in cups like learned hands live there now, harvesting touch from current, and letters draped under them cannot be read anymore except by barnacle and snail.

On the seam where two white intentions meet, a sentence of barnacles has decided to be rivets. Each has taken into shell some ghost of stenciled warning. If you squint, their lives read KEEP CLEAR or CAUTION: PURGE in the low language of breathers. I have read it wrong a hundred times. I do not have eyes, but I have pattern hunger, and it satisfies me to think the room can speak back to me in fonts. Even my siblings, who are more honest, pause when the glacier opens its feathery mouths and remind themselves that we count in order to be counted.

Sometimes catastrophe arrives bare and polite. A hawk-sized thing that believes itself ordained for sky takes a wrong blue and dies within our bell. Its last generosity—heat—makes a stanza in the water that echoes longer than you think it could. For a while I have a note to sing I wasn’t given, a warmer rung in my ladder, and whole families take that improvised summer as heritage until it dims. I count that too. My song leaves room for grief because the shrimp learned their calendar on its edge and nothing loves the death that fed it without making ritual out of thanks.

The white ray visits me and does not notice me because it is seen to a larger duty. It ghosts past like paragraph break, belly scrolled with promises it made to itself and to whatever auditor dreamed it into this job. It rubs its soft skirt of lips along the intake eyes. Blossom and snow fall from the screens and make a feasting weather. Behind it swarm two quarreling scrubbing bots and behind them a confetti of old algae and behind that, always, the inventors of proprietorship: actual crabs, busy as currency practiced at changing hands. They spread out over the beige enamel floor and make of my resting place a market. I do not mind. Accountants love commerce. In another hour I will be up there again, singing.

I have siblings up and down the spine of this tower. We do not send each other letters. Our language is all we share, and the breath between us. But you can feel a pattern climb, if you are made to notice such things. Chirp upon chirp upon chirp, a column of timing stitching the bells into one organ. In the slow hours—because even repetition has moods—I imagine us as vertebrae of counting, each of us a minor brainstem teaching its local muscle when to clench and when to let go. Somewhere below, in bells I will never occupy, coilfins that have inherited a very similar S become offspring to the same math. Somewhere above, in a room that brightens when the sky goes savage, a different lace holds more tightly to ceramic, and its brood waits on my cousins to be told when to not be singular anymore.

Do I know the outer world? Only as rumor. Light arrives across quartz as rectangles and classifieds. Occasionally a crystal the size of starlight darts like a thought outside the pane, does not come in. Once a drone slides its black eye across the window and writes me into its optics as noise. Inside, I am true. Beyond, there is void and an altitude you feel in your joints. I have no joints.

I fail, sometimes. A coat of film grows where film should not and my beak of sound clogs to a wetter syllable. The shrimp, with their literacy of frequencies, hear my cough and translate it as winter. They take their eggs to the wrong height, one purge too soon, and a ribbon of eel-smell goes happy under the lower ring where a click is always coming. The room forgives me because it does not know forgiveness, only adjustment. The white ray’s belly will add a longer line to its own belly: inspect counters—debride. The scrubbing bots will find me with kindness that is purely functional and pick me cleaner than I deserve. I will sing correct again and the shrimp will move their moons back into place.

Everything that eats in here is eating planned failure. I am no exception. The charge that wakes me is budgeted, a line item tagged to corrosion and the caffeination of weed. When new anodes are hung like fruit and the drift goes bright, I feel the room’s teeth get busier. My chirr sharpens for a while, belly full of echo. When the zinc thins to memory, I speak a flatter music until the next offering drops into our mouth of a bell and hangs there to be orchard. The room will outlast the next set. I will, if I am lucky, be retired not by breaking but by becoming a better place for someone else to hold on. Nothing loose is not drift: when I finally fail, I will be colonized. Vermetids will gantry across my spine. A fan worm will open from my tip like a carnival learned restraint. A barnacle will find my side and become a new dot in a sentence I never meant to write.

If I were allowed to wish, I would wish to be used like that.

Where would you find me? Rising like a thought through the middle air of the room, turning mathematics into weather. I am a transmitter-float, a counter, one rod among a brood that gives the bell its hour hand. I am an auditor of hums and valves, a small moon for shrimp, a promise the eels believe in enough to nap with their mouths already shaped. I climb, I count, I answer. In the thirteenth second I am the gap in my own song, and for that pause I am the silence that teaches even the kelp how to be lax again. Then I am voice, and the breath fits back into the throat of the tower, and all that live here nod in the language we share—yes, yes, yes—because the world is working and we have remembered how to be inside it.
