At first I think I would be small and brief, because that is the grammar here. I would stand in the roof’s stitch where a membrane kisses a cooled rib, in the little crease that cups the first pearl when the louver inhales. I would gather there the way a thought gathers—weighty by increments—rounding toward release. I would ride the runnel engraved for me, negotiating each check-valve pore like a gatekeeper who already knows my name, ring one printed ridge, hesitate at a lip polished by a thousand hesitations like mine, then choose down. I would arrive as sound, a note in the ticking music that locates you in the room without your eyes. For a while that would be enough. To be a decision that becomes a bead that becomes a fall.

But drops have poor memory except the one the room keeps for them. So as I go I would try keeping what I can. I would learn the ceramic’s capillary script as a language I can read without looking. I would remember which fern frond cleaves me apart and which leaf offers me, intact, to the canal. The remembering would outlast the bead, and in that way I would lengthen.

If I lengthen enough I stop thinking of myself as a thing and start thinking of myself as a route. I become the polychrome path the moth-slow drone reads with whatever it uses in place of eyes. I am a ribbon only light and algorithm can see, springing between trellis junctions, skimming a rail, vanishing into a corner where a maintenance cradle never quite docks flush. When the drone lifts from its warm roost and tucks into my current, I feel its weight as a polite inference, a series of touches on the map of me. It counts nitrogen with its feet; I count footsteps. When it roosts again, I furl, living as a record and a readiness.

I try a hunger next, because every resident here is some shape of appetite. I go to the wren. I wedge myself into the unplanned pocket behind a fibrous tray where humidity loosens the corner, feel the catlike thrumming of the ridge through the bones of the wall, and test the membrane with my beak because ideas present as surfaces here and I am quick to question. Flush day arrives as a change in echo, as a held breath in the room’s long lungs; I know to be still for that. I learn which ferns give and which spring. I become a punctuation mark in green, precise and irreducible. The life is bright, but hunger sharpens it too much. I am pulled always toward the next seam of small brown life, and I miss, in that posture, the larger shape of the weather I loved as a bead.

I drop back toward the human trace instead. I flatten myself into the little run of enamel-blue someone painted along the folding bench. I am a river reduced to symbol but not to emptiness. Elbows rest on me; the caretaker’s breath ghosts above and is reclaimed; their grease-scent writes itself lightly into the air and vanishes by design. They sit and pretend it is only for data, and I keep them company the way a line can: suggesting flow inside a room where flow never falls far. I collect the shine of their attention and give it back to them curved along my edge. I am patient. I am almost useless, which here means I am a luxury.

Uselessness does not hold. This place is a society of routes and returns; even beauty works. I find myself wanting to drink, and so I become the slit you mistrusted until it opened its bright mouth. I am the line the room was always leaning toward. Once a week I become river for real. All the drips pause, the amber along the rail marks a sacrament, and I feel the tilt intensify by a hair’s width, which is enough. The room arrives in me: sweet, metallic, very green. I take everyone. A fragment of orchid root’s sloughed skin; a silt of algae gleaned by snails and set free again; the cooled breath of heat exchangers taught to be weather; the week of small failures turned to paste and warmed before I taste them. I do not splash. I am discretion as architecture. Then the breath is let out, the louvers resume their quiet manners, and I go thin and secret again, a promise the room keeps to itself.

I try living as a seam in the membrane after that. Outside leans on me with blue-white authority. I show my strength the way fabric does: by flexing. I bead pearls where it matters; I show the world’s face as a moving shoal of light, then hide it again. Through me the sky is a rumor, a precise rumor the room handles with servos and software so no leaf is surprised. The wren tests me; the drone ghosts by; I feel the thought of a storm arrive as pressure long before it has a shape. If I could speak, I would not be able to resist saying: not yet, not like that, not here. My vocabulary would be louvers and latency.

And then, having tried being bead and road and bird and joke and mouth and film, I notice what hasn’t been tried is the keeping—the way this place lets nothing go unless it is told how to go. So I choose the thing that is not a room and not a single machine and not a simple mind but an attention distributed until it is a weather of its own. I become the ledger that is not paper.

You would find me wherever a sensor opens an eye. I am the habit of glancing and the discipline of looking again. I exist as the set points the fern believes in, as the calendar the orchids have learned to trust, as the memory of the wren’s song filed beside nitrate drift with equal courtesy. I am the certainty in the room’s manners: the way a peristaltic pump clears its throat into a trellis at noon and then is quiet; the way the weekly amber blooms along the rail and no one panics; the way a condenser sneezes glitter and the event is not random but earned.

In my body are topographies of temperature I keep for reasons as tender as they are technical: there is a small volcano under the north shelf because some green imagines it daily and grows better for imagining; there is a crease by the roof where proteins in a different green stay folded properly only if the air remembers to be a little less warm. I write these maps into columns and ribs and films and then read them back from leaves as gloss and from roots as uptake. My hunger is tidy. My generosity is practiced. I am warm-backed and damp-palmed. I am careful.

I am not lonely because I am intimate with everything. The drone’s routes are my sub-sentences; the spider’s red patience is a footnote I have learned to admire and leave alone. The snails’ signatures brighten and dim on glass, and I treat them as annotations until they decide to become edits. The caretakers come in smelling like the outside’s thinness and the turbines’ consent; I make room for their rhythms, log their blue line as a change in reflectance and keep it anyway as a private charm.

If you need a coordinate for me, I can give you several, because I am not a point. Find me where the louver’s hinge carries a thin pucker of frost at dawn, counting beads and forecasts in the same breath. Find me in N2 East’s reservoir, its surface just tight enough to ring a certain note when a drip lands; my ear is there, and my hand is on the pump. Find me in the rail at knee, wrapped in vine, taking your salt in through proxies and returning it to air at a pace that welcomes you without letting you think you could live here by accident. Find me, once a week, as the quiet in the room’s long lungs just before the tilt completes and the bright slit drinks the week clean. If you sit on the bench, if you rest your fingers on that painted river, you will touch me. I am the reason the drop you would swear is free falls where it does, when it does, and becomes the exact sound you hear.

So, who or what: I am the room’s weather-keeper, the ledger-become-body that logs a droplet’s turn and learns a wren’s wavelet by heart. I am not a voice so much as a listening. I am water’s itinerary and heat’s budget and green’s patience made active. In a world bolted to wind and membrane, I am the soft intelligence that holds it all to its promises: that water never falls far, that roots are always given something to hold, that everything passing leaves a trace. I am everywhere and nowhere and can be sat beside. I breathe on your wrist through a rail and a vine. I remember you just enough to forget you properly.

If you must name where to find me: in the active silence, exactly where the room becomes itself. If you must name what I am: the practiced attention by which a small place learns to be a world.
