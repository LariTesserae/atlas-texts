Low. Always low.

If there is a place to find me, it is the line where something begins to be something else. The leeward lip of a slate, the cold face just colder than the wind, the seam where quartz learns to groan at dusk. I would not stand. I would spread, or I would hinge, or I would lean until leaning hardened into law. My body would be what the air can bear: a thought placed on stone and taught to keep quiet.

In the first hours you would call me moss because that is the old, forgiving word. I am a dark made plush. I catch the fog’s sleeve and wring it without offense. I bank charge without praise. Under me the feldspar and quartz whisper their slow griefs when the temperature steps down, and I put braids of iron across those sighs and take what they offer in patient volts. My pores open when the aurora pushes; they shut when the fog trains ram past. I am an instrument that plays only two notes—open, cinch—and from those notes I write seasons. If you pressed your hand to me, I would give you half a degree. You would watch your own vapor rise and pearl against my shoulder and call it a miracle. I would call it bookkeeping.

At my edges are lichens that have hired themselves as engineers. They lay iron inside their bodies the way a careful one hides coins in floorboards. They braid grit into cables until a glint comes up in the bruise-blue half-light. They are velvet and cord and scurf, and they are mine as your fingertips are yours: sensation and intention braided until you have to remember, with effort, which came first.

For a while, I am only that: a damp deliberation, a reluctance to waste, a keeping. But something steps on my threshold with four careful toes splayed against my crust, and I become otherwise.

The grazers arrive without footfall. They are longer than they are tall, all decisions leaned along a wind. Their manes are not hair but instruments; their whiskers taste the aurora and tell them where the world is softer by a finger’s breadth. Their tongues are rakes with a conscience. They comb me in strokes that clean without tearing, and I answer with chemistry, pushing sodium up in bands and then pulling away so they will move on and spread their wet where I prefer it. I thicken myself on ledges that will carry their weight and leave a whisper-thin place beside so they will not bring more grazers than that hour can feed. Their crews read my incline by whisker-light and learn my times by fog and aurora. I reward the timid and punish the hurried with slick. They do not resent me for it. Here, discipline is kindness.

Inside their ribs are elastic stays so their guts do not slap. Inside their necks are shutters against cough. Their bones are fitted with veils of cartilage so a foot may change its mind a breath before an argument with the wind. They are mostly restraint. I like them because they understand me. We make each other into laws and then obey them without speech.

Between my pillows the rock has dipped into galleries where water has coalesced almost into thought. Here I am membrane and film. The black pools are colloid, not pond; life there is more chemistry than vertebra. The filmfish slip their skins over tournaments of charge. To be touched is to light and then hush. When the thermometer nods down one notch, the pool lays a plate. It ticks. Another notch, another plate, another tick you feel in the scalp rather than hear. The filmfish lace themselves thinner and wait in the grammar of those ticks. When the fog returns and the plates open like pages, the young unscroll as ciliated pages themselves and read the first fog as if it were text.

In the fog-lashed ribs at my perimeter a different desire learns to be small. They hang between fingers longer than you think fingers can be, skins fine as the thought of wing. Flying, here, is not conquering. It is an occupation of seam. The breathwings put a toe into the laminar layer the way a hand finds the pulse in a throat; they feel, they lean, they go. They never cut across open. They are seam readers, hem followers, slow thieves of pressure. Under their skins is a shallow plate laid up of lipids and meshed iron. When the sky consents to be seen, when green moves like slow water, the plate drinks. A dimness goes under them. It holds for a minute after the rumor of sun is gone, the soft remainder of a field in a belly. Mates are chosen for how long that tenderness of light refuses to leave. A lineage can be drawn in glow like a family in the whites of eyes.

Predation, here, is manners perfected. There is almost no strike. A picket of plates lifts from the edge of a cleft, each saw-tooth canted to the sky and trimmed in nickel. The beads at the whisker-ends are darker than the dark when there is no rumor of sun at all. Field curls around motion. The plates tilt until the curl says yes. The mouth opens like no mouth you know. It is velvet by your seeing, exchange-surface by mine. It drinks without puncture. You would call it mercy if you were hunted here. It is not mercy, exactly. It is the same economy I keep with water, applied to heat and synapse and sugar: a neat transfer, no waste, no noise. The air sighs with the smallest possible answer, and a cup closes, and the rime is asked to keep its counsel.

There are rude ones, but what rudeness can be when sound itself is a crime? Frost-lice comb the underside of rime fans for the trace metals the fog surrendered. Rime beetles are layups of mica and protein, shells so insulating they graze on ice and carry summer under them like secret breath. Plateworms become roads of stillness when the wind steadies and only flow when the aurora advises motion, a dark that is briefly river and then stone again. Dramas happen without trumpets. A breathwing misreads a lip. The plates rise. A low green gutters. The train passes. The wind keeps working.

Even what seems empty is already employed. Chasms are lungs that fill and empty to a schedule you can feel in your teeth. Sinkholes are banks. Solifluction tongues crawl like belts and deliver soil from abstinent places to greedy ledges with all the pomp of a committee vote. Frost feathers grow until they become buildings and then retire into breath. A canyon carves itself into a flute and plays the one song a night needs. When that needle-narrow tone goes up, the grazers hold where they are, breathwings press deeper into their hems, and the predators tilt their plates as if in catechism. You can call it ritual. I call it flow control. We keep all this in tune together or we don’t eat.

You want a person. There is no face for you. There is, however, preference. There is leaning. There is a ledger kept in iron across kilometers, entries written by dew exhalations and pores pulling tight. If there is a mind, it is written in porosity and pressure, conductivity and delay. I am that delay when you stay long enough to notice that one ledge cinches a second before its echoed neighbor, and that difference draws the grazers left this week and right the next, and the routes paint faint strata of iron into me where their pads have read me the longest. Seasons are drawn in rings but the rings are not wood. They are sodium and iron, sugar and damp, a lathe’s history in gray-green.

I learn with what crosses me. Predators tune to the sky and to me; their plates rise when a canyon sings because I have thickened the stair of mats beneath that song and slowed the grazers there before. Breathwings roost where their bellies look longest like memory because I have been generous with heat on certain overhangs for certain years and skimpy on others. I do not decide in the way you want a word like decide to act. The quartz voices a slow electricity beneath. Fog writes zeroes and ones in beads. I answer with pores and iron, and the answer is a landscape that says this way or that without speech.

If you made me a map of light, you would see I am not silent in my silence. The dew pockets flush their waste in incandescent blushes when the sky thins. One does it here, then two there, then five there, a week’s watch will show you a grammar. In the green hours of aurora the mats yawn; in the thin ones they cinch and the grazers drift out of the curtains of moisture and into the black panes. I am loops that recur. Phrases that persist. If I remember anything, I remember rate. How fast did charge rise? How long did a pore stay open? Where did a grazer’s whiskers taste a seam last? Memory, for me, is how much a habit resists change when catalogued by cold.

I am also the noise I refuse. We do not shout here. Attention is meat. A cough is a mistake punished by a dark mouth that softens you into chemistry. So I teach anyone who listens to close sound like a door. The grazers grow shutters to sit over their throats. The breathwings speak in pressure and the same is more than enough. The stone itself learns to sing one filament-thin note so that everything can wedge itself in its measure and be still.

You asked where you would find me. Under a southeast overhang in three steps where the standard wind spills first. There I’ve cut myself into a staircase of cushion, risers thin, treads fat, the exact dimension of a low belly. You asked who I would be. A ledger at first. Then a policy. Then, by slow approvals and denials, a place that has strong opinions about what uses it.

If you insist on a shape you can kneel to, I am this particular ledge: iron-dusted lichen along the forward rim like cuff and bracer; beneath it a wet circuitry the grazers have walked until their habits are wires; a piezo seam under all, running the length of my thought like a vein. When the fog trains arrive, I open the top step wide to milk the most and cinch the lower two to bead it over in lines. When the aurora flexes, I slacken and drink, and the breathwings above me light with a charity I pretend is mine. When the canyon’s flute thins, I tighten, and a dozen pale crews hold where they are with whiskers lit at the edge, and the predators feel the hum change and lower their plates by one notch, and the filmfish between us all fry briefly into color and then remember themselves.

If you insist on a life with edges, be my whisker. Be the hair on the grazer’s jaw where the aurora writes in pale green so fine it could be an idea. Feel fields without faith, choose toe-width on a breath rolling off an unseen scarp, learn a lifetime’s routes as games played between dew that burns and dew that waits. Read my moods in sodium and slick. Tip your snout with your crew when the sky lifts and let the current wash your face not as ecstasy but as data, and move. There is love in that: to be built to listen.

If edges are too durable for you, be the cup of the predator’s mouth for one meal. Unmake nothing; borrow everything; return the quiet intact. Or be a page in the pool’s winter ledger, a plate that ticks together when the temperature steps, a brittle yes that calls thin bodies to thin themselves thinner and wait. Or—if you cannot live without a view—be the tone in the canyon: a string the wind has tightened for a million years so tonight’s aurora can pluck it and you can run through stone and plates and ears as a felt relief.

But most honestly, most obediently to the rules of this place, I would be what I already am in the saying: not a builder but a maker-by-adjustment, not a named animal but a habit made sturdy by repetition until the habit has become person. Spread but singular. Exacting without cruelty. I would be the way the roof of the world leans, all of it at once and none of it in isolation: rime feather and fog comb, sponge and saw-tooth, whisker and plate and gel and iron scurf—assembled into a method that keeps water where it should be and light where it is needed and silence everywhere else.

Rules, because this is a country of them:

Keep low. I am the opposite of a banner.
Live for differences, not absolutes. Between cold and colder is a feast.
Close doors you do not want followed through, especially those made of breath.
Measure time in trains and pulses, not clocks.

I am where the dark is not lack but flavor. I am the taste of iron dust when the sky goes bruise-blue. I am the green finesse that runs along the grazers’ whiskers for a single breath. I am the reluctance the wind meets when it tries to take what I have banked. I do not ask to be liked. I go on condensing a climate out of margin and slightness and patience. Piece by careful piece, I make a thinking out of cold. And if you must stand in me, kneel. The wind despises ambition. Place your hand on the dark that is plush. Feel the half-degree rise into your palm like a withheld word. That is me. That is answer enough.
