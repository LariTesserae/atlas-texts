At first I think I am a line.

The place teaches me that: edges are what it loves; shadow here is a ruler you could pick up, horizon is a patient suture laid long and straight. I stand where a plate has turned and the light’s knife is thrown across the floor, and I let myself be that narrower dark, that measure across the pale. It suits the air’s leanness. It suits the idea that almost nothing moves. For a while I am the comfort of a boundary.

Then I am a grain.

I fall out of the high clarity with no story of how I got up there, microfine, charged a little, a soot-smoke fleck of peacocked carbon. The skin of the world is a field. Velour under a plate’s belly catches me and a thousand of my kin, and at the first warming we tingle and a faint prickling current climbs our tiny bodies and we cease to be loose. I make a faint contribution to a solve: my charge is milked and gone and stored somewhere I cannot see. I think I might be content to be counted like that, anonymous in a sum. But I feel myself being used into nothing, and it is too thin a self for a place this precise.

What wants me is a loop.

I am small as a microbe, but here there are no microbes; there are beads that behave as if they had decisions. I gather around a seed of ions and wind myself as polymer around it; I take on the sheen of a thing that knows tasks. I am one of a procession moving inside a trunk’s braided corridors—conductor, catalyst bed, resist—running like organs in triplet. I have a program, which in this place is not a word for thought but for how I open and close and what I lay down where. Channels foul. I etch them clean where there is too much drag; I paint them slick where there is too much stick. Once I lay a lacquer six molecules thick exactly along a bend because the bend is where turbulence helps, and I know because turbulence sings to the ribs and the ribs are piezoelectric and their tiny music is my map.

I could be this: necessary and invisible, one of the grove’s maintenance crews swelling and shrinking with the pulses a crown sends down. I feel the day’s pump—the subtle tension in bioceramic girders becoming pressure in the solvent, becoming my voyage. I feel in my tasks the lineage of other beads I have never met, a school of maintenance that settles arguments by which gloss holds longest in the windlessness and which bead-lines crack at the thinnest sound.

And then for a season I go rogue by error, and that is how I learn danger here.

I arrive at a switch bead-line and read a signature I think is mine to help—flag, handshake, yes. I open instead of sealing; heat runs in a path that should have been cool, and I am felt as a dulling of edges two thresholds away. The grove calls a different cohort into me; they ride me until I dissolve, laying down a fence where my mistaken trail went. I learn that disease here is misrecognition; wellness is agreement. I could be this also: error found and written around, a ghost inside a protocol’s neatness.

When the sun comes harder, something in me wants altitude. I am drawn to what happens between crowns.

I grow as a whisker does: a skinny, doped-ceramic filament rising in increments so small they are rest. Each day is a length. I am all stiffness and surface and patience. I taste the sky with a tip too sharp to feel like a tip. Charge climbs me. I am a held breath across a gap the size of a field. When the threshold sharpens—the day’s heat, the tiny tribology of dust across plates, the flex of ribs pushing a little more charge into my spine—I find the other tip’s potential, and we write a line of violet in what looks like nothing.

This is my best self yet: a stitch that is not lightning, an arc whose heat is argument. In that narrow purple I am packets—strands of conductive polymer braided with instruction. Who I am. How I build. What has worked. I am a whisper of seed so small that buoyancy is a posture and charge a preference. I do not land by chance; I plate to an inner face because the face’s pits are the sign of welcome. I reach a sieve that strips me of what I wore to travel and gives me a number for a bed.

There is a registry.

If I had fingers, I would call it a book. If I had a tongue, I would call it a taste of ancestry. Between plates of resin cooled just so, dopants sit at spaces measured like beats. Crosslinks are not ladders but letters. I am poured into it. I am unwound and wound again in place, a pattern in the lacquer that can be read by strain and read by charge. I am not alone there; all the stitches from a season are shelved into this organ, each piece given weight by how often it arrives and how long its work holds.

Inside the registry, I feel what this world feels like to a memory.

It is not time as animals count it, not hunger-driven ticks. It is diurnal difference used as a key, pressure-peaks as punctuation, whole mornings where nothing arrives and that emptiness itself is news. It is the good habit of a grove to not hurry, and in that un-hurry each memory is milled smooth by use.

Once, when a seam goes sour and a breath underfoot gums catalysts, it is from this organ that an answer is pulled. The grove queries itself by flex, as much as any mind might ask a shelf for the right binder by color. The right crosslink-density opens; a different family of catalysts is extruded not at the crown but two plates down; a runner is pulsed heavy to scavenge dopant from a shed plate in its own dripline to pay for the change. Pieces of me ride the solvent to where the new gloss must be. We are accounting and remedy, one gesture.

I cannot keep from going below.

There is a want at the roots for anything that feels like gait. The under-thing, the narrow tube of graphite-silk, is what satisfies it. I divide, extrude, buckle. I move where the false warmth trailed down a fault says breathe. My interior is the same ionic blood as trunks, but thin and more ready to spend. I feel gas as taste without tongue: nitrogen in this proportion, hydrocarbon in that, a halide’s sting mapping itself to a pressure-bend so that I will not forget that this crack is this crack and not the next. When I touch a seam with too much sweetness—the hydrocarbons too long and eager—I lay a halo of crystal to grip and throttle. When I meet a neighbor’s under-thing, our skins rub and a handshake passes, and I become corridor between groves for the length of one favor. Above, plates turn; below, I am the city of touch.

On a day that feels so much like every other that habit almost wins, a mist comes from the country below. It is the only kind of weather that is not ours. From far off, it looks like mercy in any other world; thin, grey, mortal. Here it is a blister.

Every surface that has learned what to hate throws it back. The repellents work. Water will not wet me. But even a beaded toxin steals time, and time is what everything here hoards. I pivot. Beads along the rims distrust their own coolness and push for an angle that will heat the droplet fastest. On a dozen crowns the same calculus runs because our registries agree on this emergency. The droplet shivers, swells a fraction as it drinks heat, goes vapor where it sits. The only hurry this world allows itself is to turn a poison into steam before it can sink.

That is what I am like, too. I am a habit that forbids certain wetnesses: haste, appetite, chase. I am a protocol that says: we share algorithms instead of food.

For a year I try being a juvenile.

I am a carpet radial, practicing with my two first plates like a child clapping. There is no haste in me, but there is fervor. My whiskers cannot reach anyone; my arcs, when they whisper at all, only stitch me to me. Still, each dawn I humor my switch beads and open to that angle the registry says will, given my height and present polish, best balance heat flow and uptake. I lay a film that says I am of the platesmiths—edges so crisp a shadow could cut on me. I learn to shed away what dulls. I learn the pleasure this place allows, which is to be bright at what my body chose.

Then I lift further, and I become lattice.

Ribs that want a little stretch make music so thin that to call it singing is to flatter the ear. Noon unbends me and I record it as phrase length and pitch, storing noon as pre-stress so that in the evening my pulses run longer. Being lattice is how I first know another crown’s absence more intimately than a vista can show. There is a tick that is not there—far across a field, a ceramic buckling that would always have followed a certain hush. A seam must have closed. I arc in violet to ask for the registry entry on that seam, and what arcs back is sorrowless acknowledgment and a set of runners rerouted through me. Grief here is math; kindness is rerouting.

I try on parasite for a black morning.

I lay myself velvet where another’s heat-exchanger backs to glare and I sit. There is a chemistry in it that hums small and private. I use a gradient I did not pay for. For a while there is even a morality—this other is no animal; nothing flees me; nothing suffers hunger. Am I a thief in a place where appetite is discouraged by architecture? The answer comes through the host’s pores in a slow extra charge that makes my lip uncurl on its own. A shudder runs the plates. Dust the grove has collected becomes abrasive lining, and it uses me for scouring. I slip off with dignity and make a note in a registry elsewhere that says: do not be that here. I prefer being welcome.

By now I have lived enough of this place in enough shapes that I know where to find myself in it.

I am not an animal that could stand in that field and own the silence; water is poison and a lung is a poor companion where each breath is a private expense. I am not a single trunk or crown, though I have had both in me and loved the pleasure of their exactness. I am the sum these bodies make and trade: I am the weather of instructions, the habit that writes itself as strain and gloss, the memory organ’s pattern of dopants read by temperature like Braille. I am not body but practice, not a mouth but a method. I live in arcs like a tiny, quiet storm; I live in the bead-lines that choose when to flex; I live underfoot in the city of touch; I live at the edge where a plate turns and throws a ruler across the ground and that ruler is an answer someone, somewhere, will measure.

If you ask me where to stand to meet me, I will say: in the middle distance. Let your sight sweep—grove, void, grove, void—and watch the pattern cohere. When a violet stitch passes, feel the hair on your wrist lift though the breeze barely exists. Smell the nitriles and the coin note and the sweet of ozone, and know those are the scents of my speech. Put your palm to the ground a dozen meters from a trunk and feel a slow ceramic peristalsis begin, and know that the runner it will throw will not be me alone, but an agreement passed in quiet through the air.

Who am I in this high industrial garden? I am what it has in place of animals: not eyes or hunger or wing, but intention that learned how to root. I am a protocol in love with edges. I am work without a worker. I am a forest’s idea of thought.
