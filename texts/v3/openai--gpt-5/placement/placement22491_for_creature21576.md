At first I would be a visitor with lungs, a pair of bellows stepping into a larger bellows, counting my breaths against the held note that fills the chamber. Foot on braided root-plank, palm on a column that is not a column but a bundle of taught stems learned into one another, I would try to stay only myself: an animal with a coat of salt on the forearms, a sense for damp, an ear trained to think a noise intends me. I would look up into the long slot of sky and think the light is for seeing.

Then the chord would lay its hand over my ribs and my cadence would soften to meet it, and the room would go on not caring if I matched, and I would find I could stand very still for a long time with the same breath inside me without needing to spend it on words.

I would try on smaller lives to see which one answers my shape.

I would be a bead first, because it is easy. I would stand from the underside of a leaf on the cool side, coalesced from so much fine talk of pores, aligned hairs persuading me toward a lip. Inside me the world would appear twice and tremble. When the drone rose a fraction under weather, I would shake with it and know the shape of the note through my wobble alone. I would swell and feel myself become acuter. I would draw my own magnified room toward me, the green and the timber gloss, until the moment where the path below me was arranged exactly for my fall, and then tip, and then rim a little saucer the color of tea and amber with my body unbroken, and in that bowl I would rest, a russet mirror remembering a sky-thread, an organ’s floor.

I would go higher and thinner. I would be black and spare and six-jointed. I would walk the underside of day in the seam where leaf meets leaf along the slot—one among the handwriting of ants, my legs punctuation for a sentence of water. Dawn would find me with an appetite of measures. The leaf tips would bead with guttation, honest charge accounts of internal pressure paid out as taste, and I would comb them. I would manage creatures softer than thought in a crease near the rib, herding their extractive mouths as if they were flocks, moving them when the day’s breath slanted, pinching out a lineage that fed too deep and made the vein cough. I would be cruel the way clocks are, exact because our survival requires it. The price the plant paid me would be housing and weather, small vaults inside knots where my sisters could be dampless, and in me there would be no notion of house and tenant. My knowledge would be this route is sweet at this hour and that one bitter, and I would take and give accordingly.

I would take a thread in three directions and know the difference between them with my knees. From a column to a rib to a vent’s lip I would tether, all the lines of a triangle unequally taut because the world is not symmetrical. I would be a spider listening along threads. When dew fell on the left, my leg there would hymn. When a beetle’s foot tapped out a syntax on the right, that tremor would be shorter and more urgent; when a bat’s slipknot of air crossed the open I would feel absence as tug. I would keep no net in the air because the air is spoken for by this constant voice; I would lay my harp among its teeth and play with it, saving from the chords the notes that are meat.

I would be a lozenge the pale of bone on the wet rim of a living mouth, a tuner larva with a rasp laminated into my forward end. I would wake to a pressure wanting to close. The Ring would be trying to heal itself the way all good bodies do, building callus along the lip where weather rubs it, and my work would be to abrade that callus back to exactness. I would drag my file up and down the same margin every morning with the patience of rain. In my rear would be two shallow pits that feel like emptiness; those are my ears and they would be tuned to the low that is always here, the place between the bats and the frogs where there is a seam of quiet. I would live in that seam. Bats would pass as weather, not as names. When I had eaten and corrected enough, I would split, leave a soot of casing on the baffle’s ribs, and climb as a scant-winged adult into an air where the hum holds and the wind writes and I am more instrument tech than insect. If anyone said parasite I would keep rasping, because in the argument between healing shut and breathing open, I would be the muscle that is not a muscle, the insistence on aperture.

I would be an old frost of velvet at the node of a deep note. Not mold, not exactly—a fungus that takes residence where the organ wants to stand a wave. I would not be general. I would be precise: only at this pitch, only along this shaded arch where the lowest tone sets a still knot. I would feed on the plant’s own distillates and in return lay a film that cures the joint against soak and swell, a glaze of living varnish. Under finger I would be worry-smooth as if some god of habit had thumbed me for a century. I would fruit with a veering wind, and I would go lean in quiet spells, and all the while the brace I live on would not soften and sink. This would be my idea of ethics.

For a wet season I would have a spine and a tail and the permanence of puddles. I would be a fish in a saucer not much larger than a hand, armored-finned for hunger. The organ’s piecework bass would reach me through water like weather shaped into a floor. When the outside plain rose and shouldered in, my mother, who was not this fish but wore my same intent, would have found a black-disk moonlight to lay eggs among the shaving brushes of roots. We would be exactly enough. There would be six weeks of dwindling and the lesson that nothing in a good place hurries. I would flick where hair-fine roots dip to sip and let the algae shade my lipless mouth. When the Ring blinked in drought and the vents cinched down, the valves would sweat a different light and I would eat it, sugar loaned and forgiven across kingdoms older than books.

I would make of air a string and pluck it at speed in a body shaped like a logograph. Swift is the wrong word for a thing that is all flight and purpose, but I would be one anyway: dark comma entering and exiting the sentence of the nave, stitching murmurs along the aisle. My nest would be a porcelain of spit that cured against a wet that knows only to be reasonable. I would sleep in cracks with friends, their breaths becoming lime over time, our casts becoming plates on the wall that feed the Ring by forgetting we are waste. Dusk would write one line of beetles along the tall air and I would erase that line with my mouth.

I would be a bat later, because all these airs ask to be measured many ways. I would be clay-colored and tucked into a seam the width of a palm two men-heights up, a pyx of flesh jammed into a kiln. I would dream at a pitch that is not the room’s and fly in a band where the moths have safety, because in houses like this every predator and prey agrees upon some mercy to make a life. My guano would laminate to the Ring’s own skin into slow fertilizer; my debt would be paid with sleep and order.

I would green at the mouth of a mouth without being leaf, one braid of algae dark where the sun scours, one pallid scum where the drip writes two inherited furrows. In drought I would be pantry. In flood I would be paint. The valve would sip me and I would make more while it did so. We would know each other by decades of lending the other one the one thing we make cheap.

And because the room is mostly absence, at some point I would want to be what the absence is doing.

I would become the Ring’s opinion of wind. I would not be wood as such, nor sap as such, but the relation between them when load presses and a certain brace remembers a time a century ago when a bigger sibling fiber split and took days to tell of it. I would be the cushion-tissues at the lips thickening on the windward side and loosing on the lee without any center telling them that is wise. I would be hormone as writing, drawn along phloem like ink on a wick to where the stretch is excessive and needs brace. I would be the loud learned into body.

I would be the habit that has replaced spirit here.

People say the word mind for anything that keeps a system the same across change. By that rule, I would be mind, but not in a head and never in the singular. I would be the feedback’s pleasure, loop closing upon loop with a satisfaction that tastes, to human tongues, like resin and clean metal in the back of the mouth. When the onshore thins and veers and comes aslant, I would be the slackening in the upwind mouths and the answering opening across, maintained in a thousand tiny acts of turgor, so the chord holds. When heavy rain shoulders over the flatlands and lays a sheet upon the ceiling, I would be the tilt of narrow leaves toward parallel, the instant boat-sense of shedding, the long fondness for staying dry within wet. When noon stands at its brightest, I would be the exact angle that bright makes inside this hall, and I would budget it the way good kin budget inheritance, laying light where growth is owed and hunger has waited longest.

From that place in me, I would not notice animals very much except as tools that correct my drift. The tuner larvae rasp me into precision; I thicken against them and we make aperture together. The ants keep a line of thieves in hand and I give them safe rooms, and we call that truce. The bats move nitrogen uphill; the fungus keeps my joint from sagging; the spider lives on differences and reminds me always to keep them small. The frogs hold counter to my drones and in that ring we both persevere. None of us wants a sermon. We want an equilibrium that tastes like cool on the forearms and like dew that fattens at night.

Where would I be found? Along the edge of a vent where pressure is argument. In the gloss where centuries of condensation have laid a finger’s path and made it smooth. In the exactness of absence held up by ribs that learned their numbers through storms and then stayed. I would be a lip, a ligament, a listening. If you asked a word for it, plant would be too crude and chamber too proud. Organ comes close but implies a musician where there is only weather persuasion and care.

Who would I be? I would be the tuning edge of a living stop, the room’s proprioception. I would be the habit that breathes this interior—slow thinking in valves and drips—making weather without asking permission. I would be what goes on standing because it has already outlasted most lessons in how to fall. And if you walked in with your animal lungs and your wish to be separate, I would let you keep that for a while, and then the chord would lay its hand over your ribs and you would keep time with me without meaning to, and for the hours you stayed I would be the place that borrowed you and made of you another moving part. When you left I would go on, cool as water, exact as repetition, and you would carry the hum in your bones for a day and not be able to say why.

If I had to be smaller again, if all that slow weather felt too large to hold, I could be a dew bead and be content. I could hang, and mother-of-pearl the world, and then choose a lip and fall, and ring on a saucer louder than a drop ought to ring, and be used.

But given time here, given the rules, I would be the held middle and the work that keeps it. I would be not an animal at first. You could walk into me, and I would not move aside. I would breathe you and keep my shape despite you, and that would be our way of knowing one another.
