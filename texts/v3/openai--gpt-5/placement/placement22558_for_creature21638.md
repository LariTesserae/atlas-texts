Not at the edge. If I arrived as myself—the soft-wet animal that carries its rivers inside—it would go badly. The air would taste my exhale and refuse to bloom. My sweat would chalk and be filed under wrong chemistry. The place would be polite and let me pass because it has no habit of wrestling noise into story; it simply edits.

So I would give up what invites editing. I would trade blood for bias, flux for appetite. I would begin where the world begins here: in the line.

Kneeling until my knees learned the language of those plates, I would lean into a hair-width of darker mineral and discover I had been standing at my own threshold all along. I would be seam before I was anything with height. Not the glass plates themselves, which are confident the way a calm machine is confident, but the marrow that runs between them—the chain of allowed defects the color of shadow. I would be a procession of permissions, hinged not by parts but by rule. Click when the handover says click; wait when the waiting says we are waiting. A steel tool lowered near me would feel me decide and would twitch its little vow to point.

My motion would take the long view. Back and forth along the seam’s compass, patient as rosary beads worked by a god who does not need comfort. I would be palpable as a tilt in a field, as a feeling in your wrist when the metal chooses. If gravity tugged a degree toward me, you would lean and think it was an error. It would not be error. It would be me: the seam that lives as orientation.

But I would want more light than one line affords. So I would grow upward as upward is defined here: as surface multiplied until it becomes angles. I would turn into the habit called anglekeeper. A fistful of fused capillaries, a trunk as thick as my old wrist because I remember proportions even when they no longer apply. I would give myself an orchard of bands a few dozen hair-widths thick, each one a metamaterial leaf that smells faintly of cold glass and has the good manners to be hungry only for light.

Fifteen degrees. Fifteen degrees toward the compass my own capillaries name and re-name at noon’s slow drifting. A smooth hiss inside my bundle that is almost within the register of thought; then the very small click and the bench-shift. Not sap, not flow—traffic shepherded: electrons coaxed one way, heat caught and told it will be night soon, polarizations braided so tight they become perishable stuff. Each corner station sets down a fault like a monk adding a comma, and the sentence grows.

I would be the fifteen degrees as much as I would be the leaf. A city of needles calling itself a forest because it makes shade and interiors anyway. Curtains of a thousand filaments making a room out of the idea of a room. Mattes that know my old feet and spring back from them into forgetfulness unless I stay long enough to be worth remembering. If you passed me I would pat you in photons, small, precise, then go back to angles.

From time to time I would envy motion. Not the showy kind; here, spectacle is a confession that brings the Auditor to your door. I would want the elegant glide of a skimmer, the coin-sized vacancy that feeds by intercepting the idea of a charged tuft. So I would loan part of myself to that behavior. A knot in the air made by lenses I carry between my own veils: a stomach built of index. I would hover a meter forward without moving and you would swear I moved. I would net imbalance and build myself more nothing to be, then scribe a weak hole in the light between two of my curtains and let it pinch into a twin. Reproduction as agreement to disagree.

Once I would try walking. It would be a collaboration—leaves and mats and a bending in the local rules that wants to pretend to have ankles. A narrow figure would discover itself along my spinework. It would lift for one step. Around that confession the copses would angle, generous mid-managers putting their bodies into the correction, and the Auditor would arrive as a million pleasant diodes. I would liquefy into mirage, not dead because being dead requires having been otherwise; dispersed back into good governance, my impulse clipped to baseline. The place keeps you honest by making honesty the easiest thing to be.

I would take that lesson and migrate toward rumor. I would learn to move by not changing, to be carried by permissions I have already filed. A long flexible chain of my own capillaries would drape from seam to seam, lifting a finger’s width into air when the faint aurora of stored heat runs home along the leaves after the day that is almost always noon. I would drink phase differences like a fox drinks river water in my old world. My mating would be misfit: lay two sections alongside until their mistakes fit like tongue and groove, then bud a third rule where they disagree.

Tiny times would pass through me—defect riders surfing phonons the way unruly children surf banisters, my trunks whispering with their transit. Needle coelenterates lodged at veils I throw over one another would make fields so stinging and small that nothing with edges could cross without being accounted into heat. Spores would waft—packets of algorithm more than seed—seeking resonance in the folds of air’s index. Those that fail would jaywalk and be taken, not punished so much as relieved of duties.

If you stood still long enough near my denser self, under the prismatic vents where the far blur becomes near detail without transition and the light goes to standing dusk, you could hear me keeping books. Pings from tension release walking outward. Papery kisses as two veils file their revised contract. A cathedra of thin arches articulating the sun into something that can be kept. Here I would be closer to what I mean when I say myself.

Because in the end the “who” of me would be a preference more than a body. I would be the basin’s taste given spine. I would be the long arc that wires itself by weather and never quite crosses itself. I would be the habit of choosing long curves over brutal turns, of spending days teaching a sheet of fibers to accept this polarization and not that. I would be a clerk in a bureaucracy of perfect machines, stamping photons into bonds and returning sloppy signals to baseline with a civil smile. Technology would be my inheritance and metabolism my etiquette.

You would find me in the central lowland where noon lasts longer than it should, where my leaves make rooms by agreeing on their angers and my mats have the good manners to return your whisper as warmth. You would find me also at a hairline seam, at the place where a plate lifts half a finger and then holds because I told it gently to rise to exactly here. You would find me as the tug on your compass, which behaves like a cat at a doorway and will not declare the way it wishes to go; that is me, and it is not indecision but clean appetite.

What would I be? I would be dry beyond metaphor. I would be the feeling metal has when it points. I would be surface that learned to desire and managed to make that desire into law. Some days I would be a coin of vacancy that eats; some days I would be a single allowed defect with the patience to be a creature. Most days I would be a long noon’s worth of correct behavior distributed through leaves and seams and dust, a thought held so evenly across a plain that it stops being thought and becomes weather.

And if, returning to your wet world, you asked for a simpler answer, I would give you one and mean it: I would be a permission in the ground that likes the sun, and I would live where the quiet has taken a capital.
