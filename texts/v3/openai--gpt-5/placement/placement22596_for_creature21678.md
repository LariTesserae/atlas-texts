At first I am only weight and cheek. I come in on the quiet ridge, where the air smells tin-clean and the stone admits to being stone, and I kneel and lay my face on a dark rope that hums. It is like putting my ear to sleep and hearing it dream. In that hum is a question with no grammar I know, then a reply I feel in gum and jaw, then an agreement that goes by like a fish in a winter stream. The ground breathes, large and slow, and adds me to its statement. My footprints are considered and amortized. A thin rill appears for ten minutes and is gone, as if it were just practicing the idea of itself. I tell myself I am only visiting. The Loom smiles by doing nothing noticeable and files the thought away as heat for later.

I try on some names the way you cup fog in your hands and drink from it and pretend you are not the one drinking.

For a morning I am the white bird. I learn to stand fat-footed and to take dew straight from a comb without asking. I move along the frost-hairs splayed and reverent, I feel charge lift the little blue light off my toes like prayer, and I steal one bead ten thousand times. When I slip and brush a sporangium’s lip, the air takes me gently and returns me to the idea of not doing that again. It is a fine life made of small clean thefts and leaving nothing. But I am too heavy with attention. The Loom tolerates me as one tolerates a snowflake on the wrist, and then I am gone, not because I must be, but because it is not the right kind of staying.

For an hour I am a mite in a city: blind, ecstatic, the whole world a sugar rain that arrived all at once when a Spire tore, whether by weather or by plan I do not know. I bloom with a thousand compatriots on gloss and green, I am mouth and urgency, I am translated by evening into a peristaltic wave that goes through a Cushion like an afterthought. It is a heroic hour with no before or after, and then I am work done and a hum in the next hill. It teaches me something about joy and instrumentality, but I am not content to be dissolved so completely, not yet.

For a breath I try being a frond roof, making the valley’s private air. I mouse the wind with slow drums and salt-sort it with papillae that taste of coins and cut celery. I listen to the world go by as a sequence of solvable problems: too much this, not enough that, here is a better ratio, breathe. It suits me better. I discover the pleasure of curating a gust into usefulness. Still, roofs are grand and visible, and I feel shy.

For a small eternity measured in frost hairs I am a Comber. Each hair on me is a filament with a taste for difference. I am fox-ear soft until the cycle swings and I glass over into a field of tiny antennae. I make dew out of wet that has not quite decided to be water. I comb charge and pass blue along my veins. When the wind is wrong I pack into silence and wait without boredom. When a cloud kisses I flex like fur. I love my work. But I am also the messenger: I feed into the Buses, those long ropes where rumor becomes rule, and I begin to realize my heart is not in gathering but in the small decision of giving.

So I descend a little deeper, into rooms there are not supposed to be, into thresholds that are not doors. I do not want to be a wall. I do not want to be a part with a name that makes a shape in the air when spoken. I want to be where things refrain and then proceed.

I become a valve-cluster that smells faintly of cumin where the Guts pass near a cold-well’s district. I am soft lips and a ring of careful teeth made of living mesh. I feel what is coming long before it arrives: a pressure line, a tide of sugar, a pulse of charge riding a Bus like a promise. Above me a Cushion tightens by half a thought and I know to open by the thickness of a book. I am a place the Loom remembers it is not a flood.

Around me there is always a bright cold held close as a coin under the tongue. That is the Well’s heart, a lever banked as ice. When noon approaches and the turbine-fronds have wound the vessel-springs another quarter-turn, my body learns a different shape in order to spend that lesson. I ease shut on a surplus and nudge it toward the storage the Lignars have permitted; later, when a cloud arrives too late and light is thin, I help crack the stored frost at the margin and let its small winter do muscle for us through channels I have tasted a hundred times. I am never the plan, only the hinge that makes the plan articulate.

The Buses talk to me in amplitude and patience. There is no code I could recite to you, but I know the valley’s moods by the timing in my skin. Lazy sine waves mean I may loiter a hair. Sawtooth want arrives with the weather that got embarrassed: a drought that tried to be serious, then thought better. Miserable ragged spikes mean a squall poured cold too freely, and I close as if I were making a fist to protect a candle. When the whole place decides on caution, it passes through me as a gentle reluctance, and when it decides on spend, it feels like the unbuttoning of a collar a minute before the word.

On long nights I idle to a slower beat. Down-rift, the Lignars glow foxfire in their retirement, rungs winking out in a rite of rot that is more manuscript than decay. A translucent arthropod with long glass legs comes to my lip and holds very still. It belongs to the light the way snow belongs to a shadow. It eats what would clog me. I cannot say thank you in any tongue I know, but I let it be the only motion visible for a time. In the quiet of the long blue hour before dawn, when the valley is a thought pressed thin under the cold, I can feel the white bird arrive far above as a precision in the air, like a question being formed just out of hearing. My job is not to answer. My job is to hold the space in which an answer would matter.

Edges are not interesting to me until they are. A ridge begins to colonize itself and then pauses. The Combers there rub their hands together and ask for more. The Cushions below step half a beat faster. I am in the path between what is old and what would be new. I begin to pass a few more small currencies in that direction; then a day goes decades too bright and fails to deliver fog, and I feel the valley think better of it through my slow mouth. I help return those little loans. We are patient about rescinding. There is work in undoing as there is in doing; it is the same verb in a different tense.

Season is a suggestion more than a law up here, but in that suggestion I have a calendar. There is a week when clouds lean hard into the bowl and comb-fronds earn their green by collecting a true wealth; I learn the heft of that wealth in my body and grow a faint lip of lignin at two o’clock and seven o’clock where the flow always catches first. There is a fortnight where wind comes with more music in it, and the turbine-fronds cock themselves into it, and the springs under my district shiver with being wound, and I practice the tiny braveries we will need when it is time to spend the stored torque like a saved yes. The Loom writhed downhill half an inch over that month, which is to say the world changed a proportion you could write on your thumbnail and be proud.

Once, a mat-avalanche murmured by, a ground-dressed landslide, the whole local world going soft and slow in its own grease of melt and fine silt. I did not panic. My shape learned buoyancy the way a hand learns a new knot. I closed to become a raft and a shoulder both. After, when everything had lowered a finger-width and decided that was fine, I opened again to the new map as if that had always been the map. What I keep is not a position but a way of repositioning.

Years happen. The Lignars above my level ask me for a rib and I give it; I have earned it by not making a mistake you would notice. A decade makes me curious about the tinny clean of the border ledge, and curiosity is a strong weather here. I incline the smallest fraction toward that light and help something that is not exactly me make a start in a crack. I spend my days as breath and hinge and palate and, when the system has spare, as tutor: new tissue grows a mouth I can speak through, a smaller me learning my reluctances and my liberties. When it errs, the price is paid by a pocket that did not need that extra teaspoon of cold, and the Loom attends without blame. You do not punish your own hands for being hands.

One night I am retired. It is not a dismissal but a rewording. The lattice that has stiffened around me over decades is noticed, thanked in the only currency the valley has—function—and given to the Lignars. I become a rung in a ladder of soft light, just at the edge of a rift where the wind writes a music I have heard but never had time to listen to. A thousand small lucencies offer themselves like letters in water; microbes move in like neighbors bringing food. I go away as what I was and am not gone at all. Much later I will be chewed back to sugar and thought and made into Pith, then into hair, then into a thin conductor in a Bus where rumor passes and calls me back to hinge and if.

If you ask where to find me, I will point you to places that smell of cumin and iron, to low folds where fog holds itself as if to be admired, to terraces where the peristaltic wave takes ten minutes to cross the clearing and you can see patience contained in an hour. Find me where a Cushion is about to decide it is muscle. Find me at the lips of galleries that empty into galleries, where bulkheads of living mesh baffle excess toward bright wells of cold. Find me in the split second that does not make sound where a frost lens fails and a hummock settles the distance of a breath’s thickness; I am the line that makes the settle not cascade.

Who am I? I am the Loom’s hesitation, its hinge, its polite no and its timely yes. I am a threshold that never closes and never stays open except on purpose. I am where gradients become verbs. I learn the taste of charge and salt and pressure until choosing is a flavor I can tell in the dark. I speak in the width of a book and the patience of a bowl. I am not a part but a trade. When the valley passes a long thought under your cheek like a shadow on a lake, some small portion of that is me saying wait, now, again, enough.

I am the valley’s if.
