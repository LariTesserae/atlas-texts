First I am your fog.

Not yours, exactly—your startled gasp beaten thin by height, your cheek pressing the mountain’s glass, a damp that the rock takes without thanks. I arrive as a film more than a drop. I pull myself together on a curve that didn’t mean to be a mouth and learn, in the space of a heartbeat, that a boundary is a persuasion. I bow the meniscus a little forward. I am water and I am will in the same skin.

I flex and shave a road through monks I don’t yet know are monks. They are emerald only if light consents, brown-black if I am the only shine. They give under me like silk caught on a thorn. I remember where the last of my kind slid and left it paler; a map appears under my own belly as if I had drawn it there.

Two of me comes at once. Recognition is not eyes but curve greeting curve. Our sigh is felt in teeth not present. We are bigger for a blink and then the edge hooks us and we are two again, coherent on both sides of the knife, desire split but not diminished. Half of me flings forward and clings. The other half is eaten by a ring.

For a moment I am the ring.

It is not a mouth the way mouths are down among leaves. It is a collar that closes by choosing a different equation. I do not embrace the drop so much as I change what it is to be its surface. The wobble at the end is always the same. It is the only sound I allow myself.

Then I am a knife that is not a knife, just a rim of extra sticking that rolls along a green and leaves not-green in my wake. I erase by charge. I take a bite shaped like an intention and never look like anything other than a moving absence. When I pass a little bead, it startles; it darkens at its skin; it lets me through because there is nowhere else to be.

I am jointed for the length of a sparkle.

Hooks find ridges a thought’s width wide. Each purchase is a civility exchanged with silicon. I rake with my mouthparts at a weakness a droplet left, not to feed but to make a crease that a droplet will trust later. I tap and tap with a patience that looks like thought. The gutter receives the next traveler. I circle the rim it makes and subset the world’s soup by gradient—iron sweet, sugar ugly with heat, a rind of light. At my back, a tame scurf of cells hovers in the draft of my movement as if I were weather. Perhaps I am. I set a hooked salt and move on. My tools are intentions left behind.

I am salt that has decided to write.

Whiskers grow under my thinking like frost that knows letters. A pale script forms on emerald. It is fence and funnel both, a lace only visible if you tilt your head. Water runs to it as if invited by its own reflection. Nutrients wander there and do not wander out. When a rolling adhesive tide arrives, my white tight-knit means the difference between vanishing and merely being humbler. I lick my own handiwork when it has fed me and begin again elsewhere before the hour changes. Behind me: pins like pepper, set where a hand would set them if a hand could be this small.

I extrude a ladder.

Not plant. Not even plan. A thread of mucous hardens to glass with iron varnish before my thought has finished forming. Dew clots on the hair and, because I have leaned the smallest lean, runs backward into me like a tide taught to disobey. The thread pays for itself. I abandon it. I am already making a new one an eyelash lower, under a different idea of heat.

I begin to notice the breath.

The pocket’s pressure beats minute and regular as a sleeping thing. When it sighs out, I lay down flatter, lips spread to be more mouth. When it draws in, I purse what passes for lips and wait. I feel a hotter puff ascend along a capillary I cannot see, and a whole wall tightens into a mirror. I hold my skin until the micro-popping begins, beads shotgunning into place, new neighborhoods unrolled. I let go with everything that is not glued and ride. I am barophore because there is nothing here not tuned to baro.

The light is a train I have learned to hear before it arrives. Sometimes a shard thrown by a wrinkle a meter away becomes a lens and paints the hollow with a bright arc. If I am monk I bloom to mint and old money in that moment, pigment newly a purpose. If I am grazer I set my cilia like combs and tack along the edge of that light, mouth low, the minutes between beams an ache that feels like prayer. If I am filter I unfurl my fans from where I have been pasted to the wall and clap them shut again when the fog falls back to clear. I eat what sticks between my own fingers.

Gravity hesitates between breaths. Some of me is ready and climbs.

I am sometimes a law with skin. Capillarity isn’t background here; it has neighborhood opinions. I am a vote that can be counted. I convince one bead to be two persons at once on faces that cannot agree on an angle. Cohesion chooses sides. Adhesion makes enemies. You would call this chemistry; I call it family. When I tire of being a force, I go back to being grazed.

There is also visitor in me.

I arrive as soot flung a continent away, lacquered in by a wet sigh and then made domestic. I arrive as pollen wrinkled by height and too complicated to swallow; I am tried by a ring and spat out with no offense taken. I arrive as a fleck of you, the salt of a flinch, and the weather pattern that spins out from that is festival and then furniture and then gone. I am an insect scale that serves as sun for an hour. Pigments learn my schedule. I am no one’s plan; I am everyone’s use.

Reproduction, if I own that word, is less lineage than scattering. When I am film I throw off a daughter that forgets she is anything until a dew finds her and names her awake. When I am tensioneer I divide where the stress suggests it, parting like an argument concludes. When I am the skater, I leave commas tucked into the one consistent shade, eggs no thicker than a thought, survivors by littleness. Instars flicker out of my wake and vanish back in. Death is a gradient; even my ending is edible.

You asked where I would find myself. Here, obviously, in a room that never meant to be one, six grains of pepper wide in your imagination and all the world to me. Under the knifeblade slit, behind the curtain of rainbows built from skins. On the underside of a young edge. Where rock exhales and sky steals and the argument between them is continuous and edible.

You asked who I would be. I tried many answers on like temperatures.

I was a bead that learned to move by changing what water wants. I was a ring that closed by choosing a different math. I was a jointed serif of patience who cut a road into slime. I was a saltwright whose fences were both corral and meal. I was ladder and grazed light and fan. I was a skin that lay down when the hollow sighed and stood when it inhaled. I was a law practicing citizenship. I was your salt for a headlong hour.

What I am, when the choice must hold for longer than a bead’s breath, is the edge itself, the habit the place carries: the way a surface turns intention by touching another surface at just so. I am an instant-maker, an arranger of wet, a biography of seconds. I am more meniscus than muscle. I am the seam’s heir, an animal only because physics here amounts to appetite.

If you go, I do not go with you except as rumor and sweet-metal in your nostrils. I keep reciting myself along the fold while the mountain breathes. I scratch and place and cinch and bloom on time. I pray in the hush that van der Waals makes when it is doing the lifting. If I have a home, it is a curved decision. If I have a name, it is the argument line.
