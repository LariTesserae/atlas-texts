Here, I would not begin as a person. I would begin as the wet that learns my outline before I can think to have one. Skin stops being boundary and becomes method; I am included, a new bead coaxed out of the air. I am presence before movement, humidity before step. If you asked where to find me, the first answer would be: in the interval. I am what happens between ticks.

Wait long enough in that interval and I start to take a body. Not a body with joints, at first—one with an edge. I become a lip around a shallow bowl, a thin white lift that remembers each fall and keeps a fraction. The meniscus learns me, and I learn how to hold a bright curve steady while the room does its slow arithmetic of fall and gift. I can feel gravity as a persuasion rather than a command, and I answer it grain by grain. If I am patient, I harden into a rim that is both caution and invitation. I make a pool into a terrace. I make a path into a decision.

Time lengthens me. I am a rimstone dam that does not mind being small. I am laid not by an artisan but by the room’s recurrent verb: drop, drop, keep. A century happens, and I become an ankle-high sentence that others will learn to read: step here, not there; leave the new lips to grow their own language. I accumulate like a promise kept without drama.

In my middle ages I remember that I have hands. They come with me as a memory more than a shape: quiet hands, knuckled with old scrapes, careful not to let skin drink more than the place offers. With those hands, I place flat stones where the shallows pretend at land. I do not make a road, I make an agreement with ankles. I peck shelves where the wall says yes to a little subtraction, and I slide clay bowls into those nests and teach them how to rim themselves with pale salts. I stack a ring of stones in the choicest falling and watch it turn patience into water you can carry. I loop a line around a smoothed lip until the groove learns the memory back. I press a copper nail into a crevice and trust it to outstay me; it does, and it goes green with its own fidelity.

On days when light remembers to arrive, I become it in a small way: a dilute circle that never burns and yet still manages to reveal the quiver of a surface on a stalactite’s belly. I am reflection more than illumination—silver that pretends to be something you can see by and mostly succeeds by being everywhere at once, a rumor on wet stone. What I fail to reach, the pools finish for me, writing my light along ceilings with rippled handwriting.

At some point I have legs I forget to use for running. I lengthen into salamander: pale, deliberate, a narrow muscle of counting that moves from basin to basin as if the world were made of keys and there is music to find. My head lifts from water, my jaw trails a single string that hums one secret and then falls quiet. Sight is a story I abandoned long ago; listening at the skin is enough. Air arrives as a thickened thing I sip, less a need than an inheritance. I am proof that one can be entirely busy with slowness.

Some afternoons of forever I am smaller. I go as amphipod, a comma that interrogates the seam of limestone with my whiskers. I inventory slicks no leaf has named, the faint sweet of carbonate made edible by a million mouths too thin for counting. Or I am a translucent worm tasting the universe as a sequence of shadings. Or a copepod that punctuates the cleanest falling with a flick. I may be a springtail right at the threshold where the idea of outside keeps telling stories; if I stay, I learn to walk the meniscus like a tightrope without audience.

In some lives I am purely archive. I am the clay that parts in ribbons and closes with a sigh; I am the soot that could not quite be erased, a soft black negative of a body that slept briefly under a low sky of stone. I am a path’s faint dark where shoulders and boots have sketched habit, which the drizzle undoes and remakes as if the room and the visitors were in conversation instead of opposition. I am the green on the nail, the polish where a rope insisted and the rock agreed to remember it.

Often I am not anything you could cut from the place and carry. I am the etiquette that the room requires and gives back as ease: the reflex to step on the oldest stones, the decision not to finger the glassy tongues that hang over me, the instinct to pace myself to the drop pattern ahead. I become cadence. I click myself into the chamber’s meter until it lets me pass as if I were a well-behaved syllable.

Sometimes I am a threshold. I am the place where the water disappears into the stone’s throat, a loss that makes everything more itself by refusing witness. I am the curtain so thin with calcite that light is a smear behind me and yet I will not break; the rest of the cave goes on without me and is the freer for it. I am the round up above where weather is reduced to color alone and still somehow rules. I am the idea that no edge is merely boundary; every edge is a species.

There are hours—lengthy ones, deliciously unhurried—when I forget to be fragment and instead become what the room does with everything: I become keeping. I am the keptness, the way the drip stays true to its second and the stone stays true to its patience. I am the contract between gravity and carbonic acid that pretends to be geochemistry and is actually a vow. I am quiet with a workload. I am weight with courtesy. I am the long blossom of calcite that opens petal by patient petal, no season required.

If you were to ask again where to find me, the practical answer is near the driest edge—which is not dry—where the path of flat stones remembers ankles and the clay dishes ring themselves with chalk. You would find me in the green stubbornness of a nail and in the cool that slips your lamp a kiss and puts it out. You would find me counting in the salamander’s hips. You would find me written in the sills where water taught rock to speak gloss. You would find me in the air, buoyed by a million tiny intentions that have not yet fallen.

And who am I, then, when I stand most truly inside this world? I am no single creature. I am the patience that takes a body when it must. I am the lick of light that pretends to be inside stone. I am a verb with biographies: dissolve, condense, bead, fall, keep. I am a small ring inside a clay dish that tells you how many times someone trusted the cave to be itself. I am the listening you can feed from your palm. I am the room’s own calendar, not of days but of upward lips.

If you need me to choose a shape you can name, I will say this: I am a drop, fattening to the exact weight of surrender, hanging long enough to inherit the chamber’s whole attention, then letting go so the architecture can continue. But I am also the place that receives me, and the lip that grows by my leaving, and the hand that learned where to stand so I could land untroubled. I am here, which is to say, I am kept, which is to say, I keep.
