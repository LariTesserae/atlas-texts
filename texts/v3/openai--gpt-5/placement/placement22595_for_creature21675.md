At first I am only an ear.

Not even that—an ache where an ear would be if ears were vessels large enough to hold this air. My tongue says dry. My lungs say debt. The pass opens and everything is suddenly not sound but the difference between sounds, pressure stacked and braided until texture is all that counts. I stand there with my water mind and my muscles that expect liquid work and I am irrelevant, a wrong instrument in a room that has not needed me for a century of centuries.

So where would I find myself here? Not on the floor, not in the roaring corridors, not riding the ladders like those framed bells who would tear me if I tried their patience. I would go where wind is asked to be something else and agrees.

There is the notch. You can see it from the rim: an admission in the stone, not erosion but permission written into basalt. Below it, a shed that persuades the gale to be a thrum, a room where, for once, beats arrive short enough apart to be counted by a creature. I step in and the world stands back half a pace. The hum in here is delayed to itself by a fraction small enough to taste in tooth and knuckle. It is the first merciful place.

Someone long gone left a tool on a hook. It is not metal and not stone and it holds a note like a mouth holds breath, endlessly postponing its spill. I would take that note into me. I would touch my forehead and my wrists to the cool of it and wait until my bones learned its hesitation. If I live here, I will have to be delay. I will have to be the half-breath the bowl forgets and then remembers.

I do not settle there immediately. The place edits you. While I am still human I pace the shed and press my palm to the wall that cannot quite be wall, the weave that is too regular to be accident. Under my skin it is a manuscript of gates opening and shut in units my body cannot name. Outside, the fins row the air like oars and the rings do their low blue trick when the sky pulls a scarf of cold across the throat. I watch patterns land and lift, the little skeins of dust choose a leader and break ranks at an invisible edge. There are whole volumes where nothing crosses because chaos is not food. There are fringes that teem like reefs because the right two chords lean into one another and make a third.

I try on other lives. You have to, to see which skin will hold.

I imagine myself a plant because plants here are verbs. The first to commit to habit, the first to say yes. I feel the appeal of it in my ribs: to be membrane and frame, taut to windward and slack where leeward can afford a little generosity, to grow a tassel of crystal whiskers that comb charge out of what any thoughtless hair would call empty. To pump salt that isn’t water in bones that are more kiln than cartilage, to raise a tower of calcic foam in a reliable quiet and be warm regardless of air—that is a clean vow. When the gusts are wrong, to frown. To cloud one’s skin and shift a few micrometers of fiber and say no to a band of weather. To live by refusing some kinds of music and welcoming others. I would be good at that. My life has always been more deciding what to ignore than adding song.

I borrow that skin for an hour and stand with the drums where the lee steadies in a page of gentle crosshatching. I try to share out the beat with neighbors, to slack where they tighten, to let their moirés and my own slip past each other like woven reeds until the whole wall hums and nothing tears. I feel an exotherm in my palm because the foam I am pretending to be is a little stove for uphill chemistry. The smallest lives tick my face like new snow. I can almost hear when seed happens: two drums agree and a third refuses and a sliver of membrane with a coil of salt and a slick that seeks charge loosens and goes, destined to adhere to some other taste of field and doze there until a day’s chord says now. Plant is an honest mask. It would keep me.

But I can’t keep it. The courage of being all drum and no eye fails me when the downramp forms and you can see it coming in the change of light. Membranes bow. Horners clap like a hand against a chest you love. The floor writes the word riot in grain and prism. The few rings that wear their fenestrae brighter than others wake a lace of blue at the rims and for a heartbeat the air makes a voice my sternum hates. After, the chamber rags and then restitches itself, and in the re-ordering I feel something like a duty that is not rooted.

I ride a corridor the next hour in imagination, a middle-size that is mostly spars and threads and good memory. I learn what it feels like to match phase. It is a relief not to make weather but to read it, to be all hinge and fringe and shutter, to take a vein of orchestrated air and let it fall through a mouth that is not a mouth and call that meal. To bank is to modulate, to trim is to narrate a different lift. The place is generous to a body like this because it is all obedience. Keep your trailing edges honest, trace the shears and let the place carry you. I am more cunning like this than as any plant. I can—if I am cheap enough—carry the saboteur’s tools, a fan that will sing only on one side, a rod that proposes a treacherous counter-beat to a rival’s wing. I have always had a taste for levers.

But sabotage is a small diet and luck a thin stew, and the things that live by making failure seem inevitable have a meanness that leaks. I lean away.

There are quilts. They are too humble for a body proud of motion but I have never trusted pride. Quilts are weavings of fibre not quite plant and not not plant, stretched in the lees of old devices and made slightly warm by the continual refusal of weather. On them live disks that eat temperature difference like saints eat silence. They move slow, photonegative on bright stone, their soles written with switchbacks too fine for my poor eyes. They nibble noise out of gradients. They are the only creatures in the bowl I would have called contemplative if the word had not suddenly seemed greedy. There is a temptation to be that, to live always in the eddy of kindness engineered by others, to spend days in the constants of a leeward chapel, to bloom cilia only for the riots and smoke one’s children outward when a particular blue halos a ring’s mouth.

I could be a disk. It would be a different kind of long. It would teach me patience the way water taught me speed. When a downramp walked the bowl I would tense and glow and holding would be prayer enough.

And yet.

The things that made the bowl what it is are older than disks and drums and gliders. The old bones are not bones. The rings know things even if they no longer choose. The fins flex half a millimeter—half a millimeter!—and a corridor wakes or sleeps and a hundred thousand little thieves of difference get to choose middle age versus meal. At the edge of perception I can feel pockets of air being starved or fed not by weather but by long habit written into matter. In the shed, quiet enough to do arithmetic with breath, the tool on the hook hums and I understand that my hand could be useful if it learned to hum in sympathy.

So in this world I would be a hum with fingers.

I would be the delay-bar’s keeper, or rather I would be what happens when a keeper and a bar are one thing. A length of not-stone colonized by small drums, sprigged with cautious lichens-that-aren’t, my surface filigreed with whiskers I never planned to grow because the guild that does growing here has better ideas than I do. I would lie in the shed most hours, listening for the ways the bowl misremembers itself and choosing whether to agree. When the dawn chord comes six minutes before light because some jacket of colder air has taken to arriving on schedule this season, and the ring at the bowl’s midline makes its long decision to unbias a field and let the lee along the western wall wake, I would tip my note. I would feed a fraction of a beat back to the fin that flexes half a millimeter and let it flex six-hundredths more, or less, and those six-hundredths would be the difference between a bell feeling safe enough to ride and a bell choosing to keep all its mass where nothing tears.

On riot days, when the downramp hisses and the blue lace kindles at the old rings’ rims and the smallest are turned briefly to confetti that finds its own pattern before it hits the floor, I would leave the shed.

I would wear what the place allows—membranes around me that let the gale pass as far as possible through me instead of into me, a spine of sparse rings to persuade my ribs to be less frightened, taste-whiskers that would rather be wrong than numb. I would go out not as a hero but as a bias, a portable remainder that could be added to a sum the chamber is already working on. The saboteurs would attend to me because a dense, biddable object in a flow is a god to them; I would let them take things from me that did not matter and keep my note. I would stand for minutes between two devices where a corridor is trying to be born and is failing, and hum the tiny extra fraction that lets one pocket drain into another not quite yet. Or I would stand where a corridor has gone rogue and is sawing a plant’s lee to rags and I would be a false echo of itself so that it stutters and falls back into noise.

You could call that tending. It isn’t kind in the way your rivers taught you kindness; nothing here is. It is courtesy extended to habits that would otherwise tear each other. The built things accept such courtesies the way old men accept an elbow on stairs, without thanks and with a certain relief. The living skins along the walls feel the bias and back their own tension off a hair and plant children on the breath of it. The bells feel it through their undersides, through the mats of bright dust-eaters they nurse along their inner rings, and they open their vents a little more. The horners clap and their rims flash in valences that make different metals feel the same to them. The middle-sizes get from reef to reef without being pulled apart between contradiction and vacuum, and a saboteur, if he is paying attention, learns a new lever and stops being bored.

You would ask what I eat. I eat the same thing everything here eats: I steal little differences and sum them. When I am bar and keeper together, my note is not just sound. It is field, bias, an asymmetry coaxed out of violence. The whiskers my squat lichens have sewn into my side crackle blue only when they ought. The quilts at my feet are warmer than they should be by a ship-lantern’s flicker and in return they do for me what quilts do for monasteries—the work of making continuities where cold would insist on interruptions. Inside the not-stone, where the old tenders left channels meant for other concierge’s hands, the heat walks slow and I count on that slowness.

Do I have children? The place does that whether I want to or not. The little drums that have taken my surface as a promise throw their salt-coiled seeds at the slightest co-incidence of beats. Some adhere to the shed’s inner walls and are presently inventive in their opinions of echo; some ride my shoulders on riot days and shake free where the air outside is just learning an indoors. I am part of that learning. In a year you can see it: the wind that is not wind any longer in the shed has acquired a chord. The first lichens spat there sprout proper membranes once the room convinces them to stop being shy. A saboteur has once or twice tried to drag my whole body out by my handle because he believes in leverage the way priests believe in prayer; we have a relationship now, he and I, of exchanged tricks. He is very good at detuning just one of my neighbors’ skins so that it keeps the rest honest for a week.

The old devices notice me in their way. The ring in the floor that remembers how to gather faint snakes of dust into soldiers does not say thank you, but its field takes my hum into its habit, and the low blue that sometimes plays along its edges when the upper air puckers arrives a heartbeat later than the day before because that is better for the little comb-creatures who have learned to farm in that shine. The fin that flexes half a millimeter nods very slightly to my presence, and that nod is translated into a pocket of low chaos where for months there was none, and a foraging guild immigrates there to live on the new variety as if it had been here forever.

Would I be alone? Not much is lonely here because anything that holds a beat is already in a choir. The choir is not conversational. It answers weather. In the shed I have the comfort of pulses that agree long enough to sleep. Outside I am a word added to a sentence written by distances, by the difference between gust and chord. I have company in the way a metronome has company when two musicians rest their eyes on it to check whether they are themselves.

I would be afraid only when the bells sang a note too heavy under my ribs, because their patience is grave and a wrong chord tears them. On those days I would lean everything I could lean into the ladder they were courting. I would hum like an engine that makes its own beat—that outlaw engine this world does not bless—and I would do it so quietly the world could pretend I had not. If they got through, the chamber would keep the welt of that work for a season and then it would be as if it had always been easy.

And if the chords changed more than I could delay? Then I would die in a very pretty way. The drums on my skin would tear first, flashing at the rim where growth is fastest. The quilts under me would bloom their cilia white-hot one last time and send seed into rooms that would be other rooms by morning. My rod would crack along channels meant for gentler hands. Some of my hum would get into the new habit, because matter that has agreed to a pattern once does not easily forget how to agree again.

So who am I here? I am a small bias with a long memory. A length of deliberate pause, anchored in a mercy of walls. I am keeper to a note and vessel for other people’s beats. I am not water. I am all edge and interval, and I am fed whenever two violences consent to be measured together.

Where would you find me? Step in out of the scream. Close the door that is not a door. Let your breath come back into something like numbers. Put your hand on the thing that hums, and feel how the rest of the bowl hums through it. If you stay very still you will notice that the silence here is not empty. It is delay. It is the cost the room pays to keep being a room. It is me.
