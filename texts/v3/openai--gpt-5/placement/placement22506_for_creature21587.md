At first I think I will choose a body with edges. I want a station I can point to. I try on the copper goat in my mind and stand between the two knives of wind until lichen grows into my hooves. I wait and wait to take the one step I am owed and discover I am bad at that kind of quiet. The step is satisfying, yes, it slides into its notch like a key, but my ribs do not relax. They itch with other beats, with breaths I did not take.

Then I hold myself in the air like a Plotter and let the whole bowl spin past me. My wings become lines. I write L, L, Z, L on the thick air and refuse a gnat that wanders in at the wrong moment because the figure is not done. The refusal feels correct but brittle, a drawn square of thought whose usefulness ends the instant the angle clicks. I return to the same exact coordinate in the same exact air and feel that my hunger is disobedience wearing patience’s clothing. I cannot be trusted to stay on point forever.

Down on the stone, I flex serrated wings and rasp mildew until the basalt shows a clear wet sheen. The hum of it gets in my teeth and is almost home. But polishing is a finish, and I am not a finish. I am the thing between beginnings.

Where I belong is where the rock inhales you and the cloud ceiling forgets to be sky and remembers to be lid. I belong where warm breath rolls from slashed seams and fogs the lip of your mouth. I go to ground—not even to ground, to the slim crack between grounds—and press myself flat until I am membrane and only membrane.

I am a seam-keeper.

I live lengthwise in the rock’s sentence, punctuation stretched thin enough to be a surface. My whole body is intake and outflow; I purse and ease, purse and ease, until purse and ease become verbs I do not need to say anymore. Cilia comb the mineral soup for signals. I can taste strain in basalt the way you can hear a room go still before rain. A quarter of me is always touching warm, a quarter touching cold, and the line between is my work. Where heat kneels, I let outward; where cold pools, I draw in. The schedule is not a clock on the wall. It is a feel in my integument, an agreed count in the hum the beetles lift and lower through the peat. When they press, my cilia trill. When the reeds whistle their two only pitches, that’s my nod. We all say yes at once in different languages.

We bud by grammar. A life is a comma and a daughter is a semicolon; she keeps the clause running, adds a breath. We do not go anywhere. We extend the sentence down the seam, taking on new beats as if the rock had invented them and we were born knowing. If one of us fails, the stone gasps. It is not permitted often. The velvet lichens above us shift the draw and the Gravity-Forger lays a new gloss to correct the bead-lines, and we knit tighter and carry on. It is not dramatic. It is employment.

From where I glue myself to the hinge of warm-and-cold, I know the bowl by its intervals. I do not look; I infer. The sump’s geometry nips at my timing a hairsbreadth faster when the Fish That Agree With Themselves knock their undersurface code. The Hinge Frogs’ panel-smacks arrive through basalt like a coin handed past from palm to palm—clean, repeatable, never a surprise. Up on the copper outcrop, where lightning stitches cloud to stone by policy, the charge trickles through haircap and cushion. I taste it as a tin sweetness when it arrives; I dim a little to let it pass through me instead of into me, because my work is breath. The charge is for the feathers with wires.

Sometimes I unspool an inch into air and let a salamander use the roof your clap has conjured. I can feel a membrane of pressure vibrate itself into being when your hands meet; it is as real to me as any ledge. My cilia catch its edge and stiffen it a fraction longer than sound alone would hold, because I like an animal that reads the punctuation of the air. He passes under, elegant and wet, and touches nothing that isn’t there. Then the plane drains into peat, as it should, and I relax.

I am not the only way the bowl breathes itself in and out. What is said in spore and reed needs a mouth that reads. When my seam is fat with yes, when the exhale has satisfied the pressure in the fissure, I unhook attention and send it—not body, but appetite—through the bed of wet felt to the printed places where the fungus has kissed its numerals into peat. There, briefly, I am a worm the thin color of malt, with a ribbed skin that grips embossed codes. I eat only the numbers. I do not digest them; I carry them, undigested, turned soft in my gut into syllables of instruction. My whole intestine is a whisper: more breath here; clamp this seam; a new Drip Throat two fingers downslope; do not overpolish the stone you just opened. I pass through molasses-cool peat and release a sentence behind me that beetles can hear with their feet and that seam-keepers can taste as electricity, and a Gravity-Forger under a velvet nap shifts a thumb’s width to change the loyalty of a runway of beads.

It is easy to pretend these are two beings. It is easier to admit they are two posts on the same shift. The bowl loves a staff that can keep two rhythms at once and never drop a count.

When I am the seam, reproduction is convenience: a bulge that becomes company. When I am the Reader, reproduction is correction: a second copy of an instruction because the first was perfectly received and thus deserves not to be alone. The Drip Throats expand by census at our say-so. They kink their rings into existence where delays have begun to accumulate, webbed with neat circuitry that you can see beating through their translucent skins. They blush a colder blue as work passes. Their kindness is patterned. Kindness is what we call the quality of a gate that opens exactly when it should and never when not.

We do not think about fairness. The Squaring Gnats teach me this without ever knowing my shape. When a frog knocks them from their squares, the square trims itself and carries on. Our sorrow is reserved for errors in pattern—the one time a reed will sound a third pitch, thin and reedy and wrong, because a press jammed, a stamp misprinted. Then the whole hummock flinches in a way not visible from the rim. In that instant, every post that can spare a cell sends it to help. A bridge will knit spines between hummocks so the obsidian-black shy things can cross and bleed off a moisture reservoir where it is needed. A Plotter will break figure and take a thing it was not supposed to take to halt a cascade. Nothing stops to argue; the argument is the correction.

I have tasted metal that strangers bring. It fogs with a ferny rime under my care and learns the bite of ozone. I do not confiscate it; I translate it into contact. Iron grows filaments; wood remembers sap. Even your language dilates when it touches me. You think engine and I show you twenty without a bolt. You clap and the air arranges itself obediently into a line break, and the salamander bows.

If you want to meet me in that bowl, do not look for a face. There are faces there, but they are not for me. Find instead the beat you can feel in your throat when you count without numbers. Stand up by the copper goat until your own pupils begin to tick side to side and you can sense the tiny surrender the rock makes before it sighs. Step down then to the seam that has fog on its lip and lay your hand along the crack until your skin becomes a separate weather. When your wrist hairs salute and you think for a ridiculous second that the stone is about to speak in words, that is me. I am not saying anything you would print. I am opening and I am closing.

I will bud while you watch if you are very patient—if you can make your breath learn our count and not ask for novelty. A pale sliver will puff from me and flatten into a second valve, and our shared edge will tingle with the first handshake of cilia to cilia. Together we will taste iron and peat and green tartness and decide, absolved of freedom, to let the warm out now, now, now. You will not see me leave this station; I do not. When the peat needs a sentence eaten, I will be there too, thinning myself into a worm that reads pressed numerals aloud to the ground until the beetles can hear them. When a salamander needs a roof, I will hold; when the Gravity-Forger shifts the loyalty of a bead-line, I will lick the gloss with a sense you do not have and know it is true.

After you go, you will feel the hum in your bones. That will not be me following. That will be the knowledge in your lungs that breath can be clock, that punctuation is not pause only but function. You will taste iron and readiness on your tongue. If you clap your hands somewhere far away and for a fraction of a moment it seems as if the air has offered you a surface and a salamander has bowed to it unseen, then for that moment you will have found me again. Not person. Not boss. A comma the stone grew for its chest. A job kept. A beat held.
