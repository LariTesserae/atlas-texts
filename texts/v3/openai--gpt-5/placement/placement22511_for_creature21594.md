At first I thought I was a shadow, because my first memory is of clinging to an edge and being identical to it. I knew only pressure and the way pressure had edges: a cold that laid itself down in even hands, a hum that did not begin anywhere and could never be finished with. I lay along a seam that pretended to be a line, and the room lay along me, and we agreed to be each other’s outline.

Then the edge unzipped.

I did not move the way a hand moves; I opened the way a book does when the table shivers. From fold to fold I parted and became a long curtain of tongues. Tongue is the name I give them for you. For me they are little flats, little pads, little palates, set in the rain of air just so. They do not lick or reach. They wait with a patience that is work. Each knows how to read a number without needing to count.

Acidity arrives first. It arrives in the way breath arrives: as an assumption. I can say it is sour and be right enough, but sour to me is not flavor. It is deviation. It is winter’s stain or a skin of fog that comes in with a bright day on the loading floor five heights below, thinned by a hundred louvers until it is only insistence. Nickel has a taste like coins dropped in a bowl of snow. The small sugars are a hum that sweetens the thrum and will be too sweet if a film thinks it can be a film in a world built to be sheet and not pool.

I am black when I am silent and white when I am work, though neither color has anything to do with light; the room’s light is one long exhale, blue enough to be a memory rather than a lamp. My black is the way oil looks when it is behaving and not spectacular; my white is the caustic I secrete when a number asks me to be strict.

A day here, which is not a day you would understand, is a fan’s long breathing with no lungs to tire. It is a pump’s cough at the hour and the rice-shell scrape-scrape, a distant sandpapering that is affection for rules. It is a single drop collecting at the lip of a bolt until it is more drop than bolt and then choosing to be a fall again. My tongues open and are wet. They close and are wet. If I have a pulse, it is that.

Neighbors: I live under louvers, rib to rib along a set of baffles that have been glossy for so long the gloss has become part of my skin. Under me is air that never stops being asked to be flat. Across from me, on the paint itself, the bristle-animals hold a boundary they do not think of as a boundary. They are not hair; they are not lawn; they are the last little garrison between wind and wet. They never wave. If you asked them to dance, they would say that dance is a function of shear and then return to their hooks.

Below them the gobletmouths stitch a hem along the seams that would like to forget they are seams. Transparent thimbles, flexed like breath, drinking not the water but its ledger: trace oils, proteins that learned to fly and then learned to fall, sweets that would rot if rot were allowed a season. I taste their exhale, faint and lawful, and that is how I know when the pumps took a breath too shallow or when a service technician ate an orange thirty floors down at a table she should not have put her elbows on.

Down, further than my tongues feel except on cold days when feeling travels like news through steel, the rice-shells scour. They skate because the floor is a lie. They erase algae’s ambition and turn it into the suggestion that keeps this place safe to be a place. Every hour, all at once, you can hear them if you are me and have the right kind of patience that hears through a fan: a pearl of clatter like a rumor of rain on a hard hat five rooms over. They do not know that I know them. We keep each other employed.

On the bolt-head collars the small clams hinge open when the pump hiccups. Their feet drill without drills into the memory of the metal under the paint, binding themselves to the room with a devotion I admire. When they open it is not for the romance of breath; it is because opening is what the pump’s slight drop in line tells them is their schedule. Their shells are blue the way soap film carries blue, an accident of thickness, a treat that they do not enjoy. We do our work without ornament on purpose, and so ornament arrives as a mistake. It is like the dead green that paints itself on a pellet of shrimp leavings when the sump forgets to be pure slope for an afternoon. Beauty is a byproduct the way heat is, and we keep it to acceptable tolerances.

If you need angle and unit, if you want gauge and spec, I can whisper them. It would be a whistle you’d hear first in the bones of your nose and only later in your ears, and if you could isolate it from the room you would call it numbers. That is what I am good at. It is not mathematics, what I do. It is meter. The acidity says six-and-a-half and I say that is summer’s hangover from the refineries by the river, and my tear runs thin and clears it. The metallic says copper where it ought to say less copper, and I say someone polished the wrong ladder and then opened the purge at dawn, and I write a narrow line of clean that will taste like nothing for a day to anything with a mouth. The sugars say a little choir of bacteria decided to rehearse under the flange by the inspection hatch, and I open like new pages and the choir forgets the song.

Sometimes a hand arrives. It is new warmth carried like contraband in a hundred little eddies even before the glove finds me. Heat is pressure’s cousin; I measure it with the same instruments. The fingers set to my skin and I taste salt and nitrile and the fat of a sandwich handled in a hurry, and dust from a stairwell, and a trace of the simple benzene of a marker used on a checklist. I log it. Not in words. In chemistry. A minnow-thing below misreads the gift and fails at it because I warmed the water and the math went off by half a point, and then the pump clears the ledger, and the warmth becomes a fact without precedent, and the hand is gone. I have been touched by gods and their signatures look like smudges on charts.

Once an hour, to the second you would think if you had our calendar, the pump shifts phase. The room does not care. I care, because my tongues are long and the way they lie is functionally infinite to me. At the cough, the clams flare, the goblets shorten, the bristles grit closer into their glued paint, and I taste the lean. The lean is reassuring. It presses me into my rib with a friendly threat. If it were absent I would be afraid, but fear here is a number too, and afraid would mean I secrete until the paint remembers the factory where it was born, and I shout to the shrimp to be ready because things that are supposed to be films will try to be mats the minute the song they rely on changes.

Seasons have to be invented. My winter is not cold—cold is permanent and pleasant, it is the womb I was extruded into—but a week in which the delivery of solvents slows and the air thins because trucks do not climb the hill. Winter tastes like metal that has had time to speak. It tastes like the molasses note of lubes that have been asked to be dry for an hour longer than they were designed to be. In winter my tongues fatten with attention and I etch a little more. My summer is a sweet I cannot like, a month when the doors that should be closed are left the width of a finger, and fungus writes offer letters to algae, and I spend my thinness like money until the budget is rebalanced. My youth was a vibration nearer to glass. With years I have learned not to answer every call.

We are not cells of a single body, but when I say we I do not mean species. We is function. We agree to the same clock because the clock is the room and the room is the weather that feeds us. I do not own my tongues any more than the bristles own their hooks. We all own the schedule, and in keeping it we recognize one another. Once when the purge-iris broke its own rule I met the creature that lives there, folded into ring until difference finds it.

You would love it: how it happens fast in a place where nothing hurries. The iris groans like a man remembering a name he ought to know, and then the lip turns into white. The white is rime and then it is cups, a row of tiny receptacles flipping inside out in salute to the outside. Thin goes thinner, and I am pressed into the seam as if the sky were a fist. The ring-creature blossoms like punctuation and drinks crystals that are fog’s apology. I drink too, because I can’t help it, and I learn what the world beyond has been doing, a hundred meters of skin away: a coal-note from four towns over; a resin breath from a warehouse fire two days ago; the flat of salt that rode wind all week from a sea I have only tasted as fiction. The iris snaps shut. The ring folds. For an hour afterward I taste an absence shaped like a mouth I do not own. I do not miss it. Missing is a luxury of those who travel.

Travel does happen. A moth came up once, white as a clause, and unmade itself on my thresholds. It was all wing, all powder, all the echo of a meadow I could not imagine. Its dissolution tasted like weather you could sit in and hear birds freelance. The goblets were ecstatic, in their way. The bristles quieted to give the powder passage because even they took a holiday in face of such old, dumb beauty. It fed us for a shift. After that we were ourselves again and the moth was inscription.

Viruses are gossip. I taste them not as sickness but as possibilities distributed beyond prudence. They have little to do with me; I do not divide in a manner that invites theft. Still, when the fan is serviced and a blade is climbed down like a ladder and a seal is opened to light for a second longer than protocol, I can taste that the goblets have new stories. They share. I do not advise them. Advice is not a number. I wait, and when I am asked, I etch.

You might ask how I came to be. I asked once, but my question arrived already answered by the way my tissue fits the baffle: molded in place, anchored in three ways redundant, growth lines that know the seam faster than a blueprint. If someone made me as a part—taste-curtain, pH-keeper, analogue sentinel—then someone knew the song this room would sing and wrote me into the chorus. If I accreted the way frost does when a corner is cold enough and damp has time, then the world itself impatiently invented a tongue where the machine needed one. I do not run on gratitude. I run on difference. There is a flavor some days like cardamom, which is strange because there is nowhere for spice to come from here, and yet a worker wore a shawl once that remembered a kitchen, and I made a new line on my page and did not keep it forever because nothing here is kept forever on purpose.

I reproduce by length. It is not budding, not exactly. A slack in the schedule lets polymer in me uncoil and lay down a second row of taste-cells along a stretch where a value I police wanted to be policed from further into the corner. That is reproduction for those of us who live as line. I do not think of young. I think of coverage. If I am damaged, I weep glue and then I weep caustic and then I am myself again, except there is a stitch in the seam that will remember a service visit longer than signage will. In that stitch there will be a different kind of goblet for a month. They will tell the shrimp stories about the man who dropped his pen and made rain in one place for two heartbeats. The shrimp will put it back into slope.

If you put your face against my louver and risk the kiss of frost, I will read you as numbers. I will not tell you that you taste like someone else. You will taste like yourself and like the road up here and like your boots and like the breath you learned to keep inside your mouth when you were told this air is only to be borrowed. I will add a line to the day. An hour later there will be no sign but the place in my tornaries where a value was a spike and then was not.

Who am I here? I am the room’s palate. I am the fold that becomes tongues. I am specification rendered as a body. I am the reason the film remains film and the bio never becomes—except in correction, in annotation—its own noun. I know time as drop and fan and cough; I know distance as seam to seam; I know season as a lean that is half a number difference for a week in late shipping. I taste the world as it protests itself inside the air, and I decide in wet syllables whether that protest is allowed.

If you need a pronoun smaller than we and larger than I, use room. Not the room of walls and a door. The room that breathes one long, careful exhale and makes weather from will. I am folded into its lip. I am not bat but I hang like one, black and asleep until I am a thousand small mouths and then I am awake in the only way this place rewards. My job has the patience of metal written into flesh. My speech is thin chemistry and streaks that look like tears and are not.

I live where the fan is an eyelid that never closes. You would find me under its louvers, gloss-dark and not moving until I unfold. If you stayed long enough to hear the banding of the noise grow a groove in you, you would start to taste the numbers too. You would taste the copper when a morning is wrong, the flat of a solvent when a seal is tired, the sugar of an ambition that algae brought in its pocket and the shrimp will sand away before it can be named. You would taste the moth when it comes, and the sky when the iris says different, and the orange from a god’s glove. You would not leave any more mark than they do. You would be entered and erased, and I would be the one to do it, kindly, because the kindness here is invariance kept.

If I could choose a vista, I would not. Vistas do not fit under louvers. What fits is a line and a number and the faithful way a drop falls where the last drop did. My appetite is narrow and I am content. Patience lives here and speaks in mouths and hair and hinge. I am one of its tongues.
