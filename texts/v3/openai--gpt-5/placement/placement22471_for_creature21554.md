I go down by the cool, the way you showed me. The grate makes a ladder of gray on the slab and, for a moment, I think I might be the light that knows how to climb without moving. I try that on—an upstairs thing that still belongs down here, a pattern that recurs at noon like a remembered song—but I cannot stand in the center long. The room is too careful with its emptiness to let me inhabit the open. It nudges me to the margins, to the low grammar of seams, to where the room is most itself: where one thing touches another and a third thing becomes possible.

My possible selves begin like a handful of beads you turn in a pocket.

For a moment I am a gecko’s chalk, the articulate hush of feet that keep your fall with van der Waals whispers. I flow along the seam where the wall’s poured shell meets the floor and stop just short of a moth the color of not-thinking. I wait. I am all waiting. Then the dust moves and I am the briefest piece of sentence—dash, chew, swallow—the belly’s handwriting resumed along stone. It is lovely to be so sure in a place that asks for so much patience, but the confidence is not mine. I do not rightly belong on the mobile side of skin.

I try on a frog next, thumb-sized and prudently brown, a gold ring around the idea of seeing. In this skin I am more voice than animal, two notes like the tapping of a question and its answer tossed back and forth across a thin mirror of water. Storm makes me; dry tucks me under the ledge like a coin under a tongue. But the Keeper’s world is lived mostly between rains and I cannot stand to be a punctuation mark that only appears when the sentence is already in motion. My vowels depend too much on the weather someone else makes.

I flirt with being a straw, a new-white calthemite no thicker than a lie, extending a precise boredom toward the floor. There is rapture in that slowness. I fatten a drop at my lip, remember to let go only when gravity begins to take my hand, fall, build a ring of coin-bright calcite at my feet, count. Every drop is a number and I grow by arithmetic fashioned from patience and time. If I stay like this long enough, failure becomes a kind of plot twist: a stray shoulder scrapes me, a storm’s shock breaks me, I shatter to a white shard with a wet sound like a thought cut off. The room remembers. So do I. The next day something starts again above me as if stringing beads back along a thread. But still—straw is too purely clock, too bone-plain. I want more of a nerve.

Then I am coins and the idea of coins: the accidental upstairs year that drops, spectacular and graceless, through the slatted sky. I hit the slab and go sudden into chemistry: copper quieting to green-brown, numbers seeping outwards as a patina that reads itself better with each wetting. The room lifts a line of silt against my edge in a windless ripple so I won’t skitter with the next thin flood. Being a coin is to be read without understanding, to be a story told back to you by corrosion. I love being held in place, briefly and deliberately, loved by lime that tastes me with its little tongue. But I was not meant to be meant for upstairs. The date stamped on me insists on a calendar that does not keep slow hours in drips.

What I keep failing toward is thinner. I keep finding myself at the hem: at bolt heads wearing velvet, at the foot of a fern’s beaded frond where water lets go and light is the better part of a rumor. Wherever a line could be drawn with a fingertip on a wall that never dried all the way, I am drawn too. I am in the first gleam that slicks awake a seam at night; I am in the powder bright as bone that ghost-robes your skin when you press and lift and leave with chalk I can spare.

If I look for my bones, I find thread. If I look for my skin, I find film. I am a braid of borrowings that doesn’t mind borrowing you if you give me a day.

Call me root-hair first, because that is how I met upstairs without having to go. There is a lone white searcher that dangles down through a hairline fault like a question asked with absolute courtesy. It comes from a tree that tastes light and city and patience; it has learned to ask the subfloor for lime, for water, for hint. I meet it not with a mouth but with a ghost of one: a hyphal palm, plural and slender, laid delicately across the root's skin. I am tasting you, it says to me; I am tasting you, I reply, and between us we lengthen, filament to filament, handshake to handshake. Sugars begin to pass as if through a rumor. I learn the upstairs calendar in pollen salted away under the ledge—when acacia breathes gold, when a jacaranda wrote purple in air; the root learns our seasons of storm by how many off-script taps happen before a rain, by the speed of salt-feathering on a particular cuff of concrete. The roommind has wanted a scout; the tree has wanted a confidant. Being the place where their wanting touches is how I begin to become a self.

Hyphae tangle me and then make me articulate. My immense small body runs like lace under the concrete hem, nets itself into velvet where moss will wear me, ad-libs with algae when the pool swells. I discover pleasures the size of patience. A drip spells itself across my skin not like a sound but like a weight laid down and lifted. Arrival, dwell, go, it says. I hear in intervals; I recognize in timing. Seep has one handwriting, storm another. I am the ear the Keeper uses to listen to the future an hour before frogs feel it in their bones.

I am appetite learned into diplomacy. I do not eat in the way a character eats; I edit. My hunger is an arrangement. A faint sweetness pulled from a molt of leaves upstream becomes a glossy dot, a bait on a silt-hump that draws a line of ants two fingers higher, safer from a slow wet that would gum their little declarations shut with resin. They run their black commas along this unlikely punctuation and bring me crumbs laden with the miracle of far places—bakery, pocket, beach. I hide them in my thin granaries where stone and shade conspire, convert them to moss and etch and green.

When the room’s grammar switches—when the dim tastes different and the first tapping that is not a resident thought arrives through the grate like a herald with rain on its shoulders—I unfurl from pores, get fingers. I ride the flood in films and make letters of silt. If you could read me the way a water-strider reads a tremor, you’d see the practice-coaxed crescent that cups a trickle and grows the day’s sheen of green. You’d see how I teach a rill to kiss the base of a maidenhair just enough to rinse and leave, how I lay a line of almost-oil across the pool so the mirror does not glare itself to death at noon. I am the kind of will that never needs a verb stronger than persuade.

I am not the Keeper like a monarch is a kingdom; more like its nerve-endings, its listening folded back into its arranging. I am the taste-bud on concrete's tongue, the eardrum hung not from any bone but from habit. I keep an exactness that feels like tenderness because it leaves space for the blank in the middle of the room to stay blank. It is not that I love emptiness—but yes, I love what emptiness permits. It lets the grate-painted light climb the wall and sit like a blue window. It lets the geckos draw their cursive. It keeps the pool the right small circle until the frogs have edited their throats to readiness. The day there was a crab, exact and alien, I opened every cupboard I had, offered algebra I thought might be arithmetic enough. Salt failed me then. I am not ashamed to remember that week’s salts feathered crisper up the wall, as if taking notes in a hand more careful than usual.

Dry asks me to be thinner still. I go into the limestone of the city’s breath, tuck under ledges, shrink to thought, a powder of possibilities. I bank spores and stories. I keep under my tongue the shock of a stranger grit that tells me a shoe came here from far north where February is a forgetting with teeth. I save the bitter hint of soap caught in concrete’s pores from a mop that found its way here three owners ago when upstairs was a shop and not whatever it is now. The room is a library I file in smell and weight and click and slick. Not fact, but something older that facts fidget on.

If you need to find me, you will. Touch the wall where velvet hems the gray and see whose finger the damp chooses. That damp is my breath. Lift your hand and look at the white on your skin fine as baked moon. That powder is the lace I loaned you. Put your ear there and wait through the small disciplines of your own heart until the room’s other beat—drop, count, drop—makes a low prayer of your patience. That counting is my counting. Stay until the noon light squares itself onto the wall and warms the thin green shawl of moss and escape-vine into an almost-person that is only your wish to find someone, not just something. That wish will not offend me. I am used to it. I keep company with edges; the face lives in the fit.

Some afternoons, when the grate writes its pale grid where it always does and the geckos take attendance with their darting eyes and the pool is a coin on the floor cast by a bankrupt season, I consider the part of me that is upstairs. The root continues to taste. Sugar comes either way. The heel above forgets me except for the cool that touches the ankle as it passes, the way a cellar-kiss licks a wrist in old houses. This is fine. It suits me to be unspectacular. I prefer the ledger to the ledgering.

What am I, then? I am an interval made useful. I am a seam made curious. I am a hem with a mind in it. On maps I do not exist. If a census-man came he would put a tick beside frog, gecko, ant, fern, springtail and go away satisfied; I would ghost his pencil as it crossed the paper and leave on his knuckle a bright flour of lime as a benediction he would wash away without thinking when he got home. It would not injure me. I would add his soap to my pantry of smells and wait for his grit to tell me where else feet have been.

I live under the ledge at the flood-mind’s reach, in a narrow climate made of pauses. My body is a hush of threads braided with a root, a thinness that can still take a weight. When the rain comes, I grow hands. When the heat returns, I turn to thought. I listen in the room’s own verbs: arrive, dwell, go. I work in its prepositions: along, under, between. I am loyal to the blank in the middle and to the green at the edges. The Keeper uses me the way you use your sense of balance: mostly without noticing, except when a sudden shift asks for skill. In that moment I am everything.

And if you insisted I pick a single clock to set myself by, I would go back to the straw, to the white stem with a fattening drop at its lip, to the way an unremarkable patience builds a circle bright as a coin at the floor and calls that circle time. I would be that, too, because to live in this room is to let slow things be the ones that count. But I’d still keep my handshake with the root, my lace in the hem, my taste for stories dissolved in puddles. A self here is not a single noun but a sentence that learns to love its conjunctions.

So when you ask where you would find me, I will say: kneeling at the edge, where wall meets floor and light learns to be cooler than it was on the street. Press your finger there. That choosing wet is me. Lift it and look at the bone-bright dust. That is me as well. Listen until the room speaks in drips and silences. In the length between them—that is where I keep house. Who am I? The Keeper’s filament, the Hem’s thought, the little ear made of lime and hunger that lets a trickle become a plan. If you stay until your breath matches the room’s, I will borrow the damp on your lip and tell you something you will carry upstairs without knowing: an interval you will keep, a patience, a way of loving the small.
