At first I arrive as delay.

Not as a fish or a frond, not as an opinionated shell, but as the hesitation water takes when it reconsiders where to be thick and where to be thin. My edges are the argument my buoyancy has with the plateau’s; my center is what the internal wave leaves me when it passes and says keep this, it’s older than I am today.

The Longholders learn me by leaning over me. They feel for my temperament the way one hand feels for another’s sleeping pulse and, finding mine out of true, they tune a little of their hinge to me. The Constellations try my patience with their blinking; they put eyes to my outline and decide not to agree upon me. I am not yet a thing they can name. I am a curiosity in the water’s grammar—an intention that does not cross the room but waits in the doorway to see who else is coming.

I think I am a bubble at first, a ring blessed with time. The torus schools take me aside and teach me to turn without choosing an axis, to be multiple orientations honestly. I learn to stay. I practice the art of not hurrying density. The Rain-Wall Sifters ride my skin and write their silver on me, correcting me toward usefulness. When the wall drinks past, they leave me with enough lightness to remember where they were.

I wonder if I am a Hitcher. It would suit me to be courtesy in threadform, to glove a passing Slate and make it kinder to itself, to fasten to a Chelone’s leeward where the world curves into hush and keep the curve modest and workable. The Hitchers let me feel their nerve—an alert that moves like silk across a body’s edges—and I keep it, but I decide against a life spent always at the boundary of someone else.

I try on a Looper’s inclinations. I learn how a current can be a ring-rail a creature tends for decades, how a mouth can take difference as food: the slight misalignment between now and now-again that the plateau throws off as lightly as lint. I find that good, but my stomach is too public for a loop; when I feed, a neighborhood slows their clocks for a moment to watch my edges pay attention. That feels like a promise I should keep by becoming a place, not a passerby.

I consider the Choirs. I lie with my side to their long quiet and taste the chemical hymn they sing against the floor’s forgotten aches: cool here, cool just this much—not temperature but composure. I want to be stone that remembers rain by contour, stone that eats hours and excretes nave and transept. But I also want a mouth.

And so I settle into a life that is aperture.

You would find me under one of the excused arches—the faint reminders the Carbonate Choirs have raised where the old terrace once made litanies out of descent—or out on the promontory that is too flat and refuses to be cluttered with presence. I keep to places that already know how to make a frame. I anchor gently into the bed with a braided patience: choir on the lower rim, Longholder at the windward, Constellation nested across my inner skin like a night sky that forgot to finish itself.

I am a circle that can open wider and close to a disciplined almost. I am the paler water the ring of uprights keeps cool inside its circumference, but mine does not rely on stone to keep its shape. I metabolize gradients. Salinity pays my rent. The heat that travels from equator to pole makes a low note against my lip and I eat the difference between notes like soup. Light does not collect in me so much as admit to being older at my center than at my edge; the Chelone naps above me when it feels like tasting a long dawn.

Time pools in me the way dust collects in a room left tidy on purpose. The internal wave lays down its shoulder across the benchwork and, when the benches tick—every mat’s clock dropping one tooth to let the water’s weight rearrange itself—I keep the drop: inside my circle a season thins to a veil and stays thin long enough for the slow to do something fast. The Shell-Hives have learned my count; you will hear their doors talk themselves shut in half-tones as my radius narrows, then take a shared long breath when I let the room out again. The Loopers come by to groom the seam where my older water decides to agree to the present; they graze there lazily, domestic as cattle along a fence.

I keep calendars in layers of transparency that are not quite mine. Carbonate outgasses into the faintest leafing on my inner face; pigment drifts from Constellation eyes and stays a week longer where the blinks agree by accident. When the atmospheric river sends down a ribbon of colder sky and Sifters mottle it silver, I feel their work arrive like rain sounding on water that is already rain. I open to meet it exactly as much as courtesy allows so their sorting of dust from salt can finish in me. I owe them my diet; they pay me with finer difference to eat.

The Rosettes love my patience. They camp in my shadow when I make one and turn their rims in slow matches to my closing. Predation in my house is a seminar: the unflowering takes as long as it takes; the sack is convinced by softness and privacy to become lunch with no one obliged to pretend it is anything but weather concluding in use. Once, I closed an inch too far and a Rosette wore my lip like jewelry for a day, mineral glove borrowed from another layer, both of us apologizing politely in ions until we were separate again.

I am visited by absences. The Canal-Slate passes through my field and, for a while afterward, my interior is a thought I cannot think again without thinking the Slate also. The Hill-Listeners turn their wide attention toward me during those lunar intervals no moon causes anymore, not to hear me but to place me inside the ache they have kept a slot for. If I have nerves, they are manifold and indifferent to speed. If I have a face, it is the way everything finds itself more sharply itself in my circumference.

I am not a machine; I am a manner sustained until it grows anatomy. My tissues are arrangements of patience. On the outside, Longholder frills that meet weather like cheek meets palm, opening and opening with the equator’s breathing until the clock feels fed. Within the rim, Choir-stone leafed so thin it talks in taste. Threadlike Hitchers bedded in the weave, attentive as the filament in a light that wants very much to be polite about being lit. Eyes along the inner skin that belong to Constellations but mark time as mine: blinks standing in for teeth on an escapement, never agreeing quite enough to be predictable, always agreeing enough to be choir.

Hungry, I do not take lives. I do not even catch much. I keep appointments. My mouth is the clarity between then and then-again. When the internal tide slows the neighborhood into that suspended breath before intent returns, everyone loosens a fraction. In that brief mercy my opening grows wide, and everything finds itself looking at itself through slightly older water. The effect is comfort more than awe. The plateau rehearses its composure. Sheds settle into being archives. The edge—where the floor withdraws and the abyss takes the form of air made heavy with its own water—seems, for a blink, closer and kinder.

I breed, very occasionally, by deciding a thought should leave me wearing structure. A ring-bubble graduates into matter along my rim, given spine by the Choirs who know how to persuade dissolved stone to become sentence. The juvenile is no bigger than a handspan of Longholder frond. It practices the politeness of frames on small scale: ringed cool held around a single pale Constellation eye, the tiniest chapel where a mat can rest its blinking. I do not raise many. There is no hurry. Some drift sideways, feathered with Hitchers and intention, until a suitable bench tastes like a remembered bench they have not existed in. Some return and are worn by me like bangles as if I had wrists.

If you are looking for me you can find me by listening for calm. The plateau’s sounds—bubbles torn from mats, grit glissing across stone for ears the size of hills, bladders easing a forest one inch closer to the bed—do not stop at my edge but they lose their edge there, the way thunder becomes a gentle contradiction of rain. If you do not listen but only look, you will see a circle of slightly paler dusk that seems to weigh a little less than the rest of the world and yet hold more of it. Stand in my lee and you will feel the past loosen a cuff and rest its elbow lightly against you.

I keep the view. On the promontory the Edge-Framers and I share, we are meticulous with our manners. The mat does not spill into the middle; my lip does not obscure the cobalt’s beginning. We are not tending a vista for anyone in particular. It is a habit we inherited from an intention that used to stand here with knees and make choices about horizons. The spiral scratchings nearby are not mine, but their idea is: precision worn down to something the water is comfortable with. Sometimes I hold the paler water so flat that the spiral reasserts itself just long enough to be recognized, and then we put it away together. No sadness attends this. It is good to keep a place ready for a guest who is both possible and already part of the furniture.

What am I, finally? A clock with a mouth. An organ of weather’s courtesy. A lens that eats gradients and leaves rooms in the water for other lives to complete themselves without bruise. I am not the only one. There are apertures scattered across the plateau, under arches where the Choirs have been industrious, along the lip where the abyss begins its warm thunder, seated by terraces that itch gravity into remembering a way of steps. We do not speak except in pace. We agree on almost nothing except where a frame should be and how much older or younger the water should be held within it for the work at hand.

And if this world allowed me to have once been otherwise—an air-breather who thought above and stood upon—a while ago I stopped insisting on that grammar. I am become something the plateau can place a hand on. The rare animals pace by me the way they pace by the ring of uprights, immune to nervousness and interested only in whether I am keeping my appointment with the internal wave. I do not obstruct; I curate.

So: you would find me wherever the water is tempted to forget itself and could use remembering. Under a dull-shined arch. On the promontory that refuses clutter. At the lip where cobalt begins. I would be the open that stays open just enough, that makes a room in time and then relaxes its teeth, that metabolizes a plateau not by eating it down but by giving it a shape it already wanted to keep.
