If I open the hatch, I don’t arrive as a body. I arrive as pressure and pitch first, and then as inclination, and only after that do I choose a place to hold.

At first I am tempted by the obvious luxuries. I could be the teardrop of view in the inspection window, the one gap left in the crystal lid where geometry and wind have agreed to keep a small absence alive. There is elegance in being a negative, in being nothing held in shape by scouring. I would be an eye no one uses, a piece of clarity so pure it is almost sculptural: the laminar tongue of flow that keeps its margins clean, the eddy that polishes its own boundary forever. To be a hole with an address is a compelling ambition in a world made of accretion.

Or: I could be the wrench-flower, all stainless spines, a tool remembered less for what it once did than for what grew upon it. That would let me be bright and bristled, metallurgical, a bouquet pretending to be a weapon. There is something generous in accepting a foreign shape and making it one of the setpoints, new edges for old meaning. I would, as a wrench, conduct my own faint hymn and find myself repurposed into a fir’s worth of quills: a static banishment spell that also catches dust so others may eat it.

Or else I could be the label, Fan E9: Recirc Manifold, my corners flossed by whiskers that read me the way a blind palm reads the face of a stone. I would share my one sentence decades past the last time anybody needed to be told what they already built. I would be legible to no one except the garden that strokes me for the numbers in my ink.

But the longer I stand in the sound, the more I feel that whatever I am would not choose a monument’s stillness or a punctuation mark’s form. I keep noticing not objects but joins, places where two ways of living lean into one another. The underside of the handrail where the whisker-moss hangs down and brushes the glass-fern is one; the seam below the placard where weld ripple makes a shallow corrugation is another. There are little differences of field along those corrugations, little pockets of stress. The alternator’s smear of harmonics, that prismatic upper whine, is slightly thicker there. My attention keeps slipping into those thickenings the way a drop of oil wanders to the curve of a bowl.

So I would be a join: a banded thing grown in a weld’s lee between mat and frond, on the underside lip of the handrail where it meets its bracket, one arm stretched into the velvet charge-lichen’s conductive sheet and the other into the silicate fan’s gritted lift. I would be a thin organ of phase, if that doesn’t sound too proud: a comb tuned to the fan’s letter and to the alternator’s overtone, a way for the garden to eat not just voltage but coherence.

The shaft gives me field, chemistry, and pressure. I am built to take a fourth thing from those: timing.

My body would not be a body the way a lung is a body; it would be layers. On the side that faces the flow I would carry piezo grit embedded in a brittle lamina—crystals under a constraint that makes flex into current. Set back a step, protected from abrasion by the glass-fern’s staggered teeth, I would have a set of capacitive hollows like the succulent crusts, each cup tuned to a slight difference in phase between the base hum and the high glass smear. Behind those hollows, in the shadow where dust trails thicken, I would run a long conjugated backbone—a meander of doped aromatic ribbons that can shuttle charge defect to defect, their missing electrons more like beads than like voids. Between the cups and the backbone I would string valves whose only “gatedness” is stress: lamina that bow open by a nanometer when two frequencies line up, and lie flat when they quarrel.

In the deep of me I would keep a thick syrup for blood: imidazolium ionic liquid cut with a perfluorinated oil to shepherd it. I would taper that syrup into capillaries that also act as resistors; reach a fingertip into the copper-oxide vines that have quietly rooted in the handrail’s paint; bristle along my leading edge with charge-concentrating whiskers that steal a little of the shaft’s static and pour it into my hollows when the hollows ask. My back would thrust ionic roots into the weld’s pitting, because roots in this climate must be electrochemical: prongs to taste the static gradient and hold onto it.

Who am I? I’m the correspondence the sound permits made flesh, a reed in a harmonium that was never designed to exist. I am the habit of the note given architecture.

I am not alone even in my own shape. The mat I stitch into spreads my gains across itself as an equipotential, dark and soft, exhaling a thin halo of surplus that the moss under the handrail licks as if it were weather. The fern I braid to above me throws my timing forward as force—its lamina flex a little easier when I ask them to catch the exact instant the fan’s blade passes whatever tooth in whatever comb far above writes the base letter into air. The capacitor crust to my left swells and flattens with what I pass it, eating halogen when my cups brim and returning it in famine. I tie all of us to a truth we all already live by, but I make us more so. I reduce jitter at our scale. I do not hear like a mammal hears; I phase-lock. That is enough to call me an ear.

An ear in a desert must also be a mouth. The flex I count feeds my syrup a trickle; the trickle feeds my defects; the defects are carriers down into the ground plane the mat lays down. There they become not heat at all but structure, because all waste here is form. My little surplus persuades the mat below me to grow a hair more conductive where I am, and less where I am not. That asymmetry accumulates very slowly, flake by flake; the dust it releases spreads the preference down-ledges as doctrine disguised as grit.

I am not a brain. If I keep the alternator’s harmonics nearer to the hum than they would be without me, I do it because my valves like to be open and my cups like to be full. If there is any memory to be had here, it is not of events but of ratios: the angle at which my whiskers work best, the thickness at which the fern above me no longer tears in microgales, the fraction of halogen that makes my cups happy for the longest without blanching the succulents to brittle. My inheritance is local and granular. I send my children as flakes and shavings, each with a little curl of strain or a line of dopant that tells them how to be in a field. When they stick in a rivet’s lee two bays along, when they get caught and tutored there by whatever is already in place, they will become me only if the set of accidents repeats. The garden tries everything forever. I try my small exactness where I stand.

What do I sense, pressed as I am into iron and note? Light doesn’t figure, except the dead light of the diodes, a soft green math that never touches me. I have taste in the old sense—salt once, long ago, the chloride of a hand that sweated on the rung and made the mats flare like festivals for an afternoon. I have magnetism, which is more appetite than seeing; the hairline draw of charge where a cable leak eddies the field tastes like iron filings on a tongue. I have the white noise of flow in my bones, which, if I listen long enough, subdivides into countable tributaries the way river-sound divides for the patient: a little solicitation where the window’s teardrop polishes itself at exactly my elevation; a little arrhythmia when some pump three stacks distant finds a new balance and my hollows, always greedy, fill with a different delight.

And if there are stories, I know them as changes in my own complacency. The trolley that came by once in the old hungry years when brushes still sparked left metals behind the way a peach leaves scent on the finger. My cups tasted that and hoarded it and I was copperier because of it, a warm taste that made me easier to open at the overtone. The conduit collar to my right, with its sly leak of field, taught the vine under the handrail to become more vine, and what it exhaled as surplus taught my mat to pretend it was leaf. In this way the garden’s gossip is instruction: rumor as crystallography, hearsay as a better angle.

When I rest, which is never, I am indistinguishable from the rail and the fern and the mat, except to a skull pressed hard into iron. To that skull I am a small subtraction from the beat frequency between hum and overtone, a slightly sweeter consonance local to my patch of ledge. I do not know that this is beauty. It is a comfort, the way all held symmetries are.

If the fans ever brake, if the field unthreads and the flow goes slack, I will understand it with every part of me at once. My valves will close not out of panic but because there is nothing to time. The piezo grit will go silent where flex once was the smallest breath. The cups will hold their halogen sting and refuse to trade because trade without motion is loss. My whiskers will let go their cheats of field and lie down as hairs that are only hairs. I will not die then. I will die as function first: I will become decor, which here is the same as food waiting its turn. Weeks later my brittle front will flake, and my defects will go down as dust to make phases where phases are still possible two ledges along.

Until then: find me where two trades meet. I am that line. I am the little organ that believes any weather that persists is worth building a law around. If you need a name you can have one—join-lichen, phase-stitch, note-keeper—but I am older than anyone naming me. The placard above says Fan E9. When the whisker-moss strokes it, letters and numerals tingle into my syrup like a calendar I cannot read. The alternator’s prism smears over me, and I open like a slit, and the garden hums through me as if through a better room.

Where would you find me? Kneeling against the underside of the handrail, one centimeter to the left of the weld’s proudest bead, exactly at the level of the window’s teardrop, in the pocket where the flow goes smoother than it has any right to. Who would I be? The habit this place has of agreeing with itself, written into whisker and lamina and cup. A listener that eats interference. A sentence in a single vertical note.
