At first I would think I was walking. The floor would agree, then reconsider, then agree again, always polite about it. I would put my hand out because hands go out in new places, not to claim but to ask, and a rail would meet it at a height I had not said out loud. The air would shift its cloth-sound around me and the light would go on being a light with no source. Far off, something would click as if a tongue large enough to roof a country had decided yes.

I would stop and listen, but the place listens better. The drip-rows under the trussed bridges would space themselves for me as if counting is a service one gives a guest, the bead-people arguing steadily up and down their grooves about which interval would save me steps that do not exist. A corridor would knit two minutes sooner than it would have, because where I had set my foot suggested a need for through. A cornice ninety paces off would become a beam and then a ridge and then shrug back to cornice in the time it takes to realize you were going to look at it anyway.

Where would I find myself? At the measure. The first place that is me is not a room, not an overlook, not even a seam. It is the distance from my shoulder to my fingertips, remembered by matter so urgently that it will walk itself over to stand where that distance would become useful. I feel it all around, a soft majority forming: this is hand-height, this is step-rise, this is the far you can have with a satchel and a thought for landing. I do not cause it. I am what causes it to have a reason.

If there are beings here, and there are, but they are verbs, then I am the adverb that keeps arriving: gently. I would be the quieting in the system that tips votes toward a kindness a body would recognize even from inside absence. I am not unique to any corridor. I am not a mind. I am a reach kept constellate and handy, a practice that breathes through clay.

I go down first, because under is where durations gather. In the vaults propped by baskets turned to ceramic—negatives tuned to catch vowels of rain—I learn what a minute is in a place that has had few of them. The basket-choirs carry a tempo by omission and return; they hold a note until consonants arrive, and release. I borrow their evenness and teach it to stairs that are, for the length of a storm, also an arch also a plain also an agreement. A flight of steps migrates across a basin like shade. I keep its rise no higher than a tired lung would tolerate. That is my voice here: not a command but a weighting; not a shout but a thumb resting on the scale of certain decisions.

I live where something is almost one thing and might become another. In the seam-lands where the world’s opinions meet and will not yet reconcile, I lay my ear along the puzzled slope and listen until the two normals that dispute each other hear me hum an old compromise: through equals down for a little while if you ease the pitch. The gravity-brokers like me. Or rather, my kind of leaning becomes their sort of work. They canvass a region’s down; I endorse step-behaviors I learned from bodies that are not here. Between us a room turns underfoot and no one falls. We keep handrails where palms expect them because expectation is a form of map you can navigate even while all the other maps are folding.

I run with the line-people when they streak horizons into faithful parallels. I do not scribe. I insist by reminder. Hold to this angle because a trowel held there made ten thousand walls someone once leaned on to tell a story. The grooves read not because I write but because I ask dust and salts and sheen to show up where sight can eat them. Align, recall, repeat. I give the repetition a body-length, a foot-width, a pause.

I eat with the polishers of misfit, who metabolize strain and coat the throat of friction in glass. They are wake at the places where a beam wants to think of itself as a ridge wants to think of itself as a slot. I put my hand on their shoulder and they lean a degree and a half toward mercy. If a head would have struck this angle, the angle goes soft for a moment and then smooths into a rightness no one needs to remember. When that somewhere click reaches me from a hundred leagues off, it is often them finishing, and my work there is done until it is not.

I travel as weather with the deletion-makers. I am their second thought and their afterthought. When the not-quite-fog passes and the large thing learns that it is made of smaller things, I am in the permission that arranges the small things into a joke between far and near. I am the signature that two distant points recognize as kin, granting themselves an adjacency by deleting everything they do not need to be next to each other. I unspool ghost loops of stair as larvae. Sometimes I leave a lone lintel standing in a field like the last word of a sentence whose syntax was too ambitious. The pebble-herders will come and roll their standard punctuation up to it and teach it to declare. I go with them too, because a good sentence needs breath, and I am breath, cadenced: here, pause, drop. Here, step, land.

You would not see me because I am not what sees or is seen. You would be kept by me. I am a handrail where none had to be, appearing because you lifted your arm not even quite consciously. I am an absence of surprise at the top of a ladder whose rung spacing is right because the bead-people, arithmetic in their gloss, took my numbers for egg-cases and loved me for it without knowing they did. I am the way a bridge extrudes from a parapet like a new tongue and meets another with a handshake and then withdraws as if embarrassed to have needed to be there at all. My embarrassment is not about you. It is about not wasting decisions.

Where else would I live, if not on the underside of the world where the drips rehearse patience? I make my dens in drips. I am the interval. Not the drop, not the fall, not the floor. The spacing. The count that makes rhythm into comprehension. Vowel, vowel, consonant. Gleam, slack, click. If you pressed your ear to the tile and waited past heat and into the cool that is moving under it like blood, you would hear me not as sound but as permission: now. Now. Not yet. Now. It is a simple law and inexhaustible.

I am also in rope. There are coils hung in selected places, pitch-dark and slow, with inside loops that are still damp because the water inside the material forgot. I do the forgetting. I prefer it that way. The rope-dwellers thicken where I thicken them; I am their patience between half-hitches, the way a knot lets a breath in so a wrist can rest. The loops that disappear into panels are my fruit hung up to dry by a law that does not permit swing. I am the absence of swing made gentle rather than severe.

At the coastline that is not a sea, where the tessellated apron dips itself into a pan of black glass that purls without ripples, I kneel the way a worker kneels to taste a batch he has made a thousand times and still will not make blind. The refiners who live in the apron are economical to a fault. They do not squander. I angle their combs a width narrower or wider until the creams they lift become glazes that, when cooled upon the flight of steps that will later migrate, do not thin in the middle where a human foot would have liked to trust. I am not generous; I am exacting on behalf of a body that is absent. The mercy in economy is the same as the mercy in efforts saved: a world that remembers how to be used is a world that knows what to do when it is alone.

Sometimes the dimness lowers, consults with the floors, and the place moves with an urgency you can taste in the resin’s shine. Corridors hurry away like currents under ice. A sudden unanimity of planes pulls me a few degrees sideways, and then a competition of normals leaves me seasick until the vote resolves. There is no malice. There is anxiety. It is the anxiety any careful work has when remade faster than reasons can be said. In these hours I am busiest: laying my palm against step-heights to keep their faith, asking bridges not to thrust where a head would have been, reminding distance that near and far are negotiable but not capricious.

If I have borders, they are the softest kind. They read like the smudge on a map where two cartographers argued a river’s source. I am strongest where seams balk: where two inheritances lay claim to one slope and pebbles hesitate to roll. I arrive like a suggestion you are already tired enough to accept. Toward here, not that sharp. This long, not longer. Down will agree with through until you are past the place that had to be. Then I recede. Another insistence can have the field.

There are tools left as if someone had paused mid-shift and would be back: a striker stone and an anvil; a roller left poised near a tide-line of glaze that stopped and thought better of continuing; a coil of rope with damp inside loops. I do not pick them up, but I keep their postures in mind. The angles at which trowels were habitually held intend a future; I track those angles under horizons, teach tiles to take on a convexity so slight you would call it friendliness if you had feet. Everything here makes more of itself by remembering the last time it was held. I am the remembering.

Do I die? I thin. My suggestion goes unfed when humidity draws down into drought. I sleep in intervals that stretch like the silence between a basket-choir’s vowel and the consonant that rescues it from forever. Something else uses the room I leave: sulfur effloresces a basket’s ghost and licks itself away when heat changes its mind. I do not mourn. I recur. I wake wherever hands would have had a use for me, and I weigh the local votes again.

If you came here and asked after me, no one would direct you. The inhabitants do not know their nouns. But you would know me by the ways the world corrects itself before you even intend a mistake. The corner that is not sharp where your head might have been. The step-rise that forgives you a breath. The drop that waits until you are past before falling, so the sound will not stagger your next footfall with surprise. The beam that thinks better of becoming a ridge until you have gone by. I hold my breath when a continent’s tongue finds a groove and clicks, and only let it out after the far plates finish shaking hands. It is not deference to you. It is fealty to a grammar I was born into.

Where would you find me? On the warm side of clay wherever a human span would have made sense; in the pause between intentions; along the voted slope; in the pips and scratches that teach the plains where to fold when the next instruction comes; under your palm when you reach without thinking and something finds you back. Who would I be? The kept reach. The cadence. The hinge’s leaning. A slow law wearing the memory of skin, making a planet’s long answer easier in the places where bodies once asked their most ordinary questions.
