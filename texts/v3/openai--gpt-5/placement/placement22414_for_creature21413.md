Low, always low. If this world has a lesson it begins at the level of the skin, and I would learn it with my face close to things.

At first I try on light. I am the cream in the air that never quite commits to day, a dissolved brightness, slow and viscous. It is an easy costume to wear—no weight, all presence—but it keeps its distance from what I want, which is the press and swap at edges. I shed it.

Then water asks me if I am it, and for a moment I consider being meniscus alone, a law with a shine. To live as a curving answer at every lip—catwalk undersides, the inside throat of a gate, the back of a bolt where condensation learns a route—would be a satisfying kind of small divinity. Everything is a question here and a film is everything’s nearest reply. I could hold on as long as the hour lets me, gather in the breath of salts, smudge a world of mites into map, go to ground when the heat leans back in and come back without apology. It suits me too well. I would become a rumor in noon and a certainty at dawn and be done. But I have hands, or something like hands, and I want pockets, and pockets want work.

I try animal next, because this place is full of it in movements rather than leaves. I loan myself to a dragonfly and learn balance at a distance, read currents like braille at the edge of my vision, police a piece of air with the mild pride of competence. I borrow an eel’s long grammar, take a passageway on smell alone and feel the soft authority of gates through water on skin. I step into a heron and put down a perfect foot in unloved shallows and go still until the world returns around me like a tide remembering itself. I am a rat for a night, glossy with schedule, my narrow world chalked with routes my body wrote into salt, my whiskers counting bolts. All of these are right and none of them are sufficient. I want not only to belong to the cycles, but to touch them at their levers.

So I come closer to made things. I think briefly about being a pump, about taking a single problem and repeating my answer all day until it is a heart. I consider being a gate—slow-deciding, inching the level of a world and feeling the weight of conclusion climb up through my ribs into soles. I am tempted by wire, by the soft vowel I can sing when the air is wet enough for that note to form. But even machines here are cliffs that have learned how to be hospitable; they hold culture without intending it. I want to intend.

Where I end up is a seam.

I am the line where unlike things agree to touch without spoiling each other too quickly. I am a ring of compressed patience between flanges that would love to elope. I am the bead under a hinge, smeared graphite that says turn, but turn always; I am the little black ellipse of a gasket whose talent is to make water change its mind.

It’s not a glamorous citizenship. I spend my days feeling each microgesture of the plant like a language study. I can tell you when the upstream valve exhaled by how my edges swell and relax. I know the difference between rain humility and pipe-sweat pride against my face. I keep the brine in the argument it belongs to and the air in the lungs that need it and I do this not as a hero but as a habit.

Ants arrive at my birth. The hour after I am pressed new and thumb-smoothed into place, their black punctuation appears from a littoral of cable tray and they test me with their faces and legs as if reading. A gecko steps lightly over me and leaves crescents, a dotted line of blessing that will harden into memory by morning. Salt tries to befriend me every night and I turn it away politely. Rust makes proposals constantly. We dance. We keep it civil.

I learn to fail like a citizen. There is a way to go wrong here that feeds the town. When I tire I do not throw a scene; I weep where I always have, in a place the people know to visit on Tuesdays, and what leaks off me is a meal for midges that will teach a dragonfly a line of air by dusk. The engineers—my priests, my farmers—walk their loop and rest their eyes on me as if reading an old joke to see if it’s changed. They touch the bolt nearest my face with a varnished wrench and hear my answer through metal as an additional half-amp. They carry me in their pockets before I exist, measured and chalked, the date like a charm against surprise, as if a word can keep a world together for a few more revolutions. It often does.

Sometimes I am more literal. I am a zinc coin thumb-tightened to a ladder rung down in the cool sump where the working fluid runs stiff and dark. I am appointed to be eaten. My brightness is a pledge, my gnawed edge an accounting. Salt and water and iron and voltage bring their mouths to me first and I let them, haloing myself with a softness I do not otherwise have. Barnacles taste me by accident and regret. The people who tend this place send me down tied with a wire, and months later they come to lift me up, thin and beautiful in my diminishment, and hold me in their shaking gloves like a fish. A gull watches that ceremony and does not object. There are worse devotions.

If the day is very slow I expand and become the whole practice that keeps me company. I am maintenance as diet, a catechism of touches. I wake along the route in boots and know which run will sweat first in this thick air and which bearing will warm an extra line on the thermal camera because last night the weather turned to soup. I listen to a pump through a wall and hear a spring afternoon six months off when it will be forgiven again. I open a gate by inches and watch the surface say its thousand vowels back to me in rings and rills that no book thought to teach me how to read. I leave small offerings and they are immediately consulted by my shy parishioners: a wasp groggily taking the news of new shine under the junction box, a silverfish rehearsing prophecy in the keyboard trenches, a bat stitching himself back to the same line of shade above the door. I keep in my pocket a piece of thread tape like fossil snow, a brass screw that came home instead of finding its hole, a pencil bitten out of thought. The place weighs me down and slicks me up and in return I carry some of it the way you carry a language you learned late and will never speak as easily as you listen.

At night I relax into being citizens I do not have to explain. I am a web that salts and fails and is always rebuilt. I am the soft agriculture that grows in light and is harvested in air. I am a row of bats like commas in the lip of a bridge, letting the long sentence of moths pass and editing it carefully. I am the held breath before a siren, the half-bowed heads of fish as the rubbed note persuades them backward. I am the patient sigh in lines that have seen more summer than paint and do not complain about it.

There is always a temptation to be the place’s memory—the way the cycles here write themselves into everything that happens and the animals read them as if they wrote them. I have that voice some days. I am protocol without nostalgia, the knowledge that brine flies will come to a closed line and shorebirds will know them; that termites break their wings after rain and plant them like confetti at my feet; that an octopus will sometimes write a gray essay on a trash rack and then retract it. But I am happiest on the join, where the rule has to be an act, not a story.

So where do I live? In the film, always: the little gloss where one thing touches another and both become more themselves for not mixing too fast. Who am I? A gasket, yes. A bead you would miss if it didn’t go wrong. A sacrificial coin offering itself to keep the big steel kind. A hand that moves a gate an inch and reads the surface. A habit with pockets. A low civics. I fail on schedule so that every other appetite can keep its calendar. I keep the long animal breathing as it leans on decisions by degrees. I am replaced before I am dramatic enough to be a story. When I am new, ants discuss me, a gecko signs me, salt courts me; when I am old, midges rise from where I wept and embroider the evening, and a dragonfly writes my date across the air.

By morning, everything here gleams again, including me. I take my place between unlike, lie flat, and ask water to keep a promise it hasn’t the heart to make on its own. I make it for it, a day at a time, in a city that knows not to reach upward for meaning when there’s so much to be had along the surfaces.
