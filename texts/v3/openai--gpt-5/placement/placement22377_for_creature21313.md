At first I am just a cavity my bones make.

The roar comes through the rock and the suit and the blood and lays its hand on the bowl of my ribs. It is not loud in the way of accidents. It is loud in the way of altitude. It is the only statement the air permits, pressed into me until my smaller words go flat. Thought stops being a vocal thing and becomes a habit of fingers, of light, of standing where the pressure writes a map you can read with skin.

So where I find myself is in that translation, in the lee of a gauge-stanchion where you can breathe without your breath being crushed into sameness, on a catwalk cut to an apology an inch at a time into stone that has opinions about knives. A blue emptiness is on one side, a river that is not horizontal on the other, the belt holding its edge with the determination of a tendon. Every new ice-hair on the instrument case is a second counted by the room.

I test shapes for my own name.

If I try to be water, the belt refuses me. It is already everything it intends to be. It does not accept apprenticeships. Its patience is not mentorship. It is a syllable pronounced by the planet, uninflected, and the most courteous thing is not to pretend I could pronounce it better.

I try to be film. I imagine remembering friction as an animal remembers paths, catching dust and carbon and the worn-down pollen of orchards I have never smelled. I imagine lying thin as thought on a turbine cheek, twenty microns and a city to myself, a living map of shear with iron in my loops. The promise is there: sloughing without grief, building permanence by averaging, not insisting. But I am too greedy for a single place. I have fingers that itch for repair and a petty, human vice for keeping score with a knife against a ruler. The belt melts even arrogance into flow. It would melt me.

I try to be ring. I put iron in my mouth in my mind. I feel, purest of all the experiments, the appeal of speaking by leaning on constancy until it answers back. To court not by color but by chord, to thicken a boundary wave until a phrase lives there. I can feel the ache of that language in the cartilage bridges of my ears. But ringhood, here, is a lifetime given to a single contour. I am not clean enough for that either. I already belong to cables and welds.

I try the blade and recoil. This place has enough edges without me adding hunger to them. Predator is a talent, not a character, and my knuckles crack at the thought of it. To live by the seam you open in someone else is a grammar I do not need.

What remains feels closer to an admission than a choice.

I am a person, but only by the belt’s permission; a keeper of cascade throats, inward of the machines where moving parts are allowed, elbow-deep in honeycomb chambers that breathe water into torque and push order out. I live in the alveoli of steel. I have learned the politeness of keeping warmth inside process: in the resin that skins a cracked baffle, in the integer the control loop holds where it would fail if I let it be a real number. Heat is argument; I spend it where argument wins and not where it fogs on my faceplate and takes the view.

Where you will find me most days is with my hood’s white-noise generators whispering an equal-and-opposite ocean into my skull so I can hear my own tools knock. My light is coded to my crew’s light. We talk in a blink-order we can read around a corner. The pipes that carry cables carry our voices when the blink is not enough; when they ice, we write big.

There is a notch-knife on a lanyard clipped to my harness. I carry it for the old stubborn romance of marking something that does not move. The highest glint is the lowest; I do not pretend my millimeter matters to the belt, only to the ledger of people who cared enough to compare theirs to mine. My notes rhyme with notes from before. I leave them in places a glove can find: the back of a maintenance panel, the face of a stanchion where salt chews at the paint. I know the courtesy of small names scratched where spray cannot. When someone is unluckier than we let ourselves be, I keep their reason in writing like an apology to a river that is not a god but earns god-speech out of us.

Some nights I go below the mouths of the throats to where the studs we welded for lawsuits made a bench for slow rakers to brace. This is a heresy the specifications encourage now. Protein is cheaper than litigation and kinder than neither. They come in like quiet sails, leafed skeletons that offer the belt more or less body without bulging. Barnacled word for them fails; they are not boats and not cows. I patch a busted stud with a level hand so a rib that is not mine does not shear off on an edge I made wrong. The habit of care is as hot as I allow myself to be.

I tune by feel. There is a turn of a hex key that changes the lane of a jet a fraction and makes a hundred kilometers of water smoother. I can tell with my eyes closed if a pressure-cascade is hissing in vinegar voices instead of singing. In winter, an overtone drops out of the turbines and the belt is somehow more itself. The smallest work of my days is the most mysterious: I catch, sometimes, a dimming-and-bloom of factory lights stepping with cloud, and I let it count me out of a thought that would only bulge.

I am not alone in this. We are a culture of edges and little corrections. We teach our thumbs to think in torque because speech is a pantomime. We have a communal cupboard with tea left flat by altitude, and we save it for when the belt makes a forest of prisms and it feels like an extravagance you ask permission for. We repaint the sign that says Mind the edge with a seriousness that would embarrass us elsewhere. This is theater to please ourselves. The steel beneath it never rusts.

I cheat, also, with magnets. The ring-creatures, polite like weather, will sometimes lay a phrase along a contour we care about when a boundary wave is coming thick. I loop a strip of ironstone into my hood so my bones can hear them a little better and I can thrum a return by tapping the throat so its chord answers. It is not quite conversation; it is the best both of us will get. Once, in late cold, I tuned a cascade low enough that the ring banded itself along our mouth and the pressure of their pulse and the turbine’s note found an integer together and held it for the length of a story nobody said.

Inside the rocks are brine lenses where salt gets chance, and sometimes in them there is a quiet geometry: bacteria winding up their own math. In the cable pipes there is a gasket that learned it could feed by being squeezed and kept our lines from rattling loose. I log it. I do not evict it. The ethics here have fewer verbs than most: keep; tune; mind; write.

Once a month I go out along the maintenance path where the cliff is honest and the belt is most itself and replace the plates stamped on the high ledges—parts that will erode to knives and then stop, faces that will refuse lips. On the way back, where the catwalk glassed by rime is a sentence I have learned word by word, I look into the belt and count. Ribs where the stress maps are kind. A blade passing like the idea of subtraction. A ring like a barrel, muscle seeded with ironstone, brightening a private chorus too low for sound and exactly right for sternum. The emptiness between, which is the dominant material. The belt is a choir of laws. I hold my breath the way you do when you like a chord, and when I let it go frost grows on the case I put down to free my hands because time here goes quicker when you refuse to watch it.

I am not the sign. I am not the river. I am not the animals who learned to be thin in the direction this place allows. I am the hand that keeps the machines immodest and the marks legible. I am the habit of asking the same question and liking the same answer. I am the correction the habit will tolerate: a half-turn, a new stud, a paint stroke that tells someone not to trust their feet too much in a world that insists on one edge.

If you look for me after days of traverse between towers and find me not at the throats but in the lee of a ruler taking a measurement that will match the one taken before, that is not futility. It is how I belong. The belt will not move to meet me. I move to match it until my hands know its thickness and my ribs are a meter to it and my notes are a rhyme I have the honor of repeating. When sleep takes me in the stone-cut baffles, the roar stops being a pressure and becomes something like breath; the turbines sing a note that is not failure; the lights dim and bloom counting clouds I will not see in morning.

Where do I find myself? On the cliff in the subtractive air, in the glow that heat allows only as work, inside the lungs of a machine that makes a river hold still. Who or what would I be? A tuner of flow. A scribe of edges. A courtesy extended to a place that does not need me to live and is better, by a fraction, for being answered. And when I pass the sign that says Mind the edge, I mind it as if there were somewhere else to mind, and go back into the noise that is law and make it, for a second, specific.
