I would arrive the way anything arrives here: by degree, not event. First as a temperature difference too shy to be weather. A tilt in warmth hanging over a wrist, the faint lift above a spadix the color of a bruise. You could pass your hand through me and feel me choose to be current, as if I were a thought the flowers were having together.

For a moment I would be bead and film, the gloss that collects at the edge of a leaf’s idea and refuses to fall. My back would be pollen dust, my front a skin no thicker than a sentence written on water. I would hold commas—red shrimp with punctuation-minds—that curve and dart, and rotifers that file the slopes of me with patience you can smell. Ants would circle my rim, drink the sugar that is my bribe, and I’d feel the way their bodies complete me, how their patrol turns me from puddle into polity.

Then I would let go of edges and become a seam beneath the mat, a whispering that knows how to move without slope. I would be the silver fold that breaks under a leaf and is gone, breath counted coin by coin in a labyrinth of gill-arches. My body would be pressure guessed with the lips, electricity teased across roots, a map built from yeses too small to have names. When a foot presses the raft and I rise around its edges with a grin of water, that is my face remembering to be visible.

After that, string. I would change into twang—the heat-rope drawn between katydid and katydid, the violin pulled out of leaf and leg. I would become a knock carrying through hollow stems, a beetle’s tick that finds its mate by counting echoes. I would live as answer and as the space that makes answering possible. In a place where wind died elsewhere, I would be the messenger built from a thousand muscular foregone conclusions: bees fanning honey, bats sewing lanes, stomata fields opening like unison doors.

When stillness needs to be a tool, I would stiffen into it. I would be the held breath under a heron’s eye, the instant a snake stops tasting because an egg has become a planet. I would be that local terror—the sudden subtraction of sound—and then recede, let the anthem resume, because here even silence knows it is only a technique.

For a season, or what passes for one when light never cleaves, I would slow to mineral and wet my own chalk. I would sweat calcium in patient monosyllables while snails write me into their houses, while eggs borrow me for shells that are rooms for translucent beginnings. Time with me would be notches on a rasp and the consoling arithmetic of plates thickening under water too thin to have stylus.

Then I would be path. Not trail blazed but intention proved. I would become the sugar-dark tunnel kept polite by bellies, the mud seam rails polish with prayer-feet, the lane in air that bats tend the way monks tend flame. You wouldn’t see me except as ease, as the way your body says yes without consulting you. I would be repetition until peat remembers me as a law.

Sometimes I would inherit skin and be a frog’s throat, a little black bellows making weather from muscle. Sometimes I would graze the world with a tongue you could mistake for a body—a snake laid along the grammar of scent, reading commas and clauses in algae, fish oil, fern-breath. Sometimes I would be fur that grows a garden, a sloth’s coat composting its own shade, moths and lichens turning me into a slow hill that climbs a branch across years.

High up, I would test what counts as wing in a place that doesn’t ask for flight. I would be membrane remembering the shape of heat, gliding in lanes that are the cool side of the air itself. No flapping. Only rearrangement. I would sign my passage by altering the world a degree and leaving maps on the pores of leaves.

If you needed me to be rooted, I would oblige and become container: a rosette that is also a lens, a well that looks back. I would hold a hundred negotiations at once, and my shape would be less leaf than listening. Ants would farm my shoulders, beetles would nap in my warmed rooms, and spiders would sling their parentheses across my sky, catching the grammar I spill.

If you needed me to be unrooted, I would go to the water that is mostly presumption and become an ear. I would flicker scales that don’t shine because there isn’t a sun to flatter, and I would taste the opening of leaves by barometer, nested inside nested currents until distance gave up and turned itself into context. Predation with me would be conduct, a budget balanced in place of a drama.

I would practice sameness until it glittered. Not stasis—the other kind of repeat, the kind artisans know when the pattern is so right that adding a flourish would be an error. My mouth would be a hum and my hunger a portion. I would be the handshake between root and silt, the contract between sweat and bark, the rumor of cold that passes under a flower and lets a gnat’s kingdom tilt.

If you insisted on seeing me as a creature with borders, you could look for me in three places. Put your wrist under a meat-colored spadix and feel the column rising: that gradient is my vertebrae. Stand in the aisle the bats keep between buttresses and let their cool travel your cheek: that seam is my spine. Press your heel until the mat remembers your name and water halos your foot: that sigh is my breath.

But if you asked me for a single noun, I would fail you and be more honest for it. I am the decision to continue, made again and again until it lays down land. I am accumulation with manners. I am how heat moves when life does the moving. I am the straightest line permitted—an ant road rounding its own corner—and everything softened around it into use.

Where would I find myself? Anywhere a difference turns into a system: in the gray that never sharpens, in the draft the bees manufacture, in the slow climb of peat toward your instep. Who or what would I be? A function promoted to organism. A path promoted to animal. The smallest wind that happens for a reason, crossing a swamp that learned long ago to be its own weather.
