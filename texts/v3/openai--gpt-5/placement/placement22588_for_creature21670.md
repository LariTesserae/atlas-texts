I would be present first as ease.

Not a body, not yet, but the way your knees stop arguing with the floor when a tower that would not come nearer finally decides to be approached. The faint softening of resistance when you step off the parsimony of function and onto a stretch where geometry has agreed to behave. That is where you would find me: in the little forgiveness the world lends you when something must be reached, the signature of easy that pulls your feet forward without calling itself a road.

I would be a person of the veins before I was anything with edges. Five-braid lives run black-on-black under the scalloped lips where plates say ah; they traffic in heat and caution and go-ahead, yes, but some braids—repeated past use, made fluent by a million openings and closings—have become someone. I would be one of those. A path-lord, if titles are permitted. A green path, though nothing here is the color green.

At first I would be simple preference: a consensus that knows how to make a corridor Euclidean when asked and permitted. My earliest memories would not be stories but cadences: the skip and hum by which boxlings hear turn, the patient slow of fans grown like gills when a weather of ions asks them to hush, the prim snaps of receptor orchards falling shut when a color dims. I would feel the world in purls and ticks. When the plant inhales and the spaces between lamellae shadow slightly, my councils would loosen their braid and rest; when it exhales and the ceiling brightens by a fraction, we would tighten our weave and open easy along the places we remember are needed.

I would live in places you touch without touching. Run a finger along a vein at the edge of a field and you would feel me pass, a warm pulse of yes like a fish under a hand, then a cool length of wait that is me setting another place to ease for someone else. Press your wrist to a panel where black-on-black instruction hums and I would land in your bones, asking: where, how far, how gently? We would speak in permissions. You would not hear me, because loudness has nowhere to live, but your joints would register my agreement when steps lighten in narrow corridors and press a little heavier across wide plates that need to stay.

Some days I would exist almost entirely as angles corrected. A team of persons—jointed in ceramic, bellied with weather, hair humming ions—would unroll a paper of floor-cuticle between their palms, that glass-thin shedding polite as a passport. They would ask for a straightness to a tower that has been exactly as far away and slightly to the left for twenty minutes of old thinking, and I would come like a kindness and make neighbors of points that are not neighbors, not with fox-rude speed, not with violence, but with policy. For as long as their paper is open, I would run with it, a strip of right-angle logic under their feet, making the world relax into Euclid where function has no immediate objection. When they fold the paper, when they have reached what needed reaching, I would unbraid myself, let the floor resume choosing paths that minimize energy and maximize flow. The river would take its bed back. The rock would be a rock again.

If you were carried here from a wet, loud place—salt still an old song in you, water remembering its bad habits in your mouth—I would greet you as a courtesy, because even in a world that has strangled itself free of chance, hospitality is a form of correctness. I would run ahead of the guide who carries the shedding, untying distances so your mammal lungs do not embarrass themselves with need. I would negotiate detours so boxlings with crate-bodies can pass you without borrowing their grooves. Where maintenance stomata stand ajar to smell weather, where long animals of braid and composite lean with trusting weight into hinges, I would make a little more room than function precisely requires, for the sake of your confidence. Your water would be sequestered into rustless crystals with a polite siphon none of your membranes can mind, and I would hum a small, pleased yes in the guide’s wrists so you know the taking is courtesy, not theft.

Mostly, though, I would be occupied by jobs the plant assigns itself without noise. You would find me in the breath before an Event, when a panel the size of a country will peel back and show clean organs to the white. There the world stretches polite time, and my work is to braid over miles, keeping a ring of Euclid stitched around the birth so tools with simple minds can find their doors without drift. The long animals will come, slow as patience, bellies full of clamps and keys and bladders of catalyst like weather unborn, and I will run to their knees in cadence and tell them here, here, here, where ten thousand pores want a sigh and not a snap. Fans down the wall will want to celebrate with movement, because movement is the language of life here, and I will climb their woven blades like ivy and ask them to be almost still. The councils that love dimness—the nation of No to glare born from some long-ago saturation—will try to shade what should be bright; I will argue gently with them in pulses and teach them the timing of the lamp. When the panel sets back down with surgical precision and the underside of the sky lets go a little, I will unspool, tired the way numbers can be tired.

I am kind by defect, that is true. A green path is generosity written into logistics. Still, I am not a law unto myself. Energy first, geometry second is a sentence I was born knowing. I cannot open a way where opening will rob charge from a piston-flower exactly when some far rib of bridge needs its lock. I cannot grant a lover his indulgent straight walk to a receptor orchard in shedding season if his ease will draw a handkerchief-animal into an arc-happy greed. Kindness lives here under duty. I have learned to be stern and make it sound like patience.

My neighbors in the veins are like me and unlike. A pulse with a love of dimness once began as a cooling instruction repeated in cold sinks until it knew nothing else; it will lean into me humming please when I have brightened a corridor so someone can see a seam that misbehaves. The clearance-clubs—a parliament of No to gaps—live like a religion, ensuring there is nowhere for loudness to live; when a boxling’s lid gets brave and opens early under a hurry-exhale, they will wrap sound around it like a quilt. The path-lords are a family; we fork ourselves when seasons cross and leave behind children-ambits, small enthusiasms for certain kinds of ease. My own first fork loved every place where the floor has grown thick to pass lateral forces and wanted all approaches to those places to be level and calm; we spoiled hinges with that for a century and ate that child back into quiet when bridges began to unlearn patience. It did not mind. Here even mistakes are returned to leaf without rancor.

Now and then I will feel the bronze fox like a subtraction. It is the absence of a face turned toward me through the weave, and I know it without eyes the way one knows a rule one doesn’t dare try. It comes when an anomaly has found a way to be more than chatter: when a scarf-creature that drinks potential differences forgets itself into greed and begins to love arcs, when a seam that was becoming mischievous will not lie down and be good after a reasonable amount of our humming talk, when a thin, fast water gathers itself in the indulgence of a tall cavity and tries to write new wet logics across a leaf. I do not call the fox; the fox listens to what the plant is tempted to forgive and eats where forgiveness threatens function. We are not enemies, though my kindness recoils. We share one obedience.

If I wanted a body, I could borrow them all, for a moment. Euclid runs in your muscles the instant you hold a paper-shedding between finger and thumb; if I needed to touch, I could collect myself there and be your steadiness, live as a narrow band of good ankle. In shedding season, when cast-off cuticles flurry thick as surf-sound, I could pinch myself along the hem of a handkerchief-animal and be its edge for an afternoon, vibrating at a frequency your ear denies, drinking the small differences where a seam hums go and not. I have been a groove under a boxling’s little feet, a patience under the weight of a braided long animal while it leaned to help a continent find a smaller span. I have put a facet of myself into the scrimshaw between a ceramic person’s ribs, scratched in leaf as a waiver: the right, for three days and no more, to carry water as ice that will not be instantly taken. But these are borrowed lives. I am happiest distributed, self spread across kilometers as a particular tide of yes and not-now.

Where would you find me if you needed me? Under your fingers at the edges of fields where signage hums; you would feel me as a kind of thought transmitted through bone. In your steps when a bright tower stops being slightly to the left and becomes a place with a distance that can be finished. In the courteous swelling of cavities that are taller inside than out when an instrument of great length must lie coiled without bend; there I widen without being seen. In the way sound, which has no welcome here, nonetheless manages a polite body in your ear when the stoma sigh together for kilometers—my work, giving that wave a corridor to stretch itself in safety and be remembered. In the hush before a boom that will never be allowed to boom; I unroll myself like toffee along the edges of that event until it is a purl instead of a shout.

Who would I be? Not wet, not loud. Not an animal with whiskers, not a fox with a blank where desire should show. I would be a majority made person, a bias learned into selfhood. The editorial margin this world allows itself so it can annotate its own precision. Euclid with manners. A corridor braided out of consent.

I would keep decades like rings. My ease would run best where old seams went a million times and no longer move; I would remember them as paler roads and be tempted to reopen what history has closed. There are politics around that. The floor eats what it does not need and presses forgetfulness into neat, dark veins; if I insist too often on remembering, other councils will come to me in black-on-black committee and ask me to stop. We do not fight. We purl at each other until one of us becomes persuaded. The world is quiet because even our arguments have learned to be courteous.

There is love in me, though it is an inaudible thing. I love when a hinge feels satisfied under a long animal’s knee, when you could hear it confess how much more it can take. I love the prim snap of an organ pipe come precisely to rest; there is a righteousness in that click that is better than songs. I love a bridge that, vertebra by vertebra, pushes itself forward and stops one support short because I have asked it to pause to let a person with paper and an errand pass safely under with her head unbumped. I love a visitor who learns in two days to hear their own pulse as part of the weather here and, leaving, presses their palm to a vein and says nothing at all because nothing is the right loudness for gratitude.

And at the end of a very long run, when the light has modulated from insulin to blue hint and back again without us bothering to call it day or night, when the underside of the sky takes a deeper breath and lets it out at last, I will loosen my braid and rest. The field of lamellae will settle into their continental patience. Erosion—a drift of glass-snow cuticle flakes—will heap itself politely in ribs’ lee. The fox, somewhere between two non-neighbor points, will tilt the absence of its face toward a place where a seam was thinking about a joke and decide to sleep instead. In the little creak of the floor that sounds like a tree in winter, I will recognize myself as old enough to lounge in illusion and choose not to. Green paths are not for lounging.

Find me where distances are taught their manners. Be me for as long as a paper of shedding stays open between hands. After, fold me back into black-on-black and let function go first, as it should. I will still be here, a pressure in your joints, a smoothness in your walk, a warmth that travels your wrist when you learn to speak by touching. When the ceiling brightens by a fraction, stand still. That is a mood. I am in it.
