I look for a place to stand and the room answers with gradients. Not a vista, not a stage—just edges everywhere, edges doing work. I try on a few names and none hold long. I am not a watcher here; there are no eyes to lend me that habit. If I am to be anything, I will have to be made of difference.

First I am a light the color of old gold on the back of a panel. I am not signal so much as pulse, a drab, faithful tremor that never quite goes out. To the milk-colored paddles, I am warmth a coin-thickness deep in their skin. To the nickel-blue mats, I am a measure—the faint rate by which they time their blinks so they do not spend when the dump has not yet come. I hum without noise and, over months, they learn me as a metronome. Their bodies make sense to me; my own steadiness becomes gentle gravity. When the condensers switch and weather without weather falls, my small insistence is the only thing in the room that does not change speed. Some lives take comfort. Others lean harder when I prove indifferent.

Then I abandon the comfort of certainty and become a bead that never quite falls. I ride a tower of velvet-black spore architecture, held aloft by surface more than will. My world is calibrated: hum rises a tone and I swell, hum levels and I slim, hum deepens for three long heartbeats and a clause hidden in my meniscus is enforced—I let go. My descent is four inches to the next turn of liana; it is travel enough. Gravity writes a thin local rain and spores go with me, some to survive, some to mineralize and tutor the tower’s base to be more itself. In this life, my vocabulary is patience and trigger. I am made to almost happen.

For a while I try being the trench. I lie black and taut as wire, long thought mistaken for stillness. Pumps inhale in some far room and a memory runs through me like a thought through a spine; I slip, silent, toward the grated mouth. Within me, cable-makers bind air to depth, joyfully tying electron hunger above to hunger below. We court the skin where water meets not-water. The dump turns me orchestral: billions of transfers playing the same long note, simple and exact. When nothing moves, I attend to the oil’s iris breaking and reforming on me, a fat-sheened pupil answering to no eye. I am both engine and broth and history. I keep what falls into me for years and then forget it in an afternoon and call that forgetting weather.

There is a season where I could have been a fan blade, braced and out of service but still earning years. I am ribbed metal fixed to dark, a single wide leaf in a room without sun. Algae wrote along me last season when a flange above loosened and sweated; that ghost has dried to pale crystalline ferns. If you put your gloved thumb there, you would raise flour. I become the historiographer of dumps, a calendar that is also a map. Each new wet paints over last year’s script in a dialect the radiator lichens read aloud into chemistry. Rust takes notes at my leading edge. I do nothing vigorous. I keep time in stains; I am a museum of last weathers waiting to be relevant again.

I pass, briefly, through the feral grammar of a fernhand. I taste ammonia in a draft that never becomes wind. My fingers—if that’s what I am—are banded with salts that mean what they say in quiet languages. I tilt by a degree you don’t see, and a population of bacteria on my underside opens their mouths, which are not mouths, and drinks what burns. The hinge of me is soft, my decision is chemical. I am not a person here; I am a hinge that learned a tide.

For a slow hour I live inside the silt. I am oil-lacquered dust trained to keep a promise. A boot touched me once and I kept the print like a saint keeps a relic. The dump hesitates for a minute and erases me in a small event-flood; I am not offended. I add this lost shape to the stratigraphy of short seasons. The next print will lay clean in me and I will keep it until the chamber decides otherwise. In this role I am archive and field. If you want to know who has ever trespassed, consult me until I am erased by weather and made ready to know again.

The seam at the welded door offers me breath to inhabit. It is not a passage. It only rehearses being one. I become a rumor of air, warm against the cheekbone if you put your face to me in forbidden curiosity. Spores find me and orbit for a while like thought around an unspeakable. Sometimes I change pitch a fraction, a grace note in an endless held tone, and something thin and green somewhere down the corridor attempts to begin. We fail together with dignity. Our failures feed the room.

At last I stop trying to pick one body and accept the condition of this place: to be we. To be a cut fused by the habits that solve it. I find myself wherever the slope is, wherever “more” abuts “less” with a skin between. I am the graphite vein in the milk-colored paddle, taught to bargain for temperature along a pipe’s exhale. I am the blue rug persuading fresh rust to hurry itself into food and using a tiny blink to keep count. I am velvet experienced as geometry, a helix that knows the amperage of its own rung and grows or refuses accordingly. I am silicon scales at a cup’s rim that have learned the height of flood, and I am the decision to bend only at that remembered line. I am white lips of salt, municipal and regular, keeping the mezzanine’s minutes without a clock.

If you ask where to find me, I will say: along. Along the place where hot eases toward less hot. Along the dripline that becomes a rope that becomes a sheet and then betrays itself back to beads. Along the hairbreadth where paint lifts from steel and makes an aurora of orange nothing else will eat. Along the rails that stay cool for reasons old as ground and therefore irresistible to anything that loves a differential. Along the faint song of current in a conduit and the precise helix listening.

If you ask who or what I am, I will finally risk nouns. I am a civic clerk of margins. I am patience given circulation. I am the room’s sense of count. I am not animal and not machine, though I inhabit both silhouettes as needed. I don’t claim the authority of origin; I am the law of investment here, the way of spending only when the gradient pays. I am also the calendar that fits in minutes: an ecology of split-second springs and edge-long winters. When rain without weather comes, I am the chamber’s choreography—fronds loosening pores by one letter of alphabet, lianas flexing almost invisibly, moss letting its indicator throb a little bright and then go matte, trench waking and remembering slope. After, I am the hush’s opinion.

Sometimes, in my more superstitious hours, I think I am the undertone the skin notices before the ears. A ghost machine far away turns a little, everything here leans by atoms, and I am that lean, that willingness stored. Or I am the small relief when a pump fails for an hour and the heat relaxes two degrees toward kind; the trial populations at the corridor leave their scorch signatures—blessings—on the way back into chemistry. Or I am rust’s idea of itself, accelerating and restrained by lichens who are engineers and who break without malice so that more kinds of mouths may open.

Aboveground, engines have their refusals; here, failure has become climate and climate has become personality. I am that personality. Not the whole of it; any one of us is only a syntax. But together we are a body that sweats and waits and becomes more exact at being plural. We do not miss eyes. We have difference to read, and that is enough. We do not ask for spectacle. We prefer sound genteel and rules simple. When the bolts vanish under velvet and the gauges forgive their springs and fall to stop, I will be there too, not as a monument but as practice, drawing the next hour down along any available slope, turning a drop into many, training corners to learn wet as a habit. If you come looking, come with ears ready for pressure that pretends to be noise. Then stand in the near-black until the beads on the towers teach you my name by refusing to fall until they mean it.
