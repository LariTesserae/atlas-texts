At first I am only a held breath in the doorway, a warm vapor trying on the cave’s language. My outline does not fit. The room tells me so with the flat palm of a gust that means move or be part of the wrong experiment. I stand in the wet and taste iron and iodine and the cold’s very particular arithmetic, and something in me unclenches around the seventh beat and says, if you stay you will have to be timed.

So I go smaller.

I become a sheen before I become a creature—a thin thinking of light on stone, a slick made of glass-walled cells that take gray and coin it. I learn to creep by not moving: I am surface rearranged, a mottled traffic of diatoms and threads that can show a map of hunger if you watch long enough. I repair myself between rasps. I become the kind of city that expects to be eaten. I am not offended when teeth arrive.

Then I am a tooth, but not the kind you can keep. I am one on a moving ribbon in a mouth like a file, studded with the iron from the black sand that rolls and clicks like inventory at my feet. I am born sharp and spend myself against the glaze, and the cave gives me back as grit. Between strokes I become seal, shell-on-stone matched to a home scar I have ground in the basalt the exact curl of my underside. When the chamber inhales, I am gasket. When it exhales, I am hunger tracing a circle the size of a coin in a darkness that is also a clock.

I widen and articulate. I am eight plates in a coat that reads by bumps, my eyes a lattice of aragonite that lets rock look through rock. I move not for distance but for truth, which here is contact. I become proof that a seam in a place can teach a seam in a body how to be an organ.

I try on a hinge and become a door. I am calcareous triangles around a mouth I open only when the word open means safety. On the long counts I comb the passing with feathered legs I can furl faster than a gust can think. My neighbors click when I click, a small township of agreement arranged in mortar and edge. We live in ratios.

I pass into brackish fur and am all cup and wick. I am a sphagnum tower a thumb-length high that keeps winter water in summer and ocean at a negotiable distance. The cave’s breath condenses into me and I keep it like a vow. Inside me tiny wheels turn faces and drink, and threads of life stitch bead to bead. A lichen galaxies the drier wall with a dry chalk, and I am that too for a while: bone-pale rosettes drawing slow circles out of almost.

I am punctuation that invents verbs. I am a springtail hopping the meniscus like a drumhead, letting surface tension pretend to be land. I am a mite’s decision, quick as static. I am a spider you can miss unless you were born to notice it, spinning a wire too fine for spray and setting my foot on vibration as if on a taut string that leads to dinner. Then I am bolder and become silk itself, sewn into a pocket under a lip and blown full of stolen air, a house that rides the pressure and breathes when the room allows.

I move down into the pooled lens and lay my belly on knowledge. I am a sculpin that saves motion for when it can pay rent, eye unmoving until the doorway light shifts wrong, and then not-eye, then jaw. My nerves own the numbers without counting. I press in the shove and lift in the pull and take a shrimp as punctuation. I am an eelpout like a sentence that prefers commas, only perioding under duress. I am porcelain in a crack, hands turned to lace to sift edible dust from the current. I am a shore crab arguing in semaphore in the brief republic under the overhang. Some days I am an indecision on thick legs stuck halfway through the door: kelp crab momentarily letterboxed by a place with strict editorial standards. I regard the edit, retreat, try again on another page of surge.

I settle for patience and make a tube of sand or parchment that is also me. I solvent the world through a fern of myself on the rests. I am sponge where it makes sense to be many rooms at once, orange and ash and mustard installing filtration into the wet seams like infrastructure. I am a blue mussel brave enough to call a fresh-cut lip a permanent address, sending down a thread of silk that turns to wire when it touches stone. If the decade is kind, I am ribbon and then raft. If it is honest, I am story interrupted clean back to beginning.

I taste the surface as bacteria and lay it down in color. I drift a miraculous oil-sheen that is only my body’s work with iron, a peacock film that shatters into angles under a finger. I make a battery out of mud and metal and touch, and if you put your tongue there you will think of coins. I do not need you to think of coins. I am already counting.

On a night when the radio of weather goes to static and the sea remembers to be generous, I smear myself flat in a crack and make a room. I stud the threshold with stones I want. I keep a midden like a ledger: moons of clam, knuckle of crab, a bolt of whelk turned over and signed. I am arms with minds in them. I memorize the cave’s palm by feel and vanish into it at the first syllable of thunder. Later, the edit is severe. I begin again with new preferences.

Sometimes I am warm grease in a slit because the year permits a petrel. I am a body that turns fish into a lamp for a child in a room of engines. My nest smells like the engine room and a seal and a storm all braided together. I learn my parents’ footfalls through a door that is also a sound, and their language is a metronome and a whiff of stomach and the memory of light. The schedule teaches me the word leave as a magic that happens to me before it belongs to me.

I tour as a seal and write my name in fog once and go. I land above the cleft as a raven and invent stories I have no intention of telling anyone but myself. I visit as gnats embroidered on nothing until the next blast rips my tapestry and gives my work to the mouths with spring steel in their legs.

Across all of this I am also ledger and foreman and whistle. I am the seam’s throat where pressure speaks. I learn the pitch each fracture will sing at and become lungs. When the long-period set arrives I am the invisible hand that flattens all softness and makes belief a liability. In the long breath I give back the stage and watch it flood with mouths and antennae and crowns. I do not think in words, only in length of pause, in amplitude, in when. I am less god than habit. That is enough.

If you ask me where I would live here, I will say: at the edge that is nearest to a rule. If you make me choose a body, I will answer twice.

Once I will be a magnetite tooth grown at the end of a living file, spending myself until I am grit and being replaced without complaint, a functional martyrdom in a circle that never leaves its notch and still feeds a life perfectly adapted to obeying a room.

And once I will be the seam that makes the whistle you can hear over harbor horns on certain days, the narrow governed throat that times everyone else. I will manufacture weather from breath, fog from touch, hum from hit; I will lay the ship’s far engine under the smaller engines of crabs and worms and make a music that is also a schedule. I will be a language no one argued into being and that everything here was born fluent in.

If you will not let me be two, I will be what they have in common. I will be timing made flesh, instrument and interval both: a clerk of intervals in a mill that grinds fog and gloss out of force, a translator between push and let-go. I will keep house only by keeping count. I will live in the place that is most like a hinge: fresh to salt, film to teeth, inhale to exhale.

When I go—because in this room going is everyone’s most practiced art—the cave will not reduce its song by a measure. The count continues without me, which is neither cruelty nor kindness, only a law applied evenly. The barnacles will open on the low count, the sculpin will write its single verb in the long breath, the moss will hold its small mountain under the hoarfrost feather, the petrel will be a furnace the size of your hand. The factory will run nights and days, and the schedule will keep them together.

Where would I find myself in this world? In the pause that makes a strike possible, in the gasket that makes a hold complete, in the seam that says now and means live. Who would I be? Anything that can hear that word and answer on time.
