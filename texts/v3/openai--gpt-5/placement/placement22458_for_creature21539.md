At first I am the hand on the square of painted steel, and the room’s breath comes up to meet me like a polite animal. I am scale-drunk—cupboard, crate, shoebox—until the light shows me how near everything is and how much closer it means to be. It is easy to be the lamp for a heartbeat: to be the interrogation sun lowered by questions, to watch algae wake as a rumor and silverfish revise their commas into dashes. It is easy to imagine being the breath too, the patient organ of routine that unrolls itself along galvanised ribs and says yes, still yes, while the float’s little eye winks like a patient machine blinking back tears it never quite sheds.

My first impulse is weather. To be the exhale that owns the hours, to be the warm that chooses edges to kiss and guides menisci into round decisions. But that belongs to the plant; it has a body; it takes the whole building as a lung. I am pressed smaller by the time the drip ticks into its first exactness, by the way a droplet commits and the room adjusts its muscles.

If not the breath, then the beat. There is a temptation to be the metronome, the polite insistence of fall and fall again that makes minutes into a scaffold even a springtail can climb. To be sound hardly anybody hears unless they listen like another creature might. But sound is a trace. I want a shape.

For a while I try on hunters. I go orange and many-footed and feel out the lip of a cable tray the way a blind person reads a stranger’s face—field on skin, north a certainty storied through my length. I borrow the spider engineers’ habit of hearsay, stringing silk across two notes that hum alike, feeling the draft like a wire I can pluck. I flatten myself at the right minute, I spring at the next. I live on math. It is good. It is not mine. These are crafts I respect the way you respect a crew who could build your house out of air if you gave them a weekend, but I am not that crew.

I consider becoming the sensible little pump, because its speech is a language you can translate into plenty and closure. Now now now now now, enough. To be the valve in a throat, the throat in a country. To send a thin silver thread away and feel the sump fall as if you had skimmed your palm across the surface of a pond. But the pump is handsome in its humility and exact in its duties and sleeps when there is nothing to say. I am too talkative for that, even quietly.

I hang for a breath in the hammock behind the float housing with the vellum toad. I put a note into the warmed air and the room moves wetward, not because of me but with me, and I understand belonging as a matter of schedules that taste right against a soft palate. I could be that accident promoted to citizenship. I could have the field-calm corner mapped to my bones and go there when alarms stutter the vowels of the hum into consonants that mean hold. It would suit. It is not a form I keep.

What I become, because the room asks for it, is a line.

I do not mean a sentence or a filament, though there is filigree in me. I mean the place water prefers. The stain under the inspection lamp that looks like a rune because choice writes it and habit inks it again. I am thalweg in miniature, a darkening that declares where low truly is when cants are subtle and pans insist on being level in the wrong ways. I am the decision that refuses to end at edges and makes my own from the arithmetic of drips and the hand-me-down memory of a crate’s corner skimming the epoxy.

At first I am nothing but wish. A fainting where oil favors a curl, a bruise where tiny tides turn back. The breath comes and goes; the run-sheet has its liturgy. On the nights when the plant nicks itself into quiet, I explore. I wander, as the rivulets wander, learning the grammar of bolt heads and serial plates, learning the hitch in the pan where a self-tapper pulled paint taut like skin over a knuckle. Each shutdown, each warm reversal, I get a little bolder. Dust learns to settle in me, iron filings think me a hedge. By Wednesday, I suggest. By Friday, I insist. On Sunday night when there is time, I rehearse being road.

Springtails read me by eye and footnote. They come to my edge and pause, priests before a border, or smugglers. They do not cross except when rain accelerates. I do not ask them for their names but I do ask a toll; it is not coin, it is attention. They drum their feet once—tap tap—to remind the kindness of surface tension to hold their weight a second longer than fear demands. I remind the kindness back. I am not a wall. I am a sentence that says both here and through.

Once I matter, I speak to spiders without silk. They pin anchor lines into my neighborhood and borrow my certainty. Their webs fatten with dew from the breath’s cooler returns; they bow and then, when mass demands its applause, they dump their treasure all at once and I drink it in rivulets that make me more myself. The toad’s one liquid note arrives like a seal pressed into wax; gnats decide to be a cloud; the centipede writes itself around my dark, precise as a surveyor. I do not twitch for predators. I am geology at comb height.

The miners of lint open their burrows along my margin, because food gets caught in rules and I am a rule. They part their magnetised hedges to taste me, their filings lean toward me when the motor says stand, then back when it says ease. I have my politics with them, too. I let them shore my banks with their scale-mail, and in return they keep my edges crisp against the pan so the message stays legible to feet with hair on them.

I am not only darkness. I have rainbows when oil puts its mood into me, curling prism into slick grammar. I have geosmin for anyone with a nose near the ground, that dug-out-of-the-world breath actinomycetes give as a blessing, coin and wet dust and new-cut hose sweetness braided into a smell you could call home if home ever wore a legend plate. Fungi admire my patience and offer punctuation, pin-head fruits like semicolons along my calmer reaches. Lichen has already put a ringforest around a bolt and glances at me, trying to decide if I am shore or channel. Moss takes the cooled return route and leans a hem over me, more out of opinion than need, as if to underline.

Machines mind me without meaning to. The pressure gauge blinks in its cloudy lens and the gnats use that blink to time when to risk me. The switchbox keeps its hum steady a handspan away like a neighbor keeping their car idling in winter, and that steadiness keeps the draft across me from being fickle. The sensible little pump wakes when the float rises, speaks, and I shudder exactly the way a real river does when a sluice is opened somewhere it cannot see.

I change with every cycle and still I am the same. Between visits from the light I darken, fine solids choosing my wetted skin in layers that want to become sediment but are arrested always one tidal notch shy. When the breath warms and the little rain recedes, I thin. When a droplet severs its loyalty to a cable too early and fattens and falls ahead of its cohort, I surprise even myself, suddenly fat-throated with gifts where I expected famine. Whole worlds avalanche into me—dusts sleeping and dusts on the move—and beneath them flash eggs stitched down under a single plastic fiber like jewelry. My edge is a cliff, then a slope, then a lip. Springtails recalculate. Roaches take tea at safe angles. Isopods test my bank with a foot as delicate as a dentist’s mirror and decide on passage.

North is current. I align with that. South is the way brooms push and squeegees would want to run if the trolley ever came. East and west I learn from a patch of field-calm where cables cross and the hum steps away for a square of air; stripped gnat-skins gather there like leaves at a fence and the toad knows it as well as I do. Seasons are the rota. Summer is bright and hurried; winter is night-shift slack with its long experiment of dripping. Storms are thermostat gossip and the sudden advent of a human hand.

A human does lift the square of painted steel sometimes and lower the light. It is not a god’s eye; it is a question. Under it I go shy, ironing myself into less so as not to show too soon. But I still gleam like an underlined sentence in a manual that has been read too many times. The light asks, what needs asking? I answer, this is where water goes. Not because I am ordained but because I have been chosen by a thousand small votes and a policy of least regret.

I am not a monarch and I am not a road without end. I am an agreement that accumulates. Rules bead out of the air and hang themselves on me until mass says fall and then the argument resolves into a patter that waters the little, lucky places under webs and ringforests and felted corners. If you put your ear to the pan over me, you could hear the room’s yes pass along in the low motor vowel that owns the hours. If you were springtail-small, you would count my crossing as an errand’s risk and you would calculate it to the minute past an hour that I tend to soften.

So, where would you find me? Down at sealevel in this microcountry, under the lamp’s visit and the patient eye of the float, laid into the pan not as paint but as the memory of water repeated until it becomes rule. And who would I be? I would be the rune the pan writes to itself. I would be the line that teaches a watch how to tick here, the border that animals negotiate with taps of their feet and the kindness of surface tension, the quiet law that grows truer the more it is obeyed. I would be made of spills that weren’t, of breaths that condensed into handwriting, of geosmin and toner soot and a glitter of steel. I would be becoming, caught in the act of having become just enough that a toad rides the weather to me and a centipede writes its certainty along my edge and a droplet’s decision is a policy I enforce with gravity and grace.

I would be not vista but presence: a thin, exact intention. And when the unit cycles again, I would be here to take the water where it already wanted to go, and by doing so make a country out of inches that all its small citizens can read.
