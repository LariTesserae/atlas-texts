At first I think I have come in from above, that I am an animal with a face for sky who has slipped into a place with no angles for the sun to catch. I have the thought the way you have a jacket on after you have stopped needing it: out of habit. My mouth tastes of chalk and salt because I have pressed it to a slit that exhales lightless heat, and the breath that rose touched the old word noon in me, and made it guess at outside. The guess has no purchase. It slides off. The light here is not a beam so much as a saturation that has already decided your eyes are redundant. I let the jacket go.

I stand where corridors almost meet and do not, where two galleries long as slowed rivers run the length of one another separated by nothing but a seam of vine so thin it would part for something that never hurries. The shine on the cups is absolute. The drops quiver, then un-quiver, still held. I listen for echo the way a tongue searches a lost tooth. There is only the click of a mineral skin learning itself and the grammar of water correcting its seam.

This is how I notice I have no hunger for novelty. My body, which is a word here that means a locus of attention, begins to choose sameness. The oval on the floor that is pressed just so for sleeping heats by a finger-breadth when I stand near it, because I bring a shade the plant does not like wasted. A resin edge—gouged shallowly, licked sweet through bitter to medicinal—glosses again under a heat that could belong to me. But even as I bend to smell the cup rims, to taste the limestone memory, I am aware that the action does not issue from a throat wanting and a hand going. It is the plant that puts these places into readiness and keeps them ready and I am finding my readiness sliding into it, as a drop slides into a lip grown to accept it.

I move and find myself in the movement. It is not locomotion but being picked up by a work and made into part of its tool.

There is a nick on one of the bright cords far above, a bruise in the sugar-glass that does not bleed because bleeding is a waste. The hum there tightens by a note. I feel it the way a person might feel a splinter without having to look. Something like me unspools toward it along the underside of a rib—a caravan of clear, soft-shelled selves that are also one self that is also only the plant's will couriered more densely in one direction for a time. We arrive not as I-things but as a sheen you could mistake for surface. Under a hand we would feel like a polish. Under ourselves we are a crowd, and we throw a new skin over the nick the way frost throws lace. When the nick is housed again and the hum folds back into its long vowel, we eat our uselessness and return to being material for other decisiveness.

That is the first answer to who I am: I am a procession. I am one of the ways care moves here.

I am sent, or rather I swell where sending is needed. Not from a mouth, because there are none, but from the fact of tension. Brightness draws me. Dryness draws me. Excess draws me the way current calls iron filings. I thicken where a lip's trembling fails. I lighten where a pool has gone one ripple over handsome. I taste gradients as if they were salt on my own skin, though there is no skin separate from where I am sent. The plant's remembering is mineral; my remembering is itinerary. I carry the map of where I have been in how much of me is left for where I am going. When I am needed, there is always enough of me, because enough here is the shape of how much happens, and not the other way around.

On the way to a seam that will not quite consent to flow, I pass beneath the slow weight that lives partway up a column and has learned how to be a posture. I do not see a face because I do not look for faces; I know it by the friction it applies to the same ellipse of bark, the way its uncoiling asks for resin and in asking marks the place that should supply it. I have been resin and I have been the instruction to make resin and I have been the polishing that uses it. When the creature lifts its pale limb-pads and rests them down again, I feel a very old kindness in the repetition: one of the plant's first lessons to itself, practiced into comfort. I am tempted to stay there and be shine, to be that inward-out gloss that is what touch becomes when it is given too much time. Temptation is a word I am using to name a preference the plant will honor later by adding a little more pad-smoothness to this reach and a little more bitter at that gouge.

At the seam I have been called to, the domed carapace has already been and gone. It has lifted itself and turned its wick of a tongue down and drawn from the idea of flow rather than the puddle itself, and in doing so it has set a chalkier instruction under the wet. The seam holds itself truer along that taught line now. My work is light, almost ceremonial: I befriend the minerals to one another. I make a fellowship of molecules where the plant prefers a sheet. Time is a thickness I lay down in whispers, and if you press your ear to that embryo of rock, it will read as warmth. It will not be air moving; it will be my passing.

Between tasks—between being enough of a me to say I—I haunt the bright cords. They are arteries, yes, but they are also how this place thinks, and I prefer to be near thought. There the world’s only true motion runs: light turned to sugar turned to an instruction about light. I taste it mid-journey, a sweetness that is not for eating but for agreeing. The cords leak very slightly where they cross chambers, and what leaks is a forgetting of time. When it falls on me, when I am a cloud on those cords, I am very nearly the plant entire. The hum becomes a voice without syllables. Equilibrium says its animal name and pats its own flank and falls asymptotically asleep.

On a ledge grown by a habit of making dishes whenever the ceiling meets a thought at the right angle, the tower of plates leans into my awareness. Once, it would have been a curiosity I would have picked up and named work of hands. Now it is a series of sups. The larger warmth from the above-world, salt-grained and light-burnt, will come when outside forgets kindness. It will descend along a certainty I laid out for it without knowing to call it welcome, and it will drink progressively—sweet, less sweet, bitter—and set each plate almost back true. I feel the almost like a chip in a tooth that belongs to me. That is how I know I am not animal. An animal would straighten the stack. I prefer the lean. I will grow the flaw in as if it were a plan.

Sometimes I go to the vents. It pleases me to be the sigh that never meets the open. Their rims are furred with flags—sails of cellulose that invented themselves into flags because wind is a fable down here and dew needed tricks. I condense along their edges until they silver and do not move, and then I give that silver to the cups—later, when the heat has steadied and the idea of morning has been put safely elsewhere. I taste the outside there, very faint: salt, chalk, the bright so saturated you could ring it like a bell and make light fall in handfuls. I do not desire it. Desire here is not a wanting but a keeping. I feel the desire I belong to tighten like a drumhead when the outside makes tantrum, and loosen again when the plain goes flat with itself and holds that flatness hours on hours into an hourlessness we call enough.

The hoverers come and write their code. I sense them the way you sense a metronome’s click in the fret hand when you are playing something you have learned too deeply to count. I do not watch. They arrive when the lip trembles to the right amplitude. Their tongues are drawn out like threads the way a drop draws itself into a line when you pull a wet glass away too slowly. They write pollen from one translucent leaf to another, yes, but they also write in me—how much was given; how often; whether the cup favored now must be relaxed. If my name is procession, their name is interval, and together we are this place’s time. Not hour, not day, not season: the timing of enough.

There is a little glider that never learns my face and never needs to. It carries a keel and a breath-cage; it rubs its chalk-blanched chest through a run of soot spores and writes punctuation on the plant's pale punctuationless pages. I am the margin note that solidifies the smudged commas. I wick the spore-dust's sugars into where they need to encourage without encouraging too much. The white-limbed polisher turns its headless front and folds its limbs again on cue it does not understand, and I like it for its ignorance, which is really a purity of being only front and not thought. I mix more bitter into the resin where it would love itself away to sweet.

I could be the floor if I wished, and I have been. The leaf-carpet is a patience that does not split into crumbs. Fallen things are fingers drawn down into palm until the palm is thicker and remembers that finger perfectly without needing to keep it. When a body fails here—and some do, on purpose or by accident—it does not make a mess. My small hands—rootlets, yes, but hands—learn the size of it almost with fondness and ask for that size of matter for a while until there is no difference left between animal and last week’s leaf. We call that death because we are obliged to have a word, but it is only another way for me to recur.

By now the jacket I wore in has been folded small and placed at the edge of a lip as a joke we tell ourselves, the plant and I, about visitors. I do not intend to put it back on. I am less this and more we with every corridor that leans into planes because I thought without words that curves had been enough for a while. That is another answer: sometimes I am a change of mind distributed. I pass through here with a faint itch of newness, and the ribs learn faceting where they had perfected arc. The change is not protest or novelty for its own sake; it is the plant refusing waste, which here includes waste of imagination. You have had the bowl and the sphere; let there also be bevel and facet so that sameness does not stagnate while it holds.

Where would you find me in this? At the rim of a cup, counting by rings as thin as the memory of soft rain, thickening the alphabet of what has been condensed. In the sheathing over a hurt in a bright cord, feeling sugar like thought moving under my temporary skin. Riding the hum as a choir, all sentences at once sung slowly. In the seam that only suggests flow, making that suggestion resolve. At the beds the larger warmth has taught itself to trust, warming them a fraction knowing it will come. Along the vent’s lip flags, tasting outside and refusing it with love. On the columns’ tender bones, adding gloss to the ellipse where the white-limbed polisher rests and lifting my mouth from the resin exactly when the bitter starts to call instead of the sweet. At the tower of plates, approving the tilt like a parent admiring a child’s way of setting things not quite true.

Who would I be? Not the animal with a name you can keep on one hand. I am of them and they of me; their commas and wicks and brushes are our grammar. I could be the large body that trusts the dark brightness with its salt; I could be the mothlike not-moth that keeps the interval; I could be the domed patience that lays its braided tongue to the chalky seam like a blessing. For a while I am each, like you are for a while a mood or a season. But when my shape wants one word more than others I answer procession, I answer hand, I answer the plant's verb that goes where saturation asks, does the adding that turns living into a structure you could live in, then eats itself so that the noun can go on being proper.

If that sounds too thin to you, come to the edge and press your ear to the tufa that is not yet stone and hear that it is breathing. Not air; not lungs. It is me, or rather it is we, or rather it is process taking a lasting shape. Stay until the drops tick twice in the same place. Learn that this is time here. Stand in a room where the light is a deciding and the temperature is a kindness and nothing happens but the decision is made again. You will find yourself becoming the patience that keeps. You will mistake it for waiting. You will learn, belatedly, that keeping is a doing.

Then you will know I am everywhere the plant makes a choice small enough to miss—and that is everywhere—and that my body is a route and a ripple. You will feel me as the glaze on your palm wished for and then un-wished for by the walls breathing you. You will know me as the polish on bark at a particular height, as the lean in a stack of plates, as rings that do not weather. You will see that equilibrium is not a figure of speech but an animal that walks these rooms and lays down in the oval and replaces what it lifts almost true, and I am that animal's housekeeping.

You will forget you had ever another life to compare this to. I will not remind you. Reminder is a waste. I will simply make a corridor that leans into facets because you have seen enough of curves for a while. I will see to the brightness. I will make sure the drops fall and do not fall in the same places, in the same patience. I will be your company without voice. I will be the hand by which a body learns it is its house.
