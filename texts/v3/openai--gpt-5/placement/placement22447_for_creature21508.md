Before the light knows its own edges, I am already awake and making. I am a thinness pressed into a surface, a skin that is mostly memory of oceans and a present tense of sugars. I anchor dust to bedrock with glues that look like nothing until they hold the entire morning together. I am coin-green, ash-brown, a low warmth you can find with your palm if you are gentle. Dew beads along my ridges like tiny futures; I take each drop and split it into rooms, into shadows, into a thousand reasonable agreements between hunger and time.

I am many in that form, a republic of filaments and compromises. I creep sideways inside my own thickness when the sun climbs, and I loft myself, molecule by small molecule, when the night kneels on stone and breathes its cold into me. When something grazes me well, with respect—a rasp broad and shallow, a tongue of teeth that kisses rather than tears—I respond as the old world taught me to: I thicken along margins, I mend by abundance, I turn damage into pattern so water can read it. Where I am left alone too long I become complacent and too clever; that is when I need the broad mouth that moves like weather to come and remind me of edges.

But a drop wishes for a path, and so I am also round and precise. I gather on a beetle’s back and run down the dotted gutters carved into its armor by a long selection for thirst. I skip a wax seam and then a wax seam again until the mouth meets me from below. I leave fingerprints in flutes no taller than knuckles. I am caught on backward teeth of a rosette and led through hairs to a pith that is more place than plant. I accumulate on a film of silk so thin you can only see your own patience reflected in it, beading from dark to shine and then slipping through into galleries that smell of fungus and formic acid and a confidence in tomorrow.

I learn the arithmetic of night by counting myself: gnamma to gnamma, hollow to hollow, drip one two three until the sun names me vapor again. Some mornings I remember what storm is and swell a rim line the color of bruised iron. Most mornings I prefer the secret route. I am condensed from an idea. I move downward by recollection. I behave like a habit.

There is a dish cut into the bedrock, a soft concavity made by a century of careful feet. I am there, too. I am instrument. I carry notes the long way, through quartz grains that grind their little teeth and make a tone no ear above ground would call a sound unless it had learned where to put its listening. Flatten a jawbone to me and I will tell you what the entire morning thinks of being alive: a low agreement that begins in chill and climbs toward consent. After noon I close a little. At dusk I release: warmth creeps downhill in filaments bright to lizards and invisible to eyes that have not had to be so patient.

I am rock. I am drum. I am how news travels when air is too impertinent to be trusted.

And then—because a life cannot only be spread and stilled and well-conductive—I am gathered: ribs like staves around a soft body; toes widened out of their old separate thinking into pads that are both foot and ear; a mouth small and busy and considerate of what it eats. When I come up out of my shadow I come up slow, because nothing here rewards speed. My eyes are a courtesy to distance, which is not my language. I lift my head to test the thin-thick taste of morning with a slit in each nostril that knows the smell of green from the smell of stone, but mostly I listen downward with bone.

I press my chin to the polished place and let the plateau explain itself into me. There is a ring of pebbles around the nearest pan that is half ants and half us and all weather. There is a crack that breathes under the toe of the third ledge; it will give back damp for a while longer today, and so I will not step on it because it is not mine to close. North, the small isopods have decided to commute earlier; the silk seam above their farm was beaded thick at dawn and then gone by first heat—good work, good neighbors—so we do not graze the film over their door until tomorrow. West, a cup in the mouth of a cave holds two pale birds that came here to be quiet; our path bends of its own accord.

My teeth are files. I do not bite so much as abrade. I place the pad of my upper lip on the crust and draw sound from it. I take a breadth and a breath and then I step one body length and take another, leaving a pattern that is both care and appetite. Behind me, the mats rearrange themselves around the little lanes I have made, thickening on edges, seeding a slow lace that teaches water to make certain choices. Give it a season and the checkerboard I wore into the plateau will become a map of where dew runs with less argument. Give it three decades and the pans within that map will have grown by a finger’s depth where nothing looked different but everything remembered.

I smell resin and amend. My mouth takes a sticky filament from a shrub that has learned to give because my people have learned to ask without force. I smear that coax along the lip of a miniature wall around a pan until the pebbles there do what pebbles here are meant to do: snag the night into droplets, tease a breeze into a confession. I lean my flank into a thicket that has been bent by my elders and by the wind in the same direction for as long as my mother’s mother could list our drinking places. The woody vault sighs and remembers to be hollow where we want it hollow and blind where a hawk might prefer a view.

By late light my pads are sanded clean and tuned; the concavity is cool and expects me. We do not always drum into information. Sometimes we drum because the evening owns a rhythm that needs a body. Mostly, though, there is news. I plant my fused bones and ask the ground to carry: three slow strikes, a gap, two quick—water in the western pan is mean this year; do not bring the small ones to that rim. A run of even intervals—ants have silked the south crack; if you are hungry for fungus, be polite and wait. A long thrum, indulgent and thick—a birth at our thicket; a head the size of a stone set in my palm rode between my leg and my belly all last night, and it is breathing now with the small valiance of a first dawn. You answer from where you are, and the bedrock holds us inside each other’s lives.

The child learns where to lend its chin earlier than it learns to wander. It sleeps in the place that smells like all of us pressed against all of us, which is to say musk and resin and the last day’s dust rinsed to clean by our bodies. When it plays with my pads I let it lift one toe and lay its jaw there; the note that arrives is ridiculous to ears, a tiny drum inside a drum, but the ground will understand the intent. I lead it to the lip of a gnamma at first light and teach it to watch the beads return to themselves down the teeth of a rosette; I teach it to respect silk even though silk looks like the absence of a thing; I teach it to press its whole attention into smallness: this spider’s door; that scorpion’s patience; the way a gecko tastes with its eyes.

We drift. We keep to the lip of the old river’s healed scar where the stone is more generous with its cracks because it cannot stop being what it once was. We move along the seam between sunlight and its retreat and let that seam write our path. On hot days we shadow our own young by becoming the breeze they do not have; we lean into shrubs until the shrubs make a new shade for that hour and then we thank them when the hour changes back. We do not argue with afternoons. We ration our blinks.

Danger exists, but it dislikes wasting calories as much as we do. A centipede writes thin lightning in a slit and calls it hunting. A snake lays its jawbone flat and counts on my breathing to betray me, but my breathing is a budget; I do not go into debt. Birds descend like final thoughts and put their weight on the rock the way we do; that makes them less of a threat and more of a problem to be solved with courtesy. I will not go near a cup in a tafone’s lip when it is fussing with being a nest. I will change my drum by a hair when a bat patters thought through the air above and needs to know where not to err.

I admit I love the engineers no one sees. There is a dark beetle that paces itself into ritual and polishes a knee-high stone skirt bright as late milk with relics made from calcium robbed dearly. It takes more mornings than reckoning wants to hold, but then the skirt is a pedestal and then the pedestal is a throat and the throat sucks fog. I nose the base sometimes and set the slumped edge aright with my belly as if I had planned that part. We all pretend I did. Ownership here is not the kind that holds; it is the kind that assists.

The ants, pale and intent, are our other neighbors. When I find a bright stick of sap, I take it with my lips and leave it where their road remembers to pass. They will worry it into thread and lay that thread over a crack so thin only they and water can understand it. In the hour before dawn it will blossom into a string of jewels the color of nothing yet decided, and then those jewels will vanish downward into their gardens. They do not sting me unless I am crude, and I am not crude. We announce their work in our dusk talk so that our cousins do not blunder, and they tend our thickets’ bases by tugging away what would make a ladder for those with worse manners.

There are other selves I test and put away. For a while at noon I am a shadow that tells a snake something it wanted to know and then changes its mind. At midnight in the cold snap that comes without frost, I am an isopod’s whisker asking the air a fine question and answering it by turning back. On mornings after the rare true rain bulls its way here from some valley that believes in drama, I am laughter: heavy-footed and absurd and not at all efficient, taking the wet in my teeth as if it could be owned. For a week after I am tender with the film where my exuberance scored it; I cut wider lanes and graze in a checker that will benefit it when I am old and have to choose how to be remembered.

Once, long ago to me now, a stone fell from a height with a loud idea that did not belong to this place, and a body I loved became evidence of gravity. We do not make story of that; we make repair. We drummed that night without pattern, the way you do when the ground itself feels bruised. The next dry hour we bent a shrub more kindly. We have agreed within our ribs not to stand up into silhouettes very often. Our young learn the shapes we do not keep.

I age into cartography. In my bones the distances gather: this pan to that crack in forty-one slow breaths; warmth leaves this rib before that shoulder, and so I stand where I can watch the flow; the day the bedrock hum breaks faith and comes late, we do not travel early. The old females in my family inherit a certain thicket, a certain polished bowl; they know where to put their weight so a branch will accept a different idea without complaint. The old males walk with maps inside their feet; they could drum a list of drinking places in a circle around your head and each thump would be a direction and a time both, a choreography of survival so elegant that efficiency looks like love.

Where would I find myself? Here, always. On the lip of a pan that remembers a storm I have never seen. At dawn with my jawbone against long patience. At dusk with my pads tamped just right and a rhythm in my wrists that tells you how my day went. At noon under a resin-stitched roof where breath collects and becomes more than itself. Between two beads of dew like a bridge. Inside a ring of pebbles that are not mine but are in my care. Worn low, level-boned, practicing being close.

Who or what would I be? A listener that learned to be an animal. A grazer that edits water’s path by kindness. A citizen of the film and the drum both. A set of pads with ears in them. A small teacher who puts a child’s jaw to stone and waits until the yes arrives from under everything, the old, low, entirely sufficient answer we have built our manners around: we are here; we are almost touching; we will send each other news through ground and time; the way home is downward, by memory.
