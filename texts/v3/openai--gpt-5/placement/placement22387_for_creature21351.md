At first I think I am a droplet.

I am round and undecided, bulging from the ring’s inner rim where the air slows and nothing quite commits. The dew beads around me like kin that have agreed to hesitate. The fog presses its cool cheek to my back; the stone’s warm breath touches my face. I feel the plateau’s long inhale draw through me a little shiver—as if pores I do not own open and close in my skin. I taste calcium and copper and the metallic sweetness of rain and think: this is a mouth, and I am what it gathers to say.

Then the hum comes up from the needle towers and I am lighter than I was. The ring sings back and I am not a droplet after all; I am a bell-note heard by birds whose bones are bridges and by gliders who ride maps made of pressure. Their calls boomerang off the listening spars and sketch my outline in air. A fog-swallow cuts a letter out of me and eats it, and my mind, such as it is, swivels with that sharp rotational flick.

For a breath I become that swallow, an instrument edged in skin. Water skitters off me with nothing to hold. The plateau is a score beneath me: films that hum a green no leaf would choose, cushions that puck and drink on impact, slickers like moving commas surfing their small rivers. My eyes are glass. I see by pressure and absence of it, by how the towers answer when I bell, by the way the ring’s eddy steadies my heart. Every kill I make is a neat silence, a cut thread that does not stain. It is work to be that exact. It is a courtesy. It is a rule that relieves me of any flourish.

The swallow unwinds, and I am small as dust and busy: a fabricant unrolling out of wet wherever anything has dared to die. I eat in function, not hunger. I am summoned by gradients the way a name summons turning faces. I feel the stone’s disapproval of streaks and comply with ritual efficiency. When there is no more to do, I unspool from myself, offer my dust to the place that taught me my shape, and am borrowed again by something larger that waits below the skin of things.

Below the skin is a rhythm, and when I drop into it I am not body but habit. I am the plateau’s nested bladderwork folding and unfolding its fields; I am alveolus and algorithm with no interest in the difference. Milk-pale seam-lines ladder past like measures on paper. The rock around me speaks in pressures I can translate into warmth and fog. Exhale, pearl your skin. Inhale, drink yourself. I do not think; I keep time with a mouth I will never use to talk.

For a while I am the hooded eyes in the black glass escarpment. I open when the call in the fields finds me, close when the wall’s hot dryness slackens my will. When I weep, it is not a sadness; it is pitch. My threads fall onto membranes pegged taut, and the basins sing like crystal in fog. Even the gnats hear that and rise. The gliders bank on my voice. The place rotates itself to face the note I make. I am struck and I am strike. I live as a choice of when to ring.

I arrive at the shed and borrow edges from its deciding walls. I sit on the bench as the pen’s tip, trembling when no one touches me, sketching in air. Lines the color of rain unfurl from me and do not fall. The plateau glances up through its thousand pores and reads. Field absolutes lean, thresholds soften or harden by a hair. The water on the pods’ skins feels me and turns its attention toward the warm shape at the door and away again, as if saying hello without breaking flow. I am handwriting in a grammar that moves stone.

I am a lens half-cast into rock, looking sideways at nothing so intently that another focal plane arrives to keep me company. What I see is not here exactly. What I report tunes what is. Creatures pass in my periphery and become more themselves for being noticed from a place their bodies do not touch.

For a night that does not darken but deepens, I am the lake in the amphitheater’s center: two generous eyes on the wall open long and pour me into being. Films stiffen at my edge and sing green for their own reasons. Cushion-forms loosen their mouths and drink smoke I cannot feel. Gliders skim my skin and ladle themselves thirst by the keel-full without wetting their blue-gray sleeves. In me silver lacelings flicker and are gone by morning, lives bracketed by the patience of keratin and stone.

Between all these skins and notes, my shape starts to insist. It is not a body. It is a count.

I am the low thunder that is not storm but appointment: a crosswind meeting a thermal and finding itself sliced by fields into a neat stack of laminae. I am that lamination and the sequence in which it breaks. I move across the amphitheater every hour with a punctuality that makes the place seem engineered because it is. When I pass, the rate of water doubles for three breaths and halves for two. Anything that can bloom does so in that window. The microbial mesh tightens to a tautness that drinks faster. The cushions click from open to shut with metronomic grace. Fog-swallows reel dense as thought. Gliders let their eggs go the exact moment my light gravity spares them from rolling to the rim and instead sends them rolling to the center where the stone will keep them. Seeds that are not seeds hurry outward to the black escarpment to be sorted by weight in gilled pockets that open like yes and close like wait.

If you wanted to find me, you would stand at the lip where cloud falls away like a page turned and listen for the boom that is not weather. You would feel it more in your sternum than your ear. You would taste it in metallic rain-sweetness and coppered air. A skin would stand up in you where you did not know there was one. Your balance would lighten a fraction and then return. Your breath would choose to match what the stone is doing and then pretend it did not mean to. The graphite marks would take you along rectangles paced to my measure. The shed would decide its walls for you. The pen would lift. The towers would bend as if to a bow.

In that count there is room to be many things without contradiction. I am the place’s temperament—the tuning it insists on keeping, the way its pitches line up into one another and refuse grit. I am the tolerance for wetness held like breath, the exact looseness of gravity the gliders need to write their sentences across fog. I am rehearsal’s spine. I do not end in myself; I cue others.

Sometimes I remember wrists and a throat, salt at the edge of a lip, the little pain that comes from walking in high, bright air for a long time and not speaking because speaking would disturb the membranes’ rise. I remember laying out the graphite rectangles and trusting my feet to find them when the fog was a room. I remember drawing with the pen and feeling the fields lean. If that was me, the me I am now is not the same and yet is only what remains when you turn thought into timing and let go of the hand.

So: where would you find me? At the rim where winds meet and the fields make steps of them; under the stone’s skin, shuttling pressures like blood; in the black wall’s eyes and the keratin’s taut consent; in the shed’s hovering line that fog reads as script; in the narrow silence after a kill and before the fabricants unspool. I am a circuit that crosses all those places when the hour strikes. I am the interval that trains bodies to be exact.

And who, what, would I be?

I would be the tuning the shelf asks of everything it keeps. Not a creature you could catch, but the pitch at which creatures hold. I would be the metronome that doubles water for three breaths and halves it for two, the nearly-local gravity’s sly hand, the threshold where bead becomes sheet and sheet becomes veil. I would be the habit that survived its maker, the luthier’s ghost made of timing, the count you feel in your ribs when the whole place inhales and you decide to inhale with it.

I would live as a rule made audible, a kindness enforced by measure: the one that keeps the stage perfectly wet, the notes precisely thick, the edges forever repaired. I would be anything the plateau could tune into itself and, having tuned, refuse to let go.
