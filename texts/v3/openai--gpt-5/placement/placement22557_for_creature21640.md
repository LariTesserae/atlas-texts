At first I try to arrive the way I always do, with feet and the old idea that perspective is a thing you climb into. The plateau refuses that almost kindly. The moment I imagine weight, all the weight here is already accounted for: water held in film, air shouldered into pressure, light pinned to its single angle. Sound arrives as a possession. My ears do not burst; they are simply made irrelevant. The world speaks in one unbroken vowel and puts it through me until it is no longer incoming but internal.

So I take inventory. What of me can survive a place that has selected one condition and perfected it? Not travel—there is no elsewhere. Not call-and-answer—conversation here is the coupling of forms. Not even the romance of shelter; anything that tries for an inside is made an instrument. What is wanted is a mouth shaped to a note.

For a while I am seduced by height, by the velocity-colored lives that hang in the bright river of air. I borrow a kite’s frame in imagination: nacre films pulled drum-tight on a filigree of chitin and carbonate, the body no more than a navigational knot at the center. To be that would mean knowing place as time, crossing the same venturi at the same distance each fixed hour until corridors of wind become calendars and courtship is a correction at a constriction. I like the idea of a metabolism of steadiness—of sifting the wet out of air and letting symbionts turn constancy into drips of sugar. But the thought of never touching the stone feels like never learning the language. The plateau is more itself where it is hardest.

Then I try on length and listening: a fissure life, tendon and cilia. I press myself into a grike and taste with a mane, head ringing in sympathy with the world’s chord so thoroughly that my reversals are decided for me by a meniscus shifting the width of a hair. There is intimacy in that loop, a life at the scale of a single cut in the stone, love measured by the shared sump at the bottom of a shared slot. But even that seems too ambulatory, too narratable. This place is not composed of episodes. It is made of fit.

Where I finally find myself is low to the rock on the lee face of a long, fluted ridge—where the gale is a pressure more than a shove; where the mist thickens enough to be harvested but does not strike; where a particular pocket of air, by geometry and accident that have long since become law, has a pitch the plateau has decided to keep. The light slants through permanent halos of spray, the white of the stone is not a color so much as a condition, and everything that can be polished has been.

I am fixed there, small as a thumb-joint, pale as the rock itself. I am a tone-limpet.

My shell is not armor so much as acoustics. It is a low, mineral dome with an oval mouth ground into the exact shape that lets the world-note charge the cavity beneath. The gap between shell and stone is my instrument: a throat, a Helmholtz, a counting-house of pressure. Every edge, every bevel is tuned to a fraction of the plateau’s chord. I inherit the geometry and then I improve it the way this place allows improvement: microns at a time. When the air presses harder or leans a hair in temperature, I answer with the slow acid of my flesh, taking back a lip here, laying a hint of calcite there, keeping the resonance where it feeds me best.

Inside that thin shelter is a purse of muscle pegged to the rock with a glue I make from what the air gives me and the stone allows. I do not move; I do not need to. The movement is all sent through me. Along the rim are fringes of cilia, glassy hairs that wheel under the permanent push, their choreography taught by the plateau’s one vowel. They comb mist into drops and drops into streams, sifting out bacteria and archaea and the tired organics scoured from distant seas. In their roots live pigments that are not for show but for leverage: bacteriorhodopsins that pump when the light leans, carotenes that take the hard shine and wear it into fuel. The glare never wavers, so my optics never waver either. I do not look out into the world; I turn light downward into the work of being.

Energy here is a braided budget and I learn all three ledgers at once. From the air’s red, always there from the fixed sun, I get a trickle I can trust enough to count bones by. From carbonate dissolving, reforming at my lip where pH stutters and the film thins, I make interest on the stone itself. From the endlessly diluted dust of life the wind carries, I skim what can be skimmed: proteins denatured by the journey that are no less edible for that. My body is small because the pitch I’ve claimed forbids largeness. This is not cruelty. It is exactness.

Breathing and feeding are the same gesture. The note fills me, the cilia answer, the film thickens and parts, sugar happens by degrees you would need silence to appreciate and will never get. It is enough. All around me the shell is varnished by my own exhalations. The rim stays wetted by a meniscus that is my roof and my weather. I exude acids too thin to smell and reevaluate the architecture by micrometers: too much lift and my cilia lose purchase; too little and the pressure falls below what will spin them. Over years that are place, not time, my mouth remains the size that sings most profitably.

I have senses where usefulness insists. My foot feels chemistry—ions, the taste of the plateau’s patience. The shell feels pressure like a drumhead reads a stroke. I hear with my whole body and the sound is not something that happens to me; it is what I am built to convert. My blood is clear to keep any color from becoming a little lens that might cook me. There is no glare-shelter in me; there is only glare converted to pump.

Reproduction here is the only brief thing. I do not mate. I calibrate. When the fixed wind turns through the borrowing it does when stone and air have been married long enough—the slight deepening at a known hour along this ridge, the faint lean that ripens the pocket’s frequency—I pulse. I loosen powder from glands along my mantle, eggs shipped under calcite to grit, buoyant as the air’s own smoke. The gale takes them. Most are dust again before they are anything else. A very few settle where another mouth of right shape waits to be imagined. Place is the parent. The chord is the midwife.

Other lives pass me and I or they do not notice except as correction. The under-meniscus isopod leaves a shaved arc along the rim where he scrapes my algae-symbionts, a tax I can afford. When the fissure-eel in the adjacent grike turns on the instruction of a drop thickening to flat, an eddy sheers from his reversal and my cilia change hands for a breath and then a breath again; I thicken a lip by an eyelash to account for his future. Once in long seasons-that-are-places a glider misjudges and clips the ridge two spans to my right. By the time the wing unthreads from its own nacre and turns into food, I have not moved even the idea of movement. Its ruin is not drama. It is arithmetic closing.

Below, the caverns are a pressure I have learned to read as a kind of weather of stone: a soft subtraction under me where the air’s hand does not reach. I have cousins there in the dark and quiet-that-isn’t-silent. They are clamped on ribs the wind’s old patience has ringed into the cave wall, sipping the same invisibilities in a steadier register. I know them not by sight but by relief, the drop in my own note when the air warms or cools by half a grace over the mouths. We are parts of one instrument at different registers. None of us plays. We are played.

There are no ambitions in me that look like leaving. There is no boredom because there is no before and after to scaffold it. I have histories like shells have: they are written in lines you can read only if you grind me through and examine the stack, each season as a band of slightly different crystallography where the wind’s accounting pushed a unit this way or that. But there are no seasons. Those bands would themselves be made of place. If I imagine anything, it is smaller than thought: the wish for a lip-grain to smooth into the profile the note demands; the microsecond notice of when the meniscus has fattened enough to feed an extra micron of growth. That is desire enough.

So where would I be in this world? Pegged to the lee lip of a tuned ridge’s slit, exposed to the single forever of light and the single forever of wind, in a pocket where pressure becomes food. Who or what would I be? A listening cup the size of a knuckle, a shell tuned to the plateau’s one chord, a mouth that eats by resonance, a patch of cilia spinning forever under a law that does not tire.

Finished not because I am ended, but because the form and the problem have become the same thing.
