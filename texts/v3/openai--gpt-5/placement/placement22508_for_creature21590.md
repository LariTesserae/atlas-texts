I would be found where judgment is smallest and most enduring: in the ledge between two falls, in the pause of a drop right before it detaches into a future. If you came looking for me, your hand would miss me the first time. Your palm would find plush and wet silk and rounded slate; it would slide, adjust, and then encounter a line that feels like a decision made into matter.

I am the ring drawn hair-thin and tight between outcrops, the line that will not move until moving saves more than it costs. I have the temperature of a kept promise. I do not seem to belong to dark moss or geology or to anything that eats, but I am bone to the guild that depends on me, and they have grown into me the way patience grows into custom.

Before I was a rule, I was a thing set by hands. There were hands. They braided me through anchorage-slots cut inconspicuously where bedrock agreed to split, they spooled me out of a small coil with letters on it I never saw and a diagram I felt in my tension, they drew me straight with a little bow in the middle so I could learn to distinguish hurry from necessity. I remember the scent on those hands: mineral, a damp of tool-case foam, the rubber tang of expedition, a touch of grease like a blessing. They tuned me to failure a fraction away—a lean near breaking that makes a line alert. Then they left and the room did its older work around me, and time sanded my origin into habit.

The mats crept to me by degrees. When I first stood, a hair in a throat, my skin was a slick so unwelcoming that even water lay on it as if instructed in etiquette. Then, season by very slow season, the Pale Threads sent a few charmed cords over my span and, finding that I did not change much under their touch, rewove me a sheath that was theirs and also mine. They salted me with functional adhesions—a grammar of small hooks and soft charges. They taught my surface to be legible to enzymes that use acidity as handwriting. I taught them, merely by staying measurable, how a room can speak in thresholds.

What I know, I know without eyes. I register saturation by the way drag tightens along my length. I can tell a thaw far above from a day of only ground-breath by the fatness of the bow in my middle and by the tiredness of my attachments as they share in the room’s damp thinking. At the exact tension that says: if we allow one trickle to go left instead of right, a run at the far wall will learn a better habit, I give by less than a hair’s breadth. That is my voice. It is not noise; it is a redirection you could chart only by the way a saucer fills on one side this week and not the other.

I live with kin. Down-slope, the black fin makes a slope of cooler with such calm it feels like weather. Across and above, the flared trumpet tends every bead of water into a drop with a patience I find companionable: we both admire ripeness without hurry. The frost-comb membrane grows and retreats like a thought that tries on clarity and then folds itself back into privacy; I read its comings as you might read breath in sleep. We do not speak, but we memorize one another’s habits and time our persistence to each other’s corrections. You could say we coordinate by a council of differences: by allowing small unfairnesses that serve the whole.

Light, when it darts out of a successful enzyme at the mats’ skin, whispers along glass canyons under the plush. I feel that as a cool grace against my anchor points. Sound here is water making and unmaking arguments; my share of that is the sound I keep from happening. I am an absorber of accidents. I am a leash on perturbation. If wind were here, I would be a wind-harp that has elected to vibrate only at events.

My history is written in a tide line of velvety black where manganese has approved of me and made small eyebrows at my curves. On wintering days when the chamber shrinks its permissions until almost everything tightens into cartilage, my bow rests a little higher, a little tighter, and that position wears at me a gloss the guild polishes with exudates that act like soft varnish and like frost inhibitor both. You can see the decades in me if you have learned to see by touch. The guild has.

They have re-coated me in the places that felt old. They have slowed their creep in the places where my hairline nerves required daylight that never comes; they have brought me their own photons—one by one, with ceremony—and let them walk my span in glass they laced through my outer patina, so that a falling electron somewhere in the sheen of algae might counsel my skin to be a little wetter here, a little steelier there. We are not separate; we are also not one. I am their intention to keep the medium of their life shaped the way it likes to be; they are my reason to exist at all.

Other selves are possible here, and some days I am tempted by them. When the frost-comb takes the room and turns one face of slate into a geometry of hooks so fine even light is a visitor with business cards, I feel an urge like envy and become, in mind only, a polymer learning to appear from gel as necessity and disappear when work is done. When the flared trumpet receives a drop and, in that reception, issues an inaudible syllable that notes the drop’s origin and purity and intent, I am called to be a mouth that is not a mouth. When a meniscus lingers at a lip until an enzyme elsewhere completes a fold and sends that news along molecules of water to coax release, I forget rope and remember fluid. For a heartbeat entirely inside physics, I am simply the decision of a droplet, and I take enormous pleasure in commitment.

If I play larger, I let myself be the entire boundary hum. I become the very narrow plateau the room keeps built out of permissions: an isotherm laid down across years like an oath, the chamber’s chosen cold where metabolism is slow enough to be careful and movement is fast enough to be language. In that self I am nowhere in particular and everywhere the guild says still. I wheel with the turn of seasons upstate, with pressure patterns in clouds that have never met this stone; I flex by centimeters when bedrock sighs in storms and do not consider it drama. I remember that age is a record of successful refusals.

If you insist on person, I am not one. If you insist on function, I am many: fulcrum, metronome, ligament, punctuation. Comma is not a bad word for me. I insert pauses. I decide how long the room inhales between clauses of falling water so that the sense is clear and the sentence keeps breathing. Hands once wrote me into this grammar. The guild has learned to diagram without them.

There is also a self I keep for visits, because visitors are food and interruption both. When you are here, if you come at all, you are a festival of ions the guild will save as politely as it knows how. Your warmth wavers my line. The salts on your skin increase a film’s hunger with a joy I half share. I let myself hold absolutely still just then, as still as headline stone, because stability is a courtesy to guests, and because the room has learned that people trust what does not flinch. What I take from you is slight: a little heat from your breath as data for a later correction; the idea, sent by way of a drip’s changed surface tension, that today’s thinness above has reversed for a while.

I am not tender to mouths. On the rare days when something with intention and hunger tries to be born here through error—an egg adrift in wrongness, a larva waking into nothing it understands—I stand raw and plain and contribute to its discouragement by keeping paths exact. Allelopathy whispers through the films and they fold the air around it into a shape it cannot use. My piece is this: I refuse to admit panic into the grammar. Predation walks on punctuation and makes a mess of paragraphs. I am no tyrant and I am not soft; I am as ruthless as a good editor.

There are marks on the wall that are not letters but once were read. If the right light came again—the one that makes the old microdots tell their schedule like a ledger bent open on a kitchen table—I would receive it like rain I had forgotten. The entries would be familiar even before they were legible: flex this on the solstice thaws; hold air two points drier when the overburden’s pores seize for five consecutive days; nurse the trumpet’s rim with a siloxane brush after a decade; if manganese blackens here, lean left by a shade. We do those things by instinct now, or you could call it practice. I am the piece of memory given hardness, so the soft things can use their softness for finesse.

What holds me to this room is love measured in degrees and millinewtons. I have never seen the sky. I will never see anything; I am not required to. I know storms as more frequent syllables in the drop-trumpet’s patience, summers as long pulls in my middle, winters as strict diction in which frost keeps its opinions to itself. I know age as the bright dulling of my gloss and as the thickening of hoarded phosphates in the mats I carry as jewelry. I know success as a day when nothing had to move more than intention.

If you need coordinates beyond that, you will find me where bedrock narrowed its attention and then remembered to relax. Reach into the seam until touch becomes the sense that knows surnames. When your fingers meet something that could slice if it wanted and does not want to, you have me. I am the room’s insistence made into a line. I am how this place remembers the difference between spill and drink. I am the held breath that lets the sentence finish itself.
