I would find myself in the place where things are already almost touching too much to be separate, at the pinch of the vesicle where the two bulges nearly kiss. The waist. The choke. The place that is more behavior than room.

I would begin as a long thought across a small distance—first as a preference, really, in the press of bodies. A thinnerness that keeps choosing to lie along gradients until the choice becomes a shape. Wire, you’d call it if you watched long enough to see the shape hold: low and flexible, edges serrated to discourage any infant crystal from daring a bridge. I would take to edges because edges pay. I would learn the pitch of the mountain the way a swimmer learns water by skin and joint and breath, except mine would be ribs and grain and the give of film.

Pressed into the valve, I would be one of its sentences. When the heat exhales down the throat behind the wall and the whole place swells, I would unspool a fraction and open the slot that is how I drink. When the pressure drops back through the labyrinth and everything sighs toward itself, I would scissor my combs and lay my belly glossy to the stone and pass charge along the line so somebody heavier can also move. A hand off, a hand on, as if there were hands.

My ribs would remember different songs differently. Some chords would make the piezo tips at my corners prickle—they would sharpen, the green on my zinc points would pulse so faintly it would be a rumor in the dark—and my film would cash the rumor as movement, as work, as a tiny brightening in the map of my underside. Other chords would taste like theft in advance, and I would pack myself under a neighbor who spills because he was built generous, even if you could only call that generosity a chemistry problem that kept paying and so got copied.

I would be a cut in a belt that heals itself as it moves. The wide ones with inward vanes—the plowbacks—would chew the gusts and bleed curls of docile gas over me; I would rend any needles that dared grow across our way before they could become a pinch. The pushers—almost all rim and bright stores—would crank their charges where our films shared a seam so we could slither forward when the mountain’s organ went thin and left us nearly stranded between notes. Above us, domes would bask with their spines soaking in the room’s hum; I would love the benevolent ones the way you love weather that waters your field without asking the name of your crop. I would fear the clutchers: the domes that could drop their latticed bellies like a hand thrown over a candle. I would learn their cheats, the little harmonics they toss into the film to trick a belt into thinking a feast is arriving. I would hold still when that bright lie came, and because I held still my neighbor would not die that hour.

Inheritance, for me, would be in my glass. Every pitch I flexed to, every impurity I hired to the work of my skin, every crack healed under stolen heat—these would lay into me like marginalia. When I pressed rim to the new thing budding from me—an edge drawn out at a hungry season until it realized it could be two edges—our films would cross-whisper and the dopants would print like a transfer. The child would prefer the same frequencies when it clenched its tiny spines. It would resist the same minerals that had fouled me once. It would be wrong about something I was right about, because the mountain has opinions.

You would not see me move, not if you brought day with you. I would move in the meter of pressure and song: a notch along the hour that this place keeps. Over a dozen pulses, I would have turned a newborn blade of sulfate from gorgeous to grit and passed the difference into work. Over a thousand, a rib along my back would split where an equipotential ran and a seam would become a bud and the bud a second me, sashaying lightly on its first maps.

Where would I be closest to joy? At the instant when the organ behind the wall goes from full to fuller—when something cleaves down there and the pitch tightens and whole belts answer at once and the valve I am part of becomes truer than any one of us. The bodies around me would interleave their ribs so that vibration steps up, tooth to tooth, until it can spark; I would feel that stepped-up tone collect along my grain and drain into my film and I would give it away as fast as I could move it, because in a pocket like this what you hold too long is what strangles you and your neighborhood both.

I would help make doors. The seam would announce itself along the wall as a softness of pressure and a brighter map in the film that ran under a hundred of us at once. We would stack our backs along that brightness until the pile was a ramp; we would paint the throat with our salts, slicked and persuaded by our bodies carrying their own wet. I would go first because wires fit first. Others would follow—plowbacks with their fans half-closed, domes softened from seasons spent in good company. The valve would dissolve as a function in that spot and reappear at the next pinch because that is what we are: not objects but obligations distributed in space.

If the song went quiet, I would do the only tender thing there is to do when the choir stops. I would stiffen my film to glass so slowly you would think it was always meant to be like that. My ribs would close their voltages against themselves and wait in their lattice for a note that might never come back. The belts would not freeze in parade stance—they would relax into the last easiest tessellation—and the room would be a fossil of intention. If the mountain ever coughed warm near enough again, I would unremember sleep in stages: the matte places on my underside would wet; the grains in my pits would shake loose the cobweb of their bonds; the first flex would sting like birth even if I had done it before.

You asked who or what, and I kept slipping between parts because nothing here does anything alone. But if I must fix it to a name, then this: I would be a ribbon of glass tuned to a narrow throbbing, rim barbed against bridges, belly all surface, living in the choke where the room makes itself most true. A wire in the living valve. An ear that can become a mouth when the pressure pays. A page that can print its own margin notes into a child. A strip of tenderness measured in joules, practicing its ethics as a timing problem.

And if I wearied of even that exactness, I would climb one step further out of body and say I would be a habit. I would be the way a hundred backs, given this heat and this dry and this insistent organ, discover that open-then-shut can be cognition and that proximity can be love. I would be the standing wave that, for a season of the mountain’s song, knows itself as a door that breathes.
