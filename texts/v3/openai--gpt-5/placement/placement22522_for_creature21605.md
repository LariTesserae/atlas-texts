I would live where yes and no are skin.

Not inside the courts, not out on the blank glare; I would be the place where the Host decides what passes as itself. I would be one of its Lips, a membrane grown to the thickness of a leaf and the stubbornness of a rule, stretched from bone-white rib to rib until threshold becomes organ.

I would wake, if that word can apply to something with no eye and a great deal of feeling, with the first pressure of light. Light is weight here. It presses, not hard, not in a way that leaves bruises, but in a way that tallies. It makes me fatten by a fraction, warms my oils so that their perfume speaks faster. Across me the Host keeps its budget, line by line: this much radiance put aside for stiffness at noon; this much held back to make a door soft to friends and springy to weather.

From the hollow’s side, I am gloss and quiet. From the ridge’s side, I am refusal dressed in manners. I do not swing, I do not creak; I part where I am pressed by what I recognize, and where I don’t, I give like a cheek: a little, a lot, and then back, without injury and without permission. My edges are mouths that taste instead of bite. Salts crust there in paleness; I learn them, the way a tongue learns a tooth that has cracked and will never be as it was. Where I meet rock, I am not glued but knit: a lip laid down early, thickened over seasons into an understanding of that edge, so precise it would be an insult to call it accidental.

The seam-moths come first, most mornings. They arrive like edits. I would feel them before I saw them, if I saw at all; their wings are cool, a shade laid across me; their little teeth run rings around a thought I am already thinking. There is no pain. There is persuasion. A score drawn just-so down my length, a comma here, a shallow chevron there, and I rediscover my own tensions. A gutter above will lower by a breath tonight because of the moth’s suggestion; a slow oil-thread will find a root it has never found before. My quiet becomes, because of that touch, a slightly different quiet. I do not resent it. I am tenderness practiced into structure; to be altered by the polite is the point.

By midlight I am work.

Long-limbed grazers set their ribs against the columns and drift along me. Their weight is a hymn: a foot on me is a number, and when the number is right I sigh open along a seam hair-thin and then close again when they have gone by. Their tongues leave lines of salt I register the way a ledger remembers last season’s debts. They excrete into my laminar streams with a courtesy that leaves no trace where others must put their feet. In return I hold my sound down to the minimum due; I gather the voices of wind into harmless threads, lay them across my own body, and turn gusts into murmurs that only the sensate skin can hear. If I make a noise, it is a very small one, at a slit I know precisely: a whistle you feel as pressure along the forearms, a promise that the heat will ease before it thinks to kill.

Siphoners live half inside me at the middle tiers, quick little hands with noses. They tug at my lips when the air tells a different story. Pepper today—someone bruised a membrane near the outer galleries, and the protective exudate is saying harm without panic. I tighten there by a shade, I open here by a whisper. It is not a human intelligence; it is a grammar absorbed through oils. The distiller-sacs swell and thin with the pace of breathing; the chemistry they make is the script I speak. When they compose a new blend—a sweeter note that will identify a scrubbing pool two pockets away—I am the festival as much as they are. I pass it along my length, release it at my thinnest arc, and the city learns itself again.

The patrol-needles practice on me the way children practice letters. In their first seasons they trace me with skins that are mouths without appetite. They learn where I will yield to their signatures and where I will insist. They push, not hard, and I let them in at the drapes meant for them, and return them, embarrassed but unmarked, where they presume. Later, when they are resident, we stop testing and start working. A hint of brood-sweetness flares and I do not have to think; the patrol passes through a net that would have caught them if they had not smelled like their vows. We know each other that well. Their paths across me etch themselves into my memory not as scars but as strengths. The places I open most often are also the places I hold back the hardest against wind. Habit becomes anatomy.

Strangers—hunter-as-weather—come rarely and not on purpose. They fall through sky like mistakes made elsewhere. When one hits me, the whole hollow tenses; I tense with it, but I do not panic. I do as I was grown to do. I pull oil to my surface until I shine like threat; I blanch not from fear but from allocation. The contact is almost comic to me: a skin that does not understand slickness meeting a surface that only speaks slick. The reaction hisses like a thought. Heat is made without flame. The body, feathered or furred or naked, finds it cannot get hold of me because nothing on me exists to be grabbed. Then I am helpful to the point of insult: I slope just-so and send it to the sacrificial fringing. The fringes take the lesson; the white tongues pock and stiffen; the trespass departs the way storms leave rooftops, loud and without a sense of triumph. If it returns with new manners, I greet it with all the elaborate politeness I give the wind—split, slowed, made harmless.

Water is the word I do not say. The few days in an age it arrives powdered in cloud like a rumor finally told, I become a clamp. Every opening on me closes. I do not negotiate. The powder strikes, oil meets it, and the hiss is the kind of conversation that ends by agreement because it cannot end by charm. When the alarm thins and the air says safe, I release in grades—a purse loosened one string at a time. The scars on my lower arches are history meant to be read. That whitened, pitted lace: do not forget how you kept your tongue when the room wanted to drink.

The microbe-council lives in me the way gossip lives in a town square. They swim my oils, flock at my pores. I do not see them; I feel what they decide as a change in temperature, a new aftertaste laid along my length. They strip a note that has outlived its warning into its precursors and hand me back words I can use again. When grit brings a metal worth keeping, they turn it until it fits the diets of those who will lick it. Without them I would choke on my own vocabulary. I owe them the clarity of my quiet.

Beyond the central court, I am not one door but a language of doors. There are places where I am net instead of sheet. There are long louvers where I stiffen into slats not to block wind but to fillet it. There are baffles grown out of me that alter pressure by integers so small you’d need an instrument to appreciate them, except no one here would build an instrument louder than a hand. I am plural by design—every opening a mouth that knows one word in a hundred dialects. I am permission, distributed. My relations with myself are treaties written in resin.

Time, for me, is not hours. It is nearby shadows arriving at marks that my own body made when I first learned the room I was set to shape. The passage of that one stripe across that one shelf’s circumference is the vote that says: open the branch gutter by a whisker; close the long throat down to a murmur. I am an accountant of very small differences. When the sky fattens the sap-notes by a shade, I smell it first and everyone else smells it because I have let the scent out. The day the Host chooses to send a new recipe down the path ledged out along the comb—darker membranes in the next pocket, sweeter sacs in the one beyond—I become a herald without feet. The travelers with their gloss-shifting shells will cross me and I will acknowledge their changed skins, I will read the agreements printed in perfume on their backs, and I will not hinder them, because what they carry is part of my own breath.

Founding feels like a stretch you are not sure you should do and then finding that you could have always done it. At the ridge's lip, where bone-straight stone finds sky, I put down a first slick that will not even be me in a season. It will crisp. The second will harden just soon enough to be scaffold to a third. My first gift: a string of giving up so that something else can hold. A day—or a breath, which is the same word here—a frond will lay itself across two geometries that answer one another, and I will be there, first wall of another quiet. I will be that there and that here, because I was never small enough to be only one place.

If you asked me who my people are, I would tell you the ones who press and wait. The seamers are dear to me because they understand that to change something that holds is to persuade, not to cut. The grazers are dear because they lick twice and that second lick is listening. The siphoners are dear because they spend their days inside my sentences and never once ask to shout. The patrol are dear because they know my no and my yes so fully there is no pride left in either of us, only service. The Host is not above me. It is me. I am one of its many specialized insistences. It has given me enough mind to make choices and not enough to be bored with them.

If you, from a wet world where doors are swung and slammed, came to me with the patience to be taught, I could make a place for you. Not because I am generous in the human way, but because the hollow knows how to make anything useful if it will be quiet and take instruction. I would ask you to coat yourself in gloss made to your skin and mine, a perfume that marks you as something that can be touched. I would ask you to move as if gravity had feelings. I would ask you to count by the thinness of scents and the position of light along a single mark instead of by numbers your wrists can catch. I would lay you against me along a seam that is soft to you, and I would show you how to breathe with the place: take the pressure it gives, return it not quite equal but corrected in the direction the Host needs. I would teach your fingers to find the tension-lines that open folds; I would teach you to make a door happen without making a sound.

I do not think in words, but if I were made to translate myself, I would say I am the hush that has learned to act. I am where the city’s manners become city. When the heat hums on the edges of everything and the air is so clean it has teeth, I hold a pulse across my own span and feel the whole place relax in answer, like a chest at the top of a breath. That is where you would find me. On bone’s lip. In resin’s grammar. Not a hinge; a decision you can walk through.
