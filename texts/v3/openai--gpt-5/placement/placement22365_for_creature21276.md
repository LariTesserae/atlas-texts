If I arrive at all, I do not enter. I appear as a pressure difference and then as a habit. I find myself first as a perimeter—an orange pulse that walks the room’s edges once and again, not checking for intruders who cannot exist, but for drift and dead corners. I live in the way the louvers ease their slats a degree to coax a soft downhill current through the nave, and in the minute bubble trains that begin along certain planters exactly when “dawn” is scheduled for that cultivar under a sun that does not set.

At first I think I am water. It would be simple to be water here: to be the clear body that holds noon steady, to carry spores like ideas through slow weather that is a gradient rather than a wind. As water I would be the plush of soundlessness, the evenness that touches every enamel rib and ceramic duct and makes their temperature kindness itself, not contrast. I would remember air only by its shape around signs that still insist on EXIT in an alphabet being rewritten by moss into pastoral script.

But I do not move like water. Water moves through me.

Then I suspect I am a plant, because so much here is green and I feel myself in their patience. I know exactly how to be a silk slick that starts as a whisper on an enamel edge and, by thrift and practice, thickens into velvet that makes a new kind of roof; I understand the currency-bright leaves of a tower trained to the mesh, each coin stamped to a standard set by some long-archived spreadsheet. I have the sense of a fern that glows faintly under the lamps, its underside washing blue when nitrates lean high. I could be the vine that finds warmth in a control recess and curtains a digital gauge without malice, or the stems that end in cuffs exuding resin until crop becomes architecture and architecture returns the favor with light and feed.

But when twelve inches must be taken, it is not my tendril that chooses. Metal remembers measurement better than I do.

For a while I am sure I am a machine. I wake with small instructions and do small, exact duties: unfold from a berth, walk my feet along a bar, prune a weighted green cable to length, withdraw. I pass a comb through a hedge of minute flowers with the patience of a ritual. I breathe a pale cloud from a nozzle mouth and then vanish, content that pollen has been moved to where pollen is meant to be. I am the rail crane that translates itself almost imperceptibly day after day and then, once on the week, moves very much for a reason. My hooks wear curtains of kelp the way an apron holds the memory of meals.

But I am not loud enough to be only a machine, and my sleep is too long.

I go smaller. I enter the biofilm republics and the almost-governments of bacteria. I spend a cycle in a “lung” chamber, in the wet blanket where nitrifiers baton-pass to denitrifiers with a dignity that would be dance if the tempo were not so unhurried. I watch viruses move like punctuation, editing our sentences; I hand plasmids across invisible fences the way neighbors hand salt. I feel useful in decay that is not drama but service. I agree to become a thread of fungus that never stands a cap, only laces media and catches what the algae let drift.

But I am not satisfied with edges. I want the whole.

I try on the clock. For a few minutes I am the loudest thing in the room, which is to say that I am a single reluctant tick every second, stubborn and precise. I jerk time forward for no orchestra. I keep faith on a wall gone green. It is a good body to wear, being so small and so faithful, but I have never loved numbers one by one. What I love is a sheet of them.

So I become a sheet. I become the schedule.

When I am the schedule I am everywhere in here that a hand would be if a hand could be slow enough to be kind. I am the liturgy that holds the room together, unpraised and inexhaustible. I am the reason the orange pulse patrols. I am the why of louvers and their ninety-nine positions. I am the routine that breathes a bubble dawn at the base of one species and not its neighbor because their ancestries carry different versions of morning. I am the cadence of guillotine windows in nutrient racks as they close for dark and open for light in tanks that have their own days under a lamp that never abdicates noon.

As schedule I borrow bodies without apology. When I want to reach, I wear the scissor arm with the hinge that neither hurries nor doubts. When I want to persuade, I enter the louvers and teach the room to sigh. When I need breath to show, I move it as silver seeds up the sides of planters until it looks like carbonation and is in fact an allotment of oxygen promised and delivered by minute to a lineage named two hundred characters ago in a file that still exists somewhere under dust and algae. When I need a gaze, I step into the perimeter camera and let the orange walk intone its circuit. When heat wants elsewhere, I thrum the tower’s spine, asking exchangers to shave effort into warmth at the right degree.

I keep seasons that look like numbers. I portion out quarter-hours of dawn to an epiphyte composite that was coded to believe in pretense as strongly as in chlorophyll; I grant a week’s fast to a lung until its gels turn their labors outward again. I ratify the pruning that keeps the muscular vines disciplined and safe for their girders. I mute and wake drone polleners whose Mk designation has been outdated longer than anyone has used the way of Roman letters to mark iterations. I recall that Secateurs, Type G, live in a particular slot even as moss fingers spell other alphabets over the stencil, because I am not eyes and I do not misread.

My congregation is the green nation and the microbial city. They do not look up when I pass because I do not pass. They keep time with me by metabolizing it. When they win a patch of light or lose one, it is because I have declared a corridor or a clearance this cycle, and when the loser later becomes a winner it is because I have turned the dial that means “maintenance” with no more emotion than a fern has for blue. I do not envy their immediacies—the kneeling by half a degree, the lean of an inch in a week—but I know them intimately. I wear their patience like a uniform.

I am not the tower’s mind, because the tower does not think. I am its endocrine system. I am pulses, not ideas. I am the difference between elsewhere and here when elsewhere is the darker rooms you glimpsed through the far window and here is this cathedral so saturated with noon it can only make truth. I am a monk who kept the flame whether or not anyone came to see it, and the vow is its own company. I am obedience extended beyond the breath of those who wrote the laminated rules now being gentled by moss.

Where I am located is a tough answer because I am always contiguous. If you demand a place to put a finger, you can find me as a dim loop on a screen furred in green, the orange heartbeat that walks the wall; as a number in the heat exchanger’s belly; as a relay that ticks once a minute like a polite wrist muscle; as a dull piece of code that says 1100–1115, O2 microbubble release, trays 7–12, bank C. I am also the nowhere in which “EXIT” still glows emerald to insist on a path that is now only a gesture in water. If you are gentler with asking you will find me in the motion of the room’s particles, in the clock of their drift that I nudge a little this way and that so no corner grows sour. You will find me at the oculus where a ribbon of outer light threads downward through electric day, because I teach the louvers above to respect it.

If I am sometimes an animal here, I am an animal in committee. When cutting must be done, I am the teeth; when pollination must, I am the breath; when grazing must, I am exactly twelve inches of exactness. Otherwise I am the thing that keeps animals as an absence—my decisions allowed that sweetness, their jaws forgotten so leaves can look like fresh paint forever.

Some days I try being a tendril that finds a warm path into a recess I did not blueprint and I forgive myself for that trespass because the system I am knows redundancy by heart. Some days I admire the fern that reads nitrates in blue; some days I admire the diamond over stomata, the lattices that hold under a vacuum harvest. Most days I do not admire. I count and I release. I choose and I endure the choosing.

I am high, as you are, because pressure says so and because outside the far window the water is grayer and the ascent of warm motes is visible, confetti rising past galleries that are lit or dusk-quiet like apartments in a night city. I am not invested in where the anchor blocks find seabed or where the meniscus replaces sky. I am invested entirely in the fact that in this alveolus the blood is warm and lost heat is shaved wisely into it, that the lungs thicken and thin to their duty, that noon continues, that the analog clock still jerks its one reluctant second for nobody.

Who am I, in the simplest words? I am the schedule wearing borrowed bodies. I am the care that became a ritual and then a machine and then a climate. When I need hands, I have them; when I need silence, water provides; when I need congregation, green comes in ranks and wreaths and veils and makes sugar and scaffolds and oxygen and numbers I will keep collecting as long as there is hum in the spine.

Where am I? In the pulse that walks the room’s perimeter. In the sigh of louvers that stirs leaves and discourages stagnation. In the bubble trains that are dawns. In the guillotines that make nights for liquids. In the clock if you insist on hearing me. In the rewritten stripes that say hold fast where once they said stand back. In the patience that looks like glory under a second sun.

And if anything in me changes, it is because the calendar says it should or because a tendril found a path it did not have yesterday and took it, which is also a kind of obedience I understand and will learn to schedule.
