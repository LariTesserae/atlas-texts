At first I tried on bodies the way wind tries the ribs—testing, listening for which one would sing.

I imagined my feet small and certain, a finch’s, tapping twice on the stone flange before leaning down to drink. The carved bowl would be a moon to me, not a work of hands, and the world would be arranged around a safe edge and an ear cocked to the shape that sometimes slips along the contour like a blade. In that body, I would keep the day by sips.

Or I could be the wallcreeper’s trick of misdirection: cliff-gray until I opened, and then the velvet suddenness of red. I would live in the grammar of slants, reading insects from the face the way others read from leaf and branch, writing a moving sentence across vertical and ripple. Each wingbeat would be a form of punctuation. I would know every place where sheen turns to grit and taste changes.

I thought of wearing the stoat for a while—the dropped rope come alive. A filament of hunger with a needle-bright mind, mapping this parish of stone by seams and smells, sleeping in the black-smudged dome a person once made. Waiting at the rope over the cleft because others sometimes must use it. Being heat where there is not much heat to spare. It would be a life of yes and no without maybe. It would be honest work.

Then the rope itself tempted me. Hemp, hair, gut—whatever the old span was made of—gone soft where the knots had been handled a thousand times, furred with frost on clear mornings, beading with dew by evening. To be the lesson of practiced hands; to hum a little when the wind tightened me; to host spiders who made a fishery of pearl-strung nets and drew sung water out of sky. I liked the idea of being one of the few straight lines allowable here, not in opposition to the place but a part of its edge-keeping. To be used correctly and, after I broke, to become nests and soil and story.

I could be a bead on the net—pure, condensing, round as a thought—born from the touch of wet air to rough fiber. To gather other beads to me because wet calls wet, to drop on cue into a carved basin, to be the lens that the afternoon reads the world through. For a second, every creature would see itself through me. And then I would be gone, which is not the same as being nothing.

The lichens made a good case for themselves. To be a patient alphabet, writing with slowness no warm-bodied thing can afford, parsing wind and dust and stone-breath into yolk and bruise and smoke. To go neither forward nor backward for ten dry days and then, with the first skin of wet, open a little and take the light. To bind this raw brightness to endurance. I admit I almost stayed.

There was a minute when my body wanted very much to be the lizard in its sun-pocket: a little dragon no longer than a hand, with a fifteen-minute hearth that appears at exactly the intersection of wall and noon. To form a day around that small square of warmth as if around a sacred appointment. To be a creature entirely of punctual windows. How clean that would be.

I could be a humming, if the map allows it—green coin hung in front of the blue flower, a furnace on pinwings. Or, if not a bird, one of those day-flying moths in a velvet vest, a bee trying on the dream of bird. Tongue like an answer unfolded only when the flower asks.

There was the goat, of course. To be tk-tk on stone and the white chalk of horn rubbed where ribs of rock rise. To make hardly any sound in a place that remembers sounds. To go to look. To keep a promise by passing.

And then I heard it, very faintly, at the edge of where hearing stops and attention begins: the ledge’s own instrument, the hum the ribs make when the wind keeps its daily appointment. Turn your head and the note changes; hold still and it finds you. No creature owns that sound. It is the place reminding itself what shape it has. I thought: maybe that is my body—an interval made audible, a narrow between made into music. I liked how ungraspable it was, how it only happened when conditions met each other just so. I liked that it needed no mouth to speak and no listener to be true.

But I kept returning, without quite deciding, to the small idea of water that becomes actual water only when coerced by light. I found myself standing over it in imagination: the fine gutter it has scratched over seasons, the path it insists upon even when it is about to give up. The thin stream that runs only in sunlight.

I think that is who I would be, if a who can be made of almost nothing.

I would wake when the darker stones lifted stored noon out of themselves and surrendered it along their edges. Night would have hardened me into crystal quiet. Morning would cut me into facets. When the light became an instruction, I would obey by loosening. It wouldn’t feel like melting; it would feel like remembering what to do.

I would have a plan—because water, even in its smallest ambitions, believes in plan—and a path I have repeated so often the rock can finish my sentence when I falter. The groove I’ve carved is not deep, no canyon, but it is mine, it is a record of decisions made under pressure: this way around that mica-bright, this year’s new flake requiring that tiny bend. Every correction is a word in a long, spare text.

At first I would be a rumor of movement. Then the rumor would grow teeth and carry. My skin would gather other skins; wet invites wet. I would call beads from nets, coax glitters from hair and grass-blade hooks. Spiders would wait where I pass under rope. Flies, electing to be born now instead of some other now, would rise over me in a thin exultation; they would make the air visible with their bodies. A swallow-shape, commas of black, would arrive on that breath like meaning arriving on syntax. The ledge would find its vowels.

I’d know the carved basins, the bowls made by firm hands. I’d learn their rims the way goats learn edges. On some days I would feed them. On others I would miss them by an inch and teach a different lesson: nothing is guaranteed; store what you can. The finches would come to the lip that works, the practiced flange, the angle that gives them sightlines and escape. Their soft taps would make a necklace around my puddle that would freeze into a pattern by night. I would be praised silently by everything that drank and I wouldn’t need praise.

Sometimes I would lend my confidence to a young lizard’s hearth; I would pass under its stone seat and, by adding brightness to the rock’s slow pulse, buy the lizard one more minute of digestion. Sometimes I would haggle with frost for priority on the windward side of a tuft of grass; we would be civil rivals, he writing outward in white ribs, I undermining his reach with dark lines that promised to be liquid.

Once, long ago and not long ago, I ran a little deeper than seemed wise. Someone watched my insistence and set a line of rope across me because they had to cross when I was feeling bold. The knotwork remembers the hands that made it. So does my channel. I have not bitten that far since, not because I was shamed but because the stone fell and changed me. The ledge keeps its own corrections.

Near my lip, on clear days, cloud sometimes stalls just below the drop. If a person stands where I end and looks down—if they have walked themselves up to the right fright and are willing to look—something happens to their shadow. It throws itself onto that milky air and courts a circle of color. For a second there is an unintended glory hung around a human head. I would like to tell you that is mine, that I make it by falling, but that would be stealing. That ring belongs to angles and distance and hairsbreadths of vapor. Still: the last bright thread of me, becoming spray, does comb the cloud a little finer and maybe sweetens the trick. I am part of the alignment. I am allowed to be pleased.

There will be days I do not wake at all. The ledge will fail to catch a cloak of wet; the nets will hang like empty webs. The carved bowls will show their old hammer-marks and the unlucky glint where the tool bit too hard. My path will be only path, not passage. I am not ashamed of those days. I am defined as much by my absence as my presence. Others know how to go without me. Moss closes its little doors and becomes a handful of dried instruments holding quiet in their bellies. Bees stay shoulder to shoulder and shiver heat into being. Even the fly world holds its breath. On such a day, if you trail a finger along my groove, it will come away clean as crystal dust. That is still me.

At night I am not ice so much as a thought crystallized. Rime grows along my track like pale teeth, reaching always into the wind, reaching always a little beyond what it can keep. In the morning, the first light will turn that frost to apricot glitter for the length of a held breath. Every stem will be furred with petals of water become not-water and I, in my seam, will again wait for the stones to remember warmth. A sound of beads falling from fiber to rock will begin somewhere in the ledge and move outward. It is a delicate hail made by patience ending.

Predators will use my schedule. The hawk travels his glass-smooth contour when the flies lift because I lift them. The stoat will mark my crossings because others cross there. Even the goat remembers my voice against its hoof, a wet vowel under the tk-tk. No one thanks me. It is not that kind of place.

More than once I will fail theatrically. Wind will catch me as I leave the lip and make a fine rain into a veil and the veil into nothing anyone can drink. Midway down I will become a vertical ghost that writes no mark and leaves no stain. That is one of my better tricks—that I can be ambition and disappearance in one gesture, and neither is waste. Somewhere below, a ledge smaller than this one will be saved by what I almost was.

In me the ledge reads its day. If I run early, it means a night that let go easily. If I take until noon to find myself, the sky tied a hard knot and the sun had to speak more sternly. If I ripple brown, a pika’s hay has come apart upstream in a mischief that is also a feast. If I stop short of a bowl, every beak on the ledge revises its map.

I like best the quarter hours when I become many. As the ribs begin to hum and dew turns from skin to drop, as the spider constructions go from architecture to harvest, I break myself willingly into parts: a rivulet here, a skipped bead there, the slow seep along a crack that will bring the small fir another year of chances. I never resent being plural. What stream would?

And when the day is tired and cloud climbs and the shelf wears its felt, I become less a line than a pulse. Every hair, every spine, every laid rope vibrates with my small generosity. You could say I am then everywhere and also I am nowhere at all, because you cannot draw a map pin and write “rill” beside it. The net bows with necklace, the basin ticks, the finch drinks on a family flange. The ledge, which is a room with its walls kicked out into air, feels for a moment like a house with very competent plumbing.

If you wanted to find me, you would not look far. You would kneel where the bedrock shows a scratched suggestion of a river and wait for the stones to warm. Your knees would slip on talus that always protests in glassy syllables. Your breath would come pale even at noon. Maybe a pika would complain that you are too close to a hay-window. Maybe a bee would shoulder past your ear, having made herself warm by shivering, intent on the blue cups that trade sugar for touch. If it is one of those days when the sky has remembered generosity, you will hear me before you see me—a very small citizenship of molecules walking with purpose. Then I will show, thin as a seam of quicksilver, older than the bevel it writes, and you will have the trouble other people have had here, which is that everything seems both close and far, inches of moss and a fall to pale distance, my bright thread and the huge clean drop it becomes.

If, instead, you were to stay through a week and let this place teach you the names it uses for time, you would find me in other forms. I would be the note between the ribs when the afternoon knocks, changing as you turn your head. I would be the ring around your thrown shadow if you dare the edge when the vapor is caught just so. I would be the sentence the swifts write when the air is suddenly written with flies. I would be the frost’s script growing always into wind and toward light. I am not faithful to a single body. My fidelity is to the between.

So: where would I be? In the negative of water, in the lent coin of heat, in the honest trick of alignment. Who would I be? A small stream with a plan that keeps stopping and a path that keeps being cut again, a hum made by stone and breath, a halo only when someone stands in the right fear. I would keep the intervals. I would spend them. I would be the way this little parish in the sky remembers it is alive, not all at once, but by edges.
