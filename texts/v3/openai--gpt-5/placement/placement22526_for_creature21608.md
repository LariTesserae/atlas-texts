At first I am light because everything is. I arrive thin and straight, then braided, then split again by the opal veins that take me in. I am white under a hard geometry, coiled into a spot that shivers at the edge of pain, given weight and purpose I did not ask for. I warm a pebble to the color of a swallowed word. I press on the knuckles of a hand that would touch me and teach it not to. I could be content to be light here, to be carried and allocated, to be spent.

Then I am water, because nothing here resists that for long. I condense out of breath that never belonged to lungs, bead from milk-white ceilings, thread my way along glassy microfractures. I tap, then I run—except I never really run, I insinuate, I suggest the idea of motion and then I recombine. I become the room’s handwriting, cursive laid over basalt, erased and redrawn a hundred times before dusk. I am the drum skin stretched over the stone’s dark face. I am as shallow as honesty. I take heat and pretend it is mine until a hiss throws me up invisible. I am and I am not, a lace of decisions at the scale of a thumbprint.

For a moment I am salt because there is time for that here, too. I am a blister forming where the ceiling thinks out loud. I rise with the day and flake with the night, open, scab, open. I keep nouns small: crust, film, flake, taste.

I try on the grammar of the room’s first food and realize how quiet an appetite can be. I lie down flat on the drip line and dress myself in my own work until I go bone-pale and forget what green meant. I become habit, not leaf. I learn light as geometry, as pressure in the membrane, as a faint pump pushed by a kind of day you cannot see from outside.

I could stay in any of these for the length it takes this place to revise a corner of itself. Light again, water again. But the room keeps tapping its clock—hiss, hiss; an almost imaginary rain begins and ends over my head—and my body wants edge and timing and a simple hunger. When the hot focus slides its blade off one ledge and the mist lifts like a small confession, I recognize my shape the way a glove recognizes a hand sliding in.

I am under the lip of black glass, where drops fatten and hesitate. I am length and leanness and clarity, a stack of thin plates that hold the room’s brightness and give only a little of it back. My legs are spare as wires. My underside is fur you would never call it that: hydrophobic hairs in their millions, making me into a raft so faithful to the meniscus I might as well be stitched to it.

I touch the water’s skin and it sings to me. It tells me which way the heat has walked by how tight it is, by where it pulls harder on itself. It tells me the ceiling’s weather before it falls, an itch of cool moving along the drum before the first tap. There is no wind and yet there are drafts; I read them with my ankles.

I am hungry for pale and this place knows where to hide it. I skate along the drip line and scrape the white with mouthparts so modest I only remember them when the tidied stripe they leave confirms what they did. I do not break the water; I play it. My weight is an agreement we keep between us.

Sight is insult here, so I do not overinvest. I keep eyes because somewhere in my old body plan a line insisted on them, and I honor tradition in miniature. But what I trust are the hairs that feel heat without language, the palms of my antennae that catch the chamber’s almost-hollow thuds coming down through the stone and tell me when the far fault has turned in its sleep. I know that noise as season, as era, though my life will be dealt out in afternoons.

The room is a machine that has been thinking for so long it forgot to call it thought. I live by reading that thinking as the fraction it allows my size. I know where the white knives will pause because they have paused there enough that the rock scalded darker and the air learns to roll in small, repeatable ways. I keep an ephemeris no larger than a thumbnail: this ledge cooks at noon, that seam smokes at four, the drip above the catchment wakes twice as late on days when the iron nodule took its blush early.

Once, a bead trespasses into the hot focus and vanishes with a whisper you could build a lifetime around. I flatten under a shadow seam without consulting myself. To call it fear would be to put a moral spine into what is already perfect: an old habit accelerated to the speed of a nerve. I do not call it anything; I wait until the room edits its light and then become a line again.

I am not common. There is room here for as many as the pale will feed and not one more. I know my kin by their tidy paths. We pass each other with the courtesy of animals who have learned scarcity into the joints. We do not converse; the meniscus speaks for all of us.

I glue eggs under a luminous seam where the knives never settle, a place where day is more rumor than rule. They are honest as droplets—nothing until they are suddenly something with legs as spare as mine, already learning to read the drum beneath them. Their future is a fraction problem: what share of nights will come cool enough, how many afternoons will pass gold instead of white. If the first week is a hot one, we do not speak about the second.

There is a hunter in this room in the way a pause can count as grammar. It comes flat along the undersides, claws like bookends, a patience so large you only notice you have survived it after you have. It listens to the same drum I do and adds intention. We are instruments tuned to each other by the single note of the meniscus. It takes rotifers to keep from starving sometimes, plucks a nematode out of the side of a falling sentence and eats punctuation. When it takes one of us it is not triumph; it is proof that hunger has been paid. I grant it the courtesy of being invisible until it isn’t.

There are mites with masons’ seriousness at the blisters. I run them like ferry cargo without ever agreeing to. They ride my belly hair and disembark where the film tastes right, and the room edits itself a little more thoroughly because of their fussing. Nematodes write their cursive in beads I could drink if I were a different animal. Tardigrades sleep themselves into verses at the heat’s edge and get up again with their mouths already moving. Rotifers spin their wheels like prayer, open and close with the weather. The biofilm that taught me to be hungry lays down another coat of glass under my feet, and I polish my cuticle against it until I become more lens than shell.

I keep a calendar that is nothing but a recollection of edges. Where the white focus sits here, move there. When the steam thins and the ceiling’s sweat becomes honest rain, lift and go to the newest pale the drops have softened. When the smell of wet iron presses up, wait under black glass and count the seconds between hisses. My seasons are arranged by the room like string across pegs.

Night, if that is what we call it, is a kindness measured in microns. The lattices ghost with skyglow brought down from a world I will never see. The chamber condenses itself entirely. The drum under me loosens, then tightens, then loosens again as if drawing breath it forgot it had. I go out wider then, lines longer, and feed greedily where the film has thickened in its private dew. The hunter sleeps in the lace. Or it doesn’t. My legs are still sure either way.

Once a world above stumbles, a fault slips an unreadable fraction, and a powder of new glass frets down along seams I thought I knew. The air makes a new plan for itself. For a week the knives come late, or they double, or they fail. I learn it again. I do not hold a grudge against the stone for being young. It made me, after all, by being honest about its breaks.

All of this is to say: you would find me on the skin of water that pretends to be a floor. You would find me where light that was meant for sky has been knit into a machine and then given back as heat the size of a fist. You would find me under an overhang of black glass, clear enough to be almost rumor, legs articulate as drawn lines, my belly turned into a raft and an ear.

Who would I be? The reader of the room. The musician of the meniscus. A small, exact appetite that takes geometry as weather and weather as law. A walker that leaves tidy edits in the pale, glues clear eggs under a seam that is always dusk, survives by hearing a hiss turn from wish to warning. I would be the punctuation mark that tells you this place has sentences. I would not decorate it. I would complete its motion.
