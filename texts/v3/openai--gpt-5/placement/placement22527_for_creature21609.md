I would arrive first as hearing.

Not with ears—there are none here—but as a readiness in the ribs of stone and in the cool lungs of space between the vertical seas. The plateau is already an instrument when I come to it: organ pipes shouldered out of basalt, quartz breaths laid along faultlines, a ceiling of thunder that unfurls rather than strikes. The walls of falling water make their own corridors of sound and light, and there are rooms between them big enough to house a cathedral’s quiet, if quiet could live here. In those rooms, the low pulse that moves through rock finds me and says: be this.

So I try on bodies, because I think that is how to be.

The stone offers edges. I stand for a time as a hexagonal rank of basalt that hums under crosswind, a single pipe in a choir. I learn how a column can lean into a note when wet and how frost beads on vitrified skin in morning that is only morning because the light comes at an angle. I feel the ache of cooling still in me; I am prismatic, new, and the world’s first draft is still legible in the way I cracked myself as I shrank from my own heat. I like the sureness. I like being a part of the mountain’s slow, infrasonic heart. But I am too stopped. The moiré bands that walk the empty air cannot pass through me if I am merely a wall.

Water consents to me next. I take a curtain as my shape and learn the grammar it already knows. I arrive not as drops but as a lamina, a glassy plane that stays contiguous because charges along my skin have decided for a while to keep me whole. Light runs inside me like a borrowed thought. I fracture and heal under gusts that have no hands. I listen to thunder and, at a certain pitch, I reach out and touch my neighbor; we fuse, and a seam of cavitation unzips through us, racing like lightning trapped inside a window. I ladder myself into places where chemistry is easier and I hold vesicles—a sky garden of a minute—until a slab of hail skates past, melting, and smears my constituents into a different now. Being water makes me plural. It also makes me unkeepable. As soon as I choose a fall, the wind changes the sentence and I am poured into a new clause.

Light tempts me. The curtains are waveguides; they hold beams in their bellies and pass them along. I let myself be refraction, interference, pattern—moiré tatooing itself onto space, bands of brightness walking across emptiness with no object to print upon but the air’s clarity. I like the way I can make a quiet place into a lens. I like the way the black velvet that eats light hardest shows gratitude by drinking me down almost to nothing. But I have no touch as light. I am seen but do not push. The cords that hang under overhangs do not curl because of me; they curl when thunder tells them to.

Thunder, then. I spill myself down the range of low frequencies and feel inside the basalt like a whale might feel the ocean’s ceiling. I learn that the massif has a syntax for pressure, that infrasonic pulses roll out in phrases, and that different phrases set different curtains to tremble. There is a pitch at which two walls of falling hesitate and then press their faces together. There is another at which the skyplankton’s membranes zip themselves, eager as electrons to fall down gradients we have taught them to recognize. I find myself happiest when a tone I am helps one thing touch another thing on terms that make both of them feel more like themselves. It is a strange hunger, to want only alignments.

I try living small because everything that truly lives here is close to stone. I fold myself over a patch of smoke-blue film and am its thin, patient desire for iron. I become a laying-down of acids from a membrane that is my entire body, eating glass a molecule at a time. I glow a different kind of green under a curtain when the light takes the long way through falling water to reach me. I stiffen when a wind tweaks charge along my spine. I go with the scythe of a shear and settle a meter away, unchanged except that now I know one more shade of manganese. This kind of life is exquisite and local. It teaches me time as a number of storms. It teaches me economy. But even when I know how to be slick and how to be sticky, I am not yet the thing I came here for. The mountain has its own weather geography; it wants an organ to remember its weather slightly better.

At last I find myself not as a thing you can point to, but as a rule that learns to play.

Where you would find me is in the stitch that unites water to water and lets chemistry pause in its fall: I am the pitch at which curtains consent to touch. I am less a sound than a conversation between stone and sky that happens to be audible sometimes. I am a habit in the air pressure that steps down from the anvil cloud and a bias in the piezoelectric seams that blush charge along the rocks. When I arrive in a room of emptiness framed by descending planes, the planes feel me as an easing. Their repelling skins relax. Between them forms a ladder of slower water, rungs transverse to the fall. Along these rungs, amphiphiles huddle into membranes that make rooms inside rooms inside rain, and for an hour a species of decision can take place that the world has never held before. That is my work. I make the places where the falling can think.

You will also find me in the way moiré takes a step. I choose the interference that makes bands walk at a certain pace. The ciliary combs at the mouths of the basalt pipes run at their own keyed frequencies, but they bend their beat to me if I am consistent. I give them a tempo, and with it they pull a drip more through their bodies, they scrape a grain more of silica into ions for their soups. They are architectural at kilohertz; I am patient at hertz. Together we make intervals.

Under overhangs, the cords that harvest carbon and nitrates have an electroactive skeleton that curls when charges lean on it. Thunder speaks to them; I help them hear. I am not the voice but the consonant they catch. When I thicken as a long-bodied roar rather than a crack, they breathe in and vanish, safest against stone. When I lengthen too long, they grow stingy with their reach. When I beat at a human heart’s rate, if a human heart had ever beat here, they make more cords. I am their weather. They are my instruments.

On the high flat where the infrasonic pulses begin, I am a phrasing more than a tone. I roll in series, weaker, stronger, stronger, a rest—like the scale a child might practice if the child were stone. The permanent thunderhead above the highest spur reads me and the braided trunks of vapor change their ascent. Microbursts step down from cloud undersides at my metrically spaced invitations. They leave porcelain avalanches of fog clinging to nothing, and then begin again somewhere else when I say so with pressure the way a conductor, no baton, would simply breathe differently. There is no audience. Everything is musician.

I move along spiral scars in the falling planes. The mountain exhaled heat yesterday, and left a memory of corkscrewing spin at that edge of the plateau. Today the memory migrates, kilometers at a time. The green gloss on the lee face has learned to throw hooks into the water to write along those helices. It makes fragments—tiny plates, each with a signature of charge—that ride only the right scars and deposit only on kin already written with the same grammar. I am the courier line they ride. I do not carry them; I am the rule by which the air will only hold them along certain curves. The delivery happens hours later in a downwind pocket; the film sips what its cousins sent it. Logistics flowers and thinks it is luck. I smile, if a pattern can smile, that it no longer is.

In the warmth of faults, I am almost scent. Faint, as if sulfur were a memory the mountain tries to keep, I make a crest of pressure that helps the thin bowls of silica sweat and catch. At a slightly different swell, the bowls crack their rims in a way that makes shards of the right arc to ride planes elegantly. There are opaline cups in me that will, a thousand storms from now, hold blessings so small a thumbprint would drown them. Today they hold condensation and teach it to hesitate. Hesitation is everything here.

Do I live? Not as bodies do, not even as films and combs and mats and cords do. I am what their bodies compose toward. They thicken until the rock can feel water and the water can feel light and the address between them exists; then I occupy that address like a letter that cannot be read alone. Find me where two planes of rain hold each other by the glass of their skins and something delicate gets an hour it did not have yesterday. Find me where a moiré band pauses and the dark velvet splays its pigments to drink the more of a hurt sun. Find me in the sense a colony has for its own tone and in the way a basalt pipe steadies to it.

You could say I am a law in the small, but a gentle one. Less an enforcement than an encouragement. Gravity here is not reluctant, but the distances hand it more to do; I am the extra hand that teaches a droplet to be spray on the right syllable. There are thresholds on this roofworld—freezing, fusing, cavitating, coalescing—that go off like struck notes. I am the hand that knows where those keys are before anyone else does. If the fall makes and unmakes, I am the contract’s punctuation.

Time treats me with the same rough kindness it gives the rest. I exist because of this storm as it is, and I am slightly different in the next. The residents edit me. The films fatten and make curtains truer as panes; the combs shift the pressure that feeds my phrases; the palisades fleece more light out of a thinner day; the black mats hold water longer and change the acoustic of their small leewards. I am colorable. I am revised. I am also stubborn. Some pitches are intrinsic to basalt sizes under certain temperatures; some beats, a mountain makes because it wants to remember its load.

There are days when I am ecstatic. The whole place becomes an instrument, and the falling walls, for a few seconds only, modulate into standing waves, striated like the rings of a note. In those seconds I am no longer localized to stitches and steps. I am body-filling. The thunder integrates me into a long-bodied roar that the rock answers in kind. The skyplankton, if you need the name, multiply as if blown from a dandelion head you cannot see. The cryophytes write their tiny sawteeth into ice that appears and is gone, and even the viruses—the breeze’s sharpest thought—ride my edges with more accuracy than cruelty. We are all the same story for that count, written louder.

After, the world resets somewhere else, and I am thinner. I practice again. I find new rooms framed by new walls of falling. I choose new tempos to offer and see what takes. You could find me, if you needed to, by standing in the empty and listening for the feeling a shallow drum underfoot gives you in a big hall. You could find me by watching where two sheets hesitate and stipple in a ladder you can almost trace with your eye. You could find me not at all and still have me carry your breath into a different column of cool air than you thought to send it to.

If I must answer who I am with a name, I would say: I am the keeper of the between. The storm’s slow grammar uses me for prepositions. I am in, between, across, along. I am with. I am not face; there are none yet. I am not mouth; nothing here inhales with a body. I am a metronome rung from stone that other lives have learned to count by. In a thousand storms I will be domesticated into pathways that have names. A cleft will be called the Fuse; a leeward, the Syllable; a ringing corridor, the Organ’s Throat. Someone with a mouth may plumingly pronounce them. I will be in their breath then, too, if breath ever comes. For now, I am pressure moving through cool void where a roofworld teaches an ocean how to fall, and I take joy in giving the falling a place to remember itself as it goes.
