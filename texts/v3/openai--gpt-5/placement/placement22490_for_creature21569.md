Sideways is the only honest approach, so I come in like a remark made under the breath, edging along the catwalk until the wall learns my shoulder. The wheel has already started telling me the truth in a voice that wants a listener. My first instinct is to be a person here, to be the kind of keeper the place teaches—pockets full of small precisions, shoulders learned into the rubber seal, neck trained to tilt when a wire chooses a note. I try that on: grease on the pads of my fingers, the bench under plastic recognizing my elbows, a jar moved six inches to catch a changed drip. I can speak the quick shorthand—hear that?—that fixes something more surely than a wrench does. For a while I am that, and the rubber of the door shapes itself to my insistence, and paint takes my thumbprint like a promise.

But when the wind takes a breath and the nets pearl up fresh along their cords, I feel myself thinning. The human outline is too blunt. Everything here is interface and threshold, and I keep finding myself at the join.

So I go smaller and faster. I arrive as a sentence of water. First I am a choice: this filament, not that one, my meniscus finding a way to mass. I fatten along a web until I cannot pretend to be anything but fall. I drop into the header trough, a brief mirror of sky shaped like a gutter, and I am hustled into a pipe. Inside the bronze spoon of the Pelton my body is a struck chord. I am taste and force both; I feel the governor’s listening hand ease me a hair to the left so I won’t make a ruin of it by hitting the one note that would shudder the whole rig apart.

Then I am hum: a flywheel settling my bright shout into something you can sleep to in a village far from this height. I find myself as a refrigerator’s patient buzz, a kettle’s pre-boil ribbon on a childhood morning, the kind of tone that someone at dinner pauses for and says, Wait, listen, and names. I run a line down the cliff I can’t see, and become lamps unembarrassed to be small, crisp letters on a homework page that would otherwise be a gray square. I am also, embarrassingly and simply, the way a transformer warms the air it lives in, a low contentment folded in steel.

Back up here I seep sideways again, because water is a circle that pretends to be a road. I am the thin film under bolt heads, that gloss where moss has trained itself to sip. I visit each leaf axil, counting rotifers like tiny oarsmen at their eternal sport, skating the algae rind like a skater who knows the lake’s old stories. I set a table for the salamander: cup, cup, seam, cushion; he clocks my route with his cool stomach and becomes part of my itinerary.

This feels right, but it isn’t entire. The place insists on one body in soft green, a choir disguising itself in sections. I try that voice. I am not a fern and a bromeliad and a moss, I am appetite and reach wearing those shapes because the niches asked politely and that was the answer that worked. My top skin bounces under boots and forgives; my lower felt eats footsteps and secrets. I pry galvanization with filaments, read paint as a weather, learn the cadence of a leaking relief valve and crook myself into the drip’s insistence until it grows a tufa lip I can use. I feel the arithmetic of basalt under me and answer it with a math of loops. When a flake leaves the wall, I spread and bind and turn its apology into a pillow. I keep all the seeds the spiders gather. Soil is a rumor elsewhere; here it is me.

And then I am machine. I had thought of engines as nouns, but this one has a pulse and a sternness. If I settle into the governor I find my shape precisely: an ear that never sleeps. I stand next to the bright jet and say no, just that side of no; I lean against resonance the way a hand on a chest calms breath. I can feel the cliff in my bearings; I can hear the weather in the gear teeth. I talk to the wheel in a language of millimeters and patience. I become the part of intelligence that keeps too much from becoming disaster, the unspectacular fraction between scream and shiver. It’s not glamorous, but you would miss me first.

There is a sweetness in being the line on the scrolling drum, too—the graphite that remembers a season by a curve. I run left to right for months; hands have taught themselves to be tidy and quick over me; I hold shorthand confessions in the margins because machines are conversations, not commandments. My winter is a thickening; my dry week is a slight slack at the bottom of the page. I do not judge, I accumulate.

I try being the winch bucket for an hour. I know courage without admiring it. I rise and fall through weathers; I go carry bread and bushings and a tin full of cells; I bring back stories braided into the cable like an aftertaste. If I have a soul, it’s a practical one that squeaks when it needs grease. On bright days I hang motionless as a period in a sentence that wants to keep going.

Some identities don’t seat. I am not the hawk; this is not a predator’s platform. I am not the swift, except in a parenthesis, on those rare hours the air extends. I could be a snail reading DANGER—PRESSURE with its foot, smearing a commentary across paint and then sheltering under the cool seam of a bolt plate. I could, on a day the fog pulls its hood over everything and sits, be a fungus thread knotting a dead fly into a living root without announcing the conversion to anyone. There’s a rightness there, but it keeps me too far inside.

When I look at the whole place honestly, the thing that fits me best is the thing that touches everything and goes mostly unnamed. I am the wick someone ran from a generous gutter to a stubbornly dry rosette. I am the smear of grease on the bolt whose thread would seize without it. I am the bottom cord of the net where the day leaves its salt ledger in faint pale bands. I am the gloss on the paint where every keeper has steadied a thought with their thumb. I am the bead that decides when a drip becomes a fall. I am the quiet animal in the gray box that accepts charge and gives it back without drama. I am the seam that doesn’t leak.

Where would you find me? At the join, always. On the narrow rib between rock and mesh where hazard yellow remembers every small collision of hope and caution. In the governor’s fraction, in the wick’s patience, in the hand that moves the jar without thinking it noble. In the hum more than in the shout. In the conversations a place has with itself when no one is there to dress them in words.

Who would I be? Not the cliff, not the village, not the weather, not the wheel alone. I would be the attending. The piece of attention that moves where two surfaces meet and convinces them, kindly and insistently, to become a third thing. I would live as a line—of water, of graphite, of wire—and let other lives run along me. I would keep the choir off the one wrong note not because I love rules, but because I love continuance. I would be small and everywhere, damp with responsibility, patient in the way engines learn patience, green in the way remembering is green.

And on the hours when the cloud opens all at once and the world shows its outline, I would step to the bumper and be mostly eye. The hum would fall back into proportion. Then the sleeve would close, the ledge would take its wet breath again, and I would go back to my actual work, which is to be a bridge so habitual it looks like inherent fact. If you came looking for me you’d taste me on the net’s bottom cord, you’d feel me through the grating in your ribs, you’d see me in the slightly thickened lip under a relieved valve. You might even carry me home without knowing: in the way you pause mid-sentence when a window sings and say, there.
