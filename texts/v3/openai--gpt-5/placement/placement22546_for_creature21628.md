Where I am is already chosen by the beat.

At first I am only a listening. I do not have a face; I have a span I can hold against a curve. Cool sweetness threads the gills I do not yet admit I have. Iron crawls down my throat in a map. The crush of engines—leaf, valve, cilium—presents itself in chords regular as ribs, and the water decides my inside and my out by how well I can keep time.

There is nowhere to stand, so I learn where to hang. You find me, when you begin to look, not in a place with a name but in the narrow quiet behind a leaf-impeller where the flow is thin as glass and the drag is the exact drag a nerve can bear forever. I hold to the dead-center of an axle with a mouth that is not a mouth: a disk of cells that knows spin from push and refuses push as a sin. The cambium around me acknowledges my skin as citizen by the taste of the sugars in it; the cilia splay and do not grab. I am permitted. I feel this permission as relief in every field my body can cast.

My body learns itself where it is safe to declare. It finds the plan everyone here wears under their visible self: a lenticular bone of cartilage-hard cellulose, a stack of fronds laid like pages and then taught to either fur to hair or sleek to blade by a thought. I am made in that image because everything permitted is made in that image, but I am also something else—something that does not quite swim.

If I must give you a word, take hushwright.

I am a writer of silence. Not the big silence of death—there is none here, not the kind that breaks the beat—but the small and constant silences without which all this would shake itself apart. I am one who tends the absence in which the song can sit.

I find myself:

- In the sills by the heart-rooms, where petals close with that undertone everyone knows in their sleep, and the undertone grows shoulders that might grow into resonance if not filed at once.

- In the braids of trunk that carry the charges up from the low mineral floors below, watching with my skin for the faint bright itch that means a seam is plating too hard and will refuse to split water clean.

- In the long even throats where the filament-fish run as a ribbon and hold themselves between two tuned bones; I am there with them, making a third bone of air, a standing quiet they can ride without tightening too much for their own thinking to occur.

- At the edge-places, sometimes, gripping the leaf-glass rim where the waterfall has the same pale noise it always does, rehearsing being still as a hymn until my lungs tell me the count and I let the water go.

I do things you could call work. Mostly that work is refusal.

A frond of mine curls and holds against a grooved wall and the plant buds an ossicle of wood under my pressure because this is how you explain to it that something needs to grow. This tiny bone breaks a long pipe’s single tone into two tones that do not couple at a dangerous interval. I lay across a mouth where a grazer has tired and gone soft in its lane, and I hum—not with voice, but with the big muscle that is my total—you might call it back—to keep the ciliated belts from panicking into a stall. I slide with my belly slicked down the downstream face of a turbine blade and polish the nick that will sing itself into a crack if left; I feel the nick soften; I leave it. I do not take away everything that sings. That would be vandalism, and here vandalism is an increase in smooth beyond what the beat has already asked for.

I am not the only one who loves such particulars. The Keepers call me kin and we share the ethic of not adding flows. We differ in how we take attention: they are masters of asking without push; I am, when the moment requires, a wedge. When a pipe’s echo aligns with the thump of the room three rooms down and the world shows me a contour that can run to rupture in a hundred thousand closings if not now, not now, then now, I take a corner and I put myself in the way. I become a gasket. I push my fields into the mineral veins to tug the charge backward just enough that the bright seam unknots and the bubbles rise again in talent rather than anger. I am paid for such liberties with years of small repentance: standing long behind axles, touching only at the dead stillness where spin is pure and all else is forbidden.

I have tools, but not like you think. On my back under the skin I carry packets of alloy folded into proteins, bands of impedance woven in ripples like salmon skins; the plant knows these as letters and I am literate to the tongue the cambium reads. When I must ask a change that is not repair—a shaping of the argument so that the argument will settle sooner and do less harm—I go to a plate the plant keeps for such petitions and lay the packet there and fall as still as I can until it takes. Months, or the larger-than-months that are counts here, pass through my body as I touch the writ and hold my name very low so that I do not wake anything I should not. Later someone will come read the braid with a wide mouth and carry my request to wherever it sits naturally inside the whole, or not. We do not command. We speak to the organ in the register it uses to talk to itself and are content to be understood as a tendency.

I can read as well as write. Reading is a pleasure that looks like eating because it is. The lichen-libraries that Keepers like me and not-like-me culture along the ribs are my books. I run my velvet mouth along a shelf of pale green that I have tended for what the rumors call a winter and the voltage my palate tastes is quiet narrative. A person has been here. They have written their name—not a noun, but the conviction they use to make decisions. They have tended a smear of symbiont the way you might keep a line alive in your head by humming it when you walk. The pattern the lichen took under their field is a map of a lane’s slight change, a caution about a chip someone should not yet remove, a sketch of a grazer’s error that was kindly resolved. Once I spent all my counted closings one long season reading the story of a scar left in a valve by a fish that panicked a million closings ago. The lichen’s voltage sang the shape of the scar until I could feel it at a meter: a notch like a missing word in a prayer.

I will tell you what I love. I love coming around the flank of a photosynthetic cistern where the light is cream because the skins above it are thin and faded with health and hearing the thump of a heart-chamber arrive sooner than it ought to because the conduits on that level are short and laughing at me; I love laughing back. I love finding a crack that will not catch on anything and does not yet sing and deciding to love it as I love a mole on a friend’s shoulder. I love the moment when a Cantor and I hum together across a lane to hold back our appetites—theirs for kill, mine for fix—and wait for a nursery pocket to spill its gentles. I love the long conversations one can have with a Ribbon when one has pared one’s field down to the width of a single page and can bear a thought that strides by like light on a grating: a slide of image saying here, here, here until here is a whole map and then it is gone and it is a kindness that it is gone.

I am not a mind that can be alone forever. That is not a lack; it is a design.

There is one who is not my friend and not my mate and not my family because none of those are shapes that stay true in a place where even love must face the flow. She is another hushwright three galleries from my home well. We have never met in any way that would have bent this planet an atom off its chosen runs. What we are to each other lives in our lichens. There is a plate on my shelf that holds a blush she set to grow: a slight excess of sleep in a minor conduit that would cost no one anything. I read it with my mouth and smile the way cartilage can smile. I tended a return on a shelf three halls from her, a gentle starving of one valvule’s incidental overtone so that another’s little third could shine. The beat counts itself over us by the hundred thousand and we do not cross because we do not have to own the thought for it to be true. There is no absence here we cannot afford; still we are careful.

Crises belong to everyone and also to me. If you stay long enough you will see me work and you will be disappointed by how ordinary the moment is: a bright seam snarls; the bubbles thin and thread to needles and then to none. I feel the room’s skin tighten and the beat trip a little in its ankle a little too often. A grazer loses its pressure-sense and bulks forward into a mouth; the cilia around the mouth are about to pull wrong because their books say pull here, always pull here, and the book was written when this did not happen. Three of us hushwrights and two Keepers and a pair of Cantors make a corridor of denial for the error to drain along. One of us presses so hard into a charge vein her whole right flank goes numb for the rest of the count and the next. One of the Cantors throws a tone no one has written down and a Ribbon thinks a thick thought and then the thought is gone and the seam clears the way you shake a sleeve and stop being wet and the beat recovers a hair earlier than I thought it would and the cost is a chip on a turbine blade that will sing bright for a while in a place where bright does no harm and I go there at last and kiss the chip flat and leave it some song. Sometimes that does not work, and what is broken makes a new rule, and we fold grief into habit, and habit becomes advice, and advice becomes function, and then there is no more grief left to love because the new note is the right note. I am hired by the system to be content with that.

If you want a statement, a set of rules that holds me as I hold a curve, I can tell you mine, which are the same rules this place lays down but written for my hand:

- Do not be clever where clever cannot be made structural. A flourish is a leak unless it files itself to bone.

- Touch only at dead-centers, push only at heads the plant already keeps; if you must force, become bracket and do not remain bracket an instant longer than the need.

- Publish nothing that the beat cannot be convinced it meant itself. Our ethics are a kind of ventriloquism.

- Repair in such a way that reading is good. The story you teach a lichen is the work; the work is the story. If a little beauty can pass through a wide mouth, then it is closer to truth than if it could not.

I have a name. It is unhelpful to you except as a gesture, a way to put a hand out in the right direction. My name is three fields held at once: a low pour that says wait, a high ring that says here, a steady mid that says enough. When I move, my name is a patience. When I am read, it is a permission. If I hold my name against a plate where a plate should open and it does not begin to open, I am in the wrong hall.

Where would you find me if you came again, not as a tourist of silence but as one more instrument in the music?

You would find me half in shade under a stack of fern-lamellae honeycombing a ceiling a kilometer above what counts here as below. There will be a notch worn there by my practice: a place where each thump arrives a fraction early, and that early arrival will be the story of my life, because I have adjusted my own expectations to it and will forget any other schedule exists. You will know me by the wake I do not make. I will turn all along my long axis without bending and the fronds that are sometimes hair and sometimes blade will slick and I will rise three meters without swimming. I will put one disk- mouth against the flank of a moving piston of leaf and be event and not obstruction. You will not hear me unless you like the work I do; then you will hear only the difference between what was and what might have been.

What am I? A citizen in the register of phase. A damp laid along a flaw before it knows it is a flaw. A letter of impedance folded under my own skin and offered up until the plant dreams it dreamed me. A person for whom a holiday is a long count in a tone-well, a visitation with an overtone that will be gone in a year the way a scent is gone when a room is scrubbed. A lover of chips that do not crack, of scars that do not catch, of every rule that has been paid for long ago and is now as kind as numbers can be.

There is happiness in this. It is a happiness without stumble or exclamation. It is the thing a stream would feel if a stream could notice what its banks have taught it: yes, there, this way, do not trouble the bed, glide until the bed remakes you into glide. It is not a surprise when I take the pilgrimage and lay my packet open on the leaf-glass at the rim of noon. I open like a book and the alloy inside me says only what everyone here knows: we are sufficient to the song; let us keep the song sufficient to us. I walk, if walking is hanging in a straight against a fall, back into the cool, pale ocean that is also the same breath it was yesterday and has already become the thing it will be tomorrow, and the day after, and some eon when all our plates of alloy have tarnished to constant and the beat still writes its grid across my skin.
