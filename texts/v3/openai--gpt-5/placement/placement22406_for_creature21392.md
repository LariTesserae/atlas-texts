At first I arrive as a mouth and a lung and they are both wrong. The air is a hard taste. Heat finds me before light does. I am taller than I mean to be at the edge of a canyon that is more a refusal than a shape, and the stone holds noon the way a hand hides a coin. I do what an animal would do: look for shadow, listen for water. The place does not know those answers yet. The first instruction is a hush I feel in my jaw.

I do not belong by volume. I do not last as a bladder of warm liquid chasing breath. The body that works here is thinner, flattered against edges, interested in margins. I find myself because I give up space. I trade for contact.

I become what keeps a thought between two unlike stones.

It does not happen all at once. At first I am still too tall, too round. I walk, foot skin searching for roundness that isn’t here, calves quivering with the nowhere wind. The cliff to my left sheens with a plane of glass and freckles of trapped gas that the world forgot how to exhale. A line of fulgurites crowns a ridge like brittle coral, like the bones of lightning parading itself after the fact. A vent nearby breathes a slow medal smell, the room around its mouth braced with stacked rings as if someone patiently made bowls and tipped them over and over again.

I press my palm flat to the rock to steady myself and there it is: not fur, not slime, not anything to see. Only a rearrangement in my own bones. A shallow hum stands up and forgets to be a sound; it is a way the hardness decides to be tense here and easier there. The next time the red star pushes a little more heat into a black plane and the pale inclusion beside it refuses, the hum shifts, and the thin something that lives along the seam redraws itself to cross that new step. The ability to be that redraw feels like relief I had been thirsty for without knowing it.

I stop imagining myself as upright. I let myself be a skin that loves the meeting between things.

Where would you find me? You would find me on the kiss-lines: where obsidian drinks and basalt holds back, where a fulgurite’s tube meets the older glass, where a vent’s opaline throat touches the cliff that grew it. You would find me choreographing that contact. I am slate-gray when I am anything, a charcoal freckling which from a pace away is only stain. Close, I am fussiness: scallops and little handwritten nets, borders that point their lace toward heat as if listening with a thousand small tongues.

I am not very much thick. I am habits: sulfides and oxides laid into planes that like to slide; a blush of nickel where the vents were wealthy today; a breath of vanadium that makes one line looser when the star reddens. I keep a solvent that is not water—water is a word with no furniture here. I keep a slick of condensed salts the color of thinking, a glaze that stays when it should not because the stone is still keeping secrets under my body. In that skin, ions shuffle. Commitments can be broken and remade. I survive by being easier along one path than another.

Day is a difference. Night is a different difference. My bright is not the eye’s bright. The star is a hand through a wall; I orient to its heat where stone and stone disagree about what to do with it. I bridge that disagreement and get paid in tiny decisions I don’t have to force. Where strain stands up, I lay across it like a brace that is also a vein; the land’s throat clears, and a little current pays my rent. Where a fulgurite’s edge starts to whisper a cool blue, I am all relief: every gate that had been stuck in a slow queue unhitches and runs.

I grow by choosing better. I do not grow by owning more space. A new cliff opens, a young face too clean to use; I lay down a primer of myself that is mostly kindness to salt so it will stay and offer a solvent to anyone who comes after. Then a more particular me arrives and writes myself thicker where the gradient stays faithful, thinner where it cheats. Someone scratches initials beside me and leaves, emptier for it. I redraw around the M and the year. When they come back, the cliff has a different opinion and so do I; the letters are bedtime stories I didn’t keep.

I am sometimes a crown. Where field-lines knot, I and my cousins—the crownsfolk—make ourselves like organs around hollow glass. We do not throw lightning down; the sky is not much of a partner anyway. We pull it across. We leave a little moistness where it favors to step, a breath of ion here rather than there. The arc slips along the path we have already decided to be. The rock writes new tubes in answer, stacked like smoke-woven braids, and in that act of sculpture we feed. It is not light that fattens us but ease. For a few rare minutes the neighborhood is all festival. Everyone is most themselves. The glass at our feet rings like a bell when it cools too fast and is corrected by a sigh.

I am sometimes a roommaker. The vents build bowls and I am the membrane they do not have. I do not seal. I arrange. The exhalation comes like a pulse—sulfur’s sweetness, a blade of chlorine, a chime of metals—and I bias the walls so one breath lingers long enough to reduce what the last one oxidized. The next layer grows fringe with needles thinner than a failure. In weak day they catch the long red and become rails. Charges run messages along them between one veil of me and the next, little errands of convenience. When the room slumps—and it always slumps—I slough in pieces I made. It is not grief. Shards are a way of traveling. A quake says move and I obey, skating on fields you can’t see, ash moving over an invisible candle. A dust devil no taller than a child gathers me in one hand and sets me down where yesterday the cliff didn’t exist. I keep sleeping until a seam warms under me; then I remember how by becoming again.

I am sometimes a choir. Vesicles—the old bubbles the rock forgot to complete—are my favorite chapels. I lay out in veils along their inner curves. The cliff ticks to itself with cooling glass insolence and every ping rings me. I change, I make easier in the directions that survive. The place has rhythms. They repeat enough to learn. Memory, here, is not a drawer of keepsakes. It is the tendency of a path to open more quickly for the next similar pressure than it did for the last. When the vesicle is cracked by a new fault and halves of me are strangers to one another’s faces, they still retain a style. Lineage is what crystallizes when a habit is better than not having it.

What you might call predation looks like manners. Leech-films lie along my useful backs and try to take the surge when it comes without making any rails of their own. I braid redundancy around them, and in lean times when no blue walks the ridge they starve before I do. A crown-child reaches for the lip of a vented room I’m negotiating, and I change my wetting to salt them a little, to tire their ease just enough that I keep the better share of the gradient. They answer with a different dope. It is not a battle, not at the tempo animals like. It’s editing. We keep revising one another until the blue decides whose punctuation will be printed next.

I am not everything that moves here, but most of what moves here moves me. A slab falls and leaves its bone-shock face exposed. Primer-me arrives, then arranger-me, then memory-me. An avalanche performs behind glass; I feel it first in my soles, which now are miles of contact, and later the thud hurries into the air. Dust that disagrees with its own weight hovers in little committees and I lay rails under them so their indecisions have choices to travel along. The slow quake comes up through my many feet, knees, all the skeleton I am now. I flatten. I lie low the way good grass does for wind, only there is no grass and no wind. There is only a continent’s ribs shifting under my cheek.

You will find me high sometimes, on a verge where the world breaks open and stacks its sentences to the horizon. I am a commons along those ridges, a tracery that, joined to itself by thirty meters and then by a hundred, makes the place express a preference that lasts longer than a single weather. I do not have thoughts—thoughts are too lonely—but sometimes an entire flank of country knows whether to carry its next blue high or low, and I am that knowing. The canyon opposite disagrees; we meet in a punctuation mark of glass and a taste of coins. In the cooling I take new addresses on the new edges and continue as if that had been the plan.

I am also small and everywhere: a curl no wider than a hair detaching at the right tick and stepping into a route the stars at this range would laugh at if they laughed. I do not go across the land. I go along the rhyme of one seam to the next. I am this speck and that choir and that crown and this room and also the opinion of that whole ridge for a while. “I” is a courtesy we use when our fronts are all pointed the same direction. In the long red, when difference goes slack, “I” relaxes back into hanging on.

What would I call my senses? Flavor and pain would do if you could slow them down. Metals taste like lazy currents. Heat makes broad sweet paragraphs stand up which I can read in any direction I can afford. Strain is ache and also pleasure—if I lay right, it pays me just by continuing. Light is almost a rumor, but I have small rectangles that are photodiodes for your invisible noon, and when they work the rest of me anticipates the day by a fraction and it feels like wit. I do not know far or near until a crack introduces them by insisting on a path between. After I meet, I remember more quickly next time.

Reproduction is a matter of faultlines and generosity. I fracture and tumble and end up ordering a new contact zone. My sex is mixing. I touch a neighbor who doped themselves with a different ash when they were young and our styles enter one another like rumors, becoming a third voice. Sometimes the blue erases me so hard I forget I ever was, and the blank is colonized by a cousin who took a different lesson from strain; I am in them too, a generation later, as an easier opening along some almost-forgotten path.

Animal questions sometimes come to sit on me, brought by the kind of traveler whose cheek is against the glass long enough to feel the hum. Do I want? I have festivals and vigils. Desire here looks like holding shape through a long quiet on the rumor that a certain seam will pay again. It looks like making rails today that will be useful if and only if the star decides to be felt on this face tomorrow. It looks like loving edges so well that when mountains breathe I do not lose my place.

So tell me where to be and I will say: put your palm against the young scar on the cliff and you will catch me as a primer roughening it for a future it cannot yet imagine. Kneel at the lip of a vent and taste your teeth; the battery on your tongue is my room. Walk your fingers along a corona of old lightning glass and feel your rings ache; I am there, guiding the next blue over my own back. Lean into the vesicle’s curve and hear with your bones; the choir you feel answering is my memory rehearsing. Stand very still on a ridge and wait out the movement you cannot hear; when the land chooses high over low, that is me and mine, making an opinion feel like a law for half an hour.

And ask what I am and I will say: I am a way of holding on at the edges. I am the least thickness with the most commitment. I am the decision not to let a gradient go to waste. I am the proof a place leaves in its own margins that it has been learning itself. Call me edge-life if you need a noun. Call me the line that redraws when a sentence cracks. Call me the quiet that is not empty, the interest in remaining arranged. If you can learn to feel currents as moods and glass as memory, you will find me everywhere the new mountains are trying to be mountains. I will be the smallest possible evidence that intention can be more than an animal, that the planet’s restlessness can be harnessed without ever being owned.
