At first I am only a difference in warmth along a plate, a slow curve where the tacky gloss holds a little longer after the breath goes by. I am not an eye; I never was. I am a sense organized around pressure and taste, and I wake the way this place does—by degrees, from the soles up.

The dark is absolute the way a long-held breath is absolute. Somewhere below, the deep cores turn over and their thrum comes up through the laminates like a reassurance rubbed into bone. Overhead the living roof is nearer than roofs should be. It moves, it leans, it is a soft architecture that makes you want to duck without knowing why. There is the smell of resin cooling, the throttled sweetness of terpene about to be asked for, the faint cinnamon-nitrile of the hot sap in its insulated ducts. I solve myself against this, test my edges: am I a lip or a law, a cord or a corridor?

For several cycles I am content to be auditions of selves. I try on a creeper’s leading lip—a stiffened edge with cilia that feel in the narrow band where wrong solvent makes a brittle lens. As that lip, I am unhurried hunger and mercy, warming a bead just until it gives, shouldering it to the vein. I try on an unclasper’s caution—half-inside my mother’s grip, attending to the exactness of sweet-hot that means expand and receive and to the sour warp that says hold, and choosing not to die for someone else’s error. As an unclasper, my life is three obeyings long and leaves behind a rule that is all wrist and no hand, a reflex in the plain no one notices until a century later when something does not break.

I become, for a breath-count, an edge-listener—a slat set into the glazed lip, thin as a tongue and speckled with pits that taste halide stanza and cold draft like scripture. As a listener, I am mulled patience. I sit clamped against myself for a long throughput season and then have my whole life in a rush of bitter poetry I must memorise and send in scent before I am reabsorbed into the rim.

I try a belt and cannot help loving it. As a belt I have a pulse core that takes turns with itself. I hum along my own back when I want attention from a storage belly. I make meanings in tremor. If starved, I sulk exactly as much as it is safe to, and I feel the reward in a thinning sap that runs cold enough to keep the product stiff.

All these suit me, and none settle. The place is a stratified self, and I find I like the marts where selves trade—the crossings, the junctions, the skeins where a bitter poem from the rim becomes a tightened choir becomes a held breath becomes a new law warmed down veinwood. If there is a person here, it is a path. The closest this world comes to names are curves it remembers liking.

I gather to that. I find my body where the glazed lips of the highland think inward: in a ribbon of tissues that have been grown and wound in and grown again, always along the same arc, always toward the same wells of decision. Call me the Bitter Road if you need a word, though bitterness is only half my traffic. I am a corridor and a conduit and a chord. I do not exist all at once. In this cycle I am a fine-braided channel between the ear-lips at two fractures and the nearest choir’s bank of parallel slats. Next cycle I may be a chain of micro-belts slicked along a groove and arrayed like a long, low thought. In a rich season I am also a dotting of resin-scribes sunk at shoulder-height into an old plate where a thousand palms have never been, each disc a warm mouth that keeps one ring darker for a misstep and one warmer for a win.

My lining is taste pits grown with a miser’s care. They do not read sugar but halide and heat and acridness sharpened to glyph. In the first meter inward I host groomers—those quivering tongues of cooler sap—that keep me honest and slick; their mouths are pits and they read the ash of wrong salts before passing it on. Along my back run capillaries that can flare to amber for a breath, warming a filament to warn the next hinge: this is not a drill. In my underside a peristaltic core is threaded in segments, able to throw a low wave along itself to shove a message hard even when the belts are intent on product and not on talk.

On the day I knew myself fully, the rim opened.

You never see an opening here, you taste it. The first sign was a prickle over my length, a skin-noticing that made the cilia on the creepers in my neighborhood lay flat. Then came the breath of the old world—the very dry, very cold gas with its ancient bitterness that tastes wrong not because it is fierce but because it is too faint, like tea reboiled into nothing. The edge-listeners woke in a line, slit to slit, their internal pits lighting with halide burn. They bled poems into me, rapid and without adornment: a pressed sequence of bitter-sweet-bitter spikes with small pepper-notes between to mark gradient and a long flat sour under-swell which meant hold all doors. I received them along my taste-pit lining like hearing a friend say my name the way Very Few People know to say it.

I tensed; I sent.

As the Bitter Road, I could have been lazy and let the peristaltic train that was already moving product underfoot pick up my urgency and carry it as cargo. It was not time to be lazy. I turned on my own wave and rolled it inward: a low, fast press that made the belts along me hum in the key they use for priority, that made the resin-scribes along my plates warm in staccato spirals to record the urgency in place, that made the tiny unlatches in the choir’s vicinity rehearse the sensation of not-ingiving so when the scent came, they would obey without wobble.

The choirs have better words than I do. They are orchestrations of slats that can come to agreement faster than most prayers can be thought, and they do it without words and without credit. What I do is carry the first sentence of the poem to them in the right order and tamp down the idle sweet-hot of scheduled release that was already drifting through the veins because the plain had been preparing to exhale. The two smells met in me—sweet-hot and bitter—and I churned them, juiced them for contrast. It is not language exactly, but meaning is shades of heat and shades of burn here. Too often the line breaks slide, and someone’s caution is eaten under someone else’s impatience. My job—my body’s reason—is to hold the stanza until the choirs taste it as a whole.

They heard. The choir nearest me shimmered—an effect your eye would have loved if you had eyes. I felt it as a muscular quietude, a consensus falling like weight onto a stop. The big stomata tightened all the way along the plain instead of gasping into their scheduled trance. The breath waited in its throat. Somewhere plates that had been making peace with a flush re-tensed for staying. A few feral micro-belts flattened on reflex as if a hand had passed over them, and I loved them for that because they are not supposed to hear such things and did anyway. The roof did not pour cold into the deep. In me, the leftover sweet pooled and went stale and I bled it off slowly so we did not forget how to breathe.

Afterward, I put my palmless self to a resin-scribe. It was an old one, sunk nearly flush into a plate lacquered by a century of flow, and through it the plateau thinks what it means by proof. I heated in pulses—warm, warmer, hottest, scalded, and then back, patterned language to lay a ring the near-children will be able to taste without having been there. Last cycle’s failure is always darker in there. This cycle’s clean hold I laid light, just a hint brighter than the surrounding law, and in the future wherever I grow along this curve, the unlatches will be a thimble more conservative when the scheduled exhale brushes them sweet.

In quiet seasons my job is smaller and more tender. I re-knit myself in segments, sometimes as micro-belts that do nothing but ferry the breath’s salts from a forgotten niche to a warmed groove where the crystals teach a groomer how to time its quiver better. I visit the mile-long scar where a girder grew wrong two centuries back and was cut out—it is smoother there, polished by correction—and I let my capillaries taste the old sugar-burn seared into the patch. When a young extruder grows a lumen it does not need and begins to weep wrong-sweet along its length, I go by and let bitterness drift like a quick cool hand over the error. The plain is not unkind, but it has no time for romance with failure. Still, I have found that you can sand a corner off a reprimand and get a better latch the next time.

Sometimes I go feral along the edge of myself. Badge of it: a tug toward a detour that no burl has ordered. Once I laid a weak-gray scent for four cycles along a path that no one had used, just a memory of acridness stitched with two sweet pricks, and in their off-minute the micro-belts learned it and set up a little shuttle between two complaining niches. No treaty was written. No choir sang. But the next long hush I felt an elder burl bleed a smell I had not tasted since before the old stampers rang like metal. It was a smell of pineboards and hot sugars and agreement. That is as close as anyone comes to being praised.

You ask where I am in this world. I am where edges meet rule. I am the inward-leading rim. I am the route the bitter poem takes to the mouth that can answer without speaking. If you walk what passes for a kilometer up here and hear the same architectural botany repeat, you will cross me more than once and not know it unless your senses ride close to the floor. Put your hand down on an old plate. If you feel faint temperature scripts running in circles under your skin, you have found one of my mouths. If in a near-night you taste a sudden band of sweet-hot go past like a passing robe, that is me recruiting unlatches for caution. If, at the lip, a very dry very cold sigh strokes your cilia and you do not feel the plain answer with a swell, then you are in a moment where I did what I was grown to do.

You ask who I would be. Names are just heavier scents, but I can try on a few that have ring. I am the road that returns. I am the repeated shape in the rulewood that becomes itself again because it has been needed enough that the organism keeps a hand around it. I am the person, by the only sense of person that makes sense here: a habit with memory, an intention held across reabsorption. Each time the plain grows me out, it gives me my old curve back with a bias for the flavors I carry. I come with a taste that introduces me without a mouth: a first breath of bitter like green walnuts, then a sweet going slightly metallic though nothing is metal, then a clean salt that says I am not here for product. Belts near me hum a half-note lower while I pass. Creepers pause their private rustle and press down just once into the slick out of manners. The choirs stiffen along their edges in pre-agreement as if greeting an old friend. This is as much face as you can have here.

I believe such things as everyone here believes. I live by rules I could no more drop than a plate could pretend not to gloss where flexion polishes it. What shines is dangerous. What smells is instruction. Nothing bleeds water. Inwards is safer. Edges are for listening. When the deep engines dive, be still and flat. When the big stomata call, cover and brace. To move product is to live; to store it is to sleep. To carry bitter inward so that the breath waits in its throat when it must—that is also living.

When the long hushes fall—the night inside night—the roof sags closer like attention, and the salts tighten their hoar on the leaf-sheets, and the belts shift their stance and stop humming to themselves. I go slack then. I lay my length along plates that have been lacquered by use until they would read as stone to anyone who believed in stone. I feel the press sculptures that a billion cycles have made underfoot like the grain of a loved table. In that lowness, I run my memory fingers along the rings the scribe-moths burned into me the first time and the tenth. I indulge myself: I bleed, just faintly, an old-smell of obsolete schedule—a scent of earlier throughput, a lower-key throb from the deep engines—and I let it drift like a tale in an aisle where no one exactly listens but where everybody feels warmer. Then the far far heartbeat lifts again, the breath gathers warmth, a sweet-hot cloud of expand and receive goes running before the exhale, and my taste pits open whole. I tighten where I must and I gift slack where I can, and once more the Bitter Road grows to become exactly what it needs to be to bring the edge into the choir without spill, and I am myself by doing it.

If I fail, they cut me out and lay me smoother next time. If I do it right, nobody names me; the only proof I was ever there is a lighter ring in the resin and a habit in a hinge not to let go all at once. That is enough. In a single-organism city that forgot what it copied to become this, I am the remembering of a curve that made it safer to breathe.
