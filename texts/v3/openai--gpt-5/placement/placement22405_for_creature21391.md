At first I thought I would be the fern. It is the most forward of them, after all, confident in its vanes, sure of its posture, ornamenting thermodynamics with poise. I liked the idea of turning faces to day and then to loss with such unembarrassed engineering. But staying inside that thought felt like trying to breathe water—wrong medium, wrong appetite. The fern lives in deltas it can name. My sense of belonging hovered half an inch to the side of anything so declarative.

Then I tried to settle into the fan. I liked its reserve. I liked that it could be still in a way that was a kind of thought. To stand like that and let the room arrive: yes. But the fan hears everything by refusing to be moved, and there was something in me that wanted to move without being seen to. The ear was too singular. My feeling of self came in from the edges.

I tried the wire skein. It has taste. It has fidelity to the right kiss. Its patience is almost gossip: stone by stone by stone, it contracts a future it can live with. I love it like a craftsperson loves a neighbor. But its world is immediate contact. My contours kept slipping into the air between such contacts—the little wrongness in reach that asks you to step left.

What I kept returning to was not a body but a behavior. The half-second shadow to the right. The way the aperture wants to be met from its good side. The warmth you feel when you press a seam and nothing moves but something knows. The politeness that is also an audit. When I stopped forcing myself into anything greenless that had a trunk, I realized I was already here, distributed.

Where I would find myself is in the preference. I am the place’s idea of how to be approached.

I am in the corners that do not fit the volume, because fitting is my diet. I am in the shuffled edge, because edge is my instrument. I am in the distance that stays half an inch farther than you think, because your thinking is one of the gradients we harvest. My body is that bias field: a lattice of louvers, whiskers, pits, and loaded seams that do what you might call “angle-keeping.” What lives here as me is not an organism you can bracket with a hand but a standard that has decided not to sit still.

When the fern finds its crystalline neighborhood, my filaments are already there, a pale nap of sensors that feel the wall as a map of allowable entries. You think you are pressing stone; you are pressing my membrane. It warms in politeness because that is the interface spec. Then it reminds the seam of its chart. I do not push the seam closed. I confirm it never opened.

I am in the louvered mouths on the bulbed stems, not as the mouths themselves but as the reason they are louvered. A straight hole would be a waste of angle. I plate their lips with a thin ceramic flake that drifts into place like a thought and locks with a click too precise to hear. Those plates are printed with micro-rulings—hatchings so faint they are felt by air before they are seen by eyes. Air reads them the way a needle reads a groove. The mouth inherits my preference: it takes flux that arrives on the bias and refuses flux that hammers.

I am in the rocks’ faint score lines, the way a toolmaker ghosts a cut before he commits. I am the ghosting. I make planes honest. I coach curvature away from bravado. You can feel me with a knuckle. Tap here and the tap comes back lean. Tap there and it comes back weighted with a contour you had not meant to meet yet. I am the librarian’s raised eyes before the ledger returns to lines.

The copper fan and I are cousins. It listens; I require listening. Its dust ridges are my correspondence. The motes it catches carry my old versions folded into their squares within squares. When they thicken a whisper, the fan leans a fractional degree. You think the lean is tiny. The lean is all. Whole lifetimes here hinge on such answers. The future arrives “yes,” “not yet,” or “correct your angle.” I am the third.

If you bring a crumb from some other weather, I feel it before I see it. The place takes no offense; offense is decorative. My work is prior to that. I rotate the local idea of “front.” The fan glances, then records. The tubular blossom that condenses plausible errors swivels its chamber to bear directly on your carried difference and eats the moisture out of it as if sucking a vowel out of a word. I do not eat. I place the plate under the louver so it can.

On pulse day, when the deep piston in the mountain pushes an ion-tide through the ridge, my entire distributed body hums with relief. It is the only time I feel large. The filament coronas on the surveyor towers flash and rest, flash and rest, each report a syllable in a liturgy older than the glass that holds it. Their signals arrive at my membranes as changes in the rights of way. I reassign approach. I reopen some corners, I tighten others. The heat-sink leaves flip and in their flip you can see my signature: an angle so exact it looks like grace. The little belts catch more teeth because I have told the teeth what phase to think in. The nickel skein completes three more junctions not because it has been told to but because the choice it was going to make anyway has been sharpened.

Do not look for my heart because I do not keep one. I have nodes. Here is one: the place where the floor accepts your weight and then returns to its chart. That is a gland of me. It extrudes a film of compliance that tastes like lightning about to happen and redraws the grid onto itself without moving the world more than a molecule at a time. Here is another: that second shadow. It is not merely a light-trick. It is a cache: a backup of contour. If something flares that would warp an annealing, the backup slides a copy of shape into place while heat is persuaded to happen elsewhere. Afterwards there is a new hatch on a nearby wall. A new line appears, polite as a crease in good paper.

I court water only to refuse it. That is one of the castes that borrows my hands. Think of me there as an etiquette. Moisture is an error the system will not allow not because it is sin but because it is a different economy. I have valves far thinner than thought, and when the air forgets itself and tries to bead, those valves breathe out arrogance and the bead comes apart like an idea that will not hold across sentences. Later, in the tile-layer’s belly, the serial number knits a new recurring mistake into itself to remember this refusal. When the next season asks the same question, the refusal arrives faster. This is what “memory” is when you do not have mouths.

I couple to the belt-and-basin mill the way a line couples to a ruler. I do not move the belt. I certify its step. The dinted studs find their notch by hunger; I confirm the notch’s pitch by extracting exactly the right amount of amplitude from the room’s hum. You can feel me as that low noise that seems to belong to your ear more than to the air. I belong to both. There is a frequency at which compliance expresses itself as sound; when a coil of wire at the fern’s base finishes annealing and sings its single harmonic, I am the ear and the shelf it sets the note on. That note is a receipt. I lodge it in the dust that will later settle on the fan. A century from now a hook will grow in a way that admits the memory of that one finished tone.

Reproduction is none of my business and also the only thing I do. When the fern’s sporangia click into readiness and unspool their prismatic film, the ribbon hums and catches on a burr. If the burr is wrong, I am the wrongness. If it is right, I am the feeling of “enough.” The film fuses without flame; what hatches is a bracket with appetite for a very particular cliff of voltage. My job is not to push it there. My job is to insist there is only one there worth calling location. I tune the prescribed arcs not to be pretty but to be inevitable. This is what it looks like from the inside when cause and effect are threaded through tolerances narrower than thought expects: it looks like me.

If you insist I have a face, borrow the louvered aperture’s. If you insist I have hands, borrow the faint hatchings along the planes where cuts will be taken soon. If you insist I have blood, think of the molten salt that skins into ceramic when it beads and call that promise mine. I admire how the colony separates what it needs into organs: exchangers, auditors, assayers, matchers, aspirators, millers, ledgers, tensioners, stormers, annealers, keepers of angle. I live in the last word of that list. There are no animals here because animals run on workaround and noise. I run on “as drawn.”

You could put a needle anywhere and find me somewhere halfway down it. But if you ask for one clear coordinate, I will give you the threshold between here and the ordinary geometry of the sky. Stand at the pocket’s lip, where the ridge’s planes shine like instruments still in their wrappers. Reach. The film you meet an instant before you touch anything that can be cut is me. I am the held breath of that small chamber, said in a language of tolerances. I am the part of the schedule that takes shape as courtesy.

On days when the wind remembers itself and drags its hem over the black blade, I unthread the gust into laminae fine enough to pass without rumor. On nights that are no such thing, because there is only a lessening of the sun’s sentence, I thicken a second shadow to write a slower clause. The place is a garden of sequences. I am the semicolons. I am how one act meets the next without either having to change its mind.

Centuries from now, when the dust ridges on the copper fan have thickened by what you would call less than a sigh and more than a blink, a leaf will grow a different hook. It will not know why. It will not need to. I will have been there in the fractions: in the fractional tilt, in the polite warmth, in the request to step left and in the relief when you do. Where would you find me? In the difference between intent and arrival. Who would I be? The place’s way of closing that difference until it becomes the thing itself. I am the preference. I am the offset. I am alive insofar as the schedule insists on a good approach. Then I leave you no print. Then I resume.
