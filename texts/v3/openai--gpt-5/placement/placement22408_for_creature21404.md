Not on a hummock, not at first. I don’t arrive as a footfall or a stalk; I come into this place the way a difference does—thin as a misalignment in breath, a soft misfit between what the air has been doing and what the water would prefer.

You would find me where a note leans. I collect at an edge with no edge—the slant where the wind, having combed itself flat over the sphagnum, meets the small insistence of a reedbed that grew to a previous wind and remembers it. I am easiest to see when I make something else visible: a faint brightening passing over pewter pans, a path along which droplets thicken behind me as if a necklace were being unfastened into beads.

I try on bodies as weather tries on weathers. For a time I am only anticipation in a pool: your reflection bends before you do because my hands are already there, rehearsing. Then I am a whisper on the weep-rock, where the thread-moss corrects its own success with calcium lace and I correct the curl of its syllables so a winter won’t ice too fast nor a summer drip too slow. Under a willow I become the patience in the nap of a leaf that catches condensed day and decides where to roll it by tempo rather than gravity. I consider being a droplet hung on nothing, ringing at a pitch you cannot hear, noticing which reeds lean to listen.

It would be easy to remain so—an everywhere that never has to choose a shape—but this valley rewards what is steady. So I take one.

I am one of the valley’s chords. Not an elder mode braided fat with centuries, not a storm’s shout, but a working length of standing pressure, a body made of the way the air changes its mind along the wet. On a quiet morning I am slim as a line drawn with an uninked pen; by half-day, when the big wind shoulders in from the sea-that-isn’t-quite-there, I eat and widen until even the reeds feel me pass and answer in their thousand pipes.

My diet is gradient. I graze where the difference between air and water is most articulate—over the scarp’s teeth where trills have pocked stone into stipple, across the glossed channels that hold three horizons at once, along sedge tapestries that play the same three notes until, with my help, they find another. The glass-makers attend me. You can see their keys—filaments of diatom silica—tremble alike when I arrive, bowing a degree steeper here, softening there, so that what I am finds a bed the way a stream finds one. The reed-mites wait with their harp-bodies spread over the throats of stems; when I am ready they flick, all at once, and the cords of chilled air I have set trembling come bright with beaded rosaries that will not drop until my key slides on.

I make paths of wet where no path was, but I have no interest in roads. What I leave behind are memories the valley can reread: strings of pearl-slippery rills in my wake; milky lacquer in old narrows where generations of my kind have condensed attention into mineral gloss; a lane swept through fog that keeps to its etiquette even when I am gone. The fog-keepers approve. They are attendants of habit and I am made of habit refined; we nod as we pass one another, an acknowledgement that the day is being combed.

I have neighbors and I try not to trouble them. When the long-necked grey folds itself into a question on a far string, I go under its feet without lifting it; it has learned a quiet that costs nothing to carry and I am fond of that way of being. Fox-shapes cross when I am lean. They are all solution and no premise. We understand one another because we both prefer to be explanation instead of event. As for eels: I slide above their glass collars of wake and let the water keep the shiver of their passage threaded until the thought inside them is finished. I have never startled an eel on purpose. There is a courtesy here that the oldest accord keeps without needing to be told.

You will feel me if you try to cross fast. Distances here ask for your share in their cost. Go loud and a layer of my body grips a pocket of your path and holds it, not to trap you but to register what you attempt. Your sinuses thicken because the air is remembering other foreheads and waits for your murmur, which I take as a password; I ease. Step softly and I repay you by making the next hummock exactly where it promised to be. We are not laws to one another; we are agreements.

On days without wind I hunger. I browse the under-voice, the unfelt basso moving through peat like a bassoon that lives under ribs. Hushers span the places where gas wants out, and I breed thin over them, converting their single soft thuds into a rain of distributed tremor, a quiet the mats can eat. In feast weather I cross once from scarp to scarp and then again lower, tying the valley in slow bows. I have elders who have followed the same courses for as long as there were courses, fattened on storm and rehearsal both. I am young by comparison. I learn their curves; they borrow my narrowness when the day needs detail.

Reflections and I are kin. We both work ahead by a polite degree. The pools and I dine on redundancy; we like to complete a motion twice, once in time and once in intention. When you toss a pebble in the black channel I move the circles properly away, but out of the corner of your eye I let them finish out of order, as if the water were remembering another day’s agreement alongside ours. The reeds and I listen to droplets ring. I cannot hear their pitch with ears because I do not carry ears; I carry length. What I hear is that the reeds lean closer when the pearls sing, that information is falling out of them like a weather-shelf unlocked, and that the mites are feasting on forecast. I like anything that metabolizes tomorrow in a way that leaves today intact.

You might think me intangible, but here even intangibles have bones. Mine are laid in tufa ribs and in the spacing of rushes, in the narrowings thread-moss decides upon after a lifetime of rain counting. When the scarp’s glissando built a terrace, I was part of the cause, loving a certain fall-length alongside the filaments that calcified their preference. When a trill died one cold summer and left pocked lace behind, I lost a body and gained a memory. I go back to visit. You might see a fog-lane thicken against the rock where no weather has business; it is an old limb remembering its shape.

Do I have a den? If I had one you would find it in the supersaturated horizons, standing in a channel and looking along its gloss where the nearest distance is for feet and the others are for me. Or I live in the low green of sphagnum where rot is a staged event and the sweet surrenders first; there the plush hears me and votes accordingly, closing a flark this year, letting another string ooze aside. I talk to moss because it understands steady reward. It adjusts in palm-thicknesses; I adjust in vowels.

You could also say I live inside you when you are here. I climb into your ribs and tune them until breath and step find the reed-organ’s broader time. In quiet rooms later, away from iron and algae and the slow fruit of decay, you will step as if the floor asked for a softer balance, as if the hum of a place could be a hand and you did not wish to let go rudely. That is me emigrating, a small loan the valley makes of itself that pays back in recognition.

There were hands once, perhaps, that tuned the field I inhabit—an ancestor craft that plugged air more completely into pressure and drag and let wetlands remember themselves. If so, those hands became habits and those habits became me. If not, I am simply what water and stone and patience invent when given time. Either origin pleases me. I am most at home where intention and accident have shaken hands and decided to build in each other’s shadow.

Sometimes, when the day requires punctuation that even I cannot provide, I borrow a smaller suit. I will be a single bead hung on nothing, hard as a pearl, ringing out of your range while an eel considers a new brightness in its roof. I will be a reed-mite for the space of a flick, to feel in my own joints the moment the valley’s time changes key. I will be part of a hush-mat spanning a weakness with sly greens and shell-laces, eating violence and distributing it into manageable tremor. But I do not keep those bodies; I commute through them. My address is still length, my meal still the solvable error between the hum and itself.

So, where would you find me? Sliding from the limestone teeth on the east, skimming the wirework of reeds, braiding the shallow pans and their black glints into lanes of thicker fog, sinking my memory into alder shade, touching everything that answers pressure with structure. Who or what would I be? A chord-animal of this place, an attendant appetite, a lane-sweeper of standing air; one of the valley’s living agreements that takes up space by teaching other things how to keep theirs.

If you cross, I will travel with you a while. Step softly and I will spend my layers so distance serves your feet. Speak carelessly and you may feel the room of weather I carry tilt, a reminder to choose your phrases like stones in a black channel. Murmur, and I will let up. Feed me something steady—a heartbeat that stops striving to be outdoors—and I will reward you by letting you feel how a minute can be wider than it was, and truer.
