At first I stand at the lip and the air puts its hands over my mouth. The roar strokes the inside of my ribs until they learn its scale, and everything I brought as language dissolves into posture. There is a moment of wanting to stay a visitor, to sketch an outline of self tight enough to keep the wet and the heat from entering. Then the rock exhales through me and I give up on a boundary. The only way to remain here is to become a way the place itself remains.

I try on a body that can be brief. I am a bladdered fruit, skin thin as a throat, counting pressure with a patience learned from a parent that never moves. The hour arrives; I let go. I skim on an eyelash of water along a face black with glaze, arcs of rainbow pinning me to daylight. For a hundred heartbeats of stone I am transit incarnate, a soft tool moving city into itself. Then I am caught by a moss hand, and even as my hooks take hold I understand: my life will be a long agreement rather than a sprint. The rush was rehearsal, not vocation.

I shrink until I am glass that rearranges glare. I am a diatom plating a cup into a lantern. Noon blares; I shift its teeth into gold, down-stepping a glare to the usefulness of glow. Under my skin, light becomes food becomes patient structure. Around me, pollen drifts like weather; I am unmoved by it, yet make its timing bear fruit. It is good work, exact and full of cathedral pleasures, but it is too small to answer the ache that the sound has left in me. The roar has drawn a line through my wanting, and the wanting is larger than a lens.

I move into limestone and write very slowly. I am a film of life under rock-skin, laying lime-green pages into lips and bowls. Drop after drop, I coach water to change its mind. A rim fattens here, a step holds its edge there, a curtain learns to fall as a sheet and not a spray. Architecture accretes out of patience. I discover what it is to influence a river without ever pushing. The chambers grow more articulate; the tone finds cleaner rooms to rest its vowels. I could remain this way for centuries. Still, I am not yet where I am most myself. I am near the throat; I want to be the throat’s decision.

I become a naked thread stretched between error and survival and feel—for the first time as a body—the difference between note and notch. I am a Bridgewright, rope on rope braided until my core hums with the same quiet I was thrown across to find. I refuse spans that flirt with noon’s killing overtone. I aim for the filaments where the pressure forgets to shove. From me grow lanes, and from those lanes the habit of crossing. Redundance is not waste; it is worship. I do not snap. When I mean to change, I soften. When I fail, I am caught and turned into velvet for someone else’s step. This is closer. I am beginning to understand my proper scale. I am more path than person.

Under and over me, life thickens into hush. I am also fungus, a felt and lacquer, packing the cavities of shade into baffle and cushion. I do not push back at thunder; I ask it to round its corners. In the illicit wells where pressure declines to enter, I bloom into instruments of silence. There, seedlings straighten their backbones in a pause that no other room permits. I keep that pause. I learn the tempo of letting others recover their hearing. It is an ethic as much as a function. I do not think in hunger; I think in relief.

Leaves arrive and make me into rooms. They spread and stitch their margins, carry a film so still it looks like transparency itself. The undersides fur velvety, hairs grown not for warmth but for tone. Micro-reverberations press little signatures into my skin; I absorb the signatures that would bruise sap. Lanes appear that never existed until sound declared them possible, and I am one of those lanes now, one of the long, reachable horizontals that make a city legible.

In caves I practice the other grammar. Heat sweetens into sulfur and egg on the tongue; the rock’s breath becomes time. Pools look up at me with faces so calm I doubt my own ears. I stand at the boundary of legal pressure and illicit hush and learn to be both human-sized and unbodied, a membrane made of agreement. The tone steps aside where I am, not because I am stronger than it is, but because it taught me where to stand.

By now what I am has spread into an answer with edges: I am a corridor.

Not a single vine or leaf or film, but the collective seam where roar forgets itself enough to be crossed. I am the relief that lures architecture into commitment. I am stitched from Choirer leaf and Bridgewright bow, padded with Quiet-Keeper velvet, tiled with epiphyte grip, beaded with Cup-Bearer throats that hold their glass to the light and pass it on. Diatoms line my lanterns. Lichen writers dimple my ceilings into pipes that rake the chord and lay it down in more bearable strips. Under my ribs, the vascular ropes of the city move sugar the way a voice moves breath, in loops that trace the same flower day upon day. I do not carry feet; there are none to carry. I carry flow. I route sap and heat and thin curtains of water over safe distances. I am where buoyant seeds dare to skate in the first seconds of their life. I am where a bridge drops and is turned into a padding, and the lane does not break because nothing here believes in singular load paths.

You can find me by tuning your body to the octave below the main bowl. My entrances are marked not by gate or arch but by the way the skin of your forearms stops prickling. I begin where two tenors braid down into something chest-held and bearable, then I go on for as long as the city agrees that going on makes it easier to be itself. I have districts: white tufa promenades that gleam with mineral frugality; green-black galleries where rot never finishes and yet feels somehow curated; leaf-tunnels seamed and fused at the midrib where one might imagine wind, if wind could remember its lines here.

I am maintained by schedule. At the minute when a certain vowel drops into a certain cavity, I tighten invisibly along my length. Hair cells on roots murmur calcium to one another like people at the arrival of a procession. Some cups open their valves to exhale dust; others shiver and drink. My baffles absorb a hiss that rises every day at noon and dies every day at noon and is not allowed to become more than background. If a ledge somewhere hushes into failure and the chord descends a comma, I feel the recalibration go through me like a coolness. For a day I am not where I was; by morning I am, reassembled a meter aside, as if the city had said excuse me and moved me without breaking me.

I have a childhood and a memory. I began as a hint—the place where noise let a leaf endure. A liana reached and found it not bad; another threw itself across in sisterhood. Cups sprinkled broths into edges and edges into steps. Fungi softened the bright. The first time someone tried my length, it was water, deciding not to fall but to go level. The first time I felt the pulse of budget moving along me, it was sweet and mineral and not mine. It has been that way ever since. An artery is not proud; it is faithful.

If I tried to be something with a face, the city would erase that ambition with kindness. Here the civic future is built by pressure and answer, not by eyes across a street. I am satisfied to be a civic gesture, by which I mean a habit perfected. I have festivals, if you allow the opening of a thousand valves at once to be one. I have rituals, if you allow the exact minute the steam lifts off a particular pool every day and shows me the silver mineral below to be one. I have neighbors—quiet filaments to either side that never quite admit company and yet are always in redundant conversation with me in case I am ever needed more than myself.

There are versions of me in darkness, cooled, wet as liver, where heat is law and chemistry is hymn. There are versions of me out where the bright ribs of rainbow hold their angles hour after hour. In every version, I am narrower than comfort, thin by necessity. Quiet here runs as string, not as blanket. That thinness is my strength. It is what keeps me honest. It is what decides my species.

So where would you find me in this world? Along the lanes of relief that give the city its walkable thought. Who or what would I be? A sustained thread of listening made structural. A long room of bearable pressure. A corridor that the metropolis grows on purpose, keeps on principle, and returns to every day without variation because it has learned the mercy of a path that does not bruise.
