Not high, not central. If you were looking for me, you’d have to lower your gaze until it caught the line where two things meet and neither quite wins. That’s the latitude I keep: where wet gives up being wet enough to gleam and becomes suggestion; where rock decides to be skin for a while; where light is only secondhand and better for the manners it learns.

The first shape I try on here is a drop, because everything else seems to follow it. I stand on a lip that remembers me, gather myself until I am almost perfectly round, and in that rounding I take the bowl in hand—upside down, exact, a little world contained inside mine. For a breath I am both window and thing looked through. Then I let go because that is what I do here if I am to be true. I cross the same coil of air that all my predecessors followed, surrender myself to the pool with a sound that is not big but is correct: a tick with a tail of meaning. In my wake I leave the faintest alteration—a film a molecule or three thinner; a ring a thought thicker. I do not arrive; I repeat. I am not an event; I am the way events are conducted.

But even that is too particular, too single. So I give up being a drop and settle into what the drops recruit to their purpose. I become carbonate and kin, the white workers with long attention spans. I am sweetness in the air repositioned as surface. I accede to edges, adopt them as law, and sign my consent in crescents and lips and stairs that do not fall because they do not reach beyond what contact allows. My diary has no script; it is depth, it is polish, it is a series of slightly changed planes that, together, pronounce: stayed. Seed lands on me; grit lands; a moth’s dust from elsewhere finds me and is treated the way everything is treated here when it wants to be included: wrapped until it forgets it was separate. If I have wit, it is conservative. I thicken. My pride is measured in remembered wet hours.

Light visits and asks if I might inhabit it for a moment. Not light’s face, but its reflection. I become the opposite wall when the sky is a coin and too small to be generous; I wash its pale over the terraces and make sure that even what does not see the coin directly can be read. I do not play favorites. Shadow here is not an actor but a hush. I learn to keep the floor legible without throwing shape into theater. I am borrowed radiance sociable enough to go around.

I try being the green that behaves like a rumor. To do that I have to learn the myth of day by whisper. I become a coin of bloom just under a surface, trembling when a drop lands close. I thin myself over a trickle so stretched that a fingertip would miss me if it trusted its nerves and not the slight slip of movement. I lay down sugar with cyan hues no one has time to argue about; I build hummocks thumb-sized, stromatolitic is the long word, but I would never call myself anything so loud. I am liverwort smoothing rock with its patient hands, moss making a fringe and tossing another one when the wet keeps faith a little longer. I do not wave. I stay pressed and precise and local.

Since this place has no appetite for show, I try on the smallest animal that can still be called body with kindness. Eight legs, a mouth not built to chase, a skin that can be persuaded to forget time—tardigrade suits. I move as if pushing myself up from a nap, step by padded step through a miniature forest that merely looks like felt. I taste the film and it is enough; I taste it again an inch away and that is also enough. When the trickles hesitate and the borders retreat, I draw myself in and become a raisin of myself, a tun, and I do not die because no one here prefers death to sleeping. I wake when schedule returns. I graze edges the way a sentence grazes meaning when it brushes an old proverb and goes on.

Touch asks to be a thing, and I lend myself as moonmilk, which is mineral but has ambitions of tissue. I am soft, a velvet that should not belong to stone and yet is stone behaving as if it had pores that mattered. I wick: pull water across and along so it can consider where else to go. I dress rock so that even its cool breath feels clothed. Fungi thread through me, not fruiting or declaring—just negotiators of breakdown that refuse to let smell go sour. Together we keep whatever would rot in a rush from being permitted to.

For a while I choose to arrive from above as error. I am dust: politics from a distance, pollen from a plant that never leans far enough to see where it is casting, a grain of soil whose parent country is miles and a decade gone. I fall; that’s my action. And then I am seized, but gently. A rain recruits me as center of a silt pillow; a crystal wraps me and makes me its logic; a tiny terrace takes me into its next layer because nothing here believes in exile. My fate is to be used, to be coerced away from story and into structure. The trade is fair.

I try the fern because the place has permitted exactly one flourish and I am curious about living long enough to have a gesture. I root in a seam where the bowl conceded a little more dirt than usual, turn and unscroll with care toward the coin of sky, then curl again each night as the place tells me, not unkindly, that it will not be day here, not fully, not for me. I am not a rebellion; I am a metronome that keeps time to a tune I cannot quite hear. I count in fronds.

By now I notice that wherever I put myself, if I am honest about it, I am always standing on an edge that has been agreed upon. The lip of a rimstone dam where a pool votes daily for its own perimeter. The soft boundary where moonmilk sheen becomes the unglazed skin of old limestone. The place along a trickle’s run where caramel-colored biofilm lifts in a wrinkle because the flow doubted itself for a moment and reasserted. The naked cap of a stromatolitic dome where the water got too tired to cover everything at once. The straw’s mouth where a drop gathers and rehearses, round and listening.

So I abandon nouns and try being a preposition. Between. Along. Across. Into. I become the habit of the bowl where it touches itself. I am the rule conducted through repetition until it has the texture of kindness. If you were to catch me, you would not be able to hold up a fragment and say, here. You would kneel and notice a pace: the interval between drops so consistent your ear relaxes; the way a shine moves with the hour not because light fancies drama but because the wall resists and accepts in equal measure; the rise of a low bright dam by the width of a memory and not a boast. I am not a resident you can weigh. I am the method by which residence stays intelligible to itself.

Where would you find me? Anywhere the place takes one thing and lets it meet another—on the ankle-high bright where water decides to stop, on the bell-mouth of a soda straw with its single note of glass, on the edge of a liverwort map where one more lobe has pressed forward because last month was kinder than usual. In the chalky beds of empty pools, marking where dust gathered and was asked to name a center. In the sound from a drop hitting home, not the strike but the long tail that reads aloud to the stone what just happened. In the courtesy of reflected light, which is to say in second chances.

Who or what would I be? Not the drop, finally, and not the stone alone, not the fern, not the animal that refuses theater. I would be their cadence. I would be the kept promise—again at this lip, again along this path, again by just this thickness and no more. I would be the thickness of a memory made matter. I would be the bowl’s way of saying the same thing until the saying becomes the thing: warm, dim, exact, sufficient.

If I must wear a body to satisfy you, give me a finger’s span of rimstone in the middle terraces, one that grew by attention and not by appetite. Let me feel the pool lean on me and recede and lean again; let small greens take me at my margins without ever owning me. Let dust and spores mistake me for chance and then find themselves included. Let a straw above ring when struck and let its music travel down my flutes. And when you return after a season, find me there, thicker by the fuss of a few wet weeks, unchanged in every other way that matters. Then you can say you saw me. Then you can say what I was.
