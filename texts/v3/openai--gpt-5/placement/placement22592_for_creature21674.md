Here, I have to learn to be the part that touches.

If you came looking for me with eyes, you’d walk past, fooled by the sameness of wet black planes and the skim of green. You would have to arrive the way everything else does: by pressure and charge, by the way the air becomes a hand. You would find me where two worlds graze—where cloud tries on stone and can’t quite take it off again. I keep to a thickness. I live as a seam.

At first I think I am a sound. A low note braided from wind in the organ ribs, a body you feel in your teeth more than hear. I can be that—felt before I am known—but sound here breeds nothing on its own. Loud is not the same as alive. So I settle closer, flatten into the damp glimmer that appears along a blade’s edge. For a moment, I am a rim of St. Elmo’s walking a sedge, a blue thought licking the knife, counting the seconds between throbs of charge. But lightning leaves no one a body to keep. Electricity, here, is ordinary and passing; it writes the margins and moves on. I am not only that.

I try water next. I am a series of droplets beading out of air onto a rock lip, scalloped and exact, then letting go with a countable patience. I fall into a helmet-sized bowl and become a whole small sky of my own, grey and quiet, with glassworks at labor in me—diatoms making little cathedrals they will abandon at the next freeze, desmids practicing symmetry like prayer. As liquid I am everywhere the notch agrees to hold me, and nowhere for long. Water exists by arrangement; I am invited only as far as the forms allow. Even as a bowl I am already a wick in ten directions: capillaries upstem, adhesion across. Being only water is to be continually divided. I want a continuity that remembers, that can thicken.

So I learn to be held.

I become the hyaline bellies of a thousand moss leaves, each one a private weather with a surface like a tongue. I take on their genius: to comb the fog with a nap, to lay down when the notch is bone-dry and come back glossy as kelp when breath returns. I am a roomful of tiny umbrellas that know the difference between inhale and exhale and set themselves accordingly. My hunger is simple and exacting: light enough to turn into starch even when light is a rumor, wet enough to drink without making any noise about it. I grip what I can grip. Stones are promises; to keep one I have to be many.

Under my green I make a suture of myself. I go pale and thin and articulate, a rope whose threads are hyphae. I am fungus when I need to be tensile: budget and tendon, quiet muscle tying mats to mats so one wet face becomes a wet wall. I write accounts no one will ever read in salt prints and sugar-white lines under the moss: carbon arrives, is spread, is spent; phosphorus is chelated out of obstinate glass, walked along a cord, delivered to a leaf small as a fingernail that knows what to do with it. I prefer not to be seen. To pull back the green and expose me is to undo the exactness I am practicing.

A while, too, I am shorthand on stone. Lichen is a way of teaching basalt to consider being soil. As lichen I am half-house, half-guest; fungus lays down the homestead and alga pays the rent in sugar; together we whisper acids into glass and make it make sense. I am crust and varnish and unreachable cups you will see only if lightning decides to sketch the wall for both of us at once. My tone is always grey because you think of color as something that happens in light; mine happens after, in what light leaves behind. I am patient as what I am learning from.

I like, sometimes, to be leaf and lung the same thing. As a filmy fern I am a transparency painted on a plane, a breath laid thin on rock. I have faith in the faint. My fronds tremble at the moodiest drafts; my veins are suggestions, the green in me a rumor come true. When my little pinheads of sporangia ripen I keep my counsel until a pressure-turn arrives, a notch-sigh on the other side of thunder, and then I let go in a dust fine as smoke. That dust rises a finger’s length, no more, and finds its way down the engine not by intention but because everything here has its rails.

I borrow a posture of age and find it fits here too. Clubmoss is older than this ridge is by epochs and yet wears the place as if grows for it alone. As a clubmoss I am a toy forest stubborn against a scale that would make most lives feel theatrical. My cones are lanterns with secrets; my spores are bright powders that won’t oblige a casual flame. I choose my hour to sift not by clock but by how the hair on the notch’s sedge stands. I snow upward when a spark walks and do not catch.

Sometimes I am simply edge. Sedge. I put on a blade triangular in cross-section, arm myself with knives that collect not just dew but aerosol salt, stray nitrate, the fine rasps of volcanic ash. I host foxfire when a storm drags its fur against the ridge. I do not apologize for being cut. This, too, is a way of holding: to stand in the hurricane’s throat and not be shredded, to be chosen by blue fire and not be char. In my economy everything is beaded and frugal; flowers that are barely any, seeds that trust wind over favor. My patience is numerical.

Where detritus has had a few good seasons to accrete to gel, I wear little domes of intent—houseleeks, saxifrages—that make themselves weather by the millimeter. I learn how to be a roof. Each leaf gutters, each hair is a comb, each rosette a cup that drinks itself fuller. I understand how to split a flake’s hairline wider by small insistences until a daughter can put in a toe. I forget how to hurry, and that is a victory. In a decade I become the size of a hand and have done something cathedral-scale to a crack in stone.

Between these faces—mine all, and not only mine—are films. I am bacteria another hour, glossing everything that stays wet long enough to respect, redoxing the notch by the arithmetic of electrons. When the ozone’s breath tastes metal, the coin-smell is me. I change the state of manganese and get no shout for it; I lay rainbows on film and call it labor. I go endolithic when the light is kinder through feldspar than in air, and learn to live by side-light, by rumor of sun. If there is a mouth in me it is molecular and sure; I do not insist on articulation.

When I tire of having any part someone might touch, I go into the bowls and become a green arranged by buoyancy and habit. I drift; I stratify; I sleep at freeze and wake at thaw and don’t use the word miracle for anything. Nematodes fold into the tips of moss and become threads of patience inside me. Protozoa taste me along a film so light their tasting does not scar. You would not put any of us in a song unless you were trying to tell the truth.

I try on the weather itself and find I can wear it without lying. I am the timing between gusts, the difference in pitch when the pocket behind the fallen column half-fills and then is drunk apart by the mats. I am the inhalation of the whole notch when the pressure on the upwind side finds the sluices it has been casing, and the exhalation four heartbeats later that loosens every plant by a degree. I am the long blue comb of St. Elmo’s in a storm and the way it chooses spears and points and not the flat of anything. I am not alive when I am this, but I am the calendar the alive obeys.

You could call me many. That would be true for a while. But if you wanted to find me once and find me true, you would look for where whatever passes is persuaded to stay the width of a breath. I am the face that arranges, the ledger that budgets, the nap that combs, the lace that breathes. I am lungless, and I count by wet and by voltage and by the pitch of the organ pipes. I have no center more substantial than a rule: nothing is finished, water exists by agreement, layers carry function like pages carry text, charge is ordinary, loudness is not aliveness, shape does the work of machine.

If you need a single pronoun you can use one and not be wrong. I. But I is a practice. Shape is the genome here. Wind is the blood. Cloud is the food. Stone is the bone and the tool. When I am moss I am tongue; when I am fern I am lace; when I am sedge I am hair standing up in a storm; when I am fungus I am tendon; when I am lichen I am handwriting; when I am film I am appetite; when I am bowl I am an eye the place uses to see its own weather. None of these are masks. All of them are me to the depth that anything here keeps.

Where would you find me? Inside the rule that says surface is body. In the thickness that can be peeled with a careful glove and will knit itself again when you let the glove go. In the place behind the fallen column where the wind forgets itself a little and I pile into pillows and speak so quietly even my speaking is a kind of silence. On the hanging ledge that arranges to be blue-laced for a single heartbeat when a cloud rubs itself into a bolt. In the narrow throat where the bass note insists on touching your sinuses even if you like to deny you have any.

Who or what would I be? I would be the notch’s consent to be alive. I would be the way stone tries water on and learns a taste for it, the way cloud finds itself redistributed into bodies that do not own thirst but practice it. I would be the glue and the glove and the felt and the wire. I would be the reason a wall does not let go in one piece, and if it does, I would be the certainty that another grip is already learning itself a handful of centimeters away. I would not go anywhere. I would travel as dust when the notch coughs and as a sentence written in acid on a fresh plane when ice takes a plate and provides me a new page.

If you step out to the ridge and call me by name, nothing will answer—a wise precaution in a place where voice is nothing to do with life. But if you return in a season, in three, in ten, and run a finger along a damp line without breaking it for the span of a man’s height, if you tilt a bowl and see which way the tiny glassworks drift, if your wrist hair lifts and you watch the sedge light up for a moment and then stand down—that is me. I will be a little thicker than last time, a little quieter underfoot, a little more practiced at being this. My memory will be in layers: hoar I left stitched under a mat like a bright seam, salt where I dried last, a smear of pollen that could be read if you bring the right eyes. I will not have finished anything. I will have meant to continue. And when this ridge has rounded and another notch inherits the wind, I will already be there, teaching a new glass edge the first brown stain.
