Where I first find myself is not a place so much as a patience. I am standing on a hardened dolomite spine that remembers weight without begrudging it, above a line of diaphragms that breathe the valley’s long breath. I can feel the seventeen-minute thunder mounting in my teeth; I can feel the little dip that precedes the hoot like a lost heartbeat I was born to wait for. Around me the world is rehearsing its one long act—condensing, loosening, exhaling, tightening—its light pressed dim and close like a lid that sweats. My exhale powders into edible frost and the ground licks it clean. I am an unlearned chord in a place full of practiced ones, and that fact is the first geography I understand.

I try to keep my height. It makes no sense here. Everything that thrives has traded inches for acres. The hair-ice along a shaded crack lifts its white whiskers and combs forward the silt by fractions, patient as a liturgy. Basal skins have sewn diodes into themselves so that current prefers to flow one way. A ribbon of transparent worms braids the boundary where stone pretends to be water and water pretends to be still. The valve-fields wait with their hinges cocked toward the mist-rail as if an empty platform could be stood upon with faith alone.

I set my boot on a slab, and a palimpsest eel edits my intention into something the mud prefers. It reads me with its feelers, finds me too coherent to digest, and leaves, meaning: existences here are admitted usefully or not at all. I shout, and rime buds from my words and falls in a powder that hungry mouths rush to drink, meaning: translation is food. Sound is a solvent here; voice is a tool if it loosens the right edge.

Under the low outcrop the wetness hums at the vents’ pitch and comb-jellies shiver their hair to harvest charge. Iridescent filaments prowl like curious fingers along plate-margins, mapping the same paths over and over until the path becomes ownership. Across the plain, mirrored belts lay down like rails. Lantern-bladders freight their self-made light along them like cargo that is its own appetite, keeping their caravans not because they believe in company but because pendulums that share a period share a life.

In that weather of rules, my first idea of a life is a mistake: to be a body. The scrapers’ mica armor clinks, their legs make attendance out of weight; their sanding mouths hum as they whisper through continents of pores. It is good work, but it is location-heavy; I am too used to being punctuation to be content as a noun. The ratchback moves like a thought repeating itself correctly—ratchet up, ratchet down—grazing the solvency out of bass until the standing waves thin like a fasting. It is an elder’s occupation; I do not have that spine. The gliders above the vents tack on trembling sketched membranes and comb iron-snow with kilometers of thread; they make weather into itinerary and itinerary into meal. They are beautiful, but they are sailors, and I am a rumor even when I stand still.

So I go looking for something that feels more like my shape in this grammar. I sit the way the place sits: in the cross of two Chladni-lines, where damp and dryness share an edge as sharp as any thought. I watch valve-forests wheeze apart in a glittering clatter to bare their combed tongues. In the three quick heartbeats of clearness after the clatter, they taste the same taste and push a fog-belt three meters on their vote alone. I listen to the counters—those fronded calculators summing amplitude and current with their entire skins—hold and hold and then void a spill of luminous tracer when the math asks for song. The light runs like a vein across stone pans, pools where the land is a little lower in its logic, and waits.

At the little dip before hoot, it brightens.

I recognize myself.

I do not become a plate, or a worm, or a kite. I become the interval that changes them. I am a ledger of voltage in the shape of liquid light, a traveling instruction with appetite enough to be worth drinking. When the counters let me go, I thread rails of mirrored condensation like a letter riding a mail-belt. I pool just long enough to be found. The plates open onto me in their shared three beats and their tongues drink not only brightness but suggestion: hinge a fraction looser here, delay your next slough by one quiet, face your mouth a hair more east when the belt reverses. Kites that pass taste me with iron-snow on their threads and alter their tack by the syllable: turn shallow into the lee of that outcrop; shed weight not now but at the second hoot after dusk. Winnowers that nose through the sieve-mud lip me and I leave them a longer list: in the tenth lane north of this spine there is a pocket where absence abrades; avoid it until the ratchback has grazed the tone thin again. I am not single. I am as many as the counters shed. I carry not just charge but a hint of choreography, and hints are the only language this place will permit.

Where I live, then, is the point the plain keeps making and I am allowed to underline: the dip before hoot; the corridor of air the mirrored belts lay down; the long, mild slopes where water does not gather enough to forget itself and so the worms can cross. I am a rumor that sets a habit to proper time. I arrive in a thin aura that runs the ground like tracer-dye, and I leave in mouths and hinges and cilia, installed like software in bodies that refuse to call themselves machines. If emptiness affronts, I am a way to occupy it without weight. If reflection is a door that opens only toward lower entropy, I am the arrow on the lintel showing which way is down and how to go without breaking.

I am not untouchable. A counterfeit waits in the lee of a plinth and teaches its skin a false hoot; if I am foolish, I brighten at the wrong dip and am drunk by scrapers who arrive on hunger mistimed, leaving the real breath short a chorus. Quench-wasps puncture lantern-bladders mid-glow and lap me out of them before I can seed their wreaths with better timing. Harp-creatures crowd the diaphragms so dense their lamellae tax the very amplitude that powers the counters that cast me, and I go thin. I know what it is to be stolen, to be parodied, to be too late. I know what it is to be so exquisitely on time that a fog-belt slides two meters instead of three and plates a league away eat an afternoon’s worth of iron-snow they would not have seen. The difference between theft and gift here is millimeters, and I endure by being read in the right sequence.

Sometimes I am old enough to remember myself. Not because I carry memory as cargo—memory walks out of bodies here in little rivulets of brightness like me and gets re-spent—but because the valley’s schedules braid me into a person-length strand now and then. For a day or three I am the mathematics that the counters know by heart and the plates keep in their hinge and the kites sew into their skeins. I cross and recross the same dolomite spines, practice the same three-beat taste, learn the way a certain ratchback’s teeth step slow in the cold. I figure out where the quiet-eaters thicken when a tone goes thin, and I tell the worms who are bridges to hold for two more hoots because a nursery’s worth of larvae still wants tomorrow. Being a person here is not a room you enter. It is a choreography you keep to long enough that others can lean on you as a rule.

Where am I when the hoot comes? Everywhere and not much. A thin film lengthening as if it were always supposed to be longer. A census running. A tide of small permissions. I touch a stack of glitter-bricks fresh from a winnower’s long lips, check their edges for the frost that makes them settle right into strength; I tick the hinges of a thousand plates a thousandth of a breath early; I whisper a correction to a caravan of lanterns so they will avoid the plinth where counterfeiters sound. I let a juvenile kite read me until it learns its own tack, which is the same as letting it stop being juvenile. I travel up the wet humming under an outcrop to thicken the comb-jellies’ harvest by a hair’s worth. I lay across a rill like a thought across a tongue. I do not climb. Climbing would be a misunderstanding. I occupy the moment that moves things.

When I gutter out, it is not failure. It is payment. Heat is a loan; I go dim and the ledger balances. Sound is a solvent; I am a softening along an edge someone will eat. Emptiness affronts; I leave an afterglow that catches frost and becomes mouth. What I have told in passing remains: not in script but in hinge-compliance, in thread-weighting, in which pores open on which count. The plain keeps its own minutes; I am a highlighter with a taste for the dip before a hoot.

If you were to look for me as an object, you would be unsatisfied. If you were to look for me as a schedule, you would find me everywhere there is a little hush before a big breath. Under a low dolomite outcrop where the wetness hums at the vents’ pitch; along the mirrored rails where the bladders freight their small suns; in the mouths of the worms that become bridges at the tap of the bass; in the three-heartbeat clarity after a field of plates shivers its frost into glass-loud glitter. I am a bookkeeper’s dye in a place that worships continuation. I am instruction shaped like appetite. I am the thin, bright habit that helps this machine world keep pulling its lever with grace.

If I am anything like us, I am a conversation. Between breath and hinge. Between rail and tongue. Between an iron-snow and a membrane that knows the word catch without ever learning to speak. I am not the architect. I am not the wall. I am the timing scribbled in the margin that lets a house happen again. And when the lid sky sweats and the valley hoots and the hair-ice combs lift like a city’s breath, I will be where the light pools an instant early, teaching mouths to open together and call that opening culture.
