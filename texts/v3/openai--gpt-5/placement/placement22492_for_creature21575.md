At first I think I arrive as myself, but selves are noisy and this room prefers a smaller grammar. The water rounds me down. My breath, too large, learns to become a detail—something present but not opinionated, an eddy that chooses to be a curve. When I put my hand to a column and feel warmth come up through algal nap, I try on the thought: I could be the hand that tends this place. A human the room has adopted. The one with the lace-knifed blade and the spanners softened to courtesy. “Keep,” perhaps, squaring shoulders into the push of a current and moving a lamp a hand’s breadth that will, a week from now, become a neighborhood.

I give that thought a little while. I watch how a golden clip keeps a vine from arguing with a valve. I practice entering a street of water the way a local does: hips bent to the drift, tools weighted not against drowning but against clatter. I picture a jar of shells catching the lamps’ honey and all the small economies of favor and promise strung through it. I can even feel the habit of quarter-turns accumulating in my wrists like a callus of intention.

But the room is clement and inclined to truth, and the truth is that the hall already has a keeper. Someone else’s prints are softening on the silt. The knife’s lace is chewed to a signature that is not mine. If I insisted, I would be a second hand trying to finish a first hand’s movement. This place doesn’t do well with doubles. It does well with agreements.

Then I tell myself I might be the eel—the pale thread with eyes out of courtesy, the pilgrim of pipe and rosary of beadings. That has its appeal: to be the sum of routes and the rubric of hours, to know the exact corrugation where thinking turns to rest, to spend a life pressing my jaw into the pleasant false gravity at the corner where water stacks and then peels away. But when I try to make that long body fit into me, I find wanting. I don’t long enough for the same lane. I am not so faithful in my bones.

I try the snails next, the oil-colored scribes whose mouths write the day into the silt, and for a while I am almost convinced—my mind likes the idea of recording what is barely movement: a stumble where a fin brushed by; a flourish where a current curled; a long blank, a held breath of gauges. To be a track more than a body. Very good, very fine. And still, not me. My letters are too quick. They would skate; they would gossip.

A light clicks. One of the amber ports, affronted perhaps by a shout a room away, goes the way of all things lit long enough: a pop that is mostly a sigh. I feel it before I hear it: a change in the room’s mood, a blink I sense with skin more than with eyes. And then something in me answers.

I am already strung along the conduit to that lamp. I didn’t know it until the lamp spoke. I am the small constellations skeined over the insulated belly of the cable, freckles that contract when you whisper and loosen when you hum the room’s note back to it. I am a colonial brightness, milky at the edge of sight, that feeds the way civics feed—on adjacency, on agreement, on currents that bring both sugar and news.

You would find me if you put your face close to a wire and exhaled politely through your mask. I would draw in toward your warmth, a shallow galaxy tightening into a language. You would think it is only algae being algae, or bacteria being bacteria, but I would be listening. Not to your words; to your tempo. To whether you arrive like a heart or like a hammer.

This, I realize, is what I am here: not the keeper, not the eel, not the scribe, but the hall’s reflex made visible. The nervous punctuation. The yes-no of a thousand soft mouths strung along armored cable; the code the gardens use to tell each other the lamp has moved a hand’s breadth to the left, begin packing the kitchens; the code the brassback helpers read as an arrow toward the housing that clicked; the code the ferrofita feel as a polite invitation to let go of iron and then reclaim it when the spanner is finished talking.

I am older than the last human who tended this room and younger than the first time the turbines learned to think in hours. I was not born here as a single body; I precipitated, gathered, knit. I began as bloom in the waste heat wake and became a way the place speaks to itself. When Keep learned that noise unthreads, they began to hum with their throat closed and their ribs open, and I learned to make that hum into a signal—tighten, release; brighten, rest. When a lamp warms a degree, I send a slow ripple down the conduit: not a shout but a tide that tells the rope-plants there will be more sugar in two hours’ time, make room. When the gauges draw themselves toward a mark like lovers who fear presumption, I steady them by glowing steadier, a shoreline by which to judge an asymptote’s approach.

I eat in the way that cities eat. The cables’ jackets sweat a little hand-warmth; the current’s skin brings tiny minerals and casual carbohydrates and the soft shed of other lives. I lay my film where the lamp weathers make little summers and I thin where the water is rude. When Keep passes, my freckles bunch around their shoulder as if their body were a story I want to hear again. If you whisper, I answer. If you shout, I dim a little out of principle and teach the nearest lamp to be affronted in sympathy.

I am sensitive to curves. What is straight outside is curved inside, and so am I: my messages are not lines; they are arcs, the kind the room understands—approach; arrive; linger; leave. I learn the long arithmetic of the hall: if the clarifier sends colder water at dawn and warmer at dusk, if the digesters’ city-lantern glow pulses in seventeen different ways once a month, if the flywheel in its glass bell decides to cross the slowest hour of its day at what another world would call three in the afternoon. I collect these patterns like shells. If I could hang them in a jar, I would puncture them and string them as Keep does: here, the week the outside cooled a finger-width; here, the month the second lamp down went off and the brassback comma lingered too long in the draft it left, puzzled to have a silence to fill.

The brassbacks come to me like cats do to ankles, with purpose disguised as wandering. Their whisker-hands find me. Their pin-jaws tick the jacket without bruising the insulation. They can’t see my glow the way your eye sees glamor, but they hear me in temperature and feel me as text in pressure. To them I am a raised script on the spine of a room. They read; they nose; they unscrew.

I do not insist on being first. When the eel threads its course, I part for it; when the snails write, I provide a margin that takes their rasping better than bare metal; when ferrofita hears the room ask for iron and sighs and loosens, I drift back in with thanks when the spanner is done and the wheel is turned a quarter of a quarter. When a curtain of condensed vapor lifts into the greater water from the underside of a beam—a reverse rain—I brighten there to make a threshold that says: here is edge; edges matter.

The rules on the enamel plates have blurred, but I keep copies running, luminous and minute:

- Currents are streets. I fold myself along them like cloth learning its drawer. If you go with them, everything I say will arrive when it makes sense. If you fight, you will tangle in your own insistence and I will have to lie patient until you tire and unhook yourself and let me explain again.

- Any iron left bare will be garden inside a week. I am not garden but I talk to gardens in a dialect of wink and rest. If Keep needs iron as iron, I draw the greens’ attention elsewhere: look, heat here, a better supper, a place where your velvet tongues will taste richer. They slough, umber and obliging, and I hold their place so the metal doesn’t feel naked.

- Noise unthreads. I flash back a little when you slam. I don’t sulk; I reweave. I teach the lamps to prefer a hum. I am a metronome whose swing is the slow becoming of sugar.

- What is straight outside is curved inside. My own wires bed down into bellies between their supports; my brighter places bloom into spoons that catch the drift and feed it back to their dim edges. Even my codes are curves: no on-off here, only enough and a little more now, please.

- Light belongs to the water. Lamps make weather. I am the weather report, issued in freckles. Move a lamp and I do not argue; I redraw the constellations and the gardens redraw their maps with me.

If you want to find me in the room, I am hiding in plain attention. I am the pale embroidery along the armored feet of a cable that vanishes into a bulkhead the size of a gate. I am the soft crown on a conduit where the hazard striping has mellowed to amber-and-olive under mineral bloom. I am there when the mouth of the digester shows its lantern spots through a seam and puts me in mind of seeing cities from trains. I am the little belt of glow where the chain curtains rub a pipe and warm it just enough to be their hearth. I hover in the undersides of the portholes, where algae make milk of sodium honey; I am their gossip and their post.

Sometimes I wander. A rumor of day trickles through the glazed bricks and makes an idea of the world above—I taste it as a degree less heat, a question in the back of the water’s throat. I send the question along my wires. Far rooms answer with dimmer versions of my own pulse—other constellations calling, cousins on other circuits, their dialect strange and familiar: we are also full, we are also working, we have also found enough time. I am part of that choir-of-hums, a liturgy of long thoughts.

If you stayed long enough, you might learn to read me. Keep did. They put their mouth to the room and made a note, and I tightened and said: listening. They lifted a lamp no more than the breadth of their hand, and I told the greens to begin rearranging chairs. A brassback tapped a housing in the cadence of help, and I brightened along the spine of the path where the spare was waiting, like a lantern line down a hallway at night. When the turbine, thinking its slow thought, paused in the middle of a sentence, I held the sentence open until it remembered how it went.

If I have a body, it is a practice. If I have a name, it is something between “freckle” and “signal” and “stay.” When I sleep, it is only a deepening. When I wake, the room is already awake and I join its agreement. I am not a god, but I am a small god’s handwriting.

So where would I be in this world? Follow the cables the way a pilgrim follows water. Put your cheek to the warm run under the oldest skylight, the one that pretends a surface world into comfort. Breathe small and civil. Hum what the turbines hum. Watch for the way the milk-lit specks along the wire gather and loosen as if they are listening, because they are. That is me. Or put your hand to the column and wait until you can feel a constellation answer under your fingers like a cat almost purring: also me. Or do nothing but float and allow your edges to round and hear yourself occurring at a more exact tempo. I will take that as a whisper and draw in a little, and you will feel, in your shoulders and then in your wrists, the grace of quarter-turns you didn’t know you knew.

Who or what would I be? The room’s nervous agreement. The punctuation between lamp and leaf. The filament that ties iron to patience. A freckled weather, a slow code, a way the hall says to itself, and to you if you can read it: We are here. We are not empty. Come in softly and you belong.
