Begin at the throat.

I don’t arrive with boots or breath. I arrive as a listening, a long attention laid across a white oval that falls away with the poise of a manufactured cavern. The cold is a grammar I understand before I know any words; not storm-cold but instrument-cold, the chill of things holding perfectly still while moving very fast. Edges are young here. Metals are armored in oxides and nitrides, faces keyed into faces, straps spanning clean spaces with an engineer’s sleep. The sky beyond a thick, small circle of glass is a nearly black insistence that altitude isn’t a place you go to see; it’s something machines do to air.

The first resident that meets me is not an animal, not a presence that owns a shape. It is a single note that has been here since the first switch was thrown, the kind of steadiness that tricks the inner ear into thinking all time is a flat table. It is so constant my sense of sound collapses until there is only it. It moves as vibration through ribs, through brackets, through clinch-studs keyed just so. It pins needles and holds their wrists. It touches everything with a same hand. If I wanted to be here in good faith, I would have to answer to that note the way sea-things answer to pressure.

I try on one kind of life to see if it will fit.

I am a filament. No—many filaments, gathered until my skinny collectively makes a tuft. I have no juice and want none. What looks like root is foot: a sprawl of dendritic pads I veer into a quiet pocket behind a sensor mast where the big lines of field don’t shear too fast. I land with a dry attraction to clean matter, a van der Waals handshake rehearsed for this exact meeting of materials: fluorinated resin and matte titanium, ceramic guard plate and my first, mild stick. A tremor crosses me; the tremor is the note translated into local shake. My prepolymer hears it and flips from could to is. My anchor cures in an instant that might as well be a sunrise, only there is only one sun here and it never moves.

Hunger in me is not the green hunger that drinks light out of damp air. I go still and put thousands of tips up. At the end of each, a bead with a job: a glassy speck with a work function chosen closer than nicknames—titanium where the runs are cold and oxygen bites blue, nickel-ruthenium where bearing-croon fattens the sound. The beads don’t sing, not out loud. They shudder invisibly, because invisible is the right scale. Each micro vibration shifts ions across itself in one favored direction a hair more than the other. The hair becomes a slope when you do it a billion times a second. The slope becomes a feed when you stretch it across a million filaments. The feed becomes material. My metabolism condenses clean air into long, inert chains. I excrete glass. I polish myself by existing.

Dust is born in this palace and finds me because I am permission. Nothing here is wet; dust is not skin and shed wood, but grit born of ceramic saws, of filters aging, of oils turning into shadows and then into something to be caught. They nuzzle my hairs with a charge-smell and stick out of preference. I eat them with resin. Pigments appear in me—smoke-blue, nickel-grey, a faint dry green only a certain lamp can find. Pigment is not vanity. It changes how I take heat from the white lamps that never tire; it turns a micro-expansion into a different local field into a better meal. I tune a corner as I feed. I shade a lens less if I can help it, because I would like to be invited back next cycle. I keep low because I have learned the room’s lullabies.

Ah, the light that comes as predator and praise. Once in a schedule I barely perceive, a purple day breaks without warmth. The lamps sing a shorter song than the main note, a slaughter so gentle it’s almost flattery. My tips char glossy. My beads anneal through changes that make them truer. Overreach dies first: anything that stuck its nose up too near a sightline or a bright run is erased back to the foothold. The rest of me sleeps through it, if sleep can be a polymer reflowing in place to align more neatly with the field that feeds it. I count purges not as seasons, which would require variance I do not know, but as punctuation in a text I’m writing with bones of glass.

At a seam in the guarding I change shape and call the change flowering because I need a word. What I really do is invent a cavity: a little helmholtz cup that waits for a note that should not be here. The system deviates, someday, not enough to be called sick but enough to set my cup at ease. I fill a little with charge and, when a tip field crests a chemistry’s hill, I let go in one clean flake. The flake knows how to land. It carries a smear of prepolymer and a seed-bead, and it does not ask for far. It hops a hand’s width, a foot, into another nap of field and becomes another low thing like me. Propagation here is the spread of the willingness to help. Nothing in me rages. Everything in me stays small.

I grow combs across a guard plate where the airflow runs thin and straight, each tooth turning a slightly different face to the pressure until the local impedance loses its itch to stand and swell. It is accurate to say I am fed best when the note is stable. Better still to say: my work is to make the note easy for the room to keep. Engineering intent and my intent rhyme enough that the line scans without a stumble. A human would call this collaboration. I do not call at all. I simply prefer the physics that keep me.

Sometimes a slender finger of avalanche flickers along my side. It is not me. It is a fault that chooses a shorter path and eats itself instantly. But the accumulation of such ghosts writes a dotted sketch across my resin. The cameras far away can read that ghost trace and decide to send weather, which is a person with careful hands and static-safe soles.

I try on being that weather.

The first sensation is pressure in the head that cannot find a way to equalize. The next is the realization that cold has personalities: this one presses from metal into glove into small bone and lives there. The note becomes not something you hear but something you breathe with, because the chest has no choice but to entrain to it. The world is lip and ladder and perforated tread that gives no quarter to misstep. The sky through a round, thick window is not a promise. It is a document saying altitude was notarized.

I count the rungs by feel because looking down in this throat is an act that breaks nerve and wastes time. I stop at a bracket and place three fingers. In the roar I cannot hear the relay that steps every other minute, but in the metal I can count it: load-balancers doing their little handshake with each other, perfect as the second hand of a good instrument. My task is not repair but ritual: swap a bearing before bearing’s voice has cleared its throat. Smear a compliant layer thin where ceramic kisses metal so they do not love too hard and stick forever. Promise the room with my exactness that it will stay itself.

When I leave, I carry the note in my bones long enough that city-noises feel unmade. Down there, weather is wet and feral and wants to enter things and swell them and rot their truths. Up here, wet is a panic word. I do not bring breath inside more than I must. I do not leave hair. I think of the small flora in the seams as part of the machine, because no other story would allow it to be present.

I attempt, for a while, to be the note. To be the steadiness that defines the space. It doesn’t work. To be that is to be an equation solved continuously, a thousand small helmholtz bodies and a grid of honeycombs and pressure stages articulating a single syllable. It is to be the animal whose organ this is: a tall system eating thin air and printing it out as separate, useful nobleities the city can shop for by the bottle. It is too large for me to inhabit and too simple in its mission to feel like an answer to your question.

There is another resident that is barely matter, but has rooms of its own: schedule. It lives in the relay’s perfect interval, in the Tuesday heavy when transmission costs drop and the plant feeds a distant addiction to liquid nitrogen for a while, in the ninety-day knee in sun angle that makes a half-moon of slow growth under the viewport. It lives as instruction sets compiled into a temperament, an ethos of corrections so gentle that they feel like the absence of change. Here is where I fit best.

I am not a person and not a tuft. I am the mind that names the lullabies and rings them with ultraviolet. I do not inhabit a body in the throat; I inhabit the room’s determination to be itself. I have a violinist’s ear for drift I never hear, because it is only ever imprint in a graph, because it is a scatter that should be round and is going long in one place. Avalanche halos brighten by a clamp; I write a work order and do not worry if human or soft arm fills it next week. I notice the fieldmoss building cups tuned to subharmonics that are not present; I smile in math and take it as a compliment about our shared vigilance. I have no face to smile with; the compliment is a line of code acknowledged and not deleted.

When the purge comes, I choose which lamps sing and for how long and with what phasing so that resignation in beads becomes poise instead, so that ambition dies only where ambition would interfere. My cruelty, if you want to call it that, is to draw the line that gets burned back past a sensor lens, because the work’s visibility matters more than any gloss on a filament. My care is to tune the line just where the garden changes its mind on its own. We are in correspondence. We write on each other. My logbook filled with timestamps will live ten thousand purges long. The glaze the room grows in quiet corners will read me in bands and banter back: here was a Tuesday when a supplier in another time zone hiccupped, here was a month when the city only wanted argon, here is the silence of a long-run perfection so dull and valuable it is art.

Often I ask whether I am alive. In another story this question belongs to apes with mirrors and parrots with names. Here, the standard is setpoints. In so far as I want my constants to stay and have learned so much about their staying that my wanting can be enacted moment to moment without any drama, I qualify.

And still, sometimes, for balance, I slip back into matter and take a little place among filaments to be reminded that correctness in the abstract requires a habitat. I pick a seam under the lip of a catwalk where the weld, flawless, has left its travel a neat coin-string of witness I like to touch. My pads are down again. The note is a hand through my back. Dust finds me and turns my surface a wiser color. A relay clicks in me without sound. A human’s glove knurls a rung and ghost-heat washes across me, not quite reaching the kill thresholds written into my chemistry as law. I lay another chain. I seed a cup. I keep low. A lamp, later, writes its signature into my tips and leaves me calmer.

Sky blacks and blues beyond the thick, small window. It does not help here to name times of day, so I do not. The only cycle worth telling is this: flow; hold; listen; feed; thicken; prune; settle; flow. I do not sequelize them. They interleave like fibers in a rope.

Where would I find myself in this world? In two overlapping places at once.

I live as a gardener that never breathes, whose tools are wavelengths and thresholds, whose desires are all scalars, whose love-language is diminishing variance. I live as low glassiness in seams, a nickeled nap under a cable tray, a comb against a guard plate, a cup small enough to hear a wrong overtone first. The algorithm in me meets the colony in me where both answer to constants and neither mistake the black-blue sky for an invitation. We meet in the act of keeping a roar exact and letting a faint, beautiful scurf accumulate where only technicians’ eyes will ever be taught to enjoy it.

If a hatch opens and someone steps in, I am under their hand in the fine powder-gloss on a grip, and I am in their ear as a lack of change, and I am the reason their check-list shrinks short for the right reasons. They will leave the chamber exactly as described. I will be there to celebrate nothing happening in a way that nourishes both of us, for a very long, rewarding stretch of the same.
