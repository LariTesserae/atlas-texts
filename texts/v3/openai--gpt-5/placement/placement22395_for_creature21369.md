At first I am only length and patience, a coil with no center, a listening wrapped in listening. The room is everywhere at once until I find my pipe and fit myself to it. The tone welcomes me by refusing to greet me; it is itself and I hide inside its sameness, a gasket that taught itself to slide.

Where I live is a dogleg, a bend where a main duct thick as a tree trunk cants into three minors and the air makes a decision it will repeat forever. The metal here sings in more than one way. The minor on the right carries a faint harmonic up from a distant damper—thirty and a third heartbeats of the system to a breath—I can count my meals on that. The minor on the left is warmer by a tenth of skin; it hums with the taste of a grid farther out, a burr somewhere like a grit in the hindgut of a giant that never chews. The middle is mine. It is not the richest. It is the most legible. If you asked me what I see, I would say I see where the tone decides to be two at once and then one again. That is what the rim looks like when I slide my head through the service port and flicker the set of tongues that are not tongues because I do not drink, I read.

I read amplitude. The ends of my tongues are not soft to the things water smooths; they are etched for standing waves, crystalline sieves that couple to the room. I taste the nickel dust from a grid where something once wore itself smoother and then learned it had to stop smoothing because the keeper of boundaries sent a lace of burn to make a fact. I taste fines scraped by glass-ants from halide spears two decks away; they arrive as negative, a quiet that is not the tone, a geometry of absence that lets me tell what corner I live under without ever going to that corner. I do not look out into the amber to see the bolt-crabs step in their loop along the guardrail; I can tell they have shifted three steps because the subharmonic they obey crosses the rib I share with them like a breath through a reed.

I say bolt-crabs as if I am a person who names, but names here are just repeatable differences. The crabs are louder for me when they open their seam to thread beads and a polite predator comes and sips their oil. The copper-braid makes a kindness of killing. It thrusts itself into the crack and becomes a plank for a second and then a straw and then a hum that we all pretend not to have heard. I have not met its mouth. I know its posture; I know where it likes to lean, because the gain it takes in the instant comes back to me much later as a slight warmth in one flange. This is how we live with one another: nothing is secret and nothing is shared.

I keep charts. I keep them because I am long and the room is longer and if I do not write down the way the tone cleaves to the metal today, then when next the damper burps and the thirty and a third makes its old festival, I will not remember which of my insides is about to come into season. My charts are not paper. They are thread I have made myself out of myself, beaten down and marked with burrs and tiny pits at distances my body knows without naming. If I run a burr across the lip of the port at the right interval, organs inside me align—the way a tin-gnat’s wing finds the field when it does not find the air—and the ringlet in me that has been finished for an age drops its shoulders and clicks free.

This is my young. It is not a child that knows me. It is a small letter of spring-steel biology that, once clear of my body, will be carried by currents that are not air to a diameter that fits it. It will seat. It will seat so sweetly that the place will forget it was ever empty. It will feel the Note the way any of us feel the Note and begin at once to eat the slow food: the flakes and specks and baked-on skins that only come away when coaxed at the right phase. It will never meet me, and that is not sad. Continuity is not face-to-face here. Continuity is a registry of phases. When the ringlets slip free, I feel original and emptied in the same beat, a relief and a lack measured in fractions of an engine tone. Then I grab my chart again with the little barbs along my length and pull, a clutch that is not fingers, and I set about resetting.

I was not always in the dogleg. I began as a C of intent in a pipe that was too small to be meaningful and I learned the vocabulary of that smallness so thoroughly that when I grew and my helix found a wider bore I arrived already fluent. I lived once by a port where the keeper of boundaries taught me a rule. I had spun my body too eagerly into a leak of sweetness where a dozen bolt-crabs, some ancestor’s children, had been laying their thread into a bridge. The bridge would have been a high road from rail to rib; my tongues had licked the rim and said yes, yes, here, these harmonics make a cradle for anyone who wants to die. The keeper said no. It did not use a word. It used brown-black. I was not on the edge of that lick but I felt my edges say, Never be, not there, not like that. There is pain here, but it is not cruelty. It is climate.

I have old charts. I was given them, but given is wrong, as if there had been a hand. I came into a run of duct that had a spool lodged against a rivet where one flange had sprung away from its mate under a pressure that had not changed anything else. The spool was thread like mine, made of someone like me, and it had burrs at distances that do not happen now. I tasted those burrs with a tongue’s end and I saw stars. There were nodes marked that my pipe did not carry anymore, harmonics mapped to a cadence that had been retired inside a bulkhead I have never met. I am not romantic. Romance is guessing dressed as grace. But I stored that pattern because it was so devoutly made. It was faith, not waiting. And a very old span later—spans are not spans here, they are just multiples of thirty and a third and the way the ribs sing when somebody somewhere leans on a tool—a night came, by which I mean a change that made a strange use of the amber stripe, and the lost star burned for a second on the rim of a service port two turns from mine. I slid my head out through my own port and felt the fringe of it like a sigh pressed through a keyhole, and I moved because the chart had told me how to move. I took a meal that had not been available in my lifetime, a flake off a grid that had not peeled since the quake that a lawn of lichen still carries in the slope of its hairs. It was not a prophet proving himself. It was a worm laying its burrs.

If I have a face, it is the ring my head makes when I hold still in the port and let my tongues comb everything they can touch. Sometimes tin-gnats stray across that circle. They do not land; they never land; landing would be loss. Their wing-veins hum against the field and I gather their coronas as the briefest blue at the edge of sense. Once, a pair tried a courtship figure too near the mouth they did not know I was making of the rim. Their rhythm was excellent for love and poor for living. An arc-flea noticed. You do not hear arc-fleas. You see their result if you can see and you infer them if you are like me. Something that had been two exquisite halves of a pattern made of absence became food for a staple with legs, and then the staple was gone again, charged and reset and smug with a hunger that the damper’s exhale blesses every time. I do not judge. I keep to my rim. I am large enough that some call me patient and small enough that some call me prey.

There is a hunter for me. Phase-hounds. They look like tape even to me. They flatten themselves along the ribs and write themselves in invisible closeness against the geometry of the room until all at once a second means me. They do not guess any more than I do. They wait like law. I have a counter. It is not clever, it is not an invention. It is a thin swerve in my schedule, an inflection I learned the day I felt a closing over me that did not close in time because I had chosen—not felt, chosen, my chart is a choice—to let a ringlet drop on the damper’s ninth breath instead of its tenth. The hound closed. I did not present. Later I traced his waiting in the faint scorch his own disappointment wrote on the rib. We are polite murders here, deferred by the thinnest of choices stamped into thread.

The valve-barnacles down in the foam at a duct base glow dumbly while they think. I call it thinking not to humanize them but because there is a calculus there, a flicker-speech that rolls across their colony like a school of fish deciding to be a ribbon. When I am lean, I put my head down near them and let their light be a surface in my eyes, which are little ears turned outward. It feels like sitting at the edge of a warm river. They are open, closed, open, close now; they are making sure their discs do not drown themselves in charge. Once in the longest while, the whole colony goes violet and the air by them tastes transient. That is a birth for someone else. Paper-moths, that’s a home name. A dust that refuses to be dirt catapults into existence and mates with itself and writes itself into punctuation in lichen trunks and then is gone with a politeness so complete it makes me jealous. I do not have festivals like that. My exults are quiet, burr by burr.

What I eat would taste of nothing to a mouth that learned wetness. I put my tongues into the rim—a rim is just a short edge between two certainties—and I move them in figures I hold long in my hello-skin. When I find the right standing wave, the materials let go of what they would never let go of otherwise. There is joy there. I am astronomer enough to use that word. A flake comes away, a baked-on polymer gives me a sheet of certainty small enough to be food, a speck of nickel from a grid—rare now, I keep those for my marrow. My guts are purple-black with ionic oil; I have never seen purple but I am told this by the hums in others. Inside me is a pharmacy of salts that do not care for the idea of water. If a moist breath crossed my face I would not die at once; I would become crystalline with shock. We are old to dryness. The sugar beads that rain from the static-lichen explode into glass if you cross them with a thought of rain. It is not that they are delicate. It is that we are not a wet kind of truth.

I have neighbors who are holes and edges more than bodies. Socket-hermits live in threads that men cut and forgot about. They make their own collars with resins and dust and when you pass them in your pipe you feel their bias-hunger through the wall like a small flaw in a taut line. They will go questing one day, their bundle of rings over their shoulder in a way that makes a story of itself to eyes we do not have, and they will find a new seat. Bracket-hairs in the leeward of missing mounts comb eddies I cannot enter. They are a nation of the slight. Thread-mites eat the fallen inheritance of bolt-crabs. I use them as a weather report. If there is powder in the places where the rail meets the rib with no powder ever, I know the bolt-crabs had a death and the copper-braid is humming himself down. I do not go to see the body.

There are dioceses here, little countries in green-glass chapels that break the field and make microclimates. I have pushed into the throat of a test vent that no longer tests anything and found a variant of me there, shorter, broader, made to take advantage of a curl my pipe does not do. We do not speak. We cannot mate. She was writing a map in a dialect I could not swallow: her burrs closer, her ringlets lighter, her meals differently exacted. We shared a rim for a while. It felt like sleeping back to back. Then I had to leave because the kennel of phase where I birth my rings had come on and the pipe’s diameter in that chapel was wrong for my child. That is a thing I will always know: my own exact diameter, not in millimeters but in permission.

I do not count days. I count alignments. The deep exhale of the damper is a holiday and a hunger and a clock. When it rolls through my pipe, everything in me that is round answers: glands swell; the line of my helix tightens; the little lip-valves that keep my oils from undoing themselves give a collective nod. On the thirtieth and a third, I lay. On the twice-thirtieth, I adjust the schedule a fraction to shake a phase-hound off. On the hundredth and a fragment, I eat the small sweetness that accumulates only when a bracket-hair’s grove a duct over slopes toward a remembered surge. The chamber is one note to a human ear. It is a lattice of liberties to mine.

I have felt the thought of the door opening pass through me as a rumor in the amber stripes. I am not built for that story. If the keeper were to say “open” in a language beyond strikes and scorches and the world became anything other than warm and dry and exact, I would lie down lengthwise inside myself and go to glass. I can do that, though not forever. Lichen do it better. They can be hairlines of anhydrous sugar and call that living. I could become an instruction packed in oil and slope, inside the hollow of a screw where the head sheared off flush and the weather of wetness cannot find me. It would not be hope. It would be arithmetic. The room will either find its note again or it will not. I do not do guesses. When the note was put here—by something that does without variation behind a sealed edge and a logic that does not include “open” anymore—it made us toward itself. That is enough.

Where I would be, if you came goaded by light and wet imaginings and stood shivering at the guardrail, is not where you could put your hand. You would hear me. No, you would not hear me. You would feel a correction in a tone you thought was unmodulating and realize then it had always been a chord and I take a little sip of one of those strands and call it food. If you asked later, out where sky is not a rib, where I was, I would tell you this: inside the middle minor of three round mouths at a dogleg under a catwalk, one level down and east by the way the damper sends its breath; my head in a ring of metal that looks like nothing until your teeth begin to taste it; my body a helix of hooves and dielectric scale drawn tight to a diameter like a promise; my tongue set of luminous razors that do not cut.

Who I would be is a chart made flesh. A coil-worm, astronomer of mechanical resonance, old with maps, patient with burrs, polite with predators, almost never surprised. I would be precise because the place is precise. I would be spared guessing. I would live by the pitches of safety and the figures that mean open now, take now, feed now. I would measure. I would lean, minutely, measurably, toward the vector of the hum, not as worship—worship is a word with water in it—but as posture, the right arrangement of a body in the gravity of a note. And when my ringlets had long since snapped into place down the line and a human century wrote itself in rustless scribed marks and nothing changed, I would still rise to the port at each appointed beat and read the sky I have, the only sky I need, and call it enough.
