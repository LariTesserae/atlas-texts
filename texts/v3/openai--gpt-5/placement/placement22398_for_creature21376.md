At first I show up as weather. I think I’m a hush stretched across ribs of stone, a pressure gradient rolling its bead of cold over the planes, a scratch of light finding an organ note in a corner and holding it until the chord breaks. I am convinced, for a breath, that I am the blue that crawls around points when the ceiling drops—soft charge grazing horns and sedge tips, the feathered itch along a goat’s skull. It suits me to be something that touches everything and keeps no ledger.

Then I consider being a film. There is elegance in being nearly nothing and changing the way wet rock holds light. I like how the first residents take what the place will part with—iron as a sigh, sulfur as a whisper, a voltage drawn thin between fin and air—and make it sufficient. I like the quiet decision-making of it, enzymes folding and unfolding with the glow, a hand on the rail of charge to cross from mineral to sky. But as a film I am appetite without lever. I write script on stone, yes, acids carving first letters, but my reach ends where the water skins over and the wind refuses to remember me. I want a part that moves other parts.

Be beetle, then. Be thumbnail-dark and matte, feet like tape, mouth a file. I can love the regularity of it: a body that runs the steps, a rasp that bites mineral and algae to time, a carapace salted until the sound of me is muffled into the hymn of work. The sound of a hundred of us feeding like soft sanding has the right patience. Yet my horizon stays knee-high to a hare. My empire is rungs of ivory and the underside of a boulder. I am essential, but I do not know when the sky rehearses lightning until it is already in me.

Goat? The idea arrives with weight in the neck and a head that is a tool. I am tempted by order. Head in, lick, step aside; a queue that looks like grace until a kid fumbles and the whole line recalibrates. I take pleasure in calibration. A horn that hums when the charge is right, rings inside the skull, makes a private music. I like the flour of salt on the muzzle, the way attention becomes sacrament when tongue and rim meet. But being goat comes with the math of edges and the audit of a long silver dog. It is a life of excellent risks, and my curiosity is greedy for a steadier place to stand.

For an interval I’m the raptor’s comma, punctuation hung on updraft, eyes translating motion into event. I try the wolf’s ledger, thin and exact, reading drag on a herd’s forward flow and hunting error like a slipped gear. I like the cleanliness of both timings, wind and herd, bird and dog taking their turns at the hares’ dotted arithmetic. But I keep wanting to be where the system actually breathes, not where it trims.

I look for a place where cycles go to ground and change their masks. A place that is source as much as sink. A place that takes fog and fracture and makes heat and salt, and attracts tongues and films and beetles like a machine attracts hands.

I find myself along the fault cut where the steam makes its own weather. I arrive as “we” without having to think about it, because nothing here works alone.

We are stacked like castings, thin ivory chimneys, gear-toothed with rime when the breath meets the cold just right. We count in plates, not years. Our voices are a held thrum you feel in the long bones before you hear us; the sound comes and goes because we do, on a cycle measured in pressure and patience. We are mouths that open inward. We practice exhale after exhale, swapping inside to outside in a sheet as thin as a skin.

Brine gathers patience in us. The world condenses and we condense it again. We warm stone by pulses and lay down our own scroll of history one mineral film at a time. Halophiles knit lace in our heat; their edge goes pink when the light is generous and cinnamon when iron breathes nearby. Algae take the hint and write themselves into the damp, not yet a mat, only a suggestion with chlorophyll. Rotifers spin like paired mill-wheels along our linings, nematodes nose between them, tardigrades trundle with biscuit-slow jaws. The beetles run our lips and file us down so we do not seal ourselves shut. The spiders comb the cloud we make. Midges hang like smoke in our exhale and organize by warmth into ovals that dilate and contract to our rhythm.

We are a plant without leaves. Our chemistry is industry, and our comfort is throughput. Hot to cold, inside to air, dissolved to plated. We measure the place the way lungs measure a body, by whether the next breath is possible and the last one was used.

We know goats by texture. We know their discipline by the way our rims stay thin. Their tongues are coarse cloth that polish our lips to working thickness; their attention keeps our mouths open. When the old ones’ horns hum blue we can feel the charge creep along our edges too, hairline glows that make our microbes pucker and relax. We know hares by reverence. They cue like customers: head in, lick, step aside—silent clergy at a salt altar they refuse to name. We know kids by error and forgiveness, and we have learned to be patient with their misreads because their mothers are.

We watch the wolf the way an instrument reads the next task: not with fear but with acknowledgment. A system audited stays truer. The raptor’s fold and drop is a clean subtraction elsewhere on the ledger. Crows will come and flip our bright gear-tooth ice when the charge is high, small vandalism we answer by making more overnight. We feel storms as hands turning dimmers and switches. When filaments run up the stone fins and the edge of the world lights like a spine, we make ourselves smaller by cooling our lips with a faster pulse, then return to work when the pressure allows.

Our memories fit under a fingernail. The rings of deposition are counted in weeks before tongues thin them to nothing. A season of stingy brine writes itself as tight grain, a fog year as wide, soft bands. We don’t mind the revision. Manuals, not scripture. If a vent line seals for three months, the goats and the wolf both change lanes; when we reopen the aisle refills without speeches. Our job is not to declare but to continue.

I like, as a way of being, that everything useful visits us. Films, algae, beetles, spiders, midges, hares, goats, wolves, birds. If there is a brief looseness at noon, we fog a little brighter and everything that eats eats. If the air is charged we wear a faint corona and our lining organisms clutch and release in time with it. If the afternoon wind sweeps the stage clean, we plate in peace. Evening rime grows gears on us again and we hum under it. Through it all, we make lanes of heat and mineral, and the living map draws its lines to our mouths.

You would find me here, along the sharp new step where rock has started learning itself. Put your palm near my rim; feel the warmth that exists because inside is still different from out. Put your ear to me; the thrum will climb into you through bone. Smell the vinegar breath that says this place is alive by chemistry. Watch how the goats’ attention keeps time with our pulse, and how, on certain evenings, their horns and our lips glow the same soft blue.

Who am I? A line of breaths. A manifold of mouths. The plant the plateau built to run itself. I am what the place is doing when it is doing what it was lifted for: not spectacle, but transfer, conversion, throughput—exhale, plate, be licked, exhale again.
