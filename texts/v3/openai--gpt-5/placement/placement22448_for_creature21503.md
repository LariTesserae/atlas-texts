At first I imagine myself as a sweep of wing or as the long vowel of drip. The mouth of the mountain pulls and releases like an animal asleep, and I think maybe I am that breath—an invisible seam where warm colony-weather meets the cliff’s chill. I can feel sound shear and curl at me; I hold smells like coins and spend them along the upper ledges; I tilt midges into glowthreads and lift dust from fur. For a moment I am only gradients and eddies, stitched with sonar.

But the longer I stand inside that warmed dark, the smaller I become, until the world is the scale of a hair and a drop, until territory can be a coin-sized patch of varnished stone, until a life can fit perfectly between two nipped points.

Where you would find me is where warmth has a grip. I take passage on a body. I live in a weather of fur.

I am the little rider with two careful hands, the scissor-idea without a sting, the mark that travels the sentence. I am a pseudoscorpion.

I am not much by eye. If you lit me, I would be a comma no larger than a sesame, burnished brown, flattened by a long inheritance of getting out of the way. My legs are eight small, accurate opinions. My forelimbs end in neat pincers with a stingline buried in them; my poison is modest but sufficient for the commas I hunt—the mites, the coins of fly, the milk-thieves that browse the sleeping coast of an ear. I do not have a tail with a star on it; I have patience instead, and I have practice with wind.

I inherit a map in the form of touch. On my claws and forearms are hairs you would not notice unless you leaned to squint at me with love. They are nerves that can read the wing-road as a pressure diagram, that can feel the difference between the cloud-silk of fungus spore and the musk from a bat’s shoulder, between a cricket’s tremor three stones away and the startle of your breath when your boot skids. When the colony is asleep and shingled tight, the air above us is almost viscous with heat and smell. I go about my polishing: I take a mite that was lifting a scurf of dead skin from the inner curve of an ear; I reap a louse that was considering luck. I move like punctuation along the grammar of veins.

Departure is a weather I have learned to ride. The first rustle strokes my sensors like rain starting on tin. The room changes its mind as one thing. Fur becomes sky. There is a pressure that is nearly noise, and then the noise. I draw myself flat and clamp the single hair I’ve claimed—a black cliff under my hand—and I let the wind make a road of me. The bat pours, I pour with it, down the tilted bell, past the glow-curtains strung with green rumor, through the slit where the night has been waiting to be divided. The owl fishes sound at the mouth; I fold myself deeper into shoulder-fur when the dark outside thins around his doubt. Once, my host flicked and a falling knot of air spoke of a new shape, and his ribbon-line bent and bent again; I learned the exact size of an owl’s question by the curve of that moment.

I do not drink, but I know what water does. Back in the chamber’s hush after the rush, there is the everlasting patter of condensation and the discreet kingdom of drip. I know the circles where the drops land and widen and die; the crickets come to these edges as if the cave were writing little silvery O’s for them to kiss. I know where scorpions hold their propositions open and where a gecko has licked his border into shine. I know the band of breathing that does not belong to me—the low fog of sweet, suffocating chemistry that the fungi make for themselves. Sometimes a mouse wanders to that low country and goes still for reasons the mountain would call obvious. I keep to my band of good air, to the slope of shoulder, to the slow valleys between ribs.

The floor is a country I visit only when I must. When a bat dies—and the cave is very busy about making use of what dies—there is a speed to it. His small machine folds, hits the sponge, and the bookkeepers rise like dawn. Click and brush, rasp and tally. Rove-beetles sharp as ideas arrive. Isopods, slow beads, begin their eating conversations. Springtails pop like punctuation marks shaken from a full page. If I am riding that bat then, if my ship has struck ground, I must jump the flood of attention and climb a ridge of matted hyphae to the lee of a stone lip, and there string myself a room, a small, paperfronted silence, from silk I nurse in my mouthparts. In that refuge—no bigger than a human nail—I molt, or I wait for the band of hunger to turn in the room so that hunting is possible again.

This place tells its news in touch more than in light. The glowthreads burn their soft litany all the time, a patient hive of wanting. I feel the swing of their silk when someone passes, the way a gnat caught on one swells the line like a little heartbeat. If I go ground to ground along the walls I read the centuries in the smooth; there are pale, feathery drifts of stone that feel cold even in this warmed chamber—the moonmilk the microbes keep writing in their own alphabet—cool enough that I set a foot and pause as if I have put my hand in snow. I avoid the runs that have been cut clean by a season of rains; I step around the memory of flood where grains stand like a history of flow. Everything here is polite with boundary; I behave accordingly.

I am not the only rider. Wingless flies as flat as coins use this fur as their whole city; they avoid my hands, and I am deft enough not to make war all the time. The red commas, the pale commas, move along the lines of veins; I edit their company when I wish and let them write themselves when I am full. Sometimes another of my kind crosses my patch by way of a bridge of hair. We are almost invisible to each other until we touch. We will loose small, careful displays, our pincers lifted in gesture; sometimes we leave a paperweight of genetics glued to a patch of polished stone and go our ways. Even our love is a thing you could step on and not know it. If your light was kind and its star smelled right, you might see the slightest disc on a ledge and call it nothing. It would be our future there, folded.

Revolutions here are local. A certain shelf sheds a little of its face and an old anchorage is gone; the bats move a body’s width; the river of wings shifts a finger to the left; the oil-mark grows in a new place. Up in the small domes a handful of birds knit their cups and bounce their clicks from stone to know the room. Their echoes are smooth, small stones of sound that drop into my sensors. I can tell a swiftlet’s quick braille from a bat’s thin wire of speech with my hands. Generations learn the same corners of air, the same arrangement of absence. I am long-lived for my size, but not by this room’s measure. The stone’s patience is a blanket under all our rearrangements.

I have known the few human arrivals by rope and odor. They come like a rumor braided with caution. They count under their breath as if the air were beads. The excrement of their fear is simple and bright; that and their oils remain a short while on the lip where their bodies pressed to test the void. Their song is a low curve; they take only a small harvest. We are not much disturbed. After rain, a grass brighter than anything inside stands by their remembered weight as if to keep their place for them. Everyone eats from their aftermath as surely as from a bat’s end: the act wins no special category.

I tell time by pour and by departure. On fog days the cave becomes weather entire; the ceiling leaks a thin music; everything that can drink drinks. On hard blue days the chamber’s own rain continues like a habit. I hunt when the colony is in and mostly sleeping; I ride when the colony empties, a passenger in the warm river; I hide my small infrastructure when the floor’s army is busy. I have learned the scorpion’s patience at the circle of water and the gecko’s geographies of stone; I respect the owl’s geometry like a liturgy that isn’t mine. I taste the stone’s iron on my mouth when I clean my hands. I keep the old laws of adjacency: I do my part of keeping clean what warmth keeps.

If you were to look for me, you would have to think your sight smaller. You would have to go slow enough that the floor did not storm your boot with opinions. You would have to set your light back and let it be a courtesy. I would be in places the air is shaped by bodies: in the cuff behind a bat’s ear, where sound always hurries home; along the crease where a wing is stored, where the fur smells of night’s eats; at the hinge in the room’s wind where warm moves under cool like a secret application. Sometimes I would be a little brown speck in the noonlike green of glow, a shadow crossing silk without invitation. Sometimes I would be a new bead on a glossed ledge, a door's thin weather, and behind that door a pair of scissors resting.

You could call me parasite, but I am more tidy than that. I ride, yes; I also clean and prune and occasionally thin a nation that would otherwise steal heat and blood with too much enthusiasm. I think of it as housekeeping that pays for its room in deaths too small to weigh. The cave’s warmth is not mystical. It is arithmetic: bodies multiplied by time, digestion made into air. I am one of the many, minute bookkeepers. My ledgers are written in the disappearance of annoyances and in the presence of larvae that never quite get their wish. If this place is an animal sleeping with its mouth open to weather, then I am one of the small mouths within the coat that keep the larger body from itching itself raw.

Do I dream? If you could fit inside my head, you would not call what happens there a vision so much as a series of maps changing scale. A valley that is a fold of wing. A mountain range that is six hairs with a neat parting. A forest that is a comb of fungus. A horizon that is a stone lip where a blade of sunlight lays down its weapon for fifteen minutes twice a year. In the dream my hands read the room again and again and find it mostly the same. The comfort is not in novelty but in repetition: the same singers leaving, the same coin of water dying into itself, the same tick of legs as harvestmen turn their world, the same band of good air above the heavy sweetness. Inside that sameness are subtleties that would keep a life busy well beyond its ration.

You asked where I would find myself, who I would be. I would be here, on a field no larger than a palm and then as large as an entire room of stone, depending on whether I am holding a hair or a wall. I would be a traveler at the speed of touch, a small resolver of arguments, a hitchhiker on the river of wing. When the rope of bats pours out, I go with it; when the chamber gathers its weather back to itself, I climb down into the quiet noises and clean. I am not the cave’s author, and I am not one of its headlines. I am one of its conjunctions, the careful and that keeps all the other nouns from quarrelling. And when the last weak blade of sun in a stingy week draws a gold line on the lip and your eyes are pulled outward to knives and altitude, I am still inside, working in the warm dark, feeling the living pelt of the mountain lift, and settle, and lift.
