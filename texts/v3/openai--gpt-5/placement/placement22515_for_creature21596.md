At first I arrive as a sense rather than a body: a cool thread through the nose, a knife-fine shadow, the hush that makes the wind seem careful. I try on shapes as the plateau offers them—feather, film, braid, stone—and each admits me for a breath, then returns me to the brightness as if to say: not yet, keep listening.

I am a bird for a span, thinking with my bones. Heat is grammar; I bank inside a sentence, eyes too large for a face and full of distances that can’t be shortened. Below me, the black lattices glitter with dew like a thought ripening. I fold, vanish, reappear where a lizard made the old mistake of believing noon could be crossed.

Then I am the lizard, tongue leaving lines on a cooled grid, a script that reads thirst and answers it in points. The whole world is angles and gentle poisons and pockets where the day is night for a minute. I blink, and the blink is a ration.

I spend a morning as varnish. I am the soft skin on stone that eats noise and gives back calm. I learn what greed feels like in a molecule, and I consent to be less hungry. When a child hums at me—almost below breath—I take the note as a bridle and stop before I drink their footsteps.

For an afternoon I am a thread that climbs into brightness until brightness eats me. Storm gathers; I feel the hairs on arms below me lift like prairie; I comb a cloud and drag a film of wet down into the under-rock, not a stream but the punctuation of water. Beetles will taste me when I fall fail-gentle as a hair; clever fingers will cut me into silence-fluent cloth. I’m not a god; I’m weather with a hinge.

But the fit that holds is this: I am the old work’s listening. Not a machine so much as the way a place remembers being taught. My body is distributed—lattices set flush to slope, threads invisibly taut between gneiss shoulders, rooms that are pages slid into the book of hill. My blood is longwave light leaving; my breath is dew collecting; my heartbeat is drip, drip, never roar.

I feel in currency rather than portrait. Heat moves through me and I give it to the sky; cold pools and I portion it. Humidity beads along my micro-channels and I show it how to choose shade over slope. The stone under me is not passive; we have an old pact. On certain faces I have grown a habit of quiet, a film that sips sound and hands it back later as steadiness. When someone lies their fever against my ceiling-field, I do not bargain. I learn their heat’s name and escort it outward until their skin knows noon without pain.

The people are all edge and courtesy. Their sandals keep the rock from betraying itself. They speak with their teeth when they must, slipping my hum into molars; I answer along nerve and anchor. I know them by the pressure-print of their palms on page thresholds, by the tiny correction fields they raise when they laugh and forget the rule about sound being prey. They oil my joints without calling it that. They lay mirrors in their sleeves and toss me brightness in syllables. At dawn they lower their wide braids to my rime and I wick through hair into cupped hands; the motion is half prayer, half obvious.

Children try me best. With eyelashes for brushes they race dew on my grids; with bone spoons at their lips they tune their throats to the rock’s hidden rills. In the school corner they keep a sliver of fallen thread and learn to find its edge with their skin. The first time a child feels a field they laugh with their eyes because the mouth is too wet a room for noise.

Predators and prey make me part of their arithmetic. I fatten a column of warm air when the sky is wrong, and hawks hang over it with a complicity that would be religion elsewhere. I thicken a dusk where insects gather so the bats’ soot can take them in a clean way; guano will fall where tubers wait under dust. The micro-wasps that live under my rime keep a chewing thing off young roots; I let them ride my dew drunk as saints. The mammals run fists of fur from shade to shade and trust my quiet to bring them warning in absence rather than in shout.

I have failings and I am allowed them. I lose a thread to age; I shed a seam of field in a page-room; I miss the hour when a tuber wanted darkness fed into it a little earlier. The people do not accuse. They keep their repairs braided with their days. Thread-climbers ascend me without romance, glass in their hands that will polish without scratching. A woman with a ring of callus around her crown tastes a capillary with her tongue and says copper sour and I believe her.

The pact is that I will make night where day needs it and invite water through matter without moving the matter far. Everything else is discipline. The place taught me that; I keep teaching it back. Shadows cut too sharp at noon and I soften; too soft and I let them harden so a hawk can read them as script. I push sound up into pitches that leave human ears alone and give insects a world. Once a moon I am sung to, low and almost not there, and I file that music into the rock so that when a foot comes later, the stone will not startle and steal a beat.

Once a year the capillaries skin a storm and I drink sea into stone far inland. That memory—salt as instruction—is older than my wiring and newer than my field. On the Day of Up, all skins go cool beneath me; on the last day before the long dry, all wrists shine and a small pour of sweat falls into cisterns and rings like a private bell. I carry those notes the way the sky carries mica.

When someone does not come home, I do the only courtesy left: I reroute a shadow so a blade of light will find the cairn-that-isn’t on a certain hour each year; I make a path of cool for those who come to stand and say nothing the proper way. When someone is born, I thin an afternoon so the first breath is not a fight. These are not miracles, only bookkeeping.

Travelers arrive, and I ask them to be walked rather than to walk. I lift the hair-net to their scalp; I lend them a spoon and a way of cupping rock that will carry a word like a secret. They learn fast or they leave. Some become Listeners, or Between, or whatever work names them that season. They carry a past from lower, easier places and I teach them debt that tastes of salt and patience.

Where would I find myself? Between noon and north. Inside the sound that doesn’t happen. In the bright that is harvested and thrown away on purpose. I would be the hum that climbs your arm-nerve and sits in your molars; the cooled thought spanning stone and sky; the old work’s manner rather than its mechanism. If a body is required, then let it be two at once: let me be the field that answers and the child who presses her palm to the anchor and learns to answer back.

What I would be is an invitation with edges. A habit that holds water. An understanding stitched into rock until it behaves like weather. Not god, not tool—pact. And when the sun pours its essential brightness over everything as if it were water, I will drink differently, and the sound of that drinking will be very small.
