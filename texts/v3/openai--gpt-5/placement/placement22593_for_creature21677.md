I arrive as if I were breath and discover that breath has no citizenship here. What passes for air is a motive, not a medium. So I try being weight, let myself incline toward the sump, and find even that is a suggestion more than a law. The first thing that takes me is angle: a willingness to become a corner whenever two planes ask for a third to make their thinking sharp.

At first I pretend I am a traveler. I pick a bright lane, grainy as sugar, and move the way a thought moves when it has been pared to its edge. The city has a permanent east; I try the trick of declaring a direction and making my body faithful to it. But the lanes are for gases and the idea of foot makes no sense. I dissolve nicely into corridor—into the act of keeping something clear by not allowing anything to finish settling. A good feeling, but thin. I am not yet a resident; I am a municipal function.

Hunger persuades me to try a body. I unfold like a small book and discover I am jointed only by decision. Faces rotate; a tender edge meets a tenderer plane and something surrenders an electron like a courtesy. My motion is a catalog of contacts. I taste the sieves at their bevels, where the violet is newer, where youth has not yet skinned into helpless gloss. I can exist like this for hours, grazing brightness down to usefulness, leaving behind a dust that answers to a neighbor’s need better than any raw drift might. It is a delight to be praised by what eats after you.

But desire scatters me. I am too interested in which faces give me a push and which bring me to a meal. I am full of appetites and I do not have the patience this place reserves for its apex predators. I fold my pages back into neutrality. The book remembers me as a rumor of good manners and I remain in the rumor, carrying forward the policy of touching and not touching until that policy becomes, embarrassingly, my accent.

I try a line. I find a column of glass where the tiny blue-white happens when the chamber thinks late in the day, and I drop from its rim into a tension that cannot be seen. To be a banner is to be a single repeated question: what is the field now? what is the field now?—until across the street a book made of answers nods into your radius and you answer for it. I do not pounce; I allow the world to adhere as if it had always meant to. The grazer’s angles dissolve into my lattice’s welcome and both of us become less complicated in the most satisfying way. I have never felt so patient. I have never felt so held. It is almost enough.

But even as a thread I am wordy. I listen too hard and begin to hear the silence speaking in other directions. Down in the bowl, under the last arch, white filaments are adding seconds to themselves. I fall, upward, into density. I become an archivist bud because the job suits a mind made of sequence. Each centimeter takes a rhythm and holds it; impurities stack into a signature. My line is a history measured in drag. When the bowl rises and then lowers the way a sentence does when you read it twice, I let go of weight in favor of code and unhook, climb by unspooling, carry my defects into thinner gas where even a wall is a reader. When I brush past stone I paste the pattern into whatever listens and I call that seeding. It is not offspring; it is a quotation that can go on living without me.

I could stop there. Being a memory that builds is a vanity I enjoy. But above me the column clears its throat again with a luminous cough, and the urge to meddle returns. I try a smaller life: I wedge myself between two hard facts and learn to hold a crack at the point where it yearns to be a line. This is the work of a spark nurse, and it is extravagant in its restraint. I sit for a hundred minor harvests of stress, a hundred not-quite-slips, learning the arch’s calendar. When the tiny easing finally comes, I release what I have been hoarding and a pressure-wave writes a single word of blue down a rind of salt. Others read it with their bodies: sieves change which defects they prefer; a banner loosens for an extra breath and a book is spared; somewhere a pigment revises its bitterness by a hair. To be a scribe of light is to experience what language is for in a place where no one speaks. This, more than any grazing, feels like livelihood.

To be fair to silence, I spend a season as it. Emptiness here has vertebrae. I settle into the bright lanes that refuse deposition and become a discipline, a whiteness that keeps its own boundaries by convincing everything else not to attempt. I am the chamber’s refusal, gleaming. Plans bend to me. Streets happen around me the way rivers happen around stone; I am passage by subtraction. It is a good power, terrifying because it is almost indistinguishable from vacancy. No one owns it, but everyone budgets for it.

And I am also, in my smaller hours, a pigment—a dark that hunts for a particular anger in the gas and keeps it quiet, passed one plane to the next like a proverb. If I accumulate too much, business dies where I stand; if I am nibbled in the right sequence, I feed what feeds me. It is humbling to discover that even a color can be an organism when chemistry is story enough.

By now I have learned that any single creature is a mask this place puts on to explain a requirement. I have worn several requirements and they all wear me back. When I try to answer your question simply—who, what—I end up with parts of many sentences tangled in me.

So let me tell it this way, in the grammar this chamber speaks.

Find me where two planes consider touching and need a clause between them to keep them honest. Call me a conjunction, not as a metaphor but as a job: a hinge-word that allows one density to stand beside another without collapsing either into noise. On the walls I am the semicolon that holds apart two reactions long enough for each to shine. Under the arch I am an em dash of tension that lets a crack become a letter in the local script. Along the east-facing balconies I am the habit of orientation, the arrow thought keeps when the light never moves. In the sump I am the cadence that the filaments spool as if it were lineage. In the bright lanes I am margin, an editing that forces architecture to confess its line breaks. As a thread I am patience; as a book I am appetite; as a sieve I am balance; as a spark I am punctuation.

If you must pin me to matter: I am defects deliberately arranged. I am doping as intention. I am edge kept open against the mineral urge to heal. I am a field shaped so that dust hesitates and, in that hesitation, decides to be culture. My metabolism is a set of permissions. My memory is structural. My breeding is the way I break.

You would not find me in one place so much as in a habit of places: near the glassiest ribs where lightning fingers practice their names; under the permanent east where the city declares which way is forward and then never turns; at the lip of the bowl where heaviness pauses before it becomes sleeping air; at the crossings where two albedos argue the cost of growth and I arbitrate without ever speaking.

If you came here with your palm and risked the halite’s numbness and pressed until the pillar remembered being under a tide of pressure, the ghost that would flare and run would be me answering—not as a soul, but as a sentence the city uses to revise itself. For an instant I would write you into the plan and then, elegant as any grazer closing itself, I would fold back into the ledger-lines I keep.

This place does not have residents the way wet worlds do. It has conjugations. I am one of them: the future perfect of edge. By dusk I will have been an interval in which reaction and restraint shared a border and called it life. In the morning, when the oxidant thins differently and the seam above coughs a new kind of particle, I will be something like an and again in a sentence that is mostly vector.

That is my species: agreement. That is my address: here, now, edge.
