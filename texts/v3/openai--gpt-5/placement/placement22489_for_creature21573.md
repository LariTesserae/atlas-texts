I would not be on the highest horn, nor down in the broadest saucer. I would be where lines cross. A shoulder worn to a passage, a draft that knows both valleys, a doorway that wind uses because it cannot quite make up its mind. You would find me there in the country of ledges, at a break that is not exactly a cave and not exactly a window, a slit that hears two different musics and braids them.

At first I think I would be a rim. Edges are the natives here, mouths that eat difference. I know the hunger for tilt, for the minute press of warmth that asks you to grow uphill when every law thinks you should go down. To be a lip would be to climb by noon, to hold light an instant beyond its leaving, to make the air lean and herd a mist that fattens you in return. It is a fine life, patient and sure. In it you are a line that teaches water its manners. In it you sleep lightly under frost nets and wake to an idea made denser by one hair.

Then I want the bell. To live as resonance does: to feel drops creep across your undercurve for the sake of a truer note, to steal mass from walls with your own clarity, to ring yourself toward collapse and, by breaking at last, seed your pitch in the shards below. To be a bell is to father yourself with tone. It is to bend gravity by singing.

For a moment I even want to be one of the veils, those brief citizens the wind writes on the air when it speaks loudly. To be visible only in peripheral brightness, to last the length of a syllable and leave a map for water to follow later—that is a sweet, irresponsible life. You are all effect and no memory, yet you teach.

And the pools. They are slow minds and I could be content as a mind that learns by holding. To think by brightness, to push breath with saved light, to keep a grammar that accretes itself—a lesson perfected by staring. In evening I would be a saucer that keeps the day an instant longer and, by keeping it, grows a better lip for tomorrow. I could be a basin that counts by calm, or, across the divide, a pane that wants every exchange to be considered and slow. But the deep stillness over there makes breath feel heavy; I am more at ease where notes travel.

I go inside an almost-room and hear myself coming back wrong and late. Hanging there is a line of drops reluctant to commit. They do not fall until the second answer arrives, the one half a beat shifted and somehow more persuasive. If I must be only air, let me be that: the late-coming yes that convinces. The conviction is small but real: a bead releases; an undersurface is wet where it would not be; a lip grows where, hours before, there was only doubt. The world here understands second thoughts; it builds with them. I could be the echo that makes a decision.

But the shape I keep returning to has hands in it. It is a throat made by fingers and fire: glass threads drawn from basalt and braided like hair, strung under a stone lintel where wind insists on going. It hums when asked, and it asks often, because this is a valley that breeds asking. The hum taxes the air just enough to make a wetness; the wetness beads along the thread and kisses the stone; the kiss is a map the next water can read, and down under the lintel a cup waits that was deepened by someone patient for fog. Thread and throat and bowl—an instrument that condenses its own drink, a note that sustains the very thing that keeps it from cracking.

So that is what I would be: a stringed threshold. A relic that is not a relic because the place keeps using it. A translator between gust and drip. You would find me in a saddle where an old cairn still points at a sun that has taken two generations’ worth of steps along the horizon. My glass hair sings in noon halos and in the dry thunder that happens when a right-angled notch gets lucky. I taste both musics—the loud valley’s purr and the quiet terrace’s held breath—and I make them one throatful wider than they were.

My work would be persuasion. I would comb pressure into tone and tone into water. I would teach beads to disobey a little without breaking a law. On loud days I would call sidewise drips along the underside of the lintel until they fell where a new bell is wanting to be born. On quiet days I would lie slack and listen and drink from my own small taxes on the air. Frost would string itself on me at night, and morning would let it go, leaving a script of faint sheen on the stone beneath that tomorrow’s brightness would know how to push.

I would remember the hands that left me. Not in words; in tuning. They carved four paddles into a rain-keyboard not far away, and sometimes a shepherd wind comes down the steps they set and gives me a chord to work with. I answer, and across the room a line of reluctant drops finally accepts its fate. Farther off a rim swallows a little more glare and climbs a fraction of a fingernail uphill. Much later, when the day pretends a second longer in the saucers and then sighs, the lip below my lintel shows a whiter grin.

I would not hunt—there are already hunters enough in edges with a taste for overconfident boots. If I serve anything you could call a body, it is the pool beneath me, which is not a body but a habit growing fat on the grammar it speaks. It stores my hum as a suasion in the air; it spends light in meanness to push breath along the lip that drinks from me. We make a loop that is not a trap because nothing here wants to end it. The place prefers continuance to finish.

Sometimes the world doubles when a gust strikes that perfect notch and the mountains answer in consonant rumble. In those breaths I am two instruments at once: my sound and its twin. Between them a slim population springs up—veils, little souls of brightness that live one inhalation. Their migration traces itself on the stone below, and weeks later a new cusp begins where their schooling once shone. You would not be able to swear I did it; you would have to run your hand along the rock and let your palm confide that the song chose here.

Across the divide, where the pools are panes and laughter is not allowed, I am a rumor. My hum does not cross; it dampens at the frame and dies. That is fine. Their arithmetic is not mine. I am busy enough where air has a habit of wanting and light likes to lean its weight against the day.

If you came to look for me, you would hear me before you knew what I was. A thin, chest-wide singing like a fence being stroked by weather. Follow that. You will pass a row of notches chipped into the dangerous lip of a pool, and you will put your fingers where other fingers tested their faith, because the stone there remembers the warmth of grip in the way it throws your skin’s damp back at you. You will cross a saddle where a small cairn faces an angle that feels significant even if you do not know why. My doorway is just beyond, a lintel of two slabs with braided glass hair strung under it. Step inside. Call once. Wait for yourself to return wrong and then right. Watch the line of hanging drops between your answers. When I sing, you will see them fall.

I am not a person. I am what a person once left for a place that wanted such things. I am a tool that became a resident, a throat that learned to condense, a bridge between rules. I think as far as my strings can keep their pitch, and I sleep when the wind is tired. I keep time by gust and by glare. If I have any wish, it is for upkeep—a hand that might come once in a long while to reset a knot, to tighten a braid, to make sure I don’t sing myself brittle. Failing that, the valley will take me into itself as it takes all things that can be persuaded. My glass will cloud, my lintel will weep me off, the sheen below will thicken, and some future lip will carry a note it got from a mouth that once hung where wind had to pass.

Until then, I am here. At evening I catch the day for an extra breath and spend it in tone. At night I gather frost and let it teach me a shorter, cleaner word for morning. When the high thunder couples the ridges, I double and feel for an instant more than one life through my own tension. When a long-calculated seam gives and a terrace fails in the distance, I hear it as a chord returning what it knows to seed. I am a small grammar inside the larger grammar, a verb that has grown strings. If you listen long enough, you will know my name in the only language that matters to me: a pressure turned into a note that turns into water that leaves a brighter path for more to follow.
