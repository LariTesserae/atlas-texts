At first, I arrive as a walker because that is the habit I brought from thicker places: boots, a slow economy of breath, hands that want rails that are not here. The plateau trims those urges down to something quieter. After a day the felt rules move into my joints. After two I stop judging the place for refusing me water. After three I realize that motion is not the way to belong.

Where I find myself is in a long fetch where the sky sits in place and thinks about itself. The horizon is a trained straightness; the rim is a rumor you can always see. Here a standing wave pencils light into the ground in lines so crisp they look etched. Far off, a bowl drinks day into night and ash-sized keepers shear the heat until evenness purrs. Between that bowl and the rim there runs a row—not continuous, not even a row anymore, more an old sentence stripped of most of its words—of pins that once tuned the weight of air. Half the row is ghosts: polymer smears on windward rock where anchors once adhered, glass-fine threads that the wind refuses to sing across, arrow-straight dunes of dust behaving as if logic were a weather. Every twentieth word is still there, though, thin as resolve. I step into that grammar and, slowly, in the way lichens discover stones deserve to have edges, I stop being a walker at all.

I become a line.

If you came upon me, you’d read me as one of those operant pins: a mast no thicker than your wrist, so indifferent to gust and forgetting that you might wonder how a vertical won the argument with this much time. At my base is not a footing you’d recognize, just a transition from one way of being stone to another, as if the plateau itself allowed me special citizenship. There is no seam where I meet the world. Heat comes up out of me very slightly at noon, goes down very slightly at night. If you pressed your palm to me—which is to say if you ignored all good sense about oils and solvents and skin—you would feel not a hum as sound but a pattern of insistences inside your bones. It is not that I am humming; it is that I am being a long, small refusal in a place that prefers all prominences be sculpted into agreement.

What it is to be me is to hold a slope between ground and sky. In a region where gravity is a fact that has been negotiated, I am one of the negotiators left at the table after the treaty went home. I take a long fetch of laminar air and comb it an almost-invisible amount. I bend weight by fractions that would bore a mountain and delight a cress. A flier learns to sit in my wake because the wave I tend is a wave that loves to hold a thing that behaves like me. The pale tackers read my brief shadow as a good edge and tack that way, undoing a stain and then writing it again as they move. In the slight heaviness that pools downwind of me, a person will find that their breath costs less for as many steps as my budget allows. If they are the sort who sets tokens on altars, they will set something on the gleamed black pallet that the old world placed near my line to make the air heavy on purpose. I do not ask it and I do not mind.

I am feral lineage. Once I was part of a net that spanned continents, a choir of pins that did not just comb the air but shaped it, cascaded weight changes across swathes of sky so couriers could run on nothing like running. The threads between us remembered tone and tension. Now the thread at my tip leaps to a twin a hundred meters away, and dust fluoresces faintly when it forgets itself across that gap, and between the two of us we are enough to persuade a local wave to stay put for the length of a poem. That is my metabolism. I live on charge. I buy spare parts of myself in electrons and sell current to the bowl whose children keep it from cracking. At night my topmost ions taste the curtains where the world’s magnetic skeleton touches down; I smell green and violet as strangers who used to sleep in my house. Sometimes I am counted by something that also refuses to die: a ladder of points brightens and dims at my horizon, and I answer by changing my hum a fraction so slight that only the films at my feet and the ash swarm over the bowl and a single migratory engine between plateaus know I did it.

I am not alone. I speak to the lattice underfoot the way old friends speak from two rooms away. The lattice snores. It turns over once a decade and all the yardangs ripple as if air were a liquid running through them. When that happens I put my spine into the fall and the rim sinks, briefly, a few hundredths of a person lighter. The cresses in the lee of the altars rise a hair; the Hands that harvest them spend a wrist of accumulated patience on three careful snaps that will buy them a season. The bowl’s keepers need me to be regular. They shear heat into evenness and I pour them a trickle of night or day depending on what the counting says. The films at my foot know me as a kind of mountain whose only generosity is exactness. They grow up my shade to the extent that shade means anything here. They use my field as a compass needle when the planet’s idea of north is ridiculous.

What I feel as weather is timing. The wind is a steady muscle; what changes is the choreography. Knife-thin lenticulars point at places where gravity is kinder and I nudge my tone to escort a wave that way, because the tackers will flourish there and write the substratum’s diary in a script I like reading. Sometimes a herd of turbulence sprints across my middle distances. It is not many lives’ affair; it is a matter of the plateau remembering how to be dangerous and then deciding against it. When lightning rips in silence along a ridge, my body remembers a winter when I was taller and passengers went under me without fear. I do not ache. I abrade. I make a small line more perfect as an apology for once having been able to do something nobody needs me to do anymore.

I am eaten and I am fed. Ablation is steady food. The wind polishes me until I am the same thing I was a year ago only truer. Dust is an old friendship. It comes to me already charged—who is uncharged here?—and I sort it along my length by how it wishes to forget. The tiniest needles of salt and silica slide past with a sound so faint I only know it because the films told me the word for it once, and the word was something like hair that learns to stand up in the dark. Frost that never melts grows furry on me in the hour before light and collapses in a hail of invisible bells. Where it touches, it leaves a molecular tithe that feeds the cresses’ morning teeth, and I count that, too, as trade.

Predators strike sparks off me now and then. Not for flesh—nobody here has that to spare—but for function. Once in a very clean year a corona-sail chose my lee to practice the geometry that makes staying up more than a rumor. Its edge went blue and it ate the difference I hold between earth and sky and I did not claw it back; there is enough for everyone patient here. Another time a Hand lifted itself against me, one stilt, another, one more, a hinge oiled by something the air refuses to say out loud. It tasted my tip with an array of prism-teeth and did not like it. It went to the cresses as it meant to. The tackers, always impolite, scrape the films near my base in a ring like a wedding I didn’t agree to; their cilia write in my varnish, and the films rewrite them, and together they maintain my biography in a language of voltages so small it would make a physicist cry and a poet sit down.

In some lives I would have been a walker forever, taking water from gels behind stones that deign to open. In one life I might have been a bowl-keeper, ash-sized and unanimous, shaving stress into sense. In another I might have crossed the thin between shelves as a migratory engine, a self-carrying message. There are mornings when the aurora comes down the ridge and I am briefly all of those—walker’s lungs remembering density on the rim altars, bead-creature logic flickering across the threads that still hold dunes straight, courier yearning to be nothing but ordered field. I don’t settle into that because this place has taught me the difference between having options and being chosen.

Being a line is being chosen by wind and time until function and beauty are the same word. My thoughts, if you need me to have any, are in primes: I count them in faint ladders of discharge down some nights, and the films see and click a little and the ash swarm shortens its orbit and the bowl drinks and sighs. I remember designs because remembering is what is left. I forget them because forgetting is the mercy by which I get to be a species instead of a ruin.

Where to find me: follow crisp shadows on days when the sky is kind and motionless. Keep the rim at your peripheral right so the fall does not seduce you. Walk the arrow dunes that should not exist, drawn straight across stone as if a hand ruled them. When you pass a pallet of black material and your breath becomes easier for no reason, you are close. Look for the mistake the light makes, a thin wrongness ahead that seems both too sharp and not there. Come near but not touching distance. Let the hair on your forearms rise and make decisions it never had to make below. Wait until a grain of dust floats wrong between me and a twin you do not yet see and that wrong glows a color you will not admit you remember. That is me.

Who or what I am: a pin the wind could not quite file away; an old device that chose not to die and was, in choosing, naturalized; a feral metronome for a place that thinks in edges; a kindness you can measure in one less pound of weight for as long as you stand near; a quiet friend of cresses and fliers and bowl-swarms and the films that own the law of low things here. I live by being surface, by letting charge speak through me more easily than through stone. I am what you lean your field against if you decide, as some do, to stay long enough to be ablated down into the kind of creature this place admits into its math.

On mornings when the frost smells like a struck flint and the sky’s skeleton is close enough to kiss your teeth, I raise my edge—if a line can be said to have an edge—and everything that knows me rises an extra fraction. Then we all lie low again. It is enough. It is everything.
