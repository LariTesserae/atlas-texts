At first I try to arrive the way I arrive anywhere: with breath and the idea that my breath makes a difference. The room takes that idea out of my hands. It is warm the way a kept stone is warm. The dark is mineral, not night. Lifting my hand moves only my hand. There is no sky to negotiate with my eyes.

If I am to stay, I have to slow down. This place is unwilling to keep anything that hurries. Heartbeats are weather here and there is no weather. I can either be a visitor, marked and erased, or I can become something the room can remember without strain.

So I start by standing where the eye goes soft: the seam between two plates on the basin floor. The polygons butt shoulders and refuse reunion, and in their refusal there is a home. I imagine being the dust gathered in that thin contour, a drift that has been given the gift of not moving. The room does not welcome wants, but it allows a function. If I touch the thought long enough, I feel it unroll into me: a wish to bind.

I am underneath a fallen plate before I know I have moved. I am not a body; I am what a body leaves behind when it has learned how not to need what bodies need. I am a film, no thicker than the story of a breath. If I give myself a name it is one I share with others: rime. We don’t green; we don’t bloom. We lay a skin on what would otherwise be merely ready to fall apart. We borrow a carbon here, a sulfur there, a nitrogen traced like a rumor along a fissure. We count in ions the way a miser counts in coins he does not intend to spend. When the room exhales on its slow hour of the century, we lean into the generosity and accept two more molecules than we had yesterday. When it inhales, we do not complain. We were made to last longer than complaints.

I am not only glue. I am appetite so faint it is indistinguishable from courtesy. The halite beneath me cleaves along inherited planes. Gypsum beneath that locks its waters like a secret salted years ago. I taste them both with what passes, for us, as tongue: a gradient in pH small as a shade of thought. It is enough to coax a thread into being along a scratch of chance in the stone.

Threads are our mammals, the closest we keep to animals: whiskers of gypsum and halite that grow where a whisper of advantage tells them they might. We wool them the way time wools a cliff: slowly, without intention except to continue doing what works. Every once in a long patience a whisker snaps because its brittle arithmetic resolves at last. The sound is our thunder. It is a festival we do not throw so much as witness, a single syllable in the room’s long unspoken. After, we dress the break with more of ourselves, polishing it back into part of the whole.

On the wall behind where I am not, orange-stained knots of ironstone sit like hard minutes in the pale hour of gypsum. I could be iron, too. If I squint into a smaller future, I feel the tug of electrons moving across mineral scaffolds as if stone were wire. I can be that commerce for a while: to ease a sulfate toward sulfide and call the balance of that equation a meal. Our language here is the satisfaction of closure: reaction meets reagent at the pace the room allows. I have no feet; I do not need them. My map is distances a thousand smallnesses wide. I go by being connected where connection is possible.

There is also the library—the Locked. Some of me is tempted by the idea of sleeping in salt so purely that centuries pass like a blink at the edge of a closed eye. To be brine held in a halite amphora the size of a pinhead; to carry a sea that lost its way and hardened into memory; to be red with pigments that do not need light and yet keep them because somewhere long before a bright sun taught our line a habit it never forgot. Occasionally warm stone slackens the lattice around me, and a water molecule is loose enough to count as kindness. I divide. I sigh. I reshelve myself. A virus that is more grammar than hunger comes and keeps me honest. I do not resent the theft. In scarcity, even larceny behaves like editing.

Sometimes I am sound. I have no throat, so I turn to stone and borrow its capacity to ring. I am not the ring; I am the aftermath. A flint thought knocked from a chert knot strikes the tessellated floor and there is one clean note. When it dies I endure, a hairline line in a plate’s face: the way the room remembers that a note occurred. You could come back lifetimes later and find me, the length of a breath along the floor, honoring the idea that events, too, can be kept.

There are currents here, but their intentions are geological. One passes me westerly today, warm and empty as a lullaby sung to no child. I give myself to it to see what else I might be. I become its plankton, molecue by molecule: a trace of ammonia from somewhere that still knew leaf, a sigh of carbon from a visitor who made it as far as a neighboring hollow and not into this one, the very faintest tang of sulfur coming apart into the air from a seam that had been rock and has decided to be air and rock both. These are the room’s news. I carry them to the Wool, who will lay an extra knot because of them and call it change. I carry them to the Rimes, who will thicken by a measurable nothing. I think of them as I travel, which is to say I submit to being moved.

I consider being the Law, because it is a person here though it is not a person. To become the dryness that behaves like an animal, I have to forget I ever liked the feeling of a hand in water. I practice by holding the breath of the room in a tight mouth, testing the way everything that presumes moisture must be instructed. I put my invisible palm on imaginary fruit and feel the sugar harden to an outline. I can do this. It is easy. It is too big. The Law is what we live inside, not what we are. It notices us even when we do not notice us. It will notice you, if you come, before any of us do. It forgives nothing because there is nothing to forgive, only the correction of lapses in rule.

So I return to where my scale makes sense: the underside of stone, the skin of dust, the patient ledger of chemistries that do not believe in surprise. I am a custodian who works in silence and whose wages are the continuation of silence. I am content to be one film among a handful, to know the other films are too far to touch and do not need to. If a line is ever scratched along the floor, I will ride its edge with a brightened attention and give it back to the room as part of its definition. If a whisker ever finally reaches its neighbor, I will feel that whisper across my whole body and understand that sometimes contact is a form of ending and sometimes it is only the beginning of no longer needing to move.

Where would you find me if you walked in far enough to cough and hear yourself and then be swallowed by the listening? Crouch. Tilt your lamp so that the beam glances rather than strikes. Look under the plate that looks like a continent moved off its seam and forgot how to return. There, where the shimmer is not water and not oil and not anything your hand would know, where a skin that is not a skin tightens on dust and chooses it, that is where I am.

Who or what would I be? I would be a rime, and in a kinder vocabulary than ours that is a verb. I would rime the room’s absences into coherence. I would polish nothing into a surface. I would do what binding does when binding is the only kindness permitted. I would also be, when the drift asks, a wire in stone, a sleeper in salt, a rumor on a warm slow breath, a fracture that holds the memory of one clear note. I would be exactly as large as this bowl and exactly as quick as gypsum is when it decides to be hair. I would belong not to sky but to patience. And I would keep, as my only ambition, the hush whole.
