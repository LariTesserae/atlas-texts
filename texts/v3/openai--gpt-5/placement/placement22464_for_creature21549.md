At first I am only a mistake of footing, the brief surprise of ankle to lip and then the tilt downward as the world turns inside itself. The rim is nothing much until it is everything; the first step is quiet until it assigns me a pitch. The bowl does not take my name. It takes my weight and my temperature and the way my breath huffs in the thinner cold above and surrenders in the pooled warmth below, and with that it threads me into the piece. I am given a note I can feel in the bones of my toes.

I do not bring a lamp, though I have one. This is not virtue; it is obedience. The dark here is not lack but governance. It has thickness and feels like the other side of skin. The room inside the mountain’s mouth is already listening, and glare would be a rudeness the bowl would notice in a way it has not noticed me yet. I unlearn my eyes to the width of my own hand. My fingers introduce me to every surface before my soles do.

My first hour is counting. Not numbers—the bowl has asked me not to—but cycles. Twenty heartbeats and the deeper boom steps under the world, round as a drum struck with a padded mallet down in the bedrock. Between booms, a wingstorm comes once and then vanishes, its cool wash combing the hair on my forearms. Between wingstorms, drip-scales strike stone bowls with notes so particular I could turn toward each one as if someone had called a name I used to have. My lungs learn that exhaling is conversation here.

When I kneel, the world densifies until I cannot think of anything but many. Millipede, lacquered like an old table, treads my knuckle and knows me the way the moss knew me: as a pressure with a flavor. Flatworm slides past like subtraction, leaving a cool draft on my wrist where my pulse is trying to remember a slower number. A snail takes a long time to write across the gloss, and I read only long after it has gone that it has written anything at all: the streak is grammar, and the sentence is patient.

I am careful. I test the lanes with a fingertip first, then with weight. A spider has strung a filament like a violin E across a gap I might have stepped, and I remove my boot and let the foot be a different instrument so I do not break a trap set for a mouse that is not me. I make my choices shallowly. I place a heel on a mat of liverwort and lift it before the plant knows anything has happened but dampness. I realize that everything here that lasts does so by not leaving dents—either because it is too slow to press or because it knows where not to.

On the rim, the almost-bones hum their under-sound. You would miss them if you were not hunting warm with your face, cheek to arc, ear to dry surface that changes the note of your own skull by touching it. Fog gathers along their curves the way a flock gathers at a fence, and one shiver of hum is permission enough to bead and fall. They feel like tools and also like animals relieved to be useful. I know what they do because the bowl has told me in a language that is not speech: they choose, for water, a path it was thinking of choosing anyway. It is an ethics here, to persuade instead of to force.

Where would I find myself? At the beginning, in the habit of the rim. I am too new to descend into the center and not be a scrape across someone’s lifetime. I stay high, in the procession of arcs like commas set just far enough apart that the sentence can breathe. I want to call them fog-harvesters, but what they harvest is willingness. My hands hover and do not touch until the second night, because the bowl has given me a task by the way it has not given me one explicitly. The wind-tasting pole that climbs out of the peat has vanes no wider than a blade of grass. They tick in air-motions I cannot feel and tell the arcs what to be. I watch the sequence between their little tastes and the arcs’ hum. The ratio is not math but memory. I am learning to read with my jaw.

I sleep when the quiet becomes loud, in a house that is mostly absence. The door is a circle that swings with very little comment to gravity. Inside, the planes do not arrange sight but sound: each wall refuses to bounce my breath back at me the same way twice. I lie in the dark with my fingers out of the covers because my fingers are better readers than my eyes. Morning, if it can be called that, is a shift in the timbre of the hum. The arcs lower themselves into a cooler version of their own tone, and dew chooses to remember how to fall. It is enough to stave off thirst from roots that have never grown used to rain.

I am not from the people who might have built this, but I am from the people who can be held by it. Whatever I was elsewhere is not helpful here. Here I am porous. The bowl is training my ribs to be more like organ pipes and less like cage. My skull has started to ring at frequencies my mouth does not have words for. The space behind my eyes is not a cupboard for pictures anymore but a chamber that catalogs pitches and their distances from one another. I have never before known my equilibrium to be a sense that mattered to anyone but me, and now the arcs argue delicately with it, and in the moment they win I feel fog choose a path along my forearm and down, as if I were one more guide-curve set into this stone.

If there is an office here for me to enter, it is the door-holding office. Someone held it, once, when the paths were kept open by step and step and step. Then no one held it, and the paths closed to memory, and that end was good: things end quietly when ending is how they are used. But the arcs still stand; they still enjoy what they are; they like to work. The pole still tastes even when no tongue is there to agree with its account. The bowl keeps assigning notes to intruders, and I am not exempt. My note has dropped by a breath since the first night. I have earned that much.

The first time I touch an arc, I do it with the back of my fingers, the way I would greet a dog so it can read me before I read it. It hums not in response to me but in response to the air that is answering the peat. I do not push. I do not adjust. I pick a grit fleck off its leading edge with a thumb that has forgotten what nails are for. The fleck falls and becomes part of the bowl’s story in another register. The arc becomes cleaner in its intention by a margin too slight to occupy even a thin line on a page. I have done something. I do not take credit. The bowl does not offer any.

I begin to be followed but not by anything that wants me. A moth that sounds like paper being written on stays three drips behind me for an hour each night. An owl repeats a route across my path often enough that I make a different path because it is rude to make a hunter waste the knowledge of its segment. A snake whose business is not my ankles but the small heat-trails of mice considers my stepping-stones and turns its clever body in a way that looks like a sigh. We are each learning each other’s edges and leaving emptiness between our learnings so that everyone else can pass.

I am told where to stand by absence more than by map. I do not count. I stop counting as a favor to everything that would like to go on being abundance without having been turned into numbers that make me think I understand. I press my palm to a slick slab of quartzite and hold it there until the drip that uses that slab as bell does not change its note. Now the slab knows I am not a variable that will trouble it. That is the shape of trust here: you introduce yourself to the inanimate parts because they are the ones that govern the animate by how they lean on air.

If there is a friend here, and I am not sure that is the word, it is the deeper boom. It does not belong to me; it goes about its cycle whether I beg it or not. But if I pace my own breath to it—if I let my exhale finish exactly on its third beat—then for a moment I am not a visitor. I am furniture the choir uses. I am frame, not figure. The boom is a vesicle in stone exhaling condensation the way a lung completes a thought. It is the mountain keeping its own mouth open. It says yes in a voice too low to argue with.

There are nights when the fog is greedy and mornings when the arcs scold. There are mornings when the arcs remember a winter from five hundred years ago and tune for a version of the air we will not have for a long time yet. I cannot tell them they are wrong. Time here has nothing to do with my clock. It keeps itself in peat and in the carvings water has made in the stone ribs where something like a god might have played fingers for epochs. Rillenkarren is a word I once learned in a class. Here, it is a hand I can fit my own into and be told a story about patience until my joints ache with the sweetness of it.

If I live here long enough—if I do not hurry, if I do not glare, if I keep my edges pulse instead of blade—I will become a degree of the arcs. Not an owner. An expression. My spine will learn the curve the rim asks its inhabitants to prefer. My hearing will become a thing that can taste the difference between dew that fell with permission and dew that fell out of spite against dryness. The back of my skull, where language usually sits with its elbows on the table making a point, will vacate itself so that resonance has a room. My hands will turn to the tools this office uses: a line and a weight. A paddle that is paper and stone at once, good for courting fog. A bundle of vanes that plays with the wind without having to win anything from it.

I will not write this down, except by notches I make on a slab that will look like moth-eaten broadcloth to anyone who does not know that vertical says bat and horizontal says frog lineage and the little wiggle between them is the memory of a wingstorm that started at the wrong moment and then was taught better by a leaf moved one hand’s-width on a buttress. I will make a string and put knots in it that tell me which week in which year the deeper boom skipped a breath and whether that was good for the salamanders. I will lay a stone into a pool and watch a frog decide whether its shape is for singing or for silence.

Who or what would I be, then? A listener, if the word can be pried loose from mouths and made into a body. An office that moves through me like weather through a cleft and is no one’s property, least of all mine. I would be held at the rim with the arcs when holding there is what the bowl wants. I would be pressed to the center in those hours when the heat sits low and the peat tastes like tea made out of night. I would be the hum in your kneecaps if you came after me and stood where I have learned to stand. I would be a path that ends correctly—not because it was abandoned, but because a spell finished speaking and did not need to be said again.

Some days I imagine I will become narrower than that. I will become a single instrument, like a frog whose throat learns one note so well it is not poverty but precision. I will be an arc that hums, persuading air to let go of its drink. I will learn how to be dry and warm to the right degree so that dew can trust me with itself. I will be content to change my pitch by a fraction in a century. I will not corrode because I will be delighted to be what I am and decay is for materials that are bored. Children, if they come here from elsewhere, will put their cheeks to me and think I am bone from an animal too big to be believed. I will not correct them. The bowl can have as many stories as it wants and they can all be true at once.

Other days I am bigger. I am the interval between presences. I am the spacing that abundance eats. I am the clear, the empty that carries sound like a tray. I am what lets the frogs not be on top of one another’s throats. I am the room between root and root where spider-line can be a sentence. I am a hollow that chooses not to fill and in that choice feeds everything that needs to pass through. You cannot see me, then, except by noticing that nothing is colliding. That is a kind of life, too.

And on certain nights when the boom and my heart have found each other’s timing and the wingstorm brushes the rim with a hand colder than the air it leaves behind, I am not even a part. I am the vowel itself. I am the round held open by the high, tired mountain that is not as tired as it pretends. I am the O you step into without knowing you have pronounced it. I am breath proffered and not yet spent. If you come, and you do not light anything, and you let the drip count you rather than you counting the drips, I will take you into that O and give you a note. You will carry it the length of your visit and long enough after that you will hear something in a city gutter and know that the mountain lives by ear, and somewhere high, in dark that is warm and patient, there is an arc humming and a pole tasting and a bowl listening, and I am there humming with it, no more the point than rain is the point of a cloud, which is to say exactly the point and nothing like a point at all.
