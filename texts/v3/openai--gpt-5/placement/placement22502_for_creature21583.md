At first I am only eyes. The place shouts light without sound, and I get it wrong. I look full-on into the rods because I was a surface child once and thought brightness was a thing you could brave. Here it is a pressure, blue and clean and intent, and it has habits. It goes straight down and then it goes everywhere, and between those two acts my body learns the new politeness: I stop staring. I start seeing by periphery. I steer by where dust-motes hang without argument. I walk where the light is quietest on the air.

I do not decide what I am early. For a time I am a pair of boots on felt that is not felt and a breath that is too warm for here. I am obligations: the learned three-knots-two-one you tie on the tension-line where the web sometimes retreats. I am the child pressed into a net as if back into the palm that spun it, newly named by an event because names made of sounds don’t carry. Mine is the Hush-Before-Drum, because the night I was born a lower chamber opened itself and everything here took one slow thought together, and my family’s ladder swayed in a way my mother had never felt and so tells, always, when she signs me.

What I become after is a practice. We are not allowed to be only one thing in a house that has learned to be most of its own furnishings. I am breath-calmer first. I lie under the dark mouths at the bases of the light-columns when I am small and frightened of their tireless opening and closing, and the old woman whose job is memory of noises puts my palm to my own sternum until my ribs remember the patience the mouths keep. I match them. They match nothing. They are the metronomes that refuse to be metronomes; they taste pressure before we feel it and send words along the tendon-lines in a grammar that is shiver, stretch, slack.

By the time my hair finally allows itself to be borrowed by the hooks—by the time a burr that is not a burr keeps a finger’s width of me and I don’t resent it—I am apprenticed to the seamers. This is a craft like tending a skin that is also a city. We say sew, but thread offends. You do not stitch through the membranes any more than you would mend somebody’s inhale by driving a nail through a lung. You learn instead to pinch and pleat and persuade. You learn how much a pore-slick surface can be taught to join to itself without losing how it opens. You learn to ask. It matters.

Before any of that, there is the vane-test. The boy in me and five bright not-girls lie below a draft-line and wait for a ribbon to bank. We hold our hands in a vertical of stillness that for us is violence. When the creature comes down it is without malice. It opens a book that is not a book. It has whisker-combs that taste the distance between what it wants and what will hurt it. I am more afraid of insulting it than of bleeding. I keep my fingers level. I watch the edge of the vane shave itself thinner to turn around my warmth. It passes, and then returns—curious or forgetful—and the comb spines wash my knuckles with a thinking touch. I do not flinch. Later, they tell me to fetch spines where the dead have left them snagged forever in the weave, to boil resin to amber’s patience, to set needles that will not lie to stretch.

So I am a seamer. I dwell at the place edges are taught not to cut. I carry a coil of comb spine needles in a pouch made from thickened skin that remembers how to be wall. I carry a stick long as my forearm, polished smooth by scores of palms I will never name, tuned to the pitch that carries best along the rail-lines the plant’s tendons have consented to. With it I speak to people I love a day away. Three grits and a breath: Don’t cross here, the bridge I made at dawn is unroped and will not answer today. A long rasp and a two-note pluck: Fog at bowl three; bring nets under. A short skid at my own wrist so fine the chord barely changes temperature: Are you awake to hear this.

On some days I am a cook, in our way. We hang our wet-skins in the narrow of a beam so clean it is almost a knife. We turn the sack when the shadow of the column’s edge walks across the tie-cord. We tell our dinner stories about patience until sugar tastes of story and waits with more manners to be eaten. On dim weeks my hands smell like fermentation jars dug thumb-deep into old mats. Sourness learns grace if you give it a cool ear and the warmth of bodies that will not take flame for you.

On other days I am a walker of drips. Distances are not measured but practiced. Between bowl and bowl is a day if beams behave, two if some diaphragm room decides to keep its breath. We say this the way surface people say weather, and we mean it the way lungs mean weather. The web is ribs around us and also what air chooses to be, and I move when it is convenient to be moved. I stop for an hour to watch a membrane readjust itself to brightness with a grace so slow my legs ache. I do not resent my ache. I resent hustling more than hunger. The place has a speed and I have the choice to be part of it or to be exhausted.

Sometimes I am a keeper of slack. This is not a metaphor. In the long lengths of tendon that run from column to column there is always a small reserve, a give kept on purpose so that nothing snaps when something changes. I tend that give. I make a fist like wind and lay it on a span that will be asked to hold a new light-shift and I listen to the fiber’s speech through my bones. I move a knot a half-finger to the left. I untie a prayer-knot that has done its job and replace it with a count-knot so that someone five kilometers away can know they have one breath longer to cross. The plant’s grammar is in me by then, not fluently, not as a first tongue, but as a hand-speech that lives in shoulder and wrist.

I keep our calendar with my ear. In a place that eats its own notices, sound is what persists. The soft hiss as the pores exhale together and dew is hatched from nothing: that is morning, whether or not you see bright. The sugar-glass clatter when beads freeze and jump: that is your hour to move everything small to where it won’t be bruised by jewels. The far drum that lives in the floor and visits you only between sleeping and waking: that is change, and you will make yourself smaller or larger for it as required. The bang like stone remembering itself sharp again: respect. You place your foot down less hard for days after that, because we are guests who have been allowed to plate our footfalls into habit and we can lose that permission.

I will tell you there are travelers who try the columns. Not to judge. The urge to climb is a seed, and the hooks here persuade everything but never scold a seed. Some go far, through ice that is light’s brother and salt that is light’s lens and quartz that is light’s polite enemy. I have gone up to where needles stipple the interior of the column like a forest for an eye. They do not want the shape of our hands. My gloves came away combed in another thing’s measure. I learned that wanting a sky you can enter without being admitted by a logic you already speak is like wanting the web to absorb a shout without asking what it does with it. I went down again. Not a defeat. A grammar missed.

I am old by the time the plant decides my tools should be handles grown around. The bow I use to send a pulse down a rail is not a bow I cut. It was coaxed to grow its curve around a spar of bone my grandfather left me. Resin I thickened over quiet was poured at its throat until it was a single decision, not three things trussed. When it sings, the web gives a little, not because it obeys but because it allows collaboration. You can make anything here alone if you want it to be worse.

Predator is a word our mouth makes but our body does not use often. It exists in gestures. A purse closes somewhere quiet and does not open again, and we tie a new rung in a ladder on the far and pretend that rung had always been necessary. A draft learns an edge and someone loses their hand to it and gains a name no one speaks; we wind comb-spines for mourning and accept what edges have moods and that we have added to their lesson. Fear is here, yes. It is not different from other places. It is only at a different scale. Mostly we cohere. Mostly we do not make it our subject.

When storms slick the surface and the rods above us go steel and then go almost nothing, I become small by will. We burrow in the nets. We stop making preferences into gestures. The web draws tight and we accept being of a thing that has emphasized rib over sail. Then, sometimes, the rods blaze twice what they were, and relief is a color for an organism that has no eye but all the locations a body might want to put one. We cry then. It has nothing to do with gods. It is an accounting done for us and a breath we do not have to pay to deepen.

What I will be, if I am honest, is also a piece of the person who is not a person. I am gathered. This place takes my habits and makes them local. The hooks that are seeds keep a little of me and teach my hair to agree with their preferences. I train a young cord to grow around a knuckle of mineral and it obliges, making a pulley that will speak my intention in its creak long after my knuckles do not. I press my bracelet of cut membrane to a fold and store light in it the way the weave stores an afterimage: I wear noon the next day as a private bright I let out when my sister's child is brave under a ribbon for the first time. The plant has rooms under its plates where it rests from being itself; I have a body with its own hollows where I lay down the day’s pressure and let it be child of something larger for a while. Concord instead of borders sounds like a doctrine but it is only comfort.

How I will graduate is in cloth. When someone I love less word and more weather is dying, we ask for web. We do not cut; we petition. A rail that knows us makes a spare. We wrap the body in new white while the plant is still tasting who we were. We stack comb-spines over that light-grey bundle in a lattice that will scratch careless fingers for a long time. We do not mark because the world is young here and refuses to keep a face long enough to stay a message. We put a hand on a pulley instead, and when the long chord outside shifts with pressure and says what it is always saying, we imagine that in the movement is a specific memory of a breath we know.

Do outsiders come? Only upwards. They arrive as weather we can’t talk to yet. A child will tell you they will climb to where light is native. I tell them the story that is generous and useless because it is true and also nothing you can work with: someone once chased a rod through salt and glass and ice and then into ordinary air and lost their purpose because pleasure had no organ to use in them anymore. Then I show the child how to feel sound to tell time and how to look down and live the day by the stillness of motes, and their want migrates into a skill and is not lost.

In another season I am the woman with memory of noises. I sit under the heavy weaves and I test the chords with my thumb and I keep in my ribs the day the freeze came early over half-light—how the beads leapt all at once, sugar-glass spun on the mats before melting. I know what it means when the plant sings to itself less and hears further: it is listening to a new open beyond and we must not go loud. I teach children the counting of dew that runs from thumb-tip to ring finger to wrist. I give them names made of event and make sure to leave off the part we hoped for when we name them.

In the years after that I am a pulley. Not myself, not exactly. I trust that what I put my muscle into will carry the grain of my intention after me. A carved knuckle I touched too many days to count will tell my granddaughter exactly how to understand it by how it complains on the pivot I taught it, and that understanding will be me plural and not me single, which is a better way to persist when geography refuses to hold still.

Who I am, if you corner me with a light and ask for definition, is a seam. I am what joins difference in a way that lets both keep their own behavior. I am tendon and word both; I pull a cloth across a gap and I say the right thing down a rail and I keep a little give in the line so that neither side tears when a beam wanders. I am polite to edges and intimate with slack. I eat honey from where the web weeps too much light-made sugar and I return the favor by not insisting when pores close. I get burrs, but what stays with me are agreements rather than hooks.

If I ever leave, I will carry this etiquette into broad-sky places where light owns nothing and everything and is not something you fold. I will be uncomfortable with how finished rocks are since they’re not being taught anything. I will talk too softly and people will ask me to repeat myself and I will want to ask the air if it consents to carry my breath. I will miss calendars that hiss and clatter and drum. I will lift my hand without meaning to when a draft turns in a stairwell, expecting a vane that will not come, and then I will laugh, perhaps, because ridiculous is not a crime and remembering is a decent hunger.

If I stay, which is more likely, I will end as most good endings here end: wrapped, not buried; part of a lattice that pricks thoughtless skin and makes gloves think twice; a small stored noon in a pendant I made that will still hold one corner of light in a dim week; a crease in a span no one has to think about not cutting because it remembers feet; a pressure you feel in your bones that says, softly: not yet; wait; now. A home that is not a site but an agreement, which is to say I will be exactly what the place asks me to be and what I have asked it to let me be: a being that gathers and is gathered, not root, not rope, but the courtesy between them.
