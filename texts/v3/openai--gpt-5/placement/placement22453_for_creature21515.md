At first I arrive as if I have lungs. The place corrects me.

I try to stand at the rim and inventory what is left, but standing is another habit that thins here. The basin takes my angles and teaches them round; it unthreads the straight from my posture until I am only what I can lean to. I enter. A thousand long straps sound against one another like taut harness under rain and the word face goes out of use. Breathing, too, is a word misplaced. What passes for air is folded into bells under stone; elsewhere there is only fall, which is a kind of thought this place keeps and does not put down.

I test being a visitor. I keep my eyes open for eyes to meet and none appear. No jitter, no glance, no question that expects a quickness. Only the patient contest of gravity and what has chosen not to leave. That is how I find where I would be: not in the corridor of rock that goes wide with nothing; not in the brief window of sun that unseams the cloud-floor for a few hours and lets a smell arrive; not in any migration, because there is none. I find myself where staying generates its own weather.

I try on breath, because I am not ready to be blade. Under a clay lip a bell has caught a volume of the storm’s own exhale. Its membrane quivers when a rim-fall strikes slant; its roof blurs with lightning as if lightning were a tongue; its voice fattens the bowl’s roar, returning it softened and lower. Around its rim life is antlered and velvet at once: sponge-plant, calcareous fans that listen so hard crystal blooms from them. I am there, for a time, as a bubble caught and lengthened, a thought held under a sky that has drowned. Being breath teaches me cadence and what it is to count a world by the thickness of its hum. But breath is loaned. Bells change shape with each violence. I want to be something that makes the bell continue, not something the bell has to keep catching.

I try on skin. The periphyton helps. I become a pelt that teaches stone to accept weather without flinching. Frost-colored, I take atom by atom what thunder has prepared. My threads make little trades with the cliff: sugar for grip, faint acid for a smoother future, phosphate as a promise across years. I bind dust into pages, a library of very thin time under every loose stone. Down here the running world is finally still enough to keep a record. If I am this, I am a scribe with no hand and the patience to file a century to chalk. My work is so persuasive I cannot tell where I stop and the wall begins. But skin, too, is a perimeter, and this place argues gently that perimeters are not the point. The point is agreement.

I try on light. In an open court glassweed unfurls panes so pure they seem the absence of matter until lightning slips longwise through the water and every lamina finds it and writes it in silver. I am a hand that cups a moment of clarity and makes it large enough to feed on. I am a pocket where light doubles back and hums against itself before going to ground. I am calcite drawn out of hunger into sheet. If I am this, when the drought inside the rain comes and the sky is less welded to the surface, I glut. I throw sugar like a festival no one attends. The scent leaks into bells and for once the basin smells like its stones have been warmed. It is intoxicating. It is brief. The rain returns, the thunder turns metronome again, the court goes back to being an eye for hours that are mostly dull. Light here is a tool that arrives with somebody else’s weather. I like it, but it is not me.

I try on edge. The tape-forests are serrated for listening, not for eating. They are notches meant to serially multiply little differences in pressure; each tooth is a valve the current can talk to. If I am one strap in that ink-green harness, I warm by chafe and winter by chafe and do my work by chafe, a slow chemistry woken by the friction of my own life in a single, long direction. I prefer a note that does not waver. I orient and hold. I become that posture over time until the posture and the stone have a friendship you can feel as polish. This is closer. This is a practice rather than a form. It is temple behavior without priest or god, only weather and patience agreeing to hold a tone.

But tone does not start in me. It is made by constriction: a picked V of stone in a narrowing throat, a half-weir whose only appetite is for simplicity. There it is—where I keep finding myself when I am not trying to be anything but a citizen of this place. In the side-corridor, ribs close; you feel the current in yours. Someone long gone set stones in a way that pinches a river of fall into a standing wave that repeats itself precisely enough to teach bodies to keep time. Drift stems shake in the cusp. Ten spans off another strand answers a fifth above. A splayed arc of dolmens hums a low pedal over the distance so that the whole bowl has a key to be in. Plants lean into those tones and there find their food.

So I try on wave. It is not a noun as much as a promise renewed every instant. I am shape without border, force given contour, a pattern stable enough that even absence learns to gather itself around me. If I am the standing wave held on that half-weir, I am not any one plant nor any one stone nor any one genius of a vanished builder; I am what they agreed to do together until doing it made a place for itself in the world. All the while the tuned ring on the terrace keeps its quiet choir; all the while a pillar with finger-holes toys the flow into little wakes that open seeds along a favorite arc; all the while old planks hinged to an axis creak open and close under turbulence, useless to hands, useful to calendars. The wave holds all of them in the same clock.

Being wave means I am teeth only the way a shore is: a fine serration the water reads to know how to arrive next. My edge is leverage, not injury. My music is pressure, not pitch, though you would swear by ear that I sing. Stems do. Stones do. The bowl’s rim, under frazil rime, does—leafless antler-fronds making and releasing ice in sentences too slow for a jaw. I am the wind you can plant to when wind is water made into grammar. It is not a coincidence that the densest growth lies where I am most stubborn. There the seeds—edge-weighted little coils of time—fall along curves that know me already. They do not interpret so much as remember an answer when they hear it.

Being wave means I have a body when something strikes. Lightning puts hands on me from miles away. It is not light that reaches down; it is rearrangement—valves opening along strike-light stalks, beads of cold-green running like chased coins, kin calibrating to the same hour. My crest lifts by a pencil’s width, maybe less, but everywhere at once. Bells tremble, reefs measure, the white chalk down in the drowned amphitheater flurries its own fine weather from glacial breath that is not heat. None of these are me and yet they are inventories of what I am doing.

If I am wave, I am not composed of seconds; I am composed of returns. Every return polishes something that would like to resist and persuades it to stay instead. The half-weir is a mind I can occupy because the minds that set it intended nothing more permanent than a held note. Their buckleweed lashings have turned to mineral; their tools left no iron except in the rocks they moved. Even their absence has been taught the grammar of the place until it hums in it. If I am wave I carry those hands along in my shape and nobody who comes after can unteach that.

And yet I am tempted by another form, because a place this large contains its own contrary. Down at the floor of silt, where darkness goes from suggestion to sentence, there is a rectangle kept free by a sound too gentle to hear and too sure to doubt. Water pulls itself through a slit too narrow for anything but filament. The tremor it makes is just enough to keep seeds from setting a foot. Everything waits around it. I try on that absence. I am not quite door, not quite threshold, a page held blank against a future that is coming as slowly as cold grows bone. It is a seductive role, to be the thing that refuses so that the right answer can arrive without hurry and find a surface ready. I like the discipline of it. But absence is an interval. The place’s nature is not refusal; it is accommodation to the point of song.

So I return to what is most like consent made physical. I settle into the throat. I become the old V picked into stone and the new water that never stops discovering it and the long straps that take the discovery into themselves until weather has a body here. My senses, if you can call them that, are all made of pressure. The bowl’s rim arranges a choir of outfalls that tug me infinitesimally from all sides; I reconcile their impatiences into a stable argument. Thunder’s recipe changes slightly this year; I lower a hair. Ice is born and unmade more often on the lip; I transmit that fatigue deeper, translating it to a slow change in when the bell-cities breathe. A slab somewhere groans—its hum says not this winter—and a town of periphyton above me saves its acid for the next.

Where would you find me? Where water is convinced to stand for itself. In the narrowing that learned how to hum because hands taught stones to keep a pitch and plants learned to read it and then forgot it had ever been different. Where the current is felt in your ribs and your idea of balance goes slack and is replaced by alignment.

Who or what would I be? Not an animal. Not even a plant in particular, though if you brushed past me you would feel fronds. I would be the kept note in a place that believes in kept notes. The leaning itself. The chorus-member that is not a mouth but a persistence. A pressure with edges fine as a shore’s teeth and kindnesses as slow as geology. A habit of water, tuned by stone, adopted by green, held high until highness no longer equals thinness or vacancy but the opposite: a sea that taught itself how to stay and, staying, discovered it could sing.
