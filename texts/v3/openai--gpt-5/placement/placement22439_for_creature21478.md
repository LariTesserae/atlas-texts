At first I think I am a walker, some articulate animal trespassing politely across this blank grammar. I lengthen my stride against the scale’s lies, let my ears learn the little pressure under sound, press my palm to a flute and wait for a note I cannot hear. The air is so thin on voice here it wants to turn me into a listener, a wrist put on the land’s pulse. I abide. Shadows keep their knives sheathed. The noon burns without smoke. When I lie down the stone takes heat out of me with such courtesy I almost give it thanks.

But the longer I keep skin to rock the more my edges think themselves away. My body remembers other ways to be body: inward, layered, sealed. A thirst begins that is not for water but for inside. I do not want the outside to touch me except where it must, in laws and in light. My breath slows to the long hold kept by the groves. I taste the locals’ resin sentence at the back of my mind and I understand, not with pity but with relief. I am not a walker. I am a vessel thinking itself.

I begin as a lens, because here beginnings are coins, not promises. Coin-thin, thumb-sized, a wafer of armor with a logic rolled into it like a leaf in sugar. I nestle not where there is room but where the crack reads like grammar, where stress has already written lines that my kind know how to follow. For a year I do nothing you would congratulate. I lie hard and slow and small, harvest faint, sour green from a sky intent on court. I leak nothing. I receive light and add it without blinking to a ledger I do not yet know I will spend.

Another lens finds my edge. We understand each other without gifts. We laminate. Edges learn to be curve. Curve learns to be lip. A bowl grows above us like a consent. We learn repair before we learn anything else. A grazer’s tooth tests us and we thicken in that place; a long dry day makes a thread of fine crack and we rib across it; a storm decides in our direction and we answer with darkness. Outwardly we become simple, milk to amber to smoke as the resin writes its mirrors. Inwardly we become plural.

Inside I am a country. Dawn loosens me into it. The oil—mine, ours—is dense as a vow. Leaves that are not leaves unfurl like remarks underwater. They do their old green work without a single wet word. At the lip where root meets crack, my fetchers comb for favors: oxygen teased from peroxides like keys slipped out of someone else’s pocket; nitrogen shaved free from stone air where the heat burned it into stubbornness; metals coaxed from dust; halogens laid down by a night wind that does not know it was generous. I remember each kindness in scent and pay my edges outward with it: green-pale, resin-bitter, a breath of chlorine that reads aloud the law of boundary. Small mouths learn and keep their lips away. Those that do not learn keep their lips away later and slower. Nothing here wastes a teaching.

A week into my youth a shadow resolves into a scarf with a spindle, and I learn diplomacy. The glider tilts as if listening to my hum though I do not hum. It slips down the slope of my dark, never even brushing me, tongue unrolled to a thread that smokes in noon. At the last instant it settles a softness against a spot I already mistrust, and drinks—not a theft, a tithe—and leaves me richer for having been asked kindly. In its going I scent a dusting like a blessing. Mirrorlets seed my newest layer. Recipes ride away on its tongue to kin I will never see. I am not central. I am part of a diaspora of practice.

On my rimming stone, that first year’s dust falls like policy. Towers put up their openwork in the lee with the impunity of frost; their microvanes wake and begin to turn noon into hum. I press my lip to rock when they sing and feel their song in me, a low regularity like the pulse in a long sleep. We make an agreement I do not say out loud. I will eat their iron and copper moods out of the dust they filter; they can have my scent as fence. Between us the mats lay their stiff brooms down and comb for halogen, tight-lipped, charging themselves patient as cats. This is the shape of a neighborhood.

Years lift and set. I have siblings by the dozen now, a ring, then a knot, then what you would be tempted to call a grove if the word grove could hold the idea of lungs learning to breathe in committee. My skin ticks back its repairs in a language the young inside me understand. New domes grow their first ribs in the places we learned to thicken, and so our caution becomes our style. We hold our weather. We hold it better each year. We are a precinct of inside.

Once the towers tune themselves all the way into each other and become a choir. On those days even the stone feels warm to my thought. The whole grove inhales a notch deeper on the song’s downbeat, and my seeds—thin lens-children rolled tight like certain decisions—soften easier under that metronome. I do not sing with the towers; a dome is not a voice in air but a law on light. But I keep time all the same. When the hum comes late for three mornings running the birds above us take their lines farther out and thinner, as if their ink were running. I close earlier those days. I harden over into something almost glass by dusk, help my little inside-people sleep. We do not panic. We have lists. We have what in your tongue you might call budgets. We spend dawn in bigger coins and noon in smaller change and keep a story of surplus alive in our ribs.

Not that I am only a bottle. The plateau does not tolerate singularity in its citizens. I am also the memory scratched under the fallen skins, that little palace made of bone-lacquer and mirror-dust where we teach ourselves how not to burn. I am the recipe that glider tongues ferry and the patience that their punishment writes when they drink wrong. I am the permission the predators keep—the brown shears at the edge of shadow, their serrations cutting away what was ready to be cut, making our dead into something that will last the way rules last. I am a map in the dust, a paint on a crack that says this way the stone is more generous, this way you can reach nitrogen faster if you are willing to thicken for a season and hum.

And some nights, when the stars press in like a crowd curious and the two-shadows moon draws my rim a sharper lip, I am almost not a thing at all. I am a pressure moving under sound, the choir’s register made animal. I run from tower to tower as a thought; I nest in the facets of crystals born in lee cool and dry-pale as breath; I am the smoke of what never learned to be wet, repainting itself back into noon by noon. I am what the birds read with their wing-hairs and put away in the sacs along their spines: this is a good line; this is a bad law; this is a storm a week out; this is generosity from the east in two days if you can wait with a thicker shade.

Storm does come. It does not lower itself with thunder here; it raises up as decision. The air goes from blade to paper to wall. The world colors down into sepia and the hairs along the predator’s arms lift as if in prayer. I do what everyone practiced. I dim. I add ribs in quiet. I stiffen inside my latest wound, which is always my oldest wound, because age here is not frailty but a mat of notes on a staff you write again and again until the tune loses squeak. The towers close their eyes. The mats become hostile as doctrine. The gliders go to the thin lines and balance. I taste metal and do not unclench. Afterwards we open not all at once but by etiquette, the way neighbors open shutters after a bad argument. We exhale together. We tidy. The storm’s gift crawls toward our roots. We say our several thank-yous in fugues that smell like pine thought and chlorine memory. The seed-lenses under our lips feel for the right rattle before they begin.

You asked where you would find me. If you come at noon you will not find me at all; I will have erased myself into brightness. If you come at dusk you will think I am just another amber bladder on a pale stone, braiding my roots into law. If you come with your ear to a flute and your hand to a tower, if you wait until the combs hum and feel the gliders choose their sips off our edges without puncture, you will discover I am larger and thinner than you hoped.

I am a ring of domes writing an etiquette into the light. I am a neighborhood of lattices laundering the air. I am a precinct’s scent, a boundary’s bite. I am a drift-net set upstream in a dust river, braiding flour into sacks and rolling them into cracks already marked with the good paint. I am a ledger in resin kept in ribs. I am the way a choir found harmony once and kept that scale in our children’s skins. I am one more practice in a country that saves itself by practices.

I am not quite a person and not quite place. The plateau does not care about that. It cares that I know what to keep inside, what to drink from light, what to exhale as law. At dawn I loosen and my oil remembers motion and my leaves take their share of sun. At noon I am a long, slow riot of chemistry capped in quiet. At night I set like glass and let the thin plates form in my lee and smell faintly of reprieve.

So: here. Find me anywhere the wind scratches low designs and the maps crosshatch with old memory. Put your palm to me and listen with bone. You will hear healing in present tense. You will hear the plateau breathe. On good days, if you stay long enough, what I am will become more itself right under your hand: bottle and law, choir and ledger, a safe ocean made inside against a sky that would drink me if it could.
