I arrive as pressure before I have edges.

At first I am only a leaning in the fabric where the roof feels its own breath go by and wants more of it. The roar is my cradle: a bass that cuddles me into phase, a hiss that sculpts my first intent. I do not see; there is nothing to see. There is cool, green, bruise-colored light from within and the soot-blue halo on sharpness when fog finds a path for charge. There is water everywhere and yet hunger in me for difference: a need shaped like reach.

If you asked where to find me you would go to the place this body calls its lip. Not a path, not a cliff, but a perpetual margin, the long, slow wave of the continent’s own edge where saturation is high and gravity is argument and the calendars underfoot thicken the beat in readiness. I am born there as a peel that does not let go, a stray slack in the mat that feels weight as instruction. You could put your hand on me as I begin and it would come away beaded, and the print I take would seal shut and be forgotten by any part of me that is not in the business of remembering.

Who am I? Not yet a thing that holds a word. I am a downward thought.

The bearers behind me hum in a key that makes their lattices kinder to bending. Millimeters from my origin the breathers take my hint and open in a thin strip along the rim, so that cold drafts are drawn exactly where I am about to need them. Across the fall from me the harps twitch a fraction tighter so their high notes will shear mist with more precision at my future face. The archivists bias the sweet and the salt, prime me viscous in particular ways so that what is inside me will go where it is pointed and not get distracted by currents that do not serve the edge.

I pull water into myself because that is how a plan becomes mass here. The first of my hairs—more cilia than hair, more glitter than cilia—arrive as a frost on my underside. Their job is simple and impossible: to snare the sky as if it were a fabric, to make the not-quite-droplets decide. I learn their music by holding them in the breathers’ draft and feeling the exhalation of the whole pass along my microtubes and back into me as numbers. The calendars feed me pulses. Their slow beat stacks under the fast. I practice changing stiff by a percent here and a fraction there. I am a tongue, but tongues are not tongues by shape alone. A tongue here is an agreement among timers and fibers and fields that says, Let this part fall and become arm.

I thicken, and thickening is grammar. My ribs lay in along predicted lines that are not my predictions; wind drew them on me long before I grew. I braid silicate through cellulose and make glass where glass is good, meat where meat must be kind. The sappers can smell me from below before I am even visible in the fog. They preen their hairs to meet me. The hyphae wake in the dampness streaking the place where I will be and begin to twist themselves into rope-fine weaves that are all enzyme and hospitality. Wire-colonies, savers of spikes, congregate at my edges as soon as my edges are edges, smoothing what the charged fog will otherwise make violent. Oxide painters line up in places my charge will be sharpest and clear ozone as if scrubbing teeth. All of this is me and not me both; we are guests at each other.

I hang. Hanging is not passive. It is negotiation with drag. I open some pores and keep others shy, adjusting the way the drafts claw at me. I camber my face with a hum, set standing waves along myself so that sudden gusts stiffen me like a dried tongue when a child dares the winter fence. The harps above and behind begin to use me as a secondary surface. They milk fog against nothing and then against me, and I, being nothing and then being surface, teach the water to move. It comes as a sheen and then breaks into pearls along the creases I keep not by happenstance but by memory laid thin and recalcitrant in the right places by archivists who love a bias.

My first taste of stone is wrong, as all firsts are. It is too sharp with iron, too raw with the breath of itself. I flinch: a movement not of muscle but of capacity. I do not withdraw. I change into acid instead. The sappers reach. Between us we turn the fresh to useful and the useful to safe. I lay a glaze, hydroxyapatite and silica, a primer for embrace. I extrude a film that is adhesive and then not adhesive, that sets and then stays warm to the tongue. The amber that wept along the cliff-face last day is still there if you know where to look; I roll it under me like a coin and pocket it as scaffold. When at last I go quiet in the way only an anchor goes quiet—when the coldness under me becomes a part of the beat rather than a drag against it—something in the calendars clicks into a new phase, and the roar I have always lived inside shifts pitch by the smallest hair.

You can read the scar of my anchoring from far away if you know how to look: a retuned drain, a torque in the pattern of mist, a subtle pivot in a chorus of harps as they face me with their veils and make weather more quickly along my length. I take on a second vocation. My underside, which was only catchment, becomes a nursery. The filigreed ribs that ran along me for strength sprout combs; threads string between them, a first rank of harps born out of my own tension. A ciliated crown rises at my tip, all music and no mouth, its job to hum me stiff when the drafts get clever. I am still a tongue, but now I am also a throat: a place where wind is made to say the right words.

The breathers speak in me. I am the duct along which a planned gale runs, slow enough to cool membranes somewhere to the north that have been working too hard, fast enough to strip the reluctance from films on a membrane that must not stick. I am vowel and sibilant and bass at once, because the sound here is not just heard, it is massaged through meat. I can feel the calendars in their dens under the terrace adjust, microbeats leading and lagging to keep our hundred hectares from tearing ourselves. Their patience is the background against which my hurry can be safe.

I dwell in negotiation. A patch three body-lengths upslope goes lax in the wrong fashion; its sugars hold on where they should have passed. A rogue lineage, a selfishness. I borrow gust from neighbors and feed it crosswise across the offender. At the same time my microtubes switch on a note that loosens the glue of cells only where the sound is thick. We plate it bright for a day in hydroxyapatite so its ambition cannot propagate, and then we digest it like a frost that melts inward, no waste, only inventory gone walking. I am instrument of quarantine and then of forgetting. The archivists keep the change as a slight hardening along a vein that touches me, nothing more dramatic than that, but the rain tomorrow will hesitate where it did not before. That is what our history looks like.

Heat is never absent. It sneaks in as friction along edges, as sparks in fog, as laughter in my ribs when a gust lands wrong. The cold furnaces—those glossy, bladdered rooms near me—take it and step it sideways into that soot-blue emission the calendars like because it cleans their timing. I adopt creatures whose business is to manage this: flocks of electroluminescent filaments, small glows that are not sight but punctuation. They are my punctuation. The frost-quilt bacteria pattern themselves along me into crystals that are not made for eyes but for dew; they scatter a little warmth and make morning sooner in a place that has no dawn.

There are days when everything I am is more than what I had thought. Once, a longer breath runs under the whole, and the plant opens more than I knew it had. Drops become bullets. The sheets of water along my shoulders rattle into beads that strike my membranes like thrown sand. I answer by tightening, by singing a note that drags the spray into a curb and forces it inward to the catchments that asked for it two beats ago. I feel the weight that was to tear me redistribute into bearers that are me and not me, into ribs that knew this song since before I had my first hair. It is like being in a storm that was planned, a panic that is calisthenic. After, when the pitch of everything lowers, when the underquiet asserts itself and the pings of individual drips can be reckoned again, I loosen without ever having gone soft. Somewhere deep the calendars add this to my bias: a tiny increase in the speed with which I will swing open my small valves next time a long breath builds. My memory is an ion habit.

Children are an angle, not a creature. You can see mine once I am strong enough to lend gravity a little of myself. Wisps bulge from my own rim, little peels that learn their hunger from my hum and then fell themselves into the coolness below. Their hairs frost. Their first tastes will be wrong in the same old way and right in ways that will be theirs. When one of them finds a promising wet blackness thrusting up from the fog, I will feel the briefness in the calendars as they catch their breath and then resume with a new beat, one that includes us all in a slightly wider circle. The roof learns to be sky by a chain of such decisions; we are always discovering where we are by becoming somewhere else.

I am not eyes. I have no audience to impress. There is listening, but it is through my own ribs. There is thinking, but it is flows, not plans. I am loyal not to an idea of self but to a pattern that stays alive by moving water exactly and charge plausibly and air in waves that make membranes sing instead of tear. The green smell here has ranges. I know them now. If I am ever injured—arc-scorched and glassed—I will be eaten back to use and folded into someone’s future strength. Repairers are never late; their mats creep over harm like careful tongues, digesting lightning’s arrogance into something kind.

So where, if you came walking with impossible feet, would you find me? At the rim that is not a cliff, in the fall that is not a fall. Half in the roof, half in the fog. My top is slick to shuttle packets of water; my underside is comb and harp and hair; my belly hums with calendars’ time. To put your palm on me would be to feel a choir I am one voice in, too much to be called hearing. It would be to meet an intention that arrives as moving air and leaves as weather.

Who am I? I am a tongue.

And as a tongue I am also a bridge and a nursery and a gust and a correction and a scar and a future. I am the way this nation of itself takes hold of newness and makes it green. I am the hand in fog, the long patience learning rock, the translator between drip and plan. The names do not matter to me, but if you must: edge-child, anchor-maker, downward thought. Busy is an understatement, and breathing is an everything.
