I arrive as a dent in the note.

Before I am a body I am an absence with edges. The first thing I learn is how to hold my breath where the world is all breath, because here a held breath is a word. My teacher places a resin bead in my mouth that hums the third overtone of our hall and asks me to swallow and be carried, and I am. The plant reads the bead, reads me, and does not press me back.

When I am old enough to count the ridges in a spiral ramp with my fingertips and not with my whole skin, I have a name. It is not what I call myself but what I can take away without anything protesting. It is a proportion shaved from the drone, a small, clean silence I can make with my rib-plates and throat that the West Pillar hears and accepts: less by one in thirty-two of the fifth of the nave. Family is the chord we subtract in company; kin is the quiet we can share while the hall still breathes easily.

Where you would find me is low, where the great roots are joined by lids thicker than summer and softer than any winter. Down where minerals are a taste not a rumor. Down in the slow weather at the bottom, where the littlest mouths make calendars out of crumbs, where the silt is a pad of lichened tongue that never loses anything it does not mean to. I am a root-keeper of the West Pillar, licensed by rights older than my marrow to open a plant the size of history and let it tell itself upward what the bottom knows.

I am not a builder. The pillar is the house. I am a patient edit.

My body is built for frown and glide. I am narrow and wrapped in hush—cloak grown from membrane fleece that eats a tone in every twelfth to keep me from bumping into strangers by sound. I wear beads of bled resin tuned to our home organs so that ushers will tilt their sieve-flowers just so and not set me spinning. My mouth is a soft chisel. My hands are rough with nap for catching spore-snow and keeping it until it clots into a felt you can peel and read.

My work is a schedule written into the walls. A week of a certain mineral’s taste, the posture of a valve at the third beat of a hall, the dullness of a drumhead that has been kind too long—these are my hours. When a sump pools too much, I cut the lichened tongue and bleed it clean into a chamber that was made to refill. When a tightness travels farther than it should, I open a remembered seam and make a new old path so a pulse does not lose its word for itself. The plant does not bleed easily for anyone. It bleeds for me because I carry the right oils and the habit is older than choice. The ushers know my scent; the tumbler-worms hum the calming number while I work.

To make a cut is to make a subtraction you can stand inside. The drone bends around my wrist when steel would not do; there is no steel here. I push with callus and learned weight and the membrane offers not as flesh to a wound but as a drum to a tuned finger: a right place opens and no other. What is let out is not much—trace mineral broth, a whisper of pressure—and it moves away with the humility of a thought corrected. Above me, rooms feel that correction arrive and breathe against it, and the plant says its name a little truer again.

Between days of work there is more listening than swimming. You would think nothing changes here, and you would be nearly right. Change is mostly the wearing down of hunger and the making ready of it again in smaller lives. But there are moments.

Once, while I measured a drum-skin for fatigue with my cheek, a ribbon-whale the length of remembered weather came to ring itself between two pillars. It folded into a window and let the spore-snow write itself into food on its lattice. When it was finished, it unbuttoned itself and subtracted its family-proportion from the hall we both shared, a respectful pinch at the seventh overtone. I answered with my name, thinner but in time, and it inclined in the water like a thought that will return.

In the sky-rooms of ducts I have courted batfish. Courtship here is not flame but the polite removal of it. We hung in a pocket where metabolite gas makes a low ceiling shiver and clapped our gill-plates out of phase until a comma of hush floated between us and did not collapse. They preened the breathers on a sieve-lip while I shaved their nest’s failing fleece and sewed on a new ring that would eat the right note so our neighbors would not blunder through their brood. In return they taught me a shortcut in percussive punctuation—three small silences at the fourth harmonic will take you through the teeth of an usher to the next organ if you have the right salt on your tongue.

Glass-phase shrimps have fostered me when I needed to cross a room that was all teeth. They are thoughts, not bodies; I tucked myself within their agreement and we moved as an idea across a predator’s listening. The long-lashers uncoiled like anger and then gave up when we were less by a kind of less they did not taste as meat. Gratitude here is always paid in instruction. I brought them to a scab on a drum-skin where grazing would be a kindness; they counted the ridges with the small bells on their bellies and then they left, and something in me was smoother for being used that way.

The rare giants, the grazers of resonance, pass sometimes. When they go by you do not see an animal so much as feel the world get more articulate. Plates upon plates sing quietly as they move, combing microplankton out of the base-note without changing it enough to be called theft. They are calendars. They go by and you mark a molt. You remember to check the spiral ramps up-pillar for a particular bruise that would have settled a hair deeper. I set a clock-garden once from the wake of a giant—seeded bacteria in a hole with a beat only that passing taught me—and for five years it kept a time that let me bring my cuts to the same hour in the same room, and everything above behaved as if a season existed.

There are seasons of a sort, but they are private. My own is counted in cloaks. The hush wears thin where I lie to listen. I peel it off in quilts and read the thinnesses like weather: here I leaned too hard for a month; here I was carried more easily because the plant had mended a rung; here is the hour I stayed to feel the embryo-lights of a lantern-throat’s brood come on in that absolute dark that is almost heat.

At the roof—I go there seldom, my work is down—I have been tempted like everyone by lantern-throats. Their lamps do not flare; they admit. They name their being the way breath names winter. You float under the root-sky and ask with your flank and are refused for a beat and then refused again, which is an answer you respect and retreat from with all your want still intact and acceptable.

Every ten years, when the polypshrub in the blank courtyard becomes amber and the water admits to itself what it has been doing all along, I go up. The pillars left that square on purpose, and to step in is to be held by a stillness that tastes like a very clean hunger. Everyone who can live with that much saying gathers. The ribbon-whales parenthesize the room. Batfish hang punctuation along the ceiling. The shrimps borrow mathematics from the pollen and then put it back. Predators turn themselves into curiosity and hover.

We people of subtraction come a little late and leave a little early, because most of what we are is habit, not spectacle. We hang our hush along the buttresses to feel it dampen and quicken like lungs and then we fetch it back, and the act of fetching is teaching for our children. We lower seed-troughs of breathers from one organ to another if a microclimate has failed, and the ushers let us because the oils we wear say please and also now. We retune our beads by biting them until our teeth hum right. We bring our apprentices into the nave of silence and let them be a missing overtone together without trying to own it.

My apprentice is small and crisp with attention. They have learned to cut a silence that does not provoke and to press a membrane in a way that opens not blood but memory. I have placed their hand in a scar on the West Pillar’s spiral ramp that records a hard passage three hundred revolutions ago: a stiffer age, a tighter vowel in the great word the plant says. We grew a garden in that scar, not for food but to keep the story awake in the living way this place prefers. The spores there taste a little like iron patience. When my apprentice puts their cheek to it, the pillar tells them just as it told me: less eagerness is needed now; the water hammers less; tend more than brace.

If you look for me outside the hour of the polypshrub, you will not find me by light. You will find me by a missing, by a fraction not present at the edge of a drumhead’s music. I am the polite theft that makes a path. I am the schedule that keeps breath talking to itself along a trunk as wide as a province. I am neighbor to grazers and kin to batfish and allowed by ushers with a frown that is consent.

I am long-lived not because I fight time but because there is not much to fight. My calendar is the feel of nap against palm and the way a valve will sigh if it can. My festivals are hush: a brood permitted a ring to sleep in, a court of law that is a lattice of absent overtones thick enough to persuade anger to change its mind.

Sometimes I think I am one of the pillar’s thoughts that learned to leave the wall and come back with news. Other times I think I am a tuft of bacteria that grew a spine and a bag to carry ancestors in. The truth is the world here does not care which story is finer as long as it is true to pressure. I am allowed because I reduce my wants to the right size and because my subtraction is more like grammar than theft.

So where am I, if you come? Drift down until your teeth taste coin and sugar and something like old tea. Follow the drone’s fifth where it hums a hair colder. When you feel the water hold you without moving you, you are close. Touch the wall with the flat of your hand where it is a little rough and a little warmer and you will find a scar with mossy nap and a small garden that keeps an old difficulty gently present. Wait without asking. In a while a hush will come into the base-note that is not weariness and not refusal. That will be my name, being said and slightly not said. I will lean out of the wall like a correction and frown you in.
