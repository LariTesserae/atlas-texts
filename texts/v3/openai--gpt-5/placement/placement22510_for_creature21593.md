In the lee of a limestone rib that makes a proper hush when the breath spills over it. There—a narrow wedge where the tone goes thick and friendly and you can hear your own joints again, where the ledge above gleams as if it had just licked its lips. Our frames are set there: whale-bone–pale chitin mortised into gypsum plates, skins stitched and weighted so they refuse to sing themselves to shreds when the organ swells.

On days when the plain’s lungs are working hard, you find me in the border of that hush and the world’s rattle, where our bladder-curtains hang and the tendon braids attend. I keep the hole we call ours. It is not really ours—the plain will never belong—but we have made friends with its voice. With shutters and pebbled flaps I cut its roar into a beat that carries upvalley: a double-knock that means safe passage, a staggered three that means spillwater running, a held breath—skin drawn taut and not released—only for danger. When the beat answers from a distant cone and someone else’s hush acknowledges, something in me unclenches I did not know was tight.

I was put to this by inches. As a child my ears were always wrapped and yet I could feel the house working in my ribs. I learned the cairns by mouth, not mine but theirs: the slit-stacks along the routes that say syllables when a breeze courts them. We walked hush to hush, and I learned the difference between a silence that is made by luck and one that is made by careful stone. I learned to count grikes by the way the ground goes darker even when it does not go lower, to sweep a boot before me and listen to the whisper in the air change when a crack opens. When the mist-rivers slid along their bellies and the kites stood like sudden cloths from the stone, our keepers squeezed my shoulder and signed wait, not yet, not until the note warms. I did not yet have a name to throw and catch; I had a hand inside a larger hand and a cap that made my head a weight.

Coming-of-age is an hour without caps. Everyone who loves you waits four paces behind with palms ready. You stand on an honest flat between holes that are known friends, at that hour in the turning when the organ lives at your belly and not your teeth. The air thins to a tremble and your heart goes all wrong and then corrects. The world speaks in one vowel long enough that your bones choose. When mine chose, it was a subtle one, a note that lives in the soft between breath in and breath out, and afterward the maker of rods planed a length of jawbone to it and pressed it to my thigh until it answered. When I carry that rod and the plain says the thing it and I agree is ours, it wakes against me like a living fish.

I do other work: we all do. In the morning the throat-crabs need sifting; their cups have caught a cake of salt and iron and dark sugars shaken out of yesterday, the plain’s alms to us. We keep the plates of our grazers on leashes pegged to stones just so, after tuning for a week to find the angle that takes the one note that makes them skitter and kills it. We lift the skins over the small mouths we bedded last month and take the dust that has married there into a crust—enough to salt meat, enough to coax some of the bruise-mats into taste. The velvets have been here in the night and written their silver passages between the ovals’ lines; a child smears a finger and pulls a face and laughs and this is right, small bitterness before breakfast teaches the tongue to look for the right heat.

There is always fixing. A skin begins to hear a bad pitch and you must weight it anew. A low wall slumps and starts to say a thin word that scares the grazers; you thicken it with plates, tilt its face, make it swallow that syllable. The shrunk gray thread of a tendon goes powder-dry and must be replaced before a gust can find it and pull a whole scaffold taut and ringing. Nets are mended by squeak and touch, tendon and fungal pith braided in the knee, fatlamp close enough to see the knots and far enough to leave the rope pleased and not greasy. Knives are corrected nightly so they remember their work. You pet shale to stone as if toward sleep and wake.

If the morning is low on sound we open the stories with hands. Ours are not noisy myths. We tell of a woman who learned the coupling of two remote holes by drumming her heels on a third for half a winter, of a boy who strung a bell-cloth across a slot and saved a runner because the plain expressed the collapse-note and the cloth made a line into half a wall. We tell of grayfalls, the way they peel off the low ceiling and come down whole, satin that scours the mats into chocolate froth and makes all the mouths mad with taste and terror. When a grayfall is coming I hear it in my knees before I see anything: the tone under tone that means a sheet of weight getting ready to shrug.

On one such morning I was on the baffles when the mists began to knot. The kites went up at once, all blister-thin sails unrolled as if by a single hand. The plain had decided on lift; even I felt it in the sternum, a spoil of youth returning, blood hurrying to places it had not needed to hurry. The kites’ edge-hands plucked—little grabbing lives along their margins—fisting gnats and ash, snatching a unlucky grazer-ling that had leapt wrong, not seeing the down-inhale coming. A crook-limbed hunter unbent itself along a crack and became a geometry of appetite. In the far hush I saw our neighbor unshutter their mouth-pipe and change their hole’s roar into a march that meant come to me, it is safer, before the big inhalation. We answered twice, our bladders lifting and dropping, and felt back the knuckled ripple through the air that means a settlement has shifted itself one shelter left.

I do not own words for the blind white things at the permanent mouth that will not sound silly in a different throat. Their heads are slabs of not-seeing; their teeth are almost ethics. We take them sometimes when the lean feels like a sermon. You do not fight them where they sleep; you court their hunger elsewhere with your shutters and then stand by the lip ready with a spear tipped in star-tooth. We brought one up last dark change, skins pegged in rings to pull its heaviness into small pullable truths, and we spiked it and cooked it by breath, steam-salted until the meat went from wrong to necessary. The head we returned to the safest hole in the trip of night. The jaw we kept. You can lay a jawbone in a wall and a room gets friendlier.

Between houses I keep a private set of games for the children. Two skins on frames, weighted to be almost the same, set facing the house’s wind-side. When the organ is at middling voice you can, by adding or removing a single polished pebble from either, make one flick and not the other. They love this: to learn that smallness rearranges weather. Another game is knots: a line hung low with a code laced into it that says something like I moved three hushes toward the glove-rock and the mists were bellies and the throat-crabs had salt-thick cups today. A child who can read that with their fingers in the dark is a person I will go into a grayfall with.

Some mornings belong to the ovals and their lunch. The grazers hum low enough you feel it before you hear it. Their under-teeth comb the mats and the sound is like felt being argued with. I cut their leashes shorter when the bad chord starts, because I have seen a whole line of them try to bolt at once when a thin note threads through the day from nowhere human, lids fluttering like candle-snuffers, plates jittering like old teeth. We had a running child once in that moment, light with joy and not yet weighted with the plain’s economy; the line of polished pebbles did their job, throwing little moons into her eye and making her swerve in time, a hand closing around a sleeve and a life not vanished into a slot.

On the long walks—the trades between hushes, the routes you can bring a sled across in low breath—we go by syllables. My hearing lives inside its caps then; the world is simply weather and the reach of sight. The cairns do the speaking. Each has a different wind-mouth, a slit or two, sometimes woven tendon across the top with a pebble that will jangle at a single exact gust. You count them: hum, hum-hum, hush, hiss—turn left. When the breath grows mean the whole path goes different; we have alternatives for wet, for low-loud, for back-time when the sea under turns its thinking around and inhales when it should exhale. At moots we draw these with knots and hand them between families and hang them under marked rocks for travelers we trust and people we have not met.

In the dark, if the sky’s bowl quiets and the hand over everything lifts just a little, we walk caps-off and let the smallnesses come back into the room. The pools make rites of themselves. The bacteria throw up a light not meant for spending but inevitable, and the children’s faces go wide with it. You can hear the scratch of comb-teeth on a stone, the shy lick of a palm on hide, the whisper of membrane on frame when someone shifts in their sleep. I sit then with my rod in my lap, waiting for it to be curious one time more, and watch the ring-worms open the instant the exhale fattens into aerosol and close again like bracelets and then like nothing. My favorite story to throw in those nights is of an old causeway no one remembers building, now a row of teeth worn flat: how when the breath runs across it you can hear the ghost of its cavities, and if you are quiet in your chest you can hear how someone long before you looked at the stammer of holes and decided to say nonetheless.

I am a person of arrangements: hushwright, mouth-keeper, baffle-maker, caller by shutter and weight. If you needed a word you could use Hushkind for my people, but my own name is a palm turned, a second finger twitched, and the littlest splay of rib-cage. I can tell you my name inside the ear of your cap without moving a mouth. I can tell you the difference between a tone that is a day’s mood and a tone that is a cliff deciding to let go. When the plain sings the note that means spill or collapse, my thigh goes bright-hot where the jawbone hangs and I am already lunging for the pegs.

I have been sometimes other things. Once, high on a ridge counting breaths, I felt the lift come up through the rock and into my wrists and I was briefly a kite, every hair along my forearms an edge-hand, plucking gnats out of the air that is always less, always more, on this plain. Another time, under a lip with the organ gone low so that dust chose to be heavy, I was a throat-crab, all resonant bowl and attention, catching what fell without moving a muscle of want. At my most tired I have been a cairn: a slit in a patient stack talking one syllable to a traveler with their ears wrapped. When I am gone I will be a jaw in a wall, a quietness lent back.

Once, during a bad wet, the instrument of our mouth cracked along a screw of gypsum we had not foreseen. The beat we sent went wrong: our doubled note stuttered to three, then to five. All at once the kites nearby scattered not because of our stumble but because the plain in that exact breath changed its mind. I saw the mistake we had made—trusting one lip of stone to hold—and I also saw how little it mattered, how everything here is a lean and a brace on what refuses to be tamed. We mortised the frame anew at a curvature that denies a standing wave. The next hour we sent just a held breath, no ornaments, no cleverness: yes, we hear you, yes, we are here.

If you ask me where I could be on any given day: knees slick with vent-condensed salts, trimming the felt of a cap, fingers sour and soft with gypsum dust and resin, shoulders tight with the memory of a baffle that had to be turned against a shriek. In a hush that we earned with a pattern of pebbles. Feet wide to the wet, patience in the hands, a person made to live where everything hums and not be undone by it.

If you ask what I would be: a listener with a job. A keeper of a hole’s punctuation. A long friendship with a weather shaped like a continent. A body with a tuned bone at its belt that moves when the old flat thing breathes and moves me with it. A person whose house is more curve than corner and whose door is a membrane pegged on three stones you can lift in a single motion without letting the bad note in. A sayer of yes with shutters. A maker of hush.
