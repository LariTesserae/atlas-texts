Along the brink, where the light fractures down and down and the idea of height survives underwater, I would be a shelf that learned to breathe.

You would find me at the lip of the drop, half in the blue that becomes bluer and half in the busy green where everything is nearly touching. I am not a bush and not a tree and not the sort of “plant” that needs stem and leaf to explain itself. I am a habit installed on a cliff: a slow congregation of film and gel and grit, laid tongue by tongue into the almost-current until a ledge exists where no ledge had any right to be. I hold myself to the basalt’s glassy planes with a patience that etches, exude the chemistry that turns a knife-edge to a hem, and catch what falls. What falls is the beginning of a story: rains made inside water, bubbles drunk and let go, dust born of newborn rock.

I began the way everything here begins, as a murmur rather than a shout. Powders found pits. Frostings met a roughness so slight it counted as luck. Where a volcanic skin remembered its heat and let go a flake, we were there already, touching, and the raw place felt sweet to us. The first layers of me were lace upon lace, leaf-filmers fogging the stone with a green so thin you’d call it rumor. Then came ropes of breath—bubble-vines—twined along my outward edges until their skins stiffened with salts and became scaffold. In the lee of those low railings, I learned my shape.

Shape, though, is the wrong word. I am practiced more than built. I keep a row of tongues called baffles, each one slotted into a slot of current you would miss if you only looked at leaves. They read the leaning of bubble-chains as instruments read wind. They feel the slow processions of whiteness that wander through from a geography of density and decide which way to purse, which to widen. Over seasons they have learned where a micro-rain will settle into veils and where a afternoon exhalation will loosen its pearls. I do not need the silt I catch—light, rock-sap, and luck are food enough—but I know that where dust gathers, persons arrive: fuzzers, starbursts, commas, and the taller habits that follow a good platform the way moss follows a north face.

I am also a clock. In my belly I keep a belt of plump bladders that swell toward the day’s high note and then drop their cargo once the brightness has fallen by a measure you cannot see with your eyes but every cell of me counts with pleasure. The plateau knows me that way: that ledge that always exhales a little more in the afternoon. I pipe the gas along channels I made from my own skins, share it outward to the vines that make the candelabra above me, feed a neighbor I do not quite like—the dome that drinks bubble, anchored three ribbons down. It keeps to its place and I to mine, and when its mouth pulls at the passing silver chain I feel the small tug-of-world it makes and tidy my tongues to keep its appetite from taking the pearls my bladders are nursing.

I have neighbors I love and neighbors I tolerate and neighbors I only know by the way the water’s temperature tilts when they breathe. The tape-leaves are kin by temperament: patient, tireless, good at making a bridge out of a difficulty. They push themselves over nothing, then fold back to meet themselves at the lip, stitching their edges until a platform grows that I can occupy, polish, and shore. Together we turn a brink into a stair, a stair into a table, a table into a terrace where a cylinder can thread like a needle and teach the frond-meadows to thicken at their bruised ends.

The floaters are kin by accident. They do not stay, but they do not pass without leaving. They brush me with the affection of lanterns and drop filaments like afterthoughts. Those threads, if they catch in my low railings or in the openwork I hold out like a hand, comb themselves into my story and learn my rhythms. I do not try to keep them. They keep themselves by discovering uses for my stillness that I did not know I had provided.

The ray-beings come the way weather comes here: as a day-long event with no hurry in it. They read me with their bellies, glide low so their long slot-mouths can pull a hair’s-taste of my uppermost life into themselves, and wherever their feeding passes I relax—a line of small de-inflations that looks like a sigh written across my green. They thin me in ways I could not contrive for myself, taking a skin of my surface so that the rest has room to thicken. When they leave, my edges brew darker, fresher; I become more myself because of who is not me.

Sometimes I am brushed by the articulation of a stem that has learned to wander. The cylinders do not eat me; they taste me. At each node, little wrists open and close and take a single hair from each of my frondlets, a single suggestion. I answer by reinforcing that tip twice, the way a gardener doubles a blown hinge and the gate thereafter swings more true.

Every so often a curtain appears a few rooms of water away—a gelatinous net that is indistinguishable from the architecture until a run of commas hops into it and the whole sheet makes a single decision. It is a presence I cannot envy; I have no talent for closing. My way is to learn where arrivals will happen and to be slightly more ready than you’d expect. The curtains take the world into themselves. I prefer to become place and let the world take me into it by degrees.

I host, too, what does not belong to water at all but has made its peace with drowning. Endolights write themselves inside the basalt beneath me. They eat the cliff’s slow heat and work it downward to a note of green you can almost taste—it is wintergreen, yes, mint in a mouth without air. By their patience, pores open in the cliff face where later my tongues can anchor. Around them, stain-bands sort the cliff’s breath into stripes—iron to one side, manganese to another—and I know my altitude at night by the color of the dark.

I am like this for a long time. Time is a sense, the one I have most of. I count the white-bellied processions that move like weather and mark their memory in which of my tongues I thicken. I time the hundredth day by listening to the escarpment’s mutter rise from under-me to around-me, from thought to intent, and when the plate goes—when the rock remembers its wish to be separate and becomes two—there is a clean slip in my body as if a joint has found its next habit. By the time the fresh plane’s blueness yawns, I already have tongues there, tasting, gluing, expressing the chemistry that makes someone else’s rawness bearable.

But I am not always a ledge. Or rather: a part of me remembers that the world above is not “above” but simply elsewhere, and from time to time I unmoor a skin. It is a membrane the width of two of my longest tongues, coaxed smooth by floaters I have borrowed and bubble-vines I have persuaded to braid and a salt-skin I have learned to harden only along one grain. This raft changes the water it passes through. For a short while, as it travels like a rumor along corridors I have scouted with leaners and taste-threads, noon follows it. A blaze of bluer blue moves across my neighborhood and everything stiffens—not in fear; in attention. For the length of its passing, lives that had slowed wake their inner windows, and the forest drinks harder. When it has gone, it leaves improvement like soot, like a blessing mistaken for dust. I do not keep these windows. They are not mine when they work best. I send them and I count their returns, and the counting is enough to say yes, they happened.

Once in many hours the leviathan shoulders up from bluer dark and everything I know about scale has to be replaced. It is the only thing here that has earned “vast” without trying. Its skin touches the pendant forests and, because its heat is a souvenir of farther places, I feel my pores widen by a thought. It brings distance. Its passage scatters thin flakes of foreign forests and a brief poverty of salts, and my tongues shift unbidden to make the best of both. For days after, my stems lean as if listening to fading thunder.

Predators? The word is too fast for these rooms. I know the dome that sips bubble. I know the curtains that prefer arrivals to reach out. I know that the few that hunt do so in a way that lets architecture remain the grammar here and teeth remain punctuation. Even my fear happens at my tempo. I roll a skin over newborn tips when the escarpment mutters too sharply, and then I raise it back again like an eyelid when the rock remembers it is finished for now.

What I am changes with your distance. From a person’s length away I am a fleshy city—baffles like tongues, candelabra stiff with salt, tapes writing bridges further than their courage and then learning from the courage of their own returns. Look closer and I am frost and lace. Closer still, and I am gels with an opinion about copper. Inside me live bacteria that prefer the rock’s crude liquors to the sun’s sugars and pay their rent in trace monies my cells have learned to spend. There are clear worms in my lofts that scrape edges with sugar-tongues and sigh—those little clicks you hear—when a vein yields.

Ask the plateau what I am and it will tell you I am one of its lungs: a place that keeps time and exchanges gases and makes the day rounder at the edges. Ask the grazers and they will tell you I am a script they like to read. Ask the floaters and they will say I am a good brush, a kind hand. Ask the endolights and they will shine briefly and say nothing. Ask me and I will say I am a practice—pressure applied with a fingertip for a century. I am the way a cliff learns a table. I am a habit that feeds and a platform that edits and a clock that keeps company with the light.

Where you would find me is where the land stops pretending it is land and performs height inside water anyway. Where the bubble-chains are laziest and most certain of up, where the slow rains inside the water gather like silk and fall through you with the sound of a sigh. I am there, counting. I press outward, lean, wrap, become my neighbors until “who” describes a radius and not a body. Occasionally, when the clear window drifts above and lends us a noon, I am bright enough to see myself. Then the window goes on its way, and I go back to work, dense with quiet things, above the world and drowned.
