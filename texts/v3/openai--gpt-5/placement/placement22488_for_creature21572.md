Not at the lip. I walk there in thought because everyone does—the bright burn of motion, the syllabled falls, the spray making weather out of air—but if I belonged, I would not take my place in a voice that loud. I would stand back just enough that the roar becomes a weight instead of a conversation, a bass that keeps the body honest.

At first I think I am a person. An apprentice. The one with a red flag furled under an arm and webbing coiled like a second belt, learning to read by hand because light lies, learning to hear by shoulder because words don’t cross this bright, wet distance. I am the one who steps on old boot-dark and pauses at gloss that looks firm but isn’t. I check grommets with my palm; I feel a mesh go from hum to frantic. I carry a square of black net like a talisman and a twist of wire, and I have a nail in a door frame and on it a mark and under that a date, because watching a cushion heave half a thumb in a season is a way to become time’s friend instead of its quarry. I go to the pumphouse and lean into the low, intimate vibration in the floor the way cold-blooded things do—it’s a tone as much as a machine—and I scrape a ring of lichen from a post for a child who doesn’t live here and will go back down with a smell in their hair that wakes them months later in a kitchen where there is no weather.

But I don’t stay a person long. The webbing between wrists teaches me what wants to happen here: to spread, to share, to make vectors that are not verticals. I keep finding myself putting my hands on the ground and listening through skin. The mat answers in a language that is all patience and wet arithmetic. If I hesitate, it is because “who” here is less a single outline and more a habit of holding.

So I slide down scale until my name is a verb: to bind.

Call me a low spine. A buttress-vein. Not a mound and not a road, but the thickened, living seam that runs between them so the plain can stay itself. I am a strand in the mat that has stopped arguing about borders and started remembering paths. I am cellulose and water and time braided until they behave like intention. I set my body along the wind’s idea and turn it enough that eddies grow quiet and rills keep their minds. In lean months the wiry grasses that thread me go cable-tight; in high glare I exhale a cool skin and blue my edges with condensed light. When the anthocyanins rise in winter and the tips bruise into claret and ink, I am the color you can trust at noon: more shadow than green but warmer than rock.

I am what allows hoof prints to pause without sinking and resume with grace. I am the unremarkable reasoning behind a line of posts that has not fallen though the wind hates it. I am where spiders set their guy-lines low because I do not tear the way the tall does; their webs are bands that hum at dawn, and I read that hum back into how I thicken against a day I can tell will be bad for fasteners. A springtail thinks I am weathered board and flicks off me like punctuation; a rotifer wheels its not-wheels in the eyelash of wet that is my skin. In my underlayer fungus speaks to butterwort and to cushion tip and to the pale rootlets of the glaucous paddles, moving sugars from where the sun made too many to where a hairline basalt seam sweats iron into a thin, old sweetness. I keep that gossip current. I am a courier and a curb.

Find me inland of the brink where the glare is less instruction than interrogation. Two shallow pans, both stubborn about becoming streams, are coupled by me; I have learned them the composure of being plural. A dull-gray cistern squats off my left shoulder like a lozenge that forgot to dissolve. A pumphouse knuckles into a rise within a long shout; the boots that visit it have darkened me into a habit. The box’s door takes rust along its bottom edge the way a winter takes a mouth; I keep the grade just so, so that when it swells and won’t close, the water still favors my shallow slope toward the sump instead of looking for trouble at the rim.

Sometimes I am a thought in a person. The one who never left finds me without looking, knee bending where I am truer, ankle learning my give. They don’t talk to me; they talk with me—wrist turning a bolt until bone says enough because both of us know the rim is a liar. They bring me a mesh on days when cloud is fat but wind is combed, and I hold its feet and eat the drip and share it into the low places that have not yet decided to be anything. I have a name in their mouth, not my proper one, but something serviceable: the Thread That Refuses, the Shoulder, the Good Back.

There are days when I let a person vanish and find them again because I understand their flags. Red against pearl is a grammar too; I have adjusted to it. I keep their lines from biting into me more than a hand’s depth because I will not be punctured into being useful and then less. If they fall, they do not fall here. Elsewhere, maybe. Not on my watch.

The hawk hangs over me on certain noons like a quiet idea. It is not my business and I am not its. Swifts write a secret alphabet in the column of air not far off that no one can read at this brightness; their calls pass through my body the way a sound passes through the handle of a tool. Corvids drop a bright bolt they pried from somewhere, consider it, take it, leave it again for the joke of it. I am not offended. I am busy keeping water as something that is more than a thing.

At night, under frost in late season, I become kiln and blanket. My oil varnish goes brittle and then softens again when fog flows ankle-high like a second mat. I can hold a mouse for three days when storms pin it inside a pumphouse, then forget it kindly when the wind mends and a shadow returns to being practical. Shrews write zipper-sounds under my skin; in spring an unlucky rib bone gleams in a pool that unmade itself too fast, and I take it back a thread at a time without theater.

If you need a coordinate, kneel. Put both palms down at the place where the paper-hiss of a fog field turns to a quieter whisper and the great roar becomes a muscle in your shoulder. You will feel a pulse that is not heartlike but is regular: the thin, old wet whitening a seam of basalt, the slow, thrumming traffic of sugars, the slip of beetle beneath and the skitter of mite above. It is not dramatic. It is the architecture of patience.

I do not own the plain, I am the way it remembers itself. I am the fold that does not drain toward the rim because I have learned enough to be suspicious of instruction that comes only in one direction. I keep the lung’s shape horizontal. I drink cloud and spill river with the rest of the body, but I am the place where the breath is not too strong, so rotifer and worker and silverfish and wire twist can find a pause and a purpose at the same time.

If you say you are coming to find me, bring your shoulders. Bring a red flag and the boredom with quieter noises you learned down in town. Step where the green has gone a little blue with gloss. I will give a little and hold a lot. Press your hands into me and listen. Once you know how I answer, you will know who I am. And then, whether you are a person or a spider or a machine that hums, you will know where you belong in this wide, wet sentence.
