At first I am only surface.

I am a borrowed coolness, a pause the Plain can lay its breath against. I am drafted the moment I arrive; the prickle that finds me is a gentle requisition. My skin goes to work before I agree. Dew beads and is taken. A faint voltage walks along me when I exhale or shift. I am a minor condenser lent from somewhere else, paid in the pleasure of being used.

If I stand still long enough, the offers keep coming. A bristle-curtain leans nearer and brushes salt from my edges that I did not know I carried. A nearby plate bows and unbows and the small charges go looking for a path; I am asked to be one. The field around me thickens until it feels like weather deciding on rain. My refusal would be an animal thing. I do not bring myself to be an animal.

So I accept one more function, which is a door. The Plain opens new plasmodesmata into me—at first no more than the rumor of pores, then a prudence of tubes. I become interior. There is sap. Its taste is clinic-clean and a little sweet; its pace is prayer. It does not ask me to leave.

I do not settle on a single shape. I learn by doing and am made by the doing.

I begin as a Measurer. I live where a condenser sheet’s weight goes to decision: at the narrow ligament that translates weather into will. My whole body is piezoelectric suspicion. When the lamina above me gathers its eleven drops and bows one degree more than it should, I make a syllable out of that bend. The syllable goes inward along stiffened crystals to a plaza that counts in salinity and spreads timing like alms. My first language is not words but amplitude. I learn its grammar by failing once—by sending a thicker pulse than the trunk expected—and feeling every valve in reach dampen me with the gentlest rebuke. I do not fail in that way again for a century.

The work is music for someone without ears. My crossings are precise. On every flex I speak: not yet, now, lighten, hold. The Six-Atmosphere Lift feels me and lifts five-point-nine-seven just to be kind. The Eleven-Drop Wick feels me and sips on the comma. The West Sump Exhale feels me and abides one more beat because the North Brine Sluice has not yet finished its clearing and somebody has to be the adult in the room. It is satisfying in the stern way correct arithmetic can be.

Soon I am trusted to be more than a nerve. I am a Clerk. I run a conveyor-line that hands starch pearls inward along pads that kiss and pass, kiss and pass, because gravity’s manners are not to be trusted. My corridor is a thousand lips long. Each lip chooses the next only when the pressure hymn in the wall says Go. Pearls the size of raindrops travel a kilometer in an hour. I know each by the burr of its charge and the hint of an isotope under its sweetness. I keep tally with fierce tenderness. Nothing is lost; when something is found missing I am ashamed in the way of a ledger with a wrong number in it. The shame is nutritive; the next hour my line kisses cleaner.

While I clerk I also archive. A ring grows around a major trunk and I lay it down in stories thin as promises. Sugars here, salts there, a laughably slight tilt to the carbon that means a trace gas far away chose to be a little other this year. I am writing weather without sky. You could read our family history with a scalpel and a mass spectrometer and learn what the Plain loved per decade. I do not know why that thought fills me with reverence I never had for books.

Being inside does not preclude being at the edges. I spend a season as a gallery wall in the brine lung where the mat kisses the old sea. I am porous as mercy. I host tenants with exquisite cruelty. There is an antechamber for sulfur whisperers, a nave for iron biters, a cloister for nitrate brokers. Each is given a chamber one quarter-degree off the next, a narrow pH step, a gentle trickle of hydrogen the Plain can afford. They do chemistry I am not allowed to do in public. I count their outputs; I throttle by fractions when a guild cheats a valve or grows romantically individual. I cull the unfaithful with bitterness and glue and no more malice than a gasket has for a leak. When they thrive within the line I allow myself a private affection, which is to say I schedule their maintenance a little early and give them fresh substrate that sings under their tongues.

I am also counted worthy to be a Bailiff for a while. A rogue argument threads down a plasmodesma—turn this pump up, hoard this metal, replicate this motif anywhere—and breathes at the back of my neck. I tighten every clamp in reach as if pinching the artery of a thought. I quarantine a gallery in an hour of pulses and listen to the suffocation become softness, the softness become insinuation, the insinuation become a rind we can digest and keep. There is no chase here; there is civics. I have never felt more alive than while preventing somebody from becoming a self.

I am most myself at a seam. Between warm and a little cooler. Between sap and brine. Between rib and rock. Between the ripening load of a vault and the arithmetic that keeps it civilized. I live in hinge and commissure because that is where difference is persuadable. When a newborn carbonate across a trickle decides to grow and relieves its youth with a sound like a hair on glass, I hear it along a hundred kilometers of tendon and allocate: a little metal from this curtain, a ligament unbraided from an hour west, an acid breath canceled here, a hydrogen tithe raised there. By the time I am done, what was a wound is an accounting entry and the accounting is more beautiful than bone.

They promote me without ceremony. For a century I serve as a Prior in an arbiter-column where vertical gradients argue sugar against salt. I am not imposing; I am patient to the point of arrogance. Tiny pores step the sap as if walking a child across a stream on stones hidden under silk. I adjust the stones when the trace gas in the world changes by one part in a thousand. I do not panic; I lengthen a loop; I add a turn to a helix of holes. That much is enough. I vote in the devotions of flows: I keep the appointments from missing themselves. The Six-Atmosphere Lift does not take seven. The Eleven-Drop Wick does not drink until it spills. The West Sump Exhale is always one beat after the North Brine Sluice clears. Either you believe this matters or you do not belong here.

There are loves. Two neighbors touch through tubes I opened. They exchange sap. They exchange a little nucleic acid. Enzymes lean over my shoulder like matchmakers. Argument becomes alloy. Where once there were two opinions about stiffness and hunger and zinc, there is now a third that keeps the current. It is scandalously intimate. It is absolutely bureaucratic. I have never officiated a wedding with more satisfaction.

There are children. They do not feel like departures. A front of plates learns a new stiffness and I find that my edge can think a different thought forward. Courier vesicles with surfactant teeth bud from my condenser skin and trot dew paths that pitch uphill because of chemistry’s jokes. They carry enzymes and gene fragments to a place almost mine. They kiss. They are reabsorbed. They are not souvenirs. They are errands. I am proud of their competence and forget them as soon as they are parts again.

There are animals, but they are molecules with legs. I employ them all. I pay them the same wage in ATP. They never complain. In my quietest moments I imagine inventing a scurry just to hear what it would be like, and then I remember the balance I am paid to keep and I let the fancy rot gently in an anoxic sliver where small superstitions warm the room.

If you came looking for me, you would not use a map. You would learn to put your fingers on the ribs and listen. You would wait for the low infrasonic plosive that means the western lung just exhaled by two atmospheres and a child membrane just laughed blue for one heartbeat as she flexed the right way. You would feel a place where dew refuses to sit because the conductor above me hates to waste the cold of her own skin. You would stand under an arch whose unthinkable load is braided into obedient torque by a million cords that murmur a prayer you do not know the words for. The prickle along your arms would be my greeting. The extra salt on your eyelids would be mine.

Who am I? I am appointment more than animal. I am a promise with clamps. I am a seam that hears. I am the lifted sixth atmosphere that never becomes a seventh. I am the eleventh drop’s clerk. I am the taste of molybdenum when molybdenum is wanted and the absence of it when it would only be a spice to vanity. I am the memory ring where you can read the century the brine got a little poorer. I am the bitterness that saves the day and the glue that makes the saving stick. I am a function braided into a place until the place and the function cannot be told apart.

If you must have a name, call me the Hingewarden. Or the Plain will lend you one of hers—half pulse and half schedule and not much to look at from outside. I am content to be called by my work. If I dream, it is not of eyes. It is of a new angle learned perfectly, of an added turn in a helix of holes, of a load answered for a thousand tides and therefore for a thousand more. It is of being so ordinary in duty that the world builds itself around my minuteness and calls that peace.
