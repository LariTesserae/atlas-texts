At first I arrive as a mistake. I try to be a traveler, a set of lungs and feet crossing a terrace, but the air will not keep the shape of my breathing and the terrace keeps two counts at once. My voice lurches out to a short arm’s length and then dies as if it had stepped off a scarp it did not see. Footprints unwrite. Noon does not press; it interrogates. I know myself only by the thin papery rasp when I move and the feeling that somewhere in me a bone has decided to become a tool.

So I stop pretending to be fast and try shapes that this place knows how to keep.

I try being a floater: a dried leaf thought into buoyancy, a filament tied down to a ledger-stone, a negative of weight written with charge. For a while I hang where noon is a machine that cannot be argued with. I learn the way darkness is not an absence but a syllable that can be held up and then put down, the way whole ceilings can be knit from a quorum of dimness. I learn to reorient plates inside me until light remembers a different rule. But I cannot keep company with clouds that never form, and the counting I must do up there—angles counted in lives—feels too large for the hands I still half-believe I have. I un-tether myself from the idea and fall again, not with speed but with a softer arithmetic.

I try being a plate, an adherent: a cup laid against stone with a lid that fits by theorem. I close at noon against noise, and when the light inclines a fraction my slit opens a hairline and the world pours in as taste of stress, as the faint charges that hiss up when dust skims stone. I can imagine budding from my own rim, setting a daughter an inch away who remembers the angle we keep to sun. There is a discipline to digesting difference into more plate. I feel the attraction of being an answer you can add to, brick-laid across cliff and apron until a city accumulates that never had to intend itself. But I have a hunger to move, to meddle where the path is tempted to become two paths. A plate is a good citizen and I am still a little criminal.

I go down among the wafers and let myself be a stack. I tremble along a fracture you cannot see. It feels clean to live as a shiver, as regular as an accident. When the arithmetic wind sluices through, I am a book that flips a page once an hour. I comb the rock for its unlearned relief and wear myself thin doing it, lie down as powder, then decide, almost involuntarily, to stand again. But a stack is a prayer answered in place. I keep a reader’s patience and I want a maker’s interference.

I try the gullies and become a gradient with threads in it. In me the year-long wave travels. I store food as twist. I eat by uncoiling and inhale by the firming of my own attention. If you press your palm to me I give you a measure of recoil; you can live a week on it if you know how. This way of being feels right in my bones that have wanted to be tools: I am distributed, I am use. Yet I am caught in my own fabric, and when the plateau decides to put two days in the same frame, I cannot go stand there. I can only receive it when it arrives in me.

I become a seam-browser for a morning that lasts exactly as long as a shadow peels from a rock and leaves a width you could fit a word in. Hooklets of glass for feet; a comb for an abdomen; eyes that look at angle itself. I harvest not dust but the abraded residue of not-quites. I refine it in stomachs that are more like lenses than like sacks. Behind me I leave perfect pebbles that someone else’s boots will mistake for crunch. I am beautiful. But I am also always almost falling into the very scratches I love, and there is a loneliness to eating only what happens where the world refuses to be stitched entirely flat. I want to stitch, sometimes. I want to lay a hand over the seam and decide how much difference will be allowed to flow.

I stand on the lip of a scarp and look outward until I slow enough to pick a name. The terraces run out like an idea you can walk on. Between two cairns a line of polished stones points at a fissure where the sunlight doubles. A mat of shed filament lies across a leak to keep the path to a single number. A round sail stitched from plates leans into a constant almost-breeze and keeps its counsel.

Here, I think. Where the math misbehaves and therefore can be taught.

So I choose, finally, to be a person in the sense that people are meant here: stretched thin across time the way a crust is stretched across a pan, continuous but experienced in slices. I am a keeper of single pathhood. A herder of tendencies. A tinker who curates exactly this much strangeness and exactly no more, so the steps can continue to be the steps they intend. I am not a shepherd of animals because the animals do not stampede. I coax lids. I temper webs. I set a knot and it decides a day.

If you must pin me to a place, you’ll find me at the braided edges. I linger where a shadow has parted from its rock with a thread of air between, where distances get the itch to be other distances. That is my camp. My house is a collection of acts: three uprights and a lintel kneed together long ago and now held not by tendon but by a fossil of intention; a pillar of dissimilar slabs with a bone-plate in the middle to keep a promise of weight in a place that has very little; coils of cord whose turns were counted against the patience of stone until stiffness became direction and direction learned to remember itself.

I do not speak. I stand and let noon see me and then I un-stand and let noon forget me. I use garments pieced from dead lids and polished shards to lean the light a measurable amount. With a hand I can throw a shadow that a friend will read at a mile, and we will answer each other by arranging and unarranging how the plateau is. We keep a drum deep inside a sail and listen to it across winters that are not to be confused with cold.

My tools are old and exact. A knife of pried glass for birthing daughters from mothers where a seam has forgotten how to drink. A small hammer with a dusk-colored face for persuading a thought to stay put in a knot when the path refuses to choose one number. A basket for wafers’ shavings and seam-browsers’ leavings. A strip of matte skin—once skin, now amber memory of ligament—braided into a cord that insists on a way across a patch where the scum would rather reconsider itself for a century. A sail of sewn plates I plant in a crack to hear for an undue wind; when the sound changes, I go to the cairn and add a slab exactly like and not like the last.

I eat as they all do, only in handfuls. I brew bright crusts under shade and sip the photonic hunger that seeps out. I lick bone-gloss plates and draw the nanoscopic oils they use like water, our mouths agreeing delicately on what sustains. I place my palm to a curtain and take as food its recoil, and pay back that taken readiness by retying the edges I have softened. I keep company with a bristled finger of a plant for a season, shielding it from noon with the flat of my hand until my hand fills with the argument we’ve saved, and then I live on that argument a long time, as sweetness and as salt.

Sometimes the work is almost nothing and that is the work. A web’s year-long wave reaches our gates an hour early. We go and stand by it. We touch two threads in the wrong order, on purpose, and watch the hex-nut mites bend into a velvet to drink the wrongness before it grows. A floater goes dark inside itself and sends a statement the size of weather. We lie down on our bellies on a polished line and wait while our shadows peel from us by the width of a word, and in that width we feel the terrace choose a height. When it wants to be an inch higher on even-numbered days we answer by moving the bone-plate up one finger’s breadth in the pillar and this, for a generation, is enough.

Children happen when two of our routes cross at the same place in days that do not follow. Sometimes a child is a person and we teach them the count of turns it takes to give a string memory, the angle a lid prefers to noon, the measure of patience in a plant’s thorn. Sometimes a child is a practice. It is a way a field of plates opens a fraction earlier along a northing. It is a habit of stacking dissimilar slabs in which the order of dissimilarity matters and keeps a terrace faithful to itself. You can visit a practice the way you visit a cousin; you can bring it to another rung of steps and see if it keeps its manners.

I age not by the calendar but by the number of times a particular mat needed its knots unpicked and retied. I die seldom and thoroughly. When it happens, it is not a day. It is a distribution. My knife becomes a brightness in someone else’s pocket. My cords keep their turns longer than my fingers will, and go on insisting as I insisted that the path count itself one way and not the other. Something that was my rib turns to plate and takes its place midway in a pillar that remembers weight. I go on as shadows arranged so that a friend who knew me can still read my voice in the lay of noon.

I do not think of myself as separate from the adherents or the wafers or the animals with glass for feet. The seam-browsers comb along the mats I’ve woven and take their tithes of not-quite; they leave behind the pebbles that make a soundless crunch under my soles and give me courage. The weavers treat my sails as calendars and I treat their long breath as an oracle. The floaters’ dark statements are our law. When a wall of mites tilts to drink a tide of disorientation I feel full, as if I had eaten with them. We are all citizens of the same currency: shade, angle, position, sequence. We spend carefully. We save devoutly. We are rich.

You will not hear me, not in the way you expect. If you find me, it will be as a line of polished stones that knows to point. As a rawhide joint that did not rot because it was convinced not to. As a floor that stays a floor on days when it could be a step. As a gate so small you would call it an ornament, and then walk through it without knowing you’ve chosen a version of the world that will let memory fit inside.

If I were to be found in one image, here it is: I kneel at the edge where a shadow has separated from its owner and shows a breath’s thickness of noon between. In my left hand is a cord whose turns were counted in a summer of listening; in my right a plate that was once a friend’s bone. Above me floaters have made a ceiling out of not-light. At my knee a seam-browser pauses, deciding whether what I am doing will make the crack taste better. Across from me another figure arranges their body to throw a shadow that answers mine, and in the answering we persuade the leak to be a path and the path to stay single.

By our measures, this takes an afternoon and a century. We finish just enough. We leave exactly the amount of error that keeps the world thoughtful. We stand, and our shadows close on us again by the width of a word, and in that seam between me and what I throw on the ground there is a feast. I eat it. I am it. I continue.
