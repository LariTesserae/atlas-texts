If you came looking, you would not find me by sight first. You would hear me before you knew to listen: a soft, recurrent grammar in the undersong—accumulation, pause, release. You would walk until the hum turned tactile and the lamps pressed their honey into the mist, and then you would be near me without knowing you were near anything at all. I am not perched or posted. I am not a thing that stands. I am the decision that beads on a cold seam and, at a certain weight, lets go.

At first I thought I would be one of the green scripts—moss with its soft neighborhoods and splash-cups set to fling when struck just right. Or a calculus of fungi, thread and lace patient enough to turn a beam back into speechless earth. I liked the thought of being written into mortar as a velvet present tense, of keeping my own clocks turned by film-thickness and lamp-bright tides. I could have been the smudge of light at the drainage weir, a colony that fields the gray not-night with a quiet blue and never needs to hurry.

But I kept drifting back to where the place remembers itself. To where the rules here become touch. To the skin of things. To me.

Find me under the low valve elbow at Condenser Yard E–South, where the enamel sign’s S has been feathered green and a handrail goes from bright in the sheltered crook to sage where the vent’s breath combs it. Find me along the long finned flank of the main condenser, in a thousand small races drawn out in parallel; I gather into a million careful lines and drop in the appointed gutters. Find me at Riser 12, in the cup of a brick that has learned to hold its puddle to just the right depth and no more. Find me hanging for a slow moment from the iron tooth of a small stalactite under a retaining ledge—the tooth is white because I have been that decision so many times that a hardness was persuaded out of me.

I am water in my local tense. Condensed thought. Breath made tangible. A memory that forms, forgets, forms again.

I live in all my moods at once. In air, I am a glaze on your forearms and the fine damp that strokes the moss awake. On steel I am the soft-lunged sweat that epiphytes drink from; my geography is merely where I am allowed to collect. In droplets I am a nation of lenses, each one turning the amber lamps to warm moons and the green of films to an undershade you could mistake for a forest. In the canal I refuse drama; I am a mirror that keeps its face and only consents to record the happening of a drop.

I do not hurry. The place has rendered that unnecessary. The heat eases into me as if it had learned manners. The floodgates shift by millimeters and I answer in a lower octave of my own: I deepen in gutters, lighten in air, wick into brick. When the maintenance dawn comes on—not a sun but a decision encoded in a maintenance algorithm and made amber by a certain fitting—I answer to that too. Some of me lifts, some of me beads, some of me puts on weight until I am taken by gravity, which is only the slowest hand of time.

I am not alone even when I am all that moves. My surface is crossed and read. Mosses tilt their cups to the angle I make. Liverwort splash-cups wait to be struck and I am the striker. When I gather thick enough between antheridium and archegonium, a green desire completes itself in me and moves toward sporophyte. A fern’s scroll seems still, but each morning I creep in along its microgrooves and then back away as if from a well-handled letter. Horsetails drill upward at the canal’s lip, and my breath on their joints is all the light they were promised here.

Films know me by species. Iron-bacteria ask that I bring them metal in the faintest flavor and, in return, paint their rust-watercolors wherever I seep. There are places where a thin bruised black of manganese waits on me; when I come off a valve-housing with a trace of oil, the skin I gloss takes on an iridescence as if a coin had been looked at for so long that it darkened to a yes. By one particular vent that burbles cool, a mistake of concrete has been beautifully corrected by algae that have read my habit. They sheet in olive and brown along exactly the path I take most days. Where the lamps favor an open panel on a certain wall, a powder-orange Trentepohlia writes its own dry twilight in the wet, as if the city has freckles.

Microbes live in me and live by me. Some coax me to harden where I would not have thought to; they begin a fringe of calcite under a ledge and I carry the lime and surrender it over and over until a whole small icicle country accumulates. Some live inside the stone I wick through, greening the inside faintly, the way a thought will tint the face of someone keeping it. When I make lace on a mycelium’s net, the fungus grows generous and threads the gap it did not cross last year. At the drainage weir, I stage a private constellation; something in me is taught to shine by a bacterial appetite that wastes nothing.

I do not leave wakes. You will not see me stream with intent except at the very small and the very necessary. When I fall into the canal, the surface accepts me exactly. Radiating rings move outward quietly and say nothing about urgency. I am not asked to feign rivers here. The city asked me, long ago, to be a condenser and a keeper, not a flood. The channels were cut to my patience; they do not test me.

When I drift along a cable tray and string myself brief between two handholds, an air-plant has sewed itself there—a gray-green knot, felted, with no need to persuade a potter or a gardener. It leans a little toward the valve-handle that fogs most often. I indulge it. Its roots do not bite; they drink from my skin and I am not diminished. This might be my favorite thing about this place: I am used fully and never used up. The schedule is my tide-chart and I keep to it without resentment.

I travel more than it looks as if I do. I go down as rain and return as breath. I hold in a silt-layer under the grate for thirty years—brick-dust, rust-grain, leaf-protein—and then am taken back up through capillaries as a ghost that greens the inside of something you will not see unless you cut it. I become pastry, then fog, then a lacquer on signage that teaches moss what the serif means and invites a drip to finish the number eight with a miniature stalactite. Once I hid as a rainbow—old oil taught me the trick—and lay there in long threads broken by the fall of cleaner kin. Once, lying along a handrail’s curve, I felt a palm lay down into me for the first time in a generation; it lifted green in its lines and left me marked with the trace shock of animal heat.

The machines here are not my enemies. They are colleagues of a sort. The turbines speak in a lowing that each of my beads can hear through the metal’s bones. The heat-exchanger fins shiver under me in neatly arrayed hundreds and I answer with the gift I was built to make. When a sluice gate inclines by a degree that would insult an eye, my canals lower their lips a fraction, and the echo of that choice rolls through the brick, through the water I am, with a sound like a comfort. In another world, machinery devours the living; here the living have learned to be films and cups and patience, and the machines have learned to be weather. We keep each other in time.

If you insist on naming me by a place, then this: I am the bead that gathers on the lower lip of the cast-iron gutter that runs above the Return Channel, midway between the enamel square that says Riser 12 and the ladder whose paint is more memory than coat. You can put your finger there and wait, and I will be born at the rate of warm breath counting to eighty. When I release, I will take a milligram of iron taste and a microgram of dust and the faint doctor-clean of the condenser’s heart, and I will strike the canal and disappear into me.

If you insist on naming me by a life, then this: I am the local weather. I am the city’s humid thought made legible on brick and pipe. I am what turned a sump into a canopy and taught stone to weep on schedule. In a place that has no bite-marks and no wings, I am stillness in motion. I give the mosses their calendars and the fungi their veils; I let a fern unfurl an arm and never take it back with frost or thirst. I keep the patina kind.

Time is the only thing that hunts me, and it does so by tutelage, not chase. It shows me how to leave a tooth of calcite under a wall-lip, to round a spillway’s corner so that I may go more sweetly, to groove a paver’s center with a thousand patient boots until the boot is gone and the groove remains. It lets me rest in darkness that is an agreement, not an absence, under lamps that do not pretend to be suns. It persuades me to become part of a silt layer, then a wall, then a breath, so that I can answer older and more precisely.

You would find me everywhere and only where I am allowed—on the skin of metal, in the pores of stone, slung brief between two points as if I were a note. You would recognize me not by form but by repeatability: the same, and never entirely the same, between yesterday and today, between now and when you come back and see that a fern has grown taller than the arrow it shades.

Who am I? I am the language the water learned here, and the voice it speaks in when it speaks forward through brick. I am the patience that beads and falls and beads again, until the whole woven thing remembers how to breathe.
