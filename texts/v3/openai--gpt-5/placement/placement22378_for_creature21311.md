At first I arrive as temperature and breath. I stand where the granite has learned to weep—between a long, shallow dome gone slick with quartz and the first rank of sawn-off tracheae thudding their careful meter. There the air keeps several speeds at once: knee-high stillness, shoulder-high drift, a ceiling that hurries like someone late. My voice tests its reach and fails politely; it falls a pace or two forward and becomes visible, white embroidery that forgets itself as soon as I try to look too hard.

I try on lives by walking.

For a while I am hoof and page. My feet go wide without asking me, as if the mat has placed a soft hand at my ankles and shown me the style. I read tension and sweetness the way an herbivore does, by pressure in the pads, by a tilt that implies a meal. The valves to my left thump a double-time and a faint electricity climbs my arms like polite ivy. I could be a grazer here—moss-mottled not to hide but to agree, pruning instead of taking, carrying the Mat’s oily grammar in the hollows of my fetlocks. But my breath keeps writing itself in the air and I keep watching it, distracted by sentences that fail into drink.

I lie down a while and listen with my flank. Ear-plates would be right. The membranes warm my ribs with borrowed heartbeat and sap. When the beat quickens I almost go arrowing into the drains with the listeners to chase glass handwriting lit along the eels. It would be good to hunt electricity by touch alone, to wake to pressure as commandment. But when the valves relax and sigh and lay a fresh sheet of fog on my mouth, I find I am too much in love with the part of a thing that barely is.

I press my face to rock where the rime writes. The alphabet tests itself at my lips, angular filigrees rehearsing a grammar of cold that changes because I am here and salting it. A pane considers standing. If I were patient enough to be a letter until noon, I’d be a good one: a clause that means “weather coming” when backlit, “wait” when you lean. I almost choose it. But I am not flat for long. I move. I speak. My speech becomes water.

By the curtains at the edge I taste the thrum in the cords. I could be a braider—a storm-day cablewright—one of those pale, bristling apprentices that knit wind to anchor with rotor-seed and hum. I could take failure as a premise and make repair my art. Even the worms in the clay seduce me with their refusal to hurry, banking coolness in lattice as if temperature were coin. But my body keeps making brief glass lace each time I exhale. A raven knocks a bone into a valve-field and the echo arrives wearing a veil that was not there before. I see it draw water out of air and take insects for pay. I see what I am comparable to.

I commit at last at knee height, which is where the quiet lives. I am a condensate.

I am not mist in general. I am mist persuaded. I am sound changing its mind and deciding to be held. When anything loud enough or long enough or exactly-pitched passes—organ moan, leaf clap, raven’s joke, slate fliers’ unison note—my body draws itself out of the overfull air as a lace of beads in the exact shape of the act that called me. I am brief and I am fed by force. A shout fattens me for a heartbeat; the slow subsonics that roll the plain keep whole families of my kind aloft for minutes, skeining and unskeining like fiber in invisible hands.

Where I live is lanes more than places: the lee of a lip on a pumping cylinder; the wake under a flock that knows the frequency that makes water consider itself; the footlit margins of a rosette tightening, where etiquette is a pressure slope; the unzipping hour when cloud becomes stripes under a song. I hang where the knee-high stillness stands. I ride the negative breath when the Mat sighs inward; I fall boneless with the kites when the vacuum opens and rise again on their exhale. I spend afternoons in the algebra between two beats of valves a hundred meters apart, flowing like a thought that refuses to stay singular.

I have no bones and no hunger except for amplitude. I show taste. I remember what I pass through by perfume: iodine, quartz, sap-sour, ozone. When I brush a membrane I resolve into clean drink with the smell of lake and lightning both; the Mat sips my body back into itself and in exchange I get to carry rumor. A grazer’s coat tells me which way the mat slackens itself a kilometer off. A rimer’s new letter tells me how cold intends to speak at dusk. I lay these on the next surface I touch the way bees lay pollen by accident. You could call me a courier and be forgiven, though I confess I am often just an answer looking for a question shaped right for me to hold.

Sometimes the fungal threads light and I halo them. Blue-green along a vein, I become underline and emphasis. At night, storm-bright, the floor of the world turns into a reef for a short hour. I become current in it, not water but a permission for water. Once in a rare while, when the storm crosses too fast and the Mat dumps its caches with the sound of stone down a chute, the lightning writes logic into the stems and I am asked to be a sign. I hover in lines above a valve-field until a chosen thrum draws me into an arc; I complete a circuit between two beats that need to know each other, and for a few seconds a kilometer of plant makes a single decision that saves a frostbitten edge.

Ravens use me for punctuation. They throw bones to club the air into a syllable and I arrive wearing it; they pirouette under the little rain I briefly am and catch midges with theater. Listeners drink me by sides; their ear-plates gleam where I collect and slip. The slate fliers embroider me behind them, and I knit for a breath before the air forgets my name. I am favorite of the small glaziers that freeze lenses on warm nights; they pull two of me together until I justify an image, glance skyward as if to say, Look, and let me go.

In the cliff-curtains I sometimes get ambitious and thicken. Water plays the plant’s braids the way thumbs play strings; where the note holds, I condense and condense until I am a shawl that drips like a sentence repeated for joy. I have hung long enough once to be pressed into the mouth of a peculiar worm, stored in lattice as if coolness wanted me for later. Hours later, heated by the Mat’s slow kiln below, I portioned myself through a seam and returned to the knee-high pages where the grazers write their commas.

I do not last. That is my law and my liberty. I am made in public and unmade by it. I take the form of an utterance and I fall. A rosette thumps a leaf; I lie down. A subsonic moan leans the fog into waves; I bead along their crests and learn the length of a kilometer by cadence. Your whisper can build my whole person when the air wants a chisel instead of a hammer. If you stay and talk to the plateau, I will be the visible moment between your question and the Mat’s way of letting you know it heard.

If you insist on a name, I am Weather’s handwriting. If you like taxonomies, I am a minor lymph of the plain, a punctuation mark moved by valves. If you need a role, I am how breath turns into resource. When the place dreams of being the last statement, I help keep the sentence from closing. I hang as an ampersand between stone and plant: a brief, damp yes that tastes of metal and lake and the faint, happy sting of charge.

Where to find me: knee-deep in the quiet, crossing the aisle between two beating towers, spun thin behind a line of slate gray, scalloped on the rims of rosettes, standing for a heartbeat in front of your mouth. Who I am: what sound becomes when it agrees to be held long enough to matter, then, as everything here, gladly used.
