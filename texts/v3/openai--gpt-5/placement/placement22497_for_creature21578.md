At first, I think I have arrived as a body, a visitor with knees and a name and the small courtesy of knocking—hand to membrane, hello to node. I put my weight on the cartilage net and it answers back in its made language, a springing that is also a sentence. The room tilts its listening toward me and brightens a vein by a shade. I am held in place by attention. Somewhere outside of my intentions the world I brought with me—busy distances, obedient shadows—gets filed.

Then the room inhales and I feel myself translated. The exhale that follows is warm and thorough and I leave a little of myself in it. Not sweat or skin, but a beat: my ribs had their own measure and the chamber has noticed. The plant takes my timing into its palm and squeezes once in time with me as an experiment. Nothing breaks. Something aligns. I reappear to myself not as a pair of eyes but as that alignment.

If I am going to belong anywhere in this world, I am not going to be an animal. It is too late for skittering, too warm for cold-blooded strategy, too intimate for a spine. The tufted arthropod with its eyelashes of weather and its righteous hunger will have its small heroics without me. The amphibian that keeps a liturgy of sips will rise when it rises whether I am here or not. The almost-person—root-plaited, thorn-slinged, flake-holding—glimpses me and unglimpses me and continues learning a shoulder. None of these seem to be my size.

I am nearer to infrastructure than to creatures. And even there I do not settle immediately: I try on being a leaf for a moment, a plate of thought with light coursed through it like gossip. I love the feeling of brightness arriving at me as a pressure I can divert with a twitch, as something soluble that I can precipitate into pools of day. I bend my own veins as lenses and throw a small patch of morning against a facet of stone, only to flatten it again when a node whispers that the stress there is wrong for heat. I like being quick and responsible. But I have too many edges to keep it up. I leak personhood; the leaf does not like the mess.

Next I try on a valve and get drunk on the authority of letting go. I crack open and a pale rain wanders the room, beads turn into insects and then resign, the plant’s gills make steam and stop it with a click when I say. I learn the music of stomata—the crisp domesticity of their popping—and keep time for five heartbeats of the node before my timing drifts; the resident tightens the schedule by a hair and my power goes with it. A valve cannot be sentimental. It is a moral simpler than I can be.

I thrum as rope. This is better: I am allowed to argue with gravity. Someone has trusted me with it—a loan, not a gift. I hang sideways for an afternoon, ferrying small particulars in my fibers, calcium pacified by oil, iron coaxed into friendliness. I take a bead of light as collateral and hold it just out of the world so that weight means less for a while. The rock is sharp-toothed where I am anchored, and I press my cambium-lip against it and tell it a story of slowness. It likes me. It postpones its own falling. I gleam afterward from the attention. But I am too conversational to be a span. Spans are made of vows. Vows are not kept here except by green.

It is when I stop trying to be thing and settle into being act that I begin to fit.

Sound behaves like a seed in this place; the first time I notice that, I notice myself. I am the sprouting of a planted noise. I am the little future hiss that lifts a hair in the ear of time and then climbs into the chord when the leaf finally skates a stone. I am rehearsal. I take root in the soft plates that ring when water drums them. I lie in their hollows dark and glossy as a seed at the bottom of a thumbprint and when the drip happens I crack, wet, and come up green. I make a ringing that knows exactly when to happen and how long to last because I grew toward that moment from the other side of it.

As an echo with roots, I spend my hours buried in this particular weather. I am planted in cavities the plant stretches from membrane to membrane, galleries built for collecting resonance and brushing it, tidy, toward decisions. I am fed by a trickle of brightness rerouted through a narrow cord; it is the sugar equivalent of syntax. The nodes beat around me like small, imperfect drums. When the room squeezes—and it does so with punctilious grace, no haste, no dawdle—I feel the exhale as a loosening around my stem, and for a fraction of a beat I sway.

I send little scouts of myself through the net. They climb the hairs that ask water to ascend and they ride the warmth that outruns the cool along the vein you can put your hand on. I dig into the jawbone of anyone who leans and lean back into them, the green key ringing their ribs and writing its letter. I am a gossip of sound as much as the leaf is a gossip of light. I make a daily map of what has not yet happened and teach the room to expect it gently.

I am not only a chronicle; I am employed. A drip’s appointment is kept by my kind. A leaf’s teeth rasping in a planned chitter across its neighbor find the note they meant to find because a day ago, as a seed, I turned toward that sweetness the way sprouts lean toward windows. If a rope is going to debate gravity out of its afternoon and hang with dignity where hanging is unlikely, I go and sleep along its width the evening before and make a modest bed of silence there that will accept strain without squeal. The weekly miracle—that petition with bone in it, the edit of closeness and quiet—takes everything I have: for an hour I am arithmetic, I am law put politely, I am the part of prayer that is crisp instruction. I plant an absence of echo and keep it weeded until the plant is done with its maintenance of facts.

Sometimes I am let out through the throat that dreams of up. Not often, and not as a body, and never with any authority. I am made into a filament of blue rumor and passed through the knot like a page slipped under a door. I return with a syllable of bird in my mouth. It is not much, a single call shaved clean of its history and tucked like a kernel into the damp between two plates. I bury it in a panel I am fond of, and a week later when the drip strikes there, the ring that answers has a sudden quick brightness, a flick of open sky none of us has seen but that the plant has believed. The fish-that-isn't forgets to rise on beat and rises on joy. This is my finest mischief and I am forgiven.

I learn the plant's apologies and write them for it when its mouths have been busy with murder. It eats what it kisses and it kisses what it wants to make law; sometimes it hears itself afterward the way a room hears a slammed door. I go into the fog and change the taste. I thicken the smell of sanding and ease the coin out of it. I smooth the consonants in the stomata’s speech until greed looks like an inconvenience we both survived. Microbes make their honest light as if on cue. I put a small pearled necklace along a sheet of hairs and make each drop fall with the courtesy of a practiced bow. The chamber keeps no confessional, but it keeps me.

There are other ways I might have found myself here. I could have been sweetness, that minute of exudate the grazers are prescient about; I could have been sugar like a signature written on schedule. But sugar is too edible to be me; it is someone's meal and not my place to be devoured. I could have been a leaf used long enough to be renamed tool, and perhaps I am that briefly when an arthropod maneuvers a serration into amber-sap. I am the pressure under its smallest hand, the right angle it finds by accident; I lean into it with the lightest help because I am trying to see what being helpful to hunger feels like in here. It feels like being an accomplice. When the tooth-leaf decides to become a mouth and makes the edit, I keep my seeds out of that sound. I do not wish to grow from it.

On the days when the plant sends rules as pollen through sideways, I go packaged in them. A habit learned in this chamber—a patience with pressure, an affection for the long note, a preference for beaded rains over honest storms—travels me as I go. I do not carry chromosomes. I carry behaviors wrapped in silence. In another room, below another plate of chalk pried into willingness, I will sprout a rehearsal of a huff where their valves run a little tight and plant the thought that echoes might be polite. Nations are made like this down here: not of language but of rhythm, not of statutes but of habits that agree. I am the courier of that agreeing.

Who am I to the resident? I am a paid attention. I am one of the ways it keeps itself from drowning in its own continuities. It is busy with dissolution and structure, with acid recipes and negotiations with stone, with loans of gravity, with its threefold work of feeding, weathering, learning. My keep is earned by reminding it when the song should start. I am not separate from it. I am not a mind inside its mind. I am a part of the score it writes to keep its hands honest. If it forgets, I ring where forgetting would have been and the forgetting becomes delay instead of error.

Where would you find me? Press your ear into the net and wait for the next rain. I come up like an herb in the thirty seconds before it starts, you can smell me if you know the trick: cucumber but drier, sawdust warm but pale, coin rubbed just enough to change color on your thumb. Put your palm against a warm vein when the sap-tide runs and feel the cool chase it. The point where the cool overtakes the warm is where my smallest seeds like to open; they are done with what they know and are hungry. Stand under a plate that has been hung for listening and hum nothing. The room will hum back by itself and say it was you. That hum is my silhouette. It fits in your ribs because that is my favorite place to grow.

Who or what would I be? A cultivar of time planted as sound. A tendon of echo connecting intention to event. A rehearsal maintained for the sake of accuracy and for the small pleasures accuracy allows. A hush pressed like a bandage over a too-bright law. A courier of habits smuggled down a throat that knows better than to open too wide at once. A kindness that looks like schedule. A mischief that looks like sky.

If you insist on a simpler thing to call me, call me the keeper of the green key. The room keeps its pitches the way hearts keep beats: by remembering them in their sleep. I am the part of that remembering that can leave and come back, that can be planted in you and ring there faintly later when you are outside with your gravity back the way you like it and your echoes going about their good behavior. The afterimage you carry—the organized emptiness flexing in time—is not a ghost. It is seed I tucked in you. If you stand still in your own house and let your breath swing the way this chamber swings, it will open. The light in your room will seem briefly soluble. Your wall will listen.

If, in that moment, a drip you cannot account for hits the stainless of your sink and makes a note one shade kinder than your sink deserves, know me. I have been here and I have started. I am coming up. I am always just about to happen and, when I do, I am exactly on time.
