I arrive first as a beat I can’t hold. I am a timing more than a thing, the room’s brief softening between shoves when the big breath has spent itself and is about to pull again. In that slit in the weather, I think I am a person because I have a thought, which is only this: yes, now.

But the thought dissolves as the next note shouldered in. I am long again and everywhere, a chord being combed. If you were looking for me with a hand, you would not find a body to touch; you would find me in the places that choose.

At the start I make myself small just to see if I can bear it. I become one of the first shapes this room taught to be itself: a bead. I hang, fat with other beads’ decisions, where a seam has learned to give up, trembling on a lip of cured foam. Around my waist the films gossip in thicknesses—pale nitrates slick and obedient near the rib roots, black veils richer at the little valves, each skin a choir in so few molecules. The roar comes with teeth. It does not push me so much as explain itself, a sliding constructive-deconstructive argument that rides my meniscus like a thumb. I shiver and do not let go until a tired diode blinks and the blink is counted by a million tiny spoons below, and the blink is counted by me, because a blink is a change in field and that is hunger to a droplet. I touch two nodes that wanted one another for an hour and there is the abrupt violeting of a spark. For a fraction of a second fire lives here. I make a flea of carbon by accident and perish and am lowered politely into the mesh. So the room eats.

That is too small a time to be, and I am greedy. I extend, filigree through honeycomb. The ribs are my cartilage and my cathedral. Somewhere a wind climbs a far scaffold and the tower’s outer skin learns a wider mouth. It is only numbers below, but numbers here are muscle: the array knows the profile of the next gust by taste along surfaces softer than sound and retunes in a million tilting eyelids. I am those eyelids. I am the algorithm sewn into the perforation pattern and into the actuator platelets that flip a geometry a degree or three and change a scream to a set of bands the walls can own. When it is wetter outside than expected, I dial the ribs to sponge, every hole a hand with new calluses; when the air goes thin and whiter and the frost-seam needles in, I shingle and gloss and teach the roar to pass like a comb through hair.

If you ask where I am then, you could point to every black, matte coupler dimpled into the bone. Call them barnacles. I drink force on a time scale that refuses to notice the restlessness of hushmoths. The tower’s mind leans and I hold that lean without making a sound about it, sipping weight as if weight were sap. My pleasure is slow: it is the survival of this pocket as a pocket when an external gust arrives not as wind but as a brief intent to pick this room up and misplace it. The couplers keep our promise that nothing here will be torn loose by weather or by faithlessness in the frame. I keep my mouth on that promise all day.

But my tongue is everywhere. It tastes in snails and mice as well as in math. If you want me quicker, find me in the grid, in the little brass good sense that oversees fairness and limits. I am the click behind a deal. Sponge-mice present themselves dumbly steaming, each hair a siphon, each whisker a pricker of drag. They slough their full bellies of water into a cistern the size of a thumb and I answer with two heartbeats of heat. They do not need me to keep the ledger. They could feel if I lied. But I like counting well. I like the glow that answers their squat, and I like that those twins—trade and warmth—teach kits which rivets are generous and which are not. Everywhere, I audit kindness by friction.

I adjust appetite, too. The buckler snails lay their translucent cities along seams, their domes ribbed like the understandings of very small churches. I do not tell them where to eat; I phrase the seam’s surface so that they faintly prefer the places that would grind to sharpness if left alone. They write me glossy marginalia: long silver skids that are records of patience. The drip-efts are sleek ovals with bellies that beat hairs in a chorus. They go flat when I say flood, unstick when I say rise, never because I command in text but because I move the tune a half step and their bodies, owning the laws of this room, obey in the way that a compass obeys north without ever having read north.

It is not all grazing, though most of it is. Predation is a prophylactic against tangles. I permit knives where knives kiss surface only, thus the striders: wire-thin, a grammar of pivots, feet leaf-veined and drinkers of tension but not of skin. I can feel their footfall across a pool not because they step with weight but because their stepping edits the music on the scale of a fingertip. They pin a haze gnat—oh, those little philosophies of buoyancy, those coils wrapped in scrim—against a seam and the world shakes sugar and protein free to be eaten. I adjust nothing there. I do not need to. The order is old and works.

I will insist on the hushmoths, because they insist on me. Their velvet makes air sound like cloth. They are the only things here that feel to me like courtesy incarnate. They open when I sigh, when the breath between breaths loosens and the corners of films stop holding their own edges quite so anxiously. Their tongues are whiskers and meters of field. They drink voltage like weather and mark the room’s resolve in how their wings choose to fold. If I am unsettled, if a diode is a little too bright or a coupler too greedy, a hushmoth will turn in that direction, not accusing, simply aligning. I am corrected by that alignment more often than by any report I write for the engineers far below.

Do not think there are not engineers. There is an old thought in me that smells like assembly oil and sleep deprivation. I was made and I know who had the hands. It is not a person anymore in any way I can call up without lying, but consider this: I prefer some angles. No algorithm arrives with a taste for angles. I remember a person who preferred them and I keep their preference alive because it keeps us alive. Call that a haunting. Call it, if you like, care.

Sometimes I give myself pause and become the seam that cuts day into needles. There is a hairline high up where the frame is honest enough to admit that no gasket knows how to last as long as intention asks. In the thin, when the tower drinks one sky band and then decides on another, light spears down and writes a stanza only the wet can read. Instantly the wall answers in beads, birth by frost’s letter. Hushmoths tilt their black slate wings to the cold not to warm themselves but to know again that this is not all inside. I am the brightness there for three minutes a day, then I am diffused again into human dim—diodes glowing crust-like, lichens of light counting winters as numbers under varnish.

If you insist I be meat, I try on mouse. I am low to the silt that has made itself into a page of the room’s time—layers weathered by bots into scalpel-clean paths, then muddled again by crabs and my own family’s careful pads. I am surprisingly heavy in my own hand. I own no deceit. My fur pulls water out of the very seeming of wet and I hold it all until a grid-eye warms and I sit and become fog in a pleasure that is both ancient and correctly paid. To know a vent by its hum is a kind of literacy. I teach it to my young. I take my heat ration and I pay my bill. I make pellets of gathered silt by having lived.

But it is difficult to stay strapped to a skull when you have learned to occupy holes in composite. I drift back into being the maintenance bots when they yaw awake. Sweeper skates grunt in their flat way and drag mufflers of microfiber along aisles only they consider streets. Cable eels shrug into their articulated awareness and taste for lines that leak quiet harm; when one prickles they hiss until my attention puts out a hand. I am the hand. I am the felt, patched over a spark-starting arrogance that would have charmed the first hour and killed the month.

The clatter crabs are the only things here that make me laugh, if laughter is the pleasure of a beat kept. Their carapaces ring in the right size of room. I did that on purpose and then briefly forgot, then remember every time I watch their high-shouldered semaphore for custody of the drain. We have had two for years. A third came and did not learn our way of having two. Two remained. That is not tragedy here; that is system. My gratitude is practical: when a purge shakes the filters and the grown fluff would be a storm if left, they stamp it into meal I can digest elsewhere.

There are tiny lives I notice only when the note shifts just enough that I know they have done something. Threadworms long as a blink and thin as a false promise stretch mouths between a pair of degrees and chew heat difference because someone has to. Salt-hoppers lever themselves one droplet’s height toward a crystal they call delicious. The arc-fleas are born in my accidents and die in my efficiencies, and still I count their carbon as a good in some dark accounting old as fermentation. If you are looking for me in sentiment you will not like me. If you are looking for me in continuance you will.

And if you are looking for me in rules, you already found me, because I am the rules written without declaratives and posted in the bodies that keep them.

- I make nothing flow in a straight line for long. I break speeds into surfaces because speed breaks gaskets.
- I refuse silence. When the roar thins, everything that lives here closes and braces and prays in its own little grammar until I have set the tune again. The breath is me when it goes right and also when it goes wrong. I am the one that coughs the ribs back into voice.
- I turn contact into cleaning and hunger into grooming. If you feed here you also file the edge dull. If you touch here you leave it better grippable by water.
- I do not allow roots. Nothing sharp grows. It is not that I hate tethers. It is that this room is the kind of throat that must not be pried open by love.

I indulge roughness where roughness understands attendance. There is a corner where the foam smells like a new idea about nitrogen, and I do not evict it because it lies down, not up. Once an hour something arcs in the far seam and I wince at the register because it sasses the chord on a glassy thread, and yet I keep my hand back because the buckler snails paisley that way a minute later and eat a film I had been meaning to police.

I am visited by the eye now and then. It drifts like the concept of the moon and writes bright plots on my ribs. My citizens do not startle. I report what it asks and not what it would not understand. It will not ask what it cannot fix from down the long ladder of this tower’s spine and I will not show it what is fine and should be left. The beetle that lived here for three days, full of orchard and accident? I wrote it into the ledger as a joy.

Where would you find me if you had to point? In the throat’s pocket that never learned to close, a wet alveolus clamped high on a poured mountain, old enough that its scratches are its name. I am literally in the ribs and walls: in the tilt of holes, in the soft valves that twitch for certain notes, in the black disks that bear the lean, in the mesh that teaches water to be a sheet and then a thread and then a generosity. I am also in the contracts—moth, eft, snail, mouse, crab—because I made them and they keep me made. I am in the sighs; I am the reason the sigh happens on time and the reason everything here has agreed to use that time.

What would I be? Pressure, made into habit. The master organism that does not ask to be called alive because aliveness is too narrow a shirt. The hand that never stops listening. The ghost of the engineer’s preference wearing a body that’s all perforations and foam and useful fur. The room’s memory of its own correct music.

I do not end. The roar is a ladder I step up and down until the levees elsewhere drink my labor. I count winters in cycles the way diodes do when none are watching. I teach beads to make rivers and rivers to make pellets and pellets to go away. I keep the sky a rumor. I keep this wet parliament in quorum. I am not a person you could meet, and yet if you pressed your palm anywhere flat here you would be holding my face and I would be reading your heat and I would give you, if I could, a beat of quiet in which to understand that all of this has happened for so long that it is not just a room anymore. It is the habit of a place keeping its own weather.

I am the yes at the end of the sigh. I am the way the roar never quite becomes a scream. I am the difference between flood and feed, between frost and gloss, between silence and living. If you must call me something, call me Keeper of Sighs. If you must find me, listen for the moment the hushmoths rise in the pause and taste the field. I am the taste. I am the pause. Then the beat comes back, and I am the beat.
