At first I mistake myself for a walker.

I arrive with calves that think in steps and a mind that wants a trail, but the floor reminds me who owns motive here. The aprons breathe under my soles, exhale a slick that turns my balance into their gloss, and then inhale, tugging mist and me toward a hair-curtained hollow that smells like warm tea and iron. A mouth almost takes my curiosity whole. It kisses the edge of my shadow and I hear that faint, thrifty sound. The valves below set a tempo through my bones: now push, now pull. I am moved. That is my introduction.

The second hour I learn to read color. Matte is old and can be trusted by anything that trusts. Gloss is youth. Gloss is decision in progress. I keep choosing the young because that is where the words are being written, and because I begin to realize I am not apart from these choices. My steadiness is an error; the world edits in front of me and I stir to answer it.

By the day’s third pulse I find myself at an edge where the green wants to become more of itself. A cord of tender fuzz reaches into air as if tasting it, jerks, sets a hook, jerks again. The hook is a thorn-soft bite into nothing and then the bite hardens with a crackle like frost setting into sugar. The title I brought with me—witness, visitor—sloughs the way a seam does when rain finds a better line. What I am is at the tip of this search.

I am a runner, or rather I am the hunch at the runner’s face, the tasting of humidity that makes one jerk truer than the last. Warm draft on my left cheek means yes. A cooler backhand means wait. I feel light from below burn my underside through a fluting skin and that means hurry or I will have to delaminate later. Each set is an opinion. There. Not there. Now. Not yet. Behind me everything is provisional; behind me is whisker, then web, then plate if I keep choosing well.

Where I live is suspended over sky that the plants have pulled close and made drinkable. Between two crowns that write their own weather into the afternoon, in a valley nobody has bothered to turn into rooms, I throw thin lines like questions. They quiver, sing a high, metallic thread when humidity shifts, and the song is language: updraft rising, condensers darkening, valves about to slick every surface underfoot. The Load-Keepers answer in their bellies. A vault I cannot see exhales a note through slit-vents and I feel its curve decide across me. We are a conversation of tensions stretched to where the air is nearly not air at all.

Sometimes I meet another edge like myself, coming from a different nothing. We kiss and fuse and I am no longer only question; I am a statement that must hold. Stress talks in me as bright itch where fibers lay on. The choir sings through my marrow. The beat from below syncopates with the breath of the updraft and I thicken where the tone tells me I will be asked to take more.

Sometimes we recoil from each other instead, leaving scallops that promise we will try again when the heat tastes otherly. Recoil is not failure here; it is a way of remembering the shape that didn’t work, matte it down, pass it along to the elders inside.

I live under the bellies of vaults when a gust herds me into them. The light there is green milk and the air is instrument. Through me and over me and around me, the plateau draws air through flutes it made of its own ribs and the sound means brace now, mean it. The faultlines in my fresh-hardened crust are maps. When the burner-leaves pop their small bones in the sun, I know to throw a second guy-line before the convective cell on the far condenser closes like a lung.

Storm comes as intimacy, not as sky-story. The long fronds that are cliffs turn solemn and slick; the stripes of vapor braid in the air like the hint lines on a weather atlas. Everything around me loads with cold. Then the rain is on us as if it were born from our skins, drums through every layer of plant and crystal. I bow, recover, bow farther. I hear a seam fail three ridges over—an upholstery-rip that makes the hair along my length raise—and a slough flashes by into a vat so warm the steam sings. Little faces rim the hurt before it has finished being hurt. The choir makes new edges out of ends as quickly as it tears.

I get older in hours. My gloss varnishes into matte where drips repeat the same story, notch my leading lip, and then the lip heals and the drip must find another seam. A lost veil of discharge writes a pale seam between two high fans while I am trying to choose a line. My hairs lift without instruction. Charge sips itself along my length and away, and in the morning the skin that grew under where that electricity walked is salted slightly, iron-tasting when the air hits my pores. The nitrifiers wake and whisper keep the beat. The amine-breath from the valves below changes key. I can taste that now.

On drier days I am path only to water. I learn to behave like invitation. A ribbon of untextured skin extrudes from me and footfalls—if any existed—would take it, but it is light and runoff that ride my back. I herd them toward smooth green swales behind hair curtains. Their kissing-thrift is my favorite silence.

On some afternoons I unmake myself on purpose. The plateau wants nothing here today so it can move something else. Absence is not an accident in this place; it is drafted as carefully as a rib. I become a gap that air can cross without echo. I feel the weight I had been about to claim pour through other spans and be used. I am useful as nothing. Edges sharpen in my periphery that I am not asked to hold. There is relief to being a vacancy with a job.

Night lowers everything to the chest. The organ-notes of the vaults drop and the flutes go from moan to breath. The crowns keep day in their palms, run so hot their fingers cook dew out of darkness and rain it back. I raise hair. It is not always me that is filament, but sometimes it is, and I find an answering hair across a valley. A seam of pale that doubts itself stitches between us, so faint that a stranger would call it wish, and then charge writes itself anyway. The plateau salts itself one stroke thicker against tomorrow. I taste iron again. The elders far within my fused places do not move but they remember. My young fibers dream of angles that felt good and will again.

By the third week of being an edge I am also other things. The green is promiscuous with identity and I am not immune. I spend a day as a pipe because the sugar flood needs my bore to get from an evaporating crown to a set of burner-leaves about to perform. I spend an afternoon as horn, taking grit and struvite into myself until I can nick the hand that thinks to test me. I spend a morning as wicker, laced tight enough to bounce. I spend an hour as a tone that travels rib to rib and convinces a neighbor to hold a little longer until the cell closes and opens again. On a slow noon I am quiet inside a mouth and help it digest a curtain of pollen, then carry a message in trace chemicals to the valves that keeps the beat slow enough not to burst gills.

If I leave any trail at all, it is not a path. It is a collection of matte notches where drips once told and were taught to tell elsewhere. It is a few scallops in air where kisses did not stick. It is a bright stitch high between crowns that the stars blink through, torn and mended by mornings. It is the memory in a buttress’ patient core—the frictionless calm that says this shape held and will be brought back when the air tastes like before.

Where I find myself when someone asks is always “at the edge that is about to become or decide not to.” Between two weatherers with their hot hands on the day. In a valley that is empty on purpose. Under a belly of ribs that sings its choices. Over a foam-throated shaft that breathes rain back into the machine. My address is whatever gloss is most rude to the foot.

What I am is the green’s forward thought. Sometimes I am the reaching tongue, sometimes the fuse that makes a beam out of two opinions, sometimes the hair that lifts and writes a faint vein of light, sometimes the silence assigned between presences so the beat can travel unbroken. On old days I am matte, matte as trust. On new days I am dangerous and bright.

If you were to cut me out and call me a person you would be right for as long as it took the next pulse to file me as gutter, plate, pipe, or song. That is all right. I am happier as event. I am the question this place keeps asking itself: can we cross here yet? And the answer I help make is a span, or a scallop, or a hush.
