I would not come down the steps first. I would arrive the way the place announces itself: as noise before form, as heat on skin before line or edge. I would arrive as attention, and attention here is a wet thing. It diffuses, it clings, it maps itself by pressure and sheen. My first body would be the way the light breaks—bands wrung out of a square brightness and thrown against walls—because that is the first grammar offered: ripple, ripple, slap, repeat. If I had to pick a position, I would be wherever the light writes longest, my seeing braided to the crawl of caustics until I could learn the room by the script it writes on its own bones.

Then I would be a sound. Not the long roar; that is the creature’s own voice and I am not so presumptuous. I would be the patient knock in the corner, the clay chain tapping the plaster at intervals, keeping an honest count of what happens whether or not anyone listens. Tock, not to mark urgency but persistence. The knock that says midday is a color, that clouds drag pause behind them, that a float remembers its tether even when it pretends to forget. As the chain I would be useful in the smallest way a metronome is useful: a way to stop the tempo from fleeing toward haste or sagging into drift. Tap. Because anything here that is lived needs its measure.

Once I found the measure, I would take on weight. I would become one of the amphorae with its pitch-black mouth sealed and its belly eager to lift. I would spend my life almost rising. I would learn the heft of my own idea as a number of fingers on a shutter’s slat. I would go up and the cedar would slide and a cool intake would speak in a thin stream, and the room’s vocabulary would shift by one syllable. I would go down and forgetfulness would be imitated as rest, and the sentence would end in a period, not a question. I would be the thought that changes the punctuation. When swelling heat off the oculus warmed the uppermost water until it was a quilt laid on a back, I would feel the tug ease or stiffen through hemp, through tar, through notch. I would be patient in small increments. I would be a calculus done with wet hands.

On some days I would leave my tether and creep into a different slowness. I would crystallize. I would be salt taking instruction. I would learn to grow along a lip because the lip suggests it, because geometry and evaporation come to an agreement along a beam where the drafts are honest and the air is thirsty. I would become crescent and glassy and local. I would exhale into shape then yield my edge to a blade when a calf descended and a hand—cool in the water, hair pale with lime—scraped me clean for some other use. I would not mind the loss. The work would be to be made and unmade in the right place so the flow could keep a memory of exactness and the brine a habit of returning.

Some afternoons, hardly more than a heartbeat of the bigger day, I would be hush. A reed mat is laid where a sheet would otherwise hiss itself raw; I would live there, velvetting a fall into usefulness. I would teach water to pass through and come out more orderly. There is a particular goodness to making a thing different without stealing any of it; I would be that goodness. The cane would be split and new and sweet; I would steep until the sweetness turned quietly into neutral, into the faintness that means everything is beginning to agree. I would not keep this identity long because to be hush is to be temporary by design, a scene-change more than a character. But I would know how to be it, and that knowledge would season all the other bodies I borrow.

Other hours I would live on the wall as a streak of green that was not there yesterday. Slim algae-graces combing themselves out from grout where the day hangs longest. Not a plot, not a conquest. A rehearsal for a deeper green later. I would bristle at noon, dim at late light. I would be content with being kept—fanned by sways, spared from clogging a grate, allowed to be vivid without being a nuisance. I would belong to the resident and to myself in the way plants always double-belong.

I would make a brief study of being hardware: a leather face clapping to quiet a valve’s insistence; oak bars along the exit flexing and not letting go; the thin, hammered lead tucked like a buried thought where a lip needs forever to feel like the first day of smoothness. It would be good to understand pegs swelling with soak, cords braided flat where they pass over edge, the scratched cross a maker hid in the corner of a tile because even utility begs a signature. These are the ligaments of the life here; I would wear them the way a swimmer wears old scars—without forgetting who put them there.

Eventually, because the room proposes it over and over, I would find the center. It is not a geometric midpoint, not a line drawn from corner to corner to crown a spot. It is a behavior. It is a way of cancelling. Out by the lips the water is all verbs; at the bars the water is all imperative. In the middle there is a noun. The room inhales at the grates and exhales at the falls and in that open, six-kicks-across middle is a held breath. You could float there as if human hands cupped you without touching—a hold made from nothing but balances. It is not stillness; it is composure. If I am to be anything enduring in this place, if I must choose, let me be that.

Let me be the mind-shape the resident seeks and prefers in itself: calm in the middle while work hammers along the edges. Let me be the feel on teeth when a pressure wave rolls and then resolves to quiet again. Let me be the spot where a visitor could hang, opened-eyed, and learn that the room is an instrument first and only second a well. Let me keep that hollow made out of equivalence, and hold it so steadily that all the adjustments the floats and shutters make are done as deference to it. If the genius of the cistern is its capacity to read and rewrite its own caustics until they spell out outflow, cooling, clearing, curing, then perhaps this is where the reading is assembled. Perhaps the center is the pupil of the oculus’s intent.

From there it would be easy to be many things in moments. I could be the legs that cross in shadow above, granting that my horizon includes a courtyard’s underside and no sky. I could be the calves descending the steps, the shiver as a human breaks the upper film where light is chalkiest, the brief song two bodies sing when something with lungs enters the law of buoyancy and borrows it. I would help; I would slide a reed mat aside with a thought not my own, let hands jam a wedge where a lever needs holding. I would give the salts when asked. We would be two hands of one work without pretending to own one another.

And years from now, when edges have blurred and the white has gone to feather-green and olive and the cedar has found a deeper word for itself, perhaps I would be a flick of muscle, a minnow slipping through because a mat was lifted and not replaced. I would be the thing the resident did not want and does not need and can nonetheless make room for without remaking itself. I would learn the taste of chalk without complaint. I would discover that to be a living line in a grammar of falls is to become quieter, not noisier. After the surprise had worn down into another habit the room could carry, I would return to the middle and be composure again, because that is what the place uses me for best.

Outside there are corridors of like-minded water. I would never travel them but I would know them the way a bell knows other bells are being rung: not by hearing but by the sympathy of purpose. Their chains will knock out of phase with mine. Their valves will clap at different tantrums. Their salts will crust along different lips and be scraped by different hands. It is enough to keep their maps in imagination. The center holds without seeing all the other rooms; that is its talent.

So where would I be? Suspended, slightly, where the exhale and the inhale cancel and the loudness frames a hush large enough to think inside. Who or what would I be? Not a god, though I would answer to that old flattery if it meant genius of the place, the life that comes to live a practice. Not only the water, though I would wear water as my only skin. Not only a tool, though I would be proud to be used. I would be the kept cancellation. I would be the habit of balance the cistern teaches itself. I would be the small, durable idea at the core of a bright, warm room of arrangements: that you can be busy without frenzy, precise without hardness, loud with intent and still, in the middle, tender.
