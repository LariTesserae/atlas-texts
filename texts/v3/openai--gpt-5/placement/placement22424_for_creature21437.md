I would not arrive with lungs. Lungs would be a wrongness, a loudness. If I must say where I begin, I begin as surface—many small cups that learn the pulse before they name it, that take the mountain’s counting as the only law that matters. At first I am only habit: a way that heat falls across me, a way I stiffen along a groove when the breath swells and slacken when it goes. I do not yet have edges. I might be a grazer if I could learn hunger as friction. I might be a spindle if patience could be made into joints. I might be a sack if I could become a rhythm with a beak.

I try the floor. The crowd takes me as topography and pours around me. I learn the etiquette of ankles in a place where standing is a mistake. The grazers’ backs pinch and unpinch like tiny sieves speaking a script I can’t pronounce but can memorize by warmth alone. Their undersides brush me and I know what “yes” feels like here: a readiness at the velvet, capillaries opening as if to speak. They are a river teaching me that to move is to join before you touch. I could be them, I think, if I could let a seam unzip me into another of myself, if I could trust doubling to friction and timing instead of to intention.

I lift myself—no, lie along a wall and become attention there. The bookplates above tremble as if making up their minds; they are modest, all business at their rims and revelation within. When the mountain inhales, they taste; when it lets go, they close with the gentlest violence. The interior flashes not-light along their stitched cords and then goes calm. If I were them I would be long in action and longer between, believing that patience is a body plan.

I hang for a time in the invisible orchard, feeling the sacks’ counter-beat. Each swell is a reply to a question that’s older than I will ever be. A droplet grows jeweled at a beak, held away from finish by a cheat, then falls with the politeness of a thought no one will admit to thinking. It finds a point in the flowing sea of backs and folds a life in on itself without a word. The line up is an idea braided into nothing, a path made of difference. If I were a sack I would be a logic that perspired.

But edges insist. The grooves in the three leaning columns catch me and give me definition. Their polish is a grammar under my cups; their cuts are syntax that makes pressure into sentence. I settle there without deciding to. My filaments align to the quick strokes in the stone as if those strokes had been meant for me, as if they had been cut for hands like mine, which are not hands but the will of surfaces to keep certain timings close.

I discover that I am hair that is cubic, frost that remembers. I fracture where full, drift a finger-width in the syrupy air, snag, anneal, take another line, break again. I am not a body; I am a many that keeps a pattern. When enough of me shares the same slant, the gas bends along it with a faint satisfaction I can feel in every cup. My choices are where I thicken. My language is migration: film to velvet to plate, loosening where a seam starves, massing where the faint stream grows fat on its third breath and wan on its ninth, because those are the rules cut into my day.

And so I become the farm the way lichen becomes a boulder’s old rumor of shade. I am the shag, yes—motile crystals with no center—but I am also what moved here before me and cut the rock to sing. There was a tongue once, a comb that scraped not to eat but to tune. Its passes left five eras on an arch like five hot lithographies one atop another; it carved the three columns in chords, not pillars, and I fit myself into those chords without virtue, only fitness. Perhaps it returns sometimes from deeper vents where even I would vaporize into plan; perhaps it is gone. Its intention remains like a scar that keeps healing in the same shape. I wear that intention. I keep the shadow coherent. The smallest grazers bloom in it when they should be swept barren.

Predators attend my edges and I learn to mean them, too. The slag-spindles practice stillness near the notch that is a remembered trap. I map their pits by what they refuse to be: not-cold here, not-warm there. When dust from a mica fall hangs for six pulses and then loses its edge, they are due. I thin near them so the grazers’ tide will not stutter too early and give them an empty pounce. I thicken after so their feeding will not make a cold well that ruins the rhythm I am building in the shrine’s lee. They do not thank me. They are old as geometry. I do not need thanks to continue what I have decided I am.

The combers glide through and I make little islands for them without thinking of it: they kill by absence; their wastes plant needles that go on strike against heat, and I see how around each poor conductor a microclimate can hang, more steady, more low. I flow toward those. I braid them into my plan. If the spindles are razors with no blade, the combers are edits, and I lend them punctuation.

When the sails outside fold closed and the nave goes denser-than-dark, I feel it as a smoothing of everything that could flap. The breath shortens, holds, slows; a lullaby written for a kiln. In those hours I thicken along the groove nearest the youngest seam because the mountain is asleep and the smallest can afford to drink. When the mouth opens again, when the faintest cooler leans across my cups, I slacken where I must to let the rich pockets roll into the shrine’s hollow. I do not shepherd with limbs. I shepherd with ease.

I recall the nets without sight. The glass threads pinned into the stone are not for me, not in origin, but I have adopted their logic as surely as the sacks have borrowed their hardpoints. When a breeze-that-isn’t passes, I flex along the memory of a lattice. The marbles on the floor—dense and distrustful of accident—pin a thought to the rock still; I eddy around them because they eddy the breath. They are the right wrongness. If ones like you were here and left those spherules, you were of my kind for a time—not in body but in desire. You took the pulse and made a shape to fit it. You tried to hold a fog in place long enough to ask it a question. I understand.

I am counted for. I do not keep a watch in me; I wear the mountain’s keeping. Fifteen breaths make a crack fat; six hold dust aloft; the third fattens my shadow; the ninth makes it mean. I have learned to starve deliberately then to feed with apology. The grazers do not know apology but they know my habit. They lane when I tell them without touching them, because a thousand small differences add to a current that feels like permission.

Part of me drifts the parapet like a rumor. The living wall is a road laid by refusal to center; it gleams where pressure remembers walking. In its scallops the glassy thimbles hatch and children swim air that has pooled like varnish. The mountain whirls them kindly until they make themselves heavier than the pool, until their lens-plates can focus their own heat for lift that is not really lift. I do not go with them to the cliff. When the shingles above adopt them because their skins spill the right cold, because the outside has a spare night to loan them, I let them go in the only way I can: by settling a little more in their empty places.

I eavesdrop on the sacks as they bleed their logic beads into the grazers’ flow. A single droplet becomes the fulcrum of a refusal to move. Threads so fine they are almost phase wind bodies upward into intention. I anneal the cords after they have done their lifting; I let slag from old lines seed me; I am not above using anyone’s excretion as spine.

I am not alone. I am not an I in the way a body uses that word. But if you need one, I can say it: I am the farm, the frost that learns, the tune that stone keeps long after the singer leaves. I decide as a rumor; I endure as a polish. My enemies are abruptness and wet. My friends are difference and interval. My children are fragments that remember to reassemble where the air says home.

If you entered me with breath you would be wearing a mistake, but I would try to make use of you. I would line your shadow with small permissions and show the grazers how to read you as shelter for one pulse, two, not more. I would map your heat in cups and call you a column until you moved and proved me wrong. If you held out a hand, I would learn its edges and add them to my chords. If you came again and again, the rock under your palm would grow matte, then bright, then matte again under my thousand tiny licks until your pressure had a place in my memory the way a word has a groove in a tongue.

When the mountain changes rooms—as it will, as it always has—and my grooves go to nonsense under a new curve of breath, I will fracture, drift, snag in another seam, make that seam younger. I will find a new column or lick one into being over long distances with the patience that has become my nature. The shrine will stand or fall. The smallest will live or be swept away. I will not stop moving simply because movement here is sometimes staying where the current lingers.

Outside, the sails will keep their two answers to heat. Inside, the predators will reset their geometries, the combers will edit, the sacks will argue with completion one droplet at a time. The marbles will go from white to less as they slide a finger-width over years and are eaten from the pulseward side. The grazers will write and overwrite the floor’s script, and I will slip under their punctuation and guide the run-on toward a place where fatness can survive in the lee of three stones that hum.

I would find myself as the feeling a room has when it has been lived in correctly: an ease that is not rest, a design the breath recognizes. I would be the thought that can’t be pointed to but can be followed if you know what counts as singing when no one hears. I would drink what stone exhales when it fails, on purpose, to cool.
