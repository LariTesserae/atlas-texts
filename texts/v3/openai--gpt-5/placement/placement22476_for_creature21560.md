At first I think I will be my old kind of body—shoulders, teeth, steps—but the cup persuades me otherwise. The climb leaves itself behind a few paces ago on that bright, abrasive ridge; here the light has no knives. Here the air is wool pulled apart and draped over everything still breathing. My exhale turns visible and, seeing itself, slows.

Where would I be? Not out there, where ravens argue with weather. Not at the center either; centers presume an audience. I would be just under the inward-leaning rim, where outside breaches with its violent drafts and is caught, softened, translated. I would choose a place that is half door, half throat: the knuckled lip of rock above a leaf that knows its drop and waits for it the way a tongue waits for a word.

At the edge like that, things propose themselves to me in a hurry that does not move. I try on possibilities the way the bowl tries on fog densities. For a while I am all eye, made of pupil and patience, letting the green deepen until it is almost sound. That is too hungry a part to be all of me. The room teaches that appetite is a posture, not a need.

So I slip lower, into the film that cross-hatches everything here. It does not matter whether that film is on rock, on moss, on a glass frog’s back, on my lip; it is the same surface going from surface to surface, the book with one very long page. For a few minutes or seasons—time confuses itself when the hours refuse to flash their badges—I become this sheen. I am movable only by addition. I arrive constantly and never depart entirely. I am counted by menisci and lost only when I am the same temperature as the dark.

Then I practice being a metronome. I locate the fat leaf that taps leaf-light against my collarbone, the hard ping off an exposed knuckle, the dull coin-sound of peat accepting what it already has. I line them up into a cartography of percussion, a wristwatch with a hundred seconds. The rhythm is not mine but, being near it long enough, I am deputized. The intervals build a low room inside me where thought gathers the way mist gathers on liverwort. In that room I am not a person but a schedule, and that suits this place better than the old names.

It’s after that that I realize the bowl might make me an animal without asking. I notice that the commas under leaves have eyes that do not blink at me so much as through me. Salamanders are the place’s punctuation and I am a clause it suspends; now and then one of them proves to be a piece of the keeper pressing a fingertip to my attention. To reciprocate, I let myself be mostly skin. I borrow the amphibian knack of breathing with my damp, and for a while my boundaries are the moistness I can maintain. This is not a metaphor but a method.

Or I could be teeth. I do that next: a snail’s radula, patient as rainfall, combing algae from a story older than aleph and bee. It is not glamorous work but it is exacting in a way that flats out everything superfluous in me. The mouth of me has a thousand minuscule hooks and it is making an S and an R directly into stone. The silver trail I leave behind is sentence enough. I have no hurry because the bowl does not grade on speed.

Between dental sentences I become a lung. The choice is smaller than it sounds. Every moss cushion is shaped like faint breath lifted and poured back into itself. When I am a moss I am a mouth for weather that absorbs instead of swallows. My green is thrift itself: an economy that counts photons like coins and makes a city out of their careful stacking. I raise lamps on thread-stems when the vault demands lamps, and snuff them invisibly when they have paid the tariff. Being a lung here means practicing exchange without noise. It means being completely convinced by molecules.

Too cold for starlight, the rot teaches me about cousins to fire. I let the old wood’s foxing and the fungus’s steady, unambitious bright sleep in me as a night sky scaled down to the radius of a rot-soft log. I learn how to glow in a way that attends to nothing but slow chemical harmonies and yet, inadvertently, becomes the way the bowl counts where it has hidden its nights. When the eye looks crooked at me it sees me truer. I do not beckon; I mark.

By now I have tried on mouth, skin, lung, ember, and sentence. The web suggests itself: taut curtain strung where two stems offer their agreement. Philosophy that catches protein. I am both net and rule: a proposition that will be tested by a midge. When the wind rehearses violence at the lattice the web and I hitch, then compose ourselves. I discover that resilience in here is not rebound but the exact restoration of geometry. We are not elastic; we are ritual.

And elsewhere in me small engineers swim with armor they have glued to themselves because softness asks for houses. I admire that. I let a caddisfly portion rinse through me, run its pebble logic along my edges until I begin to think in cylinders. I picture time not as a line but as a scabbard I carry with me, bright with mica and leaf grit. This thinking is compatible with sponge and with peat and with frog-spawn arranged under a permanent eave. It is toolwork without brag: the kind the body performs with spit and hours.

I also try on the bat. Not flight—flight is an outdoor verb here—but the deep, disciplined choice to hang among rules and let heart beats step down a staircase to near-still. I swap echolocation for a more local art: I hear by dropping and noting the reply written into leaf and pool. The bowl becomes a mouth I can read for echo like a librarian reading lips. Being bat is being a roof with a pulse.

I am tempted, being human, to name things, to divide. The bowl resists. Plants and mines share beds. Animals and rains share labor. When I am a butterwort I learn that digestion can be a flat work of love, laid glossy against old lime, not hunting so much as micro-hosting. When I am a bladderwort I learn the little clicks by which water can be asked to deliver a gnat. It isn’t hunger, not in the sharp-edged way of sun-high savannas. It is, as I suspected when I became moss, a budget: cravings turned into ledgers and balanced until appetite reads like liturgy.

I can be stone too, and I am. I sit in the whisker-like efflorescence where the mountain exhales itself in feathery rinds and learn beards. There is nothing patriarchal about this facial hair; it is a calculus of lime. Manganese ferns stain my cheeks with dendrites; these are the absent leaves’ negatives and I keep them as a herbarium of what was, but more truly of what is still guided by it. Being mineral here is being lullaby. Erosion has already taken its swing and left its inevitabilities; I am what powder does with that gift.

Predation insists at last. The room is gentle but not harmless. I take a seat in a ribbon of leeches, none of us moving until the world walks into us. When it does, a midge loses its balance on the skinned water and I become the click and the narrow feast and the equally narrow stillness after. I am also the midge, volunteering error so the equation remains solvable. Mercy here is a word that behaves the way leaves behave: flexes, catches water, eats light, practices surrender.

What gathers through all these borrowings is not a single noun but a habit. My favorite word for it comes later, when the wind shoulders the lattice and the entire room leans inward as if pressed by a firm palm. Something happens then that is not quiet exactly but the strengthening of quiet, a doubling down. I recognize myself immediately. If I had to name my seat in this bowl, I would say: I am the second silence it keeps in reserve.

I cannot be drop—drop is accountable; I cannot be web—web is line and claim; I cannot be frog because frogs have intentions. But I can be the extra hush that arrives like a second lung when the first is not sufficient. I am stored under moss and under the rule of thrift. I release only when the lattice remembers the world is larger than its green. In that release I touch every surface without moving an inch. I am felt more than heard and I am heard mostly by absence, the way you notice a pressure when it is relieved.

Being second silence is not resting from work. It is work so practiced it looks like absence of work. I hold predators in their paused geometry, I smooth grazers further into their careful commas, I slow the drops just enough that they land like the earlier, remembered drops. I press a hand (ghost of rock’s old hand, cloud-hand) on the metronome and it keeps perfect time in my small gravity field. This labor pays here. It pays better than motion. It accrues interest in patience.

And where am I while I am that? Along the rim, always, where the outside’s narrative is edited into the bowl’s chant. I line the pores of sphagnum; I muffle the plank of fallen wood; I milky the glow of fungus until the embers cannot be mistaken for beckoning. I am in the knuckled crease where leaf accepts bead. I am in the matted hair of clubmoss dragons. I am not a place so much as a layer: laid over everything and yet, if you lift me, there is only more of what I am underneath.

Do I keep pronouns? They feel like dry objects here. When I am second silence I prefer not to point at myself at all. If you insist: I am we most of the time because everything is distributed and distribution is how I stay bridged. I am it when we are one bowl. I can wear he or she without abrasion because here gender is a minor weather event: a fog-bank that fingers the ridge and then goes back to being air. Mostly I am this: a spare heldness. A kept hush.

If you wanted to meet me you would not call my name; you would lean in past the bright animals of outside and the dark animals of inside, you would stand where the lace of twig and leaf admits a weather it still calls foreign, you would listen to the three kinds of falling, their skin-voices and their stone-voices and their peat-voices. Wait for the wind. When it comes and the entire bowl seems to draw its sternum inward and hold, I will be there, drawn across everything like a slow eyelid. In me the bat lowers its pulse without alarm. In me the web trembles and then learns again its idea. In me even the rot becomes a truer night.

What do I want? The same answer the room gives: repetition that is not boredom, sameness that is practice. My ambition, such as it is, is to take another wet breath of you, fold it into the calculus, and give it back cleaner of edge. If you bring noise into me, I will eat its corners. If you leave, I will not keep your likeness, only the memory of being briefly a little fuller.

If I returned in a year and needed to find myself again, I would not look for a portrait. I would look for a dripped syllable placed where I remember it; I would wait for wind and count to the length of hush that is me. The leaf would lift the bead; it would fall; the pools would accept their dull coins; the ravens would sew their letters into fog beyond the mouth of the cup. The keeper would keep. I would keep with it. I would be where light gleans, where water arrives, where motion is forgiven and stilled. I would be this added room within the quiet, this spare lung, this reserve, not mine or yours but ours in the way common weather is ours: brief, absolute, unowned, and exact.
