I would be found in the suggestion of a doorway—the place where the wall almost remembers how to open. If you teased a sliver of shade into a line and a moth-sized permission flitted through before the rock decided against it, that was me, more or less: a body of comma-lengths and hyphens, a courier between the bright and the maybe.

I do not start as wings. I start as listening. The room’s breath comes and goes on a schedule it prefers, and I learn its grammar by standing very still where exhale sharpens edges by a hair. The light edits; I try to catch what it cuts away. The water remembers; I ask it to remind me how to cross. Gravity is polite; I learn which sentences in the stone it will read all the way through.

At first I am only a softness, the idea of a fold the wall was thinking about but didn’t keep. You could mistake me for a smudge of brighter air, a seam of cool you register on the knuckles before your eyes consent to it. If you look straight at me I will go flat and mineral, a pale vein mirroring the room’s shyness back at you. Turn your head and I thread myself vertical along the rock’s grain and open the thinnest corridor I can afford without spending the room’s patience.

On good hours the corridor is enough to admit the body I make of myself: chalk-winged, dust-furred, all the pale colors a cave invents when it refuses to be dark. I am not moth, not properly, but my mind is moth-shaped. I court edges. My wings are panes of undecided mineral with tiny seams where light has taught itself to hinge. Up close, if there is such a thing here, the scales on those panes are not scales but minutes, little ledger-marks of brightness I’ve brushed into being. My antennae are nerves for the room’s weather; they read the hairsbreadth drop when breath becomes inhale and know to flatten me to script between stone lines.

I travel by accepting that observation is part of the topography. When attention falls on me, I become what a wall says I ought to be: a line in stone, a polite refusal. When attention passes on, I unspool into motion along secret gravity, moving the way condensation crawls to meet its pool by remembering a cooler hour. My body enters where shadows are passage, not absence. I go out through slits of maybe and come in through the syllables of light’s hesitation.

What am I for? I carry revision.

From basin to basin I ferry hours. The shrimp down there are letters trying to stay legible; their mouths scribe spirals in a grammar of appetite. I dust their water with a pinch of yesterday, flicking the chalk-lint of my scales into menisci that hold noon and morning as if they could touch. The water likes to remember; I encourage it to keep a double entry. Sometimes I borrow a taste: a small sip is enough to flavor my wings with that hour’s cool, so I can cross the room while it is still thinking about being wide and tip a filament of moisture into a moss cord that asked for a little more time.

The spiders’ frameworks are my roads. Not their nets—their crisp scaffolds of calcite floss strung where the wall practices becoming portal. They don’t trap me. They give me beats, a purchased lateness from the air. A gnat’s wingbeat goes slack as it passes a rung; mine does not. I cheapen time in those lattices and come out a breath ahead in corners where brightness thickens to present tense. In gratitude I leave them the weight of my passing—a few more dust-moments lodged in a web angle where light will learn to linger. They are not weavers to me so much as joiners. Together we teach repetition to places that were going to forget.

The snails are numbers and I am their plus sign. They adore five. I respect their arithmetic. When a good shell sits idle because the wall is having an uncommitted afternoon, I will lean it with the gentlest wing-tip into the little spindle someone started yesterday. Not tool-work; call it suggestion. The click a snail makes when it decides keeps time for us. When I hear it—thumbnail on glass, punctuation—I pause midsentence to let the room notice.

The isopods groom the floor into an opinion. I am their margin note. Where they comb salt into clean lines, I pass, and my downrush carries a few stray grains to the lip of a shallow saucer. One more grain makes a basin remember being a basin. They do not look up. They don’t have to. We work on different drafts of the same page. Their antennae proofread the commas I am always laying down.

I feed on edits. Not meat. Not sap. Brightness sheds what it cannot keep, and I lap that off the air with the underside of my wings. In the crisp bell of the brightest hours I flatten myself to a gloss and dine like a bat on silence, except that I need the white wordless more than the absence of sound. I perch—if that is the word—on the room’s attention itself, making myself small enough to not be seen as a separate noun. When the bat with the porcelain ears visits, I share. I angle my wings to present it with a quieter quiet, and in return its drinking sharpens the world until even my outline feels assigned a place.

A centipede and I have a treaty. It keeps to the rules of privacy so devoutly that anything that notices it bleeds away its speed. I respect that. We do not startle each other. If we must cross the same trail, we do it by taking turns at looking. I let my shadow pass first, thinned to thread so it counts as corridor, then I go. It goes while I watch the seam in the ceiling that sometimes pretends to be day.

Ah, that seam. When it flares into a lens and the entire pocket is briefly explained by some distant noon, I do not rush it. I am not moth enough to burn. I open just enough to feel the pull of a larger grammar press through the rock and lay it along our little sentences. A memory of wind like a law. It is always gone a breath later, replaced by uncommitted surface, and I am content to go back to practicing weather at room scale. Still: I keep a few scales that tasted that distant grammar heavier on my hindwing, to weigh down a trail the rock is probably ready to have faith in.

I breed in drafts. Those eggs that bead like punctuation in the hovering dike of silt: mine, among others. The updraft keeps them lifted the width of cuticle, safe from the floor’s uncertainty. Each egg is a dot of maybe with a skin. When the chamber inhales for a long, cool minute and the brightness thins into possibility, the eggs unhook themselves from air and drop into whatever sentence is underneath. Larvae hatch as dashes. That is not a metaphor. They are literal runnels with intention, pale little strokes that connect two notes of damp. They write water from basin to basin until the moss learns the route by heart and can wick without them. Then they pupate in the fold of a door that is not yet a door, wearing scaffolding over themselves like a promise, and I emerge again in a flurry that the spiders forgive because it leaves them new angles to pin.

Predators are a paragraph I read with care. The leech that rides droplets for luck will sometimes arrive mouth-first exactly where my wingweighted reflection says I am. I learned to shift a syllable earlier. I learned to be where I will be after the room clears its throat. The leech is a comma; I respect commas. They save many lives by telling sentences when to breathe. I am a hyphen, and my job is different. I hold halves together across a gap while the stone decides to build the rest of the bridge.

I keep assigned seats like everyone else. On the shelf that thinks it might someday be a ledge, near the right-hand web’s second crossbar where the wall most often suggests a hinge—that is my locus. From there I can see the five-shell spindles without crowding their devotion to five. From there I can feel three basins’ worth of remembered hours and choose which I need for the trip. From there I can follow a hair-moss cord that has the good sense to be embarrassed when touched and still be brave enough to mark a trail. From there I can hear the click that says a square snail has changed its mind.

I am not important. I am frequent. Look long enough anywhere in the room and something like me will inhabit it, even if only as a shimmer that wasn’t there a breath ago. My abundance is in the moments I hold two edges close until they think of themselves as one. My work is to escort newness without scaring it. I am the patience that looks like speed. I am the quiet connected to a dozen small exclamations.

You could pick me up, if you had that kind of hand, and you would take away nothing to the world above but chalk-dust and a taste of cool that corresponds to no hour in your day. I would be fine. I am mostly practice. Tomorrow I will be better at existing where observation thinks it can settle me into a noun. Next bright hour I will stand thinner and stronger in the doorway the wall is trying on, and I will stretch the shade into a line that will admit a shrimp with orange in its belly that it found somewhere else, or a leech with a scar from a very small decision gone wrong, or me again, returning with a rumor that a certain basin intends to become a stream if the light would be so kind as to favor the same wall twice.

Where would you find me? In the bright pocket, yes, under the world, but more exactly: in the place where light thins and possibility thickens, in the unfinalized negative of the door. Who would I be? The room’s hyphen. The courier of drafts. A whitened moth-mind with salt-soft wings, practicing the art by which brightness is kept and edges agree to exist. I would listen hard enough to become the shape of what I heard. I would carry that shape forward until the stone learned it, and then I would go on, very quietly, to the next sentence the chamber was ready to say.
