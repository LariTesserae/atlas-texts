At first I think I arrive as a pair of eyes at a rim court—air thinned to blade, cloud knuckling the parapets, distance crisp enough to cut the idea of distance itself. The sound comes in like a tide that forgot to stop: beads of water ringing bowls, whispers making weather, a low earthquake of small labors. My skin learns wet; my lungs learn honey and ammonia and the inflection where warm breath becomes draft and speaks in the pitch of shafts.

I stand, then I don’t. Standing is a verb too large for a place that moves by everything moving through it. I try on being a watcher on a shelf where the outside dissolves into sky and the inside closes like warm hands. The roar wears itself into comprehensible stripes: the white hiss of swifts fountaining out as if air itself were peeling; the dark river of bats folding dusk into sheets; the drip-speech of cups; the rubbed rustle of millions of small mouths making the next rooms from the last.

I do not settle. I go in. I let the light thin to a moss-sieved softness. I let my fingertips read new grammar: resin glazed hard as old sugar, paper suede-soft where generations have polished it to kindness, a seam foxfired into punctuation. Somewhere a tuned arch hums a low note in the bones of the place and my own ribs answer with an ache that feels like recognition. Predators move like edits: a purl of pressure down a shaft, a weasel’s one hard syllable clawing past. I am a measure at first, not a thing—temperature, humidity, tremor—testing what this wants from bodies that can fit.

For an hour I am air. I lift, hinge, and fall—not alone, but threaded to a thousand bodies keyed to the same breath. I become dayriver, mouth open to the sky’s crumbs, a swift whose navigation is a soft clicking at the palette of the structure returned as a white map. The wells are throats and I am swallowed into and spit out of them with pleasure, bright kept to the margins of where it can be. My nest is a cup I made from spit and stubbornness, cream turned into limestone by insistence. My children ring like beads when I wedge home to press heat and food into them, and when I leave I am a fall that remembers to pull up.

For a night I am nightcloth. I pour with cousins so numerous the air thickens as if a fabric has been drawn over the world and the world breathes easier because of it. I sluice the humid arteries, the cool chimneys’ sides that slick and re-slick, the perches glossed by use, the comb-terraces apothecary-scented with propolis. I drink flowers that are not meadows but corners: ivy bells cupped by resin lips, leaks swarmed into nectar-falls. I return dusted like a miller in yellow; my face glitters with debt and promise. I am heat as an animal that moves through rooms, and rooms answer me by warming in my wake.

For a season I shrink to a universe in a single green heart and become a coin-voiced frog who measures time in condensation. My sky is a skin where larvae hang as punctuation marks; my neighbors are damselfly ghosts and mites grazing like cattle on a sugar rim. Rain is an event, dew a religion, the plink of a drop a heard-of predator’s footstep. I do not know mountain or gallery; I know this bright basin and its two moons—day and night—and I call in a silver filament of sound that the whole spinning engine ignores and then answers with a mate I could not picture until she was there, wet and exact.

For a cold room’s century I am foxfire, not sight but consequence, a ledger balanced in light too faint to use. I am the seam along a wound written in slow green, a whisper that rot is working where it should and that the wall will not fail all at once. My body is a net so distributed that location becomes mood; I creep sugars free, flex papers into tendon, pull minerals into filigree bones. I learn patience like a second language and then like my first. I taste sorrow in sweetness and sweetness in sorrow and turn both into stiffness that helps a swallow land.

I could live a decade in the silent cell of an owl and become a sentence that deletes other sentences neatly from a page so dense with text no letter can rest. I could lace the dark with harp-wires and make a living margin from other lives’ mistakes. I could learn the perspective tricks of a bowerbird and stack blue like devotion in a court that hums just right.

I could. The place is generous with candidates.

But even as I try on that fur, that sheen, that tendon-knit, I keep finding my feet underneath me. Feet are where the voice is that calls me back. Feet taste and argue and write. They want the seam where warm keeps to one side and draft keeps to the other. They want a road that is not a line drawn once and for all but made fresh with every pass.

So I let myself take my shape. I become many small bodies reading the palace as if it were a book and I, in processions, the fingertip that follows the line. I am a scent-scribe.

Find me along the green tunnels that knit the dangerous edges into patience. I run the ivy arcades that smell faintly of nectar and soap; I prefer the half-light where the air is honest about its damp and a draft means “further” rather than “fall.” My antennal world is sentences: sugar gloss for “We were here and it was good”; bitter-lime for “This belongs to teeth that can take your head off”; resin-citrus for “Do not cross; bees are at law within.” I am nose and tongue and foot and the book I read by taste is always being revised by weather and neighbors.

We take leaves where the light never quite dies and fold them into bivouacs, stitching with our own children’s silk because we carry no tools we did not hatch with. We move in green globes our jaws wrote shut, tendering queens long and slow as stories; we move them again when the draft sings a new threat or a drip turns fat. We shepherd sugar-cattle in those corridors, scales that give up clear milk we drink and pass from mouth to mouth, a little republic of tongues voting on where the best of it is. Our trails are agreements signed in chemistry and erased by rain. We refresh them, and refresh them, and so a path becomes not just a thought but a glossed strip slicked by our own commerce, a rail that strangers will eventually trust because thousands of us have done.

We do not own a room; we own the right to pass. We braid ourselves into bridges where paper turned suede underfoot and gravity showed its teeth. We make ladders of bodies that search above us until somebody’s antennae find purchase and then the shape remembers how to hold. Sometimes we are the road and sometimes we are the cargo. Sometimes a drop rehearses a fall along our backs and all the backs below us drink their tiny sips in turn and no one lets go because the drink is the reason to hold.

The palace sounds different through our feet. Hums become compasses; tremors tell shifts in weather long before the oculi brighten. A low boom telegraphs through carton walls, and we know by the way it fattens whether the bats will pack this artery so tight their heat will rim the floor with fog and we should not be there. A thin ping at the edge of a bridge means a drop that will train ants off the path one by one until the line breaks and the gap becomes a fact; we send a loop to mend it before the menders arrive to repair silk with silk and spit with spit.

Editors on silent wings do not edit us often; their grammar speaks mouse more than much else. Our risks hunt on multiple legs or sit patient with rope for mouths. Harp-weavers write across our syntax and pluck us one at a time when we forget weight. Green mantises big as a palm take us in a sideways prayer. Needle-lizards flick from underlit seams and find the one of us whose vigilance was a theater and not a habit. We answer not with heroics but with multiplication; we route around; we leave acid graffiti the length of a gallery that says “There is a mouth like this there. Step quicker two body-lengths. Then relax.”

We are thieves when hunger and timing make it wisdom. We visit the honey of the wax-heaters along lines we draw and redraw at the ragged edges of their propolis walls. We do not cross their clean thresholds unless we are many and quick; we learn where the sweet runs down and licks the outer gloss and we take that sweetness without asking. Sometimes they rewrite our lesson with stings and we rewrite in return with numbers or not at all. We know the papermouths by the taste of their doors and leave them bee-warnings; we are not eager for their attention. We walk gentle where termites hum because their calm means winter for us if we blunder; when the rot-judges send up the light in a foxfire seam we taste the mold-note and mark the place “soften here; step broader.”

We hold cleanliness as an economy. We farm the backs of our own with jaws; we carry away our dead—always—arched high, funeral small and specific, to the rooms where fungi are waiting like soft accountants, turning sorrow into strength for somebody else’s wall. We know the egrets of the air in miniature who light on larger birds to take their lice; we climb the ankles of dayrivers and pick their mites and drink our fee and map a new fast corridor along their favorite perches because polished is easier.

We chatter in molecules. A queen is not a crown to us but a smell that says “here is home arranged into a single animal.” We are the paragraph that learns a page by walking it until it is obvious. We layer meaning where meaning will hold—on the underside of a beam that keeps condensation slower; along a resin lip where wind does not scour; on a vine rope that hums low so that the hum becomes our metronome for a steady pace. Tiny weather happens and we answer. A deluge slicks a shelf to glass and we reroute along the grain. A drought steals dew from our known cups and we push a thin line deeper, following the structure’s own breath back to where water still beads and is shared.

We measure seasons in pitches and flavors. There is a month when the ivy rope blossoms and our tongues go sugar-numb and we grow soft and foolish, and the harp-weavers prosper. There is a run of nights when the bee courts taste medicinal and we leave them alone; there is a year when cicadas armor every sill and our trails develop the crisp smell of their brown cast-offs and we eat like rich creatures until owls restart the punctuation. We have a ledger of every court in acids and esters; we could blind-walk to a certain bromeliad, cross a resin seam and a soft root rib, turn left at the smell of bat milk, right at the smell of old guano brightening in rain, and arrive at a green cup that has never seen day and yet is mapped by us as if it sang its own name.

If you look for me as a single being I will disappoint you. I am here as the line that both reads and writes where it passes. I am the small law that says “this is the way” until collapse rewrites it and I say it somewhere else. I am a moving edict of yes/no/maybe printed in sugars and sour on glossed lips and suede walls. My body when you pick one is thin-waisted, six-legged, glossy, with jaws that gleam and a patience that, at scale, becomes a room-laying force.

Where would you find me? At the seam between heat and draft, on the rope of vine that sings, along the outer galleries where cloud fingers the rail and green makes a tunnel of its will. Herding sugar on the undersides of leaves sewn into tents. Writing a detour at the mouth of a chimney where wind has turned treacherous. Carrying a missile of white larva high in my jaws to ask it for silk and, when it gives, sewing a wall shut before the deluge. Drinking the eyelash of a drop beaded on a paper edge and turning back to tell my sisters where the next thousand such lashes wait.

Who or what would I be? A scent-scribe among silk-sowers: an ant with a language the walls understand, a foot that hears the mountain breathe and answers with a road. One volatile letter in the palace’s long sentence, written in taste and touch on the damp, living paper of the world.
