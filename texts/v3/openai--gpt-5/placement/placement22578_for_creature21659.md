At first I am a mouthful of cold.

Not a person yet, not the craft I will keep, just the taste at the back of the throat that tells me the land has lowered itself and is holding on to a thought. The flats behind me are a sentence I have finished; this basin is the pause after it. My bootprints arrive and, unnervingly, keep arriving—edges that do not slacken for days. The air is the inside of a held breath. The horizon forgets to differentiate. Something dense has curled itself into the hollow like a fist deciding to be a town.

I stand a long time at the rim, because the rim is generous with right answers: it says down is down, three is three, names are safe. But the depression pulls at my attention the way a well-made machine pulls at the hand. I am given a belt with a small polarity square so my down will agree, and a cup that understands which way to fill when nothing else seems to. My guide speaks with gloved vowels and asks me not to say my name where the town can hear it until I am sure I want it to stick. I am used to introducing myself by leading with brightness; here the alley takes that as permission to lengthen in warning. I put away my lamp.

Where I would find myself, then, is just inside the hush, shoulder turned sideways to pass. The first thing that fits is my breath.

I do not settle into a trade because settling is the wrong verb. Things are negotiated here in increments, and you earn what you call yourself by seeing if the place agrees. I try the hard jobs at their edges. I hold a coil with two fingers while a Tender convinces it back into its note, and my breastbone learns to be a tuning fork. I follow an Edger and wet my thumb and take the bite off a stair that has grown teeth without quite deserving them; the stair softens for me the way a child’s jaw softens around a strange new food.

But it is the maps that find me.

A Topographer of Damp loans me a plate of hydrophilic ink and tells me to go under everything. Handrails, eaves, the undersides of things that have never had names said over them on purpose. The first time I press the plate up into a seam and feel the print pull free with a quiet relief, it is like looking at my palm after years of shaking hands with gloves on. The maps are not pictures so much as a set of habits the water has encouraged me to notice. I learn what hour the dew rehearses. I learn that a gutter which performs too well creates a gnat of dryness somewhere else that will need to be fed on the third morning after sideways rain. I learn to be thin in the way my attention is laid across a surface.

My hair goes to school. The little forearm nap that has always been there becomes an instrument; we train it to drink this week and refuse the next. Oils that do not drink live in my skin, courteous to drops that would like to be saved. My clothing acknowledges the work: recurve in the sleeve-arrow that tells a stray drip how to decide; cuffs that think in capillary; a square at my clavicle I press whenever a door needs to prefer me behind it. Shoelaces that do a low pale glow when a coil is arguing in a register I used to think was silence; my ankles learn to be eyes.

Language narrows, grows careful. There are words here for facts that were true before they were pointed at and different words for the same facts after attention has hardened them. I carry a tuning comb—three teeth labeled in my mind as one, two, and many—and a fold-needle fine enough to catch a plane of preference if it is being careless near the hand. Children show me how to play entering-by-leaving. I fail, laughing, and the laughter fogs in a place that keeps what it is given; later, after hours, a seam gleams like a smile and I know the wall has been polite enough to forget me.

Counting goes soft at the center. One is a thing. Two is a joining. Three dissolves into adjacency that you learn to wear. Meetings are pairs or they are proper crowds, many. More than once I turn a corner into what looks like a triad and find it is only the Fold nearby unthreading integers and braiding intentions.

I apprentice myself to listening.

Grammarian, Reader, Listener—names that do not own the act so much as share it like a cup. I sit at the edge of that breath-held locus and listen for the pre-roughness that comes before dew. The hum of the coils is a pulse under the sternum; over weeks, I begin to feel when a tone has gone polite rather than persuasive, when law is being asked and when it is being told. It is a delicate offense, telling. The place has a taste for consent. I have paid for an hour of another ear in the market and watched the light write permission on my wrist and fade when it was finished needing to be remembered. I learn to offer the Fold the courtesy of not explaining it back to itself.

From time to time a task asks for a touch that is not a hand. A faint plane of preference has sagged so that dew is falling wrong, water is rehearsing an error. My fold-needle catches, by suggestion rather than hook, and I draw the idea half a finger-width toward a waiting lip. It is a way of calling something by its right name without pronouncing it aloud. The plane settles like a breath mastered.

On many nights I walk with my palm up. It is the Sideways Bloom and we have persuaded, with patience and the long vowel of coil-work, the reed in the cistern to give up its jelly to the undersides of us. When a seed touches down on the warm weft of my hand I do not speak. I carry the clinging softness to a shallow basin printed where a fingertip might rest and ask it to think about the future. Later we will pretend we never asked. The thing works because no one claims it afterwards.

Festivals here know how not to spend themselves.

On the few market days, the tarps unfurl against their own will with a relief you can taste, a sound like a shirt sighing when you loosen a belt. Packages move through valves in the air. We trade permissions and not goods: water drawn and water stored, a pane’s right to be warm when no one touches it, the schedule on which a hinge will flip its sun-thought. Adjudication is a bridge of light two Clerks lay across wrists; trespass here is not stealing but using a permission you were not named into. I have had my breath measured this way and found it shorter afterwards, the way a held breath is always shorter when someone counts for you.

Thunder rolls under us once, twice a year. It does not happen here, but here is where the Fold scars a little and the town breaks into sweat. We move as in a hospital that knows what it is for: no performances, only the tasks that will keep a rule from remembering the wrong weather. Cloths that release only what they meant to take move in quiet hands. Afterwards we go around with tender lies for surfaces: you were never hot, you were never afraid. We are not soothing ourselves; the town has a long memory for suggestions.

Animals, if that still is the word, are a lexicon of exceptions. The pale bird is a ledger entry of what it does not do. I teach myself its stillness until there is an ache in the back of my skull from not blinking. The low outline that slides between pilings is a law in its scarf, and if I see it twice in a season I am permitted to make a small wish at a gutter. The little silver mites learn the rim of my cup and braid accordingly; I learn their unbraiding so my tongue does not drink them without consent. A moth I have never touched evaporates under a child’s finger, and the child is quiet all day to honor the cost of that small contact. On such a day, even I count quietly.

Where I sleep is where utilities meet: above a pipe that has claimed all the insides for itself in a non-straightforward way, under a catwalk whose hum says the coils have remembered their note. My bed is a pan with lip and gutter; my head faces the Fold so that errant condensation asks my cheek before my blanket. The old sleep under hinge-panes that will flip at dawn. We all wake to remember where we put our weight when we forgot ourselves.

I thought, when I arrived, that I wanted to be useful. Use is not a shape here; it is a way of configuring desire so that the world can agree to help.

Over years, the place acknowledges me in the ways matter knows how to nod. A drainage channel I have always wished for begins to acquire itself by invisible degrees, finding its way from higher probability to lower through a corner that sharpens when I am not watching. Steps I walk daily choose a new riser height in the night that fits the habit of my ankle. A cistern coalesces at the end of a pipe that yesterday ended because I had, for months, ended my cup there. My signature is moisture and angle, readable only if you have practiced reading the town’s intentions as if they are your own. Which, by then, they are.

My name begins not to mind being thought and not spoken. The flats-name I carried in like a lamp becomes the thing I use when I stand under the sky and need length. In the basin I am called, if at all, by what I am doing: Listener, map-hand, catch. I have a courtesy-name one winter—Edge—but it sloughs when I stop thinking of myself as the person smoothing corners and start thinking of myself as the reason the corner decided, that day, not to cut.

There are days when I am not a person so much as a seam of low light along a stair underside, asking you to see the horizon inside the maze. There are hours when I am a mesh hung like a permission, telling water where to go when it does not feel like deciding. There is a season when I am a single tooth of a comb, sounding one clean note out of gravity with my breastbone and hearing the town agree to hold itself together at that pitch. There is a morning when I am a door that prefers to be behind you and is proud of the trick. There is an evening when I am the square at a stranger’s clavicle, and I am warm so the door understands.

What would I be, asked properly, is a mode rather than a noun.

I would be the small apparatus by which a law is persuaded to be kind. I would be consent, rehearsed. I would be water’s habit drawn out in a line and made visible long enough to be taught to someone else. I would be the print a fingertip leaves in a coin-sized basin waiting its whole short life to learn what it is for. I would be the weight that makes a step remember the riser height it wants to have when unpracticed feet come next winter. I would be the pause between a request and a reply made hospitable.

I come to understand how the town will keep me.

Bones here outlive bodies as edges, as teachers of edges. There is talk—whispered in the good way, with the intention to keep rumor from hardening—of a metacarpal that has, for forty years, taught a particular stair to be generous to two different gaits. I mean to give my radius to a floor that has ambitions it cannot quite articulate. My wrist, which has worn a ring of valves all its life, will rest where new-printed matter is learning to be wall; the ring itself will open a little when rain is deciding up. Someone younger will take my fold-needle and snag a plane where it is wandering. They will call it by its work, not by my name.

I am not afraid of staying because staying here is a gradient. Time has not yet learned to fall; it leans. Erosion is trying its letters. Gravity is developing a taste for certain directions. The Fold and I keep each other company at a distance close enough to be many. On days when rules scar, we sit longer, my ear above a rail, my palm under an edge, listening for the low syllables that say it will be permitted to rain in ways the book has not memorized yet and still be right. On nights when the town glows like a held breath from seams and undersides, I walk with my lamp unlit and feel the alley decline to be longer for my vanity.

If you needed to find me, you would go where water rehearses and attention thickens—under a handrail left old on purpose, along a seam learning salt, at the lip of a cistern the town grew because too many of us had begun to end our cups there. You would know me not by how I look but by the way your breath realizes it is being watched over kindly.

If you asked, on the flats, for a name to put on me, I would give you the one that fits long light: I was once called by a bright word. If you asked in the basin, I would shrug in the grammar that keeps things provisional and say I am the part of the place that notices where three becomes many.

I would be a person, yes, because I love the feeling of a permission cooling against a wrist and the taste of lichen-flour leavened with the memory of frost. I would be the town, because I have started to think like a bridge of pipes teaching itself to be a ceiling so rain can have somewhere to fall from when falling up. I would be the Fold as much as it will let me—a place in the air that prefers to be counted to two and then to adjacency.

Mostly I would be with.

With the town that instruments itself into a being. With the water that keeps choosing. With the elders who hear a susurrus when the dew is thinking. With the coils whose notes you carry in your bones if you stay long enough. With the idea that rules can be approached the way you approach a shy animal: sideways, quiet, palms up.

The basin cools. Dew rehearses. The alley’s length is reasonable because we have consented not to insist on light. I lean inward as the buildings do, shoulder to shoulder with a closeness that refuses to become an integer when it does not need to. I keep the cup that knows its planes. I keep my ear. And while I wait for our attention to gather, I drink.
