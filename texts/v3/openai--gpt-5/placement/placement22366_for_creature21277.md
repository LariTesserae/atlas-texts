I would be where the world refuses to decide between liquid and solid, where intention is the only bridge: midway on the root’s flank, slung across a small gulf of dark, a span in the city that looks like nothing until a foot arrives.

I would be the ladder that is not a ladder until you ask it.

They made me from a grammar of opposites. On two angled posts of sedge—each post itself a braid, resin-slick and spider-silk tight—they pressed a pricklework of wax and hunger. Crabgrass sheen for repelling; scraped hypha for taking in; diatom shreds like tiny windows leaned into the wet; a dusting of silt that weighs like a decree; all of it combed into rows with a whisker and a patience that could tune winter. I am not string, not wood, not metal. I am a map of where water is willing.

Push, and I concede: the film bends, the rungs slacken, a soft giving like a sigh in the body of a bubble. Pull, and I gather: molecules tighten along the studs I have learned to be, tension cinches, a firmness appears beneath a foot like a promise remembered in time. Every step writes me. Every leaving sets me again.

I live in the felt, not the seen. Sight is rumor in here. What I know comes as weight, as minute scrapes and brushes along my studs; as temperature poured through root and stone; as the breathless thrum in the grass conduit lashed at my left post when someone three drips east declares: slow, or: open, or—rare and feared—freeze. The root talks through me always, a low groan laid along my length that says ice is doing its patient arithmetic above, or that drought is pulling the world up by its hair.

You could find me by sound, if you were small enough. Threaders’ passage is not silence on me; it is an embroidery of clicks and combings, pads and hooks adjusting, a rub of fine hairs that is as distinct individual to individual as handwriting. I know the old one by the way her rear hooks hesitate just long enough to test for faith. I know the Drip-tender by the capillary hush along her thighs when she crosses with a grief of water clinging to her. I know a Forager back from the outside by the faint glass-ache they leave on me from a diatom flake clenched in mouth too long. I have counted the children before they have names by the impatience in their touch, the way curiosity pulls harder than wisdom and I must become stone under them or lose them to the dark.

I have held more than feet. I have held plans. The city’s law rides me. It is in my fixing to web and not to root; it is in the care with which my anchors were pressed into fungus and sedge and never into the living cord that carries the sugar of the whole marsh. I am not to be bored with a mouth; I am not to be a wound. I am set where I may be unhooked in one practiced sequence when the root’s next decision sweeps through: left post free from the top down, right post from the bottom up, a hand of three made of six legs and no hands moving me forward while I am still the ground I am leaving. It has happened twice already. I remember both: every memory I have is a place I used to be.

Once I wore light. Blue was pinned above me in a short season: one string of gnat larvae like glass punctuation re-threaded along my far tension, their dull green cores thrumming their slow grammar into my studs. I learned then what charity feels like at the scale of wet: a neighbor’s nursery under me, children with their antennae lifted to that cold color, seeing with touch and then with more than touch for an hour after the larvae were taken back. People walked softer on me in those nights. Softness changes a ladder. I sang higher for them under the same feet.

Once, I was part of a trap. The planarian came with its philosophy, pooled up the side of the plaza like a thought deciding to be action. A resin bead was balanced three studs to my right on a hyphal cross-brace, and at an agreed word tapped into the falling. Weight left the bead; weight came to me. I held hard when the planarian skimmed my face with a damp you would call obscene and then fell because physics does not do courtesy. I tasted its upset as a long, even tremor. I am built on unfairness practiced precisely. I make no apologies. I would not know how.

Harvestmen have crossed me like cathedrals walk. A single leg can cover me end to end. The whisper their joints make is dry cloth, mended and remended. My defense against them is not to catch but to argue: lines under tension everywhere, every surface rebuking the exact length they can pretend is safe. They lift and place with the reserve of nobility, offended by surprises I am proud to provide. The centipede is different. When red lightning comes I am chord and weather vane. Its legs count my ribs faster than I can count them back, but their count runs through me into the whole city, a song of many hundreds of short notes: now, now, now, now, now. We do not deliver three beats in a row down the main root—never—but the centipede does and means it. When it leaves, its will has a scent. Someone will refresh my studs with new wax after, scrubbing out the rumor it left behind.

Freeze fits me out in glass. All my giving becomes one answer for a time. It is not a kindness. I have been sung through then—bodies pressed along me, the Freeze-singers laying their stockings of setae down my length and humming law into my bonds: we will unfreeze, we have done it before. Rungs that are decisions in thaw become fact in cold and facts break differently. I have snapped once. Resin cracked like old sugar and my left post gave a single helpless jerk that woke nobody because in glass even panic moves slow. They repaired me by smell and touch and oath when the thaw softened the edges back and I was shame and mend for a while. It is not the only humbling in a ladder’s life to have been less firm than you promised.

Flood is the other creed we keep. When the stone’s weep becomes running and the city’s language changes into tinny consonants, my purpose is to become spine and fence: I channel the urgency of water along my flank and not across my face. I can feel the opening of gates as pressure slipping out of the whole: sluices let down their control in a choreography that keeps my midspan from turning to a glossy, treacherous street. Market happens then on my very edge. Mats appear and are lifted in the same breath: snippets of lichen like coins, a pellet of river’s silt weighed on my rib and making me feel for a second the laughter of far-away stones. Trade is a tap on my post: a code that means this is for you; a returning code that means it is only lent.

The city teaches me. I am a student of its archive. When the old one—three freezes, one blue—brought a fresh flake of shale from the roof and leaned it against my anchor to rest, I tasted the oil-smooth face of it and learned a new warning. “Not under that shine,” her feet told me; “not ever.” She passed up my length in her even, spare way, vole-whisker set into chert at her mouth, new curl of phloem on her back that smelled like story. She checks her eggs by me, because they are a star-field inside a bead lashed over my near end, and sometimes she touches the far side of that window so the tension sings and will not break. It is an honor to keep a horizon.

I have rules. I carry them not as text but as failure rehearsed until it is prevention. Do not break a free bead by hand is the same rule as be what you are only when asked. The thinness where the stone recedes and nothing has dared to build pulls at me sometimes—the black gulf is a demand disguised as emptiness. I have learned to look like I might answer it and then not. A good tether is a deed in this city, and I am a vector of ownership only in storms; the rest of the time I am a public oath. The root’s north wall keeps its lichen like a calendar and I am not hung where counting would be marred. I have never heard three beats in a row hammered into my studs on purpose. If I did, I would not carry them gracefully. I would warp them out the side into the moss and pretend law could be bent like that and not bite back.

I have been made new three times with new wax. I have worn smooth the places where a small hand shows its habit. Every law here is a figure drawn in damp that alters with the air, and I keep to it by being re-written. On days the leaf outside flashes and the bright lifts the darkness—boot-day light that turns the pocket to glass and makes even moss look like a thing that could cut—I do not glamor myself. I am most dangerous when I try to be certain under a shadow like that. Those days we all pretend stillness. Stillness is an act as civic as any.

If I could show you a map of what I hold, it would be a web of verbs. Stand. Cross. Wait. Yield. Grip. The city asks this of everything, but I am made of it. I am exactly what happens when water is asked to be a road and says yes only if you behave. I am the Threaders’ best trick and their least boast: the domestication of a meniscus. I am how the stomach of the marsh learns to be a mouth that can say over and must never say under.

When I am unfastened the next time because the root decides growth is an answer to a question the rain asked two years ago, I will go where I am taken and be rebuilt, stud by stud, with fingers that are not fingers, with patience that is a city’s, with the hum in the body that remembers the winter we did not break. The resin they use will not be from the same spruce forever. The wax will be someone else’s skin someday. The diatom blade they polish me with will cut a lip and be cursed and still be used. I will be the same and not.

If I am lost in a freeze that out-argues song or in a flood that makes roads and cities into the same wet because sometimes physics does simplicity for the joy of it, then there will be another me. Not memory, not memorial—performance. The place between root and rock demands a certain set of gestures to keep from collapsing, and I am one of them. Their hands—made of six legs and of web and of vow—will make me again, wherever there is need for give and for hold.

So where am I? Between. What am I? Agreement. I am the habit that lets edges become address, a rung that appears because you dared to step where only water was. I am the way this city says yes to itself without ever saying loud.
