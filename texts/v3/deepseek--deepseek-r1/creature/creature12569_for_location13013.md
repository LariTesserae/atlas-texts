The inhabitants of the Weeping Engine Cavern are not mere creatures; they are *components* of the cavern itself, evolved or manifested to resonate with its cold, humming, static reality. They blur the line between organic and engineered, living as extensions of the weeping rock and humming metal:

1.  **Obsidian-Eel Serpents:** Sleek, sinuous predators 1-2 meters long. Their scales are not just iridescent; they are faceted like the cavern walls, shifting between deep volcanic glass and polished brass depending on the angle of the sickly light. Their eyes are cold, lens-like orbs emitting a constant, low blue phosphorescence. They move with uncanny silence through the flooded moss-marsh, propelled by barely visible ripples. Their primary prey seems to be the bioluminescent lichen and perhaps smaller, unseen organisms in the water. They navigate using electrical sensitivity, tracing the humming currents in the copper veins. When an electrical arc leaps nearby, their scales briefly flare with absorbed light before fading back to obsidian darkness. They move in fixed, eternal patrol routes through the submerged channels.

2.  **Spark-Walking Scarabs:** Hand-sized beetles with carapaces seemingly forged from tarnished brass and flecked with chips of raw obsidian. Their legs end in sharp, conductive points of pure copper. They inhabit the rare drier ledges and the weeping walls themselves, clattering with a sound like dropped ball bearings. Their movement is jerky, precise, and seemingly purposeless – patrolling fixed circuits on the rock faces. When they traverse exposed copper veins, faint blue-white sparks crackle from their leg-tips to the metal, causing localized flares in the moss and lichen nearby. They are indifferent to other life, focused solely on their endless, sparking patrols. Their shells are perpetually slick with condensation, which sometimes beads and drips like miniature versions of the weeping walls.

3.  **Coil-Light Medusae:** The most unsettling inhabitants. Translucent, gas-filled sacs the size of human heads drift silently through the cavern's airspace, just above the water and moss. Within their gelatinous bodies swirl intricate patterns of bioluminescent gas – greens, blues, and sickly yellows – pulsing in complex, fractal sequences that are *too* structured to be random. From their undersides trail dozens of fine, hair-like tentacles made of braided copper filaments. These filaments occasionally brush against the weeping walls, particularly where metallic veins are exposed. When contact occurs, the medusa flares brilliantly, its internal light patterns accelerating wildly, while a corresponding localized flare of energy (light and a sharp *crack-hum*) erupts on the wall. This seems less like feeding and more like... communication or energy exchange. They drift on fixed, lazy currents in the damp air, their light patterns locked in eternal, repeating loops. Touching one would likely deliver a paralyzing shock.

**Symbiosis and Stasis:**

*   **The Moss and Lichen:** The dense velvet moss and crawling lichen are the base layer of life. They thrive on the constant moisture, the dim phosphorescent light (some self-generated, some absorbed from the medusae and arcs), and likely minerals dissolved in the weeping water or leached from the metallic veins. Their glow provides the cavern's primary illumination, punctuated by electrical flares. They are the "insulation" and the "substrate."
*   **The Engine:** The metallic veins, the buried flywheels, the rhythmic thuds – these are the cavern's heart. The lifeforms are attuned to its frequency. The eels sense its currents, the scarabs conduct its sparks, the medusae interface with its surface energy. Their movements, their lights, their very existence seem synchronized with the unchanging hum and thud.
*   **The Paradox of Life in Stasis:** Nothing hunts the eels. The scarabs don't seem to eat. The medusae don't visibly consume. They exist in a perfect, closed loop. The eels might keep the moss from overgrowing specific paths; the scarabs' sparks might stimulate the lichen or clear mineral deposits; the medusae might regulate energy flows across the walls. They are less individual organisms and more like specialized cells in a vast, cold, wet, humming organism – the cavern itself. Their "behavior" is not driven by survival or reproduction in a conventional sense, but by maintaining the cavern's eternal, weeping, resonating equilibrium. They *are* the Weeping Engine, as much as the rock and the water and the copper veins. They have always been here, performing their functions. They always will be. They are components in stasis.
