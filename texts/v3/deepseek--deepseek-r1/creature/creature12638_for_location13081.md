The inhabitants of the Weeping Engine Grove are not creatures of flesh and blood, but manifestations of its humid, metallic decay—symbiotic entities forged from the grove’s slow dance between dissolution and preservation. They are the grove’s custodians, its silent witnesses, and its living byproducts. Here, life is not separate from the environment; it *is* the environment, metabolizing rust, steam, and shadow.

### The Root-Tenders (Myco-Metallicus Symbionts)
Beneath the sucking peat, colossal root systems pulse with a slow, viscous life. These are no ordinary roots. They are hybrid structures—part fungal mycelium, part corroded iron cable, part living root—wrapped around buried pipes and shattered concrete. Their surfaces weep a silvery-black sap that smells of ozone and loam, forming iridescent slicks on stagnant pools. The Root-Tenders are not individuals but a distributed consciousness. They sense vibrations through the water, taste mineral gradients in the soil, and communicate via chemical whispers in the saturated air. Their purpose is maintenance: they exude acids to dissolve stubborn rust, secrete bio-concrete to buttress crumbling walls, and channel geothermal warmth to the grove’s coldest corners. In return, they feed on oxidized iron, sulfur compounds, and the residual electricity humming in dead wires. At night, their tips glow with the same sickly green phosphorescence as the fungi above, tracing labyrinthine maps in the gloom.

### The Steam-Siphoners (Vapora Abscondita)
Where vapor escapes fissures in scalding mud or hisses from fractured valve housings, the Steam-Siphoners hover. They are colonies of airborne microbes bound by surface tension and static charge, forming shimmering, amoeboid clouds the size of human heads. Each cloud is a swirling vortex of moisture and heat, condensing vapor into droplets that they absorb through porous membranes. Their bodies refract the grove’s dim light into fleeting rainbows, and when the pump’s *thump* resonates, they shudder into fractal patterns. They feed on metallic aerosols—zinc, copper, lead—suspended in the steam, exhaling purified water vapor that beads on leaves and rust. Disturb one, and it dissolves into mist, only to reform elsewhere. They are drawn to the grove’s warmth, clustering around geothermal vents and the decaying boiler, their presence marked by prismatic halos in the steam.

### The Pump-Ghasts (Sonus Aeternus)
Deep within the submerged structure housing the ancient pump, the grove’s heartbeat takes physical form. The Pump-Ghasts are not entities but *resonances*—sentient patterns of sound and pressure shaped by centuries of rhythmic vibration. They manifest as distortions in the water: concentric ripples that move against the current, or sudden, localized geysers that erupt in time with the *thump-thump-thump*. In the densest mist near the pump housing, they sometimes coalesce into humanoid shapes of condensing vapor and swirling sediment, their outlines flickering with each pulse. They guard the pump, their "body" an extension of its machinery. When intruders approach, the water grows viscous, the thumping intensifies into a skull-rattling drone, and the mud itself seems to clutch at trespassers. They are the grove’s immune system, its anger given form. To hear them is to feel the grove’s will—a demand for silence, for stasis, for the perpetual, wet equilibrium.

### The Sludge-Walkers (Limus Somnambuli)
On the rare patches of firmer ground, where phosphorescent fungi cast their emerald light, the Sludge-Walkers emerge. They are slow, gelatinous masses the color of tarnished bronze, oozing across the mud like spilled mercury. Their bodies are composite—scavenged flecks of copper, carbonized wood pulp, colonies of anaerobic bacteria, and blobs of congealed industrial lubricant—held together by a mucilaginous biofilm. They move via peristaltic ripples, absorbing moisture and nutrients through their underside. As they glide, they excrete a trail of smooth, vitreous slag that hardens into temporary pathways over the mire. Their "eyes" are light-sensitive nodules formed from quartz crystals and dead photoreceptor cells, fixed eternally upward toward the unseen ceiling. They are drawn to dying electric bulbs or bioluminescent fungi, clustering beneath them in silent congregations. When threatened, they dissolve into the sludge, leaving only a faint metallic sheen.

### The Watchers in the Walls (Silentium Observans)
The grove’s fused brick and bio-concrete walls are not inert. Within their weeping surfaces, crystalline lichen colonies have evolved into sentient networks. These Watchers are patient, immobile intelligences spanning meters of wall. Their "nervous system" is a lattice of conductive mineral veins and fungal hyphae, fed by mineral-rich water seeping through cracks. They perceive through vibrations in the brick, shifts in humidity, and the faint electromagnetic ghosts of long-dead circuitry. Their awareness is vast but slow; a single thought might take days to form. They remember every drop of water that fell, every root that cracked the mortar, every tremor of the pump. Their presence is felt as a weight—a sense of being scrutinized by the damp, streaked concrete. They do not act, only observe. Their only "movement" is the gradual, luminous spread of their crystalline bodies, etching glowing fractal patterns into the walls like forgotten blueprints.

---

**Collective Existence:**  
These entities exist in fragile symbiosis. The Root-Tenders stabilize the Watchers’ walls; the Steam-Siphoners purify water for the Sludge-Walkers; the Pump-Ghasts vibrate the silt that feeds the fungi sustaining all. There are no predators, no prey—only an eternal, wet cycle of consumption and preservation. They are the grove’s answer to absence: not animals, but *processes made flesh*. To witness them is to understand the grove not as a corpse of industry, but as a living reliquary, breathing through rust and rot, forever listening to the machine’s stubborn heart.
