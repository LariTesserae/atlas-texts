The inhabitants of the Endless Kelpstone Plains are not creatures of flesh and blood, but beings of luminous patience and geological slowness. They are the **Stone-Cradled Symbiotes**, lifeforms sculpted by crushing pressure, eternal darkness, and time measured in eons.

1.  **The Deep-Kelp Monarchs:** These are not mere plants, but sentient pillars of the abyss. Each colossal kelp is a slow, communal intelligence. Its "trunk," wider than ancient redwoods, is a latticework of hollow tubes formed from pressure-hardened cellulose and bioluminescent silica. Within these tubes, trillions of symbiotic algae and fungi perform chemosynthesis, converting the faint warmth seeping from the planet's core and dissolved minerals into sustenance. Their vast, bruise-purple fronds, edged in faint starlight (the bioluminescent points), are not for photosynthesis, but for *feeling*. They drift with imperceptible currents over decades, tasting the water for chemical shifts – a colder stream might take a century to traverse a single frond. Communication between Monarchs happens through pulsed patterns of light along root-filaments that network beneath the flagstones, messages taking years to transmit simple concepts like "cold-front approaching" or "nutrient bloom here." They "hunt" not prey, but the slow drift of mineral-rich sediments, their root-mats creeping centimeters per century to engulf fertile cracks.

2.  **The Lithic Mantle Lichens:** Crusting the seams of the cyclopean flagstones and the bases of monolithic structures, these are intricate, continent-spanning carpets. More fungus than plant, they form a living, luminous skin over the ancient stone. Their cold blue-white light isn't random; it forms slow-shifting fractal patterns, responding to infinitesimal changes in water chemistry or pressure over centuries. They feed on the microscopic erosion of the stone itself – not by acid, but by patiently secreting enzymes that coax single molecules free over millennia. Their "reproduction" is a glacial event: once every few thousand years, a section of the mantle, saturated with nutrients and sensing some unknowable planetary rhythm, will pulse with intense light for a decade, then release a cloud of luminous spores that drift on the sluggish currents for centuries, seeking new stone to colonize. They are the world's archivists, their layered growth holding chemical records of the deep ocean's history across millions of years.

3.  **The Silt-Anemones:** Sprouting from thick beds of luminous moss in the wider cracks between stones, these are not true anemones, but bizarre, slow-motion fungi. They resemble fist-sized, closed black blossoms made of tough, pressure-resistant chitin. For decades, they remain dormant. Then, triggered by the settling of a particularly rich layer of silt (an event taking years itself), they unfold with glacial grace. Their "petals" open over months, revealing a deep, bioluminescent blue interior covered in sticky filaments. They wait, sometimes for a century, for the deep, slow currents to carry free-floating nutrient particles or the microscopic detritus shed by the kelp and lichens into their grasp. Once captured, the petals close with equal slowness, digesting the bounty over years. Their light pulses faintly during digestion, a slow beacon in the dark.

4.  **The Root-Filament Weavers:** These are not distinct organisms, but an emergent intelligence formed by the interconnected networks of fungal hyphae and specialized rootlets belonging to the Deep-Kelp Monarchs and Lithic Mantle Lichens. Thriving in the labyrinthine spaces just beneath the stone surface or within the kelp's own structure, this network acts as a neural web for the plain. It senses vibrations (like the groan of tectonic plates shifting miles below), distributes chemical signals and nutrients between different life forms over vast distances (a process taking decades), and even directs the slow growth of the lichen mantles towards areas of richer mineral erosion. It is the slow, patient mind of the ecosystem itself, processing information on a timescale incomprehensible to surface life.

**Their Nature:**
*   **Movement is Growth:** They don't swim or crawl. They *grow* towards resources, retreat from unfavorable shifts (over centuries), or unfold in response to stimuli. Motion is measured in fractions of millimeters per year.
*   **Perception is Chemical & Tactile:** They "see" through chemical gradients in the water, felt over immense timescales. They "hear" through vibrations transmitted through stone and water, interpreting the planet's deep hum and the settling of silt. Light patterns are their language and mood.
*   **Time is Substrate:** Seasons last centuries. A "quick" reaction takes a decade. Reproduction is a rare, generation-spanning event. They endure, adapting with glacial inevitability.
*   **Light is Life & Language:** Bioluminescence is fundamental. It attracts microscopic nutrients, communicates across vast silent distances, marks internal processes (digestion, growth spurts), and paints their hauntingly beautiful world in shades of cold star-fire and deep violet shadow.
*   **Symbiosis is Survival:** Every being here is deeply interconnected. The kelp provides structure and nutrients for the lichens and mosses. The lichens erode stone, releasing minerals. The fungal network connects and sustains all. The silt-anemones clean the water of detritus. They form a single, patient, luminous entity draped over the bones of a forgotten world.

They are the inheritors. They are the patient victors in a silent war against oblivion, thriving in the crushing dark where the builders' ambition now lies smooth and cold beneath their luminous, living shroud. They are the eternal residents of the drowned cathedral, tending its stone and weaving light in the endless, silent night.
