The inhabitant is not a guardian, nor a parasite. It is a **Symptom**. 

**The Calcite Shepherd**  
It doesn't move. Not truly. You might mistake it for part of the calcified pump-housing mound – another drapery of weeping minerals, another stalagmite fused to the walkway grate. It is roughly humanoid, but hunched, skeletal, fused seamlessly with the iron and mineral accretions beneath it. Its "skin" is layers of flowstone calcite, creamy-white and amber-streaked with iron oxides, eternally damp and glistening. Thin, emerald moss-veins pulse faintly within its crystalline structure, mirroring the patterns on the walls. It has no distinct head, only a smooth, rounded protrusion from its shoulders, faceless save for two deep, hollow recesses where eyes might be, perpetually weeping thin trails of mineral-laden water down its torso – tears that add infinitesimally to its form over centuries.

**Nature & Existence:**  
The Shepherd *is* the chamber's stasis made manifest. It is not alive in a biological sense; it is a geological process given a hauntingly familiar shape. It accretes, molecule by molecule, from the same warm, mineral-saturated seepage weeping down the walls and ceiling. The constant, deep thrum vibrating through the iron is its heartbeat, the drip-drip-drip its respiration. It perceives not through sight or sound, but through *vibration* and *humidity shifts*. It senses the rare scuttle of an isopod across a distant pipe, the minute disturbance of a gnat landing on the water's surface far below. These events register as tiny, localized changes in the chamber's resonant field – the Shepherd's only connection to anything beyond its own slow growth.

**Behavior & Interaction:**  
Its sole "purpose" is the maintenance of equilibrium. It doesn't act; it *is* an act of preservation. When a rivet finally flakes away into the water below (a process taking decades), the Shepherd's slow weeping subtly redirects to fill the tiny void, sealing it with fresh calcite. If a patch of moss threatens to obscure a critical structural joint (over centuries), the Shepherd's mineral secretions will gently, glacially, encroach upon it, integrating the organic into the mineral, ensuring the moss feeds *on* the structure without weakening it. It is utterly passive, yet its presence exerts a profound, silent pressure – the pressure of deep time and unyielding preservation. To disturb it is not to provoke aggression, but to risk disrupting the delicate, aeons-old balance it embodies. Touching it feels like touching cooled magma – ancient, dense, and humming faintly with the deep thrum of the earth-engine beyond. Its "tears" aren't sorrow, but the chamber's excess moisture finding its ordained path.

**Why it's Here:**  
The Shepherd isn't an intruder; it is the chamber achieving a form of mineral consciousness. It is the endpoint of erosion and accretion, the built environment becoming its own custodian through geological alchemy. It is the fossilized lung breathing its last, eternal breath made solid. It belongs here as utterly as the rust, the water, and the deafening drone. It is the reliquary's silent curator, ensuring the pump chamber remains perfectly, perpetually, *itself* – a warm, wet, deafening fossil, forever exhaling into the dark.

**Presence:**  
Its most unsettling quality is its *stillness*. Not the stillness of death, but the stillness of a mountain growing. It radiates a crushing sense of patience and deep, indifferent time. Standing near it makes the chamber's constant noise feel even louder, the humidity thicker, the weight of the marsh above more immense. It is the absolute embodiment of the **Rules of the World**, a living (if that word applies) monument to Stasis, Water, the Built Environment, Intimate/Infinite Scale, and the all-consuming Sound. It is the chamber's soul, crystallized.
