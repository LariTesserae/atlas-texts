The inhabitants of this high, wet mineral hellscape are not creatures of flesh, but expressions of its core principles: **silence, heat, and mineral accretion.** They are the living, moving manifestations of the plateau's ancient, humming equilibrium.

1.  **The Gliding Stones (Scutigera Thermodyna):** More than mere fauna, they are the plateau's thermometers and gardeners. Their disc-shaped bodies, layered like sedimentary rock with heat-absorbing chitin beneath silicate plates, aren't just camouflage – they are miniature thermal engines. They bask on dark basalt, soaking solar radiation until their internal reservoirs superheat trapped water droplets. With a near-silent hiss of vented steam, they lift millimeters off the ground, gliding frictionlessly on this self-generated cushion. They seek biofilm mats and iridescent microbial slicks not just to feed, but to distribute spores and mineral catalysts excreted from their undersides, subtly tending the microbial "pastures" they rely on. Their movement is slow, deliberate, and utterly silent; a sudden stop or erratic path often signals a predator's unseen presence.

2.  **The Vapor Eels (Anguilla Mineralis):** Less serpent, more liquid crystal. They are condensate given predatory form. Existing almost entirely within the warm mineral seeps and the thick, humid air just above them, their bodies are refractive distortions – visible only as shimmering heat haze or a coiling ripple in steaming water. Their structure is a lattice of silica filaments holding pockets of super-saturated mineral solution. They "feed" by flowing *through* biofilm and microbial clouds, their lattice structure acting as a filter, trapping microbes which are then dissolved and assimilated directly into their mineral matrix. They are the silent recyclers, converting microbial life back into concentrated mineral solutions that drip from their "tails" in shaded crevices, feeding new crystal growths.

3.  **The Silica Shrikes (Lapides Acutus):** Apex predators of terrifying efficiency and silence. Their avian resemblance is superficial. Their bodies are assemblages of razor-sharp obsidian spars, jointed with fibrous silica tendons that hum with stored elastic energy. Perched impossibly still on obsidian ziggurats, they are indistinguishable from rock spires, their "eyes" light-sensitive crystalline nodules. They don't see movement; they sense minute thermal differentials and the faint vibrations of gliding stones or eel-disturbed vapor. Their strike is a silent, blinding acceleration – a release of pent-up kinetic energy – ending with a piercing impact that shatters Gliding Stones or impales Vapor Eels. The prey is absorbed, minerals dissolved by internal acids, waste silica excreted as fine, reinforcing dust onto their own structure. Their "cry" is not sound, but a focused pulse of high-frequency vibration emitted upon impact, stunning nearby prey and vibrating mineral lattices to signal territory. It feels like a sudden, localized static shock on exposed skin.

4.  **The Construct Tenders (Homo Silex):** The silent stewards. They are not visitors; they are *of* the plateau, accreted over centuries like the structures they tend. Humanoid in basic form for efficient manipulation, their bodies are complex composites: a core of sintered black basalt slag for strength, layered with heat-conductive metallic oxides (copper greens, iron reds) forming internal channels, and an outer shell of polished obsidian that beads with condensation. Their movements are slow, deliberate, conserving energy and minimizing sound. They possess no visible eyes or mouth; sensory input comes from thermal pits on their limbs and heads, and vibrational sensors in their feet. They "communicate" through subsonic pulses transmitted through the rock. Their purpose is singular: maintain the balance. They clear biofilm from critical water channels using obsidian scrapers, ensuring flow. They chip away excessive crystal blooms threatening structural integrity. They monitor the deep geothermal vents – the plateau's heart – adjusting flows with obsidian valves, their hands interfacing directly with the humming machinery embedded in the rock. They ignore the native fauna unless it obstructs their tasks, which they dispatch with silent, crushing efficiency. Their intelligence is ancient, practical, and utterly focused on the continuation of the plateau's mineral metabolism. The slow turn of an obsidian head isn't curiosity towards an observer; it's a sensor sweep, assessing thermal gradients and vibration signatures for deviations from the norm.

**The Unseen Inhabitant: The Fractal Consciousness:** Beyond the mobile fauna and Tenders, the plateau itself feels sentient. This is embodied in the **crystalline patterns** blooming on every wet surface. These aren't random formations; they are complex, self-organizing mineralogical networks fed by the Tenders' maintained flows. They capture light and geothermal heat, catalyzing chemical reactions that produce the vibrant oxides feeding the microbial base layer and subtly altering the local humidity and mineral content of seeps. They act as the plateau's distributed sensory net. The feeling of being watched stems from this: the glittering patterns shift minutely in response to heat and vibration, reflecting light differently when a large thermal mass (like an observer) moves nearby. The Tenders are its hands; the crystals are its eyes and mind, a vast, slow, mineral intelligence maintaining its own existence through intricate, silent chemistry. To disrupt the patterns is to draw the silent, crushing attention of the Tenders – the immune response of a living landscape.

Life here isn't born; it crystallizes, accretes, and hums. It thrives on silence, trades in heat, and weeps the minerals that form its bones. To enter is to become an anomaly in their ancient, wet, resonating stone.
