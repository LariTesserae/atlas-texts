The inhabitants of the Basin of Unfinished Cities are not masters of their environment; they are frantic participants in its chaotic, emergent dance. They are creatures forged by the Basin's relentless instability, mineral richness, cold dampness, and the desperate need to build and rebuild. Here’s who lives there:

1.  **The Grafters (Bio-Mechanical Symbiotes):**
    *   **Physiology:** These are the dominant "humanoid" inhabitants. Not born, but *assembled* and *adapted*. Their core might be organic – a human, or something vaguely humanoid adapted to the cold – but survival necessitates constant augmentation. Limbs lost to crushing shifts, liquefied ground, or pipe bursts are replaced with rugged, steam-powered prosthetics of riveted iron and brass, scavenged or forged from Basin minerals. Lungs choked by sulphurous fumes or icy damp are supplemented with gill-like filtration units grafted to their ribs, fed by condensation tubes snaking to backpack reservoirs. Eyes damaged by mineral glare or bioluminescent bursts are swapped for multi-lensed optical sensors scavenged from machinery, giving them a fragmented, unnervingly wide field of view. Their skin is often patched with thick, rubbery synth-leather or reinforced with ceramic plates bonded directly to bone, stained by mineral seepage and algae. They move with a heavy, purposeful clank-hiss, their movements efficient but lacking grace.
    *   **Culture:** Their society is built on pragmatism and rapid response. Value is placed on technical skill, adaptability, and the ability to "read" the Basin's tremors and steam sighs. Leadership is fluid, based on who can fix the immediate crisis. They possess an encyclopedic, non-verbal knowledge of pressure tolerances, mineral properties, and steam-flow dynamics. Their language is a mix of guttural clicks, steam-whistle codes, and hand-signs optimized for noisy, low-visibility environments. They have no concept of permanence; their homes, tools, and even bodies are seen as temporary configurations awaiting inevitable failure and necessary reconfiguration. Their "art" is found in ingenious repairs and the efficient rerouting of pipes or walkways.

2.  **The Lithovores (Mineral Feeders):**
    *   **Physiology:** Native fauna evolved to exploit the Basin's raw, mineral-rich chaos. These are not the scuttling beetles or shaggy herbivores, but larger, slower, more integrated beings. Imagine six-legged, low-slung creatures resembling mobile slag heaps. Their hide is a thick, crustaceous amalgam of basalt nodules, sintered mineral deposits (those blues, yellows, and purples), and metallic veins, forming natural armor. They move ponderously, "grazing" directly on the mineral aggregate, using acidic saliva secreted from beak-like mouths to dissolve and absorb essential elements. Geothermal vents are their nurseries and hotspots; they bask in the warmth, their crusts thickening. Some species have developed symbiotic relationships with the vibrant mosses, which grow on their backs, photosynthesizing weakly in the arc-light glow and providing camouflage or even supplemental nutrients.
    *   **Behavior:** They are largely indifferent to the Grafters' structures, unless a walkway blocks a mineral-rich patch or a vent. Their passage can destabilize already precarious ground, triggering minor slides. The Grafters generally leave them be; their crusts are too tough for easy harvesting, and they help break up the aggregate. They are living manifestations of the Basin's raw materiality.

3.  **The Mycelial Networkers (Fungal Symbiotes/Parasites):**
    *   **Physiology:** More than just the giant fungi, this refers to sentient (or at least highly complex) fungal intelligences that permeate the Basin. They thrive in the cold damp, their vast, unseen mycelial networks threading through the chaotic soil, binding rocks, infiltrating building foundations, and climbing pipes. They communicate via subtle chemical signals and bioluminescent pulses through their fruiting bodies (the glowing fingers, fleshy parasols). Some have formed symbiotic bonds with specific Grafter crews or Lithovores, providing early warnings of ground tremors (through hyphal vibration sense) or stabilizing loose soil in exchange for nutrient-rich waste or warmth near vents.
    *   **Manifestations:** Occasionally, a network achieves localized dominance. This might manifest as a building suddenly overgrown overnight by thick, pulsating fungal mats that seize control of its internal machinery, repurposing it to pump nutrients or steam. Or, a Lithovore might become a slow-moving "spore bearer," covered in enormous, pulsing fungal blooms. The Grafters constantly fight these incursions, using steam blasts and mineral-based fungicides, but also sometimes negotiate temporary truces with less aggressive networks for mutual benefit.

4.  **The Scavenger Swarms:**
    *   **Physiology:** Highly adaptable, fast-breeding opportunists. These include the armored beetles, the leathery-winged fliers, and myriad smaller insectoid and rodent-like creatures. They are carrion-eaters, mineral-lickers, moss-nibblers, and thieves. Many possess chitinous plates or fur matted with mineral dust and algae for insulation. The fliers have multiple eyes to navigate the steam and shifting light, and their wings are nearly silent, adapted to the muffled soundscape.
    *   **Role:** They are the Basin's cleanup crew and pestilence. They strip corpses (organic and mechanical), devour spilled nutrients or lubricants, and gnaw on insulation or softer synth-leather. They spread spores and seeds. They are a constant, buzzing, skittering presence in every shadow and crevice, a reminder of the decay that constantly threatens to overtake the Grafters' frantic industry. Some Grafters keep tamed swarms for specific tasks, like clearing clogged vents or detecting gas leaks.

**The Unifying Themes of Inhabitancy:**

*   **Adaptation is Survival:** Every resident, from the most augmented Grafter to the simplest beetle, is shaped by the need to withstand the cold damp, exploit the mineral richness, and cope with constant instability. Biology and technology blur.
*   **Temporary is Eternal:** Nothing is built to last; everything is built to be repaired, replaced, or reconfigured. Possession is transient. Structures, tools, bodies, and even social bonds are provisional.
*   **Exploitation and Symbiosis:** Life here is a complex web of taking and giving. Grafters exploit geothermal vents and minerals, but are parasitized by fungi and swarms. Lithovores consume the ground itself. Fungi stabilize and warn, but also consume. It's a brutal, efficient ecosystem where mutualism is often born of desperation.
*   **Respect for the Unstable:** All inhabitants possess an ingrained, almost instinctive awareness of the Basin's volatility. They read the hiss of steam, the groan of settling metal, the subtle tremors underfoot. Ignoring these signs means swift death. Their movements, their settlements, their very existence is a constant negotiation with a landscape that refuses to be still.

To live in the Basin is to be in a state of perpetual, urgent becoming, locked in a desperate yet strangely vital struggle against a world that is itself only half-formed. The inhabitants are not separate from the "Unfinished Cities"; they are the cities' raw, striving, unstable flesh.
