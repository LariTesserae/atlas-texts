The Cistern of Whispers is not haunted by ghosts, but by life – ancient, adapted, and utterly intertwined with its crafted stone and perpetual dampness. Here, the residents are not invaders, but extensions of the cistern itself, sculpted by time and necessity:

1.  **The Stone-Weavers (Paleolithic Isopod Colonies):** Dominating the walls and drier ledges are vast, shifting carpets of sightless isopods. These are not the tiny woodlice of the surface, but creatures the size of a thumbnail, their exoskeletons translucent white or the faintest honey-gold, mirroring the limestone. They move in slow, ceaseless waves, their countless legs a susurrus against stone. They are the primary harvesters, their rasping mouthparts scraping the olive-green and rust-orange algal films from every illuminated surface. Their colonies pulse with communal life, retreating en masse into deeper cracks at perceived vibrations, only to flow out again moments later like living rivulets. They seem less like individuals and more like a single, distributed organism – the limestone's living patina.

2.  **The Channel-Grazers (Cavernicolous Millipedes):** Gliding with eerie, boneless grace through the shallow floor channels are the millipedes. Longer than a human hand and as thick as a finger, they are blind, soft-bodied, and ghostly pale, almost luminescent in the dim light. Their countless legs ripple in perfect waves, propelling them through the thin sheen of water and mineral-rich biofilm coating the channels. They are gentle giants of this ecosystem, grazing constantly on the slippery microbial mats, their slow passage leaving temporary, glistening trails. Their bodies are segmented like articulated ivory, sensitive to the faintest currents in the water and air.

3.  **The Pool-Ghosts (Troglobitic Fish):** In the central pool's mineral-hazed water, elongated shapes flicker. These fish are spectral – completely devoid of pigment, their skin translucent enough to sometimes hint at the shadow of a spine or internal organ. Their eyes are vestigial dark spots, long since covered by skin. Instead, their world is mapped by hypersensitive lateral lines and delicate, trailing fin rays that brush the water like sensory antennae. They drift, motionless for minutes, then dart with impossible speed, snatching tiny, unseen crustaceans or perhaps even the shed skins of isopods that fall into the water. They are silent except for the soft *plip* of their mouths breaking the surface film or the swirl of their sudden movements.

4.  **The Crack-Dwellers (Microfauna Symphony):** Invisible to the casual glance but vital to the hum are the true microfauna. Tiny, translucent amphipods dart like flecks of mica in the channel water. Blind, white springtails hop erratically across damp patches like living grains of salt, feeding on fungal spores and detritus. Minute, worm-like creatures burrow in the thickest algal mats or the silt at the pool's edge. Colonies of chemosynthetic bacteria thrive in the warm, mineral-laden seeps, forming faintly iridescent, greasy films – the foundational layer of the cistern's food web. Their collective movement and metabolism contribute to the pervasive rustle and the subtle, ozone-metallic scent.

5.  **The Silent Custodians (The Biofilm & Algae):** While not fauna, the microbial films and algal patches are living entities as vital as any creature. They cling with tenacious life to every surface touched by light and moisture. They are the primary producers, converting the cistern's scant light and leached minerals into sustenance. They glow with faint, occasional phosphorescence – deep olive, rusty red, or a surprising flash of blue-green – a slow, chemical luminescence sparked by movement or subtle shifts in water chemistry. They etch the stone microscopically, their acids and physical holdfasts contributing to the chamber's slow erosion and polish.

**Their Nature and Rhythm:**
*   **Blindness:** Sight is irrelevant; perception is through touch (vibrations through stone and water), chemoreception (tasting the air and water currents), and hydrodynamic/atmospheric pressure sensing.
*   **Slowness:** Metabolism is tuned to the cistern's eternal "late afternoon." Movements are deliberate, conserving energy in a world of constant, warm dampness but limited high-energy food.
*   **Integration:** They are inseparable from their environment. Their shells match the stone, their movements echo the water's flow, their life cycles are tied to the constant drip and the slow growth of the microbial mats. They *are* the cistern's breath and its whispers.
*   **Harmony:** There are no apex predators in the traditional sense. Death comes from age, accident, or perhaps consumption by the fish or larger isopods/millipedes scavenging. Decomposition is rapid in the warm dampness, nutrients swiftly recycled back into the biofilm and algae. It is a tight loop of consumption and renewal, a damp, rustling equilibrium perfected over centuries within the hand-carved stone.

To encounter them is to witness an ecosystem that has forgotten the sun, where life has woven itself into the very fabric of the ancient cistern's purpose. They are not merely inhabitants; they are the Cistern of Whispers made animate, thriving in the profound, wet silence of their made world.
