The Singing Caldera's inhabitants are not numerous, but they are exquisitely attuned to its raw, weeping landscape. Life here is defined by patience, resilience, and a deep symbiosis with water and stone.

1.  **The Stone-Weavers (Human Inhabitants):**
    *   **Appearance:** Lean and wiry, with skin the colour of weathered basalt, etched by the high-altitude sun and constant moisture. Their eyes are often a startlingly pale grey or blue, adapted to the diffuse light, reflecting the sky-pools. Hair is dark and coarse, often braided tightly or bound under simple caps woven from tough fern fibres.
    *   **Life & Culture:** They are masters of stillness and observation. Their lives revolve around capturing and directing the caldera's weeping water. They build intricate, shallow channels of fitted stone from springs to communal cisterns lined with watertight clay. Their rounded dwellings are designed to shed the constant condensation efficiently, while thick fern-thatch roofs insulate against the cool air and collect dew, channeling it into interior clay pots.
    *   **Skills:** They possess an intimate knowledge of the rock's moods – where it sweats purest water, where thermal vents provide warmth, where fresh fissures might open. They are expert stone-knappers, crafting tools and vessels from the abundant obsidian and pumice. Their few crops are hardy succulents and mosses cultivated in sheltered, damp crevices, supplemented by rare, high-protein lichens scraped from rocks. They hunt nothing larger than the occasional large insect, viewing larger fauna as absent spirits.
    *   **Sound:** They speak rarely and softly, their voices melodic but low, blending with the ambient drips and hums. Their true "speech" is often through resonant stone chimes hung near dwellings, struck gently to signal across the mist – a deep, pure tone that carries far in the bowl. At dusk, communal hums or low, rhythmic chants might rise, harmonizing with the wind's thrum, giving the caldera its name.

2.  **The Obsidian Flitters (Meganeura-like Insects):**
    *   **Appearance:** Enormous dragonfly-like creatures, easily two feet across. Their wings are not transparent but resemble thin, polished shards of obsidian, catching the light in iridescent flashes of blue and green. Their bodies are slender, segmented, and metallic black.
    *   **Behavior:** They are silent gliders, masters of the updrafts and thermals swirling within the caldera bowl. They move with an uncanny stillness, hovering for minutes near the weeping rock faces or lush fern clusters. Their primary purpose seems to be harvesting the constant moisture; they possess delicate, brush-like mouthparts to lap condensation and the nectar-like secretions from the glassy succulents. They are rarely seen landing, perpetually aloft.
    *   **Significance:** Their iridescent wings create fleeting, prismatic reflections on the wet rock and mist, adding to the caldera's ethereal light-play. The Stone-Weavers view them with reverence, as manifestations of the mountain's breath and light. They are utterly harmless to humans.

3.  **The Moss-Shelled Grazers (Gastropods):**
    *   **Appearance:** Large, slow-moving snails. Their shells are not smooth but deeply ridged and furrowed, thickly encrusted with vibrant mosses and minute cyanobacteria colonies, making them appear like mobile, living fragments of the landscape itself. Colours range from deep jade to coppery russet, matching the moss gardens.
    *   **Behavior:** They move imperceptibly slowly across the perpetually damp rocks and moss beds, grazing on the film of algae, bacteria, and decomposing plant matter. Their trails, glistening faintly with secreted mucus, form intricate, ephemeral patterns on the stone, visible only until the next condensation cycle.
    *   **Adaptation:** Their symbiotic moss/cyanobacteria coating provides camouflage and potentially supplementary nutrients or moisture retention. Their slow pace is an energy conservation tactic in a nutrient-poor environment. The Stone-Weavers occasionally collect them (with great care not to damage the shell-garden) as a rare, delicate food source or for the faintly luminescent properties of their mucus trails used in simple rituals.

4.  **The Rootless Ones (Epiphytic Fern Symbiotes):**
    *   **Appearance:** Not fauna, but unique flora. Nestled deep within the unfurled crowns of the giant ferns, where moisture collects like tiny pools, live small, complex colonies. These appear as dense clusters of fleshy, gelatinous bulbs or intricate, coral-like growths in translucent blues, purples, and whites.
    *   **Nature:** These are symbiotic organisms – perhaps advanced lichens, fungi, or even simple colonial animals. They thrive on the pure water and trapped nutrients within the fern's crown. In return, they secrete compounds that nourish the fern and may deter any potential microscopic pests.
    *   **Significance:** They represent the caldera's intricate micro-ecosystems. The Stone-Weavers believe they are the "spirits of the ferns," visible manifestations of the plant's life force. Water collected from these crowns is considered especially pure and potent.

**Overall Presence:**
Life in the Singing Caldera feels sparse yet profoundly integrated. There are no large predators, no herds, no birdsong beyond the rare cry of a distant raptor echoing like a ghost. The dominant fauna are silent gliders and near-motionless grazers. The humans live quietly, their movements deliberate, their structures low and stone-born, mimicking the land's contours. Everything is geared towards harnessing water, enduring the thin air, and existing within the vast, resonant emptiness. The inhabitants *are* the caldera's slow pulse – patient, persistent, and harmonizing with the drip, the hum, and the deep, settling breath of the newborn land. They are less conquerors and more like careful listeners, tending the first fragile notes of a world's song.
