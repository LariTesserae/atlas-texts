The Endless Galleries do not host civilizations, but they teem with life perfectly sculpted by eternal damp, echoing stone, and suspended time. Here is what lives beneath the weight of the mountains:

1.  **The Swarmers:** The defining life forms are the invertebrates, existing in staggering, uncountable numbers. Pale, sightless cave crickets infest every crack, ledge, and ceiling fissure. Their constant, metallic chirping and scraping creates the pervasive, deafening backdrop – a living soundscape that reverberates endlessly off the stone. Scuttling across vast plains of smooth rock are countless arthropods: large, translucent cave-dwelling isopods resembling blind crabs with long, whip-like antennae feeling their way through the gloom, and smaller, multi-legged scavengers (pseudoscorpions, springtails, specialized beetles) that rustle like shifting gravel as they process decaying matter and fungi. This invertebrate mass forms the bedrock of the ecosystem, feeding on the sparse flora, microorganisms in the damp air and water, and each other.

2.  **The Drip-Dwellers:** Adapted to the constant seepage are specialized amphibians. Large, pale-skinned, sightless salamanders or frog-like creatures lurk in shallow pools, under overhangs where drips concentrate, or along the obsidian river's edge. They emit deep, resonant croaks (*gronks*) that boom through the chambers, audible even over the insect din. Their skin glistens perpetually in the fungal glow, absorbing moisture directly. They hunt smaller invertebrates and are themselves prey.

3.  **The River Ghosts:** The slow, black river hosts life adapted to its silent flow. Schools of slender, eel-like fish, devoid of eyes and pigment, navigate the obsidian depths. They disturb the surface with soft *slaps* and *gulps* as they feed on microorganisms, blind crustaceans, and anything small enough to be swept into their path. Delicate, translucent shrimp and filter-feeding organisms cling to rocks beneath the surface.

4.  **The Fungus Farmers & Gatherers:** The near-human observers are not builders or masters, but another strand in the web. They are a distinct hominid lineage or an isolated human population, generations-deep adapted to the Galleries. Pale-skinned, with eyes enlarged to capture the dimmest fungal light, they move with deliberate, silent steps, masters of navigating the echoing vastness by sound and feel. Clad in layered furs and cured hides from the large amphibians, they carry simple tools:
    *   **Bone Hooks & Sinew Lines:** For fishing the dark river.
    *   **Flint Blades:** Knives and scrapers for processing hides, fungi, and occasional catches.
    *   **Lichen-Fiber Snares:** For small game (insects, arthropods) near fungal patches.
    *   **Resin-Fungus Torches:** Used sparingly, as their acrid smoke and flickering light disrupts the delicate equilibrium and attracts unwanted attention; primarily for emergencies or specific rituals.
    *   **Woven Packs & Baskets:** Made from tough lichen fibers and moss, for carrying gathered resources.
    Their existence is one of perpetual harvest: collecting bioluminescent fungi (for light, medicine, and torches), thick grey moss (for poultices and insulation), edible lichens, the occasional fish or amphibian, and precious slivers of luminous minerals. They understand the rhythms of the drips, the paths of the river, and the territories of the larger fauna. They build nothing permanent, leaving no mark beyond worn paths and temporary campsites on dry ledges. They speak rarely, in hushed tones or hand signals, out of reverence or fear for the Galleries' amplifying acoustics and the creatures that listen. They see themselves not as owners, but as transient participants in the ancient, unchanging dance of water, stone, and life.

5.  **The Unseen Microcosm:** Beneath it all, thriving in the perpetual dampness, on the stone surfaces, within the pools, and inside the decomposing matter, exists a universe of microorganisms – bacteria, fungi, protists. They break down the sparse organic material, feed the moss and lichens, and sustain the smallest invertebrates, forming the invisible foundation upon which the entire loud, teeming ecosystem rests.

**Life here is defined by adaptation to the constants:** blindness compensated by exquisite hearing and touch, pallor replacing pigment, slow metabolisms fueled by sparse resources, and behaviors synchronized with the eternal *plink* of water and the unyielding stone. The near-humans are the most complex organisms, yet they live with profound humility amidst the scale and the swarm, more like cautious animals attuned to their environment than lords of creation. They are harvesters in the endless twilight, their survival a testament to resilience within the Galleries' damp, cool, loud, and utterly unchanging embrace.
