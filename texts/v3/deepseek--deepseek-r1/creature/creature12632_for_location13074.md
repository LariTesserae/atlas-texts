## Inhabitants of Siltstone Reach: The Grime-Caked

They are not conquerors of the plateau. They are its scum-line, its calcified sweat, its slow-rusting symbionts. The people of Siltstone Reach are the **Grime-Caked**, and they are as much a product of the weeping stone and the grinding roar as the riveted iron pipes or the iridescent slicks on the marsh pans.

### Physicality: Forged by Damp and Decibel
*   **Stature and Build:** Low-slung and broad, mirroring their city. Generations of damp air, heavy labor, and the constant, chest-vibrating pressure of the sound have compacted them. They move with a deliberate, rolling gait, conserving energy against the sucking mud and the psychological weight of the noise. Muscle is dense, built for leverage and endurance over speed – hauling pipes, clearing choked drains, wrestling corroded bolts.
*   **Skin and Features:** Permanently stained. A base layer of ingrained basalt grit and mineral dust mixes with oily condensation and factory soot, creating a unique, leathery patina ranging from slate-grey to bruised ochre. Faces are broad, noses often flattened or thickened (possibly an adaptation against the damp air pressure, or just generations of industrial accidents). Eyes are deep-set and perpetually narrowed – against the glare off wet stone, the stinging mineral-laced mist, and the sheer *effort* of focusing amidst the sensory assault. Hair is coarse, often cropped short or bound tightly, useless against the wet and prone to trapping grit. Missing fingers or limbs are common badges of honor.
*   **Senses:** Hearing is a complex adaptation. Outwardly, most wear thick, custom-molded ear-plugs or bulky, steam-powered baffle-helmets – sacred artifacts maintained with reverence. *Unprotected* hearing is often ruined early, replaced by an acute sensitivity to vibration felt through the bones and feet. They read the plateau's hum and the machines' thuds like a language, detecting failing pumps or stressed turbines through the soles of their boots. Sight is attuned to subtle shifts in moisture, mineral gleam, and the tell-tale rainbows of pollutant slicks. Smell is dulled by the omnipresent ozone and wet metal, save for detecting the sharp tang of catastrophic corrosion or toxic leaks.

### Culture & Society: The Slow Rhythm of the Hum
*   **Communication:** Verbal speech is reserved for absolute necessity, shouted directly into another's protected ear or through tube-amplifiers. Primary communication is a complex, highly developed **Sign-Tongue**. It utilizes broad, deliberate gestures visible in the murk, amplified by tool-taps on pipes or specific boot-stomps on resonant rock. Written notes on waxed slate or treated hide are common. Silence is deeply unsettling, signaling imminent machine failure or disaster.
*   **Social Structure:** Hierarchical, but based on function and endurance, not lineage. **Pump-Masters** and **Pipe-Wardens** hold the highest status, responsible for the sacred flow of Sovereign Water. **Rust-Walkers** are revered engineers and patchers, fighting the losing battle against decay. **Sump-Scrapers** maintain the vital drains and marsh edges. **Grit-Runners** operate the narrow-gauge railways. Decisions are made slowly, communally, after long observation – rushing is anathema. Elders are valued repositories of slow-gained knowledge about the plateau's moods and the machines' quirks.
*   **Mindset:** Profoundly stoic and patient. They possess a **geological perspective**. A project taking years is normal. A machine running for a decade without catastrophic failure is a triumph. Change is mistrusted unless it demonstrably serves the eternal cycles of water, stone, and sound. They find a grim comfort in the deafening constancy; it is the plateau's heartbeat. Anxiety manifests not in haste, but in increased vigilance and ritualistic maintenance checks.
*   **Relationship with the Weird:** The iridescent slicks, humming crystals, and faintly glowing water are not "strange" – they are *Siltstone*. **Slick-Gleaners** carefully harvest rainbow residues for pigments, lubricants, or dubious medicinal salves. **Crystal-Singers** (often partially deaf) commune with the vibrating mineral growths near warm effluent, interpreting their hums for insights into subterranean flows or machine stresses. They accept these phenomena as part of the plateau's slow exhalation, integrated into their practical, survival-focused worldview.

### Necessities & Artifacts
*   **Attire:** Layered, waterproofed hides and thick, felted fabrics treated with mineral oils. Heavy boots with deep, clog-resistant treads. Tool belts are essential, carrying pry-bars, wrenches scaled to massive bolts, grease-pots, and slate-tablets. Ear protection is non-negotiable. Goggles or visors shield against grit and acidic mist.
*   **Shelter:** Low, thick-walled structures mirroring the industrial buildings, often built directly onto or into weeping rock faces, using the constant moisture for rudimentary cooling or integrated into drain systems. Warmth comes from shared thermal vents bleeding waste heat from factories. Interiors are damp, dim, and utilitarian. Cleanliness is a futile concept; "clean" means "functionally free of obstructing grime."
*   **Sustenance:** Scarce native life is rarely eaten (the lizards are stringy, the sedge grass bitter). Primary food is dense, nutrient-paste bricks fermented from imported grains and algae cultured in the warm, mineral-rich marsh pans – often infused with a faint, harmless luminescence. Water is carefully collected condensate from pipes or treated marsh water, always slightly metallic. "Luxuries" might be a rare haul of hardy tubers from a sheltered crevice or a particularly vibrant iridescent pigment.

### The Grime-Caked Essence
They are the human equivalent of the riveted iron plates on the basalt – a temporary, corroding layer on something ancient and immense. They endure. They maintain. They interpret the language of water, stone, and sound. Their lives are measured in pump-cycles and the slow retreat of a rivet-head into rust. They are not happy, nor are they often sad; they are *occupied*, deeply embedded in the warm, wet, deafening permanence of Siltstone Reach. To leave the plateau is to enter a terrifying, silent, *fast* world of incomprehensible chaos. Here, amidst the eternal grind and weep, they know their place: custodians of decay, children of the hum.
