The Throat has only one true inhabitant, one organism that *is* the ecology: **The Glacial Drifters**.

**Appearance:** Individually, they are minuscule, iridescent arthropods, each roughly the size and thickness of a human fingernail. Their segmented exoskeletons shimmer under the sapphire light in deep, shifting blues, violets, and gunmetal greys, refracting the cold luminescence into fleeting rainbows across their chitin. Their bodies are low-slung and flattened, built for pressing against surfaces and each other. Multiple pairs of short, delicate legs, barely visible beneath their carapaces, end in minute, hardened points for gripping the glass-smooth basalt. They possess no obvious eyes, antennae, or mandibles – sensory and feeding apparatuses must be subtle, perhaps chemosensory pits or light-sensitive patches integrated into their shells.

**Collective Existence:** To speak of a single Drifter is almost meaningless. They exist *only* as **The Packed Density**. Millions upon millions form a continuous, living carpet, filling every conceivable space between the basalt boulders, coating the lower slopes of the rock, and flowing into the shallow basins like iridescent mercury. Their collective shell-mass *is* the terrain beneath the boulders. They move not as individuals, but as an impossibly slow tide. Their progress is glacial, measured in millimeters per year. Watching them is like watching rock flow – a hypnotic shimmer across the black stone, a constant, infinitesimal rearrangement within the crushing mass. This movement creates the low, grinding susurrus that underpins the Throat's soundscape.

**Physiology & Sustenance:** The Drifters are lithovores and condensors. They feed on the mineral-rich condensation that beads constantly on their shells and the basalt, absorbing dissolved minerals directly through specialized micro-pores in their exoskeletons. Microscopic fungal and bacterial spores drifting in the damp air are likely trapped and consumed on their shell surfaces or within the interstitial moisture between individuals. Their metabolism must be extraordinarily slow and efficient, generating minimal waste heat, perfectly adapted to the near-freezing environment. Waste products are likely re-mineralized directly into their exoskeletons or the surrounding moisture, contributing to the stasis. They require no oxygen beyond trace amounts dissolved in the water film they inhabit.

**Behavior & Purpose:** They exhibit no discernible behaviors beyond the collective creep. There is no predation, no mating display (reproduction, if it occurs, must be equally slow and communal, perhaps parthenogenic or via mineral-mediated spore exchange), no flight, no discernible reaction to intrusion beyond the constant, unceasing movement. Their sole purpose, dictated by the Light and the Stasis, appears to be **occupation and maintenance**. They fill the space completely. Their slow movement constantly redistributes moisture, perhaps prevents localized mineral build-up that could disrupt the perfect equilibrium, and ensures every drop of condensing water finds a shell or stone to bead upon. They are the living component of the suspended erosion cycle, their bodies acting as both reservoir and conduit for the omnipresent water.

**Relationship to Structures:** The origin of the bizarre crystal-chitin spires remains enigmatic, but the Drifters are intrinsically linked to them. The structures are composed of fused corundum and ossified chitin identical to the Drifters' shells. Perhaps they are colossal, ancient accretions formed by Drifters over eons, drawn to mineral seeps or points of concentrated crystal energy. Maybe they are the petrified remains of vast, slow-moving colonies that solidified in place. The Drifters flow around their bases and sometimes partially engulf their lower levels, treating them as just another feature of the landscape to be occupied and traversed with infinite patience.

**Essence:** The Glacial Drifters *are* the Throat's stillness made animate. They are life stripped of dynamism, evolution, and individual purpose. They exist solely to perpetuate the chamber's frozen moment: absorbing the light's cold energy, processing the endless condensation, filling the space with their shimmering, grinding mass. They are beautiful, alien, and profoundly unsettling – a reminder that life can exist without change, without struggle, without end, as a perfect, humming, iridescent stasis. To witness them is to witness eternity condensed into the imperceptible creep of a billion tiny shells.
