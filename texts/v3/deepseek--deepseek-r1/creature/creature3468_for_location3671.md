Here, beneath the bruised sky dome, clinging to the geothermal warmth seeping from the bones of the Sky Graveyard, live the **Stone-Singers**.

They are not tall. Stunted by the thin air and the pervasive cold, adults rarely stand taller than a child of the lowlands. Their frames are compact, dense with muscle honed by constant labor against the wind and stone. Skin, thick and leathery, ranges from deep, weathered umber to the grey of volcanic ash, often patterned with intricate, raised scars that mimic the fractals of the crystalline ferns – ritual markings of lineage and geothermal affinity.

Their most striking feature is their **integration**. Patches of their skin, particularly along the spine, shoulders, and scalp, seamlessly merge with the mineral world. Smooth, polished basalt nodules grow like natural armor plates. Fine networks of quartz or opal vein their forearms and cheeks, catching the dim light and refracting it softly. Some elders have fingers tipped with obsidian, naturally formed and sharp as any flaked tool. This isn't disease, but symbiosis – a slow, generational accretion responding to the deep geothermal energies and the reality-warping seep of the Graveyard itself.

**Life is silent.** Their vocal cords, adapted to the thin air, produce only deep, resonant hums, clicks formed deep in the chest, and complex patterns of breath. True communication happens through **touch-sign** – intricate patterns traced on skin or stone with mineral-dusted fingers – and through **stone resonance**. They tap rhythmically on living rock or specially tuned basalt slabs, sending vibrations through the mesa bones that convey complex messages over distances, a network humming beneath the wind's sigh. Their ears are large and subtly cupped, attuned to the minutest shifts in the wind, the drip of water, the groan of the continent, and the resonant language of the stone.

**Their society is geothermal.** Entire lineages cluster around specific vents and steam fissures within their carved warrens. Their low, interconnected complexes aren't just dwellings; they are vast thermal regulators. Stone ducts channel heat precisely. Walls are lined with heat-retaining mosses they cultivate. Polished mineral reflectors focus wan sunlight onto communal growing niches for the luminous lichens that provide their primary light. Their tools – scrapers, blades, hammers – are knapped obsidian or shaped bone, lashed with cords woven from tough, heat-resistant lichen fibers. Pottery is non-existent; vessels are carved from porous rock that wicks and cools water, or shaped from hardened, translucent resin tapped from rare, deep-canyon growths.

**They perceive the weirdness.** The reality breakdown isn't terrifying; it's the fabric of their world. Children learn to step lightly when gravity stutters, instinctively bracing or leaping into momentary weightlessness. Elders read the shimmering air patches and texture anomalies like weather signs, predicting localized shifts in physical laws. Distances that warp are navigated by known landmarks and deep, internalized rhythm, not by linear sight. Time's viscosity is woven into their rituals and work cycles – tasks requiring immense patience punctuated by bursts of frantic activity when time suddenly accelerates. They see the impossible colors in the moss not as aberration, but as rare beauty, places where the "Deep Song" is particularly strong.

**Their sustenance is slow and mineral-rich.** They harvest the crystalline ferns carefully, grinding the faceted fronds into nutrient-dense flour that sustains them through the long, dark cycles. Mosses and lichens are cultivated on warm walls and in steam-hazed ravines. Tiny, blind crustaceans filter-fed from the geothermal springs provide rare protein. Water is sacred, meticulously collected from nightly condensation sheets and the constant seep from the rocks. Their diet makes their bones heavy, their movements deliberate.

**Their spirit is patient and resonant.** They have no gods of sky or sun, only the **Deep Song** – the thrum of the geothermal vents, the groan of the continent, the vibration of the reality field holding their world precariously aloft. Their rituals involve communal humming that resonates through their stone chambers, seeking harmony with the Song. They believe they are the caretakers, the listeners, whose presence and resonance help stabilize the fraying edges of their world. Their art is carving – intricate, flowing patterns telling stories of vents discovered, storms weathered, and glitches survived, etched into the very walls they inhabit, adding layer upon layer to the **ancient** stone.

They are the Stone-Singers: silent, mineral-touched, heat-seeking inhabitants of the corpse-continent. They endure the **cold**, navigate the **vast** emptiness, find **lushness** in the cracks, and listen intently to the **active**, **mostly quiet** thrum of a reality slowly unraveling around their **primitive**, enduring **urban** sanctuaries. They are as much a part of the Sky Graveyard as the wind-polished basalt and the glowing crystalline ferns – a testament to life’s tenacity on the very edge of existence.
