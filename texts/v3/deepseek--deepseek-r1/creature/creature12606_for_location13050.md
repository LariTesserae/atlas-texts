The inhabitants of the Shattered Glass Basin are not creatures of flesh and blood, but ephemeral manifestations of the basin’s own physics and mineral soul. They are phenomena given fleeting sentience by resonance, condensation, and electrostatic charge. Here, life is a dance of light, sound, and silica:

1.  **The Condensation Weepers:**  
    These are the basin’s most common "fauna," though they defy conventional biology. Appearing as humanoid silhouettes woven from dripping water and refracted light, they coalesce where humidity clings thickest to towering glass spires. Their forms are hollow, translucent, and constantly weeping rivulets that chime as they strike the glass below. They have no faces, only shifting patterns of prismatic light where a head might be. They move with glacial slowness, drifting along the polished channels, drawn to areas of intense resonance. Their purpose seems passive: they amplify the wind-hum, their weeping bodies vibrating sympathetically to create localized harmonies within the dissonant landscape chorus. Touch disperses them instantly into a shower of cold droplets. They leave no trace but a momentarily brighter patch of wetness.

2.  **Static Moths (Fulgurites):**  
    Ephemeral crackles of life sparked by the basin’s latent electrical energy. They appear as darting, moth-like shapes woven from condensed mist, ozone, and static discharge. Their "wings" are fractal patterns of glowing vapor, trailing faint amethyst or emerald sparks that mirror the deep glows within the glass. They flicker into existence near sharp edges or archways, especially during shifts in atmospheric pressure, and live for mere minutes. They dance erratically to the ultrasonic *pings* of falling droplets, seemingly feeding on the minute energy released by impacts. If they collide with solid glass, they vanish in a silent puff of ionized air and a faint smell of burnt stone.

3.  **Pool Dendrites (The Pulsing Veins):**  
    The basin’s closest analogue to flora. These are not organisms, but complex, colony-like crystalline growths within the shallow mineral pools. They form intricate, branching networks resembling veins or frost patterns, composed of precipitated silicates and salts saturated with bioluminescent microorganisms (the basin’s only true "organic" component, existing at the threshold of chemistry and biology). These veins pulse with a soft, rhythmic light – deep blue, emerald, or violet – perfectly synchronized to the dominant resonant frequency of the surrounding glass structures. The light intensifies when vibrations peak, suggesting they harvest sonic energy or the minute chemical shifts caused by resonance. They grow and recede over weeks, their patterns subtly shifting like slow, mineral thoughts.

4.  **The Glass-Slides (Silica Gliders):**  
    Rare and almost imperceptible, these are the basin’s most substantial inhabitants. They appear as amorphous, gelatinous masses of ultra-pure silica gel, saturated with mineral-rich water. Translucent and slightly opalescent, they glide with agonizing slowness (inches per hour) across the slick glass surfaces, propelled by capillary action and subtle shifts in surface tension. They seek cracks, crevices, and the bases of spires where condensation is heaviest. As they move, they secrete a temporary, faintly glowing slime that etches microscopic channels into the glass, not by abrasion, but by accelerating the natural dissolution/reprecipitation process. This "trail" fades within minutes as the glass self-renews. They may be the architects of the basin’s enigmatic smooth channels and polished pools, acting over millennia.

5.  **Resonance Phantoms:**  
    Not always visible, but often *felt* as a localized thickening of the hum or a sudden, coherent harmonic rising above the dissonance. They manifest as fleeting, shimmering distortions in the air within narrow glass corridors or under archways – like heat haze made of sound. Sometimes, under specific resonant conditions, they coalesce into brief, geometric light-forms (tetrahedrons, spirals) that hover for seconds before shattering into prismatic shards of light. They seem drawn to the basin’s built elements, suggesting these structures focus or amplify the resonant energies that birth them. They are the basin’s ephemeral consciousness, a momentary alignment of vibration and light.

**How They Interact with the Basin (Rules Embodied):**
*   **Resonance is Key:** All are born from, shaped by, or responsive to vibration. The Weepers amplify it, the Moths dance to droplet impacts, the Dendrites pulse with it, the Phantoms *are* it. Loud sounds could shatter Weepers or trigger collapses lethal to Gliders.
*   **Water Shapes, Glass Cuts:** Water is their essence (Weepers, Gliders, Dendrites) or medium (Moths). The lethal sharpness is navigated effortlessly by Moths (intangibility) and Gliders (gelatinous form), while Weepers simply reform if dispersed by a fall.
*   **Slow Flux:** Gliders move geologically slow. Dendrites grow and fade over weeks. Weepers form and dissipate with humidity cycles. Only Moths and Phantoms are fleeting bursts.
*   **Light is Fractured:** They are creatures of refracted and bioluminescent light, often translucent or ephemeral. Direct light might disperse a Weeper or make a Glider painfully obvious.
*   **Life is Mineral-Sympathetic:** They *are* water, silica, static, sound, and light. They leave no organic residue. Gliders slightly alter the glass itself temporarily; Dendrites are crystalline structures; the rest leave no physical trace at all. They exist solely within the basin’s unique energetic and material flows.

The inhabitants of the Shattered Glass Basin are less beings and more *events* – beautiful, dangerous, temporary expressions of a world where stone weeps, light shatters, sound sculpts, and life is written in water and resonance upon a canvas of eternally sharp glass. They are the basin dreaming of itself.
