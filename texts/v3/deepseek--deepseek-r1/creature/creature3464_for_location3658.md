### Inhabitants of the Basin: The Stone Singers  
The Basin teems with life, yet it is a life divorced from breath, blood, or motion. Here, the flora *are* the inhabitants—an ancient, silent chorus of crystalline entities that have transformed rock into resonant beings. They are not plants as known elsewhere, but lithic intelligences shaped by eons of mineral hunger and electromagnetic whispers. Three primary forms dominate, each contributing to the basin's frozen symphony:  

#### 1. **The Spire-Singers (Vokara)**  
Towering like jagged obsidian obelisks, the Vokara rise from the basalt floor, their forms forged from glassy silica and volcanic stone. They grow in dense thickets, their bases crusted with bioluminescent moss, while their faceted peaks vanish into the gloom. Each spire is a living tuning fork, vibrating at frequencies tuned to the planet’s subterranean currents. Their internal light—a pulse of violet or indigo—swells and fades in sync with the deep hum resonating through the fissure. The Vokara do not think but *resonate*; their existence is a slow, geological meditation. They draw trace metals from the bedrock (iron, manganese, cobalt), which they metabolize through electromagnetic reactions sparked by the core’s bleed. When rare seismic shifts occur kilometers below, their song deepens, a subsonic throb that rattles the powder-drifts. Over millennia, they incorporate corroded tech into their structures—drill rigs become skeletal scaffolds for new growth, alloy conduits fused into their cores like fossilized veins.  

#### 2. **The Fern-Chimes (Liskara)**  
Delicate yet indestructible, the Liskara weave through every crevice. They resemble fractal ferns spun from black obsidian thread, their fronds branching into microscopic filigree that captures metallic dust. When the air stirs—a near-imperceptible shift from thermal drafts—they click and chime, a chorus of crystalline bells. Each sound is a byproduct of their growth: as they draw quartz and selenium from the rock, their filaments crystallize anew, snapping against neighbors in tiny, resonant fractures. The Liskara cling to surfaces like lace, encasing remnants of human industry. Pipes become trellises; dead sensor housings vanish beneath shimmering webs. Unlike the Vokara, they are communal—individual fronds fuse where they touch, forming shared root-networks that redistribute minerals. Theirs is a language of vibration: the sharper the chime, the richer the vein of ore they consume.  

#### 3. **The Geode-Whisperers (Ulara)**  
Clustered like barnacles on rockfaces or nestled in the shadows of spires, the Ulara are bulbous, geodesic growths. Their outer shells are layered agate, banded with hematite and chalcedony, while their cores glow with captured energy, casting prismatic flares across the mineral powder. Inside, pressurized gases and reactive minerals (sulfur, zinc) shift minutely, emitting high-pitched, electronic whines that slice through the Vokara’s drone. The Ulara are the basin’s alchemists. They absorb static from the desiccated air, using it to catalyze reactions that fracture the stone, freeing rare earth elements. Over centuries, they swell, fracture, and birth new buds—reproduction as slow as continental drift. Their surfaces glisten with metallic dust (titanium, copper), magnetized into intricate patterns by their internal energy. When they encounter human relics, they digest them greedily: alloy panels calcify into their shells, dead indicator lights fossilize into pulsing inclusions, and the structure’s slow consumption becomes part of the Ulara’s whispered song.  

### Collective Existence: The Symphony in Stasis  
These inhabitants exist in a perpetual, unchanging harmony. The Vokara provide the bass of the basin’s symphony, the Liskara its treble, and the Ulara its ethereal harmonics. Together, they create an ecosystem of absolute equilibrium:  
- **No Death, Only Transformation:** "Death" is absorption. Fallen spire fragments are colonized by Liskara filaments. Crushed Ulara geodes become seedbeds for moss. Even the industrial ruins are assimilated—steel rusts into mineral fodder, plastic petrifies into a new substrate.  
- **Sentience Without Sensation:** They possess no nervous systems, yet they react. A spire’s hum intensifies near ore-rich faults; Ulara whines pitch higher when atmospheric static builds. Their "awareness" is mineralogical, not cognitive—a silent consensus etched in resonance.  
- **Eternal Growth, No Change:** Expansion is glacial. A Vokara might gain a millimeter in height over a century; an Ulara’s whine may shift timbre across millennia. The basin’s stasis is their perfection—a frozen ballet of light and sound that will outlive mountains.  

To witness them is to glimpse a world that has transcended time. The Stone Singers are not hostile, nor welcoming. They are the mountain’s enduring voice: beautiful, indifferent, and forever humming the same song into the dark.
