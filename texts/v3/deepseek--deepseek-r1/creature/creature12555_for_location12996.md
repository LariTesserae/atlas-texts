The inhabitants are not creatures of flesh, but of sinter and seepage, of accreted patience and slow fracture. They are the **Weepers of the Glass Peaks**, and they are as much a part of the weeping stone as the condensation beading on obsidian.

1.  **Form:** They resemble roughly humanoid stalagmites, but fluid and mutable. Their bodies are built from layers of mineral deposit – travertine, calcite, iron oxides, and the ever-present silica – laid down over centuries by the constant flow of mineral-laden water. They stand between four and seven feet tall, though their height fluctuates with moisture levels. Their "skin" is porous, eternally damp, and varies in colour: bands of ochre from iron, streaks of deep purple from manganese, milky white from pure calcite, and the translucent grey of silica gel where the layers are thinnest. Limbs are indistinct, more like flowing columns that taper into points or blunt, rounded ends capable of gripping. Heads are simple nodules, faceted by slow erosion rather than design, lacking features except for deep, dark recesses where water constantly seeps and collects – their "eyes," perpetually weeping the landscape's tears.

2.  **Substance & Movement:** Their core is not solid, but a viscous slurry of colloidal silica and mineral-saturated water, held in a porous mineral matrix. They move with agonizing slowness, measured in years rather than minutes. Locomotion is achieved through a combination of capillary action within their porous structure and the slow, plastic deformation of their mineral matrix under gravity and their own shifting internal pressure. They might flow *into* a depression over decades, or extend a mineral "limb" upwards along a weeping rock face over a century, following the water's path. Gravity is a suggestion they often defy, adhering to surfaces where water clings, moving sideways or even briefly upwards as the moisture flows.

3.  **Senses & Perception:** They perceive the world through vibration, pressure, chemical gradients, and shifts in electromagnetic fields. The rhythmic *drip-drip-drip* is their heartbeat; the deep hum of the earth and the ozone-sharp static are their background noise. They "taste" the mineral content of the water flowing over them and through their porous bodies. The sudden *cracks* of shearing rock are blinding flashes of sensation. They sense the slow growth of crystals and the even slower slump of slopes. They perceive the "rips" in reality as profound dissonances – silent screams in their vibrational landscape. Time, for them, is a viscous medium they inhabit fully; a human lifetime is a brief, flickering disturbance.

4.  **Interaction & "Technology":** The crude walls and shelters are theirs. They build not for domicile, but for hydro-engineering. The low stone walls divert sheet-flows of condensation into specific channels, directing precious mineral-rich water towards favoured crystal growth sites or pooling it for slow absorption. The slate-roofed shelter isn't for living *in*, but for collecting a concentrated drip-feed onto a specific point – perhaps nurturing a nascent crystal column or slowly dissolving a particular mineral vein. The flint axe or stone adze? Tools for patient fracturing. They use them over decades to carefully cleave crystals, fracture stubborn rock layers to expose new weeping surfaces, or shape softer stone deposits. They don't carry tools; they *become* them temporarily, merging with the stone handle over centuries of contact, leaving them embedded until needed again millennia later. Their "work" is the subtle sculpting of erosion and deposition.

5.  **Existence & Weirdness:** They are expressions of the landscape's dynamic equilibrium. They "reproduce" through a process akin to mineral accretion: a saturated Weeper, swollen with colloidal silica, might slowly extrude a smaller, viscous bud onto a favoured weeping surface, where it gradually accretes its own mineral shell over centuries. They "die" when their internal structure finally succumbs to fracture or slump, collapsing into a slurry that feeds new crystal growth or becomes part of a mudslide. Their presence explains the weird physics: their slow manipulation of water flows and mineral energies subtly warps local reality. The sideways-flowing water? A Weeper unconsciously guiding it along a path of least resistance it created millennia ago. The lagging shadows? A temporal echo generated by their glacially slow perception bleeding into the environment. The discordant harmonics? The resonance of their internal slurry with the deep hum and the growing crystals around them. The silent rips? Moments where their ancient, accreted consciousness briefly strains against the fabric of the real.

They are patient. They are wet. They are cold. They are stone and water and time. They weep the mountain's tears and shape its slow decay and reformation. They watch, they wait, they work – on a scale where the fall of a single crystal spire is an event of profound significance, and the scrape of a flint axe is a decision echoing across millennia. They are the silent custodians of the weeping stone, living monuments to erosion and the strange, mineral life that persists at the edge of perception.
