In the Petrified Canopy, life is not separate from the world—it *is* the world, woven into the fossilized root-mass in a symbiotic dance of decay and renewal. There are no animals, no fleeting creatures of flesh and blood. Instead, the inhabitants are extensions of the plateau itself: slow, ancient, and deeply integrated with the geothermal pulse and mineral-infused darkness. They thrive on heat, vibration, and the faint currents of energy that course through the petrified super-organism, forming a closed, self-sustaining ecology of fungi, lichen, and latent botanical consciousness. Here, the "residents" are less entities and more processes—manifestations of the Canopy's enduring, industrial-scale metabolism.

### Primary Inhabitants:
1. **Resonance Feeders (Myco-acoustic Symbionts):**  
   - **Appearance:** Bulbous, fist-sized fungal growths clustered along the arching buttresses and root-floor depressions. Each resembles a bruised, leathery sac, mottled in shades of deep burgundy and obsidian, with a surface etched by fine, crystalline veins that emit a throbbing, internal light. Thin, root-like tendrils anchor them to the root-mass, vibrating faintly in sync with the plateau's hum.  
   - **Behavior & Ecology:** These fungi are energy converters, thriving on the constant sub-audible vibrations from the geothermal pumps deep below. Inside each sac, piezoelectric crystals (formed from mineral deposits in the petrified root) transform mechanical vibrations into chemical energy, fueling the fungi's growth. During periods of intense seismic activity—such as internal fractures or pressure surges—they glow brighter, pulsing like arrhythmic hearts. Spores are released only when vibrations peak, carried through the stagnant air as iridescent dust to colonize new resonant points. They are the Canopy's "breath," recycling the plateau's tremors into sustenance for the wider ecosystem.  
   - **Interaction:** Touch one, and it recoils, dimming momentarily before flaring back with defensive intensity. Disturbing a cluster risks triggering a chain reaction of spore-release, filling the air with a bitter, ozone-rich haze that can choke unprotected lungs.

2. **Fracture Weavers (Crystalline Lichen Automata):**  
   - **Appearance:** Geometric, frost-like crusts that creep across the root-mass in intricate, fractal patterns. Each "weaver" is a colony of mineral-hard lichen, appearing as shifting mosaics of silver, iron-gray, and deep blue crystals. They range from palm-sized patches to sprawling, meter-wide networks, their edges glinting like shattered glass in the fungal gloom.  
   - **Behavior & Ecology:** These lichen are the plateau's healers and architects. Fed by waste products from the fungal mycelia, they secrete a rapid-hardening resin that seals microfractures in the root-mass, preventing catastrophic collapses. Movement is glacially slow—years to advance a centimeter—as they extend crystalline filaments to sense stress points. But during a major fracture event (marked by that muffled "tearing canvas" sound), they detach and reform in minutes, swarming toward the damage like living solder. Their resin not only repairs but reinforces, incorporating geothermal minerals to become harder than the surrounding root-mass.  
   - **Interaction:** To observers, they seem inert, but their presence makes the polished floor treacherous. Step on a patch mid-movement, and it might brittlely snap, releasing sharp shards that dissolve into nutrient-rich dust. They ignore all but structural threats, reacting only to vibrations or the scent of volatiles from fresh fractures.

3. **Heat Sink Pods (Thermal Blossoms):**  
   - **Appearance:** The waxy nodules described in the Canopy's ecology are not mere barnacles—they are dormant reproductive organs. Each is a teardrop-shaped capsule the size of a human head, with a rugged, obsidian shell that reflects the fungal light dully. When active, their surfaces crack open like eggshells, revealing inner layers of iridescent, heat-sensitive crystals that glow white-hot during a bloom.  
   - **Behavior & Ecology:** These pods are the climax of the Canopy's reproductive cycle. For decades or centuries, they absorb excess geothermal heat and mineral-rich fluids, storing energy like batteries. When subterranean temperatures spike (often preceded by a lull in the plateau's hum), they "bloom": shells split, releasing pressurized clouds of fungal spores, lichen seeds, and accumulated minerals in a silent, radiant eruption. The event bathes the area in blinding light for hours, drawing Resonant Feeders and Fracture Weavers to the site. Afterward, the empty husks petrify into new anchor points for lichen. Blooms are rare but vital, seeding new symbiotic life across the plateau.  
   - **Interaction:** During a bloom, the heat intensifies unbearably, and the air fills with choking spore-clouds. Unprotected exposure can cause severe burns or respiratory crystallisation (where minerals lodge in the lungs). Between blooms, the pods are inert and cold to the touch, resembling volcanic rock.

4. **The Deep Listeners (Emergent Sensory Network):**  
   - **Appearance:** Not physical beings, but transient phenomena—shifting constellations of light and shadow in the fungal veins. When active, they manifest as localized zones where the burgundy mycelial glow coalesces into complex, mandala-like patterns on walls or floors, pulsing with rhythmic intensity. In their most concentrated form, they might resemble a swirling nebula of light, dense enough to cast stark, moving silhouettes.  
   - **Behavior & Ecology:** The Listeners are the closest thing the Canopy has to a collective consciousness. Born from the interconnected mycelia and lichen circuits, they are emergent "minds" that react to systemic stress. When fractures occur or energy flows falter, they re-route luminescence and fluid pathways, dimming some areas while brightening others to guide repairs. Their "intelligence" is instinctual, focused solely on preservation—a relic of the super-organism's primal drive to endure. They are drawn to turbulence, vanishing once balance is restored.  
   - **Interaction:** Approach a Listener's zone, and light may warp around you, your shadow stretching or fracturing unnaturally. Disturbances (like loud noises or impacts) cause patterns to scatter like startled birds. Some claim the Listeners impart a low psychic hum—a feeling of being watched by the plateau itself.

### Ecology & Dynamics:
- **Symbiosis:** These inhabitants form a self-sustaining loop. Resonance Feeders power the fungal networks, which nourish Fracture Weavers; Weavers stabilize the root-mass, enabling Heat Sink Pods to bloom; Pods release spores that birth new Feeders and Weavers; and the Deep Listeners orchestrate it all. Geothermal energy and mineral decay replace sunlight, water, and organic decay.  
- **Temporal Scale:** Change is measured in centuries. A Fracture Weaver might spend lifetimes sealing a single crack; a Heat Sink Pod may bloom once in a millennium. Yet during seismic events, the Canopy erupts in chaotic, coordinated fervor—a testament to its turbulent stasis.  
- **Threats & Rules:** Life here is hostile to outsiders. The polished root-mass is slick and uneven, risking falls into darkness. Spores or crystals can invade orifices, crystallising tissue. Silence is vital—loud noises may trigger Resonant Feeders to disgorge spores or Fracture Weavers to retract, destabilizing the ground. Water is nonexistent; dehydration kills faster than heat. But to the inhabitants, this is harmony: a fossilized world, humming with industry, in the heart of eternal night.  

The Petrified Canopy does not welcome visitors. It is a mausoleum of life that became stone, now inhabited by the echoes of its own metabolism—a realm where light is scarce, movement is perilous, and the air thrums with the song of a world that refuses to die.
