The inhabitants of Glyth-Morv are not mere creatures surviving in the damp gloom; they are expressions of the cisterns' slow transformation, evolved or adapted to thrive within its specific, patient decay. They move through the dimness like living extensions of the weeping rust, the pulsing fungi, and the silent water. Here dwells a quiet, intricate society of the damp:

1.  **The Weep-Scavengers (Crustacea *Lacrimacorrodens*):**
    *   **Form:** Resembling large, flattened isopods (think giant, silent woodlice), their carapaces are not hard shells, but thick, leathery chitin the color of wet slate and weeping rust. Their numerous legs are short, multi-jointed, and tipped with minute, hooked setae, allowing silent, adhesive movement across near-vertical surfaces, slick ferrocrete, and the mineral-crusted pillars.
    *   **Life:** They are the primary consumers of Glyth-Morv's defining feature: the weeping rust blooms and mineral accretions. Slow-moving and deliberate, they methodically traverse the pillars and riveted walls. Their specialized mouthparts secrete a mild acid that dissolves the mineral crusts and stabilized rust, which they then ingest. Their digestive systems host symbiotic bacteria that extract iron and other minerals, vital nutrients scarce elsewhere. Their waste products are dense, pebble-like nodules that fall to the floor, contributing to the slow sedimentation.
    *   **Interaction:** Utterly non-aggressive towards larger creatures. They move in slow, seemingly random paths, but are acutely attuned to the rhythm of drips, often congregating where fresh moisture seeps, accelerating the breakdown of new mineral formations. They are silent except for the faintest *scritch-scratch* of their legs and mandibles on metal and stone.

2.  **The Channel-Tenders (Pisces *Stilbocurrens*):**
    *   **Form:** Sleek, eel-like predators, but unlike the solitary hunters mentioned, these are smaller (arm-length), semi-translucent, and possess a faint, internal bioluminescence – a network of pulsing blue veins beneath their skin. Their heads are blunt, sensory plates covering vestigial eyespots, but their most striking feature is a pair of long, whip-like barbels extending from their lower jaws, glowing faintly turquoise at the tips.
    *   **Life:** They are the shepherds of the central channel. Moving in small, synchronized schools, they glide just above the silty bottom. Their glowing barbels constantly sweep the sediment, not just for prey (the blind shrimp, micro-crustaceans), but seemingly to *stir* it. They maintain the slow flow, preventing total stagnation by gently agitating the silt and clearing minor blockages of fungal debris or mineral nodules (like those dropped by the Weep-Scavengers). Their internal light may regulate their own metabolism or communicate within the school, but it also attracts the clouds of bioluminescent gnats, creating shifting halos of light around the moving schools.
    *   **Interaction:** Wary but curious. They will dart into shadow if disturbed by large movement at the channel's edge, but quickly resume their tending. They seem to possess a rudimentary collective intelligence focused solely on the channel's equilibrium. They are the closest thing to active custodians the cistern possesses.

3.  **The Spore-Sifters (Gastropoda *Fungivorus murmurus*):**
    *   **Form:** Large, soft-bodied gastropods, resembling ghostly grey slugs the size of a human forearm. Their bodies are covered in a perpetual, glistening sheen of mucus. Instead of eye stalks, they possess clusters of light-sensitive pits along their upper ridge. Their most unusual feature is a multitude of tiny, delicate, finger-like appendages (modified foot tissue) surrounding their rasping mouth, constantly waving and probing.
    *   **Life:** They are the primary grazers and cultivators of the fungal mats. Moving with agonizing slowness across the burgundy and black rubbery growths, they don't simply consume the fungus. Their probing appendages gently separate fungal hyphae, seemingly "pruning" or stimulating growth. They consume specific sections, leaving others intact, and their mucus trails are rich in spores and nutrients, actively seeding new patches of fungus on damp walls and in corners. They are chemosynthetic symbiotes; bacteria within their gut process inorganic compounds from the seep water and decaying structure, providing energy, while the fungus provides essential organic compounds.
    *   **Interaction:** Oblivious to anything but the fungal mats and the moisture gradients. Their movement is silent except for the soft, wet *shhhlick* of their bodies on stone or fungus. Disturbance causes them to retract their sensory appendages and slow even further, becoming near-inert lumps until perceived danger passes. They are vital to the spread and health of the cistern's "vegetation."

4.  **The Ceiling-Drifters (Cnidaria *Suspensus lucens*):**
    *   **Form:** Resembling small, inverted jellyfish or complex, bioluminescent sea gooseberries. They are fist-sized, gelatinous, and mostly transparent, except for intricate networks of internal organs that pulse with soft, violet or deep green light. Dozens of fine, sticky cilia hang like delicate fishing lines below them, nearly invisible except when catching the faintest fungal glow.
    *   **Life:** They float effortlessly in the cool, moist air, buoyed by internal gas bladders. They are passive filter-feeders, drifting on the almost imperceptible air currents that circulate through the vaulted space. Their hanging cilia trap the clouds of bioluminescent gnats and other airborne microbes. Their light pulses slowly, possibly as a form of communication or to regulate their buoyancy/drift patterns. They are drawn to the highest points, near the ceiling fissures where condensation forms and drips, likely absorbing moisture directly from the saturated air through their skin.
    *   **Interaction:** Ethereal and seemingly fragile. They drift silently, their faint light adding to the starfield effect created by the fungal patches. If touched or caught, they simply collapse into a non-luminescent, inert blob, eventually reforming when released. They represent the most aerial and detached layer of the cistern's ecology.

**The Unseen Whisper:** While not a distinct species, there is a pervasive sense of *awareness* within Glyth-Morv. It is not malevolent, but deeply *attentive*. It manifests as:
*   The synchronized pause of all small fauna (gnats, arthropods) when a large drip echoes unexpectedly loudly.
*   The way fungal colonies sometimes seem to pulse in subtle waves across distances, unrelated to immediate stimuli.
*   The feeling that the slow processes – the rust weeping, the drip carving, the mineral deposition – are being observed, perhaps even *tended*, by the collective metabolism of the entire ecosystem. It is the consciousness of the place itself, a slow, cool, damp sentience woven from millennia of water, decay, and patient life.

The inhabitants of the Weeping Cisterns are not intruders; they are the chamber's own children, born from its ferrocrete bones, its iron blood, and its fungal breath. They move with the slowness of geologic time, their lives intertwined with the drip, the seep, and the faint glow, perfectly adapted to the profound, wet silence of their transformed world. They are the living expression of "accepted decay," thriving in the beautiful, eerie equilibrium Glyth-Morv has become.
