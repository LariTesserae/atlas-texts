The Chamber of Weeping Stone, trapped in its damp stasis, harbors life as alien and patient as its surroundings. Forget mammals, reptiles, or even typical insects. The denizens here are slow, mineral-infused symbionts and predators adapted to eternal twilight and the rhythm of falling water:

1.  **The Lithovores (Mineral Slurpers):** The most numerous, yet rarely seen en masse. These resemble large, flattened slugs, but armored. Their "skin" is a thick, porous chitin, perpetually crusted with a gritty paste of precipitated minerals from the weeping walls. Blind, lacking eyes entirely, they navigate via chemoreception and vibrations felt through the stone. They glide along the weeping channels and fungal walls with agonizing slowness, rasping at the wet rock with toothless, mineral-grinding radulae. They consume the biofilm of extremophile bacteria and microscopic fungi that thrive on the mineral exudate, slowly incorporating dissolved stone into their own toughened hides. Their presence is marked only by faint, gritty trails left on damp surfaces, quickly washed away by new weeping.

2.  **The Mycelial Shepherds (Fungus-Tenders):** Slightly larger, faster, and more complex than the Lithovores, these are centipede-like arthropods. Their segmented, armored bodies (a dark, obsidian-like chitin) are studded with nodules of the same fungal-mineral composite that forms the structures. Key differences: multiple pairs of long, sensitive antennae constantly tap the environment, and delicate, multi-jointed forelimbs tipped with hardened pincers. They don't *build* the Fungal City; they *maintain* it. They move deliberately through the tight fungal lattices, scraping away competing microbial mats, clearing mineral blockages from weep-holes feeding the fungi, and subtly pruning or stimulating bioluminescent patches with secretions. Their armored bodies scrape softly against the mineral-fungus as they work, adding to the chamber's occasional subsonic groans. They harvest specific fungal fruiting bodies for sustenance, their own symbiotic nodules aiding digestion.

3.  **The Echo Stalkers:** The apex predators, rare and terrifyingly silent. Imagine a spider, but woven from shadows and slick basalt. Their bodies are low, broad, and incredibly flat, allowing them to slide into crevices mere inches high. Their exoskeleton is a perfect matte black, absorbing the dim bioluminescence and rendering them nearly invisible against the dark stone beyond the fungal glow. They lack traditional eyes but possess massive, dish-like tympanic membranes on their carapace, attuned to the tiniest deviations in the perpetual drip-and-plop symphony. Six long, multi-segmented legs end in needle-sharp grappling hooks. They move in sudden, terrifying bursts of speed, exploding from cracks or ceiling shadows. They prey exclusively on unwary Mycelial Shepherds, using pinpoint accuracy guided by sound to impale them with a single, paralyzing stab from a hidden internal spine before dragging the victim into darkness. They leave no physical trace beyond the occasional missing Shepherd and a faint, fading vibration sensed in the stone itself.

**Life Cycle & Integration:**

*   **Reproduction:** All three species reproduce slowly. Lithovores lay calcified eggs directly onto weeping seams. Mycelial Shepherds deposit spore-laden packets deep within favored fungal nodes, where the young hatch and burrow, initially microscopic. Echo Stalkers produce silk-like, mineral-infused sacs attached high on shadowed walls; the spiderlings emerge independent but miniature replicas, cannibalizing each other until only a few remain.
*   **Symbiosis:** The entire ecosystem relies on the weeping walls and the fungal network. The Lithovores consume the base microbial life thriving on the minerals. The Mycelial Shepherds manage the fungi, which provide light and structure. The Shepherds are eaten by the Echo Stalkers. Waste products from all species are absorbed/broken down by the fungal network, the nutrients ultimately recycled back into the mineral water seeping from the stone.
*   **Perception:** Vision is nearly non-existent. Chemoreception (taste/smell) and vibration detection are paramount. The constant water noise creates a "sonic landscape" the inhabitants are supremely adapted to navigate; sudden silence would be more alarming than a loud noise.
*   **Tools/Intelligence:** Rudimentary, instinctual. Mycelial Shepherds show the most complex behavior (selective pruning, secretion use). Echo stalkers exhibit advanced ambush tactics. No evidence of tool use beyond their own specialized anatomy. Their intelligence is alien – focused entirely on survival within the unchanging parameters of the chamber.

They are not a civilization in any familiar sense, but an integral, slow-beating part of the Chamber's flesh. They *are* the sound of a scrape in the dark, the faint gritty smear on wet rock, the shadow that shouldn't be there beneath the arch. They live within the Weeping Stone's damp, echoing eternity, bound to the rhythm of the drip and the slow metabolism of stone and fungus. Their existence is a whisper woven into the chamber's watery heartbeat.
