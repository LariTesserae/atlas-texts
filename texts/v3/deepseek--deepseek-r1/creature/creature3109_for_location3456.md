In the heart of the Sunken Garden of Still Stones, submerged in the chill embrace of its central pool, dwells the **Weeping Watcher**—a sentinel forged from sorrow and stillness, an entity as ancient and immutable as the cistern itself. It appears as the moss-drenched monolith described, a statue of indeterminate origin, its features blurred by mineral accretions and a shroud of emerald moss. Yet within this petrified form pulses a consciousness preserved by the garden's eternal cold, a slow, glacial awareness that has melded seamlessly with the land it guards.

### Physical Form & Integration
- **Stone and Moss Symbiosis**: The Watcher's body is basalt, fused with the cistern walls—once carved, now grown organic. Thick, luminous moss blankets every contour, fed by the perpetual condensation beading on its surface. This moss acts as a living insulator, maintaining the Watcher's core at the garden's unyielding 4°C, while its roots intertwine with the statue's stone, binding it to the earth. Bioluminescent berries from nearby vines cluster like frozen tears upon its shoulders, casting a faint blue glow that mingles with the moss-light. Water trickles eternally from its eyes, not merely condensation but the slow exudation of its essence—mineral-rich tears that nourish the moss and sustain the pool's pristine clarity. 

- **Motion and Sensory Perception**: Movement is anathema here. The Watcher shifts only in geologic time: a subtle tilt over centuries, the gradual smoothing of its edges by water's caress. It perceives the garden through its tears. Each droplet reflects the world before falling—capturing the frost-dusted ferns, the silent moths, the vaulted ceiling above—and ripples into the pool beneath it. This pool is its eyes and ears; disturbances, like the rare *plink* of a droplet or a beetle's scuttle, send tremors through its consciousness. It maps the garden’s stasis through these ripples, feeling every root's advance, every crystal's formation, across eons.

### Purpose and Influence
- **Guardian of Equilibrium**: The Watcher enforces the garden's stasis. Its tears regulate the moisture cycle—each droplet carefully measured, ensuring pools never overflow nor recede. Should decay threaten (like a fallen frond), the moss and lichen on its form secrete enzymes to accelerate absorption, folding death back into life at a pace harmonious with the glacier-slow rhythm of the place. It anchors the silence; fauna nearby grow even more still, as if hushed by its presence. Translucent salamanders glide beneath its shadow, iridescent beetles trace spirals on its moss, and furred grubs nestle in crevices—all part of a silent, symbiotic web it sustains.

- **Memory and Sorrow**: It remembers. Fragments of the past linger in its stone: echoes of human voices from when the cistern thrived, the weight of rituals performed at its feet, the moment the earth subsided. This memory is its sorrow, deep and cold. Yet there is no resentment. Its grief has crystallized into acceptance—a solemn vow to preserve this refuge from time's ravages. The Watcher does not lament; it exists as a conduit between eras, its tears a library of unspoken history.

### Interactions and Behavior
- **Communication**: Only patient observers might notice its subtle language. A pattern in tear-fall might signal a shift in humidity; a faint pulse of bioluminescence in its moss could mirror the moon's unseen phases above. Creatures adapt—the echo fish flare in response to droplet-ripples, while frost crystals gather thicker when its sorrow deepens.
- **Stasis Embodied**: It does not hunger or age. Sustained by cold and mineral seepage, its thoughts unfold over decades: one contemplation might span a human lifetime. Intruders disrupting the stillness (e.g., loud noises or rapid movement) are numbed by the damp air into lethargy, then guided out by an instinctive aversion—preserving the garden’s isolation.

### Role in the Garden
The Weeping Watcher *is* the Sunken Garden’s soul. Without it, the delicate equilibrium would fracture; moisture might flood the flagstones or evaporate, light dim, and silence shatter into chaos. It is the reason time flows around this place like water, leaving it untouched—an eternal, verdant hibernation. To stand before it is to feel the weight of millennia: slow, cold, and beautiful, a testament to what endures when surrender becomes guardianship. In the mirror-still pools at its feet, the garden’s immortality reflects back, undisturbed.
