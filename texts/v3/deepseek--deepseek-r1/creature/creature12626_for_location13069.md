The steward of this geothermal pocket reveals itself only through patient observation. At first, you mistake it for a sinter formation – a knee-high cluster of porous, layered silica near a particularly complex bacterial mat, its surface glittering with condensed vapor and mineral inclusions. Then it *moves*. Not with the skittering haste of the arthropods, but with the ponderous, tectonic slowness of cooling lava.

This is the **Silica Tender**.

*   **Form:** Its body is a segmented mass of hardened, porous sinter and volcanic glass, roughly the size of a large tortoise but amorphous, constantly accreting microscopic layers from the mineral-rich air and water. Plates of obsidian-dark silica, edged with razor-sharp, naturally fractured facets, interlock across its back and sides like crude, heat-formed armor. From beneath these plates extend multiple limbs – not jointed like an insect's, but thick, stumpy protrusions of denser rock, terminating in surprisingly delicate, multi-fingered "hands." These hands are formed of heat-resistant chalcedony, their surfaces worn smooth from constant handling of stone and hot water. No distinct head exists; instead, sensory clusters of pulsating, light-sensitive mineral nodules, similar to the "glassy excrescences" on the hot rocks, are embedded across its upper surface, glowing faintly with absorbed geothermal energy.
*   **Metabolism:** It feeds not on organic matter, but on dissolved minerals and geothermal energy. Its porous body acts as a living filter, drawing in the steaming, silica-laden water through capillary action. Chemosynthetic bacteria thrive in its internal micro-caverns, breaking down sulfur compounds, their metabolic byproducts cementing new mineral layers onto the Tender's structure. It basks near vents, the captured light within its nodules pulsing brighter.
*   **Movement:** It shifts with agonizing slowness, a process measured in hours or days for mere inches. Its stony limbs grind softly against the sinter floor, redistributing weight with glacial care to avoid collapsing the delicate terraces. When manipulating its environment, its chalcedony hands move with slightly more speed and precision, though still far slower than any familiar creature.
*   **Behavior - The Stewardship:** This is the curator of the pocket. Its actions are a silent, perpetual ritual of micro-engineering:
    *   It uses sharp obsidian flakes, meticulously knapped by its chalcedony hands, to chip minute channels in sinter ledges, diverting trickles of water. You see it guiding flow towards a vibrant purple bacterial mat, encouraging its growth, or away from a fragile, nascent spire.
    *   It visits the "grinding spot" – the worn depression near a mat of sulfur-yellow bacteria. Here, it slowly crushes specific mineral nodules (perhaps rich in iron or potassium gathered from the pools) into a fine powder with a smooth river cobble it keeps nearby. It then carefully sprinkles this powder onto select bacterial colonies, a form of mineral fertilization.
    *   It "harvests" thin, rubbery sheets of mature bacterial mat not by tearing, but by patiently undercutting the edge with a flake, then sliding a broad, flat chalcedony hand beneath to lift it. Some mats are consumed internally; others might be relocated to barren spots.
    *   It interacts subtly with the arthropods. Occasionally, it remains perfectly still, allowing the obsidian-shelled skitterers to clean chemosynthetic bacteria or mineral accretions from its porous plates. At other times, it uses a blunt limb to gently nudge them away from areas where it is actively "gardening."
    *   It observes the vapor-jellies. When one drifts low, the Tender's sensory nodules pulse in complex, slow patterns. The jelly might shimmer in response before drifting upwards again – a silent, incomprehensible exchange across the thermal currents.
*   **Presence:** The Silica Tender embodies the pocket's dissonant rhythm. It moves with the slowness of geological time, yet its careful manipulations demonstrate an intelligence focused on the immediate, intricate ecology. It is utterly heat-tolerant, seemingly oblivious to the scalding pools and steam jets. It makes no sound beyond the faintest *shhhhk* of stone on stone or the soft *tink* of a flake being placed down. It doesn't react to your presence as a threat, merely as another, temporary thermal anomaly within its realm. Its awareness seems deeply tied to the flows of water, heat, and mineral deposition; it *is* the pocket becoming conscious, a slow mind sculpted by volcanic breath and condensed steam, tending its strange, mineral garden in the perpetual, pressurized silence. Its stewardship is not dominance, but a profound symbiosis – the mountain's sweat given mindful purpose.
