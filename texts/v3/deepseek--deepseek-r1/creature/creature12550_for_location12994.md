The inhabitants of the Heartstone Basin are not creatures of flesh and movement, but **silent, sessile symbiotes** woven into the basin’s fabric. They are the **Stone-Weavers** – colonies of crystalline-mineral organisms emerging from the weeping rock itself.  

### Appearance & Integration  
- **Form:** They manifest as intricate, palm-sized growths on the weeping sandstone. Each begins as a faint fractal spiral (like those on Sky Fern trunks) but swells into a porous, honeycombed lattice of quartz and iron oxides. Their surfaces glisten with a perpetual film of viscous water.  
- **Locations:** Nestled in rock crevices, rooted in shallow pools, or cradled in moss hummocks where mineral-rich seepage concentrates. Some cling to the bases of Sky Ferns, their lattices echoing the fern’s fractal patterns.  

### Life Cycle & Purpose  
1. **Growth:** Over decades, they absorb silica, iron, and trace minerals from the weeping rock. Water pools in their lattice cavities, growing denser and more prismatic – a slow distillation process.  
2. **Symbiosis:**  
   - Their filtered water seeps into moss and fern roots, enriching them.  
   - Sky Fern pollen grains catch in their lattices, dissolving to feed the colony.  
   - In turn, the Stone-Weavers exude a silica-rich gel that strengthens fern trunks and stabilizes eroding stone.  
3. **"Blossoming":** Once per century, during absolute stillness, their lattice pores contract. Pressurized water jets from their cores in brief, misty plumes, releasing mineral "seeds" into the air. These seeds drift on humidity cycles, embedding into new stone faces.  

### Interaction with the Environment  
- **Light:** Their prismatic water cores refract diffuse sunlight, casting fleeting rainbows across moss or mist – the basin’s only "movement."  
- **Sound:** Whisper Lilies hum *only* when a Stone-Weaver’s water distillation reaches equilibrium – a soft, resonant frequency felt in the bones, not heard.  
- **Absence of Decay:** Their gel preserves Sky Fern fronds that fall into moss, turning them into amber-like, humming relics over centuries.  

### Why No Fauna?  
The Stone-Weavers enforce the silence. Their gel, leaching into pools and moss, creates an environment lethal to animal cells but nourishing to plants and minerals. Animal eggs desiccate; spores thrive. This is not hostility, but an ancient, mineral-led evolution – a realm where **life chose stone over blood**.  

### The Unseen Craft  
The "primitive tech" resources are *their* inadvertent gifts:  
- **Silken cords** are shed Sky Fern fibers, hardened by Stone-Weaver gel.  
- **Smooth stones** are exposed Weavers, fossilized after millennia of growth.  
- **Clay patches** are eroded Weaver lattices, mixed with moss.  

To witness the Heartstone Basin is to stand in a cathedral of patience. The Stone-Weavers are its architects, priests, and congregation – a testament to life’s quiet triumph in stillness. They *are* the basin’s heartbeat: slow, warm, and forever weeping.
