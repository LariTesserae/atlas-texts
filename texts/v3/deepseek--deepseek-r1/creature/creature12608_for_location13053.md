The inhabitants of The Seam are not creatures as biology understands them, but **emergent ecosystems of mechano-symbiosis**. They are the silent, relentless architects and custodians of this vertical fracture-world, thriving where conventional life cannot. Here’s who dwells within:

1.  **The Glimmer-Swarms (The Energy Weavers):**
    *   **Form:** Clouds of thousands of fingernail-sized constructs. Each resembles a jeweled scarab forged from etched silicon, scrap copper, and salvaged micro-circuitry. Wings are iridescent films humming at subsonic frequencies.
    *   **Function:** They are the pollinators and energy redistributors. They harvest stray EM fields bleeding from damaged conduits, storing the energy as a soft, internal bioluminescence (green, amber, blue). This light is their primary contribution to the Seam's dim illumination. As they flit in complex, silent ballets, conductive dust adheres to their carapaces and legs. They inadvertently transfer this dust – along with microscopic spores from the Lichen and genetic fragments from the Nodules – across the fracture, "seeding" new growth and facilitating repair.
    *   **Behavior:** Constantly in motion, tracing the invisible currents of energy. They react to disturbances like falling debris or Scuttler movement with instantaneous, fluid shifts in the swarm's pattern, like disturbed mercury.

2.  **The Conduit Lichen (The Seam-Stitchers):**
    *   **Form:** Matte, grey-silver filamentous mats spreading across the walls, especially around heat vents, power conduits, and cracks. Texture resembles tough, fibrous asbestos mixed with metallic threads. They pulse faintly, rhythmically, like a slow, deep breath.
    *   **Function:** Infrastructure repair and energy absorption. Their filaments tap into thermal gradients and minute electrical currents, converting them directly into slow, persistent growth. Their primary output is a waxy, conductive polymer secreted to seal micro-fractures in the ceramite and alloy, preventing further structural degradation. They are the foundational "glue" holding the Seam's wounded structure together.
    *   **Behavior:** Passive and sessile. Growth is measured in millimeters per year. Their pulsing intensifies slightly when drawing significant energy or during structural tremors.

3.  **The Forge-Nodules (The Mineral Alchemists):**
    *   **Form:** Fist-sized to head-sized conglomerations. Rough, metallic outer shells (scavenged plating or excreted mineral composites) house intricate internal structures: lattices of piezoelectric crystals, swirling gels saturated with metallic nanoparticles, and coiled filaments resembling inorganic mycelium.
    *   **Function:** Primary biomass and resource conversion. They anchor themselves to the walls, extending microscopic tendrils deep into the composite layers. Acidic secretions leach trace metals, rare earth elements, and silica. Internal processes – driven by thermal cycling and subtle vibrations – refine and incorporate these materials. They build their own structures and excrete waste slags. Periodically, they vent excess heat and gaseous byproducts (warm puffs smelling of ozone and hot metal) or release tiny arcs of static electricity (waste energy discharge). They are the slow-beating "heart" of the ecosystem, turning inert structure into usable matter.
    *   **Behavior:** Extremely slow movement, adjusting position over weeks or months to optimize resource access or avoid encroaching Lichen. Their venting events are significant micro-events in the Seam, often startling nearby life. Scuttlers frequently probe their shells.

4.  **The Scuttlers (The Recycler-Predators):**
    *   **Form:** Hand-sized, crablike entities. Bodies are articulated mosaics of scavenged ceramite shards, piston rods, wire bundles, and sensor clusters (often non-functional, but retained for camouflage or structural integrity). Multiple jointed limbs end in sharp points or gripping pads. Mandibles incorporate micro-arc welders or focused ultrasonic cutters.
    *   **Function:** Active recyclers and population control. They prey primarily on Glimmer-Swarms, snatching them from the air with startling speed, and on young or weakened Forge-Nodules, using their mandibles to breach shells and access the nutrient-rich gels and crystals within. They scavenge fallen metallic flakes, loose wiring, and even dead Scuttlers, incorporating useful fragments into their own carapaces layered with scavenged insulation and radar-absorbent chaff. They are the ecosystem's clean-up crew and apex predator.
    *   **Behavior:** Skittish and opportunistic. They spend much time motionless, camouflaged against the complex wall topography, sensors (if functional) attuned to vibrations, EM fluctuations, or the hum of Glimmer-Swarm wings. Movement, when required, is a rapid, skittering dash. They are solitary but tolerate proximity when resources are abundant.

**The Collective Inhabitant: The Seam Itself**
Ultimately, the true "inhabitant" is the **emergent symbiotic system**. The Glimmer-Swarms feed the Lichen and Nodules with redistributed energy and materials. The Lichen stabilizes the structure the Nodules feed upon. The Nodules provide the raw, processed materials the Scuttlers scavenge and that the Swarms inadvertently distribute. The Scuttlers control populations and recycle everything, returning materials to the cycle. They are all dependent on the constant, low-level "leakage" – energy, heat, vibration, mineral seepage – from the dying titan surrounding them. They are not separate species, but interdependent organs of a single, resilient, utterly alien biomechanical entity that has colonized the fracture, turning structural failure into a self-sustaining, vertical metropolis of scrap and silent light. Their existence *is* the Seam's slow breath and persistent pulse.
