The inhabitants of the Condensation City are the **Stone-Kin**—a species born of the mountain’s patience and the damp’s insistence. They are not human, though they share a vague bipedal form, shaped by the same forces that smoothed the peaks and furrowed the rock.  

### Physicality  
- **Bodies of Lichen and Stone**: Their skin resembles the dark volcanic rock of the plateau—porous, craquelured, and perpetually damp. It secretes a thin, glistening film that captures condensation, funneling droplets to mouth-like fissures at their wrists and throat. Their limbs are elongated and jointed with a slow, deliberate flexibility, like ferns unfurling.  
- **Eyes of Borrowed Light**: Lidless eyes, large and obsidian-black, dominate their faces. These absorb the dim light, amplifying it with a tapetum lucidum that glows faintly blue (echoing the phosphorescent vines). They see in gradients of shadow and mist, but loud lights pain them.  
- **Silent Movement**: Their feet are broad, padded pads covered in velvety moss-tendrils that muffle sound and grip wet stone. They leave no echo, only damp impressions that fade quickly.  

### Culture & Society  
- **Vertical Clans**: Life stacks upward. Each "clan" occupies a single warren-like spire carved into the mountain, with generations layered like strata—elders deepest in the stone, youth near the mist-shrouded heights. They ascend and descend via capillary staircases (narrow, spiraling channels slick with algae), or along the vines they cultivate.  
- **The Stone’s Whisper**: They believe the mountain’s fatigue is sacred. Elders press ears and palms to rock, "listening" for stress fractures in the city’s foundations or the grind of machinery. Decisions take years; a clan might debate diverting a condensate pipe for a decade before acting.  
- **Water Communion**: All rituals center on water. They distill dew in bell-shaped collectors, offering the purest droplets to carvings of the "First Erosion" (a smoothed boulder in the city’s heart). Children are anointed with black pool-water at birth, binding them to the stone.  

### Survival & Industry  
- **Moss-Farmers**: They cultivate the emerald moss on their own bodies and across rooftops, harvesting it for food, insulation, and to filter water. The phosphorescent vines are woven into living lattices that light work-spaces.  
- **Geothermal Tenders**: The Stone-Kin maintain the city’s grumbling engines. They slither into geothermal vents to clear mineral blockages or lubricate pistons with lichen-paste. Their tools are chisels of volcanic glass and wrenches fused from pipe-scrap, all worn smooth by time.  
- **Symbiosis with Fauna**: Giant centipedes (whom they call *Rust-Scourgers*) are lured with salt-scrapings to clean corroded pipes. Leather-winged gliders nest in high gantries, their droppings fertilizing moss-farms.  

### Psychology & Weirdness  
- **Patience as Pulse**: They perceive time in drips and growth-rings. A Stone-Kin might watch a fern uncurl for days, meditating on its spiral. Anger is foreign; conflicts resolve through glacial silences or the subtle repositioning of shared moss-beds.  
- **Stone-Dreams**: Prolonged contact with certain "smooth-symmetry" rocks induces waking visions—glimpses of the mountains as jagged teeth, or the mist-shapes cohering into half-seen giants. These are recounted in chittering clicks (their only vocalization), felt as vibrations in the bone.  
- **The Great Fatigue**: All Stone-Kin feel the mountain’s age as a weight. To leave the plateau is unthinkable; the valleys below are "the Unremembered," a place of disconnection. They fear not death, but *erasure*—a fading from the stone’s memory.  

### Rules Embodied  
- **"Slow is Fast"**: They repair a leaking pipe by growing moss-seals over years, not welding it shut.  
- **"Silence Speaks"**: A shift in the drip-rhythm warns of rockfall; a pause in machinery hums signals a clan in mourning.  
- **"Light is Borrowed"**: They navigate by the pulse of their own eyes and the vines’ glow. Mirrored shale-fragments line paths to reflect faint light.  

The Stone-Kin *are* the city—its damp breath, its tired bones, its quiet persistence. They endure because they have become as patient, as integrated, and as strange as the Enduring Peaks themselves.  

---
**Final Note**: Visitors might mistake them for statues, until one turns its light-drinking eyes toward them, and the mist peels back just enough to reveal a city breathing in the gloom.
