Here, in the high, silent foundry of the peaks, life is not an invasion, but an *expression* of the nascent rock itself. It is mineralogy made animate, geology metabolizing. There are no creatures that crawl, fly, or call. The residents are the flora, but they defy terrestrial classification – they are **litho-chemotrophs**, beings of stone and chemical fire.

1.  **The Resonant Spires (Obsidian Bamboo / Industrial Piping):** These are the dominant structures, the pillars of this silent cathedral-factory. Thirty feet or more, they rise in segmented columns of glassy obsidian fused with the dull sheen of iron-rich basalt. Their surfaces are unnervingly smooth yet striated, like extruded metal. Deep within their cores, slow, tectonic processes occur – perhaps the catalytic breakdown of deep-mantle gases seeping through fractures, or the conversion of geothermal heat into complex hydrocarbons. This internal industry manifests as the deep, subsonic hum felt through the rock and boot soles, a bass note of planetary metabolism. Growth is measured in inches per century; new segments form at the apex as minerals precipitate from internal fluids, crystallizing layer upon layer. They anchor the ecosystem, their roots fracturing bedrock to access deeper energies and minerals.

2.  **The Pulse-Fronds (Rubbery, Iridescent Blue-Black):** Nestled in crevices and radiating from the bases of the Spires, these thick, rubbery growths are like living heat sinks or coolant radiators. Their deep blue-black surface shimmers with oily iridescence, hinting at complex metallic alloys within the organic matrix. Thick, vein-like channels pulse rhythmically beneath the surface, carrying a viscous, opalescent fluid – not sap, but a supercritical mineral slurry. This fluid likely regulates internal temperature, transports dissolved metals for structural reinforcement, and facilitates the slow exchange of chemicals with the rock and the Spires. Touching one feels disturbingly warm, like sun-baked engine casing. They unfurl agonizingly slowly, seeking optimal angles for radiant heat dissipation or perhaps absorbing specific wavelengths of light for photochemical reactions unrelated to water-splitting.

3.  **The Static Carpet (Crystalline Moss):** Covering swathes of ground between Spires and spilling over rock faces, this isn't moss at all, but a dense mat of interlocking micro-crystals. Each tiny facet refracts light into dazzling, prismatic shards. The crystals are primarily silicates and metallic oxides, grown through bio-mineralization processes. Their primary function seems to be energy capture and transduction. They absorb ambient heat and light with incredible efficiency, crackling with visible static discharges when disturbed – a sign of piezoelectric or triboelectric energy generation. This captured energy likely fuels the slow chemical reactions within the mat itself (perhaps fixing atmospheric nitrogen or carbon in mineral forms) and might be conducted through the Copper-Vine network or the rock itself to power the larger flora. It feels like walking on crushed glass that hums with latent electricity.

4.  **The Copper-Vines (Oxidized Wire Vines & Blood-Leaves):** Thick as anacondas and resembling braided, heavily oxidized copper cabling, these vines are the circulatory and structural network. They snake across bare rock, anchor themselves with root-like tendrils that chemically fuse with the stone, and climb the Spires. They are astonishingly strong and flexible. At intervals, they sprout colossal, leathery leaves the color of rusted iron or dried blood. These leaves are not for photosynthesis as we know it. Their dark color suggests absorption of infrared radiation (heat). Their leathery texture hints at containment. They likely act as condensers or chemical reactors, using temperature differentials across the plateau to catalyze reactions within the viscous fluids flowing through the vine's core, perhaps synthesizing complex organometallic compounds essential for the system's growth and maintenance. They are the slow-beating arteries and workbenches of the biome.

**How They Live (The Silent Industry):**

*   **Feeding:** They are chemolithoautotrophs, deriving energy from inorganic chemical reactions (geothermal heat, oxidation of reduced minerals like iron or sulfur from the rock) and building biomass from carbon dioxide and mineral nutrients dissolved or chelated from the bedrock. Water is irrelevant; hydrogen or oxygen comes from mineral decomposition.
*   **Growth:** Excruciatingly slow. Minerals are dissolved by acidic secretions at root/vine interfaces, transported, and re-deposited as structural elements or catalysts. New growth occurs through directed crystallization, not cell division as in Earth-life.
*   **Reproduction:** Likely through fragmentation or specialized spore-like structures rich in mineral catalysts and genetic templates (encoded in crystalline lattices, not DNA). These "seeds" might be carried by rare rockfalls or static discharges, lying dormant for millennia until landing on suitable mineral substrate with the right energy flux.
*   **Interaction:** A silent, chemical symbiosis. The Static Carpet generates electrical potential. The Copper-Vines distribute fluids and perhaps this energy. The Pulse-Fronds regulate temperature and fluid chemistry. The Resonant Spires provide structure and access to deep-earth resources. They function as a single, vast, slow-motion superorganism – the Foundry itself, breathing its acrid, metallic breath and humming its deep, subsonic song of mineral transformation. They are not *on* the plateau; they *are* the plateau becoming conscious, in a purely chemical, industrial sense. They are the architects and the architecture, paused forever in the act of forging their own world.

They live utterly alone, utterly silent, in their eternal, sun-baked, mineral feast, building mountains one agonizingly slow molecule at a time. They are the Foundry Peaks, made flesh-stone.
