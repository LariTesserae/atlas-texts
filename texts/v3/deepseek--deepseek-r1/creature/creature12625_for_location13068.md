The folk of Siltgrip are not called townsfolk, villagers, or even islanders. They are **Siltkin**. They are the dampness given human form, shaped by the clutch and inseparable from its sucking mud and weeping shale.

1.  **Physique & Demeanor:**
    *   **Compact & Resilient:** Generations of navigating treacherous boardwalks, hauling against the mud, and huddling for warmth have made them compactly built. Limbs are thick and strong, tendons like woven cord. They possess a low center of gravity, moving with a deliberate, rolling gait that minimizes slips on the slick surfaces. They seem perpetually damp, skin pale and cool to the touch, often carrying a faint patina of blue-grey clay dust.
    *   **Eyes of the Estuary:** Their eyes are their most striking feature – large, often a pale, washed-out blue, grey, or green, adapted to pierce the perpetual mist and low light. They possess a watchful stillness, constantly scanning the shifting water margins, the boardwalks, the sky for subtle changes. Expressions are often reserved, etched with a quiet, resilient melancholy that mirrors their environment. Smiles are rare, brief, and often involve shared hardship overcome.
    *   **The Weave of Water:** Their hands are broad, fingers thick and strong, often with slightly webbed digits – not enough for swimming, but perfect for gripping slick shale, molding clay daub, or hauling nets heavy with mud and eels. Callouses are thick, especially on palms and knuckles.

2.  **Adaptations to the Wet:**
    *   **The Clay Coat:** Many Siltkin habitually rub a thin layer of the blue-grey estuary clay onto exposed skin. It acts as a primitive barrier against the biting damp chill, protects against scrapes from sharp shale, and may deter the tiny, persistent biting flies that emerge in less-cool seasons. It gives them a ghostly, earthen appearance.
    *   **Water-Resistant Ways:** Their movements are economical, designed to minimize splash and disturbance. They can stand perfectly still for long periods, listening to the wet quiet. They develop an almost preternatural sense for the subtle *gloop* of unstable mud or the groan of a settling piling.
    *   **Internal Fire:** With external fire so precious and hard to maintain, Siltkin seem to cultivate a slow-burning internal warmth. They tolerate the pervasive chill better than outsiders, radiating surprising heat when huddled together. This makes them seem slightly feverish to visitors.

3.  **Apparel & Adornment:**
    *   **Functional Layers:** Clothing is about managing dampness and retaining precious warmth. Base layers are of finely woven, dense reed fiber or treated fish skin, surprisingly water-repellent. Over this, they wear thicker, coarser tunics and leggings of layered, oiled reed bundles or tough, salt-cured leather from estuary creatures. Everything is patched, re-woven, and endlessly repaired.
    *   **Practical Footwear:** Feet are clad in complex, multi-layered "mud-socks" – tightly woven reed soles lashed over thick felted reed insoles, bound to the calf with leather or sinew thongs. They offer grip and prevent the deep, dangerous suck of the mud.
    *   **Utility Adornment:** Adornment is practical. Bone or sharpened shale needles secure clothing. Small woven reed pouches hang from belts, holding essential tools or precious dry tinder. Hair is tightly braided, knotted, or bound under simple reed caps to keep it out of the face and relatively dry. Jewelry is rare; a carved eel bone, a particularly smooth water-polished shale pebble worn on a thong.

4.  **Skills & Daily Grind:**
    *   **Masters of Mending:** Life revolves around constant maintenance against the water's encroachment. They are expert thatchers, weavers, daubers, and piling-setters. Nets are constantly repaired, tools resharpened, boardwalks re-secured.
    *   **Estuary Harvesters:** They know the subtle signs of the mudflats and channels. Using simple bone hooks, woven traps, and sharpened shale knives, they harvest eels, mudskippers, translucent crabs, samphire, and the tough salt grasses. Boating in their tarred coracles is second nature, navigating the narrow, shifting channels silently.
    *   **Water Wisdom:** They read the tides in the seep of water through cracks in the shale, the scent of the air, the behaviour of the mudskippers. They know which pools hold drinkable water (filtered through layers of reeds and clay) and which are stagnant death. Elders are walking libraries of water lore and construction techniques.

5.  **Social Fabric:**
    *   **The Huddle:** Density breeds intense community. Privacy is non-existent; lives are shared across thin reed walls. Disputes over inches of boardwalk space or drips from a neighbor's roof are common, but so is profound interdependence. They share warmth, food, and labor instinctively against the shared enemy of the damp and isolation. Gossip is a vital survival tool, spreading news and warnings faster than the mist.
    *   **Elders of the Silt:** Respect is earned through proven resilience and deep knowledge of the clutch and the estuary. The eldest, often bearing scars from shale or mud, advise on piling depth, thatch repair timing, and the interpretation of water signs. Their word carries significant weight.
    *   **Quiet Ritual:** Their "festivals" are quiet affairs: the successful re-thatching of a critical roof, the communal hauling of a massive piling, the rare days when the mist lifts enough to see the distant sea. Songs are low, rhythmic hums that mimic the lap of water or the drip from the eaves. Storytelling revolves around legendary feats of construction or cautionary tales of those lost to the mud or the sharp shale.

6.  **Spirit & Outlook:**
    *   **Resigned Resilience:** They possess a deep, quiet fortitude. They don't fight the water; they exist within its rules. Their resilience isn't heroic, but dogged and practical. There's a profound acceptance of their marginal existence, a knowledge that Siltgrip is temporary, yet an equally fierce determination to maintain it for this generation, and the next.
    *   **The Mud's Embrace:** They have a complex relationship with the mud. It is the enemy that sucks at their feet and rots their pilings, but it is also the foundation, the source of the clay that protects them, the home of the eels that feed them. They understand its depths and treacheries intimately. To be "deep in the mud" is a state of both danger and profound connection.
    *   **The Sharpness Within:** Like the young shale, there's a hard, unyielding edge beneath their damp exterior. They are suspicious of outsiders, protective of their meager resources and hard-won space. They endure, but their endurance has honed them, making them pragmatic to the point of ruthlessness when survival demands it.

**In essence, the Siltkin *are* Siltgrip.** They are the human expression of its dampness, its density, its quiet struggle, and its sharp, provisional beauty. They breathe the estuary, taste the mud, and move with the slow, persistent purpose of water seeping through shale. Their lives are a testament to existence on the very edge, perpetually damp, perpetually mending, perpetually clutching to their fragile, muddy home. Their names often reflect this: Clayfoot, Shalehand, Reedweaver, Dripping, Eelcatcher, Mistwalker. They are the children of the clutch.
