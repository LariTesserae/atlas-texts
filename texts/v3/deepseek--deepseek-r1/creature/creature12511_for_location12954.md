The inhabitants of the Stillness Grotto are not separate from its ecology—they are its *expression*. Time’s suspension has sculpted them into eternal custodians of equilibrium, their forms and functions woven into the grotto’s humming bones. Here is what lives within the velvet-dark:

### The Resonant Weavers (Intelligent Inhabitants)
- **Physiology:** Tall, slender figures of layered chalk-mineral and obsidian, resembling the grotto’s own formations. Their bodies are striated with the same spiralling patterns etched into the bulbous growths, glowing faintly with gold-green phosphorescence. Joints are seamless, movements fluid yet infinitesimally slow, conserving the stillness. They lack mouths or eyes; nourishment seeps through mineral pores from the air, while perception is vibrational—felt through filament-fine crystalline hairs covering their limbs.  
- **Purpose:** They maintain the grotto’s resonance. With hands like fused basalt shards, they adjust the stone markers—not as art, but as *tuning forks*. Each cairn or slab anchors a harmonic node in the grotto’s hum. When metallic clicks fall out of sync or floaters drift too far, a Weaver glides to realign the stones, their touch emitting subsonic pulses that correct the frequency.  
- **Society:** There are perhaps three. They move in glacial, non-repeating paths across the pebble floor, yet their routes intersect at precise intervals (every 47 years, were time linear). The vine-woven mat and fruit-bowl in the alcove are theirs—tools to harvest glowing amber fruits. The pulp is not eaten but smeared on rocks to boost phosphorescence, sustaining the light-life cycle. They are patient, indifferent to outsiders. To witness one is to feel the hum *deepen*, as if the air itself bows to their presence.  

### The Eternal Cohort (Fauna)
1. **Click-Beetles (*Scarabaeus immotus*):**  
   - Iridescent carapaces (deep blues, volcanic reds) split light into prismatic shards. No wings; legs end in glassy hooks for gripping smooth stone. Their clicks are not communication but *cleansing*—each strike shatters microscopic mineral deposits on basalt walls, releasing nutrients into the air. They exist in fixed colonies: 812 beetles, no more, no less. When "damaged," they retreat into purple moss, emerging hours later restored.  

2. **Lumenslugs (*Gastropoda profundus*):**  
   - Translucent bodies pulse with internal light—swirling gold and iron-oxide clouds. They glide along etched spirals on the chalk formations, absorbing excess phosphorescence through their bellies. Trails left behind are concentrated mineral dust, instantly consumed by moss. Their paths are immutable: a slug ascending the western wall today traced the same route when the basalt first cooled.  

3. **Harmony Floaters (*Suspensus aeternus*):**  
   - Gelatinous amber orbs tethered to vine clusters. Each contains a lattice of mineral fibers that vibrate with the grotto’s hum, amplifying it. They drift in fixed formations, like constellations. If displaced, the hum frays into dissonance until a Resonant Weaver restores them. Dissection would reveal no organs—only resonance given form.  

### The Unseen Cycle
Life here does not propagate—it *persists*. Moss regrows identically where beetles scuff it. Fruits detach from vines when perfectly ripe, are harvested by Weavers, and reappear days later, unchanged. Death is alien; a "dead" beetle is merely inert until the hum revives it. Predation, decay, and growth violate the stillness. Even the Weavers are not born—they emerged with the grotto, as much a part of its geology as the basalt.  

**Why They Matter:**  
They are the grotto’s immune system. An intruder’s breath, heartbeat, or footfall introduces chaotic vibrations. Immediately, click-beetles swarm the disturbance, their clicks dampening foreign sound. Lumenslugs divert to absorb heat from footprints. Floaters contract their filaments, tightening the hum’s frequency. If disruption persists, the Weavers converge, placing hands on the intruder. Not to harm—but to *still*. Those touched report feeling time thin, their pulse slowing toward eternity… before fleeing, haunted by the echo of a world that has no need for them.  

In the Stillness Grotto, to live is to resonate. To be silent. To endure, unmoved, in the warm, humming dark.
