The inhabitants of the Weeping Engine Halls are not creatures of flesh, but of *adaptation*. They are the silent, skittering, and luminous echoes of the Engine's will, carved by cold, moisture, and vibration. Forget vibrant ecosystems; here, life is a tenacious patina, a whisper against the industrial roar.

1.  **The Corrosion Eaters (Lichen & Biofilm Colonies):**
    *   **Form:** Vast, thin, grey-green sheets and fibrous mats clinging to ferrocrete seams, weeping pipe joints, and the undersides of dripping gantries. They appear inert, but closer inspection reveals slow, amoebic pulses within their depths.
    *   **Life:** They feed directly on mineral-laden condensation, metallic oxides (rust), and microscopic flakes of paint or lubricant suspended in the perpetual dampness. Their metabolism is glacial, fueled by the constant, slow drip. They secrete weak acids to break down surfaces, accelerating decay where they cluster thickest – a biological counterpart to the water's relentless work.
    *   **Interaction:** Utterly passive unless disturbed. Touching a thick patch risks a mild acidic burn or a slick, disintegrating surface giving way. They are the hall's slow, inevitable digestion made manifest.

2.  **The Phosphor Slimes (Luminous Fungi):**
    *   **Form:** Gelatinous, fist-sized blobs or sprawling, irregular patches adhering to cold metal beams, valve housings, or mineral-crusted drain lips. They pulse with a faint, cold blue or sickly green bioluminescence, brightest where mineral runoff (copper sulfates, zinc oxides) is richest.
    *   **Life:** They metabolize toxic heavy metals and chemical byproducts leaching from the machinery and seeping rock. Their glow is a byproduct of this detoxification process, intensifying with nutrient influx. They reproduce by budding or releasing luminous spores in steamy exhalations when a nearby vent erupts.
    *   **Interaction:** Their light is a precious, unreliable beacon in the gloom. Disturbance causes them to retract luminescence momentarily, plunging the immediate area into deeper darkness. Some larger, older colonies might exude a paralytic slime if prodded aggressively.

3.  **The Resonant Skulkers (Pallid Cave Orthoptera):**
    *   **Form:** Six-legged, fist-sized arthropods with elongated, translucent bodies and spindly limbs. Their exoskeletons are near-opalescent, revealing shadowy internal organs. They lack eyes entirely; large, dish-like tympanic membranes dominate their heads.
    *   **Life:** Masters of vibration. They navigate the treacherous gantries and pipe bundles by reading the thrum, clangs, and drips like a seismic map. They feed on the Corrosion Eaters, Phosphor Slime spores, and the rare, blind microfauna (like translucent mites) that graze on the biofilms. They are incredibly fast and silent when moving, freezing instantly when vibrations shift unexpectedly.
    *   **Interaction:** Utterly harmless to larger beings but startlingly fast. They flee *towards* predictable machinery sounds (like a steady clang) and *away* from anomalies (a footstep, a sudden screech). Crushing one releases a sharp, ozone-like smell.

4.  **The Pipe Leeches (Vibrio-Thermica Sanguisuga):**
    *   **Form:** Thin, eel-like creatures, rarely longer than an arm, with smooth, metallic-grey skin. They lack distinct heads, instead having a circular, lamprey-like mouth lined with rasping, crystalline teeth designed to abrade metal.
    *   **Life:** They dwell in the ankle-deep, oily water of the service trenches and the condensation pools within larger pipes. They are thermovores, drawn to the faint, residual warmth bleeding from active power conduits, steam lines, or recently active machinery. They latch onto warm metal surfaces with their mouths, slowly scraping microscopic particles and absorbing the minute heat differentials through specialized cells.
    *   **Interaction:** Not aggressive, but their bite on bare skin feels like coarse sandpaper grating flesh. They are drawn to active tools, power cells, or even a warm hand placed on a pipe for too long. Removing one requires prying or applying sudden cold.

5.  **The Echo Stalkers (Cataracta Sonusvora) - RARE:**
    *   **Form:** Larger, more sinister versions of the Resonant Skulkers, reaching the size of a large dog. Their bodies are segmented, armored in overlapping plates of chitin fused with corroded metallic flakes, giving them a jagged, mottled appearance. Multiple sets of tympanic membranes line their flanks and limbs.
    *   **Life:** Apex predators of the Halls. They hunt the Resonant Skulkers and Pipe Leeches using hyper-acute vibration sense. They move in terrifying bursts of speed between periods of absolute stillness, triangulating prey through the complex soundscape. They are ambush predators, often clinging to high, shadowed beams or lurking within deep service trenches, waiting for a telltale skitter or the warmth signature of prey near a pipe.
    *   **Interaction:** Dangerously aggressive if they perceive vibrations as prey or threat. They attack with powerful, serrated forelimbs and a bone-chilling, ultrasonic shriek designed to disorient and pinpoint targets through echo location. Their armored hide deflects glancing blows, but they are vulnerable in the joints and rely heavily on surprise.

**The Rules They Live By:**

*   **Water is Life:** All depend on the constant moisture – for sustenance, movement (leeches), or the vibrations it carries (skulkers, stalkers).
*   **Cold is Home:** Their metabolisms are adapted to the deep chill. Heat is a lure (Pipe Leeches) or a deadly anomaly that disrupts their vibration map (Skulkers, Stalkers).
*   **Sound is Everything:** Navigation, hunting, feeding, and evasion are dictated by the Hall's symphony. Silence is suspicious; anomalous sound is terror.
*   **Sharp Edges are Shelter/Weapon:** Crevices, jagged metal, and broken concrete provide hiding spots. The Echo Stalkers *are* sharp edges.
*   **The Engine's Rhythm is Law:** Activity peaks and ebbs with the thrum. Skulkers forage more boldly when the thrum is steady; Stalkers hunt when load changes create confusing sonic cover. Disrupting the Engine disrupts *all* life catastrophically.
*   **Light is Alien:** Bioluminescence (Slimes) is functional, not navigational. Most fauna shun even the sickly work-lights, operating perfectly in the near-total dark. Sudden light is often perceived as a violent intrusion.

These are not animals as known on the surface. They are biomechanical ephemera, born of oil, condensation, vibration, and cold metal. They are the weeping halls given fleeting, hungry form. To encounter them is to step into the Engine's shadowy, dripping bloodstream.
