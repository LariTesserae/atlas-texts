## Fauna of the Saltwater Pumping Station Gamma-7

Life in Gamma-7 is less a thriving ecosystem and more a series of desperate, specialized footholds in an environment actively hostile to biology. The inhabitants are rare, spectral, and profoundly adapted to the crushing pressure, corrosive brine, deafening noise, and mineral-heavy darkness. They are less distinct organisms and more like ephemeral manifestations of the Pocket's enduring rage and decay. Here is what endures:

1.  **Gamma-7 Ghast Eels (*Abyssoconger gammaensis*):** These are the translucent, eel-like drifters mentioned. They are not true eels, but highly degenerate, pressure-adapted chordates. Up to a meter long, they are little more than pulsing, semi-transparent digestive tracts sheathed in a thin, mucus-coated dermis resistant to the brine's sting. Vestigial, fin-like appendages provide minimal propulsion; they primarily drift in the warmer, mineral-rich currents expelled by the massive pump housing or near micro-thermal vents. They possess no eyes, only rudimentary light-sensing cells utterly useless in the gloom. Instead, they navigate via an exquisite sensitivity to the station's vibrations – the thumping pistons, the grinding impellers – using modified lateral lines that map the chamber's acoustic landscape. They graze on the thin, chemosynthetic microbial mats that cling to the warmest metal surfaces near the pump core, absorbing nutrients directly through their skin and gut lining. They are silent, ghostly threads in the murk, vanishing into shadow or turbulence instantly. Their transparency is a chilling adaptation; glimpses of their internal workings – the slow pulse of a primitive heart, the dark mass of ingested microbes – are fleeting and unsettling.

2.  **Rust Biter Isopods (*Crustacorrodens obsidianus*):** Larger and more robust than their shallow-water cousins, these pale, eyeless isopods (reaching 15-20 cm) are armored scavengers. Their exoskeletons are thick, calcium-carbonate reinforced plates, heavily mineralized with accreted sulfides and manganese, giving them a lumpy, almost geological appearance that blends with the crusted walls and grates. They scuttle with surprising speed across surfaces, their powerful legs tipped with hardened, chitinous points that find purchase on crumbling concrete and corroded metal. They feed primarily on the thick microbial mats, but are opportunistic detritivores and micro-carrion feeders. They use specialized mouthparts to scrape and bore into the mineral accretions and decaying biofilms, extracting trapped organic particles and chemosynthetic bacteria. Their primary defense is their near-invisibility against the mineral tapestry and their ability to wedge themselves deep into cracks and crevices when the station's tremors intensify or water surges threaten to sweep them away. They communicate (if it can be called that) through substrate vibrations sensed by sensitive hairs on their legs.

3.  **Concretion Lurkers (*Incrustatia sessilis*):** At first glance, these are easily mistaken for bizarre mineral formations – knobby, fist-sized growths of layered sulfides and carbonates coating pipes, valve housings, or the undersides of grates. They are, in fact, extremely slow-moving, sessile filter-feeders. A rudimentary molluscan body lies hidden beneath the mineral armor it secretes constantly as protection from the brine. Periodically, when nutrient-rich water swirls past (often triggered by a valve spasm or a surge from the pump), a delicate, frilled feeding appendage, resembling a tiny, ghostly sea anemone, extrudes from a central aperture. It waves weakly in the current, capturing suspended microbes and organic detritus before retracting swiftly at the slightest disturbance. Their growth is glacial, adding mere millimeters per century. They are living stalagmites, embodying the station's slow mineral conquest of all things.

4.  **Pressure Spinners (*Vortispira tenebris*):** The smallest and perhaps weirdest inhabitants. These are not animals in a conventional sense, but complex colonial protists. They form whirling, vortex-like structures about the size of a human thumb, composed of thousands of individual flagellated cells bound in a shared, tough silicate membrane forged from dissolved minerals in the brine. They spin rapidly in the turbulent eddies near water inflows, outflow grates, or steam vents, creating localized micro-currents that concentrate microbial prey, which they absorb. When turbulence becomes too violent, the colony momentarily fragments into individual cells, only to reform when conditions stabilize. They emit a faint, eerie bioluminescent pulse – a deep, bruised violet – detectable only in absolute darkness and likely a form of communication or coordination within the colony. This light is utterly drowned out by the failing phosphor strips under normal conditions, making them virtually invisible unless witnessed by accident in a moment of total blackout.

**Ecological Niche & Behavior:**
*   **Extreme Specialization:** Every organism here is a hyper-specialist. They exploit minute energy sources: geothermal heat driving chemosynthesis, microbial mats, sparse organic detritus, or concentrated particles in turbulent zones. None are apex predators; the environment itself is the primary predator.
*   **Vibration Dependence:** Sight is useless. Sound and vibration are everything. All inhabitants are exquisitely attuned to the station's rhythmic and chaotic vibrations for navigation, feeding cues, and danger avoidance. The Ghast Eels are the most sophisticated in this regard.
*   **Ephemeral & Elusive:** Encounters are brief and startling. Fauna appear as pale flashes against mineral darkness, translucent shapes coiling in a sickly light-pool, or simply vanish back into the indistinguishable mineral crust when approached (or when the light flickers). They are more like environmental phenomena than creatures.
*   **Mineral Integration:** Life here is merging with the station's decay. The Rust Biters wear mineral armor, the Concretion Lurkers *are* mineral growths, the Pressure Spinners build their bodies from dissolved station components. They are becoming part of the geological process reclaiming the machinery.
*   **Survival Amidst Chaos:** They thrive *because* of the turbulence, not despite it. The surges bring nutrients, the vibrations provide sensory maps, the warm currents offer micro-habitats. Their life cycles are synchronized with the station's violent rhythms.

**The Verdict:** The fauna of Gamma-7 are not conquerors, but desperate tenants clinging to a sinking, shrieking ruin. They are mineral-encrusted, vibration-sensitive, pressure-forged ghosts. More than mere animals, they are biological expressions of the Deep Furnace Pocket itself – rare, resilient, deeply strange, and slowly becoming one with the drowned, grinding heart of a forgotten machine. They are the final, flickering sparks of life in a place actively trying to erase all traces of it, embodying the uncanny paradox of industrial relic and primal abyss. They are truly the **ghosts in the machine**.
