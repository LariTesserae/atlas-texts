Here's who and what lives in the Mossbound Valveworks, woven into the fabric of its damp, quiet, moss-governed existence:

1.  **The Mosskeepers (Symbiotic Isopod Colonies):** The most numerous residents are colonies of large, blind, slate-grey isopods dwelling deep within the Siltmoss mat. Adapted to perpetual cool dampness, they are not mere scavengers but integral custodians. They gently churn and aerate the deepest moss layers, preventing stagnation and distributing the bioluminescent algae spores that give the shadows their twilight glow. Their constant, subtle movement through the moss-veins is felt as a faint vibration underfoot, a low hum beneath the dripping water. They "read" the contours of buried structures through sensitive antennae, instinctively avoiding areas where the moss thins over voids or sharp metal, reinforcing the stability. They are the unseen hands maintaining the Balance.

2.  **The Whisper Moths (*Lichenala murmurans*):** Delicate, slow-flying moths with wings the precise color and pattern of aged, water-stained brick and lichen-encrusted iron. They sip moisture directly from the perpetually sweating surfaces of structures and the dew-laden Frostwort Ferns. Their flight is silent, save for the faintest papery flutter heard only in utter stillness. They are drawn to the faint ozone scent near the patinated metal and the bioluminescent glow at dusk, their presence a fleeting dance of camouflage. They lay their eggs in the densest, coolest moss clumps near foundations, where the larvae feed sparingly on specific algae, never overgrazing.

3.  **The Pipe-Skulker Amphibian (*Scutiger subterraneus*):** A rare, reclusive amphibian, resembling a stout, damp-grey salamander with broad, sticky toe pads and vestigial eyes covered by thick skin. It lives primarily within the network of smaller, inactive pipes and conduits choked with cool moss, emerging only during the deepest, most saturated mist at dawn or dusk. It feeds on the blind isopods it finds in the pipe-moss and tiny, moisture-loving springtails. Its call is a low, resonant *thrum* that perfectly mimics and harmonizes with the deep vibration of the pressurized water moving in the main pipes below, making it almost impossible to distinguish from the station's own pulse. It moves with glacial slowness, adhering perfectly to Rule #4: Quiet is Strength.

4.  **The Dewcap Sentinels (Semi-Sentient Vegetation):** The **Dewcap Cypresses** and **Whisker Vines** are more than mere plants; they are active participants shaped by the rules. The Cypresses, clustered near sumps, aren't just surviving; their intricate root systems actively channel and filter water, creating micro-currents that feed the Siltmoss and stabilize the mineral content in the puddles. They subtly lean *towards* persistent dampness, not away, acting as moisture gauges. The **Whisker Vines** exhibit a slow, purposeful growth. They seek out cracks in mortar, coil tightly around loose rivets on girders, and form dense lattices over areas of fragile brickwork. They aren't destroying; they are binding, acting as organic reinforcement straps, embodying Rule #2: Water Moves, Life Adapts, and Rule #3: Old Metal is Alive. They react to significant vibration (like heavy, clumsy footsteps) with a barely perceptible tightening.

5.  **The Pump Guardian (Residual Mechanic Spirit):** While not biological, the lone semi-functional auxiliary pump in its shed is an active inhabitant. Its rhythmic *chug… hiss…* is as much a part of the ecosystem's soundscape as the dripping water. The Whisker Vines embrace its housing, not smothering it, but seemingly regulating the moss encroachment around its base. The Siltmoss grows thickly on its inactive sides but remains thin and patchy near its heat vents and moving parts. It operates on a cycle dictated by its own decaying logic and the subtle pressure changes within the moss-bound system, a drowsy, persistent spirit of the station's past purpose, tolerated and integrated by the green present.

6.  **The Glow-Weavers (Bioluminescent Algal Symbiotes):** Thriving within the deep, cool, stable layers of the Siltmoss, colonies of specialized algae pulse with a faint, ethereal blue-green light at twilight. This isn't random; the glow intensifies subtly around areas where the Mosskeepers are most active (indicating healthy aeration) and near the deepest sumps where minerals concentrate. They are the subtle "weirdness," the living record of micro-environmental health within the Moss, making the darkness not empty, but softly inscribed with the biome's vital signs.

**How They Live Together:**
Life here is defined by slowness, symbiosis, and profound integration with the non-living environment. Energy flows through the constant, slow growth of the Siltmoss, powered by filtered light and condensation, not predation. The isopods tend the moss, the moss feeds the algae and supports the vines/cypresses, the plants stabilize the structures and moisture, the structures provide niches and condensation for the moss and moths, the pump's cycle provides minor heat shifts, and the deep water thrum sets the tempo. The rare amphibian and moths are ephemeral highlights, not drivers. Disturbance is minimal and swiftly healed by the moss. Sound is rhythmic and low-frequency. Movement is deliberate. The residents are less distinct species occupying a space, and more like specialized organs within a single, moss-bound, brick-and-iron organism – a perfectly adapted community thriving within the cool, damp, quiet equilibrium of the Gutter Hollow Valveworks. They *are* the balance.
