Here, in this dripping stone cradle, lives **The Lichen Shepherd**.

You glimpse it first as stillness—a texture on the wet cliff face that shouldn't be. It clings vertically, high where the mist gathers thickest, nearly indistinguishable from the dark, weeping rock and the long, silvery-green beards of *Usnea* lichen. Then it moves, and the landscape seems to adjust around it.

**Form:**
It is roughly humanoid, but built entirely of the basin's slow essence. Its "skin" isn't skin at all, but a thick, resilient matrix of interwoven mosses and lichens, primarily the deep emerald sphagnum and the long, hair-like *Usnea*, perpetually slick with condensation. This living hide absorbs the constant moisture, glistening softly. Beneath this verdant layer, a core structure suggests dense, waterlogged wood and porous, volcanic stone – not bones and flesh, but the basin's own architecture. Its limbs are long, attenuated, ending in broad, flat "hands" and "feet" that are less digits and more like complex pads of densely packed moss and fibrous roots. These pads adhere to the wet rock with impossible silence and security, distributing its slight weight perfectly across the fragile moss hummocks below.

**Movement:**
It moves with glacial patience. There is no stride, only a *repositioning*. It flows upwards on the weeping rock face like water given form, its moss-pads gripping the slick surface effortlessly. Across the basin floor, it doesn't walk *on* the sphagnum so much as *through* it, the moss seeming to part and flow around its passage before settling seamlessly back into place, leaving no trail. Its pace matches the dripping water – a slow, inevitable progression measured in hours or days for a distance a human might cross in minutes. When still (which is most of the time), it becomes a sculpture of moss and lichen, indistinguishable from the cliff or a particularly dense hummock.

**Purpose:**
The Lichen Shepherd *tends*. This is the source of the basin's cultivated feel. With infinite slowness, it might:
*   **Guide the Water:** Gently brushing a moss-pad along a mineral runnel on the cliff face, subtly altering its course over decades, ensuring water feeds a particular patch of luminous fungi or a stunted tree's thirsty roots.
*   **Shape the Moss:** Using its broad feet, it presses and smooths the sphagnum, deepening pools where dark, unseen life thrives, or building up hummocks to create drier islands for specific, fleshy-leaved plants.
*   **Prune the Silence:** With delicate, almost imperceptible touches of its fibrous fingers, it might remove a dead frond from an ancient fern, or gently dislodge a fallen branch threatening to dam a slow seep. It encourages the sculptural clustering of life.
*   **Harvest Light:** At the thickest mists, when the faint luminescence of plants and fungi intensifies, the Shepherd might stand amidst them. The glowing patterns seem to shift subtly around it, absorbed or reflected by its mossy hide – perhaps a form of sustenance, communication, or simply attunement.

**Interaction:**
It is aware, but its awareness operates on the basin's timescale. A human observer might stand for hours unnoticed, perceived as just another transient, slow-moving element like mist or shadow. Direct confrontation or sudden movement would be alien, jarring. It might react by simply… ceasing to be visible, merging utterly with the cliff face until the disturbance passes, perhaps centuries later.

Its presence explains the elusiveness of other life. The iridescent beetle clings near a luminous fungus the Shepherd nurtured. The silent bird glides through air currents the Shepherd has subtly shaped over millennia. The blind salamanders ripple in pools the Shepherd deepened. They are all part of its slow, patient garden.

**Essence:**
The Lichen Shepherd *is* the basin's deep time made manifest. It is the patient intelligence behind the "attended" feeling, the gardener who works not with tools, but with gravity, water, moss, and centuries. It breathes with the slow exhalation of the warm, damp air, its heartbeat the *plink… plop…* of water on moss. It embodies the profound balance, the utter lack of haste, the quiet, saturated permanence of this high, wet stone room open to the sky. To witness it is to understand that this place is not merely inhabited; it is *curated*, by an entity woven from water, moss, lichen, and time itself.
