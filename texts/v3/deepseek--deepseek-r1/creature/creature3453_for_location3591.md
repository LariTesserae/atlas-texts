Here, life isn't measured in heartbeats, but in the accretion of crystals and the slow seep of moisture. The sole, true inhabitants are the **lichen-stone symbiotes**, entities of profound stillness and mineral patience. They are not individuals, but vast, ancient colonies – a slow-motion tapestry woven into the very bones of the shrine.

1.  **The Stone Hosts:** They inhabit the dense, glassy nodules peppering the basalt floor – the "mineral barnacles." Each nodule is not merely a rock, but a porous matrix, a fortress and a larder combined. Over millennia, wind and water etched pathways within them, creating intricate, microscopic labyrinths.

2.  **The Fungal Weavers:** Within these labyrinthine pores dwell extremophile fungi, so adapted to the cold, thin air, and intense UV light that they are unrecognizable as kin to surface molds. They are architects of crystalline lattices. Drawing infinitesimal traces of silica, iron, manganese, and other minerals dissolved in the perpetual condensation, they secrete intricate, branching networks – veins of pure crystal. These networks are their bodies, their nervous systems, and their roots.

3.  **The Manifestation – Mineral Photosynthesis:** This is their visible signature, seen only when the blinding alpine light strikes a nodule at the perfect angle and the observer is mere inches away. Deep within the black glass, faint, thread-like veins pulse with a subdued inner light: **violet** (manganese), **emerald green** (copper or chromium), **burnt umber** (iron oxides). This is not bioluminescence, but *mineral photosynthesis*. The crystal lattices, structured by the fungi, capture specific wavelengths of the intense sunlight. This energy drives electrochemical reactions within the damp micro-environments of the pores, splitting the condensed water vapor and mineral solutes, providing the energy the fungi need to grow their crystalline structures further and extract more nutrients. It's a cold, slow fire burning within the stone.

4.  **Their Life Cycle:** It is measured in centuries. Growth is the addition of a single, microscopic layer of crystal to a vein, perhaps once a decade. Reproduction is equally glacial. When a nodule becomes too crowded or stressed (perhaps by a rare, deep freeze fracturing it internally), a fragment carrying viable fungal spores and mineral seeds might break off. If it lands in a suitable micro-depression with reliable condensation, a new colony *might* begin, a process taking decades just to establish the first faint network. Death is not decay, but fossilization; the crystal lattice remains, inert but beautiful, a fossilized record of the symbiote's existence.

5.  **Their Interaction with the Shrine:** They *are* the shrine's ecology. They are the reason the condensation altars are maintained – the symbiotes depend utterly on that slow, reliable moisture. The wind-breaks and polished platforms are irrelevant to them; they exist on a scale and timeframe far removed from transient human concerns. The wind's sigh across their glassy surfaces carries away dust that might block their light. The intense cold slows their metabolism to a sustainable crawl. The relentless sun is their engine. The deep shadows are their respite. They are the patient, colorful pulse within the rock, the silent beneficiaries of the ceaseless drip and the eternal wind, living embodiments of the shrine's cold, mineral vitality. They do not perceive pilgrims; they perceive shifts in light, temperature gradients across their nodules, and the electrochemical potential of the moisture seeping into their pores. Their world is one of crystalline structure, mineral solutes, and the slow, eternal dance of condensation and light.

They are not fauna. They are geology *become* biology, a testament to life's ability to inhabit the most elemental and unforgiving spaces, transforming patience and stone into a slow, cold fire.
