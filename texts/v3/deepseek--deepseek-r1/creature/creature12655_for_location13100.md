The inhabitants of this luminous stone womb are not creatures of blood and bone, but entities of cold light, relentless growth, and mineral tension. They thrive in the thin, electric air and the ceaseless, damp roar.

1.  **The Breath-Moss (Luxumbra spirans):** This is the dominant life, the smothering, luminous carpet. It clings with microscopic ferocity to *every* surface – the jagged basalt walls, the slick floor, the stumps of nascent stalagmites. Its glow isn't passive; it *pulses* rhythmically, a slow, cold throb of blue-green light synchronized not to any sun, but seemingly to the deep, resonant drones of the wind vibrating through the mountain's fresh fractures. It feeds on the perpetual condensation, the scant minerals leached by the seeping rivulets, and perhaps even the faint electrical charge in the ozone-laden air. Its presence is aggressive, leaving no bare rock, creating the oppressive, jewel-box claustrophobia. It is the cavern's luminous skin and its suffocating blanket.

2.  **The Silvervein Ferns (Ferrumfrondis lucidus):** Rising like sentinels from cracks and crevices where deeper moisture gathers, these ferns are monuments of rubbery resilience. Their broad, dark leaves are rigid against the buffeting wind-tremors, yet their veins blaze with a constant, cooler silver luminescence – a stark contrast to the moss's pulse. They seem to draw sustenance not just from water and air, but from the metallic veins snaking through the rock beneath them. Their unfurling fronds, gleaming faintly, look like the slow articulation of stiff, mechanical limbs frozen in the act of reaching.

3.  **The Weeping Caps (Lacrymofungus viscosus):** Clustering in bulbous mounds on damper ledges and around the bases of the ferns, these translucent mushrooms are the cavern's glands. Their caps exude a thick, viscous sap that glows with a dim, sullen yellow light where it pools. This sap constantly drips, adding its erratic *plink* to the water's percussion. When a drop lands precisely on the thin, metallic mineral sheets formed by evaporating, mineral-rich water, it produces the uncanny *clang* or *ping*, a sound jarringly industrial against the organic backdrop. They seem to be processing the mountain's raw mineral seepage into this glowing, sticky effluent.

4.  **The Resonant Veins & Fractures:** While not alive in the organic sense, the **quartz and metallic ore veins** are active participants in the environment. They are the cavern's exposed nerves and unfinished machinery. They catch and harshly reflect the bioluminescence, not with warmth, but with a cold, steely glare. Their geometric intrusions – pistons, pipes, gears – are accidents of mineral deposition and fracturing in the young, stressed rock, yet they *feel* intentional, straining towards function. The wind finds their edges, whistling over them with a sharper, more metallic keen than through mere rock fissures. The dripping water interacts with them, creating sounds that reinforce the illusion of a dormant, mineral engine. They are the mountain's industrial skeleton, laid bare and resonant.

5.  **The Wind & The Drip:** These are the active forces, the constant inhabitants. The **Wind** is a violent, invisible entity outside, but within the cavern, it becomes a chorus of voices – shrieking through high fissures, moaning down narrow chimneys into the black void above, rattling loose shale like chains, and setting the giant ferns trembling in a silent, glowing dance. It carries the thin air, the ozone sting, and the mountain's own groans. The **Drip** is the counterpoint, the liquid heartbeat. It’s the condensation beading on cold stone and cool fungus, the slow seep from fractures, falling endlessly into unseen pools. Its rhythm is irregular, persistent, and intimately tied to the metallic percussion it creates. Together, Wind and Drip compose the chaotic, loud symphony that fills the luminous space – the breath and the pulse of this living stone chamber.

**The Nature of the Inhabitation:**
Life here is **vegetal**, **mineral**, and **elemental**. It’s a realm of aggressive photosynthesis fueled by cold light and thin air, of fungi processing damp stone, and of geological formations that mimic industry. The inhabitants are locked in a dynamic tension: the lush, smothering flora versus the sharp, young, resisting rock; the chaotic energy of wind and water versus the slow, rhythmic pulse of the light. There are no grazers, no predators, no symbionts beyond the moss clinging to rock and fern. It’s an ecosystem of solitary, overwhelming presence and relentless, abiotic force. They exist in a state of potent, alien fertility – a glowing, dripping, roaring engine of life and geology running in the cold, dark, high-altitude heart of a mountain still shuddering into being. The only movement is growth, drip, tremor, and the endless sigh of the wind through stone. It is life stripped back to its most luminous, persistent, and strangely mechanical essentials within a prison of raw, adolescent earth.
