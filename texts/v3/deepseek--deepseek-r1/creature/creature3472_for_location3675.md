The true inhabitants of the Sump Chamber aren't the desperate humans clinging to Fissure Hold's riveted iron shacks. They are merely tolerated squatters, scratching at the fringes. The **rulers** are the teeming, churning, relentless **fauna**, evolved for millennia in this wet, hot, mineral womb:

1.  **The Slick-Lizards:** Masters of the glistening expanse. Their iridescent scales shed water like liquid mercury, allowing them to skitter across wet rock and metal faster than a human can blink. Tongues flick, tasting the air not just for prey (roaches, mites, unwary silverfish), but for faint gradients of heat and humidity – their compass in this steaming world. They rule the dripping stalactites and runoff trails, swarming wherever condensation beads thickest. Their eyes, lidless and obsidian black, see perfectly in the dim light, missing nothing.

2.  **The Cave Roach Legions:** The relentless engine of decomposition. Fist-sized, their chitinous bodies clack like pebbles falling as they move in endless, seething carpets. Drawn like magnets to the generator's heat, leaking pipe warmth, and the organic waste of Fissure Hold, they are the chamber's recyclers. Their rasping mandibles devour anything softer than rock: mold, fungi, shed lizard skin, spilled lubricant, corpses, even scraped-off biofilms. They avoid open water but thrive in the humid air inches above it, turning the dry*er* stretches between human structures into treacherous, skittering plains. Their sheer numbers are a suffocating pressure.

3.  **The Blind Silverfish:** Lords of the shallow, steaming pools. Eyeless, with bodies like shards of tarnished silver, they navigate the warm, algae-rich waters solely through vibration and chemoreception. Their gaping mouths constantly filter the cloudy broth stirred by the eternal drips, feasting on Mineral Mites, algae filaments, and decaying matter. They dart and shimmer in the weak electric light, a fluid, metallic counterpoint to the roaches' crunching terrestrial march. Anything slow enough to be caught below the waterline is consumed.

4.  **The Hanging Bats (Glidermaws):** Silent sovereigns of the shadowed heights. Not true bats, their leathery wings are adapted for short, silent glides rather than powered flight. Clinging upside-down in the higher, darker crevices where the electric light struggles to reach, they possess heat-pit sensory organs beneath their furred muzzles. With near-silent drops, they plummet to snatch unwary Slick-Lizards or surface-roaming Cave Roaches. Their soft, high-pitched squeaks weave through the chamber's symphony – locating, claiming, coordinating in the gloom humans fear to tread.

5.  **The Mineral Mites:** The unseen emperors, the foundation. Billions of near-microscopic, white crustaceans swarm in the warmest puddles, especially near geothermal vents or generator runoff. They transform stagnant water into milky soup, feeding directly on dissolved minerals and simple bacteria. They *are* the base. Everything larger feeds on them directly (Silverfish) or feeds on what feeds on them (Roaches consume the algae they sustain; Silverfish attract Glidermaws). Their explosive blooms follow warmth; their absence creates lifeless, cold voids.

**The Symbiosis of Dominance:**

*   **Moisture & Heat:** All are exquisitely adapted. Slick-Lizard scales repel water while utilizing it for traction. Roaches dehydrate rapidly without humid heat. Silverfish require the warm broth. Glidermaws sense heat signatures. Mites thrive only in warm mineral soup.
*   **The Constant Cycle:** It's a slow-motion maelstrom of consumption. Mites feed on minerals. Silverfish feed on mites and algae. Lizards snatch mites near water edges and hunt surface prey. Roaches devour detritus, corpses, and weakened creatures. Glidermaws prey on Lizards and Roaches. When any die, Roaches and microbial activity recycle them swiftly. Nothing escapes this loop for long.
*   **Shaping the Territory:** Roaches define safe passage by *inhabiting* the danger zones between lights. Lizards guard the vital water trails. Silverfish control the life within the pools. Glidermaws patrol the high shadows outside the human light-islands. The mites dictate where primary production is possible. Humans move through *their* realms, not the other way around.
*   **The Pace:** Despite the frantic-seeming skitters and darts, their existence embodies the geological rhythm. Reproduction is constant, massive, and fast. Death is constant, efficient, and recycled. Population booms and busts follow slow shifts in heat vents or water seepage, not days or weeks. Their lives are measured in drips, crusted mineral layers, and the hum of the generators they exploit.

Fissure Hold is a brief spark compared to the ancient, wet fire of this fauna. The lizards shimmer like spilled oil on the rocks, the roaches form living carpets, the silverfish churn water into froth, the glidermaws whisper in the dark, and the mites paint the pools white. They are the Sump Chamber – wet, hot, hungry, and utterly, overwhelmingly *at home*. Humans are the interlopers sweating against the walls, tolerated scavengers clinging to the fringes of an ecosystem that barely notices them and would reclaim their iron sheets within weeks if they faltered. The true sound of the Sump isn't the generator hum or the dripping – it's the low, pervasive *crunch-scrape-sizzle* of countless chitinous mouths consuming the world, endlessly, under the weeping rock.
