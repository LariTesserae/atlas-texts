## The People of the Basin: The Stone-Weavers

They are called **Kethra** in their own soft-click tongue, meaning "Those Who Listen to the Stone's Tears." To an outsider, they seem like ghosts of the mist, pale figures moving with glacial patience through the pearly gloom. They are not tall, their stature compact and low-centered for stability on the perpetually damp, uneven rock. Generations living in the cool, diffuse light have bleached them: skin the color of weathered limestone, ranging from near-translucent grey-white in the elders to a slightly warmer, faintly bluish-grey in the young. Their hair, fine and perpetually damp, hangs straight and lank, often woven with strands of dark, water-slick moss or delicate grey fern fronds – not decoration alone, but camouflage and a subtle collector of condensation.

**Physique & Adaptation:**
*   **Skin:** Smooth, cool, and perpetually glistening with a fine sheen of condensed mist. It possesses a slight, natural waxiness, repelling excess water while allowing the vital moisture to be absorbed slowly. Fine traceries of faint blue veins are often visible beneath the pale surface, mirroring the water channels in the stone.
*   **Eyes:** Large, wide-set, and the most striking feature. Irises are the deep, clear grey of a still pool reflecting a cloudy sky, or sometimes a pale, startling blue like glacial ice. Pupils are large to gather the low light. They blink slowly, lids heavy with collected moisture. Their gaze is unnervingly patient and observant, missing little in the soft-focus landscape.
*   **Movement:** Deliberate, silent, and fluid. Every step is placed with conscious care, avoiding loose scree, respecting established paths, and minimizing sound. They seem to glide rather than walk, their bare feet (tough-soled and sensitive) reading the subtle vibrations and dampness of the rock. Their posture is slightly stooped, not from burden, but from an instinct to move beneath overhangs and through low cave entrances.
*   **Hands:** Long-fingered and dexterous, with broad, strong palms. Fingertips are slightly thickened and incredibly sensitive, capable of detecting the faintest seep of water through stone or the minute vibration of a distant droplet. Nails are short and hard, like chisel points.

**Culture & Society:**
*   **The Silence:** Speech is rare, a soft murmur of clicks, hums, and breathy vowels that carry only as far as necessary. Loud voices are jarring, even offensive. Most communication is through touch (a hand on an arm, pressure on a shoulder), subtle shifts in posture, and intricate hand-signs evolved over millennia. Their sign language is rich and complex, describing water flows, rock formations, time passing, and emotional states with nuance.
*   **Water Wisdom:** They are hydrological engineers of profound subtlety. Every Kethra child learns the hidden arteries of the basin – the fracture lines, the seep points, the convergence zones. They maintain the ancient, unseen channels within the rock and the surface runnels with reverent care. Cisterns are sacred spaces, tended by specific lineages. Water is never wasted; it is sipped, collected on skin and moss, channeled to the hardiest terrace plants (pale tubers, grey-leafed herbs), and its sound is considered sacred music.
*   **Stone Partnership:** They do not conquer the rock; they converse with it. Building is an act of revelation, not imposition. They follow the natural fractures, exploit existing hollows, and shape only where the stone allows. Their tools are simple: hard stone hammers, bone chisels, and abrasives made from finer grit. Structures emerge *from* the landscape, not upon it. Their artistry lies in finding the form the water and time have already suggested.
*   **Pace of Being:** Life moves with the rhythm of dripping water. Decisions are made slowly, after deep observation and silent contemplation. Tasks – tending moss gardens, repairing a runnel, carving a new storage niche – are performed with meditative focus, taking the time required by the stone and the moisture. Their concept of time is geological, measured in the deepening of a channel or the spread of a moss patch.
*   **The Mist-Veil:** The mist is their protector and their shroud. It shields them from the harsher sun and winds beyond the rim. They navigate by its subtle shifts in density and temperature, reading it like a map. It diffuses their presence, making their settlements nearly invisible until you are upon them. They believe it carries the breath of the mountain and the whispers of ancestors.

**Dwellings & Crafts:**
*   **Homes:** Carved into the living rock. Entrances are low, arched apertures disguised by natural folds or hanging veils of moss. Inside, chambers are cool, dim, and filled with the soft sounds of dripping water channeled into small, central cisterns. Walls are left rough-hewn, showing the stone's natural texture. Furniture is minimal: raised stone sleeping platforms covered in dried moss, niches for tools, woven reed mats.
*   **Craft:** They excel in working with what the basin provides. They weave tough, water-resistant fibers from alpine plants into mats, bags, and clothing (simple tunics and leggings, often damp). They carve intricate channels and catchment basins into stone. Their most valued craft is the creation of "whisper-jars" – porous ceramic vessels that capture condensation slowly and release it drop by pure drop into collecting basins below, a practical and spiritual art form.

**Presence:**
To encounter a Kethra is to encounter the Basin itself. They are patient, observant, and saturated with the quiet, damp essence of their world. They move through the weeping stone like water given form, their large, grey eyes reflecting the mist and the ancient stillness. They are not hostile, merely profoundly *elsewhere*, existing on a timescale and within a sensory reality utterly shaped by the slow weep of water on eternal stone. They are the listeners, the tenders, the quiet children of the mountain's damp breath.
