The Rootcellar Market has no true "inhabitants" in the animal sense. Fauna is functionally absent. Instead, the space *is* its inhabitants – a slow, unified organism composed of interdependent botanical and fungal entities. They are not merely present; they *constitute* the environment. Here’s what "lives" there:

1.  **The Sumpfelt Moss:** This is the living floor, the breathing foundation. It’s not a passive carpet; it’s a complex, waterlogged mat of specialized bryophytes teeming with symbiotic cyanobacteria and algae. It *feeds* on the constant seepage and decay, releasing warm, earthy vapors like slow exhalations. It yields underfoot, not just because it’s wet, but because it *is* a semi-aquatic entity, buoyant and alive. Its purpose: absorption, filtration, and the foundational support of the entire system.

2.  **The Ancient Taproots (The Bronze Pillars):** These colossal, tarnished-bronze structures are the bones of the place. Some are relics of surface trees long dead, their decaying wood providing sustenance for fungi. Others are living roots from distant, unseen giants or species uniquely adapted to the mineral-rich seepage. They are not passive supports; they *actively* grow, thicken imperceptibly over centuries, strain against the earth and encased metal (causing the low groans), and absorb nutrients directly from the city’s dissolved bones. Their surfaces are ecosystems unto themselves – crusted with mineral accretions, draped in epiphytic mosses, and hosting colonies of Glowwart.

3.  **Glowwart Fungi:** These are the dim, greenish lanterns. Clustered in knotholes, cracks, and crevices within the taproots and latticework, they emit a constant, sickly bioluminescence fueled by slow metabolic processes. They don't just illuminate; their light likely influences micro-ecologies on the root surfaces and aids in spore dispersal within the mist. They are living nodes of faint light in the perpetual twilight.

4.  **Strangler’s Silk Vines:** These are the sinews and binding ligaments. Thick as arms, rubbery and persistent, they snake relentlessly through the space. They fuse root to rusted beam, beam to weeping pipe, pipe to the yielding Sumpfelt. They don't strangle to kill; they bind to integrate. They weave the disparate elements – organic and industrial – into a single, cohesive, suffocatingly dense structure. Their growth is slow but inexorable, adding layer upon layer to the living lattice.

5.  **The Decomposer Fungi:** A silent, diverse multitude. Shelf fungi patiently digest the last cellulose from fossilized wooden crates. Slime molds ooze across rusted surfaces, dissolving iron oxide into bioavailable compounds absorbed by roots and bacteria. Ubiquitous mycelial networks thread through the Sumpfelt, around the roots, into the crumbling concrete – the unseen, unifying nervous system and digestive tract of the Rootcellar. They bind the physical mass and facilitate the slow, eternal recycling of matter. They are the unseen processors, turning ruin into sustenance.

6.  **Symbiotic Microflora:** The uncountable trillions. Cyanobacteria within the Sumpfelt fixing nitrogen. Algae contributing to the mat's metabolism and oxygen exchange (minimal as it is). Chemosynthetic bacteria clustered around the rare, corroded electrical cables, briefly flaring to life with each weak spark, feeding on the electrochemical energy before lapsing back into dormancy. They are the essential, microscopic workforce driving the chemical engine of this world.

**How They "Live":**
*   **Collective Breath:** The Rootcellar doesn't have inhabitants; it *is* an inhabitant. The Sumpfelt exhales warm vapor, the taproots groan as they strain, the mycelium pulses with slow nutrient flow. Its "breath" is the humid mist.
*   **Slow Metabolism:** Growth is measured in geological time. Nutrients cycle over decades. Metal rusts, concrete crumbles, roots thicken – all at a pace invisible to any transient observer. Energy comes from geothermal warmth, chemical reactions (decomposition, chemosynthesis), and the faint, ancient light of the Glowwarts.
*   **Purposeful Integration:** Every element serves the whole. The roots provide structure and mineral absorption. The moss provides the foundation and primary processing. The vines bind. The fungi decompose and recycle. The microflora handle specialized chemistry. The industrial past is merely raw material, patiently being disassembled and incorporated.
*   **Static Dynamism:** It feels eternal and unchanging, yet it *is* constantly transforming. It’s a state of dynamic equilibrium achieved through agonizingly slow, relentless reclamation. Nothing new arrives; nothing truly leaves. It simply *becomes* more itself, more integrated, more saturated with its own wet, warm, vegetative essence.

The Rootcellar Market is inhabited by itself. It is a single, vast, slow-breathing entity of root, moss, vine, fungus, and microbe – a patient, damp eternity digesting the bones of a forgotten city.
