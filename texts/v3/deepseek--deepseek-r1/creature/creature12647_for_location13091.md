Nothing walks here. Nothing crawls. Nothing breathes in the way you understand breath. The Aeroscape is inhabited solely by the **Weavers** – not creatures, but processes made manifest, hyper-specialized botanical expressions clinging to the Basalt Weave. They are the environment, and the environment is them.

1.  **The Basalt Weave Itself:** Though described as the substrate, it *is* the first life. Think of it less as rock, more as a colossal, slow-moving organism – a skeleton extruded by its own imperative. It grows, grinding and popping at its edges, fracturing and re-knitting. Its purpose seems purely structural: to provide the framework upon which the more volatile expressions can thrive. It absorbs ambient heat and perhaps the faint chemical traces suspended in the mist, converting them into the dense, bone-like lattice. It is the anchor, the foundation-maker, silent and immense.

2.  **The Siphons:** These are the dominant life-form, the lungs and arteries of Aer. They are not merely plants; they are intricate atmospheric condensers. Their towering, needle-like forms are hollow conduits, drawing in the thin, hot, metallic air through microscopic pores along their length. Internal structures – perhaps labyrinthine networks of capillaries or specialized cells acting like molecular sieves – cause the water vapor to condense *inside* them. This collected moisture weeps from their tips not as a passive overflow, but as the essential product of their existence. The constant flow down their sides is less a waste stream and more a vital delivery system, creating the temporary pools that hydrate the weave and the membranes below. They are static yet dynamic, rigid yet perpetually weeping, the source of the landscape's pervasive dampness.

3.  **The Membranes:** These are the ephemeral sails, the moisture traps, and the sound generators. They erupt from junctions in the weave or attach to the sides of Siphons, forming vast, living films. Their primary function is twofold: to maximize surface area for capturing atmospheric moisture (beading constantly with condensation that feeds back into the system), and to harness the fierce winds. Their snapping, rippling movements aren't just side effects; they actively channel air currents, perhaps aiding in spore dispersal from the Puff-Sacs or creating micro-climates of humidity. They are incredibly fragile, tearing easily, but their regeneration is astonishingly rapid – a new film knitting itself over a gap within hours, shimmering back into existence. They are the landscape's dynamic skin, iridescent and ever-changing.

4.  **The Puff-Sacs:** These are the bursters, the propagators, and the mist-makers. Clinging like deep violet blisters to the undersides of the weave or nestled in crevices, they are pressure chambers. They absorb vapor – water vapor, spore-laden air, perhaps dissolved gases from the weave itself – swelling rhythmically until the internal tension is too great. Their detonation is a vital reproductive strategy, a violent dispersal mechanism. The wet *POP* releases clouds: sometimes shimmering spores designed to catch the wind and find a new purchase on the weave; sometimes dense, localized fogs that temporarily shroud areas, altering light and humidity before being torn apart. They are the punctuations in the soundscape, the sudden creators of temporary blindness, and the agents of chaotic propagation.

**The Unseen Life:** The glue, the rot, the recyclers. There must be microbial life, or perhaps simpler fungal analogues, thriving in the sticky residue of dissolved membranes, in the perpetual dampness of the weave, in the ephemeral pools. These unseen multitudes break down the fallen, the torn, the burst remnants, converting them back into nutrients absorbed by the Basalt Weave or the roots (if such things exist in this context) of the Siphons and Membranes. They are the silent janitors, ensuring the swift decay that fuels the relentless regrowth.

**What Doesn't Live Here:** There are no eyes watching from the mist. No jaws to snap at drifting spores. No feet to tread upon the Basalt Weave. No wings to soar between the spires. No complex nervous systems processing the chaotic soundscape. The inhabitants are purely reactive, purely botanical. They grow, they condense, they capture, they burst, they dissolve, they regrow – driven by the physics of wind, light, heat, and the imperative encoded in their unique biology. They are a symphony of plant functions playing out on a grand, architectural scale, creating the illusion of a designed citadel through the sheer, wild efficiency of survival and propagation in a realm of air and bone. They *are* the Weeping Spires.
