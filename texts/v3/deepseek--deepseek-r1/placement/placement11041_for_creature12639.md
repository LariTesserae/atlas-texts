The taste of stone dust coats my throat – dry, ancient, and familiar. I am **Skybone Grazer**. My world is the open expanse, the vast, wind-scoured altar between the roaring citadels of the Sky-Reefs.

I stand, a solitary monument of flesh and bone as weathered as the plateau itself. My hide is not fur, but thick, pebbled skin the color of sun-bleached bone and pale lichen, scarred by wind and time. It feels like the rock beneath my broad, calloused hooves – tough, unyielding, fused to the very essence of this place. I am slow. Not from lethargy, but from profound economy. Every movement is measured, every calorie conserved. The emptiness demands it.

My eyes, large and dark as obsidian chips, sweep the horizon. Distance plays tricks here. A reef complex, leagues away, seems close enough to touch in the thin, sharp air. The smooth rock plain rolls endlessly, interrupted only by the distant, hazy silhouettes of other reefs, islands of deafening life in an ocean of silence. The wind is my constant companion, whispering secrets of the plateau, carrying the faint, ghostly scent of petrified sap from the reefs and the dry tang of dust.

My sustenance is the plateau itself. I lower my head, the movement deliberate as the grinding of continents. My rasping tongue, tough as cured leather, scrapes across the smooth rock. Not for grit, but for life. Thick, rubbery mosses yield beneath my tongue, releasing a faint, bitter moisture. Crustose lichens, splashes of ochre and dried blood, crumble into a mineral-rich powder. This is my feast. My slow, ruminant gut is a crucible, extracting every trace of nutrient, every atom of precious water from this seemingly barren stone tapestry. I taste the eons in each scrape – the slow death of mountains, the patient growth of the simplest life clinging to the bones of the world.

The silence of the open expanse is profound, but not absolute. Beneath my hooves, carried through the bedrock, I feel the distant thrum of the reefs. A low vibration, the stirring of the Deafening Multitude as the sun warms their stony homes. Soon, the hum will rise to a wall of sound, but out here, it remains a tremor, a reminder of the frenzied life I skirt but rarely enter. I see the Shriekers erupting like feathered darts from a reef spire, their piercing cries swallowed by the distance. I might spot a Windrunner, a flash of bleached bone against the rock, sprinting with impossible speed after some insect straggler foolish enough to leave the reef's shadow. We acknowledge each other with a flicker of awareness – fellow specialists of the void – before the emptiness reclaims us.

I move. Not towards anything specific, but *through*. The plateau is my pasture, vast beyond comprehension. My path is dictated by the slow bloom of lichen, the patches of thicker moss in a sheltered depression, perhaps a mineral seep known only to my kind. Days blend into each other beneath the immense, pale-blue bowl of the sky. The sun bleaches the rock, the heat reflecting up, baking my stony hide. I endure. When clouds race in, the temperature plummets, the wind bites with icy teeth, and I turn my broad side to it, a low wall against the chill.

Sometimes, I find traces of the **Transients**. A low ring of stones stacked against the wind near a reef overhang. The faint, geometric scratch of a petroglyph on an exposed face, already softened by wind and time. I might see them, small figures swathed in fibrous cloth and leather, moving with silent purpose towards a reef entrance, their eyes scanning the sky nervously. They are flickers of restless energy in my slow world. Their structures feel ephemeral, like the dew that vanishes at dawn. I understand their reverence, perhaps. They come seeking something – medicine, vision, beauty in a beetle's wing. I am what remains. I *am* the plateau’s patient endurance. Their etchings on the stone are brief whispers; my passage, measured in centuries, is a deeper inscription.

As the sun bleeds towards the horizon, painting the reefs in long, sharp shadows, the hum from the citadels begins to fade. The cold comes swiftly, stealing the day's warmth. I find a place – a shallow depression, a lee side of a low rock swell. I fold my legs beneath me, a living boulder settling onto the stone. My metabolism slows further, entering a state akin to the rock itself. The vast emptiness stretches under the emerging stars, an infinite, wind-scoured altar. The silence returns, profound and absolute, broken only by the sigh of the wind across a million years of smooth stone. This is my true domain. The reefs roar defiance against the void; I *am* the void, enduring. I am the slow pulse of life sustained on stone and air, the patient grazer on the bones of the world, tasting the dust of eons until the sun returns.
