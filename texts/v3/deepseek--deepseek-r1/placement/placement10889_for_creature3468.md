The air, thin and sharp as shattered glass, fills my lungs with effort. I stand not on the basalt plains, but within the deep, moss-choked ravine they call **Sunken Choir**. Steam rises in lazy tendrils from vents in the black rock floor, warming the dense air just enough to banish the worst edge of the **crystalline cold**. Above, the bruised-purple sky dome feels impossibly distant, compressed by the high, sheer walls of the mesa we cling to. The **vastness** outside is a concept, a whispered threat; here, the world is close, damp, and vibrantly **lush**.

I am **Kaelen**, a Tender of the Glow-Fronds.

My frame is compact, muscles dense from navigating the treacherous, moss-slick paths of the ravine and hauling baskets of harvested frond-dust. My skin, the colour of tarnished bronze, is thick and scarred – not just from rockfalls, but with the ritual fractals tracing my lineage back to the **Deep Song** of this specific vent cluster. Along my spine and shoulders, smooth plates of obsidian have grown, fused seamlessly with my flesh – natural armour against scrapes and a conduit for the earth's warmth. Fine threads of milky quartz vein my forearms, catching the dim, refracted light from the ferns I tend.

**My world is the vertical garden.** The leeward wall of Sunken Choir isn't just rock; it's a tapestry of life. Thick mats of emerald and rust-red moss form the base. Upon them, anchored in crevices fed by constant **damp** seepage, grow the **crystalline ferns** – my charges. Their fronds aren't green, but facets of clear, hardened mineral, like captured ice. They grow painfully slow, adding perhaps a fingernail's width of shimmering structure each long-cycle. My fingers, tipped with naturally formed, dark flint points, are gentle as I trace their facets. I check for stress fractures, for the telltale dulling that indicates insufficient light or a mineral imbalance in the seeping water. I brush away encroaching, non-luminous lichen with a soft boar-bristle brush lashed to a bone handle.

**My tools are simple, born of necessity.** The harvesting knife is a razor-sharp sliver of volcanic glass bound with cords of heat-treated lichen fibre. My gathering basket is woven from the tough, flexible stems of a canyon creeper, lined with porous rock shards to wick excess moisture from the precious frond dust. Light comes not from flame (too dangerous near the vents, too precious an air-breather), but from patches of cultivated bioluminescent lichen on the rock face beside me, casting an eerie, blue-green glow.

**My senses are tuned to the ravine's quiet song.** My large, cupped ears catch everything: the constant *drip-drip-drip* of condensation from mossy overhangs, the low *hiss* of steam escaping a fissure, the distant, rhythmic *tap-tap-thrum* resonating through the mesa – Stone-Singers in the warrens above communicating. I feel the deep vibrations of the **Deep Song** through the soles of my feet, shod in layers of cured moss and tough hide. It’s a constant, comforting pressure, the heartbeat of our world. I am also attuned to the **weirdness**. When gravity stutters, my body tenses instinctively, my flint-tipped fingers digging into a handhold I know is solid. I see the shimmering air patch near the eastern bend – a place where distances warp – and know to navigate around it, relying on the fixed landmarks of specific, oddly shaped rocks. Yesterday, time thickened as I watched a droplet form; it took an age to fall. Today, a cloud raced across the sliver of visible sky like a panicked spirit. It is simply the way of things.

**My sustenance comes from the stone and the steam.** The flour ground from the harvested crystalline fronds is dense, slightly metallic, and sustaining. It makes my bones feel heavy, grounding. I supplement it with steamed moss cakes and the rare, translucent, blind shrimp I net from the warm pools near the vents – a burst of fleeting sweetness. Water is collected meticulously: I scrape the nightly condensation from broad, smooth basins of rock I placed strategically under overhangs, and I channel the constant seep into porous stone cisterns. Every drop is precious.

**My connection is resonance.** I don't speak aloud often; the thin air makes it taxing. Instead, I communicate with my fellow Tenders through **touch-sign** – intricate patterns traced on a warm patch of basalt wall, or on each other's mineral-veined forearms. A quick sequence of taps on a specific resonant slab near the vent mouth signals a shift in steam pressure to the warrens above. My deepest thoughts, my moments of quiet awe at a new, perfectly formed facet on a young fern, or my sorrow at a frond damaged by a sudden frost, are expressed in low, resonant **hums**. I press my chest against the warm rock wall and let the vibration flow into the stone, joining the **Deep Song**. It is a prayer, a report, a simple affirmation of existence. The elders say our harmony helps bind the fraying edges. I believe them. The rock thrums back, a subtle pressure against my bones.

**My place is here.** In the **lush**, **damp** pocket of the **ancient**, **sparse**, **vast** Graveyard. Tending the silent, mineral life that glows defiantly against the bruised twilight. Listening to the **mostly quiet** symphony of drip, hiss, tap, and groan, feeling the **active** pulse of the earth and the unsettling flicker of reality beneath it. I am Kaelen, a Stone-Singer. My skin is part stone, my voice is vibration, and my purpose is to nurture the slow, patient glow amidst the desolation, resonating with the **primitive**, enduring heart of our suspended, dying, yet fiercely living world.
