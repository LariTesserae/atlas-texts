The air tastes of wet stone and secrets. My claws—black obsidian chisels honed on weeping basalt—scrabble for purchase as I emerge onto a sun-warmed slab. Silver fur parts the mist, each strand beaded with cold light. I am Stone-Singer. The mesa thrums beneath my belly.

**Home** is the warren: a labyrinth chewed deep into peat-sodden earth beneath the Sentinel Boulder. Its sides weep constant tears, feeding the emerald moss that carpets our threshold. The scent lives in my nostrils—damp rot, crushed blue-berries cached in dry crevices, the iron-tang of lichen-scrapings. At dawn, we emerge. The colony fans out across the polished stone, silver backs catching the weak sun like scattered coins.  

**The Work** is written in teeth-marks. I scrape moss from rock faces with methodical precision. *Scritch-scratch-scritch.* My incisors pare away layers, leaving gleaming trails on volcanic skin. The mesa allows this. We do not take more than regrows between mist-tides. When the Wind-Dancers pipe overhead, we freeze. Not from fear—from calculation. Their shadows ripple across the stone; we count them, measure their circles. One less today? A thrumming silenced in the night? The balance shifts. We adjust.  

**The Voices** are everywhere.  
- The *chatter-chatter-snap* of my kin: *"Hawk-shape east!"* *"Berry-thicket ripe!"*  
- The low drone of Deep-Thrummers vibrating up through the rock—a warning of approaching fog.  
- The wind’s true whispers: sighs through fissures that taste of cloud-sea salt, groans as it presses against the Cairn’s ancient stones.  
- And the **weeping rocks**. They sing of deep time. When I press my ear to their damp faces, I feel the mesa’s slow heartbeat—the seep of water through its bones, the grind of continents still settling.  

**The Rituals** are etched in erosion.  
At twilight, we gather on the Offering Stone—a flat expanse scarred by generations of claws. We gnaw blue-berries, letting juice stain the rock like old blood. The bioluminescent mosses pulse around us. We do not know why we do this. The mesa remembers. Sometimes the mist thickens into serpent-shapes above the cliff-edge. We watch. We thump our hind feet in unison. The vibration travels. The mist shivers.  

**The Rules** are absolute:  
1.  **Touch the edge-clouds, fall forever.** The void hums below. Only fools chase berries that roll too close.  
2.  **Moss sustains, but peat drowns.** Dig too deep into the soft places, and the earth swallows you sighing.  
3.  **The stones listen.** Confide in them. They hold secrets tighter than any warren.  
4.  **When the almond-scent blooms, silence.** Something walks then. Not predator. Not kin. Something that tastes the wind with fingers of fog. We vanish. We wait.  

Tonight, the mesa holds its breath. The wind drops. The thrumming stops. Only the drip-drip-drip of the Sentinel’s tears breaks the stillness. I crouch on polished stone, silver fur bristling. The mist parts.  

A shape coalesces near the fallen Cairn: tall, thin, woven from moonlight and vapor. It has no eyes, yet I feel its gaze scrape across my pelt. The almond scent thickens, cloying and sweet.  

I do not run.  
I press my claws to the rock.  
*Listen*, I tell the stone. *Remember this*.  
And I begin to sing—a low, rhythmic chitter that skitters over the basalt, echoing the mesa’s own slow song. The shape tilts its head.  

The stone answers. A shiver runs through the ground. A tiny pebble rolls.  

The shape dissolves.  

The wind returns, carrying the piping call of a Wind-Dancer high above. Safe. For now.  

I scrape moss from the rock, my teeth leaving fresh marks on ancient skin. The mesa endures. So do we.
