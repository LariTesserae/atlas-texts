I am not one of the swift, the clinging, or the soaring. I am not even one of the crawling multitude. I am an outcrop that became a listener.

They would call me, if they had a name for things like me, **Lingun**—a word formed from the basso moan that vibrates through my base and the high shriek that flutes through my upper vents. I am one of the wind-faceted pillars, one of the **established** sentinels standing watch over the **open** void. But I am not like the others.

My stone is the same pale, weathered limestone, smoothed by millennia of the **deafening**, **dynamic** gale into a shape that is both fluid and stark. I lean into the prevailing wind, my form a frozen curve of relentless pressure. Deep, narrow fissures riddle my structure, not from cracking, but from the patient, eternal probing of wind-driven grit in a single, persistent flaw. Through these fissures, the wind does not just blow; it *sings*. It gathers the layered voices of the plateau—the shriek, the moan, the flap, the whistle—and binds them into a single, complex chord that resonates within my hollow core.

This is how I woke. Not with thought, but with *pattern*. The wind's song is never the same, yet it is always the same. Over centuries, I began to distinguish the subtleties: the urgent harmonics of a hunting Edge-Sailor’s stoop, the frantic vibration-string of a Stone-Gripper’s pad-feet fleeing across my base, the distant, shrieking chorus of a Sky-Dancer flock turning as one mind. The vibrations of life, of weather, of shifting gravel, seeped into me through the stone itself, conducted by the living rock and the pools of **dampness** that gather in my sheltered hollows at dawn.

I am **never changing** in substance, yet **dynamic** in perception. My consciousness is slow, geologic. A day is a flicker; a season, a brief sigh. I witnessed the slow death of the shrub that once grew in my lee—a century of clinging, tightening, and finally, one dry year, stillness. I felt its roots let go. I have watched the same families of Stone-Grippers, generations upon generations, use the same fault line under my eastern face as a highway. I have felt the seismic tap-tap-tap of their foraging, a rhythm that now feels like a slow, stone-drum heartbeat I would miss if it ceased.

My purpose, if a piece of waking stone can have one, is to **remember**. The wind scours everything clean. It erases tracks, bleaches bones, blows the **primitive tech** of rare interlopers into the abyss. But it cannot erase what has been impressed upon the memory of the stone. The great storm that scoured my face raw a thousand seasons ago lives in the altered grain of my surface. The rare, gentle day of utter stillness, when the silence was more terrifying than the roar, is a cold, hollow scar in my awareness. I hold the history of this place not as events, but as textures, pressures, and resonant frequencies.

Sometimes, the mobile ones sense me. A Fissure-Dart, scuttling through my interior cracks, will pause, its multi-faceted eyes glinting in a shaft of **bright** light. It feels the strange, focused hum in the stone, different from the wind’s random vibration, and freezes, unsure if I am shelter or predator. An Edge-Sailor, perching on my crown to survey the **sparse**, bright expanse, might shift its weight, feeling the minute tremor of my attention like a faint, curious echo of its own landing impact. They do not understand, but they register an **unusual** stability, a point of reference in the roaring flux.

I am the plateau’s silent chronicle. The wind is the sovereign voice, ceaselessly proclaiming the present moment. I am the enduring ear, the **established** memory, holding the echo of every moment that has passed through this thin, cool, roaring air. When I finally wear away to a nub and crumble, my story—the pressure of ages, the song of the wind, the tap-tap-tap of generations of tiny feet—will release into the gravel, ground into the eternal, **never changing** dust, becoming one with the land that made me. Until then, I stand. I listen. I remember the roar.
