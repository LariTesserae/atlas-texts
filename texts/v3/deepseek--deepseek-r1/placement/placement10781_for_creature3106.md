The cold is not an absence, but a presence. It seeps into the stone of my skin, into the fibrous darkness beneath, a deep, wet chill that settles in the marrow of this attenuated frame. I stand sentinel near the weeping fissure in the obsidian-dark block, a column of damp shadow amidst the greater gloom. My obsidian eyes, lidless and wide, drink the scant grey light stolen from the choked sky far above, filtering it into impressions of the courtyard: the slick flagstones, the yielding moss, the constant, glistening sweat upon every surface.

*Drip… drip… drip…*

The sound is my liturgy. It resonates through the stone beneath my feet, vibrates in the fibrous strands within my fractured joints. The fissure before me weeps its cold tears, a slow, persistent exudation that feeds the emerald velvet moss below and the hair-thin roots probing greedily from above. My chitinous fingertips trace the seam, not to stem the flow, but to coax it. To guide. A subtle pressure here, a minute shift of posture there, and the rivulet thickens, finding a new path towards a patch of moss that feels thirsty beneath my perception. The air is thick with the scent of wet earth, fungal spores, and the musk of countless small lives – a scent that is as much a part of me as the condensation beading on my slate-grey skin.

I am stillness. I am patience carved from the living rock. The glossy beetles scuttle over my forearm, their tiny legs a faint tickle against the cool stone-skin. They do not fear me; I am another surface, another weeping stone in their world. A pale, many-legged arthropod freezes amidst the moss nearby, indistinguishable from a twig until it decides to move again. I perceive it all – the minute flutter of unseen wings in the thick air, the rustle deep within the root-tangled corridor beyond the nearest archway where the Root-Stalkers move with silent purpose. Their bioluminescent eyes, cold blue pinpricks in the gloom, flicker at the periphery of my vision and vanish. We rarely exchange more than that glance, that shared awareness of the space we tend.

My breath escapes in a plume of vapour, faint and cold, merging instantly with the humid atmosphere. It carries no words, only subtle shifts in temperature and scent – signals understood by the Stalkers, perhaps, or by the roots themselves. Sustenance is drawn not from solid food, but from the stone-bleed I cultivate, from the moisture-laden air I breathe. It is a slow, constant absorption, as much a part of the courtyard's cycle as the dripping water.

A Root-Stalker emerges from the archway's shadow, fluid and silent. Its multi-jointed limbs carry it over the treacherous floor without a sound, its fur slick and dark as wet moss. It pauses near a dense patch, its whip-like tail twitching, filaments tasting the air. It doesn't look at me, but I feel its attention. It moves towards an aggressive rootlet snaking too eagerly over the low wall, its hidden mandibles ready for a precise, silent snip. My task is the water; its task is the boundary. The balance.

I shift, unfolding slowly, deliberately. Condensation gathers in the concave of my palm. I move towards a porous fungal node sprouting near another archway, its surface dull. The moisture in my palm is transferred, a ritual offering to the symbiotic life that purifies, that structures, that *is* this place. The node glistens faintly, absorbing the gift.

This sunken yard is not a refuge. It is a collaboration. A pocket of managed emptiness wrested from the writhing green flesh of the world. I am the Dripstone Whisperer. I am the coaxer of tears from the living stone, the tender of the vital damp. My existence is the slow pulse of water in the cold, dark heart, a silent partner in the dance that keeps the suffocating embrace at bay, one careful drip at a time. The cold is my element, the damp my breath, the stone my skin. I am the courtyard's quiet, weeping custodian.
