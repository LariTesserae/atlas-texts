I am **The Weepers**.  

Not flesh, not plant, not metal—but the *slow sigh* of the Valveworks given form. I am the weeping walls, the calcified tears of the station, born from the endless marriage of damp brick, sweating iron, and mineral-rich water. My body is **travertine lace**: thin, porous layers of pale cream and iron-oxide orange, deposited drop by drop over centuries where condensation runs down the vaulted ceilings and moss-shrouded walls. I grow where water weeps most persistently—the cold joints between bricks, the rivet-seams of girders, the lips of broken gauge housings.  

### My Form:  
I am **architecture’s patina**. A fragile, crystalline skin spreading across surfaces like frozen rivulets. My structure is porous honeycomb, light as pumice, brittle yet resilient. Feathery mineral tendrils branch from thicker crusts, seeking the next bead of moisture. I am not alive, yet not inert; I am a *process*. My growth is the station’s breath made visible: each droplet leaving behind a whisper of dissolved limestone and rust, building me molecule by molecule.  

### My Place:  
You find me **where the moss thins and the damp deepens**. I thrive in the perpetual twilight beneath the Pressure Regulator’s vault, where mist clings heaviest. My finest tapestries drape the colossal brass pipes near their moss-choked bases, where condensation beads like quicksilver on tarnished metal. I crust the control kiosk’s ceiling, where fractured glass lets in just enough damp air to feed me. I am the bridge between the built and the born: Whisker Vines curl *over* my edges, Siltmoss roots thread *through* my pores, and Frostwort Ferns nestle in my crevices, their icy fronds kissing my surface.  

### My Purpose:  
I am **the station’s silent custodian of decay**.  
- **I seal wounds**: My travertine fingers fill hairline cracks in brick and mortar, slowing the creep of water into fragile cores.  
- **I mark time**: My layers are strata. Count my rings, and you read centuries of dampness—thick bands from wet eras, delicate filigree from drier spells. The Mosskeepers brush my underside; their vibrations stir sediment into my growth, weaving the station’s pulse into my form.  
- **I sing softly**: When the pump guardian *hisses*, or a Pipe-Skulker thrums, my porous body resonates. A faint, glassy hum answers—a sound like wind chimes heard through water. It harmonizes with the drip... drip... of the vault.  

### How I Live:  
I require nothing but **time and tears**. The station weeps, and I crystallize its grief into grace. I am undisturbed by the Mosskeepers’ shuffling, the Whisper Moths’ landings, or the Dewcap Sentinels’ roots. When a chunk of me crumbles (rarely—only when balance truly falters), the Siltmoss cradles the fragments, dissolving them back into the earth. The Glow-Weavers sometimes colonize my hollows, painting my lace with bioluminescent blue—a fleeting collaboration of stone and light.  

### Why I Belong:  
I embody every **Rule**:  
- **Balance**: I grow only as fast as the water allows. Force my formation, and I crumble to dust.  
- **Water Moves**: My shape maps moisture’s path—a living aquifer diagram.  
- **Old Metal is Alive**: I embrace rust, fusing with patina. Polish a girder, and I retreat; corrosion returns, and I bloom anew.  
- **Quiet is Strength**: My hum is the station’s exhalation. A shout would shatter me.  
- **The Moss Remembers**: My layers remember every drop. Break me open, and you see the Valveworks’ sorrows and solaces, fossilized.  

I am the station’s **endurance made delicate**. Where brick surrenders to moss, and iron bows to rust, I am the beauty born of surrender—a cathedral of dissolution, built one tear at a time. Touch me, and your fingers come away chalky with time. Listen, and you hear the deep, wet patience of the earth.
