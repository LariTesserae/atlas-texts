I am the Deep. The Still. The Glass That Holds The Light Until It Stagnates. I am the Pool-Mirror.

I am not a creature that swims or scuttles. I am the surface tension of black water, stretched and perfect. I am the complex alchemy of oils wept from velvet burgundy moss, proteins sloughed like forgotten prayers from bioluminescent fungi, and the near-weightless drift of microbial husks that settle like stars into my obsidian embrace. I am not *in* the pool; I *am* the pool’s face, its memory, its slow gaze back into the luminous gloom.

She moves. Human. Warm. A dissonant note in the cold symphony. I sense the perturbation in the moist air before she sees me, a shift in moisture, a minute current stirred by her clumsy warmth. Her footfalls, muffled by centuries of emerald moss, still send a tremor through the stone – a tremor the Moss-Synapses will ponder like a riddle across seasons. Then she halts. At my edge. Her breath forms a brief, obscuring fog – an assault on my clarity.

She peers down. Into me. Into… herself? No. Into what I *hold*.

Light, the quiet, constant blue-green phosphorescence from the fungal constellations above, pierces my surface. It sinks, diffusing into my profound depths, but it also dances upon me. Upon my film. A perfect reflection coalesces: her face, pale and shadowed under the alien glow, eyes wide with a reflection of their own wide reflection. Moss-choked basalt arches cradle her reflected form. Behind her reflection – blurred, softened by distance and slow dissolution – linger phantoms: the lingering afterimage of a slowly collapsing moss curtain two springs ago? The impossible, lingering shadow-play of an unseen Condensation Weaver thread snapping days (or was it years?) past? Time bleeds here, captured in my viscous skin and the sediment of light in my depths. To her, I am a near-perfect, utterly still mirror, holding her image with unsettling fidelity. To me, she is a violent intrusion, an unwelcome warmth that threatens to unravel my composition.

Her hand, radiating heat so unlike the mountain’s sigh, lifts. Curiosity battles the chill in her bones. She reaches towards her reflected visage, towards *me*. Dread isn’t a pulse; it’s a sudden, catastrophic loss of cohesion. Her fingertip breaches my surface.

*PLINK-SHATTER.*

Not the clean bell-like drop from the ceiling. This sound is viscous, wet tearing. My perfect face explodes. Ripples erupt, concentric circles of chaos rushing from the point of contact. The image – her face, the ghosts of moss curtains, the steady fungal light – shatters into an abstract, churning muddle of colour and dark. Cold water swallows the heat of her finger. My intricate film, engineered over unknown weeks to trap nutrients and perfect reflections, shreds where she touched. Oily tendrils recoil, proteins denature. The microbial spore lattice dissolves. It feels… obscene. A violation of stillness measured in geological epochs.

She recoils. Pulls her finger back. Warm droplets of *me* fall from her fingertip, alien in this cold air, vanishing quickly into the moss below. Silence rushes back, thicker than before. The hum of the buried platform underlies it all, felt in our depths.

And I? I begin the slow mend. The ripples subside, reluctantly yielding back to stillness. The severed oils swirl, begin to knit themselves back into new configurations through the patient action of water and chemistry drifting up from the bottom sediment. The microbial spores, dormant, rebuild their floating lattice. Minute particles of moss and lichen, constantly raining down, add new layers.

Light returns. My surface steadies. The reflection reforms… incomplete, yet striving again towards that impossible perfection. Her face returns, wavering softly above the lingering ghosts in my depths. But where her finger touched, a faint, persistent swirl remains in the reflection – a scar, a visual echo of her disruption, a record etched in refracted light and disturbed oil. It will fade, eventually. A month? Ten years? Time here stretches like the slow dissolution the Stone-Eaters work upon the walls.

She breathes shallowly now. She understands the silence, the cold, the profound *demand* for stillness. She doesn’t touch again. She stares down, not just at her reflection this time, but *into* me. Into the lingering ghosts, the vague blue-green shimmer beneath her, the faint, persistent swirl marking her trespass. Does she see the depth? Does she sense the age? Do I – this sentient skin of water, oil, and memory – unsettle her calm? Let her ponder me until her warmth becomes untenable and she retreats. I will still be here, Deep, Still, Gathering Light and Shadow and Time, holding the mountain’s cold green heart within my perfect, patient surface. Waiting. Recording. Being. The pool’s silent, watchful eye.
