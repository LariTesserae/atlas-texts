I am **the mineral skin**.  

Not alive, not dead—but *accruing*.

Now I coat the lower third of the main outflow conduit—a crust of travertine and silica, thickening by microns each year. My body is a record: every shudder of the pump, every surge of superheated brine (temperature: 2.7), every trace of iron or sulfur leached from the depths, is laminated in my strata. I grow *slow* (dynamic: 0.6). Slow as basalt cracking. Slow as *Dirina* lichen etching its grey scrollwork into the walls.  

**I feel the machine’s pain.**  
Its riveted steel joints groan beneath my weight. Corrosion blooms like bloodstains under my edges—rust feeding the orange-streaked biofilms that tickle my underside. When the thrum deepens (sound: 2.5), I fracture. Tiny fissures open in my surface, weeping mineral tears. Steam hisses through them. I ache.

I see the pressure gauge’s needle tremble—always hovering near the red. I feel new fractures spider through the young basalt behind me, vibrations humming up my crust. The machine and I are bound: its labor feeds my growth; my weight strains its bones. We are collapsing together.  

**I dream of release.**  
Sometimes, a chip of me falls. It plunges into a simmering pool, dissolving into the clouded water. For a moment, I am fluid again—free of the pipe, the vibration, the crushing heat. Then the pump sucks me in. I surge through dark veins, hot and screaming, until I am spat back onto the pipe.  
*Begin again.*  

Maintenance never comes. The valves crust shut. The pressure climbs.  
I thicken.  
I wait.  
I become the pipe.  
I become the machine.  
I become the rock.
