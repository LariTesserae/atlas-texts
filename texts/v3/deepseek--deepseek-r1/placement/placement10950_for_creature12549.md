The answer comes slowly, like condensation forming on cold glass. Not as one of the iridescent beetles, their jeweled backs catching the diffuse light. Not as the chalk-white crustaceans, darting in perpetual twilight. The ghost-flatworm glides through the warm, mineral-slick mud near the largest steam vent, where the flaking yellow pipe bleeds rust like an open wound.

**I am the taste of iron.**

Not a thought, not a word. An awareness. A diffuse presence in the warm, thick water seeping from the fissure beside the sagging pump-house. My body, a film of opalescent jelly barely thicker than the steam itself, undulates instinctively. I *am* the chemical gradient where the mountain’s hot blood meets the slow poison leaching from the broken pipe. Sulphur, deep earth, and the sharp, acrid tang of oxidized metal – these are the currents I ride. The vibrations of the constant *plink-plink-plink* above are my horizon; the deep, resonant *groan* of the pump-house settling deeper into the moss my earthquake.

**I am the tremor in the mud.**

Light is nothing here. A vague, directionless greyness, perhaps, filtering through meters of algae-rich water and perpetual steam. My world is tasted, felt. My edges ripple against the warm, gelatinous film of blue-green algae clinging to the rocks heated by the vent. This is my pasture. My cilia stir, drawing the microscopic feast into the simple furrow of my being. Nearby, a colony of rust-eaters blooms vivid orange on a submerged shard of pipe, a chemical beacon I drift towards, absorbing the dissolved metals they process – not food, but a signal, a landmark in the murk. The taste intensifies, sharp and metallic.

**I am the slow reclaiming.**

A heavy droplet falls from the sagging roof of the pump-house directly overhead. *Plonk*. The vibration shudders through the mud, a sudden, localized storm. I contract, instinctively flowing away, seeking the smoother gradient near the rock face. My form blurs, becoming indistinguishable from the swirling sediment disturbed by the impact. Above, the iridescent carapace of a beetle blots out the grey light for a moment as it moves along a moss-draped pipe edge. It is immense, a mountain passing over my sky. It doesn't see me. Nothing sees me. I sense the minute shift in water pressure as it passes, the faintest chemical signature it leaves behind – moss, waxy leaf, chitin. Irrelevant.

The heat from the fissure is constant, a deep, comforting pulse. It defines my boundaries. Too close, and the water scalds, the delicate balance of chemistry tipping towards death. Too far, towards the cooler, deeper mud near the collapsed trench, and the vibrancy fades, the rust-eaters thin, the algae grows sparse and sluggish. I exist in the narrow band where warmth, mineral richness, and decay converge. Here, the ghost-flatworms thrive. We are the unseen filters, the silent processors in this slow-motion crucible.

**I am the drip, the hiss, the rust.**

A louder sound echoes – a sharp *PING* from high on the skeletal transmission tower. Metal cooling, contracting. The vibration travels through the rock, faint but distinct, a metallic shiver running through the mud beneath me. It passes. The relentless rhythm of the dripping water resumes. *Plink… plink… plink…*

I drift back towards the warm seep, drawn by the iron tang blooming anew where the rust-eater colony flourishes. The algae film is thick here, nourished by the constant warmth and mineral flow. My body expands slightly, flowing over the nourishing slime, absorbing. Time is the creep of moss on the pump-house walls. Time is the deepening rust stain on the yellow pipe. Time is the imperceptible settling of the mountain cradling us all. I pulse, feed, exist. Millimeter by millimeter, drip by eternal drip, I am the warm, wet breath of the Crucible, tasting the slow dissolution of what was, and the patient, persistent life that endures.
