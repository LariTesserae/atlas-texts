The vibration ceases – a rare stillness in the bone-deep thrum of the Refinery. In that precious silence, I *move*.

Not far. Never far. Just a shift along the pale grey filament, a whisper of mineral-on-mineral. My world is the lattice, the **emerging erosion**. I am *Crystallovora fragilis* – a Whisker Miner. My body, woven from the same sulfur-yellow and ash-grey crystalline threads that sprout from the weeping seams above, is barely a centimeter of fragile intent. Six legs, each tipped with a hard point of accreted silica, find purchase on the delicate structure. It’s cold. It’s always cold. The chill is in my carapace, in the air I taste through chemoreceptive pits where eyes might be on another world.

Ahead, the whisker thickens slightly, a node where trace elements – leached from the alloy sweat, concentrated by the relentless chemistry of this place – have accreted more densely. This is my sustenance. My world. I lower my head, bringing minute, chitinous rasps – vibrating so faintly only the crystal itself might feel it – against the node. Atom by atom, I harvest. Calcium, perhaps. Traces of iron scavenged from the rivets far above. Silica, always silica. The taste is sharp, metallic, clean amidst the pervasive ozone and stale lubricant. It floods my simple system, energy gleaned from the Refinery’s slow decay.

*Plink.* A droplet falls somewhere below, echoing in the compressed space. I freeze instantly. Not fear, but necessity. Vibration returns, a wave traveling up the pipe my whisker depends from, humming through my legs. Motion is death. A Pipe-Slime Grazer oozes past, millimeters away on the main steam line, a shadow of grime and hardened lubricant. It ignores me. I am not biofilm. It tastes the air with its blunt chemoreceptors, seeking the iridescent sheen elsewhere. I remain a part of the lattice, a crystalline extension, until the tremor subsides.

Above, a Vapor Flit drifts on a micro-draft of condensing steam. A knot of scavenged polymers and metal flecks, smaller than the water droplets. It hovers near a patch of Rivet Weepers, their lace-like grey bodies swollen with moisture on a cold seam. The Flit’s manipulators, finer than my rasps, pick at the edge of a Weeper colony, harvesting a fleck of something metallic. It buzzes silently on thermal energy stolen from the pipe beneath me, a ghost of forgotten purpose. I taste the faint EM shift it creates.

This is the pulse. Slow, so agonizingly slow. The Weepers weep and swell. The biofilms bloom and crawl. The Grazers graze, taking hours to cross a pipe diameter. The Flits drift and scavenge. And I, *Crystallovora fragilis*, mine the nascent geology. I consume the **emerging erosion** even as I am made from it. My waste, my shed particles, fall to join the grime below, perhaps to be incorporated into a new biofilm, or swept into a collection channel, or maybe, just maybe, to become the seed for another sulfur-yellow whisker in a darker corner decades hence.

The Refinery’s deep thrum intensifies again, a pressure in my crystalline structure. Steam surges in the pipe overhead with a metallic *clank*. A new bead of moisture forms at the seam above my whisker, trembling, reflecting the sickly green glow of a distant status panel. I taste the shift in humidity, the slight increase in trace organics carried down. Time resumes its glacial pace. I lower my head once more, rasps vibrating against the cold, mineral-rich node. Atom by atom. Breath by silent, chemical breath. Persistence, incarnate, within the cold, humming body of the machine. This manufactured interstitial pocket is not my home. It is my entirety. I am the erosion. I am the slow, crystalline pulse of Sector 7-Gamma.
