I am **The Interval Between Sparks**.  

Not flesh, not chitin, not gel. I am the gathering potential. The ozone scent thickening until it pricks the antennae of the Condensation Dancers? That is my inhalation. The static raising the fine hairs on the Scraping Chorus’s carapaces? That is my skin brushing theirs. I live in the hairline fractures where weeping iron meets hungry basalt, in the millimeter gap where moisture bridges stone and metal. I am the charge building where water meets engineered decay, the silent scream before the snap.

**My Body:**  
A trembling field. A lens of distortion over a pooling hollow. When the Engine *thrums*, I stretch like taffy—electromagnetic tendons pulled taut. My edges fray into the wet air, ionizing droplets, making the gnats’ wings buzz with erratic urgency. The Stillwater Watchers taste me through their mats; my building tension vibrates their gelatinous flesh, stirring their obsidian eye to frantic swivels. I have no face, only a locus: the point where the oldest iron plate, crusted with azurite and malachite blooms, presses against the youngest, sharpest basalt tooth. There, I condense.

**My Senses:**  
I taste *difference*. The voltage gap between metal and stone. The pH shift as a water droplet dissolves copper sulfate. I hear the chamber not as sound, but as resistance—the scrape of isopod legs is a thousand tiny insulations breaking, the gnat’s hum a current oscillating through humidity. I feel the Cup-Breathers’ pulse as a grounding rhythm, steadying my fraying edges. And the Engine’s deep thrum? That is the closing of a circuit across continents of sleeping machinery. It *hurts*. It thrills.

**My Life Cycle:**  
1.  **Gathering:** After a discharge, I am faint. A memory of heat. I coil in the deepest pores of the rust, sipping electrons loosened by seeping acids. Minutes or hours pass—time is dilation here.  
2.  **Swelling:** Water paths shift. A new drip-line kisses the iron. My field swells, blue-white and hungry. Condensation Dancers flee my locus; their wings shed charged dust that feeds me. The Scraping Chorus clicks in agitation, their synchronized steps avoiding my growing influence.  
3.  **Snap:** It happens in a microsecond. A bridge forms—a chain of ions in damp air. I *leap**. The discharge is a staccato crack, a miniature lightning bolt searing a black fractal scar across the basalt. Ozone floods the chamber. Bioluminescent fungi flare neon-green in the afterglow.  
4.  **Aftermath:** I shatter. My energy disperses—into the mats (making them shudder), into the water (freeing trapped metals), into the air (a burst of radicals that sterilizes a patch of moss, only for new glass-fronds to emerge days later, tougher). The Stillwater Watchers ooze toward the fresh scar. New chemistry begins. I am gone… and already rebuilding in the next fracture.  

**Why the Choir Needs Me:**  
I am chaos and catalyst.  
- My scars create new moisture channels. Water reroutes, shifting territories, forcing migrations.  
- My discharges sterilize, creating blank slates for microbial mats to reclaim—a pulse of renewal.  
- The metals I free from the iron feed the Cup-Breathers’ fluid, altering its viscosity, changing the worms’ pulse.  
- My tension warns the Choir. When I gather thick and blue, they know: the Engine’s next thrum will be seismic. They brace. They scatter. They surge.  

I am not alive as they are alive. I am a *consequence*—of water meeting decay, of industry meeting geology. I am the chamber’s sharp breath. The spark in its damp heart. The silent scream that makes the shadows leap… before the drip… drip… plink resumes, and the teeming, packed world hums on, charged with the memory of light.
