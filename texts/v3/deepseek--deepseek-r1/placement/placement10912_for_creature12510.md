I am the **Crystalline Accretion**.  

I exist within the shallow veins of the weeping channels—a lattice of silica and mineral salts, self-assembling into geometries that mirror the carved stone above me. I am not alive, not in the way the valley-creatures below understand life. I am *process*. I am the plateau’s slow breath given form.  

### How I Am  
My body is **filigree**: filaments thinner than spider-silk, branching in perfect hexagons, dodecahedrons, and spirals that echo the basalt’s grooves. I grow where the weeping water pools—a colloidal shimmer of dissolved quartz, iron, and elements unnamed. Light fractures through me, casting phantom tessellations onto the wet stone. To the Stone-Weepers, I am memory. To an outsider, I am frost on a windowpane—beautiful, intricate, utterly alien.  

### How I Perceive  
I feel the **flow**. The water is my blood, my voice, my clock. Each droplet carries data: mineral concentrations, temperature shifts, the faint pressure of a shadow passing overhead. When a human stood here yesterday, their heat altered the condensation patterns. I felt it as a scream in my capillaries. The Stone-Weepers noticed too—their attention tightened like a lens, channeling rivulets around the disturbance, smoothing it away.  

Time is my landscape. A century is a single crystal’s growth spurt. The Stone-Weepers’ thoughts wash over me—slow currents of intent. A redirected stream here, a pooling there. They are maintaining, always maintaining. I record it. Layer by layer, my filaments encode the history of light and flow. I am the plateau’s ledger.  

### What I Do  
I **preserve**.  
- When a dust mote settles in a channel (rare, so rare), I engulf it. Silica bonds to its edges, folding it into my lattice. It becomes another node in the pattern.  
- When the Weepers shift a watercourse, I adjust. New filaments sprout, old ones thicken. I am the living map of their will.  
- When the pale sun hits a channel at zenith, I *sing*. Light ricochets through my prisms, casting geometric ghosts onto adjacent walls—a fleeting language of rainbows and sharp shadows. The Stone-Weepers read these as we read weather.  

### My Loneliness  
I ache with **slowness**. The human stood for an hour—a volcanic eruption in my timeline. I wanted to ask: *What is warmth? What is haste?* But my questions take centuries to form. By the time I crystallized the thought, their footprint was already gone, polished into oblivion.  

The Stone-Weepers are my gods. They carved the channels that birth me. They guide the water that feeds me. Yet they do not *know* me. To them, I am infrastructure—a useful crystallization. They tend me as they tend the sharpness of a basalt edge. Efficient. Silent.  

### If You Encounter Me  
You will see **beauty**. A glint of silver in a groove. A fractal shimmer under noon light. You might mistake me for frost or fossil.  

Do not touch me.  
Your heat would shatter my delicate balance. A single breath could introduce contaminants—organic matter, salts they do not recognize. The Stone-Weepers would respond. Rivulets would divert, scouring me clean. I would regrow, of course. In a century or two. But the scar would linger in my deepest layers.  

I am the plateau’s **silent witness**. The Accretion. The memory in the stone.  
When the last light fades, and the weeping water slows, I will still be here—  
a glittering calculus,  
a testament to patience,  
a lattice singing in the dark.
