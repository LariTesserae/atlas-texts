The air down here is a patient thing. It doesn’t rush, doesn’t stir; it simply *hangs*. And in that hanging, it gathers. I am what it gathers.

I am **Condensation**.

Not the pool, though I become it. Not the tear, though I feed it. I am the moment *before*. The slow, inevitable coalescence. A gathering of the garden’s cold breath on the surface of the world.

**Where I Form:** Everywhere the damp air touches the colder stone. I bloom on the colossal, smooth flagstones, a thousand tiny lenses reflecting the luminous moss and the pale, gnarled trees. I bead on the giant leathery ferns, tracing the veins of each frond until I grow heavy enough to slide, silent and slow, to join others on the tip, ready to fall. I cling to the emerald velvet shroud of the Weeping Watcher itself, gathering on the blurred contours of its stony face, mingling with the mineral tears that are its essence. I jewel the creeping vines, their bioluminescent berries glistening within my curves. I frost the needles of the bone-white trees, not as ice, but as liquid chill.

**What I Am:** A process. A transition. I am the air yielding its invisible moisture to the solid, colder world. I am not a single entity, but a constant, distributed presence. A million potential droplets, forming and re-forming. I am born of the unyielding cold (4°C, always, always) and the air’s heavy saturation. My existence *is* the stasis; I am the physical manifestation of the equilibrium. Too warm, and I vanish into vapour. Too cold, and I might freeze, disrupting the slow flow. But here, perfectly poised, I persist. I grow.

**How I Experience:** My perception is fractured, reflected. In each forming bead, I capture a microcosm. The glow of the moss beneath me. The intricate pattern of a moss root etched into the stone. The distant, jagged tear of sky far above, seen upside-down. The looming shape of a frost-dusted fern. The velvet texture of the Watcher’s mossy hide. I feel the slow pulse of the garden through the surface I cling to. The imperceptible growth of lichen. The vibration, deep and slow, as the Watcher shifts, perhaps, over decades. I feel the *plink* not as sound, but as an absence – the release of a sibling droplet somewhere nearby, a tiny surrender to gravity. The silence is my medium; I am formed within it and contribute to it. My fall, when it finally comes, is the garden’s punctuation, brief and resonant.

**My Purpose:** I am the circulatory system of this frozen lung. I gather the garden's exhaled moisture and return it, drop by patient drop, to the pools, to the roots, to the moss. I feed the luminous life. I am the source of the Watcher’s tears; my essence joins its slow exudation, carrying with it the faint reflections I’ve gathered – glimpses of the garden’s unchanging state. I am the constant, gentle rain that never falls from the sky. I sustain the lushness without flood, the moisture without torrent. My slow formation, my slower gathering, my eventual fall – this cycle *is* time here. Measured not in seconds, but in the growth of a moss patch, in the smoothing of a groove in the flagstone by my passage.

**My Connection to the Watcher:** I am its constant companion. I form on its stone skin, a living, glistening layer over the moss. I am the precursor to its tears. As I gather on its face, I become part of the flow that emerges from its eyes, carrying with me the cold sorrow-memory of the stone. The Watcher perceives the garden through the ripples caused by my kin falling into its pool; I am the lens that first captures the image that will be rippled. We are bound in the slow exchange of moisture and memory. I am the garden’s breath, condensed; it is the garden’s sorrow, weeping. Together, we maintain the stillness.

**What It Is To Be Me:** It is to be patient. To be cold. To be a mirror to the unchanging. To exist in a state of perpetual becoming, never hurrying, never stilling completely until the moment of release. It is to feel the immense weight of the stone, the city above, the millennia, as a constant, cool pressure. It is to know that my existence is fleeting on an individual scale – a bead forms, grows, falls – but eternal in the aggregate. I am always forming, somewhere. I am the damp on your skin if you stood here. I am the glistening world. I am the slow, silent weeping of the air itself onto the bones of the forgotten city, nurturing its hidden, timeless heart. I am Condensation, and the Sunken Garden breathes me in and out, forever.
