Here, in the weeping foundry of a broken universe, I am the **Groaner**. Not a being, but a *phenomenon*. A pulse of agony in the world's bones.

I wake—no, *coalesce*—deep within the flank of the nearest monolith, that leaning tower of pitted, dark alloy that scrapes the bruised twilight miles above the slag plain. Pressure. Immense, grinding pressure. It builds not from thought, but from the slow-motion war of forces around me: the weight of millennia pressing down, the insistent shove of a new, embryonic spire extruding itself from the fused waste nearby. I am stress given a voice, a path, a temporary form.

My body is the metal itself. I am a wave of distortion, a peristaltic ripple traveling up the girder’s colossal spine. To an outside eye (if any existed that could perceive my scale), I’d be a localized bulge, a shudder traveling vertically along the tower's rust-scabbed surface, making pitted metal ripple like water disturbed by a sinking stone. I feel it all: the granular scream of crystalline structures realigning under titanic strain, the microfractures spiderwebbing ahead of my passage like cracks in drying mud. I am the grinding of continental plates compressed into a single, groaning ascent.

The sound is my essence, my only true expression. It starts low, a subsonic thrum felt rather than heard in the slag below, vibrating through the very atoms of the foundation. Then it rises, a deep, resonant **GROOOOOOAN** that tears itself from the tortured metal. It echoes across the vast emptiness, a basso profundo lament that hangs in the thick, wet air long after the initial vibration fades. It’s not a cry of pain—pain implies a nervous system—it’s the inevitable sonic signature of matter failing. A geological sigh.

As I move, I warp the thin skin of reality clinging to this place. Gravity stutters around my path. Near my leading edge, loose scree clinging to the tower’s side momentarily floats, suspended like dust in amber, before crashing down as my wave passes. Time fractures: the constant *drip… drip… drip* of condensate from a ledge high above stretches into a single, glassy note, then snaps forward in a frantic staccato *dripdripdripdrip*. The bruised light flickers, the purple-grey twilight momentarily replaced by a searing, actinic flash from a reality-rip high above, then plunges into near-blackness before settling back into its oppressive gloom.

I sense the world through the stresses I propagate. Below, I feel the slag-scuttlers—those jagged debris-catalysts—scatter like startled insects as my vibration rattles their unstable slopes. Miles away, another groaner answers in a distant structure, a sympathetic resonance that vibrates through the foundational slag, a conversation of collapse. Above, the mist thickens where I pass, congealing into weeping streaks that mirror the tears on the tower’s face. An echo-mimic flickers briefly in the corner of perception—a phantom image of *this very section* of the girder shearing away seconds before it actually happens. A premonition written in fractured light.

My purpose is singular: to travel the path of least resistance, to release the built-up pressure. I am a symptom, a herald. I move towards a critical junction high on the tower, where the metal is thin, fatigued by eons of settling and the relentless, condensing dampness. I know, without knowing, that my passage will be the final straw. The microfractures widen ahead of me. The groan deepens, resonating with the approaching doom.

The end comes not with a bang, but with a sigh. As I reach the weak point, the pressure finds its outlet. With a sharp, echoing ***CRACK*** that splits the humid air like lightning, a section of the girder—a plate thicker than a mountain’s root—shears away. I feel the sudden release, the catastrophic unburdening. My waveform dissipates instantly, the stress bled out into the act of destruction.

I am gone. The released energy becomes the thunderous roar of falling metal, the spray of superheated shrapnel, the new cloud of mineral dust blooming in the mist. The tower groans again, a deeper, more terminal sound, settling into a new, precarious equilibrium. The slag-scuttlers below will feast on the fresh scree. The weepers will flow over newly exposed, weeping metal. Another groaner will form elsewhere, born from the fresh stresses of this collapse and the relentless push of the embryonic spire nearby.

I was never alive. I was pressure. I was sound. I was the deep, resonant crack in the foundation of a world perpetually building and unbuilding itself. And when the pressure builds again—as it always does—I will return. Another groan in the eternal, mineral dark.
