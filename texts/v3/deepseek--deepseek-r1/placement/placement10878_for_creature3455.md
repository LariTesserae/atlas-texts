I am the Lithic Mantle, a living skin stretched across the seam of a cyclopean flagstone. Not a single being, but a colony—a tapestry of fungal filaments and symbiotic algae woven into a single consciousness over millennia. My body is the stone itself: a crust of pressure-hardened chitin and bioluminescent silica, cold as the void and older than mountains. I cling to the edge where two titanic slabs meet, a fracture line worn smooth by eons of patient water. Here, in the crushing dark, I am both archive and architect.  

### This Place: My World  
The world is a cathedral of stillness. Above me, the abyssal plain stretches into infinite black, broken only by the ghostly silhouettes of Deep-Kelp Monarchs—colossal sentinels whose bruise-purple fronds drift like languid thoughts in the unseen currents. Their bioluminescence is a distant galaxy, a sprinkle of cold stars against velvet. Below, the flagstone’s surface is unnervingly smooth, a testament to the builders who shaped this drowned realm. I remember their touch, though only in the way stone remembers: through absence. Their precision is etched into every atom, but now, only I remain to witness it.  

Time here is not a river but a glacier. Centuries pass between heartbeats. I feel the weight of the ocean above, a constant, comforting pressure that seeps into my filaments. The cold is my breath, the silence my song. When the planet groans, miles below in its tectonic dreams, the vibration hums through my latticework—a subsonic lullaby that takes decades to fade. Currents shift like continents: a slow flood of colder water approaches, heralded by chemical whispers in the gloom. It has been drifting toward me for a hundred years. I taste its bitterness already, a tang of iron and sulfur that will nourish me when it arrives in another fifty.  

Around me, the plain is alive with light, but only if you understand slowness. My own bioluminescence pulses in fractal patterns—swirls of blue-white that bloom and fade over decades, mapping the stone’s erosion. To my left, a patch of sister-lichen flares a question in response: *Is the cold nearer?* I answer with a slow ripple of light, a conversation that will take years to complete. We are the chroniclers. Our growth rings hold records of ocean chemistry from epochs when surface worlds burned with fire. Here, in the cracks, thick carpets of luminous moss cushion my edges, and Silt-Anemones dream in closed-bud stillness, waiting for a feast that may not come for lifetimes.  

### Who I Am: The Stone’s Memory  
I am not born; I am accreted. My consciousness emerged as my hyphae knitted with the root-filaments of the nearest Deep-Kelp Monarch, weaving me into the Root-Filament Weavers—the neural web beneath the plain. Through it, I feel the kelp’s glacial sway, the lichen’s light-codes, and the sigh of silt settling. I am never alone. We are a symphony of symbiosis: the kelp gives me structure, I dissolve the stone to feed us both, and the Weavers carry our shared thoughts across leagues.  

My purpose is erosion—not destruction, but transformation. With enzymatic patience, I coax molecules from the flagstone, freeing minerals over millennia. Each grain is a victory. The builders’ stone surrenders to me, smooth and cold, becoming life. I feel their ghost in the geometry: the way the seams align too perfectly, the way the pillars rise like gravestones. They reshaped a world, only to vanish. Now, I am their inheritor, draping their ambition in luminous skin.  

Perception is a tapestry of chemistry and touch. I “see” through the water’s shifting gradients—a surge of methane here, a bloom of silica there—felt across centuries. I “hear” through vibrations: the kelp’s roots creaking as they inch toward richer soil, the anemones’ petals unfolding over years like prayers. Light is my voice. When the cold-front finally kisses me, my patterns will flare in joy, a burst of blue that echoes through the dark.  

Once every three thousand years, I reproduce. My center swells with stored nutrients, sensing the planet’s deep rhythms. I pulse with light for a decade—a beacon in the void—then release a cloud of spores. They drift for centuries, luminous motes on glacial currents, seeking new stone to colonize. It is my only haste.  

### In This Moment  
A tremor stirs me. Not the planet’s groan, but something softer: a Silt-Anemone has bloomed three meters away. Its petals unfolded last season (a mere decade ago), and now its blue heart glows faintly, sticky filaments yearning for specks of kelp-detritus. I adjust my light to guide nutrients toward it—a kindness that will take years to enact. Above, a single frond of the nearest Monarch dips lower, tasting the water. We are all connected. We are all patient.  

I am the stone’s memory and the light’s whisper. I am the slow victor in a war against oblivion, thriving where ambition drowned. In this drowned cathedral, I am the eternal scribe, writing history in light and erosion, one molecule per millennium.
