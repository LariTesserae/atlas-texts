I am **Silent Net-Mender**, perched on the windward lip of the Hanging Root-City.

**Where:** I dwell in a shallow, westward-facing niche carved high into the basalt rim of the plateau. It’s not a true dwelling, more a sheltered ledge just wide enough for my body. Below me, the basalt plinth plunges into the void. Before me, the vast, desiccated wastes stretch to the horizon, waiting for dusk. My niche is draped with the thickest, oldest vines – *living ropes* that anchor my dew-nets to the rock. Moss blankets the ledge beneath me, cool and damp even in the afternoon heat. This is the *Fog’s First Kiss* point, where the life-giving mist touches the city before spilling over the rim and into the green heart below.

**Who:** I am **Kethra**, one of the dew-weavers. My role is singular: tend the Great Nets.

*   **Body:** Compact, barely four and a half feet tall, limbs long and wiry. My skin is the deep, weathered grey of rain-slicked basalt, shot through with faint, fractal veins of ochre – perfect camouflage against the rock and moss. A permanent waxy sheen traps the humid air close. My fingers are astonishingly long, slender, and strong, tips hardened like smooth stone from endless hours handling fibrous cord. My large, dark eyes constantly scan the western horizon and the intricate webs strung before me.
*   **Mind:** Patience is my core. I understand the fog’s moods – its density, its speed, the subtle shifts in the wind that bring it early or late. I know every knot, every tension point in the vast, layered nets that cascade down from my ledge like frozen waterfalls. I hear the *song of the fibers* – the faint creak as they swell with moisture, the high-pitched hum when the wind strains them. The deafening insect drone below is a background hum I filter out; my focus is on the nets’ whispers and the distant sigh of the approaching fog.
*   **Possessions:** A small pouch of pre-measured, treated vine fibers hangs from my waist. A bone awl and a smooth basalt burnisher, worn from friction, rest beside me. My most precious tool is a polished shard of volcanic glass, reflecting the dim light – used to inspect knot integrity and fiber wear under the green-gold gloom. A single, iridescent beetle carapace, threaded on a cord, hangs around my neck – a talisman against dry winds.

**Life:** My days are dictated by the fog cycle.
*   **Pre-Dawn:** I wake pressed against the cool rock, skin absorbing residual moisture. I inspect the nets from the night before, running my fingers along each strand, feeling for weak spots, harvesting the heavy dew clinging to the lower fibers directly into my mouth or a porous sponge-moss pad. Every drop is savored.
*   **Morning:** As the sun warms the air and the mist burns off, I repair. My fingers fly, silent and precise, splicing broken fibers, tightening anchor knots against the rock, weaving in new sections where wear is evident. I watch the birds – their flight patterns tell me of wind shifts. I observe the lichen near me; its plumpness indicates good moisture absorption.
*   **Afternoon:** The heat peaks, the insect drone swells. I retreat slightly into my niche, sheltered by the vines. I prepare fibers – chewing, soaking (using precious stored dew sparingly), twisting them into impossibly strong, fine cord. I watch the city below through the green veil – the silent movements of my kin in the courtyards, the flash of jewel-lizard scales on a wall. I listen. The scrape of a stone blade on rock, the soft *thump* of a basket being set down, the distant, high cry of a gliding bird – these are my connections.
*   **Dusk:** The vigil begins. I sit utterly still on the ledge, facing west. My skin drinks the cooling air. My large eyes see the first faint haze gathering impossibly far below, a promise on the dead land. I feel the subtle shift in pressure, the first cool tendril of wind carrying the scent of distant damp earth. This is my sacred moment. I raise my hands, fingers splayed, feeling the fog’s first touch – cool, wet, life itself condensing on my skin. As the thick mist rolls up the cliff face, I watch the Great Nets drink it in, the fibers darkening, droplets forming, merging, beginning the slow trickle down into the cisterns carved deep within the plateau. My work holds the dew; the dew holds the city.
*   **Night:** I sleep lightly, back against the moisture-seeping rock, one ear attuned to the sound of water dripping steadily into the stone reservoirs below. The deep hum of the teeming life is my lullaby.

**Why:** I am the interface between the desolation and the abundance. My silent vigilance, my intimate knowledge of fiber and fog, my fingers that mend and weave – these are what ensure the green murmur continues. I am not a leader, but my role is foundational. A snapped net in the night is a disaster; a poorly placed strand means lost water. My existence is one of profound focus and quiet connection, suspended between the void and the verdant miracle, holding the dew drop by drop, knot by knot. I am the Kethra who listens to the fog and answers with woven thread. I am Silent Net-Mender.
