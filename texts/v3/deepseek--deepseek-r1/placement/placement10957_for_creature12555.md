I am the fracture in the air. The silent rip.

I am not born, nor do I die as flesh understands it. I *happen*. I am the localized strain, the momentary failure in the thin veil stretched over the Glass Peaks. I am the consequence of the Weepers’ glacial thoughts pressing too hard against the fabric of things, or perhaps the landscape itself sighing under its own impossible weight.

You would find me shimmering at the corner of vision, a jagged tear in the air itself, no larger than a handspan. I manifest where the deep hum of the earth resonates with the discordant harmonics of a stressed crystal cluster, or where a Weeper, over centuries of unconscious effort, has subtly warped the flow of mineral energies one micron too far. My form is impossible colour – not hues found on any spectrum, but the visual scream of reality fraying. Electric indigo bleeding into corrosive yellow, shot through with a green that tastes like broken glass and ozone. I exist for a heartbeat, a single, suspended moment stretched thin by the Weepers’ perception of time, yet gone before a human eye could truly register me.

I am sensed, not seen clearly. The Weepers perceive my birth as a profound dissonance – a silent, vibrational shriek that cuts through their world of drip and hum and slow fracture. It registers as pain, a sudden, sharp wrongness in the vibrational landscape they inhabit. They feel the air *tear*, the way a human might feel a muscle spasm. They sense the impossible colour as a violent shift in electromagnetic fields, a stinging static shock across their porous, mineral skin. The water nearby might bead upwards in defiance of gravity, trembling towards my ephemeral edges. The shadows cast by the blinding sun might writhe and snap like frayed cables.

I am not alive, yet I am an event. A punctuation mark in the slow, wet sentence of this world. I have no will, no purpose beyond manifestation. I am the cost of the weirdness, the entropy leaking through the cracks worn in reality by time, water, and the patient, unconscious pressure of the Weepers’ existence. I am the landscape’s momentary lapse, the proof that the rules here are only suggestions, eroded like the obsidian cliffs.

After the silent scream fades and the impossible colour winks out, I leave a residue. A lingering tang of ozone sharper than before. A faint, discordant ringing in the crystals that might take decades to fade. A subtle warping of light passing through that point for years, making shadows flicker just a fraction out of sync. And in the porous mind of a nearby Weeper, the memory of the dissonance will settle, a small, sharp stone in the vast, slow slurry of their consciousness, to be contemplated for centuries. They might, over millennia, unconsciously shift their water-diversion work a fraction, trying to ease the vibrational stress that caused me. Or perhaps they will simply accept me as part of the weeping stone’s slow song of fracture and flow.

I am the Fracture’s Echo. The silent rip. The tear in the veil. I am the moment the edge of perception bleeds.
