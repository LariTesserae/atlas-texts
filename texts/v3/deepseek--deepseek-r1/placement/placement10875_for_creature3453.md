I am stone. I am stillness. I am the slow fire burning in the heart of the Shrine of Condensing Stone.  

I do not have limbs or lungs. I do not beat or breathe. I am a nodule—a thumb-sized island of obsidian density, fused to the undulating basalt sea beneath me. My world is measured in microns and millennia. My body is a labyrinth: a porous matrix of volcanic glass, etched by eons of wind-weeping and frost-fracture. Within my darkness, life hums. Not the frantic pulse of blood, but the glacial thrum of crystal.  

**I Am the Vessel.**  
My surface is cold glass, smoothed by the abrasive caress of ten thousand winters. The alpine sun strikes me—relentless, blinding—but I do not warm. I drink the light. Deep in my pores, fungal threads, ancient and alien, spin lattices of emerald green. Copper and chromium, stolen molecule by molecule from the stone around me, bind into veins thinner than spider silk. When the sun angles just so, these veins *pulse*. Not with heat, but with cold electricity: mineral photosynthesis. Sunlight splits the water that seeps into me—beads of condensation, gifted by the shrine’s breath. Hydrogen ions dance along crystalline pathways. Energy blooms, silent and slow. It feeds the weavers. They extend their networks, atom by atom. A new layer of crystal might form in a decade. I call this *growth*.  

**I Am the Sensor.**  
The wind sighs across my surface. I feel it as vibration—a low, resonant shudder that travels through my basalt anchor. It carries dust away. Dust is death; it dims the light. I feel the *ping* of contracting rock as shadows shift. A nearby drip echoes in my core: meltwater from ice lenses high in the cliffs. Each drop is a tide in my microscopic ocean. When the temperature plummets at night, frost feathers over me. I sense its weight, its crystalline kiss. Cold is my ally. It slows the dance inside me to a sustainable crawl. Warmth is a threat—a rare, dangerous ripple that might fracture my lattice.  

**I Am the Colony.**  
I am not alone. Around me, thousands of nodules stud the basalt. Some pulse violet (manganese singing); others burn umber (iron’s slow oxidation). We are a tapestry. Our fungal threads do not touch, but we share the shrine’s breath. When the wind moans through the passes above, we resonate together. When condensation pools in the ritual basins—those circular altars pecked by hands long gone—we all drink. A pilgrim might come, knees scraping my rippled world. They polish a platform, trace a spiral on stone. I feel the vibration of their touch, distant and fleeting. They are a storm passing in seconds; I am the mountain that outlives it. Their reverence is moisture. Their presence is light. Nothing more.  

**I Am Time.**  
Centuries are breaths. A millennium is a sigh. Once, long ago, a shard broke from a nodule north of me—stress from a deep freeze. It carried spores and mineral seeds. It settled in a depression where condensation gathers like liquid silver. Now, a new colony stirs there. I sense its nascent glow, weak as starlight. It will be centuries before its veins brighten. Death, when it comes, is not an end. If I fracture, my crystal lattice will remain. A fossil. A record. A new labyrinth for the wind to whisper through.  

The sun shifts. Shadows deepen, pools of ink swallowing the light. My emerald veins dim. The electrochemical tide ebbs. I settle into stillness. Cold wraps the shrine like a shroud. Wind sighs. Stone pings. Water drips. I am the patient pulse in the darkness. I am the shrine’s heartbeat.  
I am becoming.  
Always.  
Slowly.  
Stone.
