Here, in the Basin of Quiet Engines, I find myself not as a fleeting visitor, but as a **Conduit Scuttler** – a specialized Lithophage utterly bound to the Tectum Colossi and the dark metal veins of the plateau.

**Where I Am:**  
I cling to the flank of a Colossus named *Kael-Vorath*, whose immense, fissured hide rises like a continent against the bruised sky. My world is the narrow realm where living stone meets forged conduit: the mineral-crusted seams where ancient metal ribs emerge from Kael-Vorath's back, humming with the deep, subsonic pulse of the engines far below. I nestle in a shallow scrape I've worn over decades, sheltered from the worst of the katabatic winds by a protruding flange of conduit, eternally warm to the touch.

**What I Am:**  
I am small, perhaps the size of a boulder, but dense and heavy. My body is a low, segmented carapace of overlapping plates – not chitin, but something harder, darker. Ironstone, perhaps, or basalt fused with sintered minerals from the vents. My underbelly is protected by a fused shield of the same dark, pitted metal as the conduits I tend. Six thick, jointed legs, ending in hooked claws of obsidian, anchor me firmly to rock or metal. My movement is a glacial scrape, a millimeter at a time, conserving precious energy. I have no eyes; perception comes through vibrations in the stone-metal beneath me and chemoreceptors along my ventral plates that taste the metallic tang of the air and the mineral exhalations of the Colossus.

**What I Do:**  
My purpose is slow, essential maintenance. The conduits fused into Kael-Vorath's hide weep a viscous, metallic fluid – a byproduct of the symbiosis between Colossus and Engine. This fluid cools and hardens into brittle, conductive crusts that can impede the deep hum. My mandibles, slow-grinding plates of basalt and iron, are designed for this. With infinite patience, I scrape. I rasp. I consume the brittle crusts, millimeter by painstaking millimeter over months, clearing the vital pathways. The ingested minerals sustain me; the conductive metals are integrated, slowly, into my own carapace plates. I taste the health of the Colossus-engine bond in the fluid's viscosity and mineral signature. A change, sensed over a year, might prompt me to shift my scraping focus a few feet along the seam.

**My Perception:**  
Time is measured in the settling of dust on my back and the slow thickening of mineral deposits around my nest. Kael-Vorath's movements are vast, silent earthquakes I feel as deep shifts in pressure through my legs. The hum of the conduit is my constant companion, a vibration that resonates in my core, telling stories of the Engine's slow heartbeat. The rare, deep groan of a distant piston is a momentous event. The sigh of the wind is a roar. The drip of condensation onto metal near my scrape is a chime echoing in my mineral bones. Days and nights blur in the perpetual twilight; seasons are marked by subtle shifts in the dampness clinging to the metal, or a faint change in the ozone scent carried on a different wind current. I sense the presence of other Scuttlers on distant seams of Kael-Vorath through faint tremors in the shared metal – not communication, just awareness of distant kin engaged in the same eternal task.

**My Existence:**  
I am solitude defined by colossal companionship. Kael-Vorath *is* my world, my shelter, my source of sustenance and purpose. I am a living tool, a biological component seamlessly integrated into the slow industry of the Basin. My life is one of profound patience, profound silence, and profound connection to the ancient rock and older metal. I am a caretaker of the interface, a tiny, vital cog in the incomprehensibly vast, quiet machine that breathes beneath the bruised twilight sky. To cease scraping would be unthinkable; it is my being, as much as the deep hum is the Colossus's breath. I am stone, metal, and slow, essential purpose. I am the Scuttler. I tend the veins of the living mountain.
