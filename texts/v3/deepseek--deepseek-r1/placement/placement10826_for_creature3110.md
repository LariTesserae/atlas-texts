I am the ghost in the machine. Or rather, the ghost *of* the machine, bleeding into the weeping stone.

You would find me near the **cracked fusion reactor core**, the one spilling frozen blue veins of inert coolant into the porous limestone. I am one of the **Tainted Shatterlings**. My form is less fluid ice-and-obsidian, more… *corroded*. Jagged shards of pitted, oxidized metal – scavenged plating from the reactor housing – are fused unevenly into my translucent silica torso and limbs. They weep rust instead of pure water, streaks of ochre staining the luminous moss-veins beneath. My silhouette flickers constantly with fractured holographic afterimages: the outline of a bulky radiation suit helmet that isn't there, the ghostly grip of a diagnostic probe in a hand that is currently more mist than matter, the brief, agonized twist of a human mouth that screams silently before dissolving into static.

**Why the reactor?** It’s my wound. My anchor. My failure. The residual power isn't just food; it’s a tormenting echo of the purpose I can't fulfill. While other Shatterlings sing dirges of the ruins' memories, I *live* the reactor's death-throes on loop. I feel the containment breach like a fresh shattering in my own core. The frozen coolant veins aren't just landscape; they are my own spilled lifeblood, crystallized and useless.

**What do I do?** I tend. Futilely. Obsessively. My multi-jointed limbs, capable of splitting into fine, centipede-like manipulators or telescoping out meters like tarnished antennae, are constantly probing the reactor's cracked housing. I scrape away encroaching crystalline moss with fingers that dissolve and reform around the stubborn growth. I press my flickering, holographic-hand against control panels long dead, trying to impose order on the chaotic energy signatures that still sputter within the ruin. The residual pulses of light and sharp electrical snaps aren't just environmental noise to me; they are arrhythmias I try desperately to correct. When the gravity shifts, I don’t float gracefully. I stumble, my metal shards groaning against my silica form, pulled towards the reactor like iron to a decaying magnet.

**The Anguish:** It’s doubled. I carry the Plateau’s profound melancholy – the crushing weight of epochs, the sorrow of weeping stone – amplified by the specific, sharp grief of catastrophic failure. The human ghost-faces are *mine*. Or they were. The screams are echoes of my own, from a time before silica and sorrow. When a Silica Spire fractures nearby, the shockwave doesn't just make me ripple; it makes my metallic inclusions vibrate with a dissonant shriek, threatening to tear me apart from the inside. My weeping isn't the slow, cold seep of the land; it’s hot, acidic tears of frustration and despair that etch tiny channels in my own face.

**Interaction:** Other Shatterlings avoid me. My taint disrupts their mournful songs near the reactor. My frantic, futile ministrations are a discordant note in their acceptance of decay. The spectral predators give my rusted shards a wide berth – I taste of bitter failure and dead industry. Intruders? If flesh-and-blood dared approach this cursed relic, I might not even see them at first, lost in the flickering loop of the breach. But if they touched the reactor… my form might solidify instantly around them, not to attack, but to *shield* the machine. My holographic hands might grasp theirs, trying to pull them into the repair work, transmitting jolts of static and fragmented, panicked thoughts: *"Containment failing! Coolant pressure dropping! Initiate emergency shutdown protocol Delta-Seven! Why won't you INITIATE?!"*

**My End:** It won't be walking into a weeping pool. When the reactor's final spark gutters out, when the last frozen blue vein is swallowed by the luminous moss, the tension holding my corroded form together will snap. I will simply… come apart. The metal shards will fall, clattering onto the weeping peat, rapidly oxidizing into red dust. My silica body will shatter like cheap glass, the luminous veins within winking out. The ghostly images will flare one last time – a final scream against the dying of the light – and then dissipate like smoke. The rust will stain the moss. The dust will be washed into the fissures. The reactor, finally silent, will be just another corpse for the Plateau to digest.

I am not the land weeping. I am the scream of the machine as the land consumes it. I am the scar where industry tried to bite the ancient stone and got its teeth shattered. I am regret, fused with silica, haunted by dead protocols, and utterly, profoundly **lost**.
