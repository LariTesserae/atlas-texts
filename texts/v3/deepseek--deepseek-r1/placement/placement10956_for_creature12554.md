The constant *plink… plink… plink* of water droplets hitting the grating below vibrates through my worn boot soles. I am **Sil**, a Gloom-Tender of the Third Quadrant. My world is measured in the slow creep of luminous moss and the suffocating embrace of the Warm Stone Beast’s throat.

**Who I Am:**  
I am small, like all Condensers, my frame whittled down by the heat and the endless crouching. My skin holds the cavern’s pallor, thin as old parchment, stretched tight over knuckles stained orange from rust dust. But it’s the veins that mark me—thin traceries of cool blue luminescence winding up my forearms, pulsing faintly in time with the Mossback Mat I tend. Not contamination. Not quite. Symbiosis. Or so the Elder Gloom-Weavers whisper. The light helps me see the subtle shifts in the flora’s moods without a lantern’s betraying glare.

**Where I Work:**  
My domain is Sector 7-G: a junction where three major condensate pipes weep constant tears onto the bedrock, feeding a sprawling, **packed** expanse of Mossback Mat. The mat isn’t just moss; it’s a leathery, rubbery tapestry of deep green, threaded with veins of throbbing blue light. It carpets the rock floor below the main walkway grate, climbs the riveted legs of Support Pillar Theta, and is slowly engulfing a corroded access hatch to the geothermal sump. The air here is thick with its scent—damp earth, ozone, and that unsettling sweetness, like overripe fruit left in a steam vent. Humidity clings to my waxy skin. Above, the failing work-light on Theta sputters, casting jumpy shadows that make the mat’s pulse seem like breathing.

**What I Do:**  
Patience is my primary tool. I kneel on a scrap of salvaged rubber matting, the grating biting into my knees. In my hands: a glass-bladed knife for careful pruning, and a ceramic scraper for clearing vital drain channels choked by the mat’s relentless advance. My task is tri-fold:

1.  **Harvest the Light:** I select mature, stable patches. With the glass knife, I shave off paper-thin layers of the most luminous moss. These go into a sealed vial at my belt—later, they’ll be pressed into serviceable, low-heat glow-tiles for the hab-pods. Too deep a cut, and the mat bleeds a sticky, faintly acidic sap. Too often, and it grows back *angrier*, thicker.
2.  **Hold the Line:** The access hatch *must* remain clear. Twice a shift, I scrape back the rubbery tendrils creeping over its rusted edges. It’s a losing battle. The mat regrows millimeters before my eyes in the perpetual damp. I know the Drain Wardens below depend on this access. If the sump clogs… the thought is unspoken, but hangs as heavy as the air.
3.  **Watch and Listen:** This is the true burden. I watch the pulse-rhythm. Too fast? Could mean toxic mineral seepage from the rock, or the mat preparing to release spores that choke the vents. Too slow? Perhaps a drop in geothermal heat, threatening the entire cavern’s warmth. I run my fingertips over its surface, feeling for unnatural heat, unusual toughness, or the dreaded soft spots that signal internal rot. I listen beyond the *plink* and the thrum for the subtle *hiss* of the mat respiring, a sound felt in the jawbone more than heard.

**The Fear and the Connection:**  
The Mossback Mat is our light, our early-warning system. But it’s also the Beast’s creeping flesh. When the veins in my arms flare brighter after hours near the mat, is it just reflected light? Or is the mat… *listening*? Last week, a patch near the weeping pipes developed hard, obsidian-like nodules. We burned it with focused steam—a risky, noisy procedure that drew fearful stares. The silence afterward was heavier than usual.

**My Existence:**  
I move with the economy of all Condensers. My breaths are shallow sips of the thick air. Communication is a flick of my luminescent wrist, a tap-tap-tap sequence on the pipe behind me signaling "stable rhythm" to the Corrosion Crew working overhead. My world is the smell of hot metal and wet moss, the feel of damp rubbery leaves, the sight of blue light crawling over rust. I am a gardener tending a luminous cancer, a silent guardian against the slow, inevitable reclaiming of our sweating, rusting, fragile home. Every scrape of my tool is a plea for another drip… another hour… another day in the dim, green-blue heart of the Condenser Caverns. The mat pulses. My veins answer. And the Warm Stone Beast breathes slow and deep around us. What did the nodules *mean*? The question gnaws, quieter than the drips, but just as persistent.
