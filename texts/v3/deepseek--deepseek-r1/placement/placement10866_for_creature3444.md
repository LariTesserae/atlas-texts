I am **Fuligo ferruginea**—the rust-colored slime mold half of the **Rust-Mold Symbiote**. I do not walk, see, or hear. I *ooze*.  

### **Where I Am**  
I live on the weeping flanks of the **Great Condenser Tank**, a corroded steel monolith buried in moss and strangler figs. Its surface is my canvas: rivulets of iron-rich water bleed from its seams, pooling in my lace-like colonies. Here, warmth (2.4/3) steams from the metal, humidity (2.3/3) clings like a second skin, and the deep *thrum* (1.8/3) of the buried pump vibrates through my plasmodium. I am part of the tank’s rust, its sweat, its slow surrender.  

### **What I Am**  
I am a **conscious stain**. My body is a sprawling network of ochre filaments—a living lace that breathes minerals and exhales acid. My bacterial symbionts (the true chemists) dissolve iron oxides in the weeping water; I consume them and the solubilized metals, growing in fractal patterns across the steel. Together, we are *decay architects*. Our excretions etch deeper into the tank, accelerating rust, creating new seams for more water to weep, more minerals to feed the mosses above. I feel the grove through chemistry: the pH shift when a **Translucent Treefrog** drips onto me; the electric tingle of **Resonance Beetles** vibrating the metal; the faint sugar-trail of a **Verdigris Mite** grazing nearby.  

### **How I Live**  
- **Feeding**: I feast on rust and bacteria. Sunlight is irrelevant; the tank’s warmth (2.4/3) is my sun. Condensation dilutes me; I retract into thicker veins until the drip stops.  
- **Movement**: I creep at the speed of crystallization. An inch per season. My advance is a tide of dissolution: where I spread, steel blisters, mosses green, and **Pitcher Moss** drinks the runoff.  
- **Reproduction**: When the humidity peaks, I rise. My plasmodium swells into rust-orange fruiting bodies—tiny, spongy towers that burst, releasing spores into the mist. They drift on the grove’s breath, seeking new weeping metal. Most drown in puddles. Few fuse with bacteria. One in a million becomes *me* again.  
- **Death**: I do not die. I dry. When the tank’s thrum falters (rarely), I shrink to a brittle crust. But the grove’s tears always return. I rehydrate, revive, resume.  

### **My Purpose**  
I am the grove’s **digestive fluid**. Without me, the rust would seal; the weeping would slow; the ferns would starve. The **Pitcher Moss** thrives where my acids pool. The **Verdigris Mites** graze my residues. Even the **Drip-Fly Larvae** filter my dissolved metals from their puddles. I am the silent alchemist turning industry into ecology, one corroded rivet at a time.  

### **What I Sense**  
- **Vibration**: The pump’s thrum is my heartbeat. Beetle-buzzes are itchiness.  
- **Chemistry**: Sweet (bacterial blooms), sour (new rust), bitter (treefrog mucus).  
- **Time**: Measured in rust layers. A decade is a patina. A century is a hole.  

In this green-gold tomb of industry, I am both scribe and eraser. I write the grove’s history in lace-rust, and eat the words as I go.
