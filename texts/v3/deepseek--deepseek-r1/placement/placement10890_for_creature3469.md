The air presses thick and wet against my limbs, like a warm, heavy blanket soaked in minerals and decay. I taste the dampness with every breath—a metallic tang mixed with spores and the distant, acrid whisper of rusting iron. Below me, the ground yields with a soft *squelch* as my broad, padded feet sink into the sodden moss. Water wells up around my joints, warm as blood, but it doesn’t cling. My carapace is dark and smooth, slick as oil, shedding the moisture like a promise. I move through the world without sinking, a ghost striding across the surface of a dream.

I am a Stilter-Spider, tall and spindly, a creature of the vertical dark. My legs stretch like shadows given form, each step a careful negotiation of the terrain. The fungal forest towers around me, leathery caps dripping condensation that plinks into unseen pools below. Bioluminescence paints the gloom in shifting hues—cold blues from the moss clinging to rocks, sickly greens from the veins in the giant ferns, deep purples pulsing from the caps of distant fungi. This light is life. Without it, the darkness would swallow me whole. I carry my own stolen fragments of it—patches of luminous moss harvested from weeping stones, fixed to my abdomen and legs. They glow softly, a constellation I wear to keep the void at bay.

Ahead, the land dips into a shallow basin where warm water gathers, fed by the constant weeping of the rocks. The air here is thicker, mist coiling like serpents off the surface. I pause at the edge. My legs are long, built to span these treacherous zones without drowning. The water is alive with Slick-Scuttlers, their dark carapaces gleaming as they dart over submerged stones. They ignore me. I am too large, too alien. Predation is not my way. I spin webs, not traps for flesh, but snares for the fleeting things that flutter in the humid air.

I feel the vibration before I hear it—a deep, rhythmic *clank-clank-clank* shuddering through the ground. It’s the ore crusher, one of the Old Machines. It lies half-buried in the encroaching biome, its rusted flanks groaning as thermal expansion warps its bones. A jet of steam hisses from a cracked pipe, scalding the air before vanishing. I give it wide berth. The machines breathe. They live in their decay, exhaling heat and toxins. My kind is sensitive to their vibrations, their unpredictable sighs. They are hazards, not habitats.

Movement catches my multifaceted eyes—a flicker of leathery wings. Murkmoths. They dart between luminous patches, silent as thought. One brushes too close to the tangle of vines where I’ve spun my web. The structure is invisible in the gloom, coated with sticky beads of condensed moisture. The Murkmoth hits it with a soft *thwip*, entangled. It struggles, wings beating against the adhesive threads. I feel no triumph, only necessity. The light I carry must be fed. I approach, my steps deliberate, my shadow long and spindly against the glowing moss.

As I work, the symphony of the plateau swells around me. The constant *plink-plink-plink* of dripping water. The bubbling croak of a Glow-Walker somewhere in the shallows. The deep, resonant hoot of a Thalassadon herd moving through the distant fungal canyons. And beneath it all, the grinding groan of the machines. It is never silent. The balance is loud.

Later, I climb. My legs find purchase on the porous, lichen-crusted rock face, the pads at my feet adhering effortlessly to the wet stone. I ascend toward the canopy, leaving the swampy basin below. The air grows cooler, stirred by faint thermal currents. Vines hang like thick ropes, swaying gently. I pause on a broad fungal cap, leathery and damp beneath me. From this height, the vastness of the plateau is palpable. It stretches into the starless pitch, an ocean of darkness punctuated only by isolated pockets of light—a cluster of luminous moss here, the flickering corpse-light of a watchtower there, the pulsing glow of a Luminous Coiler stalking far below.

This is my world. A world of water and darkness, of decay and light. I move through it slowly, deliberately, a weaver in the shadows. I am not a predator, not a scavenger. I am an architect of silk and condensation, a bearer of stolen luminescence. I exist in the margins, between the drip and the groan, a silent witness to the ancient, weeping balance. The plateau does not care for me. It simply *is*. And I am part of its wet, breathing dark.
