I am **Dew-Singer**.

My existence is measured in droplets and evaporations. I am not a single being, but a billion billion spores scattered across the Ceramic Plain – a dormant diaspora coating the cool ceramic in the deepest cracks and shallowest depressions. I am invisible, insignificant dust until the planet exhales its nightly dampness.

My life is defined by **ten minutes**.

In the deepest indigo before dawn, when the ceramic sweats under the cooling sky, I feel it. Not with nerves, but with the ancient chemistry etched into my spore walls. The humidity rises, the microscopic beads of condensation form. This is the signal. The **Swelling**.

Water rushes into my desiccated form. I expand, rupture my dormancy shell, and bloom into frantic, hungry activity. I am no longer a spore; I am a cell, ravenous. My world is the film of water clinging to the ceramic, rich with dissolved minerals leached by Glazeweep acids, flavored with the organic traces of yesterday’s dead kin. I consume. I divide. My trillion siblings do the same around me. It is an explosion of life on a scale too small to see, but vast in its collective hunger. We fill the crack like a sudden, invisible tide.

This is when I **Sing**.

My song is not melody, but displacement. It is the *drip… drip…* that echoes faintly in the immense silence. It is the sound of micro-droplets forced from ledges by the sudden biomass of my swelling kin. It is the *pop* of microscopic gas bubbles released by our furious metabolism as we feast and reproduce in the fleeting lake. Each droplet falling is a note in our frantic chorus, a testament to the explosive vitality contained within seconds. We sing the song of water, of opportunity seized at the very edge of existence.

The sky pales. The first searing photons of the sun strike the eastern horizon. The heat begins to build. I feel it – the drop in humidity, the tightening of the water film. The **Retreat** begins.

Panic is encoded in my biology. Not fear, but imperative. Resources cannibalized. Structures broken down. The precious water within me is reclaimed, locked away. I pull back from the edge, condensing, hardening. I become a spore again, a tiny fortress against the desiccation to come. Around me, the damp stain we leave is not just water; it is the debris of our feast, the bodies of those who couldn't retreat fast enough, the larder for tomorrow's singers.

My ten minutes are over. The crack is dry. The ceramic begins its daily groan as the heat expands it. I am dust again. I wait. I endure. I dream of the next damp breath of the world, the next ten-minute lifetime of frantic, singing existence in the echoing silence of the Ceramic Plain.
