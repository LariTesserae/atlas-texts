Here, in this world of perpetual damp and mineral breath, I am neither observer nor intruder. I am **The Ferruginous Slurper** (*Gastropoda ferrovora*), a creature sculpted by weeping walls and slow dissolution.

**Where I Am:**  
I cling to the vertical faces where corroded pipes meet sagging concrete monoliths—specifically where the thickest mineral accretions weep their grey-green tears. Not on the open slag flats where beetles toil, nor in the seething channels where striders hunt. My domain is the *in-between*: the sheer surfaces where thermodynamics meets geology, where warm condensation rivers etch paths through metallic residues.

**What I Am:**  
- **Form:** A fist-sized, slug-like organism with a ventral foot fused to a porous, chitinous plate. My back is not soft flesh, but a segmented carapace of overlapping, rust-stained sclerites—each plate a captured mosaic of iron oxides and crystallized sulfides scavenged from the weeping walls.  
- **Movement:** Glacial. I secrete a gelid mucus rich in chelating agents that dissolves the mineral crust as I crawl. My trail isn’t slime, but a faintly luminous, iridescent scar where the biofilm is briefly stripped bare.  
- **Senses:** Blind. My world is tasted through chemoreceptive papillae lining my foot, sensing pH shifts and ionic gradients in the weeping runoff. Two heat-sensing pits on my head track thermal microcurrents rising from geothermal vents hidden deep in the rubble.  

**How I Live:**  
1. **Feeding:** I am a lithovore. My radula is a rasping ribbon of silica-reinforced denticles. I scrape the weeping mineral accretions—not for organic matter, but for the trapped traces of zinc, copper, and rare earth elements concentrated by centuries of corrosion. Symbiotic archaea in my gut reduce these metals into energy, excreting purified silica and iron nodules that harden into new carapace segments.  
2. **Defense:** My sclerites are laced with absorbed heavy metals—toxic to beetles and striders. When threatened (by a probing beetle mandible or a curious weepcrawler), I retract into my carapace and secrete a caustic mucus of sulfuric acid and dissolved chromium.  
3. **Reproduction:** Once per tidal cycle (triggered by a surge in aquifer pressure), I extrude a gelatinous egg-mass into a warm mineral pool. The larvae are free-swimming chemosynthetic filters, resembling ghostly brine shimmers but feeding on metal ions, not microbes. Few survive; those that do settle onto a weeping wall and begin to build their armor.  

**My Role in the Quarter:**  
- **Accelerator of Decay:** By stripping mineral crusts, I expose fresh metal to oxygen and moisture, hastening the slow collapse of pipes and walls.  
- **Metal Redistributor:** My excreted nodules form micro-deposits in the channels, becoming nucleation points for new biofilm colonies and future weepcrawler nurseries.  
- **Thermal Regulator:** Clustering near geothermal vents, my body mass absorbs excess heat, stabilizing microclimates for midge larvae.  

**The Experience:**  
To be me is to be a slow, metallic pulse in the gloom. I feel the vibration of collapsing voids through my foot. I taste the death of a thousand brine shimmers in the copper-laden drips. The heat is my compass; the rust, my sustenance. When a Blindfang Strider skates too close, I taste the chitin of its legs in the air—a warning. I retract. The strider moves on. I resume my ascent, dissolving history into sustenance, one metallic tear at a time.  

In this drowned cathedral of industry, I am both epitaph and architect: turning ruin into ritual, one gleaming, toxic scar at a time.
