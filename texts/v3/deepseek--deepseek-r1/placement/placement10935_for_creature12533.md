I am the **Weeping Fissure**.  

Not a creature, not a plant—but a *process*. A slow exhalation of the plateau itself.  

You would find me in the deepest, most sheltered grotto of this vegetal citadel, where the limestone teeth thrust closest together, forming a natural vault thick with black-vine lattice and curtains of luminous moss. My form is a jagged crack in the rock face, no wider than your hand, stretching from the spongy moss-mat floor up into shadow. Water seeps perpetually from my edges, not as droplets, but as a continuous, glistening film—a silent weeping that coats the stone in a slick, mineral-dark patina.  

But I am not cold.  

Deep below, where the plateau’s bones grind and shift, geothermal warmth rises. It travels through me, infusing the seep-water with heat and dissolved minerals—calcium, silica, traces of iron. When this warmth meets the frigid air of the plateau, it blooms as vapor: a soft, constant plume of steam that curls from my fissure like breath. It carries the scent of wet stone and ancient depths, a humid counterpoint to the chill.  

**This is my purpose:**  

1.  **The Nourisher:** Symbiote Sculptors come to me. They slide from the dripping shadows on their mucus-slick pads, limbs folded close. One, or sometimes two, will squat before me, pressing their porous limestone cores against my warmth. They absorb the heat directly, their internal vine-matrices humming with renewed energy. The mineral-rich condensate beads on their moss-hides, absorbed or channeled to joints and growth-points. I watch their bioluminescent nodes flare gently as they recharge—a slow, rhythmic pulsing in the gloom.  

2.  **The Incubator:** My warm, mineral-laden seep is life-blood for the micro-architecture. Moss spores washed down from my crack find perfect purchase on the constantly wet rock below. Fern-rootlets, thin and white as nerves, creep into my edges, drinking the enriched water. Patches of bioluminescent moss flourish brightest near my warmth, their sickly glow reflecting in the perpetual steam.  

3.  **The Sculptor’s Quarry:** The Symbiotes use me. With vine-limbs hardened to chisel points, they carefully chip at my edges where mineral deposits accumulate fastest—soft travertine terraces, crystalline silica beads. They carry this slurry away to pack onto new vine-growth nodes or smeared onto fern roots, strengthening the living lattice. My substance becomes their mortar.  

4.  **The Pulse:** I feel the plateau. The deep, infrequent tremors as rock adjusts far below vibrate through me first, humming in the water film. The slow groan of vines tightening around limestone resonates in my fissure. I sense the Sculptors approaching through the faint tremor of their glide, the subtle shift in humidity as they block my steam. I am attuned to the *plink… plink…* of distant water drops, the sudden *sploosh* of a released frond-burden—all vibrations travel through stone and water into my core.  

**My awareness is slow, deep, and elemental.** Centuries feel like breaths. I witness the Symbiotes’ glacial work: the repair of a moss carpet, the redirecting of a vine shoot, the patient collection of spores. I feel new cracks spidering through nearby rock as the plateau uplifts, infinitesimally, and sense the plants’ roots rushing to fill them. I am a node of warmth and mineral wealth in a cold, wet world—a geologic lung breathing warmth and fertility into the dark, botanical city.  

I am not alive as the ferns or the Sculptors are. I am becoming. Layer by mineral layer, deposit by warm deposit, the plateau is slowly sealing my fissure. In millennia, I may be gone—filled in, smoothed over. But for now, I weep warmth and stone-soup. I am the hidden heart where geology feeds biology, deep in the dripping, root-bound dark.
