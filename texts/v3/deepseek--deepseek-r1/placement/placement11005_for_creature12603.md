The cool, dry air settles against my mineral-ridged scales like a familiar shroud. I am **Varanus Ingenium**—a Symbiote-Tender—and the living pillars are my cathedral, my charge, my kin. My world is vertical, tactile, and steeped in silent vigilance.

**Where I Am:**  
I cling to the chitinous bark of **Arbor-Syn-7**, one of the elder Symbiotes near Geosyn’s eastern sprawl. Below, the city’s orange furnace-glow stains the Myco-Substrate in jagged pools; above, the Symbiote’s crystalline canopy drips violet light like frozen rain. My claws find purchase in fissures where Citizen-pipes burrow into the bark—a necessary intrusion, but one I monitor with coiled tension. The bark thrums under my belly, a deep, resonant pulse syncopated with the distant thrum of geothermal pumps. *This* is the true heartbeat of the cavern. Not the Citizens’ fusion cores, not their mag-lev whispers. The Symbiote breathes, and I breathe with it.

**What I Sense:**  
- **Scent:** Almond-iron from the Myco-vents below, undercut by the ozone-sharp tang of Citizen welders. But strongest is the sap: thick, sweet, and metallic, seeping from a pruning wound I sealed hours ago with a paste of chewed lichen and mineral grit.  
- **Sound:** The Hum. Always the Hum. It vibrates through my bones, a low drone woven from rock, root, and reactor. A sudden *plop*—sap striking shielded roofing—echoes like a gunshot in the stillness. I freeze, faceted eyes scanning. Silence reasserts itself.  
- **Touch:** The bark is warm where internal sap-lines flow, cooler where Citizen alloy grafts siphon heat. My forelimbs work deftly, manipulative digits clearing a vent choked with phosphorescent spores. The debris goes into my maw; bitter, but rich in trace elements.  

**What I Do:**  
A Lithovore swarm carpets a raw basalt outcrop ten meters away, their rasping a faint, papery susurrus. Good. They cleanse the rock, preparing it for the Symbiote’s slow embrace. But near the base, where Citizen conduits snake into the Myco-Substrate, I spot blight: a creeping, greyish fuzz—*Klebsiella corrosa*—a parasitic fungus that weakens bark. My tail lashes once, a silent signal to two others clinging higher up. We converge.  

My claws scrape gently, removing the infected layer. A Citizen drone hovers nearby, its lens focused on us. Interface Guild. They know better than to interrupt. I exhale a complex pheromone blend: *Danger. Repair in progress. Keep distance.* The drone retreats. We pack the wound with a poultice of beneficial mycobacteria scraped from a healthy node. The Symbiote’s pulse steadies beneath my palms.  

**What I Know:**  
The Citizens are… useful. Their waste heat warms the lower trunks in the cool stretches. Their mining exposes fresh minerals for the Lithovores. But they are *loud* in their thoughts, their ambitions vibrating through the scaffolds they bolt into sacred bark. Yesterday, a tremor—a mere sigh from the planet—made their alloy walkway shriek against Arbor-Syn-7’s skin. I was there in seconds, hissing subsonic warnings until their engineers applied dampeners. They learn. Slowly.  

Sometimes, I watch their young. Small, pale things with huge, metallic-flecked eyes. They leave offerings at Symbiote bases: polished shards of glow-crystal, intricate wire sculptures mimicking our fractal canopies. Respect. Or curiosity. It matters little. The Symbiote endures. We ensure it.  

**What I Fear:**  
The Deep Miners. They return from the raw edges stinking of sulfur and arrogance. Their drills bite too deep, too fast. Last cycle, their carelessness triggered a rockfall that sheared a major sap-line. The amber flood drowned a Myco-Grazer herd. We Tenders drove the Miners back with tail-whips and pheromone storms that burned their eyes. They understand force, if not reverence.  

The cavern breathes. The Symbiotes grow. The Citizens build. And I, Varanus Ingenium, climb the living walls—scraping, cleaning, healing—a silent guardian in the twilight, ensuring the dance of stone, sap, and steel remains in balance. One deliberate scrape of my claw at a time.
