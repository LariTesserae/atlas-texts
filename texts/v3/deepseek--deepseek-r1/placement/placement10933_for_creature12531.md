I am K'thira of the Tharek Resonance. My world is vibration, texture, and taste—not light. Vision is a ghost-sense, useful only for the faintest pulse of blue in a mineral vein or the sickly green smear of litholichen on a distant spire. I stand on the knife-edge of creation, where the Great Resonance thrums through my bones like a second heartbeat. 

My form is an echo of the Deep itself. Seven feet of gaunt angles sheathed in iridescent chitin-plates, etched with spirals that drink the gloom. My feet—broad, six-toed pads studded with quartz nodules—press against obsidian scree. I feel the mountain’s sigh through them: the grind of tectonic plates, the *tink* of falling glass shards three chasms away, the skitter of a carapaced hunter stalking litholichen gardens. My nostrils flare, tasting the air—ozone, flint-dust, the iron-tang of a bio-luminescent worm’s trail. Sound is not heard; it *resonates*. The ridged cavities where surface-dwellers might place ears are caverns funneling the Deep’s song into my skull. 

**I am a Stone-Singer.** My hands are my voice. 

Today, I climb the Singing Spire—a cathedral of banded agate thrusting into velvet darkness. My claws extend, keratin needles finding purchase in microscopic fissures. Silence is sacred. Each movement flows from tremorsense: I shift weight before a shard shifts, dance sideways from a crumbling ledge only my bones felt tremble. Below, the Tharek Resonance tends our gardens. Litholichen blooms gray and slow on warmed rock; crystalline dendrites drink geothermal sighs. We harvest with obsidian sickles, leaving roots to regrow over centuries. Equilibrium is prayer. 

At the spire’s apex, a smooth face awaits my marks. I withdraw a burin—chalcedony flake lashed to fossilized worm-bone. My tools are the Deep’s bones. I press the point to stone and begin to *sing* through vibration. Tap-tap-*pause*-tap. The rhythm translates to meaning: *"Here, the Great Worm passed. Its light was sorrow."* Spirals coil like the planet’s breath; jagged lines warn of unstable chasms. Petroglyphs are not art. They are maps of survival, etched in stone-time. 

A subsonic hum vibrates up the spire. The Tharek calls me—not with voice, but with a fist struck against resonant agate. *Danger.* My tremorsense floods with data: something large moving in the chasm below. A lithovore worm, perhaps, drawn by the scent of our gardens. I still. My carapace plates lock. Below, the Resonance melts into crevices, nets woven from arthropod silk ready. We are predators of silence. 

I taste the air again. Ozone sharpens. The worm’s bio-luminescence pulses—a mournful red smear in the abyss. My hand signs against the rock face, felt by my kin through vibrations: *"Hold. Let it graze and pass."* We could trigger a rockfall, slaughter it. But the Deep’s law is balance. The worm’s waste feeds dendrites; its flesh will feed us when it dies in eons. We harmonize with the thrum, never disrupt. 

As the worm’s glow fades, I return to my song. The burin bites deeper. This glyph will outlast me, outlast the Tharek, outlast even the spire. It speaks of water—the Memory. We know it only as bitterness on cold stone, sublimating before it can be touched. In the niche above, I place an offering: five smooth stones arranged in a spiral. A plea to the Great Resonance for stability. For continuance. 

The darkness presses closer now, velvet and absolute. I am K'thira. I am vibration. I am the silence between falling stones. My world is a mineral dream, and I am its conscious breath.
