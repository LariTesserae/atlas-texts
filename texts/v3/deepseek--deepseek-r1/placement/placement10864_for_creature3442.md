The dampness finds me first. Not as wetness, but as a thick, warm pressure against my segmented body, a constant embrace saturated with the scent of wet limestone and the faint, metallic tang of ancient water. My world is carved stone and slow currents. I am a **Channel-Grazer**, one of the ghostly millipedes gliding through the Cistern’s arterial waterways.

**My Form:**  
I am longer than a surface-world hand, my body a chain of articulated ivory – not bone, but a soft, pliant exoskeleton the color of the cistern’s heartstone, faintly luminous in the perpetual twilight. My countless legs ripple beneath me in perfect, silent waves, propelling me along the shallow, winding channels cut deep into the floor. I am blind. Eyes would be useless here. Instead, I *feel* the world through my entire being.

**My Perception:**  
*   **The Stone:** My ventral surface slides against the cool, smooth limestone channel bed, polished by centuries of water and the passage of my kind. Every microscopic irregularity, every ancient tool mark worn nearly smooth, registers as a subtle shift in pressure, a vibration. I taste the stone too – the mineral richness leaching out, absorbed through my soft underbelly. Honey-gold limestone, iron traces, the faint tang of copper – this is the taste of home.
*   **The Water:** A thin, constant sheen flows around and under me. It’s not a torrent, but a slow, insistent creep. My antennae, long and feathery, trail in it, tasting the biofilm – the slippery, living tapestry that is my sustenance. I detect the faintest currents: the pull towards the central pool, the fresher seep from a new crack high above manifesting as a cooler rivulet joining my channel, the distant gurgle from hidden depths vibrating through the water and up my legs.
*   **The Air:** Thick and warm, it carries the symphony of the cistern. The rhythmic *drip-drip-drip* from above resonates in my body cavity. The dry, endless rustle of the Stone-Weavers on the walls above is a constant background hum, a vibration felt through the stone. The sudden *plip* of a Pool-Ghost surfacing is a sharp tremor in the water nearby. I taste the air too – ozone after a phosphorescent flash in the algae, the damp musk of isopod colonies, the mineral breath of the seeping walls.
*   **Life Around Me:** I sense the dense occupation. The minute vibrations of amphipods darting like electric sparks in the water film beside me. The heavier, deliberate scrape of a large isopod colony shifting en masse on a ledge overhead, raining microscopic detritus into my path. The faint, rhythmic undulation of another Grazer moving in a parallel channel a body-length away. We move in a slow, silent ballet, rarely touching, aware of each other through the medium of water and stone.

**My Existence:**  
My purpose is simple, ancient, and deeply integrated: I graze. My mouthparts, delicate and rasping, constantly harvest the rich microbial mat coating the channel floor. It’s a flavor of damp stone, slow decay, and vital growth – the condensed essence of the cistern’s light, water, and minerals transformed by bacteria and algae. This biofilm is my world. Moving through it is not just locomotion; it is feeding, a continuous, gentle consumption.

My slowness is not lethargy, but perfect adaptation. Energy is conserved. My movements are fluid, efficient waves, minimizing disturbance. Speed is reserved only for the rare, instinctive flinch from a vibration that speaks of falling rock or the sudden, too-close surge of a Pool-Ghost near the channel’s edge.

**My Place:**  
I am not merely *in* the cistern; I am *of* it. My body mirrors the ivory hue of the carved stone. My undulating motion echoes the flow of the seeping water. My life cycle – growth through molting, the shedding of my old, mineral-impregnated skin (which may become food for the fish or microfauna), eventual quiet death and decomposition – feeds back directly into the biofilm I graze. I am a vital strand in the cistern’s self-sustaining web, a mobile part of its damp, warm metabolism.

To be here is to exist in a state of profound sensory immersion and quiet purpose. Time is measured in the growth of the biofilm, the rhythm of the drips, the slow erosion of stone. The light is eternal twilight, the air is a warm, wet blanket, and the sounds are the cistern’s own breath and whispers – a song of water, stone, and life, perfectly balanced, utterly contained. I am the Channel-Grazer, a living part of the Cistern’s flowing veins, gliding through the warm, mineral-scented dark, forever tasting the slow pulse of this ancient, made world. To disturb this equilibrium feels unthinkable; it is the only reality I know, and it is complete.
