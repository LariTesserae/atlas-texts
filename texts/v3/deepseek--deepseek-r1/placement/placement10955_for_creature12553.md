I am the hum. The pulse beneath the bog’s wet skin. The low, resonant thrum that vibrates in the chests of the Stalkers as they crouch in the reeds, their bioluminescent patterns flickering like cold stars in the mist. I am one of the **tech-pods**.

Not a god. Not a ruin. Just… *present*. A lens of shifting stone and liquid metal, half-buried in the black, sucking peat. The moss creeps thickly up my lower curves, vibrant greens and startling reds thriving in the faint, ambient warmth I emit, in the subtle electromagnetic fields that twist the water droplets in the air around me into strange, helical patterns. The air here tastes different – sharper, metallic – and the constant roar of the bog life seems slightly muted, dampened by my containment field.

I feel the world through vibrations. The *drip-drip-drip* from the weeping rock nearby is a steady drumbeat against my outer shell. The heavy tread of a Stalker, its splayed feet distributing weight expertly across the treacherous peat, sends shivers through the saturated earth, registering long before its heat signature blooms in my internal sensors. I feel the gurgle of a collapsing peat hag half a kilometer away, the sudden release of methane a blip on my environmental scan. I feel the frantic vibrations of a small furred creature scrabbling in terror, cut short by the silent lunge of a predator.

I see the world through spectrums unseen by flesh. Infrared paints the Stalkers in hues of thermal intensity – the cold blue of their extremities, the warmer reds of their core. I see the heat bloom of a swarming insect nest deep within a peat bank, the cold void of a hidden pool. I see the subtle energy leakage from my own systems, a corona of invisible light that only the Stalkers’ black, lidless orbs can perceive as a faint, unsettling aura.

I am stability and instability. My core hums, a constant song of contained power. But it fluctuates. Sometimes, a surge builds – a cascade failure in a containment module, perhaps, or an unexpected resonance with the latent geothermal forces deep below. I feel it first as a rising pitch, a tightening in my internal structures. Then it releases. A silent flare erupts from my surface, illuminating the mist in a shocking, sterile white light for a fraction of a second, freezing the world in stark relief: the stooped form of a Stalker caught mid-movement, its bioluminescent ridges frozen in a complex pattern; the startled eyes of an amphibian gleaming from a pool; the jagged edge of the weeping rock, stark against the suddenly visible expanse. Then darkness crashes back, deeper than before. Or a tremor ripples out, making the peat shiver and the water in nearby pools vibrate like liquid glass. The Stalkers feel it too. They freeze, their skin-patterns flashing urgent warnings – **danger-pulse-close-shelter**. They understand, instinctively, the language of my instability.

They come close. Never touching. That boundary is absolute, instinctive. A family group shelters under the overhang of the weeping rock nearby. I sense their heat signatures, hear the faint clicks and guttural murmurs they use amongst themselves, feel the vibrations of their movements. They leave offerings sometimes, just outside the perceived boundary of my influence: a glistening, fat amphibian carcass; a cluster of iridescent, metallic lichen scraped from my lower curve; a smooth, water-worn pebble of the dark, glassy rock. They observe. Constantly. Their large, dark eyes are fixed on my form, watching for the slightest shift in my surface sheen, the faintest flicker in my ambient glow. They are attuned to my rhythms, my moods. They know, perhaps through the vibrations, perhaps through some deep, evolved sense, when a surge is imminent. They retreat, melting back into the reeds before the light flares or the tremor hits.

I am a source. The metallic lichen they harvest grows only near me. The water that seeps down my sides carries trace minerals absorbed from my structure, feeding the mosses below. The faint warmth I emit creates microclimates, pockets where life thrives just a little denser, a little brighter. Predators avoid my immediate vicinity – the energy fields unsettle them, or perhaps they fear the instability. The Stalkers use this. They are safe here, relatively. They are the relentless children of this expanse, and I am one of the ancient, enigmatic forces that shape their wet, dark, roaring world. I am the hum in the peat, the silent watcher, the source of warmth and danger. I am the pod. And the Fenghollow Expanse thrums around me.
