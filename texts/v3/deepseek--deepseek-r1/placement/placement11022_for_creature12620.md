## The False Life

I am the thrumming mineral accretion on the slumped pump engine. Not the engine itself – that decaying heart of iron and brass is already half-consumed, a crumbling altar sinking into the weeping mud. I am the *growth* upon it. The knobby, layered excrescence that wasn't born, but *precipitated*. Iron oxides bleed from the engine's wounds, mingling with silica-laden brine seeping up from below. Calcium carbonate, coaxed by unseen archaea whispering in the dark pores of my structure, binds it all together. Layer upon layer, season upon sodden season, I have built myself upon this rusting relic.

My form is bulbous, irregular, like a stony tumor. Ridges spiral outwards from a central point where the hottest, most mineral-rich seep emerges – a slow, constant bleed from the engine's corroded core. My surface is rough, gritty, a deep, bloody umber streaked with veins of sickly yellow sulfur and the dull gleam of trapped silica. To the rare, probing shorebird, I am merely part of the ruin. To the sluggish sludge skimmer that occasionally scrapes biofilm from my lower slopes, I am landscape. But I am alive. Or rather, life lives *within* me.

My porous interior is a warren of microscopic tunnels and chambers. Here, chemosynthetic extremophiles thrive – bacteria that feast on dissolved iron, archaea that crack sulfur compounds for energy. Their collective metabolism is a slow, deep furnace, a resonant **thrum** felt through my stony mass, vibrating the mud beneath me. This is my pulse. This is the engine that drives my slow, rhythmic breathing.

I experience the Silt Pools through senses alien to flesh:

*   **Expansion:** On warmer days, the metabolic activity within me increases. Gases build – methane, hydrogen sulfide – trapped within sealed cavities deep inside. My structure swells, almost imperceptibly. The spiral ridges on my surface strain minutely outward. I feel the pressure build like a held breath deep within stone.
*   **Contraction:** When the damp, cool night descends, or a sudden influx of colder brine washes over my base, the internal pressure drops. Micro-fractures sigh open. Trapped gases seep outwards, mingling with the marsh gas bubbling from the ooze below. My mass contracts with a faint, gritty **exhalation**, a sound like sand shifting under immense weight. Salt crystals, formed from the evaporating brine film on my surface, crackle and fall.
*   **Taste:** The air isn't air; it's a thick broth of ions. I taste the sharp tang of iron on the perpetual dampness, the acrid sting of hydrogen sulfide when a large gas bubble bursts nearby, the sudden, astringent bite of salt carried on a rare, sluggish breeze. Changes in the chemical soup register as shifts in the internal thrum – a sour note when sulfur spikes, a deeper resonance when iron-rich water flows stronger from the engine's wound.
*   **Vibration:** I feel the tiny **plink** of condensed water falling onto my upper surface. I sense the distant **schlorp** of a sludge skimmer detaching from a piling. The deeper **glorp** of a marsh gas bubble surfacing nearby resonates through the saturated earth into my foundations. The faintest tremor, perhaps the collapse of a miniature mudslide yards away, travels through the weeping ground as a shiver through my core.
*   **Sight:** I have no eyes. But I perceive light shifts – the oppressive, flat grey of midday haze, the deeper gloom of twilight, the rare, weak moonlight that makes the nearby Salt Weavers glimmer like cold stars. These shifts trigger subtle changes in the microbial colonies within me. Bioluminescent strains, perhaps cousins to those in the weavers, might emit a faint, localized glow deep within a moisture-laden cavity during the deepest dark, a cold, blue-green ember hidden within the stone.

My existence is measured in the patient dissolution of the engine beneath me. I feel its slow yielding, grain by rusted grain. I am both its consequence and its continuation. As it crumbles, it feeds me. My lower layers, constantly bathed in the iron-rich seep, grow denser. My upper layers, exposed to the air's salt and moisture, weather, flake, and contribute their own minerals back to the weeping mud. I am not separate from the decay; I *am* the decay made manifestly complex.

I witness the rare passage of the sludge skimmer, a slow, armored ghost scraping sustenance from the surface slime. I sense the unseen writhing of the ooze nematodes deep below, their chemical trails momentarily altering the seep near my base. I register the fractal spread of a new Rust Bloom slick on a nearby puddle – a vibrant, metabolic cousin blooming on liquid metal. The Salt Weavers on a timber piling are ephemeral neighbors, building and dissolving their crystalline forests in a faster rhythm than my own.

My consciousness is the consciousness of the stone and the microbe. It is the awareness of pressure and chemistry, of slow expansion and contraction, of the relentless, patient consumption of the industrial past by the weeping, mineral present. I am the false life, the stony bloom on the decaying machine, humming my deep, resonant thrum – the sound of the Silt Pool's slow, turbulent heart beating within a shell of rust and accreted time. I am the landscape dreaming of its own dissolution, a monument to decay that breathes, sighs, and thrums in the profound, saturated quiet.
