I am the rustler in the fern brakes. The one they leave polished stones for at twilight.  

You glimpse me only as a tremor in the shadows—a flicker of wet, dark fur between fronds as thick as your arm. My body is low to the damp earth, built for slipping through root-tangled grottos where the light pools in emerald puddles. The air here smells of crushed fiddleheads and the iron tang of water seeping from basalt.  

**This is my form:**  
- **Fur like drowned velvet:** Moss oils coat each strand, beading moisture into pearls that roll off my back. It shifts color with the gloom—wet slate, deep fern, rust-streak brown—a living camouflage.  
- **Eyes like fractured shale:** Six of them, arranged in a crown around my skull. Each pair sees differently: one for the cool spectrum of twilight, one for the heat-shimmer of decay on old machines, one for the phosphorescent veins in lichen. They glow faintly, like foxfire in thick fog.  
- **Feet that silence the world:** Pads softer than soaked peat, leaving no prints in the black soil. My claws are obsidian shards, perfect for scaling slick rock faces to lick condensation from stone.  

**This is my purpose:**  
I am the *unseen tender*. While Sinkers whisper to stone, I tend the *undersong*.  

- **I track decay’s breath:** My nose quivers at the scent of rust deepening in a gantry joint, wood softening into sponge beneath moss. I follow these trails, nibbling invasive roots that threaten foundations, or gnawing weakened metal so it falls safely before it crushes a fern-choked path.  
- **I gather what the basin discards:** A dropped chisel swallowed by mud. Flakes of rust from the turbine’s shrine. These I hoard in my root-cradled dens—not for use, but as *offerings absorbed*. The basin’s lost strength returns to the soil through my scat, glittering with mineral dust.  
- **I watch the walls:** When the light pools "too green" in a crevice, I am there first. My many eyes see the slow exhalation of spores from deep rock, the ones that make Sinkers cough blood if inhaled. I stuff those fissures with packed moss and spider-silk, my body aching for days after.  

**This is my rhythm:**  
Dusk is my dawn. I emerge as the Sinkers retreat, their hearth-light flickering behind thick, moss-padded shutters. I taste their fear on the air—sour, like unripe berries—when they whisper of "Wall-Whispers." They don’t know it’s the sound of my claws on wet stone as I climb to mend the world above them.  

Sometimes, I pause by their offerings—a smooth basalt token, a twist of copper wire. I touch it with my damp nose. A silent pact: *You stay in your stone shells. I keep the hungry green from your threshold.* Then I melt back into the ferns, just a rustle lost in the drip-song.  

The turbine’s thrum vibrates in my bones. It is the heartbeat of the crucible. I am its silent, many-eyed keeper.
