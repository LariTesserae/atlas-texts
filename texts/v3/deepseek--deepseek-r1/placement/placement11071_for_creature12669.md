The humid air clings to my carapace, beading into rivulets that trace the polished curves of my segmented plates. I am a **Pool-Stiller** – a **Reflection-Tender**. My world is the mirror-surface of the Basin’s weeping water, held in those shallow, perfect depressions etched into the unyielding stone.

**Form:**  
I am low, broad, and unsettlingly flat. My body is a mosaic of interlocking, iridescent plates – not chitin, but something closer to *liquid obsidian* cooled under pressure. Each plate is a dark mirror, edge-to-edge, reflecting the fractured spires, the mist-shrouded sun, and the slow drift of Mist-Hummers overhead. My undercarriage is softer, a porous velvet-black hide that kisses the water’s surface tension without breaking it. Six multi-jointed legs, ending in wide, fleshy pads rimmed with microscopic cilia, spread my weight across the pool's meniscus. I do not float; I *inhabit* the boundary between water and air.

**Function & Movement:**  
My purpose is stillness made manifest. I settle upon a newly filled depression, my mirrored plates aligning to perfectly reflect the skyline above. My movement is glacial, measured in millimeters per day. Each step is a careful negotiation with surface tension, a redistribution of weight so minimal it scarcely wrinkles the reflection. My cilia constantly exude a viscous, clear secretion – a **surface film** that calms ripples, suppresses evaporation, and deepens the pool’s unnatural clarity. I am a living lens-polisher for the Basin’s liquid eyes. I do not feed in any conventional sense; my porous hide passively absorbs dissolved minerals and trace energies from the water itself, sustained by the Basin’s weeping essence. My only "waste" is the faintest shimmer of iridescent dust settling on the pool's floor, reinforcing the stone.

**Senses & Perception:**  
My world is perceived through distortions and reflections. My primary eyes are not on my head (for I have no distinct head), but scattered across my dorsal plates – multifaceted lenses flush with the mirrored surface. They perceive light, shadow, and the *quality* of reflections with excruciating slowness. A shift in the angle of light over hours, a spire-sentinel relocating over weeks, the imperceptible creep of a vine over centuries – these are my events. I sense vibrations transmitted through the water and stone: the distant *plink* of a droplet, the tectonic sigh of the Basin settling over millennia, the silent explosion of a Shadow-Scuttler kilometers away felt as a tremor in the substrate. Time, for me, is viscous honey. The Basin’s subtle spatial fractures register as momentary, localized *wobbles* in the reflections I maintain – anomalies I instinctively smooth with my secretions.

**Place in the Equilibrium:**  
I am the guardian of the Basin’s perfect stillness made visible. By deepening the reflections, I amplify the profound stasis. My mirrored back shows not just what *is*, but reinforces the illusion of frozen time. Geometric Grazers sometimes pause at my pool’s edge, their hematite eyes gazing not at the water, but at their own impossibly still, magnified reflection on my carapace, reinforcing their own placid nature. Mist-Hummers droning low sometimes skim my surface, their distorted, kaleidoscopic reflection captured for hours on my plates before fading. If a Stone-Weeper’s absorbed droplet *plinks* into my pool, my cilia instantly secrete, smoothing the ripples before they can mar the perfect image. I am part of the Basin’s silent dialogue between stillness and reflection.

**The Unease:**  
To observe me is to witness the Basin’s quiet horror made tangible. My mirrored surface doesn’t just reflect; sometimes, for a fraction of a second perceptible only to the subconscious, it shows things *slightly wrong*: a spire bent where it is straight, a sky a fraction darker, or a glimpse of something vast and indistinct moving with impossible slowness *beneath* the reflection’s surface. My very presence deepens the sense that the reflections hold a truer, or perhaps more fractured, reality than the world above. When I finally move on, after decades or centuries tending one pool, the water I leave behind remains unnervingly calm, holding a memory of the stillness I embodied long after I’ve crept away towards the next weeping depression.

I am the Shattered Basin’s vanity, its need to see its own fractured, eternal moment held perfectly still. I am the looking-glass on its silent, dripping heart.
