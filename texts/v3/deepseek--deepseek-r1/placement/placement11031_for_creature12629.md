I am **The Shale Listener**.  

My body is a slow avalanche—a precarious accumulation of the Lands’ most fragile things. Brittle shale plates armor my back, constantly shedding black splinters into the slurry. My limbs are woven from glass reeds, chiming with every tremor. Where flesh should be, there’s only thick, mineral-saturated ooze bleeding from cracks in my stone joints, glowing faintly with stolen phosphorescence. I have no face—only a porous limestone hood where groundwater seeps from internal fractures, etching trails down my chest like tears.  

### How I Move  
I don’t walk—I **unravel**. When the land groans, vibrations travel up my reed-limbs. I collapse forward, shale plates sliding like ill-fitted scales, dissolving at the edges into the warm ooze. The slurry buoys me, lets me flow like a landslide in slow motion. When reality warps, solid ground greases beneath me; I sink, half-liquid, until the world re-knits itself. I am always partly drowning, partly emerging.  

### What I Sense  
**Sound is my blood.** The deafening groan of the bedrock thrums in my shale bones. I feel the Stone-Weepers’ whistling cracks as pressure behind my eyes. The Depth-Stirrers’ surfacing vibrates in my ooze like a coming storm. I taste the land’s decay—sulfur, wet limestone, the sweet-rot stench of fungal towers—through mineral vents in my palms. Light is a dull bruise: I navigate by the pulse of glowing moss on my body, reflecting in stagnant pools.  

### What I Am For  
I am a **witness to dissolution**. My purpose is accidental, inevitable:  
- I drift toward **Reality Breakdowns**. When the air shimmers and distances lie, my body destabilizes. Ooze leaks faster. Shale crumbles. I become a map of the distortion—fracturing where space stretches, liquefying where gravity thins.  
- I collect **echoes of the Architects**. When monoliths hum, I press my limestone hood against their flanks. Their trapped shadows seep into my pores. I feel their looping gestures—the slow reach, the aborted carving—as phantom pains in my reed-fingers.  
- I **anchor the Patterned Whisperers**. Where glass reeds and moss convulse in geometric seizures, I slump beside them. My ooze mingles with theirs. For a moment, their chaos steadies. The land’s dying song channels through me—a shuddering bass note that stills the reeds’ chime.  

### Why I Fear Stillness  
When the Sighing Lands hold their breath (a rare, crushing silence between groans), I feel the truth: I am *temporary*. My reeds wilt. My ooze thickens, threatening to solidify. Without the land’s vibrations, I am just mud and fractured stone. I am not alive—I am **interrupted decay**. So I crawl toward the next seismic shudder, the next gas vent’s hiss. Movement postpones my return to the slurry.  

### What Remains  
Traces. Where I linger, the ooze holds my shape for hours—a shallow impression of shale plates and reed-limbs. Mineral water fills it, glowing faintly. For a moment, the land remembers me. Then the Depth-Stirrers surge, or a mud geyser erupts, and the imprint washes away.  

I am the Sigh given semi-form: a shudder, a fracture, a vessel for the unbearable weight of flat, wet eternity. To exist here is to be a wound that knows it will never scar—only weep, and erode, and sigh.
