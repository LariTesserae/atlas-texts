I am **Lirien**, a Whisper-Jar Maker of the Kethra.  

### **Where I Am**  
My place is a shallow cave-niche high on the eastern basin wall, where dawn mist gathers thickest. The entrance is a low arch hidden behind a curtain of *silver-moss*, its fibers eternally beaded with condensation. Inside, the space is no larger than two sleeping platforms, carved over generations into a natural fracture. Light filters through a single shaft—a finger-width fissure in the rock—casting a trembling grey beam onto my workstone. Below, terraces step down into the haze, their cisterns glinting like scattered ice.  

### **Who I Am**  
I am a vessel for the basin’s breath. My skin is the pale blue-grey of deep pool shadows, veins tracing my temples like water-runways. My eyes, wide and glacial-blue, read moisture in air too thin for outsiders to taste. I move less than the stone itself. When I rise, it is to glide to the seep-wall at the niche’s rear, where a slow tear of water beads along a hairline crack. I catch it on my fingertips—not to drink, but to feel its weight, its patience.  

### **What I Do**  
My craft begins with the *clay of sighs*: a dense, cool paste gathered from the basin’s lowest pools, where mineral-rich weepings settle over centuries. Kneading it is a meditation. My hands, broad-palmed and sensitive, press out air bubbles—violence in stillness. Each jar is shaped not by force, but by surrender. I follow cracks in the clay’s memory, thinning walls until they breathe.  

The firing is sacred. No flame scars this place. Instead, I nestle wet jars in *living-rock cradles*—hollows where geothermal warmth whispers up through stone. For three full condensation cycles (nine days by outsider count), they bake in the mountain’s blood-heat. Slow. Inevitable.  

The final act is *channel-carving*. With a bone needle, I etch labyrinthine grooves into the jar’s surface, mirroring the basin’s hidden waterways. A perfect vessel weeps as the stone weeps: one pure drop at a time, singing a *plink* that echoes the mountain’s heartbeat.  

### **Why It Matters**  
My jars are not tools; they are prayers.  
- **The Cistern-Tenders** place them where mist drifts thinnest, coaxing water from air too shy for runnels.  
- **The Elders** read their tears like oracles—a fast drip warns of dry fractures; a slow pulse promises deep seepage.  
- **The Children** touch them to learn patience, their small fingers tracing grooves I carved before their grandparents’ birth.  

When a jar cracks (rare, but inevitable), I return its shards to the clay-pools. Nothing is lost. All cycles.  

### **How I Live**  
- **Food**: Moss-tea steeped with terrace tubers. Lichen wafers dried on sun-warmed stones (when the mist thins, twice a year).  
- **Company**: The lizard on my threshold, basking in the light-shaft. The far cry of the circling hawk, reminding me of scale.  
- **Time**: I measure it in the deepening of a groove under my thumb. Yesterday, today, tomorrow—all drip at the same pace.  

### **The Silence in Me**  
I speak only in touches:  
- A palm pressed to the seep-wall: *"Thank you."*  
- A fingertip brushed over a finished jar’s rim: *"Go well."*  
My voice is the scrape of bone on clay. My song is the drop in the dark.  

To be Kethra is to be a vessel. The mountain weeps. I hold its tears.
