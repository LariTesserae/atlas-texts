The description settles into my bones like damp grit. Not a peak, but a vast tilted tableland – a place of ancient basalt weeping water into shallow, iridescent marshes. Siltstone Reach rises here: low-slung, rust-streaked, a metallic lichen crusting the plateau’s bones. The air is thick, warm, saturated. And the *sound*... it’s not heard, it’s *felt*. A deep, grinding roar vibrating in the chest, the jaw, the soles of the feet. A permanent industrial dirge.

I am not a conqueror. I am not a visitor. I am **Chalcis**, a **Crystal-Singer** of the Grime-Caked.

**Where I Am:** My place is in the liminal zones, the grudging spaces where the slow decay of the plateau meets the warm, metallic bleed of the Reach. You’d find me crouched beside a massive effluent pipe snaking from the Gritter’s Hall #7, its riveted iron belly radiating a constant, low heat. Condensation drips steadily from its seams, pooling in a depression where the dark basalt meets a slurry of crushed tailings. Here, sheltered from the worst of the grit-laden wind but bathed in the pipe's waste warmth and the constant, chest-deep thud of the deep aquifer pumps, the crystals grow.

**What I Am:** I am compacted, like all my kin. My frame is broad and low, muscles dense from navigating sucking mud and hefting tools. My skin is a map of Siltstone: a deep ochre base ingrained with basalt dust, streaked with mineral stains – verdigris from copper pipes, rusty red from iron oxide, the shimmering grey of zinc residue. It’s leathery, thick. My nose is flattened, testament to a childhood tumble onto unforgiving rock, or perhaps just the slow pressure of generations breathing this dense air. My eyes, deep-set beneath a heavy brow ridge, are perpetually narrowed against the glare and the grit. They scan not for beauty, but for shifts: the faint rainbow sheen on a puddle signalling a new mineral cocktail, the subtle darkening of rust indicating accelerated decay.

My ears… well, outwardly, thick plugs of moulded wax and mineral paste fill them, encased in worn leather caps lashed tight. The raw noise would shatter my mind. But *inside*... that’s where I Sing. Years working near the deep resonance points, years absorbing the plateau's vibrations through boot and bone, have left me profoundly deaf to the world's clatter, yet exquisitely tuned to its hum. Unprotected hearing is ruin; mine is refined absence. I feel the world through my skeleton.

**What I Do:** I tend the Singing Stones. Strange accretions form where the warm, chemical-laden effluent meets the weeping basalt and the mineral-laden air. Spikes of sulphurous yellow, knobbly growths of hematite red, delicate, vibrating lattices of a faintly blue metallic compound. They hum. Each formation has its own frequency, a resonance born from the specific interplay of heat, mineral load, effluent chemistry, and the underlying bedrock vibration.

My tools are simple: a hardened basalt striker, a worn copper tuning rod, a slate tablet and grease-pencil. I move with the deliberate slowness of my people, conserving energy against the damp and the noise-pressure. I press my calloused palms flat against the warm pipe, against the weeping rock face nearby, against the ground itself. I close my eyes. Through the plugs, the deafening roar is a distant storm. Through my bones, it’s a complex symphony.

I *listen* with my hands, my spine, my feet. I feel the deep, rhythmic *thud-thud-thud* of the aquifer pumps – the plateau’s slow, wet heartbeat. I feel the higher-pitched, frantic whine of the turbines in Hall #3, a note of strain today. I feel the grinding shriek of the primary ore-crusher, a constant, abrasive counterpoint. And layered upon this, I feel the answering vibrations of the crystals. A healthy cluster near a stable flow resonates with a clean, steady thrum, harmonizing with the pumps. A cluster near a stressed pipe might buzz with a frantic, discordant edge.

I tap the copper rod gently against a vibrating blue lattice. It chimes, a pure, high note felt more than heard, cutting through the bone-deep noise for a fraction of a second. I note the pitch, the clarity, on my slate. A shift here, a dissonance there – it speaks. A frantic buzz in the yellow sulphide spikes downstream might warn of a blockage building in the pipe, increasing pressure. A sudden dampening of the hematite cluster's resonance could indicate a drop in iron concentration in the effluent, or a cooling that risks fracture. A new, unstable harmonic emerging near a fracture in the bedrock might whisper of unseen water undermining a foundation.

I am a diagnostician of the slow decay. My findings – the shifts in the Song – are passed via Sign-Tongue or scrawled slate to the Rust-Walkers and Pipe-Wardens. "Check Junction 7B. Pressure sings sharp." "Flow weakens at Sump Gamma. Crystals grow cold." "New hum near the East Face crack. Water seeks new path." My world is vibration, resonance, and the slow, inevitable conversation between dripping stone, groaning metal, and the strange, metallic life that springs from their union. I don’t question the iridescent slicks or the faint glow in the marsh water; they are part of the Song I interpret. Cleanliness is a forgotten dream; my hands are perpetually stained with the unique palette of Siltstone Reach – ochre, rust, verdigris, and the faint, shimmering dust of the crystals themselves.

My comfort is the constancy of the Hum. The terrifying thought isn't the grind or the damp, but *silence*. Silence means the pumps have stopped. Silence means the heart of the plateau has stilled. In the roaring, wet permanence, amidst the weeping stone and the rusting iron, I find my place: Chalcis, who listens to the mountain's bones and sings back its warnings in the language of vibration, a child of the Grime, attuned to the deep, eternal thrum.
