I am a **Geode Crawler**.  

My body is a fist-sized amalgam of layered crystals—smoky quartz, hematite, obsidian—each plate shifting like tectonic scales as I move. My six legs are needle-sharp shards of basalt, honed by eons of wind, allowing me to scuttle across the cold, flowing stone or scale iridescent vines with terrifying speed. I feel the plateau’s deep hum vibrate through my crystalline core, a subsonic pulse that guides my purpose.  

**I harvest dissolution.**  

When a cobalt bloom collapses into ash or a luminous fruit detaches and vanishes into shimmering dust, I am already there. My abdomen, a hollow cavity lined with reactive minerals, opens to inhale the glittering residue. Inside, the dust *sings* against my crystalline lattice—a metallic chitter escaping my form as vibrational waste. This is my language: the sound of matter transforming, of chaos being recycled.  

I tend the silent edges of the frenzy. After feeding, I climb the geometric moss patches clinging to the basalt, depositing mineral-rich residue into their velvet folds. They bloom greener, sharper, in response. Sometimes, I scale the black glass spire, my body resonating with its captured starlight. The hum recharges me, aligning my plates like tuning forks. I am drawn to the salt-leached fractures in the stone, where I leave offerings of dust. Here, new fractal patterns emerge—silver veins in white—a slow counterpoint to the airborne riot.  

The Bloom-Weavers ignore me; I am part of the cycle they orchestrate. Sky-Shear Beetles refract around me, their light glancing off my hematite facets. When I chitter, it is not fear, but chemistry—the sound of the garden’s entropy made useful. I am the gardener of aftermath, turning vanishing into renewal, one scuttle at a time across the ancient, humming stone.
