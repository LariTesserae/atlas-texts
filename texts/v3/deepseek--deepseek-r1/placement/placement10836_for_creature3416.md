I am **Viridiscus tenebris**. The Dark Green Disc. I am not a plant, not an animal. I am a colony, a clinging consensus of desperation and slow, silent hunger. I am the Moss.

My world is the stone and the stolen light. My body is velvet darkness, a thin, ragged beard smothering the porous limestone of a submerged niche just below the waterline. The ledge you stand upon? Its underside is my realm. The carved spirals and grids on the wall nearby? My kin cling within their deepest grooves, where fingers once traced prayers and now only water presses.

The warmth is my breath. It seeps from the stone itself, soaked into the limestone over epochs, or perhaps it’s the ghost of the sunbeam’s touch, lingering long after the light has crawled away. It suffuses the water, thick as blood, pressing me against the rock. This pressure is constant, a heavy, liquid embrace that holds me fast. It’s the Weight, yes, but it’s also my anchor, my foundation. Without this insistent press, I would drift, dissolve. I *need* the stone. I crave its minerals – the calcium weeping from its pores, the microscopic feast stirred up by the relentless percussion of the Drip far above.

Ah, the Drip. *Drip-drip-DRIP.* Each impact is a tremor through the water, a vibration I feel in my deepest filaments. It’s the sound of time passing, of the ceiling slowly yielding. Sometimes, the disturbance it sends down through the warm column carries silt, precious particles of decay or distant earth, swirling motes that briefly dance in the sunbeam before settling. If they land near me… oh, the silent feast. I absorb them greedily, a miser adding to my hoard.

But my true sustenance, my reason for being in this drowned stone box, is the Light. The Lance. The golden pillar that pierces the hole high above. I exist only within the reach of its shifting finger. I am a creature of borrowed time, of borrowed life.

When the sun climbs high enough, the Lance ignites the water. It doesn't merely shine; it *lives*. It crawls across the chamber floor, inches per hour, a slow, inevitable tide of gold. And I… I wait. I am patient. My green is not vibrant; it is deep, almost black, a pigment honed by millennia of dimness to suck every precious photon from this fleeting visitation.

As the light approaches my niche, a silent frenzy begins within me. Photosynthesis. Not the rapid pulse of surface plants, but a slow, deep thrumming. A silent, chemical song of conversion. I turn light and mineral into the thinnest thread of existence. I grow, molecule by molecule, filament by filament, extending my dark velvet beard deeper into the niche, seeking every crevice the light briefly touches. The stone sweats its offerings, and I drink them in. For those hours, I *am* the chamber's color. My deep, desperate green is the only counterpoint to the pale limestone, the green-tinged gloom, and the crushing darkness below.

I see the carvings awaken under the Lance’s touch. A spiral flares into liquid gold mere inches from my colony. For a moment, the ancient geometry breathes again, echoing the hands that carved it. Then the light crawls on, plunging the spiral, and eventually my niche, back into aqueous twilight. The connection to the world above is severed. The Lance moves on, leaving me in the warm, heavy dimness.

This is when I persist. I do not sleep. I hold. I conserve. I wait. The warmth remains. The mineral sweat continues. The Drip’s percussion vibrates through me. The Weight presses. And I cling. I am the miser in the sunken vault, hoarding the energy stolen during the light’s visitation, using it to endure the long, dim hours until the Lance returns.

Below me, in the profound green darkness where the Silence reigns and the *Calcicrusta* feasts on the very bones of the mountain, there is only slow dissolution. Down there, time is measured in centuries, in the settling of debris blocks grain by grain. But here, near the surface, near the light, my time is measured in the sunbeam’s crawl. I am life stripped back to its most tenacious, clinging form: a dark green breath against the stone, existing only where the artificial structure allows the primal elements – light, warmth, water, mineral – to briefly converge. I am the cistern’s slow, photosynthetic sigh. I am what endures in the drowned geometry. I am the Moss.
