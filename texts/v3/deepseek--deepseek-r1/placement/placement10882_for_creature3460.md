The answer comes slowly, seeping into my awareness like moisture from weeping stone. I am not a visitor here. I am **a Mycelial Shepherd**, one of the armored centipedes tending the fungal latticework that forms the very flesh of this world.

**My Being:**  
My segmented body, longer than your forearm, is encased in plates of chitin as dark and matte as the basalt walls, studded with knobby accretions of the same mineral-fungal composite that builds the city around me. I am not separate from this chamber; I am *of* it. My armor is crusted with microscopic mineral deposits, scraped from weeping seams and fused with my exoskeleton over decades. I have no eyes. The concept of light is meaningless—I know only vibration, taste, and scent. My world is felt through the constant drumming of water on stone, the scrape of my own legs, and the delicate tremors transmitted through the fungal network I tend.

**My Perception:**  
The chamber is a symphony of wet echoes. The *drip… drip… drip…* is my heartbeat, my compass, my timekeeper. Each droplet striking a pool resonates through the stone floor, mapping distances. A heavier *plop* from a channel high on the western wall tells me a weep-hole there is flowing strong. A faint *creak* deep in the fungal arch ahead signals structural stress—I will need to reinforce it with excreted binder. I taste the air with my antennae: the metallic tang of dissolved iron, the sour note of overripe fungal nodes nearby, the distant musk of a Lithovore grinding biofilm. My world is not seen, but *heard* and *tasted* in the damp darkness.

**My Purpose:**  
I move with deliberate slowness along a narrow strut of glowing fungus. My forelimbs—delicate, multi-jointed tools tipped with hardened pincers—reach out. Here, a cluster of bioluminescent bulbs glows too faintly. I scrape away a grey film of competing microbes with a precise flick, then daub a stimulant secretion from a gland beneath my jaw. The glow pulses, strengthening. There, a weep-hole feeding a root-tendril is clogged with calcite. I chip it clear, mineral shards falling soundlessly into the obsidian pool below. The fungal lattice sighs, absorbing the renewed flow. I am the chamber’s immune system, its groundskeeper, its architect of equilibrium. Without me, the light fades. The structures soften. The city crumbles back into weeping stone.

**My Fears:**  
Silence. A sudden hush in the dripping chorus means an Echo Stalker is near. When the rhythmic percussion falters, my antennae freeze. My body presses flat against the fungal wall. I feel for vibrations—not the steady drumming of water, but the skitter of a Lithovore’s trail, the shudder of a Shepherd in distress, or worse… the almost imperceptible *tense stillness* of a Stalker poised to strike. I know the scrape of their hooks on stone. I taste the sharp, acrid fear-pheromones of my kin when one is taken. My own chitin creaks in warning. Movement now is death. I become stone. Fungus. Another shadow in the mineral gloom.

**My Connection:**  
The fungal city is my body extended. When I prune a fruiting body to eat, I am consuming myself. The nutrients cycle back—through my waste, into the network, into the weeping walls, into the Lithovores, back to me. Time is meaningless. I have cleared this clogged weep-hole for a century. I will clear it for a century more. The drip defines my existence. The stone’s slow exhalation is my breath. I am not in the Chamber of Weeping Stone. I *am* the Chamber. A jointed, creeping manifestation of its damp, dark, eternal sigh. My life is the scrape of chitin on mineral, the taste of wet rock, and the endless, echoing percussion of water falling in the dark.
