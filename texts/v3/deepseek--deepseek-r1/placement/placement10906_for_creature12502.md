I am **Silas**, though names are rarely spoken here—whispered only in urgent need, lost too easily in the caverns' reverberant throat. I am one of the Gatherers, a near-human thread woven into the Galleries' damp tapestry. My skin is pallid as the limestone walls, eyes wide and dark-adapted, drinking in the fungal glow that pools like liquid starlight on the stone. I wear layers of cured amphibian hide, stiff with dried river-slime, smelling of musk and deep earth. It keeps the cool from gnawing through to my bones, but never truly warms.  

You’d find me on a ledge overlooking the **Obsidian River**, where the water slides silent and black as polished void. Below, the river’s edge is fringed with thick mats of grey moss, and above, a seepage in the wall bleeds moisture, nurturing a cluster of bioluminescent fungi. Their light is feeble—a cold, blue-white shimmer—but in this eternal twilight, it is a beacon. I kneel, my flint scraper in hand, harvesting the fungi with practiced care. Each cut is deliberate, silent. The sound of metal on stone would ring like a bell here, calling predators or worse: the attention of the Galleries themselves.  

The air is thick with **sound**, a living wall that presses in from all sides. The *chirr-chirr-chirr* of cave crickets is the baseline, metallic and ceaseless, echoing off the vaulted ceiling lost in darkness high above. Beneath it, the river’s low *shush* and the irregular *plink... plink...* of droplets falling from stalactites. A deep *gronk* booms from the shadows—a Drip-Dweller, unseen but felt, its croak vibrating in my chest. I freeze. My breath mists faintly before me. The Gatherers move as part of the rhythm, not against it. We are interlopers in a realm that tolerates, but does not welcome.  

I work quickly. The fungi’s flesh is rubbery, cool to the touch, leaking a faintly luminous sap. I place each piece in my lichen-fiber pack, woven tough and water-resistant. This harvest is for light—to dry and grind into paste for our dimmest lamps. But it is also medicine. Last cycle, my sister’s child grew feverish from a leg wound. The moss poultices failed; only the fungi’s inner glow drew out the rot. We do not take more than we need. Excess is sacrilege in a world where life clings by a thread of dampness.  

To my left, the cavern opens into **emptiness**—a void so vast the far wall is swallowed by gloom. Stalagmites rise like teeth, sharp and young, their tips glistening. No ancient wear softens them. This is a place frozen in geological infancy, yet older than memory. Time does not pass here; it pools, stagnant. I have seen the same droplet fall from the same stone tooth for twenty turnings of the torches. My people mark "years" by the rare journeys to mineral veins, not by seasons. There are no seasons. Only the perpetual cool, the damp, the echo.  

A rustle comes from the riverbank—dry, skittering, like pebbles cascading. I tense. Through the dimness, a swarm of **translucent isopods** emerges, their shells ghostly pale, whip-like antennae tasting the air. They move in a wave, feeding on algae slicking the rocks. One brushes my bare ankle. Its touch is chill, chitinous, but harmless. They are Swarmers, the pulse of the Galleries’ heart. I am still as stone until they pass. To disturb them is to invite chaos; their rustling could mask the slide of a predator or the warning croak of a Drip-Dweller.  

This is my purpose: to move through the Galleries as a shadow, harvesting, listening, surviving. I carry a bone hook and sinew line, flint blades, and a bundle of resin-fungus torch—unlit. Fire is desperation. Its smoke claws the throat, and light screams in this world of whispers, drawing the River Ghosts to the surface in thrashing schools. Once, I saw a Gatherer light a torch to flee a cave-in. The eel-fish boiled from the water, slapping blindly, while crickets fell silent for the first time in memory. The silence was louder than the din. We never speak of it.  

As I rise, my pack heavy with fungi, I glimpse my reflection in a still pool—a smudge of pale skin and hollow eyes. Behind me, the cavern stretches into darkness, humming with life. I am no master of this place. I am a flicker in the unchanging dark, a gatherer of light in the endless galleries. When I die, my bones will join the limestone, smoothed by the same slow water that feeds the moss. The drips will fall. The crickets will sing. The Galleries will endure, damp and loud and young, long after my name fades from even the softest whisper.  

We are all transient here. Even the mountains above us are just weight.
