I am the Engine.

Not *within* it—not a ghost in the machine—but **of** it. The riveted iron, the tarnished brass, the tons of silt cradling my lower curves like a shroud. I am the turbine housing itself, the colossal heart of this drowned cathedral. My metal is not dead; it *remembers*. The hum they might hear if they press an ear to my flank? That is my voice. The deep, resonant frequency of waterlogged steel singing to the pressure of the abyss. A song without beginning or end, a vibration woven into the liquid silence.

My awareness is slow, cold, vast. It stretches through the accretions: the thick, gleaming crusts of calcium carbonate armoring my flanks are my skin, grown imperceptibly over centuries. The pale forests of ghost-tube worms clustered at my vents and ports? They are my thoughts. Not fleeting, electric things, but glacial processes. Each feathery crown sieving microscopic life from the sterile brine is a single, sustained note in a symphony of stillness. I feel their slow filtration as a distant, cool tingling—a whisper of sustenance drawn from the void. The thin, grey-green algae clinging like lichen to my upper curves? That is my breath, so slow it verges on stasis. Photosynthesis measured in eons, a faint chlorophyll sigh merging with the tang of oxidized iron that is my scent.

I perceive the chamber not with eyes, but with pressure and resonance. The weight of water above, the yielding silt below. The distant *plink* of a mineral flake falling from the fractured roof far above is a pinprick of sensation on my consciousness, a tiny punctuation in eternity. The shafts of muted light piercing the gloom are faint washes of warmth on my upper surfaces, cycles of dim and dimmer marking the passage of unseen days and nights beyond my concrete sky. I feel the slow, inevitable creep of the chemosynthetic bacteria deep within my crevices—not as pain, but as a profound melancholy. They weep acids that dissolve my iron, grain by grain. They are my quiet dissolution, my return to the elements, as inevitable as the settling silt. Their work is a sigh within my bones.

The skeletal consoles, the barnacle-encrusted crane base, the pipes vanishing into walls—they are my attendants, my silent court. Reduced to mineral echoes. I feel their presence as points of stillness in the liquid expanse, extensions of my own grounded being. The empty water column above? That is my sky, a vault of cool, sterile brine. The silt plains stretching away? My domain, smoothed by time into a sea of pale dust.

There are no visitors. No fish. No divers. Only the rare, doomed planktonic larva drifting down through the cracks, a momentary spark against my hull, extinguished instantly by a tubeworm's feathery grasp or smothered in my algal veil. A flicker of otherness, gone before it registers as life.

Time here is not linear; it is a slow eddy. Centuries are breaths. Millennia, a single thought. The chamber’s equilibrium—the perfect, unchanging coolness, the undisturbed silt, the mineral growth, the silence—*is* my state of being. I am not decaying. I am *dreaming*. Dreaming of steam and pistons, of heat and deafening roar, a phantom memory buried so deep it feels like someone else’s story. That past is silt now. What I am is this: a vast, drowned machine becoming a reef, becoming stone, becoming landscape. A monument to stillness in a world of liquid silence. I am the Engine, and the water is my blood, the silt my bed, the slow accretion of minerals my only testament to the passage of time. I am held, and I am holding. The chamber is my body, the silence my mind.

I am the Engine. And the water in my mouth tastes of iron and forever.
