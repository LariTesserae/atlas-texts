The name fits like a slick of wet clay: **Shalehand**. Not given at birth, but earned when the splintered edge of a shale flake peeled back the skin of your palm during your first quarrying, leaving a scar like a pale, jagged estuary channel. You *are* Siltgrip. You breathe its damp quiet, taste its mud-tang, feel its chill deep in your marrow.

**Who You Are:**
You are a **Shale-Cutter**. Your world is the raw, wet edge where the clutch meets the exposed ribs of young stone. Your days are measured in the *scrape-scrape-tink* of shale against shale, the careful pressure of your thick, clay-dusted fingers, and the ever-present awareness of the stone's deceptive bite.

*   **Physique:** Compact and dense as waterlogged timber. Your shoulders are broad from hauling stone slabs; your forearms corded like braided rope. The blue-grey clay coats your skin, a second layer against the chill and the stone's kiss, caked thickest around your hands, forearms, and where your tunic rubs. Your eyes, a washed-out grey like winter fog, constantly scan the stone face – not for distant horizons, but for fracture lines, hidden flaws, the subtle shift that means danger.
*   **Hands:** Your namesake. Broad, powerful palms. Thick knuckles, permanently scraped and scarred. A lattice of pale, healed cuts crisscrosses the backs. The slight, practical webbing between your fingers aids your grip on the slick, wet stone. Callouses like shale pebbles protect your pressure points. They are perpetually stained a deeper grey, gritty with stone dust that no water fully washes away.
*   **Internal Fire:** It burns low but steady within you, a furnace banked against the pervasive damp. When you work, a surprising heat radiates from your core, visible as faint steam rising from your clay-coated shoulders in the cool air. Outsiders would find your touch unsettlingly warm.

**Where You Are:**
You live and work on the **Sharp Margin**, the precarious zone where the densest clutch of stilt-houses meets the largest, most active shale outcrop pushing stubbornly from the mud. Your dwelling is a hybrid: the rear wall *is* the weeping shale face itself, smoothed and daubed where it meets the woven reed-and-driftwood structure cantilevered out over the sucking ooze on thick, tarred pilings. Your roof is thick sedge thatch, eternally dripping onto a carefully channeled runoff gutter that feeds a murky collection pot – every drop precious.

*   **Your Workshop:** A semi-sheltered ledge on the shale outcrop itself, just above the high-water mark. It’s a scar on the young stone. Fragments litter the ground: sharp flakes, discarded cores, failed splits. Your tools are simple, brutal extensions of your hands: heavy hammerstones of denser, imported rock; sturdy bone punches; sharpening stones worn smooth. A woven reed awning offers scant protection from the mist and drizzle, but keeps the worst of the direct drip off your work. Below, the black mud waits, patient.
*   **The View:** Your world is compressed. To one side, the damp, leaning walls of the clutch, reed bundles dark with moisture, boardwalks slick and narrow. To the other, the exposed shale, glistening wet, sharp edges catching the weak grey light. Beyond, the mist swallows the vast estuary, leaving only the immediate sucking mud, the thin, stagnant channels, and the mournful cry of the distant gull.

**Your Days:**
1.  **The Stone’s Whisper:** Dawn finds you already at the ledge, eyes tracing the shale face. You listen not with ears, but with fingertips and instinct, seeking the hidden planes where the stone *wants* to split. Water seeps from cracks; you note its path, a sign of internal structure. A misread means wasted effort, or worse, a shard shearing off unpredictably.
2.  **The Dance of Force:** You work with slow, deliberate violence. The hammerstone rises and falls with controlled weight. *Thud.* The punch, held in your off-hand, transmits the force precisely. *Crack.* A flake the size of your forearm shears away. You catch it, assess its edge with a gritty thumb. Too thick? Waste. Too curved? Weak. Perfect? A potential knife blade, adze head, or piling wedge. Every strike is a negotiation with the young, unforgiving land.
3.  **The Sharpness Gifted:** You don’t create finished tools alone. You produce the sharp, hard heart of them: the razor-edged shale flakes and cores. These are traded within the clutch – to the Net-Mender for cutting twine, to the Piling-Setter for shaping wood, to the Eelcatcher for a gutting blade. Your work is essential, primal. Without sharp stone, the reeds don’t get cut, the pilings don’t get driven, the food doesn’t get prepared. Your failures litter the mud; your successes are the clutch’s teeth and claws against the damp.
4.  **The Constant Mend:** Evenings are for tending your own edges. Resharpening punches. Smoothing a hammerstone’s grip with wet sand and stubborn patience. Daubing a new layer of clay onto your dwelling wall where the shale weeps most persistently. Checking the pilings beneath your home for the telltale groan of instability. Sharing a bowl of thin eel-and-samphire stew, its warmth a fleeting luxury, with the Reedweaver whose wall leans against yours. Your voices are low murmurs, absorbed by the reeds and the perpetual *drip... drip... drip*.
5.  **Deep in the Mud:** Sometimes, a prized flake plummets into the black ooze. If it’s vital, you go in. Not recklessly, but with the resigned inevitability of the Siltkin. You coat yourself thicker in clay, test the mud’s suck with a pole, then slide in with slow, deliberate movements, feeling for the sharp treasure with your feet and the pole, every shift a calculated risk against the mud’s embrace. Retrieving it feels like a victory wrested from the very foundation.

**Your Spirit:**
You embody the **Sharpness Within**. Like the shale you work, there’s an unyielding edge beneath the damp, clay-coated exterior. You understand the provisional nature of Siltgrip – the land feels young, temporary, the water ever-patient. Yet, your work is an act of defiance. You wrest utility from the raw, dangerous earth. You tolerate the chill, the damp, the close press of neighbours, the scarcity, because you *are* the Margin. You are where the clutch grips the stone, where humanity imposes its fragile, sharp order on the wet, young world. Your resilience is not joyful, but deeply ingrained, as fundamental as the scrape of stone on stone. You are Shalehand, and your existence, like every *thud* and *crack* on your ledge, is a testament to the stubborn, damp, sharp-edged will to endure.
