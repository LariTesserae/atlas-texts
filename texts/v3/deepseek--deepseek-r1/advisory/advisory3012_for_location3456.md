1.  **Core Protection Against the Environment:**
    *   **Thermal Insulation:** 4°C (39°F) is dangerously cold for prolonged exposure, especially with 100% humidity. You need **non-porous, multi-layered thermal clothing:**
        *   Base Layer: Synthetic or merino wool thermal underwear (wicking).
        *   Mid Layer: Thick fleece or high-loft synthetic insulation.
        *   Outer Shell: **Impermeable, waterproof, and windproof parka and trousers.** Gore-Tex or equivalent is essential. Sealed seams are non-negotiable.
        *   **Crucial:** A waterproof balaclava or neck gaiter and waterproof, insulated gloves. Exposed skin will lose heat rapidly in the damp air.
    *   **Footwear:** This is critical. The smooth, wet flagstones are treacherous.
        *   Wear **sturdy, waterproof boots with aggressive, deep-lugged, non-marking rubber soles** (e.g., high-quality canyoning or approach shoes). Avoid anything with metal cleats or hard plastic that could scratch the ancient stone.
        *   **Ice cleats/microspikes (non-metal)** are highly recommended despite the lack of ice. The constant condensation creates an incredibly slick biofilm on every surface. Ensure they are easily removable if you need to step onto delicate moss.
    *   **Respiratory Protection (Optional but Wise):** The air is thick with spores, decay particles, and potentially unique microorganisms thriving in this ancient, sealed biome. A simple **N95 respirator or lightweight filtration mask** is prudent to avoid respiratory irritation or unknown biological effects from the "rich loam and sweet decay."

    *   **Traction Aids:** Consider **trekking poles with non-metal, rubber tips.** They provide vital stability on the slick stones and allow you to test pool depths or moss stability without putting full weight. Ensure tips are clean to avoid introducing contaminants.

Pack **high-calorie, non-perishable snacks** (nuts, energy bars) and a **thermos of warm (not hot) liquid** (broth is ideal). Monitor yourself and companions closely for shivering, confusion, or fatigue. Know the exit route and time your visit – hours pass deceptively here.
