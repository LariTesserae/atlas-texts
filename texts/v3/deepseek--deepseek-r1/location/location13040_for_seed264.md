The Saltstone Basin unfolds under a pale, heat-hazy sky, a vast expanse of lowland locked in a state of ancient, resonant stillness. This is a world sculpted by immense time and the slow, patient work of minerals, where the hum of forgotten industry vibrates through the very bones of the earth.

**Terrain & Materials:**  
You stand upon a fractured plain of basalt, weathered over eons into smooth, undulating waves. The dominant feature is a colossal, exposed vein of *saltstone* – a sedimentary rock formation of layered gypsum, halite, and volcanic ash, compressed into bands of chalky white, sulfurous yellow, and deep, rusty ochre. Wind erosion has carved this vein into a surreal landscape: towering hoodoos like melted candles, deep, narrow slot canyons snaking across the plain, and vast, shallow depressions crusted with a glittering rime of pure salt. The ground underfoot is a mosaic of fine, grey volcanic grit and sharp, angular shards of obsidian, crunching with each step. Pockets of dampness manifest as dark stains weeping from the saltstone walls in the deepest canyons, or as fleeting patches of slick mud where geothermal warmth (a constant, low-grade heat radiating from the bedrock) meets trapped condensation overnight. There are no rivers, only the memory of water etched into the stone.

**Atmosphere:**  
The air is warm and thick, carrying the mineral tang of salt and sulfur. It feels heavy, amplifying sound rather than muffling it. Despite the warmth, there's a profound sense of sterility – no pollen, no decay, just dust and mineral vapor. The light is flat and pervasive, bleaching color from the landscape under a high, diffuse cloud cover that never seems to break or change. The horizon shimmers with heat haze, blending seamlessly into the pale sky, reinforcing the sense of endless, open expanse.

**Sound:**  
The silence is not silent. It is filled with a deep, resonant **hum**. It’s not biological; it’s mechanical. It emanates from below, vibrating up through the soles of your feet and resonating in your chest cavity. It’s the sound of massive, subterranean machinery – turbines, perhaps, or immense pumps – operating endlessly in some hidden geothermal complex far beneath the basalt plain. The hum is constant, unchanging in pitch or volume, a foundational drone upon which occasional sharper sounds play: the distant, metallic *clang* of shifting plates deep underground, the high-pitched *whine* of stressed metal carried on the wind, or the sudden, sharp *crack* of a salt crystal fracturing under thermal stress in the canyons. The soundscape is industrial, yet there is no visible source, only the evidence in the air and the ground.

**Ecology:**  
Life, as commonly understood, is absent. There is no vegetation – no moss, no lichen, not even the hardiest scrub. The "rare fauna" parameter manifests as something other than animal life: **Resonance Feeders**. These are complex, slow-moving crystalline structures, perhaps a form of extremophile mineral-based pseudo-life or self-assembled nanotechnology long abandoned. They resemble clusters of iridescent, vibrating quartz or metallic geodes the size of a fist, slowly migrating across the warmest rock faces. They absorb vibrations and faint electromagnetic fields from the subterranean machinery, converting them into the energy needed to gather mineral particulates and grow, molecule by molecule, over centuries. Their movement is glacial, their presence sparse and easily missed against the mineral backdrop. They are the basin's only "inhabitants," utterly alien and silent except for a faint, subsonic thrum when closely approached.

**Rules of the World:**  
1.  **Permanence is Law:** Change is measured in geological epochs. Erosion has reached an equilibrium; the wind scours, but the saltstone reforms through capillary action and crystallization at a near-matching rate. Structures, if they existed, would have long since been subsumed or eroded flat. Nothing new grows.
2.  **Sound is Substance:** The industrial hum isn't just noise; it's a pervasive energy field. It shapes the Resonance Feeders and may subtly influence the crystallization patterns in the saltstone. Silence would be unnatural, even catastrophic for the basin's fragile pseudo-ecology.
3.  **The Warmth Below:** Geothermal energy is the hidden engine. It warms the air, drives the subterranean machinery (its purpose lost or automated beyond reckoning), and creates the microclimates of dampness where vapor condenses on cooler stone.
4.  **The Basin Endures:** It is open, sparse, and settled. It exists in a state of dynamic inertia – the machinery runs, the Feeders move infinitesimally, the salt weeps and reforms – but the overall form, the feeling, the oppressive hum, are constants. Time flows, but the landscape remains fundamentally invariant. It is a place that has found its final, resonant form.

**Overall Impression:**  
The Saltstone Basin is a paradox: an untouched, natural-seeming landscape utterly dominated by the invisible hand of ancient, buried industry. It feels vast, ancient, and profoundly empty, yet filled with the oppressive, unchanging drone of machines that work on, unseen, for reasons no one remembers. The damp patches and weeping walls are fleeting anomalies in the mineral dryness, the Resonance Feeders are cryptic monuments to adaptation in a sterile world, and the warmth is a reminder of the fire beneath the stillness. It is a landscape of profound equilibrium, where the loudest sound is the voice of the earth's hidden engines, and nothing ever truly changes. It is beautiful in its stark, resonant desolation, and deeply unsettling in its eternal, industrial hum.
