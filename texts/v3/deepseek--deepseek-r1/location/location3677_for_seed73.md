The Sky-Mouth Caldera breathes out a world of vertical wetlands.  

At 5,000 meters, where the air should be thin and biting, the caldera’s geothermal heart exhales. Warmth bleeds from deep vents, trapping humidity against a crown of jagged peaks eternally capped in ice. This is not a marsh *down*—it’s a marsh *up*.  

**Terrain:** A vast, open basin stretches ten kilometers across, ringed by treacherous, near-vertical cliffs. The floor is a mosaic: hummocks of thick, fibrous peat rise like islands from shallow, steaming lagoons. Rivers of milky, mineral-rich water, heated by subterranean fires, ooze across porous volcanic rock polished smooth by centuries of gentle flow. Weeping obsidian cliffs eternally shed condensation in rivulets that feed the wetland below. Slopes leading up to the cliffsides are treacherous, sculpted mud-stone terraces glistening with constant moisture.  

**Materials:** Everything is saturated. The rocks—dark basalt, striated rhyolite, glassy obsidian—ooze or weep. The peat is a dense, black-brown matrix of centuries of saturated, compressed mosses and reeds. Water itself feels thickened by dissolved silica and rare earths leached from deep magma chambers. Thin veils of iridescent mineral deposits, like fragile mother-of-pearl, crust the edges of warm pools.  

**Atmosphere:** Heat presses down with physical weight, a wet blanket soaked in sulfur and ozone. Visibility hangs perpetually at mid-range: veils of geothermal steam rise incessantly, cloaking the upper cliffs and swirling between the peat hummocks. A faint, pervasive mist hangs knee-high over the water. The light is a diffused, pearly grey, the sun obscured by high-altitude vapor and the steam itself, casting soft shadows lacking sharp edges.  

**Sound:** It is a place of constant, layered drone. The loudest element is the *hiss-rumble*: the hiss of steam vents punctuating a deep, tectonic murmur rising from below. This is overlaid with the gurgle and trickle of countless waterways weaving through peat and stone. An orchestra of amphibians—chirrups, deep resonant croaks, and metallic clicks—is thick in the air. Higher up, the sharp whistles of specialized birds cut through. It’s loud, yet the volume feels absorbed by the sheer density and humidity, creating a cocoon of saturated sound.  

**Ecology (Flora):** Life is overwhelmingly lush and dense. Carnivorous moss mats glisten emerald and crimson, trapping insects drawn to the heat. Giant, rubbery-leaved heliconias unfurl near steamy vents, dripping condensation. Feathery black ferns cling to weeping cliffs. Stout, wind-gnarled conifers somehow thrive on higher terraces, draped in moisture-trapping epiphytic lichens that glow faintly bioluminescent blue-green after twilight. The water is choked with bulbous, oxygen-pumping algae and dense mats of floating bladderwort. Every surface is colonized, layered – moss over fern over lichen over warm, wet stone.  

**Ecology (Fauna):** Abundant, and strange creatures thrive here. Enormous, semi-aquatic arthropods scuttle through the shallows on stilt-like legs, their carapaces iridescent blues and greens reflecting the mineralized water. Filter-feeding salamanders the size of otters bask on warm rocks, tongues flicking out to catch clouds of buzzing midges. Agile, long-necked avian reptiles glide between peat islands, snatching crustaceans from the milky streams. Bioluminescent insects pulse like low-flying stars in the perpetual mist. Colonies of burrowing eels hum electrically as they navigate the warm mud.  

**Built Environment:** Scattered and deeply integrated. Single structures of a material resembling seamless, obsidian-infused ceramic thrust up from peat islands or cling impossibly to sheer cliff faces. They are monolithic—smooth, windowless spires, collapsed arches, or half-buried domes—seemingly grown or extruded rather than built. They show minimal weathering; the humid air and mineral deposits create only a faint patina. They possess an unnerving stillness; their purpose and operation, if any, remain incomprehensible. Some hum almost imperceptibly, in tune with the earth's rumble.  

**Rules/Tech/Weirdness:** The defining strangeness is the convergence of extreme altitude, intense heat, and perpetual wetland sustained by deep geothermal activity *and* technology. The scattered structures suggest a level of engineering operating beyond conventional physics—perhaps manipulating geothermal energy or local space-time gradients to maintain the impossible microclimate. Water is manipulated too; it flows *up* rock faces in defiance of gravity near the monoliths. Time feels viscous here; the "established" erosion suggests the ecosystem is in a deeply stable, self-sustaining loop, potentially engineered millennia ago. Life has hyper-adapted—filtering mineralized water, breathing thin but wet air, thriving on geothermal heat. The air carries faint distortions, like heat haze, that play tricks on distance perception near the structures. This is a place settled into an eternal, steamy equilibrium by forces only hinted at by the silent black towers.  

**Dynamic:** Change is slow, almost imperceptible on a human scale. Peat hummocks shift and grow incrementally over decades. The mineral deposits thicken glacially. Structures stand unchanging. Even the fauna moves with a slow, deliberate energy dictated by warmth and abundance. The steam rises, the vents hiss, the water trickles—a cycle older than recorded time. Only the occasional shift in a deep-earth tremor or the inexplicable, slow movement of a black monolith might signal processes operating on a scale of centuries. It is a place of immense energy held in profound suspension.  

The Sky-Mouth Caldera: a high-altitude paradox of heat, steam, and teeming life—a sealed, self-sustaining world humming above the clouds, echoing with ancient technology holding the impossible in perfect, damp stasis. You breathe its wet heat as much as you breathe air.
