The air hangs thick and warm in the Cathedral Cavern of Mycelion, a forgotten subterranean nexus where industry choked and life swallowed it whole. You stand not on rock, but on a dense, resilient mat of interwoven fungal rhizomes and mosses, springy and damp beneath your feet. This is the **Floor-Forest**, the foundational layer of a world built entirely from **vegetation**. The **scale** is intimate yet immense: a single, vaulted chamber perhaps a hundred meters across, its ceiling lost in perpetual, **dim** twilight fifty meters above. The boundaries feel absolute – damp, living walls curve seamlessly into the spongy floor.

**Light** is a memory here, provided only by the cavern itself. Patches of ceiling and walls pulse with a sickly, bioluminescent green and ochre – enormous, parasitic lichens feeding on minerals leached from the rock by acidic root-secretions. Below, clusters of fist-sized, glassy nodules embedded in bulbous fungal stalks emit a faint, cold blue radiance, casting long, dancing shadows. The primary illumination, however, comes from swarms of **overwhelming fauna**: trillions of thumb-sized, winged insects with translucent, furnace-orange abdomens. They swarm in dense, churning clouds around the light sources and through the cavern’s open spaces, their collective glow forming shifting auroras in the humid air. Their constant, high-pitched drone is the **loud** baseline hum of the cavern, a living static.

**Water** manifests as a pervasive dampness. Warm condensation beads on every surface – the smooth, leathery leaves of ceiling-hanging air-plants, the rubbery stalks of the luminous fungi, the intricate lattice of woody vines that form the cavern’s structural ribs. It drips constantly, a syncopated percussion adding to the insect drone. In shallow depressions in the Floor-Forest, murky pools reflect the bioluminescent glow, home to wriggling, blind larvae and skittering crustaceans the size of your hand.

The **vegetation** isn't just present; it *is* the architecture and substrate. Towering, fused fungal columns, wider than ancient oaks and striated with deep, **eroded** grooves from centuries of acidic drips, hold up the ceiling. Between them, colossal, hollow root-trunks – remnants of some surface giant long dead – pierce the floor and vanish into the rock above, their interiors now colonized by cascading ferns and phosphorescent mosses. Thick, woody lianas, smooth and hard as petrified wood, weave through the space like organic buttresses and walkways. Smaller, carnivorous pitcher plants yawn hungrily from crevices, their nectar-laced rims glistening. Every surface crawls, sprouts, or drips with life. Yet, the **density** remains **sparse**; the immense pillars and root-trunks create vast pockets of open, echoing space filled only by the swirling insect clouds and the hanging gardens.

Signs of the **built** and **tech** environment are spectral, almost subsumed. Against one cavern wall, half-buried by a pulsating mound of bioluminescent slime-mold, lies the corroded skeleton of an immense machine. Gantries of rust-eaten iron, twisted and skeletal, are embraced by thick, woody vines that seem to be actively pulling them apart. A section of riveted pipe, large enough to walk through, emerges from the rock only to be swallowed whole by a gargantuan, pulsating fungal bole that has grown around and through it. Faint, erratic sparks sometimes flicker deep within the slime-mold mound, powering nothing. This isn't a functioning **industrial** complex; it's the carcass of one, **established** and weathered over eons, its bones becoming anchors and nutrients for the conquering biome. The air carries the faint, metallic tang of rust beneath the dominant scents of loam, decay, and ozone from the insects.

The **dynamic** is ceaselessly **active**, a complex dance of life and decay. The insect swarms (**fauna** at its most overwhelming) feed on the luminous lichens and smaller airborne spores, only to be snatched mid-air by swift, silent predators: gliding lizards with skin like iridescent oil slicks, or swooping, furred things with too many wings and needle-sharp beaks. Below, armored millipedes longer than your arm trundle through the Floor-Forest, grazing on mosses, while bioluminescent slugs the size of small dogs leave shimmering trails as they navigate the pools. Near the decaying machine, metallic clicking sounds betray the presence of large, chitinous beetles with acid-dripping mandibles, slowly consuming the last vestiges of the iron gantries. Condensation drips, insects drone, predators strike, scavengers click, and the plants visibly grow – tendrils inch upwards, fungal caps expand with soft, leathery sighs.

The **weirdness** is inherent, profound. The sheer dominance of the living architecture, the bioluminescent ecology fueled by decay, the uncountable, glowing insects, the fusion of rusted industry with pulsating fungus, and the constant, humid warmth of this buried world – it feels alien, a self-contained, breathing entity far removed from the sunlit surface. Time here feels thick and slow, measured in drips and growth spurts, yet the pace of life is frantic, relentless. It is a place of profound contradiction: a **sparse** cathedral teeming with life, a **warm**, **damp** tomb built by plants upon the corpse of industry, illuminated by the **dim** glow of its own overwhelming, churning **fauna**. The sound is a **loud** tapestry of life and dripping water, the air vibrates with wingbeats, and the very walls seem to breathe. This is the Cavern of Mycelion – not a landscape observed, but one endured.
