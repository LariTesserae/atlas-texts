## The Chimney Stack Abyss

You are suspended in cool, weightless dark, a mere speck within a vertical cathedral of water. Not the crushing abyssal plain, but something stranger: a drowned skyscraper of stone, thrusting up from unseen depths toward a surface too distant to matter. This is the Throat of Gehenna—a solitary volcanic spire, young and jagged, rising miles through the ocean column. Its flanks are sheer obsidian cliffs, fractured and gleaming with volcanic glass, untouched by coral or kelp. There is no plant life here, not even the ghostly fronds of chemosynthetic bacteria. This world is mineral and machine and moving flesh.

The density is overwhelming. Every cubic yard teems. Not with fish as you know them, but with the *Screaming Swarm*. They are the sole, abundant fauna: fist-sized, chitinous creatures like obsidian beetles crossed with deep-sea anglerfish. Their carapaces are polished volcanic glass, reflecting the only light source: clusters of immense, industrial floodlights bolted directly onto the raw rock face. Who bolted them? There are no platforms, no walkways, no signs of builders—only the lights themselves, crusted with mineral deposits, humming with a low, pervasive thrum that vibrates the water. This is the ‘industrial’ aspect—raw, functional, seemingly self-anchored technology, its purpose inscrutable. The light is stark, almost clinical, carving sharp-edged shadows in the particulate-heavy water, making the volcanic glass facets glitter like shattered mirrors.

The Screaming Swarm lives *within* the light. They cluster around the floodlights in seething, shifting nebulae, millions strong. Their name comes from their communication: a constant, deafening barrage of clicks, shrieks, and grinding chitters amplified and distorted by the water and the resonant stone. It’s a wall of sound so dense it feels physical, pressing against your senses. They feed not on plankton, but on the *light itself* and the minute electrical currents leaking from the fixtures. Their glassy carapaces glow faintly from within after feeding, casting fleeting, prismatic patterns on the dark rock. Occasionally, a section of the swarm will detach and flow like liquid obsidian towards a fracture in the spire, vanishing into the absolute darkness within—perhaps to lay eggs in the geothermal warmth, perhaps to perform some unknown maintenance on the lights embedded deeper in the rock.

The atmosphere is one of profound contradiction: immense scale (the towering spire vanishing above and below) confined to a small, intensely focused area around the floodlit rock face; utter natural wildness (no built structures, raw young geology) permeated by functional, industrial light and sound; abundant, teeming life completely devoid of flora, sustained solely by alien technology and geothermal seepage. The water is cool, heavy with suspended volcanic ash and the faint tang of ozone from the electrical leaks. Time moves with the slow pulse of geology. Mineral accretions grow imperceptibly thick on the light fixtures. The swarm’s patterns shift over hours, not seconds. A fresh obsidian flow might gleam wetly for centuries before dulling. The only rapid movements are the frantic, light-drunk dances of individual swarmers within their overwhelming, slow-burning mass.

**Rules of this World:**
1.  **Light is Life:** The Screaming Swarm *is* the ecology, utterly dependent on the inexplicable industrial lights. Without them, silence and sterility would reign instantly.
2.  **The Stone is Sovereign:** The volcanic spire is the only solid ground, young, sharp, and actively repelling colonization. Nothing grows *on* it; life clings *around* the foreign implants.
3.  **Sound is Substance:** The swarm's cacophony defines the space, shapes behavior, and may serve purposes beyond communication—perhaps navigation in the absolute dark beyond the lights, or even a form of sonar sculpting of the water itself.
4.  **Technology is Environment:** The lights aren't tools; they are geological features now, as much a part of the landscape as the obsidian. Their origin and purpose are irrelevant mysteries. They simply *are*, and life has adapted utterly to their presence.
5.  **Slow Metabolism:** Change here operates on tectonic or geothermal timescales. The frantic energy of the swarm is surface noise on a deep, patient stillness. A new light fixture might appear over millennia; an old one might flicker out over centuries. The swarm adjusts, slowly, inevitably.
