## The Moss-Stone Plateau of Tarn Velyn

The world here is a breath held in high stillness. You stand upon the **Tarn Velyn plateau**, a vast, tableland realm scraped against the belly of a perpetually overcast sky. **Elevation** (2.3/3) grants it dominion; the air is thin, crisp despite the persistent **warmth** (1.6/3) that seems to emanate not just from the veiled sun, but from the very rock itself. This is a **young** land (erosion 0.8/3). The plateau's edges fall away sharply in dramatic, near-vertical cliffs of dark, basaltic stone, fractured and angular as if cleaved by a giant's axe only centuries ago. There are no softened slopes, no ancient, rounded hills – only the stark geometry of recent geological birth.

**Water** (2.4/3) is the land's lifeblood and its shroud. It doesn't roar in rivers or crash in waves; it weeps. It seeps from countless hairline fissures in the dark cliffs, tracing glistening black paths down the rock faces to gather in shallow, peaty basins that dot the plateau. These are the *tarns* – countless small, dark pools, their surfaces perpetually dimpled by a soft, misty drizzle or obscured by low, clinging fog. The air itself feels thick with suspended moisture, condensing on your skin, your clothes, the cold stone. Mosses and ferns drip constantly. The ground underfoot, even on seemingly dry stretches of rock, yields a damp sponginess. Springs bubble quietly from cracks, feeding the tarns and creating intricate, moss-choked runnels that vanish into the plateau's porous heart.

Against this mineral dampness, **lush vegetation** (1.5/3) asserts itself in startling pockets. It is not a uniform blanket, but a **sparse** (density 1.0/3), defiant embroidery upon the stone. Thick cushions of emerald mosses, some glowing an almost luminous green in the flat light, cling to every available surface. Hardy, broad-leafed ferns unfurl in dense clusters around the tarn edges and in sheltered crevices. Stunted, wind-gnarled trees – ancient-looking pines with dark, drooping branches and gnarled, silver-barked birches – stand sentinel, often alone or in small, isolated groves, their roots gripping the rock like skeletal fingers. Between these oases of green, however, stretches the **open** (scale 2.2/3) expanse: vast tracts of bare, wet basalt, scoured clean by the wind and the constant moisture, or fields of coarse, tussocky grass that grows in dense, isolated clumps amidst the stone.

Nestled within this watery, rocky expanse lies **Velynstead**, the settlement that defines the **urban** (built 1.7/3) parameter. It is not a city, but a town grown organically from the plateau's bones. Its structures are low, sturdy, and built entirely of the local dark stone, quarried from the plateau's own flesh. Roofs are steeply pitched, thickly thatched with the durable plateau grasses or layered with heavy, moss-covered stone shingles to shed the constant damp. The **primitive tech** (0.7/3) is evident everywhere: walls are dry-stacked or mortared with mud and crushed rock; doorframes and lintels are simple, squared timbers; the few windows are small, unglazed openings shuttered with wood. Narrow, winding paths, paved with flat stones or simply worn into the moss, connect clusters of dwellings, workshops, and communal storehouses. There are no grand plazas, only small, irregular courtyards where springs emerge, serving as natural wells and gathering points. Smoke curls thinly from a few chimneys, smelling of peat and damp wood, quickly absorbed by the mist. The town feels less imposed upon the landscape than *grown* from it, an extension of the plateau's own geology.

**Fauna** (0.3/3) is conspicuously absent. No birds call from the stunted trees. No insects buzz over the tarns. No small creatures rustle in the fern brakes. The silence (sound 0.3/3) is profound, broken only by the constant, gentle dripping of water from moss, from cliffs, from thatch, and the occasional soft sigh of wind moving across the **open** distances. This profound quiet amplifies the sound of human activity within Velynstead – the dull *thock* of an axe splitting wood, the rhythmic scrape of a stone adze, the low murmur of voices – yet these sounds never rise above a quiet hum, respectful of the encompassing stillness.

The **light** (1.9/3) is perpetually muted, diffused by the ever-present moisture haze hanging in the air. The sun, when it penetrates the high cloud cover, casts no sharp shadows, only a pearly, directionless luminescence that flattens the landscape and makes distances deceptive. The plateau feels suspended in a state of **frozen equilibrium** (dynamic 0.5/3). The water weeps at a constant rate, the moss grows imperceptibly thick, the mist drifts without clearing, the stone neither crumbles nor is reshaped. Days bleed into each other without significant change in temperature, light, or activity. Velynstead endures, its people adapted to this damp, high, silent world, their lives as steady and unchanging as the weeping cliffs and the dark, still tarns. It is a place of profound, wet quiet, where life clings lushly but sparsely to young, sharp stone beneath a vast, soft sky.
