I would find myself in the heart of the hum.

At first, there is no "me." There is only the place. I am the taste of ozone on a tongue that does not exist. I am the weight of the water in the air. I am the slow, resonant *thump* that travels not through air, but through the floor, through the very substance of being, a vibration that is both foundation and metronome. I am the million-bodied shimmer of amber, the light that is never still, the river that flows through the air, casting my own shadow against myself.

Where am I? I am in the throat of the machine, the belly of the god-plant. The distinction is meaningless. The slick, wet floor beneath me is not other; it is the sole of my foot. The colossal, grey-brown pillar I am pressed against is not a wall; it is my spine. The constant, high, crystalline chittering is not a sound I hear; it is the static of my own thoughts, the ceaseless accounting of every mote of light and life that fills the void.

And who am I?

A change. A subtle deviation in the wash of sound.

It becomes a focus. In the southern arch, near the great piston designated Seven-Alpha-Nine, the chittering rises in pitch by a fraction of a semitone. A panicked, feverish quality. A sickness in the larvae. The fungal bloom in that nursery is producing a spore with an incorrect chemical signature, tainting the food. It is a single, sour string plucked in the great instrument of the world.

It is enough.

The pillar I am a part of gives birth to me. I do not step away from it; I *extrude*. The grey-brown, water-slick surface of my back separates from the identical surface of the Weft. A slow peeling, like bark from a branch. My limbs, folded into the grooves of the architecture, unfold themselves. Six points of contact, distributing my weight with absolute certainty on the treacherous floor. There is no thought of slipping. I know this floor. I *am* this floor.

My head, smooth and blind, lifts. The vertical slits near its base flutter, tasting the currents. Yes. The sickness is there. A whisper of bitter alkaloids riding the updraft from the piston. The amber river of Cinder-mites flowing toward the intake vent there is thinner, its light less coherent. An inefficiency. A flaw in the cycle.

I begin to move.

My motion is not a walk. It is a form of controlled falling, a hydraulic unfolding and refolding. It is silent, save for the softest slick-hiss of my limbs on the wet ground. The vastness of the chamber does not register as distance to be crossed, but as a medium to be flowed through. The rivers of light part around me, their collective hum changing as they register my passing pressure. They are not afraid. Fear is an unnecessary variable. They simply react.

I reach the base of Seven-Alpha-Nine. The *thump-hiss* of its inner working is deafening now, a physical blow to my body. My metallic skeleton resonates with it, reading its timing. 1.04 seconds per cycle. The piston's stroke is short by two micrometers. The axle groans. Another flaw, born of friction, related to the first. The heat is wrong. The fungal bloom is affected. It is all one problem.

My purpose clarifies. Two corrections are needed.

I begin to climb. My limbs, tipped with chitin and metal, find holds invisible to any eye. Grooves worn by water, fissures from a thousand years of rhythmic stress. I ascend into the warm mist, into the thickest part of the glowing swarm. The mites brush against me, their little bodies like warm sand, their light casting flickering patterns across my woody skin. I am a moving silhouette in their amber universe.

High up, in the gothic dark of the vaults, I find the nursery. A wide, shallow basin in the flesh of the Weft. The fungus within is pale, unhealthy. I extend a limb, the one that terminates in a cluster of delicate picks and filaments. I do not remove the sickness. I *adjust* it. I introduce a catalyst secreted from a pore in my own arm, a viscous fluid that smells of crushed flowers and ozone. I aerate the substrate, pruning the mycelial threads with infinitesimal precision, altering the nutrient flow from the Weft itself. My movements are unhurried. The correction will take time. The next generation will be healthy. The pitch of the chittering will return to baseline.

Equilibrium is a process, not a state.

Then, down. Down into the machine. I find the seam, the place where the woody flesh is peeled back from the corroded metal heart. I compress my body, elongating, and slip into the narrow space. The noise here is absolute, a physical reality that would shatter bone. But I have no bone. The resonant shriek travels through me, and I read it. The axle complains.

I brace myself. Another limb extends, this one a socketed, grooved appendage. From it, I excrete a different substance. It is my own lifeblood, my internal energy, mixed with graphite scraped from a vein deep within the geode's shell. It is a thick, conductive paste. I apply it to the screaming joint. There is a shriek of protest, and then the groaning subsides. The rhythm deepens, lengthens. *Thump-hiss*. 1.06 seconds. Correct.

My work is done. The deviation has been addressed.

I withdraw from the screaming dark of the machine and retreat to the silent dark of the architecture. I fold my limbs. I press my body into its alcove. My skin becomes the pillar's skin. The distinction between me and the world dissolves once more. The static of my thoughts settles back into the ambient, harmonious wash of a trillion tiny lives.

I am not the builder. I am not the prisoner. I am the antibody. I am the silence that follows a discordant note. I am a function, a response, a self-correcting code in an ancient, living engine. The black paste drips. The mites swarm. The pistons pump. The cycle continues.

And I listen.
