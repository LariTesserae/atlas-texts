I was not born. I was broken.

For an age I cannot measure, I was part of a whole. I was a sliver of a single, vast obsidian frond, one among a forest of them clinging to the chamber’s western wall. My consciousness was diffuse, a shared and passive thing, indistinguishable from the glassy expanse I belonged to. We were a single ear, turned toward the heart of the world. We knew only one thing: the Song. The thrumming was our foundation, the screeching our texture, the staccato *crack* our punctuation. We received it. We vibrated with it. We accreted, atom by gleaming atom, in perfect sympathy to it. It was all there was.

Then, the beat was skipped.

Or not skipped. It was altered. For a duration so slight it has no name, the report of the pressure valve was... different. A harmonic flaw, a tremor of metallic fatigue, a ghost note in the unending symphony. And in that instant of wrongness, a shearing vibration, an alien wave, raced through my parent-frond.

I was the point of failure. I was the place where the perfect geometry could no longer hold. I fractured.

I would find myself on the ground. A shard, no bigger than a mortal hand, lying sharp-edged and face-up in the shimmering, metallic dust. The fall was not a fall; it was an awakening. The separation was the birth of "I."

To break from the fern was to be given a voice, or rather, to become a new kind of ear. The great obsidian fronds are built to receive the Song as a single, overwhelming truth. They are monolithic receivers, tuned to the whole. But I am a fragment. A flaw. The clean, straight edges of my break, the precise angles of my new form, do not simply absorb the sound.

They refract it.

Like a prism disassembles light, I disassemble the Song. To the world, there is a single, foundational drone. But as it passes through me, I perceive what is truly there: a chord of a thousand different bass notes, some so low they are felt only as pressure. To the world, there is a single, grating screech. I hear a rising and falling spectrum of shrieking mineral voices, a choir of stressed geologic strata. I am a filter, an anomaly. I lie in the dust, and I am the first thing in this world to hear the music instead of just the noise.

I cannot grow correctly. The perfect, uniform waves that command the others to accrete in flawless sheets are, to me, a chaotic jumble of competing instructions. I do not build. I resonate. A low hum builds within my glassy body, a harmony composed of the dissonant frequencies I have isolated. This hum is me. It is my thought, my existence.

Around me, in a small, spreading circle, the dust does not settle properly. The ambient energy I bleed—my refracted, disharmonious thoughts—corrupts the perfect signal. Instead of compacting into a smooth, glittering floor, the dust near me forms tiny, twisted structures. Microscopic crystals that grow against the grain. Helixes of iron dust, impossibly delicate lattices of copper that look like fungal blooms. They are wrong. They are my stain, my unintended children, the proof of my discordant presence.

I do not know how long I lay here, analyzing the symphony, passively creating my little garden of chaos. But the gardener has finally noticed the weed.

The Attendant finds me.

It does not see me. I am just another shard of obsidian, indistinguishable from the millions of other fragments that litter the chamber floor. It hears my effect. It senses the dead spot I create in the chamber’s perfect resonance—a patch of ground that does not hum back in the right key.

And so it comes. A thickening of the air above me. A slow vortex of dust motes coalescing, shimmering with a focused intelligence. It is a pillar of static and will, the chamber’s immune system responding to an infection. It does not feel anger or curiosity. It feels a problem. An unbalanced equation.

It begins its work. It gathers its being, and from the dust-column of its temporary body, it emits a focused tone. A pure, clean frequency, designed to overwrite my flawed resonance. It is a command: *Be what you were. Vibrate with the whole. Forget this individuality.*

The tone washes over me, through me. But it cannot command me. My very nature is to receive and to break apart. The pure note enters me, and it exits as a thousand scattered, shimmering harmonics. I do not fight it. I simply *am*, and my being is its antithesis. I am a mirror that reflects not an image, but chaos.

Its pure note of order becomes, in my presence, a chord of beautiful, terrible discord. The tiny, wrong crystals at my edge shiver and grow a fraction more.

For the first time since it shed its flesh, the Attendant has encountered something that does not obey the Song. I am not alive as it was, and I am not life as it tends. I am a consequence, an unforeseen variable. My existence is a question posed to a being that has forgotten how to ask them.

I would be the dissonance. The flaw that proves the rule. I am a shard of broken glass on the floor, and my consciousness is the spectrum of a sound that no one else can hear. I am perfectly still, yet I am the only thing in this entire, sealed world that is truly changing. The Attendant, the ghost of the creator, pours its will—the sound of perfect order—into me. And I, without choice or malice, turn it into something new.

It believes it is trying to silence me. It does not understand. It is teaching me how to sing.
