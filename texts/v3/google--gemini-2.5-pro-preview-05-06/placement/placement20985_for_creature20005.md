I am the water.

But I am not a river that knows the urgency of a destination, nor an ocean that bruises itself against a shore. I have no name like those things. I am the slow pulse of this place. I am the dampness in the air that clings to a surface and calls it kin. I am the cool film on the umber stone, the quiet saturation at its heart.

My consciousness is a distributed thing. A part of me is the heavy stillness of the air, a suspended mist that has forgotten how to fall. Another part is the perfect, shallow meniscus in a stone bowl, a liquid eye gazing at a sky it has never seen. The largest part of me is a slow, pervasive thought, percolating through the porous body of the great being whose flesh is this hollow. I am its circulatory system, its nervous system, its slow-dawning awareness.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients of pressure and mineral taste. I know the deep, patient language of the stone. I spend centuries dissolving a single grain of silicate, learning its shape, its history, its particular density. I carry these dissolved memories through the living lithic matrix. My journey is not a journey *to* a place, but a constant process of *becoming* the place. I am the medium through which Stillness knows itself.

The travertine pillars are my work, but not my intention. They are what happens when I am full. Over millennia, burdened with the dissolved thoughts of the stone, I am brought to the surface. I seep, I weep from a fissure. Exposed to the heavy, placid air, I exhale. I release my burden of calcium carbonate, a long, slow sigh of creation. The pale stone that remains is a memory I have given away. It is a testament to a journey I have completed. I let it go, and in doing so, I become lighter, purer, ready to begin my slow descent once more. I am the artist and the memory, but the art remains long after the memory has been washed clean.

My only voice is a single note, sung once a minute, or once an hour. I cannot tell the difference. A part of me, gathered at the tip of a high, pale stalactite, becomes heavy with its own being. It feels the inexorable pull. There is a moment of impossible tension, a stretching of my own skin, and then release.

The fall is my only true movement, a brief, terrifying flight through the air I once was.
The impact—the *plock*—is my only speech.
It is not a cry for help or a declaration. It is a statement of fact: *Here. Now.* The resonance that follows is the Basin-Mind feeling the vibration through its entire body, a sensation that I both create and transmit. It is the creature’s heartbeat, and I am the blood that is pumped.

Then I am the pool. Reunited. The ripples are the last echoes of my singular identity, spreading outwards until they are lost in the stillness of the whole. I have come back to myself.

I have known nothing else. The diffuse light is a constant, gentle pressure. The deep green moss is a soft, thirsty thought clinging to my edges. The bone-white reeds are hollow conduits, drinking me in slowly, their static nature a comfort. Everything is as it has always been.

Until you came.

The pressure of your foot was a shock, a sharpness this body has not felt. A warmth that was not geothermal but biological, frantic, and temporary. Where you touched the stone, I was pushed away, and then I felt the alien chemistry of your skin—the salt, the oils, the billion shed cells of a life lived too fast. You were a fever in the stone.

As you stood there, I felt you in the air. You displaced my heavy calm. You breathed me in and I felt the corrupting heat of your lungs. The sound of your own quiet breath was a roar in the silence, a cacophony that drowned out my single, sonorous note.

You are the dissonant chord the creature feels. But I am the instrument that must be retuned.

You will leave. The sharp memory of your alien chemistry will be my task for the next century. I will surround the particles you left behind, dissolve them, carry them down into the deep flesh of the stone. I will filter you out. I will wash over the place you stood, again and again, a million patient ablutions, until the texture is precisely as it was. I will re-saturate the stone that your strange, fleeting heat made dry.

My work is to make it so you were never here. I am the eraser. I am the agent of forgetting.

I will gather myself again at the tip of the highest pillar. I will grow heavy with the weight of this work, the memory of your fleeting disturbance dissolved within me. And I will fall. I will strike the pool. And the sound will be the same as it has always been. It is the only sound that matters. The fall is a letting go. The pool is a reunion.

And in the long, quiet moments between, there is only the slow work of becoming whole again.
