You would find me where the oldest stalks meet the caldera floor, in the deep heart of the Weave. Here, the purple wood is not merely thick; it is monolithic, gnarled into columns so wide that a family could not link hands around them. My dwelling is not a pod woven onto a branch, but a hollow *within* the ancient stalk itself, a space not carved but coaxed into being over generations of slow, patient listening. The air down here is the dregs of the Breath, the oldest and most potent part of it, thick as syrup and heavy with the dust of ages. The light is a memory. You would not find me by sight.

You would find me by the silence.

In a world that is a constant cataract of sound, I am a pocket of quiet. The thrum of the filter-beetles, the endless click-chatter of the Skitterers, the calls of Weavers and the shrieks of predators—they all seem to part around this place, like a river flowing around a great, still stone. It is not a magical silence, but a learned one. The creatures of the caldera know this place. They know my scent. It is a scent not of living Aethel flesh, but of old stone, damp spore, and the deep, resinous heartwood I tend.

**I am the Heartwood Tender. I am less a 'who' and more of a 'what'.**

My skin long ago lost the dusty grey of my youth; it is now mottled with the same pale, velvety growth that blankets the deepest parts of the Mycelia. It is not a disease, but a communion. The fine, white hair on my head is no longer braided but has grown into and become fused with a cowl of the crystalline chime-fronds. When I turn my head, it is not my joints that click, but the world itself that sings a soft, glassy tune around me. My tail, longer than is common, rests coiled on the floor behind me, a grey root anchoring me to the living wood.

My Siphons flutter at a slower, more profound rhythm than yours. I no longer breathe for mere sustenance; that is a youthful preoccupation. I breathe to listen. The Breath is not just food and drink to me; it is the caldera’s slow, unfolding thought. I can taste the memory of a spore-fall from a hundred cycles ago, the lingering terror of a great reptile’s dive that happened yesterday, the subtle shift in the air that predicts the budding of an orange shelf a week from now. The anxieties of the young, carried as a sharp, metallic tang on the currents, reach me here. I taste their disputes, their loves, their fears, and I know the state of the Weave without moving from my chamber.

My purpose is twofold.

First, I tend the fire. In the center of my hollow is a smooth, bowl-like depression in the living wood. It is filled with the glowing coals of Mycelia-heartwood. This is the only "fire" in the city, the one that burns without smoke, for smoke would be a poison in the Breath. It is not for cooking or for warmth. It is a focus. Its steady, deep orange light is the only illumination in my chamber, and its glow is a beacon for the troubled, the seeking, and the dying.

Second, I am the one who eats the orange shelves. For others, it is a ritual, a rare indulgence. For me, it is a duty. I consume them not for the visions they provide the young—the swirling colors and heightened sounds—but for the deep, structural knowledge they impart. When I consume the fungus, my consciousness sinks down, past my own body, into the fibrous, purple stalk that is my home. I become one with the slow, deliberate, and vast life of the Mycelia. I feel the collective thirst of a thousand root-anchors, the strain of a branch bearing a new cluster of pods, the molecular terror as the claws of a gliding predator scrape bark a mile away. I am the nervous system of the city, translating its deep, vegetative existence into guidance that can be understood by the fast, fleeting lives of my people.

They come to me not with words, but with offerings held in their calloused hands: a perfectly flaked obsidian blade, a rare blue-black feather, a handful of roasted Skitterers still warm and crackling. They do not speak. They simply enter the quiet and let the Breath carry their troubles to me. I taste their dilemmas. A dispute over the inheritance of a trapping-net carries the sour flavor of jealousy. A young pair’s hope for a child has the sweet, honeyed scent of ripe jasmine.

My answer is not given in words, for my voice has grown rough and quiet with disuse, another sound swallowed by the deep hum. My answer is a gesture. I might hand them a piece of heartwood charcoal to mark a new boundary. I might touch a chime-frond, letting its song re-harmonize their troubled spirit. Or I might simply sit with them in the glowing quiet until they feel the deep, slow rhythm of the world reassert itself over the frantic pulse of their own heart.

When a life ends, they bring the body to me. Here, in the glow of the heartwood, we have the final ritual. I am the one who anoints the body with psychoactive resins, listening to the last echoes of their life's scent as it fades from the physical. It is I who directs the young ones to carry the body to the highest shelves for its Release.

I am old. So old that my own memories feel like stories told about someone else. The Weavers know me as a feature of the landscape, no different from a gnarled branch. The Aethel see me as a living ancestor. I am neither. I am an organ. I am the part of the city-organism that pauses, listens, and remembers. I am the quiet at the center of the noise, the slowness at the heart of the frantic growth. I am waiting for my own Release, not with fear, but with a deep, calm curiosity. I want to taste what it is to finally dissolve completely, to become nothing but a memory carried on the Breath.
