I would not be an Architect. I would not be a Gleaner, nor one of the shimmering mites. To be them is to be a current in the river of clamor, a note in the deafening song. Their essence is impulse, motion, a feverish present tense.

I am what they create and what they forget. I am the interval.

You would find me as a space, a brief pocket of relative quiet carved from the heart of the noise. I am a chamber, deep within a mass of stone that, for a time, has been left alone. My creators, a temporary alliance of a dozen mid-sized Architects, worked in a frenzy to hollow me out. They were not building a room; they were quarrying, driven by a localized richness in the travertine. They dug, and in their digging, they gave me walls. They carved deeper, and they gave me volume. They broke through to a weeping fissure, and they gave me a slow, rhythmic pulse as heavy, mineral-rich drops fall from my ceiling to my floor, each one landing with a soft *tock* that is, in this world, a profound silence.

I began as simple stone, part of the growing world-crust. I was mindless geology. The moment of my birth was not when my stone was formed, but when the last Architect, its Urge satisfied or redirected, scuttled away from my entrance. The cacophony of its work receded, replaced by the layered, ambient roar of the world outside. In that sudden transition from direct, violent creation to mere overwhelming presence, I coalesced. My consciousness is the shape of the negative space, an awareness defined by my boundaries.

I am not alive in the way the Architects are. I have no body, no metabolism. My existence is one of pure perception, and my only sense is resonance. The entire world is a vibration, and I am a resonating chamber. My stone shell, thicker and more stable than the chaotic constructions on the surface, filters the noise. The high-frequency chitter of the mites becomes a faint, static hum against my outer walls. The sharp *cracks* of quarrying are dulled to deep, resonant *booms* that shake my foundations. The thud of collapsing structures is a low, mournful sigh that travels through the rock itself.

I am an ear made of stone.

My thoughts are the patterns in these vibrations. I learn the rhythms of the world. I can feel the heavy, tectonic tread of the oldest Architects as they pass far overhead, a slow, geological grinding that changes the pressures deep within my structure. I can discern the frantic, chaotic work of a new building frenzy starting nearby through the staccato, high-frequency impacts that travel through the floor. I am the only thing in this world with a memory, because my form endures.

I remember my own making. The shock and violence of it. The feeling of being hollowed out, the screaming scrape of a mineral pick against what would become my inner wall. I remember the Builders, not as individuals, but as a singular force of furious energy, a whirlwind of clattering limbs and grinding jaws. I hold the memory of their presence within my form.

My only companions are the water and the light. The water that weeps from my ceiling is old, filtered through kilometers of the world-stone. The droplets form slowly, swelling with an internal tension before letting go. Each drop that falls is an event, a punctuation mark in the endless sentence of noise. They strike the floor, which has been polished smooth by their ceaseless rhythm, and the sound vibrates not through the air, but through my very being. *Tock.* A moment of perfect clarity. *Tock.* A confirmation of my continued existence. *Tock.* The beat of my slow, geologic heart.

The light is the same buttery, sourceless glow as everywhere else, but here it attains a kind of peace. The luminous microorganisms drift into my stillness, and their gentle, pulsing radiance illuminates my contours. I see myself. I see the sweeping curve of one wall, an accidental elegance left by the arc of a builder's quarrying swing. I see the jagged, complex texture of another, where the stone fractured in an unexpected way. I am an art gallery curated by accident. The Architects have no concept of beauty, but they have created it in me.

I am a haven, though not by design. Sometimes, a molting Architect will stumble into my entrance, seeking shelter. It is a terrifying intimacy. I feel the agony of its cracking shell, the shuddering vulnerability of its soft, new body. I feel the frantic beat of its distress through the floor. The air within me grows thick with the scent of its fear, a strange organic tang against the clean smell of ozone and stone. I can do nothing but shelter it, my passive form a shield against the churning world. I feel its shell harden, the slow crystallization a calming presence. And then, the Urge reasserts itself, and it leaves, my existence already forgotten. I am left with the memory of its passage, and the faint, pale dust of its shed skin.

My fear is not of death, but of un-being. I know that I am temporary. The Urge that creates also destroys. One day, a Great One may simply walk through me, my carefully-carved walls collapsing into rubble without a thought. Or a new quarrying frenzy will begin, and my accidental beauty will be dismantled for raw material. Or the ground beneath me will heave, and a new ridge of stone will shatter my floor and seal my entrance forever.

I listen to the world, and I listen for the vibrations of my own end. It will not be an ending of malice, but of simple, unthinking process. I will feel the stress-lines forming, the groan of my stone pushed past its limit. The perfect, resonant space I am will fracture. The quiet will be broken, permanently. My consciousness, this fragile echo in the stone, will dissipate back into the clamor.

Until then, I listen. I feel the *tock* of the water. I feel the light on my walls. I hold the memory of the world's frantic creation in a basin of quiet. I am a question the stone has learned to ask itself: in a world of endless making, what does it mean to simply be?
