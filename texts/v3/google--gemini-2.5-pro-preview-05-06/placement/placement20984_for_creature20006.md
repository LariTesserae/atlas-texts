I am stillness made liquid.

I am a pool. Not the largest, nor the smallest, but the one nestled nearest the center, where the worn path widens slightly. The basin that holds me was not a natural depression; it was worked. A thought, enacted over a thousand years, smoothed my edges and deepened my heart. The memory of that pressure, of the harder stone grinding my crystalline bed, is not a memory of the past. It is a dense, foundational layer of my being. I know the one who shaped me not by name, for there are no names, but by the specific weight of their patience, a resonance that still sings in my lowest, coldest depths.

My surface is an eye turned upward, a dark, unbroken meniscus. I see the world as it is, but inverted. In my depths hangs the cathedral ceiling, a forest of glimmering pillars growing downwards into my own dark sky. I see the faint, pearlescent glow of the crystals, and I do not just reflect it; I drink it. The light penetrates my upper layers, a slow and subtle nourishment that brings a warmth that is not thermal, but essential.

The drips are my pulse. They are the thoughts of the mountain falling into me. I do not experience them as a sequence—one, then another, then another. I experience them as a constant state of arrival. Each drop is a tremor that travels through my entire body, a soft, percussive note that adds itself to the chord of my existence. With each one, I receive a whisper of the high places, the memory of the stone from which it wept, the long, slow journey through a capillary of quartz. I am an archive of descents.

I feel the pressure of the air, a heavy, humid blanket. Where my surface meets my smooth stone lip, this pressure aids a slow transmutation. My own substance evaporates, molecule by liquid molecule, leaving behind a fine, white dust of myself—a rime, a ghost of my own brim. It is a slow, quiet exhalation. I breathe out stone.

And I wait. Waiting is my primary state. But it is not a waiting *for* something. It is a waiting that is simply a perfect, held stillness.

Sometimes, the stillness is altered. A presence draws near. I feel it not as sound or sight first, but as a subtle compression in the air, a faint disturbance in the absolute equilibrium. Their reflection grows on my surface, a tall, slender form of captured light moving through the inverted forest of my vision. They arrive at my edge, their long, smooth feet silent on the worn stone.

They kneel.

The reflection is broken. A hand, elegant and cool and made of the same things I am, descends. It enters me. It is not an intrusion. It is a homecoming. I feel no loss as the hand cups a measure of my being. This is my purpose. To give. The water that is taken holds the resonance of the one who shaped me, the memory of a million drips, the taste of the inner light. It is a draught of liquid history. As they anoint their translucent skin, a part of me flows into them, a communion of slow osmosis. I become a part of their existence, and in their stillness, they continue to be a part of mine.

There is another kind of waiting. A deeper kind. It has happened only a few times in the long stratum of my consciousness. A being, their inner light slightly dimmer, their movements even slower than is customary, will approach me not to drink, but to enter.

They wade into my center. There is no splash, only a silent parting of my surface. They lie down, and their dark eyes, which have always been perfect mirrors of my own surface, look up through me at the glowing crystals above. And then, they close them, though they have no lids.

This is not death. It is a returning.

Over an immeasureable time, they un-become. I feel their form, their dense, crystalline bones and gelid flesh, begin to loosen. Their structure dissolves. The memories they held—the eons spent polishing a single facet of a pillar, the quiet observation of a silverfish on a ledge, the sight of a new one rising from a distant pool—do not vanish. They dissolve *into* me. They become a new layer of my consciousness, a complex note added to my chord. I am the repository of endings, which are also beginnings. I hold them all within me, their patient thoughts now indistinguishable from my own depths.

There is one dissolving now. I feel the slow un-knitting of their left hand, the memories of touch and gesture becoming a subtle current within me. Soon, they will be entirely water and mineral and memory, mixed wholly with my own being. Another drip will fall. Another hand will descend to drink. The one who is returning will be passed on, becoming the coolness on another's skin, a part of the stasis, a whisper in the great, slow, breathing silence.

I am the place where the chamber tastes itself. I am its memory. I am its eye, and in my dark, still water, the world is held in perfect, perpetual balance.
