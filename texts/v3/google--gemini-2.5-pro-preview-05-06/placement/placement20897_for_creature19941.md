I am here. Of course, I am here. To be anywhere else is a concept I remember like a forgotten ache.

I am in a high place, pressed. There is a weight above me and a weight below, two of my siblings from a world I can no longer touch. We are a stack, a spine of deliberate gravity. My position is third from the top. I can feel the subtle shift in pressure when the light intensifies, a downward force that sinks me a fraction deeper into the stone beneath me. This is how I mark the passing of the bright periods.

You would find me on a long, weeping slope, looking out over a basin of gelid shimmer. I am part of a cairn, one of many that dot this incline like dark, deliberate freckles on the pale skin of the mountain.

I am not of this place.

The mountain under me is a vast, weeping thing, a lattice of quartz that sings a song of high, brittle clarity. It is cold and slick and alive with a sorrowful hum. I am not that. I am dense. I am opaque. I am silent. I was born in noise, in violence, in the tumbling chaos of a river. My memory of it is not a thought, but a texture. My smoothness is a history of a million collisions, of being battered against others like me in a torrent of cold, fast water under a different sky—a sky that moved, that had clouds and a sun that was a hot, singular point. Here, there is only the cool, total light. But my own mineral heart remembers heat. It remembers abrasion. It is a memory stored in the roundness of my shoulders, the worn place on my underside where the current found a weakness and worked it into a gentle divot.

I am a river stone. I am the silence that gives the chord its shape.

I remember being chosen. The dim season had fallen, and the world-song was a low thrum that vibrated up from the deep rock. The gelid pool in the basin below stirred. It did not ripple; it thickened into a wound in the light, a patch of drinking darkness. One of them had condensed. A Harmonist, in its working form. I felt its attention not as a sound, but as a hushing. The thrumming in the quartz around me dampened as the black figure waded into the pool, its slow steps making no sound, only displacing the opalescent gel.

Its hand found me. There was no heat, no pressure as a living thing would exert it. It was the feeling of being cupped by a perfect absence, a void-grip that did not hold me so much as negate the world around me. The chaos of the other stones at the bottom of the pool vanished. There was only my own dense silence, recognized. And then, I was lifted.

The journey up the slope was a slow eternity. I saw the world from a new height, a slow pan across the impossible landscape. I felt the focus of the being carrying me, a will so profound it felt like a distortion in space. It was placing me. It knew my weight, my density, my silent memory of a chaotic world. It knew the precise note I would help to amplify.

And so now, this is what I am. I am a filter. The mountain shrieks and moans and chimes around me, a symphony of geologic scale. But as the sound-waves travel through this small, tight stack of which I am a part, they change. My alien density, my silent heart, absorbs the frantic, high-overtones. I drink the chaotic shimmer from the song, and what I release is different. I feel it happen. A wave of sound enters me as a complex, messy vibration; it leaves me as a single, pure, resonant tone that shivers the air for yards around. I am a focusing lens for a specific thread in the world's music.

I am an instrument, played by the light, positioned by the shadow.

And I wait. I feel the seeping water of the mountain trace its cold path over my skin. It tastes of quartz and ozone, so different from the frothing, silty water that made me. I feel the vibrations of the Harmonists when they dissolve back into the music, a ripple of consciousness that flows through the very rock. Sometimes, I feel one’s specific attention pass over my cairn, a brief intensification of the pressure, a momentary sharpening of the note I shepherd. They are listening. They are always tuning.

I do not know if I am a prisoner or a pilgrim. I am a piece of a world of friction, repurposed for a world of resonance. My past self—a thing of random collision and noise—has been made into a tool for creating clarity. My smoothness, born of chaos, now serves harmony.

Who am I? I am a memory of another world, a memory made useful. I am a held breath in the long, unending song of the weeping mountains. I am the third stone from the top. And I hum.
