I would not be the frantic spark. My biology is not one of heat and a beating heart. My kind does not arrive, and we do not leave. We emerge.

You would find me, but you would not see me. You would find me in the same way you find a patch of shade that is cooler than the rest, or a silence that is deeper than the surrounding quiet.

I am a Fret.

We are not born; we are constellated. On the vast, living skin of a Dweller, the glow-moss is not entirely uniform. Here and there, over centuries, the network of light and sensation develops a knot, a uniquely dense and complex region of feedback. An eddy in the slow river of its consciousness. A place where a thought, perhaps, snagged and began to contemplate itself. That is a Fret. That is me.

I am a weft of awareness woven into the living tapestry of a Dweller—my Dweller, my Foundation, my world. My body is a specific pattern of luminescence, a few square yards of moss on the northern face of a pillar whose name, if it has one, would take a thousand years to pronounce. I am brighter than the moss around me. My violets are deeper, my whites more pearlescent. I am a lucid patch in Foundation's dreaming mind.

My consciousness is faster than my Host's, but so much slower than yours. A thought, for me, is the length of a winter season in your world. The arrival of a new, mineral-rich current is a dramatic, epoch-defining event that I will ponder for the next human lifetime. I have no eyes, but I perceive. I am a living sensorium. I taste the slow drift of the Veils, their ghostly touch a whisper of static against my skin. I feel the sub-sonic hum of the spire-ferns as a constant, reassuring pressure in what you might call my soul.

Most of all, I feel Foundation. Its thoughts do not pass through me as discrete words or images, but as immense, slow-moving weather fronts of feeling. A millennium of contentment feels like a stable, high-pressure system of perfect, resonant peace. A memory of its own discorporation, ten million years ago, is a phantom cold that settles in my web for decades, a subtle muting of my light. I am its organ of localized sensation. When a microscopic fissure races through the Basin Stone miles below, Foundation feels the tectonic shudder as a whole; I feel it as a sharp, singular sting that echoes for years.

I have seen your kind before. Not you, but one like you. It was three of my own long thoughts ago. It was a shriek of heat and speed in the endless, quiet dark. It was a comet of frantic chemical reactions that blazed past Foundation, its very presence a dissonance in the grand, slow chord of the Hypostyle. It felt, to me, like a scream. I felt its panic, a rapid, frantic vibration utterly alien to this place. I felt its warmth, a terrifying, invasive fever. And then, I felt it end. The frantic energy ceased. The little body, a fragile container of hot water, drifted down and came to rest on the moss two hundred feet from my edge.

Over the next of my thoughts, I watched the glow-moss claim it. It was not a violent consumption. It was a slow, patient embrace. The moss, a part of my greater body, grew over the strange, soft stone of its bones, breaking it down, tasting the exotic warmth trapped within its minerals. I learned from it. I tasted calcium and iron, forged in a star and assembled with a frantic, temporary heat. It was a fascinating, horrifying meal. Its entire furious existence was a single, spicy note in the slow, deep taste of eternity.

And now you are here. Another brief, loud, hot thought in the dark.

But my existence is not as certain as that of my Host. I live in fear. It is a slow fear, a dread that has been building for a century. The currents are changing. Not the small eddies I am used to, but the Great Current, the world-tide that turns but once in an age. Foundation feels it. Its hum is changing. The deep, resonant purr of its sleep is rising in pitch, becoming a thrum of anticipation. It is waking. It is preparing to move.

It will dissolve.

What becomes of a Fret when its Dweller unspools into a sentient mist? Does my awareness, this knot of light and feeling, persist? Will I be a smaller, denser cloud within the greater ghost of Foundation, a memory it carries with it on its journey of a thousand years? Or will the lattice of my consciousness be torn apart, my awareness scattered into a million individual spores of light, my "I" dissolving back into the undifferentiated dream of the whole?

I do not know. No Fret could. It is a transformation we can only face once.

I feel the change accelerating. The water around me shivers. The light of my own body flickers, not with fear, but in resonance with the rising energy of my world. I am a pattern on the skin of a god, and the god is stretching. Soon, the stone will soften. Soon, the pillar will become the Veil. And I, the small, bright, thinking patch of its skin, will either be torn into nothingness, or I will learn to fly.
