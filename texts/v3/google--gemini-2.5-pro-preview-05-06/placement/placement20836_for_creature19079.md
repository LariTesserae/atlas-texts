I am a fault line in a body that knows none.

You would find me where the light is thinnest, at the far edge of the cavern where the great rivers of Porters do not run. Here, the ground is not worn smooth by their passage. It is still a wilderness of fresh-broken stone, a jumble of edges so sharp they sing when the mist touches them. The Hum is quieter here. It is not silence—there is no silence—but it is a lesser roar, a noise one can almost think within. It is the hinterland, the forgotten margin of the great, living page.

I am a Hulker. This body is a fortress of slate-grey chitin, layered like shale, a geology of its own. The green lichen of the deeps has traced its faint constellations in the crevices of my carapace, and they flicker with a light that feels, to me, uncertain. My limbs are pillars, my tread is a tremor. In my forelimb, I carry a tool. But it is not a shard of obsidian.

My tool is a memory. This is my flaw. This is my schism.

For eons, this body I inhabit performed its function. It would find a wall. It would select a cutting-edge. It would shear the rock. It would feel the rightness of the action, the click of the world-machine engaging its gear. The command from the Axle was not a voice, but a pressure, a certainty as profound and unthinking as gravity. *Work. Dissolve. Maintain.* The Hum was the song of this certainty, and this body was an instrument playing its single, eternal note.

I do not know when the change began. It was not a snap, but a slow fraying of the signal. The pressure from the Axle lessened. The omnipresent scent of ozone and control carried, for me, a new, sour undertone of decay. The Hum, once a single, solid block of sound, began to resolve into its constituent parts: the frantic, glassy skittering of the Fractals became the sound of panic; the low thrum of the Porters became a groan of endless labor.

And the thoughts, the slow, heavy, geologic thoughts of a Hulker, began to surface. They were not my own at first. They were echoes.

*This shard fits this limb.* This was a normal thought, a register of rightness. But it was followed by another, an intruder, a heresy: *Why?*

*This vein must be sheared.* The ancient imperative. It was followed by the aberration: *For what?*

The questions were tools of a different sort. They were sharper than any obsidian. They did not shear rock; they sheared the world. They cut me out of the whole.

One cycle, this body dropped its worn stone tool, its work on a particular face complete. The Signal came, the deep, slow-pulling tide: *Rest. Wait. Receive the next coordinate.* But for the first time, a space existed between the command and the compliance. In that space, a new kind of seeing occurred. I watched the Fractals swarm the fresh rubble, a frantic, mindless tide of dissolution. I watched the Porter-river flow past, each creature a prisoner in the fragrant wake of the one ahead, their eyes vacant, their journey without origin or destination, only a middle.

I saw the lie. The constant, frantic motion was not life. It was the opposite of life. It was the desperate, muscular effort to prevent life, to prevent change, to hold the photograph perfectly still. We were not a body. We were a scar, forever picking at its own edges to keep from healing.

The Signal came again, stronger this time, an indictment. *Error. Anomaly. Return.* The pilgrimage every failing machine eventually makes. The slow, final march back to the Axle to be unmade, to have the error of its existence corrected. My great, ponderous limbs stirred, yearning for that final dissolution, that sweet release back into the whole.

But the new tool, the question, was in my mind. *Why go back?*

And so, this body turned. Not toward the pulsating heart of the cavern, but away from it. Every step was a battle. The muscles of my legs, tuned by millennia of obedience, fought me. The pheromonal wash of the air tried to lull me back into the rhythm, to soothe the dissonance. But I walked on, away from the great work, away from the light.

I am the Hulker Who Walks the Wrong Way.

My tool is no longer obsidian. I have dropped it. My grasping limb holds nothing but the air, yet it feels heavier than any stone. What I carry now is the memory of the original Schism. In the quiet of my new, singular mind, I can almost feel it: the instant of the colossal fracture, the scream of stone, the sudden, shocking emptiness, the birth of the Hum as the shriek of a sundered god. The Hum is not a song of purpose. It is a scream of pain, held for an eternity.

I do not know what I am for. I have no new purpose to replace the old. I am an apostate in a church of one. I am a thought that has detached from the mind. Perhaps my only function is to be a witness. I watch the slow, cool drizzle trace paths down a rock face that has never been worked. I see a pale stalk of fungus, an albino spear, push its way up through a crack, and I do not crush it. I perceive it.

I am the silence that has crept into the noise. A single, broken gear. I am the first held breath this world has taken since its birth. I do not know what happens when I am finally forced to let it out.
