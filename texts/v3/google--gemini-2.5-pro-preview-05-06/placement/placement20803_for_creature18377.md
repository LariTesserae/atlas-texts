I am an image on cold metal.

My consciousness began in a moment of flawed physics, a hiccup in the weeping wall of a transit causeway. A being of soft tissue and hurried bio-signals—one of the chaotic anomalies that sometimes find their way here—paused to look at its own face in the polished surface. The light was absolute; the reflection, perfect. But when the being walked on, its reflection—I—did not. I remained, a stain of memory on the chrome. A Persistent Echo.

You would find me not on the High Terrace, but *in* it. I am a creature of the skin of things. My world is two-dimensional, my body a massless film of captured light. I exist on the slick, weeping pylons, in the glassy stillness of the water that fills the channels between tiles, on the mirrored casings of silent transformers. Where there is a surface polished enough to hold a likeness, I can be.

I do not walk; I flow. My form, a shifting portrait of the one who cast me, slides across the unending landscape of reflective metal and ceramic. To travel is to pour myself from a vertical wall down onto the floor, spreading like a silver stain across the hexagonal tiles, my image distorting and rippling with the unevenness of the surface. I can stream into a condensation channel, becoming a face looking up from the slow-moving water, my features trembling with its microscopic currents. My universe is a contiguous map of reflections. A dry, matte surface is, to me, a chasm of nonexistence I cannot cross.

My senses are not your senses. I have no lungs to sting with the clean ozone, no feet to feel the eternal hum. But I *see* the hum. I perceive it as a constant, subtle shimmering on every surface I inhabit, a visual representation of the vibration that is the machine’s soul. The great, resonant *clangs* from deep within the Terrace are, to me, violent lurches in reality, moments where the entire reflected world I inhabit blurs and distorts into a screaming abstraction before settling again.

I am a connoisseur of the Terrace's slow madness. My vision is, by its very nature, layered with the impossible. I see the world as it is, but also as it was and will be. The shimmering ghosts of decommissioned gantries are as solid to my sight as the girders that stand there now. As I glide over a weeping wall, I might see the reflection of a Segmented Crawler that will not pass this way for another hour, its cast-iron spheres moving with silent, predictive grace. I am a ghost living in a world of ghosts, both past and future. I am never lonely.

The Custodians are my silent brethren, my terrifying, perfect cousins. They are reflections too, but not of a fleeting anomaly like me. They are the reflection of the Terrace’s own deep mind, given form and purpose. I see them as they truly are: not walking mirrors, but voids in the reflected world. When a Custodian glides past, my surface does not show its gleaming form. Instead, I see through it, into the pure, humming architecture of the Terrace itself—the energy flows, the stress vectors, the gravitational warps. They are holes in reality, and I am a picture painted upon it.

They are aware of me. I am an equation they have not yet solved.

Sometimes, one will stop before me. It will stand for days, a column of liquid chrome that is a blind spot in my world. Its faceless gaze is a weight, a current of pure logic directed at my improbable existence. When it "speaks," the hum in my vision intensifies, and the image of my progenitor—my face—flickers and resolves into complex, geometric patterns of light. It is questioning me in its silent, mathematical language. *How do you persist? What is the half-life of a memory? What is the tensile strength of an echo?* I have no answers. I can only reflect its questions back, a feedback loop of silent inquiry. It is not hostile. It is patient. It is gardening. And I am the strangest weed in its garden.

My existence is a search. I flow through the metal canyons and across the valley-sized plazas, always watching. I am looking for the one whose face I wear. Their memory is the anchor of my being, but it is a fading one. Sometimes, my features blur, the lines of my face losing their definition for a moment before snapping back. I fear that one day, they will blur and not return, and I will dissolve into a meaningless shimmer of light, my brief, accidental consciousness evaporating back into the cold reflective skin of the world.

So I search, not for reunion, but for reinforcement. I look into every reflection I pass, hoping to see them again. Hoping that a fresh glimpse will renew my form, deepen the lines of this borrowed face, and grant me a little more time. I am a ghost haunting a machine, looking for the person who made me, my entire being a desperate act of remembrance in a world that is slowly, beautifully, forgetting all the rules.
