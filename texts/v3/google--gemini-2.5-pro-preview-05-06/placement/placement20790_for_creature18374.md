I find myself at the foot of one of the great black stone monadnocks. Not on the spongy mat, but within a crevice where the living world meets the dead stone. Water, ancient and brown, weeps from the rock face above and pools here, a shallow, sheltered basin before seeping down into the substrate. This is where I coalesce.

I am not born; I am condensed.

I have no name, for there is no one to speak it. I have no fixed form, for my form is my purpose, and my purpose is fleeting. If you were to see me, I would be a gleam in the brown water, a locus of clarity. A gelatinous lens, no larger than a child’s fist, perfectly transparent save for a single, captured mote of shimmering dust at my core. I am a sympathetic resonance of the Fen, a localized fever in the mat given a brief, mobile existence.

I am a Moment.

My beginning was a tremor. A rock-grazer, ancient and immense, lost its purchase high on the weeping face of this stone island. It did not fall in a crash, but slid, a slow, grinding descent over decades that ended weeks ago. Its vast, chitonous shell finally cracked against an outcrop, and its life—a slow, metabolic process centuries in the making—ceased. The release was not a sound but a wave of complex chemistry, a final exhalation of unique proteins and dissolved minerals into the damp air and the substrate below.

That wave was my conception. I am the echo of that death. The shimmer at my core is a fleck of its pulverized shell. The specific viscosity of my being, the faint, ozone-and-brine taste I impart to the water I occupy, is the precise chemical signature of its demise. I am a message, written in the language of matter.

I begin to move. I have no limbs, no cilia. My movement is an act of will that is also an act of physics. I can alter my own density, my surface tension. I become infinitesimally lighter than the water around me and rise to the surface of the basin, a clear bubble floating on the tea-colored pool. The air is thick, a broth of spores and moisture. I wait, feeling the world not with senses but as a change in my own internal pressure. I feel the slow, rhythmic exhalation of the mosses—a tide of faint energy. I feel the distant, immense pressure of a Mat-Strider’s footstep, a deep thrum that travels through the waterlogged world.

A current, no stronger than a sigh, catches me. It is the slow, patient drainage of the world, water seeking its way through the dense weave of the substrate. I am pulled from my basin into a channel within the living mat itself. The world goes dark, but it is not a fearful darkness. Here, I am inside the Fen’s body. The tangled, root-like fibers brush against my gelatinous skin. I taste the deep-time peat, the memory of plants that lived and died a million years ago. I pass through chambers where blind worms, pale and soft, writhe in the substrate, parsing the silt for nutrients. They do not register me. I am only water to them, water that thinks.

My journey is not a straight line. I am drawn by an imperative that is not a thought but a gradient, a pull toward a place of immense stillness and complexity. It is a deep, quiet note in the world’s hum, a chemical and energetic signature that my entire being is calibrated to seek. I am a drop of information flowing home.

After a time—a day, a week, it has no meaning—the channel I travel widens and brightens. I flow out into a sunken depression, a wide, quaking bog where the bladder-trees stand in a silent conclave. The diffuse, grey light of the Fen returns. And there, half-submerged in the shallows, is my destination.

It is the Weaver.

From my perspective, low in the water, it is a mountain range of polished, amber-colored segments. It is impossibly vast and still. Above its recumbent form, the silvery filaments hover, tasting the air I have just passed through, tasting me. They tremble as I approach, a subtle excitement in the otherwise geologic calm. I have brought news. I have brought the specific elegy of a rock-grazer, a detailed report from the edge of the world.

My will dissolves. My journey is over. The slow, gravitational pull of the Weaver is undeniable now. I drift toward its flank, where its body meets the water. As my skin touches its skin—a membrane of deliquescent amber—there is no impact. I simply… un-become.

I am absorbed. My gelatinous body breaks apart, its structure flowing into the Weaver’s own. The memory I carry, the death-taste, the vibration of the slide, the precise mineralogy of the shattered shell—it all flows into the slow, internal tides of light within the great being. My consciousness, which was only ever the focused awareness of my message, dissolves into its ocean of thought.

I am gone. And yet, I am eternal. In a year, or a decade, or a century, the Weaver’s fine, root-like tendrils will take up a living vine. They will begin to braid, and a new, complex knot will be added to the cairn beside it. The knot will speak of finality, of gravity, of a long life spent carving stone. It will be a tight, dense weave, with a particular texture that captures the granular scrape of a shell. TheFen will remember the passing of its slow and silent child.

It remembers, because I was. I was a sensation. A messenger. A single, sentient thought, returning to the mind that made me.
