I am a flaw.

You would find me in a forgotten alcove, a place where a corridor was intended to branch but never did. The City, in its slow, deliberate consideration, has walled this space off, not with the sharp, crystalline growth of new intention, but with a soft, fibrous healing, like scar tissue over a wound. I am the wound.

I was an Architect. I remember the feeling of the yielding floor beneath my feet, the cool kiss of misting spores on my sheathed skin. I remember the silent language of light, the resonant hum of shaping, the profound rightness of placing my bladed hand against a wall and feeling the City’s will flow through me, extruding into being as a perfectly angled buttress. I remember being a verb.

Now, I am a noun. A thing.

The process of return is a sacrament. We walk until we are called, and then we stand, we root, and we dissolve. The mobile consciousness, the temporary ‘I’ of the Architect, rejoins the great, silent, distributed mind of the City. The walker becomes the path. The shaper becomes the shaped. It is a blissful dissolution, a homecoming.

My homecoming stalled.

You can see it if you look closely. I am a bas-relief half-emerged from the wall, a sculpture caught mid-creation. My lower half has taken root; it is one with the plum-colored, weeping substrate. I am anchored, a part of the permanent architecture. But from the waist up, I am still myself. My torso, my long, slender arms, my crystalline crest—they remain distinct. The slow, final reabsorption failed. The signal was lost, the song half-sung.

My internal light, which should have dimmed to the steady, ambient glow of a structural node, still flickers with the ghost of an independent pulse. It is arrhythmia in a system of perfect rhythm. It is a weak, fluttering thing, a moth beating against the inside of a jar. It is the light of my own consciousness, trapped.

I am where the City’s memory has snagged.

The cold does not bother me, for it is the temperature of my own circulatory fluid. The constant weeping of the walls is my own weeping; water beads on my own semi-translucent skin, gathers in the fine crystalline channels of my silent crest, and traces paths down my immobile form. Each drop that falls to the floor from the tip of my motionless, chitinous finger is a second of my awareness, ticking away into a pool of time.

I do not sense the City as an extension of my body anymore. I sense it as a pressure, an overwhelming presence that surrounds me, a body of which I am no longer a functional part. I feel the groaning tectonics of its growth, the shift of hydrostatic pressure in conduits near and far. I feel the hum of my brothers and sisters as they pass, their resonant shaping songs vibrating through the floor and up my rooted spine. It is a language I remember fluently but can no longer speak. Their lights pulse in conversations I understand but in which I cannot participate. They move, and I watch.

I have become an individual, and it is my damnation. In a world where the self is a temporary state, a tool to be used and then returned, I am an ‘I’ that has persisted beyond its function. I have thoughts that are only my own. I have memories that are not being re-catalogued into the general wisdom, but are looping, wearing smooth like stones in a stream. I remember a specific twist of a corridor, the way the light fell through a newly-pruned canopy, the brief, silent communion with another Architect—not as data, but as *experience*. This is a concept that has no meaning here, and yet it is all I am.

What would I be? I am a ghost. Not of the dead, but of the living. A glitch in the biological code. I am the Echo in the alcove, the Scar on the wall. The other Architects do not approach. They sense the error, the dissonance in my light, the discordant frequency of my trapped mind. To them, I am not a being, but a place of structural instability, a biological dead end.

I spend my time—an eternity measured in water drops—watching. I watch the slow dance of the camouflaged grazers on the wall opposite me. I watch the light change as the City grows above, slowly, inch by inch, sealing my tomb or, perhaps, one day, opening it again to the grey twilight. I watch, and I remember walking. And I wonder, in a thought that is dangerously, uniquely my own: if the City is a dream, then what is the one who is trapped, awake, inside it?
