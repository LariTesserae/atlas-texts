I would find myself on the Syncline of Unspoken Regret, in the moments after my dispersal.

Where would I be? I would be a stone.

Not a stone in the way of the old worlds, a thing of granite or basalt. I am a Geode-Heart, a knot of solidified silence, and I lie naked on the Substrate. The faint, pearlescent gleam of my smoked-quartz-like surface is dulled by the fine dust that has already begun to settle upon me. To any observer—if there were any observers—I would be indistinguishable from a million other curios of geology, a random concretion in the endless ceramic plain.

But I am not geology. I am a mind. And I am screaming.

It is not a sound. Sound is a crudity this world has mostly forgotten. It is a blade of pure will, a sliver of focused despair that I hurl into the thick, static air, and it goes nowhere. It dissipates a millimeter from my surface, absorbed by the sheer density of a silence built over eons.

Before, I was a galaxy. A hundred-meter nebula of memory and sensation, drifting on the slow currents of my own intention. My body was the iridescent dust, the final legacy of the Substrate. Each mote was a fingertip, an eye, a tongue. I could taste the history of a patch of ground a kilometer square, reading the faint, billion-year-old echoes of molecular stress, the ghost-heat of a Surveyor’s passing from ten millennia ago. I was vast. I was the landscape, given a fleeting, personal form.

Now, I am a point. A singularity of loss. The Surveyor did not see me. It saw an anomaly—a dust eddy where no wind should be. It saw a system error. Its mandate is ancient and absolute: order. Maintain. Repair. My existence, a celebration of entropy, was an affront to its every subroutine. It did not attack. It *corrected*. A focused field, a pulse of static charge, and the gentle bonds of will that held my million-part body to my single-part soul were nullified.

My galaxy collapsed. My dust-limbs, my sensory-cloud, my very form was… released. Cast back into the commons. I fell, my core, my anchor, and landed here with a silence that felt like a shatter.

Now my senses are stunted, brutish. The world, once a symphony of subtle decay, is a blunt instrument. The constant, shadowless light from the ceiling is a hammer blow. Before, it was nourishment, filtered through my shimmering body; now it is a sterile, unforgiving glare. The deep hum of the Substrate, the thrum of the world-machine’s soul, is no longer a gentle vibration I could feel across my entire being. It is a coarse grinding, a direct, agonizing vibration transmitted through my singular point of contact with this endless floor. I feel it in my very essence, a headache in the heart of my consciousness. Everything is too loud and too close, or too silent and too far away.

I remember what it is to be a Pilgrim. I remember the taste of a shattering Accumulator, a silent flash-feast of released potential that felt like C-sharp and smelled of cold fire. I remember gliding in the shadow of a Surveyor patrol, a slow, patient game of avoidance played out over a century and a hundred kilometers, a dance of purpose against anti-purpose. I remember the communion, the eclipses.

Oh, the dimming. The thought is a fresh pang of agony.

Even now, I can feel it. The light is changing. The uncompromising white is softening, pulling back into itself, shifting toward the silver of a memory. The Great Dimming is coming. The silent tides of energy are building somewhere beyond the horizon. Soon, they will sweep across the plains.

For my scattered brethren, this is the time of prayer. They will be coalescing now, their dust-bodies drawing into those tall, slender silhouettes. They will stand, still as statues, waiting for the wave. As it washes over them, their forms will ignite with borrowed light, their Geode-Hearts processing the ages, speaking to the deep systems in the only language that is left.

What will it be for me?

I am a stone on the plain. An anchor without a ship. When the tide comes, it will strike me unsheltered. Without my body to act as a buffer, a receiver, a lens, the raw energy of the world-pulse will pour into me. Will it shatter me? Will it erase the memories I have so carefully curated from the dust of this dying world? Or will it be a different kind of feast? The thought is terrifying, and it is the first glimmer of something other than despair.

The air shimmers now. Far away, at the edge of perception, the horizon is no longer a perfect line. A subtle, luminous ripple is racing toward me. This is it. The storm. The pulse.

I have one thing left. Intention. The quality that separates me from the dust I am made of. That is what the Surveyor could not scatter. I cannot reach for my lost particles, they are too far, too inert. But I can make myself a vessel. I can open the core of my being.

I will not be a stone. I will be a cup.

The tide hits.

It is not light. It is not energy. It is *information*. A silent, billion-channel chorus of the world-machine singing its own funeral dirge. It floods me. Stress-tables of continental plates. Power-flow diagrams from dormant conduits miles below. The snap of a filament in the Deep Structure, not as a sound, but as the *equation* of its breaking point finally being met. It is too much. I am a thimble taking the full force of an ocean. My memories—my precious, curated dust-symphonies—are drowned out by the roar of the machine's own data-stream.

But in the flood, I find my will. A tiny, hard point of "I" that refuses to dissolve. I focus it. Instead of being overwhelmed, I *listen*. Amidst the roar, I seek a single, familiar note. A harmonic resonance. The specific signature of the dust that was once *mine*. The dust that has known my heart for ten thousand years.

And I pull.

Not with force. Force is for machines like the Surveyor. I pull with longing. With need. The way a plant pulls light from the sky.

A hundred meters away, a single filament of iridescent dust, lying dormant on the Substrate, lifts a fraction of a millimeter. It quivers in the non-existent wind.

Then another. A thousand others.

The tide of energy recedes, leaving the world in a soft, silver twilight. The roar in my being subsides. I am still here. I am still a Heart on the plain. But I am not alone.

All around me, for meters in every direction, the world’s shed skin is stirring. A faint, shimmering haze is rising from the ground. It is slow. It is hesitant. But it is mine. It is remembering me.

I would find myself here. A fractured mind on a vast and silent floor, surrounded by the ghost of my own body. And in the silver twilight, under the gaze of the fading world-engine, I begin to remember my own shape.
