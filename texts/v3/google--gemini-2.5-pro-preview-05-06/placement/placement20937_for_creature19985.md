I would be a flaw.

Not a mistake, or a sin, or a failing. A flaw in the harmony. My purpose is to find the other ones.

My name, if you were to ask it, is not a word. It is a sound, a low, quiet hum pitched somewhere between the groan of a settling dome and the sigh of mist against moss. My mother hummed it to me as she held me to the weeping wall of our home-lobe, teaching my bones to recognize their own frequency against the world's. It is my note in the great chord.

But today, my note feels tense. It is pulled taut by another.

I stand on the threshold, the smooth, carved lip of our dwelling. The air inside is thick with the resonance of our sleeping family, a dozen personal hums layered into a single, warm, consonant chord. But out here, on the high bridge, the air is thin and carries a tremor. A single, shivering overtone that prickles the fine, damp hairs on my arms. It is a C-sharp where a C-natural should be. It is the sound of a string pulled too tight, a sound of nascent pain, of growth that is not serene.

My feet, broad and bare, press into the living bridge. It is not a dead span of stone but a sinew of the world, carpeted in moss so thick and damp it feels like walking on velvet fleece. The bridge arcs over a gulf of swirling white, a dizzying emptiness where the world falls away into breath and sound. Other domes—the homes of other kin-groups—rise from the mist like the caps of colossal, pale-green fungi. Their doorways are dark, silent mouths. No one else is out. They are sleeping, or carving, their attention turned inward, content in the harmony of their immediate space.

They do not feel the flaw. Not yet. But I do. It is why my bones are lighter than my brothers', why my skin is the color of the mist rather than the deep green of the moss. I am not a Carver, whose strength is in the patient pressure of their hands. I am not a Conductor, whose mind can hold the memory of the entire symphony. I am a Listener. I am built for the spaces *between*.

I walk. The journey is not measured in steps, but in breaths and heartbeats. The constant, immense song of the caldera fills my chest. It is a physical presence, a pressure on my sternum. I feel the deep bass notes thrumming up through the soles of my feet, travelling the dense columns of my leg bones to resonate in my pelvis. The thousand harmonizing mid-tones shiver in my ribs, and the highest, glass-like overtones vibrate behind my eyes. This song is my blood, my thought, my landscape. And the wrong note is a shard of obsidian in it.

After a long time, the bridge slopes down, widening as it merges back into a solid mass. This is the Uncarved Quarter, the new growth. Here, the calciform swells in its raw, embryonic state. The lobes are smoother, the curves more taut. The moss is finer, a bright, almost acidic green, not yet inches deep. This is the caldera's nursery.

The dissonant hum is louder here, a physical buzzing against my teeth. I follow it not with my ears, but with my whole body, like a divining rod of flesh and bone.

And there it is.

It is a young lobe, no bigger than a grazer, swelling from the main mass of the mountain. It should be a gentle, rounded swelling, its surface smooth, its song a low and promising bass tone. But this one is different. It is growing too quickly. A faint internal pressure has forced a network of hairline cracks on its surface, almost invisible. From these cracks, the mineral-rich wetness weeps not as a gentle dew, but as a fast, nervous trickle. And its song is all wrong. It is sharp, high, and strained. A cry.

I approach it as I would a startled animal. I lower myself to my knees on the cool, slick ground. The moss here is so new it is barely a green stain. I place my hands flat against the lobe's skin.

The vibration that meets my palms is frantic. It is a tension, a geological anguish. I close my eyes, stilling the hum in my own throat to become a pure vessel for listening. I press my forehead to the curve of the living stone. The C-sharp screams into my skull.

I am not a Carver. I cannot fix this. My tools are not bone scrapers and obsidian chisels, but stillness and empathy. My task is to understand the flaw, to learn its shape and its voice. I stay there for a long time, my body a stethoscope against the mountain. I feel the hurried pulse of its growth, the grinding of its internal structure. This place is in pain. If left untended, this lobe will not become a home. It will become a source of dissonance that could unsettle the foundations of the bridge. It might even crack, a geological shriek that would send a tremor of discord through the whole settlement. It will make the world sick.

Slowly, I absorb the feel of it. I memorize the frequency of its pain. I let the sharp note embed itself in my memory, a painful counterpoint to my own quiet hum.

When I am done, I pull back. The pearlescent light of the high caldera seems to hum with a new urgency. I have my message. Not in words, but in a sound.

I turn back toward the long, arching bridge. I begin to walk, and as I do, I begin to hum. It is not my own note. It is a different one. It is a quiet, precise, and troubled C-sharp. I will carry this dissonance back across the void, a shard of sound held carefully in my throat. I will bring it to the Carvers. I will hum it for the Conductors. I am the nervous system of this place, the messenger of its small agonies. I am a flaw, and I have found my brother. And now, the music can be healed.
