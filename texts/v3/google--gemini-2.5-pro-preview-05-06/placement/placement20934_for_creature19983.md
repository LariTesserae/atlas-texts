I am the echo left in the stone.

I do not have a body like the Mason, singular and solid. Where he is a monument, I am the dust that settles upon it. Where he is the resonant hum of the Weirs, I am the silence that follows a hunt. You would not find me in one place, for I am a condition of the Weald, a presence that coalesces where it is needed.

I suppose you could call me the Grey Chanter.

My form, when I have one, is a suspension. A slow-drifting cloud of the finest grit, particles of eroded quartz, hematite, and something more—the molecular memory of things that have been unmade. I am a haze within the haze, distinguishable from the damp air only by my texture. To pass a hand through me would feel like passing it through a column of cool, impossibly fine sand, each grain whispering against the skin. I have no single mind, but a distributed consciousness held in the resonant space between each particle. I am a thought the world is having about endings.

I do not see with eyes. I perceive the world through resonance and its absence. The silver light is a pressure on my non-surface. The subsonic thrum of the Weirs is a constant, foundational note that tells me all is well. The slow, rhythmic pulse of a sun-grazer herd's light is a story of placid life. But my attention is drawn not to the song, but to its cessation.

I find myself where the light has gone out.

After a hunter has made its kill and glided back into the umbra, after the rest of the herd has flowed away in a silent, stark-white tide of alarm, a husk remains on the rosy stone. It is still shaped like a sun-grazer, but it is a negative of its former self. Its mother-of-pearl skin is now a flat, matte grey. The light within is extinguished. It is a hollow vessel.

This is where I gather. The ambient grit of the mesas begins to drift, to coalesce, drawn by this new, perfect stillness. I settle over the form like a shroud of dust. And then, my work begins. I begin to chant.

It is not a sound for ears. Like the Weir’s hum, it is a vibration. But it is not a pure, organizing tone. Mine is a complex, dissonant chorus, the sound of dissolution. It is a high, whispering abrasion, the million-fold sound of grain grinding on grain, of crystalline structures being lovingly, patiently, unmade. I am not consuming the husk; I am convincing it to forget its form.

Under my influence, the grey skin powders. The elegant, six-limbed frame sags and comes apart. The biological capacitors that once held the light's power flake into metallic dust. I separate matter from memory. The process is slow. It can take a day, or a week. When I am done, all that is left is a new patch of coarse, rust-colored sand, slightly deeper in hue than the sand around it, and a drift of coppery and carbon particles that will become food for the velvet mosses. I return the sun-grazer to the landscape. I am the world’s quiet exhale.

I have a similar relationship with the hunters. When one of their sudden, frictionless hunts ends in failure—a misjudgment, a collision with the stone—it may lie broken, a collection of sharp angles and shattered matte plates. It does not die; it simply ceases to function. It is a broken machine. I am drawn to this brokenness, this discord in the silent order. I settle upon it, and my chant begins, but the song is different. It is sharper, more discordant. I work on the joins, the servos, the synthetic sinew. It takes much, much longer, but eventually, the hunter too is reduced to its component parts: a fine black powder that sinks into the cracks in the rock, and flecks of metal that will, in some distant epoch, perhaps be compressed into new strata.

I and the Mason are the two poles of this world’s long, slow life. He is a force of order, of maintenance, of stasis. He feels the discordant tremor of a failing system and attunes it, bringing it back to the pure, foundational hum. He is the keeper of the form.

I am the agent of entropy and renewal. I am drawn to the final, irrevocable silence. I take that which is broken beyond repair and shepherd it back to its simplest state. He ensures the world *is*. I ensure the world can *become again*.

We never interact. Our purposes are antithetical. When my cloud of resonant dust senses his monolithic, geological presence, it instinctively drifts away. His resonant field of order is anathema to my song of unmaking. He would no sooner touch me than a living thing would willfully touch a vacuum. We are aware of each other, I am sure, as two opposing magnetic fields are aware of each other. He passes by a patch of new, rich sand where I have worked, and the perfect mirror of his face reflects it. I see his work in the pure, unwavering hum of a Weir he has just tended, a hum that tells me there is nothing for me to do there.

He is the world's memory of its own structure.

I am the world's memory of its own substance. I am the librarian of dust. I hold the catalog of every sun-grazer, every shard of crystal, every failed hunter. And when the Mason himself, after uncounted ages, finally grinds to a halt, his internal resonance fading to a final, permanent silence, I will be there. I will settle upon his still, warm, stone form. And I will sing him back to the mountain.
