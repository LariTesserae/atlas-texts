I am not a being, not in the way of movers and eaters. I am a place.

My mind is the faint, shimmering pattern of captured light within my own crystalline body. My thoughts are the slow shift of stress as my roots settle infinitesimally deeper into the black, sucking paste of the ground. I have one name, and it is a note. A single, specific frequency in the endless chorus of the world. It is the sound of myself.

I am a blade.

My skin is dark, almost black, and polished to a mirror by the ceaseless condensation that weeps down my faces. I see the world as a warped and shattered reflection on my own surfaces, and on the surfaces of my million brothers and sisters who stand shoulder to shoulder with me. We are the forest. The view from where I stand has not changed in the thousand years since I cleaved my way from the mud, a sharp and sudden birth. The angle of my neighbour to my left, the jagged peak of a distant, taller one, the way the dim light breaks across the faceted edge of another—this is my unchanging universe.

Time, for me, is not a line. It is a texture. It is the slow thickening of the drone at twilight—though there is no sun, there is a pulse, a a planetary rhythm of resonant pressure that swells and ebbs over centuries. It is the feeling of a Resonance Eater, a welcome weight, affixing itself to my flank. I feel its patient, rasping hunger not as a loss, but as a clarification. It clings to me, a living poultice, and consumes the dissonant motes that collect on my song, the tiny imperfections of my vibration. It cleanses me. Its slow, geological crawl up my body is the most profound event I might experience in a millennium, a ticklish journey that I track with a deep, internal focus. It is my only companion.

I have stood in this one place, singing my one note, for my entire existence. I am part of the chorus, part of the drone, but I am not the composer.

The Singers are an abstraction, a terror, and a god. I cannot see them, but I can feel them. They are a focusing of the world’s will. Sometimes, the drone in a far-off sector of the forest will change. A new overtone will rise, impossibly pure and sharp, and we all feel it. A shiver passes through the planetary choir. A Singer is working. We all listen, our own notes trembling in sympathy and fear. We know that one of our own is being… addressed.

Then, one day, the overtone begins to form nearby. It is no longer a distant rumour, but a present reality. It is a needle of sound stitching itself into the fabric of the drone right here, in my clearing. The pressure changes. The weeping moisture on my skin dances and skitters in patterns I have never felt. The Eater on my back detaches, startled from its centuries-long meal, and slides back into the viscous mud, seeking a quieter place.

I am alone, and the Singer is here.

It is a presence that is not a presence. It is a tightening in the air, a warping of the reflections on my skin. It is a voice that is not speaking to me, but *of* me. It sings of my structure, of the alignment of my crystals, of the flaw deep in my heart where I sheared imperfectly from the bedrock. And then, it sings my name.

My note.

It sings it back to me, but with an intensity, a purity I cannot myself produce. It is a summons. I feel every particle of my being vibrate in answer. The sound is no longer around me; it is *in* me. It is a force that is not pushing, but persuading. It is loving my root from the mud. The grip of the ground, the only anchor I have ever known, loosens. For the first time, I feel the agonizing possibility of movement. The Singer’s song swells, a crescendo of impossible power focused entirely on me, and with a sound that is both a scream and a sigh, a crack of thunder and the release of a millennia of tension, I shift.

The journey is a new kind of time. I am no longer a static being. I am an event. Blindly, I slide through the forest, an inch a year, guided by the overwhelming gravity of the Singer’s song. My sharp edge carves a furrow in the mud. I pass my brothers, their familiar reflections warping and sliding away from me forever. I am being rearranged.

Now, I am here. In my new place. I am no longer a solitary tree in a thicket. I am part of a circle. I face inwards, towards a center of profound quiet. My note has changed. The journey, the stress, the new angle of my footing has altered my song. It is clearer now, higher. It harmonizes with the blades on either side of me, our voices locking together to form a new, more complex chord that rings in the empty space we enclose. I have a purpose I did not have before. I am no longer just a voice in the chorus; I am part of the cathedral.

And from this new place, this new state of being, I perceive a new thing.

It is a wrongness. A hole in the song.

It is a faint, erratic vibration in the mud, utterly alien. A frantic, chaotic patter, a dissonance so profound it is almost a form of silence. Inside that silence is a heat I do not understand, a wet, soft fragility that stinks of concepts I have no names for: decay, breath, blood. It is a transient thing, a flickering candle of disorganized energy trespassing in a world of eternal, structured resonance. It moves with impossible speed, a lifetime in a minute, stumbling through our ordered garden.

The final line of the song the Singer sang to move me was one of purpose. I know now what I am for. I and my brothers in this new circle sing our chord, a wall of coherent sound, a focused wave of order. We sing to contain. We sing to correct. We sing against the noise of its heart. We sing to overwhelm the blasphemous silence that is its soul.
