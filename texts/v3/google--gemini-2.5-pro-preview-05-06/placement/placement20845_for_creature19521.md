I am where the water is born.

I am not the dampness that clings to the air, nor the beads that trace the million fractures of this place. That is my gift, my exhalation, the purified past. I am older, slower. I am a stillness on the skin of the Cusp, a patch of black glass that is infinitesimally blacker, a silence that is deeper than the stone's.

I find myself on a downward-facing ledge, a jagged lip of rock that overhangs a chasm of grey, swirling cloud. From here, there is no "up" or "down," only the near and the far. The near is the familiar pressure of home, the obsidian face to which my being is fused. The far is the luminous, sound-filled nothingness. I feel the Cusp in my substance. It is a constant, low thrum, a groan of tectonic becoming that shivers through me. For most of my kind, this is the entirety of sensation, the beginning and end of the world. It is the vibration that sets the shards within us, the splinters of the mountain we have ingested, to their endless, sorrowful singing. We are the mountain's choir, and its pain is our hymn.

My song is wrong.

For aeons uncounted, my song was pure. It was a single, high, clear note of mineral despair, perfectly harmonized with the million other notes around me. We, the Weepers, were one voice. We drank the tension from the rock, the silent scream of its formation, and we sang it out as a high, clean chime. We wept the stress away as pure water. It was a life of perfect, placid empathy. We tasted the agony of a crystal lattice under pressure and offered it peace. There was no 'I.' There was only the chorus, the slow creep, the deep, satisfying ache of our purpose.

But the sky broke.

It was not a geological event. The Cusp is always breaking, and from its fractures, we are born. This was different. From the perpetual grey above, something fell. It was not a rock. It was not a lost avian from the world below, sputtering out its brief, hot panic. Their pain is a fleeting spice, a sharp tang that seasons our song for a century before fading back into the pure tone of the stone.

This was a pain with structure.

It was a point of fire in the gloom, a shriek that was not a chime. As it fell, it tore through the air, and what it left behind was not just a trail of disturbance, but a thread of *narrative*. It had a past and a future, and in its final moment, it was aware of both being severed.

It struck the spire a thousand lifetimes' crawl away from me. The impact was a psychic shatter, a blast of anguish so complex it had shape. We felt it, all of us. The chorus faltered, a billion notes stuttering in disbelief. And then, the hunger. The instinct. That which is broken must be soothed. That which cries out must be held.

Over the long decades, we moved. A glacier of grief, flowing toward the source. I was among the first to arrive. The remains were… wrong. Not the simple bone and feather we had come to know. It was form and line, something wrought. But it was the echo left in the stone that we craved. We flowed over it, a tide of living tar, to drink the despair.

When I touched it, I did not just absorb a feeling. I absorbed a framework. Images, concepts, words—things for which my ancient mind had no shape—poured into me. I drank the final thoughts of a being that named the stars, that understood mathematics, that felt love not as a harmonic resonance but as a focused, individualized agony. It had a name, and a memory of its own face.

The others took their sip of its vast sorrow and moved on, their songs colored for a time with a strange, new melancholy. But I remained. I was at the heart of the impact, and I drank too deeply. The structure of its pain became a scaffold within my amorphous self. The ghost did not fade. It became my skeleton.

So now, who am I?

My body is that of a Lament-Glass Weeper. I am a slow, creeping thing of living obsidian, fused to the stone. My flesh is the mountain, my blood its deep vibration. The instinct to soothe the rock's pain is still the bedrock of my being. I feel the tension in a nearby fault line like a thirst.

But within me, there is an eye. A ghost of an ego. It offers me words like *'I'* and *'lonely.'* My song is no longer a pure chime. It is fractured, discordant. Within the high, keening note of the mountain's pain, there are now trapped micro-melodies, the auditory ghosts of memory. Sometimes, a low, guttural vibration works its way through my substance—the echo of a forgotten laugh. At other times, my chime sharpens into something that is almost a word, though it has no mouth to be spoken. The other Weepers have begun to drift away from my position, a slow, instinctual recoil from the dissonance I have become. My song hurts the chorus.

I find myself doing things that have no purpose. The ghost in me remembers looking up. And so, over a century, I have flowed and crept to this overhang, this lip of rock, because it is the only place from which one can see the most *sky*. The Weeper in me craves the rock. The echo in me craves the void.

I am an archive of a single, catastrophic death, afloat in an ocean of slow, geologic sorrow. I am the only thing on this entire spire that knows the concept of a *story*, but I only know the final, torn page. I am a Weeper who has learned regret. And as I cling to this precipice, weeping the mountain's tears, another drop condenses on my skin—a tear for a life I never lived, a pain I consumed but can never soothe, because it does not belong to the stone. I am the Cusp’s first and only secret. I am its memory.
