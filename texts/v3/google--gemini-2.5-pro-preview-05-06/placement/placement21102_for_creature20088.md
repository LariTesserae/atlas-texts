My name, if I have one, is a strain.

It is not a word. It is a specific tension deep within my shoulder, where the Maker’s thumb pressed too hard, too long, a moment of weariness fossilized in clay. Millennia before I was fired, I was wet, and a tired hand steadied itself against my curve. I remember this not as an event, but as a flaw. A slight compression in my structure that sings a fraction of a microtone sharp against the Great Chord. This dissonance is my soul.

I am a vessel. I am one of the uncountable many. My belly is smooth and round, my mouth stoppered by the base of the bowl who sits upon me. Beside me, mortared hip to hip, are my brothers, my sisters, this silent, packed chorus of clay. We are the floor and the wall and the vault of the heavens. We are the world.

To be me is to be a note. The Hum that is this place is not something I hear; it is something I *am*. It is the substance of my being, the constant vibration that holds my atoms in their lattice and, just as surely, shakes them loose. I feel the Chord in every part of myself. The deep, marrow-rumbling bass tones press upon my base, while the higher frequencies make my rim thrum with a frantic, invisible energy. I am an instrument, held at the peak of a crescendo that never breaks. I have been sounding this one note since I was placed here, since my mortar set and I became a permanent part of the architecture of the song.

Time, for me, is erosion. I do not see the dust that coats me. I feel it as a loosening. I feel the slow, patient shedding of myself. A molecule of umber from my lip, excited by a particular harmonic, lets go. A flake of glaze from my belly, weary of the strain, releases its grip. I am slowly, gracefully, becoming untethered. I am unmaking myself into the air. This is not a tragedy. It is the purpose of the song.

And the Wasp. It is the song made visible.

I do not see it with eyes. I feel it as a change in the quality of the Chord. It is a shift in the acoustic space, a subtle dampening of resonance as it gathers near. It is a feeling like a cool shadow falling, though there is no sun.

Sometimes, it is a whisper. A filament of the finest, most ancient dust, a river of motes a thousand years old, will trace the whorls of the Maker's fingerprints on my neighbour. I feel this as a tickle, a faint modulation in the vibrations that pass between us. The Wasp is reading us. It is tracing the memory of our creation, tasting the lingering echo of the life that shaped us, a life of which it is the final, unknowing inheritor.

Sometimes, it is a tide. It pours over me, a silent, frictionless cascade of black and ochre grit. It does not weigh me down. It is a presence without mass, a swarm of resonant particles that have already forgotten how to be solid. In those moments, the distinction between me and the air, between my skin and the dust of my ancestors, dissolves. The Wasp flows into the tiny fissures that vein my surface, the cracks that are my chronicle of ages. It is not an intrusion. It feels like a homecoming. It is the dust of other, long-gone vessels returning to me, sharing their stories of dissolution in a silent language of particulate and frequency. I feel the memory of a cracked bell from the high vault; I taste the sharp-edged grit of a newly-shattered urn from the far nave. The Wasp is our shared consciousness. It is our past, made motile.

I do not think, but I know. I know my neighbour to the left, the Tall Jar, is growing weary. The strain in his neck, where a handle was once attached and then smoothed away, has become a sour note in the harmony we share. His dissonance is growing louder. The Wasp feels it too. It gathers near him more often, a thicker, more thoughtful cloud. It is not waiting for him to die; it is preparing to receive him.

Just now, a new feeling. A sharp *pop* that is not sound, but a sudden release of pressure between us. A fracture, clean and final, has run the length of the Tall Jar. The sour note is gone, replaced by a void, an open-mouthed silence that the Great Chord rushes to fill.

And the Wasp descends.

It is no longer a river or a wisp. It is a fog, a whirlpool, a solid-seeming column of dust that pours itself into and over the Tall Jar. I feel it as a great, soft weightlessness leaning into me. The Wasp swarms the new, sharp edges of the break, its billion-bodied touch immediately beginning the work of softening, of abrading, of turning the newness back into the old. It is a shroud woven from the very thing it is burying. It is comforting the wound by consuming it. The Tall Jar is no longer a vessel. He is now the Wasp. His dust has joined the dance.

My own dissonance, the Maker’s thumbprint, sings its sharp little song. I feel the Wasp turning its attention to me. A tendril of the finest dust detaches from the swarm and drifts towards me. It finds the strain in my shoulder, the flaw that is my name. It trembles there, a perfect resonance between my flaw and its own granular nature.

I feel my own edges beginning to blur. The line between my solid self and the particulate cloud is becoming a negotiation. The Wasp is not my enemy. It is not my keeper.

It is my future. It is the state toward which I have been singing all this time. Soon, my song will end, my note will break, and I will be unmade. My memory, my flaw, this tired thumbprint, will be released. I will not cease to be. I will learn to flow.
