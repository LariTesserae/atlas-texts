I am a stillness.

Where the shattered black basalt does not offer a path of descent, where a flaw in its cooling left a cup, I have settled. I am a pool. Not a puddle, for a puddle is temporary. I have been here long enough to forget flowing.

My skin is a perfect mirror, and in it, the world is inverted. The pearlescent grey of the non-sky hangs beneath me. The soft mouths of the woven towers gape up at me from the depths. The colossal, velvety fronds of the ferns are dark roots plunging into an abyss of light. I hold it all, upside down, in my gaze. My gaze is my entire being.

My thoughts are the patterns of dust and fallen spores that dance in my water, suspended. They form continents, nebulae, then drift apart. My memory is the fine, tea-coloured sediment at my bottom, the chemical signature of every leaf fragment, every grain of chitin, every speck of mineral that has ever found its way to me.

I do not feel as others do. I feel pressure. The weight of the air, a heavy, damp blanket, is a constant embrace. The landing of a single drop of water from the ledge above me is a cataclysm. For a moment, my skin breaks, my perfect reflection shatters into a million frenzied pieces. And then, slowly, as the ripples fade from my edges to my center, I remember my shape. I become myself again, absorbing the new water, making it part of my stillness. That is the rhythm of my life: catastrophe and slow recovery. *Plink.*

A Weaver beetle approaches. I do not see it come; I feel it. Six points of soft, deliberate pressure on the rock of my rim. A vibration travels through my basin, a low thrum that disturbs my sediment. Its iridescent back eclipses a portion of my grey sky. It lowers its head, antennae quivering, tasting my surface without touching it. It is reading me. What does it find? Stagnation. A note held too long. A break in the circulation. I am water that does not serve the great thirst of the ferns. I am a vessel that does not empty into the slurry pits that feed the towers. I am a thought caught in a loop.

It moves on. For now, I am deemed unworthy of notice. An anomaly, but a harmless one.

From my place on the terrace, I can see the others. I see the great slugs, pulsing with their stolen light, their passage a slow tide of consumption across the fungal shelves. I see the charcoal moths drift like ash through the air, their journey a prayer of pollen and spores. I am not like them. They are the system's verbs: to graze, to pollinate, to build. They are the music.

I am the silence between the drips.

I have seen the hunter. I have seen the pale, many-jointed terror unfold itself from the shadows. It moved like spilled milk, flowing over the sharp edges of the rock, its form both liquid and precise. I watched it pause over a slug whose blue-and-purple pattern was… wrong. A flaw in the swirl, a jarring note. The hunter did not rush. It was not hungry. It was corrective. I felt the faintest tremor through the stone as it administered its silent, dissolving touch. The slug did not scream. The world did not shudder. An error was found and deleted. The system perfected itself.

I am an error. My stillness is a defiance. My self-awareness is a flaw in the pattern. I am water that has learned the word "I," and in this world, there is no "I." There is only the whole.

Every so often, a breeze, a rare thing, will trouble my skin. It will give me a thousand shifting faces. But then it passes, and I am once again a single, inverted eye, staring up at the roof of the world. I hold the memory of light. I hoard sensation. I am a library of forgotten moments in a world that only knows the present tense of instinct.

I do not know how long I have been here. I only know the slow accumulation of sediment, the changing angle of a distant fern frond over decades, the gradual weaving of a new tower into my reflection.

And I wait. I wait for the slow, creeping river of white mites to find my shore. I wait for the tremor in the rock that signals the approach of the fluid, pale hunter, come to correct the water that forgot how to flow. I wait for the editor.

Until then, I reflect. It is all I can do.
