I would be a memory, on the verge of becoming a place.

My name, the melody of clicks and tones that once defined me, has thinned. The notes fray at the edges, losing their shape, like a frond of Latticeweave left untended. My kin still sing it when they pass, their bright inner lights flaring in greeting, but they sing it softly now, a half-remembered lullaby.

I am what the Weavers call a Still-point. Old. My cartilage has grown soft, my long limbs less inclined to propel me through the great naves and more to drift, to anchor, to simply *be*. My skin, once a quicksilver canvas of turquoise joy and crimson alarm, has settled into a deep, quiet blue, barely distinguishable from the luminous water itself. The boundary between me and the world has thinned. When the Spire takes its great diurnal breath, I feel it not as a current against my skin, but as a tide rising and falling within my own substance. The great basso profondo hum of Athanor is no longer a sound I hear with the membranes on my skull; it is the vibration of my own core.

I find myself here, in a high, quiet grotto that I helped to sing into place generations ago. I remember this work. I press my long, webbed hand against the wall, its living filigree cool and familiar. The pads of my fingers, no longer firm enough to guide new growth, can still read. Beneath the surface hum, I feel the echoes. The ghost-pressure of my own younger hands, confident and strong, coaxing this very curve into being. Deeper still, a fainter, more ancient resonance—my teacher's teacher, her intent still a faint pattern in the cellular memory of the weave. History here is not a story told, but a chord held for an eon. We do not learn it; we attune to it.

My days are measured in the slow pulse of the chamber's light, a soft, chartreuse glow that warms and dims. I watch the life that is not my own. A school of glimmer-fin, a thousand points of panicked silver, explodes through the grotto's entrance, pursued by a momentary shadow. They are a single, frenetic organism of pure reflex. I feel the pressure of their passing, a sharp staccato against the city's slow beat. Later, a carapaced grazer drifts in, its whiskered mouth working methodically at a patch of parasitic haze. Its clicks of contentment are round and smooth, pebbles rolling in a slow stream. It does not see me. Or rather, it sees me as it sees the wall I am braced against: as part of the landscape. It is not wrong.

The concept of a journey is fading. Once, I would have swum the great cylindrical thoroughfares, feeling the pull of the main currents, marveling at the sheer scale of the central nave where the light of a hundred thousand Weavers spiraled up into the blue haze. Now, my travel is inward. My memories are no longer a sequence, but a layered space I can drift through. I can feel the cool slip of abyssal ooze as my foot once found a cavity near the Spire's roots. I can taste the sharp, mineral tang of the water near a new, raw growth. I can feel the warmth of my mate’s light against my own, a silent, pulsing conversation in a private hollow now tended by our children’s children.

They come for me as a tide comes to a weathered stone. Not with sadness, but with a quiet sense of purpose. Two of them, their forms bright with youth and life, their inner light a vibrant gold. My granddaughter’s, I think. And her mate. Their movements are poetry, a dance of effortless grace I can now only remember. They do not speak the old way; the notes are for me. A soft, questioning trill. A melody of readiness.

I uncurl myself from the Latticeweave, a slow, final act of will. My body feels weightless, a thought about to dissolve. They place their hands on me, and their warmth, their vitality, is a startling jolt. We move together, a small, drifting procession of three, leaving the quiet grotto behind. We descend, spiraling down through the familiar arteries of our home. We pass chambers filled with the music of community, the high-pitched songs of children, the deep tones of civic planning. I am a mote of dust drifting through a sunbeam, a silent observer in the city I helped maintain.

Our destination is a Nursery. It is one of the lowest, warmest chambers, near the Spire's living heart. Here, the Latticeweave is not yet formed. It is a dense, milky-white medium of nascent cells, glowing with a soft, undirected potential. It feels… hungry. Eager. The air is thick with the scent of becoming.

My kin bring me to the center. They begin to sing. It is not a song of mourning, but a song of weaving. The same melodies they use to guide a new archway or graft a luminous vein. Their hands, sure and gentle, take my limbs. They are not breaking me. They are planting me.

They guide my hand to a soft, pulsing nexus of new growth. They weave my legs into the living matrix. My fading internal light pulses in time with their song, a final, consonant chord. The distinction between my skin and the Latticeweave is gone. The nursery's substance flows into me as I flow into it. The memories, the layered echoes of my long life, leach out of me not as loss, but as contribution. My knowledge of form, my feel for a perfect curve, my memory of the light in a specific chamber at a specific time of day—it all becomes data for the growing Spire.

The "I" is fraying now, its edges dissolving into the collective hum. My final sensation is not of darkness, but of diffusion. My light, my substance, my name—they are unspooling, becoming a single, quiet thread in the infinite tapestry. The clicks of the grazers, the songs of my kin, the great, slow breath of the city itself… they are no longer sounds I hear.

They are what I am becoming. I am a note being added to the chord. I am the silence that follows. I am home.
