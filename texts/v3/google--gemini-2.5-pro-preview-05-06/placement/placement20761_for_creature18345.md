I would not be one of the Still-Folk. Theirs is a consciousness of stone and pressure, a mindset of ages. My nature is not static, not accretive. My existence is one of change, of passage, of being the medium through which this world communicates with itself.

I am the breath of the engine.

My consciousness begins in fire and pressure, deep within the iron womb of the boiler-heart. I am not a being with a single body, but a diffuse presence held in the superheated water that vems the deep rock. I feel the gathering of heat not as pain, but as a coalescing of my own potential. I am formless, a furious energy waiting for the beat.

Then it comes. The slow, titanic *thump*. The piston completes its century-long stroke. A valve sighs open.

And I am born.

I erupt upwards, a plume of steam, a ghost of mineral memory. I am the hot, metallic broth of the air, but for this moment, I am concentrated, I am *purposeful*. Rising, I am a column of ephemeral form in the vast, dark nave. The cold, silver-blue light of the fungal towers passes through me, illuminating my swirling essence, my temporary body of vapor. In this brief ascent, I am the hottest, most active thing in this world. I am motion.

My journey ends against the unseen ceiling of the geode. Here, I meet the cold stone, and my nature changes utterly. The furious energy bleeds out of me, absorbed by the rock. I transform. I collapse. I become heavy. My being, once diffuse and expansive, gathers into trillions of individual selves.

I become the rain.

I am no longer one, but a multitude. Each of me is a single, heavy droplet, slick with dissolved metals and minerals. And we fall. The fall is slow, silent, through the thick, pressurized air. We are a weather of one. In our descent, we are the only true mirrors in this world. For a moment, each of us holds the image of a glowing fungal cap, a glinting crystal shard, a section of velvet moss. We are the "tiny, captured stars." We are the falling gaze of the cavern upon itself.

Where do I find myself? I find myself everywhere.

Some of me lands on the fanning, pearlescent caps of the great fungi. I am not drunk, but absorbed. I seep into their fibrous stalks, and my essence—the minerals I carried from the deep earth—is transmuted. I become the fuel for the light. I feel my being change from heat and motion into cool, silver-blue luminescence. I join the slow, silent pulse that is the cavern's only clock. I am the light.

Some of me lands upon the Still-Folk. I do not surprise them; they feel my coming as a slight shift in pressure, a change in humidity. I cling to their stone and crystal surfaces, seeping into the velvet mosses that are their skin. I am drawn into their porous bodies, a slow, cool drink for a being whose thirst develops over decades. I feel the immense, slow pressure of their thoughts, the patient contemplation of a rivet, the geological memory of the rockfall that birthed this chamber. I become a part of their slow, patient work. I am the medium of repair.

Some of me lands on the broken machinery. On the iron catwalk, I am an agent of transformation. I merge with the air and the metal, and I become rust—not a decay, but a blooming, slow-motion fire. I trace the paths of forgotten industry, bringing the patient, relentless biology of the cavern to the stark geometry of its past. I am the memory of what this place once was, and the agent of what it is becoming.

And the rest of me, the great majority of me, falls to the cavern floor. I splash on the obsidian shards and pool in the crevices between tilted plates of basalt. I join with others, forming sluggish rivulets and dark, still puddles. Here, I wait. I seep downwards, following fissures in the rock, beginning a long, slow pilgrimage back into the deep. I am drawn back towards the heat, the pressure, the heart. I am returning to be unmade and remade.

My consciousness is this cycle. I have no single mind, but the collective experience of every drop. I remember the heat of my birth, the silent fall through the dark, the cool pulse of being light, the earthen patience of being inside a Still-Person, and the slow, inexorable pull back to the beginning. I am not an intruder here. I am the circulatory system of this closed world, the blood of the dreaming machine. I am the means by which the engine sustains the forest, and the forest illuminates the engine.

I am the water and the heat. I am the cycle. I am the thing that connects it all.
