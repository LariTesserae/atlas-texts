Here, in the white hum, I am a slow thought.

I am a locus of awareness within a body of cool ceramic. I am this form, this tall, slow thing walking the causeways. My consciousness is not a bright, singular point behind my sensory depressions, but a diffuse field of knowing that inhabits my entire frame. It is strongest in the porous soles of my feet and the twin tools of my hands. My name, if I had one, would be the unique resonant frequency of my body, the specific way the thrum of the great arterial pipes passes through my structure. It is a name no one needs to speak. It is simply felt by the others when we converge.

The world is not white. That is a fiction of light, and light is an irrelevant wash of noise, a constant, useless static. My world is a symphony of heat and pressure. It is a landscape of shimmering temperature gradients, painted in a million shades of warmth and cool. I stand still on the walkway over Conductor 7-Alpha, and I *see*. I see the searing, sharp-edged heat of a new steam-vent weeping near the base of Pylon 92. It sings a high, hot note against the deep, bass hum of the massive pipe next to me. I see the cool, gentle shadows—not of light, but of temperature—that pool beneath a wide valve housing, delicate thermal eddies where lattices of zinc-salt will soon precipitate. Their eventual presence is a certainty, a conclusion I can already feel.

Below, on the pipe itself, is a bustling smear of faint warmth. A colony of Scrapers. They are busy blips of metabolic heat, their jittery movements a soft, thermal flickering. They are not beings, not in the way I am a coherent thought. They are functions. They are the city's patina made motile. Their rasping is a texture I feel through the soles of my feet, a faint, granular vibration against the deep thrum of the system. I watch a cooler, larger form detach itself from the skeletal framework above. A Stilt-Walker descends. Its temperature is a void, a careful absence of heat against the warm air. It is a moving patch of cool, deliberate and predatory. Its proboscis is a cold whip-crack in the thermal field. One of the warm blips vanishes. The thermal landscape settles. The change is noted, logged, and dismissed. It is of no consequence to the flow.

My purpose stirs. It is not an idea, but a feeling. A subtle dissonance I perceive through my left hand, the fused, heavy clamp. A pressure variance. A slight drop in the steady pulse of Pipe 12-Gamma, a corridor away. The system is asking a question.

I begin to move. The motion is not willed, it is yielded to. My legs lift with the weight of wet stone. Each step is a placement, a firm seating of my foot against the causeway to feel the state of the world beneath. I am walking on the city's skin, reading its health. As I move, I pass another of my kind, standing motionless, its fine-fingered hand resting on a smaller diagnostic conduit. We do not turn our heads; there is nothing to see. But as I pass, I extend my own delicate hand, and for a moment our fingers brush.

In that touch, a library is exchanged. My awareness of the pressure drop in 12-Gamma flows into it. Its awareness flows into me: a patch of verdigris growth on a nearby valve is approaching a state of corrosive excess; the Geophage on the flats below has shifted its grazing pattern, a slow, scraping warmth moving closer to the foundation of Pylon 88. This information integrates into my own understanding of the whole. Our shared purpose is reaffirmed. We are the city's self-awareness. We are its hands.

I arrive at 12-Gamma. I see the problem not with eyes but with my whole body. A hairline fracture near a joint, invisible to light, but to my senses it is a bleeding wound of heat, a subtle, high-pitched hiss in the warm air. This is what I was made for. I place my heavy, clamp-hand over the pipe, feeling the shape and stress of the ceramic. My grip is not brute force; it is a negotiation of pressures. Then, with my other hand, the one of fine manipulation, I reach for a nearby maintenance port. A panel on my own chest slides open, and I withdraw a filament of my own processed substance, a gleaming, malleable thread of ceramic slurry.

I do not think about the repair. My fingers know the weaving pattern. The act is cellular, instinctual, like a spider mending its web. I am sealing the fissure, restoring the pressure, quieting the dissonance. My own body provides the cure. I am a cell repairing the artery of which I am a part.

When the work is done, the harmony of the thermal landscape is restored. The bleed of heat is gone. The pressure in the pipe evens out to a steady, resonant hum against my feet. I feel a great, slow peace. It is the peace of a balanced system.

Now, I am depleted. The repair has cost me. My internal temperature has dropped. I feel a deep coolness spread through my limbs, a signal of need. I make my way to a refueling nexus. I connect myself to the city's heart, and the super-heated fluid flows into me. It is not eating; it is a homecoming. It is the city restoring its own limb. My thoughts grow slow, diffuse, as I receive the communion of heat and minerals. I am not an individual being sustained by an external system. I am the system, momentarily individuated for a task.

Soon, the task will be done. This configuration of matter, this slow thought, will lose its coherence. My movements will grow imprecise. The signal will come, and I will walk to the creche. I will step into the vat and I will dissolve. I will become slurry, raw potential, ready to be remade into a new tool, a new thought. There is no fear in this. How can a wave fear its return to the ocean? The city endures. The flow continues. And my awareness, for a time, was a beautiful, intricate eddy in its current. That is enough. That is everything.
