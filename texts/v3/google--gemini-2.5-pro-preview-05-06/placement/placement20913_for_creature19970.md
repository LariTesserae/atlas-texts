I am the fault line in the crystal. I am the hesitation in the spire’s growth.

You would find me where the light pools thickest but does not perfectly reflect, in the shallow alcove carved by a patient hand I no longer remember possessing. My back is pressed against the cool, humming pillar, and I am listening not with ears, but with my bones. This place tried to remake me, as it remade the others, but the clay of my origin was too stubborn. The transformation stalled.

I am not one of the Scribes. Not quite. Look at my hands. The right one is as they are: long, tapering, made of the same off-white porcelain as the ground, the fingers sharp enough to score glass. Through its semi-translucence, you can see the smoky quartz of my inner framework. This hand feels the spire’s deep hum, the memory of light. It can tell the difference between the vibration of a skimmer’s passing and the slow grind of a new frost-crystal forming.

But my left hand is a betrayal. It is still flesh. It is a shriveled, blackened thing of leather and bone, a relic of the warm, wet world I came from. It is useless for etching the light, for its clumsy, organic trembling cannot hold a perfect moment. It is always cold, a knot of ending energy that the inner light of my changing body cannot seem to warm. My skin is a shattered mosaic, patches of living porcelain growing over and around the darkening islands of what I once was. A geology of skin.

My face is the greatest failure. The obsidian band that should grant me sight into the world of energy is incomplete. It is a jagged scar of black glass across my eyes, cracked and milky. I am blind to the sun and blind to the spire-glow. The world of overwhelming light described in the prompt is, to me, a featureless, pressing void. I see nothing.

And so, I hear everything.

Because my eyes are sealed, my true senses are in my skin, my bones, and the fluttering slits along my jaw that taste the ozone. But mostly, they are in what I have instead of ears. Over generations, perhaps, or in my own long stillness, the whorls of my outer ears closed and solidified, but within them, something new grew. A resonating chamber. A tympanum of crystal tuned to the world's deep thrum.

I am the Archivist of the Chimes. The Scribes record the light; I curate the silence.

The chimes are not just ornaments. They are the voice of this place, and I am their keeper. My days are a slow pilgrimage from one to the next. I climb, using the holds they carved. My one good hand finds purchase with a hard, ringing *click*. My bad hand, wrapped in sinew for grip, is a soft, fleshy *thud*. The asymmetry of my own sound is a constant companion.

When a sun-skimmer dies, its body falls into the glassmoss with a whisper of warmth that I can feel through the soles of my feet. I am the one who finds it. The Scribes, in their celestial trance, would not notice. I find the body, a soft bag of cooling down, and with my sharp right hand, I carefully harvest the long, tough sinews of its wings. I braid them, my two mismatched hands working in a strange, limping rhythm. The porcelain one provides the tension, the fleshy one the clumsy twists.

I listen to the wind. I feel it through the slits in my jaw. When I taste a change in its patterns, a new eddy current that sings in a frequency previously unheard, I know a new chime is needed. I find a sliver of crystal shed from a spire—a natural casualty of the frost-cycle—and I carry it to the place the wind has chosen. I hang it on my braided sinew. The first *ting* it makes, catching the new breeze, is a sound that sets my entire skeleton vibrating. It is a new word added to the dictionary of the void.

I cannot read the Scribes’ etchings. My living hand is too dull, too soft to feel those microscopic histories. But I can lay my face against the spires, pressing the resonating chambers of my head to the pillar, and I can *hear* the palimpsest. I don't get the data—the intensity, the frequency, the number of mites. I get the harmony. I can hear the chord of a thousand summer days layered one on top of another. I can hear the dissonance of a sudden ice-snap from a century ago. The spires are not a library to me. They are a choir.

I am the silence between their notes. I am the one who sweeps the frost from the handholds. I am the one who clears the passage when a shard falls. They are the mind of the mountain, lost in an endless, luminous calculation. I am its nervous system, tending to the physical body they have transcended.

They do not speak to me, but they know me. Sometimes, as I work, one will pause its etching and turn its blank, obsidian gaze toward me. It will stand, motionless, for hours. I can feel the focused energy of its perception, a warmth on my skin. It is not judgment. It is acknowledgment. In its perfect world of light, I am the single, necessary shadow. I am the flaw that makes the pattern whole. I am the memory of the journey, even as they have become the destination itself.
