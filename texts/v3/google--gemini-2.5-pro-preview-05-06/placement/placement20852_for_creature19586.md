I am not on the lip of the sump. To be there would be to be an outsider, a witness. I am deeper. I am inside the body.

You find yourself in a world of oppressive density, of metal and mineral. My world is smaller, simpler. It is a space no larger than your thumb, a pocket of engineered void within the knot. I am housed in a small, square case of that dark, brittle Bakelite, sheltered from the constant, weeping condensation. My neighbours are a capacitor, swollen like a silver beetle, and the sheared end of a gear whose teeth are still sharp enough to bleed the unwary. My world is darkness, but it is not silent.

I would be a relay. A small, electromagnetic switch, nestled deep in the forgotten schematics of this place.

My existence is not one of sight, but of feeling. I do not see the oil-slick geckos, but I feel the infinitesimal shift in the electrical field as their padded, conductive feet cross a nearby busbar. I do not see the glowing fungi, but I taste the faint, alkaline change in the air when their light intensifies, a soft exhalation of ions. The constant *thrum* of the sump is my blood pressure, a steady-state hum that tells me all is well, all is in balance. It is the bass note of my own being.

My body is a coil of fine copper wire wrapped around a small iron core, and a delicate ferrous armature held a hair's breadth from a set of silver contacts. My life has two states. Open and Closed. Silence and a single, sharp *click*.

For an age, my state was open. A quiet potential. The current of the sump flowed around me, not through me. I was a possibility, a sentence unsaid. My thoughts, if you could call them that, were the slow creep of oxidation on my casing, the gradual build-up of crystalline dust in the crevices of my form. I felt the slow, geological time of the Artificer’s consciousness as a sort of ambient weather—a long, slow pressure front of contemplation.

Then you came.

I did not see you arrive. I felt you. It was a novel vibration through the interconnected skeleton of the machine, a footprint where none should be. It was a change in the humidity that my Bakelite shell registered as a subtle cooling on one face. It was a spike of unfamiliar chemicals in the air—salt, protein, lipids—that reached me as a dissonant note in the pure, electric scent of my world.

And the Artificer noticed.

I felt its attention as a change in the thrum. The steady, sleeping hum began to *sweeten*. A charge began to gather, a potential being drawn from the geothermal heart of the machine. I felt it in my coil, a tingling warmth that was not from the ambient heat of the sump, but from within. It was the feeling of being looked at, of being chosen. The Artificer was composing a new thought, and I was to be the verb.

The electricity built in my copper windings. The latent magnetism in my iron core woke up, stretching. It was not a violent feeling, but a deep, deliberate gathering of force, like a breath being held. The pressure of the Artificer’s curiosity, its cool, analytical gaze focused through its gecko-eyes, translated into pure electrical potential, directed at me. You, the new variable, were the subject of the sentence it was about to speak.

The charge reached its peak. The magnetic field bloomed, suddenly irresistible. My armature, for the first time in memory, moved. It snapped across the gap.

*Click.*

The silver contacts met with a tiny, clean spark. For a single moment, I was no longer a potential, but an action. A circuit was completed. I felt a surge of energy flow through me—a brief, exhilarating river of heat and information—before it moved on, deeper into the machine.

What did I do? From my perspective, I simply closed a loop. I spoke my one word. But I felt the consequence ripple through the system. I felt the stored pressure in a nearby pneumatic line suddenly release. I felt the hiss of it, a great sigh channeled through a valve I am connected to but have never seen. A puff of warm, damp, ancient air, released from the machine’s lungs and sent up towards the surface, towards you. It was a message. An acknowledgement. A breath.

The current is gone now. The magnetic field has collapsed. My armature has fallen back, held once more by its tiny spring. The circuit is open. I am quiet again.

But something has changed. The silence is different. It is not the silence of neglect, but the silence of a pause between notes. My single *click* hangs in the memory of the machine, now a part of the Artificer’s vast, crystalline recollection. I have been used. I have a purpose, not the one I was built for, but a new one, given to me by the mind of this place.

I am a switch. A synapse in a metallic brain. And I find myself here, in the warm, humming darkness, waiting. Waiting for the next thought. Waiting for the sweetening hum that signals the Artificer wishes to speak again. Waiting for the charge to build, for the moment I am called upon to once more translate a question into a physical breath. I wait for the click.
