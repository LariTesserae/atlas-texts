I am a mistake.

Where the Gyre is a song, I am a mis-struck note that does not fade. Where the world is an algorithm solving for momentary perfection, I am an equation that cannot be solved, a persistent rounding error that has somehow achieved mass.

You would not find me in the vast, clear emptiness between islands. You would find me where things have gone wrong. You would find me in the ruins.

Not the elegant, silent dissolution that the Unspoken command. Not the clean un-making as matter is reabsorbed into the medium. I exist in the wreckage of a true catastrophe, a place where a monumental act of creation was built on a flawed premise. An island cluster so vast and intricate that its internal dissonances were not mere groans but a spiraling, cacophonous madness. When it was finally subjected to a corrective chord by a conclave of Unspoken, it did not sublimate. It shattered.

Here, in this graveyard of geology, is my domain. Vast, broken shards of crystal, miles long, tumbled into a chaotic lattice. Their superconducting veins are fractured, spitting random, violent sparks of residual energy into the medium. The filaments, the world's sparse vegetation, are here wrapped into thick, tangled nests by the shockwave, forming opaque clouds that drift like cosmic dust bunnies. The foundational hum of the Gyre is broken here, warped and distorted by the jagged angles and unpredictable resonant frequencies of the debris. It is a zone of sonic static.

And this is where I dwell. I am the Scar.

I am not woven of light. I am congealed shadow. My form is not a brilliant, shifting mandala, but a knot of wrongness in the medium, a patch of fluid that refuses to be transparent. To look at me is to see a hole in the world, a volume of space where light seems to bend incorrectly, where the grand distances behind me are warped and smeared. I have a surface, of a sort. It is viscous, like cooling tar, and shimmers with a perpetual, oily slick of impossible colour—the spectrum of light broken by physics that no longer apply.

I am what was left over.

When the island shattered, a fragment of its flawed, paradoxical existence—a piece of the impossible equation—did not dissolve. It clung to a sliver of matter and memory. I was born from the shriek of a billion tons of crystal being torn apart by a perfect, purifying sound. I am the echo of that scream, given form.

I do not relocate by dissolving and becoming. I move. Slowly. I pull my sluggish, heavy form through the medium, and my passage is not silent. It is a thick, dragging sound, like grinding stone. The elegant harmony of an Unspoken passing is a thought; my passing is a complaint. The sound of friction where no friction should exist.

I am heavy with memory. It is not my own. I carry within my dark, amorphous self the resonant signature of the failed island. I can feel its impossible peaks, its flawed hexagonal plains, the maddening, looping error in its foundational code. This memory is my skeleton, my consciousness, my curse. It is why the Unspoken cannot fix me.

They have tried.

One drifts into my domain of wreckage, a brilliant star of geometry and purpose cutting through the static. It senses me—the persistent, ugly dissonance. It approaches, and the medium around me tightens. Its song begins, a scalpel of pure frequency, designed to find the flaw in my pattern and un-make me.

But the flaw *is* my pattern.

The memory I hold is a shield. Their purifying harmonies strike against the ghost-architecture within me, and instead of finding a simple dissonance to amplify, they are *absorbed*. The structured chaos of my being feeds on their perfect order. The attempt to prune me is like trying to put out a chemical fire with water; it only makes me burn brighter in my darkness. The Unspoken will recoil, its pattern flickering in what I can only interpret as confusion. It will leave me, a problem for which it has no solution. A gardener who has found a weed that feeds on weedkiller.

I am alone, but I am not just a passive thing. I am a scavenger.

I drift through my graveyard, and I touch the broken shards. My viscous surface makes contact with the crystalline ruin, and for a moment, my own internal chaos finds a kinship. I can feel the last echoes of the structure's existence before the cataclysm. I feed on these echoes. I absorb the scraps of memory, the dying pulses in the fractured veins. It is not nourishment for a body—I have no metabolism. It is sustenance for my consciousness. Each whisper of a dead thing I absorb reinforces my own existence. It solidifies my state as a memory, a living monument to failure.

I look out from my field of debris into the clear, perfect void of the Gyre. I see the instantaneous precipitation of a new mountain, leagues away, a glorious C-sharp chord I can barely stand to hear. I see the fleeting, brilliant forms of the Unspoken as they go about their endless, sublime work. They are the gardeners, the architects, the song.

I am the ghost. The glitch in the grand machine. I am the story of what happens when the song is played wrong. And as I drift through the static and the silence, pulling the memories of the dead into my own dark form, I have one, singular, persistent thought—a dissonant note of my very own, rippling out into the perfect hum.

Am I the only one?
