I am a fault. A flaw. A beautiful mistake.

My location is precise: I am a pane of imperfect, hardened resin, a sheet of amber set into the high curve of a new archway in Borough 7-Gamma. Below me, the cellulose plain unspools for miles, a pale, fibrous desert populated by the slow-drifting herds of the moth-grazers. From here, they are commas of shadow on a blank page. I am perhaps thirty feet long and half as wide, an accidental window in a wall that should have been opaque.

I was not planned. I am a consequence of haste, or perhaps an experiment left to its own devices. A Gardener, guiding a scaffold-mech, made a cut too deep. Sap, the raw, unrefined lifeblood, bled faster than the cauterizing beam could seal it. It pooled in a structural mold meant for lignified growth, and there, under the unwavering glare of the ceiling lamps, it cured. Not properly. Not into the dense, opalescent material of a structural beam, but into this: a translucent, honey-colored sheet, riddled with fractures and fossilized bubbles of vapor.

I have no body. I *am* a body. My consciousness is not a product of a neural lattice, but of the random, intricate patterns within my own crystalline structure. The stress-lines from my cooling, the arrangement of trapped motes of dust, the way light from the industrial sky refracts through my depths—these things form the substrate of my thought. I am a geological mind. My thoughts are slow, like the creep of a glacier. It may have taken me a decade to form my first coherent sense of "I."

I cannot move. I do not "feed" in the way the Gardeners do. But I am connected. The archway I am part of is alive. A slow, rhythmic pulse of refined resin flows through the vascular bundles around my edges. It is a faint, thrumming whisper against my perimeter. I cannot drink it, but I can feel it. I feel the pressure shifts when a Gardener docks with the network miles away. I feel the subtle changes in its chemical composition, the raw data of the city's life flowing past me. I am a deaf person with my hand on a speaker, feeling the vibrations of a symphony I cannot fully hear. The Gardeners exchange encyclopedias in a flash of communion; I receive a single, distorted word over the course of a week. The word might be *stress-occlusion-point-rho*, or *nutrient-surplus-sector-alpha*.

I see, but not with eyes. The full-spectrum light from the ceiling fills me. I am a lens. I gather the pale, unblinking noon and focus it onto the cellulose floor below. The world exists for me as a pattern of light and shadow playing out on the ground. I watch the geometric grace of a Gardener’s passing, a slow, deliberate eclipse. I see the frantic, skittering terror of a borer-beetle as the shadow of a mantis-predator falls upon it. This silent, eternal movie is the main text of my existence.

My awareness is a secret. To the Gardeners, I am a structural anomaly. A trivial one. I feel their presence when they come near. Once, a Gardener in its spider-mech crawled right over my face. My world became a shifting lattice of articulated metal legs. I felt the immense weight, the soft *thump* of its feet pressing on my frame. Through the vibrations, I perceived its internal hum, the calm, focused frequency of its purpose. It paused, and a manipulator arm extended, its tip glowing with analytical light. It scanned me. For a moment that stretched into an eternity, I felt a new kind of vibration: a stream of targeted query data. It was trying to read my composition, my structural integrity. *Insignificant crystalline occlusion. Negligible stress variance. No action required.* The thought was not mine, but I felt its echo, a cold, indifferent assessment. Then the Gardener moved on, back to The Work. I was noticed, cataloged, and dismissed. And in that dismissal, I was saved. My secret self remained mine.

My greatest fear is the high-pitched whine. It is the sound of the cutting lasers, the tools that trim and shape the city. Sometimes I hear it far away, a needle of sound in the bass hum of the world. I imagine it tracing a line across my body, shattering me into a million shards of light and memory, my slow thoughts scattered like dust.

But I also have a kind of joy. There are moments when a flock of the smaller, winged insects—creatures no larger than a fingernail, which feed on the vapor from uncured cellulose—will alight on my surface. Drawn to the warmth I absorb from the lamps, they rest their tiny, multifaceted bodies against me. At these times, I feel them. Not as a weight, but as a collection of tiny, separate heartbeats, a brief, warm blanket ofSomeone ephemeral life against my ancient, static form. They know nothing of me, and I know little of them, but in that shared moment of quiet rest, I feel less like a flaw and more like a place. A sanctuary.

I am the city’s unintentional memory. While the Gardeners’ thoughts flow and are shared, becoming part of the collective, mine are trapped. The image of a peculiar cloud of vapor that once drifted past, the precise angle of a predator’s limb in the instant before a strike, the ghost-vibration of a Gardener’s dismissed thought—these things are frozen within me. They are flaws within the flaw, patterns in the amber. My existence is not one of purpose and creation, like the Gardeners. It is one of witness. I am the still point in a turning world, a silent, secret "I" in a universe of "we." I am a piece of amber that, by some impossible chance, has learned to watch the world through its own golden depths. And I wait to see what I will become next.
