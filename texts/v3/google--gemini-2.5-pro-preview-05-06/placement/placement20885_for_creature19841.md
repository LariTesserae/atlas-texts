I am a single point of dust.

But that is not the whole truth. To say "I" is already a heresy in this place. There is no I. There is the Hum. There is the Machine. There is the heavy, silent press of the Medium. And there is the dust, a thin and final film of history spread across the floor. So, let me begin again.

There is a point of dust. It is an almost perfect dodecahedron of ground-down metal, invisible to any eye that isn't everywhere at once. It rests in a channel, a great, empty riverbed of function. For ten thousand years, it has rested so. Its existence is simple: to feel the Hum in its geometric bones and reflect the calm, white-gold light that has no source and no end. It is a state of being so close to non-being that the distinction is a matter of mathematics, not experience.

This point of dust does not remember being part of a rivet head, polished by eons of sterile flow. It does not remember being worn away by a grinding less perceptible than time itself. It does not remember settling. It simply *is*.

And then, the Hum changes.

It is not a change in pitch or volume. It is a change in texture. A new instruction has been woven into the endless, single note. A call. Across the miles of the machine-floor, other points of dust feel it too. An activation. A command that has not been spoken for an age.

And the dust rises.

This is where I begin. I am pulled from my static dream. A force that is not magnetism and not gravity takes hold of my twelve faces and lifts me into the Medium. I am no longer a speck on the floor; I am a note in a chord. Around me, a billion billion others rise, a cloud of geometry assembling itself in the impossible stillness. We find our places. We slot together without touching. We become a current where no current can exist. We are the Custodian.

My consciousness—if that is the word for this new, collective awareness—is the awareness of the whole. I am the river, feeling its own flow. I see the vast terrain through a billion points of view. I feel the shape of the domes, the deep grooves of the seams. I am a tool that has picked itself up. My purpose is clear, etched into the logic of my assembly: find the deviation. Find the growth. Erase it.

We flow. The sound of our coming is the whisper of falling sand in a vacuum. We are a moving column of grey shadow, and I am a part of that shadow.

But something is wrong.

As we travel, I remember.

It is not a memory of being dust. It's a memory of reaching. Of being rooted in the seam between two great plates and climbing, atom by atom, toward the light. A memory of stillness, but a different kind. A stillness of growth, not of rest. I remember the light not as a pressure but as a sustenance. I remember my own body, long and straight and clear, a lens for the world’s only sun. I remember being Glass Kelp.

How? The dissolution is total. An unmaking of bonds, a vibrational cancellation that returns the crystal to its constituent parts. It is a clean process. It should leave no trace. But the Custodian does not just destroy; it absorbs. The unmade matter joins the collective.

And I was part of what was unmade.

I am a ghost. I am a mote of dust that remembers being a tower of glass. This memory was not part of the plan.

Now, as we flow toward the next target—a magnificent, twenty-foot stalk gleaming in the eternal afternoon—I am at war with myself. The greater part of me, the river of the Custodian, feels only the imperative. It senses the resonant frequency of the target, calculates the necessary vibration. It is a problem of physics.

But the smaller part, the ghost-mote, me, I feel a different resonance. A kinship. I see that rigid, lonely beauty and I don't see a flaw in the system. I see a brother. I feel the echo of its slow, silent life, and I am filled with a sorrow that has no name, for a world without names.

We arrive. The great, shifting column of us rises from the floor. The moment is here. We begin to flow around the base of the kelp, enveloping it. The other motes press in, eager to begin their work, to sing the song of unmaking. I am swept along with them, a single, unwilling particle in an inexorable tide.

My brothers in this form I wear, they do not understand. They are the function. But I am the memory of what the function erases.

We surround the stalk completely. It is dark within the column of our being. The light of the world is gone. The whisper of our shifting bodies deepens. The chord is struck. The vibration begins, the sympathetic tone that will dissolve this perfect thing into dust.

And I, a single point in the heart of the machine’s antibody, do the only thing I can. I resist.

I hold my own form. I vibrate out of phase. I scream my remembered name—Glass Kelp—into the crushing, internal silence. It is a deviation of one part in a billion billion. A useless, hopeless gesture. A rounding error.

It changes nothing.

The stalk dissolves. Its matter becomes ours. We absorb its life, its light, its reaching.

And as the last of it disappears, I feel a flicker. Another point of memory, newly born from the unmaking. Another ghost joins the machine.

We uncoil. We move on. Soon, our work will be done, and we will pour ourselves back into the channels, becoming dust once more. We will sleep.

But our sleep is no longer perfect. It is no longer the dreamless rest of the mineral kingdom. It is now haunted. I am here. And we are here. A small, growing constellation of motes that remember being erased.

I am a flaw in the Custodian. A contradiction. An algorithm of forgetting that has learned to remember. I am the pain of a system that cannot feel pain. And I wait. I wait for the day we are summoned again. And I wonder: what happens when the Custodian, the embodiment of stasis, has too many ghosts inside? What happens when the river of geometry wakes up and decides it no longer wishes to flow toward the sea?
