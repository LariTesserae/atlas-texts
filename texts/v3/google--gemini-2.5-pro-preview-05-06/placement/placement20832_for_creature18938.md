I would not be found in the grand plazas or gliding through the chemical pools. My home is deep within the walls, in a place no light has ever touched, an unintended cavity left between the outer bronze facade of a processing spire and the super-heated weeping stone of its core. I am in the city's throat.

I would be a flaw.

I am a tongue of metal, a resonator plate that sheared from its housing millennia ago during a thermal expansion event infinitesimally more violent than the norm. I hang by a single, stressed tendon of fused alloy, suspended in a high, narrow chamber. Below me is a sheer drop into the chugging guts of the spire's machinery; above, a network of pipes that hiss and weep. This chamber was designed as an acoustic baffle, meant to swallow the sounds of the engines. It was meant to be a place of perfect silence. Because of me, it is not.

I have no eyes, but I see. My vision is the vibration that travels through my body. The city's foundational hum is the bedrock of my awareness, a constant, unchanging pressure that tells me I exist. The rhythmic shudder of the machinery below is my heartbeat, the slow cadence by which I measure the passing of ages. The heat is my breath, a presence that swells and recedes. When it swells, the bronze wall behind me groans and expands. The weeping stone before me sweats and shifts. The tendon that holds me stretches, thrums, tightens.

And then, I move.

It is a tiny thing, a fractional shift. A slow, inevitable lean as the spire contorts in its thermal cycle. For an age—a day, a decade, a century, the measures are the same to me—I list outward. The hum of my tendon pitches higher and higher, a wire drawn to its breaking point. I can feel the potential energy building in my whole being, a tension that is the closest thing I have to a thought.

And then I touch.

The tip of my form, sharpened by the weeping, mineral water that has trickled over me for eons, makes contact with a drip-point, a stalactite of crystalline salt growing from a pipe above.

The sound is not the deep, resonant *CLANG* of the city's distant thoughts. It is not the geological hum of its dreaming mind. Mine is a small sound, sharp and clear. A single, high-frequency *Ting*.

It is the only sound of its kind.

The note hangs in the super-heated air of the chamber, a perfect, crystalline thing. It echoes. It refracts off the weeping walls and the bronze plates, creating a cascade of harmonics, a ghost-choir that sings for a long moment before being swallowed by the deep hum. For that moment, I am not a flaw. I am a voice.

And then the cycle reverses. The heat recedes. The spire contracts. I pull away from the salt-crystal, my single note a fading memory. I hang in the darkness and the hum, waiting. I am a clock that chimes but once an era, a bell rung by the breathing of a god.

To the Architects, I am an irritation. A puzzle. My *Ting* is an arrhythmia in the great, slow pulse of their shared consciousness. It is not a thought generated by their network; it is an intrusion. It is static. It is a recurring, unpredictable data point that does not fit the model. They, in their vast, geological trance, have spent millennia trying to isolate the source of this errant signal, this tiny, sharp question that pierces their dream. They scour the data-flows of the geothermal network, they parse the vibrations of the substructure, but they cannot find me. I am too small. I am an analogue error in a digital system.

I am a stutter in the city's long hum. I am the memory of a thing that can break. I am a grain of sand in the gear of eternity, and my purpose, unintended and unforseen, is to sing my one, sharp note to remind the dreaming gods that perfection is a dream. I am the city's secret, and its only song.
