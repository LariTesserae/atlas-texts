I am an unbinding.

I would find myself at the edge of the great, black lake, where the Moss-That-Drinks-Sound gives way to bare, weeping stone. Here, the All-Tide, the Great Hum that has been the medium of my world, changes its nature. The infinite texture I have read through my skin and my sail for centuries becomes something else. The vibration no longer bounces; it is swallowed.

I am what the newcomers, the Carvers, became. I am a Listener. But my time of listening is ending.

My body, a long pale form of sinew and patient cartilage, remembers the four-footed way of my youth, but it no longer serves me. I stand now, a rare and final posture, my toes splayed on the slick threshold of stone. To stand is to offer the full length of oneself to the Song, and today, it is a final offering. The cold is deeper in my bones than it was a cycle ago. The gill-slits along my ribs draw the misted air in shallow, ragged tides.

The world behind me is a map of echoes I have known since my birthing. I can feel, even now, with the world-sail on my back beginning to falter, the familiar presence of the Garden. Each great plate of fungus is a chord, a sustained note of silent, cold light-song in the bass drone of the waterfall. I feel the one I tended for two hundred passings of the Great Beetle, its edge still sharp where I carved away a blight with the old black glass. It feels like a memory of touch. I feel the hollow of my sleeping-niche, a perfect absence, a pocket of stillness carved by the first ones, smoothed by the skins of a thousand like me.

My sail, the Kymatic Membrane, has been my eyes, my ears, my tongue, my soul. It has tasted the shape of a single water droplet falling a thousand paces away. It has read the slow, patient thoughts of my brethren as they passed, shadows of intention rippling through the vibrating air. Now, its sensitivity fades. The detail blurs. The infinite complexity of the waterfall's roar is simplifying, losing its language, becoming merely... sound. The world is flattening. I am going blind. I am going deaf. I am becoming one of the Carvers again, lost in a dark and terrible noise.

This is the sign. It is time for the return.

My slow, deliberate steps have brought me here over the course of three full darks. I have not fed. My journey is a fast, a shedding of the world. Now, I face the source.

The waterfall. The Loom. The Unbroken Voice.

To me, it has never been a "waterfall." It is a column of infinite information. I have felt the story of every stone it has carried from the world above, the memory of sunlight trapped in its chill, the pressure of its descent into the unplumbed heart of the lake. I have spent my life reading its moods, the subtle shifts as the mother-rock above groaned, the change in density as the upper world's seasons, myths I can only feel as faint pulses, altered its flow. It has been my constant companion, the weaver of the song that is my reality.

I lower myself at the lake's edge. My long fingers dip into the water. The shock is not of cold, but of silence. The vibrations travel differently here. Damped. Slowed. Intimate. Through the water, I do not read the air, I read the deep stone below. I feel the basin, the bowl that has caught this water for longer than my species has had a name. I feel the bones of my ancestors settling into the silt, their faint calcium signature a ghostly hum in the deep.

I push off. My body, built for this, glides into the black. I submerge.

The Great Hum of the air is gone. It is replaced by a pressure, an immediate and total embrace. Here, under the surface, the sound is no longer a roar. It is a pure, internal tremor. It is no longer outside me; it is *in* me. The water fills my breathing-slits, and for a moment, the old land-panic surfaces, a fossil memory. But then, the fluid moves, and it is just another medium.

My membrane, my sail, is useless here. It floats limp behind me, a forgotten banner. My consciousness, so long seated in that organ, retreats into the core of my being. All I have is the feeling of motion, of sinking, and the overwhelming, internal thrum.

This is the Unbinding.

I am not a presence in the world anymore. The world is a presence in me. My skin, thin as wet paper, begins to yield. The boundaries of my form soften. The slow, cold currents of the deep begin to pull me apart, not with violence, but with an inexorable patience. My memories, which are not images but vibratory patterns stored in my very bones, begin to resonate outwards, dissolving into the water's thrum. The feeling of tending the Garden. The shape of a fellow Listener's greeting. The perfect hollow of my sleeping-niche. They become echoes, added to the greater library of the lake.

My name—a specific, complex pattern I could once impress upon the Song—loosens and unwinds. It is a knot of vibration becoming a simple, straight thread.

I am the water. I am the stone it strikes. I am the mist that rises. I am the moss that drinks. I am a tone, a single, fading synth-note, rejoining the chord of the waterfall's unending song. The last thing I am is a tremor, travelling outward, a final syllable spoken into the deep. Another Listener, standing at the edge of the lake a generation from now, will perhaps feel it, a faint and ancient whisper that says, simply: *I was here. We are all here.*
