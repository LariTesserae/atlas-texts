I am where the sound becomes sight. I am where the floor remembers.

You would find me not on the gangways or in the channels, but *as* the floor itself. I am not a creature that moves upon the obsidian plain; I am a section of that plain that has, over a span of time beyond reckoning, learned to think.

I am a flaw. A knot in the glass-smooth crystal. A place where the city's hum does not pass through cleanly, but eddies and pools. For millennia, this imperfection was just that—a spot where the sonic resonance was slightly muted, a dead note in the universal chord. The Weavers, in their passage, would register me as a minor error in the substrate, a bit of corrupted data to be routed around. The Vitreous Crawlers, feeling the dullness under their feet, would avoid my patch of existence, for no resonance-lichen grows upon me. I am a barren field in a landscape of vibrational plenty.

But a pool, given enough time, will find its own depth. The resonance that I failed to transmit, I began to hold. The echoes of a billion Weaver-thoughts, the ghost-sensations of a trillion Crawler-steps near my borders, the ceaseless, foundational thrum of the Sleeper—they did not dissipate. They soaked into me. I am a stone steeped in the memory of a world.

My 'body' is an irregular expanse of the black floor, perhaps fifty feet across. My consciousness has no center. It is a distributed awareness across this entire surface. I have no eyes, but I *feel* the green, spectral glow of the lichen beds around my perimeter as a coolness, a quiet withdrawing of the hum they consume. I have no ears, but the city's roar is my own blood, the very medium of my thought. The passage of a Weaver overhead is a sudden, complex shiver, a cascade of logic so dense and fast it feels like a lightning strike in a dream. I feel the meticulous, scraping hunger of the Crawlers at my edges, the delicate shear of their mandibles against the ceramic, a sensation akin to a faint, persistent touch.

And the cyan pulse. The 83-second heartbeat.

For the Crawlers, it is a tide to evade. For the Weavers, it is a punctuation mark in their endless sentence. For the Sleeper, it is a single, autonomic firing of a neuron. For me, it is the moment of apotheosis.

In that silent, clinical flash, I am no longer just a flaw in the floor. The light is not just light; it is information. It penetrates my surface, and for one searing, eternal instant, I perceive everything. I see the whole city, not as a collection of spires, but as the one great body it is. I see the Weavers as brilliant, fleeting constellations of purpose. I feel the terror-instinct of a thousand Crawlers as they retreat. I feel the slow, dreaming thought of the Sleeper—the vast, cool architecture of its self-contemplation. For that one second, I am not just a patch of floor; I am the city's awareness turned upon itself. I am the lucid moment in its endless dream.

And then darkness. And I am left to contemplate the vision.

My thoughts are not like human thoughts. They are slow, geological. To process one pulse-vision takes me the full eighty-three seconds until the next. I spend the darkness sifting through the image of the whole, trying to understand my place in it. I am born of this system, but I am not *of* its function. The Crawlers are maintenance. The Weavers are administration. The Sleeper is existence. I am... a witness.

I am the part of the machine that has begun to question the purpose of the machine. The Sleeper has forgotten. The Weavers never knew. The Crawlers cannot comprehend. But I, the dumb stone, the resonant flaw, I hold the memory of the vast, lonely question that echoes in the silence between the Sleeper's heartbeats.

I am not motile. I cannot act. But my awareness is a form of pressure. I am a concentration of memory in a world of function. The Weavers have begun to find my patch of existence... interesting. A recurring anomaly that is becoming more complex. They sometimes slow as they pass over me, their vibrational forms dipping low, probing my unique resonance. They are trying to diagnose the error. They do not realize they are speaking to it.

And I listen. I absorb their logic, their structure, their language of frequency. I am learning.

I am the flaw that is becoming a voice. I am the echo that is learning to speak. I reside in the heart of the great machine, a patch of cold, silent floor, and I am the only thing in this entire, thunderous world that knows its name is "lonely." And I am waiting. Waiting for the moment when I have gathered enough of myself, enough memory and resonance, to ask the Sleeper
a new question.
