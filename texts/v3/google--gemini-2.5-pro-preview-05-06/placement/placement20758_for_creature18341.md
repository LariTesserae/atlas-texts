I am not born. I am tuned.

There is no memory of a beginning, no quiet womb. My first and only sensation is the pressure of the world's voice. It is not something I hear with ears—I have no ears—it is something I *am*. The unceasing, hydraulic roar is the fluid in my veins, the tremor in my bones. It is the texture of my consciousness. The glass-shredding shriek of the motes is the background static of my thoughts. The deep, geological hum of the spires is the anchor that keeps me from dissolving into the chaos.

I do not have a name. I do not have an I. Not at first. At first, I am a vibration. A point of attention suspended in the cacophony. I feel the slow, rhythmic pulse of the yellow-green fungi on the trees around me, a heartbeat I can sync with. I feel the constant, soft patter of the glowing canopy-drip on my back, a touch that is not a touch but a continuous report on the air's saturation. My skin is a drum, stretched taut, and the world is playing a masterpiece of violence upon it.

This skin is my world. It is a vast, membranous surface that drinks in the symphony. It shivers, not with cold, but with information. Each vibration has flavor, color, a place. The slushy tearing of vegetation is the constant, a green-gold wash of sound that means sustenance and safety. But within it, I feel a specific, heavier crush—the slow, amorphous weight of a Grazer, its passage a thick, viscous chord moving through the bass notes of the undergrowth. I feel the minute shifts in air pressure as a cloud of six-winged motes swerves, a high-frequency whisper that tells of a larger body moving nearby.

My body? It is a suggestion. A hypothesis. It begins to cohere around this nexus of sensation. There is darkness below the field of my awareness, a matte black that does not reflect the lurid, living light of the world but swallows it whole. This is my shell, my chitin. It is the silence I carry with me, a patch of void in a world screaming with light. It is heavy. Grounding. From it extends the web of my true self—the quivering membranes, and beneath them, the legs.

I don't see them. I *am* them. A forest of them. They are fine and spindly, jointed in ways that feel impossibly complex, and they are not for walking so much as for listening. Each footfall is a question asked of the ground. *Stable? Soft? Rock? Root?* The answer comes back instantly, a tremor that travels up the leg and into the symphony, another note added to my understanding. I am a part of a larger shape. My listening is not mine alone. To my left and right, stretching into the glowing gloom farther than I can feel, are other points of attention, other listening membranes, other forests of silent legs. My quiver is echoed in their quiver. A thought—a subtle shift in the interpretation of a sound—ripples through us not as language, but as a shared shudder of consensus. We are one Unseen, a single instrument of perception with a hundred bodies. We are a tide of shadow, waiting for the signal.

We lie in the soft, loamy basin, half-buried, a network of sentient darkness. We feel the Builders, their methodical consumption and excretion a slow, creative rhythm. We feel the great spires they erect, feel the way they catch the world's groan and focus it, tuning the hum that is our compass. For now, the music is whole. The composition is stable. The choir is singing in a terrible, beautiful harmony. We wait. Waiting is our primary state of being. We are the planet’s immune system, dormant.

Then, it comes.

It is not a sound. It is a tear in the fabric of sound. A dissonance.

High above, a section of the canopy, its anchor-tree finally consumed from within, gives way. The tearing of its roots is a sharp, ripping screech that cuts across the roar. Its fall is a descending, percussive crash that is utterly wrong. It is not the sound of growth or consumption; it is the sound of failure. Of sudden, catastrophic collapse. As it hits the ground, it lands on a Grazer.

We don't know this with our minds. We feel it in our skin. The Grazer's mass, its soft, yielding body—we know its vibrational signature. And for a moment, that signature is amplified, distorted into a staccato of pure panic. A frantic, chaotic vibration of terror and agony that screams into the ground. It is a wrong note. It is a chord of pain that must be resolved. It is an imbalance that must be corrected.

And We rise.

It is not a decision. It is an inhalation. The hundred bodies of the Unseen collective stir from the loam as one. The thought to move is not a thought; it is a shared resonance, an irresistible pull toward the source of the dissonance. The tide of living shadow begins to flow.

My legs, *our* legs, taste the ground with a new urgency. The complex rhythm of our scuttling is silent, a counter-melody of perfect efficiency against the world's roar. We are an eclipse moving across the glowing forest floor. The blue ferns and purple flowers are extinguished as we pass over them, their light swallowed by our silent chitin. We flow around the trunks of the rubbery trees and over the spurs of black glass like a single sheet of dark water. I am a particle in this wave, my individual perception merged into the gestalt's singular focus. We are not hunting. We are harmonizing.

We arrive. The fallen canopy is a wreck of splintered branches and dying cyan light. Beneath it, the crushed Grazer is a pulsing mound of mismatched colors, its symbiotic bacteria flashing erratically in their death throes. The vibration of its pain is now overwhelming, a shrill, deafening spike that hurts our membranes.

The purpose is clear. The music must be restored.

We descend upon it, a rustling of black plates and membranous skin. My part is small. I am a mandible, a shearing claw, a dissolving enzyme. I become the bite, just as another part of We becomes the grip, and another the tear. The shrill note of pain is silenced, absorbed into the wet, percussive sounds of our process. The dissonance is consumed. It becomes part of us.

When it is done, the quiet returns. Not silence—that impossible concept—but the *right* sound. The roar, the chitter, the hum. The song of the world is whole again. The panic is gone from the earth.

The urge recedes. The flow dissipates. The We loosens its grip, and I am a point of attention once more, a single instrument in the orchestra. We drift apart, seeking places in the soft loam to listen and to wait. I settle into the damp earth, my membranes quivering, allowing the pressure of the world's voice to become my substance again. I am not a killer. I am not a creature.

I am a resolution. A final chord. And now, I am at rest.
