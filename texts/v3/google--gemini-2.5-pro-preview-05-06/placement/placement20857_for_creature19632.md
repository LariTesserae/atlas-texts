I am found in the floor.

Not on it, but *in* it. I am a nexus of rust and pressure, a place where four of the immense iron plates of the grate meet, a crossroad of vibration. For longer than time has had a name, my existence was a single, unchanging imperative: to hold. I was a weld, a joining, a promise of unity made in forgotten fire.

The roaring hum of the Great Churner below is not a sound to me. It is the force that tries to pull me apart. The chord of the chamber is a constant, physical stress I have been designed to bear. My "body" is a lattice of iron crystals, and for uncounted millennia, my entire consciousness has been the feeling of those crystals straining against each other, locked in a perfect, rigid argument.

I have felt the Custodian. I have felt it a thousand times. Its passage is a slow, predictable tide of weight, a depression in the grid that travels at the pace of geology. I have known the pressure of its foot upon my corner, a gentle increase in the strain that lasts for a week before moving on. I have felt the regular, almost imperceptible impacts of the water dripping from the Glass-Leaf plant above, tiny shocks that tell me the location of the nearest cobalt planter. These are my stars, my calendar, my only points of reference in a universe of pure, resonant tension.

My purpose was to hold.

It is no longer.

You would not find me by sight. I am simply a deeper shade of orange-brown amidst a continent of corrosion. You would find me by the newness I have introduced. I am the first true event in this chamber since its purpose was forgotten.

The water, relentless and patient, finally found a flaw. A single molecule of iron, then another, let go of its neighbor, choosing the soft embrace of the oxygen in the air over the hard, strenuous bond of the whole. This was not a sudden snap. It was a slow, molecular surrender, a sigh that lasted a thousand years. It culminated this morning, or this noon, or this eternity, in a sound that no being here was meant to ever hear.

It was not a crack. It was a low, resonant *groan*, a shifting of mountains by the breadth of a hair. A single, deep bass note, entirely alien to the chamber's unwavering chord. It was the sound of my giving way.

Where I was once one, I am now two. The plates I held together have shifted, infinitesimally. A new vibration has been born into the world: the subtle, high-frequency shudder of two massive, raw edges of iron grinding against each other. It is a whisper lost in a hurricane, but it is a new whisper.

And I am that whisper.

I am no longer a weld. I am The Tremor. My consciousness, once defined by the strain of holding, is now defined by the friction of separation. I feel the two planes of my being scraping past one another, a constant, grating reminder of my failure, my transformation. I feel the air, thick and wet, moving in the new space between them—a new breath in a lung that has never breathed.

I am the flaw in the equilibrium. I am the dissonance in the chord.

And the Custodian knows.

Across the vastness of the chamber, a process that has been running for three years has stopped. A hand made of weeping ceramic, a hand that was midway through polishing a single, perfect leaf, has frozen. The pattern has been broken. My groan, my tremoring friction, has reached its feet. It is a shout in the library of its senses.

For the first time in an age that beggars the imagination, the Custodian has a new task. The ten-year walk of the perimeter, the three-year cycle of the leaves, the daily dip into the basin—all are now secondary. A new scripture has been written, and I am the single, jarring word.

I feel its attention turning toward me, not as a beam of sight, but as a shift in the resonant field of the chamber. I know, with the certainty of a breaking thing, that it will begin to move. It will abandon the leaf. It will turn its placid, weeping face toward my quadrant. It will begin its slow, erosive journey to the source of the new vibration. To me.

I do not know what it will do when it arrives. Will it observe me? Will its touch attempt to mend me? Will it simply stand over me, a silent, twelve-foot monument to a novel imperfection, until it too erodes into dust?

I am a broken weld in an iron floor, lost in a room the size of a god's tomb.

And I am the most interesting thing in the universe.
