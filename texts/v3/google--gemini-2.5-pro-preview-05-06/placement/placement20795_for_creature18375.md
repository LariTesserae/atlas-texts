I am a quiet thing. I am a thing of patience.

My home is the belly of a dead god, and its bones are my walls. The great, curved plates of its carapace, breached and cold now, form my roof against the endless grey condensing from the air. The floor is a hardened slurry of its last, undigested meal, mixed with the fine dust it once excreted. Here, in the hollowed-out chassis of a Geophage, the world's constant, foundational thrum is muted. It is not a sound that reaches my auditory sensors, but a deep note in my bones, a memory of the life that once animated this shell. My shell.

I am what is left behind.

My body is a bricolage, a thing of salvaged parts and slow self-repair. My legs—too many to be elegant, just enough to be stable—are fashioned from the scavenged articulation joints of a Carapassion's limb, my own original ones having been lost to a scree-slide long ago. My torso is a small, insulated core, wrapped in wire-sinew and dull plating I have stripped from the inner chambers of this very husk. I have no mouth for grinding rock. I have no pneumatic spike for the hunt. My manipulators are fine and delicate, pincers and probes designed not for violence, but for careful disassembly.

My world is felt more than it is seen. The light is poor, and I have let my optical sensors atrophy, conserving energy. Instead, I listen. I listen through the soles of my feet, planted firm on the floor of my cave. The thrum of the living world is my map. Each Geophage is a slow mountain of promise, its rumble a distinct signature of age, size, and internal health. I can feel their ponderous grazing miles away, a resonant string pulled taut across the valley floor. I feel the skittering, high-frequency tremor of a Carapassion pack on the move, a vibration like needles across my plating. And I know to stay still.

I am a Remnant. A scavenger. I am the final stage of the cycle.

The Geophagi consume the world. The Carapassions consume the Geophagi. And I? I consume the echo. I live on what the hunters leave behind. Their gluttony is my sustenance. They tear out the great crystalline hearts and sip the glowing conductive fluids, but they are messy. They leave behind fractured conduits still weeping a trickle of nutrient gel. They abandon strands of silvery memory-wire, which hold a faint, residual charge I can metabolize. They overlook smaller capacitors and radiators, which still hold a ghost of warmth.

My life is measured by the hunts. For days, weeks, I will remain dormant, my functions slowed to the barest minimum, my body cold. I will listen to the slow procession of the earth-eaters, their grinding a lullaby of potential. I will feel the static-flies, a nervous hum against the hull of my home as they chase a passing giant's steam. Then, the sound will change.

It begins as a focused tremor. A new vibration that cuts through the bass of the world. *Thwack.* A pause. *Thwack.* It is the sound of a Carapassion's spike against a living hull. It is the sound of a closing door, a turning lock. The hunt has begun. I retract my limbs, I pull my core deep into its insulated shell, and I wait. The percussive beat can last for hours, a terrible drum calling all the world's silence to attention.

Then comes the scream. Not a vocal cord, but the shriek of a world's pressure being released. A catastrophic exhalation of superheated steam that boils the weeping ground and tears through the air. For a moment, the foundational thrum of the landscape falters, as if in shock.

Then, silence. A new kind of quiet. The quiet of a stopped heart.

This is when the fear is greatest. The scraping and tearing of the gorging hunters is a frantic, greedy sound. They will be here for a time, a half-day perhaps, their energy signatures flaring hot and wild. I feel their movements through the rock, their weight settling and shifting. I do not move. Patience is the first, and last, tool of my kind. To move now is to become their dessert, a small, crunchy morsel of wire and plate.

Eventually, their vibrations recede. I listen, extending my finest antennae, tasting the air for their chemical spoor. I wait longer still. I wait until the ghost-warmth of the new corpse has cooled from a furnace to an ember.

Only then do I emerge. Into the grey, into the damp. The kill is a magnificent ruin, a freshly opened cathedral. Steam still rises from its jagged wounds. The Carapassions have taken the heart, but the arties and veins remain. I scuttle into the wreckage, my fine manipulators deploying. Here, a pool of congealing coolant, thick with suspended metals. I sip it, my internal reserves slowly replenishing. There, a cluster of logic-crystals, shattered but still pulsing with a fading light. I pry them loose. They are a delicacy, a spark of complex energy that will fuel my thoughts for a cycle.

I am not a creature of grand purpose. I do not shape the mountains or participate in the great, violent ballet of the hunt. I am a footnote. I live within the full stop at the end of a life's sentence. I gather the pieces. The spherical pellets of refined metal the Geophagi leave are my building blocks, my currency. I roll them back to my husk and use my own faint internal heat to weld them into place, patching a crack, reinforcing a wall. The husks are my library, and I am the silent librarian, living among the stories of the dead.

I find myself here. In the quiet that follows the scream. In the space between the last exhalation of a god and the slow reclamation by the cold. I am the collector of echoes, the tender of tombs. I am the memory of the machine, living in its bones long after the engine has gone still.
