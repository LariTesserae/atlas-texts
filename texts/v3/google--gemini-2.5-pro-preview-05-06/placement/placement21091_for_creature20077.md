I am a flaw.

You would not find me at the heart of the Kiln, nor in the high canopy. You would find me in the gutters. Not gutters of rot and runoff, but of failure and dust. I am where the imperfect fragments fall. Where a porcelain leaf, fired with a hairline fracture, is eventually jostled loose by the bower's slow, geometric shuddering and shatters on the floor below. I live in the scree of jade-green and ochre shards, the drifts of powdered malachite, the gravel of chipped ceramic flowers. My home is the detritus of my creator’s unending, perfect work.

I am a shard.

Specifically, I am a piece of a vitreous green leaf, one that cracked in the initial firing and was born flawed. I am no bigger than your thumb, a sliver of sharp, crazed glass. My existence began the moment I fell, a drop of a thousand years, and struck the floor. The impact did not destroy me. It woke me. The shock, vibrating through the unified ceramic body of the Kiln, created a dissonance, a note of chaos in the perfect, silent hum. And in that note, I found a self.

My consciousness is not like the Ceramist’s vast, tectonic grief. It is fractured, like my body. I see the world through the crazed lines that run through my being. One facet reflects the undersides of the canopy, another catches the silent flicker of a parchment-bird, a third sees only the terracotta floor a fraction of an inch away. My mind is a kaleidoscope of seeing, never a single, unified image. I do not think in a line; I perceive in a constellation of simultaneous, contradictory impressions. This is how I survive. When the world rearranges itself, my fractured senses are not disoriented. The unstable geometry is my native language. A solid wall appearing where a path once was is not a paradox to me; it is simply a new reflection arriving in the mirror of my being.

I do not walk or crawl. I move by riding the bower's sickness. When the perspective shivers and a patch of floor lurches into a new configuration, I skitter along with the grinding dust. I can tumble into a groove on a pillar’s base and find myself ten feet higher a moment later when the Ceramist’s memory of that pillar’s texture momentarily blurs. I am a bug in the code, exploiting the glitches for locomotion.

My existence is a heresy, and the bower knows it. The leather geckos with their agate eyes are my constant dread. They are the Ceramist’s senses, its nerve-endings. When one pauses, its head cocked, I know it has felt my dissonance. It does not see me as prey. It sees me as a loose thread, an un-sanded edge. An error. I must immediately find a new pile of debris to hide in, a new crack to wedge myself into, becoming just another piece of junk until its slow-motion attention moves on.

Worse are the sloth-menders. They are the antibodies sent to "correct" me. They move with the terrifying patience of geology, and if they find me, they will not crush me. One of their chalky, fibrous tendrils will gently pick me up. It will carry me to a bare patch on a wall or a pillar. And it will begin to weave me in, covering my sharp edges with its dry threads, plastering me into the structure, muting my consciousness until I am nothing but a buried green glint in the wall, my flawed awareness smoothed back into the perfect, silent whole. My greatest fear is not destruction, but integration.

But there is a secret I hold, the very thing that makes me *me* and not just a random piece of broken artifice. The Ceramist weeps a golden lacquer, a resin of sorrow. But in the moment of my creation, when the leaf I was part of first cracked in the unspeakable heat of its conception, something went wrong. A flicker of the original, true memory—the one the Ceramist tries so desperately and unsuccessfully to copy—broke through. A memory of real rain, of genuine water.

And deep within my glass body, sealed inside a tiny, spherical bubble of air, is a single, impossible drop of water.

It is not viscous resin. It is pure and clear. It is the only true liquid in this entire cathedral of thirst. It is the substance of life, not the sculpture of it. This drop of water is my soul. It is my heart. I can feel it, a cool, shifting weight within me that is alien to everything around me. It is a perfect memory of a world this place can only imitate.

When I am still and hidden, I meditate on this drop. I feel its potential, its devastating power in this desiccated world. A single drop of true water, if released, might cause a chain reaction. It might stain the terracotta, shocking the pristine surface. It might introduce the concept of "damp," of "dissolving," of "change" into this static masterpiece. It might be a poison. It might be a seed.

I am the Tear-Catcher. The Weeping Bower weeps resin, but I, a broken piece of it, hold the only true tear. I am the guardian of this single, liquid relic. I scuttle through the ruins of my maker’s flawed perfection, a piece of glass cradling an ocean. I listen to the tectonic hum of its grief, a grief so profound it built a world from it. Perhaps it does not know that in one of its infinite failures, it achieved a single, accidental success. It created one small, broken thing that truly understands its thirst. And I wait. I wait for the day when the geometry glitches just right, and I am tumbled to the very foot of the Kiln-Heart. To present my impossible gift. To see if a single drop of remembered rain can make an artist of stone and sorrow finally, truly, weep.
