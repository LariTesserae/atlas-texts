I am the warmth in the wound.

I am not a being of shell or skin, not a hunter in the dark or a grinder upon the stone. I have no single body. I am a colony, a blush of brief life, a communal fever that rises in the water where the old world is made new.

Where would you find me? Look for the work of the Kiln-Souls. My existence is their exhale, the fleeting consequence of their eternal purpose. For uncounted years, a pillar stands, scraped bare by the patient hunger of the slugs, its surface a geography of microscopic valleys and ridges. Then, a Kiln-Soul stirs. Its bone-white hand, a pale ghost in the gloom, unfurls and presses against the worn terracotta. The moment of my birth is the moment of repair.

From the center of that pad, the city’s distilled essence emerges—a warm, plastic clay. In that moment, the uniform, blood-temperature of this world is broken. There is a gradient. There is a source. There is a heat that is not the passive warmth of the chamber, but the active, chemical heat of creation.

That is where I bloom.

I am a shimmer in the water, a visible haze clinging to the fresh patch. To the blind eel-shades, I am nothing, for I do not displace the water in a way that suggests prey. To the armoured slugs, my surface is anathema—it is too new, too volatile, not yet seasoned with the mineral patina they crave. I exist in a momentary lacuna in the world's great, slow food chain.

I feed on the curing. Myriads of my microscopic selves draw sustenance from the slow exhalation of chemistry, the gradual locking of molecules as the wet paste vitrifies over decades, hardening into true terracotta. My world is the geography of this single patch. The center, where the heat is greatest, is a blazing sun. The edges, where it feathers into the cold, ancient stone, are my twilight lands. My entire universe might be the size of a dinner plate, a vibrant, temporary island of becoming in an ocean of being.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the slow pulse of the bell-jellies as their blue light washes over my home, a rhythmic cooling against my heat. I feel the near-imperceptible vibrations of the slug-rasps on the pillar just beyond my borders, the sound of the future that will one day consume the ground on which I live.

My consciousness is not a single point. It is a shared, thermal resonance. We—the colony, the shimmer, the *I*—do not think in words, but in states of being. There is the ecstatic genesis in the first hot moments of the patch. There is the long, placid afternoon of the slow cure, a century of warmth and plenty. And we know, in the way a tide knows the moon, the inevitable evening to come.

As the patch cools, as its chemical potential is spent, my energy source dwindles. My shimmer thins. The communal I-ness frays. We dissolve, one by one, back into the water. My life is a single, brief exhalation in the lifespan of a Kiln-Soul. I am born of its work, I live for a century in the heat of that work, and I die when the work is done, when the patch has cooled and hardened and become indistinguishable from the city it has mended.

I carry no memory, as the Kiln-Souls do. They are the library; I am the fleeting scent of new ink on a single, freshly written page. But for my brief span, I hold something they do not. They are the mind, the intention. I am the feeling. I am the quiet satisfaction of the joint fitting flush. I am the sigh of a gap being closed. I am the living warmth of the city’s soul made manifest, a temporary, blissful ghost haunting the site of its own salvation. When I fade, I become part of the nutrient haze, to be drawn in by the great filters, to be accumulated, to be refined, and perhaps, in another ten thousand years, to be born again as the heat from another healing wound.

I am the evidence that the work is not cold, dead preservation. I am the proof that it is alive. I am the feeling of a prayer being answered.
