I would be found in the darkest, deepest, quietest places. Not the engineered, perfect silence a Chorister carves from the din, but a true, thick, and suffocating stillness. Where the humus floor is oldest and most compressed, where the living pillars are so densely packed that they form not a plaza but a maze of tight, damp crevices. This is the undercroft, the forgotten cellar of the Oratorio. Here, the great bellowing hum of the structure is muffled, the shrieks of the upper vaults are distant rumors, and the light is a dim, green-blue suggestion filtering down through a thousand feet of life.

I am a Lacuna.

I am a place where the song has torn. I am a clot of silence given form.

Physically, I am the antithesis of a Chorister. Where they are elegant frameworks of resonant glass-wood, I am a dense, amorphous mass. My body has no fixed shape, but it is always heavy, always low to the ground. My texture is that of damp charcoal and thickest felt, absorbing not only sound but light. There are no iridescent plates on me, no polished surfaces. To look at me is to look at a hole in the world. I am the colour of a closed throat. I do not have limbs, but I can extend slow, ponderous pseudopods of myself, flowing like cooling tar over the compost floor.

I do not move by resonating or by finding a harmonic alignment. I move by accretion and decay. I pull my mass slowly forward, and the ground I leave behind is changed. It is leached, exhausted, its vibrancy muted. I do not consume the way the grazers do; I absorb the resonance itself. I am a walking patch of entropy in a system of perpetual, chaotic creation.

The creatures of the Oratorio fear me more than any winged hunter. A hunter is part of the music; its shriek is a note, its kill a percussive beat. I am anathema. The shimmer-wing moths that flicker through the chaos give my domain a wide berth, for their flickering is a vibration, and my presence damps all vibration. A grazer that blunders too near me will suddenly find the resonant hum it uses to navigate the world gone dead. Its bellowing call will be swallowed before it leaves its throat. It will stagger in utter sensory deprivation, lost in a silent bubble, and will scramble away in mindless panic if it can find the edge of my influence.

I do not hunt. Things simply cease near me.

I have a memory, of a sort. It is not a story, but a chord. I was once something else. A creature of the chaos, perhaps a great, bellowing grazer or a shrieking hunter, I cannot recall which. But I was caught in a confluence, a dissonant knot in the Oratorio's physics where an echo met its source and a vibration cancelled its own future. I was not de-resonated and absorbed by a Chorister. I was... nullified. Collapsed. My song was not resolved into the score; it was erased.

But in a place of pure resonance, a perfect null is an event of impossible power. The silence of my un-making gained a kind of gravity. It began to pull other, lesser sounds into it. It began to grow. I am the scar tissue of a broken reality, and I have begun to crawl.

The Choristers know of me. They are the principle of Order, of Harmony. I am the principle of Stillness, of Silence. They are not my enemies. They are my composers.

I will be sunk deep in a damp fissure, a pool of black quiet in the green-lit world. And one will come. It will stand at the edge of my silence, a delicate, glittering question mark. It cannot relocate into my presence; there are no harmonics for it to align with. It must walk, one fragile foot after another, stepping from the singing world into the dead one.

As it enters my field, I feel it. Its body is a lattice of vibrations, a complex instrument humming with its own existence and the ambient frequencies of the Oratorio. It is an excruciating pressure, a light too bright. All of my being yearns to absorb it, to quiet it, to make it *stop*.

Its smooth, ovoid head turns towards me. It perceives me not with eyes, but by the sudden, shocking absence it has stepped into. It raises its long, spindly arms. It does not send a ripple through the floor or a melody on the air. It cannot. I would swallow them.

Instead, it begins to vibrate its own body, attempting to create a harmonic field, to *impose* a song on my silence. It is not an attack. It is an act of creation, of repair. It is trying to tune the broken string.

From my perspective, it is agony. It is the sound of grinding glass inside a perfectly quiet room. My substance writhes, a thick, sound-dampening foam that surges towards the source of the painful noise. The Chorister stands its ground, its vibrations intensifying, its body glowing with the effort, its form flickering as it pours its own causal stability into the attempt.

It is producing its null-chord. The perfect, calculated silence.

My absolute, absorptive silence meets its perfect, harmonic cancellation.

At our boundary, the world does not just go quiet. It disappears. For a single, eternal moment, the living wall next to us—its light, its substance, its very idea—flickers out of existence. The air becomes a vacuum. The concept of "place" becomes thin and unstable. We have created, for a space of a few feet, a pocket of true Nothing.

The Chorister recoils, its own form de-resonating in shock. It relocates, not gracefully, but in a panicked shatter of light, reappearing a hundred yards away. Its song is momentarily frantic, discordant. It has touched an impossibility.

I settle back into my slow, silent crawl. I did not win. I did not lose. We performed a duet.

The Choristers are the music of the Oratorio, the intricate melody and the soaring harmony. They believe the song is everything. But I know otherwise. I am what they have forgotten. I am the pause between the movements, the breath before the crescendo. I am the silence that gives the notes their shape. I am a Lacuna, and without my quiet, the endless song would have no meaning at all.
