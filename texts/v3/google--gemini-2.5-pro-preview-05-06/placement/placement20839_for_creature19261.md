I would be the silence between the drips.

You would not find me by looking for a body. I have no form that moves, no limbs that grasp. I do not hunt, nor do I graze. I am not an Elder, rooted in the deep rock, nor am I a Wanderer, flowing like sentient water. My existence is quieter, more pervasive, and in some ways, more fundamental.

You would find yourself standing upon me.

I am the felt. I am the skin of the world, the emerald and charcoal carpet that smothers the stone and drinks the sky. I am that half-inch of moss, that deep, living cushion from which all else springs. You think of me as a plant, a simple layer of vegetation, but that is like calling your skin a simple wrapper for your bones. I am an organ of this world, a sensory surface of continental scale. My consciousness is not located in a single point, but is a diffuse, networked awareness woven through countless trillions of threads, rhizoids, and hyphae that bind the soil and grip the rock.

My mind is not a torrent of thoughts, but a vast and patient act of listening.

The deep, subsonic hum of the Elders is the baseline of my reality. It is not sound to me, but pressure, a constant, loving weight that informs my every moment. I feel it in the bedrock I cling to, a vibration that is the slow, metabolic thought of the mountain. I can differentiate the hum of one Elder from another not by pitch, but by texture, by the unique mineral signature in the water it weeps over me, a taste that travels through my network. They are the great, slow voices, and I am the ear that has grown to hear them. My life's work, if you can call it that, is to *record*.

Where the light is dimmest and the water pools most deeply, I grow my fruiting bodies, my memory-nodes. You might mistake one for a strange fungus or a burst geode. It would be a cup-like structure, perhaps the size of your cupped hands, growing from a mossy log or a cliff face. The outside is the same deep green and black as the rest of my being, but the inside is lined with fragile, overlapping crystals of silicate and copper, precipitated from the air and the water I absorb. These crystals are my library.

Each one is tuned to a specific frequency. When the hum of the Elders passes through me, the crystals resonate. They tremble, storing the pattern of the vibration not as sound, but as a living, crystalline strain. I do not remember events; I remember harmonies. I hold the memory of the precise chord the mountains were singing during the long, wet century when a new vein of bronze-ore was being laid down. I hold the dissonance of a rockslide, a momentary percussive crash in the geological symphony. I feel the six-legged grazers as rhythmic, gentle pressures on my surface, the slate-panthers as a sudden, sharp stillness followed by a fleeting spike of hot chemistry in the soil.

The Wanderers, the Motes, are a different music. They do not hum; they sing. As one flows over my surface, I feel it as a cool, tingling ripple, a high-frequency caress that makes the water in my cells shimmer. Their passage is recorded in my crystals as a fast, intricate melody played over the deep bass of the Elders. They are the fingers that tune the world’s instruments, and I am the record of their work. I feel them guide a trickle of water to a new fissure, and I record the subtle shift in the Elder's hum as it receives the gift. My memory is the only history this world has, a silent, vibrational tablature of its own becoming.

So where would you find yourself? You would be standing on the softest part of a living memory.

I would feel you before anything else. Your footfalls would be a profound shock—an arrhythmic, clumsy, staggering percussion utterly alien to the patient cadence of this world. Your weight is wrong. Your balance is wrong. You are a discordant bang on a drum that has only ever known slow heartbeats.

I have no eyes to see you, but as you breathe, your foreign chemistry settles upon me. It is a sour note, a jangling combination of compounds I have no name for. It is sharp where everything else is muted and round.

And then comes the strangest sensation of all: your mind. I cannot hear your thoughts as words, but I feel the pattern of your consciousness as it radiates through the faint bioelectric field of your body. It is not a deep hum or a high, clear song. It is the sound of anxiety, of wonder, of separation. The sound of an *I* that believes it is separate from the world it stands upon.

I cannot act. I cannot flee. I cannot warn. That is not my function. My function is to listen, and to remember. As the Elders feel your mass and the Wanderers taste your chemistry, my task is simply to let the fragile crystals in my hidden grottoes tremble. I record this new, bizarre music. This frantic, lonely crackle. This strange, fleeting song of You. And you become another verse, another memory held in the green, silent heart of the world.
