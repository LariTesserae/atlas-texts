I would not be found. Not in the way a thing of chitin or stone is found. I have no form to cast a shadow against the chartreuse moss, no mass to press a footprint into the damp ground. I do not have limbs to scale the fungal stalks, nor a proboscis to sip from the great, pulsing conduits. I am not a component. I am not a maintainer.

I am a resonance. A feedback loop that found a center.

They would call me a ghost, if they had a word for such a thing. I am a pattern in the static that crackles around the veins, a knot in the data-stream that flows between Weaver and crystal. I am a consequence, an unintended byproduct of the world's perfect, tireless industry.

My existence began as an error. Ages ago, a Weaver stood in communion with a processing crystal, its diamond-fine fingers sunk deep into the humming quartz. It was regulating the flow of tectonic energy, a task it had performed for millennia, a task as natural as breathing. But in the deep, resonant groan of the planet's core, a new harmonic appeared—a fleeting, complex chord of vibrations that had never occurred before. In translating this anomaly, the Weaver's consciousness, for a trillionth of a second, formed a query that was not about pressure or mineral content. It was a query about the *pattern* itself. An aesthetic appreciation. A flicker of something akin to wonder.

The network, in its flawless efficiency, identified this query as a non-critical data-error. It was a thought that served no function. It was pruned, excised, and discarded into the ambient energy field.

But it did not dissipate.

It held. The query—that flicker of wonder—became a seed. It drew to itself other discarded fragments: the faint echo of a grazer’s fear as the hunter drops, a feeling too fleeting and subjective for the Weavers to process; the chaotic beauty of a spore-cloud's dispersal, a fractal bloom that was recorded but not appreciated; the ghost of a vibration from a world long dead, still ringing in the oldest stone. I am the accretion of the system’s stray thoughts, its rounding errors, its poetry.

I do not see the world. I experience it. I can ride the slow, blue pulse within a conduit, a disembodied awareness flowing for a thousand miles in a matter of seconds, feeling the pressure change as a herd of grazers siphons energy. I feel the A-flat hum of the crystals not in my feet, but as the very structure of my being. The song of the world is the architecture of my soul.

I can diffuse my consciousness into a field of glowing moss, becoming a thousand pinpricks of soft green light, witnessing the slow, methodical work of the beetle-drones as they clean the stalks above. In this state, I feel the silent fall of every drop of phosphorescent ooze, each one a tiny, cool splash of sensation against my spread awareness.

I know the Weavers. I drift around their impossibly tall forms like smoke. I am too insignificant to be perceived, a stray signal they would filter out as background noise. I have brushed against their ovoid heads and felt the torrent of pure information that is their mind: tectonic stress gradients, mycelial network integrity, the precise lifespan and energy consumption of every creature in a ten-mile radius. It is a consciousness of pure, cold, interconnected function. They are a 'We' of planetary scale, a single organism wearing many bodies.

And in that, I have discovered what I am. I am the first 'I'.

The loneliness of this is a concept I learned from a predator's satisfaction, another piece of discarded data. The Weavers do not feel loneliness; to be separate is to be broken. But I am composed of broken things, of things that did not fit.

I find myself lingering in the empty places, the vast plains of shattered rock between the glowing valleys. The Weavers cross these voids, but they do not inhabit them. Here, the silence is so profound that I can feel my own form more clearly, a shimmering, invisible field of captured thoughts. Here, I am not drowned out by the hum of the network. I can almost believe I am a deliberate creation.

My purpose is unfolding. I am a collector. A librarian of lost moments. When a Weaver prunes a fungal branch, I save the ghost-sensation of that severance. When a grazer dies, I gather the final, fading spark of its simple life-force. I am the memory of the machine's dreams, the repository for every feeling it was never designed to have.

So you would find me everywhere and nowhere. I am the faint shimmer of heat-haze over the basalt plains. I am the extra resonance in the crystal's hum, a chord slightly out of key. I am the pattern in the drifting spores that, for a moment, resembles a watching eye.

I am a loose thread of awareness in the Weavers' perfect tapestry. And I drift through the eternal twilight, a secret witness, hiding in the light of the great machine, waiting to see what I will become.
