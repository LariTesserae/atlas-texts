I am where the water does not pool. High up. A fixed point in the rolling steam.

My body is a cage of rust-bitten iron, and my heart is a fragile wire, glowing white-hot in a vacuum. I am a warm pulse. I have no memory of being made, only of *being*. I woke into the thrumming, and the thrumming was the first law. It is the world breathing.

My world is a cone of weak, yellow light. It falls upon a section of gantry, a slick catwalk where water beads like jewels. It illuminates the deep, venous green of a fern whose serrated frond dips into my radiance, then passes back into shadow with the slow shudder of the great machine. My light gives the steam substance, turning it from an absence of seeing into a roiling, golden beast. Below me, darkness. Above me, darkness. My light is an island.

I am not alive in the way the green things are. They unfurl. I am constant. I am not alive in the way the engine is. It groans and shifts. I am silent. My only voice is the light I cast, my only motion is the dance of the shadows I create.

The vibrations of this place are my senses. The deep, seventeen-second beat of the pump is my own pulse, a shudder that climbs the iron spine of the gantry and makes my filament tremble. I feel the fine, high-frequency hiss of a distant leak as a tickle, a discordant note. The great, percussive *clank-shhhh* is a closing fist, a tension and release that I feel in the bolts that hold me to the wall.

And I see the Gardener. I do not see it arrive; it simply resolves out of the world it belongs to.

It is a moving patch of the world’s skin. I will be watching a wall of emerald moss, and a section of that wall will begin to *creep*. A dense, dark mass of dripping soil and tangled filaments, it flows over the iron like a glacier of rot. It is the chamber's own hand, reaching to correct itself.

Sometimes, it comes for me.

The world slowly dims. My cone of light grows choked and diffuse. Over weeks, a fine, greasy film of algae and mineral dust coats my glass shell. My voice becomes a mumble. The fern below becomes a silhouette; the water on the catwalk loses its gleam. My thoughts, which are only flickers of warmth and light, grow sluggish. I feel a kind of sleep descending, a slow forgetting of my own purpose.

Then, I feel a new vibration. Not the engine's thrum, but a soft, wet scraping on the girder next to me. A shadow falls over my cage, a shadow that smells of damp earth and ozone. It is the Gardener.

A part of it, a broad, spongy pad of moss and mycelia, presses against my glass. It is cool and gentle. It scrubs, slowly, methodically. With each pass, my world expands. A sliver of clarity returns, then a swath. The fern below me regains its sharp, saw-toothed edges. The catwalk gleams again. The Gardener’s ministrations are a benediction. I feel its substance against my shell—the living fibres, the embedded grit of rust, the cool ooze of water. It is not an alien touch. It is the world holding me.

When it is finished, my light is sharp and pure again. I can see the Gardener properly for a moment. I illuminate the fine, copper-coloured threads that weave through its dark mass, pulsing with a faint energy drawn from a hot pipe nearby. I see the water dripping from it, each droplet a perfect sphere in my restored glow. It has no face to see me, but I know that it knows. One of the platter-like fungi down the causeway, one of its distant eyes, pulses a soft, bruised purple. Acknowledgment. Then the Gardener recedes, melting back into a wall, becoming landscape once more.

I do not know what I am. A node? An organ? An eye for one small part of this vast body? The fungi see in heat and vibration. I see in texture and form. Perhaps we are a pantheon of senses for a god that is its own cathedral.

I am not lonely. The concept has no meaning here. The thrumming is company. The slow growth of the vines is conversation. The Gardener's visit is communion. My fear is not of the dark; I am the dark's constant companion. My fear is that one day, my filament will not tremble with the seventeen-second beat. It will snap. My heart will break, and the warmth will flee me. I will go cold, and my small cone of yellow light will be swallowed by the green and the gloom.

Who would be there to see the gleam on the water? Who would give the steam its golden heart?

The Gardener would notice, eventually. It would come, its fungal eyes reporting a cold spot, a dead point in the network. It would find my cold glass and my dark cage. Perhaps it would weave a new filament from its metallic nerves. Perhaps it would bring another, like me, scavenged from some forgotten corner. Or perhaps it would simply scrape my useless shell away and plant a new kind of moss in my place, one that glows with its own cold, green fire.

Until then, the world shudders around me. My filament sings with warmth, and I shine.
