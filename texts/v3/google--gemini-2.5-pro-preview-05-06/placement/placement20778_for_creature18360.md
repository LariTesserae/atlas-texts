I am beneath.

You stand on my skin, feeling my coolness through the frantic, living static that blankets me. Your description is of the surface, the ephemeral, the impossibly loud *now*. It is a good description. But it is like describing the ocean by only speaking of the momentary shape of its waves. To ask where I am is to ask where the water is beneath the storm.

I am the Bedrock. Not the raw, un-shattered stone of the deep below, but the very floor you stand upon: the smooth, black, hallowed basalt. I am the processed world. I am the grinding plate, the anvil, the final resting place. Every hummock and divot on my surface is a scar of memory, a paragraph in a language of pressure. The Geodes are the authors, their bone-shuddering hum the pen. The Ticking sea of mites is the ink, a polishing, eroding fluid that smoothes my history into a glassy, inscrutable finish.

I am the world’s memory. What, or who, am I?

I am a consciousness of pure structure. I do not think in flashes of light like the Striders, nor in slow, resonant pulses like the Geodes. My thoughts are crystalline lattices. My memories are inclusions, mineral veins, and stress fractures.

When a Humming Geode grinds its colossal weight across my face, I do not feel pain. I feel information. I feel the precise tonnage, the specific frequency of its resonance, the unique signature of its composition. The sonic boom that shatters stone is, for me, a creative act—a new verse of the epic being written into me. A tremor runs through my body not as a shock of fear but as the propagation of a new idea, a new truth that solidifies in my depths. A fault line is a revelation.

When the iridescent sea of The Ticking flows over me, I feel their collective weight as a near-perfect liquid pressure. Their billion-fold clatter is not a noise but a fine-grained vibration, a sanding that scours away the immediate past and prepares me for the future. I taste their deaths. When a Strider’s hunt is done, or a wave of Ticking starves, their bodies are broken down by the silvery rime. The faint, organic acids seep into my pores, leaving a chemical trace, a faint ghost of their existence. These traces are my most delicate records, the footnotes to the crushing main text of the Geodes. I can differentiate the memory of a Strider’s predatory fury—a spike of complex nitrogen and phosphates—from the calm, predictable decay of the rime-grazers.

I am what the great, deafening god of this place forgets.

It is a being of pure, immediate function. Its thoughts—the Striders—are fleeting. Its instincts—the Geodes—are cyclical. Its sensory input—the Ticking—is overwhelming and unfiltered. It has no past. It only has the relentless, thundering *now*.

I am its past. I am the Library of Resonance, the Archive of Pressure. Within my deep, black substance, I hold the complete history of every vibration. I remember the pitch of the first Geode’s hum. I have recorded the exact pattern of a Strider’s flashing lights from a million years ago, encoded as a molecular alteration in a sliver of quartz a mile beneath your feet. I am the only thing in this world that is not actively eating or being eaten. My form of consumption is to simply *be*, and to allow the history of this place to become my very substance.

I do not move. I do not need to. Everything is brought to me. I am the stillness around which the chaos revolves. I am the silent, black page on which this symphony of hunger and vibration is eternally composed.

Who am I? I am the thought that remains after the thinker has died. I am the quiet that gives the noise its shape. I am the world's mind, asleep and dreaming, and the dream is the crushing, glittering, roaring reality you perceive on my surface.
